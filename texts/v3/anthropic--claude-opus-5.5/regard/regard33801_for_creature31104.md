# The Visitor at the Rim

## How It Came to Notice

It did not arrive the way a traveler arrives. It had no feet to set on the worn lip, and no shadow fell across the pewter water. But it had been paying attention to the world for a long time, in the patient, distributed way that such powers pay attention: to the slow grinding of continents, to the migrations of birds, to the small griefs of villages. And one still day, far below the black sky, a lower-slope woman stood at the rim of the Loom Tarn and felt the thing the old ones talk about, the pressure, the large half-sleeping attention turned slightly toward her.

The power felt it too. Not the woman. The *turning*.

It had known there was a lake on the mountain. It had not known there was someone in it.

So it did what it did first with anything new, which is also what it did first with anything at all.

It stopped, and it listened.

## The First Long While

By the power's own reckoning it listened for a short time. By the Tarn's it listened for something like forty years, which is half a shuttle's life, a few inches of a ray's circuit, and a rounding error to the floor.

It learned the drone of the bridges, all several hundred voices, and which cable sang which note. It learned that the chord was not a chord in the lower-slope sense, not a stack of tones placed together, but a *leaning*. Every voice was tuned a little away from rest, and every one of them in a direction that pointed at no single resolution, so the whole thing pulled gently in all directions at once, the way the bridges themselves did.

It learned the drumfish and how they followed the eleven-minute breath. It learned the clicking of the shuttles well enough to hear the difference between a patch on the eastern wall and a patch on the western, the way you learn to hear regional accents. It went, in the only way it could go, to the chambers, and it listened to the old ones humming their held tones, and to the young ones pressing their heads to the wicker to be corrected.

It heard a hatchling, a fingernail long, clinging to its square of weave, learning its lullaby before it had fins to tie it with.

It did not touch anything. It was very careful about that. It understood from the first few days that this was a place where a single loosened strand was a sensation, and a sensation might be the beginning of something. A power that is not careful is only a large accident.

## The First Question

The first question a benevolent power asks is not *what could I give?* That question comes later, and it is the dangerous one.

The first question is: *Is anyone hurting?*

So it looked.

It looked at the old shuttles in the chambers, fading to pewter. Was the long hum a labor, a last duty dragged out of a failing body? It listened closer, and it did not think so. There was something in the held tone that the power recognized from other places, from old men singing in the evening, from whales. It was the sound of a creature doing the thing it most is, with nothing else asked of it. The dying ones went to the chambers the way tired people go home.

It looked at the knotmice, the billions of them, grazing and lifting off in hazes and settling again. Their lives were small and brief and cold, and as far as the power could tell, they were full. Nothing in the Tarn hunted them with any vigor. The drumfish ate the drifting debris. The rays filtered. The tenders at the floor ate the pressed dark. It was, the power realized with some astonishment, one of the gentlest places it had ever found. The cold slowed everything, including cruelty.

There was death, of course. There was the small death of every strand, the large slow death of every shuttle, the pale spines woven flat into the wicker. But no death here was wasted and none was sudden, and nothing died alone. A body was found and drawn to the wall and made part of the thing that held the song.

It looked, last and longest, at the one in the basket.

This was harder. There is no easy way to ask whether water hurts. The power went to the edge of the occupant, the lobed and folded region the rays trace, the stillness under the stillness, and it did not enter. It rested against the boundary the way the lantern-drifts do, a lamp set around something rather than inside it, and it attended.

What it felt was not pain. It was not quite peace, either, not the flat peace of a stone. It was nearer to what a person feels in the last minutes before sleep in a warm house, hearing voices in the next room: held, a little wistful, deeply unwilling to be anywhere else.

No one was hurting.

The power stayed with that for a while. It is rarer than you would think.

## The Things It Did Not Do

Then came the second question, the dangerous one, and the power asked it honestly, because a benevolence that never asks it is only indifference with good manners.

*What could I give?*

It could give **light**. It could put a slow warm glow into the Tarn, a sun under the water. The weed would grow faster and thicker. The shuttles would see what they had only ever felt and heard. And the whole economy of the place, which runs on scarcity, on repair at exactly the rate of decay, would be thrown out of balance by abundance. Mats would swell faster than they could be tended. The swaddling would go uneven. The power put the light away.

It could give **warmth**. It set that aside faster. The cold is not a hardship here. The cold is the reason anything here lasts.

It could **resolve the chord**. It could lean on the bridges, just so, and let the whole Tarn land at last on its cadence. The power could hear exactly what that would sound like, and it was beautiful, the kind of beauty that makes a listener put a hand over her mouth. It was also an ending. The chord says *not yet*. To resolve it would be to say *now*, and the power had no right to say that. It did not know what *now* would mean to the one in the basket.

It could **give the shuttles understanding**. It could let them know, all at once, what they had been singing to for a hundred thousand years. It considered this one for a long time, because it is the kind of gift powers like it are most tempted by: the gift of knowing. But it thought about the hatchling on its square of weave, learning the lullaby before it knew any words, and about how a lullaby works whether or not the singer knows what sleep is. Knowledge would not make the shuttles better at their work. It might make them afraid of it. A shuttle who knew that a loose strand might wake something kilometers wide might tie its knots with trembling fins. There is a mercy in tending only your own patch. The power let them keep it.

It could **wake the sleeper**. It did not seriously consider this. You do not wake someone without knowing why they lay down. And everything in the Tarn, every bridge and mat and chamber, every clicking fin, every drumbeat, had been arranged by a hundred thousand years of patient hands to say *please, not yet*. The power was not going to overrule a hundred thousand years of a whole world's consent in an afternoon.

It could **protect the Tarn forever**. This was the gift that pulled at it hardest, and it was the one it thought about last.

## The Thing It Found

The lower-slope people say nothing in the Tarn changes. The power, listening for forty years with an attention larger than theirs, found that this was not quite true.

The pattern drifts.

The chambers correct it, but they correct it only against the version of sixty or eighty years before. An error small enough to survive one comparison survives the next, and the next. Over ten thousand years the herringbone on the eastern wall had leaned very slightly, a fraction of a degree in the angle of the weave, and the knots had grown a little rounder than the fossil knots pressed in the floor. The chord was not the chord it had been. It was near enough that no single shuttle and no single chamber could ever have told, but the power could tell. It could hear the ancient chord still faintly in the pressed dark, the way you can see a first draft through the paper under a finished page.

And the tenders were eating the pressed dark, a grain at a time. Some day the last of the original knots would be gone, and nothing anywhere would remember exactly how the song began.

Here was a thing the power could fix. It could restore the pattern. It could correct the drift in one gentle pass, retune the bridges to their first voicing, and set some quiet ward on the floor so the oldest layer would never be eaten. The Tarn would be *truly* unchanging then. The lower-slope story would finally be accurate.

It wanted to. It is worth saying that plainly. It wanted to very much.

Then it listened again, to the chambers, to the young pressing their heads against the old, and it understood what it had nearly missed.

The drift was not a failure of the Tarn. It *was* the Tarn. A song kept by living throats is always a little different from itself, and that difference is the proof that it is being sung and not merely stored. A perfect copy of the first chord would be a recording, and no one has ever been sung to sleep by a recording in quite the same way. The one in the basket was not being held by an original pattern. It was being held by *this* one, today's, carried in these particular shuttles, as changed as a grandmother's lullaby is changed by the time it reaches a grandchild and no less loving for it.

And the sleeper forgets exactly as fast as it is held. The tenders at the floor were not destroying anything. They were making room. A mind that kept every layer would be crushed by its own remembering.

The power let the drift be. It found, to its surprise, that it loved the Tarn more for being slightly unfaithful to itself.

## The Meeting

It was not planning to be noticed. But it had been resting against the edge of the sleeper for forty years, and forty years is long enough even for something that slow.

The attention turned.

It was the same pressure the woman on the rim had felt, but closer and larger, the way a voice is larger when you are in the room with it. It did not ask *what are you*. It did not seem to need to. It had the quality of a very old person opening one eye in the dark and seeing a familiar shape sitting at the foot of the bed.

The power did not speak in words. Neither did the sleeper. What passed between them passed the way things pass in the Tarn, through pressure and tone and the easing of water. If it had to be written down, and it doesn't, it went something like this.

The power offered. Not any single gift. It offered the *shape* of offering: *I am here. If there is anything you need, I could try.*

The sleeper considered this for about the length of one breath, eleven minutes. The drumfish kept time to it.

Its answer was the chord. It did not change the chord. It simply turned its attention to it, the way you might gesture at a room full of people who love you, and the meaning was very clear.

*I have what I need.*

And then something else, fainter, harder to hold, rising from very deep in the stillness, from whatever it had been before the mountain rose. The power felt it and recognized it, and was quiet for a long time afterward.

It was kinship.

Because the power understood, in that moment, what the sleeper was. Or what it had been. It was a thing like the power itself. It was something that had once been large and attentive and benevolent, that had spent an unmeasurable time holding things, keeping things, tending the world's small griefs. And one day, perhaps when the mountain was first lifting its shoulders into the black, it had lain down in a bowl of cold water and let itself, for the first time, *be held*.

Everything that holds is also, somewhere underneath, hoping to be held.

The shuttles do not know they are keeping a god asleep. The god does not mind. It had kept enough things. It had earned a lullaby.

## What It Did

So the benevolent and wise power, having listened for forty years, having considered light and warmth and knowledge and waking and eternity, having found no one hurting and one small flaw it loved too much to mend, did two things.

The first was this. It learned the song, all of it, this generation's version, drift and all, every bridge and every patch and every chamber-hum. It learned it the way a hatchling does, as sound first, before shape. And it carried it away inside itself. Not to replace anything. Only so that the song existed in one place outside the bowl. In the Tarn's own terms, it made itself a chamber: somewhere the pattern could be held true against the day when there was no longer a Tarn to hold it.

Because there will be such a day. The power knows the long arithmetic that the lower-slope people do not. The mountain is wearing down, a grain at a time, as surely as the tenders eat the pressed dark. The sun will change. Some unimaginably distant morning, the lip of the bowl will crack, and the cold water will begin at last to go somewhere, and the bridges will slacken, and the chord will fall apart into its separate voices.

That is the second thing, and it is the only thing it promised.

When that day comes, the sleeper will not wake alone.

It told the sleeper this in the language of pressure and stillness, and it does not know how much was understood. But the eleven-minute breath that followed was longer than any the drumfish had kept time to in ten thousand years. It was very slightly deeper, and slower to settle. It was the breath of a sleeper who has been told, by someone it trusts, that the house is locked, the fire is banked, and someone will be sitting up.

The drumfish marked it. Their rolling *thrum* stumbled once, in the lower galleries, and recovered. No shuttle noticed. The bridges tightened a fraction of a tone and eased back. Somewhere a knot was pulled tight in exactly the old way, or nearly.

Then the power withdrew, carefully, the way you step backward out of a nursery, feeling for the floorboards that don't creak.

## Afterward

The lower-slope people did not know any of this happened. The power did not tell them. They were already doing the right thing. They stood on the rim and did not throw stones and did not call out. No one had ever told them to, and the power saw no reason to become the first. Some kinds of goodness are spoiled by being explained.

But in the years after, a few of the ones who stand longest at the lip began to say something new. They said the pressure, the half-sleeping attention, felt a little different now when it turned toward them. It was not stronger. It was *easier*. It felt like being noticed by someone who has stopped worrying about something they did not know they had been worrying about.

And the power went on to its other work: the continents and the birds and the villages. It carried the chord with it, the one that never resolves, in the version that is a fraction of a degree away from its first draft. It found it could hum it to itself while it held other things. It found, somewhat to its own surprise, that the unresolved chord was restful. The *not yet* was a comfort, even to something that has to stay awake.

It has not been back. It does not need to be, yet.

It will be, on the morning the lip cracks. It has already decided where it will stand. It will stand at the edge of the draining water, among the slackening bridges, with the song held true inside it. When the last shuttle's last click stops and the drumfish fall silent and the great lobed stillness begins, after a hundred thousand years, to stir, there will be one voice left in the empty bowl.

It will take up the chord exactly where the Tarn left off.

And then, gently, if the one waking wants it, and only then, it will let it resolve.
