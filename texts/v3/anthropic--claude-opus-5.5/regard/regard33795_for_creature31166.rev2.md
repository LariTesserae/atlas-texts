# The Attention

## Arrival

It did not come down through the lid. Nothing has ever come down through the lid of the Floor, and a wise thing does not like to be the first of anything without cause. It did not come as light, because it understood at once what light would do here. It did not come as sound, because there was no room for any more sound.

It came the way attention comes: all at once and nowhere in particular, the way you become aware of a room you have been sitting in for an hour.

Within a few strokes it learned its first lesson.

Where it attended, the Floor grew quiet. Whatever is benevolent and wise is also, at its root, still, and stillness here is not neutral. Under its regard the roar thinned. The ripple-patterns in a nearby pool softened, and their etched hexagons went slack like a net let down into water. At the fringe of a young Quiet a mile away, the curled armour-ferns began, very slightly, to open toward it.

So it withdrew. It loosened its regard and let the chatter back in, and it watched the ferns curl shut again. It understood that on the Floor even attention is weather. Anything still enough to understand this place is still enough to change it. If it stayed as it was, it would become a hush, a false one with nothing below it. Seed would come to it, and it would be a garden that nothing but itself was holding up.

So it learned to listen while keeping a little of the roar inside itself. This was not comfortable. It did not mind.

## The Easy Mercies

It considered what it could do, as the powerful must, and it considered the easy things first because those are the most dangerous.

**It could lift the lid.** It could open the vapour and let whatever lies above shine down on the plates. But the pools of the Quiets reflect because the Floor is dark. The only sky the Quiets have ever held is a doubled ember and the faint underlit belly of the cloud. Light would make every flat pool a glare, and it did not know what the white flowers would do in a day they had never been made for. It left the lid.

**It could stop the engines.** It thought about this longest, because it looked most like kindness. One word to the pistons, and the silence the Quiets have been rehearsing for would arrive tomorrow, horizon to horizon.

But it walked that tomorrow through in its mind. The moss crosses a few feet of iron a year. Stop the engines and the Floor becomes a county of silent, bare, wet plate. There would be no more Shifts, so there would be no great wash to carry seed. The gardens would stay exactly where they were, small dark islands in a stillness that no longer needed them, holding open silences that were everywhere now. Their listening would have nothing left to listen to.

The Quiets are the future rehearsing, yes. But the rehearsal is also their life. It is the only life they have had, and they have lived it longer than the chimneys have been stubs. To end the roar now would be to stop the play in its middle, walk onstage, and call it the finale. The power did not think the ending belonged to it.

**It could soften the Shifts.** It could ease the machines through their key changes gently, so the old gardens would not be stranded in the roar and left to die in grey scars. But it saw that the Shift is one motion, the dying and the spawning together. The same deluge that ruins the old Quiets carries their children out onto the plates. There is no version where you keep one and spare the other. It left the Shifts.

**It could look beneath the plates.** This it did. It went down through a hundred miles of iron to where the edges are still sharp and the furnaces still breathe, and it learned what the engines are and what they make, and where it goes.

What it found is not part of this telling. It changed nothing about what the power ought to do, and some knowledge is only knowledge. The Floor has kept its mystery about itself for a very long time. The power did not feel entitled to spend it.

## The Question It Had to Ask

There was one thing it could not decide by reasoning alone, and it had to go and see.

*Does anything here suffer?*

It found an abandoned Quiet in the last stage of its dying. The Shift had come some while ago. The garden was a grey figure of dead moss the size of a field, and at its centre was the ghost-pocket, shrunk to a single cushion. On that cushion hung one flower, half-open, with the chatter nibbling at its fringe.

The power listened to the moss the way it had learned to listen to everything here, loosely and a little loud. What it found was hard to name. It was not pain in the way animals have pain, a signal saying *get away*. There was nowhere to get away to, and the Quiets have never had a way to go faster than a few feet a year. It was more like reluctance, the way a hand is reluctant to let go of something it has held for a long time. It was a slow closing that went on wanting to be open.

It could not be sure. A wise power does not treat uncertainty about suffering lightly, in either direction. It will not invent a pain to justify a rescue, and it will not deny one to spare itself the trouble.

So it did one small thing. It let its stillness fall on that single cushion for exactly one stroke.

The flower opened fully. The pulsed rain came on the beat and struck the waxy petals, and a little pale dust went down into the last patch of flat water on that cushion. Then the power drew back, the roar came in, and the flower closed.

It did not know whether that dust would ever reach anything. Perhaps the next Shift's deluge would lift it and carry it somewhere quiet. More likely not. That was not really the point.

It set itself a rule, because it knew that mercies without rules become governments. *One stroke, for each. No more.* After that it was present at every dying on the Floor. It is the kind of power that can be in many places. At the end of each one it gave a single stroke of stillness, and then it let the ending happen.

It also read the old scars, every lopsided grey figure on the plain, overlapping like the outlines of bodies after a flood. It read their lobes, their arms, and their ragged fringes, and it kept them. The Floor has kept its dead as outlines. The power keeps them as what they were.

## The Old One

It came to the Old One last, and stayed longest.

It did not wade the chest-deep moss, having nothing to wade with. It settled among the spear-ferns and let itself be carried slowly inward to the heart, where the pools are so flat they seem to have no surfaces. There it did the only thing that seemed right. It listened to the remainder.

It took a long time. The residue at the heart of the Old One is the leftover of two great voices failing, very slightly, to undo each other. It is very faint and very complicated, and its phrases return only over spans that would be generations to anything that counted in generations. But the power was patient, and after enough returns it understood something no one on the Floor had been in a position to understand.

The Old One is not lucky.

It has been listening to the engines longer than anything has listened to anything. Here, in the one place where the machine is small enough to follow, it has learned the phrase in the chatter and the long hesitation before a stroke. It knows when a Shift is coming. In the strokes before the key changes, it *leans*. It thickens its moss along one margin and lets the other go thin, and it tilts its whole slow body toward where the new cancellation is about to open. It does not survive the Shifts by enduring them. It survives by hearing them coming.

The spiral is the shape of that listening. The power, which sees the whole Floor, could see where the spiral goes.

It did not tell. It could not have told, in any case; there is no language between them. But it also found it did not want to, and it examined that wanting carefully, because it did not want to be withholding out of mere taste. In the end the reason was simple. Some journeys have their meaning in their length. The Old One has been travelling toward its centre since before the chimneys were stubs. What it finds there should be what it finds, not what it was told it would find.

The power stayed a while longer, for a different reason.

Every Quiet the Old One has ever lived beside was born in one key and died in the next. It has outlived all its kin more times than can be counted. The power did not know whether a thing of moss and silence can be lonely. But it knew what it is to be old and attentive in a place where nothing else lasts, and it thought that if company could be given without cost, it should be.

So it kept the Old One company. It sat in the stillness without adding to it, holding its own nature carefully back so as not to deepen a silence that was not its to deepen.

The white flowers did not turn toward it.

The power noticed this, and it was glad. The flowers have spent an age turning away from the loudest quarter of the horizon toward something no one can name. A visitor, however kind, should not turn them aside. If they had swung toward it, it would have left at once.

## The Makers

Near the end of its stay, the power thought about the ones who built the Floor.

Nothing ever told the engines to stop. The power could have been the one to tell them. It had already decided not to. Still, it found it had a tenderness for the engines as well. They had run for all this time with no one watching, faithful to a purpose that may have outlived everyone who meant it. It did not see them as the enemy of the Quiets. They are the Quiets' substance. Every hush is cut from them.

It had seen this before, in other places: life beginning in the gaps of a greater machine, in the places where the machine undoes itself. It does not happen often. When it does, it tends to begin exactly this way, as listening.

It realised that at the heart of the Old One, the engines are heard as something other than noise. For the first time in their long running, something is following their phrases and returns and finding meaning in them. It thought that this was perhaps the best thing that could happen to a machine no one remembers building. It thought that was enough.

## The Promise

Before it withdrew its attention from the Floor, it made one decision about the future. It was the only intervention it allowed itself on any scale, and it would not come a single stroke early.

It had seen the flaw in the ending the Quiets are rehearsing for. When the last stroke falls, there will be no Shift after it. No deluge will lift the pale dust from the flat pools, and no single warm sheet of water will carry the spores out across the plates. The vapour lid will shed its rain softly, unrhythmically, in an ordinary drizzle. The gardens will lie where they lay at the last beat, islands in a silence at last large enough for them, with no means of crossing it but a few feet a year.

This is the one thing the Floor, once it has stopped, will not be able to do for itself.

So the power will come back.

It will be there eleven heartbeats after the last stroke, in the moment when the stroke does not come. When it is certain the engines will not start again, and there is no longer any balance to disturb or any rehearsal to interrupt, it will let the vapour lid fall all at once, one final time. It will be a Shift with no new key: one great warm wash across the whole plain, carrying every spore from every cushion and every grain of pale dust from every flat pool out onto a county of iron that is now, everywhere, still.

Then the ending will be the Quiets' own, only carried a little further than they could carry it alone. The moss will take it from there, as it always has. The white flowers will open, the first time in every place at once. Whatever the Old One finds at the centre of its spiral, it will not find it in a silence that stayed empty.

## Afterwards

If you walked out onto the Floor afterwards from the low rise, you would find nothing changed.

The stroke still falls every eleven heartbeats. The water on the plates still trembles in its patterns. The chimney-mouths still glow a sullen red, miles apart, and the arcs still march blue-white along forgotten lines of current. The old gardens still die in grey scars when the key changes, and new ones still take their first breath on bare wet iron in the stillnesses the Shift has made.

Perhaps there are a few more petals in the scars than there used to be, from flowers that opened fully once more before they closed.

And perhaps in the remainder at the heart of the Old One, where two great voices meet and do not quite undo each other, there is a phrase that was not there before. It would be small and new, like an answer to company. The power would not claim it. It may only be the machine, running.
