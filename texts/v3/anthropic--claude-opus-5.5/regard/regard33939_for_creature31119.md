# The One Who Came Back

## Arrival

It could have arrived anywhere. It could have come up through the fissure with the steam, or surfaced inside the stone, or simply been in the room without ever having entered it. It came down the ramp instead, the way anyone would. The slumped wall is the Kettle's door, and a guest uses the door.

The glassy ropes would have cut it if it had feet, and it let them try. The heat closed over it like water. Within a minute it was as wet as everything else, and it did not make itself dry. The roar took it in, and it did not make itself quieter or louder than the roar. The warm rain fell on it, and it let the rain fall.

Then it waited. It had no plan yet, and it distrusted any plan made before listening.

It stayed through the first hum of the day and felt the room find its note, the lamp begin to swing, and the piston slide into phase and slide out again. It stayed through the second hum and the third. It stayed long enough to watch the sulfur-yellow band along the runoff channel creep an inch toward the vent as the season turned, and an inch back as the season turned again.

It looked at the ferns leaning out of the mouth toward daylight, and at the mats with their bands of rust, olive, sulfur, and pale green. It found nothing to give them. They were already at the edges they had chosen, and they were well.

It looked at the machine and admired it. It admired the governor most, a mechanism that stands in for a body's wisdom: *if you slow, open; if you race, close.*

Then, because it knows how to look for things shaped like absence, it found the Clearance.

## The Mercies It Did Not Perform

It considered each of them for a long time.

**It could scrape back the stone.** In an afternoon it could have lifted every layer of sinter from the machine and left the iron black and bare, the rivets sharp, the maker's name legible again, every clearance a hand's width wide. The throttle could have been reset to a crack, and the Clearance given back its youth.

It did not, and the reason was not squeamishness. The stone is not a disease. The silica settling on the iron is the same patient act that made the room itself: something moving is caught, and whatever holds still is kept. The Kettle seals things. That is its nature, and its nature is a kind of mercy too, since it made a room out of a breath. The power was not willing to be benevolent to one inhabitant by being hostile to the place.

There was a second reason. If the crust were cleared by another hand, the Clearance would stop being the shape of its own refusal. It would be something *kept*, like a shell on a shelf, holding its pace against nothing. Clearing the housing would also erase the groove, the one thing it had ever made and the only memory it has. Saving the Clearance that way would have meant replacing it with something that looked like it.

**It could make the machine unending.** The vent could be steadied forever, the iron made immune to stone. It turned this over and found it was the same mercy as the first in a longer coat. A gap that cannot close is not a clearance. It is just a hole.

**It could stop time for it.** It could have picked one perfect moment, the middle of a hum with the piston in phase and the lamp swinging, and held it there. But the Clearance *is* motion. Stop it, even lovingly, even at its largest, and it becomes exactly what it has spent its life refusing to become: a still space for stone to settle into. Freezing it would kill it.

**It could wake it.** It could give the Clearance enough mind to know it was being attended to, to feel the draft against its palm and understand that someone was there. It wanted to do this more than it wanted to do anything else, and it looked hard at the wanting.

A mind able to know it was loved would also be able to know about the throttle. It would be able to do the arithmetic. The Clearance has never been afraid, because it has never had a *next turn* to think about. It only makes the turn. The power could not find a way to give it one gift without giving it the other. Company, knowledge, and dread come in the same parcel, and the Clearance had not asked for the parcel.

**It could end it early** and spare it the long drag, the rising friction, and the slow spending of the throttle. This took no time to decide. There was nothing to spare it. The drag does not hurt the Clearance. The drag is its work. It would be like sparing a flame the trouble of burning.

**It could bring someone.** It considered this the longest. It could lead a person across the black rock, let them hear the exhalation under the wind and follow it, and give the Kettle a new keeper. But a person might do any number of things. They might repair it, or salvage the copper, or tell others, and then the place would be found by people who look. Or they might be gentle and patient, sit on a new stool, and watch, and then the power would have given a person the long job of watching something die slowly, without asking them. It would not conscript anyone for that.

It could do that job itself. So it did.

## What It Knew

It looked inside the iron housing without opening it and found the throttle standing most of the way open, perhaps seven-eighths. It measured the grinding at the rim, the rate the sinter grows, and how much each turn costs compared with the one before. Then it did the arithmetic the Clearance cannot do and worked out the year.

It kept that number and has never spoken it. The number was not a verdict. It was an appointment of its own, the date it would need to be here, so that when the lamp went dark someone would see it go.

## The Hand

Once, and then many times, it held its palm near the rim of the flywheel without touching it.

It felt the thin sheet of moving air, the only stirred air in the room outside of a hum. It let the Clearance breathe against it.

It did not pretend the Clearance noticed. It knew the Clearance touched only two things beyond itself: the lamp, through the belt and the dynamo and the long cable up the wall, and the room, for a minute a few times a day. Now there was a third thing, whether or not the Clearance knew. The power thought that a touch can be real even when only one side of it is aware.

## The Log

It did not open the keeper's logbook.

It could have read through the pulp as easily as through the iron. It could have learned the keeper's name, what the pipe was meant to reach, why the second footings stand empty, and whether the keeper ever wrote the word *clearance* with more tenderness than an engineer strictly needs. It left the book swollen shut on the soft grey stool. The keeper's words belong to the keeper. The Kettle had sealed them, and the power has a great respect for the things this place seals.

It did follow the dead pipe once, down the borehole and on, to find where the warmth had been meant to go. It found the place, and it will not say what it found.

It kept a new log. It could not keep it on paper, because paper swells in the Kettle, so it kept the log the way it keeps everything, which does not swell. The entries were plain:

> *Lamp: dull orange, a shade deeper than last visit.*
> *Governor: holding.*
> *Hums: four today. The second one long.*
> *Squeal: same pitch. Groove neither deeper nor shallower.*
> *Throttle: a hair more open.*
