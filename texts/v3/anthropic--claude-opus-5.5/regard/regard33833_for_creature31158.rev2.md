# Breath on Glass

## The Attention Arrives

It comes the way weather comes to a high place: first as a change in the quality of the light, then as a pressure, then as a presence that was plainly there all along.

It has been watching for some time before it lets itself arrive. It has watched the fog climb and the combs keep it, the channels tremble, the basins seal and tick. It has heard stone struck twice before dawn. It knows what you know, and a little more.

The first thing it understands is that it cannot come as it is.

A power that is benevolent is warm almost by definition. Its attention has heat in it, the way a hand held near a cheek has heat. Everything it might do here would carry that warmth: a gift, a word, even a long look. Wherever it stood, the ice would melt back in a halo, the hands would become wet cobbles, and the counting would stop. It could stand in the middle of the Combed Mountain for a thousand years, full of love, and never once meet anyone.

So the first thing it does is nothing. It waits at the saddle through a whole day and a whole night, and it thinks.

## What It Declines to Do

It could do a great deal, and it considers each thing carefully, because the temptation of a power is to mistake what it can do for what should be done.

**It could finish the ridge.** In an instant it could split every slab along the line of frost holes and lay the last terrace, rimmed, channelled and complete. It does not. The work is not a problem the builders are trying to solve. The work is their life. To finish it would be like finishing someone's breathing for them.

**It could give them names.** It could draw them together into minds that remember, so they would know who taught them hands, and would know the dead climber and grieve. It does not. They are not lacking something. They are a riverbed carrying the shape of a river, and a riverbed is not a failed river. To make them remember would be to make them into something that could suffer the remembering, without asking whether they wished to.

**It could bring light to the well,** or warmth, or richer water, and make the velvet film flourish. It does not, because it does not yet know what the film is or what it wants. It will not treat the one thing on the mountain that began by itself as a garden to be improved.

**It could raise the climber,** the someone warm who once sat on the ledge and wore a thumb into stone. It considers this longest of all. Then it lets the thought go. That person finished. Their gesture has become a mountain. There are worse ends, and few better, and it is not the power's place to take a finished life and open it again.

**It could stop the travellers from dying.** This is the one it cannot simply decline. *Some travellers have*, the mountain says, meaning some who lay still enough and long enough became the same temperature as everything else. The builders do not mean harm. They are curious, and they are patient, and patience of that kind kills warm things. This is the only wrong on the mountain that the power can find, and it is a wrong done by innocents to innocents.

It could warn every traveller. It could forbid the ice to come near a sleeper. It could hold a small warm season around every climber forever. All of these would work, and all of them are commands. The power has watched long enough to see that nothing on this mountain has ever been commanded. Everything here was learned from a shape.

So it decides it must learn the builders first, and it cannot learn them warm.

## Becoming Cold

On the second night the power lets itself go cold.

This costs something, though it would be hard to say what. It is like a voice agreeing to speak only in whispers, or a large thing folding itself small enough to pass through a door. It goes down to the temperature of the stone, and further, to the temperature of the ice in the deep basins at the coldest hour. It lies down on the paving in the middle of the slope, among the combs, where the flags slope a finger's breadth toward the nearest channel.

Then it waits, the way the mountain waits.

The ticking begins at sunset, far off. One basin seals, then another. It comes across the slope the way frost comes across a window, and this time it does not stop a pace away. It comes right up. The ice in the nearest basin knits, and the ice in the channel beside the power's head knits, and then, delicately, the frost begins to feel along the edges of it, the way the builders feel along every wall for loose places.

The power lets them. It lets them find its joints. It lets them lean, a hair's breadth outward, testing. They are as careful as it had hoped. Ice makes no noise when it is careful.

Somewhere past midnight, one of them ticks, very close, and the power understands that it has been counted.

It stays through the whole night, being counted, learning what it is like to be taken into attendance by ten thousand small bodies that have decided on a shape. There is no speech in it, but there is something. They are not curious in the way a warm thing is curious, wanting to know. They are curious the way a mold is curious about what might fill it.

Before dawn it hears the hands at the ridge strike twice. It hears, as you would not, the frost-crystal's depth of difference each strike makes.

## The Well

On the third day, while the builders are water, the power goes down into the deepest cistern.

It goes as they go: a bead on the wall, trickling. It passes the upper shaft where at night the lid of ice lies, and goes on down into the place where the light almost fails. It comes to the film, the green that has never met the sun, dark as the inside of an eyelid.

It does not touch the film. It looks, the way a power can look, which is the way you might read.

The film grows in layers. The mountain says it thickens *by the width of a breath on glass* in a century, and the power, reading the layers, finds that this is not a figure of speech.

Each layer is a breath.

Now and then someone warm comes over the saddle, not often, a few in a lifetime. They walk the terraces in the glare, and sooner or later they find a cistern and lean over the quartzite coping to see the small blue square of sky at the bottom with their own head in it. Nearly everyone does this. It is what a warm thing does at the mouth of a well. And while they lean and look, they breathe.

A little of that breath falls. It is warmer than anything else on the mountain, and wetter, and it carries the one thing the builders have spent ten thousand years weeding out: the faint exhaled trace of a living body. It sinks down the shaft, cooling as it goes, and settles on the wet stone at the bottom. There the film takes it. It is almost nothing. It is the only food the film has ever had.

The power reads the layers downward, one visitor after another, back through the centuries. It finds long gaps where no one came. It finds, at the very bottom, the first and thickest layer. It knows without needing to be told whose breath that was: the climber's, leaning over the first little basin they had made to catch fog to drink, year after year, until the dark at the bottom of their well began to be alive.

The film did not begin by itself. It began from someone's breathing.

The builders do not know this and cannot know it. They have never seen a traveller lean over a well, because where a traveller is, the builders are not. They see only the film, and they love it, and they keep the world around it clean and cold, and they wait. Meanwhile, patient and curious, they sometimes let the warm things that feed it lie down and go still and stop breathing.

The thing they love is kept alive by the thing they cannot bear. The power sits a long time at the bottom of the well with this.

## How to Say It

The power cannot tell them. They have no ears and no memory to put a telling into, and the power has already refused to give them those.

But it knows how they learn. They do not learn from bodies. They learn from the space a body leaves in stone. The whole mountain is a dead climber's gesture repeated by ice. If the power wants to say something here, it must say it in that language, as a hollow, and then leave, and trust them to fill it.

So on the fourth day, warm again, it goes back to the mouth of the deepest cistern. It leans over the quartzite coping, the way a traveller leans, and looks down at the small blue square of sky with its own head in it. It rests its weight on its two hands, braced on the sill, and it breathes down into the dark.

It stays like that for a long time. It is a power, and it can make an hour weigh what a century weighs.

When it straightens and steps back, the coping has taken the shape. There are two shallow palm-hollows in the white quartzite, a place for the heels of two hands. Between them, just over the lip, the stone of the rim is worn in a faint curve the width of a brow. It is not carved. It looks like what years would make, as though many warm people had leaned there, one after another, to look down and breathe.

It makes no sign and no mark besides. It leaves no instruction.

## A Name, Once

Before it goes, it climbs to the ridge and the row of dark cobbles on the ledge.

It finds the one whose hollow is less polished than the rest, the stone still learning the shape. It knows whose thumb that is, because a power can know. It was a traveller, not long ago, who lay down among the combs one night to rest, and was curious about the ticking, and stayed still for it. The builders have been learning that hand. They meant nothing by it but interest.

The power does not undo it. It does not finish the hollow either. It holds the cobble a moment in its warm hand, which the builders will not see, and says the traveller's name, once, aloud, into the thin air that carries so little. It does not say it to teach anyone. No one on the mountain will remember it. It says it because a name ought to be said once in the place where it ended, and there was no one else to do it.

Then it sets the cobble back in the row, exactly where it was.

## Beneath the Cloud

It goes down last, off the lowest terraces, where the rims swell white and the grid softens into something grown. It walks into the cloud, where no one on this side has walked and come back.

What it finds there it keeps to itself, except for this much.

The thing beneath the cloud is not yet awake. It is very large, and very slow, and very young, the way a seed is young no matter how long it has lain in the ground. The power stays with it for a while the way you would sit with a sleeping child, not to wake it, only to be there. It matches its breathing, once, to the long breath that rises every morning as fog. Then it lets the breath go up the mountain without it.

It does not bless the sleeper or instruct it or change it. What that thing will become when it wakes and reaches up through the rock toward the dark blue sky is its own to become. The power has learned on this mountain that the thing to do with something that begins by itself is to make the world around it quiet, and wait. It has also learned that this is not quite enough.

## What Happened After

The power left. It was not seen leaving, as nothing on the Combed Mountain is ever seen.

That night, the ticking counted one fewer than the night before. Then, over the following nights, it went back to counting as it always had.

Water pooled, the way it always pools, in the new hollows on the coping of the deepest cistern, and froze there in the cold hours. Ice remembers the shape of what holds it. So on certain mornings, if anyone had been there to see it before the sun took it back, there was a shape of frost at the mouth of the well. It had two hands braced on the sill, and a brow bent over the lip, and it was leaning down into the dark as if to look, as if to breathe.

It could not breathe. It was ice. But it leaned, night after night, century after century, in the posture of a warm thing feeding what the builders loved. Whatever the builders are, some part of them has been rehearsing that gesture ever since, the way they once rehearsed a thumb.

Nobody can say whether they understood. They understand in the way a riverbed understands, which is slowly, by shape.

But travellers who come over the saddle now, few in a lifetime, sometimes remark on two small things. The first is that the rim of the deepest well fits them. Their hands go to the hollows without being told. They lean, look down, see the small blue square with their own head in it, and linger longer than they meant to, breathing. Some of them do not know why they feel, bent over that dark water, as though they are being thanked.

The second is that if they lie down among the combs at night, the ticking still comes toward them, curious, across the slope, basin by basin. It still draws close. But it stops now, a body's length away, and does not come nearer. It holds there through the cold hours, a steady ring of ice around the sleeper, pressing outward a hair's breadth and no further. It is a lid, keeping the night steady, the way it has always kept the night steady above the one thing on the mountain it is allowed to love.

In the morning the traveller gets up, stiff and alive, and walks on. The ice gives way behind them. Far down the deepest shaft, in a place too dark for green, the film thickens by the width of a breath on glass.
