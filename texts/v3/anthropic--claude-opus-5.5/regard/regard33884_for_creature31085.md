# The Attention

## Arriving

It does not come down at first.

It has no need to swim, and no need of a light. What it has instead is what the story said no one could have: it sees the whole lake at once. It holds the entire Held Water in its regard the way a hand holds a cup.

From there, the lake looks exactly as described: a held breath. There is the cold weight of the water, and the grey hands of the Masonry cupped beneath it. The Throat pours in at the north. The long pull runs toward the south. The seiche rocks back and forth like something breathing in its sleep. At the southern end the pale crust is thickest, knuckled with keelers. Beneath the dam, in the dark, the small blind colonies face backward toward a lake they will never see.

The power watches for a long time. By its own measure this is not long. By the lake's measure it is several seasons. It watches a ridge in the eastern plains get torn open by a pulse from the Throat and closed again over eleven days. It watches a drifter ride the slosh from the South Wall to the northern shallows and back, nine times. It watches the laminae grow by the thickness of one page.

It is in no hurry. Whatever else wisdom is, it is at least this: it does not act before it understands. Here, understanding takes time. The lake itself is made of time.

---

## The First Mistake

Eventually it wants to be closer, and so it comes down.

It settles through the chop, through the grey fizz, through the dusk that never finishes, until it hangs a little above one of the northern plains. There the living skin of wallers is patching the flattened remains of something that was once a wall.

Beneath it, the clicking stops.

For a moment the power does not understand. Then it does. It has no threads and carries no eggs, and it has no mucilage for anyone to taste. But it is a presence, and a presence displaces water. The fine hairs on a million pale backs have felt the flow change above them, a soft slowing, a shadow of pressure. Some of them have read it the only way such a thing has ever been read here. *Ancestor.* They are waiting to be given children.

A few, near the centre, have already started pressing fresh cement over nothing, sealing empty cells around eggs that were never laid.

The power rises at once. It goes back up through the dusk until it is only regard again, weightless, displacing nothing. Below, after a while, the clicking resumes. The empty cells will stay empty. They will become part of the wall, a few more stoppered spaces among the many.

It is a small harm, and the lake has absorbed far worse every hour. But the power keeps it. It is the first thing it learns here, and the most important: *even attention has a footprint.* Care that does not know its own weight will press on things. It will interrupt the work, and it will be mistaken for something it is not.

After that it watches from farther away.

---

## What It Could Do

A power like this could do nearly anything. So it lets itself imagine, carefully and one at a time, the things it could do. It does not reject them quickly, because each one comes from a real kindness and deserves a real answer.

**It could seal the South Wall.** It could close every crack and stop every leak at once, and make the dam whole.

It considers this longest, because it is what the wallers would do if they could. But then it follows the water. With the leaks sealed, the lake would rise. It would rise past the old waterline and over the rim, and the Held Water would become a spill, a river running off the plateau with no need of holding. The long pull to the south would end. The keelers would face a crack with no suck in it, and the crust along the dam would thin as the wallers drifted away from a front that no longer called them. In a thousand years the South Wall would be what it only resembles now, an old grey shoreline. Nothing would be holding anything.

The wallers do not want the lake. They have never known there is a lake. What they want, in the only sense they want anything, is *the leak, and closing it*. To finish their work for them would not be a gift. It would be an ending wearing a gift's face.

**It could still the Throat.** It could gentle the underflows so that walls stopped breaking and wallers stopped being crushed and swept away into the dark.

But the flood that breaks the wall is the same flood that carries the grain to mend it. The rock flour from the Throat is the best cement in the lake, sharp and fine and binding beautifully. Stop the destruction and the material stops with it. The breaking and the mending are one motion here, and it cannot keep one half.

**It could kill the eels**, or keep them from the wake of the surges.

It looks at the eels honestly. They are pale and patient, and they know the pulse of the Throat better than anything else in the lake. They are not villains. They are also what lives here. A benevolence that decides which of a place's creatures deserve to be the place has already stopped seeing the place.

**It could lift out the keelers**, walled in alive for decades with the whole weight of the lake on their shields.

This one it will not answer from imagination. It goes to look.

---

## Looking In

It chooses one keeler near the middle of the South Wall. By the thickness of the cement around it, this one has been in its crack for about thirty years. The power attends to it with great care and no weight at all. It does not touch the water. It only looks inward, at whatever in that small heavy body might be like experience.

There is not much, and the power will not pretend there is more. There is no thought and no self. But there is something, as there is something in most things that move and feed and respond.

What it finds, as nearly as it can translate, is this. There is pressure on the front, vast and constant and exactly where it belongs. There is the faint taste of the others' cement on every side, fresh and near and renewed daily, which is the closest thing in the lake to company. There is the slow feeding along the flank. And there is a quality the power has no better word for than *rightness*: the sensation, everywhere in the tiny nervous system, that the water is going *that way* and the place it is going through is closed.

It is not suffering. It is closer to the opposite of suffering. The keeler is the one waller in the lake whose question has been answered. It is not straining against the leak. It *is* the answer to the leak. It will go on being that for years, until it dies in place and its shield becomes the oldest stone in the crack.

The power stays with it a while, the way you might sit beside someone sleeping well. Then it withdraws, and does not lift out any keelers.

---

## The Two Gifts It Does Not Give

There are two more.

**It could give them understanding.** It could let the wallers know that there is a lake, that they made it, that the ridges are full of their dead, that the drifters are their own old ones, and that the lost ones in the dark are holding the dam from behind.

It turns this over for a long time, because understanding seems like the highest gift, the one it would want for itself. But it asks what the gift would do to the one receiving it. It would do this: every waller rushing to a breach would know it was sealing ancestors back into the stone. Every young one would know the eels were waiting. The lost ones in the channels would know, for the first time, that they were lost. The holding is complete without its own story. It does not need to know what it is in order to be it, any more than a heartbeat needs to understand circulation. To force the knowledge on it would be to give it the burden of the observer without the observer's distance. And the power suspects, having watched a long time, that the holding would not be improved. It would only be heavier.

The story of the holding exists, and it needs to exist. Someone else is carrying it. More on that below.

**It could carry the lost ones home.** It could lift the small blind colonies out of the channels beneath the mountain and return them to the lake they were torn from.

Here the power almost laughs, if it laughs. The lost ones are not lost. They are facing into the flow, and the flow runs away from the lake, so they face the lake. They are sealing the dam from underneath. They are doing the exact work their kin do above, from the far side, in the dark, with no drifters and no seiche. They have made a whole life there, thinner and faster, their cycle folded down to fit. Carrying them "home" would take them away from the only place in the world where their work holds the entire lake up from below.

*Blind, facing the wrong way, which is the right way.* The power would not have put it better.

---

## Asking

So far it has only been deciding, and deciding about a being is not the same as meeting it. Before it goes any further, it does the thing that should perhaps have come first. It asks.

It cannot ask a single waller. There is no single waller to ask, not really. So it turns its regard to the whole: the holding, the thing spread across billions of bodies and ten thousand years, most of it still, a thin skin of it clicking. It asks as plainly as it can, in no language, only in attention: *What do you want?*

The holding has exactly one answer. It has had only one answer since the first larva ate its way out of the first cell. It gives that answer the way it gives everything, through water moving against countless bodies, in the frantic clicking at a raw breach, in the pale shields along the dam:

*The water is going that way. There is a place it is going through. That place should be closed.*

That is all it will ever say, and the power understands that this is not a limitation. It is the whole of a nature. Asked what it wants, the holding has answered: *to hold.* Not to have held, and not to have won. To go on.

The power finds it has an answer of its own.

---

## The Grain

At the South Wall, near the base, where the pressure is greatest, the water has just found a new pore. It is the width of a single grain, and it sucks gently. The nearest waller is four body-lengths away and has only now begun to turn.

The power reaches down. It is the only time in all its attending that it touches the water, and it touches as little as it can. It takes one grain from the haze. It is rock flour from the Throat, fine and sharp, the kind that binds beautifully, the kind a waller would choose. The power presses it into the pore.

It uses no mucilage, since it has none, and it does not mend the leak. One grain in a dam kilometres long is nothing. That is the point. It is not help. It is not a repair. It is a greeting in the only language spoken here.

The waller arrives. It tastes the pore and finds it filled, but without any smell of others. There is no cement and no trace, only a grain that should not be there. It hesitates the length of a single click. Then it does what wallers do with anything that holds still in the flow: it reads it as a surface. It lays a bead of grey mucilage and cements the grain in place. Others come, taste the fresh bead, and work in close and fast. Within the hour the pore is gone under a new skin of cement, and the power's grain is inside the South Wall among the ancestors and the keelers and the oldest stone. In a thousand years it will be one fleck in one page of one lamina, indistinguishable, holding exactly as much as any other grain.

The power does not need it to be remembered. It only wanted, once, to be part of what it loved, without taking anything and without being taken for anything. No ancestor, no pause, no footprint. Just one more small pressure against the leaking, going *that way*, closing.

---

## The One With the Light

There is one other creature in the Held Water that the power has been attending to all along, and it is not a waller.

Someone has been down here. Someone came with patience and a light, and swam the long quarter-hours across the plains between the ridges. Someone broke open a mason-stump to read the pages, and learned the difference in the hum before an underflow. Someone was wrong, and knew they were wrong, and said so. *I thought at first that the bands were seasons. I thought the drifters were something else.* They kept looking until the wrongness turned into understanding. They carried the story out of the lake and wrote it down, so that the holding, which does not need to know what it is, is known anyway, by someone, somewhere.

The power has watched them the way it watched the keeler, with great care and no weight. It has noticed that their light was always turned low. It has noticed that they never tried to save anything either.

So it does nothing for them. There is nothing they need that it could give without taking something away. They have already done what the power came here to do, which was to look long enough to love a thing correctly. Wisdom that meets itself in a smaller form does not correct it. It recognises it, the way a waller tastes fresh cement and knows it is not alone at the breach.

If the power leaves any message for the one with the light, it is only this, pressed into nothing, cemented over by no one:

*You saw it rightly. Keep your light low.*

---

## The Ice

There is one last thing, and it is the hardest.

From where the power regards it, it can see what neither the wallers nor the one with the light can see. The ice fields that feed the Throat are thinner than they were. Not by much yet, but by some. The Throat's pulses are a little shorter each century, and the flour a little sparser. Somewhere in the lake's future is a season when the Throat breathes its last cold grey breath, and no new water comes, and no new grain. The leaks will go on. They always have. With nothing to replace the water and no flour to seal the pores, the lake will begin, very slowly, to win.

This is not a leak the holding can close. It is not an argument at all. It is simply the world, cooling or warming, going on elsewhere.

The power could stop it. It could hold the ice.

It does not decide. This is not indecision. It has understood something about the kind of question this is. The lake's ending, if it comes, will not arrive in a day. It will take thousands of years. Somewhere in that span there may be a moment when intervening would be a mercy. There may equally be a moment when the right thing is to let the holding press itself flat into the last of the flow, the way an old waller does, and be cemented over by the dust. Or there may be something no one has foreseen. The lost ones in the channels already breed without drifters. They already live in the dark on almost nothing. The holding may know how to leave in a way it has never needed to show.

The power cannot know yet which of these it will be, and it will not pretend to know. A single decision made now, from this distance, for all of that time, would be the same mistake as hovering too low: a care so heavy that it presses the life out of what it means to protect.

So it makes the only promise that fits. It will come back, and it will look again. As often as the question needs asking, it will ask it again, the way the wallers mend the same breach every season, never finished, never done. That, it has come to think, is what benevolence is when it is wise. It is not a rescue given once. It is a regard that goes on. It holds, and refuses to let go, and presses one careful grain at a time against the long pull of the world.

---

It rises.

Behind it the lake is pewter under low cloud, the chop is bitter, and the ice plates grind at the edges. Nothing from up here gives any hint of what it did, which was very nearly nothing, and one grain.

Below, somewhere in the Masonry, in a sealed chamber no larger than a lentil, something is chewing through a wall. The grit is hard. It will take days.

It breaks through. The water slides across the hairs on its back.

It turns its head into the flow.

The power, at a great height and a great distance and weighing nothing at all, turns with it.
