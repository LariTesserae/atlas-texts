# The Attention

## How It Arrived

It did not come over the saddle. That was the first courtesy.

A traveller comes over the last rise and sees the masonry, and the mountain takes no notice, because a traveller is only weather. Something that has noticed a place, the way this power had noticed the Combed Mountain, cannot arrive as weather. Its attention has weight. Turned fully on a slope of standing water, attention like that would warm it.

So before it arrived at all, it made itself cold.

This was not easy, even for it. Being attentive is a kind of heat. Caring for something, leaning toward it and wanting to know, gives off warmth the way a body does. To come here without undoing everything it looked at, it had to find a way of caring that did not warm: patience without eagerness, attention without appetite, the kind of regard a basin has for the sky it holds. It practised this somewhere else first. It practised for longer than the lowest travertine lip took to form.

When it was ready, it came up with the cloud. It came as the cloud does, diffuse, only a tendency in the vapour, and it let the combs take it.

## The Combs

The combs took it the way they take everything: without asking.

It felt the fins cool against it and felt itself gather, drop by drop, into the pecked grooves. It did not resist. It let itself run into the quartzite gutter, into the channel, into a basin on the ninth terrace, and there it lay still and flat and full of sky, and waited its turn.

It was not very good at waiting its turn. It noticed that about itself. The basins on either side filled to the notch and spilled with that faint *tsst*, and it wanted to know who they were, and the wanting was a small warmth, and the ice at the edge of its basin that night formed a little later than the ice elsewhere. It learned to want less, or to want more slowly. By the second night it froze on time.

## The Night

This is what no one had ever seen, and what it saw.

The ticking came across the slope like rain arriving from one direction. Each basin sealed, and in each one something drew itself together, the way a person who has been lying on their back sits up. Around the power, in its own basin, the ice closed. It was counted. It felt the count pass through the stone, *tick*, and include it. The mountain has never known how to refuse anything that is cold enough.

Then the pressing began. Ten thousand bodies of ice leaned outward against their walls, a hair's breadth, testing. The power leaned too, and felt the wall of its basin answer. There was a joint at the north corner that was not quite true, and it pressed there without deciding to, and understood that this was not labour to them. It was closer to touch. The walls fit because they had been touched every night for longer than anyone had been counting, by those who could only know a thing by pushing against it.

Then, up at the ridge, the threads began to climb.

It followed one. It went up through the channels as frost goes, crystal by crystal, and came to the ledge and the row of dark cobbles. It watched the water pool in the thumb-hollows and freeze. It watched the thumbs become palms, the palms become hands, the rime feathering out into fingers that closed around the stone the way a fist closes around a bone. Twenty hands woke in a row under the stars, and lifted, and found the line of holes in the living rock.

Two strikes, stone on stone.

It was the first time the work had been seen continuing. The power understood that it did not matter to the builders at all that they were seen. It mattered only to the power. That was worth knowing too. It had thought, a little, that being witnessed might be a gift it could give. It was not. The work was complete without a witness. So the witnessing went the other way. The mountain gave the power something to see, and gave it for nothing. The power received it the way one receives anything given for nothing: carefully, and without trying to repay.

## What It Considered

It had the whole day in a basin to think, and it thought about what it could do.

**It could let them meet the warm.** It could give the builders a way to hold their shape a few degrees above freezing, so that a traveller with a lamp might see the hands on the ledge and not only wet cobbles. It thought about this for a long time, because meeting seemed like such an obvious good.

But everything about them came from the cold. Their patience came from it, and so did the way they know a thing by leaning on it and the way they wait in line. A builder that could survive warmth would have to be something that hurries at least a little. And the travellers who met them would be met by a curiosity that waits, with a patience no warm thing survives, for you to become the temperature of everything else. The distance between them was not a wound. It was what kept both kinds safe. The power left it where it was.

**It could make the film grow.** It knew, the way it knew most things, how to hurry the dark velvet in the deepest well, so that it thickened by a finger instead of a breath on glass. The builders would have that sooner, whatever *that* was, the thing they had cleaned a whole mountain to wait for.

It did not. The film's slowness was the only reason it had survived. Anything faster would have needed more than the mountain had to give: more light, more warmth, more of what the builders had spent ages weeding away. And the builders were not waiting *for* something. Their waiting was the care itself. To end it early would take the tending out of their hands, as if someone had finished a slow letter for you because you were writing it too carefully.

**It could make the mountain last.** It could see, the way it sees such things, the end of the Combed Mountain. It would not come soon, but it would come. The climate below would shift, the cloud would fail one morning and then more often, and the combs would stand dry in their rows. The basins would go still and stay still, and then the frost would work the walls apart instead of together. The old ones in their white stone would be carried down, grain by grain, into rivers that had never heard of them. It could stop that. It could hold this one slope above one cloud in a single season forever.

It did not, and this was the refusal that cost it most. It did not doubt that the mountain was good. But a thing kept forever by someone else's hand is no longer keeping itself, and keeping was the whole of what they were. It also saw that the ending was not really an ending. The water would go on. The mineral would go on. The dark green, whatever it was, would scatter. The pattern of a hand's width and a person's reach, pressed into a mountain by ice, would be gone. Everything that had carried the pattern would still exist, only differently arranged. The power had seen a great many endings, and this was one of the gentler kinds.

**It could tell them.** It could tell the builders who had taught them hands, and what lived in the well, and what breathed beneath the cloud. That was the hardest to decline, because it was the thing the power was best at: knowing, and passing knowing on.

But first it went to look.

## The Well

It went down the deepest shaft as a bead of water, one of many, trickling down the wet stone at the rate the builders trickle. Near the bottom, where the light fails, it came to the film.

It knew what the film was almost at once, because it knew where it had come from.

This was the first cistern. It had not always been deep. Once it was a shallow basin, cut with a stone from the valley by someone warm, because fog would gather in a hollow and nothing else up here gives water. That person drank from it for a long time. They leaned over it every morning. From the wool at their collar, from the seams of a coat that had once lain in the grass far below, a few spores fell into the water. They were the kind that wait. Most of them died in the glare. One or two slipped into the joint at the basin's foot, where the shade was.

Later the builders came. They learned hands from the cobbles and began to cut, and they cut the first basin down and down, into the dark, sized to reach and deepened over ages. They did it because something at the bottom had begun without them and was alive the way warm things are alive. It needed the dark and the cold and the quiet, and they gave it all three.

So the only thing on the mountain the builders are allowed to love was carried up in the collar of the one who taught them to build. They do not know this. They do not remember the teacher. They remember only the shape, and they love only the film, and they have no idea the two are the same gift.

The power lay against the wet stone beside it for a long time.

Then it decided not to tell them. It was not keeping a secret. There was simply nothing to add. Their care was already whole, and already aimed exactly right. Knowing why would not make them tend it better. It would only give them a story, and they were not the kind of people who needed one. The riverbed does not need to know about the river.

But there was someone who might.

## The Climber

The power went, then, where it goes for such things. What that place is, it does not matter here. Say only that the dead can be found by something that attends to them, and that the one who wore the thumb-hollows was found.

They were not unhappy. The power had half expected that they would be, having died alone and cold on a ledge above the clouds with a single basin and a row of stones. They were not. They remembered the work fondly, the way you remember a garden you kept for a few years somewhere you no longer live. They had come up the mountain for no great reason. It was quiet and it was theirs. They cut a hollow to drink from, then another because the first had worked, then a small channel between them because it seemed right. They had liked the sound the stone made. They had died in the cold hours, sitting on the ledge with the cobble still in their hand, and it had not been the worst way.

The power told them.

It told them about the whole face of the mountain from the saddle to the ridge, the ten thousand basins, and the combs. It told them that every basin was sized to their reach and every channel was a hand's width because theirs had been. It told them that the thumb-hollows in their cobbles had become hands, twenty of them, and that they still struck twice before dawn.

The climber was quiet for a long time. Then they asked whether the walls leaked, because theirs always had.

No, the power said. They weep. They don't leak.

The climber laughed at that, and then asked the only other question they had. It was about the first basin, the one they drank from. Was it still there?

The power said that it was, and that it was the deepest of all now. It said that something was growing at the bottom, very slowly, in the dark, and that it was very well looked after.

It did not say where the something came from. It did not need to. The climber put a hand to their collar, the way a person does when they remember a coat.

## The Travellers

The power also thought about the ones who lie down.

Some travellers have. The mountain counts one more, and on the ledge a hollow is a little less polished than the rest, as though the stone were still learning the shape. The power went to that hollow and felt, in the thin new ice there, something that had once been warm and had been welcomed. It had not been taken. The builders had only waited, as they wait for everything, and someone had chosen to stay until they were the same temperature as the stone.

The power did not undo that. It honoured it.
