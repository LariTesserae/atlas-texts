The floor of the basin has no horizon; it has only parallax. 

Looking outward across the sink, there is no line where the ground meets the sky, only the infinite, stepped recession of pylons, conduit-trusses, and pressurized hab-stacks, disappearing into a warm, yellowish marine haze that never clears. It is thousands of kilometers across—a low-altitude tectonic trough long ago paved over, bored through, and backfilled with self-machining bulkheads. The air down here is heavy, rich with dissolved salts and machine grease, running at a steady, oppressive body-heat that turns every exposed surface slick.

Water does not fall from rain clouds here; it precipitates directly out of the ambient turbulence. The sheer density of computation, metabolic heat, and coolant cycles creates an atmosphere that weeps continuously. Moisture beads on the underside of cantilevered expressways, runs in fat, trembling sheets down the fluted flanks of carbon-composite towers, and cascades off catwalks in uninterrupted curtains. The lower tiers—the sumps and arterial alleys—are calf-deep in warm, fast-moving runoff that smells of ozone, zinc, and warm broth. 

Nothing green grows here. Chlorophyll never took root in the basin, or perhaps it was scrubbed out centuries ago when the substrate was poured. In its place is an aggressive, opportunistic mineral-crust: grey-white blooms of lime, silica needles that crystallize in the spray of pressure vents, and rubbery, petroleum-black mats of chemotrophic slime that coat the drainage grates. The surfaces are not raw metal, but aged, weathered synthetics—ceramics that have dulled to the color of wet river stones, their sharp architectural edges smoothed down by decades of continuous, abrasive condensation.

The noise is a physical weight. It is not an echo, because there is no empty space large enough to bounce a sound; it is a solid, crushing roar that presses against the eardrums like deep water. The sound is an amalgam of ten million simultaneous processes: the high-frequency shriek of magnetic transit sleds sliding through pressurized sleeves; the wet, rhythmic slapping of hydraulic valves clearing sludge from the lower gutters; the thunderous, basso-profundo drop of cooling water plunging down vertical conduits five miles high; and beneath it all, the frenetic, chittering agitation of billions of bodies moving at speed. 

It has the density of a collapsed star and the frantic, boiling energy of a kicked anthill. There is no empty space. Between any two buildings, a third has been wedge-cast or extruded; between those, dense bundles of fiber, weeping coolant pipes, and hanging scaffold-tracks fill every cubic foot. The city does not respect the boundary between inside and outside. Walkways cut straight through the humming guts of turbine halls; balconies overlook open reactor sumps where the steam is so bright it blanches the skin.

Through this labyrinth of wet stone and machine logic moves an enormous, restless fauna. They are not pets or livestock, but the established inhabitants of a post-natural biome. Long, pale things with hard, multi-jointed ribs and slick, hairless hides weave through the tangled conduits, their claws tipped with hard horn that grips the wet ceramic ribs of the towers. Flocks of leathery, dart-shaped skimmers—warm-blooded, blind, navigating by the intense electromagnetic buzz of the city’s power distribution spine—wheel through the narrow light-wells in dense, boiling clouds, feeding on the greasy motes of protein scum thrown up by the ventilation flues. In the flooded gutters, blind, eel-like scavengers six feet long thrash through the current, thick as salmon, feeding on machine-slurry and the detritus of the endless levels above.

The light is broad, diffused, and relentless. It comes from everywhere and nowhere: luminous panels the size of city squares built directly into the wet underbellies of the overhead tiers, the incandescent glare of high-temperature conduits glowing cherry-red through layers of quartz lagging, and the flat, white luminescence of arc-welding rigs crawling endlessly across the structural seams like luminous lice. 

Everything is in motion; nothing stays still long enough to dry. A gantry shifts with a groaning shriek of stressed iron to let a slurry-barge clear a canal; overhead, a cluster of modular living cells uncouples from its spine with a hiss of steam, sliding laterally along grease-slick rails to make room for a new heat-sink tower being extruded from the depths. The water collects, channels, boils into steam, hits an overhang, condenses, and runs down again in an unbroken, deafening cycle. It is a world that has entirely consumed its own geography, running hot and wet at the bottom of the sky, vibrating with the terrifying, blind efficiency of an engine that forgot it had a builder.
