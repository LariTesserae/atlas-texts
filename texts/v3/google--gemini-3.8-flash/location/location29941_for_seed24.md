The air inside the pocket does not move. It is warm—not with the radiance of direct sun, but with the heavy, stored heat of deep bedrock, an isothermal warmth that has settled into the silt and limestone over centuries without weather. 

This is a sunken blind, a dead-end chamber carved out of the lower foot of a pale sandstone escarpment. It sits low in the topography, beneath the reach of wind, bounded on three sides by curving walls that have sloughed off their sharper angles. Long ago, water or sand-scour ground the stone down to soft, vaulted overhangs; now that work is finished, and the surfaces have the dull, matte finish of an old bone left in a dry cupboard. The ceiling stays close, four or five body-lengths high at its arch, dipping toward the rear into an apron of talus that has not settled by a millimeter in human memory.

Light barely enters. The mouth of the hollow opens onto an alley of sheer, towering rock that blocks the sky, leaving this chamber in a perpetual charcoal gloom. Objects do not have color here; they have degrees of absorbency. The eye, after adjusting, finds no sharp edges, only gradations of gray and warm umber where the faint ambient scatter from the gorge mouth expires against the floor.

The floor itself is a level bed of compacted, floury dust mixed with degraded shale. It is bone-dry. There is no trace of mud, no salt-heave, no seasonal crusting. A few sparse presences punctuate the flat expanse: low, skeletal shrubs, spaced widely apart, each claiming several square yards of territory with an austere economy. They are woody, crooked things, knee-high at most, their bark smooth and tight like dried sinew. They do not drop leaves because they bear none; instead, their thin, green-black twigs terminate in blunt, hardened thorns that hold a resinous, peppery scent. They do not seem to be dying, but they do not visibly grow. They exist in a suspended metabolic balance, nourished by deep taproots that vanished into the jointing of the bed-stone long before the upper world dried out. Between them lies generous emptiness—open drifts of fine, pale grit that carry no footprints.

Near the back wall, where the rock forms a low shelf, there is a scatter of dark chert flakes. They are not an architecture; nothing has been constructed, piled, or cleared. But the stones do not belong to the sandstone matrix. They are foreign river-cobbles, brought here when the air outside was different, broken open by direct percussion. Six or seven sharp, Conchoidal chips lie beside a rounded hammerstone that retains a faint indentation where a thumb once rested against its balance point. The edges of the flakes are still keen, protected from tumbling or grit-drift by the absolute deadness of the air, yet they are filmed over with a microscopic patina of undisturbed mineral dust. The work of striking them ceased, the hands that held them left, and the silence closed back over the space like water over a pebble.

Sound has very little substance here. A shifted foot produces a soft, muffled puff in the powder, immediately swallowed by the porous stone and the heavy air. There are no insects ticking in the deadwood, no bats in the fissures, no trickling seep. The warm rock insulates the hollow from whatever seasons pass over the rim high above. Winter and summer do not reach down this far; day and night register only as a slight thinning or thickening of the gloom at the chamber’s entrance. 

Everything here has reached its angle of repose. The plants have found the exact volume of foliage their deep roots can sustain without water; the sand has filled every depression to an even grade; the heat remains equal between the air and the stone. It is a place that does not await anything, existing wholly within its own small, warm perimeter, complete and unbothered by time.
