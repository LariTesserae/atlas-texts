There is no sight here. The blackness is total, absolute, and unyielding—not the twilight of deep woods or the charcoal gray of an overcast night, but the pure 0.0 zero of an optic void. To exist within this space is to encounter it entirely through the pressure on the skin, the acoustic return of walls, the wet heat of the air, and the unceasing collision of life moving through the dark.

The scale is that of an immense cathedral or a hollowed bell within the summit of a mountain. Though the elevation is extreme—thousands of meters above the lowlands, where the outside air would be thin, dry, and freezing—this pocket is sealed and pressurized by something ancient. The air is remarkably thick, heavy with moisture and sitting at a perpetual, feverish warmth. It smells of wet river stone, hot vegetable mulch, and the sharp, alkaline copper of hyper-dense alloy. 

Water does not pour here; it weeps and suspends. The atmosphere is permanently near its dew point. Microscopic beads of moisture gather on every hair, leaf, and surface, forming heavy droplets that break and slide down vertical planes before falling into the void below. Every few seconds, from varying heights, a fat drop strikes a broad, fibrous pad far beneath with a hollow, echoing *tock*.

Because the density of solid matter within this chamber is so sparse, the space feels like a cavernous hollow. There are no cluttered piles of rubble, no maze of tunnels. There is only an enormous volume of open, warm air, bounded by distant, weeping perimeters. Across this gulf, sound behaves strangely. The space is loud, filled with a dense, textured roar of reverberation. The constant drizzle of condensation from sixty meters overhead strikes the lower basins with a continuous hiss. Beneath that hiss lies a low, resonant, subsonic thrum—a vibration felt in the teeth and ribs rather than heard in the ear, the lingering idling pulse of technology that operates on principles far past conventional mass and current.

Scattered throughout this cathedral-sized hollow are the bones of that tech. They are not consoles or girders, but enormous, monolithic curvatures that have weathered beyond all utility into pure landscape. The surfaces of these objects feel impossibly smooth where untouched, but aeons of mineral-rich drip have worn them into softened, organic profiles. A sweep of a hand across what feels like a granite wall reveals a seam where stone merges into a cool, frictionless material that repels water at the molecular level, forcing the condensation to leap away in tiny, spitting beads. These ancient pylons do not generate light; they generate the pressure, the warmth, and a subtle gravitational tether that holds the atmosphere trapped high in the cold peaks, operating with the unhurried, patient rhythm of geological time.

Where the ancient alloys meet the worn rock, the vegetation has claimed every micron of foothold. Without sun, this is not a green flora, though it is aggressively lush. The plant life has evolved to feed on the radiant heat of the monoliths and the dense, ion-rich moisture of the air. It is a jungle of touch and texture: mats of thick, blind velvet that muffle footsteps to nothingness; fleshy, swollen succulents as large as carriage wheels, their skins taut with sweet, warm water; and hanging cables of fibrous creepers that drop straight down for thirty meters into the dark, swaying gently in the slow, convection-driven draft that rises from the floor. The foliage is so thick around the margins that it creates vertical, suspended swamps—bogs of root and peat clinging to shear walls, saturated like soaked sponges.

In this warm, wet, echoing silence, the fauna is teeming. 

Because there are no eyes, there is no stillness. The black air is occupied. Blind, broad-winged things—neither bat nor moth, covered in a dense, oiled down that deflects the mist—sweep through the immense clearance between the walls. They do not fly silently; their wings churn the heavy air with a rhythmic, leathery *whuff-whuff-whuff*, accompanied by high, crystalline clicks used to map the curvature of the vault. Thousands of them roost in the upper hollows, shifting and rustling against one another with the sound of dry parchment.

Along the wet vegetation of the walls, life is even denser. Segmented crawlers the size of forearms glide through the velvet moss, their carapaces rubbing against the slick rock with a wet, scraping chatter. Blind amphibians with external gills as long as willow leaves cling to the weeping pylons, exhaling short, percussive barks that echo off the curved ceiling, their calls answered in staggered choruses from opposite sides of the void. 

Everything here moves according to a slow, ancient metabolism. There are no seasons, no day and night. The temperature never drifts more than a fraction of a degree. The water falls, pools in the lower sump, is drawn up through capillary action and subterranean heat, condenses on the cold vault above, and falls again. It is a closed, perfected biome that has run out of reasons to change, settled into an eternal, loud, and bustling equilibrium where life thrives entirely in the dark, cradled by a forgotten machine inside the skull of a mountain.
