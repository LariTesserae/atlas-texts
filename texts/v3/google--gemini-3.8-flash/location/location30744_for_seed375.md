It occupies a sunken basin no larger than a municipal square or a cluster of four city blocks, bounded on all four sides by sheer, fracture-fresh cliffs of cold gray siltite. The edges where the rock sheared away are knife-keen; the break is so new that no detritus has gathered at the bases, and the stone looks as though it were sliced by a wire yesterday afternoon. 

Yet within this modest, sunken clearing stands what can only be described as a town—not ruined, but actively organizing itself out of dry biological secretion.

There is no metal here, no mortar, no evidence of chisel or wheel. The architecture is a marvel of blind, instinctive masonry, erected by no hands at all. It resembles an impossibly dense, tiered settlement: multi-story tenements, colonnades, covered lanes, and high, ribbed aqueducts that march across the clearing only to terminate abruptly in empty air. The substance of these buildings is a pale, bone-like mineral foam, chalky to the eye but glassy and hard as flint to the touch. The walls are thin, brittle, and pierced by millions of uniform rectangular apertures, like the cells of an immense, desiccated hive that has adopted the civic geometry of a human port.

The air is utterly dry. A tongue exposed to it goes tacky in seconds; no dew has ever condensed on these lintels. The temperature sits at a steady, cellar-like cool, preserving everything in a state of brittle suspension. 

Despite the total lack of moisture, the basin is intensely restless. It changes by the minute, though it does so in profound, absolute silence. 

The porous stone does not merely exist; it grows, extrudes, and collapses in a continuous biological metabolism. At the northern end of the clearing, a row of three-story facades rises from the dust at a visible crawl, extruding fresh chambers of pearlescent lime like teeth pushing through a gum. At the southern end, an older arcade, having completed its brief cycle, silently fractures along crystal stress lines, its pillars sliding downward into their foundations with the smooth, oiled grace of pistons, vanishing back into the pale bedrock without a scrape or rattle. 

The silence is physical, almost oppressive. The stone itself is a labyrinth of microscopic sound-traps; every echo, breath, or impact is instantly swallowed by the infinite porosity of the ground. When a cantilevered balcony shears off thirty feet up and strikes the pavement, it falls in eerie pantomime: a puff of chalk-white flour blooms outward, but there is not even a thud. The basin drinks all vibration.

Light here is thin and sourceless, a twilight filtered through a high, stagnant ceiling of dry haze that never gathers into clouds. It casts long, faint, multiple shadows, as if several dim moons were caught just above the basin’s rim.

Life within this mineral city is sparse and alien to water. Clinging to the sharp, newly formed cornices are tufts of what appear to be silver filings: dry, metallic lichens that do not root, but anchor themselves by electrostatic tension. Lower down, along the silent avenues, grow low hedges of bellows-weed—segmented, desiccated bladders made of something like parchment. They pulse with a rapid, erratic rhythm, pumping the cool, dry air through internal filters to harvest ambient mineral dust, collapsing and inflating like paper lanterns.

True fauna is exceedingly scarce. Occasionally, an inhabitant appears high on the walkways: a creature with the dry, multi-jointed gait of a whip-scorpion, but the size of a hound, its carapace composed of the same chalk-glass material as the streets. It possesses no eyes, moving along the narrow balconies with absolute spatial certainty, adjusting the angle of a wall here, scraping away an irregular deposit there, operating not as a builder with a blueprint, but as a wandering white blood cell maintaining a vast, non-human infrastructure. Its pads make no sound upon the sharp pavements. It pauses, tests the arid air with long, whip-thin antennae, and then retreats into an aperture that immediately begins to close behind it, healing over with a fresh, paper-thin skin of stone.

The scale never broadens; the entire system is contained within this cold amphitheater of cleaved rock. It is a city that is also an organism, barren of water, furiously constructing and dismantling its own dry avenues, forever busy, entirely indifferent, and perfectly mute.
