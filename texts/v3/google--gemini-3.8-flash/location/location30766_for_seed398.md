At eighty-four kilometers above the planetary crust, the sky has long since yielded its blue to the black of near-vacuum, but within the thermal mantle of the Third Pylon, you would never know it. 

You are inside a void that was never meant to be empty, yet is. 

It is a pocket district, a recessed urban hollow scarcely a kilometer across, suspended within the spine of an orbital tether. Outside its armored walls, the thermosphere is virtually nonexistent and bitterly cold, but inside, the air is thick, stifling, and heavy. Heat—radiant, relentless, and trapped—bleeds out from the core conduits that channel field-energy from the stars down to the world below. The ambient temperature hovers near the threshold of human endurance, a dry-bath heat that catches in the lungs until the moisture cycles trip.

And they trip constantly. Moisture here does not fall from clouds; it sweats from the engineering. Every few minutes, a massive phase-cooling loop somewhere deeper in the machinery purges, venting high-pressure vapor through hair-thin micro-fissures in the black ceramic ceilings. The mist drifts downward through fifty fathoms of dim, cavernous stillness, cooling just enough to condense on cold-rolled alloy lattices and unpolished vitreous planes. It gathers in sudden, heavy beads that drop without warning onto the stone-faced walkways below, splashing against edges so sharp they look as though they were sheared from their molds only yesterday.

There is no soil here, no lichen, no moss. Life has not been invited, nor has it had the centuries required to hitchhike up the pylons and establish a foothold. The materials are raw, pristine, and aggressively mineral: slabs of isotropic carbon, slabs of pale silicon-nitride, and vast, mirror-sheened ribbons of room-temperature superconductor that snake through the avenues like dormant black rivers. The architecture has the deliberate, terraced logic of a high-density city—cantilevered residential hives, modular administrative plazas, multi-tiered pedestrian viaducts—yet it was manufactured in a single, continuous additive-print cycle that seems to have terminated only recently. The corners of the buildings are razor-keen; no wind has rounded them, no boots have worn the stair treads. It is a metropolis caught in the first week of its existence, or perhaps its abandonment.

The scale feels secretive, almost stolen. Standing on an overpass, you can see the curving bulkhead of the outer structural shell just a few hundred meters away, curving upward to form a vaulted, ribbed sky. It is a miniature world, an architectural diorama sealed inside a structural rivet.

Yet, despite the urban density of the buildings, the feeling is one of profound, hollow emptiness. The city was built to house tens of thousands of technicians, yet there are none. The avenues are completely vacant. There are no abandoned tools, no detritus, no signs of panic or departure. There is simply the unpopulated shell, built by machines for an occupant that failed to arrive before the access lines were severed.

Light is sparse and sullen. The sun does not penetrate here; illumination comes only from the low-draw diagnostic bands embedded in the curbs—thin strips of pale, amber phosphorescence that pulse at a slow, biological cadence. In their dim wash, the condensation glistens on the walls like a layer of oil. Further up, near the apex of the vault, an erratic coronal bleed from a power transformer throws off an intermittent, violet twitch, casting long, jumping shadows across the canyon-like alleys.

Sound behaves unnaturally. The air is dense, but the geometry of the district is baffled with non-reflective acoustic tiling. Footfalls do not echo; they are swallowed instantly by the floor. What remains is a profound, suffocating quiet, punctuated only by the internal hydraulics of the pylon. A deep, sub-audible thrum vibrates through the soles of your shoes—the kinetic tension of the entire planetary counterweight flexing in the solar wind. Occasionally, the sudden, sharp *tink* of an alloy beam expanding under heat rings out like a rifle shot, followed by the steady, rhythmic *drip-drip-drop* of condensed coolant falling from a gantry into a shallow gutter.

The gutters drain to nowhere. The water, warm and smelling faintly of ozone and synthetic esters, collects in thin, shimmering pools in the sunken plazas, reflecting the faint amber strips of the streetlights. There, it sits until the radiant heat from the carbon flagstones boils it back into vapor, sending it swirling back into the rafters to begin the cycle anew.

It is a machine running an idle program. A freshly minted city, baking in the dark, perched at the very edge of space, breathing its own sweat in total isolation.
