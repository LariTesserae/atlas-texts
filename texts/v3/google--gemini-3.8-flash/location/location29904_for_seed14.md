The water here has the mass of iron. It sits under hundreds of meters of overhead blackness, chilled to a fraction above freezing, heavy with the minerals of a drowned landmass that sank long before names were given to the continents. 

This is a vast benthic plain—a drowned valley system stretching out for leagues, but there is no emptiness in it. The space is entirely occupied, dense to the point of suffocation. The floor is not soft silt, but an unbroken, packed expanse of cobbles and rounded boulders, worn down through hundreds of thousands of years of ceaseless grinding into perfect, flattened ovals. There is no dirt between them; they sit shoulder to shoulder, locked into a colossal, pavement-like matrix that stretches toward every horizon.

Above this stone floor, the water does not drift; it shears. Violent, blind sub-surface currents—internal storm-surges born of abyssal tides and cold-water cataracts from distant shelf breaks—churn across the terrain in a frenzy of silent turbulence. Because of the density of the brine, the current does not merely flow; it drags and hammers. Millions of fist-sized river-stones, smoothed aeons ago by long-vanished glacial meltwaters, are kept in a perpetual, tumbling agitation. They roll over one another in a crawling, chaotic carpet three stones deep, an endless shuffling of hard surfaces.

Yet, despite this chaotic mechanical violence, the sound is swallowed by the pressure. Water deadens the clatter into a low, chest-vibrating shudder—a continuous, dull thrumming that travels through the bedrock rather than the open liquid. If a creature were down here to listen, the sound would arrive not as noise, but as an ache in the bones, a rhythmic vibration of stone grinding stone under sixty atmospheres of weight.

Light has no foothold here. The surface is too far above, veiled by leaden skies and leagues of salt. What exists is the total, heavy blackness of the bathyal depths, interrupted only by the rarest cold flash of green-blue bioluminescence when a wandering comb jelly or an abyssal ctenophore is torn apart in the sheer currents. 

Vegetation is reduced to its most austere limits. There are no kelp forests, no swaying fronds. What little plant life exists takes the form of thin, crust-like encrustations of calcified algae—dull, iron-gray sheets clinging to the lee sides of the few boulders too massive to be rolled by the current. They grow at the speed of stone itself, nourished by chemical drift and cold nitrates, grazed only by the sparse, pale brittle-stars that wedge themselves into the seams between cobbles, their five arms tucked away from the abrasive churn above.

The land itself is ancient, flattened to its final structural truth. The crags, the jagged peaks, the young terraces—all of them were long ago sheared away by sea-level rises, marine transgression, and the relentless hydraulic milling of the ocean floor. Everything has surrendered to curvature. The landscape is a series of low, undulating rises, like giant sand dunes, but made entirely of dense, interlocking shingle.

And buried in that rolling pavement are the stray marks of an earlier, dry world. They are rare, nearly digested by time, but persistent: in the hollows between the boulders lie heavy, fire-cracked river rocks from campfires lit before the ice melted, and thick, bifacial hand-axes of dark chert. Their knapped edges have been battered blunt by thousands of years of tumbling under the sea, their facets softened until they look almost natural, but the unmistakable geometry of primitive human hands remains locked in their cores. Deep-sea detritus has settled into their flake scars. Nearby, half-embedded under a drift of quartzite pebbles, rests the petrified crown of an elk antler, grooved by flint blades to yield harpoon blanks, now worn as slick and black as whalebone, enduring the dark, freezing churn of a world that forgot it had ever been dry.

The turbulence does not abate. It shifts with the distant oceanic tides, swinging a few degrees north, then west, dragging the saturated floor along with it in a blind, churning frenzy that accomplishes nothing, reshaping the already-smoothed stones into slightly smoother stones, cold and eternal in the absolute dark.
