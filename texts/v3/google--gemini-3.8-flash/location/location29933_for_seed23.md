The glare is the first fact of the shelf. At this altitude, the atmosphere is thin enough to sting the eyes, yet saturated with a suspended, hyper-pressurized spray that splits the unmitigated sunlight into blinding, circular halos. It is brutally cold—far below the threshold where breath becomes frost—yet the water refuses to settle into stillness. It sheets over every surface in razor-thin, vitreous films, kept liquid by an extreme load of dissolved salts and the constant, violent churning of its delivery.

The place occupies a hanging limestone amphitheater, no larger across than a town square or the floor of a ruined cathedral, bounded by towering, fluted scarps that have been ground smooth by millennia of chemical dissolve. Because the scale is compact, the density inside it is staggering. Every cubic fathom is occupied by stone, bone, timber, water, or breath.

An entire human collective lives here, compressed into a vertical, labyrinthine favela lashed directly into the hollowed runnels of the rock face. The architecture is ancient and crude, achieved entirely with stone adzes, ground-slate gouges, and hand-twisted sinew. Thousands of small, cell-like dwellings are stacked atop one another like the cells of a frantic hive, constructed from slate slabs, whale-ribs dragged up from prehistoric lowlands, and stiff, wet walrus-hide hardened into amber-colored plates by the brine. Walkways made of notched driftwood poles and tensioned gut crisscross the chasm, vibrating underfoot. There are no chimneys, no smoke; fire cannot survive the saturating, high-velocity vapor that tears through the cleft. 

What makes the shelf strange is its silence against its speed. 

It is a place of manic, almost violent kineticism that produces almost no sound. Torrents of supercooled, mercury-bright water rush from siphon-holes in the cliff overhead, smashing onto stone baffles, yet the sound is swallowed completely—absorbed by the deadening blanket of dense, churning foam, the porous vesicular stone, and the strange, thick viscosity of the air. When an outrigger platform collapses under the weight of rime, it falls without a crack, landing in the churning lower reservoirs with only a dull, muffled thud, like wet felt striking slate. People move through the climbing paths in frantic, hurried streams, skin greased with rendered tallow against the cold, communicating not with voices but with rapid, fluttering sign-systems executed with broad, leather-mittened fingers.

The fauna outnumbers the stones. In the absence of green plants—save for a few black, leathery encrustations of cold-lichen that weep bitter oil into the cracks—life here is entirely animal, carnivorous, and hyperactive. 

Thousands of pale, grease-skinned lung-eels, blind and corded with muscle, thrash endlessly through the sluices, their bodies churning the brine into a warm, yellowish froth. Compact, fat-insulated birds the size of fists, completely featherless and covered instead in a dense down of water-shedding bristles, dart in crazed, erratic clouds between the suspended huts. They dive through the blinding sheets of water to snap at the eels, colliding with walls, rolling, scrambling back into the air with frantic leg-kicks, never resting. Beneath the walkways, clinging to the underside of the dripping timber pilings, are clusters of translucent, cold-water barnacles that open and snap shut with the rhythm of rapid pulses, filtering the spray.

The light does not dim; it ricochets. The white stone, the vitreous sheets of liquid, the perpetually renewed mirrors of fresh ice, and the suspended vapor multiply the sun until the space has no shadows, only differing intensities of white and silver. Men and women work within this searing luminescence with eyes shielded behind slits of blackened ivory. They work continuously, forced by the environment into an unstable, Solaris-like maintenance: scraping away rapid mineral blooms before their doorways are sealed shut, re-tying lashings that rot under the sulfurous moisture, and harpooning the heavy, blind river-beasts that are thrown out of the mountain's high sifters.

Everything is wet; everything is frozen; everything is moving at the speed of panic, yet when you close your eyes, you hear only the faint, dull lap of foam and the wet *slap-slap* of thousandfold leathery bodies shifting in the light.
