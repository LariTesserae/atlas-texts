The basin lies in a wide, sunken depression near sea-level, stretching out for leagues beneath a hard, pale daylight that leaves no edge softened. There has not been time for the wind to round anything here; the bedrock is scarcely older than the scaffolding bolted into it. Raw, unplaned basalt and pale, glass-like silicates push upward in jagged sheets directly from the mantle, still glossy from their recent solidification, without a grain of soil or sand to pad their seams. And yet, this embryonic floor does not wait for quiet to settle before it is claimed. It is being seized, smothered, and built upon in a single, continuous convulsion.

Across miles of visible expanse, the city exists in a state of violent, silent metabolism. Cast-iron gantries, pneumatic conduits, and multi-tiered boilerworks are jammed against one another with an unbearable spatial density—there is virtually no dead room between presences. Where a corrugated factory wall shears away, it does not open onto an alley or an empty lot; it gives way instantly to a wall of wet, fibrous green, or the cold flank of a hydraulic cylinder four stories tall, or a fresh upthrust of vitreous stone that has cleaved through a lower floorboard within the last hour. 

Despite the frantic mechanical churning—the stroke of massive counterweights, the cycling of valves, the furious knitting together of brick vaults and iron trusses—the place is eerily quiet. A heavy, deadening hush hangs over the basin, swallowed by the cold air and the endless carpets of thick, water-retaining felt-moss that sheath every surface. Metal strikes metal not with a clang, but with a dull, grease-muffled thud. The vast pneumatic networks exhale in long, whispering sighs rather than sharp whistles. Even the continuous cracking of the earth below sounds like nothing more than the splitting of dry timber under a heavy boot miles away.

The chill is constant, hovering just above freezing. It does not freeze the water outright, but holds it in a state of perpetual, sluggish dampness. Cold sweat beads on the lacquered iron skin of the engines and runs down copper rivets in steady, braided trickles. Intermittent, fine sprays drift through the upper air—not quite rain, but the dense condensation thrown off by miles of cooling towers, dropping through the bright air to settle on the vegetation. 

And the vegetation is ferocious, fed by the mineral-rich sweat of the machines. It is not an old forest, but an aggressive, pale, cold-loving surge of life. Fronds as thick as leather and gray-green lichens with the tensile strength of hemp wrap around drive shafts, forcing engineers to fit rotating blades to their flywheels to keep from being strangled mid-stroke. Pale liverworts and spongy, bladder-like succulents crowd into the shadow of the boilers, blooming with small, odorless wax-white flowers that drink the oily runoff from the joints. 

The turbulence here is not atmospheric, but structural. The place behaves like a living tissue caught in a fever of repair and cancer. An entire block of red-brick textile tenements, mechanized loom-halls, and steam-distribution manifolds will be erected over the course of three mornings, complete with glass-paned dormers and zinc guttering. By the fourth morning, a sudden lateral shift in the unweathered stone will buckle the foundations, and the city’s autonomous industrial apparatus will not dismantle it, but simply build *through* it: plunging iron pilings through sitting rooms, draping high-voltage copper lines through shattered hearths, and feeding new pneumatic slurry pipes down the stairwells. Whole levels are swallowed while their hearths are still lukewarm, their rooms instantly colonized by thickets of ghost-white ferns and the scuttling things that live between the bricks.

Fauna thrives in the saturated interstices. Small, flat-bodied mammals with silver, grease-repellent fur thread their way through the cable-bundles, hunting for blind crickets that feed on industrial tallow. In the damp, drafty spaces between parallel iron bulkheads, colonies of swallow-like birds with serrated, horn-tipped beaks nest in the wet insulation, their calls reduced to clicking, dry rattles that carry no farther than an arm’s length. Larger things—heavy-shouldered, slow-moving scavengers with skin like boiled leather—lumber through the semi-submerged sumps beneath the machine floors, feeding on the heaps of vegetal sludge chewed up by the pumps. They do not run from the machinery; they move with the cadence of the pistons, leaning their ribs against the hot piping for warmth.

Above it all, the light is remarkably clear, cold, and steady. There is no smog to stain it—the combustion here is chemically lean, the exhaust scrubbed through massive banks of wet gravel and moss filters before it reaches the open sky. Shadows fall long, blue, and razor-sharp across the multi-tiered landscape, illuminating the uncanny geometry of the place: miles of gleaming, newly minted roofs stacked tight like shingles on a pinecone, interspersed with sudden vertical fins of volcanic glass, smoking valve-sheds, and hanging gardens of cold, wet greenery that drop twenty feet down into the narrow flues between competing foundries. 

Everything is happening at once, and nothing has had the leisure to decay. There is no rust here, only raw iron filmed with oil, fresh stone slick with condensation, and the bright, wet sap of crushed vines leaking into the seams of engines that never stop running.
