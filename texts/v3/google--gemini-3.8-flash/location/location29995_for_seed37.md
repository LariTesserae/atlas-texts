It is no larger than the hold of a small skiff, buried several meters beneath an unremarkable crust of cold, grey shale, yet it possesses no ceiling that the eye can verify. The light here does not come from above or from bioluminescence; it is simply that the air inside this pocket is intrinsically bright, white-gold and non-directional, casting no shadows from the few things that occupy the room. Because there are no shadows, spatial depth folds up strangely: a ridge an inch away looks like a horizon, and a pebble in the middle distance seems to float against the eyelid.

The stone is still wet with the dampness of its own birth. This is not ancient geology being worn down by water, but embryonic stone still assembling itself out of a milky, semi-firm sludge. The walls—if they can be called walls—feel like cold, curdled fat where they meet the air, hardening only along a microscopic front where the bright air touches them. There are no cracks, no tumbled scree, no signs that a single grain has ever weathered away. The floor is an uninterrupted expanse of tallow-colored calcite, slick and cool to the skin, sweating tiny beads of sweet, odorless moisture that well up from nowhere, sit perfectly spherical upon the stone, and then abruptly vanish by falling upward into the luminous void.

Very little grows, and what does has not yet decided whether it belongs to the plant kingdom or to geometry. Every few feet, rising from the soft pale sediment, stands a single, brittle spine of something resembling translucent glasswort. These stems do not branch; they simply terminate in clean, blunt prisms that softly hum when moisture touches them. Between them lies generous, cool emptiness. The space is uncluttered, nearly vacant, save for the movement of the things that live here.

And the life is everywhere, moving with urgent, silent purpose.

The fauna here does not obey the normal divisions of body and environment. There are creatures resembling tiny, legless mice made entirely of compressed pond-water, swimming through the bright air three inches above the stone floor, their internal organs visible as clusters of amber beads that click softly when they bank or turn. There are flat, moth-like organisms with no wings, only broad sheets of dry skin that skim along the slick surfaces like magnets repelling one another, grazing on the faint, newborn minerals before they have even finished setting into crystal. Long, threadlike silver eels, no thicker than a horsehair, knot and unknot themselves inside the solid translucent rock, weaving through it as if it were warm honey, occasionally surfacing to let out a bubble of dry cold before sinking back into the limestone paste.

Nothing makes a sound above the breath of a sleeping animal. A faint, wet ticking occurs when the damp beads rise from the floor; occasionally, the leathery glide of a flat moth scrapes the mineral curd with a hiss like silk pulled over glass. Otherwise, it is an absolute, preserved quiet.

Yet the chamber is not entirely wild. Half-sunk into the soft stone, like coins pressed into butter, are the faint, chalky traces of something intentional. A doorframe, or the suggestion of one, stands freestanding in the center of the pocket: three slabs of pale, unbaked siltstone, joined without mortar, roughly four inches high. It frames nothing—only the empty, brilliant space beyond—yet the water-mice carefully swim *through* it, never around it, their amber beads flaring with a pale, cold flash as they cross its threshold. Elsewhere, half-buried in the virgin calcite, lies an ancient plumb-bob made of hollow, fired clay, tied to a loop of petrified gut; it does not hang down toward the earth, but sideways, pulled gently by a gravitational center that exists only for it, pointing steadily toward the chamber’s left wall.

Processes run continuously in this hollow, but they run sideways to normal time. The limestone floor is rising by hair-breadths every hour, slowly burying the artifacts, yet the artifacts themselves seem to be slowly dissolving the floor around them in self-defense, leaving shallow, dry moats of negative space. A pale, centipede-like thing with thirty pairs of glass needles for legs crawls toward a patch of wet mineral; where its feet prick the stone, tiny droplets of red water form, linger for a second, and then evaporate into pure, sour light.

It feels like a place where a world is being auditioned. The cool temperature keeps the materials from settling too fast; the dampness keeps the edges blurred. If you were to slide an arm into this pocket through whatever unseen seam connects it to the dark earth above, the brightness would coat your skin like warm oil, but you would feel the frantic, cool, silent brushing of dozens of tiny, formless mouths, tasting your knuckles to see what category of matter you might eventually become.
