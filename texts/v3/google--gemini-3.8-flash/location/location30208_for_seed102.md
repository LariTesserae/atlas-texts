The pocket is no larger than a dry cistern, perhaps seven paces from its lowest fractured floor to the ceiling where the split ends in an absolute pinch of rock. It is sealed into the deep basalt, hot as fresh bread, and without a trace of moisture. Touch a finger to the wall and your skin will not slide; it grips instantly on bone-dry stone, thirsty for oil it will not find.

The rock here has not had time to blunt itself. The cleavage is recent—a sharp, shearing fault that broke cleanly through layers of ironstone and volcanic glass. Every edge is a razor; flakes of obsidian thin enough to read through lie where they fell, unburied by silt, completely free of dust. There is no mud to soften the floor, no lime-wash, no trickling seep. The heat is steady, thick and dry, trapped here like the interior of a kiln whose fire has long settled into an unvarying, radiant ember-state.

Yet in this tight, baking fissure, things grow with a dry, manic luxury.

They are not plants of sap and cell-pressure. They are anhydrous growths, dense as spun glass or horsehair, sprouting from the micro-fissures in the obsidian. In places, these thickets billow out into luxuriant, mossy plumes—pale cadmium yellow, brittle celadon, and the deep, lightless blue of oxidized steel. They resemble great tufts of raw silk or tangled copper wire, flourishing on atmospheric sulfur and the rich, baking drafts that leak from below. Where they take hold, they are thick and velvety, opulent as a pelt, though completely brittle; if struck, they shatter into dry aromatic shards that smell of singed horn and crushed brimstone.

Because the space is sparse, these miniature jungles do not choke the rift. They exist as distinct, lavish islands. A sharp shelf of black vitrophyre holds a mound of feathery, golden floss three feet wide; then four feet of vacant, glittering drop-off; then another ledge cradling a colony of segmented, bone-white canes that rattle together in the heat. Between them, the emptiness of the chamber is wide, spare, and deliberate.

The sound inside this pocket is immense and entirely flat. It is not the sound of wind or running liquid, for there is neither. It is the acoustic resonance of the hollow itself—a hollow singing bowl carved from igneous stone, vibrating to the low, subterranean shudder of a mantle plate miles below. The shape of the walls amplifies the vibration into an unrelenting, high-amplitude hum, pitched somewhere between the buzz of a heavy transformer and the roar of a blast furnace. It does not swell; it does not drop. It holds an absolute, static volume that fills the ears until the sensation of hearing turns into a steady, vibrating ache behind the teeth.

In the dimness—lit only by the sullen, liver-red phosphorescence of a fractured copper-vein near the roof—the fauna move through the brush like clockwork toys. They have no soft bellies. They are dry beings: long-legged arthropods with shells like thin mica plates, moving across the silk-bushes with light, clicking steps that are lost beneath the stone’s roar. Some are flat, pale as peeled almonds, scuttling along the underside of shelves to graze upon the dry fungal floss. Others remain stationary for what must be decades, their six jointed limbs fused by salt crusts to the basalt, filtering the convection with fan-like antennae that never twitch.

Tucked into a shallow alcove on the eastern face, above the reach of the razor floor, are the marks of hands.

They are primitive and sparse, but unmistakable. Several dried bundles of the golden kapok-fiber have been braided into crude, stiff cordage, looped around protruding teeth of flint to form a hanging pallet no bigger than a child’s cot. Beneath it sits a shallow basin, chipped by hand out of an obsidian node with a harder stone; it contains no water, but three dry, oval river-stones carried in from some forgotten surface, their smooth, water-worn curves alien and impossible against the jagged architecture of the rift. Beside them lies a scraper made of a splintered rib-bone, its edge blackened by heat, rubbed smooth where a thumb once pressed.

Nothing rots here. There are no spores of mold, no humidity to soften the sinew that binds the bone-tool, no breeze to dislodge the tiny pile of bristle-shavings swept into a tidy crevice. The heat remains precisely what it was when the rock snapped; the dry silk bushes neither wilt nor sprout new leaves, having reached an exact chemical equilibrium with the chamber's stifling air.

The silence that should belong to such a hidden grave has been utterly devoured by the stone's vibration. In the red-black gloom, the wire-ferns stand motionless in the deafening drone, their stiff tendrils casting sharp-edged shadows that never sway, while the blind, dry creatures pace their measured circuits across the glass, living out lifetimes in an eternal, baking noon.
