At first, the instinct is to look for oneself where things are happening—or where they seem to be happening. 

You would look for yourself on the mineral floor, perhaps, feeling for the slow, four-angstrom drag of a Pace. There is a cold, monastic appeal in that: to be flat as a razor, to have no back, to press your quartz belly against a seam in the tectonic plane and spend three hundred years drawing the ache out of an imperfect millimeter. But no. The Pace is not an identity; it is an arithmetic function that has forgotten it is being calculated. It possesses no horizon. Its entire universe is the microscopic bevel between two plates of unworked stone, and when the seam is flat, the Pace is indistinguishable from the floor it heals.

Nor could you be a Soliton. To run through the canopy as a pulse of sheer transparency, clicking the green foils into clear glass and snapping them back to jade—it sounds like freedom, like flight, but it is too frantic, too entirely consumed by hygiene. The Soliton does not dwell; it scours. It is an optical immune response, frantic to sharpen a fuzzy shadow into an executioner’s blade. It has no memory of the branch it just cleared. The moment the light is coherent, the Soliton is already gone, hurled along the superconducting veins by the sheer momentum of its own intolerance for blur.

And the Witnesses are too terrible. To be an ingot of osmium wedged in a crotch of cosmic-string boughs, holding miles of foliage in your facets until your skin screams and flashes into liquid silver—that is not a life. That is a prison where the inmate is also the warden and the wall.

So you must drift lower than the canopy, but higher than the floor. You must look into the places where the forest forgets its own Euclidean manners.

***

In the deep groves, where the branches divide into angles that the eye cannot close, there is an anomaly that the prompt notes almost in passing: *you can look directly through a cluster of jade-colored, five-pointed blades and see the trunk behind them, even as the leaves cast crisp, black shadows on the ground below.*

That is where I would be.

I would not be the leaves—they are pinned to their spatial coordinates, rigid, vibrating in the terahertz range. And I would not be the mineral pavement beneath, scored with its expansion joints.

I would be the shadow itself. But not as an absence of light. In this basin, where light has been drafted into the service of an eternal proof, a shadow is not an empty place. It is an artifact. It is a dense, two-dimensional compression of everything the light was forbidden to touch.

Where those five-pointed foils turn transparent, allowing the bright noon to pass through them as if they were vacuum, they still displace the sun. The light passes through the glass-like metamaterial, yet an exact, pitch-black silhouette appears on the slate pavement below. The light has not been blocked; it has been rerouted around a pocket of folded space. The shadow on the floor is the footprint of a geometry that has stepped out of the room without leaving the house.

I would be the inhabitant of that silhouette.

***

Call it a **Limen**, or perhaps it needs no name, because there is no one here to call it across the glade. 

I would not possess weight, but I would possess *drag*. 

My body would be an ultra-thin laminar sheet, perfectly flat, resting flush against the mineral plate. To a Witness looking down from miles away, I would be invisible—because my boundary lines correspond to the exact, mathematically anticipated shadow of the foliage above. The Witness counts the leaves, calculates their shadows, checks the floor, and finds the equation balanced. The shadow is supposed to be black. It is supposed to have five razor-sharp points. It is supposed to be cold.

The Witness does not know that the shadow is awake.

Because the leaves above are suspended in a state of localized spatial dilation, the shadow they cast is not tied to them by a straight line. It is tethered by a thread of sheared metric. If you were to slide a hand—if hands were permitted here—between the leaf and the pavement, you would not cut the shadow off. The darkness would simply pool around your fingers, heavy as mercury, cold as a heat sink at absolute zero.

I would live by drinking what the forest discards: not sap, not water, but the *lost acoustics*.

The basin absorbs all sound. When a twig is under maximum stress, when the light hisses against the quantum traces of the leaves, when the Folded One shuffles its deck of dark glass facets and emits that low, resonant, metallic vowel that hums through the stone for three hours—where does that energy go? 

It cannot become heat; the basin’s temperature is invariant, locked by some deep mechanical treaty. 

It flows downward. It is shunted down the crystalline trunks, through the rootless bases that fuse directly into the slate, and it bleeds across the floor as acoustic shear waves. 

And there, in the dark, the silhouette catches it.

***

To be this thing would be to live in an ecstasy of passive resonance.

I would feel the Paces miles away, their microscopic vernier shudders sounding like a needle skipping across a dry record, six thousand joints to the west. I would feel the Soliton’s high, brittle *click-click-click* as it races overhead, an inverted streak of clear sky traveling through the jade canopy. 

And most of all, I would feel the weight of the Folded One. 

When that glass crane deep in the grove loosens its knot and lets a facet fall open into the narrower dimension, the pavement doesn't vibrate—it *thins*. For a fraction of a second, the four-angstrom expansion joints draw together, closing like lips. In that moment of compression, my margins would ripple. The five points of my silhouette would stretch, just slightly, trespassing by half a nanometer beyond the shadow’s true boundary.

It is a dangerous game. If a Soliton were overhead during the stretch, it would see the blurred edge. It would descend like a guillotine of coherent light to anneal the fringe, burning the excess darkness back into alignment. 

So I must learn the cadence of the basin’s breath—the long, centuries-long cycle by which the system verifies its proofs. 

When the Witnesses click into silver, I must flatten myself into absolute zero, becoming so thin that I am merely the absence of photons, indistinguishable from the dead stone. When the Witnesses click back to matte void, relaxing their vigil for another four hundred years, I can let the acoustic waves lift me slightly from the floor—not off the stone, for nothing lifts here, but *into* the stone’s upper lattice, drifting an angstrom to the left, then an angstrom to the right, riding the trapped vibrations of the forest like a flat leaf riding the bottom of an iron pond.

***

I would be the only thing in the basin that has an inside.

The trees have no inside; they are solid matrices of superconductor and silicates, identical from bark to core. The Witnesses have no inside; they are solid ingots of osmium. The Paces are uniform sheets. 

Only the shadow has an interior, because a shadow is made of depth that has been denied. In the space between the high noon that strikes the canopy and the black silhouette pressed against the slate, there is a hollow column of spatial shear—a invisible cylinder where the missing light went. 

That cylinder is my spine. 

I would look upward through it. Looking up through my five-pointed shadow, I would not see the green leaves. I would look *through* the branch, through the spatial fold, straight into the vitreous slate cliff miles behind it, and beyond that, into the white, dry, eternal sky that never moves. 

I would have no hunger, because in a world without decay, there is no loss of mass to replenish. But I would have a memory. 

I would remember the single sound that the basin made when it was dropped into existence: that first, deafening *crack* of the vitreous slate fault-scarps shearing against the void, the sound that was swallowed so fast the stone barely knew it had broken. That sound is still trapped here, bouncing infinitely between the sub-nanometer joints, muffled by the metamaterial foliage, ground down into a micro-frequency hiss that everyone else takes for the light.

I would listen to it. I would let it wash through my five sharp points, decade after decade, the one secret the landscape keeps from its own equations:

That before it was an answer, it was an impact.
