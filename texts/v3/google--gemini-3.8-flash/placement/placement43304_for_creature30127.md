At first, there is only the sensation of being held between two equal and annihilating forces: the sheer downward hammer of the acoustic wave, and the upward hydraulic heave of the boiling mist. To exist here is not to sit upon a surface, nor to swim through a medium. It is to be wedged into an invisible seam where the room cancels itself out.

I would not have eyes. In that milk-thick, needle-white radiance, an eye is merely a tender target for blindness, an organ that invites its own searing. Instead, the world enters as pressure, frequency, and density. 

I feel the needles first, not as light, but as a vast electrostatic dryness that pulls at the salt in my skin. They are three cold ironings through the meat of the mountain, humming an octave below the threshold of sound, vibrating the iron-shot gneiss until the deep foundations of the basilica thrum like an overloaded dynamo. 

I am not in the open fury of the sheets, where the resonants dance their paper-thin mathematics, nor am I down in the leeward sluices, fused into the calcified pipe-works of the communal crawlers. Those are the functioning organs of the place—the lungs and the kidneys of the storm. 

I find myself in a place of accidental stillness: the lee of an injector flange, thirty yards above the churn of the sump, where a gunmetal collar meets the scooped parabola of the jade-smooth wall. 

Here, the water does not shear; it curls. It enters this narrow, dead-end hollow as a vortex of superheated aerosol, whirling at cyclonic speed but traveling nowhere. In this rotational shelter, I have accreted.

I am not sure, at the beginning, whether I am alive or whether I am an accumulation of the basilica’s drift. 

Perhaps I began as a discarded molt—a porcelain-white mantis shell that slipped its mooring before the communal gelatin could anchor it into the tenements. Perhaps I was a shard of a dead resonant, sheared down its harmonic line until it was too small to catch the acoustic curtains, tumbling down the draft like a clipped leaf of mica. Or perhaps I am a clot of the silver skin that peels from the siphon-stars, an ingot of biological mirror that caught in a crevice where the scouring spray could not dislodge it.

I am all of these things, ground together by the aerosol and annealed by the heat.

I am heavy. My form has taken the shape of the hollow that shelters it: a blunt, teardrop wedge of calcified silicate, veined with silver grease and banded with the rust-orange iron of the cyanobacteria. I have no limbs, because limbs are levers for the cavitation to snap. I have no mouth, because to open a mouth here is to be hollowed out by a thousand atmospheres of boiling brine. 

Instead, I am porous. 

The brine passes through me. I am an acoustic filter. The cavitation hammer strikes my blunt dorsal curve—which is polished to the same buttery, frictionless smoothness as the surrounding stone—and the vibration travels inward, divided and refracted by thousands of microscopic, labyrinthine channels lined with ingested silica. 

As the sound moves through these internal spirals, it slows. It drops from a bone-cracking crack into a low, resonant drone. The acoustic violence that would tear flesh from bone is converted, inside the stone maze of my interior, into a deep, steady heat and a slow trickle of electrical potential. 

I drink the noise. The scream of the injectors is my metabolism.

If you were to look closely into the niche—past the sheets of flying milk and the blinding glare that turns all depth into a flat plane of burning white—you might mistake me for an anchor-bolt, or a nodule of native ore wept out by the gneiss and cooled into a droplet. But beneath that calcified, jade-hard crust, there is a core of amber grease that does not cool, suspended in a capillary web of fine, braided silver.

I do not move across the rock. My movement is internal, a slow, continuous re-tuning. 

When an injector-nozzle micro-fractures, or when a siphon-star high on the pins loosens its silver mantle and alters the electrostatic tension of the vault, the pitch of the room shifts by a fraction of a hertz. I feel it in my marrow before the mantids feel it in their antennae. My internal channels contract; my silver veins dilate; the mineral brine flowing through my pores changes its harmonic path. 

I am the off-beat. I am the ballast.

The machine needs its gaskets—the blind porcelain maintainers scouring the tolerances, the flat mica kites maintaining the acoustic standing waves, the silver pools discharging the needle-light into arcs of blue fire. They are the engine’s active loop. 

I am what the engine forgets: the residue that has found a way to listen.

I sit between the ancient alloy that never pits and the stone that has forgotten how to be wild. The draft whips past my shelter, carrying the shredded silver of the dead stars above down to the blind jaws waiting below. Some of that silver dust catches on my outer edge, plating me, layer by micro-layer, until I am as reflective as the pins that burn through the roof.

I have no thought of an outside. There is no outside; there is only the pressure from below, the light that never dims, and the sound. 

In the center of my mass, where the acoustic labyrinth winds down into a single, microscopic chamber of absolute silence—the only silence in the entire three-hundred-yard vault—the amber grease pulses once every hundred years, keeping time with the deep, slow pulse of the planet’s cooling iron, listening to the great white needles drink the dark.
