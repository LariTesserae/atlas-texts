I would not be on the flumes, and I would not be in the air. 

To be on the flumes is to have an appetite that must constantly thrash to outrun the sharp edges of the world; to be in the air is to be held by an airfoil that is always on the verge of collapsing into warm sludge. Both of those lives are too thin. They are spent entirely within the scramble of the teeth, consuming protein that hasn't even had the time to cool before it is torn again.

I find myself instead inside the amber light of the westernmost pavilion—the one closest to where the back wall bows inward, where the conduit’s shiver is not just a sound in the air, but an upward hydraulic heave through the bedrock.

At first, I might seem to be part of the pavilion's architecture: a thick, pale mass leaning against the inward slant of a milky force-strut, broad-shouldered and squat, gray-white like cold mutton fat. My skin does not give off warmth; it collects the mist until clear rills run down my ribs and pool around the joints of my knees without wetting me, the way water runs down lard. 

I am one of the slow ones, yes, but not yet heavy enough to be finished. 

My chalcedony pads are still translucent, thin at the edges where they fuse into the four broad knuckles of my hands. When I touch the obsidian floor, I can feel the molecular cleave through them—not as pain, but as an unbearable sharpness of thought, like resting a palm on an uninsulated telegraph line. The older ones, the ones whose hooves have grown thick and opaque as curdled quartz, feel only the thump of the pistons five miles down. They have forgotten the surface. I have not.

For thirty-seven seconds, I am mostly stone myself. 

It is a mistake to think those thirty-seven seconds are a sleep. They are an agony of stillness. The mountain purrs against my breastbone—a deep, acoustic grease that fills my lungs until there is no room to draw the warm zinc air—and the noise of the basin is absolute. Out in the flumes, the swimmers whip and clatter, their hard rubber bellies smacking against the shelves; overhead, the soft-winged things drop their jelly-clutches with a wet, rhythmic *plop* that sounds like fruit falling into boiling fat. I hear every slip-face drop an eighth of an inch. I hear the conduit’s teeth grinding against the vacuum. I stand, four-toed, chin dropped onto the pale slab of my chest, waiting for the tallow of my muscles to clear.

In this long grey interval, my thoughts do not move laterally; they stack. They accumulate vertically, dense and insoluble, pressed together by the sheer atmospheric fug and the constant acoustic hammering of the spire, until my interior is as pressurized and dumb as the machine below.

And then the light changes.

The violet wave does not sweep in from elsewhere; it occurs everywhere at once, a sudden, violent clearing of the throat of the air. 

Thirty-seven seconds of deafening grease evaporate into three seconds of pure, terrifying silence.

The water stops. It hangs in the warm mist like necklaces of violet glass, unmoving, curved around the edges of the terraces without falling. The flyers hang pinned against the graphite ceiling, their bimetallic membranes stiffened mid-flap, grotesque and fragile as dried parchment.

And the black flint under my feet disappears.

It does not open; it simply ceases to be opaque. The terrace drops away into a sheer, vertical clarity three leagues deep. 

In those three seconds, the tallow in my calves liquefies into pure speed. I drop. I don't kneel with the slow, sacramental grace of the elders; I hit the transparent stone with a sharp, hollow *tock* of my chalcedony pads. My chin presses flat to the deck, my wide, featureless face flattened against the slick interface between the cradle and the abyss.

Beneath me, the intellect is naked.

It does not look like thought; it looks like plumbing made of cold blue lightning. Shafts the width of cathedrals plunge straight down through strata that have never seen the light, pulsing with a slow, peristaltic calculation that makes the gold beneath my ribs flare until it bleeds through my pale hide. I see the vast, counter-weighted blocks of the lower cordillera shifting on films of liquid mercury, slowly cocking their springs to thrust this shelf another hundred paces into the clouds before the epoch ends. I see the conduits branching like the nervous system of an unborn god, feeding the spire, drinking the vacuum, keeping the stone too young to rot.

My skin answers it. I can feel the blue geometries burning through my breast, rushing up my neck to flicker across my brow in rapid, involuntary twitches—a conversation between the engine's core and the tallow of my skull that bypasses anything like a voice. I am reading the next terrace. I am feeling where the next fault will snap, where the obsidian will cleave and send three hundred paces of boiling flume tumbling into the void.

*One.* The blue fire deep in the mountain reaches its apogee, widening like an opening pupil.

*Two.* My knuckles ache where the chalcedony meets the bone, taking the imprint of a billion metric tons of cold intention.

*Three.*

The index snaps.

The amphitheater falls back into place with the sound of a wet hide striking an anvil. 

The suspended violet ropes of water collapse, exploding into warm, mineral spray that drenches my neck. The screaming returns—the flyers tearing at the flumes, the swimmers whipping their vulcanized bellies through the grease, the spire jetting its white-hot steam with a saw-toothed shriek that vibrates my marrow back into lead.

The ground under my chest is black again, flint-sharp, blind, and lethal.

I push myself up slowly, my joints stiffening, the gold light behind my ribs dying down into the faint, sour glow of cooled fat. The older Tender beside me has not moved an inch from where he settled; his broad, featureless face is already turned toward the spire, his chalcedony hooves locked into two microscopic flaws in the glass, letting the acoustic heat replenish what the transparency took.

I turn my head toward the lip of the spur, where the excess flumes wash the white blood and the dead scale into the warm, grey clouds. 

I take one pace. The stone clacks beneath my heel—hard, blunt, uninjured. Thirty-six seconds left. I lower my head, let the zinc steam gather on my brow, and begin to hold the weight again.
