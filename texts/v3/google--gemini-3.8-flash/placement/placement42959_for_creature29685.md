At first there is only the tension between two kinds of hardness.

There is the hardness that has surrendered—the cobbles beneath, smoothed into yielding ovals, sliding over one another with the heavy, hydraulic indifference of grease—and there is a different hardness, sharp-cornered, unnatural, stubborn. A tiny concavity. A flake scar where a wedge of dark chert was driven off by a sudden, decisive strike long before the water had this terrible weight.

I am wedged into that strike. 

It does not feel like an idea; it feels like an anchor. Tendrils of stiff, tanned keratin, coiled like dried vines, have bitten into the micro-fissures left by the flint-knapper’s hammerstone. They have held through decades of blind cataracts. They hold against the sixty atmospheres pressing downward, a column of cold, iron-saturated brine that does not fall so much as sit, flattening every hollow until only the densest structures remain.

Inside the capsule, there is no light to wait for. Light is an abstraction that belongs to skies that dried up and blew away before the glaciers melted. There is only the brine, seeping through micro-slits in the leathery wall, bitter with copper, dissolved silica, and the liquefied fat of things shredded miles upstream. 

I am small here, folded in four, my snout curled down toward the dwindling knot of yolk at my belly. But I am not soft. Softness cannot endure the teeth of this floor. My skin is already setting into rough dermal denticles, minute teeth of enamel and bone that rasp against the interior of the purse whenever the violent sub-surface swells roll the shingle overhead.

Above me, the mill is always turning. 

I do not hear it. There is no air to carry a sound, no eardrum to flutter. Instead, it enters through the rock. The knapped chert transmits the shudder of the entire drowned valley directly into the base of my skull. It comes as an ache, an oscillation so deep and constant that my blood paces itself to the rhythm of stone abrading stone. 

And in that dark, within the cavities of my head, something is growing to meet it: two stones. 

They are not bone; they are heavy, dense concretions of calcium carbonate and iron, suspended in pockets of clear, viscous acoustic oil. They are my ear-stones—my otoliths. As they accumulate mass, pulling mineral from the slurry that washes through my gills, they grow heavier than the flesh around them. When a boulder three leagues to the west is dislodged by a benthic squall and rolls across the pavement, the valley rings like an iron anvil. My body shifts, but the otoliths lag behind, dragging against their nerve-linings, registering the weight of the stone, its speed, the pitch of its destruction.

I know the geography long before I have touched it.

I know that three feet to the north, the shingle is loose, rolling in three crawling tiers that would crush a snout to pulp. I know that beneath the petrified crown of the elk antler, the current stalls, deadened by the black tines, leaving a pocket of water so thick with drifting organic flour that it barely moves. I know where the grit-chitons are rasping their tracks into the flint, because their magnetite teeth make a high, dry click that travels along the bedrock like a spark.

The yolk is nearly gone. The leathery square of the egg-case is softening, abraded thin by the fine quartz flour that settles between the rocks. The tendrils that hold me to the ancient tool are beginning to fray, worn through by the ceaseless friction of water that shears like a blade.

Soon, the seam of the purse will part. 

I will not rise. To rise is to be caught in the blind gale of the cataract, stripped, and thrown against the shelf breaks. I will drop directly into the seam. I will let the sixty atmospheres press me down into the gray mineral paste, fitting my shovel-snout into the narrow gaps between the locked cobbles, where the tallow-worms slide like cold lard and the water is still.

My wings will spread—stiff, muscular, slate-gray sheets of sandpaper cartilage—hugging the contours of the flattened stones. I will become another tier of the pavement, another heavy, blind thing sliding through the cold grease of the seabed, reading the low-register groan of the continent as it grinds itself into dust.

And behind me, left in the notch of the dark chert where a human thumb once pressed to test an edge, the empty black husk of my case will remain: anchored, torn, slowly filling with the iron sand of a world that has forgotten the sun.
