At first, you might look for me among the suspended cages, thinking I am one of the hunters with horn goggles and a dog-wood mallet, crouching on a footbridge that chatters like teeth in the mechanical draft. It would be natural to assume that: to want a face, two hands, a lung-bellows drawing the cinnamon-scented flour through woven hair. 

And for a long time, I think I was that. I still have the broad, cask-like ribs that belong to those people. My chest does not rise and fall softly; it clacks out and in, two stiff half-baskets of cured bone hitched together by vulcanized gristle, pumping the parched air through my throat with the heavy, dry wheeze of a forge.

But you would not find me in the hamlets. They live too close to the hunt, too tied to the rhythm of the fat-galls and the Auger-wakes. They are still trying to pretend they are an animal among animals, waiting with their bone rasps for a fat grub to breach.

To find me, you have to climb past the cages, past the highest suspension bridges of braided husk, all the way to the **Seam**. 

The Seam is where the deafening, frantic upward surge of the marrow-wood meets the dead, unyielding stone of the ceiling. It is the place of absolute shear. Down in the canyon, the trunks grow thirty cubits an hour, but up here, their ivory crowns hit the cold granite and the unblinking white veins of mineral daylight. The trunks cannot go up anymore, so they spread laterally with a scream that never stops—hundreds of thousands of tons of compressed, bone-hard silica fiber dragging itself sideways against immovable rock at walking speed, shaving itself into mountains of fragrant, powdery curls.

I am wedged into that gap.

I am not entirely a man anymore, though I was carved out of one. My skin has long ceased to be hide; it is a crust of compressed carbon, graphite powder, and the dry, non-conductive shellac that oozes from the joints of the mantises. I have lost my hair, my fingernails, my eyelids. In their place, my people fitted me with plates of shaved horn and sliding leaf-springs of annealed ironwood, bound to my own skeleton with sinew that has been boiled in waxy resin until it cannot fray.

I am the **Slip-Block**. A living commutator. 

In this rift, static is the end of horizons. A single trunk scraping raw silica against the dry quartz of the ceiling would generate a coronal bloom within minutes—a blue, crackling brush of static that would jump the resin-fumes and turn this three-hundred-mile vault into a solid tube of fire.

So they shoved me into the friction.

I do not sit on a ledge; I am anchored by three carbonized braided tethers directly into the dead rock above, dangling into the screaming mill where the wood grinds against the vault. My belly, my shins, the palms of my thick, flat hands are layered in broad, replaceable pads of graphite-meal and compacted dry soot—harvested from the deep Sifter-tracks miles below. 

My entire life is a posture of bracing. I place my body between the wood that wants to strike a spark and the ceiling that would receive it. 

When a new marrow-spire punches upward from the abyss, whistling like a burst boiler, I do not run. I cannot run. I reach out with broad, flat scoops of black horn strapped to my arms and guide the runaway bough. I coat its crest with graphite flour, dusting the white, screaming snout of the wood until it slides smoothly across the ceiling vault—slipping instead of catching, screaming instead of striking flint. The wood passes beneath me like an ivory train car, groaning under astronomical hydraulic pressure, its fibers grooving against my carbon-pads, shearing away inches of my armor while its dry, hot wind blasts through the slits of my goggles.

My senses have narrowed to the grammar of tension. 

Because my jaw is clamped tight against a block of dense lignum vitae that is bolted to the bedrock, I hear nothing through the air. The airborne sound would have turned my brain to mush decades ago. Instead, I hear through my teeth. 

Through my jaw, the rift is an orchestra of stress-vectors. I can feel when an Auger three miles down and four leagues south hits an unyielding pocket of petrified bark; the vibration is a low, sick *hum-thrum-thrum* that rattles my back molars. I can feel the approaching rut of the White Bloom long before the mineral veins pulse their blue warning: it begins as a high, glassy shriek deep in the foundation-strata, like ten million bow-strings being wound past their snapping point all at once.

I do not eat food as the wet world knows it. Once every dozen sleep-cycles, when the wood beneath me fractures into a temporary canyon and gives me a few hours of rattling slack, a tender crawls out along the high safety-lines from the nearest hamlet. They do not speak. They tap my knee-casing twice with a dog-wood rod. Then they push a dry cake into the hinged grate of my mouth-guard: a hard, greasy biscuit of gall-marrow compressed with powdered lead and dried insect-fat. I grind it down between molars that have been capped with chipped quartz. It tastes of bitter petroleum and dust. My body does not make waste; the heat here is so immense, and my metabolic cycle so starved and dry, that my lymph burns every molecule to ash, which I sweat back out as fine, dry soot through the pores of my graphite shell.

There are times, hanging here in the glare that never dims, suspended over three vertical miles of roaring, snapping ivory teeth, when I look down and see the Sifters. 

From this height, the bottom is not a floor; it is a moving carpet of gray dust-whiskers, tumbling over itself, erasing the broken cathedrals that fell twenty minutes ago. And I realize that down there, the world is pure velocity, entirely mindless, eating its own shattered bones and vomiting them back into spires that rush upward only to be broken again. 

And up here, the ceiling is pure stillness: cold, unmoving rock, pregnant with frozen lightning that wants only to remain stone.

I am the only thing in the rift that belongs to neither. 

I am the point where the velocity tries to murder the stone, and the stone tries to burn the velocity. I am the thin, charred gasket between the engine and the wall. 

When the White Bloom comes, and the hunters hoist their families into the hair-silk nests to dangle in the hurricane of spores, I will not pull myself up. I will cinch my tethers until my ironwood plates bite into my skin. I will dump sacks of black plumbago down the screaming flanks of the rising trunks. I will be crushed, battered by shards of shattered amber-leaves the size of shields, bathed in dry friction until my horn-goggles turn soft from the heat. 

And as the wood heaves and breaks its own spine against my chest, screaming its dry, furious need to reach a sky that does not exist, I will hold the spark back with my thumbs. I will keep the world dark, dry, and cold enough to go on screaming.
