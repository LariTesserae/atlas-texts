At first, there is only the sensation of being held. 

Not by arms or by intention, but by the brine itself—a medium so dense with dissolved iron and bitter salts that it resists parting. It does not feel like immersion; it feels like belonging to a fluid that matches, grain for grain, the salinity of the tissue it presses against. There is no cold, and therefore no concept of skin as a barricade against the world. 

Where do I begin? 

Not at an eye. There is no socket, no lens, no rim of bone waiting for a photon that will never descend through the miles of black ocean overhead. To search for a horizon would be like trying to breathe through a stone. 

Instead, there is a low, humming perimeter. It begins in twin discs on either side of what must be my mantle—smooth, pale pads, taut as stretched tallow, ringed with fine, shivering hairs. Through them, the dark is not empty; it is crowded with shapes made entirely of charge. The water is a web of faint, crackling voltages. Every living thing leaks a ghost of its own metabolism into the broth: the dull, throbbing iron-fields of the Hearth-Isopods down in the flues; the quick, jittery static of the Glass-Toroids wheeling in their hollow towers; the slow, massive capacitance of the Chandelier Polyps hanging miles above on the frozen lip of the abyss, their stalks charged like wet Leyden jars.

I find myself suspended three meters above an avenue of pulverized shell, drifting without stroke or effort, buffered by the warm, upward draft of a ventilation chimney. 

My surface is not smooth. It is clefted—split into thousands of deep, velvety furrows that drink the water and deaden every shock. When a gas pocket ruptures in the silt a quarter-mile away, the thump does not strike me; it settles into my skin like a hand pressing into goose down. I am an echo-sink. I leave no acoustic wake. The blind fish grazing the stone ten inches from my flank do not turn; their lateral lines register only a gentle thinning of the thermal plume, a momentary warmth that might be an eddy or a stone.

I extend two arms outward. They do not grope. They drift out like heavy ribbons, webbed together by a skirt of thick, elastic muscle. 

The tips graze the wall of a residential spire. 

Instantly, the city floods inward through the lip-discs along my underside—not as an image, but as a dense, layered narrative of flavors. The lime here is old, weathered down by forty years of current, its aragonite softened into chalk. Beneath that: the faint, sour bite of sulfur-enzymes left by the luting-worms when they sealed a structural hairline during the last seismic heave. Overlaid upon that: a thin, sweet ribbon of Pellicle-slime, alkaline and greasy, laid down perhaps twelve cycles ago by a grazer moving methodically toward the pinnacle. 

I taste the path the slug took. I taste the trace of copper in its wake. I taste the shadow of an isopod that paused against this lintel to scrape the white bacteria from its knee-joints three days before.

The wall is not masonry. It is a registry of everyone who has leaned against it.

In the cradle of my inner arms, curled tight against the beak at the center of my web, there is a weight. 

It is a nodule of pure pyrite, two inches through, heavy as a fallen star. It has no edges. For centuries—perhaps across the lifespans of six or seven of my kind—it has been rolled between suckers, smoothed by the slow friction of leathery skin until the natural crystalline cubic geometry has surrendered to an absolute, unblemished sphere. It is cold—colder than the brine, because iron carries the deep bedrock’s chill longer than flesh can. 

As I turn it, the pale vibrissae around my sensory discs twitch. The sphere has its own electrical grain, a tiny, polarized field that orients toward the iron vents in the floor. It is a compass, but it is also an anchor. Without it, I would be too buoyant, too easily swept into the high, killing drift where the plume meets the boundary-layer. The stone holds me to the streets.

A faint pressure wave passes through the channel: *tuck-tuck-tuck*. 

Far down the alley, a hundred thousand tube-doors have snapped shut. Something broad and heavy has entered the thoroughfare. 

I do not flee. There is nowhere to flee to that is not the city. I tilt my siphon, venting a soft, oily stream of spent water, and descend toward the pavement. My arms spread, kissing the shell-gravel. The gravel is warm, fermenting beneath a carpet of bacteria that smells of rot and burnt wire—a comfortable, home-flesh smell.

I drift toward the central plaza. 

The space opens up: an amphitheater formed by the concave flanks of four immense, fluted ziggurats. The water here is almost still, rotating in a sluggish, blood-warm gyre driven by a dozen vents in the floor. At the center of the plaza, half-sunken in the settled silt, lies a knot of pale leather.

Three of my kind are already there. 

They are resting together in a hollow smoothed out of the shell-bed, their long mantles collapsed like empty sails, their arms loosely braided into a single, breathing mass. They do not turn to face me; they have felt my electrical silhouette since I entered the conduit a hundred meters back.

I slide into the hollow beside them. 

My skirt slackens. My skin meets theirs with a soft, yielding friction—suede against suede, fold settling into fold. Where our surfaces touch, the tiny galvanisms of our bodies begin to align. My hearts—all three, slow and thick-walled—drop their tempo, falling into the metronome of the cluster. 

One of them—an elder whose mantle is already graying with the white, calcified crust of encroaching lime—slides a tasting-disc across the back of my third arm. It leaves a smear of chemical memory: *the eastern flutes are venting cold; a silt-fall has crushed the conduits near the salt-spring; the chandeliers are heavy with grenadier-flesh.*

In return, I open my web. 

I pass the pyrite sphere into the tangle of their limbs. 

A dozen suckers receive it, tasting the track of my voyage, the mineral savor of the minaret I grazed, the faint, lingering sweetness of the slug-trail. A younger octopod takes the sphere, rolling it slowly against its belly, feeling the perfection of its curvature, polishing it against its own leather, adding the grease of its own skin to the surface before it will pass it on.

Here, in the knot, the boundary between myself and the basin begins to blur. 

The heat coming up through the gravel under our bellies is the same heat pumping through my veins. The lime being excreted by the millions of blind threads inside the stones around us is the same mineral hardening in the tips of my suckers. We are not visitors here; we are the city’s soft, mobile thoughts, moving through its stone skull, carrying its tokens from room to room.

Far above, inside a dark chimney, a shoal of Glass-Toroids snaps in unison—a dry *crack* like breaking bone that reverberates through the lime. 

A tremor. A shift in the earth's marrow. 

None of us move. The elder’s arm tightens slightly over my mantle, holding me down against the warm gravel. In the walls, the builder-worms will already be vomiting their aragonite glue into the fractures. The Hearth-Isopods will be wedging their iron plates deeper into the scald. The city will hold its shape, because it cannot conceive of any other, and we will stay in the hollow until the water clears, trading the cold stone between us in the dark.
