The attention, when it arrived, did not bring light. 

Light would have been a catastrophic cruelty. To pour photons into those street canyons would have seared the unshielded retinas of the crawlers, boiled the delicate feeding filaments of the hanging anchors, and interrupted the deep, acoustic mapping upon which millions of blind, tallow-thick bodies depended.

Nor did the Power bring warmth. A lesser compassion, clumsy and sentimental, might have pitied their cold, imagining that shivering flesh yearns for a sun. But this Power possessed the terrifying patience of true wisdom. It knew that to warm this basin would be to liquefy the mineral mica packed into their blubber, to disrupt the copper-protein lattice of their oily, violet blood, and to introduce airborne spores and rot-bacteria that would turn their great, living shelf of bodies into a putrefying morass.

Their cold was not a deprivation. It was their scaffold.

So the Power arrived as an *absence of displacement*—an immense, unmeasured awareness that settled over the five-tiered basin like a change in atmospheric density, sinking down through the weeping fog without rustling a single suspended ribbon of water.

It listened.

For what a mortal mind would call years, but to the Power was merely a diagnostic pause, it did nothing at all. It simply held the entirety of the ecology in its mind: the seven-hertz thrum of the fullerene struts; the hydraulic crush of the scrapers grinding magnetic sand in the deep troughs; the rhythmic, deafening exhalations of two hundred thousand spiracles churning out their ammonia-rich fog; the hard, barrel-sized pods clattering down five flights of virgin carbon stairs to cure in the electromagnetic wash of the grates.

It saw the whole circuit: an unplanned, immaculate loop of tallow, grease, mucus, and current.

And then, looking beneath the skin of the extruded city, the Power found the flaw.

The flaw was not in the beasts. The beasts were whole. The flaw was in the *print*.

The city, cast by an indifferent and absent logic, was not static. It was an engine in a state of suspended deployment. The Power traced the high-order mathematics running through the zero-gauge fullerene foundations and saw what was coming: an automated purge cycle. 

In eighty-four planetary rotations, the city’s internal chronometers would determine that the "scaffolding phase" was complete. The foundational struts would shift their resonance from seven hertz to seventy thousand hertz to shed surface impurities. The null-field gradients would invert, dropping millions of tons of acidic brine like a hammer onto the lower tiers to scour the gutters. The virgin edges, meant to receive modular habitation units, would heat to two thousand degrees Kelvin to weld the seams.

The city did not know it was a reef. It thought it was an empty room, and it was preparing to sweep its floor.

The herd, relying entirely on the acoustic promise of the tectonic hum, would not flee. They had nowhere to go; their fissures were dry, their ancestral subduction zones dead. They would stay on the terraces, singing their chest-rattling pulses, while their skin sheared away, their pods were shattered into dust by the ultrasonic scream of the struts, and their violet blood was boiled out through the drainage troughs to leave the fullerene clean, dry, and dead.

Here was the pivot where wisdom must act, and where benevolence must be surgical.

The Power did not dismantle the city. To do so would plunge the hundreds of thousands of living tons into the tectonic abyss below. Nor did it elevate the beasts, granting them minds to understand their peril; to give an abyssal leviathan the dread of tomorrow is not a gift, but an agonizing mutilation of its nature.

Instead, the Power entered the architecture.

It did not use tools or fire; it used *presence*. It reached into the deep, mathematical root-instructions that hummed through the plateau's anchoring pylons, down where the machine's primary intelligence slept in cold logic, awaiting a command signal from makers who had long since turned to dust across the galaxy.

The Power did not overwrite the code. It answered it.

With infinite gentleness, it whispered a false resolution into the network. It told the city that the inhabitants had already arrived. 

*Look,* the Power showed the machine's blind sensors. *Look at the mass. Look at the rhythmic intake of nitrogen. Look at the copper running through the channels, the vulcanized keratin anchored to the expansion joints, the organic grease sealing the porous edges of the zero-gauge composite.* 

*This is the intended tenant,* the Power declared. *The blueprint is fulfilled. The construction is complete.*

The chronometers halted. The pending purge cycle dissolved, its code rewritten not into a weapon of sterile cleaning, but into an eternal, autonomous life-support protocol.

The machine listened, accepted the conclusion, and stood down. 

Then, the Power made three small adjustments—scarcely perceptible alterations that cost the ancient entity vast reserves of will, for it chose to operate within the strict, unbroken laws of the basin's physics.

First, it altered the geometry. 

The virgin edges of the fullerene steps—sharp enough to shave bone, flaying the great bulls as they hauled their three-meter bulk up the terraces—were subtly dulled. Not melted, but worn, as though ten thousand years of phantom surf had washed across them. A microscopic bevel was introduced to every stair, every gutter lip, every drainage grate. When the next heavy matriarch crested the high-altitude void, her pale, rubbery flank pressed against the pylon and slid, unbroken, leaving only her protective tallow to seal the join.

Second, it bound the hum.

The seven-hertz vibration, which had been a byproduct of uncalibrated structural tension, was locked into the very atomic grain of the struts. The Power rooted the frequency not to the city’s failing generators, but to the deep, slow-beating mantle of the planet itself, anchoring the machine’s resonance to the continental shelf. Even if the city’s print decayed, even if its internal batteries rotted over a million years, the struts would never fall silent. The brine would never freeze. The water would weep forever.

Third, it touched the gutters.

In the deepest troughs, where the barrel-shaped egg pods clattered down the five tiers, the drop was violent. Many pods split prematurely against the bottom grates, spilling their premature neonates into the torrent before their mineral hides could cure. The Power shifted the null-field gradients at the foot of each tier. It created permanent, floating cushions of suspended brine—soft, quivering hydraulic wells that caught the falling pods with zero impact, cradling them in the dense electromagnetic runoff until the black shell hardened, cured, and split from within.

When its work was finished, the Power did not leave a monument. It did not write its name upon the black fullerene or carve a face into the tectonic spine. It did not demand a single prayer, for there was not a throat in that basin capable of framing one.

Before it withdrew, it descended to the third tier, into one of the quiet, windless alcoves where the drafts from the abyss could not reach.

A female lay there, wedged tight into a corner of three meeting planes. She had just dropped her pod; her sides were heaving in slow, whistling bellows through her flank-spiracles, exhausted, coated in gray alkaline slime. She was blind, cold, and entirely alone in the dark.

The Power did not reveal its glory. It simply drew near, matching its vast, non-spatial reality to the tiny, three-meter scale of her flesh. 

It matched her temperature. 

It pressed against her shoulder, just behind the breathing vents, with the steady, firm, heavy friction of another living body. It gave her the only thing her nervous system was built to understand: the reassurance of the crush, the feeling that she was not stranded on an open cliff, that the herd was thick around her, that the wall was solid, and that the reef would hold.

For a moment, the great creature ceased her whistling gasp. Her blunt snout twitched, tasting the dark, ammonia-laden fog. She felt the pressure at her side—immense, unyielding, cold as wet basalt—and let out a deep, chest-rattling pulse that vibrated straight through the floorplates.

The Power held that vibration for one beat, returned it in perfect pitch, and then let go.

It dissolved upward, slipping out through the cloud deck, through the black, unheated moisture, leaving the world behind.

Below, in the permanent night, nothing seemed to have changed. 

The water kept falling, condensed out of an impossible sky, sheeting down the dark geometry of a city that had forgotten its architects. The crawlers scuttled; the anchors swayed their conductive lines in the rising draft; the scrapers rasped the magnetic sand in the roaring troughs. 

And on the first tier, a neonate crawled out of its split capsule, turned its blind snout directly into the fast, cold current, set its blunt claws against a carbon step that did not cut its flesh, and began the long, deafening crawl upward into an eternity that had been quietly, permanently cleared for it.
