A lesser power would have brought sun.

A clumsy compassion, seeing the cellar-chill of the depression, the bruised violet of the light-trapped reeds, and the tallow-thick hides of creatures that have forgotten how to burn, would have breached the cloud shelf with a lance of golden, standard-thermodynamic fire. It would have sought to thaw the ground, to balance the weak force, to set the metric straight, to turn the upside-down cataracts right-side-up and teach the Chime-strays the familiar, bloody mercy of lungs and a red heart.

And in doing so, it would have murdered every atom in the basin.

The reeds, stripped of their coherent latency, would have flash-ignited into piles of tin-smelling ash. The Drift-veils, suddenly subject to standard terrestrial mass, would have fallen like wet leather sacks from the air and burst against the stones. The Slump, its mercury veins ripped by an abrupt Euclidean flattening, would have de-cohered into thirty leagues of toxic broth; and the Tallow-gangers, their cork-textured fingers torn from their quartz rods by the sudden restoration of textbook inertia, would have had their grey matter sheared to mist by the snap-back of ten million years of accumulated metric debt.

True wisdom understands the terrible sanctity of an accommodation. It knows that after enough eons, the scar *is* the body. You do not cure an ecology that has built itself out of the shrapnel of broken laws; you do not insult ten million years of cold, hydraulic patience by calling it an error.

So the power did not descend as light. It did not speak. It had no face to press into the mist.

Instead, it adjusted its own mass-differential until it was exactly eight degrees Celsius—the temperature of wet slate and old peat—and it drifted downward through the permanent grey shelf without disturbing a single nodule of floating mist.

It came as *counterweight*.

The first thing it addressed was the tearing. 

Beneath the wet roar of the cataracts, the basin had lived for ages with that frantic, mechanical shear—the sound of space-time attempting and failing to resolve its own altered geometry, the constant, underwater *thwip* of metric tension snapping like cables. That snap was the terror of the valley; it was what kept the Slump awake and rigid, sweating its dark mercury through the drowned limestone galleries, bracing the valley floor against the sky like a blind titan whose shoulders have begun to calcify under the load.

The power sank down into the muck. It did not alter the altered laws; it simply stepped beneath the hinge.

It let the shear pass into itself.

Where the weak-force anomalies dragged against the local vacuum, the power offered its own vast, supple continuity as a buffer. It was like sliding an oiled silk sheet between two grinding sheets of slate. The shear did not stop, but the violence went out of it. The percussive *thwips*—those sudden, violent releases of metric strain that blasted cold aerosol twenty feet into the air—slowed. They did not disappear; they stretched. The sharp, cable-snapping violence softened into a deep, rhythmic sigh, like the settling of a ship’s timber in deep ocean swell. 

In the depths, the Slump felt it first. 

Three miles of living sediment, an ancient colonial mind thick with heavy salts and old grief, felt the sudden, impossible presence of a second foundation. For the first time since the unknown catastrophe broke the bedrock, the ground was not holding itself up by sheer desperation. The Slump did not dissolve; it did not change its nature. But the mercury veins, which had pulsed with a rapid, defensive stutter, slowed to a steady, majestic pace—once every three hours, cool and silver-dark in the grey grease of the limestone. The Slump slackened its terrible, cramp-locked tension. It spread. It rested.

Above, in the iron-cut channels, the water did not turn right-side-up. That would have starved the Chime-strays. But the current through the centers of the stationary pools grew clearer. The vitreous phosphate ribs of the Carcasses, suspended from the peat overhangs, began to chime with a clearer tone—not the desperate, jagged rattle of bone under stress, but a bell-like, resonant pure fifth that drifted through the mist like music played on crystal horns. Small, three-lobed swimming things still drifted into their clear lymph, because the Chime-strays must drink, and the three-lobed things must go back into the mud; the power did not abolish hunger, for hunger is the loom on which the basin is woven. But it removed the friction that made existence feel like an amputation.

Then, the power went to the hummocks.

A Tallow-ganger was sitting on the spine of a slate whale, its three-toed hands resting on a thirty-foot shaft of quartz that angled down into an inverted spray of pale water. It had been sitting there for ninety years, turning the stone by widths of a hair, listening with its blind, wedge-shaped prow of cartilage for the catastrophic drift that would tell it the sky was falling.

The power did not reveal itself as a majesty. It took the form of another body—seamless, hairless, cold as lard, with deep runnels wept into its forehead to catch the dew. 

It climbed the slate hummock with the slow, double-jointed roll of the valley’s kind, its feet leaving quiet rings of oil on the moss. It did not touch the quartz rod; that was the ganger’s work. 

Instead, it sat down in the peat directly opposite the creature. 

For two days, neither moved. The dew gathered on them both in identical pearlescent globules. The bruised-violet stalks of the light-reeds hummed around them, shedding their low interference patterns across the stagnant pools. Loom-leeches drifted through the shallows nearby, their margins blurred, drinking the glow of snapped rushes until they were stiff and quiet as lead.

On the third day, the power leaned forward.

The Tallow-ganger felt the displacement of air, the subtle dip in the metric gradient that meant another mass was near. It did not pull away. It could not fear; fear was too metabolic, too expensive for this place. It simply turned its blunt, grooved prow toward the visitor.

They touched foreheads.

Between their brows, where the skin was thick and white like rind, there was no burst of holy light. There was only the tiny, sparkless discharge that smells of turned earth and cold tin. 

Through that contact, the power did not offer language. It did not offer a vision of blue skies, or the warmth of stars, or the memory of what the world had been before the weak force was unstitched. Such things would have been poison. To show an eye-less thing a dawn is not mercy; it is an insult.

Instead, the power showed the Tallow-ganger the *balance*.

It showed the creature that the stone it held was not holding back a cosmic error. It showed the ganger that its forty years on the slate were not an absurd, mechanical accident, but that the valley had heard every adjustment; that the peat was shaped by its patience; that the hum of the bruised reeds was not a scream, but a hymn that had simply forgotten its own words.

And into the Tallow-ganger’s cork-like hands, through the vibration of the quartz, the power passed an infinitesimal adjustment—a shift of three seconds of arc, a tiny turn that resolved a harmonic knot eighty fathoms beneath the hummock.

The rod stopped vibrating against the creature’s palms. The strain died out of the stone.

The cataract fifty yards away did not become ordinary water; it remained suspended, running upward toward the clouds like a ribbon of black glass. But the spray it threw off was no longer aerosolized by violence. It fell back into the moss in soft, coherent globes that rolled across the liverwort like drops of mercury, unbroken, perfectly at peace with their own impossibility.

The Tallow-ganger did not weep—it had no eyes, and its fluids were reserved for the grease of its joints. But its vertical mouth, set deep between its collarbones, parted a fraction of an inch, releasing a slow, bubbling breath that smelled of wet shale and ancient, settling silt. It did not let go of the stone rod, but its shoulders, which had been drawn tight against the shear for a century, dropped an inch.

It knew, in the small, deep way a stone knows the earth beneath it, that it was no longer alone in the listening.

The power remained. It did not leave the basin.

It did not build a temple, nor did it pull back the grey shelf of cloud. The sun remained a smudged nickel coin, far off and impotent. The cellar-chill remained eight degrees. The Drift-veils still skimmed the reed-tops on their keels of vacuum, drinking the heavy water from the throat of the air, their sickle-wings silent against the dim violet twilight.

The power simply became the basin’s lowest stratum. It became the stillness beneath the Slump; it became the hand under the quartz; it became the quietness into which the metric tension unspooled without breaking.

And so the basin went on—vast, altered, and strange. Its inhabitants still softened when their time came, their light unspooling with that faint, lonely *whick* into the mist. But they no longer dissolved into an uncaring wound. When they slipped from their phase-state, they slipped into an earth that had been met, that had been understood, and that had been held by something that knew their impossible, beautiful laws by heart, and loved them enough to leave them wild.
