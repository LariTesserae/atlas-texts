The Power that arrived had no face, nor did it carry with it the insulting intrusion of a sun. 

It was neither an engine nor an emissary. It was an awareness vast enough to inhabit the weight of a world without displacing an ounce of its water, and wise enough to know that to love a thing is not to alter its nature, but to comprehend its necessity.

It descended through the pewter dark, moving not against the currents, but through them, like thought through flesh. It did not warm the water. To heat this sea would be an act of slaughter: it would choke the wedges, whose calcite carapaces were tempered by the near-freezing brine; it would slacken the silica ropes whose high, singing tension depended on the bitter cold; it would rot the leathery flesh of the discs, who knew only the freezing sulfur-snow boiling from the lightless trench.

The Power listened first.

It felt the sub-audible shudder of the plateau passing through its own vastness. It mapped the endless avenues, the smoothed megaliths like cakes of tallow, the blind, manic ribbons knotting themselves in the lee of the bastions, the rollers grinding their fifty-pound cobbles in centuries-long migrations across the stone. It saw that there was no sin here, no error, and no fall. There was only the magnificent, terrible economy of a machine that had outlived its designers by an eternity, and in outliving them, had become a cradle.

It saw that the creatures did not mourn. They did not weep for trees, because they had the cables of living tallow; they did not pray for light, because the rebounding roar of the water gave them a shape far more honest than color. They were whole.

Except at the lip.

The Power drew itself down to the southern rim, where the artificial world broke off into the miles-deep drop, where the water leapt upward in reverse cataracts, and where the iron-colored things hung by their tails in the vertical gloom.

It hovered beside one of them.

The creature was three fathoms long, jointed like a rusted chain, anchored to a fissure that had been widening for six hundred thousand years. Its multi-jointed arms moved in their slow, agonizing cadence: *clack, clack, clack.* Its small horn spatula was worn down to an uneven stump. It was clearing a grain of quartz no larger than a tooth, dislodging it from a seam where two townhouse-sized blocks met.

The Power looked into the creature’s marrow.

There was no thought there—not as men understand thought. There was no memory of the hands that had hauled the granite, no myth of the sun, no song of a dry earth. But there was something heavier than thought: there was *tenacity*. There was a rhythm set into its nerves before the first stone grew cold, an imperative that had survived the extinction of its masters, the drowning of the sky, and the slow dissolution of the continent:

*The joint must be clean. If the joint fills, the frost will bite. If the frost bites, the block will shift. If the block shifts, the seam will fail. If the seam fails, the sea will fall.*

The creature did not know what the sea was. It did not know what falling meant. It only knew the agony of an uneven line. It knew that the wall was leaning by a fraction of a hair, that the grit was winning, and that its spatula was almost gone.

A lesser power would have lifted the creature up. It would have carried it to a calm pool, given it eyes, fed it soft flesh, and set it under a gentle sky—and in doing so, would have murdered it entirely, leaving it to thrash and die in the horror of an unresisting universe.

A proud power would have remade the plateau in diamond, banishing the currents, smoothing the seams forever, rendering the creature’s vigil absurd.

The benevolent and wise Power did neither.

Instead, it sank.

It passed into the granite. It seeped into the cyclopean joints, downward through the millions of tons of hand-hewn stone, down to the subterranean truss-work that held this impossible roof above whatever dead void lay beneath. 

It did not change the stone into something foreign; it simply reminded the stone of its density. It entered the microscopic shear-cracks where the hydraulic gale had begun to pry the megaliths apart. With an effort as slow and patient as the plate tectonics of a dying planet, the Power eased its own being into the gaps, becoming a cold, unyielding friction. It took upon itself the torque of the abyss. It braced the cyclopean stairs against the lateral rage of the sea. It arrested the catastrophic rot of the shelf, not by removing the strain, but by bearing the heavy, unseen center of it.

Then, it returned to the lip.

It did not speak to the hanging thing. It did not touch its blind, segmented face.

It touched only the seam.

Where the creature worked with its worn horn spatula, the Power subtly altered the resonance of the masonry. When the creature tapped—*clack*—the stone did not give back the hollow, shivering rattle of a failing lintel.

It rang solid.

The sound that traveled up the creature’s spatula and into the cartilage of its forearm, through its iron-jointed spine and down to the anchor-tail wedged in the dark, was a clear, low, structural *hum*. It was the sound of a wall that has found its footing. It was the report of a mountain that has agreed to stand.

The tiny grain of quartz, freed from the crushing pressure of the shifting block, popped loose. The upwelling caught it and carried it away into the gray-green murk.

The creature’s spatula slid forward into the seam. The stone was smooth. The joint was true.

For five minutes of unbroken stillness—the first stillness the plateau had seen since the water came—the creature did not tap. Its horned appendages rested flat against the seam, feeling the immense, cold vibration of the plateau. It felt no slip. It felt no failure. It felt the vast, subterranean bedrock holding firm against the howling drag of the deep.

The creature’s segmented chest expanded once, drawing in the mineral-thick wash. 

It did not drop its spatula. It could not leave the wall; it was the wall’s own nerve, made flesh. But the manic, desperate twitch in its joints eased into a long, measured sway. It reached down, found another flake of loose grit, and levered it out. 

*Clack.*

The sound was no longer an alarm sounded in the dark. It was a liturgical strike. A watchman confirming the hour to a city that was, finally, secure.

The Power remained. It did not leave for other stars or grander thrones. It stayed down in the dark between the stones, acting as the grout between the megaliths, the counterweight to the abyss, and the silent listener to the tapping.

Far above, the rollers continued their blind, two-hundred-year tumble across the smoothed plazas. The ribbons mated in the fury of the standing waves. The wedges gripped their trenches, their carapaces shining like ivory under the pressure of the unceasing gale.

The sea remained violent, lightless, and ice-cold.

And at the edge of the world, suspended over the void, the last hands kept the seam clean, no longer alone in the dark, tapping out their endless, peaceful work upon the bones of an answered prayer.
