A lesser benevolence would have wept. 

It would have looked upon the cold-rolled hematite, the dry brass umbrellas, and the severe, unlubricated lives of the tines and sleds, and mistook their clean austerity for deprivation. In its clumsy, wet-hearted pity, it would have gathered clouds. It would have sent down rain. 

And rain would have been an apocalypse. 

The soft, hydrogen-bonded moisture would have crept into the razor fractures of the floor, swelling and splitting the Embryonic stone. It would have coated the mirror-lapped underside of the planers with an insulating, corrosive film, breaking their galvanic circuits. Water would have oxidized the native copper coils into powdery verdigris, shorted the bimetallic wafers of the tines, and rusted the case-hardened springs of the wedges until they seized in their tracks. Under the rot of humidity, the great tellurium sleds would have suffered dielectric breakdown, their walking crystal lattices dissolving into sluggish, muddy sludge. Within a week, the tableland would have become a bog of toxic slime and pitted slag, silenced forever by a mercy that did not understand.

Wisdom, however, does not impose its own physiology upon the things it loves. 

Wisdom knows that life does not require marrow to be sacred, nor blood to be whole.

Therefore, when the Power drew near, it did not arrive as an incarnation. It did not take a shape that carried mass, breath, or heat, for mass would warp the delicate diamagnetism that suspended the foils, breath would introduce atmospheric moisture, and heat would ruin the steep, razor-sharp thermal gradients that kept the ecosystem alive. 

Instead, it arrived as an *adjustment of field*. 

It entered the valley shelf like a thought passing through an unclouded mind: a subtle, lossless coherence that settled into the vacuum between the atoms of the tableland. 

It saw how the world lived—within the tolerances. It saw the beauty of the bimetallic tuning forks shimmering on the hematite, shedding their dry graphite scales; it felt the deep, rhythmic grind of the sleds’ graphite pads against the phosphor bronze; it tracked the piezocrystal patience of the wedges waiting on their needle points of corundum. 

And then, it looked deeper, down into the crystalline architecture of the matter itself. 

There, it found the only sorrow this floor possessed: *work-hardening.* 

It saw the slip-planes forming in the leaf-springs of the tines after centuries of thermal vibration. It saw the microscopic dislocations migrating through the case-hardened latches of the wedges, the subtle lattice-creep in the tellurium hulls of the sleds as they hauled their massive bulk across the flats. It saw that the mortality of this world was not a biological failing, but an accumulation of mechanical fatigue—the inevitable drift of ordered crystal toward brittle exhaustion. 

The Power did not abolish death, for it understood that without cleavage, there could be no fracture, no release of bismuth, no fallen metal to serve as conductive footing for the seedlings of brass-foil. A world frozen in stasis is merely an unmoving monument. 

Instead, the Power offered an act of silent, metallurgical grace: *annealing.*

Far below the surface, where the native silver taproots drew their galvanic charge from the unquenched mantle, the Power gently tuned the earth-currents. It altered no chemistry; it added no foreign energy. It merely smoothed the waveform of the planetary hum, organizing the chaotic static of the core into an immaculate, ultra-low-frequency harmonic. 

Through the conductive plain, the new wave rose. 

It passed into the groves. As the coherent resonance washed through the bimetallic stems of the tines, it did not disrupt their thermal harvest; it gently vibrated their crystalline lattices at their exact restorative frequencies. At the submicroscopic scale, the dislocations dissolved. The atoms slipped back into their ideal, low-energy configurations. The microscopic cracks in the molybdenum healed, purged of their internal strain without the need for melting heat. 

The wedges on their cooling fins felt their brittle spring-steels relax, shed their internal shearing forces, and regain their initial, unworked temper. Under the hulls of the great sleds, the wandering antimony atoms fell back into perfect geometric array, clearing the thermal channels through which the Peltier effect fed the beasts. 

The organisms did not know they had been blessed. They had no nervous systems to register salvation, no minds to formulate a prayer. 

Yet the quality of the floor changed. 

The tines ceased their frantic, high-strung whining; their hum deepened, dropped an octave, and became rounder, purer, ringing with the effortless sustain of bells cast by a master founder. The planers moved with less drag, their bismuth bellies expanding and contracting with vernier perfection, gliding over bronze plates that seemed suddenly, impossibly slick. 

Then, the Power did one thing more. 

It realized that for all the centuries this place had existed, its music had been lost to the sky. Because the air was thin, dry, and clean, there was no atmosphere to carry an echo. When an expansion seam yielded five miles out, its singular, crystalline *ping* traveled once across the flats, struck no surface, and died into the void of the azure abyss. The tableland was an open circuit, singing into an unanswering universe.

So, high above the tableland—far above the reach of the brass canopies, at the very boundary where the thin air gave way to the black vacuum of space—the Power established a boundary condition. 

A mirror. Not of glass or stone, but an acoustic-electromagnetic reflector, perfectly impedance-matched to the metallurgical soul of the plain below. 

Hours passed in the unchanging noon. 

Then, out on the mirror-bright void between the third and fourth groves, an expansion seam shifted. A hairline crack yielded to the solar load. 

*PING.* 

The note was struck. It leaped from the cold-rolled hematite, sharp as a diamond edge, clean, solitary, and bright. It raced through the dry air, through the roots of the brass trees, vibrating the molybdenum wafers of the tines and making the golden foils ripple three inches above the iron. 

It rose into the sky, climbing toward the azure abyss. 

And there, for the first time since the cooling of the world, it met a surface that did not swallow it. 

A fraction of a second later, the return came down. 

It was not a distorted echo, but the phase-conjugate harmony: the world's own note, resolved, completed, and returned to it as a gentle, downward pulse of electromagnetic warmth. 

Below, every tine on the tableland took up the chord. Three thousand bimetallic forks shivered in sympathetic resonance, their polished leaf-springs blurring silver, their ozone scent spiking sweet and clear into the unmoving air. The great tellurium sleds slowed their caterpillar crawl, their massive hulls humming in unison with the sky, while the gold foils drifted upward an inch higher on the rebounding wave.

The open circuit had closed. 

Satisfied, the Power withdrew into the quiet structure of things. It did not linger to be worshipped, nor did it leave a footprint on the planed bronze floor. 

It left the world as it had found it: severe, unweathered, anhydrous, and bright. But now, in the long, motionless noon, the engine ran true to its design; its tolerances were held by an unseen hand; and whenever the iron spoke its solitary word into the silence, the universe listened, tuned itself to the pitch, and sang it back.
