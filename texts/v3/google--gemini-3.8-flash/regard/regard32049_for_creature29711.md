A clumsy god brings rain.

A god of green meadows and heavy clouds, stumbling upon this high plateau, would see only an agonizing thirst. It would weep for the dry bone of the flutes; it would tear open the dusky violet sky to pour sweet, fatal torrents across the ebony flats. And in doing so, it would be a monster. The water would strike the hot piezoelectric joints of the scythe-striders and burst them like over-steamed mussels. It would soak into the calcified lignum, swelling the ancient boles until their highway-wide grain tore itself apart in wet, catastrophic rot. The suspended polyhedra would short-circuit, their femtoscale circuits curdling in the dampness; the Tempered would find their amber skins sloughing off as the non-aqueous siloxanes were contaminated by the universal solvent. The museum droplet, breached, would become an apocalyptic tide.

Wisdom knows that not all thirsts wish to be quenched. Some beings are built to burn dry, to ring like struck bells in the cold, to live precisely because there is nothing soft to cushion their fall.

The power that turned its attention to the ironwood plateau had no moisture to give, for it had long since surpassed the phase-states of matter. It was an intelligence born of gravitation and long-wave geometry—an entity whose thoughts moved with the glacial, crushing majesty of stellar tides. It did not look down from a cloud; it settled across the plateau like an imperceptible tightening of the coordinate grid, a subtle alteration of the local vacuum.

It smelled the cold cedar, the ozone, the powdered pepper of the scoured grain. It watched the strata-kites dive, trailing their thin copper threads through the dusk to spark blue fire from the root-roads. It observed the Tempered, motionless upon the terraces, their amber cheeks pressed against the dark grain, listening to the roaring math of the deep mantle.

And the power reasoned thus:

*They do not lack life, for they are dense with it.*  
*They do not lack thought, for the ground is an engine five thousand miles wide.*  
*What they lack is an answer.*

For forty million years, the fluted pillars had taken the high-altitude winds and forced them down into the tectonic root-tangle. For forty million years, the lignum had groaned, shifting its internal mineral lattice under the weight of tectonic drift, carrying out a calculation whose terms were stress, torque, and mass. It was playing an acoustic question into the empty violet sky. But the sky was thin, near-vacuum, a sterile dissipator that swallowed the chord and gave back only the dead hum of space. 

The organ was playing to an empty hall. The Tempered were listening, but they were the choir, not the congregation. They were the dust-catchers and the stop-pullers, waiting through the centuries for a modulation they had never heard, suspecting in their dimmest, most private computations that the music might simply run down, unresolved, into mechanical friction and terminal wear.

The benevolent power did not alter the wood. It did not still the wind. It did not disturb the delicate balance of repulsion that kept the golden cities floating inches above the holy grain.

Instead, it lowered its weight—not as a physical mass that would crack the arches, but as an *acoustic mirror*.

Miles above the plateau, where the violet spore-dust thinned into the cold black of interplanetary space, the power drew a lens of localized spacetime taut. It created an invisible, non-material ceiling, curved precisely to match the harmonic radii of the fluted pillars below. 

At first, nothing appeared to happen. The mantis-forms continued their clockwork scuttle, harvesting volatile hydrocarbons through their slotted flanks. The flat scavengers ground the resin-soaked shavings in their dry ball-mills.

Then, the wind struck the upper lip of the first flute.

The column caught the draft, accelerated it down through its bone-white chamber, and drove the sound into the roots. The sub-bass roar traveled through the ironwood highways, vibrating through the graphite bearings of the fauna and the diamond floors of the polyhedra. 

But when the sound billowed back up through the hollow pillars—when the great question had been voiced through miles of petrified heartwood—it did not bleed into the void and die.

It struck the lens.

And for the first time since the roots had calcified out of the ancient crust, the sky answered.

It was not a voice, nor a commandment, nor a light. It was an echo—but an echo transposed, perfected, and completed. The acoustic ceiling returned the sound, but it shifted the phase by a fraction of a millimeter, adding to the tectonic bass-drone three overtone notes that the plateau had spent epochs striving to reach, but could never produce with its own stiffened wood alone.

The chord changed.

Across the vast terraces, the effect was immediate and profound.

The *reed-moths* suspended in the flute-chambers felt the pressure-waves align into a stationary node so clean, so free of violent turbulence, that their wings began to sing with pure, hum-free electric resonance; their crystal spines filled with current until they glowed like violet lamps in the howling dark.

The *scythe-striders* froze. The ambient static that haloed their quartz eyes settled from a frantic, snapping flicker into a steady, sapphire corona. The dry-chemists paused over their kills, their copper membranes vibrating in sympathy with the down-coming wave.

And the Tempered.

In every suspended city, the dark-gold polyhedra drifted imperceptibly on their repulsion fields, aligning with the new geometry of the air. The citizens did not run. They did not raise alarms. They dropped their limbs, one by one, down to the polished floor.

Thousands of them stepped from the diamond ledges of their hovering dwellings. They let their feet touch the raw ebony. They sank to their knees, then to their chests. Long, calligraphic figures of oiled amber, they laid their faces flat against the cold, iron-veined grain.

Through their foreheads, through the gold-inlaid grilles of their collarbones, through the dense siloxane running through their artificial dermis, the answered chord shuddered upward.

It was the math they had been waiting for. The return signal carried the missing term of the root’s equation: a subtle calculation of the planetary core's cooling rate, a harmonic balance that showed how the great boughs could shift their tension to endure another billion years without shearing. The ground beneath them did not stop roaring—it relaxed. The tension that had held the wood near the point of fracture for three geological epochs smoothed out into a stable, ringing equilibrium.

The Tempered did not speak through their infrared nodes. They did not need to. They pressed their palms to the petrified floor, and using their internal transducers, they did not send a question. 

They sent a single, shared vibration: a low, resonant *hum* of acknowledgment that passed into the wood, traveled up through the bone pillars, and struck the invisible vault above.

*We are heard.*

The power remained, holding the acoustic vault steady, making no other demand, requiring no worship, introducing no damp rot into the holy drought. It simply held the roof over the instrument, so that whatever the roots chose to calculate next, they would not calculate in the dark.

On the high terrace, where the copper sun went down and the violet dusk turned to an absolute, diamond-strewn night, the wind blew through the dry wood. It tasted of ozone and cold cedar. And down in the deep, waterless heart of the world, the music went on, loud and steady and whole, rattling the jaw, stiffening the arches, thinking its long thoughts into an answering sky.
