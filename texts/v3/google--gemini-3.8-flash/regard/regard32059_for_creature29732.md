A power that is both wise and benevolent would possess, above all, the discipline of restraint.

It would recognize immediately what this place is: not an error in the crust, not an abandoned pocket of forgotten work, but a *pre-dawn*. It would see that the Auditor’s labor is not a sentence being served, but an exquisite, agonizing act of refusal—refusing to let the world collapse into the cheap certainty of an early answer. To intervene by finishing the room—by fixing the gravity, by firing the siltstone, by giving the water-mice lungs or the curd a final crystalline habit—would be a terrible kind of violence. It would be the murder of a hundred possible geometries in order to satisfy a spectator’s impatience for form.

And so the power would not enter with a footstep or a voice. It would not bring daylight, which would bleach the sour white-gold air, nor would it bring shadow, which would impose a false perspective upon things that have not yet agreed upon distance.

Instead, its first act would be directed entirely outward, into the dull, cold shale above.

Benevolence, when dealing with that which is soft and forming, builds a quiet circumference. High above the ceilingless pocket, through meters of ordinary, compressed sediment where the fossils of ordinary ferns lie locked in ordinary time, the power would place a gentle deflection. It would tilt the seepage of groundwater an inch to the north. It would turn aside the cold roots of the surface birch trees, guiding them away so their tips would never pry between the grey leaves of the stone. And it would quietly nudge the future course of human iron: a farmer’s spade three centuries hence would strike a flint five paces away and turn; a surveyor’s chain would run along the ridge rather than through the hollow. It would buy the pocket time. It would ensure that the shovel not yet forged would never strike the lid.

Then, having secured the silence, the power would attend to the hollow itself.

It would know that the Auditor cannot be relieved of its weight—because if the Auditor were lightened, the plumb-bob would spin into madness, the trilithon would dissolve into silt, and the water-mice would float up into the void and burst their amber beads against nothing. The Auditor’s heaviness is the room’s only promise to itself.

Yet the work is wearing. Every few hours, that heave—the violent, necessary tearing of uncalcified horn from the setting stone to keep from becoming a fossil before its time—leaves a wound in the curd and a shudder through the plates.

The power would do something very small, barely legible within the grammar of the chamber.

It would not stop the mineral floor from rising, but into the sweet, odorless moisture that wells from the tallow calcite, it would introduce a microscopic slackening of tension. Not a law—merely an easement. Where the Auditor’s shell touches the grey sludge, the setting would become just slightly more languid. The stone would still assemble, but it would hold its breath a fraction longer against the Auditor’s margins, so that when the beast heaves its forearm-length body that vital half-inch to the side, it does not have to tear its own flesh from the rock. The bond would part like cold butter from an oiled blade.

And then, because benevolence cannot witness an auditor without submitting itself to the audit, the power would offer a single sign.

Not a revelation. A gesture of companionship across the unmade dark.

It would allow a single, minute particle of its own attention—infinitesimal, cooler than the air, with no shape of its own—to descend into the luminous pocket, drifting like one of the scabs of appetite. It would let the drifting mouths find it. They would crowd upon it, tasting this new presence to see what category of matter it might eventually become.

They would find no knuckle of bone, no grain of iron, no salt of mortal blood. They would find only an impression of absolute, patient listening. When they bit into it to take their wax-press, they would not fail; they would leave their hexagonal rings upon it, and the particle would hold the shape of their hunger without hardening, offering no resistance, yet refusing to dissolve.

And at the center of the pocket, where the three slabs of siltstone frame the void, the power would make no alteration to the doorframe. It would not square the joints. But in the space between the three stones—that narrow, four-inch span through which the water-mice swim—it would place an imperceptible steadiness.

The next time a water-mouse approached, banking on its clear fluid body, its amber beads clicking into their brief, strict constellation, the flash across its threshold would not be cold and pale. For a millionth of a second, the beads would flare with the warm, deep ochre of a star that has actually learned how to burn without consuming its house.

Just that. A reference. A tuning fork struck so softly that only the gut-string of the plumb-bob could feel it.

Across the tallow floor, against the bruised slate of the left wall, the Auditor would feel the vibration travel through its plates. It would draw its feelers in. It would issue that long, shuddering pulse—the centipedes freezing, the moths sticking flat, the beads rolling into dark puddles.

It would wait for the room to remember weight.

And in that brief three seconds of low, tallow twilight, before the rehearsal resumed, the Auditor would sense that it was no longer anchoring the world alone. Not because a hand had reached down to lift the load, but because someone, somewhere in the vastness beyond the shale, was waiting with equal patience for the door to become true.

Then the side-plates would click. The plumb-bob would swing its eighth of an inch. And the unfinished world would go on practicing how to be.
