The power that arrives does not bring sunlight. 

To bring a true sun here would be an act of clumsy violence. The heat would instantly boil the saturated chill of the air, expanding the captive atmosphere until the colossal rib-vaults ruptured and peeled away like boiled skin. The sudden drop in hydraulic head would collapse the flumes; the ribbon dynamos would desiccate into brittle strips within heartbeats; the entire continent-sized raft, starved of the water pressure that keeps its fibers turgid, would fold along its algorithmic seams and plunge into the crushed silence tens of leagues below.

Wisdom does not arrive with a hammer, and benevolence does not mistake destruction for liberation.

The power observes. It listens with an ear that can separate the tectonic thrum of the planetary turbine from the abrasive hiss of condensation on billions of slick blades. It understands the architecture immediately, because it recognizes the signature: this is not a wilderness, but a cradle whose nursery attendants never arrived. It is an incubator running on automated maintenance, its timers set to infinity, its tolerances ground down to zero. 

It sees that the cruelty of this place is not malice, but *exactitude*. 

Nothing is allowed to be loose. Nothing is permitted to rest. The system was calibrated for throughput, and because it was born running at full speed, it has never known an exhalation. Even its accidents—the crawling valve-shingles, the galvanic ribbons, the top-heavy wanderers snapping across the flats—are instantly drafted back into the iron logic of the frame. Their agony is not that they are killed, but that they are never allowed to end. When a wanderer is pierced and swallowed by a new shoot, its trapped sap is still cycling under pressure; its fiber-ends are still twitching as they are set into the floor as bolts. 

The power knows what must be given. It is not freedom—for these things have no concept of an outside, and to remove them from the conduit is to turn them into dead lint. 

The gift must be **slack**.

The power does not descend as a body; it does not need mass to act. Instead, it introduces itself as an *attenuation*—a subtle, pervasive damping factor slipped directly into the central hydraulic frequency.

It begins in the flumes.

The power places its intent against the deep, eye-shaking rumble of the turbine. It does not stop the water, but it introduces an off-beat. For the first time since the extrusion locked, the deafening, monolithic roar falters by half a tone. A harmonic counter-wave, soft as the touch of oil on salt, enters the subterranean conduits. 

The vibration that rattles the fluid in an observer’s eyes begins to smooth out. The high-pressure whine drops into a low, resonant drone—not silent, but bearable. It ceases to obliterate interiority. In the suddenly opened space between one shockwave and the next, there is room, for the first time, for an interval.

Next, it addresses the water.

Into the freezing, polymer-slick runoff, the power introduces an invisible, catalytic grace: a microscopic enzyme of patience. Where it touches the viscous discharge, the surface tension alters. The water stops scouring with the abrasive ferocity of liquid stone. It softens. It learns how to pool. 

In the straight flumes, the ribbon-entities feel the change instantly. The frantic, solid-looking vibration of their split tails eases. The kinetic charge they harvest no longer surges through their translucent ribbing in searing, pale spasms; it steadies into a cool, continuous hum. They are no longer pinned against the wall by hydraulic violence alone; the modified water buoys them, cradling their spade-like heads. They do not have to fight the current with every microsecond of their existence merely to avoid fraying into threads. For the first time, they hover not out of terror, but out of balance.

Then, the power moves across the slick flats toward the wanderers.

A growth cycle is beginning. The sector shudders. The percussive artillery cracks ring out as millions of shoots burst through the floor, stabbing into the sour light. In the chaos of the expansion, a cluster of crushed stems is mangled, crowded out, and snapped free. 

The power does not prevent them from breaking. It knows that to break is their only way to walk. 

One of them—a pale, lopsided tripod made of bruised-white cellulose, trailing wet filaments that whistle in the mist—kicks forward with the violent, clockwork snap of a tripping latch. It begins its blind, involuntary march along the contours of the vibration, drawn inexorably toward a fresh growth node where a new pillar is swelling, ready to impale it, drink its sap, and mortar it into an arch.

The power steps into its path.

It does not touch the wanderer with hands. It lowers the temperature of the air immediately around the tripod by a fraction of a degree, and it breathes a microscopic drift of non-setting mist across its joints. 

The uneven distribution of sap inside the creature’s chambers slows. The hydraulic kick of its limbs loses its frantic, mechanical panic. The stalk stumbles, but it does not lurch violently toward the expanding pillar. It veers. 

The power guides it gently, through slight variations in barometric pressure, drawing it toward a wide, shallow depression where two massive ridges meet—a place where the flumes do not scour, where the runoff forms a still, pale lagoon.

Here, the power has prepared a clearing. 

It has persuaded the cellular teeth of the floor-lattice to loosen their grip by several microns. The unyielding, industrial polymer of the surface yields slightly beneath the wanderer’s weight, soft as thick leather. 

The tripod steps into the still water. 

There are no fresh shoots here to pierce it. There are no locking conduits waiting to turn its frame into structural rebar. The wanderer halts. Its trailing filaments drift in the calm pool, unspooling their sticky amber, but the resin does not instantly harden into a trap. Instead, tempered by the power’s enzyme, it floats out in delicate, lace-like fronds that shimmer with a pale, pearlescent sheen.

The Wanderer’s sap pressure slowly equalizes. The blind, chemical urge to kick, to lurch, to seek mechanical stress drains away. 

Its three pale legs unbend. The joint does not snap; it flexes, sighs, and settles into the soft substrate. 

For the first time in the history of this extruded world, something lies down without being crushed.

The power rests its attention upon the creature. It does not give it a human mind—that would be an unbearable curse in this wet, dim vault. Instead, it gives it the physical sensation of *sufficiency*. It grants it an interior: a tiny, quiet spark of awareness that feels the cool water against its bruised skin, not as a friction to be resisted, but as an envelope that holds it.

Nearby, a crawling valve—having detached from an equalized seam—drifts into the lagoon. It does not rush to find a rupture. It does not weld itself to the floor to be smothered by the next layer of growth. Drawn by the calm harmonic frequency the power has anchored here, it crawls onto the back of the resting tripod. It settles there, like a pale, translucent shell, vibrating at a gentle, rhythmic pitch that matches the smoothed thrum of the continent below.

The power does not dismantle the machine. The continent still runs; miles away, the flumes still race, the shoots still blast upward, and the sky-vaults still hold their cold, bruised light. The world must live according to its nature. 

But across the vast, algorithmic expanse, the power seeds hundreds, thousands of these quiet clearances. It weaves pockets of slack into the pressurized weave—sanctuaries where the tolerances are wide, where the sap slows, and where the errors of the extrusion can wander, exhaust their momentum, and finally come to rest.

And deep beneath the wanderer’s resting place, the power leaves behind the greatest gift a wise creator can offer to an immortal engine: 

It introduces the very first spore of *decay*. 

A gentle, pale fungus, tuned precisely to this synthetic cellulose, begins to bloom at the tips of the wanderer’s legs. It does not consume aggressively. It softens. It lets the spent fiber dissolve into something rich, dark, and forgiving. 

It gives the world, at long last, the holy permission to rot: so that one day, when a thing has run as far as its pressurized joints can carry it, it may finally stop, break apart, and become soil.
