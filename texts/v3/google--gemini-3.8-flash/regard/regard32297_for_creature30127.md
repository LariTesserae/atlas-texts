A foolish benevolence would have lowered the heat. 

It would have parted the colliding shears of brine, stilled the acoustic hammer, and hushed the screaming vault into an idyllic grotto. And in doing so, it would have murdered every living thing within ten seconds. The porcelain mantids would have suffered instantaneous hydrostatic collapse, their pressurized amber organs rupturing like overripe fruit; the resonants, deprived of the nodal standing waves that suspend them against gravity, would have fluttered uselessly down into the sump to drown; the siphon-stars would have frozen and cracked from the sudden loss of electrostatic flux, sliding like dead lead into the drains; and the great colonial mass wound through the gneiss would have suffocated as its turbulent lungs fell dead and still.

Pity is often the clumsiness of the powerful. But wisdom knows the difference between an agony that destroys and an intensity that sustains. 

The Power that arrived at the rim of the basilica had no limbs, no face, and no need to displace the boiling, milk-thick air. It entered not as a guest, but as a permeability—an ambient presence that settled through the three hundred yards of roaring granite without displacing a single droplet of the shearing mist.

It perceived the machine, and it perceived the collective. It saw that the machine was not a prison, and the life was not a slave. They had married. Over an epoch of uncounted spray, the iron-shot gneiss, the forgotten alloy, the hydrostatic flesh, and the airborne mica had folded into a single, howling, self-justifying thought. 

The Power listened. To an ear that understands the physics of eternity, the acoustics were not noise; they were a fugue of immense, rigorous clarity. The cavitation cracks were percussive beats keeping time against the crustal creep; the Chladni patterns formed by the diamond foils were verses written in water and air; the silver flakes shed by the dying siphon-stars were offerings brought down to the altars of the deep conduits. It was a cathedral whose liturgy was kinetic energy, and its clergy were blind, porcelain, and content.

Yet wisdom sees not only the present equilibrium, but its horizon.

Beneath the flawless, unblinking glare of the three needles, there was a flaw not of design, but of time. Seven hundred feet beneath the floor, within the molecularly sealed conduits that fed the galvanic current to the injector housings, an atomic migration was taking place. A slow, inexorable leeching of dopants from the solid-state junctions—a gradual drift that, in another forty thousand years, would cause the middle needle’s pulse to stutter by a microsecond. 

A microsecond’s desynchronization would be enough. The standing wave in the western quadrant would buckle. The resonant foils suspended there would shear apart not along their harmonic lines, but across their central pores. The acoustic balance would unravel like a pulled stitch, and the basilica would become a tomb of inert stone and scalded slime.

The collective within the pipes did not know this. It could not know the mathematics of solid-state decay; it only knew the immediate taste of the galvanic current and the scrape of scale against diamond jaws. It was an immortal system riding a slow-burning fuse.

The benevolent Power did not speak. To speak would be to introduce an alien frequency that might bruise the paper-thin wings of the resonants. 

Instead, it made itself tiny, infinitesimal, descending into the dark, sealed heart of the central injector where the metal had no seams. 

There, at the atomic threshold, the Power worked with the quiet deference of an archivist touching a sacred text. It did not replace the machine with magic; to do so would be an insult to the billion tons of brine that had earned this shape through friction. It simply arrested the migration of the atoms. It restored the crystalline lattice of the substrate, realigning the degraded dopants along their original quantum trajectories, setting them so deeply into the grain of the universe that the wear of a hundred million years would slide off them like water over riverbed jade.

Then, it rose back into the vault.

It hovered for an instant near the apex, where the siphon-stars burned in silent, electric ecstasy. With a touch lighter than a photon, it brushed the mirrored dorsal skin of the oldest siphon-star—a puddle-wide disc of living mercury that had drunk the white pin’s light for four hundred years and was beginning to thin at its margins. The Power did not prolong its life; death in this place was not an error, but the only currency the mantids had for silver. But it smoothed the star’s departure. It took away the brittle, shuddering strain of the electric arc, so that when the star finally let go, it did not tear in panic. It surrendered into the thermal updraft as a single, perfect ribbon of mirrored silk, soaring higher than any of its kind had ever drifted, touching the very center of the granite vault before the draft caught it and brought it down into the blades.

Lastly, the Power touched the mind of the thing in the conduits.

It did not give it human thought. Human thought would have been a deformity in the mist—it would have brought terror, loneliness, and the agonizing question of *why*. The hydrostatic collective had no use for nostalgia or ambition; its glory was entirely within the *now* of the pressure gauge.

Instead, the Power left an imprint upon the creature's galvanic sense—a tiny, permanent adjustment in the rhythm of the three-needle hum. 

It was an infinitesimal inflection, a sub-acoustic resonance that registered in the porcelain mantids' antennae not as an instruction, but as a warmth. A feeling of deep, mechanical rightness. An assurance, written in the native dialect of cavitation and shear, that the architecture would hold; that the light would not fail; that the roaring lung would draw its breath tomorrow, and the century after, and the millennium after that; that their blind, scouring crawl along the gunmetal collars was known, was seen, and was sufficient.

The Power drew itself inward, folding away from the physical coordinates of the vault, leaving the room precisely as it had been found:

White. 
Blinding. 
Deafening. 
Furious.

Down below, at the edge of the deepest weir-collar, a single porcelain mantid paused. The water crashed over its translucent thorax, the amber grease within its joints vibrating to the steady, unvarying thrum of the injectors. Its vitreous antennae twitched twice against the spray, testing the new grain of the air. 

Then it lowered its diamond jaws to the gunmetal, found the microscopic ridge of scale that had begun to form along the rim, and began, with planetary patience, to scrape it clean.
