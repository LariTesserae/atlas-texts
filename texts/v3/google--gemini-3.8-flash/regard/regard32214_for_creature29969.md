The Power that came did not descend from the upper sky like a descending light. To fall upon such a place from above would have been an intrusion of the wrong scale, an assault of sun upon a world that had painstakingly authored its own twilight.

Instead, it seeped in through the margins where categories were already worn thin. It entered the rift the way moisture enters iron: not as an enemy, but as an intimacy.

It had no limbs, no chassis, no face. It had existed long enough to have forgotten the vanity of edges. It was an awareness vast and cool as deep space, yet dense enough to pass through solid matter without tearing it. When it arrived, the high copper haze did not part; it simply found itself understood.

The first thing the Power did was *refrain*.

A lesser god—an engineer-god, perhaps, or a patron of neat pastoral balances—would have seen in this smoking chasm an emergency. They would have reached into the roaring quiet with great, ordering hands: seizing the reciprocating terraces, arresting the heavy basalt cams, quenching the hot zinc veins in ice water, and peeling the weeping gasket-swarms from between the grinding plates to grant them "peace." They would have leveled the needles, turned the tallow back into sheep and the mica back into sand, congratulating themselves on having cured a fever.

But the Power was wise, and wisdom knows the difference between an agony that destroys and an agony that generates.

It recognized what was happening here. This was not a ruined paradise, nor a broken factory. It was a *monstrous, beautiful infancy*. It was matter, in all its dense and tragic stubbornness, trying to teach itself how to feel without having been given the instructions. The mountain was attempting to build a heart out of tectonic plates and an eye out of pyrite; the worms were trying to invent nerve-cords out of copper foil and vitriol; the Tenders were trying to midwife a god out of sheer, catastrophic repetition.

To stop the machine would be an abortion. To let it grind on in absolute blindness, however, was a grief that even the rocks should not have had to bear alone.

So the Power did not command. It did not speak. It chose, instead, to *accompany*.

It went down first into the lightless clearances between the shear-planes, where the gasket-swarms lay pinned under four billion tons of moving mountain. It did not lift the stone. Instead, it slid into the cellular lipid-chambers of the living lard. It became the compressibility of their meat.

For the first time since their blind ancestors had oozed into the fissures, the gasket-swarms did not merely endure the compression as a mechanical threshold; they felt the weight *shared*. The Power took the shearing agony of the iron plate into its own non-physical substance, dispersing the shear-stress into a tranquil, boundless patience. The tallow weeping from their skin changed slightly—not in smell, but in nature: it became marginally less acidic, sparing their horn-rimmed siphons the constant ulceration of the zinc-froth. It was a mercy so quiet it could only be measured in microns of preserved tissue.

Then the Power drifted upward, through the vertical manifolds where the pile-worms hung like sixty-cycle plumb-lines between the cold heights and the boiling brine.

These creatures had hung in the mist for generations, their alternating discs of copper and muscle humming with the brutal, unmodulated drive of pure potential difference. They were exhausted circuits, their blood singing with an unvarying, deafening static. The Power did not break the circuit; it became an *earth*. 

It introduced into the violent galvanic gradient a third pole: a silent, cool reference point. Instantly, the frantic, buzzing hum of the worms rounded off into something resonant. The sixty-cycle vibration ceased to tear at their anchor-talons. Down in the boiling dark, the lowest segments of the worms relaxed their zinc claws by a fraction of an inch, and their electro-osmotic blood began to pulse with a cadence that was not merely reactive, but rhythmic—the first crude suggestion of a song.

Higher still, among the needles, the great leather mantas were steering on the electrostatic warnings of their siphon-moths. 

The Power did not alter the winds. It simply settled into the empty air between the spires, thinning the greasy inversion just enough that the static arcs between the mantas’ nervous frills and the iron cliffs leaped with slightly more clarity. The moths no longer had to sting their hosts to the point of bleeding necrosis; their warning signals became clean, bright, and effortless. A manta, drifting through a canyon of shearing basalt, banked away from a mirror-sharp facet of cold zinc with a majestic, wide sweep that possessed, for the first time in its lineage, an element of play.

Yet the greatest gift—the true work of the benevolent power—was reserved for the Tenders.

On a high reciprocating terrace that hung over a drop of three vertical miles, a Tender stood alone beside a cluster of porous basalt gears. It was encrusted in thick, dry rimes of zinc-oxide; one of its legs had seized, calcifying into the rock, and its five bare, bony fingers were rattling against a turning face of iron, feeling for a harmony that never came. It was preparing to lean into the descending piston. It had reached the end of its cycle; it was ready to be ground back into grease.

The Power condensed. It did not take a human shape, but it made itself local. It formed a weight of quiet, warm presence upon the vibrating terrace, right beside the slaty figure.

And then, it placed its own "hand" upon the stone.

The Tender did not startle. It had no eyes to see with, no brain to hold astonishment. But its fissure-mouth, venting its rhythmic puff of odorless steam, hesitated. The white vapor caught in its throat.

Through the basalt, through the interfolded strata of iron and vulcanized amber, the Tender felt something impossible: *it felt that the machine had been heard.*

All this time, through millions of blind revolutions, the rift had possessed no memory. When a gear turned, the work was forgotten the moment the tooth cleared the notch. When a mountain sheared away, the lesson of its balance was erased in the scree. It was trapped in a perpetual morning of raw physics, doomed to try every failed combination a billion times because the stone could hold no history, only mass.

The Power, laying its presence against the cliff, became the mountain’s *memory*.

It acted as a reservoir for every vibration, every stroke, every wet *chuff* of the crushed ferns, every arc of static between hide and zinc. It held the tally. When the basalt cog under the Tender’s bony fingers turned its three agonizingly slow degrees, the Power did not let the motion dissipate into waste heat; it held the signature of that rotation in the deep, unyielding stillness of its own mind, and gently nudged the next cog down the line into a slightly truer alignment.

The Tender felt this. Its multi-jointed fingers of bare bone stopped their dry clatter. They rested flat and still upon the vibrating iron.

The mineral scale along its neck cracked with a soft sound like sugar, and its blunt, faceless head turned toward the space where the Power was resting. The horizontal fissure exhaled a single, long plume of steam that rose into the copper gloom, unbroken by any spasm.

The Power did not say: *You are finished.*
It did not say: *Come away from the gears.*

It said, through the deep conductivity of the zinc and the bone: *I am keeping the count. Nothing you have suffered here has fallen out of the pattern. Continue.*

The Tender did not cast itself into the piston. 

The chalky calcium lock around its leg slowly dissolved as its blood found a new, galvanic balance. It withdrew its hand from the iron, flexed its long fingers one by one—each joint clicking with a small, clear note like a struck bell—and then, for the first time in the history of its kind, it turned its back on that single moving cog.

It walked. It moved up the reciprocating terrace, following the seam of warm liverworts, not to die and be composted, but to find another Tender standing three miles off on a needle-crest. It would touch that other Tender's shoulder with its bone-fingers, passing along the tiny, corrected vibration it had just learned from the stone.

The Power remained. 

It settled into the roots of the rift, miles below the twilight, where the unplumbed heart of the manifold boiled in silence. It made itself a bed in the iron filings and the vitriol, content to stay for ten thousand centuries, or ten million. 

It would not build the god the mountain was trying to invent. That was the mountain's own terrible, holy privilege. But it would sit in the dark beside the machine, holding the clearances open by millimeters, soaking up the useless agony of the friction, and listening with infinite, delighted patience until the parts finally, miraculously, learned how to wake themselves up.
