To visit this place, you must understand a fundamental truth before you even pack a bag: **this is a world constructed out of mineral geometry, not biology.** It does not hate you, but it lacks the basic physical vocabulary to support flesh, water, and breath. It will pull the water from your tissues like dry paper absorbing ink, and its gravity will drop you into an endless trough the moment it forgets you are there.

Here is how an ordinary human traveler must prepare to survive and witness the vertical city.

---

### 1. The Body Envelope: Protection Against Total Desiccation

Do not treat this as high-altitude mountaineering. Treat this as a deep-space EVA conducted within a planetary carcass. 

*   **Hermetic, Closed-Circuit Environmental Suit:** The air is not merely thin; it is essentially devoid of ambient moisture and violently cold. An open-circuit oxygen mask will fail you: every exhaled breath will throw away precious internal humidity, and your bronchial tubes will dry, crack, and bleed within forty minutes. You need an entirely sealed, positive-pressure suit with a **closed-loop rebreather (CCR)** equipped with an artificial humidifier. 
*   **The Cut-Resistant Shell (Aramid/Titanium Weave):** Your suit cannot have an exposed rubberized bladder or thin thermal foil. The “storms” of this realm carry clouds of paper-thin, flint-sharp shale-leaf flakes orbiting along magnetic lines. A standard spacesuit would be shredded like parchment. Your outermost layer must be made of high-density ballistic aramid, flexible chain-mail weave, or hardened composite plating.
*   **Active Thermal Regulation:** The cold here is an absolute, dry sink. It will leach heat through the soles of your boots via pure conduction with the basalt. You need an active liquid-heating undergarment, with heavily insulated, aerogel-lined boot soles.
*   **Eye Protection (Double-Layered Polarized Visor with Internal Wiper):** The light is an eternal, pale indigo, but the air is thick with suspended mica dust that catches the twilight and produces sharp, prismatic glares. Furthermore, your eyes will want to water instinctively; without an internal moisture-wicking and defogging system inside the helmet, your visor will frost over from the inside while remaining bone-dry outside.

### 2. Locomotion: Respecting the "Habit" of Gravity

Gravity here is not a law; it is a memory. Because it periodically forgets its jurisdiction, traditional climbing gear, crampons, and ropes are dangerous traps.

*   **Mechanical Micro-Spine Grippers (No Pitons):** Do not hammer pitons into the masonry. The unmortared shale and basalt are held together by tectonic equilibrium and friction. A hammer blow can introduce a vibration that initiates a silent, lateral slide of an entire city block. Use pneumatic micro-spine clamps that grip the minute surface textures of the stone without fracturing it.
*   **Electrostatic Boot Anchors:** In areas where gravity lapses and dry slate pebbles float like bubbles, magnetic boots are useless—the stone is non-ferrous basalt and shale. You will need switchable electrostatic pads on your boots to adhere directly to the silicate surfaces. 
*   **Tethering Protocols:** Never tie yourself to a single feature. If you tether yourself to a terrace, and that terrace is one that suddenly slides five hundred yards out over the abyss like a drawn tongue, your rope will sheer or drag you over. Tethers must be equipped with **auto-sensing tension-release clutches** and spatial anchors that lock into your local inertial frame, not just the nearest rock face.
*   **Cold-Gas Thruster Pack (Emergency Descent/Ascent):** If you drift off a ledge in an anti-gravity pocket, you cannot swim through the thin air. You will hang there, motionless, until an updraft sweeps you over the chasm, where gravity will suddenly "remember" and pull you down forever. A lightweight nitrogen cold-gas thruster pack is essential to push yourself back toward stone.

### 3. Navigation: Unlearning Your Senses

You cannot navigate by the sun, the stars, shadows, or a compass.

*   **Ignore the Shadows:** Do not use shadow angles to judge direction or time. The shadows here crisscross, fall toward different horizons, and do not blend. Looking at them for too long causes rapid, severe spatial disorienting vertigo. Keep your eyes fixed on structural planes.
*   **Inertial & LiDAR Mapping (With Anomaly Alerts):** Magnetic compasses will spin erratically due to the shifting corridors that guide the shale storms. You must rely on optical LiDAR and continuous-wave spatial scanners. Set your computer to alert you to *closed-loop topology*. 
*   **Protocol for the "Self-Loop":** During high-pressure drops, space bends. If you see the back of your own coat walking three hundred yards ahead of you:
    1.  **Stop immediately.** Do not try to catch up with it; you will simply accelerate yourself into exhaustion.
    2.  **Do not turn around quickly.** Turning into a lintel will place you directly behind yourself, compounding the recursion.
    3.  **Sit down, anchor your boots, and wait.** These atmospheric snaps are temporary. Wait for the pressure to equalize and the shale-leaves to drop back into the abyss before resuming your march.

### 4. Footing, Efflorescence, and Sanitation

*   **Do Not Step on the Pale Crusts:** When moving through tenement alcoves, watch your step. The salt-efflorescence resembles ferns and cup-moss, but stepping on it triggers an explosive fracture into sub-micron powder. In the still, thin air, this powder remains airborne for days. It will coat your visor, foul your suit’s joint-bearings, and if your seals are breached, it will instantly dehydrate your respiratory lining. Walk exclusively on the smooth, worn centers of the flagstones.
*   **The Dust-Seal Routine:** The dust in this world has never known water; it has no surface tension, meaning it behaves like a dry fluid. It will infiltrate screw threads, zipper tracks, and mechanical seals. Carry pressurized dry-gas blowers to clean your airlock seals before any cycling.

### 5. Biological Protocols & Encounters

*   **Pack Twice the Normal Water Supply:** Your metabolic need for hydration will skyrocket. The suit’s dehumidifier will recycle some sweat, but the stress of high-altitude vacuum, combined with psychological dread, burns hydration rapidly. Drink warm, electrolyte-dense fluids constantly via an in-suit straw.
*   **The Narrow Doorways:** The tenements have rooms built for entities with height, but almost no breadth. Do not force yourself into them if your life-support pack is wide. If you get wedged in a basalt frame during a district shift, the sheer lateral force of the moving blocks will slice through your equipment without meeting resistance.
*   **Encountering the Chalk-White Wanderers:**
    *   They are not predators, but they are made of the same mineral logic as the architecture. They do not perceive you as meat; they may not perceive you as existing at all.
    *   **Listen for the *Thwip*:** Because the city is utterly silent, the muted sound of air collapsing into a newly vacated vacuum is your only warning that one is moving above you.
    *   **Stay Clear of Coordinate Seams:** They congregate where two streets overlap in the same spatial dimension. If you see an area where the stone appears blurred, doubled, or vibratory, do not enter it. If a wanderer moves while wedged into a seam, the displacement of space can tear your limbs out of alignment with your torso.
    *   Never touch them. Their carapaces are dry flagstone-logic; contact may initiate an abrasive spatial friction that your organic tissues cannot survive.

### 6. The Mind: Psychological Readiness

The hardest preparation is mental. 

*   **The Horror of the Absent Floor:** Humans rely on the subconscious comfort of the earth beneath them. Here, the floor of the world has fallen so far that it is erased by indigo haze. Looking down into thousands of meters of pure, empty verticality while standing on a terrace that has *no foundation and no mortar* triggers a primitive, paralyzing vertigo. You must train extensively in sensory-deprivation tanks and high-altitude skydiving simulators until your brain accepts the absence of a ground plane.
*   **The Crushing Silence:** There is no wind howl, no creaking wood, no hum of engines. The air is too thin to carry sound far, and the city shifts without a grinding noise. The only sounds you will hear for days will be your own breathing, the click of your suit's valves, and the wet pump of your own heart. For many, this leads to rapid acoustic hallucinations within forty-eight hours. Carry an audio feed inside your helmet—low, rhythmic, familiar sounds from home (rain on glass, forest winds) to keep your sensory cortex tethered to reality.
*   **The Freestanding Gate:** If you look out into the gulf and see the cyclopean gate floating miles away in the middle of nowhere, framing darker air, **do not stare at it.** It outlines towers that have not yet been built. Looking at prospective architecture through a lens of shifting time induces severe temporal sickness, migraine, and an overwhelming compulsion to leap into the gulf to reach it.
