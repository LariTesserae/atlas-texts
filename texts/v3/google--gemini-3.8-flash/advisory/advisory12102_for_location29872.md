To enter this place is to enter an artificial womb that was never intended for an animal to inhabit. It is pristine, warm, and entirely indifferent. You will not face rot, toxic radiation, or wild beasts, but rather the sterile perfection of pure engineering and unchecked botanical architecture.

Here is how an ordinary human must prepare for immersion in the Cistern.

---

### 1. The Respiratory Rig: The Absolute Rule of Closed Systems
Do not make the mistake of packing standard open-circuit SCUBA gear. 
* **Profile and Snag Hazard:** Standard dual-tank rigs protrude too far. The aisles between the copper steam lines and the rubbery walls of leaves are sometimes less than sixty centimeters wide. Cylinders mounted on your back will catch on I-beams or wedge between the brass perforated planting sleeves. You need a **chest-mounted or sidemount Closed-Circuit Rebreather (CCR)** with a fully recessed, snag-free harness.
* **Gas Consumption and Duration:** The water is thirty meters deep. If you descend to the lower catwalks, you are under four atmospheres of pressure. A rebreather is essential not just for gas longevity, but because exhaust bubbles will collect under the broad, horizontal fronds, violently dislodging mats of moss-analogues and obscuring your path with floating debris.
* **The Nitrogen Trap Overhead:** Remember that the single foot of headspace above the water is **moist, pure nitrogen**. If your equipment fails, surfacing will not save you; pulling off your mask to gasp for air at the ceiling will result in instant anoxia, loss of consciousness, and drowning. You must treat this environment as an absolute overhead environment—like a flooded cave—with **zero possibility of emergency ascent to air.**

### 2. Thermal Balance: The 34-Degree Paradox
Thirty-four degrees Celsius (93.2°F) feels deceptively comforting at first touch—it is the temperature of a warm bath. But for a diver performing physical work, it is a dangerous thermal trap.
* In water of this temperature, your body cannot easily dump metabolic heat. If you wear a typical neoprene wetsuit, you will be suffering from severe **hyperthermia** within forty minutes of swimming through dense foliage. 
* Conversely, you cannot dive naked or in a simple swimsuit; the freshly milled steel, stamped serial numbers, and sheared edges of the zinc-coated catwalks are razor-sharp. 
* **The Solution:** A custom **puncture-resistant, single-layer aramid/Kevlar dive skin** worn over a lightweight moisture-wicking undergarment. It must provide zero thermal insulation (allowing your excess body heat to slowly equalize with the 34°C water) while acting as chainmail against the microscopic burrs of the structural steel. 

### 3. Locomotion and Tooling
Leave your long open-water fins behind. If you flutter-kick here, you will instantly tangle yourself in the jade tendrils or kick a fragile glass-covered floodlight well.
* **Propulsion:** Wear **short, stiff, negative-buoyancy technical fins** (such as jet-fins), and rely almost entirely on modified frog-kicks and pull-and-glide techniques using non-energized structural framing. 
* **Cutting Implements:** The strap-shaped leaves are two inches thick, veinless, and turgid with starch. A standard dive knife will bind and wedge in their dense, rubbery flesh. Bring **heavy-duty trauma shears or ratcheting botanical loppers**. If a tendril coils around your manifold or ankle, you cannot tear it with your hands; you must cleanly slice through it.
* **Guide-Lines:** Sightlines are capped at six paces. The endless repeating patterns of industrial brass, gray paint, and identical jade fronds make navigation visually impossible. You must follow standard **cave-diving protocol**: run a continuous, braided nylon guideline from your entry hatch, secured with non-destructive line-arrows to structural I-beams. If you lose your line in this green labyrinth, you will die looking at an identical wall of leaves.

### 4. Acoustic and Vibrational Defense
Water conducts sound four times faster than air, and dense process-water held in a steel hull acts as an acoustic amplifier for mechanical energy. 
* **The Impeller Pulse:** The tower’s basement houses a five-meter impeller revolving at sixty cycles per minute. You will not hear this with your ears as much as you will feel it in your skeleton. The 1-Hz to 60-Hz infrasonic resonance will vibrate your sternum, liquefy your sense of balance, and eventually induce profound nausea or cardiac arrhythmia if unmanaged.
* **Ear Protection:** Wear a full-seal diving hood equipped with **gel-filled acoustic-dampening ear covers**. Standard earplugs will interfere with pressure equalization; specialized vented musician-grade plugs or fluid-damping hoods are required to soften the mechanical conduction without bursting your tympanic membranes during depth changes.
* **Pneumatic Discharges:** The steam collector vents with a sudden, dead thump that displaces water bodily. Do not linger within two meters of bronze purge-valves. The pressure shockwave through non-compressible water can cause internal tissue contusions or rupture your sinuses.

### 5. Skin and Eye Integrity
* **The Purity Hazard:** The water is demineralized and chemically scrubbed. Osmotic pressure means this water will aggressively leech salts and electrolytes from human skin and exposed mucous membranes. A flooded mask will sting your eyes not from salt, but from the *absence* of it—hypotonic swelling of your corneas will turn your vision milky within minutes. Keep a sealed full-face mask with redundant positive-pressure seals.
* **Chemical Residues:** While the water is sterile, there are micro-globules of machine oil suspended near the joints and dissolved ions from the sacrificial zinc/copper anodes. Bring sterile saline rinse for post-dive decontamination.

### 6. The Psychological Landscape
Prepare for the profound, uncanny stillness. 
There are no eyes watching you. There is not even the gentle, comforting rustle of a wild ecosystem. It is an immense, vertical machine running in utter isolation high above the clouds, completely silent except for the thrum of its own steel bones. 

The light—cool white glare from above, warm sodium amber from below—eliminates all natural shadows. It creates an oppressive, dreamlike clarity where distances are impossible to judge, and the jade ribbons of foliage seem to go on infinitely in every direction. It feels less like swimming in nature and more like crawling through the flooded circulatory system of an engine that has grown its own flesh. 

Move slowly. Keep your heart rate below the threshold of sweat. Touch only what you have visually cleared of sharp zinc edges, respect the slow breath of the steam lines, and never, under any circumstances, let go of your line.
