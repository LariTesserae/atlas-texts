To visit the plateau of Oun is to step into an ecosystem that operates on entirely different biological and mechanical axioms than our own. It is a world without flora, choked by sheer animal biomass, ancient iron, and a perpetual, warm, lubricating grease. An ordinary human entering this environment without specialized preparation would likely suffer heat stroke within hours, slip to their death within a day, or be stripped of soft tissue by opportunistic predators within a week.

Here is how an multiverse traveler must prepare for the continental terrace of Oun.

---

### 1. Thermoregulation and Respiration: Surviving the "Fat Air"

Although Oun rests at an altitude of six thousand meters, you will not need supplementary oxygen. The immense thermal plumes rising from the salt basins act like an atmospheric piston, ramming a dense, moisture-saturated column of air onto the plateau. The danger here is not hypoxia; it is the **wet-bulb temperature**.

*   **Active Cooling Garments:** Because the air is warm, slack, and held at roughly 100% relative humidity beneath the sun-glazed haze, **your sweat will not evaporate.** Natural thermoregulation will fail completely. You cannot wear ordinary hiking clothes. You need a closed-loop microclimate cooling vest—either thermoelectric or circulating chilled liquid through a manifold worn directly against the skin. Without this, walking up a single flight of scooped basalt stairs will push your core temperature into lethal heat-stroke territory.
*   **Dual-Stage Respirator (Organic Vapor + Heavy Particulate):** The atmosphere is not merely wet; it is chemically and biologically foul. It carries an aerosolized slurry of sulfur crust, volatilized animal-bone lubricant from the bronze gears, fine lime dust, and the powdered, calcified guano of millions of mast-gulls. 
    *   Carry a low-profile, positive-pressure or powered air-purifying respirator (PAPR). 
    *   Ensure your filters have an organic-vapor rating (to block the gagging stench of rendering fat and damp worm-hide) paired with an N100-grade particulate trap. Breathing this air unprotected will give you a debilitating case of chemical pneumonia and avian lung granuloma within forty-eight hours.

### 2. Vision: Defeating the Foundry Glare

The light on Oun does not behave like alpine light. There are no blue skies, no passing clouds, and no sharp shadows to provide depth perception. The ceiling of white haze scatters the sunlight into a continuous, omnidirectional, white-hot glare that bounces violently off bleached lime masonry, iron slag canals, and wet stone.

*   **Category 4 Glacial or Industrial Foundry Eyewear:** You need dark, polarized lenses with side shields to prevent light leak. Photochromic lenses will not work fast enough when passing under viaducts. 
*   **Amber or Rose Contrast Enhancers:** Because the light produces no sharp shadows, the world is visually flat. You will not be able to tell if a stone surface drops away into a sluice or slopes smoothly upward. An amber or rose tint will help delineate the subtle contours of wet, scooped basalt and the camouflaged carapaces of resting crabs.
*   **Wiping Mechanism:** The condensation that coats the city will coat your lenses continuously. Treat your goggles with hydrophobic, grease-shedding coatings, and carry a supply of lint-free, oil-absorbing lens pads sealed in dry pouches.

---

### 3. Footwear and Mobility: Traversing the Universal Slick

On Oun, every horizontal surface is a slip hazard. Basalt, smoothed by centuries of leathery hooves and clawed crabs, is covered in a permanent film of warm, greasy condensation mixed with machine tallow. 

*   **Studded, Oil-Resistant Soles:** Standard Vibram hiking soles will behave like ice skates on this greasy, polished stone. You need footwear patterned after industrial ironworker or river-wading boots: dense, sticky rubber outsoles studded with **tungsten-carbide micro-spikes**. The rubber must be resistant to animal fats (which dissolve ordinary shoe adhesives).
*   **Ankle Support and Gaiters:** The stairs are scooped into slick bowls pooling two inches of warm grease-water. Wear rigid, calf-height boots sealed with impermeable gaiters to keep the slurry of lime, guano, and industrial run-off from reaching your skin.
*   **Traction Gloves:** You will need to touch railings buffed to silver ribbons and climb iron ladders dripping with fat. Wear work gloves with palms faced in textured, oil-impervious nitrile or micro-cleated silicone. *Never grab an uninspected surface with bare hands;* the round, buckler-crabs cluster on walls, and crushing their shells under your palms will earn you defensive gouges from their peg-legs.

### 4. Armor and Outerwear: Navigating the Fauna

You are entering a landscape of heavy, indifferent bodies. The animals do not have natural fear of humans, because humans are simply another slow-moving, thick-fingered entity in the canals. However, you are soft-bodied in a world of hard carapaces.

*   **Hard-Shell Exterior (Slick Poncho/Armor):** Your outermost layer should be a non-porous, smooth, hardshell composite—such as polyurethane or polished polycarbonate. Avoid any exposed, coarse fabric. 
    *   *The Mast-Gulls:* These six-winged carnivores roost by the millions above you. When they take flight, sheets of guano and displaced water fall like toxic rain. A slick, hard outer shell allows guano, boiling condensate, and bird lime to slide off without soaking in.
    *   *Predation Warning:* The gulls are carnivores. While they primarily scavenge sulfur-crust and the carcasses of crabs and ungulates, a limp, injured, or unarmored human traveler lying on a rooftop looks like soft meat. Wear a **lightweight, smooth helmet** with a rigid neck gorget. Do not leave your scalp or the back of your neck exposed to the air.
*   **Blunt-Force Deflection:** The carapaced ungulates moving across the terraces are the size of oxen, packed wall-to-wall. They will not deliberately attack you, but they will inadvertently crush your hips or knees against the stone monuments as they rub their flanks to scrape off grease. Rigid hip, knee, and shin protection will prevent crushing injuries in crowded thoroughfares.

### 5. The Cellulose Anomaly: Tools, Trade, and Material Protocol

Oun has *never known cellulose*. There is no wood, no cotton, no paper, no plant fiber. The entire civilization is built on iron, stone, bone, chitin, and the tanned skins of river-worms.

*   **Do Not Bring Cotton or Untreated Organic Fibers:** If you bring cotton clothing, it will rapidly absorb the ambient grease and humidity, becoming a foul, waterlogged rag that will rot and foster aggressive, novel bacterial colonies within days. All your apparel must be synthetic: ripstop nylon, polyester laminates, aramid, and neoprene.
*   **Documentation Systems:** Do not pack paper notebooks. The constant weeping of moisture and airborne animal oil will turn a paper notebook into translucent mush within three hours. Use **synthetic stone-paper**, sealed e-ink tablets, or grease-pencils on matte titanium/plastic slates.
*   **Cellulose as Sovereign Currency:** In a world that has never produced a single splinter of pine or a single strand of thread, **cellulose is a divine anomaly.** 
    *   A small block of dried cedar, a box of wooden matchsticks, a single spool of cotton thread, or a blank linen sheet will possess incomprehensible value to the water-wardens and canal-men. 
    *   It will smell completely alien to them—sweet, dry, and clean. You can trade a few wooden dominoes or a handful of dried tea leaves for passage on an iron scow, access to dry docks, or safe lodging in a master-founder’s stone hut.
    *   *Caution:* Keep it hermetically sealed. If ungulates or vermin catch the scent of plant carbohydrates, their spade-mouths may attempt to strip your pack to find it.

### 6. Navigation and Acoustics: Surviving the Continental Monotony

The Great Aqueduct of Saint-Malo and the surrounding smelter districts are continental in scale, architecturally recursive, and visually identical.

*   **Magnetic Compasses are Useless:** The city is made of pig iron, basalt (which has high iron content), phosphor-bronze, and slag canals. A magnetic needle will spin erratically or lock onto the nearest steam-cable housing. 
*   **Inertial Dead-Reckoning (IMU):** Use an altimeter coupled with an inertial navigation unit (pedometer/accelerometer/gyroscope) that does not rely on satellites (the haze blocks skyward reception) or magnetism. 
*   **Acoustic Ear Protection (Infrasound Filtering):** The massive, slow-moving machinery—the four-story waterwheels groaning once every seven minutes, the 40-second piston strokes, the quarter-hour trip-hammers—generates massive, bone-deep **infrasound**. 
    *   You will not merely hear the city; your liver, teeth, and sternum will hum with it. Over 48 hours, exposure to constant low-frequency industrial vibration causes profound nausea, anxiety, vestibular disorientation, and auditory hallucinations.
    *   Carry custom-molded high-attenuation earplugs that specifically damp sub-bass frequencies while allowing the mid-range clack of crab legs and the croaks of the mast-gulls to pass through for situational awareness.

### 7. Sustenance: What to Consume

*   **Do Not Drink the Sweating Water:** The water weeping from the walls and pooling in the six-cubit gutters is a chemical broth of heavy metals, lime runoff, sulfur, dissolved guano, and bone tallow. Even standard water filters will quickly clog from the grease. 
    *   You must carry an **evaporative/condensation recovery system** or a reverse-osmosis micro-pump with dedicated pre-filters to scrub heavy oils before water touches the membrane.
*   **Pack All Calories:** The local diet consists of steamed river-worm bellies, the chalky flesh of mud-crabs scavenged from steam exhaust, and nutrient-poor broths boiled over long-burning animal-tallow stoves. It is high in strange sulfurous amino acids and mineral ash. Eating it will cause violent gastrointestinal shock. Pack dense, fat-stabilized emergency rations in hermetically sealed foil pouches.

---

### The Psychological Reality of Oun

When you arrive, your first sensation will be the claustrophobia of wide-open space: an endless, horizonless terrace bathed in shadowless, milky foundry-light, where every wall is weeping, every surface is crawling with crabs, and the deep, seismic groan of a four-story waterwheel shivers up through the soles of your boots. 

Walk slowly. Keep your center of gravity low, as if walking on grease-slicked ice. Do not look up with your mouth open under the roosts of the mast-gulls. And remember that in this world, where everything is stone, cold iron, or hard shell, your soft, warm, plant-dressed body is the most fragile and astonishing thing the plateau has seen in ten thousand years.
