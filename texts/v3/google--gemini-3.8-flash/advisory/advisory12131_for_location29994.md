To survive a descent into this vault, an ordinary human traveler must prepare not for an expedition across an alien landscape, but for entry into the interior of a sealed, self-lubricating, non-biological engine. 

The vault is not simply dry; it is an *anhydrous sink*. It possesses a total chemical hunger for moisture, surrounded by razor-edged geology, hyper-specialized mechanical fauna, and a lethal, suffocating atmosphere. 

Here is how you must prepare.

---

### 1. Atmospheric Containment: The Total Seal
The ambient gas is an unbreathable, inert blend of nitrogen and argon, completely devoid of free oxygen and stripped of every molecule of humidity. A single direct breath would not merely suffocate you; it would flash-evaporate the moisture from your tongue, bronchial tubes, and alveoli, inducing violent, dry spasms, mucosal rupture, and unconsciousness within fifteen seconds.

*   **Closed-Circuit Life Support (Positive-Pressure):** You must wear a fully sealed, positive-pressure environmental suit. Open-circuit SCBA tanks will run out too quickly; use a closed-circuit rebreather with a minimum four-hour scrub cycle. The air delivered to your helmet must be actively humidified within the loop—standard scrubbed oxygen will dry your airways over hours, but in this chamber, even microscopic gasket seepage will draw moisture out of your suit like a vacuum.
*   **Vapor-Barrier Hydration:** You will need an internal, valved drinking straw built directly into the helmet. You cannot "wait until you get back." At a constant ambient temperature of 38°C (100.4°F), sealed inside an impervious envelope, your body will dump liters of sweat that cannot evaporate to cool you. 
*   **Active Microclimate Cooling:** A passive suit will become an oven within twenty minutes. You must wear a Liquid Cooling and Ventilation Garment (LCVG)—a network of flexible tubes worn against the skin circulating chilled water to an integrated heat-exchanger backpack. 

### 2. Outer Armor: Razors, Splinters, and Jaws
A standard soft EVA (space) suit will be shredded within minutes of arrival. The physical threats here are uniformly sharp, hard, and devoid of rounded edges.

*   **Hard-Shell or Cut-Level-9 Composite Outer Skin:** The pyroxenite walls are freshly broken volcanic glass; brushing against an outcrop will shear standard ripstop nylon or rubberized canvas like paper. Furthermore, the skeletal flora spanning the gantries is composed of silica and organo-lead polymers that snap like tempered glass needles when brushed. A punctured suit means instant decompression and lung destruction.
*   **Armored Articulation Points:** The elbow, knee, and neck joints must feature hard, interlocking plates (titanium or ballistic ceramics). The fauna—both the knuckle-sized carapaces grazing the metal and the scissor-jawed cling-bats above—do not eat flesh, but they cut, shear, and grind materials of high density. A cling-bat severed from its perch, or an apatite-crab attempting to crawl across a warm, foreign object, will use its hardened spurs and shearing mouthparts against whatever it contacts. If your suit looks or feels like something that can be clipped or cleared away, it will be.
*   **Smooth, Recessed Profile:** Your suit must have zero exposed wiring, zero external hoses, and zero soft exterior seals. The crab-like scavengers crawl over everything by the millions. Any external lanyard, rubber gasket, or valve will be explored by thousands of sharp, searching legs grazing for graphite and friction points.

### 3. Locomotion: The Graphite Problem
Walking across this chamber floor is one of the most counterintuitive hazards of the trip. 

*   **The Floor is a Zero-Friction Sheet:** You are walking on unjacketed stone coated in layers of microscopic, continuously ground graphite dust, quartz silt, and pulverized apatite shell. This mixture acts as a high-load, super-fluid solid lubricant. Standard rubber hiking lugs will have zero purchase; it is slicker than wet black ice.
*   **Footwear Solutions:** You need wide-base, rigid-sole magnetic or micro-cleated boots. Heavy, diamond-carbide pins embedded into the soles are required to physically bite through the dry dust layer into the raw pyroxenite rock underneath. If you slip and fall, you are falling onto sharp, unrounded stone that will crack hard armor or fracture bone.
*   **Strict Avoidance of the Beam Sweep:** The walking beams are sixty tons of swinging pig iron moving with unyielding pneumatic momentum. Because there is no steam, there is no thermal warning hiss; they move with concussive, robotic indifference. You must map their 30-meter arcs immediately upon entry and treat the drop-zones of their counterweights as instant-death perimeters.

### 4. Acoustic and Neurological Protection
The acoustics described do not echo; they occupy the room like physical weight. The *thrum-chock* of the valves and the dry screech of the graphite bearings create a relentless wall of infrasound and low-frequency vibration.

*   **Dual-Layer Acoustic Attenuation:** Wear fitted, moldable high-density acoustic earplugs beneath your helmet, paired with active noise-canceling communication cups tuned specifically to deaden frequencies between 20 Hz and 250 Hz. 
*   **Vibrational Damping:** Unmitigated, the low-frequency acoustic energy will pass through your skull and sternum, causing nausea, eyeball resonance (blurring your vision in the dim red light), and severe disorientation. Your suit's boots and chest plate should incorporate elastomeric dampening layers to decouple your body from the vibrations singing through the floor and the air.

### 5. Optics and Lighting
The vault's lighting is monochromatic and disorienting: dull cherry-red incandescence from the engine beds and a sickly, jaundiced amber flicker from ancient vacuum tubes.

*   **Auxiliary Lighting (Broad-Spectrum LEDs):** You must bring your own high-CRI white work lights. The native amber-red light strips away depth perception, making it nearly impossible to distinguish between a safe, flat walking surface and a sheared, three-foot drop-off in the pyroxenite floor.
*   **LIDAR/Proximity Visor Heads-Up Display:** Because the air is constantly swirling with a glittering, suspended silt of pulverized chitin, lead-polymer spores, and quartz dust, your own flashlights will create a blinding backscatter effect, like high-beams in a blizzard. A helmet visor equipped with real-time LIDAR mapping will allow you to see the true geometry of the machinery and rock faces through the drifting mineral fog.

### 6. Behavioral Protocols: What Not to Touch
*   **Do Not Introduce Water:** Carrying an open or leaking fluid source is a catastrophic error. The fauna run on anhydrous lipids and dry minerals; the introduction of liquid moisture will disrupt the thermal stability of whatever it touches, potentially flash-boiling on the incandescent engine beds or triggering an erratic, hyper-aggressive investigative response from millions of organisms that have never encountered surface tension.
*   **Do Not Disturb the Wire-Flora:** The copper-colored filaments look like wiring or dry lichen. When snapped, they release clouds of fine, hexagonal spores loaded with toxic organo-lead. While your suit protects you inside the vault, coating your outer armor in lead-doped micro-shards creates an extreme decontamination hazard for your return.
*   **Ignore the Bats; Watch the Airspace:** The cling-bats are engaged in a non-stop cycle of predation, severing chitin with metallic snaps. They drop fragments of hard, slate-like shell continuously from the 30-meter ceiling. Wear your helmet's ballistic crown at all times; being struck by a falling shell fragment from thirty meters up is equivalent to being hit with a dropped chunk of masonry.

### 7. Decontamination and Re-Entry
When you step out of the vault and back into your transfer point or airlock, your suit will be coated in a lethal residue: fine graphite, pulverized quartz, fluorocarbon films that smell like burnt bone, and toxic lead-laced spores.

*   **Dry Vacuuming First:** Never wash the suit with water initially. Adding water to this ultra-dry dust creates an abrasive, reactive paste that can seize suit mechanisms and ruin seals. 
*   **High-Volume Electrostatic Scrub:** Run a dry-air, electrostatic downdraft cycle to pull the clinging dust off the composite plates into specialized filtration bins.
*   **Respirator Mandate:** Do not break helmet seal until your primary suit has been completely stripped, quarantined, and verified free of airborne quartz and organo-lead particulates.

To visit this place is to stand inside a lung that has forgotten breath, watching an iron heart pump stone through an eternal fever. If you go, step with heavy feet, keep your air pure, and touch nothing that is not your own iron harness.
