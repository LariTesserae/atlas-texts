To see them, you must adjust for a pulse that does not run on biological time. 

The assertion that the pocket is sterile is technically true only to an instrument tuned for albumen, lipid membranes, or the slack, wet chemistry of surface life. Under pressures that turn fluid into something with the stiffness of cold resin, a cell made of water and grease would be sheared into monomolecular ribbons before it could divide. 

The citizens are here, but they are not soft. They are made of the same cold-drawn tungsten, sputtered quartz, and high-dielectric ceramics as the curbs they cling to. They are native to the tolerances.

At first glance, during the screaming cycle, they appear to be architectural trim. Along the needle-thin cornices of the middle spires, what look like decorative rows of lapidary dentils—serrated ridges barely thicker than a shaving from a lathe—are actually clusters of them, locked shoulder-to-shoulder, hunkered flat against the downdraft. They are segmented like silverfish or the articulated slats of a blind, each perhaps the length of an eyelash hair, ground flat to a zero-clearance fit. They do not fight the hypersonic current; they bolt themselves into the EDM burrs with micro-dovetail feet that seat into the kerfs of the stone with an audible, metallic *clink*.

Their metabolism is acoustic. When the cavitation bubbles crackle against the towers, popping in blinding flashes of microscopic sonoluminescence, these creatures do not flinch—they feed. Their dorsal plates are laminated wafers of synthetic tourmaline and barium titanate. Each microscopic shockwave that strikes them deforms the crystal lattice, generating a sharp, transient spike of piezo-electric potential through their carapaces. They drink the sound. The multi-tonal scream of the fluid is not noise to them; it is a violent, down-pouring caloric bounty. They shimmer with faint, phosphorescent green along their seams, shunting the captured voltage inward to maintain the internal magnetic coils that keep their joints from cold-welding together in the brine.

Yet they are not purely stationary. Their entire society, such as it is, takes place inside the isobaric pause.

When the three-minute threshold approaches, a perceptible tension mounts through the needle-spires. The dentils begin to loosen. Their carapaces flex, venting micro-jets of counter-fluid from their flank-ports. 

Then comes the halt. The screaming torrent hits the brake. 

In that fraction of a second—a dead-still, unbreathing hiatus where the heavy brine turns instantly glass-clear and motionless—the city erupts into frantic, frantic life.

To them, that tenth of a second is an expanse of usable time. Their neural architecture does not rely on chemical ions crawling along wet axons; their thoughts propagate at the speed of sound through crystalline rods. In the terrifying hush, they detach. 

They pour from the cornices. They do not swim—the fluid is too dense, too unyielding—they scuttle, leaping across the narrow abysses between balconies on legs shaped like triple-jointed watchmaker’s tweezers. Some are grazers: they descend upon the shiver-fans of spun-glass weed, shearing the brittle silica fibers with diamond-tipped mandibles to harvest the static charge stored in the glass cores. Others are couriers or scavengers, sprinting along the gossamer cat-walks, trading microscopic pellets of sintered alloy or re-seating loose lock-gates that have vibrated out of true. 

There are larger things, too, that dwell exclusively in the suction wells of the lower tiers. These are cylindrical, segmented things the size of a finger-joint, ribbed like bellows and plated in lead-grey carbide. They never leave the deep flanged tunnels. They possess no eyes—for in this glare, an optical organ would simply burn out—but navigate by the ringing of the walls. They act as dynamic valves. When the flow stops, they heave their mass into the bypass ducts, wedging their wedge-shaped skulls into the intake mouths to modulate the pressure of the coming reversal. They are living chokes, biological gaskets whose only sense of self is the crushing, ecstatic satisfaction of being slammed against a valve seat by thirty tons of returning thrust.

High above, clinging beneath the faceted heat-sink cupolas, hang the elders—or perhaps the central nodes. They resemble flattened, star-shaped calipers, spanning nearly half an inch across. Their plates are matte black, pitted with micro-craters from thousands of flow-cycles. They do not scuttle. They remain suspended in the narrowest gaps between the cupola facets, using electrostatic tendrils to feel the regional resistance of the entire pocket. When two of them touch their serrated arms together, a spark jumps through the heavy brine—an instantaneous exchange of municipal data, routing instructions, structural stress reports, and the cold, mathematical equivalent of memory.

They have no names, but they have alignments. A citizen belonging to the Eastern Conduit Spine will have carapaces scored with parallel flow-grooves running at thirty degrees off-axis, tuned to shed the specific turbulence of that neighborhood; an inhabitant of the lower honeycomb is squat, cubical, and dense, its edges chamfered to withstand the brutal down-draft of the displacement valves.

And then, before a human mind could even register that the roar had ceased, the pause ends.

A single, metallic chime rings through the rock vault—the sound of fifty thousand hydro-elastic hinges unlocking at once. The facades snap. The sluices drop.

In an instant, the inhabitants are back in their seams. If one is caught mid-leap across a street canyon when the counter-current hits, it does not drown; it is simply obliterated by the shear. It becomes a flash of cavitation, a bright, diamond-hard crumb swept instantly down into the suction wells to be ground into raw silicate paste, absorbed by the glass-weed, and drawn back into the cycle. 

Those that made the kerf lock down. Their plates slide shut. Their dovetails bite. Their dorsal crystals turn to the incoming blast, drinking the shock, humming with cold power as the white froth rolls over their backs. They go rigid, blind, and ecstatic once more, pressed so hard against the architecture that they become indistinguishable from it—waiting, in the blinding light, for the next three minutes to tick away.
