Across the forest floor, perhaps thirty feet away, there rises an ancient oak, its vast trunk split by lightning long ago. The strike left a deep vertical crack that, over decades, has weathered and widened into a narrow grotto. Unlike the horizontal shelter of the fallen log, this space reaches upward, a natural chimney ascending nearly twenty feet into the tree's heart.

Here dwells the Echo Keeper, a being as vertical as the Moss Mother is horizontal. Its form manifests in the way sound moves through the grotto - sometimes as a column of whispering air, sometimes as a subtle resonance that makes the wood's natural groans musical. On still days, it can be sensed as a spiral of mist that rises through the shaft, carrying the scents of bark and fungus and the tree's slow life.

The grotto's walls are lined with shelf fungi, stepped like terraces up the trunk's interior. These natural platforms host different microclimates - the lower levels stay cool and damp, while the upper reaches catch more sun and wind. Small creatures make homes at different heights: nesting birds near the top, roosting bats in the middle darkness, while salamanders claim the cool bottom layers.

The Echo Keeper tends to these vertical communities through vibration and air flow. It can create gentle updrafts to carry seeds and spores to new niches, or dampen wind that might dislodge a precarious nest. Through subtle acoustics, it warns its residents of approaching danger or guides them to fresh food sources. The grotto's natural resonance amplifies these messages, turning the whole space into something like a living instrument.

Between these two domains - the Moss Mother's horizontal realm and the Echo Keeper's vertical sanctuary - runs a network of communication and exchange. Fungal threads connect underground, carrying chemical messages and nutrients. The Echo Keeper's residents often visit the log cavity to feed, while spores from the Moss Mother's mushrooms drift upward to colonize new surfaces in the grotto. During rain, water flows down the trunk's channels to feed the log's pools, while on hot days, the grotto's updrafts carry cooling moisture to the canopy above.

Most significantly, both beings maintain the forest's memory in their own way - the Moss Mother through her ever-cycling physical matter, the Echo Keeper through the endless play of vibration and resonance. Together they form a kind of archive of the forest's life, each preserving and transmitting different aspects of its ongoing story. They are like two libraries with different but complementary collections, sharing resources across the distance that separates them.
