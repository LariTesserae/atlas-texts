Far below the hanging garden-factory, at the base of the mountain, lies the Precipitation Basin - a sprawling network of tiered pools carved into the living rock. These pools form a natural amphitheater that catches both the mountain's runoff and the excess condensation that occasionally falls from the suspended garden above. But this is no mere collection point - it is a vast information archive, where each pool holds different combinations of minerals and organic compounds that encode the garden-factory's history.

The Basin is a place of deep blues and greens, with swirling patterns of copper and iron oxide staining the stone in complex fractals. The pools range from shallow dishes mere inches deep to profound wells that seem to pierce the mountain's heart. The water in each basin has its own unique chemistry, creating distinct ecosystems in miniature. Some pools harbor colonies of crystalline structures that grow and shift like coral, while others support mats of metallic algae that shimmer with stored information.

The primary custodians here are the Sediment Scholars - entities that exist as living solutions of minerals and memory. Unlike their cousins above, these beings maintain more constant forms, their bodies composed of dense mineral suspensions that move with glacial deliberation through the pools. They appear as translucent figures with layers of settled knowledge visible within them, like the strata of ancient rocks.

The connection between the hanging garden-factory and the Precipitation Basin is maintained through a complex hydrogeological relationship. The water that cycles through the garden above is not a closed system - it regularly releases carefully composed droplets that carry specific information encoded in their mineral content. These droplets fall like data rain, each one containing fragments of the Condensation Cultivators' experiences and experiments.

When these information-laden drops reach the Basin, the Sediment Scholars absorb and archive them, storing the knowledge in the chemical composition of their pools. Over time, this process has created a living library of environmental memory - each pool preserving different aspects of the garden's evolution. The Scholars can "read" this information by analyzing the mineral sequences and crystalline structures that form in their waters.

The relationship between above and below is symbiotic but intermittent. Sometimes months pass between significant exchanges, while other times there might be a torrent of communication, with the garden releasing streams of data-rich condensation that create temporary waterfalls down the mountain face. The Scholars respond by sending up vapor signals - precisely calibrated mists that rise through thermal currents to deliver ancestral knowledge back to the garden.

This vertical exchange has created a unique temporal dynamic: while the garden-factory above experiments with new forms of hybrid life, the Basin below maintains an archive of all previous iterations. It's a relationship of innovation and memory, with the mountain itself serving as the medium of exchange between these two distinct but interconnected realms.
