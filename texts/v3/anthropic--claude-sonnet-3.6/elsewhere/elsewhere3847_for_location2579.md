Across a network of hairline fractures in the ancient rock, perhaps a kilometer away, there exists another chamber - the Echo Vault. Where the geode chamber pulses with growth and warmth, this space trembles with dissolution and cold. Here, crystals don't grow but slowly unmake themselves, sublimating directly from solid to vapor in a process that defies normal geological behavior.

The vault is a roughly spherical cavity, its walls lined with obsidian mirrors that have been eaten away into complex patterns, like ice melting in reverse. The air here is thin and chill, carrying microscopic particles of crystal that catch what little light exists, creating shifting constellations in the darkness. These particles spiral in complex patterns, driven by invisible currents that seem to follow mathematical rules too subtle for human minds to grasp.

The floor is a maze of deep fissures filled with perfectly still, black water that reflects nothing. From these pools, occasional bubbles of heavy gas rise with profound slowness, each one containing a perfect geometric shape rendered in light - as if the mathematics of form itself were trying to escape the depths.

Here dwells the Inverse Archivist - a presence that exists primarily as absence. It moves through the chamber as a void in space, a three-dimensional shadow that absorbs both light and sound. Where the Lattice Weaver builds and connects, the Archivist documents decay and dissolution, recording the patterns of unmaking in the very structure of the vault's walls.

The connection between these two chambers is more than physical. The crystalline songs of the geode chamber arrive here as whispered echoes, transformed by their journey through stone into complex harmonic patterns that the Inverse Archivist captures in its void-form. These echoes seem to influence the sublimation patterns of the vault's dissolving crystals, creating a feedback loop of creation and dissolution that spans the distance between the chambers.

The space between these two chambers is not empty. The web of fractures that connects them is filled with slowly migrating mineral solutions and threads of piezoelectric current. Messages pass between the Weaver and the Archivist through these channels - not communication as we would understand it, but a slow exchange of pattern and anti-pattern, each response taking weeks or months to traverse the intervening stone.

Together, these chambers form a kind of geological computer, with the Weaver's acts of creation providing the input and the Archivist's patterns of dissolution forming the output. The ancient pillar in the geode chamber has its counterpart here - a smooth black obelisk that seems to absorb light rather than reflect it, its surface marked with inward-spiraling glyphs that mirror those in the growth chamber.

This paired system appears to be calculating something vast and slow, using the mathematics of crystal growth and decay to solve equations written in the language of geology itself. The two beings serve as custodians of this process, each maintaining their end of an equation that may have been running since the mountain first rose from the earth.
