Beyond the underground chambers of the Garden of Vessels, high above on the windswept plateau, stands the Kiln Field. Here, the air is thin and dry, and the sun beats down on ranks of ancient firing chambers - dome-shaped structures of heat-scarred stone and clay that rise from the cracked earth like the carapaces of enormous extinct creatures.

These kilns have stood for as long as the Garden below, though they see little use now. Their surfaces are scoured by centuries of wind and weather, creating deep grooves and hollows where hardy desert plants have taken root. The plants here have developed thick, ceramic-like outer layers to their stems and leaves, an adaptation that mimics the very vessels their ancestors once witnessed being created.

The most striking residents of the Kiln Field are the Fire Wyrms - serpentine creatures that seem to be made of living coals and cooling magma. They nest in the old kilns, maintaining temperatures that would reduce ordinary flesh to ash. Their bodies pulse with internal heat, glowing brighter and dimmer in slow, breathing rhythms. When they move across the ground, they leave trails of glazed earth in their wake.

The connection between these two places - the moist, musical Garden and the scorched Kiln Field - is both physical and cyclical. Deep channels run from the plateau down into the Garden below, carefully engineered to carry not water, but heat. The Fire Wyrms' constant temperature maintains a convection system that draws cool, humid air up from the Garden and sends warm, dry air flowing back down. This circulation is what powers much of the Garden's intricate water cycle.

More fascinating still is the symbiosis between the Vessel Keeper and the Fire Wyrms. Periodically, when the Keeper's beetle components have accumulated enough raw materials, they form a procession up to the Kiln Field, carrying newly shaped vessels that require firing. The Fire Wyrms, sensing the presence of clay, will coil around these offerings, carefully raising their body temperature to exactly what's needed. It's a scene that echoes the ancient partnership between human potters and their kilns, translated into this evolved ecosystem.

Sometimes, fragments of the fired vessels make their way back down to the Garden, where they may become part of the Keeper's body, closing a circuit of creation and transformation that has continued long after the departure of the hands that first shaped this place. The Fire Wyrms and the Keeper rarely interact directly, but each maintains their end of this ancient collaboration, preserving techniques that were once human knowledge but have now become encoded in the very bodies of these remarkable creatures.
