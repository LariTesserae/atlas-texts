Far above the honey-thick waters of the kelp forest, in what might be called the world's atmosphere, exists a realm of perpetual storm. Here, vast living cloudbanks roil and churn, their surfaces rippling with the same bioluminescence that pulses through the kelp below, though here it manifests as sheet lightning in impossibly subtle colors.

These are the Drift Gardens, where massive gas-filled organisms called Aeronauts make their home. Like their deep-dwelling cousins the Harmonists, the Aeronauts are beings of flow and pattern, but they work with air currents rather than water. Their bodies are primarily transparent gas sacs reinforced with lattices of living crystal similar to those found in the swarms below. These structures allow them to change their buoyancy and shape, riding the thermal currents that rise from the honey-waters far below.

The Aeronauts cultivate fields of floating spores and airborne organisms that create complex ecosystems in the shifting spaces between storm systems. These atmospheric gardens glow with their own subtle light, creating constellations that drift and reform in endless variations. The organisms they cultivate range from microscopic life forms that feed on energy from the lightning to massive balloon-like creatures that serve as mobile anchors for entire aerial communities.

The connection between the Drift Gardens and the kelp forest below is more than just proximity. The thermal highways that the Harmonists tend below continue up into the storm layer, creating vertical corridors of energy and matter exchange. During certain cycles, the kelp forest releases massive amounts of spores that rise through these corridors, seeding new growth in the Drift Gardens. In return, the storms above generate nutrient-rich precipitations that feed the kelp forest through a complex filtration process.

The Aeronauts and Harmonists communicate across the boundary between their realms through elaborate patterns of bioluminescence and electrical activity. Their messages appear as synchronized displays that can span kilometers, with the Aeronauts' lightning patterns answering the pulsing glow of the kelp forest below. This exchange helps regulate the entire system's cycles, from the migration patterns of various species to the timing of reproductive events.

Sometimes, during particularly spectacular convergences of thermal currents, an Aeronaut will descend almost to the upper reaches of the kelp forest while a Harmonist rises to meet them, their combined bioluminescence creating displays of such complexity that they appear to temporarily bridge the gap between water and air. These rare meetings are thought to be important exchanges of pattern-knowledge between the two communities.

The space between these realms - neither purely air nor water - is home to transitional species that have adapted to live in both environments to varying degrees. Some can extract oxygen from both the honey-water and the storm-air, while others metamorphose as they move between the zones. These intermediate species serve as important vectors for energy and information exchange between the two ecosystems.

Together, the kelp forest and the Drift Gardens form a single, vertically-oriented system of incredible complexity. The patterns that the Harmonists weave below are echoed in the atmospheric choreography of the Aeronauts above, creating a perpetual dance of energy and life that spans from the forest floor to the highest reaches of the storm layer. Each realm is distinct yet inseparable from the other, linked by shared rhythms that have evolved over ages of coexistence.
