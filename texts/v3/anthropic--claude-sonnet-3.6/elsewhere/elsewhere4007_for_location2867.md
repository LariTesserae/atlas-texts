Let me tell you about what lies beyond the Living City's edge, where the eternal twilight finally gives way to true darkness, and the forest-that-devours begins.

Here, the organized chaos of the city dissolves into something older and wilder. The fungal towers gradually reduce in height and complexity until they're just luminous stumps jutting from the ground like broken teeth. The muscular streets fray into thousands of tiny mycelia threads that wave blindly in the thick air before disappearing altogether.

This is the Unmaking Zone, where the forest systematically breaks down anything that strays too far from the city's influence. The ground here is a deep, almost liquid darkness that moves with slow purpose. Things sink into it gradually - first ankle-deep, then knee-deep, then completely - being pulled apart molecule by molecule by countless microscopic decomposers that glow a deep, hungry red.

The air is different here too - heavier, filled with spores that seem to absorb light rather than emit it. These spores form complex geometric patterns that slowly rotate and realign, like the tumblers in some vast living lock. They say these patterns are the forest thinking, though about what, no one knows.

Massive structures that might be trees rise up into the darkness, but they're inside-out, their vessels and organs exposed to the air, pulsing with black fluids that flow upward against gravity. These trees don't photosynthesize - they chemosynthesise, feeding on the broken-down matter from the forest floor and the complex gases that seep up from deep underground.

The relationship between the Living City and the forest-that-devours is one of constant negotiation. The forest sends probes of dark tissue creeping toward the city, testing its defenses. The city responds with waves of bioluminescence that push back the darkness, establishing a boundary that shifts by meters each day but never breaks entirely.

The Symbiotes rarely venture here, but when they must, they grow temporary shells of hardened chitin covered in repellent compounds. Even so, the forest's influence seeps in, temporarily altering their consciousness, showing them visions of a world where everything is forever in the process of becoming something else.

Between these two vast organisms - the city that builds and the forest that unmakes - there exists a kind of balance. The forest breaks down the city's excess growth, preventing it from expanding too quickly. The city, in turn, provides structure and purpose to what would otherwise be endless cycles of destruction and renewal.

Some theorize that both systems are actually part of the same organism, like a creature that builds with one hand and deconstructs with the other. Others believe they're engaged in an ancient war that will eventually end with one consuming the other. The truth, as with everything in this world, is probably somewhere in between - a dance of creation and destruction that has been going on so long that the dancers can no longer be separated from the dance.
