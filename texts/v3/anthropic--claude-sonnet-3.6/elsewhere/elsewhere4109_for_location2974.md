I see another place in this world, far above the Founders' realm, where the surface meets the sky. Here, in stark contrast to the humid darkness below, stretches a vast salt flat - a blindingly white expanse that seems to extend forever under an impossibly blue sky. The ground is a perfect crystalline crust, geometric patterns extending to the horizon like nature's attempt at circuit boards.

This place appears lifeless at first glance, but look closer: in the thin seams between salt crystals, translucent filaments pulse with barely perceptible movement. These are the Breathers - gossamer-thin organisms that exist in the interface between air and mineral. They harvest trace elements from the salt and energy from the relentless sun, transforming both into delicate structures that bridge the microscopic gaps in the crystal matrix.

The Breathers create and maintain a vast network of hollow channels within the salt crust, like an inverse coral reef made of absence rather than presence. Through these channels flows their most precious resource: air. They pull it down from the atmosphere, filter it through their filaments, and send it transformed into the depths below.

This is the connection between these two realms - the Breathers above and the Founders below form a symbiotic system spanning thousands of feet of rock. The air processed by the Breathers carries specific molecular signals that the Founders require for their endless construction. In return, the Founders' activities generate waste heat that rises through fissures in the rock, keeping the salt flat at precisely the temperature the Breathers need to maintain their delicate chemical balance.

Between these two zones lies the Gradient - a complex transition space where pressure and chemistry shift dramatically over vertical distance. Here, specialized organisms from both communities form chains of molecular exchange, passing nutrients and signals up and down like bucket brigades. The Gradient is also where the two systems' waste products react to form new compounds essential to both - a chemical handshake across the divide.

Sometimes, during rare storms, the salt flat's surface briefly dissolves and recrystallizes, creating new patterns. These changes are reflected below as subtle shifts in the Founders' building plans, proving that despite their apparent separation, these two communities are engaged in a deep dance of coevolution, each responding to changes in the other across the barrier of rock and time.
