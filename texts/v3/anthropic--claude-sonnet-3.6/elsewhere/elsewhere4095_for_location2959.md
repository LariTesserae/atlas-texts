Across vast distances of the continental plateau, far from the industrial chambers and their Tender societies, there exists another realm within this same living world - the Deadlands. Here, the great mat has died back, leaving behind a petrified landscape of hollow chambers and calcified root-pillars that stretch toward a naked sky.

The death happened centuries ago, when a critical balance in the mat's metabolism shifted too far, too fast. Now these chambers stand as a ossified echo of their former vitality. The once-living walls have transformed into stone, their patterns preserved like fossils, creating labyrinthine spaces of eerily beautiful mineral formations. Where bioluminescent pods once pulsed with life, there are now crystalline growths that catch and scatter what little light filters down from above.

In these spaces dwells the Hollow Society - descendants of Tenders who chose to remain when their section of the mat began to die. Over generations, they've adapted to this harsh environment in ways that make them distinctly different from their living-chamber cousins. Their bodies are shorter, denser, with chalk-white skin that reflects the scarce light. Their eyes have grown larger, adapted to the true darkness that falls when the sun sets.

The Hollow Society survives by harvesting the mineral remains of the dead mat, which still contain trace elements of the complex chemicals their ancestors once refined. They've developed techniques to extract these substances from the stone, using tools that would seem primitive to the Tenders but are perfectly adapted to their environment. They live in carved chambers, wearing clothing made from crystalline fibers, and have developed their own language of echoes and vibrations that carry through the stone corridors.

Between these two realms lies the Gradient - a zone where living mat gradually gives way to stone. This transition space stretches for kilometers, creating a spectrum of environments ranging from barely alive to newly dead. Here, the root-pillars stand in various states of petrification, some still showing faint signs of life while others have fully mineralized.

The Gradient is where the two societies occasionally meet, though such encounters are rare. Tender expeditions sometimes venture into this region to study the process of mat-death, hoping to prevent such disasters in their own territories. The Hollow People journey here too, seeking living samples to sustain their ancient technologies, or to trade their unique mineral extracts with the Tenders.

This relationship between the living and dead zones creates a crucial balance in the larger ecosystem. The Deadlands serve as a constant reminder to the Tenders of what can happen when the mat's delicate systems are pushed too far. Meanwhile, the mineral resources of the dead zone - and the knowledge of the Hollow Society - have proven invaluable in maintaining the living chambers' industrial processes.

The mat itself seems to respond to this duality - new growth in the living chambers often follows patterns that echo the mineralized formations of the Deadlands, as if the entire organism carries some deep memory of its lost territories. In this way, life and death, growth and decay, organic and mineral all exist in a complex dance across the plateau's vast expanse.
