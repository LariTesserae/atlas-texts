Across the mountain range, in a valley that catches the last light of day, there is a meadow that never stops moving. Unlike the frozen hollow, here everything is in constant flux. The grass grows visibly, reaching up toward the sun and withering away in the space of hours, only to begin again. Flowers bloom and die like time-lapse photography, their petals opening and closing in waves that ripple across the field like wind on water.

The air is thick with seeds and spores, creating a constant golden haze that shifts and swirls in complex patterns. These seeds never reach the ground but remain suspended, caught in invisible currents that move them in elaborate dances. They trace mathematical patterns in the air - spirals, fractals, perfect geometric forms that form and dissolve moment by moment.

The most striking feature is the butterflies. They emerge fully formed from the air itself, condensing from the golden haze like thoughts taking wing. Their colors are impossible to pin down, shifting between all possible hues as they move. They live for exactly one hour each, their whole life cycle compressed into sixty minutes of frenzied motion. As they die, they dissolve back into the haze, becoming seeds for new flowers, new butterflies, new patterns of endless change.

---

Between these two places - the hollow of perfect stasis and the meadow of perpetual transformation - runs a narrow path that exists in both states at once. On one side, nothing moves; on the other, nothing stays still. The boundary between them is paper-thin but absolute. Where they meet, strange things happen to time itself.

The being in the hollow and the meadow's endless dance are two expressions of the same force - like the fixed point at the center of a spinning wheel and the blur of motion at its edge. They maintain each other, the perfect stillness making the endless motion possible, the constant change preserving the eternal moment.

Sometimes, a butterfly will drift too close to the hollow and freeze mid-wing-beat, becoming part of its crystalline perfection. Sometimes, a bird will stray toward the meadow and dissolve into a shower of seeds. But these small exchanges only strengthen the balance between the two places, like breathing in and breathing out.

The path between them is rarely traveled, but those who walk it experience time as a physical substance, something they could reach out and touch. They feel the tension between stillness and motion in their very bones, understanding for a moment what it means to exist in all moments at once, and in no moment at all.
