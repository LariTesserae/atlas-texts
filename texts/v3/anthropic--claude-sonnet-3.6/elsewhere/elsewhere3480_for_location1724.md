Let me tell you about what lies above, at the peaks where those ancient mountains meet the sky. Here, the air is thin and sharp, and the stone rises in bare, wind-carved spires. This is the domain of the Windweavers, beings as different from the Moldkin as air is from stone, yet connected to them through ancient and vital cycles.

The Windweavers are creatures of light and vapor, their bodies constantly shifting between visible and invisible states. They appear as intricate patterns in the air, like heat shimmer given purpose and partial form. Their movements follow the complex choreography of high-altitude winds, and they can stretch themselves thin enough to ride the finest air currents or concentrate into more substantial forms that cast prismatic shadows on the stone.

Their realm is one of extremes. During the day, the sun's intensity is almost painful, the light reflecting off exposed crystal faces in brilliant arrays. At night, temperatures plummet and ice forms in geometric patterns, only to sublimate directly into vapor when morning comes. The Windweavers thrive in these transitions, their essence participating in each phase change.

Their architecture, if it can be called that, consists of elaborate wind tunnels and pressure systems they've gradually influenced over centuries, encouraging the mountain winds to flow in specific patterns. These invisible structures are marked by strings of ice crystals that form and vanish, by particular sounds as the wind moves through natural flutes in the rock, and by the paths of high-altitude birds that have learned to use these air currents.

The connection between these two realms - the hidden city below and the wind-realm above - is vital to both. The Windweavers gather moisture from the high atmosphere, concentrating it through their own essence before releasing it in forms that can seep into the mountain stone. This is the source of the constant moisture that sustains the Moldkin's city. In return, the fungal networks that permeate the Moldkin's realm release spores that rise on thermal currents, providing essential nutrients that the Windweavers use to maintain their semi-physical forms.

Between these realms lies a zone of constant transformation, where water cycles between its states - liquid seeping up through stone through capillary action, vapor rising on warm currents, ice forming in delicate lattices. This intermediate space hosts its own unique ecology of beings that participate in these transitions. Crystal formations grow here that seem to hover between solid and liquid states, their structures incorporating both the mineral patterns of the Moldkin and the atmospheric geometries of the Windweavers.

The two peoples rarely interact directly - their modes of existence are too different - but they are deeply aware of each other through the materials they share. The Moldkin can read the history of the winds in the patterns of mineral deposit left by the water that flows down to them. The Windweavers sense the Moldkin's activities through subtle changes in the thermal currents that rise from below and the composition of the vapor that reaches them.

There are times, though, when communication becomes more direct. During certain astronomical alignments, when the angle of light and the temperature gradients are just right, the boundary between air and stone becomes more permeable. At these times, the Windweavers can descend in visible spirals of mist into the hidden city, while the consciousness of the Moldkin can rise with the vapor, experiencing momentarily the vast perspectives of the heights.

These moments of connection are crucial for both peoples, maintaining the balance of their shared world. Each exchange allows them to adjust their activities to better support the whole - the Windweavers might alter their wind patterns to direct more moisture to areas where the Moldkin's gardens need it, while the Moldkin might encourage their fungal networks to release different combinations of spores to sustain the Windweavers through coming seasonal changes.

Together, they form a complete cycle - the Windweavers gathering and concentrating what disperses, the Moldkin shaping and channeling what condenses, and between them the endless transformation of elements that keeps their world alive.
