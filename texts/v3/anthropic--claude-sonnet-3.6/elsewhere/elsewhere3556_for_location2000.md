Across a vast distance from the glass plain, yet connected to it through deep vibrations that travel through the planet's crust, lies the Howling Canyon. Here, the landscape is composed of towering walls of porous stone that rise like organ pipes into perpetual storms above. The rock is riddled with millions of perfectly circular holes of varying sizes, from tiny pores to caverns large enough to swallow cities. Each hole is worn smooth by eons of wind, and each produces its own note when the endless gales blow through them.

The weather here is the inverse of the glass plain's eternal clarity - dark clouds roil continuously overhead, sparked with strange lightning that moves in geometric patterns. The wind never ceases; it only changes direction and intensity in complex cycles that might take years to complete. The result is a wild, ever-changing counterpoint to the mathematical precision of the glass plain's symphony.

Within this tempestuous amphitheater dwell the Wind Weavers. They manifest as living currents of air, visible only by the debris they carry - glittering particles of stone dust that swirl in hypnotic patterns. The Wind Weavers dance through the holes in the canyon walls, their movements determining which notes sound at any given moment. They are the conductors of this massive geological instrument, though whether they created the holes or merely discovered them is unknown.

The connection between the two realms exists in several forms. Most directly, certain deep frequencies from the glass plain's pools resonate with specific caverns in the canyon, creating harmonics that travel both ways. The Wind Weavers can feel these vibrations and respond to them, while the Resonators can detect the canyon's wild music as subtle variations in their own carefully maintained patterns.

The space between these two places is a gradient of increasing chaos - the ordered glass slowly giving way to increasingly fractured terrain, until the first wind-carved holes appear. In this transitional zone, both species' music can be heard clearly, creating unique interference patterns that neither group fully controls or understands.

Sometimes, during particularly powerful storms, the Wind Weavers' music becomes strong enough to excite sympathetic vibrations in the glass plain's pools, causing them to deviate from their usual mathematical progressions. Conversely, when the Resonators achieve especially complex harmonies, the vibrations can cause new holes to open in the canyon walls, adding new notes to the Wind Weavers' repertoire.

The two species rarely interact directly, but they are aware of each other's existence through their music. They represent two fundamentally different approaches to sound - the precise, crystalline perfection of the Resonators versus the wild, improvisational chaos of the Wind Weavers. Yet together, they create something greater than either could alone - a planet-spanning symphony that combines order and chaos, mathematics and weather, crystal and stone.

Between them lies the possibility of evolution - new music that could only exist through their unlikely collaboration across the distance. Neither species seeks to change the other, but their unavoidable influence on each other's realm ensures that both places are slowly, imperceptibly, transforming over millennia. The glass grows more intricate; the winds find new patterns. The song continues, endlessly changing while remaining eternally itself.
