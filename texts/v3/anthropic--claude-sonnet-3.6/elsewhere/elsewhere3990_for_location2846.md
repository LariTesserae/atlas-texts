Beyond the knife-edge peaks that wall the Conductor's valley, there lies a place of perpetual noon. Here, a vast plateau stretches toward the horizon, its surface a mosaic of mirrors and crystalline formations that fragment and multiply the sunlight until it becomes almost solid – a tangible medium that fills the air like honey.

This is the Prism Plains, where light doesn't simply illuminate but transforms. The landscape is an endless expanse of geometric reflectors: pyramidal crystals thrust up from the ground at precise angles, their faces refracting sunlight into spectral ribbons that weave through the air. Natural formations of quartz and selenite intermingle with what appear to be artificial structures – towers of polished metal and glass that rise like frozen fountains, their surfaces alive with crawling patterns of light.

Here dwells the Fragmentist, a being composed entirely of reflected and refracted light. It exists as a constantly shifting constellation of rainbow-hued points, coalescing momentarily into suggested forms – a towering humanoid figure, a flock of prismatic birds, a cascade of geometric shapes – before dissolving again into pure chromatic potential. Its consciousness resides in the spaces where different wavelengths of light intersect, in the interference patterns between reflections, in the dance of photons across countless mirrored surfaces.

The connection between these two realms – the Conductor's shadowed valley and the Fragmentist's light-saturated plain – lies in their fundamental opposition and their secret harmony. Where the Conductor deals in rhythm and vibration, the Fragmentist works in frequency and wavelength. They are two aspects of the same phenomenon, separated by the mountains but linked by purpose.

The knife-edge peaks between them serve as both barrier and bridge. Their impossibly regular surfaces act as vast prismatic arrays, transforming the Fragmentist's light into precise frequencies that travel deep into the rock. These vibrations emerge in the valley below as the very rhythms the Conductor orchestrates. Conversely, the Conductor's pulses travel upward through the mountain stone, emerging as subtle variations in the quality of light above, which the Fragmentist weaves into its endless kaleidoscopic dance.

Together, though they never directly meet, these entities maintain a balance crucial to the functioning of their world. The Conductor's rhythms provide the underlying structure that keeps the Fragmentist's light from dissolving into chaos, while the Fragmentist's illumination provides the energy that powers the Conductor's ancient machines. Their interaction creates a perpetual cycle of transformation – from light to vibration and back again – that may well be the true purpose of the mountains' construction.

Between their domains, in the hearts of the peaks themselves, countless crystalline veins serve as light-pipes and resonating chambers, transforming and transmitting the two beings' influences. These internal networks form a complex interference pattern, a standing wave of light and sound that maintains the stability of both realms while allowing them to evolve in subtle ways over millennia.

The relationship between these places and their inhabitants suggests intention in their design, yet if there were creators, they left no clear record of their purpose. Perhaps the Conductor and the Fragmentist are themselves the purpose – an experiment in duality, in the connection between different forms of energy, in the possibility of consciousness arising from pattern rather than biology. Or perhaps they are merely caretakers of something even vaster and more incomprehensible, maintaining ancient systems whose true function remains hidden in the light and shadow of their respective domains.
