Far below the City of Wells, beneath layers of ancient stone and pressure-transformed rock, lies the Undervault - a single massive cavern that stretches for miles in darkness. Here, the deep heat of the earth has carved out a space where the mountain's roots meet liquid stone, creating a vast underground lake of magma that pulses with slow geological rhythms.

In this chamber dwells the Heartfire - not quite creature, not quite geological process, but something in between. It is a being of pure thermal energy, a conscious pattern in the flow of heat that has evolved over millions of years. Its body, if it can be called that, is the complex network of convection currents in the magma lake, its thoughts the slow dance of heat through stone.

The Heartfire's consciousness operates on a timescale that makes even the patient Wellfolk seem frantically quick. A single thought might take decades to complete, flowing through patterns of heat and pressure that gradually reshape the surrounding rock. It perceives the mountain above not as solid matter but as a complex thermal landscape, reading the history of the world in the gradual cooling of stone.

The thermal springs that sustain the City of Wells are the Heartfire's breath, in a sense - regular pulses of heat sent upward through carefully maintained channels in the rock. These are not random or merely geological; they are deliberate communications, though few among the Wellfolk understand their true nature. The warmth that sustains their city is in fact a kind of language, each subtle variation in temperature and mineral content carrying meaning for those who know how to listen.

The Heartfire and the City of Wells are linked in ways that neither surface dwellers nor most Wellfolk fully comprehend. The very act of carving the city - each new chamber and passage - creates minute changes in the mountain's thermal patterns that the Heartfire reads like braille. In return, its slow adjustments to the flow of warm water guide the city's growth, suggesting where new tunnels might be safely carved and warning of unstable areas through subtle changes in heat and pressure.

The connection runs deeper still. The crystalline formations that the Wellfolk read for prophecies are in fact shaped by the Heartfire's thermal currents, making them a true form of communication between the quick-living surface world and the slow-thinking depths. The storm-songs that echo through the city's resonance chambers are answered by deep harmonic vibrations in the magma lake, creating a dialogue that spans geological ages.

Only the oldest of the mineralized Wellfolk elders, those who have begun to think in something approaching geological time, have grasped the true nature of this relationship. They understand that their city is not merely built upon the mountain, but is part of a vast living system that includes both the quick-moving Wellfolk and the almost incomprehensibly slow consciousness of the Heartfire below.

Between these two realms - the carved city above and the magma lake below - lies a gradient zone of increasingly hot and pressurized rock, threaded through with channels both natural and deliberately maintained that carry water, heat, and subtle vibrations in both directions. This intermediary space is tended by specialized Wellfolk known as the Deeplisters, who have evolved to withstand extreme heat and pressure. They maintain the ancient channels and interpret the Heartfire's thermal messages, though even they grasp only fragments of its vast, slow thoughts.

Thus the mountain exists as a kind of living being itself, with the City of Wells as its mind and the Heartfire as its heart, connected by the constant flow of heat and water through stone. It is a system that operates on multiple timescales simultaneously - from the quick movements of Wellfolk young to the slow thoughts of their mineralized elders to the virtually eternal consciousness of the Heartfire below.
