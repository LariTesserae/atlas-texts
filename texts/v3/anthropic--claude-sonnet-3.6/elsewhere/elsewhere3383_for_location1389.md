Beyond the shimmer of the Living Cathedral, about three kilometers deeper in the ocean's twilight zone, there exists its shadow-twin: the Detrital Gardens. Where the Cathedral rises in geometric precision, the Gardens spread in organic chaos - a vast field of specialized filter-feeding structures that process the Cathedral's waste and the ocean's natural sediment.

The Gardens exist in perpetual twilight, illuminated only by patches of bioluminescent tissue that pulse in slow waves across its expanse. The structures here are more tentacular than architectural - long, branching filaments that wave in the currents, each optimized to extract different nutrients from the falling organic matter. The entire system extends across several square kilometers of the seafloor, a dark mirror to the Cathedral's upward reach.

Here dwell the Deep Tenders - humans modified even more extensively than the Resonance Monks above. Their bodies are squid-like, with multiple limbs adapted for manipulating the Gardens' delicate structures. Their skin contains pressure-resistant chambers that allow them to move freely at depth, and their eyes are huge, adapted to see by the Gardens' own light. They speak in patterns of bioluminescence, their skin flashing in complex sequences that mirror the light-language of deep-sea creatures.

The connection between Cathedral and Gardens is constant and vital. A network of living tubes - part vascular system, part data network - connects the two systems. Through these channels flow not just organic matter but information: chemical signals, pressure changes, and complex biomolecular codes that maintain the balance between above and below. The Sediment Mind extends into these depths, but here its thoughts are slower, more dreamlike, shaped by the pressure and darkness of the deep.

The relationship is symbiotic but hierarchical - the Gardens process and recycle what the Cathedral discards, but in doing so, they generate new compounds that the Cathedral requires. The Deep Tenders and Resonance Monks rarely meet physically, but they maintain constant communication through biochemical signals carried by the connecting tissues. Their separate evolutions have made them almost different species, yet they remain bound by their shared purpose of maintaining this complex engineered ecosystem.

Together, Cathedral and Gardens form a complete system - one reaching toward the surface light, the other embracing the depths, connected by flows of matter and information that mirror the ocean's own cycles. The distance between them is not just physical space but a gradient of pressure, light, and biological adaptation, each zone carefully engineered to perform its role in this vertical ecosystem.
