Across a vast salt plain, far from the terrarium-cities, lies the Archive of Discarded Forms. At first glance, it appears to be a sprawling field of crystalline structures - but these are not the living glass of the terrariums. These crystals are the preserved remains of evolutionary dead-ends, failed experiments in biological engineering that preceded the current ecosystem.

The crystals grow in irregular spirals from the salt crust, each one containing the genetic memory of an abandoned design: photosynthetic organisms that produced too much heat, communication networks that became cancerously self-replicating, structural tissues that proved too brittle or too flexible. They rise from the ground like frozen fountains, their internal structures visible through translucent layers of mineral preservation.

Walking through the Archive feels like exploring a cemetery of possibilities. The air is perfectly dry and still, heavy with the weight of accumulated failure. Occasionally, a crystal will catch the light in such a way that its contents seem to momentarily animate - a glimpse of what might have been.

The connection between these two places - the vibrant terrarium-cities and the stark Archive - is maintained by the Harvesters. These are simple organisms, something like a cross between fungi and wind-blown seeds, that drift out from the terrariums carrying samples of successful adaptations. When they encounter the crystals of the Archive, they create new preservation layers, adding contemporary genetic information to the historical record.

This process creates a living history, each crystal growing new layers that document the ongoing evolution of the terrarium ecosystems. The Harvesters also extract information from the Archive, carrying ancient genetic codes back to the terrariums where they might be reintegrated into new experiments. This circulation of genetic information creates a kind of dialogue between success and failure, between the living and the preserved.

The Tenders are aware of the Archive but rarely manifest there directly. Instead, they experience it through the Harvesters, reading the accumulated data like a vast library of evolutionary history. The Archive serves as both warning and inspiration - a reminder of the delicate balance they maintain and a source of raw material for future innovations.

The salt plain itself seems to pulse very slowly, its crystalline surface expanding and contracting with temperature changes that take years to complete. This geological rhythm provides a temporal framework even slower than the Tenders' plant-time consciousness, marking eras of biological development like rings in an ancient tree.

Between these two domains - the lush complexity of the terrarium-cities and the stark simplicity of the Archive - lies a gradient of increasing crystallization and decreasing biological activity. The transition happens so gradually that it's impossible to point to where one realm ends and the other begins. This interstitial zone is where much of the actual work of evolution happens, as genetic information flows both ways through the Harvesters' networks, and new forms emerge from the interaction of living and preserved systems.
