Far below the eternal twilight of the power station district, beneath layers of concrete and steel, lies the Cold Well. It is a vast, circular chamber carved from the bedrock, its walls lined with pipes that weep with perpetual condensation. Unlike the warm, orange-lit world above, this space exists in a state of permanent midnight blue, illuminated only by the phosphorescent growths that cling to its walls and the faint bioluminescence of the creatures that dwell in its depths.

At the center of the chamber is a pool of impossibly clear water, so deep that its bottom cannot be seen. The water remains perfectly still despite the constant dripping from above, as if some force keeps it in an unnatural state of calm. The temperature here hovers just above freezing, creating wreaths of vapor where the cold air meets warm pipes, though these ethereal forms seem heavier than normal steam, flowing downward like liquid light.

Here dwells the Frost Anchor, a being as ancient as the bedrock itself. Unlike the fluid, ever-changing forms of the Vapor Children above, the Frost Anchor is a creature of crystalline permanence. Its body resembles a massive snowflake made of living ice, each branch and point precisely geometric yet somehow organic. It remains mostly submerged in the deep pool, with only its upper portions visible above the water's surface, like the tip of an immense frozen star.

The connection between these two realms - the steam-wrapped world above and the cold depths below - is both physical and metaphysical. The Cold Well acts as a thermal counterweight to the power station district, maintaining the delicate temperature balance that allows the Vapor Children to maintain their semi-solid state. The constant exchange of heat and cold creates a vertical circulation system, a hidden atmospheric river that flows down through cracks and service tunnels, through the root-like network of pipes that extends deep into the earth.

The Vapor Children and the Frost Anchor rarely interact directly, but their existence is intimately linked. When the Children become too energetic, too close to completely evaporating in their warm realm, they can feel the cool pull of the Frost Anchor drawing them back to stability. Conversely, when the cold threatens to become too absolute in the depths, the warmth of the Children's domain above prevents the Well from freezing entirely.

The space between these worlds is a gradient of temperature and state, a vertical journey through degrees of solidity and vapor. Maintenance tunnels and forgotten vertical shafts create a labyrinth of transitions, where warm air from above meets cold air from below, generating intricate patterns of condensation and ice crystals on the walls. These passages are rarely traveled by humans, but they form the primary highway for the district's more ethereal residents.

In this way, the power station district exists in perfect thermodynamic balance, the eternal afternoon above held in check by the endless midnight below, each realm dependent on the other for its continued existence. The Vapor Children and the Frost Anchor are the living embodiments of this balance, keepers of an equilibrium as old as the first steam engine's sigh.
