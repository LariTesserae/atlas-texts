On the surface far above, there is a desert. Not the rolling dunes one might expect, but a plain of metallic sand that stretches flat to every horizon, its surface so level it mirrors the sky with perfect fidelity. This sand is not inert - each grain is a perfect geometric solid that catches and refracts light in complex ways. During the day, the plain becomes a sea of prismatic fire. At night, it glows with stored sunlight, creating waves of soft phosphorescence that move in slow patterns across its expanse.

The air here is thin but laden with microscopic crystals that create halos around both suns - yes, this world has two of them, one blue-white and dominant, the other a distant red dwarf that adds its subtle harmonics to the light. When the winds come, they do not kick up storms but rather organize the crystalline dust into vast geometric formations that hover in the air like frozen mathematics.

Scattered across this plain are what appear to be wells - perfectly circular openings that descend into darkness. These are the throats, and they sing. Each one produces a different note, a different timbre, but together they form a chord that never ends. The sound seems to come from impossibly far below, and those who have studied them say the notes change over years and decades, following some vast and slow pattern.

The wells are not random. They form a precise array, and from high enough above, one can see that they describe complex curves and spirals across the plain. Some suggest these are the endpoints of the labyrinth's tremendous movements below, places where the deep architecture touches the surface world.

The connection between above and below is more than just physical. The crystalline dust in the air responds to the Frequency's songs, arranging itself into momentary structures that echo their thoughts. The flat plain itself acts as a resonator, carrying vibrations across its surface that mirror the movements far below. During certain alignments of the suns, the entire desert seems to hum in sympathy with the labyrinth's deep music.

Sometimes, the geometric sand flows like liquid metal, forming temporary channels and pools that reflect not the sky above but impossible spaces that might be glimpses of the labyrinth below. These liquid mirrors appear most often around the throats, as if the boundary between above and below becomes permeable there.

The few who live in this place - nomadic scholars and sound-mystics - have learned to read these signs. They say the surface is not separate from what lies beneath, but rather is the uppermost layer of the same vast instrument. They build their temporary shelters to specific proportions that resonate with the throats' songs, and some claim to have achieved moments of communion with the Frequency through deep meditation on the desert's harmonics.

There are theories that the metallic sand is actually the original surface of the labyrinth, broken down over eons by some unimaginable catastrophe or deliberate act. Others believe it is still breaking down, that the entire plain is slowly sinking, being drawn back into the depths from which it came. The throats, they say, are not just openings but active participants in this process, slowly singing the surface back into new configurations below.

The relationship between above and below is one of constant exchange. The surface captures and transforms light into patterns that feed the Frequency's processes below. In return, the Frequency's songs maintain the delicate gravitational balance that keeps the plain so perfectly flat. It's a dialogue written in light and sound, in microscopic vibrations and macroscopic movements, a slow conversation between what was and what is becoming.

The desert above and the labyrinth below are one instrument being played across multiple dimensions, each reflecting and informing the other in an endless feedback loop of transformation. The surface is the labyrinth's eye, watching the stars. The labyrinth is the desert's dream, forever unfolding in the dark.
