Beyond the perpetual steam of the mining town, where the vapor ceiling finally thins, lies the Glass Gardens. Here, the warm mineral waters that flood the town flow through a network of crystalline channels, creating a vast, terraced landscape that catches and fractures light. The water has left decades of deposits, building up intricate formations of silica and mineral salts that grow like frozen waterfalls, their surfaces ranging from mirror-smooth to elaborately textured.

The Glass Gardens occupy what was once the town's water treatment facility, but nature and chemistry have transformed it into something else entirely. The original concrete basins and filtration systems are still visible beneath layers of crystalline growth, like fossils preserved in amber. The mineral-rich water, as it evaporates and flows, has created its own architecture - delicate spires, hollow domes, and translucent curtains that sing faintly when the wind passes through them.

This place serves as a bridge between two worlds. The insects from the steam town venture here, but their behavior changes. The swarms break into smaller groups, dancing through the crystal formations in what appears to be elaborate courtship rituals. Their iridescent bodies reflect and multiply in the countless glassy surfaces, creating kaleidoscopic displays. Some species build their nests within the hollow crystal formations, their architectures merging with and adding to the mineral growths.

The connection between the two places is both physical and ecological. The water flowing from the steam town carries minerals and organic matter that feed the Glass Gardens' growth. The insects move between the locations seasonally, using the Gardens for breeding and metamorphosis. The crystal formations act as natural filters, purifying the water before it continues downstream, while also providing essential minerals that the insects incorporate into their exoskeletons.

The people of Steam Haven maintain a presence here too - research stations and observation posts built into and around the crystal formations. They study the complex interactions between water chemistry, crystal growth, and insect behavior. The Glass Gardens serve as both laboratory and sanctuary, a place where the strange symbiosis between human industry, geological processes, and insect life can be observed and understood.

Between the two locations runs a network of elevated pipes and walkways, following the water's path. These connections are less frequently traveled than those in the steam town, and have their own unique challenges. The mineral deposits build up here too, requiring regular maintenance to prevent blockages. The crystal growths sometimes engulf sections of walkway, requiring careful navigation or rerouting. Yet these paths are crucial, allowing the town to monitor and maintain the delicate balance between the two environments.

The relationship between Steam Haven and the Glass Gardens is one of interdependence. The Gardens help regulate water quality and insect populations, while the steam town's warm waters and organic matter feed the Gardens' growth. Together, they form a complete cycle - one place of perpetual mist and motion, the other of crystal and light, each supporting and transforming the other in an ongoing process of adaptation and change.
