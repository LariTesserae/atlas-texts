Far above the Library, nearly touching the surface where fractured sunlight still penetrates the water, floats the Exchange. Where the Library is all right angles and mathematical precision, the Exchange is organic chaos - a sprawling network of transparent bubbles, twisting tubes, and undulating membranes that more closely resembles a colonial organism than a constructed space.

The material that forms its walls is neither glass nor plastic, but a living tissue cultivated from ancient deep-sea creatures - strong enough to maintain its shape against the water pressure, flexible enough to move with the currents, and clear as crystal. The bubbles range from intimate chambers barely large enough for two beings to vast amphitheaters hundreds of feet across. Unlike the Library's flooded halls, these spaces are filled with breathable air, maintained at comfortable pressure by complex biological processes within the living walls.

Here is where the surface dwellers meet the deep dwellers, where information and resources flow between the worlds above and below. The Exchange serves as both marketplace and embassy, neutral territory where a thousand different species can interact in relative comfort. Specialized chambers can be adjusted to accommodate virtually any environmental needs - pressure, atmosphere composition, temperature, gravity.

The structures are in constant motion, shifting and reconfiguring themselves according to the needs of their occupants. New chambers grow organically while old ones are absorbed back into the system. Bioluminescent patterns ripple through the walls, serving as both decoration and communication system. The entire complex pulses gently with the rhythm of the waves above.

What connects these two places - the rigid, eternal Library and the fluid, ever-changing Exchange - is the Path. It begins as a single massive tube of the same living material as the Exchange, but as it descends, it gradually transitions to match the Library's stone architecture. The journey takes three days at standard speed, though emergency transits can be accomplished much faster.

The Path is more than just a physical connection - it's a carefully maintained gradient between two worlds. The pressure changes are managed so gradually that travelers barely notice them. The water temperature drops by fractions of a degree per mile. The very chemistry of the water shifts, becoming progressively more like the Library's preservative medium.

Regular convoys move up and down the Path - Archivists bringing carefully selected knowledge to be traded above, surface dwellers making pilgrimages to study in the Library's depths, diplomatic missions between the countless civilizations that share these waters. The Path itself is heavily defended, though few ever see the security measures. Both the Library and the Exchange depend on controlled access to maintain their delicate equilibrium.

The relationship between above and below is complex. The Exchange's chaos would be impossible without the Library's stability beneath it. The Library's isolation would be meaningless without the Exchange to give its knowledge purpose. They are two halves of a single system - memory and expression, preservation and growth, depth and surface, forever linked by the long, slow journey between them.
