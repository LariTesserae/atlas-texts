Across a deep ravine from the fungal metropolis, where the limestone ridge splits and drops away into shadow, lies the Undertangle - a dark mirror of the luminous city above. Here, the mushrooms grow downward instead of up, their caps hanging like stone umbrellas from the cavern ceiling. The space beneath is a maze of dangling mycorrhizal cords, some as thick as tree trunks, others as fine as spider silk, all glistening with moisture in the dim light.

This inverted forest is home to the Deepspun, distant cousins of the Sporefolk. Where their surface kin are translucent and luminescent, the Deepspun are opacity embodied - their bodies composed of dense fungal matter that absorbs light rather than emitting it. They move through their suspended world by braiding themselves into the hanging mycorrhizal networks, becoming nearly indistinguishable from the structures they inhabit.

The Undertangle processes all that the upper city sheds. Dead matter, excess water, spent spores - all filter down through complex mineral channels in the limestone. The Deepspun transform this decay into new forms of life, their bodies producing unique compounds that can only be synthesized in darkness. The air here is thick with complex molecules, creating a psychedelic fog that induces visions in any surface dwellers who venture too deep.

Between these two realms lies the Seepage Zone - a transitional space where water and nutrients flow both up and down through the porous stone. This is where the two communities meet and trade. The Sporefolk bring light-harvested nutrients and surface spores, while the Deepspun offer their dark-brewed compounds and mineral-rich solutions. Their exchanges look like slow-motion dances, their bodies temporarily merging as they share substances and information through direct chemical transfer.

The relationship between the two societies is complex and complementary. The Sporefolk's luminous city cannot exist without the Undertangle's processing of waste and death, while the Deepspun depend on the surface dwellers for energy-rich compounds and new genetic material. The limestone ridge itself has been transformed over centuries by their combined activity - the stone now more like living tissue than rock, shot through with channels and chambers that regulate the flow of water, nutrients, and information between the two realms.

Through these channels flows a shared memory, a chemical record of both communities' histories. Surface events are reflected in deep compounds; undertangle processes rise in luminous patterns above. Each community sees the other as both foreign and familiar - distinct yet utterly necessary, like organs in a larger body. Their technologies, their arts, their very modes of being have evolved in constant dialogue, creating a system where above and below sustain each other in endless cycles of light and shadow, growth and decay, sending and receiving.

Some say there are beings that move between both worlds with ease - hybrid forms that are neither Sporefolk nor Deepspun, but something else entirely. They appear in the stories both communities tell through their chemical songs and light patterns - mysterious figures who understand both the language of light and the grammar of darkness. But such entities remain elusive, glimpsed perhaps in the shifting patterns of fog in the Seepage Zone, or sensed in strange harmonies that sometimes ripple through the entire system, momentarily synchronizing the rhythms of both realms in ways that defy explanation.
