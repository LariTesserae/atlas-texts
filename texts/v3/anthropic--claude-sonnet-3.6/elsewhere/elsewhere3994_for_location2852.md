Far above these sunken valleys, where the water grows warmer and lighter, lies the Drift Gardens - a vast network of floating vegetation that stretches across the surface like a living quilt. Here, massive circular platforms of interconnected plants form natural islands, their roots hanging down into the depths like curtains of silver. These are the Surfaceweave - cousins to the Tideborn who tend the boundary between water and air.

The Drift Gardens exist in perpetual motion, their position dictated by wind and current. The platforms themselves are engineering marvels - layers of fibrous plants grown in specific patterns to create stable, buoyant surfaces strong enough to support small structures. These floating islands range from modest family pods to larger communal spaces where several platforms have been carefully lashed together with living vines.

The Surfaceweave have developed differently from their deep-dwelling kin. Their skin is tougher, darkened to protect from direct sunlight, and their eyes can shift quickly between underwater and surface vision. They are equally comfortable diving beneath their floating homes or walking upon them, though they retain many of the Tideborn's aquatic adaptations.

Between these two realms - the sunken city and the drifting gardens - exists a complex vertical ecosystem that connects surface to depth. This interface zone is marked by the Root Roads - those long tendrils descending from the Drift Gardens that have been carefully cultivated into living highways. The Surfaceweave and Tideborn meet here regularly, using these natural pathways to exchange goods and knowledge.

The relationship between the two peoples is deeply symbiotic. The Surfaceweave harvest materials that can only be found in sunlit waters and maintain trading connections with land-dwelling peoples, while the Tideborn provide minerals and deep-water resources essential for the floating gardens' stability. More importantly, they share the responsibility of maintaining the water column's health - the Tideborn managing the depths while the Surfaceweave tend the surface ecology.

Messages travel between the communities through a sophisticated system of pressure pulses in the Root Roads, creating a vertical network of communication that spans the water column. During certain alignments of current and season, the two peoples gather for shared ceremonies, their different songs - one adapted for deep water, one for the surface - combining in the middle depths to create harmonies that travel for miles through the sea.

The space between surface and depth is not empty - it is rich with life and meaning, a gradient of pressure and light that both peoples understand as sacred. They see their two communities as reflections of each other, different expressions of the same water-borne wisdom, forever linked by the living bridges of the Root Roads and their shared stewardship of this liquid world.
