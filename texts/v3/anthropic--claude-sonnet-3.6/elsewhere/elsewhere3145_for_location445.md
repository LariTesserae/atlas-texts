Beyond the ridge line, hidden from the Shapers' city by the mountain's peak, lies the Valley of Endings. Here, in stark contrast to the perpetual growth and careful maintenance above, is where bamboo comes to die.

The valley floor is a maze of fallen giants - bamboo culms thick as tree trunks, their golden-brown lengths scattered like massive pick-up sticks in seemingly random arrays. Yet there is pattern here too, for this is where the Gleaner dwells.

The Gleaner is as different from the Shapers as death is from life, though they are bound in the same cycle. Where the Shapers are tall and willowy, the Gleaner is compact and dense, their skin the deep brown of well-aged bamboo. Their hands are broad and strong, with thick calluses from handling the dead culms. Their eyes hold the golden sheen of mature bamboo stems caught in sunlight.

The valley is the Gleaner's workshop, where they harvest what the mountain provides. Every piece of fallen bamboo is assessed, sorted, and transformed. The Gleaner's tools are simple but precise - saws and splits, awls and augers, each worn smooth with decades of use. They work with the same patience as the Shapers above, but theirs is the patience of decomposition rather than growth.

The connection between these two places is both physical and metaphysical. Water that flows through the Shapers' city eventually reaches the valley, carrying minerals and nutrients leached from the managed forests above. This enriched water feeds fungal networks that help break down the fallen bamboo, creating soil that eventually washes back up the mountain slopes during the rainy season.

But more important is the flow of materials and knowledge between the two realms. The Gleaner regularly makes the journey up to the Shapers' city, bringing tools and objects crafted from the fallen bamboo - things that can only be made from culms that have died and dried naturally. These items are essential to the Shapers' work: the precise measuring rods used to gauge growing stems, the specialized tools for binding and weaving, the frameworks that support new growth.

The Shapers, in turn, guide certain bamboo culms toward natural death when the time is right, directing them to fall toward the Valley of Endings. This is done with the same care and precision as their growth-guidance, ensuring that each stem's death serves a purpose in the greater cycle.

The Gleaner and the Shapers rarely speak, but their communication through the language of the bamboo itself is profound. Each tool the Gleaner crafts carries within it an understanding of death's role in renewal; each culm the Shapers guide toward ending holds the promise of transformation.

Between these two realms - the city of eternal growth and the valley of purposeful endings - lies a narrow pass. Here, mist from above meets the dry air from below, creating ever-shifting veils through which travelers must pass. It's said that to walk this path is to understand the full cycle of existence - growth and decay, preservation and transformation, all part of the same endless pattern.
