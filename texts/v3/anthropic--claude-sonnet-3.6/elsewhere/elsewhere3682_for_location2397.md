Across a maze of partially submerged roads and fallen structures, where the water grows deeper and the air gradually clears, stands The Dry - a stark contrast to the perpetually damp world of the Moisture Miners. It rises from a rare patch of elevated ground, a massive concrete and steel fortress deliberately sealed against the surrounding humidity. Its walls are thick and windowless, broken only by heavily engineered airlocks and filtered ventilation systems.

Inside, the air is artificially crisp, maintained at precisely 40% humidity. The sound of dehumidifiers is constant but muted, a soft mechanical whisper that residents stop hearing after their first few weeks. Every surface is treated with moisture-repelling compounds, creating an almost clinical atmosphere. Water is precious here, measured in milliliters and carefully recycled through complex filtration systems.

The inhabitants call themselves Preservers. They are the guardians of everything that water destroys - paper books, delicate electronics, biological specimens that would quickly rot in the outside environment. Their archives extend deep underground in climate-controlled vaults, containing what might be the last surviving examples of many pre-flood technologies and documents.

The Preservers themselves are easily identified by their pale, almost papery skin and their tendency to develop chronic coughs when they venture outside. They wear static-free gloves and breathing masks even inside their fortress, treating their own bodies as potential contamination sources. Their children are born into sterile rooms and taught from infancy about the vital importance of environmental control.

Between these two contrasting worlds lies the Exchange - a carefully maintained neutral zone where the communities meet to trade. It's a series of pressurized chambers where humidity is gradually stepped down, allowing people and goods to acclimate. Here, the Moisture Miners bring their carefully collected water, each batch tested and graded for purity and mineral content. The Preservers trade knowledge, preserved medicines, and restored technology.

The relationship between the communities is complex. The Preservers need the Miners' water to maintain their cooling systems and sustain their population, while the Miners rely on the Preservers' technical knowledge to keep their own infrastructure functioning. Yet there's mutual incomprehension too - the Miners see the Preservers as fearfully hiding from life itself, while the Preservers view the Miners as primitives who have surrendered to entropy.

The children of both communities are particularly fascinated by each other. Young Preservers press against their climate-controlled windows to watch the Miners' children scrambling along their aerial pathways. The Miners' children make up stories about the mysterious pale people who live in the box where nothing ever gets wet.

Some rare individuals move between both worlds - particularly the Translators, who have adapted to the extreme environmental transitions and serve as cultural interpreters. They suffer for this ability, requiring constant medical treatment for the strain on their bodies, but they're essential to maintaining the delicate balance between these radically different approaches to survival.

The space between the communities is treacherous, yet heavily trafficked. Water levels shift unpredictably, hiding the remnants of old roads and buildings that can tear the bottom out of a boat. The air is often thick with strange fogs that play havoc with navigation equipment. But necessity keeps the route open - neither community can truly survive without the other, no matter how different their ways of life have become.
