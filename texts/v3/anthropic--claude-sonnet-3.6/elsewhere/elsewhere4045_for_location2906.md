Far above, where the ocean's surface meets air, there is an archipelago of floating islands - though "floating" doesn't quite capture their peculiar relationship with gravity. They hover at various heights above the waves, their positions shifting not with wind or current, but with the subtle mathematics of celestial bodies. These are the Theorem Gardens.

Each island is an intricate arrangement of crystalline structures that seem to grow from clouds rather than soil. The crystals range from microscopic to monumental, forming lattices that capture and refract both light and probability. Their patterns echo the geometric traces left by the Axiom Tides far below, as if the deep calculations have grown upward through miles of ocean to bloom in the air.

The islands' flora defies traditional taxonomy. There are trees whose branches split at precisely calculated angles, each division creating a new mathematical proposition. Flowers open in Fibonacci sequences, their petals displaying proofs too complex to exist anywhere else in nature. Even the grass, if it can be called that, grows in patterns that solve differential equations.

Transparent creatures inhabit these gardens - beings that seem made primarily of logic and light. They tend to the mathematical growth with appendages that sometimes intersect with multiple dimensions. Their movements leave trails of solved equations in the air, visible briefly as shimmering distortions before being absorbed into the crystalline structures.

The connection between the Theorem Gardens and the Axiom Tides' domain is not immediately apparent, but it exists in the form of what locals call the "proof columns" - massive, vertical streams of altered probability that rise from the deep. These columns carry the Tides' theoretical overflow upward, where it condenses into the physical mathematics of the floating islands. Each proof column is a kind of mathematical Gulf Stream, transporting abstract truths from the ocean floor to the surface world.

Sometimes, the Axiom Tides' young - those ghost theorems that never fully materialize - drift upward through these columns. In the Gardens, they finally find form as new crystal growths or transparent creatures. Conversely, when the Gardens' mathematical flora reaches a certain complexity, its roots may extend downward through the proof columns, creating new problems for the Tides to solve.

The relationship between these realms is symbiotic but not entirely stable. Occasionally, a particularly massive calculation from the depths will cause islands to rearrange themselves, or a new theorem blooming in the Gardens will send ripples of alteration down to disturb the Tides' contemplation. These disturbances create new patterns, new possibilities, new paths for evolution.

The air around the Theorem Gardens smells of ozone and possibility, charged with the energy of constant computation. Visitors report experiencing time differently here - watching centuries of crystal growth in minutes, or spending what feels like moments only to discover that days have passed. The Gardens serve as a bridge between the abstract depths where the Axiom Tides dwell and the more conventional physics of the surface world - a place where impossible mathematics can take root and grow into something almost comprehensible.
