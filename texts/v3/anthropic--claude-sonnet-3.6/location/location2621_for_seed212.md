In the Deep Palace, water has forgotten it needs containers. It flows in vast vertical sheets that never fall, suspended in impossible geometries that have become, over endless time, load-bearing structures. The water-walls hold up vaulted spaces large enough to house cities, and indeed, cities there are - though not like any above the waves.

The inhabitants are primarily the Glass Whales, enormous crystalline beings that swim through both water and water-architecture with equal ease. Their translucent bodies catch what little light makes it down here and scatter it in complex, living patterns across the water-walls. They communicate in deep harmonics that resonate through the liquid structure of this realm, their songs becoming part of its architecture.

Smaller creatures move in dense schools through the spaces between - some bioluminescent, some transparent, some seemingly made of living shadow. They flow in endless unchanging patterns, like blood cells through veins, maintaining ancient circuits that may be migration routes or information networks or both.

The built environment, if it can be called that, consists of structures that seem to have been grown rather than constructed. Primitive tools have shaped living coral into dwelling spaces, but the coral itself has continued growing, creating organic geometries that defy euclidean space. The sparse vegetation that exists here has adapted to anchor itself directly into the water-walls, spreading in delicate fractal patterns that serve as road maps and calendars for the inhabitants.

The light is constant - neither day nor night, but a perpetual blue-green twilight created by the interplay of bioluminescence, filtered sunlight, and the optical properties of the water-walls themselves. This light seems to bend in ways it shouldn't, sometimes forming loops or knots in space.

The soundscape is overwhelming - whale song, the movement of vast schools of creatures, the deep resonance of the water-walls, all combining into a cathedral chord that never changes or resolves. The acoustics follow their own rules here; sound can be seen as ripples in the water-walls, and the Glass Whales' songs leave permanent echoes that become part of the architecture.

Temperature remains cool but stable, maintained by currents that flow in perfect loops through the water-walls. These currents carry memories in the form of temperature differentials - ancient information preserved in the endless circulation.

The laws of physics seem more like suggestions here. Distance and direction are unreliable concepts. Schools of fish swim in möbius strips. The Glass Whales pass through each other without collision, their crystalline forms temporarily merging and separating in geometric patterns that encode deep mathematical truths.

This is a place where stability itself has become strange - nothing ever changes because change itself has been transformed into a spatial dimension rather than a temporal one. All possible variations exist simultaneously in different regions of the Deep Palace, creating a kind of living library of forms.

The water-walls don't just ignore gravity - they seem to generate their own local gravitational fields, creating regions where 'down' is sideways or circular or elsewhere. The inhabitants navigate these gravity fields instinctively, their movements describing complex orbits through overlapping gravitational centers.

This is a place that was never built or grown - it simply discovered itself, emerging from the intersection of water and impossibility, and has remained in perfect equilibrium ever since, unchanging yet never still, a monument to the moment when reality forgot its own rules and decided to keep forgetting.
