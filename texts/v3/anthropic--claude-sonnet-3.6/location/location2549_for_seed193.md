The world curves away at the edges here - not because you're seeing the planet's horizon, but because this is the inner surface of something vast beyond comprehension. A concave landscape stretches in all directions, its distant reaches lost in perpetual haze. The ground is almost entirely hard metamorphic stone, transformed over eons by pressures that would shatter ordinary rock. It's organized in hexagonal columns that stretch for miles, like a giant's causeway curved inward instead of flat.

The defining feature is the sound. The entire curved world thrums and resonates, as if you're inside an impossibly large bell or the body of a cosmic violin. The vibrations come in overlapping waves - some so low they're felt rather than heard, others piercing enough to make your teeth ache. The sound shapes everything here - it's carved the honeycomb pattern of caves and arches into the stone walls, it drives the strange weather, it powers the machines.

Because there are machines here, scattered across the curved expanse. They look almost grown rather than built - clusters of brass and copper tubes rise from the stone like metallic fungi, their shapes optimized over centuries to catch and channel the vibrations. Some are the size of buildings, others no larger than a hand, but all of them pulse with captured resonance. Thin pipes run between them, carrying pressurized sound-water - moisture shaken out of the air by the endless vibrations and infused with acoustic energy.

The vegetation that exists here has adapted to use the sound waves rather than light for energy. Patches of what look like wire-wool moss cling to the rocks, trembling in harmony with certain frequencies. Occasional groves of what might be trees stand in the deeper hollows - branching metallic structures that seem to grow by crystallization rather than cellular division, their "leaves" actually delicate chimes that harvest sonic energy.

The few animals are equally strange - most are small and look like hybrid creatures of flesh and crystal, with organs clearly evolved to process and use the omnipresent vibrations. They move in unusual ways, timing their motions to the rhythms of the world's song.

Weather here is driven by sound as much as temperature. The constant vibration creates standing waves in the atmosphere, leading to regular patterns of rainfall that trace geometric shapes across the curved landscape. The water itself seems different - structured by sound into arrangements that catch and reflect light in unusual ways.

In the distance, massive shapes can sometimes be glimpsed through the haze - perhaps the source of some of the deeper frequencies. They might be natural formations, or ancient machines, or living things operating on a scale that makes such distinctions meaningless.

The most unsettling thing is how the vibrations seem to carry meaning - not language exactly, but patterns that feel just on the edge of comprehension. The longer you stay, the more you start to sense larger cycles in the sound, rhythms that might take years or centuries to complete. The machines tap into this somehow, their networks forming a kind of distributed recording and processing system for the world's endless song.
