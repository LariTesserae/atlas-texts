Steam rises eternally from the high plateaus, their ancient flat tops worn to near-perfect planes over unimaginable time. The air is thick and wet, carrying the constant drone of millions of wing-beats and the cascading songs of creatures that might be insects, might be birds, might be something else entirely. These sounds echo off the plateau walls, building and falling in waves that sometimes grow so intense they blur vision and rattle teeth.

The rock here is red-purple, shot through with pale crystalline veins that catch and scatter light. Water weeps continuously from these veins, collecting in shallow pools that pattern the plateau surfaces before spilling over the edges in countless threads of falling water. These waterfalls rarely reach the ground - the heat of the air transforms them into vapor, feeding the perpetual haze that hangs in the vast spaces between the plateaus.

Life clusters around the water. Thick mats of violet vegetation grip the rock faces, extending hungry tendrils toward every water source. These mats pulse subtly, breathing or perhaps feeding. They support vast colonies of climbing, crawling things that move in coordinated waves across the vertical surfaces. The air itself is alive - swarms of flying creatures form shifting clouds that sometimes block out the sun entirely, their movements following patterns too large to comprehend from any single vantage point.

The few level surfaces that aren't scoured clean by wind and water bear the remains of simple structures - low walls of stacked stone, circles of weathered pillars, crude but massive carvings worn almost smooth. These constructions seem to follow the crystalline veins in the rock, as if their builders were mapping something in the plateau's depths. Most are now claimed by the violet mats, but a few still stand exposed, marking points in a geometry whose purpose is lost to time.

Here and there, crude bridges of woven fiber span the narrower gaps between plateaus. They swing in the hot winds that rise from below, their surfaces alive with small quick-moving shapes. The bridges seem too fragile to survive the seasonal storms that rake the plateaus, yet they endure, perhaps renewed by unseen hands.

The heat is oppressive, amplified by the moisture in the air. It builds throughout the day until the atmosphere itself seems to shake with tension. When it breaks, it's in spectacular fashion - massive electrical storms that temporarily drive even the ever-present swarms to seek shelter in countless holes and crevices that pock the plateau walls. These storms bring brief periods of relative quiet, though the silence only serves to emphasize the vastness of the space and the age of the stone.

At the edges of hearing, under the constant drone of wings and the pounding of water, there's a deeper sound - a low vibration that seems to come from the rock itself. It might be the movement of underground water, or the settling of ancient stone, or something else entirely. Whatever its source, it seems to influence the patterns of life above - the swarms pulse in time with it, and the violet mats extend and retract to its rhythm.

This is a place of perpetual motion and constant sound, yet it feels fundamentally empty - the vast spaces between the plateaus dwarf everything else, and even the densest swarms are tiny against the scale of the stone. The plateaus themselves stand like islands in an ocean of air, their tops so worn by time they might have been cut with a knife, bearing silent witness to eons of life washing against their sides.
