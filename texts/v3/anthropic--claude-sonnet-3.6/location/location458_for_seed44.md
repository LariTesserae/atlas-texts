A greenhouse district spreads through the valley bottom, hundreds of connected glass chambers that have long since surrendered to their contents. The original geometric panes are still visible, but now they serve mainly as scaffolding for the dense mat of vegetation that has claimed every surface. Condensation beads eternally on the inside of the glass, running in rivulets down engineered channels that feed the hydroponic systems below.

The air is thick and warm, carrying the green scent of photosynthesis and the mechanical drone of circulation fans that never cease their turning. Their sound mingles with the steady patter of recycled water through the irrigation networks - a technological rain that feeds the endless growth. Ancient pumps pulse somewhere in the infrastructure, their rhythms felt more than heard through the metal walkways that thread between the growing chambers.

Each greenhouse section has its own microclimate, carefully maintained by systems installed generations ago. Some areas are wrapped in artificial mist, others maintain a drier warmth, but all are overwhelmingly green. Vines thick as power cables wind through the support structures. Leaves press against glass in complex tessellations. The original growing beds have long since overflowed, with plants establishing themselves in every crack and crevice, creating their own soil from decades of accumulated organic matter.

The walkways and maintenance corridors between sections feel like canyons, their walls alive and breathing. Workers move through these spaces deliberately, their movements slow and efficient in the humid air. Their protective gear is bleached by time and spotted with algae that thrives in the perpetual moisture. They tend to the ancient machinery with practiced care - adjusting flow rates, replacing filters, maintaining the delicate balance that keeps this artificial ecosystem functioning.

Few animals make their home here - the environment is too controlled, too saturated with human presence and mechanical rhythm. Occasionally small birds find their way in through ventilation systems, but they rarely stay long in this green-tinted, ever-humming world. The real inhabitants are the plants themselves, and the patient machines that serve them.

At night, the glow of status lights and climate control displays creates a dim constellation through the foliage. The fans continue their endless turning, the pumps their steady beating. The plants grow invisibly in the artificial twilight, adding their microscopic contributions to a process that has been running uninterrupted for longer than anyone can remember.

This is a place of ordered abundance, where technology and nature have reached a kind of synthesis - neither truly wild nor fully controlled. The greenhouse district breathes its warm, green breath, and the machines hum their eternal song, and the water flows in its endless circuits through glass and steel and living things.
