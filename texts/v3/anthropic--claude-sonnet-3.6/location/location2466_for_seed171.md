In the high thin air, countless bodies press together in the dark. This is a cathedral of flesh - a vast natural chamber where creatures have gathered so densely that they form the very walls and ceiling. Their bodies are translucent, almost crystalline, letting through only the faintest hints of whatever dim light source lies beyond. They might be some cousin of sea anemones, but adapted to air, their tentacles and organs refracting what little illumination reaches them into subtle prismatic effects.

The chamber maintains a constant, almost feverish heat from the press of bodies and some internal metabolism of the creatures themselves. This heat draws up what little moisture exists here into a perpetual light condensation - not quite fog, but a kind of living dew that beads on the creature-walls and runs in silent rivulets down their length.

The beings do not move, yet they are undeniably alive. They may have been here for centuries or millennia, growing into each other until no space remains between them, forming this bizarre living architecture. Their translucent tissues have developed into lens-like structures that bend and focus the weak light in strange ways, creating patches of impossible color in the dimness.

The floor is a continuous surface of what might be the same creatures in a different form - flattened, their upper surfaces smoothed by eons of whatever process brought them here. The condensation pools in subtle depressions, forming mirrors that reflect the twisted light from above.

Though the chamber is silent, there's a sense of constant communication - perhaps chemical signals passing through the mass of joined bodies, or subtle vibrations below the threshold of hearing. The air feels thick with unspoken meaning.

The heat is not quite uncomfortable but insistent, like a fever dream. There's no wind, no movement of air - the chamber feels hermetically sealed by its living walls, preserved in a moment that stretches across geological time.

Small creatures - perhaps younger or different versions of the wall-beings - move through the condensation pools, leaving trails that slowly fade. They seem to feed on the moisture itself, or perhaps on microscopic particles suspended within it. Their movements are deliberate but unhurried, as if they too are part of this eternal moment.

This place feels like a temple, but one with no builders and no worshippers - only this strange confluence of life that has found its perfect stasis and maintained it beyond all reasonable spans of time. The darkness, the heat, the press of bodies, the silent communication - all of it holds in perfect balance, neither growing nor decaying, simply persisting in its own peculiar perfection.
