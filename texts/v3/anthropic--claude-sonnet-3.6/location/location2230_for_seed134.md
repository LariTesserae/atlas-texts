A tiny grotto nestles within the sheer face of a young volcanic mountain, barely visible unless you know exactly where to look. The entrance, no wider than a person's shoulders, opens into a space that feels both cramped and somehow infinite – a volcanic bubble caught in the mountain's flesh.

The air here is suffocating, thick with heat that radiates from the dark basalt walls. Water weeps continuously from hairline cracks in the ceiling, creating a constant patter of drops that collect in shallow, terraced pools carved into the rock. The pools descend in a spiral, each slightly lower than the last, their edges sharp and new, unweathered. Steam rises constantly, making the small space feel like the inside of a lung, breathing hot moisture.

The walls glisten with more than just water. They crawl with life – thousands of tiny translucent creatures no bigger than fingernails move in coordinated waves across the surface, feeding on the thin film of bacteria that thrives in the heat and moisture. In places, the creatures cluster so densely they look like living jewels, their bodies refracting what little light makes it through the entrance.

Above the pools, the ceiling is dotted with the nests of peculiar birds, their bodies adapted to the constant steam and heat. They dart in and out through the narrow entrance, their movements precise and practiced. Their nests are simple depressions in the rock, lined with their own shed feathers which have taken on the texture of felt in the humid air.

In the deepest pool, strange amphibians pulse and writhe, their skin nearly transparent, showing organs that glow with faint bioluminescence. They seem to respond to the rhythm of the dripping water, moving in synchronized patterns that might be courtship, might be hunting, might be something else entirely.

The heat is most intense near the back wall, where a crack occasionally releases a breath of volcanic gases, creating momentary swirls in the constant steam. The rock here is almost too hot to touch, and the water that runs down it is warm enough to scald.

The whole space feels like a secret heart chamber of the mountain, where the boundary between living and mineral blurs. Despite its small size, the grotto contains entire ecosystems layered upon each other, each adapted to slightly different conditions of heat and moisture, all packed into a space barely large enough for a few people to stand.

The continual drip of water, the soft shuffling of the wall-creatures, and the occasional flutter of birds creates a subtle percussion that seems to pulse with the mountain's own rhythms. Everything here feels both ancient and new – as if this pocket of life could have existed for millennia, yet every surface still holds the raw edge of recent formation.
