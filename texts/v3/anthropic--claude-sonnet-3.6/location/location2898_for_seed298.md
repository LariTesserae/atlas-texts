In the high reaches where air grows thin, a city of birds claims the ancient peaks. Not built by human hands but carved by countless generations of beaks and claws, the cliff faces are riddled with nesting holes that pepper the stone like the chambers of a giant's honeycomb. The rock itself is a pale granite worn glassy-smooth by millennia of wind, its once-sharp edges rounded to flowing curves that catch the wan sunlight.

Moisture seeps constantly from the stone, not quite water and not quite fog - a kind of perpetual weeping that traces dark veins down the cliff faces. These tears collect in shallow basins and curved channels, works of erosion deepened by generations of birds drinking and bathing. The water has a slight mineral taste, carrying dissolved traces of the mountain's heart.

The residents are everywhere - wheeling in the updrafts, perched on impossibly narrow ledges, disappearing into their holes in synchronized waves. Mostly small, swift creatures in greys and browns, moving with the precise choreography of schooling fish. But there are larger ones too - great soaring forms that nest in the higher reaches, their wings spanning meters as they glide on thermal currents with barely a twitch of feathers.

The sound is a constant soft susurration - thousands of wings, quiet calls, the subtle scrape of claws on stone. It rises and falls in waves as flocks return or depart, but never quite reaches cacophony. The birds seem to modulate their voices in deference to the mountain's solitude.

At certain heights, the nesting holes have been worn into interconnected passages, creating warren-like networks through the rock itself. These tunnels hold their own ecosystems - smaller birds, creatures that have forgotten flight in favor of scrambling through the dark, specialized hunters that have learned to navigate the maze-like passages.

The air grows colder as you ascend, but the birds have engineered their environment over centuries. The countless bodies and carefully positioned nests create pockets of warmth, and the constant movement of wings stirs the air in complex patterns that distribute heat through the colony. Certain chambers stay warm even in winter, while others channel cooling breezes in summer.

There is an order to it all - territories maintained through generations, flight paths worn into the air itself, complex social structures expressed through elevation and proximity. Not built with conscious intent perhaps, but shaped by time and necessity into something that feels deliberately crafted.

The whole complex extends across several peaks, connected by invisible highways of air current that the birds navigate as confidently as paved roads. In the distance, similar colonies can be glimpsed on other mountains - separate cities in an aerial civilization that humans never built and barely notice.

Water continues to weep from the stone, birds wheel in their ancient patterns, and the mountain slowly surrenders to time, one grain at a time, as it has for millions of years. The colony is both permanent and constantly renewed, existing in a kind of dynamic stasis that might outlast the mountain itself.
