A young city grows up the sides of raw stone mountains, their peaks still sharp and unworn. The mountains themselves seem to be still pushing upward - not through any dramatic volcanic action, but through a slow, inexorable rise that adds a few centimeters each year. The bedrock occasionally groans deep beneath the streets, and the city must constantly adjust itself to the shifting grade.

The buildings are a dense weave of industrial forms: factories and warehouses interconnected by covered bridges and cable systems, their materials ranging from sturdy stone at the bases to lighter metals and composites higher up. They adapt to the steep terrain through a complex system of terraces, stilts, and anchors. Steam pipes and electrical lines create their own architectural language, wrapping around buildings like vines.

The air is cool and often misty, especially in the morning when condensation from the higher elevations drifts down through the city. This regular moisture feeds an abundance of moss and ferns that grow opportunistically in any crack or crevice, spreading across walls and roofs in thick carpets. The vegetation has been incorporated into the city's engineering - certain species are cultivated deliberately to help stabilize the shifting ground and prevent erosion of the new-formed stone.

Water doesn't pool here - it's always moving, either down through an elaborate system of channels and pipes, or up through mechanical pumps that maintain the city's water supply. The sound of water is a constant background noise, but muffled and distant, mixing with the low hum of machinery and the occasional deep settling of stone.

The height and density create numerous microenvironments - some areas are perpetually in shadow, while others catch surprising amounts of light reflected off metal surfaces and pale stone. Small gardens are tucked into unexpected places, taking advantage of these variations. The plantlife is carefully chosen and managed - while abundant, it's clearly cultivated rather than wild.

Few animals make their home here besides the occasional birds that nest in the higher structures, and some specialized insects that have adapted to the industrial ecosystem. The city feels more like a vast machine-garden hybrid than a traditional urban space, its systems optimized for managing the unusual geological conditions rather than following conventional urban planning.

At the highest elevations, the city thins out into a network of monitoring stations and maintenance facilities, where the newest stone emerges. These structures are perpetually being rebuilt and relocated as the mountain continues its slow growth. Their lights can be seen from below on cloudy nights, like a scatter of grounded stars marking the transition between the built environment and the raw geology above.

The overall impression is of a place that's simultaneously under construction and fully realized - a city that's learned to thrive on instability, incorporating the mountain's growth into its own continuous evolution. The air smells of stone dust, steam, and the sharp green scent of the cultivated moss gardens. Everything feels both precisely engineered and slightly experimental, as if the entire city is an ongoing research project in how to build on moving ground.
