The Glass Garden lies hidden within a massive geode, a crystalline chamber discovered beneath the foundations of the ancient city. Its entrance, a triangular portal hardly wider than two shoulders, descends through forty feet of living rock before opening into the impossible space beyond.

The chamber stretches perhaps sixty feet in diameter, though distances are deceptive here. Every surface is faceted crystal, mostly smoky quartz shot through with veins of rose and citrine. The crystals grew in geological time, but now they sing – a constant, overwhelming resonance that visitors feel in their bones before their ears. The sound comes from everywhere and nowhere, a chord that shifts and pulses as you move through the space.

Despite the complete absence of water, life thrives here. Crystalline formations that might be mistaken for mineral growths are actually living things – translucent "plants" that have evolved to photosynthesize the intense light that refracts endlessly through the chamber walls. They grow in fractal patterns, branching and rebranching in mathematical precision, their tissues clear as glass but shot through with threads of green and gold. These crystal-plants catch and amplify the chamber's resonance, each species vibrating at its own frequency, contributing to the overwhelming symphony.

The light seems to have no single source, but rather emanates from the walls themselves – or perhaps from the strange flora, or perhaps from the intersection of the two. It creates a disorienting effect where shadows fall in multiple directions simultaneously, and depth perception becomes unreliable.

The city above has built into this space over centuries, adding delicate wooden walkways and viewing platforms that hover above the crystal floor. These structures are clearly handmade, their joinery and construction techniques preserved from the earliest days of the chamber's discovery. The wood has aged to a deep amber, worn smooth by countless hands. Crude astronomical markings have been carved into the railings, tracking how the chamber's light shifts with the seasons above.

Only a few creatures make their home here. Occasionally, something like a moth but with wings of living crystal will flutter past, its movement creating brief harmonic overtones in the chamber's endless song. There are rumors of larger crystal-fauna deeper in the unexplored crevices, but most believe these are merely echoes and reflections playing tricks on the mind.

The air is warm and completely dry, and seems to vibrate with potential. Some visitors report that their metal jewelry begins to grow tiny crystal formations after prolonged exposure. Others claim that the chamber's resonance continues to ring in their dreams for days after visiting. The city's Guild of Harmonists maintains the space, though they admit they understand little of its true nature. They've learned only that the crystal-plants respond to certain frequencies of human song, unfurling new growth in response to specific notes held for long periods.

The longer one stays, the more the boundary between mineral and living thing seems to blur. Is that branching formation ahead a crystal or a plant? Did that cluster of geometric shapes shift slightly, or was it a trick of the ever-changing light? The chamber's song offers no answers, but seems to smile at the questions.
