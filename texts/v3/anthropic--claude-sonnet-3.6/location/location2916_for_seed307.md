A vast salt plateau stretches to every horizon, its surface not flat but formed of endless hexagonal pillars rising at slightly different heights, creating a stepped topography that extends as far as the eye can see. The pillars, some as wide as houses, others narrow as doorways, are packed tightly together with hair-thin cracks between them. Their surfaces are a pristine, almost painful white that reflects the intense light of three small suns hanging at different points in the sky.

The air is thin and cold at this elevation, but water is everywhere - not as pools or streams, but as a constant microscopic rain of mineral-laden droplets that seem to form spontaneously in the air. These droplets build up on the hexagonal surfaces in perfectly spherical beads that grow until they reach a critical size, then roll down the geometric faces of the pillars. The sound of these countless droplets moving across the salt surface creates a continuous crystalline chiming that echoes across the plateau, punctuated by sharp cracks as thermal stress occasionally splits new fissures in the mineral formations.

The sparse life here has evolved to harvest water from the air rather than the ground. Thin, nearly transparent filaments stretch between pillars like spider silk, collecting moisture and channeling it to small colonies of pale, lobster-like creatures that scrape algae from the salt surfaces. These organisms are bioluminescent, adding a subtle blue-green glow to the already brilliant landscape when the suns set in their staggered sequence.

The perpetual moisture and cold have created strange erosion patterns in the salt - not smoothing the pillars but actually sharpening their edges, as if the plateau is growing more crystalline over time. New pillars occasionally thrust upward from below, driven by some deep geological process, their emergence heralded by deep booming sounds that resonate through the mineral matrix.

At random intervals, massive electrical discharges arc between the pillars, their paths influenced by the conductive mineral content of the falling droplets. These silent, blue-white flashes temporarily transform the water vapor into brief, geometric patterns of plasma that float in the thin air before dissipating.

The entire plateau appears to be slowly rising, millimeter by millimeter, day by day - a new mountain range being born, but one formed of salt rather than stone. The process creates deep harmonic vibrations that can be felt through the soles of one's feet, a constant reminder that this vast crystalline landscape is very much alive and growing.
