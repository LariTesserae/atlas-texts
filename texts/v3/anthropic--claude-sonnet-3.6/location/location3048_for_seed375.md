A vast plain of black glass stretches to a perpetually smoke-stained horizon. This is not natural obsidian, but rather the fused remains of an ancient sand sea, transformed in some forgotten catastrophe. The surface isn't smooth - it's a dense maze of geometric fractures and upthrust shards, forming a crystalline cityscape that emerged from the cooling process itself.

The air shimmers with relentless heat, but it's the sound that dominates everything - a constant, overwhelming resonance as the glass field sings. The countless fracture planes and crystal faces catch the dim, copper-colored light and vibrate against each other, producing a deafening chorus of pure tones that never varies or ceases. The sound is so intense it manifests as visible ripples in the hot air, creating interference patterns that dance across the glassy landscape.

Sparse vegetation manages to persist here, taking the form of metallic-looking growths that seem more mineral than plant. These "crystal flowers" grow in the deepest cracks, their structures perfectly tuned to the landscape's endless song. Their translucent, faceted forms catch and redirect both light and sound, contributing their own harmonic overtones to the glass-field's chorus.

The settlement here is as geometric as the landscape that birthed it. The people of the glass plains build by carefully breaking and repositioning the existing crystal formations, creating hollow spaces within the natural architecture of the cooling fractures. Their tools are simple - wooden mallets wrapped in thick felt, bronze chisels dulled to prevent sharp sounds that would clash with the landscape's tone, copper wire for measuring and marking perfect angles.

Their buildings are impossible to distinguish from the natural formations unless you know exactly what to look for: slightly more regular angles, carefully positioned light-wells, and paths that wind through the matrix of black glass in ways that subtly favor human movement. The entire settlement exists in a sort of crystalline continuity with its surroundings, more grown than built.

The dim light filters down through layers of smoke that never clear - some say they're left over from whatever ancient cataclysm created this place, others insist they're produced by the endless vibration of the glass itself. The light that does make it through is split and reflected countless times, creating pools of colored illumination that shift position as the smoke layers drift overhead, though the fundamental song of the place never changes.

No animals survive here - the constant resonance disrupts their nervous systems. Even the humans who live here develop a particular kind of deafness, losing the ability to hear certain frequencies while becoming preternaturally sensitive to others. They communicate through a sign language that incorporates the rhythms of the glass-song, their gestures echoing the geometric patterns that surround them.

This is a place that exists in perfect, eternal stasis - the same notes ringing out forever, the same light filtering through unchanging smoke, the same crystal flowers catching and redirecting the endless vibrations. Even the human settlements seem to freeze in time, their modifications to the landscape becoming indistinguishable from the original formations within a generation.
