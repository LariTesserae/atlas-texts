In the steep-walled throat of a newborn mountain, there is a city being born. Not built - born. The rock itself is extruding structures, pushing up through layers of gleaming micaceous schist like teeth through gums. The process is nearly silent but relentless: crystalline geometries emerge dripping with mineral-rich waters, their edges still soft enough to be shaped by careful hands.

The inhabitants - few, scattered, knowing - live in these growing spaces, guiding the mineral emergence with wooden tools and copper wire. They speak in whispers and work in darkness, their movements lit only by the dim bioluminescence of cave moss and the occasional glint of mica. The darkness is necessary; too much light causes the emerging structures to harden too quickly, becoming brittle and unusable.

Water is everywhere but hidden, moving through the rock in countless capillary channels. It seeps and weeps from walls, collecting in carefully carved channels that the shapers have integrated into their growing architecture. The sound of it is a constant susurration, a background pulse that seems to match the subtle rhythms of the mountain's growth.

The vegetation here is paradoxical - despite the darkness, plants thrive. They are strange, translucent things with leaves like wet paper, feeding on the mineral-rich waters and whatever light filters down from the narrow strip of sky visible far above. They grow in vertical gardens, their roots becoming part of the living architecture, strengthening and guiding the mineral growth.

The space feels intimate despite the towering height of the cavern walls - the inhabited areas are pocket-sized, connected by twisting passages that seem to shift and adjust themselves daily. The whole place is in constant, subtle motion: stairs extend themselves step by step, doorways widen or narrow, rooms slowly expand as their inhabitants need more space.

At times the entire structure shudders - these are the growing pains of the mountain itself, still pushing upward, still becoming. During these moments the mineral flows faster, and the shapers must work quickly to guide it, their hands moving in practiced patterns passed down through generations.

The few animals here are as strange as their environment - blind, pale things that swim through stone as if it were water, leaving trails of altered crystal in their wake. Sometimes their movements create new spaces, unexpected chambers that the shapers must quickly integrate into their plans.

The air is cool and thick with moisture, carrying the sharp smell of new-formed rock and the subtle sweetness of the cave moss. Every surface feels alive under the touch - not warm, exactly, but responsive, like sleeping flesh. This is a place of perpetual becoming, never quite finished, never quite stable, a city perpetually dawning into existence.

High above, through that narrow strip of sky, clouds can sometimes be seen racing past, hinting at fierce winds in the upper reaches of the mountain. But down here in the growing dark, in these intimate spaces of constant change, the only motion is the slow pulse of stone and water, and the careful work of those who guide its growth.
