A world-sea of liquid crystal, deep and vast as any ocean but with the molecular precision of a display screen. The liquid is warm and dense, more viscous than water, flowing in great continental currents that carry information as much as mass. When the currents collide, they generate vast interference patterns - geometric storms that propagate for thousands of miles, their edges dissolving into fractal branches.

The light here is intense, almost painful - not from above, but generated within the liquid itself. The crystal sea is self-illuminating, each molecular layer exciting the next in endless chains of phosphorescence. The light moves in waves and pulses, following mathematical rules that emerge from the fluid's own structure. Sometimes the patterns cohere into vast displays, like aurora spanning an entire hemisphere, before breaking apart into chaotic sparkle.

Where the currents slow, crystal seeds form - not organic growth, but something between computation and crystallography. They emerge as transparent geometric frameworks, glassy branches that begin to form lattices before the next surge of current dissolves them back into the flow. The process is constant, an endless cycle of emergence and dissolution, like a planet-sized chemical computer trying to solve itself.

The sound is overwhelming - not the muffled quiet of Earth's oceans, but a constant chorus of crystal chimes as the molecular structures form and break, overlapped with the deeper resonance of the continental currents. The frequencies shift with the density of the fluid, creating standing waves that can trap pockets of different viscosity for hours or days before releasing them back into the flow.

The few life forms here are more like specialized regions of the liquid itself - areas where the molecular patterns have achieved a kind of self-sustaining complexity. They move through the crystal sea as shifting geometries, leaving trails of altered information in the fluid's structure. Some are vast but ephemeral, forming and dissolving over days; others are tiny but persistent, surviving in the boundaries between currents.

The whole system operates at an industrial scale of energy and complexity, but it's a technology that grew itself, that continues to grow and change without external control. The crystal sea is simultaneously ancient and newborn, each moment bringing novel patterns while following rules laid down in the first moments of its formation.

At the very bottom, where the pressure forces the liquid into a quasi-solid state, there are hints of more permanent structures forming - the first suggestions of a true crystalline basement that might, over geological time, become the foundation of a more stable world. But for now, the surface layers remain in their eternal dance of creation and dissolution, a global storm of light and geometry seeking some ultimate form it may never achieve.
