In the suspended aquarium-city of New Thessalonica, the perpetual drip of condensation from the cathedral-scale glass dome provides the only rhythm in an otherwise preternaturally quiet world. The city hangs in a vast cavern of dark water, protected by its crystalline shell - though "protected" may be the wrong word, as the barrier seems to exist as much to keep the city's inhabitants in as to keep the pressing ocean out.

The settlement consists of stacked rings of corroded copper and brass buildings, their green patina catching what little light filters down from far above. Industrial pipes and tubes connect everything, creating a dense circulatory system that maintains the delicate balance of oxygen, pressure, and waste processing that keeps the city alive. The buildings grow more cramped and twisted toward the center, like a nautilus shell compressing inward.

Through every window and portal, through every transparent section of the dome, they are visible: the creatures. An endless procession of marine life, pressing against the glass with apparent curiosity or hunger - it's impossible to tell which. They range from finger-sized bioluminescent organisms to vast, shadowy forms that pass slowly by in the darkness beyond. Some seem to be fish-adjacent, others defy any natural classification. Their presence is constant, overwhelming, like living wallpaper on every external surface.

The few plants that grow here are cultivated in vertical hydroponics chambers, pale and strange-looking things that have adapted to the perpetual twilight. They wind through the industrial framework like anemic vines, their leaves translucent enough to show the nutrient solution flowing through their veins.

The temperature inside remains consistently warm and humid, maintained by ancient climate systems whose hum has become so constant it's beneath notice. This technological heart of the city never falters - it can't afford to. The machinery that maintains pressure, processes oxygen, and filters water has achieved a kind of perfect stasis, running the same cycles it has for what might be centuries.

The city itself feels like it's underwater even though it isn't - every surface bears the marks of constant moisture, every metal is chosen for its resistance to corrosion, every corner hosts colonies of specialized fungi that help process the endless humidity. The architecture curves and flows like liquid, whether by design or by the slow reformation of materials under constant water exposure.

At the very center, in the densest part of the city, there's a structure that might be a temple or might be a machine - it's impossible to tell where one category ends and the other begins. Its surfaces ripple with patterns that mirror the movements of the creatures outside, though no one can remember if it was built that way or if the patterns developed over time.

The whole place exists in a perpetual twilight state, illuminated by a combination of bioluminescent creatures outside and pale, efficient lighting inside that seems calculated to maintain some long-forgotten circadian rhythm. The light never changes, the machines never falter, the creatures never stop their endless patrol of the exterior. New Thessalonica simply continues, suspended in its perfect moment of equilibrium, neither decaying nor growing, neither drowning nor truly dry.
