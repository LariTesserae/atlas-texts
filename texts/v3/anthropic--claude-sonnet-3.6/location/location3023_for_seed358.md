The terraces rise impossibly, kilometer after kilometer of intricately carved steps that could never have been built by any reasonable means. Each level is perfectly flat, perfectly maintained, despite the complete absence of workers or tools. The surfaces are a material that seems almost like nacre or mother-of-pearl, but stretched to architectural scale - iridescent, warm to the touch, faintly damp with perpetual condensation that never quite dries in the beating sun.

And everywhere, the birds. Or rather, the things that occupy the ecological niche of birds, though they follow none of the expected rules. They flow like liquid mercury through the air, splitting and rejoining, their bodies more suggestion than substance. Some are the size of hummingbirds, darting between the sparse crystalline growths that might be plants. Others span dozens of meters, their wing-shapes casting complex shadows that seem to move independently of the creatures casting them. Their calls range from subsonic vibrations that make the terraces hum in sympathy to piercing metallic songs that sound almost like data transmission.

The "plants" grow in precise geometric patterns, each exactly equidistant from its neighbors. Their translucent structures catch and fracture the intense sunlight, casting prismatic patterns that drift across the pearlescent surfaces even though the sun never moves from its fixed position overhead. They seem to be growing directly from the constructed surface rather than soil, their root systems visible through the semi-transparent material like frozen lightning.

The air is thick and hot, but not oppressive - it feels almost supportive, as if it has slightly more substance than normal atmosphere. Occasional clouds form and dissipate in moments, dropping brief showers of rain that beads up on the nacre surfaces before evaporating, leaving temporary patterns of darker and lighter regions that slowly fade back to uniformity.

The horizon is visible in all directions, the terraces extending beyond sight, but distance behaves strangely here. Walking toward the horizon doesn't seem to bring it any closer, yet somehow you can see details of structures that should be far too distant to make out. The mercury-birds flow between these impossible distances as if they're no distance at all.

There's a sense of vast age here, despite the fresh-carved appearance of every surface. Nothing erodes, nothing decays, nothing changes except the constant motion of the not-quite-birds and the cycling of the brief rains. The geometrical plants neither grow nor die, the sun neither rises nor sets. It's as if time itself has been polished to a perfect smoothness, with no purchase for change to grip.

The sound is constant - the hum of wings, the crystalline songs, the occasional patter of rain, all overlapping into a complex harmony that seems just on the edge of resolving into language. But it never quite does, remaining eternally in that moment of almost-understanding, like a word on the tip of the tongue.
