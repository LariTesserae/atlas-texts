A crystalline geode chamber grows before your eyes, its walls pushing outward with audible groans and sharp crackles. This hidden pocket within ancient volcanic rock is barely larger than a small room, but it churns with metamorphic energy. The air is thick and warm, carrying the scent of hot stone and mineral vapors.

Along the curved ceiling, hexagonal quartz columns emerge like teeth, extending downward millimeter by millimeter. Their growth creates a constant high-pitched singing, a choir of mineral friction that echoes in the confined space. When they occasionally snap under their own weight, the sharp reports sound like gunshots.

The floor is a shifting mosaic of geometric plates, some rising while others sink, creating an ever-changing topology of platforms and depressions. Glowing veins of red-orange material – perhaps iron-rich deposits catching light from above – trace the boundaries between these plates, making the whole chamber look like a living circuit board.

Small, hardy lichens cling to the more stable sections of wall, forming abstract patterns in muted greys and pale greens. They seem to migrate slowly across the surface, abandoning areas of high activity for more settled regions. In the warmer crevices, colonies of tiny, translucent arthropods make their homes, scurrying away when the rock faces they inhabit begin to shift.

Near the chamber's center, a lone structure stands: a weathered stone pillar, clearly artificial, its surface carved with spiraling glyphs now half-obscured by mineral deposits. The geode seems to have formed around it, or perhaps it was deliberately placed here long ago to witness this perpetual act of geological creation.

The air grows warmer by degrees as new crystal faces emerge. Occasionally, pockets of trapped gas escape with sharp hisses through tiny fissures, releasing brief puffs of heated air that carry the taste of metal. The entire space feels like a furnace, not in its intensity, but in its steady, inexorable process of transformation.

The few plants that have found their way here are strange, stunted things, their roots threading through crystal seams, their leaves thick and waxy. They appear to be thriving despite – or perhaps because of – the constant change, having adapted to grip the moving stone and absorb moisture from the warm air.

This is a place of becoming, a geological nursery hidden within the mountain's flesh. Its creation song echoes continuously off the faceted walls, a percussion of growth, decay, and transformation played out in crystalline frequencies.
