The condensation farmers work in the deep galleries, their harvest-frames stretched between the ancient pillars. Each frame, a delicate lattice of copper and silver mesh, draws moisture from the perpetually-humid air. The water doesn't fall here – it hangs, it drifts, it accumulates in pregnant globules that defy gravity's simple rules.

This is Dewcatcher's Realm, a vast network of artificial caverns carved not down into earth but sideways into the face of what was once a seaside cliff. The ocean withdrew centuries ago, leaving behind these chambered spaces where the boundary between air and water never fully resolved itself. The galleries extend for kilometers, connected by slick-walled corridors that occasionally spiral into vast rotundas where maintenance dirigibles dock, their envelopes swollen with the day's water harvest.

The surfaces here are alive with bioluminescent films – not planted but emerged, a symbiosis of fungal and bacterial colonies that paint every surface in shifting patterns of blue-green light. They feed on the peculiar chemistry of the stone itself, which seems to sweat continuous beads of mineral-rich moisture. The light they cast is sufficient for navigation but creates more shadows than it banishes, leaving the higher reaches of the galleries in perpetual twilight.

Between the harvest-frames, the space teems with the descendants of sea life that adapted to this hybrid environment. Schools of air-swimming creatures, translucent and no larger than a hand, navigate in tight formation through the humid atmosphere. They feed on the microscopic droplets and the abundant microbial life suspended in the air. Occasionally, larger predators – ribbon-like things with gossamer fins – drift through, causing the smaller creatures to scatter in synchronized pulses of panic.

The human presence is everywhere but concentrated in the infrastructure nodes – pumping stations that throb with the rhythm of massive steam engines, processing centers where the harvested water is filtered through layers of engineered substrate to remove the peculiar properties it accumulates in this place. Workers move between these stations on elevated walkways of perforated metal, their boots ringing against the grating. The sound mingles with the constant drip and patter of water, the hum of machinery, and the keening of the air-swimmers to create a cathedral choir of industrial-organic harmony.

In the deeper galleries, where the harvest-frames are older and the bioluminescence grows in different hues, there are places where the water begins to organize itself into forms that mimic the creatures swimming through it. These manifestations are carefully monitored by the Hydrology Priests, who wear suits of brass and glass and take readings with instruments that measure properties for which there are no names in surface-dwelling languages.

The whole complex is in constant motion – water migrating, creatures swimming, machines pumping, workers tending their frames. Yet there's an underlying pattern to it all, like a tide that moves in three dimensions, or a breath taken by something far too large to comprehend. The few plants that grow here have adapted to take their moisture directly from the air, developing transparent tissues that seem to merge with the omnipresent humidity.

Near the processing centers, there are areas where the harvested water is temporarily stored in spherical tanks of polished copper. These tanks have developed their own ecologies – colonies of specialized air-swimmers that have learned to feed on the energy gradients around the warm metal, and patches of crystalline growth that the maintenance crews carefully remove before they can spread to the tanks' internal mechanisms.

The deeper one goes into the galleries, the stranger the physics of water becomes, until in the furthest chambers, droplets can be found that fall upward or hover in perfect spheres, untouched by gravity or air currents. These are left undisturbed, marked with warning signs in three different symbolic languages, their meaning clear even to those who cannot read: here is where our understanding ends, and the true nature of Dewcatcher's Realm begins.
