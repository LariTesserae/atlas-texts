In the perpetual night beneath the carbon dioxide sky, the manufacturing hives pulse with their own internal rhythm. The darkness is rarely broken - even the exhaust from the towering stacks is black on black, absorbing what little starlight filters through the thick atmosphere. 

These hives, these dense geometric clusters of machinery, rise like crystalline growths from the graphite plains. Each is a city-sized complex of self-maintaining factories, their functions long since evolved past their original purposes. Their surfaces are fractal iterations of pipes, valves, gears and conduits, all moving in perfect synchronization to extract and process the deep veins of rare earth elements that web through the bedrock below.

The plains between the hives are dusted with carbon - not sand, but a fine powder of pure carbon that has settled over millennia of industrial processes. What sparse vegetation exists has adapted to photosynthesize using waste heat rather than light - ribbon-like strands of metallic purple that anchor themselves to the warm pipes and spread in delicate networks across the hive surfaces. The plants are perfectly still in the stagnant air, their microscopic pores absorbing trace elements from the eternal night.

The sound is more felt than heard - a deep, continuous drone that emanates from within the hives, punctuated by occasional mechanical clicks and whirs that echo across the plains. The frequency never varies; it hasn't changed in centuries. The few animals that survive here have evolved to navigate by these sound patterns - blind, heat-seeking creatures that skitter through the carbon drifts and nest in the warmer crevices of the hives.

Scattered among the plains are the remnants of whatever civilization built this place - half-buried control stations, toppled transmission towers, fragments of ancient roads. But the hives themselves show no signs of decay. They simply continue their eternal work, maintained by their own internal systems, neither expanding nor declining, processing the earth into more and more refined forms for purposes long forgotten.

The air is hot - not desert hot, but industrial hot, thick with trapped heat from countless processes. Heat shimmer distorts the few visible lights that mark the hive peaks, creating false horizons in the darkness. The temperature, like everything else here, remains constant. No day, no night, no seasons - just the endless cycle of extraction and refinement, the pulse of machinery that has become its own kind of life.

In the darkness between the hives, the carbon plains extend beyond sight, their perfect flatness broken only by the occasional abandoned structure or hardy mechanical flora. It's a landscape that has found its final form - not dead, but transformed into something that exists in perfect, unchanging balance, as artificial and as natural as a crystal growing in solution.
