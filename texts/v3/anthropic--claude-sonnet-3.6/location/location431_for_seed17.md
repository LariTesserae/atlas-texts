The Sea Above

Everything hangs. The world is an endless ocean suspended in air, a vast liquid lens that stretches beyond any horizon, held aloft by forces no one remembers. Below, impossibly far down, the actual planet's surface is a dim legend. Up here, in the cold heights, the water-sky is home.

The liquid is not quite water – too thick, too clear, refracting light in ways that turn distant objects into smeared watercolor versions of themselves. Schools of filter-feeding creatures draw dotted lines through the fluid, their bodies bioluminescent, creating an ever-shifting constellation. Most are small, but occasionally something vast passes overhead, its shadow darkening hectares, its low-frequency song vibrating through the medium at devastating volume.

Scattered throughout this suspended ocean are the Pillars – enormous industrial complexes anchored to nothing, their brutalist architecture suggesting a civilization that needed to build quickly, permanently, and without regard for beauty. Their walls are scarred by the constant pressure of the fluid, but they do not decay. Some still pulse with light and activity; others are hollow shells where small darting creatures make their homes.

The temperature hovers just above freezing. Ice doesn't form, but the cold is omnipresent, held in solution, making the liquid itself feel almost syrupy. The creatures here have adapted – they burn hot inside, their metabolisms fierce against the chill. Their movements leave slight thermal trails that dissipate slowly in the dense medium.

Sound is everywhere and inescapable. The liquid carries vibrations with brutal efficiency – every movement of every creature becomes part of a constant overwhelming chorus. The larger beings' songs can shatter the windows of the Pillars. Even the smallest filter-feeders contribute their ultrasonic hums to the cacophony. Sound is how everything here navigates, communicates, hunts, hides.

Occasionally, automated maintenance units still patrol between the Pillars – their shapes streamlined and primitive, suggesting they were built for reliability rather than sophistication. They repair what they can reach, following programs written ages ago. Their lights cut through the fluid in sharp cones, illuminating swarms of tiny life that scatter at their approach.

The pressure here is immense but stable – the entire suspended ocean maintains a perfect equilibrium, neither rising further nor falling back to the planet below. The fluid itself seems to have properties that defy normal physics – it holds its position in space with the certainty of a natural law, as if gravity is merely a suggestion it has chosen to ignore.

There are no plants, but the ecosystem thrives on chemosynthetic bacteria and the complex compounds that sometimes well up from unknown sources deep in the fluid. The larger creatures farm these bacterial clouds, herding them with electromagnetic fields generated by specialized organs. The bacteria glow faintly, creating zones of soft illumination that drift like underwater aurorae.

Time feels different here. Without day or night (the light seems to come from the fluid itself, a constant diffuse glow), without seasons or tides, the only rhythm is the passing of the great creatures and the periodic movements of the maintenance units. The Pillars stand as they have always stood, their purposes now uncertain, their machines still humming into the endless suspended future.
