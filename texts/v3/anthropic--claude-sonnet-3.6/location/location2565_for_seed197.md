In the flooded remains of what was once a small mountain town, schools of silvery fish dart through submerged streets. The water is remarkably clear here - not the murky green of most lakes, but a crystalline blue that lets sunlight penetrate deep into the drowned valley. The town sits in a natural bowl between rising hills, now transformed into a contained aquatic world roughly a mile across.

The buildings remain largely intact, their stone and brick walls softened by a fine dusting of pale mineral deposits rather than the usual algae or water plants. No vegetation grows here - something in the mineral-rich water prevents it - but the structures are draped with delicate lattices of white crystal that grow with geological patience, transforming the human architecture into something that resembles a cave formation.

The water is cool but not cold, moving in slow currents that follow the old street grid. These gentle flows carry swarms of small, translucent creatures unlike typical fish - their bodies seem to fold through dimensions that shouldn't exist, origami-like, as they navigate the underwater townscape. Larger forms move through the deeper areas, their shapes suggesting sea mammals but their movements too precise, too geometric.

At the town center, the old church tower still reaches toward the surface, its clock face now a perfect circle of opalescent crystal. The town square has become a gathering place for the larger aquatic creatures, who move in slow, ritualistic patterns around what was once a fountain. Their movements seem purposeful, perhaps even ceremonial, though no human logic could decipher their meaning.

The ambient sound is minimal - mostly the soft resonance of water moving through stone archways and window frames. Occasionally there's a crystalline chiming when schools of the origami-fish brush against the mineral formations, creating brief harmonies that fade into the aquatic silence.

The whole scene is lit by normal daylight from above, but it refracts through the water in unusual ways, creating shifting patterns that seem to follow rules different from standard optics. Shadows move when they shouldn't, and sometimes light bends around corners as if following the paths of the strange fish.

What's most striking is how the flooding feels less like a disaster and more like a transformation - as if the water didn't drown the town but rather translated it into a new language. The basic infrastructure remains, but it's been repurposed by these geometric sea-creatures into something that serves their own incomprehensible society. They've maintained the buildings using primitive tools visible in their fins or appendages - simple implements for clearing debris and encouraging crystal growth - but their methods seem to work with the water rather than against it.

The border between what was built by humans and what has been modified by the current inhabitants is increasingly unclear. New structures, if they can be called that, grow in the spaces between buildings - crystalline formations that might be homes or meeting places or something else entirely, their geometry just slightly wrong for human eyes to parse comfortably.
