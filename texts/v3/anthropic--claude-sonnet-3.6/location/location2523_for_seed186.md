A hollow in the high rock, where the air shimmers not with heat but with something stranger. The chamber formed over millions of years as water wore through limestone, though now it seems impossible that liquid ever flowed here. The walls gleam with a greasy iridescence that suggests moisture but feels dry and fever-warm to the touch.

The space is cathedral-sized but feels smaller, more intimate, as if the very geometry of the cavern is gently folded in on itself. What little light exists seems to pool in impossible corners, casting shadows that move independent of any source. The rock itself occasionally shifts spectrum, bleeding from limestone-white to obsidian-black in slow pulses that take hours to complete.

Near the center, a formation that might be a stalagmite hums at the edge of hearing. The sound changes pitch so gradually that the transition is only noticeable after leaving and returning days later. The vibration attracts small, translucent creatures that might be crickets, might be crystalline growths - they flicker in and out of existence, leaving ghost-trails in the air that slowly decay like old photographs.

Sparse vegetation clings to the walls: pale, leafless vines that grow in Möbius strips, feeding not on light but on the pocket universes that occasionally bubble up through hairline cracks in reality. These bubbles release tiny puffs of foreign atmosphere - sometimes argon-purple, sometimes a color that exists only in mathematical equations.

The temperature is crushing, yet there's no convection, no movement of air. Heat simply exists as a property of space itself, like a fever dream made solid. What little moisture remains has been transformed - not evaporated, but shifted sideways into some adjacent possibility of water. It beads on surfaces in perfect spheres that, when watched long enough, reveal infinite recursive depths.

In one corner, a patch of wall flickers like an old television set, revealing glimpses of industrial machinery that seems to exist in a perpendicular dimension. The sound of distant factory work occasionally bleeds through - metallic clanks and steam releases - but muffled, as if heard through miles of solid rock.

The whole space feels like a forgotten anatomical feature of the mountain, a lymph node in the body of the earth where natural laws go to molt and regenerate. Time moves strangely here - not necessarily slower or faster, but perhaps more sideways, or in loops, or in shapes that human consciousness slides off of like water from wax.

What few living things make their home here have evolved, or perhaps devolved, into forms that barely qualify as biological. They cast shadows in spectrums that don't exist, leave trails of probability rather than mucus, and their lifecycles might take centuries or microseconds - it's impossible to tell when observation itself becomes part of their existence.

The erosion that shaped this space continues, but it's no longer mere water wearing at mere rock. Reality itself is being slowly polished smooth, worn down by the friction of overlapping possibilities, creating geometries that can only be perceived through their after-images in the mind's eye.
