A sunken marketplace carved from living stone, where water perpetually weeps down obsidian walls polished by generations of hands and shoulders brushing past. The passages are narrow - in most places only two can walk abreast - and they wind like river channels between stalls and dwellings cut directly into the rock. The ceiling is low, rarely more than twice human height, creating an intimate closeness that amplifies every sound: the constant drip and trickle of water, the echo of footsteps, the overlapping voices of vendors and residents that bounce and reverberate until individual words become an organic drone.

The stone itself seems almost alive with moisture - not just the obvious rivulets that trace established paths down the walls, but a persistent seepage that makes every surface gleam in the steady light from oil lamps mounted in carved niches. These lamps are carefully maintained, their small flames reflected and multiplied by the wet stone until the whole space glows with a warm, diffuse light that never changes.

Where the water pools on the ground, it carries a fine silt that has, over years, been worked into intricate patterns by countless passing feet. These patterns, though constantly renewed by fresh sediment, never truly change - the same paths are walked every day, the same conversations held, the same gestures made. The residents have adapted to the perpetual dampness: their leather goods are treated with wax, their clothes are close-fitting, their tools kept in oiled wrappings.

The few animals here are purely domestic - cats that keep the vermin at bay, and occasional mules bringing goods to market, their hooves loud on the stone floors. No plants grow in this mineral world, though the walls sometimes develop a slick biofilm that must be regularly scrubbed away by the residents.

The architecture is organic but intentional - each space flows into the next with the logic of water finding its path, but also shows the marks of careful tool work, of chambers expanded and refined over generations. Storage alcoves are carved high into walls, accessed by permanent stone ladders. Sleeping quarters are set above the water's reach, while market stalls occupy the lower levels where the constant moisture keeps perishable goods cool.

The soundscape is dense and constant: water dripping, feet scraping, voices calling, tools tapping against stone, all amplified and transformed by the rock surfaces until it becomes a single complex atmosphere of human activity. The acoustics make whispered conversations impossible - everyone speaks in clear, carrying voices that contribute to the general chorus.

This is a place of eternal present moment, where the same patterns of life repeat day after day, where the water has always flowed and always will flow, where the lamps have never gone out and never will. The stone remembers every touch in its polish, every voice in its echoes, every footstep in its worn patterns, creating a living record of countless identical days.
