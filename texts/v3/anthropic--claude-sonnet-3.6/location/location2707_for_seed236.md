In the liquid towers of New Bathys, the water-pressure never settles. It pulses and thrums with the heartbeat of the thermal vents far below, where the city's foundations merge with the extinct volcano that birthed these heights. The water here is thick - not quite liquid, not quite gel - enriched with suspended metallic particles that catch what little light filters down from the distant surface, creating swirling auroras of bronze and copper in the perpetual twilight.

The towers themselves seem to grow rather than being built, their surfaces a maze of crystalline formations that emerge from the volcanic glass core. The ancient settlers who first came here learned to guide these growths using carefully placed mineral seeds and crude but effective pressure differentials. Now, generations later, their descendants inhabit these living structures that continue to expand upward through the dense water-medium, reaching impossible heights that would collapse under their own weight in air.

The inhabitants move through their vertical world in schools, like fish, but their bodies have begun to change. Their skin has taken on the translucent quality of deep-sea creatures, and their movements have become languorous and deliberate in the resistant medium. They communicate through patterns of bioluminescence, a technology of the body that evolved alongside their primitive tools - mostly pressure-worked volcanic glass and minerals harvested from the vent fields.

At certain elevations, the water becomes so dense with suspended particles that it forms distinct layers, like strata in rock. Some of these layers harbor vast colonies of creatures that seem to exist between states of matter - neither solid nor liquid, their bodies incorporating the metallic particles in ever-shifting geometries. These beings pay little attention to the human inhabitants, instead engaging in vast, slow dances whose purpose remains mysterious.

The boundaries between architecture and organism have grown indistinct. What appears to be a doorway might pulse and shift its shape as you approach. Windows aren't carved but grown, their transparent surfaces formed by colonies of crystal-producing organisms that the first settlers brought with them and which have since evolved into something else entirely.

The highest levels of New Bathys pierce into layers of water so pressurized that physics begins to break down. Here, time moves differently, and light bends in impossible ways. The few who venture this high speak of seeing multiple versions of themselves performing tasks they haven't yet begun, or watching buildings grow old and crumble while simultaneously being born from the volcanic glass.

Sound travels strangely through the graduated densities of water - sometimes carrying conversations from kilometers away with perfect clarity, other times absorbing all noise into profound silence. The overall effect is one of whispers and echoes, punctuated by the deep, irregular pulse of the thermal vents that keeps the whole ecosystem in constant, gentle motion.

In the darkest reaches, where the pressure makes light behave like sound and sound like solid matter, the original nature of reality becomes uncertain. Some say this is where the water itself begins to think, and that the strange geometries of the highest towers are not built or grown at all, but dreamed into being by the conscious ocean.
