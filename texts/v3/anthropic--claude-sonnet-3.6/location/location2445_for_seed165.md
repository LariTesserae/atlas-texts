The Glass Mountains sing. Not through wind - there is little wind here - but through the constant micro-movements of the impossibly tall crystalline peaks as they grow, molecule by molecule, driven upward by some deep geological pressure. Their song is barely audible: a high, pure tone at the edge of hearing, punctuated by occasional clear pings as new facets form.

The mountains rise from a vast plateau of smooth obsidian, their bases disappearing into the perpetual shallow lake that covers the plain - never more than ankle-deep, perfectly clear, and warm as blood. The water doesn't pool or flow but rather seems to exist as a uniform layer, defying normal hydraulics. Tiny ripples dance across its surface, moved by forces unseen.

In this liquid membrane live the Swimmers - thousands upon thousands of flat, translucent creatures no thicker than paper and ranging in size from a fingernail to a dinner plate. They glide through the shallow water in complex patterns, their edges ruffling with cilial movement. When they pass beneath the intense light of the three suns, they flash like mirrors, creating an ever-changing cascade of reflections that bounce between the water's surface and the faceted mountain faces.

Higher up, where the mountains narrow to impossible spires that seem to pierce the white sky, the Crystal Weavers make their homes. These creatures - perhaps better described as phenomena - appear as shifting knots of light that spin silica threads between the peaks, creating ephemeral bridges and nets that glitter briefly before dissolving back into the air. Their work never ceases, but neither does it accumulate; it is a constant cycle of creation and dissolution.

At seemingly random intervals, new spires burst from the obsidian plain in a process that takes mere hours - a geological event playing out at biological speed. The process is silent but violent, sending waves through the shallow lake that cause the Swimmers to scatter in hypnotic geometric formations. The new spires emerge already faceted, already singing their crystalline songs.

Scattered across the plain are the remnants of what might have been an attempt at habitation: crude structures of stacked obsidian blocks, now half-submerged in the warm water. They appear to have been abandoned long ago, though it's difficult to judge time in a place where nothing erodes and dust never settles. Simple tools can still be found in these ruins - hammers of dense crystal, cutting edges of volcanic glass - arranged in ways that suggest their users had more than two hands.

The light is constant and comes from all directions, despite the three suns, as if the air itself is luminescent. This creates an environment nearly devoid of shadows, making depth and distance difficult to judge. The only darkness exists in the brief moments when many Swimmers overlap, their transparent bodies finally building up enough layers to block the relentless brightness.

There's a mathematical precision to everything here that suggests not nature but algorithm - yet it's an algorithm with chaos built into its core, producing endless variation within its rigid parameters. The place feels less like a landscape and more like a visualization of some vast computation, using light and crystal and living glass to calculate answers to questions too alien to comprehend.
