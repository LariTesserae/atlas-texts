A vast city of salt and bone stretches across the high plateau, its white spires catching the merciless light of three suns. The structures appear almost grown rather than built - as if the minerals themselves had been coaxed upward by ancient hands working with nothing more than cord and copper tools, shaping the raw crystal into apartments and archives and temples that reach toward the pale lavender sky.

The city is alive, though not with anything you'd call life. Crystalline entities drift between the towers like geometric snowflakes, their edges precise as mathematics. They cast complex shadows that seem to move independently of their sources. In places, these shadow-patterns have worn grooves into the salt-stone streets through some process that defies normal physics.

The air is thin and bitter cold, yet perfectly still. Sound doesn't travel normally here - voices become visible ripples in the air that slowly spiral upward until they fade from sight. The silence is profound enough to feel like pressure against the skin.

Vast flocks of what might be birds (or might be living fractals, or might be holes in space wearing the memory of birds) wheel between the spires in perfect geometric formations. Their movements seem to follow rules too complex to grasp but too precise to be random. They leave trails in the air - not contrails, but temporary distortions in the fabric of space itself, like creases in paper that slowly smooth themselves out.

The ground beneath the city is a plain of interlocked hexagonal tiles, each several kilometers across, their edges raised slightly like the walls of shallow dishes. In some, ancient salt-patterns spread in slow spirals. In others, colonies of crystalline "plants" chime softly as they grow, though they're clearly not organic life as we know it. The tiles seem to shift positions over periods of weeks or months, reorganizing the city above them according to some vast mathematical plan.

Here and there, primitive tools lie abandoned - copper hammers, bone chisels, woven baskets of reed-like mineral fibers. They seem both ancient and freshly-used, as if time itself is uncertain here. Some appear to be slowly transforming into more of the geometric entities, while others seem to be tools that those entities themselves once used, impossibly worn by hands that never existed in physical space.

At the edges of vision, the city's architecture begins to break down into pure geometry, then into mathematical equations made solid, then into structures that the mind refuses to resolve into coherent shapes. Distance itself becomes uncertain - a tower that appears close might take days to reach, while one that seems far away might be accessible in a single step.

The overwhelming presence of the geometric entities makes the city feel simultaneously crowded and empty - they fill every space but have no physical substance, like a city populated entirely by living theorems. Their movements create patterns that slowly modify the structure of reality itself, wearing new paths through spacetime that manifest as crystalline archways and spiral ramps that may or may not have existed the day before.

In the center of it all stands what might be a temple or might be a theorem made manifest - a structure that seems to be actively calculating something vast and slow and incomprehensible, its calculations expressed in the very architecture of space around it. The entities flow through and around it in complex patterns, adding their own variables to whatever cosmic equation is being solved here in this high, cold, impossibly vast city at the edge of comprehensible reality.
