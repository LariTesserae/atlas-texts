A drowned engine room, long ago given over to life. The ceiling sweats constantly - not drips, but a steady silver sheen that runs down curved walls in mathematical patterns. The water that pools on the floor is only ankle-deep but feels deeper, its surface tension holding like a membrane against your steps. Beneath it, a maze of pipes and conduits crawls across the floor, their original purpose lost but now serving as highways for iridescent amphibians and articulated creatures that might once have been crustaceans.

The air is thick and warm, carrying both the mineral taste of rust and the organic musk of the moss that has colonized every horizontal surface. This moss glows - not brightly, but with a steady phosphorescence that creates overlapping pools of soft blue-green light. It's unclear whether this is natural bioluminescence or some reaction with the ancient machinery it covers.

The room's original dimensions are hard to gauge. Columns of unknown purpose rise from the water at regular intervals, their surfaces now serving as vertical gardens for climbing vines that seem to pulse slowly, like blood vessels. Between these columns, tangles of cables and smaller pipes create a dense canopy overhead, from which hang translucent stalactites that might be mineral deposits or might be living things - they seem to shift position when viewed over hours, but never while being watched.

The sound is a constant presence: the deep, almost subsonic thrum of some still-functioning machinery buried in the walls, overlaid with the liquid echoes of water moving through hidden spaces, and punctuated by the calls of the room's inhabitants - chirping things, clicking things, things that make sounds like steel being bent very slowly.

These creatures are everywhere, though many are barely visible: thumbnail-sized gastropods with shells that look like miniature circuit boards, blind fish that navigate by tasting electromagnetic fields, colonies of something that might be coral or might be crystals growing in patterns that suggest binary code. Most numerous are the thumb-sized amphibians, their skin a perfect mirror-finish that makes them seem to disappear and reappear as they move through the reflected light of the moss.

In one corner, a control panel still blinks with incomprehensible patterns, its surface covered in a film of living material that seems to respond to the lights, creating ripples of bioluminescence that spread out across the nearby walls. The panel's rhythmic clicking sounds almost like language, but not quite.

The air moves in slow currents, carrying spores or seeds that catch the light like tiny stars. They gather in corners, forming drifts that sometimes spark with static electricity. The overall feeling is of a place that has found a new equilibrium - no longer what it was built to be, but perfect for what it has become.
