The condensation tanks rise like brass cathedral spires, their surfaces constantly beaded with moisture from the warm, thick air. This is a harvesting district - one of many identical sectors where the city draws up the endless groundwater that saturates the porous rock beneath. The tanks' intricate pipework creates narrow passages between them, just wide enough for maintenance crews and the ever-present water skinks that have made this artificial ecosystem their home.

The skinks are everywhere - iridescent, semi-translucent creatures that seem more liquid than flesh, flowing through gaps in the machinery and lapping at the perpetual dampness. They range from finger-length to forearm-size, their internal organs visible as darker shadows through their gel-like bodies. Their soft chirping echoes off the metal surfaces, mixing with the steady thrum of pumps and the occasional hiss of releasing pressure valves.

The ground is a masterwork of engineering rather than nature - layers of permeable stone and channeled metalwork that guide the underground waters up through capillary action. What little light makes it through the perpetual industrial haze catches on the wet surfaces, creating a dim, shifting array of reflections that dance across the corroded copper and brass. The air tastes of minerals - iron, copper, calcium - carried up from deep below.

There's a precise order to everything here, unchanged for generations. The tanks and pipes follow mathematical patterns, their placement optimized for maximum water collection and minimum energy expenditure. The maintenance paths trace hexagonal patterns between them, each intersection marked with gauges and control panels that glow with a soft blue light.

The warmth from below meets the slightly cooler air above, creating permanent ribbons of mist that wind between the tanks. These fog-rivers have their own ecosystem - colonies of tiny floating creatures that feed on mineral particles in the water vapor, their bodies giving off a faint bioluminescence that adds another layer to the complex play of light and shadow.

Above, the network of collection channels carries the harvested water toward the city proper, creating a low but constant background roar. The sound mingles with the mechanical drone of pumps and the countless small noises of the skink colonies. Despite all this activity, there's a sense of absolute stability - this place operates like a single vast machine, its rhythms as regular as a heartbeat, its patterns unchanged since its construction.

The entire district is perhaps only the size of a small neighborhood, but its vertical complexity makes it feel like a world unto itself. Every surface that could conceivably hold water does, and the boundary between deliberate engineering and opportunistic adaptation has long since blurred, as the various creatures that have made this place home have modified it in subtle ways over the years, their bodies and behaviors becoming as much a part of the water collection system as the tanks and pipes themselves.
