A vast field of black moss stretches across the curved horizon, its surface rippling in patterns that seem too regular to be natural. This is the Carbon Garden - though "garden" is a human imposition of scale on something that spans continents. The moss isn't moss at all, but rather a dense network of carbon filaments that somehow maintain their structure in the thin atmosphere, growing in geometric fractals that repeat every few kilometers.

The darkness is near-total, broken only by the dim phosphorescence of the pseudo-moss and occasional arcs of static electricity that leap between the highest filaments. These electrical discharges produce a constant background drone, a deep resonant hum that rises and falls in long cycles. When the larger arcs occur, they trigger cascading harmonics through the carbon structures, creating cathedral-like choruses that echo across the empty plains.

The entire ecosystem runs on electrical energy rather than photosynthesis. Massive electromagnetic induction currents flow through the planet's crust, and the carbon structures have evolved - or perhaps were engineered - to tap directly into this power source. The "plants" are actually more like living transmission towers, sharing energy through their networked connections.

Despite the cool temperature and high elevation, there's no ice or snow - the electrical activity maintains a perfectly regulated thermal equilibrium that prevents any moisture from condensing, if there were any to begin with. The atmosphere is thin but stable, composed mainly of noble gases that light up in subtle colors when excited by the stronger electrical discharges.

The carbon structures maintain themselves with machine-like precision, breaking down and rebuilding their own components in a perfect steady state that might have been running for millions of years. Dead filaments are quickly absorbed and recycled, their carbon atoms reorganized into the same endlessly repeating patterns. The whole system operates like a planetary-scale circuit board, processing energy flows too subtle to measure.

Looking closer, the "empty" spaces between the carbon frameworks aren't empty at all - they're filled with almost invisible networks of even finer filaments, creating a hierarchical structure that extends from the kilometer-scale patterns down to the microscopic. The hum of energy moving through these networks creates standing wave patterns in the thin air, making certain areas effectively impassable due to resonant frequencies that can shatter ordinary matter.

This is a place that operates entirely by its own rules - not quite alive in any conventional sense, not quite machine, maintaining a perfect equilibrium that makes it seem almost frozen in time despite the constant movement of energy through its systems. The darkness and the drone create an atmosphere of industrial meditation, as if the entire planet is a single vast processor running an eternal program we can't begin to comprehend.
