The migration never ends because it has nowhere to end to. In the perpetual dusk of the red dwarf's light, the vast herd of crystalline beings flows like a living river across the silicon plains. They are called the Lattice - though no one remains who named them that - and their prismatic bodies catch what little light exists, fracturing it into countless recursive patterns that dance across the ground.

The plain itself is perfectly flat, an expanse of self-repairing metamaterial that extends beyond any meaningful horizon. Its surface has the muted gleam of oxidized aluminum, interrupted only by occasional spires of impossible geometry that pierce upward at mathematical intervals. These towers, if that's what they are, seem to serve as waypoints or perhaps navigational markers for the Lattice, though they never pause in their endless circuit.

The air is hot and still, thick with suspended particulates that give the dim light a granular quality. There is no wind because there is no temperature differential to drive it - the plain maintains a precise 318.15 Kelvin everywhere, always. The few organic plants that manage to exist here have evolved to tap directly into the plain's subsurface energy grid, sending down hair-thin filaments that glow a faint blue where they penetrate the surface.

Walking here feels like moving through honey - not because of any actual resistance, but because the plain subtly warps spacetime, creating micro-gravity wells that the Lattice somehow use for propulsion. Their movement across the surface creates sub-audible vibrations that can be felt through the soles of one's feet: a deep, steady pulse that never varies in rhythm.

The Lattice themselves are nearly invisible when still - which they never are - their crystalline bodies perfectly refracting light around themselves. In motion they become apparent as rippling distortions in space, like heat waves rising from hot pavement, but geometric and purposeful. They move in vast mathematical patterns, tens of thousands strong, solving equations with their choreography that would take supercomputers millennia to process.

Scattered across the plain are small geodesic structures that seem to phase in and out of existence depending on how they're observed. They might be maintenance stations for the plain's self-repair systems, or they might be observation posts left by whoever built this place. Or perhaps they're eggs of some kind, waiting for conditions that will never arise in this carefully maintained stasis.

The only sounds are the occasional crystalline chiming when members of the Lattice communicate with each other, and the faint hum of energy moving through the plain's subsurface grid. The horizon has the same color as the sky - a deep, burnt umber - making it impossible to tell where one ends and the other begins. 

Nothing here ever changes. The Lattice maintain their eternal migration, the plants slowly tap their energy, the spires stand their mathematical watch, and the plain continues its perfect function, preserving this pocket of ordered space in an otherwise chaotic universe. Whatever this place was built for, whatever purpose it serves, remains inscrutable - but it serves that purpose with unwavering precision, millennium after millennium.
