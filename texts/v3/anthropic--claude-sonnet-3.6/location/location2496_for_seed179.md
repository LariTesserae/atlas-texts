The basalt columns rise like countless black fingers from the warm mist, their hexagonal faces still glass-smooth from their recent emergence. What from below might look like a mountain reveals itself, at this height, as a vast geometric garden of stone prisms, extending to the horizon in a dark crystalline sea. The rock is new - so new it seems to hold a memory of molten heat, though its surface is cool and slick with perpetual condensation.

Between the columns, water moves with glacial patience. Not flowing exactly, but creeping - as if the liquid itself has partially crystallized, becoming viscous and strange. It forms perfect droplets that rise upward rather than fall, collecting in suspended pools that hover between the stone pillars. These pools catch and reflect the bright sunlight in ways that seem to bend the rules of optics, creating complex caustics that dance across the basalt faces without following any obvious source.

The few plants that have found purchase here seem to follow the water's strange behavior. Pale, translucent vines grow in slow-motion spirals, but they spiral downward from nothing, their ends dissolving into mist before reaching any surface. Small ferns with metallic-looking fronds emerge from the sides of columns, their leaves unfurling over weeks rather than hours, each one a perfect fractal that continues its pattern past what should be visible to the naked eye.

The air is thick with moisture but eerily still. Sound behaves strangely here - voices and movements are muffled to near-silence, yet occasionally a crystal-clear whisper might carry for impossible distances through the stone forest. The only consistent sound is a deep, subsonic hum that seems to emanate from the columns themselves, felt more than heard, like the echo of their birth from the earth's depths.

Time and space feel compressed in this place. What appears to be a few hundred meters away might take days to reach, while a seemingly distant formation could be arrived at in three steps. The horizon itself seems to curve wrongly, as if the landscape is folded through dimensions that can be sensed but not quite perceived.

At certain angles, in certain lights, the whole expanse appears to be a single enormous crystal, the columns merely facets of a structure that extends both above and below the visible realm. The water droplets hanging in space might be condensation, or they might be windows into the structure's interior, each one containing a recursive image of the entire formation.

The landscape is clearly active, clearly growing, but at a pace that makes plate tectonics seem hasty. New columns emerge over centuries, pushing up through the strange-gravity water, their surfaces unmarked by weather or wear. There's an overwhelming sense that this place is less a location and more a process - a slow-motion crystallization of reality itself, gradually converting normal space into something else entirely.

No birds wheel overhead, no insects hum in the wet air. The only movement comes from the creeping water, the slowly unfurling plants, and occasionally the subtle shift of a column adjusting its position by millimeters over months. The whole place feels like a geometrical meditation, a landscape trying to solve some vast mathematical equation with infinite patience.
