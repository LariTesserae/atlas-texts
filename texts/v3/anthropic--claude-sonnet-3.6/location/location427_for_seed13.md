A city spreads across a series of ascending plateaus, each level rising like broad stone steps into mountain mists. The structures are built from local slate and granite, stacked without mortar in intricate patterns that lock the stones together. These buildings cluster in concentric rings around central courtyards on each plateau, their steep-pitched roofs tiled with overlapping sheets of thin-split stone that shimmer with condensation in the cool, still air.

The plateaus are connected by wide spiral ramps that wrap around the mountainside, their edges marked by carefully-fitted stone railings worn smooth by generations of hands. Between the buildings, narrow channels are carved into the stone streets, collecting moisture that perpetually beads on the cool rock surfaces. These waterways converge into larger channels that cascade from level to level in carefully engineered falls, the soft sound of moving water ever-present but hushed.

Vegetation thrives in this environment of constant dampness. Moss carpets every horizontal surface not worn smooth by feet, and ferns sprout from between building stones. In the courtyards, gardeners tend to terraced beds where medicinal herbs and edible plants grow in spiral patterns. Vines with thick, waxy leaves climb the walls, their tendrils finding purchase in the unmortared gaps between stones.

The air is rich with life. Birds nest in the higher reaches of the buildings, their wings casting brief shadows in the clear light. Small lizards sun themselves on warm stones, while beetles with iridescent shells move purposefully along the moss-lined water channels. In protected corners, luminescent fungi cast a subtle glow that becomes visible only in deeper shadows.

The city feels ancient yet new, as if it emerged fully-formed from the mountain and has remained unchanged since that moment. There's an architectural precision to everything - each stone, channel, and garden bed placed according to some grand design. The buildings show no signs of weathering beyond the smoother patches where hands and feet have passed for generations. It's as if time moves differently here, touching only the living things while leaving the stone structures in a permanent state of freshly-finished perfection.

Workers move quietly through the streets, using bronze tools to tend the gardens and clear the water channels of accumulated moss. They wear thick wool garments dyed in subtle greens and grays that echo the colors of the stone and vegetation. Their movements seem practiced, almost ritualistic, each task performed exactly as it has always been performed.

The air carries the green scent of moss and herbs, mixed with the mineral smell of wet stone. Sound behaves strangely here - voices and footsteps seem to fade quickly into the background hush of flowing water and distant bird calls, as if the city itself absorbs excess noise. The overall effect is one of perpetual dawn or dusk, though the light remains constant, creating an environment that feels simultaneously energized and restful.

In certain spots, usually at the intersections of water channels or at the center points of courtyards, the stone seems to vibrate with a barely-perceptible frequency. The residents treat these locations with particular reverence, though they offer no explanation to visitors about their significance.
