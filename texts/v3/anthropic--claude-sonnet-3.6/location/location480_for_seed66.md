The city rises in terraces of weathered copper and brass, its surfaces etched by centuries of acid rain into delicate, lace-like patterns that catch the amber light. Each level hosts countless nesting chambers for the Migrants - vast colonies of creatures somewhere between birds and flying cetaceans that have made this mountain metropolis their permanent home after millennia of seasonal returns.

The structures weren't built for humans. The scale is wrong - doorways thirty feet tall, corridors that could accommodate small aircraft. The original builders left long ago, but their work endures, transformed by time and new inhabitants into something between architecture and living reef.

Warm updrafts rise constantly through the city's canyons and chambers, carrying moisture from the thermal springs that riddle the mountain's core. This creates a permanent zone of condensation at certain elevations, where water beads constantly on the metal surfaces and runs in established channels, feeding the complex hydraulic systems that still function after all this time.

The sound is constant - the deep resonant calls of the Migrants echoing through the vast spaces, the percussion of their wings against air, the eternal drip and flow of water through ancient pipes, and beneath it all, the low thrum of machinery that still runs in the city's depths, maintained by generations of specialized Migrants who have adapted to tend these systems.

There are few straight lines left in the city. Every surface has been rounded by erosion or built upon by the Migrants, who construct their own additions from regurgitated metal and mineral paste. These accretions follow organic patterns, like the growth rings of some massive metal tree, or the layers of a pearl.

The air is warm and thick with moisture, carrying the mineral taste of the springs and the peculiar ozone scent that accompanies the Migrants' electromagnetic sensing organs. In certain light, you can see the bioelectric fields they generate shimmer like auroras between the buildings.

No plants grow here - the metal surfaces and mineral-rich waters don't support them. Instead, the ecosystem is built on the relationship between the Migrants and various species of chemosynthetic organisms that process the mineral-rich waters and thermal emissions. These form sheets and stalactites of living material that glow with a soft bioluminescence in the deeper reaches of the city.

When the Migrants move in great numbers - during the internal migrations between levels that mark their social seasons - the very air seems to become solid with their bodies. The sound becomes physical, a wall of wing-beats and calls that sets the ancient metal humming in sympathy.

The city is vast enough that different sections experience their own weather patterns. Warm air rising through the central shafts creates artificial rain in the upper levels, while the lower reaches experience perpetual mist from the hot springs. The Migrants have learned to use these patterns in their social movements, entire populations riding the thermal currents between levels.

It's a place that operates on its own timescale - changes happen over decades or centuries, new layers slowly accumulating, old ones gradually wearing away. Even the Migrants, for all their constant motion, are part of cycles that span generations. The city endures, adapting and being adapted, neither fully artificial nor entirely natural, a machine that has learned to breathe.
