The first thing you notice is the screaming. Not voices - the land itself screams, a constant hair-raising keen that rises from countless razor-thin canyons cut into the ancient plateau. The sound modulates as wind patterns shift, creating overlapping harmonics that can drive visitors mad if they stay too long in the open.

This is the Whistling Plain, though that gentle name barely captures its character. The plateau stretches for hundreds of miles, its surface carved into an impossibly complex maze of narrow fissures. From above, it resembles cracked glazing on pottery, or the dried mud of a drained lakebed - but scaled up to terrestrial size. The canyons, most no wider than a person's arm, plunge hundreds of feet deep. Their walls are polished glass-smooth by eons of wind, creating the perfect conditions for their song.

The few hardy plants that survive here have evolved to use the vibrations themselves - their root systems form living tuning forks that resonate with and dampen specific frequencies, creating small zones of relative quiet. These "silence gardens" are oases of green, their leaves collecting the regular morning fog that rolls across the plateau. The plants grow in thick clusters, their interwoven roots preventing them from being shaken apart by the surrounding cacophony.

The fog itself behaves strangely, forming geometric patterns as it interacts with the standing waves of sound. Sometimes it coalesces into floating spheres that drift across the landscape before dissolving in the warming air. During the wet season, rain falling into the canyons creates percussive undertones that can build to literally earth-shaking crescendos.

The only permanent human presence is a scattered handful of sound-hermits - mystics who believe enlightenment can be found in the patterns of noise. They live in caves carved into the canyon walls, their homes marked by intricate arrays of hollow tubes that modify the local acoustics. Their faces are wrapped in thick cloth, and they communicate entirely through elaborate hand gestures.

The hermits harvest resonant crystals that grow in the deepest fissures, fashioning them into protective amulets that they trade to the occasional passing merchant. These crystals seem to form in response to the plateau's voice, growing in shapes that mirror the sound patterns above. Some say the entire plain is slowly crystallizing, the rock itself transforming under the influence of its own song.

Wildlife is almost non-existent on the surface, but deep in the canyons, bizarre creatures have adapted to the constant vibration. Blind, translucent things that navigate by feel alone, their bodies tuned to specific frequencies like living seismographs. The hermits say these creatures are slowly working their way upward, year by year, as the plateau's song grows imperceptibly louder.

At sunset, when the cooling air creates powerful thermal currents, the sound reaches its peak. The entire plateau seems to shiver, and standing waves become visible in the air itself - shifting geometric patterns of compressed atmosphere that can lift loose objects off the ground. The hermits gather at these times, standing in their carefully chosen spots where the acoustics align, experiencing something they refuse to describe to outsiders.

The longer you stay, the more you start to sense patterns in the chaos - or perhaps you just go a little mad. The hermits insist the plateau's voice is a language, if only we could learn to comprehend it. They point to the way the sound changes with the seasons, with the alignment of the stars, with events yet to come. Some say the plateau is a musical instrument left behind by forgotten gods. Others believe it's a warning system, counting down to something no living being will survive to witness.

What's certain is that the sound is getting louder, year by year. New fissures appear regularly, adding their voices to the chorus. The hermits track these changes obsessively, marking them on maps that look increasingly like astronomical charts. They say that when the pattern is complete, the real song will begin.
