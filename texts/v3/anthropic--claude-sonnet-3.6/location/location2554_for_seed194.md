Deep within the mountain's heart, the Machine City drips. Not with rain or groundwater, though moisture beads constantly on every surface - rather, it drips with concentrated time, viscous and heavy, pooling in crystalline collectors that pulse with dim violet light.

The space exists as a vast hollow sphere carved from living rock, nearly a kilometer in diameter, yet feeling impossibly intimate. Suspended throughout are thousands of interconnected platforms and structures, forming a three-dimensional labyrinth that defies conventional geometry. Their surfaces are dark metal, pitted and corroded by centuries of temporal condensation, yet somehow maintaining perfect structural integrity.

The ambient sound is a constant low thrum, punctuated by periodic deep resonances that seem to emanate from the very fabric of space itself. These sounds have a physical presence, causing ripples in the time-fluid that coats every surface. Occasionally, a sharp crack echoes through the sphere as a temporal eddy collapses, briefly illuminating the darkness with abstract patterns of probability.

There are no plants here, no organic growth - yet the surfaces aren't sterile. Strange crystalline formations spread in fractal patterns, feeding on leaked time and warped physics. They catch what little light exists, refracting it in impossible directions and occasionally revealing glimpses of other moments, other possibilities.

The few inhabitants move slowly, deliberately, their environmental suits designed to protect them from chronological exposure. They tend to the machines: enormous clockwork assemblies that process and refine raw time, extracting it from the mountain's peculiar geology. The mechanisms appear ancient, yet show signs of continuous modification and improvement across decades or centuries.

The air is cool, almost cold, though temperature becomes an increasingly abstract concept near the sphere's center, where reality grows thin. There, maintenance workers move with extreme caution, their tethers anchoring them to normal spacetime as they adjust calibration mechanisms that seem to fold through extra dimensions.

Water - real water, not time-fluid - constantly seeps through the rock walls, but it behaves strangely here. Some drops fall upward, others freeze in mid-air, while still others age thousands of years in seconds, leaving trails of rust and mineral deposits hanging in space. Collection systems capture this anomalous water for study and power generation, the pipework forming another layer of complexity in the spherical maze.

The overall impression is of a place that shouldn't exist, yet maintains itself through careful engineering and constant maintenance. It's a pocket universe of industrial-scale temporal engineering, hidden away from the normal flow of time, where the laws of physics are treated more as suggestions than rules.

In the deepest shadows, rarely glimpsed, something moves that might be called fauna - temporal parasites, perhaps, or entities that evolved in the warped conditions. They leave no traces except subtle distortions in the chronological field, and the maintenance crews studiously ignore them.

This is a place of perpetual twilight and eternal industry, where time itself is harvested like a mineral resource, and reality wrinkles at the edges of perception. The mountain above protects it, contains it, and perhaps prevents it from unraveling the orderly progression of moments in the world above.
