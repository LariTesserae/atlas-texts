The water column stretches forever up. Or down. Direction lost meaning cycles ago, when the last bubbles of normalcy burst against what might have been a surface. Now there is only the endless warm brine, thick with life that shouldn't exist at these pressures, these temperatures.

Vast kelp-analogues twist in currents that follow non-Euclidean paths, their fronds both meters and kilometers long depending on how you measure, which you can't. They glow with bioluminescent patterns that seem to encode messages in languages that evolve and die between one pulse and the next. The plants - if they are plants - grow in impossible geometries, creating negative spaces that hurt to look at directly.

The water itself is alive, or perhaps just conscious. It moves with purpose through itself, creating persistent whirlpools that spiral in five dimensions, their edges marked by clouds of microscopic creatures that flare and dim like neural networks learning and forgetting. The pressure should be crushing at this depth, but instead it feels anticipatory, as if the weight of the water column is holding its breath.

The sound never stops. It comes from everywhere - the groaning of water layers sliding past each other, the submarine roar of geological processes that might be miles or millennia away, the clicks and songs of vast creatures that remain permanently just out of sight. The cacophony forms patterns that almost make sense, then collapse into new arrangements that feel even more meaningfully meaningless.

Schools of organisms that might be fish sweep through in synchronized matrices, their movements describing mathematical proofs of impossible theorems. They leave trails of light that persist long after they've passed, forming slowly rotating sculptures of pure trajectory. Larger shapes move in the darkness beyond, things that might once have been whales or squid before the laws of biology bent and broke.

The warmth is wrong - it doesn't dissipate as heat should. It pools and flows like honey, creating thermal currents that ignore convection and instead follow emotional gradients. Patches of cold appear spontaneously, last for subjective eternities, then collapse into brief searing bursts that leave the water unchanged but somehow more complex.

There are gaps in the water, places where the liquid simply stops, revealing nothing behind it - not air or void or other matter, but the absence of possibility itself. These holes in reality drift like amoebae, their edges rippling with fractals that extend inward forever. Sometimes they sing, in voices that sound like colors that don't exist.

The deeper layers (though direction is still meaningless) host vast gardens of creatures that might be coral or might be thoughts made manifest. They build structures that can only be perceived through their effects on the surrounding water - architectural dreams that filter reality like light through a prism, splitting it into its component impossibilities.

Time moves like the water - in all directions at once, eddying and pooling, sometimes flowing backward or sideways or through itself. Moments stack up like sediment, but the layers keep shifting, never settling into a definitive past or future. The present is a negotiable space, as fluid as the medium it exists in.

The entire environment pulses with a slow, massive rhythm that might be a heartbeat, might be the breathing of the water itself, might be the universe trying to wake up from itself. Each beat sends ripples of transformation through everything - subtle changes in the laws of physics, brief inversions of cause and effect, temporary rewrites of evolutionary history.

Nothing here has edges anymore. Everything bleeds into everything else in a constant exchange of properties and possibilities. The water is alive is conscious is time is space is light is sound is life is death is change is eternal is now is never is always.

And still, somehow, it feels like home.
