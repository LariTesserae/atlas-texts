The Canopy Cities stretch across the continent - perhaps across the entire world, though no one has managed to reach its edges. Here in the high plateaus, the ancient megaflora has evolved into something between forest and metropolis. The fundamental unit is not the tree but the Grove - a colonial organism that extends for kilometers, its vast root system stabilizing the eroded highlands into terraced bowls.

The air is thin and cold at this elevation, but the Grove-cities create their own microclimate. Their uppermost branches weave together into vault-like canopies that capture moisture from passing clouds. This water condenses continuously, dripping through layers of engineered foliage. The sound of it is everywhere - a constant patter ranging from gentle mist to thunderous falls where multiple streams converge.

Each Grove is effectively a living arcology. The branches have evolved (or been engineered) into distinct architectural forms - broad platforms, enclosed chambers, transport corridors. The vegetation is so dense it creates its own structural logic, with newer growth filling in any gaps until the optimal spacing is achieved. The overall effect is of an organic Gothic cathedral scaled up to city-size, all sweeping buttresses and soaring vaults, but rendered in living wood and leaves rather than stone.

The engineering that shaped these Grove-cities operated at the molecular level, programming the growth patterns of the megaflora to serve as infrastructure. Photosynthetic panels are woven into the leaves. Specialized tissues conduct power and data as well as water and nutrients. The wood itself incorporates metamaterials that strengthen it far beyond natural limits.

Water flows everywhere but never floods - the hydrologic cycle is perfectly regulated through the Grove's vascular system. It feeds the constant growth needed to maintain the city's form, powers biological computing nodes, and maintains the comfortable microclimate inside the living walls.

The few animals here are mostly small maintenance species - pollinators, decomposers, and symbiotic creatures that help manage the Grove's systems. The dominant sound is the overlap of water flows punctuated by the deep groaning of massive branches shifting in the wind and the occasional crack of new growth breaking through old structures.

This is not a young ecosystem - the Groves have stood for thousands of years, possibly much longer. The underlying plateau has been eroded nearly flat, and the Grove-cities have evolved into their most stable form. They grow and regenerate but do not fundamentally change. The basic patterns of their architecture are encoded in genes that have been optimized over centuries.

Looking out from one of the higher platforms, the Grove-cities extend to the horizon - a forest of living skyscrapers wreathed in mist, their canopies merging into an unbroken green sea above the cold, thin air of the plateau. Water droplets catch the diffuse sunlight, creating a constant shimmer throughout the layers of engineered foliage. It's a landscape where the boundaries between natural and artificial, between city and ecosystem, have long since dissolved into something entirely new.
