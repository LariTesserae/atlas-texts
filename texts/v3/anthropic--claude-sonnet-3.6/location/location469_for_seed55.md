In the endless dark waters, the Library drowns. Not recently - it has always been this way, purpose-built for submersion. Its halls stretch for thousands of miles in every direction, a vast labyrinth of stone corridors and chambers that house the accumulated knowledge of a civilization that chose to sink beneath the waves.

The walls are megalithic - enormous blocks of a dense gray stone shot through with veins of pale blue crystal that gives off a dim, diffuse glow. This minimal illumination reveals the persistent growth that covers nearly every surface: delicate fronds of deep purple and midnight blue that wave gently in the eternal current, sheets of paper-thin fungal matter that phosphoresce weakly, colonies of tiny tubular structures that might be coral or might be something else entirely.

The water itself is cold and incredibly clear, allowing visibility for hundreds of feet through the crystalline liquid before the passages fade into darkness. The architecture follows a precise mathematical progression - each corridor exactly thirty feet wide, each intersection occurring at perfect right angles, each vault ceiling reaching exactly fifty feet high. The proportions create harmonious acoustics in the liquid medium; the slight movements of water through the halls generate deep, barely-audible tones that resonate at the edge of perception.

The Library was built by hand, block by block, using nothing more sophisticated than rope and pulley and lever. The precision of its construction borders on miraculous. Every few miles, there are great circular chambers where the ceiling opens into vertical shafts that stretch up for hundreds of feet, allowing the natural circulation of water through the structure. In these spaces, the vegetation grows most abundantly - cascading curtains of silver-green strands, bulbous shapes that pulse slowly with bioluminescence, delicate fractals of living crystal that have taken root in the ancient mortar between stones.

There is no random growth here - everything that lives in the Library was deliberately cultivated, each species chosen for its ability to maintain and protect the structure rather than degrade it. The plants clean the water, strengthen the stone, and mark the paths through the darkness. They grow with extreme slowness, taking decades to spread across a single block.

In the deepest chambers, the Library holds its secrets. Vast rooms filled with tablets of imperishable crystal, encoded with text that can only be read by touching them with bare skin. Sealed vaults containing preserved specimens of things that once lived in the waters above. Machines of bronze and stone that still turn with glacial slowness, tracking the movements of stars that can no longer be seen through miles of ocean.

The water pressure at this depth should be crushing, but something about the Library's construction creates zones of stability where the normal rules of physics seem suspended. The temperature remains constant, just above freezing. The water moves in consistent patterns, like breath or heartbeat. The Library lives, in its way - not as an organism, but as a system, a designed ecology that has achieved perfect equilibrium over millennia of fine-tuning.

There are no fish here, no drifting plankton, no wondering squid - only the cultivated growth, the eternal flow of water, and the endless corridors of knowledge stretching away into the darkness. The Library drowns, as it always has, as it was meant to do, preserving its secrets in the cold and silent deep.
