A hollow place in high stone, where the world sweats. The chamber isn't large - perhaps forty paces across at its widest - but it feels dense with presence, cramped despite its cathedral ceiling. The rock here is old, smoothed by eons of mineral-laden water that still seeps and trickles from countless fissures, leaving behind delicate traceries of copper-green and sulfur-yellow deposits.

The air is nearly unbreathable, thick with steam and the eye-watering tang of volcanic gases. In the darkness, broken only by the dull red glow of heat-cracks in the floor, countless small bodies move in endless patterns. They are something like crustaceans, something like machines - their shells click and scrape against the stone as they crawl in spiraling processions, following chemical trails left by their predecessors. Their carapaces, when briefly caught in the ruddy light, show complex geometrical patterns that seem almost carved, almost grown.

The noise is overwhelming. Beyond the constant percussion of hard-shelled bodies on stone, there is the hiss and bubble of superheated water, the deep resonant boom of steam chambers collapsing, and the endless chittering communication of the creatures themselves - a sound that seems to carry meaning without language, information without mind. The sounds echo and overlap in the enclosed space until they become almost solid, another presence in the crowded chamber.

Water runs hot here, sometimes boiling where it emerges from the rock, creating a constant veil of steam that condenses on the cooler ceiling and falls back as scalding rain. The floor is a maze of shallow pools and mineral formations, the water stained with dissolved metals and the mysterious secretions of the creature-machines that harvest them.

There are no plants, nothing that could be called life as we know it, yet the place teems with activity. The creature-machines are everywhere, moving with purpose, building their incomprehensible geometries from precipitated minerals, destroying them, building again. Some are no larger than a fingernail, others the size of dinner plates, all of them connected by their endless patterned movement and the chemical songs they leave behind.

In the deepest pool, something larger moves - a colony-form perhaps, or a progenitor, its movements causing ripples that the smaller ones respond to like iron filings around a magnet. The water around it glows with a pale, sourceless light that seems to pulse in time with the chamber's cacophony.

This is a place of perpetual transition, where the boundary between mechanical and biological, between made and grown, has long since dissolved in the mineral soup of the deep rock. It has been here for millions of years, will likely remain for millions more, its processes as natural as erosion, as artificial as mathematics.
