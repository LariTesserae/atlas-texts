The cave moths make the silence impossible. Their wings, each as wide as a human palm, beat with a low thrumming that echoes through the hollow stone chambers. They navigate by the bioluminescent patches on their forewings - pale green markers that paint weak, moving constellations across the cave walls as they fly. The light is just enough to reveal the strange acoustics of this place: a vast network of interconnected chambers, some as large as cathedrals, others tight enough to require crawling.

The stone itself seems young, almost soft - not the smooth-worn limestone of ancient caves, but something more recently torn open. The walls have a faintly oily sheen, and in places they still weep a clear, slightly viscous liquid that pools in depressions or traces lazy paths downward. The liquid carries a metallic scent, and where it gathers, pale fungal structures grow in geometric patterns, their surfaces covered in tiny crystalline protrusions.

The cave moths aren't alone. The chambers pulse with life, most of it adapted to the dim conditions. Translucent amphibians with oversized eyes slip through the shallow pools. Colonies of click-beetles communicate in percussion, their synchronized signals bouncing off the walls in complex patterns. In the larger chambers, bat-like creatures with too many wing-joints roost in dense clusters, their bodies covered in fine filaments that seem to wave in unfelt currents.

Human habitation is sparse but persistent. Rough shelters of woven fiber and treated hide huddle in the more protected chambers, always positioned near the water-seeps. The people here use tools of bone and stone, along with a curious glass-like material they harvest from certain fungal formations. Their presence is marked by the smoke-stains on the higher reaches of the chambers, where they funnel their cooking fires, and by the intricate markings they carve into the softer portions of the walls - patterns that seem to echo the geometric growths of the fungi.

The temperature remains consistently cool, but the space feels alive with motion. The constant movement of the various flying creatures stirs the air into lazy currents. Periodically, deeper tremors pass through the stone itself, and the seeping walls flow more heavily for hours afterward. During these times, the cave moths swarm in agitation, their wing-beats building to a thunder that drowns out all other sounds, their bioluminescence flaring brighter in pulses that transform the chambers into a disorienting maze of moving shadows.

It's a place of constant rhythm and response - the click-beetles answering each other across vast spaces, the people's marking-patterns following the fungal geometries, the moths' light-shows synchronizing with the deep earth-movements. Everything here seems caught in a complex dance of call and answer, though it's not always clear what's leading and what's following.
