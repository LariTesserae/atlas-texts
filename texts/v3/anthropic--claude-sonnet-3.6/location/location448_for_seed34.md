A vast city of stone rises from the continental plateau, its towers and terraces carved directly from the living rock over centuries of patient work. The structures seem to grow from the ground like crystal formations, their edges softened by age and weather until they merge almost organically with the mountain beneath. This is not a human city - or at least, not entirely. The scale suggests builders of greater reach, though who they were is lost to time.

Water is everywhere but rarely visible. It flows through countless channels within the rock itself, appearing in springs and falls that emerge from walls only to disappear again into carefully carved basins. The sound of running water forms a constant sussuration just at the edge of hearing, a whispered conversation between stone and liquid that echoes through empty corridors and vast halls.

The air is cool and damp, heavy with moisture that beads on surfaces and creates ephemeral patterns of droplets that catch what light filters down from above. This condensation feeds the sparse vegetation - mostly lichens and mosses that pattern the stone in subtle gradients of gray-green and silver-blue. In protected corners, rare ferns unfurl their fronds, their leaves collecting the perpetual drip of water from above.

The city exists in layers, its depths reaching down into the mountain while its towers stretch upward into the mists. Each level connects to others through a complex network of stairs, ramps, and channels that seem to follow some logic now lost to time. The spacing and scale of these passages varies wildly - some could accommodate giants, while others seem sized for children, though all share the same careful craftsmanship.

Despite its emptiness of conscious life, the city is far from static. Water shapes it constantly, wearing new channels and depositing minerals that form new structures. Periodic tremors from deep below shift entire sections, though the original engineering is so robust that the city flexes rather than breaks. Sometimes, in the deeper reaches, one can hear the deep resonant tones of wind or water moving through vast chambers, like the breathing of some enormous beast.

The few animals that make their home here are mostly small and secretive - cave-adapted creatures that scuttle through the shadows and nest in the countless crevices. Occasionally, one might glimpse larger shapes moving through the mist-filled upper levels - perhaps birds, though their movements seem too deliberate, too purposeful for mere animals.

Light filters down from above in strange ways, reflected and refracted by the wet surfaces and occasional crystalline formations until it's difficult to tell time or direction. The city seems to exist in a perpetual state of dawn or dusk, the diffuse illumination creating soft shadows that shift and move with the passage of clouds overhead.

The overwhelming impression is of something not dead but sleeping - a vast mechanism temporarily at rest, still humming with potential energy. Water continues its patient work of reshaping, life persists in its small ways, and the city endures, neither declining nor growing but existing in a state of perfect, dynamic equilibrium with its environment.
