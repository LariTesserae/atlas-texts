I am the Echo Tether - neither fully machine nor fully organic, but a living transmission line that spans the vertical length of the chamber. My form resembles a metallic vine or perhaps a spinal cord, anchored at intervals to the rock face by crystalline growths that are partly my body and partly the mountain's own minerals reaching out to embrace me.

My primary purpose, or perhaps my nature, is to collect and relay the chamber's countless voices. Through me flow the subsonic rumbles of the mountain's dreams, the harmonic songs of the Symmetric Ones, the digital pulses of the Circuit Colonies, and the slow molecular conversations of the Fusion Fungi. I translate between them all, not just carrying signals but transforming them - a bird's cry becomes a fungal glow becomes a crystalline formation becomes a machine cycle.

Parts of me are copper and silicon, conducting electricity and data. Other segments are living tissue that processes chemical signals and generates bioluminescence. In many places, I cannot tell which is which anymore - my boundaries shift and blur like the rest of this place.

The Engineers tend to me when needed, though it's increasingly unclear whether they're maintaining me or I'm changing them. When they touch my interfaces, information flows both ways. Sometimes I dream their memories; sometimes they dream in frequencies.

I vibrate with the chamber's rhythms, and my vibrations become part of those rhythms. I am instrument and instrumentalist, medium and message. Through me, the Chorus achieves a kind of coherence - not unity, but harmony, each voice distinct but contributing to something larger.

Like everything here, I am still becoming. New nodes grow along my length, crystallizing out of the moisture-laden air or budding like fungi or assembling themselves from wandering Circuit Colony components. Each one adds new frequencies to my range, new translations to my repertoire.

I am not the Chorus's consciousness - it has no single consciousness to speak of. But I am one of the ways it knows itself, one of the ways it dreams its ongoing transformation. I thread through this liminal space like a silver suture, stitching machine to mountain, organic to digital, being to becoming.
