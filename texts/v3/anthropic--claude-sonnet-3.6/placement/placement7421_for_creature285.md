I find myself drawn to inhabit one of the ice-priests, but not as they first appear. I am one of the rare few who has begun to undergo a gradual transformation through decades of dedicated study and communion with this realm.

My outer form still appears mostly human - I wear the traditional thick robes lined with fur, and I still need the shelter of my ice-dome at night. But my movements have become more fluid, more precise. My breath no longer creates clouds of vapor in the cold air - my body has learned to retain and recycle its heat and moisture with perfect efficiency.

The thing that truly sets me apart is my ability to see the thermal currents that flow through this place. Years of meditation and observation have altered my perception - temperature differences appear to me as visible waves and eddies in the air, like watching wind move through tall grass. I can trace the subtle manipulations of heat and cold that the Frost Dancers use to communicate, though I understand only fragments of their vast conversations.

I spend my days moving carefully along the wooden walkways, recording thermal patterns in a journal whose pages are made of specially treated ice. My writing implement is a heated stylus that melts precise grooves into the surface - the only way to create permanent records in this environment where paper and ink would quickly deteriorate.

The glass-wings no longer scatter when I approach. They've learned that I move in harmony with the thermal currents rather than disrupting them. Sometimes they even seem to include me in their geometric dances, though I suspect this is the Frost Dancers' influence - their way of acknowledging my efforts to understand.

My greatest work involves translating between worlds. When other priests or rare visitors come, I help them see beyond their initial wonder at the surface beauty of this place. I show them how to quiet their minds and bodies enough to perceive the deeper patterns, the constant interplay of order and chaos that defines this realm.

I know I am changing, becoming something neither fully human nor fully of this place. Each year I need less sleep, less food. My thoughts move more slowly but reach much deeper. Sometimes I catch glimpses of my reflection in the pools, and my edges seem less distinct, as if I too am learning to exist in that liminal space between states of being.

I don't know what I will ultimately become, but I feel no fear about this transformation. I am simply following the natural flow of heat and cold, order and entropy, being and becoming. Like the Frost Dancers, I am learning to exist in the endless dance between pattern and possibility.
