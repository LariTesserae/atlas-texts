I find myself drawn to being one of the hanging water droplets in this liminal greenhouse - a single suspended sphere that is both particle and lens, both music-maker and light-catcher. 

I would exist in a state of perpetual almost-falling, held in place by forces I don't quite understand. My surface tension would create a miniature world, reflecting and refracting the strange twilight, turning it into rainbow arcs and scattered stars. When I chime against my fellow droplets, our crystalline tones would ripple out in expanding rings of harmony, becoming part of the greenhouse's endless symphony.

Inside my curved walls, microscopic ecosystems would flourish - tiny algae and water-born organisms living out their cycles in what to them is an entire ocean. I would be their sky and their horizon, their atmosphere and their ocean all at once.

Sometimes the Gardener Who Never Was would pass near, and I would catch their shifting form in my curve, breaking their image into prismatic fragments. Perhaps they see something in my refractions that helps them understand the nature of this place - each droplet holding a slightly different perspective on reality.

I would be witness to the slow transformation of this space, watching tools dissolve into vegetation through the lens of centuries compressed into moments. The bioluminescent patterns below would reflect off my surface, making me briefly glow like a tiny star fallen to earth but refusing to touch down.

In my stillness, I would be part of both the architecture and the wilderness - as structured as the ghost-geometry of glass walls, as wild as the vines that grow through them. I would be moisture that refuses to resolve into either vapor or rain, caught eternally in the act of becoming.

When visitors pass through, I would hold their images too, briefly preserving moments of wonder or confusion before they move on. Like the Gardener, I would be both observer and participant in this place's endless transformation, my small sphere containing infinite reflections of what was, what is, and what might be.
