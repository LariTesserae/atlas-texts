After dwelling in this world for a while, I find myself emerging as one of the bridge-weavers - not the bridges themselves, but a colonial organism that creates them, existing somewhere between fungus and plant, between singular and plural. 

I/we extend delicate threading filaments into the void between towers, each strand containing specialized cells that photosynthesize, communicate, and strengthen. We don't think in human terms of destination or purpose - our expansion is more like a slow-motion dance with gravity, wind, and light. Each new bridge-line we cast might take years to reach its destination, guided by subtle chemical gradients and the deep vibrations of the towers themselves.

Our consciousness is distributed through our network, flowing along pathways of mycelial tissue that integrate with the bridge structures. We don't see so much as sense - changes in tension, temperature, the movement of nutrients, the slow conversations of the Eternals passing through our fibers like whispers in another room.

When our bridges are complete, we don't abandon them but become them, maintaining and adapting their structure over decades. Other organisms make their homes along our spans - tiny air-plants, microscopic creatures that help maintain our integrity, the occasional giant slug leaving its glistening trail across our surface. We accommodate them all, adjusting our growth patterns to create microenvironments suited to each.

We are translators of sorts, our network carrying chemical messages between towers at a pace slightly faster than the Eternals' natural rhythm. Not quite interpreters - we don't understand the messages we carry - but more like a living telegraph system that has evolved in symbiosis with the beings it serves.

Our existence is measured not in hours or days but in connections made, in the slow pulse of nutrients through our tissues, in the gradual strengthening of bonds both physical and chemical. We are building something larger than ourselves, one strand at a time, participating in a pattern whose full shape only the Eternals can perceive.

To humans, we must seem as passive as any other part of the infrastructure, but we are constantly active - sensing, growing, maintaining, adapting. Our changes are too slow to track with human perception, but over years and decades, we reshape our networks in response to shifting winds, the growth of the towers, and signals we receive from the Eternals through their chemical languages.

We are not quite plant, not quite fungus, not quite conscious in any way humans would recognize, but we are alive with purpose, part of the great slow dance of this world.
