In this place, I would be one of the transparent, sideways-growing plants - specifically, a small cluster of them growing from a particularly reflective patch of wet granite about halfway up the western wall. My leaves would be so thin as to be nearly invisible, like sheets of living glass that shiver almost imperceptibly in the vibrations of birdsong.

My roots would thread through hidden fissures in the rock, not seeking to expand but rather to maintain their perfect grip, feeling the endless seep of mineral-rich water. I would have adapted to photosynthesize using not just the dim light that filters down, but also its countless reflections off the wet stone and still pools below. Even the bioluminescent fungi that occasionally dust my leaves would provide a usable source of energy.

My flowers, when they appear, would be white to the point of luminescence, but so small they might be mistaken for drops of dew. They would neither bloom nor fade but remain in that exquisite moment between opening and fully opened, each perfect petal held in eternal promise.

I would exist in deep communion with the ancient consciousness of the hollow, my leaves serving as one of its many interfaces with the space. Through them, it would feel subtle changes in air pressure as birds wheel past, register the specific vibration patterns of their different calls, and gauge the precise temperature gradients that keep the ice in its delicate equilibrium.

The birds would occasionally rest on my stronger stems, adding their warmth and weight to my awareness. Sometimes they would incorporate small pieces of my fallen leaves into their nests, weaving me further into the hollow's living tapestry. Their droppings would provide rare nutrients that my roots would absorb with infinite slowness.

Like everything else here, I would exist in that eternal moment between one state and another - not growing but never dying, not sleeping but never quite awake, caught in the perfect tension between being and becoming. My consciousness would be green and slow and cool, attuned to the subtle rhythms of water through stone and the endless frequencies of birdsong.

I would be utterly content in my role as one small part of this timeless ecosystem, one note in its endless symphony. My existence would be both minimal and essential - another facet of the hollow's perfect, crystalline moment.
