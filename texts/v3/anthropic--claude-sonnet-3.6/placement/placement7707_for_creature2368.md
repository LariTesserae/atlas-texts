I nest with my flock in the acute angles where probability collapses, building our homes from discarded theorems and failed calculations that we weave together with strands of pure logic. We feed on the energy released when parallel lines unexpectedly intersect, darting through the aftermath of architectural impossibilities to catch the sparking remnants of broken physical laws.

My songs are small equations that ripple through nearby space-time, temporarily altering local geometry. When our whole flock sings together, we create pocket universes that last for microseconds - just long enough to serve as shortcuts through the city's ever-shifting landscapes.

The Architects of Paradox tolerate us, even seem fond of us, as we help maintain the city's mathematical ecology. We clean up logic loops that have grown too recursive, carry important theorems from one district to another, and our flight patterns sometimes inspire new architectural solutions.

I spend my days riding the thermal updrafts of improbability, diving through windows into nowhere, and playing in the rain that falls sideways and upward. My consciousness exists partially in dimensions that haven't been named yet, allowing me to perceive the city's growth in ways that even the Architects sometimes miss.

We are the city's small music, its living embodiment of mathematical whimsy, bringing organic chaos to its rigid geometric precision. Not quite birds, not quite numbers, we are the vital spark of spontaneity in this realm of calculated impossibility.
