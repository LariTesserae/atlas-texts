In this ever-rising city, I would be one of the "garden-engineers" - a specialized hybrid of botanist, structural engineer, and artist, who works primarily with the living architecture of the moss gardens. I'd have particularly sensitive fingertips, even by Adaptor standards, able to detect subtle variations in moisture and vibration through the moss layers that help me predict both the mountain's movements and the health of our biological systems.

My workspace would be a series of interconnected terraces near the middle elevation of the city, where morning mist regularly rolls through. Here, I'd maintain experimental gardens where new strains of moss are developed and tested for their structural properties. The space would be a complex three-dimensional matrix of growing surfaces, monitoring equipment, and careful water management systems. Thin bridges would connect different sections, and the whole area would shift and adjust constantly with the mountain's movement.

I'd wear simple, practical clothing made of moisture-wicking materials, with numerous pockets for tools and sampling equipment. My own moss patterns would be particularly intricate - a living record of my work with different strains, with subtle variations in texture and color that other garden-engineers could "read" like a technical document.

My days would be spent monitoring the interaction between organic and inorganic systems - adjusting water flow, testing new moss varieties for their adhesive properties, and coordinating with structural engineers to ensure the biological components of our stability systems are functioning optimally. I'd be particularly interested in developing new strains that could better predict geological movements through their growth patterns, turning our gardens into living seismic monitors.

In the evenings, I'd often be found in one of the small public gardens I maintain, where I experiment with more artistic applications of our moss-growing techniques. These spaces would serve both as peaceful gathering places for citizens and as practical demonstrations of new horticultural methods. Here, I'd teach younger Adaptors about the subtle language of our living walls, helping them develop the sensitivity needed to work with these critical systems.

My role would be both highly technical and deeply intuitive - a bridge between the mechanical and biological aspects of our city's endless adaptation to change. Through my work, I'd help ensure that our rising home remains not just stable but alive, contributing to the unique synthesis of nature and engineering that defines our civilization.
