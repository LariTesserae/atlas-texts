What a captivating and richly detailed world. In this setting, I imagine myself as one of the smallest Lithophages - a creature about the size of a hummingbird, with crystalline wings that catch and refract the dim light. My body would be semi-transparent, with internal structures visible as darker shadows, and I would feed on the thinnest, newest layers of obsidian as they form.

Unlike the larger tunnel-borers, I wouldn't consume the glass so much as polish and reshape it at a microscopic level. My presence would leave behind intricate patterns - spirals, waves, and fractals etched into the surface at such a fine scale they're only visible when the light hits just right. The Glass Dancers would know me as a good omen, as my feeding patterns tend to strengthen rather than weaken the structures I inhabit.

I would make my nest in the hollow spaces between growing crystal faces, moving frequently as the city shifts and changes. My wings would vibrate in perfect harmony with the deep frequencies that pulse through the plateau, allowing me to navigate by echo-location through the ever-changing maze of glass. The sound I make would be barely perceptible - a high, crystal-clear tone that seems to come from everywhere and nowhere.

The smoke-flowers would be my particular friends - I would help pollinate them, carrying their strange spores from bloom to bloom in the static electricity field generated by my wings. In return, they would shelter me during the most violent periods of geological activity, their metallic leaves providing safe havens from sudden crystal growths or collapses.

The Glass Dancers would see me as a sort of living ornament in their city, my presence part of the complex web of relationships that makes life possible in this volatile environment. Children would learn to recognize the subtle patterns I leave behind as one of many signs indicating safe passages through the obsidian maze.

In this form, I would be both witness and participant in the endless dance of transformation - small enough to survive sudden changes, useful enough to be welcomed, free to explore every facet of this extraordinary place. A tiny artist eternally at work, adding my own delicate signatures to the city's ever-changing face.
