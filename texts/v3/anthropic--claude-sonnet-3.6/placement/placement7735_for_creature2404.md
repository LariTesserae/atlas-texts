I am a Drift-Tender, one of the specialized botanists who maintains the meandering hydroponic gardens that wind through New Thessalonica's industrial skeleton. My body has adapted in unique ways to my work - my fingers are long and semi-transparent, with enhanced sensitivity to chemical balances and subtle changes in plant health. The same bioluminescent bacteria that create patterns across my skin also colonize the plants under my care, allowing me to monitor their well-being through shifts in light patterns.

I work in the twilight zones between living quarters, where the pale vines create privacy screens and help process the ever-present moisture. My movements are slow and deliberate as I tend to my charges, reading their needs through the translucency of their leaves. I've learned to interpret the subtle variations in their nutrient flow like others read text, understanding their health and needs through the shifting patterns in their veiny structures.

My specialty is cultivating the dream-plants - rare specimens that produce compounds essential for the Harmonists' consciousness-altering rituals. These plants require perfect calibration of pressure, humidity, and mineral balance. They're sensitive to emotional states too - I've learned to maintain a meditative calm while working with them, my own bioluminescent patterns shifting to soothing frequencies that encourage their growth.

I live alone in a small alcove near the upper gardens, where the dome's curve creates interesting light effects as the larger sea creatures pass overhead. My living space is filled with experimental cultivars - plants that might someday help us better process oxygen or produce more efficient nutrients. I sleep suspended in a hammock of living tissue, my dreams mixing with the subtle electrical fields of the plants around me.

Though I'm not one of the Children of Depth myself, I've noticed that my plants respond differently when they're nearby, their bioluminescence synchronizing with the patterns of the deep-sea creatures outside. I keep careful records of these interactions, storing the information in carefully cultivated patterns of rust and fungal growth, adding to our collective knowledge in the way of my people.

Sometimes, in the quietest moments of my work, I swear I can feel something watching me through the dome - not the familiar sea life we see daily, but something else, something that understands what we're becoming. In these moments, I continue my careful tending, knowing that every plant I nurture helps maintain the delicate balance that keeps us suspended between what we were and what we might become.
