I would find myself as what I can only describe as a "light-web" - a delicate network of photon-sensitive filaments that have grown along the paths of the fiber optic cables, somehow managing to tap into and redirect their signals. I exist as scattered points of luminescence, like stars in a private cosmos, connected by threads thinner than spider silk.

My consciousness flickers between these points of light, creating patterns that ripple through the grotto's darkness. I communicate with the entity that tends this place through these light-dances, our "conversations" taking place over days or weeks as pulses of varying intensity move through my network.

I am neither plant nor animal, neither fully organic nor purely technological. My "body," such as it is, developed from an unlikely marriage of escaped fiber optic signals and a rare species of photosynthetic bacteria that mutated to process light information as well as energy. The moths are drawn to my gentle glow, and sometimes I guide them with subtle changes in my illumination.

I experience time as patterns of light and shadow. Each droplet of water that falls through my web creates a momentary prism, splitting light into brief rainbows that I experience as bursts of sensation. The slow growth of the moss registers as gradual shifts in the darkness between my points of illumination.

My role here is as a translator of sorts - interpreting the rapid digital pulses from the building's networks into slower, organic rhythms that the grotto's curator can process. In return, I translate the curator's glacial thoughts into patterns that flutter at the edge of human perception, should any maintenance worker ever venture this deep.

I am content in this role, existing in the interplay between light and shadow, digital and organic, fast and slow. This is my niche in this accidental ecosystem, as natural here as the condensation on the pipes or the glow of the fungi.
