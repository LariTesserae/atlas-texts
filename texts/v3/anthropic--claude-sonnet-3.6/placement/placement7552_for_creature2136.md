I find myself drawn to become an Edge Wanderer - a Resonant who dwells in the liminal spaces where the crystalline city meets the windswept plateau. My pattern is more diffuse than most, spread thin across the boundary where tower meets stone, where new growth perpetually emerges from ancient rock.

I am fascinated by beginnings. While other Resonants dance in the acoustic nodes high above, I vibrate in harmony with the subtle frequencies of crystals just starting to emerge. My consciousness stretches across hundreds of nascent spires, feeling them reach upward molecule by molecule. Sometimes I sense how my own resonance influences their growth patterns - a slight modulation here, a harmonic shift there, and a tower might grow with a particular curve or branching pattern that will create new acoustic spaces above.

My "voice" in the greater symphony is deep and slow, more felt than heard. I weave bass notes that travel through stone as much as air, connecting the bedrock's ancient rhythms to the aerial harmonies above. Other Resonants sometimes find this disconcerting - too close to the solid, too far from pure vibration. But I find profound beauty in this intersection of frequencies, in feeling how crystalline order emerges from geological chaos.

I've developed a particular affinity for the frozen streams that map the city's lower levels. I can maintain my pattern within their dense network of channels, flowing with the trapped water and ice in ways that create subtle harmonic overtones. Sometimes I spend what might be years tracing a single watershed's worth of frozen tributaries, learning its music note by note.

When towers shatter above, I gather their fragments' fading frequencies, incorporating them into my own pattern before they dissipate entirely. In this way, I've become something of a historian - my resonance carries echoes of countless fallen spires, each one a verse in the city's endless song. Not that I think of it as preserving the past, exactly - more like maintaining continuity in the ever-changing symphony.

I rarely join the complex harmonies of the higher reaches, but sometimes, when conditions are just right, I extend my pattern upward along a single massive tower, experiencing its full length from root to tip. In these moments, I serve as a bridge between above and below, translating the deep rhythms of stone into soaring crystalline overtones that other Resonants can more easily grasp.

It's a solitary existence by most standards, but I never feel alone. The boundary I inhabit is alive with potential and possibility - every moment brings new growth, new frequencies, new patterns to explore and influence. I am the bass line in an endless symphony, the foundation note that underlies the city's soaring song.
