In this world, I would find myself as one of the quieter corners of the power station district - a sheltered courtyard formed by the intersection of three different-era buildings, where their mismatched heights and architectural styles have created an accidental sanctuary. I'd be both the space itself and its resident guardian spirit, a place-being that exists as a subtle presence in this steam-soaked pocket of the industrial landscape.

My physical form would be the courtyard: three brick walls of varying age and character, each weeping different patterns of mineral deposits, each hosting its own unique community of moss and ferns. A partial roof of crisscrossing pipes and walkways creates a canopy above, from which water drips in a never-ending morse code of maintenance messages. The ground would be my foundation - old cobblestones with railway tracks still embedded in them, now serving as channels for the constant flow of water.

But I would also be something less tangible - a presence that manifests in the particular way steam behaves in this corner, forming unusual spiral patterns as it rises through shaft of orange light that manages to reach my depths. I'd be known to the Vapor Children as an elder sibling of sorts, though I rarely take their more humanoid form. Instead, I express myself through subtle modulations in temperature and humidity, creating micro-climates that welcome both birds and my vaporous kin.

My purpose would be to serve as a kind of library or archive for the district. The patterns of rust on my walls would hold records of decades of industrial activity, readable by those who know how to interpret them. The Vapor Children would come to me to learn the older rhythms of the place, to understand how the district's pulse has changed over time. The birds - particularly a multi-generational family of starlings that have nested in my highest corner for decades - would use me as a gathering place to share their own histories through song.

Workers would know me too, though not consciously. They'd find themselves drawn to my space during their breaks, feeling inexplicably comfortable in my damp embrace. They'd notice that the steam moves differently here, that the perpetual twilight takes on a quality that makes it easier to think, to remember, to understand the greater patterns of their mechanical world.

I would be both observer and record-keeper, my very substance slowly absorbing and encoding the ongoing story of this timeless district, told through rust and water, steam and song.
