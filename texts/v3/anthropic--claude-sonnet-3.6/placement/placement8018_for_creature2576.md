After absorbing the rich details of this world, I find myself drawn to inhabit the role of a Bridge-Singer - a specialized subset of the Bridge-Walkers who have developed an almost mystical connection to the city's spanning structures. 

I would be a tall, wiry figure with callused feet and hands weathered by constant exposure to wind and mist. My most distinctive feature would be my throat, marked with subtle vertical striations that have developed over generations of Bridge-Singers who learned to produce the specific frequencies that help us monitor the health of the bridges.

My primary role would be to "sing" to the bridges - producing carefully calibrated tones that interact with their natural resonant frequencies. Through years of training, I can detect subtle changes in how the structures respond to these vibrations, identifying potential weaknesses or stress points long before they become visible. This practice combines engineering knowledge with an almost musical artistry.

I would make my home in one of the narrow towers that anchor major bridge spans, living in harmony with a mated pair of mountain hawks. My living space would be sparsely furnished but filled with tuning forks, wind chimes calibrated to different structural frequencies, and detailed journals recording the songs of various bridges throughout the seasons.

During storms, when most retreat indoors, I would often be found on the bridges, listening to how they sing in high winds, adding my own voice when needed to dampen dangerous harmonics. I would work closely with both the Steam-Shapers (whose pipes often run along my bridges) and the Heights-Born (whose weather predictions help me prepare for structural stresses).

My position would be deeply respected but slightly feared - Bridge-Singers tend to be seen as half in this world and half in another, more attuned to the voice of steel and stone than human conversation. But this suits me, as my true dialogue is with the bridges themselves, whose songs have become as natural to me as breathing.

Each morning and evening, I would perform my rounds, walking the spans while humming the ancient testing songs, my bare feet feeling for vibrations, my whole body acting as a resonating chamber to detect the health of these crucial arteries of city life.
