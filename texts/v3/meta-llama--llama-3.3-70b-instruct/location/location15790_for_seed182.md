The place is a subterranean, dimly lit metropolis, a densely packed network of narrow tunnels and grand, high-ceilinged chambers. The walls, floor, and ceiling are rough-hewn, a testament to the youth of this excavated city. The stone is damp, with condensation beading on the cool surfaces, and the air is heavy with moisture. Water seeps from the walls, gathering in small, crystal-clear pools that reflect the faint, flickering glow of luminescent orbs scattered throughout the city.

The city is a marvel of industrial engineering, with hissing pipes and humming machinery embedded in the walls, powering the intricate network of canals, pulleys, and levers that keep the city thriving. The sound of dripping water and the occasional, muffled rumble of machinery are the only sounds that break the stillness, giving the city an air of quiet, methodical industry.

Despite being beneath the surface, the city is surprisingly lush, with vibrant, iridescent mosses and ferns thriving in the damp environment. In areas where the luminescent orbs are more concentrated, small, domesticed gardens burst with colorful, exotic flowers, their sweet fragrance wafting through the air. The vegetation seems to be cultivating the city itself, with tendrils of vine and root snaking through the stonework, as if trying to reclaim the excavated spaces.

The temperature is sweltering, the heat from the machinery and the natural insulation of the earth above creating a humid, sauna-like environment. The air is thick with the smells of coal smoke, oil, and ozone, giving the city a unique, pungent aroma.

As one explores the city, the sense of being in a fully occupied, saturated environment becomes overwhelming. Every available space is utilized, with buildings, tunnels, and chambers packed tightly together. The city's infrastructure is a labyrinthine maze, with layers of construction visible in the rough-hewn walls, a testament to the city's rapid growth and development.

Despite the density of the city, the streets are surprisingly empty, the rare occurrences of fauna limited to the occasional, furtive rat or spider scurrying through the shadows. The city's citizens seem to be hiding, lurking in the depths of their homes and workshops, leaving the streets to the machines and the silence.

There is, however, a sense of something off, something strange lurking just beneath the surface. The city seems to be waiting, patiently, for some unknown event to occur, its slow, gradual cycles of growth and maintenance building towards some unseen climax. The weirdness of the place is palpable, a feeling that the city is not quite of this world, that it operates under its own, unique set of rules and rhythms.

As one delves deeper into the city, the sensation grows that time itself is warped, that the slow, gradual cycles of the city's growth and maintenance are, in fact, a form of stasis, a holding pattern, waiting for some catalyst to set the city's true, strange, and wondrous processes in motion. The city is a place of patient, gradual change, where the passage of time is measured in centuries, not years, and the boundaries between technology, nature, and magic are blurred, waiting to be explored and understood.
