A place this tightly closed upon itself has long since stopped asking what lies beyond. Inside its low rim the basin holds its own population—slow, film-thin, almost weightless—each life shaped by the rule that nothing is ever allowed to finish becoming.

The membranes that skate the water are not single creatures but a single circulating agreement. They cannot leave the light, so they make the light their medium; each meeting re-composes the same small atmosphere, returning the excess warmth to the stone as a cooler film. Their lentil-sized bodies are continually reassembled rather than born: two membranes exchange droplets, fall apart, and the new individual already knows the temperature map of the next few centimeters. Because they cannot rise higher than a hand-span before they fall back, their motion writes the same faint script over and over, a living palimpsest that the condensation immediately overwrites.

The blue-green felt survives by the same contract. Its cushions occupy the narrow throats between swells only because those throats trap the calmest air; the felt’s tiny leaves act as a net, catching stray droplets that otherwise would re-join the general film. When brushed, the resin scent is not a defense but an invitation: the vapor rises just far enough to intersect the descending membranes, giving them a slightly denser droplet to carry home. The felt never thickens beyond a thumbnail because the membranes are also its ceiling; any higher and the falling membranes would be torn.

The black cylinders are not objects but punctuation left by an earlier grammar. Their slow oil and rising bubbles mark time for nothing in particular; the reflected motes that skitter across the floor are simply the only motion fast enough to be noticed. The membranes sometimes gather above a cylinder, dimming its momentary spark; this seems accidental until one watches long enough to see that the dimming delays the next reflection by exactly one more heartbeat, as though the living film were politely spacing the punctuation.

Nothing here breeds in the usual sense. The felt spreads by thread-thin runners that stop at the first patch of open stone. The membranes trade interior droplets and continue. The stone trembles once per degree, a single, respectful acknowledgment that the temperature has chosen to stay the same. Only the condensation keeps a stricter schedule: it pauses when the membranes coalesce into a lens, resumes when they scatter. In that brief arrest the basin appears to listen, though listening is merely the absence of its own quiet noise.

Beyond the shin-high lip the brightness continues, unchanged and unentered. Inside, every motion has already agreed to return to its starting point, so the population never expands and never quite finishes disappearing. The basin is therefore occupied by a single, endlessly rehearsed hesitation—life reduced to the width of a droplet, time reduced to the interval between two reflections on black glass.
