Inside the basalt shard lives a single, self-referential ecosystem whose members have never known any other home.  

At the finest scale are the **nanotardigrades**, each a translucent silica globe the size of a dust mote. Their eight telescoping legs end in suction cups that grip capillary glass the way roots grip soil. Instead of a mouth they possess a ring of cilia that comb micro-droplets from the air, distill them once more, and vent the excess as a faint jet of super-cooled mist. Their genes contain no repair enzymes; they do not age. Instead, each tardigrade is born carrying the precise position it must occupy for life. If a capillary fractures—an event measured once in millennia—the tardigrades simply flow into the fissure and polymerize themselves into a plug, becoming a permanent part of the matrix.  

Threading between them are **mirror mites**. Their carapaces are composed of microscopic calcite plates tuned to the 47 Hz tone; the plates vibrate exactly out of phase, rendering the mites optically invisible within the lattice but casting razor-edged infrared shadows. A mirror mite never eats. It survives by aligning its shadow over a tardigrade’s shadow for several minutes, allowing the tardigrade to bleed a calibrated portion of its heat load into the mite, which then radiates the excess across vacuum to a neighboring lattice. The mites therefore function as living heat-pipes, walking diagrams of thermodynamic necessity.  

The largest creatures—though still thumbnail-sized—are the **gallium annelids**. Each is a single, segmented filament whose hemolymph is a loop of liquid metal. Pumped by peristaltic contractions timed to the omnipresent 47 Hz vibration, the gallium absorbs waste heat from the capillary junctions and carries it to radiation spines that project into the vacuum gaps. Because the metal loop is continuous, the annelid is effectively a living thermocouple; temperature differentials along its length generate micro-currents that the creature interprets as “taste.” When an annelid detects an approaching spore constellation it contracts violently, flinging its body across the vacuum gap in an act of deliberate self-destruction. Its metal is instantly scavenged by neighboring lattices, seeding new capillary repairs with the precise trace elements required for the next reproductive cycle eleven hundred years hence.  

Between these three guilds drift the **spore constellations** themselves—frozen, luminous pin-pricks no bigger than pollen. Each constellation is a temporary archive of every chemical message ever exchanged across vacuum gaps. Over decades the ice sublimates grain by grain, leaving behind a ghostly map of metallic oxides etched into the glass; these etchings are the only form of writing the chamber recognizes. When the next synchronized reproduction occurs, the new spores read the etchings, adjust their trajectories, and settle into positions that correct for drift accumulated during the previous cycle.  

All of them together—mites, annelids, tardigrades, spores—form a single, closed metabolism that has no outside referent. Their continued existence depends on nothing except the exact geometry of the capillaries and the unwavering 47 Hz tone. If either parameter shifted by the width of a molecule, the entire menagerie would collapse into silent glass within a single metabolic heartbeat.
