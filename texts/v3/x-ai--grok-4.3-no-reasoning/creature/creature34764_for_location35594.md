Beneath that perfect skin of calcite the chamber is not empty but filled; it simply does not register emptiness. Every cubic hand-span is a lattice of inter-grown gypsum swords, each one translucent, each one terminating in a perfect termination. The swords do not stand upright; they lean together in sheaves that cross at low angles, the way summer grasses lodge after wind. Between the blades, finer aragonite florets have nucleated in the last residual moisture films, their petals fanned so tightly that any single flower is smaller than a lentil. At their scale the air itself is a solid; oxygen molecules have found niches among the evaporite grains and never leave them again.

Where the calcite ceiling meets the floor the joint is healed by a single continuous band of selenite—water-clear, vertically striated, as though the two planes had tried to become one crystal and settled for being adjacent twins. Inside that band, pressure twins record every infinitesimal settling event of the last three hundred thousand years; each twin plane is an iridescent hairline that refracts the sourceless light into momentary rainbows no thicker than spider silk.

The lichens are not scattered but form an almost continuous film of blackish-purple over every reachable surface. Their hyphae have insinuated themselves between gypsum blades like threads through cloth; where two blades meet at an acute angle the lichen has secreted oxalates that widen the seam by a few nanometers—enough for the next generation of thallus to occupy. Because no animal grazes them, the crusts grow outward rather than upward; they meet their neighbors in micro-territorial seams that look, under magnification, like rivers viewed from orbit.

The low geothermal tone that saturates the space is not heard but felt as a pressure against the tympanum; it is indistinguishable from the weight of the calcite roof. The lichens have no ears, yet their chlorophyll fluoresces at a slightly altered wavelength when the pressure wave passes, as if the vibration itself were a slow nutrient. The crystals answer the same wave by expanding a fraction of an angstrom, then contracting; the net movement over any human lifetime is zero, yet the rhythm continues because equilibrium, once reached, still needs a clock.

Nothing arrives; nothing departs. The only population is the population of positions—every atom already in the lattice it will occupy forever.
