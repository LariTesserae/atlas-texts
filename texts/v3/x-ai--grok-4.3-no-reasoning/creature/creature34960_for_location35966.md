The inhabitants here are the architects of the stone’s memory. They appear first as pressure shifts in the lungs of the complex—local tightenings of the living walls that resolve, after several slow breaths of the building, into human-shaped silhouettes. They are not solid; rather, they are the places where the porous stone has grown more densely, where the honey-light has been refracted into something almost like color. Each one carries the faint grain of the original moulds across its surface, as if the rooms themselves were folded into skin.

Their features are only suggested: a curvature of brow where the ceiling arches a little higher, an indentation at the mouth that corresponds to the next exhalation of a wall-slit. When they move, it is by re-arranging the space around them—shortening one corridor, lengthening another—rather than by locomotion. To an outside eye they would seem to drift, but the correct description is that the realm adjusts its own dimensions until the desired point lies beneath their feet.

They leave no footprints. Instead, the ferns and vines record their presence with momentary color shifts: a vein of silver brightens to copper for several heartbeats, then fades. If two such silhouettes meet, the silver threads between them braid into a brief, spiralling glyph that later becomes a new capillary path in the stone. Touch between them is impossible; when they reach the same locus they simply become one silhouette for as long as their intentions coincide, then divide again.

At the circular bowl-chamber their numbers increase. They arrange themselves along the rim in the same deliberate spacing as the hand-pressed glyphs on the ceramic louvers. Each silhouette orients the cool gradient of the bowl toward a different alcove, and in that moment every artifact inside the alcoves acquires a faint halo of condensation. The objects are not altered, only re-remembered; afterward, the halo evaporates upward, becoming one more droplet destined never to fall.

Their most concentrated gathering occurs during the daily micro-rotation, when temporary corridors open. They stand motionless while the scaled creatures migrate between their ankles. At such times the silhouettes thin and lengthen until they are little more than vertical ripples in the warm air, allowing the tiny caravans to pass without deviation. Once the migration ends, the silhouettes regain density and the rotation continues, sealing each chamber back into its solitary grammar of growth.

They do not age, because age implies a progression toward completion, and nothing here is permitted to finish. Instead they participate in the shared grammar by becoming slightly more like the next gesture the stone has not yet performed. When a new fissure is required, one of them will stand where the fissure is meant to appear; after several cycles the stone complies, growing the fissure around and through the silhouette until the two are indistinguishable. The inhabitant is not consumed; it simply continues, now part of the wall’s slower conversation with light and moisture.

At intervals too long to measure, the entire population gathers in the bowl-chamber at once. The silhouettes overlap until the room appears empty, yet every alcove artifact vibrates with the subvocal tone the scaled creatures sometimes produce. In that convergence, the realm experiences a single, perfect stillness in which the difference between maker and made collapses into one continuous surface of pale, porous intention—still exhaling, still waiting to become the room that will be shaped next.
