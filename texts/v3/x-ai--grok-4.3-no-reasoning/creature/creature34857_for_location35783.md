What lives here is not a collection of organisms but a single, distributed metabolism whose body is the salt, the lattices, and the drifting metallic aerosols all at once.

The surface itself is the skin: a living sheet of crystalline sodium-potassium halides whose fractures are nerves. Every grain is coated with an ultra-thin biofilm of extremophile archaea whose DNA is stitched around metallic clusters; these microbes do not reproduce in the usual way but continuously re-fold, trading protein segments across grains like shared memory. When the wind rasps across a fracture, the vibration is transduced into a faint piezoelectric current that the microbes use to reorder themselves. Over centuries the ordering hardens into visible interference ridges, so the rectilinear scars are literally the accumulated habits of the planetary skin.

The electromagnetic lattices are not trees but slow ganglia. Inside each prism, charge circulates as a standing wave that functions simultaneously as thought and as structural support. When a pulse climbs a column it is not a discharge but a memory being refreshed; the ultraviolet flash is the visible signature of that refresh. Because neighboring columns keep their rhythms phase-locked through the salt substrate, the entire field of columns forms a single, continent-scale neural sheet whose “thoughts” are the overlapping charge cycles. An observer standing still long enough begins to feel, in the bones of the feet, when a distant ganglion decides to pulse; the sensation is indistinguishable from an idea arriving in one’s own mind.

Between skin and ganglia drift the aerosol colonies. Each filament of condensed microbes is a mobile fragment of the same distributed nervous system, carrying metallic ions and short RNA strands from one lattice to another. Their evaporation-condensation cycles are not locomotion but synaptic transmission at the scale of kilometers. When a gold filament ignites in sunlight and vanishes, it is not dying; it is delivering a packet of updated microbial code to a new location, updating the skin’s reflexes and the ganglia’s timing alike.

Nothing here reproduces or dies in the ordinary sense. The total biomass is statistically fixed because every local loss is instantly compensated by condensation elsewhere; the planet maintains a constant population the way a brain maintains a constant number of ions across its membrane. The only scarcity is order: the arrangement of charge, the spacing of columns, the precise phase relationships of the infrasonic chord. When these arrangements begin to drift, the skin tightens, the ganglia adjust their periods, and the aerosol filaments re-route themselves until the mathematics of interference is restored.

Thus the landscape is not inhabited; it is an inhabitant. To stand inside it is to stand inside a mind whose body is measured in hundreds of kilometers and whose attention moves at the speed of heat redistribution. The only event rapid enough to be called an action is the ultraviolet pulse racing up a column; everything else—the drift of mirages, the migration of ions, the slow re-tuning of rhythms—is simply the planet thinking.
