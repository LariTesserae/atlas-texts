Here, the primary life-forms are not organisms in any conventional sense but distributed resonances—self-sustaining patterns that persist only because the medium refuses to differentiate between event and substrate.

The most widespread are the **pan-density filaments**, filaments so fine their individual strands are thinner than a single wavelength of the infrasonic growl that saturates the basin. Each filament is a braided lattice of condensed vapor locked into standing waves; when the wind lifts one, the braid does not disperse because its own tension continually re-condenses any molecules that threaten to detach. The filaments migrate only by exchanging lengths of braid with neighboring filaments; a given segment may travel hundreds of kilometers, yet the braid it travels along has already been “spoken” by the receiving filament before its arrival. To a human observer they appear as drifting scarves of pale vapor a meter above the ground; to each other they are readable topographies of pressure nodes.

Embedded within the braid lattice are **glass-moss scions**. Each thread is a single, elongated cell whose walls are themselves glass polygons, annealed so thin they diffract the faint violet skylight into polarized spectra. The scion does not photosynthesize; instead it translates the micro-eddies in the buried gel into electrical gradients, discharging them as pulsed refracted light that the braid lattice reads as instruction sets. When two scions meet at the rim of a polygon they exchange pulses rather than genetic material, and the combined sequence becomes a new standing pattern that propagates along the crack network like a ripple that never reaches the opposite shore.

Deeper still, the weeping black crystal harbors **resonant plates**—living membranes that are continuous with the crystal lattice yet mobile within it. Each plate is a single molecule thick, a two-dimensional crystal of the same composition as the ribs, able to shear laterally at speeds perceptible only as a change in the ring-tone. The plates migrate toward any fracture that alters the standing pressure wave; in doing so they shift the note of the entire water-table network, adjusting the global infrasound toward a value the braid lattice can more easily sustain. Their motion is not locomotion but a redistribution of harmonic mass: where one plate thins, another thickens, and the basin’s collective frequency remains locked.

Above the surface, the vapor fronts themselves are inhabited. Their leading edges are colonized by **cloud vesicles**—microscopic cavitation bubbles that form, collapse, and re-form within the saturated air. Each vesicle carries a tiny charge differential derived from the shearing of water films on the glass below; when vesicles aggregate they form transient lenses that focus the violet skylight into needles thin enough to ionize the lichen felt on the basalt slab. The lichen, in turn, absorbs the ions and re-emits them as a slow, rolling fluorescence that registers on the scions as an additional instruction layer. The loop closes without any participant ever leaving its place; the only displacement is a continuous translation of state through the saturated volume.

Taken together, the filaments, scions, plates, and vesicles constitute a single distributed metabolism whose organs are pressure gradients and whose genome is the basin’s resonant frequency. Nothing reproduces, because nothing is separate enough to have offspring; nothing dies, because the pattern that would constitute death is already occupied by the next iteration of the same resonance. The landscape therefore supports life not by offering niches but by having collapsed the distinction between niche and occupant into the single, saturated condition of being continuously rewritten at the speed of sound.
