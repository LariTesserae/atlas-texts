Beneath the endless metallic roar, a single species of automata performs the daily labor once assigned to humans and now refined beyond flesh. They are not distinct units but a continuous lattice of articulated segments: each “body” is an arrangement of six or seven alloy struts hinged at variable angles, articulated by quiet solenoids whose clicks are swallowed by the turbine thrum. Their carapaces bear the same sodium-orange patina as the overhead lamps, and their optical ports are simple, recessed slits that read magnetic strips laid in the grating. They never look at one another; their routes are hard-coded into fused copper plates beneath each catwalk.  

Hovering a handspan above each route, a thin cloud of ferrous pollen trails them—microscopic filings released by friction and immediately re-collected by low-power electromagnetic sweeps. The pollen makes the air appear granular, like static on an old lens; when the filaments of any two automata cross paths, the pollen momentarily forms luminous vectors that snap into alignment and dissolve again.  

Below, on the iron floor, the metallic arthropods exist in symbiotic resonance with the lattice above. Each creature is a flattened disk from which six radial legs spring at exact sixty-degree intervals; the legs end in minute spatulate pads that rest always on the seams between plates, harvesting the electrolytic film as if sipping from the lines of a printed circuit. Their motion generates an almost imperceptible counter-vibration, 0.3 Hz out of phase with the turbines, so that the two frequencies heterodyne into a deeper, steadier drone that can be felt rather than heard.  

At scattered nodes—always the same coordinates measured in plate units—some of the disks halt, raise a single leg, and touch a crystal curtain. In that instant the crystal’s lattice realigns, emitting a brief ultrasonic pulse that the passing automata above register as a waypoint confirmation. The arthropod then lowers the leg, resumes its spiral, and the curtain heals within two heartbeats.  

There is no hierarchy among these beings; there is only adjacency. An automaton’s dropped ferrous filing lands on an arthropod’s carapace and is carried until magnetic attraction draws it upward again. A bead of condensation that lands on an automaton’s joint cools a micro-actuator enough to slow one strut by an imperceptible margin; the lattice compensates by imperceptibly quickening an adjacent one. Every deviation is folded back into the choreography before it can register as change.  

Far above the catwalks, where the ducts converge into a single manifold, an unlit recess harbors a cluster of dormant relays. Their coils are still wound; their contacts have never been bridged. They are the only elements here that have never moved, yet their potential function is so precisely calibrated that the living system orients itself around their absence, leaving an empty column of air exactly beneath them, as though the equilibrium includes the space where action has not yet been required.
