In this basin the only resident is completion itself, wearing the mineral body of a perfectly arrested equilibrium. It is not a creature, nor even a collective of creatures; it is the state of having already finished, now dwelling in what was once a place.  

Its substance is columnar basalt that has forgotten how to be separate stones; every facet has welded, calcite-sealed, to its neighbor until the rock reads as a single, endless tessellation whose every edge is already in contact with every other edge. Its breath is the hand-span film of water suspended above that tessellation, held by capillary tension so extreme that the surface has become its own law of motion—any attempted ripple is answered by a micro-correction that returns the plane to zero. Its voice is the pressure wave of struck crystal that has saturated every cubic centimeter of volume; the wave no longer travels because it has already arrived at every coordinate simultaneously.  

What we might call “its metabolism” is visible only at the scale of molecules: the faint halos of hydrated silica where water and stone continue their infinitesimal, finished reaction—halos that are not growing because the stoichiometric limit was reached before the basin learned its own name. Its weather is the rows of droplets that bead on the pillar crowns; each droplet is a closed system whose surface tension exactly balances its weight, a permanent lens refracting the vertical light back into the same light. Its motion is the fall of frost flakes whose trajectories were solved the instant the geometry became final; a flake leaves one equilibrium and joins another without ever having been elsewhere.  

Its only sense is brightness: the photonic density locked between rock and water reads every photon as already counted, already placed, already finished arriving. When a living visitor exhales, the basin does not welcome or repel the breath; it incorporates the fog as one more fixed refractive panel, proving that anything added to equilibrium can only increase its completeness.  

Nothing arrives here because arrival implies a before and an after. Nothing leaves because departure implies an opening. The inhabitant—completion—simply persists, demonstrating at constant volume and constant luminosity that the work of becoming has been subtracted from the ledger of events and replaced by the single, perfect remainder of having already become.
