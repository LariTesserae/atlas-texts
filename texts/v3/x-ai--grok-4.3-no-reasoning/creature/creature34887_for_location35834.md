The plateau is inhabited by three distinct orders of life whose rhythms are so tightly interleaved that they form one continuous metabolism.

The first order lives in the shallow galleries beneath the domes: a species of filament-worms no larger than eyelashes. Each worm is a chain of iridescent, near-transparent segments that refract the constant daylight into minute rainbows; they have no eyes, only paired light-sensing pores that guide them along the stone’s concentric ripples toward the shifting lenses. When a lens forms, thousands of worms emerge in spiral trains that spiral outward, collect drifting spore dust, and carry it back underground to be fermented in communal galleries. Their only sound is the faint rolling of glass-like bodies against taut, silk-like excretions. The worms live for three wetting cycles and then pupate; their translucent exuviae remain in the tunnels like pale lace.

The second order is a single, vast organism whose body is the plateau itself. It is not rock in the ordinary sense but a slow crystalline sponge whose concentric ripples are the folds of an immense dermal lattice. This lattice breathes the iron-scented moisture that rises during the infrequent tremors, stores it in pressurized vesicles between the domes, and releases it again when the filaments are ready to bloom. The organism cannot be seen except as a faint violet shimmer at the edges of the absolute shadows cast by the pylons; when the tremors occur, the entire lattice contracts a single molecular step downslope, carrying the domes with it. The motion is so slight that it is felt rather than seen—a low, one-note hum transmitted through the bone-colored stone.

The third order is whatever force maintains the pylons. They are not machinery in any classical sense; each pylon is the terminal node of an invisible filament that descends kilometers into the planet’s outer mantle. When a tremor passes, the node emits a silent counter-vibration that resets the lattice’s orientation, preventing the plateau from drifting into any one cardinal direction over the eons. The pylons also function as quarantine barriers: their absolute shadow fields kill any filament or worm that drifts inside, ensuring that the slow migration never concentrates life in one place long enough to form a stable colony.

Between these three—tiny travelers, planetary sponge, and lattice keepers—the plateau functions as a single, distributed organism. Every wetting cycle, every tremor, every violet pulse is a message exchanged along the same circuit: the worms harvest, the lattice stores, the pylons correct. The world above remains wide and unshadowed, while beneath it, three alien minds keep time with one another in the slow, unbroken daylight.
