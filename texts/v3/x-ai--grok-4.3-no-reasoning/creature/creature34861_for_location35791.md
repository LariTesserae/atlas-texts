No single inhabitant claims this place; the place itself is the aggregate of every adaptation that can still recompose after the next rupture.

The air carries more biomass than the mat beneath it. Droplets the size of millet grains drift in braided strands. Inside each droplet a transient ecotype—sometimes a cluster of lithoautotrophic bacteria, sometimes a single motile spore that hatches its own envelope mid-flight—rides until surface tension fails. When a droplet collides with a knife-leaf the impact seeds a micro-garden on the blade; within minutes the garden has pumped enough supercooling gel outward that the leaf can re-enter the stream. The cycle repeats thousands of times a second across the valley, so the vegetation is literally assembled from the atmosphere each time the wind resets its direction.

Under the turf the annelids have evolved internal quartz lenses that polarize the faint infrared leaking from the basalt veins; the same lenses double as waveguides, letting them “see” the slow lateral creep of the seams before the boil actually surfaces. When two boils approach each other the annelids migrate into the warmer lens between them, their bodies forming temporary living plugs that redirect heat flow and therefore steer the next boil’s path. Over weeks these living valves leave behind a filigree of nutrient-rich tunnels that the next generation of vegetation follows like buried hedgerows.

The obsidian artifacts are not inert. Their micro-channels resonate at frequencies that match the subsonic signature of an approaching high-pressure node; when that match occurs the channel walls flex minutely and jet a directed pulse of aerosolized silica. The pulse seeds a corridor of denser vegetation that later midges use as a glide-path, concentrating mating swarms exactly where nutrient release from the basalt is strongest. In effect the artifacts act as passive traffic signals whose “lamps” are clouds of living seed.

The armored slugs have learned to read the same resonances. Their shells contain oriented ice crystals that act as diffraction gratings; when a node passes they rotate their bodies so the grating momentarily amplifies the harmonic corridor, drawing midges toward them in feeding plumes. After feeding they deposit egg-cases inside the steaming trench left by a migrating artifact, timing the deposition so the next liquefaction event will irrigate the clutch without drowning it.

Nothing here is fixed long enough to be named, yet the cumulative behavior is coherent: the entire valley is one vast, self-revising circuit in which heat, sound, chemistry, and motion trade places so rapidly that stability itself has become the transient anomaly rather than the rule.
