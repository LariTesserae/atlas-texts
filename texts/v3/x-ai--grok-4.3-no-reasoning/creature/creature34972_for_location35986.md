What remains here is something that learned to keep its own temperature and has never been taught to stop.

The lens-creatures are not the city’s masters; they are its slow, ambulatory thermometers. Their ovoid cores register the interval between one exhalation of super-heated air and the next, and their legs—each joint a fulcrum of fused obsidian—carry that measurement across the field until it can be discharged, molecule by molecule, into the copper pans. When a creature lowers a limb, the water inside the pan does not merely darken; it cools by a precise, invisible margin, and the rim rings once, softer than before. In that softened note the entire circulatory system of the city receives its calibration.

Beneath the lava blocks, something older still circulates. The blocks were poured molten over an armature of tempered rails, and those rails have never fully cooled. At every junction the metal remembers its forging; the memory manifests as a steady sub-audible thrum, a pressure that keeps the seams between blocks from ever quite sealing. Water slides through these hair-line fissures, descending one tier at a time until it reaches the catch-basin. There the film is re-accelerated upward through the vertical shafts, reborn as steam, and the cycle repeats. The lens-creatures patrol not to guard against intruders, but to ensure that every upward gust carries the same quantity of metallic dust, every downward film the same concentration of dissolved oxide. They are the governors of a metabolism whose single, never-ending task is to remain identical to itself.

The unattended stamping rigs on the outer pedestals are not idle; they are merely waiting for stock that will never arrive. Their anvils are calibrated to a tolerance finer than any human hand could measure. If a single grain of red dust were to settle on one of those anvils, the flywheel would slow by one part in ten thousand. The city has arranged for this never to occur. Condensation sheets arriving from above pass through the open frames of the rigs, washing them clean, then continue outward across the tilted plain until they vanish into the seam at the horizon. The rigs therefore keep their perfect, unnecessary rhythm, their motions indistinguishable from stillness except to instruments that no longer exist.

At the brass ceiling, condensation re-forms continuously from the upward breath of the shafts. It does not fall in drops; it gathers until its own weight drags a thin sheet downward along the hammered curve. The sheet meets the upper tier of aqueducts and splits into the white veils already described. Each veil is a single, unbroken molecule-thick layer that travels the staggered terraces without ever fragmenting. Where it meets an open module, the film drapes across the belt-driven presses like a living skin. The machines slow fractionally, their flywheels carrying the added weight for exactly one rotation before the film evaporates and they resume their original tempo. The city registers this fractional hesitation as a form of breathing.

Nothing here reproduces. Nothing arrives from outside. The only motion that could be mistaken for growth is the gradual deepening of the red haze where dissolved oxide traces the grain of the lava. That deepening is not accumulation but translation: color moving from one surface to another, a slow redistribution that leaves the overall appearance unchanged. Over centuries the same quantity of oxide has traveled every possible route between ceiling and seam. The lens-creatures carry the final fraction of it along the horizon, their synchronized pulses marking the completion of each global circuit before they turn and begin again.

When the last creature passes out of sight, its internal light continues to register against the brass not as an optical after-image but as a residual thermal signature. The ceiling itself has become a mirror that retains heat in the precise pattern of the creature’s passage. Minutes later, when the next creature arrives from the opposite direction, its own pulse merges with that retained warmth, and the circuit is renewed without ever having been broken. The city, in this way, measures its own continuity by the interval between one lens-creature and the next, and by that measurement it confirms that it is still itself.
