What lives here is a population calibrated to the same tolerances as the city itself.

Most inhabitants are technicians, surveyors, and horticulturalists who treat their own bodies as another scheduled system. Their skins are pale from never meeting unfiltered sunlight; fine capillary webs show through at wrist and temple, traced by the same pale green that stains their gloves when they touch the fern trays. They wear modular coveralls whose pockets are sized exactly for torque wrenches and nutrient test strips; when the garments wear out they are recycled into insulation batting rather than discarded. Their speech is a clipped lexicon of pressures, lux values, and cycle counts. A person who begins to lengthen sentences is quietly reassigned to drone calibration until the habit fades.

A second cohort, smaller and less uniform, tends the gliders. They carry portable electrostatic readers instead of spanners, and they read the angle of each translucent wing as if it were a dial. Over years the membranes begin to echo the technicians’ movements; a human arm extending toward a conduit is answered, several seconds later, by a matching tilt in a nearby flock. These handlers sleep in truss-side hammocks whose mesh mimics the graphite lattice; their dreams are said to contain brief, silent flights between numbered stanchions.

Far rarer are the Cartographers. They appear singly, always at intersections that were not on yesterday’s schematic. Each carries a slate whose screen shows only blank tolerance bars until an anomaly is logged; then a thin blue line crawls outward, recording the corridor that ought not to exist. Cartographers never stay in one place longer than a single work cycle. When they leave they step through bulkhead doors whose serial numbers have already been reassigned to another corridor. No one watches them depart, yet their absence registers as a micro-shift in local air pressure, as though the city inhaled.

At the extreme edges, where walkways terminate above the grey haze, dwell the Margin Keepers. Their faces are shielded by reflective visors whose curvature repeats the arc of the city’s under-curve. They speak to no one, yet every few rotations a thin filament is lowered into the haze; when it is reeled back in, the attached sensor spool shows temperature, particulate count, and—occasionally—a single, unreadable glyph. The Keepers file these readings under codes that do not appear in the master index. Their gloves, unlike everyone else’s, are stained with a darkness that never dries.

Collectively these residents constitute an organism whose metabolism is maintenance. Their pulse is the drip of condensate; their breath is the calibrated exhalation of the vents. They do not reproduce so much as replace themselves on the same rotation schedule as the lamps. When an individual’s cumulative lux-hours exceed tolerance, they are retired to the lowest inhabited deck. There they tend the nutrient salts whose slow color shift marks the only acknowledged time. Their final task is to record their own departure interval on a slate that will later be collected by a Cartographer, whose blue line will draw one more corridor that no map contains.
