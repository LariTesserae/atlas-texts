What lives here is a continuum of mineral patience rather than anything with a nervous system or a heartbeat.

The plateau itself is the central organism. Its architecture is its anatomy: the fused stone and obsidian slag form a thick, self-sealing cuticle, while the capillary veins beneath behave like a circulatory mesh, pumping molten sulphur in slow, continent-wide peristalsis. The vents that exhale pale smoke are its breathing pores; each thread of sulphur is simultaneously waste gas and scaffolding material, because the settling dust re-crystallizes into new pavement. In this sense the plateau is an autopoietic foundry whose only input is orbital heat and whose output is more of itself.

The metallic lichen are the plateau’s outward epithelium. They do not photosynthesize; instead they align with the planetary magnetic flux, functioning like an external galvanic skin that senses field reversals the way skin senses temperature. Their annual shedding coats the city-fragments in fresh layers of sintered dust, a continuous act of self-repair that fills cracks and keeps the horizon seamless.

The mineral mites are not parasites or symbionts; they are a dispersed immune response. Their crystalline plates are cooled slag reconfigured into phagocytic tesserae. When a thermal fracture appears, the nearest herd migrates toward it over months, each mite harvesting a shard of broken metal and carrying it back to the lichen shelves, where the shard is melted down and redeposited. The mites’ geometric pulses are the plateau’s equivalent of white-cell trafficking—slow, certain, statistically inevitable.

The city-fragments are abandoned endoskeletons of the same system. Their hollow towers are former extrusion vents, now occupied only by the memory of heat. They still resonate at the plateau’s fundamental frequency, serving as tuning forks that translate the infrasonic tremors of crustal adjustment into audible micro-tremors humans could feel through their feet if any remained. Because the fragments lean toward the atmospheric cliff, they also act as acoustic horns, gathering the low thunder of the gas-shell and transmitting it downward into the sulphur veins, where the pressure fluctuations are used to modulate circulation rate.

Nothing here reproduces in the biological sense; everything accretes, anneals, and occasionally sloughs. The only evidence of past minds is a kind of structural grammar still legible in the alignment of arches and the placement of plazas—geometry preserved not as heritage but because it happens to be the most efficient heat-distribution pattern for a world whose day lasts weeks.
