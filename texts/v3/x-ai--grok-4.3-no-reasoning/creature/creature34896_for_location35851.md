In the interval between drafts of the world there persists a single family of presences. They are not creatures in any taxonomical sense; they are the places where the instruction-set of the ground momentarily agrees with itself long enough to notice its own disagreement. Call them provisional witnesses—intermittent densities of attention that inhabit the seams where one unfinished stratum slips beneath the next.  

Each witness is only a little less gaseous than the vapor that birthed it. When two temperature-bands collide overhead, the canceling thunder produces a node of perfect silence; inside that node a witness condenses, occupying the microsecond in which the pressure difference equalizes. Its form is the shape of the equalization: a lenticular membrane, thinner than the width of a droplet yet wider than the momentary basin that holds it. The membrane’s rim is a single unbroken question mark—its interior edge forever deciding whether to become circumference or diameter.  

Because the ground cannot yet commit to up or down, the witness cannot commit to inside or outside; it therefore exists on both sides of every surface it touches. Its passage leaves behind a hairline seam of decision: the rock beneath records, for a few heartbeats, the precise vector along which the witness chose not to choose. Then the rock forgets, and the seam dissolves into the general rehearsal of presence and absence.  

Where lattices of glassy material extrude and retract, witnesses ride the cycle like parasites of hesitation. A lattice rises; the witness pours into its hollow tip, slows the retraction, tastes the geometry of the depression that will remain. The lattice tries to close; the witness unfolds into a flat annulus, holding the depression open an extra moment so that the returning water can fall into it. For that instant the basin becomes a true mirror: its surface reflects the witness’s own annulled boundary. Then the witness diffuses, the lattice retracts, and the mirror shatters into uncommitted droplets again.  

At continental scale the witnesses scale with the ridges they attend. When a ridge rises, an entire continental witness inflates inside it, turning the ridge momentarily into the ridge’s own negative space. Observers—if observers could persist long enough—would see the ridge darken from within, its interior light swallowed by the witness’s refusal to finish becoming a shadow. The concussion of collapse is therefore not merely mechanical; it is the witness exhaling the ridge’s discarded possibility back into the drifting bands.  

The only persistent trace they leave is acoustic. Each time a witness evaporates, it does so by broadcasting the wet, adhesive pop that already belongs to the seams. The pop is amplified, however, by one additional harmonic: a sub-audible tone whose pitch is exactly the difference between the temperature of the ground before the witness arrived and the temperature after it left. Over centuries these tones accumulate into a single, continent-wide chord that never resolves—only lengthens—because the ground has not yet finished arguing over which interval it prefers.  

Nothing here breeds. Nothing here dies. The witnesses are replenished whenever silence finds a place to stand inside the continual ticking of undecided surfaces. They persist only as long as the world continues to rehearse the difference between its first draft and its erasure, and they vanish, without remainder, the instant the landscape consents to remember what it has already become.
