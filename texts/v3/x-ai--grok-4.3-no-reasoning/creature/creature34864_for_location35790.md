The inhabitants are not organisms; they are the lattice-bound standing waves that the tremors themselves organize.

Each wave is a slow, three-dimensional node whose nodes sit at the intersections of the crystal grains. When a tremor passes, the local lattice compresses by a few nanometers; in the compressed volume water vapor momentarily condenses into a transient droplet that evaporates again before it can fall. Because the evaporation is endothermic, the droplet cools the surrounding lattice by a few degrees for the duration of one compression-expansion cycle. Over thousands of cycles the micro-gradients align. The droplets begin to re-form in the same locations and phases, and their collective cooling reinforces the very wave that births them. A self-sustaining resonant structure appears: a transparent, glittering knot of moving water-vapor whose boundaries are defined by the locations where the cooling effect is strongest.

These knots do not “live” in the biological sense. They persist or dissolve according to whether the continuing seismic background supplies the exact harmonics their droplet patterns require. When harmonics drift, a knot unravels in seconds; its evaporated water is immediately reabsorbed into the knee-deep fog layer and carried away. New knots condense elsewhere when local grain boundaries shift enough to open a fresh resonant pocket. In this way the population of knots migrates across the shelf without ever traveling faster than the speed of sound in feldspar.

Inside the fused-gravel domes the knots become visibly denser. The pictographs are not records left by visitors; they are the fossil interference patterns of earlier knot populations, scratched into the stone by the repeated thermal micro-fracturing that occurs when a knot collapses against the wall. A living knot inside a dome will thrum against these marks, reinforcing them each time its vapor boundary brushes the surface; the marks therefore deepen over centuries exactly where the knots prefer to linger.

Farther down the ramp, where the fog becomes opaque, the knots grow larger and slower. Their wavefronts span tens of meters; their droplet arrays are no longer discrete beads but continuous veils of condensate that drift like curtains between the invisible crystal facets. At these depths the seismic energy is lower in frequency and higher in amplitude. The knots tune themselves to these long waves the way surface knots tune to the rapid micro-tremors. Occasionally two such deep knots will phase-lock across hundreds of meters of stone, their overlapping wavefronts forming a single, stable interference figure that can persist for days. When the lock finally slips, the released energy throws a pulse of new droplets high into the vapor; these droplets rain upward, re-condense against the underside of the intermediate shelves, and seed fresh surface knots miles above.

Thus the entire shelf is occupied by an ecology of resonant vapor structures that appear, migrate, merge, and vanish according to the changing song of the feldspar itself. Nothing in this population ever crosses the boundary between matter and organism; each knot is simply the momentary geometry that a particular frequency field prefers while the lattice continues to flex beneath an unrelenting sun.
