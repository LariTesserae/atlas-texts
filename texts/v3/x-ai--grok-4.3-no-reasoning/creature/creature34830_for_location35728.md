A miniature ecosystem keeps the Passage functioning at its low, persistent level.  

The liverworts and the single pale fungus are anchored to the only dependable moisture schedule; they divide the meager light without touching one another, each patch a few centimeters wide and no more. Their slow metabolism produces a faint, earthy odor that mixes with the metallic draft coming up from the throat and never leaves. When the droplets fall they land at the gutter seam; the millipedes rise out of hairline cracks like animated grains of rice, drink as they travel, then vanish back into darkness before the next drop gathers.  

Above them, the rust-colored spider maintains its single thread beneath the conduit. Each rust flake that falls from the pipe becomes both meal and repair material; the spider never leaves its elbow and never spins more than the one strand. Once every quarter-hour, when the warm draft surges, the cluster of translucent isopods appears, feeds in a thirty-second blur, and retreats; their synchronized departure turns the crack into a momentary silver seam and then into nothing again.  

The mechanical interruption runs on the same clock as the biology. The hand-crank gearbox, salvaged motor, and short length of oxidized belt form a self-contained duty cycle. When the thermal switch allows power, the belt advances a single tooth, raises a whisper of dust, and stops; the dust settles before the next cycle begins. The millipedes alter their path to skirt the settled film, and the spider’s thread vibrates once with the metallic click, yet none of these events change the posture of any other resident.  

Farther in, the scored grooves mark where something large and blunt has been hauled through the throat repeatedly, then stopped. Beyond the last mark the passage continues at the same cross-section; the warm metallic breath continues to arrive from it; the light, the drip, the small lives, and the interrupted machine all remain suspended at the density they achieved when the larger motion ceased.
