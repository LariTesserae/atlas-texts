A single maintenance skeleton, ferrocrete-grey polymer stretched over knobby titanium spars, has fused itself into a service niche where a collapsed duct once required the reach of an arm. Its joints are immovably seized, each actuator packed with the same oxide flakes the thumbnail grazers sift from the insulation; in the places where polymer skin has torn, the lichen has bridged the gaps like scabs of rust, turning the machine into a lumpy extension of the corridor itself. It cannot move, yet every few decades a thermocouple housed in its heel joint still fires a redundant safety subroutine, sending a quiver through the lattice that the walls translate only as a whisper of thermal strain.

Where the black water lies three storeys down, a slow, transparent meniscus of perfluorinated oil—leaked from an ancient rotary actuator—floats on the surface like a negative mirror, never mixing. On nights when the thermocouples can gather no voltage, the faint green of the plankton refracts upward through this lens and paints on the ceiling a liquid chart of micro-currents that only the skeleton’s dead optical array records.

In a side spur too narrow for even the lichen’s coin-sized colonies, the corridor’s ferrocrete skin has delaminated from the steel in a single hanging flap the length of a forearm. Inside the airless pocket lives a minute membrane of vacuum-adapted archaea: they metabolize only the slow release of hydrogen from the corroding rivets, exhaling methane that condenses on the inner surface as a reflective frost. When the pressure equalizes and the flap flexes, the methane ice fractures into glittering micro-dust that the corridor’s breathing rhythm eventually wafts into the main passage, where it settles on the glass floors and feeds a second, even slower bloom beneath.

At the extreme terminus, where every pipe has pinched shut and every conduit dead-ends in a blind bulkhead, there is a single rivet that never lost its factory-applied zinc coating. Over centuries, zinc atoms have migrated outward, forming a crystalline bloom so pure it refracts the infrequent red LEDs into a faint violet corona. The thumbnail grazers avoid the rivet; their silver trails halt in a perfect radius five millimetres away, as though some forgotten subroutine in their electro-sense still recognizes a surface too alien to graze.
