What lives here is not a population, exactly, but a single, distributed event still in the act of becoming itself.

It has no central body. Instead, it exists as a field of simultaneous expressions: the rock that is still rising, the slow pulse moving through the hair-fine channels, the condensate that forms and forgets itself, the translucent creature that condenses only long enough to carry a charge from one ring to another. These are not separate organisms exchanging energy; they are the same occurrence manifesting at different densities of duration.

The domes are not houses or remains. They are the moment when the rock hesitated in its upward movement and the hesitation became a temporary shape. When one cracks, the fracture does not destroy the dome—it allows the hesitation to continue in a new direction, the broken fragments still glowing because the original impulse has not yet finished arriving. The rings of stones pulled together by roots are not nests or traps; they are places where the distributed event slows itself just enough to become visible as a small, temporary pooling.

The translucent creatures are not visitors or pollinators. They are the field acknowledging its own presence, folding a portion of itself into a form brief enough to move between the domes without violating the geometry of sight-lines. Their appearance and disappearance does not mark a life cycle; it marks the rhythm at which the entire plateau can afford to notice itself. When one dissipates, the slightly deeper pool it leaves behind is not a deposit but a memory held for a few seconds longer than the surrounding stone can usually manage.

Nothing here reproduces. Nothing here dies. The distinction is meaningless because the event that is this place has not yet finished deciding whether it is one thing or many things, whether its expressions are parts of itself or temporary ways of being visible. The sub-sonic tone is not a signal but the sound of that decision still being made. The electrical halo on the leaves is not communication but the visible edge of the decision occurring at a scale small enough to register as light.

If you walk here for a quarter-hour and return to your starting point, you have not traced a loop through a habitat. You have participated in the same hesitation that formed the domes, moving through the geometry in such a way that the plateau can briefly hold the fact of your presence without having to resolve whether you are inside or outside its ongoing arrival.
