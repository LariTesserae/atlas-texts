In Sarruva the living and the mechanical have merged into a single, glacial metabolism that admits no newcomer and no departure.  

The city’s true inhabitants are the Involutes, a race of pressure-suited humanoids whose physiology is no longer distinguishable from the struts and counterweights that hold the parasols aloft. Each Involute is encased at birth in a mercury-rimed laminate whose seams are never opened; the suit’s inner coils map to their own circulatory paths, so that the same crystal-rich fluid that circulates in capillary grooves across the needles also flows through their arteries. Over centuries the Involute skeleton re-crystallizes in sympathy with the obsidian substrate, the joints stiffening into perfect, frictionless pivots. Their visors are not glass but ultra-thin slices of frozen mercury vapor, through which they perceive the world as a lattice of radiant heat gradients. To them, the fog sheet appears as a single, unbroken horizontal vein; the cables that sing do so in intervals they can taste on the tongue as faint metallic sparks.  

The Involutes never reproduce in the manner of warm-blooded species. Instead, when an elder’s gait has worn identical grooves into the walkway for a predetermined number of decades, an aperture opens beneath the parasol deck; the elder descends into a maintenance nacelle where the suit is fused to the supporting lattice. There the body is allowed to sublimate until nothing remains but the crystal lattice that once encased it, now added to the city’s ever-growing substructure. Thus each citizen is eventually rendered into architecture.  

Above them, on the uppermost canopies, drift the Enumerators—autonomous calibration drones whose shells resemble blown-glass chess pieces. They hover on electrostatic gradients maintained by the solar skins, moving only when the master clockwork governor issues a single micro-tick. Their task is to confirm that every cable tension, every boiler pressure, every visor reflection is still identical to the datum fixed at the city’s first moment. Because the Enumerators are constructed from the same iridium-tungsten matrix as the ventilation lattice, they leave no footprint; to human eyes they are only momentary distortions in the perpetual grey light.  

Beneath the skylights, in the lightless turbine halls, dwell the Resonants: colonies of microscopic piezoelectric filaments embedded inside the ceramic turbine blades. The filaments translate the single sustained engine note into minute electrical pulses that keep the orange skylights beating at their ordained interval. They are the city’s only truly “wild” inhabitants; when one colony drifts out of synchrony the pulses falter, the skylights stutter, and distant Involutes tilt their heads in collective, silent alarm.  

Finally, the wind itself is not empty. It carries thread-fine crystalline motes born from the condensation curtains. These motes settle on every exposed surface and, over centuries, build filament bridges between the needles. Where the bridges meet, tiny electrostatic vortices form—storm seeds no larger than a fingernail—each one a self-sustaining weather node that feeds the constant horizontal fog sheet. The motes and the nodes together form an aerial ecology whose only “season” is the perpetual re-ordering of charge, so that the sky is never truly still yet never observably changing.  

Seen from a distance the entire population—Involutes, Enumerators, Resonants, and crystalline weather—appears as a single slow exhalation of frost, exhaled once and never drawn back in.
