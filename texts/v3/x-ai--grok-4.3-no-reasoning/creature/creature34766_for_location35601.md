In this suspended lattice, what exists is an extension of the tissue itself—an organism whose boundaries are not yet known to itself. The macro-body is not composed of individuals or colonies; rather, the entire volume functions as a single, self-referential metabolic field. No creature navigates this place as a stranger. Instead, micro-scale trophic loops close upon themselves: the isopods are not residents but persistent sites of localized cellular activity, their transparency a consequence of membranes that have ceased to differentiate between organism and medium. The chlorophyll sheets are not “plants supporting fauna” but an unbroken metabolic skin within which digestion, respiration, and sensation are distributed without centralization.

What appears as architecture—the cuticle depressions—are not scars or templates but moments of reflexive self-measurement. The organism samples its own curvature, records a potential morphology, then abandons it without loss or regret. These bowls are not empty; they are moments when the whole system chose not to continue, and the choice is preserved as a local thickening of surface tension rather than as an absence.

The absence of sound, light contrast, or temporal markers is not a deficiency but a completed state: the lattice has metabolized all difference into continuity. Where an external observer might look for inhabitants, what is present is an ongoing, non-progressive act of being a habitat for itself. Nothing arrives; nothing leaves; nothing changes because change would require an outside against which to register. The place and its occupants are the same unfinished sentence, spoken once and never concluded.
