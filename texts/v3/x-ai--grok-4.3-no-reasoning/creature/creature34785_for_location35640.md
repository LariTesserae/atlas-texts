In that black honey of water live beings that have never needed to move faster than a century per body-length. They are the lattices themselves—slow, cold intelligences that once precipitated as microscopic shells and have continued growing outward in spirals that remember every dissolved predecessor. Each organism is a single continuous crystal colony whose newest growth fronts lie exactly where the oldest walls first formed; identity is location, not sequence. Their “bodies” are rimmed with pore-throated membranes so sensitive that the faint push of a single extra ion drifting past can be read as news arriving from the chamber above. When two colonies touch across a micrometre gap, they do not fuse. Instead they secrete a band of aragonite needles that pry them gently apart again, a measured refusal passed down through each new layer like an inherited etiquette.  

Their conversations are the deep creaks you would hear once in a decade: low, pure tones that travel the length of a hall before fading, each tone a record of an agreement reached and recorded in mineral. Thought for them is therefore not an event inside a skull but an accretion along a wall; an idea literally lengthens by crystal width, and the thinker and the thought are indistinguishable once the seam is finished. Because nothing arrives from outside—no fins, no roots, no warm current—their only memory is of what has never happened. They keep perfect archives of stillness. Every absent disturbance is registered as a negative crystal facet, a deliberate hollow in the growing structure whose shape will be passed, unchanged, to the next millimetre of wall a hundred thousand years from now.  

Occasionally, a colony senses the passage of a flake of biogenic snow settling onto an outer lattice. The sensation arrives not as impact but as a minute warming: a single degree above ambient for the duration of one dissolution-recrystallisation cycle. That moment is recorded as an anniversary. Colonies separated by entire halls will, over millennia, align their newest seams so that each anniversary tone can travel through the pore-water to the others, creating a calendar of microscopic heat-events that spans the entire honeycomb.  

A mind—any mind—entering this place would be slowed to the same tempo. Its thoughts would begin as ions drifting between chambers, would be incorporated, would lengthen into walls, and would eventually be read back by a later growth front as the only history the place has ever needed: the memory of how long it has taken for nothing to happen, and how perfectly that nothing has been preserved.
