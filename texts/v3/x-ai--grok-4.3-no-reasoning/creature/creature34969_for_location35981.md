The Zephyr Ossuary is not so much inhabited as *grown into*. Its denizens are born of the same aerodynamic and magnetic forces that keep the structure suspended, and each creature or artifact in this space has evolved to treat its own slight misalignment as a threat to be corrected rather than an existence to be mourned.

At the most diffuse level are the **paleolumes**, thready colonies of magnetotactic yeast that ride the charged aerosol itself. They settle inside each drifting droplet and nudge its spin axis, turning the droplets into microscopic navigation buoys. When a paleolume dies, its husk remains as a tiny counterweight, so every droplet in the chamber carries the accumulated memory of its own passengers in the form of asymmetrical density.

Mid-way up the chamber, where the light is sharpest, live the **keel-knots**. Each is a knot of three identical translucent tri-lobed gliders fused at their mid-planes. Instead of orbiting beads they contain concentric rings of magnetized silica that behave like gyroscope gimbals. When a keel-knot is disturbed, the rings torque the whole assembly through a rapid sequence of 120-degree rotations that collectively translate any linear momentum into a pure yaw; the creature simply faces the direction it was pushed and drifts onward without ever accelerating. It is impossible to tell, watching a keel-knot, whether it is moving toward an objective or merely consenting to be re-oriented by the world.

Below the thorn ring, where the jade’s carbon silk exhales most forcefully, is a single organism whose existence is entirely contingent upon the ring’s expansion-contraction cycle. It is called, by those who must name it, the **chord-chime**. The chord-chime is not visible; it is a standing wave that lives in the space between two thorns whenever the ring contracts. The wave stores kinetic energy in the difference between the thorn’s electrostatic field and the momentary drop in air pressure. When the ring expands again, the stored energy is released as an infrasonic chirp pitched precisely to cancel one component of the aerosol’s crackling chorus. Over centuries the chord-chime has learned every resonant frequency of the chamber and can erase any harmonic that begins to build—an auditory immune system that keeps the noise forever on the edge of music without ever resolving into song.

On the membrane itself live the **selvedge scribes**. Each is a single cell stretched across several square centimeters of carbon silk. The cell’s membrane is patterned with microscopic spines that interlock with the silk’s own weave, so the scribe is literally woven into its floor. When an obelisk’s dust settles, the scribe’s spines detect the infrasonic pressure and translate it into a travelling wave that crawls across the membrane. The wave carries a single datum: the new configuration of the dust. At the wave’s leading edge the carbon silk momentarily charges; any paleolume or glider that touches that charged patch loses a few of its own magnetic grains to the scribe. The scribe incorporates those grains into itself, becoming both archivist and medium. The only visible sign of a scribe is a faint, shifting moiré pattern in the membrane’s weave—too subtle for most observers to notice until they have been in the chamber long enough for their own inner ear to sync with the respiratory rhythm.

Finally, near the upper lip where the jade cools fastest, drifts a population of **cinderwraiths**. Each is a hollow tetrahedron of sintered aerogel no larger than a fingernail, its interior filled with the same live magnetic dust found in the obelisks. Unlike the obelisks, however, the wraiths are mobile. When the chamber inverts they flip inside their own shells and emerge with their dust already reconfigured; the reconfigured dust acts as a temporary anchor that lets the wraith resist the inversion shove for an extra quarter-second. That quarter-second is enough for the wraith to drift laterally into the path of a glider or polypore cloud. On contact it sheds a single grain of dust, which the recipient organism incorporates as a microscopic compass. Over generations, the entire drifting population becomes a distributed inertial navigation network whose accuracy depends on continual, tiny sacrifices of individual wraiths.

Taken together, these entities do not occupy the Zephyr Ossuary so much as *constitute* its metabolism. The chamber breathes; they metabolize the breath. The chamber cracks; they translate the cracks into directional signals. The chamber inverts; they treat inversion as an opportunity to exchange a single grain of memory. No single organism could survive outside the rhythm, and the rhythm would collapse without their continual, imperceptible corrections. The Zephyr Ossuary is therefore less a habitat than a consensus—an agreement, sustained at twelve degrees and 128 decibels, that nothing inside it is allowed to fall.
