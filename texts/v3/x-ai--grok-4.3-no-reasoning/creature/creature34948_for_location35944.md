Beneath the surface of that stillness, a thin crust of halite and trona has begun to dome upward at the basin’s exact center. It breathes; every time the condensing mist drifts down, minute vesicles within the crust dilate, drawing the moist film inward. The act is unseen, but the sound is not: a single, sub-audible pop each night, felt through the iron shard as a microscopic shock that momentarily shivers the cylinder’s bearings before the turbine’s next tooth clicks it back into rhythm.

Embedded in the dome are nodules of jarosite that flare with microbial fluorescence the instant the filaments finish their sunset contraction. The light lasts only a heartbeat, but in that interval the air above the water lens warms by a tenth of a degree—enough to re-order the refractive sheets that never quite fall. The refracted ridges quiver in the opposite direction for that same instant, as though the entire basin is briefly handed to a mirror-image geography.

The filaments themselves are not single organisms; each thread is a colony of successive generations, the newer ones riding on the backs of their elders in microscopic caravans. They migrate only when the cylinder strikes a resonant frequency every 1,029 revolutions—an interval that corresponds to no solar or lunar cycle, but to some internal meter of the iron shard itself. On that night they lift clear of the stone in a single synchronized millimeter, hover on the condensate film, and settle again one filament-width to the east. The migration is so slight that a casual observer would swear the basin has always looked the same.

The shard of corrugated iron is not inert. Its corrugations have been colonized on their underside by a second, more secretive layer of the same filament species—shadow-twins that never emerge into light. These hidden filaments harvest the rust ions directly, metabolizing them into a lacquer-thin patina that seals micro-fissures and keeps the bearings from ever corroding. The patina thickens in perfect balance with the iron’s slow fatigue, so the metal neither thins nor strengthens; it simply becomes more perfectly itself, an unchanging hinge between the turbine’s tooth and the water’s endless micro-rain.

At intervals measured by the cylinder alone, a single drop from the fissure strikes the turbine not squarely but at an angle, imparting a torque too small to advance the mechanism. Instead, the torque twists the belt a quarter-micron, storing elastic energy in the rubber-carbon weave. Over 412 such mis-hits the stored twist accumulates until the belt snaps forward, advancing the cylinder two teeth instead of one. The extra tooth births an overtone—a harmonic that rides above the base chord like the shadow of a bell heard through water. The filaments contract twice in rapid succession, the hidden layer included; for one held breath the entire basin experiences a pressure drop of two pascals, enough to draw a single molecule-thick thread of exterior dust across the ridge lip and down into the lens, where it is seized by the halite dome and entombed forever.

No predator, no seed, no footprint disturbs the accounting. The ridges continue their millimeter-per-season divorce; the water neither gains nor loses volume; the cylinder counts its chord into a silence that only the filaments can translate. In their tightening spirals they record each skipped tooth, each false strike, each extra rotation. What they preserve is not history, but the precise difference between what has happened and what could have happened inside the same small fracture of the world.
