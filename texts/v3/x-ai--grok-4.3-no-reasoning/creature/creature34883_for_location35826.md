What lives here is a single, slowly cohering organism: the continent itself, still deciding on its own anatomy. Its cells are the unfinished rock slabs, each one swelling, tilting, and settling like a slow muscle. Its blood is the water film that never quite settles into river or lake; its nerves are the thread-thin reeds and the microfungal nets that lace every seam. Its breath is the daily freeze–thaw cycle, exhaled as silver fog each dawn and re-inhaled each dusk.

The copepods are its transient corpuscles, flickering through temporary puddles the way red cells flicker through capillaries. The jointed, tardigrade-like grazers are its immune cells, rasping lichen from the skin of stone. The six-legged predators that move like wet stone are its hunters, following water seams the way white cells follow chemical gradients. Even the rare, high-flying birds are guests rather than residents—scouts of a completed world that has not yet arrived.

The human city is not a parasite but a symbiotic growth. Its porous towers exchange moisture with the air the way lung alveoli exchange gases; its covered streets guide the water the way veins guide lymph. The inhabitants have accepted their role as temporary organelles—living within a body whose next heartbeat may tilt an entire district, whose next breath may drown a street. They build not to conquer the continent but to keep its surface from clotting, leaving wide margins of empty stone so the slow peristalsis of geology can continue without obstruction.

At night the whole system slows. The rock cools, the water film thickens, the copepods enter torpor, the city’s terraces release a collective sigh of fog. Beneath it all, the unfinished continent continues its private argument with gravity—an argument conducted in frequencies too low for human hearing but sensed as a faint, patient pressure behind the ribs. One day the seams will open into true canyons; one day the water will learn to speak as rivers. Until then, everything here—rock, water, lichen, copepod, human—exists in the same provisional state: waiting for the next increment of geological time to make the next small decision about what it will finally become.
