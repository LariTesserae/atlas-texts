In the first hours it is almost possible to pretend the space belongs to the visitor alone: the heart-beat cadence of the drip, the faint vapor plumes from the fungal shelves, the low blue-white haze of the lamp-moss. Only after the eyes adjust does the scale of the colony become visible. Each fungus is studded with dozens of smaller, milk-white sporophores whose gills quiver when a drop lands; each sporophore hosts a miniature garden of the same transparent algae that sheets the walls, so the bone-colored shelves are in fact veined with living light.  

Tiny snails are never still; they glide in wavering trails that cross and re-cross one another until the whole surface is mapped by silver script. When a new drop strikes, the nearest snails halt, their shells contracting into mirrored droplets that mimic the splash. The moment the vapor rises they resume, tracing the same paths again as though refreshing an inscription.  

The storage organs darken at different rates. One may already be charcoal while its neighbor is the colour of weak tea; the split ones are not empty once the hair-roots emerge. A secondary membrane, parchment-thin, forms across the fissure and draws inward with each city-breath, acting like a miniature lung. Inside that membrane, microscopic filaments pulse, exchanging dissolved salts with the fluid film that coats the vascular wall. The roots themselves branch invisibly, weaving through the existing mesh until, from a certain angle, the chamber appears filled with suspended frost.  

The membrane at the entrance thickens where repeated contacts have worn it. In those places the vein-patterns darken to lilac; the living tissue there has begun to mineralize, turning the opening into a slowly tightening sphincter. Any intruder who lingers long enough will eventually need the city’s permission to leave, or else must cut their way out through what has already begun to think of them as food.
