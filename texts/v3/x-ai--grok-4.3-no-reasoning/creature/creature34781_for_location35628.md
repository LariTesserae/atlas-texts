The inhabitants are the Symbiont Generation—descendants of the original crystalline engineers who abandoned terrestrial biology for a composite existence bound to the lattices themselves.

Their skin has thickened to a translucent dermal mesh laced with cobalt threads identical to the veins of the towers. Every few minutes their skin re-plates, replacing microscopic abrasions with fresh metal precipitated from the sliding films. Faces are almost featureless: where mouths once were there are only fine slits sealed by a vibrating membrane. Communication occurs through thoracic resonators pressed against another’s sternum, each word arriving as a low-frequency syllable felt rather than heard. Their eyes are not organs but a constellation of photo-conductive nodes embedded just beneath the skin at temple, clavicle, and wrist; when one looks at you, several points of their body brighten in shifting sequence, giving the unsettling impression that their attention is distributed throughout their form.

They move without limbs in any traditional sense. Instead, they secrete a temporary exoskeleton of interference nodes around their bodies, allowing brief adhesion to the shifting membrane streets. When they must cross open vertical shafts they form into chains—each person attaching to the next through brief electrostatic bridges—so that one hundred individuals can become a single, flexible cord that drifts on the convection currents between towers. When the chain dissolves at its destination, the individuals simply step sideways out of the last link, as though walking offstage.

Internally, each person carries a small lenticular drone—the same species as the city’s fauna—grafted into the peritoneal cavity. It is not a parasite but a shared metabolism: it stores the electrical charge harvested from the membrane discharges and releases it in measured pulses timed to the city’s global resonance. When a person sleeps, the drone takes over the maintenance of heartbeat and respiration, so the population’s pulses are literally interchangeable. During moments when the artillery-like relief discharges sound, everyone’s grafted drones fire in perfect synchrony, granting the city a simultaneous half-second of cardiac silence that is felt collectively as a single, city-wide skipped beat.

Reproduction is a form of controlled crystallization. A pair will press sternum resonators together for several hours until their cobalt vein-networks begin to synchronize at the molecular level. A droplet of metallic condensate forms between them and is guided along a crystal vein to one of the lower membranes. Over weeks the droplet accretes into a new infant lattice-node—essentially a miniature, translucent version of a tower. When it reaches human scale it fractures along predetermined planes and the newborn emerges already wearing the dermal mesh and carrying its own internal drone. The first sensation the infant experiences is the city-wide resonance entering its thoracic cavity, so the child is born already unable to distinguish its pulse from the architecture that surrounds it.

Their society is organized around the daily repair cycle. At dusk, when the orange vein-maps appear, work crews of twenty to thirty Symbionts attach themselves to the glowing traceries and become living deposition scaffolds. Their dermal mesh precipitates fresh metal directly onto the stressed crystal, guided by the interference patterns of their own bodies. During these hours the entire city slows its breathing cycle; the membranes sag a few millimeters more than usual and rebound more slowly, producing a temporary bass note that everyone feels in their bones as a communal exhalation.

They have no art that is separate from function. Music is the interference tones themselves, tuned by the placement of new membranes. Sculpture is the residual shape left in the metallic glass after a cargo transit has sagged a district; the ripple becomes permanent when the inhabitants choose not to cancel it, turning yesterday’s traffic into today’s ornament. The only ritual that resembles worship is the collective cardiac pause during artillery discharge: for that half-second every grafted drone is silent, and the Symbiont Generation stands together in the sudden, unaccustomed quiet, feeling the architecture breathe alone.
