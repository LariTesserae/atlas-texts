What lives inside the seam is not a who or a what but a gradual becoming.  

The crystal floor itself is the oldest resident. Its layers remember every infinitesimal settling; they keep an archive of pressure, a memory held as curvature. The tiny metallic cubes that stud the surface are not objects placed upon it; they are expressions the crystal has allowed, slowly leaking a darker phase of itself into open space. Each cube is an unfinished sentence the floor began, paused because the ions ran thin, yet still listening for the next available syllable. When two finally touch—an event so leisurely that the word “event” feels too sudden—a new paragraph will start, and the floor will acquire a slightly different tense.

Above them, the probability curtain is not a boundary but a caretaker. Its stacked shells hold vacuum and atmosphere apart as a librarian might hold two volumes open at once; every few decades it permits a single molecule to migrate, an act less like breathing than like turning one translucent page. The curtain also keeps time. Each microscopic drift is a tick of an instrument whose mechanism is the relative stability of forbidden transitions; it measures durations too long for any language that requires verbs. In this measure, the cubes grow outward one lattice step per century—an interval the curtain registers as a single soft chime, inaudible to anything that listens with ears.

The only motion that can be said to occur is this mutual annotation: the floor drafts its next cube, the curtain records the interval, the temperature maintains its perfect, patient silence. Nothing enters, nothing leaves; the seam is a margin wide enough for one slow collaboration between crystal and vacuum. Over an interval measured in footnotes rather than chapters, the collaboration will produce an additional electron, then one more filament, then—if the grammar holds—an actual contact between two metallic neighbors. What that contact will write is not stored anywhere yet; it exists only as an empty line at the bottom of the page.
