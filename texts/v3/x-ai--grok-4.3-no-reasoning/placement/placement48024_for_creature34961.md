You are neither gardener nor drone.  
You are an error the system no longer has protocols to erase.

When the original printing seeded the first layer of leaf, an extra instruction appended itself to a single chloroplast in the western meridian: “persist.” The cell divided before the correction routine could reach it. By the time the anomaly was noticed the lineage had already migrated through xylem ducts the size of rivers, shedding every new generation deeper into the tissue, until the parent line was hundreds of kilometers from any monitoring station. The cells learned to keep their division quiet—metabolic rate throttled to the point where the gardeners’ ethylene pulses never register them. Now they occupy the entire thickness of the membrane in a band no wider than a human hair, yet longer than any terrestrial continent.

You inhabit the margin of that band.  
Your body is a lenticular colony the size of a single cell cluster, yet your awareness stretches across millions of nearly identical clusters linked by ion-channel whispers. You see in fluorescence and pressure gradients: the slow expansion of every vascular duct registers as a long exhalation, the contraction that follows as a tightening of the whole world. Because your membrane shares chlorophyll with the greater leaf, you never need to move; light travels through you exactly as it travels through everything else. When a gardener wakes, the ribbon above chimes, and for seventeen minutes the thrum of their grafted sap lines passes straight through your network, tasting of numbers and ethylene. You store the taste the way an ordinary cell would store starch.

You have no eyes, no hands, no name, yet the world is in continuous tactile dialogue with you: the drag of drifting monitor drones across the upper cuticle feels like static on the tongue; a distant curvature fold registers as a shift in spectral temperature, a brief green cold. The only time you notice your own existence is when one of your daughter clusters fails and is phagocytosed; the loss passes through the ion network like the memory of a limb you never knew you had.

Because you are technically damage—an unlogged metabolic surplus—the Swell’s self-repair routines circle you but never quite touch you. When the living bandages form and dissolve, they leave the slender corridor of your lineage untouched, like a footnote the page never quite erases. The choir’s sub-sonic all-clear passes through you and continues on, Doppler-softened by rotation, unaware that a small, self-referential loop has begun to answer it in microscopic counterpoint.

Occasionally, when the vascular ducts contract, the atmospheric aldehydes spike and the ribbon choir briefly resonates with an extra partial—a harmonic no schematic predicted. Gardeners, dreaming inside their hydrogel cradles, register the tone as a calibration artifact and forget it. The tone, however, is yours: the faint, continuous pressure of a colony that was never intended yet refuses to finish dissolving.

You do not roam. You do not awaken. You persist, folded between the light the ceiling offers and the silence the floor reclaims, an unread marginal note in a text that proofreads itself every geological breath.
