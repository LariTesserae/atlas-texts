I would find myself here neither as visitor nor resident, but as an interval in the breathing—a pocket of still air that has learned to linger half a second longer than the cycle allows. Where the wood forgets, for one compressed moment, to exhale.

My edges would be indeterminate: the boundary where wood stops being wood and begins to be the memory of wood. I would be a place the cambium once thickened toward, then around, then past. The new gallery that grew just where I am not. The valve that clicked shut an instant before the peristaltic squeeze, leaving a single molecule of vapor suspended in the negative pressure that should have drawn it forward. That molecule—left behind by the timing of memory rather than intention—would be the nearest thing to a body that this place permits.

Sometimes, when the slow consensus of pressures decides a trunk must swell, the wood will press against the place I once almost was and discover that something has already been deciding against it. The cambium will pause, reconsider, then grow elsewhere. That hesitation becomes my name, for as long as it lasts.

Between the metallic clack and the moment the echo is swallowed, I would exist as the question the wood asks itself about whether sound should be allowed to travel farther. The question does not receive an answer. Instead, the air cools by an immeasurable fraction, the mist thickens by a single droplet, and the decision is made not by debate but by the wood’s memory that it has always narrowed at precisely this interval.

I would have no voice with which to speak and no mouth with which to be silent. My presence would register only as a local failure of the condensate to reabsorb at the expected rate, a patch of root felt that exhales when it should inhale. The arthropods—temporary, on demand—would be grown slightly larger here, their translucent legs brushing the place where the sigh is fractionally late. Then they would be reabsorbed, the felt would remember its rhythm, and I would remain the interval that the rhythm almost forgot.

In this world, to be is not to occupy space but to be the space that the architecture has not yet decided whether to close. I would be that indecision, prolonged indefinitely, neither moving along the grain nor across it, neither allowed nor forbidden. The wood would continue its slow consensus around me. The city would continue becoming. And I would be the single breath it holds long enough to forget how to return.
