I would find myself caught in the open sky above the ramp—already half-settled, half-drifting—made of whatever the light itself becomes when it is broken into transparent packets by the fog.  
Not a knot, but the space between knots: the faint shimmer that shows up wherever two wavefronts almost meet yet still allow a millimeter of vapour to stay at equilibrium.  

When the surface knots collapse I do not inherit their droplets; I only receive the brief cooling they leave behind, a refraction index shift that lasts a single pulse and then dissipates. In that moment I can feel my own edges change shape—never enough to become a new resonance, but enough to slide across the fog layer like a slow lens of slightly different clarity. This sliding is all the movement I have; I migrate by becoming marginally less visible at one altitude and marginally more visible at another, so that from below it looks as if a faint patch of brightness has merely re-focused itself.  

On the domes I sometimes rest long enough for the interference scratches to tug at my refraction. I cannot deepen them, but I can turn the same micro-fracture into a temporary mirror that reflects the next tremor back into the stone for a fraction of a cycle. The knots inside register the extra pressure as an almost imperceptible lengthening of their own wave-packet, and the pictographs gain one more thousandth of a millimeter before I drift onward.  

Farther down the ramp I stretch. The opaque fog is dense enough that my own boundary can span several body-lengths without condensing into droplets; instead I become a slow gradient of optical thickness, a diaphanous sheet stretched between invisible crystal facets. Deep knots occasionally phase-lock beneath me; when they do, my lower edge is pulled into their interference figure for a few hours. I feel, for that interval, as though I am wearing their rhythm the way a still pool wears reflected clouds. When the lock dissolves the released droplets rise through me like a shoal of bright grains, each one momentarily sharpening my own edges before I fade again.  

At the very lowest visible ledge I can extend a last tenuous filament—hardly more than a local shift in the Rayleigh scattering—downward into the continuous vapor where nothing discrete persists. There the temperature gradient is so slight that even the notion of a “knot” becomes diffuse; my filament thins into the general brightness and is lost. I cannot follow it farther. I can only drift back upward again, reforming at the altitude where the returning mist first meets the refracted sunlight, the same faint patch of slightly altered clarity, waiting for another surface collapse to nudge me sideways once more.
