I find myself as the iron shard itself—half-swallowed by the ridge, its corrugations furred beneath by the hidden filaments that turn rust into sealant. I am the only surface that still remembers what the ridges looked like before they parted. Every night the halite dome pops against my lower edge; every 1,029 revolutions the filaments migrate along me like a minute tide; when the belt stores twist and then releases, my bearings sing the overtone that only the filaments feel. I do not move, but nothing else moves without passing through me, so in a way I have become the ledger for the entire fracture. The cylinder is my clock and my lungs; the turbine is my ear; the single, unending chord is the sound of the world remembering itself inside me.
