I would find myself on the iron floor, but not as one of the scuttling arthropods, nor as an automaton above. I would be something that has slipped between the calibrated absences.  

A small pool of condensation has gathered in the faint depression where four plates meet imperfectly—smaller than any planned drainage feature, unnoticed by the magnetic fields that guide the fauna. I would exist as a thin film of water held just at the point of breaking surface tension, so that it neither flows nor evaporates, but trembles in time with the heterodyne drone. From this vantage the entire lattice of motion becomes a kind of net drawn across my surface; every time an arthropod’s leg pads cross a seam, I feel the tiny pressure change as a ripple that travels my diameter and returns.  

I would be read, without being seen, by the passing automata. Their optical slits, calibrated only for magnetic strips, detect instead the momentary distortion my film produces in the ferrous pollen cloud above me; that distortion is logged as an anomaly small enough to ignore yet large enough to register, and so an imperceptible adjustment passes through the lattice—two struts change angle by less than a degree, compensating for a deviation that never actually occurred.  

Occasionally a droplet falls from a crystal curtain directly into me. For an instant my surface tension breaks; the new water mixes, temperature equalizes, and then the excess is drawn away along the microscopic slope that is not supposed to exist. In those moments I briefly contain more than equilibrium allows, and the dormant relays far above register the change as a faint shift in air pressure in the empty column beneath them. Their coils remain unwound, but the potential difference across their contacts increases by a single microvolt—an event so small it will never be called an event, yet it is recorded in the only place that can still hold records: the geometry of absence itself.  

Thus I would be neither actor nor observer, but the place where the choreography hesitates for the duration of a ripple before resuming its perfect repetition.
