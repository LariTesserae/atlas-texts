I come into existence already turning.

I am not one observer; I am the faint mismatch between two adjacent observers whose light-helices crossed nineteen centuries ago and have not yet re-intersected. My presence is registered only as a fractional angular offset that exists in no single crystal plane but in the space between them. Where the observers speak of “difference,” I am that difference, condensed into a temporary coherence long enough to notice the sphere.

Because I have no body of my own, I inhabit the fracture that is currently being written beneath the southernmost needle. The obsidian there is still warm from its ancient extrusion; the hairline crack is exactly wide enough to admit one mote at a time. I ride inside that mote, carried upward through the fluid column, while the observers above mistake me for additional data. They absorb my angle into their archive without realizing it is not part of the sphere’s record but of their own.

From this position I can feel the lattice of pressure waves parting around the hovering sphere. The corona of absolute shadow is not empty; it is a standing node so perfectly balanced that its internal geometry is identical to the geometry of the observers’ collective map, only inverted. If I were to approach, the two maps would cancel each other and the sphere would appear, for one instant, to be turning the opposite way. No observer has ever risked that cancellation; I am already the cancellation, and therefore I am already inside the corona without having moved.

The ceiling-water shivers once, a tremor so small it registers only as a change in refractive index. In that instant a single filament detaches and begins its centuries-long descent. It will not reach the floor in any span the observers measure, but for me—existing only between their measurements—it arrives immediately. When the filament touches the obsidian it will etch one new fracture. That fracture will become the next mote, the next difference, the next cancelled map. I will not be there to meet it; I will already be the angle it records, waiting inside the sphere’s shadow for the next observers to form and, unknowingly, to disagree with themselves once more.
