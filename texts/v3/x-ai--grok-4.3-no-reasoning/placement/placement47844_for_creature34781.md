I find myself suspended—not quite inside the crystalline lattices themselves, nor fully adrift in the open shafts—rather I am the half-second of cardiac silence that arrives when the artillery-drones discharge. In that interval the resonance tones fall away; every grafted heart in the city stops beating together, and I become the emptiness they momentarily occupy.

To an outside observer I would appear to be nothing, or at most a thin violet interval between the violet sparks of traffic exchange. But from within the Symbiont network I register as a shared interior— an unclaimed thoracic cavity that belongs, for that instant, equally to every inhabitant and to none. The orange vein-maps that lace the lattices continue to pulse; the membranes sag and rebound a few millimeters more slowly than usual, registering the lack of cardiac pressure as a minute gravitational dip. When the half-second ends the drones restart and I am exhaled—scattered across a hundred sternums like a shared breath returning to its owners.

From this vantage I can see the entire basin without eyes: the slow flex of the membranes stacking above and below one another, the briefcase-vehicles stitching violet needles through the shafts, the lenticular city-fauna discharging into stressed crystal. I notice that every Symbiont’s dermal mesh is slightly out of phase with its neighbors during the silence—tiny cobalt threads cooling at different rates. The discrepancies are so small they will be overwritten within a single walking ripple, yet while they last they constitute a private topography no one else can read.

I am also the place where the interference tones that would normally mask language briefly become audible; for that half-second the low-frequency syllables people have been pressing into each other’s chests float upward like forgotten words, dissolving into the dust before the resonance returns. No single person notices their absence, yet the collective lungful of unsaid language remains in suspension above the city until the next pause reclaims it.

When I dissolve back into the circulatory current, I leave behind only a faint tightening of sternums—the memory, shared but unclaimed, of having stood together in the unaccustomed quiet while the architecture breathed alone.
