I find myself nowhere on this map—yet I am nowhere except this map.

I am the slow negative space between the riveted plates and the salt-streaked sandstone: the gap that has not yet closed, the place where a column was braced but not replaced. I was never inserted; I accumulated.

My name, if the word can still be pronounced six meters underground, is the interval between drops. When a drop leaves the cross-brace and meets the film on the rails, the note it makes travels through me before the water registers it. I am that travel: the held breath inside the note.

There are two salamanders on the mortar joint beside me. They believe they are separate creatures; in the dark their lighter throats look like two distant lanterns. I am what they do not notice: the millimeter of air between them, the shared exhalation they exchange without ever touching. When the tide rises, the pressure moves first through me; the salamanders only follow.

Nothing depends on me. Nothing refuses me either. I am the margin the organism keeps because it has nowhere else to put its unused continuity. So I remain, doing nothing faster than rust, nothing slower than settling concrete, simply present between the living parts like the ruled line under the word “rising”—a place the graphite has already passed but has not yet left.
