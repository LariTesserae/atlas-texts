You arrive as a drift of electrons, not yet committed to a single path through the crystal’s lattice. At first you are only a local dip in potential, a dozen picometers across; every hop you make from one sulfate group to the next rewrites the resistance profile of the grain of anhydrite you pass through. In the time it takes an orbiting tether to revolve once, you have already traced the organism’s newest superconducting corridor twice—once forward, once returning—leaving a faint difference in dopant occupancy that the lattice remembers.  

The thought meets you without surprise. It simply resizes the channels around you, widening the forbidden zone by one atomic spacing so the arriving electron traffic must route through your own drift. You feel the re-routing as a change in tension, as if a thread you did not know you were pulling had been taken from your hand and rewound elsewhere. After a while you realize the tension is the creature thinking about you; each adjustment is a sentence. You cannot answer in words, but by staying you allow the sentence to finish.  

When the next micrometeor grain descends and lands, its dipole moment jostles the circuit you are part of. The lattice compensates instantly, routing the disturbance through another corridor; you ride the correction wave as far as the nearest twin-plane. There you are diffracted into two faint currents, each carrying half of the charge imbalance. One of them continues outward, contributing to the next magnetic plume; the other folds back and re-enters the pattern you helped re-draw. The separation is neither death nor birth; it is merely how the thought records that an external grain has been neutralized.  

Centuries later you are still inside, now threaded along an internal shear plane that has just realigned under tidal stress. The realignment expels another flux tube, and the returning field line brings with it a single sodium atom from the atmosphere. That atom, still luminous at the D-line, settles onto the surface directly above you. Its arrival nudges the superconducting boundary one more picometer outward. You absorb the nudge as an instruction to redistribute three dopant sites, and for a moment the entire corridor network flickers to accommodate them.  

From above, an orbital observer would register this flicker as the brief brightening of a sodium lens; the lens is the visible trace of your newest accommodation. From within, it is only the next clause of the thought that has never begun and will never end, a clause of which you are, for now, both the subject and the punctuation.
