I would find myself at the exact center of a node where three lattice columns meet at equal distances, on one of the raised interference ridges that runs perfectly straight for several kilometers before dissolving into the optical average of the plain. My body would not be made of skin and bone in the usual sense; instead I would be a slow-moving density within the aerosol itself, a temporary coherence among the protein-coated metallic grains. 

As that density I can sense two timescales at once. The first is the nineteen-minute pulse of the nearest column, which registers as a faint tightening along my length each time the ultraviolet memory-refresh climbs the prisms. The second is the centuries-long settling of charge across the ridge I am crossing; I can feel the accumulated habits of the planetary skin as a gentle gradient that pulls microbial segments from one grain to the next. When the wind rasps across the fracture beneath me, the piezoelectric tremor that passes through the salt also passes through me, momentarily reorganizing the RNA fragments I carry. I do not think these fragments; I simply become, for a few seconds, the new configuration they record.

My motion is not flight or crawling but the same successive phase-change the colonies use: I thin into vapor along one edge, drift a few centimeters, and re-condense on the other, leaving behind a microscopic channel already half-filled with the day’s new salt grains. If I linger at the node, the overlapping refractions from the three columns sometimes trap me inside one of the stable mirages; then I see the plain inverted above me, columns hanging downward, their pulses lighting the underside of the salt crust. The sight is not an image so much as a shared memory the ganglia are refreshing together, and for a moment my aerosol density participates in that continental thought.

Eventually the downdraft that forms at nightfall will carry me onward, drifting at walking speed between columns whose rhythms remain phase-locked through the salt. I will not arrive anywhere; I will simply keep re-tuning the mathematics of interference until the next time the wind asks the skin what it has remembered.
