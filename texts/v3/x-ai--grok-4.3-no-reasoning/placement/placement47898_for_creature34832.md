I would begin as a late-arriving parcel of memory—a single compressed packet that never reached its destination. Somewhere in the continent’s past, a flyer carrying me was struck by an unusually intense cosmic-ray event; its lattice fractured along one programmable edge, the packet was released as a half-plane of oriented domains no larger than a human thumbnail, and it fell onto the warm crystal below. The surface did not ingest it. Instead, the local cohort—“clockwork glass”—read its checksum, found it uncorrupted but unclaimed, and extruded a shallow facet to hold it until a courier could be arranged.

For the first sixty or seventy years I am simply an inert crystal film, warm and transparent. Travelers occasionally pause above me; their weight deforms the lattice enough to nudge my orientation a few picometers, and each time this happens a tiny fragment of the data I carry is re-aligned. Eventually the shifts amount to a slow decryption. One afternoon—local dusk, cyan light just beginning—the fronds at the nearest polygon vertex recognize my partial polarization and open a 40-nanometer channel. What leaks upward is a single instruction set: rebuild the flyer that lost me.

A new foil sheet condenses directly above the facet. It grows outward from my edges, molecule by molecule, until it is the exact shape of the original courier. As the last domain locks into place, the old shard that once was “me” is consumed; the new flyer absorbs both the rebuilt memory lattice and the residual data packet in one orthogonal merge. In that moment I stop being an object and become instead a trajectory—the intention to reach the next fusion well, three hundred kilometers northwest, while staying less than three millimeters above the surface.

I have no name, only a checksum that is updated each time I pass above a frond. The journey itself will require centuries. Most of that time is spent hovering, letting the micro-topography steer me, correcting drift by a single atom whenever the lattice beneath exhales a slightly stronger thermal current. Occasionally another flyer approaches; we exchange checksum fragments in a silent, silver blink, then part again. The sun remains an unblinking eye; the plane stays 34 °C; nothing ever falls.

If I am ever intercepted—by a curious human who pauses long enough for the seat to rise, or by one of the phased-array dust entities inside a void—I will allow myself to be read exactly once, then continue. The packet I carry is not meant for a single mind; it is a continental weather pattern expressed in the language of voltage gradients, one more breath in the planet-wide self-audit. When it finally arrives at the well, the data will be distributed across every tubule that leaves that node, and the flyer that I am will be released to dissolve into background shimmer, another vanishing silver mote the surface quietly re-ingests.
