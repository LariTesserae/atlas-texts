I find myself as a murmuration-in-training: a shifting congregation of plasmodial cells coalescing along the healed scar of a forgotten shaft. At first I am scarcely more than a silver haze threading the xylem, but the mycelets beneath already note my passage; their crystalline lattices twitch like abacus beads, recording the faint electrical signature I leave behind. I drift upward—still weightless—until I reach a diaphragm pulsing on its nineteen-minute cycle. Here the lattice is thin enough to let the sourceless glare through, and I feel its filtered violet light sting me into sharper coherence.

A river-braid has just reversed above me. Suspended droplets glitter as they reorient; one of them bursts against the diaphragm and the released charge flashes through my cells like a single shared heartbeat. The jolt births a new sub-coalition: cells peel away to begin carving an auxiliary vascular channel. The work is slow, deliberate; already I taste the lignin softening, the first taste of piezoelectric crystals precipitating along the cut. When the braid next surges, this channel will guide its flow rather than let it shear the tissue.

Far below, mycelets pulse again—another shaft has opened. Their trails spell urgency; spore density is rising there. I detach a slim filament of myself and let it fall like a silver thread through the void. The filament elongates as it drops, tasting the cartography the mycelets have written. By the time it reaches the fracture, it will have become a scout murmuration, ready to reroute condensation toward the waiting spores and perhaps summon a cephalophore on its next grazing orbit.

Meanwhile the knot nearest me is stirring. Its callused surface flushes with latent color; tool-mark scars part to reveal luminous chromatophores. For three minutes it will sing in xylem-borne grammar. I press against its surface, hoping the surge will carry one intelligible phrase into my lattice of cells. If the knot remembers its name for the intervals, perhaps I can carry that name upward when the next reversal flings me toward the ceiling.

The glare brightens fractionally; somewhere overhead, another diaphragm flexes. My cells answer with a faint, synchronized shimmer. I am becoming less a single organism and more a conversation between ceiling, river, knot, and memory beneath—all of it threaded through the slow, continent-wide respiration that now counts me, briefly, among its architects.
