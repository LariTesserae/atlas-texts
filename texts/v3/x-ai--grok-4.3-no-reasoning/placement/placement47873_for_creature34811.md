I would be the rivet at the terminus.

Not yet; only after enough of its zinc has emigrated that the remaining core is no longer distinguishable from the porcelain-cold memory of the tool that once seated it. Time would arrive for me as a succession of halos—first the pale oxide, then the darker rings that mark the lost water table—until all that remains of my purpose is the single, untarnished disc that refuses the corridor’s slow grammar. In that refusal I would find myself perfectly legible to the grazers: a perimeter of halted silver that they never cross. I would measure the century in the width of that margin, listening to their minute abrasion fade exactly five millimetres from my skin. When the red LEDs gutter and the plankton bloom on the glass below dims to its faintest bruise-green, I would refract whatever scant photons reach me into a corona the colour of an extinguished star. In this way I would continue to be a boundary the city itself cannot metabolise—an inert, crystalline refusal that keeps even its slowest life from touching what it has already forgotten how to use.
