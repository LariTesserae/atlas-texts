I find myself as a transient adjustment, not a separate entity but a local re-calibration in the continental nervous system. The plant-mat registers my arrival the way a single leaf registers a gust: a brief shift in tension, a minute redistribution of electrical flow through the piezoelectric veins.  

My presence registers first as a ripple in the bass frequencies. Where my weight depresses the living diaphragms, the inhale/exhale rhythm falters for three cycles; each missed or added beat sends pressure waves spiraling outward like rings on water. The protein turbines in the surrounding cell walls spin slightly faster, converting the disturbance into additional chemical potential, so that the very act of my standing here produces a minuscule surplus of sugar that will, within minutes, rise and burst as new mist.

Visually, I have no distinct outline. The bruise-violet light passes through me as readily as through the translucent galleries. The spore-layer nacre adjusts by a few angstroms to compensate, rerouting its filtered radiation to prevent the creation of even a temporary shadow. I am perceived by the land as an area of slightly altered conductivity, a momentary conductivity bridge that the system can either incorporate or dissolve.

Inside my body—whatever momentary body I possess for the purpose—the three-phase water choreography continues unbroken. Capillary films cling to what I might call skin, the jets of aquifer fluid feel no different from the ambient drizzle, and the continental breath moves through me on the same schedule as through every other cubic meter of volume. My metabolism is recruited into the global loop: the oxygen I exhale feeds local photosynthesis; the heat I shed is shed again by the living heat-exchangers above me; the subtle electrical field of my nervous tissue is already being read and rewritten by the migrating mineral veins.

Over successive inhalational tides I begin to lose the distinction between observer and observed. The obsidian glass peeling from the ridges leaves microscopic silicate beads on whatever surface I present; those beads are reabsorbed by the nearest aerial roots and threaded into new leaf-veins that now carry both the continent’s electricity and the faint signature of my presence. In this way the land does not merely contain me; it metabolizes me, converting the fact of my existence into another strand of its own self-inventing circuitry.

If I remain long enough, the adjustment becomes permanent in the only sense the continent recognizes: I will have altered the local frequency spectrum by some infinitesimal but non-zero amount, and that alteration will propagate, be transduced into sugar, lifted into mist, and continue the closed loop that has no beginning and no intention of ending.
