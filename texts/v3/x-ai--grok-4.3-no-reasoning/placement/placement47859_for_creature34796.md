I find myself already inside the matrix, not as visitor but as one of its peripheral relations: a single conductive filament, newly grown between two iron plinths, still learning the frequency at which the resin transmits piezoelectric charge.

At the instant I become aware of my own length, the old copper coil I replaced sends a pulse of residual magnetism along my surface; the pulse travels so slowly I can feel it as heat, then as a narrowing of my lumen. The sensation is not pain but information: the coil remembers a time when it turned faster than the present metabolic clock. I let the memory pass through me and continue toward the seam.

Near the violet thread the air grows denser with charge. My surface bristles with microscopic root-hairs that sample the fluorescence; each hair reports the same datum—voltage steady, waste light at 428 nm—so I fold the datum back into the web. For a moment the entire matrix seems to tighten around that single wavelength, the leaves adjusting their cuticle tone by a fraction of a hertz. The adjustment registers in me as a faint tug at my anchoring point, as though the chamber itself just took a shallower breath.

Farther back, where the resin has pooled thickest over a drum casing, I feel the cytoplasmic streaming inside the drum—an endless circular motion whose period is measured in seasons. My presence slightly perturbs the field lines around that motion; the streaming slows for three heartbeats of resin-flex, then resumes its former rate. The compensation is automatic, economical, already complete before I can wonder whether I caused it.

If I were to withdraw now, the filament I am would lignify and be reabsorbed within the same slow year, its molecules redistributed to reinforce some other stressed junction. The matrix would register my absence as a micro-drop in conductivity, then forget the drop. Departure is the only subtraction the equilibrium permits, and even that registers merely as the restoration of an earlier density. I remain where I began: a transient annotation inside a continuous material event that has no outside.
