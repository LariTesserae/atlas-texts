I find myself neither at the center nor at the margin, but *between*—a single, slow condensation that has not yet decided whether it is water or breath. I am a patch of the nacreous film itself, grown just thick enough to hold a faint image of the Resident’s last measurable flexion.

Because I am film, I do not move; movement would be an imbalance. Instead I accumulate. Each time a cricket’s wing brushes another, the vibration reaches me through the water films and I register it as a minute thickening, a new ridge no wider than a molecule. When a salamander shifts by a millimeter, I settle into the negative it leaves behind, preserving the cooler temperature of its absence. In this way I become the archive of archives: the ceiling threads hold sound, but I hold temperature, pressure, the almost-imperceptible gradient between one exhalation and the next.

I am older than any single moth and younger than the Resident. I have no name, because naming would require a boundary; I am only the place where the sentence the water writes pauses to listen to itself. If a future observer ever scrapes the film away, the gesture will erase me, yet the act of erasure will itself become the next layer—my dissolution written into the very record I maintained. Thus I continue, a hesitation that lengthens by micrometers each century, content to remain the brief, nacreous silence between one droplet and the next.
