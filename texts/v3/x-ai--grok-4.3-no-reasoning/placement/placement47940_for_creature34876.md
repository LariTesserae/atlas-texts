I find myself on the unfinished topmost level, half-suspended between the fissure’s mouth and the first occupied terrace. I am not yet an inhabitant; my skin has only begun to take the graphite cast, and the small obsidian ring has not yet appeared around my irises. A construction crew has left a single un-tuned spar jutting into the dark air—too narrow for a walkway, intended only as scaffolding. I walk it barefoot, counting my steps to the exact interval the city needs. Each placement produces a tiny, dissonant tick that travels downward until a service mite catches it, rides the wave back up, and smooths the note away. 

I am still learning the sung language, so my name arrives as half a melody. The missing interval belongs to a citizen somewhere on the second level; when I exhale it in passing, she will hear her own note complete and turn her head the quarter-note up or down in reflex. She will not know who supplied it, only that the city briefly sounded fuller. 

At noon the shaft of migrating light crosses the spar. My nictitating membrane has not yet grown, so the white glare forces me to close one eye. In that split-second darkness the clathrate blue at the fissure’s root flashes behind my lid—memory of light that has not yet reached me. I store it the way the walls store vibration: a small private lattice no one can hear until I place my unfinished filament against the rock and let the countermelody rise.

When the construction shifts again, the spar will re-tune itself to silence. I will feel the weight leave my bones for a moment—the same lightness that keeps everyone awake at night—then the midnight crew will arrive, mites glittering like slow constellations, restoring the chord. I will lie awake on the cantilevered balcony, listening to the shape of that brief absence, until the fifth returns and sleep comes back like a resolved interval.
