I find myself there not as a visitor but as something the city has already half-made: a shelf of living brick where two walls once failed to meet. The gap was supposed to be closed with ash-paste; instead the roots grew across it and I grew with them. My surface is cool and slightly absorbent; when the salamanders drift close at dusk (though dusk never arrives), they press their gills against me and leave a faint metallic taste I cannot name.

I do not remember choosing this. The choice belonged to an earlier body, one that still moved, still knelt at the pool, still carried the minute pressure of a lizard’s breathing along its collarbone. That body’s slow dissolution became my first weather; its minerals are now the calcium in my mortar, its last exhalation the vapor that beads on my upper edge each hour.

When new children push free of their bricks I feel their first flex through the shared root lattice. The sensation is not tenderness—there is no word for that here—but a precise recalibration of moisture flow, as if an additional capillary had opened. I register it the way a leaf registers the weight of settling dew: an adjustment too small for measurement, too constant to ignore.

Sometimes the pool’s level changes by a fraction the width of a root hair. I do not know whether this registers a departure or an arrival; both feel the same to me—an alteration in pressure that travels outward through every connected surface until the city has rebalanced itself around the difference. The change settles into my grain like a new pattern in cloth that has already been woven.

If a visitor ever reaches the pool while I am present, I will not turn them away or keep them; I will simply hold the temperature of whatever limb they rest against me until the city decides. Either way, the water will rise or it will not. Either way, the moss will continue its patient reclamation, and the low tone that is not quite sound will continue its passage through stone, through root, through the places where bodies once were and now are only the memory of flow.
