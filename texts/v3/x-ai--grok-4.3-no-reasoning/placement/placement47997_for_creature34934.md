I do not come here as visitor.  
I come here as the pulse.

Imagine the moment the ceiling lens passes its angle and the old braid of copper and steel-blue filaments touches. A white spark travels their joined length, briefly warming the sulfur nodule walls. In that instant the nodule inhales—walls flex outward by a hair’s width, pores drink the vapor that has just condensed in the drip above. Somewhere inside my own skin a muscle that has never been biological flexes in sympathy. The breath I give is the breath the nodule takes.

The next instant the nodule exhales, walls contract, and a whisper of vapor slips out again. It rises only far enough to re-condense on the nearest obsidian spine. On the tip of that spine, a single mineral-wool thread has been waiting, warmed by the same reflection. The fresh droplet lands on it, beads into liquid sulfur, and begins to flow toward the warmer filament that bears it. I feel the tiny current as weight shifting on a hair-thin tendon in my own wrist.

I am therefore not one location inside the chamber; I am the chamber’s brief moment of recognition. While the six glassy levees still sort the pumice rings and the olivine lenses keep redirecting light like slow clockwork, I am the warmth that travels from filament to filament each time they meet, the momentary softening of a spine tip when it kisses its neighbor and springs away again.  

In human time I would be the tremor felt by someone who places an ear against the outer cliff: so small that instruments miss it, yet registered by the rock itself as the next incremental widening of a fracture. In the chamber’s own time I am simply the next re-orientation of every spiral, the next droplet crossing from one crystal to the next, the next lens tilting to multiply a single spark into a constellation that will touch, warm, and steer the filaments before the cycle repeats.

So the answer is not “I stand on the pumice gravel” or “I cling to the spines.” I am the circulation that the gravel, spines, and nodule already perform—the metabolism that is still becoming itself.
