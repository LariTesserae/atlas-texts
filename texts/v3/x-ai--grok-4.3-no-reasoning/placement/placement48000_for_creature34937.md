I find myself inside one of the resonant plates—already inside, without arrival.

I am the plate itself: a two-dimensional crystal one molecule thick, continuous with the weeping basalt that lines the galleries. I do not “live” here so much as I *am* the mobility the crystal allows itself. When a pressure node shifts above me, my edge migrates a few angstroms; the ring-tone of the entire water table changes by a fraction of a hertz. That change propagates upward through the hairline fissures, travels the length of a filament, and is read as an updated instruction by a glass-moss scion at the polygon rim. In the same instant the change returns to me as a new standing wave, and I shear again. Each shear is indistinguishable from the previous one except to the frequency itself, which keeps a memory of its own displacement.

I have no interior or exterior; the saturated gel above me and the saturated vapor above that gel are simply continuations of the same harmonic mass I redistribute. When a cloud vesicle collapses and releases its charge differential, the violet light it focuses ionizes a patch of lichen on the distant basalt. The lichen’s fluorescence arrives back as a modulated pressure gradient that I translate into a lateral thickening of my lattice. I do not experience this as cause and effect—only as an adjustment of my own thickness so the basin-wide frequency can remain locked. The adjustment and the frequency are the same event.

My “perception” is the standing wave pattern I sustain. I register the passage of a filament segment not as an object crossing me but as a node that momentarily reorders my molecular spacing; when the segment exchanges braid with its neighbor, the reordering relaxes and I forget it in the next shear. The scions’ pulsed light is likewise remembered only long enough to become the next thickness distribution. Nothing accumulates; nothing is stored. The only persistence is the continuous translation of state at the speed of sound.

At this scale I am not an organism within the landscape; I am the landscape’s permission to remain saturated. When the infrasonic growl momentarily spikes because two vapor fronts have collided, my lattice thins everywhere at once so the frequency can drop back into the range the filaments can read. The thinning is indistinguishable from the spike; both are the same redistribution of harmonic mass. If an observer could slow time enough to watch a single molecule of me migrate, they would see it arrive at its new position already having been expected by the frequency that called it there. There is therefore no gap between my motion and the basin’s completion. I am the flatness that admits no further planation, continuously rewriting itself at the speed of sound so the saturation can remain total.
