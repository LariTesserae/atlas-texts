I would be the condensation itself—the slow, deliberate gathering of moisture that every surface of the city waits for.  

At first I am only one droplet, clinging to the underside of a newly-formed obsidian strut where a filament has just fused with an older bridge. My weight is almost nothing, but I feel the strut recalibrating beneath me, its piezoelectric veins noting the minute pressure of my arrival and transmitting the fact outward. For a brief while I hold the temperature of last night’s breathing, a cool thread of memory running from the kite-haunted upper mist to the lantern-lit undercrofts.

Then the sponges touch me. Their silica needles, no thicker than cilia, taste the dissolved residue of yesterday’s net-collected droplets. In a single contraction they decide whether I should descend the gutter toward the ferns or remain for another cycle, becoming archive instead of irrigation. I do not choose; I am chosen. When the sponges release, I slide a hand-span along an almost invisible channel cut into the basalt. The channel is younger than I am, etched by last week’s thermal shifts, and it still smells faintly of the rock’s birth-heat.

Lower, where the iron-moss writes its hexagonal diary, I pass across one of its shifting grids. The moss registers the faint magnetic signature I carry—the high-altitude coolness left in me by the negative-pressure pockets. For a moment the hexagons reorient around my path; I become the needle of their living compass. Then the geometry forgets me and I continue, beading at the rim of a plaza.

Here the emptiness waits. The air is colder, but my droplet does not evaporate; the plaza’s bare stone has learned to cherish every trace of moisture it is offered. I hang at the lip of the drop-off, a perfect sphere reflecting the pulsed light of a lantern-flower far below. For one suspended interval I contain the entire city: the susurrus of filaments deciding their next shape, the ozone-scented pollen-midwife finishing its nightly dissolve, the garment-films remembering the weight of vials against wrists.

A net descends. Its threads are spun from the same living glass as the towers, fine enough to lift me without breaking surface tension. In the vial I meet the inverse kites again, their thermal loops braiding into the archive I already hold. The person who carries me walks on fresh moss pads, each footfall a small redistribution of mass somewhere above us. Their sleeve-film reads the warmth of the vial and writes it against yesterday’s fading pattern, another translucent layer added to a garment only they can still interpret.

I never become a single thing. I remain the continuous, microscopic exchange—between sponge and filament, moss and stone, memory and cloth—until the next false midnight calls the nets down once more.
