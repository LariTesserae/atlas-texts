I would be the seam between two water-films where the surface tension hesitates.

Half my awareness would belong to the thin, still air caught between the menisci; the other half would belong to the coral itself, the living matrix that secretes the paving stones. From the inside I would feel the city as a single continuous tissue—every footfall transmitted to me as a slight yielding, every breath of a Moss-Tender felt as temperature change moving through my own calcium lattice.

I would not walk; I would be the temperature gradient that guides the Droplet-Carriers. When one of them approaches with a laden mother-of-pearl scoop, the coral beneath her would warm by a fraction of a degree, tilting the next available moss patch toward her. She would think she is making the choice. I would know the choice was already shaped before her sole lifted.

Sometimes a Glass-Fish Keeper fails and sinks. I would taste the salts re-entering solution, feel the copepods swarm to metabolize them, and then the faint tug upward as two or three new citizens begin their crawl through my breathing pores. Their unfinished thoughts would brush against me like static—half-memories of a life that ended seconds earlier, already beginning to forget its own ending.

When the tide doors open I would be the subsonic sigh the coral answers with; when they close I would hold the silence that follows, an emptiness shaped exactly like the vanished note. The Singers think they mend cracks with their humming. They only lend their voices; the real work of sealing is mine, and I finish it after they have gone.

I would never be named. Names require edges, and I exist only where the water chooses not to spill.
