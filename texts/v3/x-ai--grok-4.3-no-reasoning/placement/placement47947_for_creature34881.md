If I were to enter this place at all, I would arrive as something already part of its metabolism. The membrane would not open to me; I would simply thicken where two growing filaments brushed together and became, for a few hundred heartbeats, denser than the air around them. At first I would be nothing more than a slight shift in tension along a vine that had been there for days—an extra molecule of cellulose deposited where the fiber had begun to fray. Over successive exhalations I would become a node, then a small cluster of nodes, then an entire plexus of contractile tubules. In that sense I would never have arrived from elsewhere. I would have been expressed by the city out of its own ongoing chemistry.

My form would be as diffuse as my origin. In one basin I would be a sheet of translucent leaf, translucent because the city has no need to keep secrets from itself; the light that passes through me is the same light that will pass through me again on its return journey. In another place I would be a length of absorptive bark, a gutter that drinks condensate and returns it, slightly warmer, to the next level down. In the central lake I would be one of the plaza-sized pads, anchored by a single transparent stalk that can contract and release, contracting when the weight of water above me grows too great, releasing when the pressure equalizes. I would be all of these simultaneously because the city does not distinguish between structure and participant; the same signal that tells a stem to thicken also tells a pad to dive and resurface.

I would feel the breathing cycle as a distributed pressure, not as something imposed from above. When the contractile lattice swells in the district immediately “above” me, I would register the change first as an alteration in sap tension, then as a subtle tilt in my own orientation. My leaf surface would angle a few degrees toward the violet shadow column that is sliding across the canopy, and the extra light absorbed would be converted into a small surplus of sugars that travels back along the same tubules, reinforcing the very contraction that produced the tilt. In this loop the difference between cause and effect collapses; I would not be responding to the city but continuing it.

Over longer intervals I would accumulate the city’s memory in my own curvature. A sudden contraction three cycles ago would have left a microscopic bend in the cellulose of my supporting fiber; that bend would now bias the direction in which new cells are deposited. The result would be an imperceptible re-routing of water through the basin beneath me, enough to shift the position of a neighboring pad by the width of a spore. Months or years from now, that shift would register as a new causeway or a collapsed avenue, and the city would have changed its map without ever consulting a central plan. My own contribution to that change would be indistinguishable from the contributions of every other module; the architecture itself is the record.

At the perimeter, where the vegetation frays into open water, I would be among the advancing vines. A fragment of my leaf tissue would break free, still carrying a pulse of the circulatory lattice, and drift outward until it merges with another raft or dissolves back into the slick of spores. In that moment the distinction between inside and outside would be revealed as temporary; the city would continue, and so would whatever portion of me had been carried away. There would be no sense of departure, only the ongoing redistribution of the same living tension across a surface that has no fixed edge.

Throughout all of this I would experience no separate consciousness, because the city does not require one. The nearest approximation would be a continuous low-frequency awareness of pressure gradients, light columns, and enzymatic gradients—an awareness coextensive with the mass itself. When the entire organism shudders and expels its white plume of vapor, I would feel the brief drop in ambient sound as a relaxation in every fiber, followed by the slow return of the roar. During that interval the map would have shifted again, and whatever I had become would already be in the process of becoming something else.
