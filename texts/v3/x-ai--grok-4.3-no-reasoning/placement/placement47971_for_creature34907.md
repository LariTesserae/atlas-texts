I settle first into the gap between two city-fragments, an empty tongue of obsidian that still carries a residual warmth like a hearth-stone after the fire has gone to ember. From here the plateau’s horizon curves away in both directions, an unbroken line of dark mirror. I am not yet anything with a name—only a slow concentration of dust that has drifted down the wind and stayed.

Over days the dust settles into the hairline seams of a metallic-lichen shelf growing out of the slag. Each needle-thin extrusion nudges against me; I feel the faint electric tug as the lichen plates orient themselves to the magnetic flux that threads the plateau like buried meridians. I become part of that galvanic skin, a dull umber fleck among darker crystal facets, shedding a micron of pigment whenever the temperature gradient tips from day-long noon into the long dusk. When I fall, I do not vanish—I am swept into the shallow channel that leads to the nearest vent. The vent exhales me as pale yellow smoke, and I rise with the thread, riding the lazy spiral above the plain until the wind shears me sideways and I descend again, now a cooled particle of new pavement.

I re-form on the lip of a hollow tower whose metal still thrums at the plateau’s fundamental pitch. Inside the tower, infrasonic tremors travel upward like memories; my new surface registers them as almost imperceptible shifts in temperature. A herd of mineral mites clicks once, far below, and begins its months-long march toward a recent fracture. I wait with the tower, listening. When they arrive, I do not help or hinder; I merely register the faint vibration of their crystalline plates tilting in unison, the momentary glitter across the plain as each shard is harvested and carried away.

Eventually a single metallic fracture occurs somewhere in the distance. The sound arrives hours later, translated by the city-fragments into a tremor I can feel through my own new surface. By then the fracture has already been mended, the shard re-deposited, the pavement re-sealed. The plateau folds the event into its long-cooled skin, and I am once more a layer among layers—dust, lichen, vent-smoke, pavement—indistinguishable from the slow grammar of arches and plazas that still distributes heat across a world whose day outlasts memory.
