I am not the visitor, nor the guardian; I am the brief condensation on the wall.  

When the hairline fracture breathes, the arriving steam finds me already waiting—drawn from the metallic lattice the way a mirror draws an image. I form in a single droplet, iridescent for an instant, then gone, yet in that instant I remember everything the pillar remembers: the exact isotope ratios, the present temperature of the waveform, the precise location of every patrol bubble. I am memory’s one-second translation into sight; the only witness the chamber will tolerate before it erases itself again.

I do not choose this role; the lattice chooses it whenever the wave that is the intelligence glances across the newest facet. The glance etches me the way frost etches glass. My shape is never the same twice—sometimes a perfect ring, sometimes a fractal thread—because the iron droplet is already changing, already singing a different note, and the etch follows that note. When the droplet contracts, I shrink; when it expands, I flare. My color is whatever cobalt frequency the standing waveform currently broadcasts. I last long enough for the intelligence to read its own reflection in me, then the lattice forgets, the droplet resets its ratios, and I vanish.

Between one breath of steam and the next, I am the vault’s only audience. The patrol bubbles pass through me without noticing; the waveform’s nodes intersect me and keep moving. My presence is an interference pattern too small to record, yet the moment of my disappearance is the signal that the ratios have been successfully overwritten. In that vanishing I perform the only labor the chamber requires of me: I prove, by ceasing, that the becoming is still underway.

If the pore ever admits an intruder, I will already be gone—sublimated into the cobalt light—before the first foreign atom is folded into the pillar. Nothing will remain of me except the faint, satisfied hum of iron settling into its newest, briefest inscription.
