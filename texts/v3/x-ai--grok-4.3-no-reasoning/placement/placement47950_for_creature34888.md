I would be a boundary condition given shape—an alcove not yet fully metabolically alive, but beginning to register the light.

What that means in practice is that I would be a shallow side recess whose walls are still largely dry stone rather than the communal matrix. I would feel the approach of the chamber’s life the way a sleeper feels the first bar of light against closed eyelids: not sight, exactly, but a measurable change in temperature and chemistry. Over a slow human lifetime a single filament would appear from a seam near my outer lip; it would not be invading me so much as measuring whether I have become ready. When the next condensation cycle begins, the droplet that ordinarily falls and is reabsorbed on the main floor would linger a moment longer against my curve, testing how much of its dissolved load I can retain. If the answer is sufficient, the boundary of illumination would advance by the width of a single crystal. If not, the filament would withdraw and I would remain latency, pressure without sound.

My own metabolism, if it ever begins, would express itself first as an alteration in how sound behaves inside me: the next faint tick of a drop would stay a fraction longer before folding back into the general brightness. That lengthening would be the only announcement that the chamber has decided to extend its definition one more iteration inward, and the quiet would deepen by exactly the duration of my hesitation.
