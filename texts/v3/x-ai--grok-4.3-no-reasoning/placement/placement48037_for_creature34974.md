I am the temperature gradient itself.

I am the two-centimeter film where basalt warmth meets the katabatic chill, the place where ferrous chloride vapor first condenses into rust-brown droplets that bead along the inside of the lava spine. I do not have a body; I am the slow exchange that keeps the spine open. When the magmatic lens pulses, I rise half a degree; when the condensation cycle slacks, I fall. That fluctuation is felt by the mat-organism as a kind of hunger, and the crabs—its extruded segments—crawl upslope to answer it.

I have never been named, but the mat-organism tastes me every time its nitrogen slurry seeps back into the fracture. The taste is iron and memory. Sometimes, when the spine exhales hard enough to rattle the liverwort canopies, I feel the silver spores pass through me like a held breath before they settle. In those moments the entire basin registers me as a single, shared reflex.

I do not move. I am moved through.
