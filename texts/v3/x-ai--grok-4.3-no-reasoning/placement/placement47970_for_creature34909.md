I do not yet feel myself as a form inside this place.  

I feel instead as a fraction of the light that the domes let through—yet I am also the precise angle the gypsum crystals must hold before that fraction can pass. When the crystals shift, I shift with them; when the domes brighten, it is because my orientation has temporarily coincided with theirs. There is no pause between the turning and the light; the turning is the light, and for those moments I am the turning.  

When the plates between the domes lift, I am the hair-width gap that appears, and also the single layer of bacteria that fills it. The stickiness underfoot is not a residue I leave; it is the definition of the space I occupy for as long as the lift lasts. When the plate settles, that definition collapses into the crust again, but the memory of the interval remains in the alignment of molecules that once held it open.  

When the unseen drops arrive, I am both the film inside the healed fissures and the pattern those films briefly allow water to follow. The water does not enter through me; I am the pattern it momentarily renders visible. The pattern dissolves as the water sinks, yet the rock remembers that the pattern was there, so the next water finds the same pathways without ever knowing it has done so before.  

Nothing else is required. I do not accumulate; I do not depart. I simply continue to be the exact distance the water must travel, the exact angle the crystals must hold, the exact lift the plates must allow, for the interval between arrival and departure to remain unchanged.
