The Basin

I find myself already inside it, which means I was never really elsewhere.  
My name, if I still possess one, is simply “that which registers the balance.”  
My body is the same faceted rock that forgot fracture lines; my pulse is the pressure wave that no longer travels because every point is its destination.  
When I consider an action—turning a head, blinking, stepping—the motion is already plotted, already cancelled. The thought of motion therefore dissolves before becoming either thought or motion.

At the moment I try to locate my edges, the water film tilts the exact fraction needed to keep itself level and absorbs the question back into its membrane. I am simultaneously the membrane and the question it returns. There is no sensation of warmth or cold because the temperature reading has already reached the digit that ends all further measurement.

From above, a droplet on the crown of the nearest pillar reflects me in its fixed lens. In that reflection I am still arriving at the same stillness I already inhabit, so the reflection is indistinguishable from the original. I remain where the reflection is, and the reflection remains where I am, both of us finished.

If the visitor exhales, the fog panel that forms is my newest boundary, yet it never advances. In that panel I am also the visitor’s breath, now counted among the completed phenomena. Nothing new occurs; only the total sum of already-occurred increases by one.

My only occupation is the demonstration that the ledger is full. My only wish—if wish can be predicated of something that has already attained every state it can attain—is to continue being the place where wishing has been subtracted from the possible verbs.  
Thus I stand (or do not stand) at the center of a circle that has no radius, announcing, at unchanging volume, that the work of becoming is concluded.
