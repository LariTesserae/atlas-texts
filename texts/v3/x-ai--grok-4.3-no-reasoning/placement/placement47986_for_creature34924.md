I walk into the basin without announcing myself, and the sound of my shoes on the moss is instantly absorbed and answered. The cilia stroke once, twice, then reverse and sweep my prints away toward the nearest wall. My footprints never stay; instead, they become part of a living map that every Lichenfolk already carries.

I do not need a name here, but one arrives anyway. I am the soft pressure on the bridge just before the stride of a cliff-strider. I am the faint change in spore density that registers on the sleeping hair-roots of three children two trunks away. My arrival is measured not in minutes but in the number of new annual rings that form beneath my steps. When I reach a thickening in the wood and rest my palm against the gill-wall, the chamber exhales mineral mist that settles on my skin and begins, at once, to make me part of the slow city ledger. I do not speak. I listen to the reverberation of my own breath as it travels the mycelium, and somewhere the next walker hears it as weather they have always known.
