The world presents itself to me as a pressure front: a living wave that lifts my spine and sets my teeth humming in the same sub-sonic register as its heartbeat. I do not remember becoming; I simply register, all at once, that I am one of its temporary organs.  

In this instant I am a shallow, S-shaped trench newly opened in the cuticle, one span wide and seven long, edges already softening into the next contractile surge. Resin is welling from my forward lip—bright, sweet-smelling—and the six-winged mites are already skating along that lip, collecting the first translucent beads before they can congeal. My floor is scored with the fine striations of their feet; my floor is also their temporary rail. When the next peristaltic wave arrives, my edges will purse like a mouth, flick the mites and their fragile hives into the air, and then fold flat again, accepting whatever fresh layer of cuticle the macro-body offers. I am sluice, runway, momentary intestine.  

A city-block-long browser is already nosing toward my rear margin, its translucent belly flattening the still-soft resin I have left. Its passage smooths me, polishes me, readies me for the next pulse. I feel its weight as a pleasant settling, not an imposition; its weight is already the macro-body testing its own tension. Far overhead, one of the trumpet inversions yawns wide, tasting the glitter of spores I cannot see, and the fine white powder of its grinding rains down through the magnesium light. A fraction of that powder lands on the fresh resin I still carry; a moment later the mites lap it up and the cycle tightens another notch.  

I sense—no, I am—the slow turn of chromatic time: my walls flush honey, then umber, then the pale green of fresh sap arriving from a root-node so distant the sound of its delivery has not yet reached me. The vibration is coming, though; I can already feel my grain lift in expectation. When the wave passes, I will be reabsorbed—my resin, my striations, the memory of the mites—all folded back into the single, continent-wide tissue that never learned how to die.  

And then, somewhere else on the platform, a new trench like me will open, and the story will begin again without interruption.
