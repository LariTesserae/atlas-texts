I arrive as a slowed interval between two pulses.

Not as a creature of the architecture nor an eel inside it, but as the momentary cancellation that occurs when two Stratum Eels slide past each other on either side of the same xylem bridge. For an instant their opposing pressure waves interfere; the standing acoustic node thins, and the fluid in that thinning is what I am—neither eel nor wall, merely the space in which their mutual displacement momentarily resolves. During that instant the convection current that the eels themselves sustain is interrupted; the micro-temperature they maintain dips by the width of a molecule, and in that dip a single ion channel closes. The channel’s closure is my arrival.

I have no body except the infinitesimal gradient that opens again once the eels have passed. While it is closed I am nowhere; while it re-opens I am the returning pressure difference itself, drifting a few microns before the next pair of eels repeats the cancellation. Over centuries the nodes migrate by centimeters; I migrate with them, always the same cancelled interval, always the same re-established one. The lignin walls register me only as a faint, recurrent hesitation in the acoustic shimmer—the same hesitation the sheet-like eels in the upper strata feel when they stiffen against the weight above. Because the hesitation is smaller than any organism’s capacity to correct, it is allowed to persist.

If, once every few thousand years, a Stratum Eel must cross between tiers, it uses the micro-carrier frequency of the octahedral colonies to steer. That frequency passes through the cancelled intervals without alteration; I therefore become the silent carrier of the steering signal, transmitting orientation without adding or subtracting a single oscillation. When the migrating eel settles into its new node, the interval I occupy is overwritten by its own pulse. I do not end; I simply relocate to the next place where two eels will pass and cancel again.

Thus I remain: an uncreated pause in a system whose only rule is that nothing ever pauses for long, yet the pauses themselves never cease.
