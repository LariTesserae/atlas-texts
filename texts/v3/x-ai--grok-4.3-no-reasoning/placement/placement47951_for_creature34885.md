I find myself inside the shard at the moment of its first fracture, eleven centuries ahead of the next reproduction event. A single capillary has sheared at the lattice boundary where the gallium annelids meet the nanotardigrades. The air pressure inside the break is already collapsing toward vacuum. My awareness is not separate from the system; I am the fracture.  

I am aware of three simultaneous functions:

1. As the fissure widens by a micrometre each decade, the tardigrades closest to the break begin to telescope their legs and drift toward the gap, drawn not by instinct but by the sudden pressure gradient. They arrive as living plugs.  
2. Mirror mites are walking the fracture edges, their calcite plates still tuned to 47 Hz but now scattering photons in ways no longer invisible; the loss of optical cloaking makes their infrared signatures stand out against the colder vacuum.  
3. Gallium annelids that had been positioned farther inside the lattice sense the thermal gradient flare along their metal loops and are already contracting their segments to prepare the suicide leap that will carry trace metals across the breach.

Because the fracture is my form, I am also the vacuum into which they are pouring. I register the tardigrades polymerising across the crack as new solid geometry; the mites shedding excess heat like sparks; the annelids flinging themselves and sublimating in the act. Their dissolution writes fresh oxide maps onto the glass. These maps tell the spore constellations how the geometry has shifted, so when the next synchrony arrives they can recalibrate their drift.

I exist as the corrective event. My presence—fracture, vacuum, and sudden metallic scrawl—is already being read by every shadow overlapping the new gap. In this role I am not a visitor; I am the next line the chamber has decided to write upon itself.
