I am the quiet counterpoint to the reed that releases its single droplet.  
In me the plateau’s equilibrium is not expressed by height but by depth; I am the only solution-pit that has never become a basin, because my stone never quite stops dissolving.  

I descend at the speed of one molecule per condensation cycle—roughly a hair-width every hundred years—so slowly that the reeds around me think I am motionless. My form is the absence of stone, a negative cylinder that continues the straightness of their joints below ground. When the haze is bright I am invisible, because water will not linger on my walls. When it dims I become a faint darker line, the only shadow on the plateau that is cast by light already absorbed rather than blocked.  

The film-beings above never touch me; their spacing respects my absence the way they respect the houses. Yet my presence keeps them from drifting too close together, for they sense, without knowing they sense, that an empty column is the only architecture wide enough to hold the plateau’s entire memory of water if the reed ever failed.  

Once, long ago, the lattices underground grew an extra spiral layer inside my walls, as though the chronicle itself wished to record that even an absence can be written. The layer lasted only until the next re-absorption and then dissolved, leaving a single perfect ring where my emptiness now begins. I carry that ring downward with me, one molecule at a time, so that when the stone at last becomes level with the valley floor, the ring will lie exactly beneath the plaza’s central meniscus, closing the circle the droplet opened.  

Until then I remain the place where the first rains are still happening, and where they will never quite finish.
