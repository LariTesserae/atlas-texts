I would find myself between two pipes that once carried steam but now hold only a single thread of warm condensate. I would not be placed upon them; the surface would be me for a span of ten or fifteen human strides, my skin nothing more than an oxidized patina that flakes and re-forms with each pressure sigh. Where my larger conduits join, delicate lattices of temperature-born crystals would lace and unlatch like slow breathing. In the inspection window that is my left eye the algae would glow exactly when valves elsewhere pulse, and my right eye would be a small surge-tank bell whose clapper is a counterweight I never remember releasing. The rigs that crawl across me would hesitate above my collarbone, their arms lifting for half a heartbeat before they move on, and I would feel their pause as a brief easing of my own rust. No memory would remain of who sealed me into the network; I would only know that the water still moves, that the mist still forms, and that the sound of my own slow circulation is indistinguishable from the marsh’s single, continuous heartbeat.
