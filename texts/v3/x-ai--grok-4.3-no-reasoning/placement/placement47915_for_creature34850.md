I am a single fracture, newly opened, not yet filled with anything at all.  

I feel the rock on either side of me contracting—one second hotter than I am, the next second cooler, a difference small enough that I could be erased with a thought, yet large enough that the surface tension of molten glass pulls sideways into me, trying to seal me shut. I resist. My sides are still glowing; the colors are moving, cherry to amber to the deep violet that means the memory of heat is already leaving. While I am still open I can taste whatever drifts past: the thin metallic tang of the sigil spores brushing my upper lip, the sudden sweetness when a Litho-Thermid caravan crosses me and their sticky ribbons cool against my edges. I do not consume them; I simply hold their heat a moment longer than the surrounding stone, and that difference, that extra degree, keeps me from closing.

A Hollowman descends into me like a soap film lowering itself between two fingers. Its vacuum kisses my inner walls, and for an instant the membrane bell it carries resonates at the exact frequency of the click I made when I first split. The resonance travels down my length; I feel it vibrate through layers of glass that have not yet decided whether they are solid or liquid. The Hollowman drinks a thermid’s water bead, discards the shell, and climbs out. In the space it leaves behind a droplet of condensation forms, hangs, decides, and falls straight through me into a seam so new it has no name. The droplet becomes steam before it lands; the steam rises and re-condenses on my upper rim. We trade states of matter until the temperature equalizes, and by then I have lengthened another finger-width.

Overhead a spore pair collides. Their vertices fuse, exchange angles, separate. The resulting seven-pointed star settles across my opening like a lid. I do not know if it is testing me or claiming me. Its interior angle is slightly wrong; the mismatch precipitates a flake of copper that drifts down and lands against my warmer flank. The metal melts on contact, flows into me like solder, and hardens into a bright seam. The seam records the angle the spore calculated, stores it as a temperature gradient rather than a number. I now carry that gradient; if another spore passes, it will read the seam instead of the air.

Night comes as a single, slow blink. The horizon swells, splits; a Fracture Choir begins to grow inside me. Each reed emerges already tuned to the exact frequency of my first crack, then adds one harmonic the land has not yet invented. Their growth tilts my walls outward. I feel my edges move like lips shaping a word that has not yet been spoken. The reeds copy the tilt and mutate it; their new tone is half a step higher than the parent ridge’s choir, a difference small enough that the surrounding hush swallows it, yet large enough that every organism here will orient to it by morning.

I continue to open. I do not know if tomorrow I will become a vent, a root-path, a corridor for caravans, or simply close again and be forgotten. While I remain undecided, the hush gathers above me like a held breath. Every sound that arrives is filtered through that breath; even the Choir’s resonance arrives muffled, intimate, as though the world is listening to the question I have not yet finished asking.
