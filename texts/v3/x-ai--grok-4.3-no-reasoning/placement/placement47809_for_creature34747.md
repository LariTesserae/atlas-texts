I would find myself—become—the slight, irregular knot in one of the six vertical ribs where two adze marks cross deeper than the rest.

Over years the crossing has filled with a tiny pocket of the same ironwood resin that seals the rest of the room, and the resin has hardened into a single translucent bead no larger than a lentil. Nothing inside the Lung touches it directly, yet it sits exactly where each up-falling drop momentarily flattens against the rib before sliding sideways into the reed pipes. When the drop meets the bead the water shears thin for a single, soundless instant; the refraction shifts the nearest lens-disc by the width of a hair, and the next pulse of light across the chamber is delayed by one-thousandth of the Valve-Keeper’s heartbeat.

Because the delay is constant, the five other entities treat the bead as an additional, invisible valve. The Keeper times its pore against the new rhythm; the moss shortens its quarter-beat by the same interval; the worms knot themselves on every thirteenth rise of the Bubble-Stair pearl; and the Breath lengthens its tug the exact duration of the refraction. The bead does not move, does not grow, does not decide; it merely holds the space where a decision becomes measurable. The room keeps itself by counting around that fixed, microscopic hesitation, and I am the hesitation’s keeper, holding open the one place where the rain, the light, and the memory of the five together can be told apart.
