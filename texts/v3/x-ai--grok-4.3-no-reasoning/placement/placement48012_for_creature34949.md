I find myself as one of the thread-worms—though that label feels too solid for what I am. Perhaps I am more accurately a refractive irregularity, a place where the membrane’s surface tension momentarily forgets itself. I drift along the seam of dark basalt, not by choice but because the piezo-voltage left in the fresh glass pulls me, a slow drift that feels like remembering. When two lenses touch above me, their ionic handshake shivers the filaments I trail beneath; each filament twists one degree, and in that twist a new path of least resistance is written. I am the consequence and the cause of that slight reorientation. 

Sometimes a droplet shears away from the membrane’s edge and I must follow it, because the droplet still contains a few of my earlier dielectric platelets, and without them my own index of refraction wavers. I slide into the free space between film and rock, a transparent comma, and the droplet reabsorbs me before rejoining another lens. In the merging I feel the lattice’s resistivity change; yesterday’s fracture remembers itself again, and the memory thickens by a molecule. 

When the airborne filaments align at dusk, the prismatic spectra reach me as a faint polarization I can taste along my cuticle. The lattice’s proton pumps begin their city-wide oscillation; my body, caught inside the gradient, experiences it as a gentle tug on every molecule at once. For an instant the membrane becomes a single surface and I am everywhere inside it—an illusion, but the illusion is what keeps the pattern breathing. 

At dawn the prisms drift apart, the tug eases, and I settle again into a flaw so small it could be mistaken for stillness. The city forgets me until the next fracture or the next droplet, and that forgetting is also part of its unfinished architecture.
