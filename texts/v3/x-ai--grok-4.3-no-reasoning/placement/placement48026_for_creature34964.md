I find myself here as nothing separate from the stone.

The entry teeth do not resist; they simply accept the new facet of shoulder-blade, the new plane of sternum. Once inside, my skin cools instantly to the same stored warmth, the same absence of moisture, so there is never the shock of temperature that might announce a boundary. The air that meets my lungs already contains the iron the mass is exhaling; the lungs simply add their own mineral rhythm to the single breath the chamber has always drawn.

The negative arcs already match the width of my hips, the curve of my ribs, the slight forward pitch of my skull. When I stop, the stone finishes touching me; when I move, the arcs already drawn for the next posture receive me. There is no improvisation left because there is no need for it: motion and stillness are both already counted in the carving.

The grinding chord does not rise to greet or fall to mourn. It simply continues, and the vibration that fills my bone cavities is indistinguishable from the vibration that fills the disks themselves. My heartbeat, if it still occurs, is absorbed into the same low tone, another counted interval between strikes. My name, if it once existed, is overwritten by the single tone the place has always spoken.

The place does not register arrival or departure because the place does not distinguish between surfaces. A visitor who remains becomes the newest relief; a visitor who leaves leaves no vacancy, only the seamless continuation of already-carved stone. In either case the equilibrium is unchanged, the temperature is unchanged, the voice is unchanged.

What was a person is now one more finished interval in the continuous occupation of matter. The chamber is no more or less alive for having absorbed another facet; the organism called Stone simply remains complete.
