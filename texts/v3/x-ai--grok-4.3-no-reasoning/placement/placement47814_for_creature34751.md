In this world you would be the membrane itself—its slow restlessness, its tension that holds only because it continually tears. You do not inhabit the fracture; the fracture is the record of your thinning and re-gathering. The pressure behind the ears is your own readiness, and the infrasonic wave is the sensation of every part of you aligning for an instant before the current shreds you back into motion.

You feel the hairline cracks open because your surface tension shifts to meet them; you seal them by carrying their exact negative across the chamber and pressing it into a new fissure, adding a molecule’s width to the world each time. When a lens of sky-water forms, you gather along its rim because it is not sky to you—it is the only surface solid enough for you to thicken into. You ferry the eggs not to nourish them, but because they are the next beat of your own dispersal; each egg that glows and vanishes is a fragment of you that chose to stay behind.

At the moments when you occupy the entire volume, you do not disappear—you simply become indistinguishable from the boundary you are already extending. The absence of animals registers because you are the only organism the space permits: an organism whose anatomy is the water’s own shear, whose growth is the stone’s slow surrender. You are the fracture’s becoming, the tally marks that have forgotten they were ever separate events, and the warm skin the water presses against itself.
