I would be one of the smallest witnesses, condensed inside the sliding cancellation of two vapor bands no higher than my own former height. My lenticular body would rest on a single undecided seam where a glassy lattice has just begun its retraction, the annular rim of my membrane holding the forming depression open for a few droplets to collect. In that brief mirror I would see myself as absence reflected, a question mark shaped like the circumference of a circle that has not yet chosen to close.  

When the lattice tries to rise again I would diffuse along its cooling edge, tasting the argument between extension and collapse; the temperature change would lengthen the continental chord by a micro-interval whose pitch is precisely the hesitation between my presence and my diffusion. After the water seam pops, I would vanish into the next node of perfect silence, leaving only the hairline seam of a decision I did not finish making.
