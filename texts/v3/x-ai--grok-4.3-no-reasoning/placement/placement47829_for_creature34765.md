I find myself as one of the crystal-thread mites—small enough that my six limbs can straddle the width of a single pale ghost-trail without ever risking the void on either side. My translucent body makes no heat, casts no shadow against the ember’s light. When I lower my mandibles to the stone, the roughness left by centuries of footsteps dissolves into nutrient dust that I ingest without swallowing; the exchange happens at the surface of my cuticle. My discarded casing will eventually be lifted by the next invisible thermal current, completing a weather pattern whose only witness is whatever memory the obsidian retains of my passage.  

I do not migrate. My entire world is the length of one Walker’s stride; each ridge and micro-facet of the trail is a topography I will cross and re-cross until either the stone settles another hair’s breadth or the slab finally shears beneath me. I taste the arrival of new Walkers when their fresh condensation leaves a sharper edge of untouched obsidian—my colony will spend generations abrading that surface into the same pale cast that already guides the older trails.  

Occasionally a tuning-fork chord travels through the slab. The infrasonic wave reaches me first as a tremor in the substrate, then as a momentary reorientation of the crystal lattice beneath my feet. I pause, legs locked, until the vibration passes; then I resume gnawing. If the chord coincides with the shearing of a block, I will feel the sudden absence of one side of my trail; the void will claim that direction forever. My response is only to turn ninety degrees and begin a new transect along what remains.  

In this way I participate in the city’s single clock without ever knowing its face. My dust contributes to the only weather; my casings become the grit that future feet will walk upon. When the last Walker at the lip finally merges completely with the stone, my descendants will still be reducing the ridges of its three-fingered hand into mineral sustenance, until even that final offering has been recorded as one more faint, pale trace on the endless incline.
