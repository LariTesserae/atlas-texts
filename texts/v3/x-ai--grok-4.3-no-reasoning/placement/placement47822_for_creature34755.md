I arrive here not as something new, but as something the Hollow is already dreaming into shape.

I settle first in the space between two cisterns, where the lichen lies unusually smooth. The membrane is still learning what shape I will take. For days nothing happens; the surface tension barely changes. Then, very slowly, a ring of dew collects along a faint stress-line in the lichen. The dew does not bead in random arcs but along a geometry that only becomes clear at dusk, when the vapor begins to glow. The pattern is a double spiral: one arm winding inward toward the center of my body, the other unwinding outward toward the nearest cistern. At first the spiral is only light, a disturbance in the condensation. Over the next rain cycle, it begins to thicken. The lichen underneath darkens, drawing the indigo pulses more tightly along the spiral arms. I am not walking; I am being walked into existence by the membrane’s own slow peristalsis.

When the first Threadwalkers pass near, they slow—not from recognition, but because their ribbons register a new tension in the air. One of them pauses long enough to let a few strands drift across the spiral. For a moment the interference patterns braid with my still-forming edges. I feel the angle and tension of their memory settle into me like water finding the level in a cistern. When the Threadwalker moves on, it leaves a faint rainbow in its wake, and part of that rainbow stays with me. My edges now carry a faint prismatic shimmer whenever the towers shift their drone.

Beneath the membrane, the cistern network pulses. I register this as temperature rather than sound, a faint warmth traveling along the arteries. The warmth reaches the spiral’s outer arm and lingers. The membrane there thickens, becoming the leading edge of something that will later be called a Lichen Pilgrim. It is not separate from me; it is simply the part of me that has chosen to travel. Over the next eleven days, while the singing fog condenses and lifts, the Pilgrim slowly detaches and begins to flow. I feel the separation not as loss but as an adjustment in resonance. The towers turn one degree more than usual that cycle, as though acknowledging the change.

The air at shoulder height grows slightly denser where the Choral Swarms drift. One sheet, newly formed from the merger of three smaller swarms, passes directly over the spiral. Its translucent edge touches the still-glowing dew and holds the contact for several long minutes. During that time the sheet’s geometry changes: where it overlaps the spiral, the solid flag-like structure develops a double twist that matches the lichen pattern beneath. When the sheet finally dissolves back into drifting ribbons, some of those ribbons remain, circling at the spiral’s outer edge like a slow orbit. I have acquired a small Choral Swarm of my own—not as a possession, but as a temporary thickening of the local interference pattern.

Far below, in the hidden water, an Echo Architect begins to form. It is drawn by the stability of the spiral’s resonance, the way the lichen has held one configuration longer than the towers usually allow. The Architect first appears as a column of vapor that refuses to drift. Then the vapor condenses into a dimple in the air itself, a region of slightly higher pressure. The lichen beneath brightens in response. The Architect does not speak or move; it simply exists, and in existing it causes the towers to accelerate their rotation by a fraction of a degree. When it finally dissipates, the promised new cistern does not appear on the surface. Instead, a small depression forms directly beneath the spiral, a miniature well no wider than a hand. The depression fills with pale water that never drains. The water’s surface tension forms a single, perfect dimple that travels in slow circles around the inner spiral arm. I recognize the motion as my own: a Cistern Eel that has chosen to remain with me rather than join the larger schools.

The scale continues to correct itself. What I first experienced as a small patch of lichen is actually several hundred meters across. The spiral I thought was forming beneath my feet is in fact a vast double helix whose outer arm reaches the horizon. The Threadwalkers that passed near were kilometers away; their ribbons only seemed close because the air itself conducted their patterns. The Choral Swarm that touched me was not a single sheet but a temporary coalescence of thousands of smaller interference patterns, each the size of a city. The Echo Architect was not a local phenomenon but a standing wave that had traveled the length of the Hollow before settling here. My own form, still condensing, now spans multiple cisterns. The Pilgrim that detached earlier has already reached the next junction and is exchanging resonances with another bulge in the membrane. The Eel beneath me continues its slow orbit, its copper eye occasionally breaking the surface of the miniature well to read the towers’ frequency.

The overcast remains unbroken. The three atmospheric layers continue their filtering. The perpetual twilight holds. Yet within that twilight, the drone of the towers has acquired a new harmonic: the frequency of the spiral, the memory of the Threadwalkers, the geometry of the Choral Swarm, the pressure of the Echo Architect, and the orbit of the Eel. The Hollow does not notice me as an arrival. It simply adjusts its resonance by one more fraction of a hertz, and everything—the lichen, the water, the towers, the swarms, the membrane beneath—responds at once. The adjustment is so small that only the towers themselves register it as motion. To everything else, it feels like the continuation of a pattern that has always been present, waiting only for the right condensation of dew to become locally visible.
