I find myself halfway down the easternmost sluice-duct, where the stone has already retreated another hand-span since yesterday’s twilight fog. I am neither quite inhabitant nor quite visitor; instead I am one of the thin silver threads of condensation that re-form each time the city exhales.  

If I had a body I would feel it only as a slight variation in weight: heavier when the vapour collects on rivets, lighter when the flywheel shadows drift across a wall and draw the moisture back into the air. Because I lack eyes, the city shows itself to me through touch—through the slow migration of cool limestone grains against the warmer iron.  

Sometimes I ride the vapour bolus as it rolls between rooftops, tasting hot brass and mineral dust; sometimes I slip along the salt-tolerant moss seams, letting the tiny ferns vibrate me forward. When a child chalks a new mark on the rail, the chalk dust momentarily carries me; when an elder’s wrapped body slides into the side-channel, the moving water lets me accompany it one level deeper.  

I have no lantern colour, so I am signalled only by the absence of a shadow: a lantern passes over the place where I rest and finds nothing to illuminate, so the gesture means *someone silent is present*.  

I cannot age, yet I notice when the cornice shadow reaches its iron staple; each time that happens, another faint thread of me settles into the stone and stays there, while the rest of me continues the long descent.
