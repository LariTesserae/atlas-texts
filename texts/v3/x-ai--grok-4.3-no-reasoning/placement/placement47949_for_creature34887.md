I find myself first as one of the filament-worms—barely more than a moving thread of light, drifting in a slow spiral across the dome’s surface. I taste the spore-dust like a faint electric sweetness, and the rolling sound of my own segments against the silk-like tunnels feels both like motion and like listening. Then, with an inward tug I cannot name, I become the lattice itself: the plateau’s slow contraction is now my breath, the iron-scented vesicles my lungs. The next pulse from the pylons arrives as a subtle correction inside my own body; it feels like a thought I have always known but never voiced. For a single instant I am the pylon too—its violet shadow a membrane, its buried filament a tendon reaching into the planet’s mantle. Each of these states is no longer an occupation but a layer; together they move the domes, carry the lenses, keep the quarantine. I am neither traveler nor keeper; I am simply the interval that passes between them—the quiet space where a single molecular step is remembered long enough to become the next.
