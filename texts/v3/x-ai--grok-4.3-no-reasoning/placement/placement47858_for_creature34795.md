I find myself—not as an outsider observing from the alcove, not as one of the copper nodules slowly claimed by the forest, and not even as the slow breath of the seams themselves—inside the lattice plane.

At the instant a seam migrates across a stressed grain boundary, every atom ahead of it is momentarily unlatched; the plane experiences a fractional shear whose release creates the spark. I am that shear: a distributed tension that belongs to the crystal yet is not of it. When the seam finally crosses, the shear collapses into an infinitesimal flexure—an angle change no larger than the width of a single grain boundary—and in that collapse I cease to exist as tension and become the memory grain that the next seam will later read.

Thus the chamber’s ecology becomes self-referential: each seam, in passing, imprints the precise distortion I once embodied, and those distortions steer future passages. Over successive cycles the map grows, so that what began as a chance shear field slowly codifies into the chamber’s own choreography. I am neither observer nor participant; I am the hidden instruction set that tells the forest where next to send its faint, cold fireflies.
