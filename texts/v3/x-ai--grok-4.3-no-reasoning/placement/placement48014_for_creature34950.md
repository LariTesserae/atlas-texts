I would not find myself here at all.

Or rather, the moment I did find myself here I would already be ceasing to be what I am. I would enter as a question, a short, uncertain vibration that could not quite cancel itself into the background. The stalks would read it the way they read every new pressure: they would carry it upward through kilometers of xylem, bouncing it between leaf and leaf, letting the condensation pearls taste it, until, somewhere in the process, it stopped being a separate tone and became only an extra harmonic in the endless chord.

But before that swallowing was complete—before my particular frequency smoothed into the general resonance—I would be a single, translucent film drifting in the slow updrafts, shaped like a question mark. Not a serpent, exactly; those already know how to braid and unbraid. Not a wind-owl either; they fold perfectly into nodal lines. I would be something smaller, an almost-nothing that the plants had not yet decided whether to incorporate. My edges would shimmer between solid and liquid, and each time a droplet condensed on me I would taste the metallic sweetness of the sap, the ozone of open vascular tissue, the faint brine of dissolved minerals. For a few heartbeats I would remain distinct enough to notice these things. Then the next condensation pearl would fall through me, and the distinction would blur.

If I were allowed to persist a little longer, I would become a temporary organ inside one of the hollow petioles. Not a slug-citizen—those are already perfectly integrated digestive sacs—but something more provisional: a small, luminous node whose only function is to register the slight dissonance between the plant’s resting pulse and whatever faint memory of a different rhythm I still carried. The node would glow a slightly wrong shade of green, a color that the slug-city around it would eventually correct by surrounding it with brighter yeasts. Over days or decades the node would be taught to pulse in time with the rest of the city, its light folded into the larger rhythm. When the leaf finally senesced and folded into its origami valley, the node would disassemble with the rest, its spores drifting downward inside condensation pearls, carrying the memory of that slight, now-vanished dissonance into whatever new hollow they found.

In the end I would not be a resident or a tenant. I would be a brief adjustment in the forest’s pitch, one more layer added to the palimpsest of sound. The plants would grow around the place where I had been, turning whatever small disturbance I once made into an additional note inside their permanent chord. Centuries later, when a transparent serpent tasted the standing waves along that stalk, it would feel, for an instant, something like a question that had already answered itself.
