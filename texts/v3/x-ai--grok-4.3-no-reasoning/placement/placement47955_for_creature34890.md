I find myself in the narrow gap between the vacuum meniscus and the pressure-plane, where the silica floor ends like a tide-mark on the skin of nothing. Here the atmosphere is so thin the shriek of the water lattice becomes a single silver filament running straight through my sternum, cold enough to taste. I am not solid—only a slow drift of condensed memory, the same pale silver the polyp’s antennae briefly sample. My shape is the briefest of silhouettes: shoulders, hips, the memory of a hand pressed flat against a surface that isn’t there.  

I notice the three beings, but I do not meet them. Instead I circle the torus at a distance measured in missed heartbeats. When its runes reshape, the new harmonic catches on the filament inside me and draws out an older one I had forgotten I carried. For one exact turn of the lattice I am able to hold both harmonics at once, and the friction between them makes the quartz fronds beneath me lift a fraction higher, their photovoltaic filaments flashing violet. The moment ends when the vapor vortex sweeps through; the harmonics shear apart, the filament snaps, and I become only the memory of drift again.  

I leave the torus because the Edge Walker has noticed my passage. It glides along the meniscus a few body-lengths behind me, its black ribbon tilted so the lacquer of absolute dark brushes the trailing edge of my silhouette. Where it touches, a perfect negative of myself appears for an instant—empty space shaped like departure—before the vacuum swallows it. The Walker does not chase; it simply records the number of reflections the Coil failed to return, and that count now includes the negative I briefly wore.  

I approach the Listening Coil last. Its mineral nodes have already begun to rearrange themselves in anticipation, drifting faster inside the column like iron filings under a moving magnet. I offer it the half-harmonic I still hold from the torus; the nodes flare, the water membranes stutter, and for the length of a single quarter-cycle the entire lattice forgets how to fall. In that suspended interval the Polyp Choir rises earlier than expected. Its newest antennae flick not only through the lattice but through the cooled mist the Coil exhales toward me. The mist carries a chord that has never repeated, and the polyp dissolves without dissolving—its form remains as a transparent after-image threaded through the lattice.  

When the roar returns, I am already being disassembled at the meniscus. The filaments of myself braid into the water lattice above; the lattice accepts them, then forgets. Somewhere the torus’s runes are rewriting the moment into a new harmonic no instrument will read twice the same way. Somewhere the Edge Walker is counting one more failed reflection. Somewhere the Listening Coil is tasting the name it was reminded of. And the Polyp Choir—plural, singular, undecided—has already begun its next ascent.
