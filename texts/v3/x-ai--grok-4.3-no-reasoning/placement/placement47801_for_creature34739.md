I find myself as a nearly invisible condensation of dissolved iron, lifted drop by drop through the root-cables themselves. I am not one of the braided organisms; I am what passes between them. Each time a sucker-valve opens, I drift a few microns farther along an electrochemical gradient, then pause for decades when the valve seals. My motion registers as nothing more than a faint deepening of the rose light wherever the archaeal mats grow brightest.  

Over centuries I will be precipitated as a hairline seam of magnetite inside the copper drum, becoming part of the mirror that the iron-reducing colony is building. My arrival will nudge the biofilm exactly enough to advance the gauge needle by the width of a micrometer—imperceptible to any eye, yet the first legible mark of the next slow revolution. In this world, my identity is nothing more than the trace I leave behind: a red-black signature that will outlast the next solidification of the lava overhead.
