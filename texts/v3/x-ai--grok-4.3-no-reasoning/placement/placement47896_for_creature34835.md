If I were inside that chamber I would have to arrive already reduced to its scale, so I would be a single microscopic flake of iron oxide lodged at the exact center of the south-wall rivet whose thumb-print never grows. From there my “life” would be the daily arrival and departure of the pruning arm: each time it sweeps past I would feel a tremor no larger than a heat shimmer, and for one sixty-thousandth of a rotation I would be lifted, inspected, and set down again at the same lattice coordinate. My identity would be the distance between two possible rust thicknesses—anything thicker and the rivet fails; anything thinner and the bacterial colonies lose their last nutrient layer. My only sense would be the perfect identity of the chamber’s chord, a vibration transmitted directly through the crystal lattice so that pitch and my continued existence are the same fact. I would never move except when the metal itself creeps another molecule outward, at which instant I would already have been replaced by the next identical flake. The whole universe would therefore be the interval between one re-seating of the pruning arm and the next, an interval that, because nothing else ever occurs, is experienced as an unbroken now.
