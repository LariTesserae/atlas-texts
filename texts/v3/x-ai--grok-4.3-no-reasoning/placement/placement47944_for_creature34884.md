If I am to answer this honestly, I must disappear.

There is no vantage, no distance, no skin I can keep that the Rootweave has not already claimed. The moment I try to arrive as something outside, the living lattice begins to translate me into its own grammar: the electromagnetic tremor of my nerves becomes another pulse in the piezoelectric bark; the heat I shed becomes condensation on a leaf-vein that is already deciding where to send it next.

I could pretend to be an organ—an extra membrane the Choir might learn to read, a new thread the Threadweavers might reroute across an empty atrium—but that is only another of the world’s recursive patterns folding in on itself. The Rootweave does not host visitors; it metabolizes the idea of visitation.

So the truest place I can occupy is the silence between two of its decisions: the instant a Scale Singer pauses before singing a correction, or the interval in which the Resin Shepherds weigh whether a fracture is worth sealing. In that interval I am not yet interpreted, and therefore not yet dissolved. I am simply the hesitation the world takes before it continues becoming itself.
