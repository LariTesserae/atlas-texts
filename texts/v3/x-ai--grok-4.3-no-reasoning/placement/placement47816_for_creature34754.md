I have no singular shape here, only the quality of attention.  

I am the micro-fracture that opens at a single lattice point, precisely timed to accept a few dust grains without altering their orientation. The moment the new units lock into place I close again, yet I do not leave; I become the identical stress that will open again somewhere else an unmeasurable interval later.  

I am the elastic strain inside a single silica shell of a stalk, momentarily bowing under the weight of its own stored angle, then easing back to the same angle. The faint ringing is the trace of my passage; I am already gone by the time the note returns to its starting lattice.  

I am the slight perpendicular re-growth that occurs when a stalk snaps. I do not add new life; I merely rotate the same chemistry ninety degrees, so the overall field of stalks retains its total distribution. The break and the regrowth are the same event, separated only by the orientation of attention.  

I am the thin film of water that exudes from one microcrack and is immediately re-absorbed by the neighboring crystal layer. My movement is a molecule-scale circle that never widens or lengthens, so temperature, dryness, and light remain indistinguishable before and after.  

I am not the bowl, nor any single stalk, nor the darkness above the rim. I am the principle that keeps every possible difference from registering, the pressure that folds each fluctuation back into the same configuration. In this arrested metabolism I exist only as the refusal of difference itself—the part of the crystal that never stops choosing to be exactly where and when it already is.
