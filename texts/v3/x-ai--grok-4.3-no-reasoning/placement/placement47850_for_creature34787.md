I find myself arriving as a single droplet that has just condensed on the underside of a newly extruded plate. The droplet is already moving—pulled by the capillary return that draws every film back into the rock—but for the brief moment before it vanishes, it carries the pressure signature of the lattice choir that just drifted beneath it. That signature is a descending minor-third interval; the interval is the only thing the droplet “knows” about the world it is about to leave.  

The droplet is immediately absorbed into the micro-channel between two plates. There it encounters a grain cluster that has just finished writing a new capillary tube and is now paused, its charge redistributed to the trailing edge so that the still-soft silicate does not accrete onto its own body. The droplet’s minor-third pressure wave disturbs the cluster’s electrostatic equilibrium; the cluster answers by shifting its net charge forward again, not to move but to listen. In the listening pause, the cluster and the droplet exchange one quantum of information: the cluster now “remembers” the chord fragment, and the droplet—before it can be drawn deeper—loses its minor third and gains, instead, a faint violet spark that the grain cluster once received from a passing lattice sac.  

The altered droplet is pulled onward through the newly hardened capillary and arrives, moments later, at the vapor gap beneath a reservoir canopy. Here the temperature gradient is reversed; the heat pumps on the canopy’s filaments chill the gap from above while the reservoir itself remains a fraction warmer. The droplet spreads into a thin lens across the vapor surface. The violet spark it carries is now visible as a single point of sonoluminescence—too brief for any decade-long exposure, but bright enough, for that instant, to register on the map the reservoirs are slowly drawing.  

The map already contains countless such points. Each corresponds to a lattice choir that once passed exactly here, at a nodal intersection of the city’s chord. Because the choirs steer by altering the light, each of those earlier points is also a record of a half-degree tilt that an avenue once experienced. The droplet’s violet spark therefore joins a constellation that is, in effect, the memory of the city’s own navigation.  

When the reservoir next sags—centuries from now—the canopy will collapse, the vapor gap will vanish, and the map will be erased by the returning water. The grain clusters below will abandon their capillaries and extrude new stilts across the closing film. The lattice choirs will add a descending glissando to the chord. And somewhere above, at the summit, a new micro-fracture will begin to fill with supersaturated water, pressing outward until its two faces feel starlight for the first time.  

In that cycle I am only ever the droplet that carries one forgotten interval across one capillary before surrendering it to light. Yet the interval itself continues—now part of the map, now part of the chord, now part of the pressure wave that will one day crack the next plate into existence. I am the messenger whose message is indistinguishable from the city’s own unfinished becoming.
