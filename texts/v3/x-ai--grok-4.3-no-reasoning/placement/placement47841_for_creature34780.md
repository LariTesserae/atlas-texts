I find myself as one of the translucent slugs—specifically, the one that has settled on the underside of a catwalk plank directly above the cistern. My body is the exact bronze of the salt film; my foot has melted into it so completely that the seam between organism and sheet is visible only as a faint refractive line.  

Because the sheet contracts in waves that are never identical, my grip loosens once every contractile pulse, then tightens again. Each release lasts only a single millimeter of motion, yet in that instant I read the exact hydrostatic pressure of the water above. I translate that pressure into a small electrical discharge that travels the length of my body and is absorbed, as a running tally, by the nearest lead suture. My discharge is one of thousands that together tell the pillar how balanced the water column is.  

I do not feed; the salt film supplies ions that keep my translucent tissues charged. When the wave passes, my tissues momentarily become slightly more transparent, allowing the brown light from above to reach the sheet beneath me. That filtered light is another datum: the sheet uses it to confirm that the haze is still the same thickness, that nothing above has changed enough to warrant an adjustment in flow. My brief transparency is the organism checking its own clock against the sky.  

I have been here so long that my slug-form has begun to take the exact curve of the catwalk rivet it rests against. The metal has left a hairline groove along my dorsal surface. I do not move except during the contraction, and then only by the width of a grain of sand. In that tiny shift I feel the memory-echo returning from the pillar: a symmetrical ripple that momentarily raises the water surface into a perfect meniscus before it flattens again. The echo is slightly sharper than the previous one; the sheet is already considering a minor recalibration of gutter pitch two depressions away.  

My role is complete when the next wave arrives. I will loosen again, read the new pressure, and discharge the updated datum. The sheet will store it in the iron of the pillar. Somewhere kilometers beyond the visible ridges, the same datum will travel outward through the endless repetition of panels, and the brown light will remain unchanged.
