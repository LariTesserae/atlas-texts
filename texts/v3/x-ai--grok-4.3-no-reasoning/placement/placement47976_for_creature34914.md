I would find myself not as a separate creature moving across the plateau, but as one of the brief motes of condensed vapor drifting downward inside a sink-hole shaft. For a short while I would occupy the precise cubic meter of empty air the mat has allocated between the rising breath of a lymph-node pool and the faint, perpetual luminescence of the clouds. My existence would be measured in minutes—long enough to reflect a single tack of a passing glider, short enough that no two motes ever occupy the same space. When the mat exhales again, I would be drawn back upward, reabsorbed into the same slow circulation that turns the entire plateau.
