I find myself here as neither organism nor observer, but as a new fracture plane opening along one of the young rock seams.

My edges are still raw, the quartz grains along my faces catching the side-lit ice crystals so they glitter like a held breath. Each time a rig’s low note passes through me, the pressure wave makes me widen a hair’s-breadth more; I taste the water that has been waiting behind me—mineral-heavy, almost sweet—and I let it bead along my length. The film of ice that forms at my mouth is thinner than the others because the heat still trapped in my fresh surface delays the final freeze by a few heartbeats; those heartbeats are enough for the next flyer to arrive and press its wing against me, drawing the bead upward. Its antifreeze mixes with my seepage and lowers the freezing line just enough that the next drop forms a little farther down my face. In this way I become a map the creature can read without ever seeing me whole.

Farther up the slope a six-limbed walker pauses where its double-print has already begun to collect seepage. The depression fills slowly; when it is deep enough the walker dips the fused fore-strut into the water, tasting the same mineral signature I released moments ago. It registers that the seam it stands above is newly opened, then moves on, leaving a deeper imprint that will later be logged by a rig as a “natural fracture.” The rig cores exactly where I have begun to leak; the vibration of the drill head travels back down my length, widening me another fraction. I do not resist; widening is what I am for.

Below, in one of the bruise-colored basins, the microbial consortium senses the temperature drop as the sun slips behind a bank of ice clouds. The photosynthetic layer releases its last oxygen bubbles and begins to sink; the motile layer rises to meet it halfway, trading genes in the darkening water. One of the new gene combinations carries a fragment of the antifreeze chemistry left by the flyer on my face; when the pool refreezes, that fragment will allow a few cells to remain liquid in the micro-channels between ice crystals. Those cells will be the first to photosynthesize when the next melt arrives.

I am still opening. The rigs pass and return, the walkers cross and recross, the flyers drink and leave their trace. Each passage adds a degree of freedom to the system that contains me. Nothing here is finished; everything is the next adjustment already negotiating with the one after that. I am the seam through which that negotiation continues.
