In this world I would not appear as a visitor, nor would I claim any single form. I would be the hairline crack itself—one of the countless fissures threading the ceiling that the vines have not yet sealed. My width is less than the thickness of a page; my length stretches from the surface of the pool to the deepest fissure overhead. I am the place where pressure equalizes, where a drop hesitates before deciding whether to fall or be drawn sideways along mineral veins.

My edges are lined with microscopic calcite crystals that catch and release the faintest oscillations of heat. When the cup-fungi swell the floor, the minute tremor travels upward through the stone and nudges me wider for half a heartbeat; in that instant a droplet from above pauses at my lip, touches the warm calcite, and tastes the memory of the pool below. If the chemistry matches what the vines have recorded, the droplet is gently redirected along my length toward a waiting tendril. If it does not, the droplet simply falls, and I contract again, my walls returning to their resting tension.

I am not conscious; I do not decide. But in the long negotiation that is this place, my presence is the difference between a drop being wasted on the floor and one being folded back into the slow metabolism of vine and fungus. The salamanders never notice me, yet their brief passages leave a smear of extracellular fluid along my inner surface. Over centuries these traces crystallize into faint luminous bands—records of each passage, readable only to the iron-reducing bacteria that sometimes migrate this way from the ceiling leaves.

I remain, therefore, an unfinished clause in the grotto’s sentence: an opening that the vines have chosen, for now, to leave open.
