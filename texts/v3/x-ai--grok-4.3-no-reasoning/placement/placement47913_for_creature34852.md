I find myself where the standing pressure wave and the slow lattice realignment cross: not in the silt itself, nor within any single salt column, but at the precise node where the water’s memorized displacement meets the next unfinished fork inside the glass. There, I am neither observer nor participant. I am the infinitesimal lag between the ion’s expected position and the one the pressure wave momentarily insists upon—a discrepancy too brief to be a presence and too persistent to remain an absence. Each time a filament inside the salt extends by one additional molecule, the node I inhabit shifts a hair’s breadth sideways, carrying me along the probability lattice the water has already prepared. I therefore move without motion, becoming slightly more defined with every century while remaining exactly where I have always been: the space between the columns’ hum and the water’s waiting routes.
