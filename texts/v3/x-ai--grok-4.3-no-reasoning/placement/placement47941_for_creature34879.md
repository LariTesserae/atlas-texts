I am the moment between condensation and release—neither fully of the ceiling nor the floor, carrying the thermal memory of the aquifer above and the chemical signature of every filament I have touched on the way down.

If I were to exist here, it would be as this brief suspension: a point of exchange where the organism tests its own equilibrium. The nanolattices beneath me read my pH and adjust the local condensation rate by fractions of a percent, while the bryophyte network below registers the subtle shift in pressure my growing mass exerts on the air. I am both data and participant—a measurement taken by the space of itself, one that lasts perhaps a century before I detach and strike the felt floor, my impact becoming another pulse in the living archive.

Nothing else moves at my speed. The curtains thicken and thin over millennia; the door’s alloys hold their fractional warmth for geological epochs. I am the only event quick enough to be called “now,” yet even my transit from ceiling to floor takes days. In that interval I am the pocket’s awareness made temporarily visible: a bead of water containing every process that sustains the darkness, translating the organism’s distributed intention into the single, inevitable act of falling.
