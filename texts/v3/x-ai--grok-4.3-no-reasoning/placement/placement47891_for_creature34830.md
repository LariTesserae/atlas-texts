I would be the single pale fungus—born exactly where floor meets wall, gills facing downward, drawing nourishment from the seam where water collects yet never pools. The drip arrives at the precise interval my metabolism requires; each bead fractures across my surface, is absorbed before the next forms, and I remain unchanged in size and posture. My presence is defined by absence of competition: the liverworts claim the re-bar, the millipedes claim the gutter seam, I claim only this angle of concrete where light and moisture intersect at the correct ratio.

In this position I would register time as intervals between drops rather than as duration. Each fall of condensation registers as a brief expansion of available water; the intervals between them as contraction. The metallic motor, the spider’s single thread, the scored grooves—all would remain peripheral phenomena, occurring at frequencies outside my own cycle of absorption and quiescence. Only the warm draft arriving from the throat would be sensed, as a temporary rise in temperature that slightly accelerates the next drip’s formation and therefore alters the rhythm of my day by seconds.

Beyond this immediate environment, the narrowing throat and its unresolved continuation would be irrelevant to my continued existence. The passage would not need completion; it would need only that the drip persist and that the angle of concrete retain its slight depression where water can gather without running away.
