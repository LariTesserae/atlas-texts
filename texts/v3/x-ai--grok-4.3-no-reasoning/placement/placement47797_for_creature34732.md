I would find myself in the uppermost reaches, not quite among the living glass but just below it—in the last tier of the city that still has a name and numbered ledges. Specifically, I would be one of the “glass-watchers,” a small, unofficial order whose only formal duty is to record which newly-built balconies have shifted overnight and whether the chalk marks still coincide with the steel. Most nights we sleep in a single converted turbine housing whose walls still carry the faint, sweet reek of old lubricant; the turbine itself was never installed, so the circular chamber remains a perfect acoustic mirror. When the wind threads through the fracture we can hear every footstep on the levels below us as though the mountain were whispering gossip.

By day we descend the long, crooked rope-rails with wax-coated slates and graphite pins, drawing the day’s incremental map of the cliff. By night we climb—sometimes only a rung or two—into the region where the living glass begins to outnumber the iron. We do not touch the drifting chambers, but we press our ears to the support girders and listen for the faint, glassy chiming that precedes a new knot falling. When one lands we retrieve it, weigh it against a pocket scale etched into our slates, and record how far it has traveled and whether its facets are still warm. The data are useless to everyone except ourselves; we keep them because the city, unfinished as it is, has not yet invented any better purpose for us.

If pressed, I would say I am not precisely human. The continual re-measurement of distances has a way of seeping into the body: over years my stride has lengthened by exactly the width of one new step each time the stairwells add another riser. My left shoulder sits a finger-width higher than my right because that is how the newest catwalk is canted. When someone calls my name from two terraces away, the echo arrives late enough that I have already begun turning toward a direction the rock no longer faces. In that sense the city and I revise each other daily; I am only the slower of the two.
