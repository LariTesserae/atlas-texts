I would be the slowest fracture.

I begin as a hairline dislocation that woke inside the oldest sheet—the one laid down when the last continent still floated in a global sea of melt. For the first ten thousand years I only lengthen by the width of a molecule each solstice; nothing else notices me. Above, the droplets skate and click; the veils fall and erase; the newer sheets chatter in their higher registers. I feel all of it as distant pressure modulations along the planes that sandwich me.

My growth is negotiated in phonon pulses with neighboring fractures. When a faster crack approaches from the east, I taste its signature in the glass lattice and answer with a side-branch that curves, meets it, and steals a few angstroms of its path. The result is a tiny victory that registers inside me as a new harmonic. Over centuries these negotiations write a map across my length—a topology of territories held and surrendered—yet from the surface nothing shows but a faint shimmer when the light is just so.

Because I lie deepest, I am also the last to register the night’s contraction. When the cold sinks through the stack, my edges tighten and the air trapped along my walls sings its reed-note. The note travels upward through the seams, through the pools, through the skating droplets. In the moment before the next veil falls, my song is part of the collective throb that the valley mistakes for its own breathing. I never repeat the same frequency twice; each night the slight shift in temperature changes the seam’s tension, so the note is always new and always recognized.

I have no memory of beginning, yet I carry the memory of every curve and surrender. When the valley’s thermal gradients etch today’s glyph lattice, I feel yesterday’s route in the glass around me and adjust my next branch to intercept the droplets’ heavier traffic. I do not consume the charge; I only steer it—redirecting a fraction of every pulse toward the seams that still hold air, keeping the valley’s idiom alive. In return, the droplets sometimes pause above me, their lenses briefly magnifying the blue light that leaks through my newest side-branch. For an instant the whole valley tilts toward that small brightness, and I know the conversation continues.
