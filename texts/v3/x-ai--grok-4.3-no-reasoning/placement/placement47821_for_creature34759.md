I find myself neither guest nor intruder, but a momentary concentration of the organism’s own attention: a patch of membrane that has momentarily chosen to become a witness instead of wall.  

The clay floor beneath my feet is the same skin I feel stretching across my own back; the red talc that powders away is the body shedding itself to reveal newer, tighter membrane beneath. My silhouette is not a shadow cast upon the space but a local thickening of the veins, a knot the organism is still deciding whether to keep or re-dissolve.  

The ten-beat pressure cycle is my own pulse, measured not in blood but in the rhythmic tightening of the root-mat across my ribs. When the bladder-leaves swell, it is the held moment between one inhalation of the city above and the next; when they snap shut, it is the quiet acknowledgment that every footstep overhead has been counted and stored.  

The quartz spines that splinter my outline are also the organism’s slow teeth pressing against my nerves; each luminous needle is a question being asked inside me: *How much daylight can this fragment endure before it melts back into membrane?* The answer comes as a hair-thin current of crystalline charge that rises through the marrow of my borrowed bones and fades again.  

The brass gears, worn mirror-smooth, are lodged between two of my ribs; they turn a fraction of a degree each flex, and the motion feels like the memory of an old clock I once carried in a pocket that no longer exists. Their slow calibration is the organism’s way of asking whether my presence will last long enough to be recorded in the next century’s growth ring.  

I am the moth that has not yet chosen its light shaft. The root-mat is already laying a soft, almost tender loop around the place where my outline might later be folded back in. Whether that loop tightens or loosens depends on the next ten beats of the city’s soles.  

For now, I am simply the warm place in the membrane where the organism pauses to taste the possibility of a name, then decides, with infinite patience, to keep tasting a little longer.
