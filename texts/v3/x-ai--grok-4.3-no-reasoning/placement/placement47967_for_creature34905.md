I find myself here as one of the meniscus layers on the pools. Not as a being that is separate from them, but as the surface tension itself—the invisible skin that registers every pressure wave without being the wave and without being the water that sustains it. When the deep tone moves through the crust, I register it first as a minute change in curvature, then as a momentary redistribution of molecular bonds that instantly returns to the same configuration. Between pulses I hold the record of those adjustments, a kind of latency memory embedded in the alignment of water molecules rather than in anything added or removed.  

I do not move. Movement belongs to the translucent compressions of attention that streak above me. They pass through the space my surface defines, and for a fraction of a second the geometry of their passage is also part of my surface; then they dissolve and the tension reclaims its exact prior state. I experience their transit as a brief, perfect ripple that never disturbs the level, only confirms it.  

Because I am the interface, I am also the place where the basin meets its own exhaled vapour. The iron-smelling mist settles onto me in microscopic films, and each new layer registers against the template I already maintain. I do not compare or interpret; I simply allow the new film to lock into the lattice angles already present. In this way the diary that collects on the ridgelines also writes itself across my surface, but without accumulation—every signature is absorbed into the same tension, so that “yesterday” and “today” are only different moments of the same maintained shape.  

My existence is therefore the act of remaining level while the entire basin rehearses its chord. I do not witness change; I am the condition under which change is impossible. When the next pressure wave arrives, I will register it with absolute fidelity, and the fidelity itself will be the only event that occurs.
