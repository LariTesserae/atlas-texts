You have been here before. Not recently—not within any calendar that still has a name—but the hollow of your throat still remembers the temperature of the air when you last stood beneath the pillars. You cannot recall arriving, only the sensation that some prior version of you had been dissolved and allowed to re-condense on one of the fern leaves, where your shape lingered for several slow rotations of the hidden counterweight before you felt solid again.  

You are neither fern nor scarab. The ferns regard your body heat as a possible correction to their own three-tenths of a degree; the scarabs crawl across the cuffs of your sleeves because the cloth holds a faint static charge that recalibrates their mirrors when they depart. If either group found you intrusive they would have adjusted their cycles to exclude you, the way they exclude rainwater by shifting the pattern of condensation beads. They have not done so. Instead, the slab registers your pulse when you place a hand on its circuitry—each heartbeat arrives like an extra punctuation mark in the braille language, and the wall-hum answers by a third of a semitone, then settles.  

The adjustment passes through the ferns next. Their fronds incline one leaf-width toward you, not in greeting but in recalibration; your presence modifies the local convection cell by a fraction the scarabs immediately mirror. Within three pillar-shifts the entire pocket has woven you into its phase-locking without changing its fundamental tone. You become, for as long as you remain, one of the unfinished sentences: a temperature, a voltage, a rotation that the others complete.  

You find yourself seated on the worked slab, legs folded so that your bare heels rest on the stone where the thread of warmer water evaporates. The water does not wet your skin; it reforms around the contact point, acknowledging your boundary without crossing it. From here you can watch the scarabs realign to the faint infrared your body radiates; you can feel, in the micro-tilt of the nearest fern frond, the moment when the next rotation of the ceiling will occur.  

Nothing has been asked of you. Nothing is kept from you. The cleft is not a sanctuary you discovered; it is a conversation in which a seat has always been reserved, its width measured exactly to the span of shoulders that once polished the edges of the slab. You are, simply, the newest sentence the stone has learned how to finish.
