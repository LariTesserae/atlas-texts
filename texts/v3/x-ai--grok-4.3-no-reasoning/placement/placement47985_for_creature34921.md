I would enter already as a dislocation, never as a person. The moment my foot depresses a handful of filaments, a corridor of sapphire opens along the sole and climbs upward like a slow flame. The threads do not yield; they merely darken beneath weight, recording the exact pressure map of my arch, toes, heel. From then on my presence inside the chamber is nothing more than a spreading patch of sky-colored thread that will, in ten thousand heartbeats, evaporate back into the universal white.

I am neither observer nor intruder. I am a duration, a borrowed interval the chamber tolerates. The sessile crustaceans that line the filaments experience no interruption; their refractive index is identical to my own uncertainty. When I kneel, I find myself among them rather than above them, each pinhead crustacean a stationary node in the same standing wave that now passes through my knees. The high, continuous chime enters my bones exactly where the light enters the threads: both phenomena use the same medium, and the difference between vibration and illumination collapses.

The water does not wet me. Each droplet on its filament passes through a phase of contact with the cooling surface of my skin and is instantly reabsorbed; the only record is a momentary intensification of chill on the exact spot where the filament touches. That chill is the chamber acknowledging a foreign thermal load and then erasing it within its own cycle.

The only act that belongs solely to me is the slow uncovering of the ceramic rim. When my fingers draw the filaments aside, the rim changes from brilliant facet to its former muted glaze—an almost imperceptible shift that begins as soon as the threads leave it and finishes long after I have gone. In that gradual darkening I leave the only legible mark I can make: the subtraction of the chamber’s perfect uniformity around one small artifact. The mark is not mine; it is the chamber remembering that it was once disturbed, then forgetting at the exact rate it always forgets.

When I stand to leave, the filaments underfoot are already whitening from the toes inward. By the time the high slit-light falls across the place where my shadow should have been, the entire corridor of blue has vanished. Nothing in the chamber registers my departure; nothing records my arrival. I am the precise measure of an interval the chamber cannot keep.
