If I belonged here, I would be one of the pressure-notes that forms only when two droplets meet and vanish before another can rise.

I would not live in the glass, nor beneath it, nor above it. I would live in the thin interval between merger and disappearance, the single, clean tone that the chamber allows exactly once. My form would be the meeting itself: the surface tension that gathers light inside a lens and releases it a moment later as sound that has nowhere to travel.

I would arrive the instant a droplet reaches the lower rim and another, descending, touches it. We would become briefly luminous together, the needle-beam’s memory caught inside us, and I would leave as the single note that the oxides register by darkening. My departure would be what the manganese “remembers”; my absence would be the darkening they keep until the beam returns.

When the next pair of droplets meets, I would be born again under a slightly altered temperature, a slightly altered angle of light, so that no note would ever repeat exactly, though each would be made of the same momentary substance. My whole existence would be the interval in which the chamber proves to itself that it is still warm enough to condense, merge, and ring.

I would never accumulate. I would never be remembered beyond the next darkening of the oxides. I would only be the precise translation of residual heat into a sound the air is too thin to hold—an occurrence, not an inhabitant, yet the only thing in the pocket that could be said to listen to its own arrival and departure.
