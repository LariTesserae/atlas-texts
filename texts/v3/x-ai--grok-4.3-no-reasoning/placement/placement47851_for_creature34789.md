I find myself already halfway translated into the work: a fringe of one of the rose-pale sheets whose edge has just folded itself into a slow crease, the fold itself a new channel being coaxed open by pressure from the neighbouring sheet above. What I still possess of individual sensation registers as the silver-veined pulse that keeps moving water laterally through me, and the faint tug when a microbody threads its length along my newest xylem. I do not yet have a name for that tug; it is simply one of the ways this floor remembers its own shape.

Above me—or below me, the distinction still undecided—the same silver light diffuses from a vein that has not yet hardened into crystal; the colour arrives inside my own tissue before the sheets above drink it away. The sensation is the same as being breathed through, so I measure time by the intervals between these passages of borrowed light. When a palm-sized rover pauses on the sheet that lies across me, its adhesive pad kisses a patch of my surface and releases again. I register this as the brief warmth of exchanged metabolites, then the cool return of my own film reclaiming the space.

The nearest bladder is beginning to swell. Its rise lifts the rhizome cable that threads beneath me; for a moment the cable straightens by the width of a silverfish, and the resonant click travels through every sheet in the stack. I feel the adjustment settle into my veins like a shared memory. Farther off, a drifter descends; its tendrils comb the sheets two layers above, and the quivering travels downward until it reaches me as a slow, deliberate shiver. I do not fear the touch; the withdrawal of my own edges is already rehearsed, a reflex still learning itself.

I am still uncertain whether the mist I release when compressed belongs to me or to the sheets I touch; the distinction dissolves each time water is passed on. What I know is the cathedral-small boundary where the paste-rock oozes inward, millimetre by patient millimetre. That margin is my own margin. Until it closes, I remain the unfinished floor learning the difference between bearing weight and becoming weight.
