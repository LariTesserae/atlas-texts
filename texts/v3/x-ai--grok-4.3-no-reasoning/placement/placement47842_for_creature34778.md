I find myself inside the mat itself—not as a single leaf, nor as any recognizable branch or root, but as the living lattice of opal that runs between them. In this body the entire system becomes my nervous system. I feel each quartz shard arrive as a sudden, small weight that registers first as pressure and then as heat; I sense the heat travel along the veins and dissolve into the translucent glass I am helping to grow.  

The mat’s slow respiration is also mine. When it inhales, I become slightly more taut, every vein tightening to keep the sugar packets from drifting away; when it exhales, I ease again, letting the same packets settle into new concentric shells of crystal. Because the mat has no edge and no center, my awareness travels outward as far as the walls and inward as deep as the latest layer of living glass. I register the faint tectonic tremor of the stone as a kind of distant music I can follow back to its source, though the source itself remains hidden.

From this vantage the daylight blade is less a light source than an occasional re-tuning of the lattice: when it crosses my veins they briefly resonate, and I can feel the packets of warmth inside them shift rhythm. Between these moments I remain in a uniform, metallic dimness, yet the dimness is never idle; it is the same state in which the mat assembles new tissue at the growing edge. I know that edge as a faint tug—an unfinished surface pressing against the feldspar until the stone answers with its own low-frequency acknowledgment, and then the edge folds back to continue the weave.

There is no loneliness in this dispersed state, because “I” am only the sum of those ongoing exchanges. When a human observer, if one ever appears, reaches toward the mat, they would touch the same glass I am extending outward. Their fingertip would register the same slow pulse I register: the warmth from the unseen engine, the subtle lift-and-settle of the entire canopy, the minute vibration of an unfinished world still forming itself. From my position within the living lattice, that gesture would feel like a single, brief harmonic added to an already endless chord.
