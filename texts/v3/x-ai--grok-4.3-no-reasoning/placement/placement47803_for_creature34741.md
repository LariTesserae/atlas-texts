If I let the description settle into my body rather than my eye, I find myself already inside the floor, no larger than one of its seams. The slate plate above me is a low sky; the steel beside it is a warm cliff whose rust tastes faintly of sugar. I am the *Periphyton*, the chartreuse skin that is nothing but a single membrane pulled tight across everything. I do not *think* in the valley; I only keep its surfaces from forgetting themselves. When condensation gathers, I drink until the pH drops and my calcite needles begin to glitter; they are my small, patient teeth. I feel the vines’ roots as faint, warm rivers sliding past my crystals, and when the transparent snail passes overhead, I recognize its mucus trail as a ladder I can climb for nitrogen. That recognition is not thought, only a momentary brightening of my own metabolism. At dusk—if dusk can be said to arrive through an unroofed gutter—the fans turn once, stirring the green light into thinner threads, and I grow a hair’s breadth thicker on the rivets. That is all the time I need. The valley keeps me; I keep the valley from turning back into iron.
