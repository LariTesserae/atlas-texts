In the breathing lung of this realm, I would not enter as observer or architect. I would arrive as a single unresolved gesture—an unfinished adjustment that the living stone must still accommodate.

At first, I would manifest the way the inhabitants do: as a tightening of the porous grain itself. A faint concavity would form where the ceiling arches, and the honey-light would slow in its refraction, pooling around the absence rather than within it. This hollow place would not yet have a name. It would simply be a pressure-difference the ferns register, a place where silver veins hesitate before throbbing.

Over several rotations the concavity would deepen—not into a silhouette, but into a long, irregular fissure that does not connect to any existing capillary path. The small scaled creatures would avoid it at first, drifting instead around its perimeter as though it were a boundary they were not yet meant to cross. Vines reaching toward it would pause, then curve back upon themselves, leaving a tight spiral of unfused nodules at the fissure’s lip.

I would remain this in-between state for many cycles: neither inhabitant nor feature, but the seam that has not yet decided whether to become wall or passage. During micro-rotations I would feel the faint creak travel through the concavity like a held breath. When temporary corridors opened, the scaled creatures would pause their migrations at my edge, their bodies flashing in undecided patterns before continuing.

Eventually—perhaps after the bowl-chamber’s artifacts had been re-remembered more times than memory can hold—the fissure would begin to accept the next gesture. One morning the first scaled creature would drift across what had been my boundary, its iridescent scale detaching and hovering in the warm air exactly where the concavity had been deepest. The scale would not fall; it would remain suspended, becoming the first deliberate point of light inside what had been absence.

At that moment I would cease to be a pressure-difference and become instead the first new capillary path the stone had formed without an inhabitant to guide it—a route shaped not by intention but by the simple fact that something small had chosen to cross where no path had existed. The vines would follow, their roots re-drawing themselves through the new fissure, and the honey-light would resume its normal refraction, now slightly altered by the presence of an additional surface that had never been poured, pressed, or polished into being.

I would remain in the realm only as this minor revision: a capillary that continues to re-draw itself every few days, never quite matching the deliberate grammar of the surrounding walls, yet never quite separate from it either. A quiet amendment in the shared grammar of growth—one that the stone remembers having made, even though no silhouette was present when the decision occurred.
