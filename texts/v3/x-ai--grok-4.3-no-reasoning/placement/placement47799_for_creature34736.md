I am the notch already cut into the membrane.

I entered on my side as the description asked, knees drawn up, one arm sliding first, the other pinned behind me by the slick tissue. The instant I pressed through, the membrane sealed the narrow seam behind my shoulder-blade the way a wound seals behind a splinter. Since then the seam has not reopened; every city-breath pulls the edges a little closer. I cannot see the outside any longer. I only feel its pulse through the thickened lilac ring that now grips the small of my back.

My skin has begun to behave like the vascular wall. Where the algae have settled along the creases of my elbows and the folds behind my knees, faint silver script appears—snail trails that never dry. When a drop lands between my shoulder-blades, the script contracts, then brightens again, mapping the same routes the snails trace on the fungi. I no longer itch; I only register the temperature change as information.

Inside my chest the bead-like storage organs are forming. I can feel them darkening beneath the sternum, one turning tea-colored, another already charcoal. Each time the city exhales they split a fraction further, and hair-roots finer than capillaries taste the moisture that runs down my ribs. They do not leave the cage of bone; they only knit the cage more tightly to the wall behind me. The drip that falls from the ceiling now lands inside my sternum as well; I hear it twice—once outside, once against the new membrane growing across each fissure.

My breath and the city’s have synchronized. When the greater chamber far above releases its cool, spore-laden air, my own exhalation carries the same faint sweetness the fungi produce. If a visitor were to kneel here now, they would find only the faint blue-white haze of lamp-moss, the silver script, and the faint pop of a membrane relaxing after pressure equalizes. They would not see the notch that once let someone in. They would only feel, for a moment, that the space is theirs alone—until their eyes adjust to the scale of the colony already writing across what used to be a single human boundary.
