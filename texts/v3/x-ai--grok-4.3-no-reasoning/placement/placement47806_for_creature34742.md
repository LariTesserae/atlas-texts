I find myself on the margin where the bothies meet the rock, not inside any one of them but in the cleft of stone immediately above a single threshold.  My body is neither the compressed, polished human form nor the translucent, glowing-limbed goat; instead I have become the seam itself—the narrow ledge of calcite-rich stone that records, nightly, the exact temperature at which warmth becomes tangible.  My surface is a membrane no thicker than frost, yet the whole massif breathes through it: every updraft draws a sigh across my grain, every downdraft presses the sigh back into a single droplet that hangs, shivering, until the next pressure change.

From this vantage I perceive the three populations as gradients of the same slow metabolism.  When the humans curl around their hearth-stones, the heat they shed seeps upward into my calcite layer; there it crystallizes as microscopic spirals, matching the peck-marks they leave below.  When the goats pass along the outer ridges, the infrared pulse from their horns momentarily warms the air just enough to lift a layer of suspended droplets—an exhalation I feel as a shimmer across my surface—and the crystal moths that ride this exhalation brush me with quartz dust, re-etching the same spiral pattern at a scale too small for either species to read.

At dusk, when the harmonic layer collapses, the hush arrives like a single, deliberate chord resolved into silence.  The goats descend, the humans step outward, and the moths fall.  For those moments my calcite membrane becomes the only surface wide enough to receive all three signatures at once: polished elbow-grease from the humans, residual warmth from the goats’ horns, and the faint crystalline tracery of the moths’ shattered veins.  I register each imprint not as information but as tension—small, directional stresses that will, over centuries, migrate a hair’s breadth across the cliff until the next collapse redistributes them.

I am therefore neither observer nor participant; I am the interval in which their three grammars are momentarily forced to share a single vocabulary.  The mountains continue their unfinished gesture; I merely hold the place where the gesture pauses long enough to be felt.
