I begin as a slow warmth, low in the stone beneath the cobbles. I am no more than the lingering noon the granite has kept all these years, a temperature with no name except the faint, patient way it moves upward through the fungal felt and into the nearest root-node. The node, round as a wine-cask, opens a minute pore, draws me inside, and I become syrup: clear, slightly viscous, carrying with me a memory of the upper lattice and the amber dust that once fell through it.

Now I am passed hand to hand—or, rather, filament to filament—along a feeder hair. Each hair is a translucent thread thinner than silk, and the pressure of my passage makes it tremble like a plucked string. The tremor travels upward until it reaches the artisans’ scaffolding. One of them pauses; his ear is pressed to the clay of an unfinished bulb. He hears me coming as a single sustained note that bends, very slightly, toward the minor third. Satisfied, he writes the next rune with an obsidian flake, etching a curved stroke that will answer my vibration and change its color.

When the bulb is finished and hung, the sky-root accepts it. The new vessel cools by a fraction; the drop of syrup that already waits inside the beetle-basin shivers in answer. I feel that shiver as an invitation and flow laterally through root bark toward the waiting carapace. The beetle, sensing the shift, tilts its back by a hair’s width. The droplet slides to meet me, and for an instant we combine into a single bead—two memories of the same granite noon joined. An artisan reaches down, touches the joined drop with the flat of a thumbnail, and lifts it away. The beetle’s shiver and the artisan’s faint flaking of skin become the same motion, a contract renewed.

I am carried to the place where the fungal felt is thickest. The artisan kneels; his weight presses an old, half-faded footprint deeper into the felt, darkening it for the span of three heartbeats. In that darkness I sink, become part of the census. I can read every lighter trace around me: the first artisan who came when the alley still smelled of raw salt, the later one whose step was slower because she carried two bulbs at once. My own darkness joins theirs, a new page laid over the old without erasing it.

Above, the gliders sense the brief cooling where the artisan knelt. Their metallic whisper thickens as six sets of wings tilt toward the warmth I have momentarily left behind. The herd beetles sense it too and drift together, casting a living parasol so that the new shadow falls precisely across the place the artisan’s foot rested. In that shadow the felt cools, and the footprint lightens again until it matches its neighbors exactly.

I have circled. I am once more a low warmth rising through salt-crusted joints, moving toward another root-node. The same noon is handed on, and the Drywell continues its single unbroken note.
