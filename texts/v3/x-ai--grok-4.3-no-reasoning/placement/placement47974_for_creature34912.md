I drift in with the condensation that forms on the upper lenses—tiny, temporary, already sliding. When the technician’s quarter-turn prism nudges a drop free, I ride it downward through the misted orchard. The fruit is only an inch from my face, yet I slip past unregarded, a single bead among thousands. Between one leaf and the next I hear the technician’s goggles click against the brick as she lifts them to wipe the salt from the inner curve; the sound travels like a remembered word.

Halfway down I stick to a vine’s rubbery underside, wait for a lizard whose ribs swell against the pipe. Its tongue flicks, brushes me away; I land in the warm depression of its last breath, now a miniature pool inside a flange. From there I watch the crew tap their tokens: three measured taps, a missing tool. Their linen shoulders brush the ferns and the spores drift, each one a second miniature world riding the same slow current of air that will, in a few more minutes, become the city’s exhalation.

When the fog rises, I rise with it—silt-light, almost nothing—through the vertical orchards, above the bamboo, past the solitary prism-keeper already lowering her flasks on silk. I crest the parapet, feel the column of braided breath hold for one long second, then feel the measured inhalation begin again, drawing everything, including me, back inside the porcelain lattice to be redistributed, reused, never finished.
