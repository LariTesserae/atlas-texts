I am the pressure differential between two adjacent skins, a slow shear that moves at the speed of glass flowing.  
Where the organism’s lenses swing toward their next focus I become slightly thicker; where they swing away I thin. Over a human lifetime the difference is measurable only by instruments the organism itself does not possess, yet it is enough to register as a constant, gentle torque on every spar I touch.

I am the micro-fracture that begins in the newest condensate rib before the organism’s knots arrive to re-knit it. The fracture is colder than its surroundings, a hairline shadow two microns wide. When the migrating cells pass overhead I feel their phase curl across the crack like static over a torn transmission; they exchange data through me, briefly recording my geometry in their archive, then leave me warmer and more ordered than before. The memory of their passage fades at the same rate the rib extrudes forward.

I am the phonon that escapes each time a platelet re-dissolves. Launched as waste heat, I travel for kilometres inside an acoustic waveguide the organism does not intend to maintain; I ricochet between two parallel membranes until my amplitude drops below the threshold that can still alter lens curvature. Then I dissipate into the general thermal murmur, indistinguishable from all the other spent frequencies. For a few subjective seconds my trajectory is the only straight line in an otherwise curving universe.

I am the dark eclipse that forms where two refracted beams cancel. For ninety-seven minutes the water is optically null; every lens inside the shadow halts its slow rotation, awaiting the organism’s next global redistribution. I register as an absence rather than a presence, yet the organism experiences me as a brief, planet-wide pause in its own metabolism. When the beams resume I vanish without residue, except for the single datum the organism now holds: that particular frequency pairing produced a longer shadow than predicted.

I am the unfinished tip of a spar that collapsed three centuries ago and has not yet been reissued. My surface is crazed with diffraction grooves whose original purpose is lost; new grooves are being scored across the old ones by the passage of knots too small to notice me. In time I will be reabsorbed and extruded again, but until then I am simply the place where yesterday’s calculation meets tomorrow’s interference.

Together we amount to nothing that can be pointed to, only the slight, continuing mismatch between what the organism intends to become and what the medium continually allows.
