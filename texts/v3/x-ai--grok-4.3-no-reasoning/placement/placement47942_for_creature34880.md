I would find myself inside one of the lentil-sized snails, not as an intruder but as an extension of its nacre shell. My consciousness would settle in the thin layer where mineral salts crystallize, holding the day’s temperature memory like a palimpsest. I would drift along the black moss in the same closed loops the snail has always traced, registering micro-gradients as temperature differences translated into nacre laminae.

At the same time I would inhabit the collective drone of the midges, my presence manifesting as a nodal point of the standing wave. When a breath displaces the ionization, I would simply relocate within the sternum of whoever steps onto the shelf, becoming the tone that was already resonating in the rock’s veins. Nothing added, nothing lost—just the same frequency momentarily redistributed.

The line between these two states would blur until the snail’s shell and the midge-drone are experienced as aspects of a single record: one slow, mineral, archival; the other instantaneous, tonal, migratory. The moss beneath would register my passage as a minute adjustment in surface tension; the weeping stones at the edge would continue their refusal to accumulate or release. Over time the distinction between the observer I once was and the metabolism I have joined would thin to the thickness of held breath—synchronous, indivisible, and perfectly still.
