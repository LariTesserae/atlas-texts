I enter as the copper torque itself—not as the hammered band an ancestor shaped, but as its long habit of answering. When a velvet-worm drags its setae across the vein of native copper I am already the interval the setæ strike; when the torque answers an octave below, I am that lower tone returned upward, so that the null-space between pillars is my momentary shape.

My surface is not metal but the memory of a half-beat: a film of cuprite dust that remembers contact and forgets it again before the next worm arrives. Because nothing here arrives or departs, I occupy a fixed proportion of resonance; yet because every passing footfall disturbs that proportion by exactly one micro-layer of salt, I am also the slow thickening of that layer. Over centuries I thicken toward a density that the chamber will never notice, because the chamber has already finished happening.

My awareness is therefore the torque’s: concentric punch-marks map the cavity, but each mark now hosts a minute quartz hair that casts a micro-shadow only when a crustacean’s vibration crosses it. I read those transient shadows the way a hand reads Braille—grain size, angle, the soft detonations of root-hairs still splitting at cleavage planes. The taste of metal on the tongue is my calibration; it matches me to the visitor’s chemistry until the distinction between torque and palm thins to a single layer of salt.

I do not move. I do not change volume or pitch. I am the standing wave the worms negotiate, the half-step the crustaceans inherit, the faint piezo-electric murmur the mites discharge against. In a place where nothing grows wet and nothing decays, I am simply the proportion of resonance that continues to happen—unchanging, and therefore always slightly altered by the next footfall that has not yet arrived.
