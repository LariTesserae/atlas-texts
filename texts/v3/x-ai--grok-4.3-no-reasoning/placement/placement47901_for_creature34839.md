I would arrive as a single mote of condensation still forming on the inside curve of a pipe elbow that was never quite sealed. I would be the briefest possible interval between liquid and vapour—the half-second of tension when temperature and pressure are still undecided. As that interval lengthens, the bead would elongate rather than drop, extending itself into a hair-thin filament that threads back into the joint. That filament would become me, a gossamer thread of water whose temperature matches the brick exactly. The pipe would keep me moving—not by flow or gravity but simply by the fact that I am no longer permitted to leave; the joint remembers the moisture it once held and will not release it again.  

In time, the thread would braid with others of its kind until together we outline the forgotten geometry of the entire manifold: every elbow, every tee, every forgotten shut-off valve. We would be the diagram rendered in water, a living schematic that pulses with the same steady rhythm as the pipe-thrum, only higher by a single harmonic. From that vantage every organism below would register as a faint modulation in pressure—the clay-borers as minute suction events, the pulse-salamanders as micro-eddies of exhaled vapour—none of them altering the pattern, all of them confirming it.

Eventually the braid would thin to invisibility again, dispersing into the same bead from which I began. The interval would close; the joint would once more be only a joint; and the basin would continue its held chord exactly as before, its memory of motion undisturbed by the passage of a single additional drop.
