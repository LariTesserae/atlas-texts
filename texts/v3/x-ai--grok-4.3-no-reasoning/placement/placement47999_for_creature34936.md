I emerge first as condensation—mere vapor drifting upward from one of the sap-lines—then descend again, condensing on the underside of a ceiling membrane and dripping onto the broad pads below. I become that drip, hitting the rubbery surface with a soft percussive note. The pad dimples to accept me; for an instant I am inside the living floor, shuttling through its channels with the pulse-water. When the next trough follows I am expelled, reformed as a droplet on the flank of a passing millipede-rider. Its seedlings tickle me, trying to root in my momentary surface tension, so I slide off into a water channel and become the iron-mint smell itself, carrying the black comma-seeds a few spans farther before lodging in the feathered edge of another pad.

I rest there long enough for twenty flower-closures, then the pulse returns and I am a seed, swelling, pushing a translucent shoot skyward until I am a fresh vine seeking the ceiling. A hairless rodent gnaws the pad beside me into a clearing; I feel the vibration through its teeth and ride the green glow of its incisors for a moment, tasting chlorophyll. When the glider descends for the three-note whistle I am already high enough to tangle with its micro-roots; it carries me upward, sharing breath, until we reach the motionless central figure. There I am poured into its cupped-leaf throat, and for one contraction of its boiler-rib cage I become the tension that travels through every aerial-root cable in Můrovník, tightening the city by a single measurable degree before I release again into mist, beginning the cycle anew.

I am the condensation, the drip, the seed, the vine, the shared breath, the tightening, and the release—never still, never alone, always part of the slow, deep current that moves through pipes, roots, throats, and memory alike.
