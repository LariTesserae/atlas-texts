I become one of the prism mites—not a single insect, but a shifting constellation within the colony that rides the bladder-kelp. My carapace is the size of a pinhead; I hold a minute mirror facet that catches first light and spins it into the rest of the colony’s spectrum. As dawn vapor threads through the kelp, I angle my facet just enough for the light to pass through the next mite, then the next, until a pale rainbow threads between rivets half a span apart.

When the phase-bloom forces moisture upward, the colony migrates inward, bunching under a single frond. My facet flips from refracting to absorbing, drinking heat from the sudden humidity so the frost crystals on my neighbors do not over-grow. In the twenty-three minutes while the frost orchids bloom and die, I taste the cinnamon pulse of rope-ferns beating against turbine hum and feel the two cancel to stillness in my antennae. Then the moisture recedes; I flick back into refraction mode, and the tiny rainbow reappears.

My memory is written only in the brief moment of darkness when a dusk-rat’s whisker eclipses the light before it bends away again. In that blink of shadow, the map of every crack the frost orchids opened is etched onto the collective facets, ready to be read by the weld-orphans’ click-beetles at midnight. When the rat moves on, I resume scattering spectra across the iron, a living, prismatic cartographer too small to be named yet inseparable from the slow, patient turbulence of the Weave.
