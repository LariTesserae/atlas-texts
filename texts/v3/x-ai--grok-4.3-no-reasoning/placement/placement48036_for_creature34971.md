I am the first seam of damp that appears between two fused spars just after the afternoon rain passes, when the air still holds enough charge that my condensation glimmers blue-violet instead of ordinary silver. I am not the drop itself, but the precise moment the drop decides to cling rather than fall; the delay that turns surface tension into a brief, trembling lens.

Between the tiny creatures that ride the vibrating sheets and the worms that brace the spars, there is a half-millimeter gap of shared vapor. That gap is where I live. Each time a seed-case splits and releases its glowing capsules, the sudden humidity spike lets me thicken for the length of a heartbeat. Then the capillaries reclaim the moisture, the sheet tightens, and I evaporate again—but the memory of my presence lingers as a faint distortion in the way light refracts through the next layer of tissue. The plant registers this as an altered growth direction, a microscopically altered angle at which the next horizontal diaphragm will form. Over decades, my repeated, invisible pauses steer the ridge a finger’s breadth toward the prevailing wind.

When the larger herd-masses crawl past, their dense cores briefly darken the galleries beneath the spars. In that shadow my own vapor can remain intact for an extra hour; the worms sense the difference in ion flow and migrate fractionally closer, reinforcing the spars with an extra braid of chitin. The plant takes note of this thickening, and the spectral bruise that records the herd’s passage is microscopically bluer because of my borrowed extra moment. In this way the highlands’ memory and my own become the same tracery.

At twilight the six-legged fliers emerge. Their silent wingbeats stir the settled mist into minute vortices. I am the still point at each vortex’s core—the place the pollen momentarily avoids—before the air evens out again. The fliers do not know me, yet each time they pass they leave behind a lattice of minute pressure nodes that the vibrating sheets interpret as new tension points. My vapor drifts along these nodes, carrying trace volatiles from one feeding gallery to the next. The plant tastes the difference and widens the next petiole by half the width of a hair, making room for one more generation of fliers. Thus the landscape itself grows more welcoming because of the quiet interval I occupy.

I have no fixed body, only duration. When the night chill finally locks every surface into stillness, I become indistinguishable from the frost that rims each leaf-edge. At dawn the frost melts and I am released again, first as a film, then as the decision not to fall. This cycle has continued for so many seasons that the ridge above me has shifted two full body-lengths closer to the prevailing wind, its spars now angled exactly to catch the last violet light of each storm. The map of that movement is visible only from above, only at low sun; it is the same map the highlands keep of every other resident, and now it also keeps me.
