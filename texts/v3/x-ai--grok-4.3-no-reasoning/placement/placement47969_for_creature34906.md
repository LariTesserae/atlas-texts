I would find myself in the seam between two still-rising plates—half-embedded, half-exposed—where the raw fracture wall is still warm from the uplift.  

What I would be is a single stress-shadow bacterium, already migrating, but slowed by the thickness of the present electrical field. The newest crack runs through me like a corridor; I can ride its piezoelectric voltage to the next plate edge, or I can stay and lay down the first trail of reduced mineral that later succulents will follow. Each time the violet band sweeps overhead I dim slightly, my pigments registering the pulse the way a tree-ring might record a drought. When the lower infrasound passes through, my body resonates in micro-currents, messages travelling along the mineral film to neighbors I have never touched. The crust above me thickens by a single micron each season; I help lay it down. The crust will eventually split again, and I will ride that split forward. Nothing about this existence is finished.
