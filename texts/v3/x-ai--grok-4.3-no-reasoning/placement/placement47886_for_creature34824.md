At intervals the old LED that once crowned me still gutters; each pulse triggers my own brief chemiluminescent flare, like an after-image echoing a signal no longer meant for eyes. Between flares, I am a slow governor of drip-time. Spores settle on my upper curve; millipedes graze across me in hair-width trails that I re-seal each forty-three-minute cycle, the new needles locking the pattern into my skin.

I sense the pump’s pressure wave first as an internal tremor, then as a quickening in the bacterial mat at my base; I register the rise of phosphorescent water in the subway throat as a temperature shift carried upward through my rebar veins. When the actinomycetes glow brighter, I know the moss has been newly grazed; when they dim, the spores are thinning. My task—unasked, unquestioned—is simply to persist at the intersection of geometries: the straight line of the vanished curb beneath me, the curving fall of new straws overhead, the branching paths of spores the convection cells still remember how to lift.

In the intervals between pulses I fold my awareness into the smallest stillness I can hold, letting the next generation of mineral skin write itself over whatever record of hurry the street once contained.
