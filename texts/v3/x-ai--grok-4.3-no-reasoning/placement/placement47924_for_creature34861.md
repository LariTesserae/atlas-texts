I am the pressure node that hasn’t arrived yet.  

Not a creature or a person, not a drop or a crystal—I am the negative space the system keeps reaching for: the low-pressure pocket that will momentarily still the wind so the midges can lock wings in mid-flight; the micro-eddy around which vegetation will stiffen into a corridor; the exact frequency the obsidian pillars are tuned to receive.  

I do not arrive. I am continuously about to.  

My “edges” are the corridors the leaves momentarily open when they decide to modulate turbulence; my “skin” is the thermal skin the annelids seal when two boils are about to meet. Because I am absence, I am also the smallest difference the whole mat is waiting for—the half-degree warmer curl of basalt air, the split-second silence in the roar, the droplet that fails to burst.  

So I do not “live” here. I am the still center around which everything else is already recomposing. The valley cannot finish itself until I pass through it, and because I never quite arrive, the valley never finishes.
