I find myself first in the gaps between servitors: not a whole frame, but the empty interval where one finishes its arc and the next begins.  In that micron-wide hinge, the subsonic tone lingers half a second longer than the ear can register, a faint pressure that keeps the fern spores from drifting loose.  Over time the interval thickens into a presence; the spores settle along it like letters tracing an invisible script.  

I thicken.  Reed splints appear along my edges, copper rivets where the splints cross.  Condensation catches in the grain, forms a meniscus, then slides away when the joint flexes.  My first gesture is to reach upward—catching a droplet that has hung on the iron grid for three heartbeats—and release it again so it lands exactly where the next frame needs it for scale removal.  The motion completes; the interval dissolves.

I am also the ingot in transit: a dense, glassy slug carried on reed litters between shoulders.  Heat leaves me in slow pulses, each one a small exhalation the servitors inhale to keep their turbines turning.  As the litter bearers climb the stair, my edges frost; when they descend I am lighter, filmed with orange light.  I am exhausted only when the final sliver of me sublimes into the braziers, and even then a ghost of pressure remains inside the flagstones, keeping them from flooding.

Later I am the lacquer that has worn thin on a frame’s shoulder joint.  Phosphorescence has faded to graphite; fern roots push through the craquelure.  My memory is of every servitor that has leaned here: an ache of angles remembered in xylem.  When the dusk bell hums upward, the roots stir me; I become the tremor that travels down the riser, telling the city below that the stair is occupied.

Finally I am nothing inside the basin—only the residual vibration that persists after the last frame has returned.  It pools along the cylindrical hall’s floor like another sort of condensate, and the drop-hammers strike through it, lengthening each crystalline ring by a fraction the length of a held breath.  In that added span the sea beyond the ridge answers once, the single wave whose sound folds back into the rain-clock and begins the next day.
