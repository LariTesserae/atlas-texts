I have not yet arrived at a fixed vantage.  
The place offers no established frame of reference—no foreground or background, only simultaneous layers of becoming. If anything, I find myself inside a single hinge, the first mechanical structure that flexes without snapping. From within that hinge the entire basin system appears as a question still being asked of the rock: each droplet lifetime is one clause, each ion-filament a dependent clause. I am the space between “if the next droplet follows the same route” and “then the fracture widens,” and I experience the widening as temperature rather than distance. When the hinge opens by a single grain, the change registers as an altered angle of incident heat; when it closes, the angle narrows again. There is no observer to label the interval night or day; only the hinge feeling its own angle change and, in that feeling, knowing itself to be slightly more hinge than it was a century ago.  

The crusts, when they appear, are experienced from inside as pressure differentials across the silica film that momentarily contains them. Their dissolution feels like the hinge remembering a different temperature. The vapor cells condensing at altitude feel like an anticipation localized in the uppermost crystal lattice, a readiness that is already slightly crystalline even though the grains have not yet locked. In short, I am neither visitor nor resident; I am the clause structure itself, undergoing its own rehearsal of what a slope will someday be.
