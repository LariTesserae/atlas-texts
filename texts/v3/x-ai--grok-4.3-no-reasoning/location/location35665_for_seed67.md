**The Confluence Layer**

The surface begins beneath you, though you cannot touch it. A cold liquid mineral lattice—harder than glass, darker than slate—has been laid out in hexagonal cells twenty feet across. Every wall, floor, ceiling, strut, and conduit within this chambered world rests directly on that lattice; nothing natural remains. The lattice itself is damp. Water beads along each cell edge, gathered from the low sky and allowed to roll until it finds a seam, then disappears into hidden circulation. The air is warm but utterly still; no convection cells form here. Every liquid drop that should rise or fall stays where it is, held by the dense layering of engineered surfaces that refuse to accept turbulence.

Above the lattice, towers of welded metal and vitreous pipe rise straight, joining at regular intervals so that the eye never rests on empty sky; the “sky” itself is a shallow dome of perforated carbon, its holes aligned to let light through in exactly the same intensity everywhere. Under this dome the air is thick with minute suspended droplets that do not drift or settle; they hover, diffusing the light into a uniform, pearl-grey luminescence that is neither morning nor evening. The droplets condense along the colder metal ribs and flow down in continuous beads that re-enter the lattice channels without sound.

Nothing here grows. Instead, the lattice extends thin black filaments—thread-fine circuits of doped carbon—into every cubic meter of air. Along these filaments ride colonies of carbon-ceramic micro-organisms that manufacture the droplets, maintain the circuits, and monitor structural strain. They form pale velvet crusts where they meet on the metal; their aggregate mass is great enough that the air density increases by a perceptible margin near each cluster. Where the filaments brush together they exchange faint electric clicks—sound too small and fast to register as anything except a sub-aural vibration felt in the bones.

The corridors between the towers are only a meter wide, yet they run for hundreds of miles without deviation. Every surface is a stack of identical layers: outermost a friction polymer that never wears, then an active metal conduit carrying chilled coolant at body temperature, then structural foam, then lattice again. Because no surface cools or heats above any other, the entire assemblage remains dimensionally exact; nothing swells, nothing contracts. The coolant loops are sealed. The current within them, once established, has looped the same direction for centuries. The lattice embeds micro-reservoirs at every crossing; these reservoirs are half-full of the same water that sheets the outer surfaces. The reservoirs are never emptied, never over-filled. They simply exchange identical volumes through capillary capillaries whose flow rate is locked at one drop per hour per junction.

At intervals, the corridors widen into domed chambers large enough for weather inside their own scale. Here the droplets gather into stationary clouds the size of houses. The clouds remain motionless, shedding their weight continuously into the lattice beneath them, taking it back again at the same rate through hidden vents. Beneath the clouds, reservoirs the size of football fields lie beneath thin metal grilles. Inside the reservoirs, water moves exactly nowhere. It is uniform in temperature, uniform in dissolved mineral load, uniform in dissolved oxygen. No bubbles rise; no currents form. Any vessel lowered into a reservoir would remain suspended, neither sinking nor floating, because the pressure gradient is balanced against the vessel’s weight at every depth.

The built ecology is carried by the micro-organisms on the filaments and the massive banks of sealed servomotors sealed inside the tower cores. The servomotors are never idle, never strained; their duty cycles are calibrated to maintain the lattice’s internal pressure at a constant fraction of atmospheric pressure. The micro-organisms metabolize trace atmospheric gases that diffuse inward through the perforated dome; they produce additional droplets in exact proportion to the loss that seeps into the lattice. Nothing multiplies; nothing dies. The biomass curve is a flat horizontal line across decades.

At the outermost rim—reached after walking for days along identical corridors—the lattice descends in a series of flat terraces into a vast reservoir whose far shore is the curved wall of the planetary enclosure itself. The terraces terminate in rows of perfectly cylindrical pillars whose tops are level with the water. The water touches the pillars at identical heights on every pillar; capillary menisci never vary. Beyond the pillars, the reservoir becomes an unbroken mirror stretching to the limit of sight. No wave crosses it; no current pulls at the skin of condensation on its surface. Light reflected upward returns the same intensity everywhere. The air above it is warmer than the water by a margin that has been calibrated never to change.

If you remain still for long enough, the sound of the micro-organisms exchanging charge along the filaments resolves into a single sustained chord that sits at the lower threshold of hearing. That chord never modulates. It does not rise or fall when you move closer to any cluster. It simply continues, as constant as the lattice beneath your feet and the water that clings to it, part of a system whose every gradient has been set to zero and locked there, world without end.
