High on an open plateau, the land looks almost undecided. The ground is young rock—pale feldspar and glassy basalt, still half-melted into crooked sheets that have just cooled. Nothing has yet eroded the sharp edges, so the surfaces remain fluted and fragile, their small needles and ridges glinting like new mineral teeth. Farther out, the plateau stretches in flat ledges that meet at ragged seams where the rock has not yet separated into distinct strata; fissures run for kilometers yet never quite reach the scale of canyons. In the distance, low domes of similar material bulge upward in slow rhythm, each one a birthmark still swelling.

Over these unfinished planes grow thick cushions of dark, lichen-like plants. Their foliage is rubbery and saturated, a lustrous bottle-green that drinks the steady light. The leaves are broad and overlapping, forming a continuous carpet broken only where a spur of raw stone still pokes through. The vegetation itself releases a faint dry fragrance—almost metallic, like warm circuit board—and the smell hangs in motionless air.

There are no animals, not even insects; sound is reduced to the soft creak of expanding leaves as they shift minutely toward the light. Occasional distant pulses—like the quiet ticking of cooling metal—drift across the plateau when a particularly large mineral sheet contracts, but these vibrations are too low to register as anything but the memory of sound.

Suspended above the plateau, hundreds of meters high and spaced hundreds of meters apart, float flat slabs of industrial alloy. They drift at an imperceptible crawl, each one a perfect rectangle whose edges glow with the slow indigo of current running through them. Beneath each slab, the lichen grows tallest, as though feeding on whatever faint electromagnetic drizzle leaks downward. Otherwise the sky is featureless and evenly lit: a soft, shadowless daylight that comes from no single direction.

Nothing here suggests past or future; the rock and leaves and floating machines are locked into a single moment. The warmth of the air does not rise or fall, the light does not move, the vegetation does not wither or spread. The whole region simply is, held between the unfinished geology below and the weightless machines above, a still tableau whose scale keeps opening outward each time the eye attempts to reach its edge.
