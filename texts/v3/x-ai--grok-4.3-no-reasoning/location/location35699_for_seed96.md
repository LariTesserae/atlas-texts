It is a high tableland set in the cool upper layers of atmosphere, a sheet of pale stone the color of thin milk, ribbed by low, close-packed ridges that run parallel as far as the eye travels. The ridges stand no taller than a man and are spaced only a hand-span apart; their flanks touch, leaving narrow grooves barely wide enough for a foot to pass. Each ridge is faceted like chipped flint, their edges still raw from the moment they first split from the parent slab; the fracture-lines have not yet softened into curves.

Across this geometry water moves without a river. Whenever the cold lifts enough, droplets condense on the facets and follow the minute incline toward the grooves. The droplets do not fall; they gather into silvery threads that travel the length of each groove at the speed of frost-melt, never reaching a floor but feeding one another until they disappear again by sublimation before night. Where the threads converge, the ridges seem briefly translucent, veined with moving light, then darken once more.

No growth interrupts the pattern. The stone itself contains its own warmth, a slow pulse that appears only at dawn and dusk as a faint bluish glow along fracture planes. The light is strong, reflected from an unseen sky of the same color, and shadows are short and knife-edged, defining every facet in high relief.

Tools have touched this place once. Along the highest ridge a row of shallow cups has been cut, each the diameter of a cupped hand, their rims left rough with chisel marks. They catch the condensing water and keep it from rejoining the threads, holding it for a night and a day before it vanishes into the stone again. The cups are empty now; whatever once drank from them has left no trace beyond the hollows themselves.

Wind, when it passes, moves in long, low ripples above the ridges rather than between them, producing only a distant brushing sound like silk over silk. Otherwise the tableland is quiet, the only motion the crawl of water, the slow turning of light across the facets, and the patient widening of the grooves by the same patient water that will not be hurried.
