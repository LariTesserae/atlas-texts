Between two razor-backed ridges that have only just stopped bleeding rock, a basin no wider than a single freight car collects the hush of its own heat. The ridges themselves are the edges of a fracture that opened three seasons ago; their new stone is still pale where the overburden tore away, the fresh surfaces glimmering with needle-feldspar that cracks underfoot into platelets no larger than fingernails.  

At the center lies a shallow lens of water no deeper than a hand’s span. It does not evaporate so much as thicken: every dawn a skin of condensate forms above it and, by midday, descends in a slow rain so fine the drops never quite hit—they hover, refracting the ridges into trembling mirages. From the same water, veins of darker mineral have begun to crust at the lip of the basin; they ooze outward in millimeter bands, each band marking a year’s patient precipitation.  

Nothing green grows here, yet the ground is not barren. Salt-tolerant filaments, no thicker than thread, loop in tight spirals across the pale stone, feeding on the nightly dew rather than on soil. The spirals follow invisible convection cells that rise from the warming water lens and fall against the ridge walls; in the hours before sunset the filaments pulse once, a coordinated contraction that sends a miniature bore of displaced air whispering along the basin floor.  

Half-embedded in the eastern ridge is a shard of corrugated iron, edges scalloped by the same mineral that rims the water. Inside its shallow cavity a clockwork cylinder turns on jewelled bearings. The cylinder is belt-linked to a miniature turbine set into a fissure where condensing moisture continually drips; each drip releases a microliter of pressure, each pressure nudges the turbine forward one tooth. The whole mechanism produces a single, unending chord—an industrial hymn so low that only the salt filaments seem to hear it, their spirals tightening in sympathetic vibration. No other moving parts exist. The device has no operator and needs none.  

Outside the basin the ridges continue their slow separation. Inside, the water neither rises nor falls, the filaments neither spread nor recede. Only the cylinder turns, counting the slow hours with a sound like far-off turbines in an empty hall—loud enough to fill the pocket, soft enough never to escape it.
