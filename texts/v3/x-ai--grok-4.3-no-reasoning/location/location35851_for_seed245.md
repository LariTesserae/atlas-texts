The ground itself has not yet chosen a shape.

Across an immense, slowly roiling plain, raw geometric strata shoulder one another like unfinished blocks still remembering the quarry. The rock is not rock in any familiar sense; it is still cooling from a state of pure instruction, its grains aligned according to momentary blueprints rather than mineral habit. Between the slabs—kilometer-wide and only a few meters thick—thin seams of pale steam rise, condensing against the low ceiling of the sky into droplets that never quite finish falling. The air is thick with them: uncommitted water, suspended, heated from below by whatever process continues to write the ground.

There is no soil, yet the surface is never dry. Each plane weeps along its edges where its substance meets its neighbor; rivulets collect into temporary basins no larger than a footprint before the basin itself dissolves and the water is taken back into the forming stone. From a distance the basins look like mirrors laid flat on the world; closer, they prove to be only the absence of whatever the ground is deciding not to become in that instant.

Light arrives late and tired, filtered through bands of suspended vapor that drift at different heights, each band a different temperature. The bands slide over one another like slow cards, dimming or briefly brightening as they pass. When two bands cross, their collision births brief thunder that has no accompanying lightning; the sound is the cancellation of two pressures, not an electrical discharge. The land answers with a soft, continual ticking, as if uncountable small surfaces were deciding, grain by grain, whether to remain surface or become something else.

Nothing grows because growth is still an argument the world has not yet granted. In its place, lattice-like intrusions of glassy material extrude upward a hand-span at a time, freeze, and then retract minutes later, leaving behind shallow depressions whose edges are perfectly circular for a little while before the plain folds and the circles stretch into ovals, then into lines, then vanish. These motions are not erosion; they are rehearsal. The ground is still auditioning the difference between presence and absence.

Farther out, where the eye gives up measuring distance, the same rehearsal is performed at continental scale. Low ridges rise into being, catch the drifting vapor, and collapse again, each collapse releasing a soft concussion that travels through the stone and arrives underfoot as a brief, directionless nudge. The ridges do not repeat their shapes; each is an experiment the land conducts once and then discards. In their place, shallow valleys appear—temporary concavities in which the suspended droplets finally commit to falling. For a few heartbeats the valley holds a shallow lake whose surface reflects nothing because the sky above it has already moved on. Then the lake evaporates upward into the next drifting band and the valley itself folds shut.

Sound travels strangely here. A footstep (if one could be taken) would arrive at the ear both from the usual direction and, a moment later, from beneath the step, as if the ground were still deciding where the impact occurred. Voices—if there were voices—would seem to arrive from inside the listener’s mouth. The only reliable acoustics belong to the water seams themselves; when they open and close they produce a wet, adhesive pop that is the same everywhere, a constant low-level punctuation that marks the difference between before and after without ever measuring time.

Temperature moves in slow tides across the plain. Heat wells up wherever the lattices are extending, then sinks again when they retract, so that a traveler—if travelers existed—would cross from feverish ground to merely warm ground every few hundred paces without ever leaving the same featureless horizon. The heat does not radiate upward in the usual way; it pools just above the surface in invisible lenses that magnify or shrink distant ridges according to their own internal geometry.

Nothing here is finished, and nothing here is becoming finished. The landscape is a set of instructions still arguing with their own grammar. Each moment is both the first draft and the erasure of the sentence that preceded it. The only stable rule is that the next rule will be different, and that water will still be present to record, in droplets, the brief interval between one version and the next.
