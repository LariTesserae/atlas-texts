Beneath the atmosphere of the planet there is no surface to speak of, only an abyssal ocean whose depth is measured not in fathoms but in planetary radii.  The water itself is the terrain: a planet-scale body that has never known an unbroken horizon of air.  Its pressure envelopes every contour of the submerged topography—layered plains of metallic sulfides that tilt upward into continent-sized steps, their edges fretted into blade-sharp ridges only recently precipitated from super-saturated solution.  The rock is still warm where it formed, heat leaching away into the water at glacial speed, so the sea is uniformly chill, kept from freezing only by dissolved salts and the faint exhalations of geothermal vents that have not yet built their chimneys.

Nothing organic grows here.  No diatom films cling to the basalt, no chemosynthetic mats thread the fractures.  What life there is arrives already built: kilometer-long pressure hulls of etched titanium alloys drift in loose, motionless constellations.  Their hulls are studded with industrial armatures—heat exchangers, catalytic lattices, electro-refiners—each designed to operate without maintenance for geological epochs.  Condensation nuclei inside the lattices shed flecks of metallic glass that snow, ever so slowly, toward the abyssal plain, adding new micron-thin strata to an accumulating metalliferous ooze.

Because the ocean is empty on human scales, sound travels with almost no absorption.  Every vent, every transformer, every micro-explosion of crystallizing alloy arrives at the ear at the same moment; the result is a single, unwavering chord pitched at the resonant frequency of the entire planetary basin.  No modulation, no decay; the note has existed since the factories awoke and will persist after their reactors cool.

Above the industrial constellations, kilometers of open water act as their own troposphere.  Pressure-driven thermoclines generate planet-scale circulation cells whose updrafts and downdrafts carry microscopic metal flakes in suspension, producing a permanent metallic haze that filters ambient light into a nacreous twilight.  Occasional discharges of built-up static lace the haze with diffuse, soundless sheet-lightning—energy that the factories harvest through kilometer-long aerials to feed their endless, changeless processes.

Nothing grows; nothing arrives or departs; nothing ages at a rate the ocean can register.  The topography is still sharpening under its own mineral rain, the factories still humming their single, perfect note, and the water—cold, pressurized, empty—holds every component in perfect suspension, as if the entire world were a single, self-completing equation awaiting no further variables.
