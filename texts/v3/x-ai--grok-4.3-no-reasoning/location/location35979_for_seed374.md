The terrain is a broad, low plain still in the first hour of its own existence. Sandstone and silt, pale and newly formed, lie in thin, unbroken sheets across the ground, their edges soft and still settling, as though the earth were poured here yesterday and has not yet decided where to end. The water table sits barely a hand-span below the surface; every footfall releases a muted sigh as trapped moisture rises through tiny channels, beading against bare skin. Condensation rims every pebble with a faint halo. Rocks do not weep so much as perspire—slow, continuous droplets that never quite fall, held by surface tension until another droplet merges and the whole bead rolls, re-forming elsewhere.

No plants of any lineage grow here; the plain is mineral only. The grains that make up the sand are half quartz, half some translucent carbonate still crystallizing, their facets not yet dulled. Small, kiln-bright nodules of raw glass litter the ground where lightning or simple exothermic reactions fused the silica moments before. Their edges are knife-sharp; none have weathered. Between them, the sand shifts in minute, perfectly repeating wavelets—half a millimetre high—locked into place by an invisible equilibrium that refuses wind or time.

The city is already dense within this newborn geology. Low, single-storey arcades of unfired mud-brick honeycomb the plain in irregular but repeating spirals. The bricks are thumb-printed, still damp at their cores. Wooden scaffolding of green saplings props up half-finished walls; the bark has not yet dried. Rope bridges of braided reed sag between rooftops, their fibers sweating. There are no metals, only copper-streaked nodules hammered flat where they were found already half-native in the earth. Light inside the covered walkways is reduced to a permanent dusk, filtered through reed lattices and the steam that rises from every alley.

Sound is everywhere at once, the same everywhere, unchanging. A single, continuous low-frequency tone issues from the ground itself, generated by water vibrating through microscopic tubes in the fresh rock. It is joined by the soft, relentless patter of droplets striking droplets, a white-noise hiss that never varies in volume or rhythm. Human activity layers on top: the scrape of wooden trowels smoothing fresh plaster, the thud of tamped earth, the overlapping footfalls of hundreds of inhabitants moving along fixed routes that have already worn shallow grooves in the un-set stone. None of these noises modulate; they are the same at dawn as at dusk, an acoustic steady-state.

Fauna crowd the interstitial spaces. Tiny, translucent crustaceans scuttle through the condensing film on every surface, their bodies the same pale carbonate as the sand grains. They click in perfect synchrony with the omnipresent tone, mandibles opening and closing like minute metronomes. Larger, blind amphibians with skin the color of wet clay cling to the undersides of the reed bridges, gills fluttering in the saturated air. They neither hunt nor rest; their metabolic rate is locked to the constant humidity, so they simply persist, gills pulsing, until the next identical moment arrives.

The atmosphere is warm and motionless. Heat radiates upward from the damp ground, keeping the air at a stable skin temperature that never spikes or drops. Fog does not form because the moisture never quite reaches saturation; instead a permanent, invisible film of water molecules hovers at ankle height, diffusing light into a uniform grey shimmer. There is no wind to stir it, no temperature gradient to drive convection. Every breath tastes the same: wet mineral and warm dust.

Because the world is still crystallizing, every surface carries fine geometric ridges— nascent crystal lattices just beginning to order themselves. Where bricks touch, these ridges interlock; given enough identical hours they will fuse completely, the city becoming a single lithic mass. For now, the seams remain visible, hairline, already tightening.

The plain extends without edge into the same repeating pattern: arcades, glistening sand, crouching amphibians, continuous tone. No horizon interrupts it; distance simply layers more of the same density until the eye can no longer resolve individual bricks and the whole becomes a single, unbroken texture of warm, wet mineral and sound.
