The vastness announces itself not in horizon but in texture—layers of living material stacked and interwoven until the distinction between ground, wall, canopy, and sky becomes untenable. Everything is plant, and the plants have forgotten where one organism ends and the next begins. The dominant growth is a deep-claret phyllotissue, slow-moving and vascular, forming hanging terraces that sag under their own accumulated water. Each terrace is a living catchment; droplets gather, linger, then coalesce into rivulets that slide along veins, drip onto lower plates, and finally disappear into the sponge-like rhizome core that constitutes the actual “soil.” The water index hovers at 2.1 because the system itself is an enormous, slow-moving sponge—never flooding, never parched, always just damp enough for continuous internal circulation.

Because the elevation parameter is mid-high (2.0), the entire mass sits on a plateau whose lower edge is lost in mist many days’ travel away. The plateau itself is not stone; it is the compressed residue of earlier, even larger plant generations, reduced over millennia to a leathery, almost woody substrate that rings faintly when struck—more hide than timber. Nothing erodes in the mechanical sense; the surface simply continues to lignify at its own geological pace, the parameter of 2.7 registering as primordial quietude rather than ruin. Time here is registered in the deepening of pigment and the lengthening of drip-intervals between terraces.

Temperature lingers in the upper temperate band; the canopy is porous enough for a constant exchange of warm, spore-laden updrafts, yet dense enough to mute direct sun. The light is correspondingly dim—perpetual chlorophyll-green dusk filtered through dozens of translucent strata. Photosynthesis still occurs, but it has slowed to a patient simmer; leaves respire on a roughly lunar cycle rather than a daily one.

Fauna is abundant yet unobtrusive. The majority of mobile creatures are endosymbiotic with the plant matrix: soft-bodied, three-lobed grazers whose translucent flanks contain chloroplast colonies they “farm” internally. They drift rather than walk, buoyed by hydrogen-filled lacunae, grazing the terrace rims where fresher tissue continually extrudes. Larger, rarer motile forms exist—tubular browsers whose exhalations of warm, moist air help maintain the micro-atmosphere of each terrace. Because sound ranks low (1.1), these movements register mainly as minute pressure changes felt through root hairs, tiny liquid tremors, and the almost inaudible stretch of expanding cell walls.

Built presence is limited to scattered artifacts left by prior, slower lifeforms: occasional hollow seed-cases the size of cathedrals, their lignified ribs now colonized by epiphytic gardens; thin arcs of calcified resin, half-buried, their once-functional channels now occupied by capillary roots. None of these artifacts are tools in the technological sense; they are architectural remnants of earlier plant generations, abandoned or absorbed according to the same metabolism that governs everything else.

Dynamic processes are glacial. A single droplet’s descent from upper terrace to rhizome core may take the equivalent of a human decade. Seasonal shifts—if they exist—are expressed as slight changes in the viscosity of internal sap rather than dramatic color changes or migrations. Weather, such as it is, manifests as vast, slow pressure domes of humidity that drift across the plateau, condensing against the coolest strata and feeding the system’s internal aquifer without ever breaking the canopy’s continuity.

Weirdness (2.0) resides in the way growth and decay have become the same verb. When tissue finally expires, it does not rot; it lignifies, then gradually mineralizes into the plateau foundation while its successor generations unfold above it. The entire landscape is therefore both the newest and oldest expression of a single, ongoing metabolism—one whose scale is measured not in kilometers or eons but in the number of simultaneous cell cycles it can maintain without ever accelerating beyond its own deliberate tempo.
