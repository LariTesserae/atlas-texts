It begins with water. Not falling, not rushing—simply *there*, thick in the air as though the atmosphere has just finished sweating and forgotten how to stop. High on a ridge that should have no right to be small, a platform of rock no larger than an abandoned chapel holds a shallow mirror-pool that neither overflows nor shrinks. The pool’s surface is glassy, yet under its skin the water moves in imperceptible swirls, warm currents threading through colder ones like veins in a living membrane.

From the pool’s lip the land drops a body’s length, then levels into a shelf no wider than two outstretched arms. The stone is dark basalt, not long exposed; its edges are still knife-thin where freeze-thaw once prised flakes away. No soil has gathered in the joints; instead, a felt of moss, nearly black, lines every seam. The moss drinks the constant condensate and never dries, yet its cushions are spaced like constellations—tiny galaxies separated by bare, gleaming rock. Water trickles along these bare lanes in braided filaments no thicker than thread, guided by gravity so gentle it seems to hesitate between choices.

Above the moss, air cools fast. Breath fogs even inside the lungs. Temperature never rises enough to stir convection, so every exhalation lingers, turns faintly luminous, then sinks back into the pool like slow snowfall in reverse. Light arrives pre-softened: a nacreous wash seeping downward from somewhere above the visible ceiling of mist, never bright enough to cast shadow yet sufficient to turn every droplet into a suspended lens. The droplets themselves drift rather than fall; surface tension and micro-eddies keep them hovering at chest height until they spiral into the moss or merge with the pool.

Fauna here is both abundant and miniature. Tiny white snails no larger than lentils glide across the black moss, their shells catching the nacre and returning it doubled. Between them, swarms of gnat-sized midges hang in stationary clouds, wings beating so rapidly the sound is a single, unwavering tone pitched just below human hearing—a drone that saturates the shelf without variation. When a snail pauses, several midges settle on its shell to drink the condensate film; the snail does not retract. The contract is older than seasons.

Further out, where the shelf narrows toward its vanishing point, a line of weeping stones leans over the drop. Each stone exudes a single bead every few heartbeats; the beads descend in perfect vertical threads until they meet rising vapor and are absorbed again. Nothing splashes. Nothing accumulates. The system balances itself at every increment.

Here the industrial layer manifests not as machines but as metabolism. Within the rock, thin veins of native metal—untouched by tool—carry faint thermoelectric currents generated by the temperature gradient between the pool’s hidden warmth and the constant chill above. The currents pulse once per exhalation of the observer, not because they respond, but because the observer’s breath momentarily alters local ionization. The rock sings back at the same frequency as the midge-drone, a sympathetic chord that never rises or falls.

Sound, therefore, is not layered but total: a single standing wave of infra-bass and insect-wing that occupies every cubic centimeter. It does not propagate; it *is*. A listener who steps onto the shelf feels the tone relocate inside the sternum, then discovers it was already there.

Elevation enforces scale. From any point on the shelf the visible world ends at a wall of vapor thirty paces away, curving inward like the interior of a throat. Beyond that wall the ridge presumably continues, but the mist and the unchanging pressure gradient render distance meaningless. The cathedral has no nave, only this one sealed transept where water, stone, current, and tone have reached an accord that refuses alteration.

Nothing arrives, nothing departs. The midges neither reproduce nor die within memory; the snails trace closed loops whose paths never intersect. Even the condensate, cycling through lung, air, rock, and pool, returns to its origin without measurable loss. The only motion belongs to an observer’s mind as it attempts to introduce sequence, and even that motion slows, synchronizes, then stills—absorbed into the equilibrium until the boundary between witness and witnessed thins to the thickness of a held breath.
