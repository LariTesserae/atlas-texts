A pressure-vault the size of a cloister sits a few dozen meters beneath the true skin of the world, the ceiling a single, upward-curving lens of pressure-hardened translucent quartz. Inside, the air is soft and tropically warm, a continuous condensation-glitter pattering down in slow arcs because the quartz is slightly cooler than the upward-rising breath from the bioluminescent reef that lines the floor. The reef is not coral, nor plant; it is a living water-filter built from microscopic pressure-engines, each hexagon about the size of a thumbnail, tiles that unroll and re-roll in slow peristaltic waves every eleven minutes. When a tile opens it captures a finger-width of condensate; when it closes it forces the droplet through its own engineered capillaries, feeding a paper-thin forest of copper-colored, feather-branched fungi that never touch the floor but hang a hand-span above it, their hyphae glistening like wet silk.

Because the space is small and empty of partitions, every sound travels cleanly. The dominant sound is a rolling, bone-deep bass note—seven hertz, three-second duration, four-second silence—produced by the quartz lens itself. The lens is a resonator tuned by the continuous temperature difference above and below it; every contraction swells the standing wave again, and the wave is amplified by a shallow water-mirror that covers two-thirds of the floor. Each pulse briefly lifts the mirror into a low dome; when the pressure drops the water falls back with a soft slap that becomes the secondary, higher register of the sound. In the pauses between pulses you can hear the individual dripping impacts of condensate on the lens, the tiny ticks of the reef tiles opening and closing, and once every nineteen minutes the papery rustle of a single silver-gray moth the size of a thumbnail that wakes, drinks from a condensing stalactite, and returns to stasis inside a wall niche cut for it millennia ago.

There are no corridors leading anywhere else. The only exit is a single vertical pressure-lock—three nested iris leaves of the same quartz—set flush with the floor and sized for one standing adult. When the lock cycles, it does so in perfect synchrony with the lens’s bass note, a coincidence engineered so that the sound-pressure assists the final seal. Beyond the lock is simply the unlit bulk rock; the city above is unaware that its foundations have grown a single occupied room that obeys its own humidity budget, its own sonic calendar, and its own slow cycle of breath and condensation.
