In the perpetual gloaming of the plateau's sodden sprawl, life clings not in multitudes but in attenuated threads—faint, fibrous presences woven into the wet stone and rusted iron, as if the mountain itself had coughed up half-formed souls to tend its mechanical innards. They are the Dampfolk, or so the eroded runes in the basalt whisper when the geysers subside for their brief, shuddering pauses; not a bustling populace but a residue, a scant hundred souls scattered across miles of viaduct and factory vault, each one a lone sentinel in their isolated spires. Born here or arrived in forgotten migrations up the talus slopes, they have surrendered to the place's alchemy: bodies softened by humidity, hardened by vibration, their forms echoing the landscape's patient erosion.

Glimpsed first from a catwalk's perforated edge, a figure emerges from the haze like condensate beading on a girder—tall and elongated, shoulders stooped under layers of waxed oilcloth that sheds water in endless rivulets, hooded cowls drawn tight against the mist. Their skin, glimpsed in rare unravelings, is pale as inner quartz, slick with a perpetual sheen of subcutaneous moisture, pores dilated to drink the air's saturation. Faces are narrow, jaws elongated slightly from years of silent shouting into the roar, cheeks hollowed not by starvation but by the caloric density of fungal pastes harvested from vat underbellies—ochre loaves of mycelium bread, flavored with mineral tang and machine oil, sustaining them on sparse rations. Eyes are wide, irises faded to milky amber, pupils contracted against the diffused light yet piercing the fog with predatory focus; they see in layers, discerning the subtle shift of a flywheel's wobble or the widening of a seep's mouth amid the cobbles.

These are the Maintainers, the core of their kind, spectral as the description warrants—ghostly not in death but in dilution, their numbers thinned over generations by the plateau's indifferent cull: stillbirths drowned in boggy cribs, climbers lost to abyssal slips, the sound-mad who wander into geyser vents during dilation episodes when time folds and hours stretch to days. A Maintainer moves with hydraulic grace, joints lubricated by self-applied greases fermented from effluent runoff, gloved hands—fingers fused faintly at the tips from chemical burns—probing conveyor belts with hooked probes forged from scrap rail. They wear earplugs of wax and moss, packed deep to muffle the dirge to a chest-deep thrum, communicating in vibrations: boot-stomps patterned on metal decks, semaphore flags snapped in the viaduct winds, or subdermal tugs on knotted cords that relay haptic pulses across kilometers. Speech is obsolete, a relic pantomimed for visitors who never come; instead, they hum subsonics in harmony with the turbines, throats vibrating like tuning forks to sync with the industrial pulse.

Deeper in the galleries, where geothermal capillaries vein the bedrock and warmth blooms to 90 degrees in vaulted warrens, the brood reveals itself in familial clusters—never more than five or six per factory hall, burrowed into alcoves honeycombed from cliff faces. Matriarchs, broader of hip and girthier from geothermal feasting, tend nurseries of damp-swaddled young: infants born slick and quivering, lungs filling not with cries but with gurgles of aerated slurry, eyes opening to the lantern-glow of phosphorescent larvae they will later harvest. The young mature slowly, time's dilation etching adolescence into decades; by puberty, they scale catwalks unaided, small hands already callused from flywheel polishings, bodies adapting with vestigial gills—slitted neck-folds that flare in the steam, filtering oxygen from the ochre fog. Gender blurs in the humidity's forge: many are amphigynous, beards of wiry moss-fungus sprouting on fertile bellies, pairings ritualized by synchronized dances atop gantry cranes during geyser eruptions, when seismic belches shake loose new ore for the lines.

Not all are human-touched; the plateau claims variants, outliers evolved in its slow gyres. The Deep-Scuttlers dwell in mineshaft warrens, pale troglodytes with elongated limbs and bioluminescent nodules embedded in scalps—grafts from cave-crickets, symbiotic lights pulsing to guide through fog-choked adits. They emerge rarely, trading fungal spores for machine parts, their language a rasping Morse tapped on walls, bodies furred with mineral crusts that chime like wind bells in motion. Higher on the shoulders of granite, the Wind-Watchers perch solitary in thornbush aer ies, raptors in human skin: emaciated ascetics with feathered cowls of sedge, eyes telescoped by selective breeding, hooked noses snagging mist. They wheel the thermals on harnessed gliders of riveted canvas, cry-songs swallowed by distance, dropping semaphore flares to warn of lowlands storms that never breach the haze.

Strangeness inhabits them as they inhabit it: shadows trail elongated in the mirage-lakes, whispering of absent multitudes—echoes, perhaps, of prior tenants whose faces peer from the rock, or phantasms conjured by toxin-veined lungs. Maintainers report synchronization fevers, bodies locking into machine rhythms unbidden, spines arching to the steam-hammer thud until flesh and flywheel blur. Clocks in their spires lag variably, fostering cults of temporal drift: hermits who fast for "day-skips," emerging days younger, skin tauter, convinced they've bartered years to the mountain. Fungi integrate overtly—the Elderbloomers, revered ancients whose torsos swell with symbiotic polypores, caps fruiting from sternums to heal wounds or intoxicate with visions of submerged spires. They prophesy in spore-clouds, inhaled by the young to dream of imperial rebirth, when the city fills with bodies and the roar crescendos to song.

Yet persistence rules them, as it rules the plateau: no wars, no hordes, just the languid tending of assembly lines that produce enigmatic exports—crated ingots of refined geothermal crystal, shipped down viaducts to lowlands ghosts via funiculars that creak eternally empty on return. They endure the roar's annihilation, deafened to crescendo yet attuned to its ebbing modulations; the wetness beads on their forms without chill; the emptiness vertigo-forges stoic isolation, kinship forged over kilometers of arc-lit viaduct. Wander their plazas at gloaming's peak, and a Maintainer might pause mid-gesture, hood tilting in inscrutable regard—profile shifting like the rock-faces when unobserved, as if appraising you for integration, for erosion into their kind. Here, life is not conquest but osmosis: bodies exhaling into infrastructure, machines inhaling their labor, all in the warm, unyielding hum of slow-becoming stasis, where the Dampfolk persist as the mountain's damp pulse, half-swallowed yet eternally vigilant.
