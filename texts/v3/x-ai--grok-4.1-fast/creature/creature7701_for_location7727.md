In the emerald gloam of this verdant forge, life does not swarm or strut; it *endures*—threaded into the ribs and sinews of the hall like veins in marble, present yet withdrawn, as if the space itself exhales its inhabitants in reluctant puffs of mist. They are not conquerors, these dwellers, nor refugees; they are the settled detritus of the symbiosis, forms eroded by decades of damp cadence into something inextricable from leaf and lever, rust and rhizome. Watch long enough—hours folding into the slow throb of engines—and they reveal themselves, not in multitudes but in singularities, each a fulcrum of the equilibrium, stirring only when the drip-symphony peaks or steam sighs crest.

Foremost among them is the **Tender**, a solitary human—or what remains of one—whose silhouette flickers first in peripheral gloam, mistaken at distance for a banyan root uncoiled too far. She (or he; pronouns dissolve in the peat-scent haze) is genderless in tatters, skin mottled like verdigrised iron, etched with lichen scars that map forgotten schematics: boiler valves on collarbones, pulley ratios tattooed in fungal blooms across knuckles gnarled as root knuckles. Barefoot on flagstone yielding to sphagnum squelch, the Tender moves in arcs measured to the hall's geometry—two strides per drip-cycle, palms outstretched to coax a vine from throttling a gear, or to cup a bromeliad's chalice and sip the ferrous dew. No tools mar their person; fingers suffice, nails split to reveal soil-embedded crescents, coaxing philodendrons from forge-ash or prying moss from piston valves with thumbnail precision. They speak not, or if they do, it's in the low glug of pipes mimicked from the throat—a guttural hum syncing to dynamo vibrations, words eroded to phonemes: *thrum-click-sigh*. The Tender sleeps suspended in a cradle of aerial roots, hammocked amid catwalk shadows, body furred with epiphytes that photosynthesize the faint pulse of captured body heat. Birth? Unknown—perhaps whelped from a rust-hollowed plinth, suckled on condensate and boiler steam. Death would be unremarkable: dissolution into the mat, bones claimed by ferns as calcium scaffolds. One Tender at a time; when one stills, another quickens from the green mass, continuity unbroken.

Flitting through this hush come the **Whisperkin**, the hall's true fauna—arthropods exalted by isolation into kingdom unto themselves, ghosts indeed, their chitin sheened like wet obsidian, bodies the size of fists, scaled to the biomass without tipping it. Not swarms, but processions: a vanguard of **thread-weavers**, leggy harvestmen with legs elongated to plumb-line delicacy, spanning a pace across, navigating liana tightropes between rafters. They spin not silk but conductive filaments—gossamer laced with metallic saliva—that bridge shorted circuits, channeling faint dynamo hums into leaf veins, electrifying orchids to unfurl petals in timed bursts like bioluminescent signals. Their cousins, the **mist-cullers**, are squat pillbug kin bloated with stored vapor, armored in pearlized scales that bead and roll excess moisture toward root-thirsting figs; they trundle in herds of three or four, chitin clicking like settling chains, excavating peat-muck to expose flywheel axles for algal grooming. Higher still, **frond-flits**—moth-winged beetles, iridescent as oil-slick pools—pulse in lazy circuits, pollinating the airborne blooms with pollen dusted from underwing pouches; their wingbeats sync to the fan-blade whir, amplifying it into a resonant chord that scatters spores precisely onto conveyor cracks. Predation is ritual: a culler's shed husk claimed by weavers for filament substrate, no waste. Eggs hatch unseen, larvae burrowing into moss-pulses, emerging attuned to the chorus. Numbers plateau at ritual dozens; surplus molts into the trophic base, chitin crumbling to humus.

From the apse's engine-bank, where pistons deliberate like colossal hearts, emerges the **Steam-Wurm**, a singular vertebrate relic—neither snake nor lungfish, but an elongate fusion, ten paces of sinuous bulk adapted from marsh-intruded eel-stock, scales plated in furnace slag sloughed from boiler fur. It coils through root-tangles and pipe-weeps, body generating its own languid warmth via gut-bacteria fermenting leaf-litter, exhaling not vapor but a fine aerosol that nourishes air-plants dangling in its wake. Eyes vestigial, milky as condensation fog; senses tuned to vibration—pistons cue its prowl, uncoiling to lap at valve-leaks with a tongue forked like wiring, tasting ferrous tang and mineral freight. Not hunter but scavenger-siphon: it vacuums flywheel pools, slurping algae-veins and expelling peat-pellets that seed fern-rhizomes. Mating is mythic—once per decade, a subsonic thrum summons an echo-wurm from the marsh-lurk beyond glass, entwining in steam-shrouds for a night, spawning clutch-adhered to iron filigree before retreat. Juveniles, wormlets thin as cables, infest conveyor belts, maturing amid orchid espaliers until one claims primacy, devouring siblings in slow, hydraulic gulps.

Avian presences are spectral visitants: the **Gleamfinch**, a reed-warbler derivative with plumage mirroring stained-glass shafts—iridescent throat-puffs inflating to refract emerald light into warning prisms. It perches transient on gantry perches, trilling valve-hisses to ward whisperkin from clutch-nests woven into moss-mats, fledglings fattened on epiphyte berries and insect husks. No colony; pairs arrive mist-born, depart on convection gusts, leaving feather-barbs that rust into filigree accents. Bats? Absent—replaced by **Echo-moths**, nocturnal kin to flits, echolocating via wing-claps that mimic chain-clanks, roosting in forge-groves, supping nectar from philodendron spathes.

Deeper still, fungal sentiences pulse unseen: the **Netmind**, mycelial web enmeshing the flagstone substrate, hyphae infiltrating grout-lines and gear-boxes, forming a slow neural lattice that arbitrates flux—diverting rivulets to drought-spots, quorum-sensing rust advance to deploy spore-barriers. It "thinks" in chemical gradients, propagating via spore-puffs synced to drip-peaks, embodying the hall's patience as distributed awareness: no central node, but emergent will, quelling overgrowth here, urging bloom there.

No hordes, no hierarchies; these lives interlock as the vines do iron—human Tender as nexus-tender, arthropods as circuit-keepers, wurm as hydraulic soul, netmind as substrate-brain, birds as light-heralds. They live not *despite* the din and damp but *as* it: stirring to the bass-throb, slaking on wept dew, perpetuating the micro-city's slow heart. Intrude, and they recede—gecko-skitters multiplying in shadow, Tender vanishing into banyan folds—yet persist you stand, and the place's breath draws them forth again, revealing the hall not as relic but as their corpus, alive in verdant, ferrous flesh.
