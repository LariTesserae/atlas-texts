They emerge not as conquerors of the crater but as its exhaled children, born from the same slow contractions of stone and seep that nudge the pillars skyward. You sense them first in absence, in the vast airy gulfs between domes where no footfall mars the dripping symphony—then, peripherally, a silhouette uncoils from mist-shrouded vine, tall as three humans stacked, yet lithe as the seeking tendrils that birthed it. Not giants in bulk, but stretched by the thin air and patient time, their forms averaging thrice the height of a mortal frame, bodies elongated like the basalt spines themselves: limbs attenuated, torsos narrowed to a spindle-waist, heads crowned with frond-like crests that quiver in the wind's low moan. Their skin—or hide—is basalt-born, a living laminate of glossy black stone sheened perpetually wet, veined with silver threads of mineral oil that pulse faintly in rhythm with the ground's sighs. It weeps in sympathy with the fissures, beading droplets that trace slow paths down elongated limbs, pooling at knuckle-joints rough-hewn like mallet-heads. No hair, no fur; instead, from shoulders and spines sprout epiphytic mosses and ferns, plump and exhaling that cool vegetal breath, roots delving shallow into the stone-flesh without wounding it. Their faces are masks of the land: high cheekbones faceted like crystal planes, eyes paired inclusions of milky quartz that gleam inward, reflecting the diffuse gray light as if gazing into personal abysses. Below, mouths are lipless slits, exhaling steam-faint vapors scented of deep earth—nascent life, unmetallic, like birthing clay kissed by geothermal blush.

They number few, two dozen at most, ghosts in their own infrastructure; the domes accommodate singly or in pairs, each a solitary vault where one might recline for weeks, limbs folded into meditative coils, body thrumming harmonious with the walls. Density is their abjuration—the settlement's airy solitude mirrors their nature, for crowding stifles the slow accrual of self. They move rarely, and when they do, it's a procession glacial as geological uplift: a single stride spans the gulf between structures, footfalls muffled to mere vibrations felt through stone soles that grip the slick footing with natural suction. No haste; a migration from rim to platform consumes an hour, each step savored, pillars twitching in salute as they pass. Their voices, if voiced, are subsonic rumbles borrowed from the wind's flutes, felt in the chest before heard—conversations unfold over days, a phrase lingering as echo, layered with drip-percussion until consensus accretes like moss.

Life here is attunement to the cradle's becoming, their days bled into the crater's cycles. At the central platform, they gather thrice a lunar turn—not for feast or rite, but convergence: forms ring the fire-pits, kindled rarely from bone-shards scavenged from below (for meat is myth; they sip the seeps directly, mouths widening to funnels that draw mist and mineral, leaves fattening on the surplus). Hands—four-fingered, thumb opposed like a vine-curl—etch spirals into totems with crude wedges, eyes carved not for vigilance but resonance; touch one, and it hums your own pulse back, amplified, drawing the quartz-eyes to track your form through the haze. They craft sparingly: vine-ropes twisted for binding errant fronds, slabs levered into pathways that arc toward nascent fissures, as if channeling the mountain's next sigh. Tools are extensions of body—dense wood hammers gripped till splintered, then composted into moss-pockets on their backs. No children visible, yet the young betray themselves in shallower domes, forms embryonic and pillar-stiff, growing millimeter by millimeter, exhaling first vapors after months of gestation in seep-pools lined with fern-wombs.

Fauna skirts them warily, respecting the shadow-presence: leathery-winged gliders—bat-like, vast as carpets—circle overhead, dropping rare guano that quickens moss-clumps; scuttlers in vines are their kin, smaller silicate kin, pebble-bodied with questing tendrils, harvested occasionally for totem-eyes. No predators; the chill and thinness cull excess, but predation is needless—their sustenance is the place itself, drunk from stone-veins, breathed from mist, pulses syncing so that when a pillar shifts, their limbs elongate imperceptibly in echo, spines cracking softly like distant avalanches.

Watch longer, days folding into the unshadowed gray, and their weirdness deepens: in repose, they photosynthesize faintly through moss-veins, bodies warming a degree above ambient, radiating that expectant charge you felt at the rim. Touch approaches warily—they permit it, but respond by thrumming your bones, quartz-eyes dilating to map your foreign rhythms against the bowl's. Solitude claims them utterly; one might pace the rim for a week, fronds swaying to wind-moans, before descending to etch a new spiral: a tally, perhaps, of exhales endured, or a map of veins pulsing toward some unborn expansion. They are the town's dreamers, its skeletal citizens—lush-limbed ghosts of basalt and green, wet and waiting, their slow urbanity a scaffold for the earth's half-formed thought. Encroach too boldly, and the acoustics turn accusatory: your steps boom thunderous amid their hush, pillars twitch faster, eyes converge; yet they claim no violence, only assimilation's patient pull, inviting you to thin and stretch, to weep mineral and sigh with stone. This is their cradle, their becoming—and in its loud void, they live as if the mountain itself, embryonic, eternal, alone together.
