These dwellers are the chamber's children, their forms a lewd matrimony of flesh and forge, scuttling, schooling, drifting in a symphony of engineered savagery. Let them uncoil before you, layer by layer, their strangeness sharpening with every eddy.

Not true fish but cyborg remoras, birthed from eggs laid in the pump housings, their flanks etched with fractal tattoos of corroded copper—once functional sensors, now vestigial scars that flicker with ingested bioluminescence. They school in iridescent clouds of a thousand, a liquid mercury that parts the kelp veils like smoke, driven by insatiable hunger. No singular ruler claims this realm; they are a heaving polyculture, bound by the biomechanical pact—buoyancy their god, erosion their sculptor, the engine their womb. Cyberfish nibble custodian legs, jellies shock manta gills, rays stir swarms into custodians' jaws, all recycled in the 47-second pulse. Wander their midst, and they enfold you: a reaver nips your fin, an anemone latches to purify your breath, a forge-wing brushes past in indifferent majesty.
