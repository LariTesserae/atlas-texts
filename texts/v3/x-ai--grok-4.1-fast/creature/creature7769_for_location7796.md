They are not born of conquest, these dwellers, but of seepage and unfurling—like the ferns that pioneer the cracks, or the moss that blooms without fanfare. You sense them first as displacements in the hush: a frond bends where no draft stirs, a pool's ripple lags behind the drip from above, a shadow among shadows elongates just a fraction too smoothly for stone. Fifteen huts, yes, but they are less built than coaxed—ovoid pods of woven liana and fig-root lattices, daubed with clay the color of wet basalt, roofs humped low like the backs of burrowing beasts, blending so seamlessly into ledge and wall that a stranger might pass blind. Doors are slits parted by hand-woven reed curtains, heavy with dew, exhaling the musk of slept-in earth and fermented fig-paste. Smoke? None rises; their fires are internal, bioluminescent molds cultured in clay pots, casting the interiors in the same sickly yellow pulse as the cavern's fungi.

The folk themselves number perhaps forty souls, though "number" misleads—their lives blur into clutches and drifts, kin-groups that fission and reform like the mist cycles. Call them the Veilborn, if a name must cling, though they name nothing fixed; words are for the drip's cadence or the mustelid's yip, murmured in throat-songs too soft for echoes. Small they are, no taller than a child's height at full stretch—neotenous forms, embryonic in their persistence, as if the fissure's youth arrested them midway from some deeper mammalian root. Skin pale as quartz-veins, slick with the ambient oil of thermal condensate, hairless save for downy fringes at nape and groin, frond-like in their fineness, stirring to catch scents. Eyes vast and lidless, orbs of liquid indigo that drink the gloam whole, reflecting bioluminescence in star-pricks; they navigate not by sight alone but by the thermals on their cheeks, sensitive as beetle-wings to the vents' sighs. Limbs slender, fingers elongated and webbed faintly at the palms for gripping slick stone or cupping dew; toes splay broad for purchase on moss-mats, nails hardened to chitin-sheens from gnawing mineral flecks.

Their bodies hum with the place's biology, extensions of its sparse abundance. Symbioses mark them: pale lichens speckle their shoulders, photosynthesizing faintly in light-slivers to trade sugars for salts drawn from skin-glands; tiny iridescent mites herd in the creases of elbows, grazing dead cells while secreting a numbing balm against the humidity's itch. No garments clothe them outright; instead, living mantles—vests of maidenhair fronds clipped to nipple-rings of twisted vine, skirts of overlapping fig-leaves that rustle prophecy-soft with each step, shedding and regrowing seasonally. Males and females blur in dimness, both bearing shallow pouches at the belly where infants nestle for moons, suckling milky sap expressed from wrist-veins; births are silent throes in hut-pods, the afterbirth composted into the walls to quicken the daub.

Life pulses to the fissure's rhythm, intimate and unhurried. Dawn-gleam (that bruised paling overhead) summons the forage-troops: five or six Veilborn per cluster, loping quadrupedally on knuckles and toes, tails—prehensile, tufted—coiling for balance as they pluck figlets and orchid nectar, lips pursed to sip dew from leaf-cups without a slurp. They share in rounds, passing fruits palm-to-palm, no greed in the circuit; excess mashed to paste, stored in gourd-sacs slung from shoulder-vines. Hunts are communal whispers: snares of fiber-looped vines tensioned across lizard-paths or lemur-leaps, checked by fingertip-trill that mimics prey-calls. The mustelids they court as familiars, tossing berry-bait to draw them near, reading their stalk-pounces for signs of tunnel-shifts below. Evenings gather them to the central pool, a dimple widened by generations' digging, where they immerse to slough skin-scurf, emerging anointed in sulfur-mud that hardens to protective sheen. Songs then—ultrasonic harmonies pitched to the bats' echolocates, weaving lore of the rock's first pry: pressures from the core-god's breath, birthing this wound-home.

Society coils loose, emergent as the vines: no chiefs, but "whisper-elders" whose eyes have filmed with mineral cataracts, voices thinned to drip-mimicry, consulted for vent-predictions or root-routes. Huts realign with the stone's heave—micro-quakes parting cracks for new fern-beds—prompting ritual rebuilds: walls stripped, fibers rewoven under moon-filtered slivers (those rare indigo-bright nights when clouds part). Mating drifts through the clusters like mist, bodies entwining in moss-nests, friction hushed to breaths, pheromones blooming orchid-sweet to summon partners from adjacent pods. No violence scars them; disputes dissolve in shared submersion, the pool's warmth enforcing truce. Yet peril whispers: the throat-squeezes claim the unwary, walls clasping in thermal spasm; deeper tunnels birth sulfur-blinded young from clutch-eggs they tend as oracles, feathered things that screech prophecy in the yips of mustelids.

Deeper still, their essence entwines the geology's adolescence. Some Veilborn bear ventral vents—faint pores exhaling mineral steam, siblings to the fumaroles, used in rites to steam-clean snares or quicken spore-sprouts. They dream-collectively, pouches pressed cheek-to-cheek in the dim, visions of sibling-fissures: hotter vaults where crystal-tapests bloom, or drowned seeps teeming blindfish kin. Wander among them unobserved, and they fold into the sparse—perched lizard-still on ledges, breath synced to the drip, forms dissolving into frond and shadow. Touch a hut-wall, and it quivers responsive; brush a dweller's arm, and it yields like overripe fig, reforming with a sigh. They are the fissure's exhaled children, sparse as its life, quiet as its pulse—constructing not empires, but echoes of the earth's own becoming, whispering back to the wound that birthed them.
