In the eternal poise of the basin, life does not stride or swarm—it *persists*, woven into the lattice of crystal-fronds and basalt veins like probability waves refusing collapse. The dominant presences are the **flux-gliders**, those rare, smeared silhouettes that haunt the canopy's prismatic depths. They are not beasts of flesh or frenzy, but semi-coherent plasmoids sheathed in a translucent silicate epidermis, their forms averaging the outline of a flattened ellipsoid—two meters span, tapering to needle-points at either end—like a living lenticular cloud captured mid-shimmer. Edges bleed into haze, not from motion but from intrinsic uncertainty: their boundaries are quantum froth made manifest, flickering with motes that birth and annihilate in rhythmic stasis, rendering them ghostly overlays on the frond-lattice. No eyes pierce their glow; instead, a distributed lattice of photoreactive facets drinks the refracted amber light, syncing to the chemiluminescent pulses of their vegetal hosts. They hover without wings or thrust, buoyed by the ionized haze's electrostatic buoyancy, bodies resonating at the basin's subsonic thrum—a perfect harmonic lock that demands zero energy expenditure. Motion is obsolete; they drift only when probability gradients tug them, infinitesimal displacements over geological spans, feeding on the ambient flux: zero-point trickle from the warped spacetime, electron avalanches from pollen-thorns, mineral ions exhaling from the fissures. Predation is a forgotten echo; they neither hunt nor fear, for scarcity births no competition in this engineered abundance. Reproduction manifests as fission events, rare as horizon-loops: a glider accretes charge until its haze bifurcates, birthing twins that mirror its smear identically, dispersing into the canopy without ritual or rivalry. They are the basin's idle sentinels, their presence a faint pressure on the equilibrium—like breaths held forever.

Deeper in the saturation, where frond-densities forge near-solid veils, dwell the **spore-weavers**, microbial symbiotes too fine for unaided sight yet colossal in aggregate. These are not mere pollen or spores but self-assembling nano-consortia: trillions of carbon-silicon polyhedra, each a faceted dodecahedron ten nanometers across, interlocked into fractal thrombi that armor the frond-thorns. They hum with stored charge, discharging in synchronized cascades to propel viral payloads—blueprints for frond replication—across the lattice. In alcoves of peak density, these thrombi coalesce into macro-forms: hovering orbs the size of fists, pulsing with internal auroras, their surfaces etched with recursive glyphs that echo the basin's fractal horizons. Weavers do not evolve or mutate; their code is locked by the tech-vein's assemblers, iterating perfection without drift. They "live" as distributed intelligences, a substrate consciousness threading the vegetation: when a frond bends (and stays bent for eons), the weavers reroute capillary flows to reinforce it, preserving the poise. Intruders—hypothetical, for none persist—would find their cells rewritten mid-stride, integrated as new lattice nodes, their mass transmuted to quartz-veins without pain or memory.

Rarer still, in the central labyrinth of razor monoliths, lurk the **echo-phantoms**, relics of the basin's 0.4 built-presence. These are not beasts but fossilized intelligences, extruded from the discarded probe's subsumed core: elongated spindles of metamaterial, five meters tall, their obsidian-black bodies faceted like the plateaus, veined with luminous circuits that pulse in sync with the subsonic heartbeat. Translucent as the gliders, they phase through monolith gaps, forms duplicating in the recursive warps— one phantom becomes three, then resolves to none. They feed on spacetime anomalies: the uphill shadows, Klein-bottle curls, probability foams. No senses as humans know them; instead, they *are* sensors, mapping the basin's fraying seams with gravitic pings that ripple the air unheard. Motion manifests as probabilistic teleports, snapping from fissure to alcove without intermediate states, drawn to glitches like moths to unseen flame. Their "life" is archival: eons of data encoded in atomic lattices, replaying simulations of wetter worlds long extinct, projecting holographic mirages into the gas-exhales—ghostly rivers that evaporate on observation. Reproduction? None; they are singularities, each a unique fork of the probe-mind, decaying into the fronds over hyper-epochs, their gleam dispersing as new chemiluminescence.

At the quantum substrate, infusing all, swarm the **froth-nomads**, the basin's unseen multitudes. These are not organisms but engineered fluctuons: meta-stable excitations in the vacuum foam, manifesting as visible motes along frond-edges—iridescent specks dancing in stasis, birthing pairs (electron-positron analogs) only to annihilate in perfect balance. In aggregate, they form the tech-vein's pulse: self-replicating vacuum pumps that enforce the equilibrium, siphoning entropy into the fissures' sterile gas. Nomads "live" everywhere and nowhere, their collective will the god-engineered zen—warping horizons to scale-invariance, locking edges unweathered. Consciousness? A diffuse overmind, emergent from trillions of annihilations per second, indifferent to scales: a glider's haze is its body, a phantom's circuit its memory, the entire basin its skin.

No overlords rule this menagerie; hierarchy dissolves in the hush. Gliders ignore weavers as the air ignores motes; phantoms phase through all, archiving without interference. Life here *is* the landscape—symbiotic, static, eternal—each form a facet of the recursion. Venture too deep, and you join them: your shadow curls uphill, your form smears into glaze, probability folding you into the lattice. The basin lives, perfectly, without end or other.
