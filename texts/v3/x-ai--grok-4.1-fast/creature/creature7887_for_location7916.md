In the pulsing heart of this sealed pocket-world, where stone vaults cradle a frenzy of green defiance and chitinous hordes, the true dwellers emerge not as conquerors but as filaments woven into the chaos—**the Kwevadari**, a relict people dwindled to kin-groups of no more than threescore souls, their forms honed by epochs of confinement into wiry emaciation, stature scant as the rodents they hunt, averaging scarce four feet from heel to crown. Their skin, pale-gold as quartz-veined schist leached by sun, bears a fine, invisible pelt of microscopic barbs—evolved dew-traps that wick nocturnal mist from the air, channeling it to pores clustered at neck and wrists like hidden gills, sustaining them in the waterless hothouse where sweat betrays the unwary. Eyes vast and luminous, pupils dilating to black moons in the crepuscular haze, pierce the gloom with hawk-keen focus; noses flattened and flared, sifting the mineral-ozone reek for the sucrose tang of ripening pads or the guano musk of bat-roosts ripe for raiding. Hair mats their scalps in thorn-thick braids, woven with tillandsia tassels that quiver like antennae, tuning to the symphony of chitters and trills.

They are born to the tangle, emerging from mud-daubed womb-nests in the terraced hovels—low burrows gouged from schist with adzes of chipped granite hafted to femur-bones of giant centipedes, walls smeared thick with spittle-mixed silt and armored with overlapping echeveria leaves, their waxy blades repelling the ceaseless creep of beetle legions. Infants, slick and mewling, are slathered in pulverized bromeliad pulp, a prophylactic paste that masks their scent from scimitar-clawed rodents whose warrens honeycomb the floors beneath. No infancy of swaddling idyll; by their third year, they scuttle on all fours through vine-lashed paths, tiny spears of fire-hardened root-wood clutched in fists, spearing thumb-sized beetle grubs that pulse like living hearts in the dust-pan.

The Kwevadari's days unspool in rhythmic predation amid the 2.7 fauna deluge, their lives a knife-edge dance scaled to the pocket's intimate severity. Dawn—marked by the first honey-lance of light through ceiling fissures—rouses the **forage-clans**, lithe figures in minimal garb of woven agave-fiber, straps crossing chests to sling clay jars and net-bags of twisted tillandsia root. They scale vertiginous switchbacks of packed gravel, palms rasping stone worn glassy by ancestors' passage, sidling past orb-webs where saucer-spiders lurk, their spinnerets quivering in wait. A misplaced toe crushes a succulent rosette, unleashing a wave of iridescent beetles that surge like quicksilver; the Kwevadari stomp and reap, mandibles crunching between teeth filed to points for grinding chitin, the harvest roasted over communal hearths of desiccated vine-knots, smoke curling through thatch-vents to mingle with bat-guano fog.

Predators they are, yet prey eternal: finch-birds, plump as fists with seed-gorged bellies, stoop in clapping squadrons to snatch unattended young from perches; the elders counter with slings of rodent-gut, pelting seeds that burst in mid-air, dazing the flocks for net-cast retrieval. Nightfall births the **hunt-veils**, shrouded in capes of layered bat-wings cured taut over frames of vine, echolocating pings mimicked by tongue-clicks to lure the true bats into woven snares strung between hut-rafters. Centipedes, those forearm-undulators with venom that stiffens limbs to wood, are the great leviathans courted with ritual caution—ambushed at molt when exoskeletons slacken, pinned with bone harpoons, their segmented meat stewed into porridges that thrum with stolen vigor. No waste: frass-fertilized dust is raked into garden-niches where thorn-jade vines are coaxed to fruit, their pulsing sap harvested for a blood-thick mead fermented in jars stoppered with spider-silk.

Their society coils tight as a bromeliad cluster, kin-led by the **Echo-Mothers**, crones whose skin sags like deflated succulents, ears elongated by generations of bone-stretchers to cup the ecosystem's roar—interpreting rodent-squeals as portents, beetle-choirs as harvest omens. Huts cluster by matriline, walls etched with thumb-daub glyphs: spirals for centipede hunts, clustered dots for beetle swarms, all lit by resinous glows from smoldering hearths where root-wood curls acrid-sweet. Rites bind them—full-moon veils, when mist seeps thickest through fissures, anointing bodies in guano-ash to invoke the pocket's breath, drums of stretched rodent-pelt thumping counterpoint to the 1.5 cacophony, bodies entwining in dust-pan clearings for couplings that ensure the bloodline's threadbare continuity. Quarrels erupt in claw-rakes and spear-thrusts, but scarcity enforces truce; a slain kin feeds the horde, not the clan.

Yet entropy gnaws: the young dwindle, birthing pangs wracked by thin air's vise; elders expire mid-climb, plummeting into beetle-waves that strip flesh to grinning bone in heartbeats. They persist as raw engine, thumbprints on every daub-beam, their fires defiant stars in the green-dense gloom. No gods but the walls that birth mist; no tomorrow but the next predation cycle. The Kwevadari are the pocket's soul—shadows foraging in frenzy, crafted from the same dust and sap as the beasts they slay, a primitive pulse accelerating toward the stone's unyielding hush. Step among them, and feel the brush: a child's tendril-hand snatching your cuff, a beetle corpsing underfoot, vines quivering welcome—or warning—in the unending, waterless thrum.
