In the perpetual gloam of this vegetal rookery, life is not a parade of beasts or a clamor of voices, but a congress of rooted multitudes—beings who *are* the city, their existences etched in cellulose and chlorophyll, pulsing in slow symphonies of sap and spore. They are the true metropolitans, a polity of green intelligences forged not in fire or forge, but in the patient alchemy of mycorrhizal webs, where fungi thread the peat like subterranean telegraph lines, ferrying sugars, signals, warnings of frost or surfeit. No single organism reigns; instead, a fractal hierarchy emerges from the tangle: colossal firs and spruces as elder patriarchs, their boles the unyielding spines of the nave, hearts pounding with annual rings that chronicle millennial sieges against ice-winds from the peaks. These arboreal ancients, some predating the mountains' upheaval, measure their days in centuries; sap ascends their phloem in dawn's pallid light, a viscous tide bearing minerals leached from the seeping schist, only to descend at dusk into frost-bound dormancy. Their bark, armored in moss-fur two inches thick, exhales faint volatiles—terpenes sharp as pine-needle stings—that ward off the rare fungal insurgencies probing their wounds.

Flanking these titans dwell the vinous nomads, cables of green muscle that traverse the canopy's vaults like living aqueducts. Traveler's-joy and woody clematis, tendrils coiling with prehensile cunning, inch across voids at rates imperceptible to warm-blooded eyes: a millimeter per day, questing for light or leverage, their grip-vines tipped with adhesive pads that secrete a mist-beaded gum, tasting the air's humidity before committing. They bridge gorges between boles, forming swaying causeways where dew collects in pendent droplets, each a cistern for the epiphytes clustered along their lengths—orchids and bromeliads, pale ghosts dangling like lanterns, their roots aerial and insatiable, slurping fog-condensate through velamen sheaths. These high-dwellers photosynthesize in the diffuse pewter haze, fronds unfurling in ziggurats against the cliffs: ostrich ferns and lady-ferns layered in herringbone precision, their sori shedding spores in diurnal puffs that drift like smoke-signals, colonizing fresh rock-fissures overnight.

Lower still, in the spongy boulevards of peat and bramble, the underclass thrives in compressed exuberance. Mosses—*Sphagnum* hummocks swelling to ankle-depth, their capillary cells wicking water like living sponges—form the foundational commons, a quivering pelt alive with contractile hyphae that squeeze excess moisture into communal reservoirs. Here burrow the rhizomal insurgents: couch-grass and alpine bistort, their underground rhizomes a warring lattice, tunneling for turf in eternal skirmishes resolved not by fang but by allelopathy, exudates poisoning rivals' roots. Bramble thickets patrol the defiles, their thorns recurved barbs glinting with rime, berries clenched tight against the cold—scarlet promises deferred until a fleeting thaw coaxes ripeness, feeding not mouths but the soil's decomposers. Sundews and bladderworts lurk in the marsh-edges, carnivorous sentinels snapping at fungal spores or errant nematodes, their mucilage glistening like dew-kissed jewels, digestion a leisurely bath in enzymes.

This green populace stirs in rhythms dictated by the mist's ebb and flood: predawn pallor triggers uncurling, a collective exhalation of oxygen that thickens the fog; midday's weak sun coaxes stomatal gasps, trading CO2 for growth; twilight's chill folds them inward, vines contracting with audible creaks, ferns veiling their fiddleheads in protective crooks. Communication hums subsonically—vibrations through wood and web, chemical plumes wafting on updrafts—alerting to threats: a ptarmigan's beak scarring moss, or the seismic thump of a rockfall avalanching from the rim. Predation is intimate, vegetal: mistletoe haustoria piercing host-bark like hypodermic roots, siphoning sugars; dodder vines, threadbare parasites, strangling saplings in lemon coils.

Fauna haunts the margins, interlopers in this botanical dominion, adapted to spectral transience. Ptarmigan ghosts—plump, white-mottled wraiths—flit through the alleys on velvet feet, gleaning frost-berries and lichen-scraps, their gizzard-stones grinding moss into paste for eggs laid in hidden cups of sphagnum. Enchytraeid worms, pink filaments no thicker than thread, aerate the peat in ceaseless peristalsis, their casts heaping into ziggurat-mounds that seed new moss-clumps. Spiders—*Tegenaria* sheet-weavers and orb-spinners of genus *Araneus*—string their hammocks between vine-struts, silk taut as harp-strings, ensnaring pollen-grains and springtails more than moths; their egg-sacs, pearl-clutches beaded with rime, overwinter in stump-caverns. Rarest are the lepidopterans: alpine blues and grizzled skippers, wings fog-dulled, nectar-sipping at dawn from dew-slick bracts before vanishing into the canopy's gloom. No mammals claim tenure; pikas might hoard hay below the rim, but within, the cold and crush brook no dens.

Yet whispers persist of deeper denizens, those who *chose* amalgamation—outcasts or ascetics from lower vales, drawn by the hollow's siren logic. Half-remembered in climber's tales: sylvan symbiotes, skin pallid and veined with green, flesh grafted to bark via deliberate wounds sealed by propolis and their own clotting sap. They perch in hollowed boles or vine-cradles, respiring through leaf-lentils stitched to lungs, imbibing xylem fluid via rootlet-tubes thrust into wrists. Sightless, they navigate by tendril-touch and mycorrhizal murmur, tending the city's balance: pruning errant shoots with bone-knives of shed antler, pollinating by hand in spore-dances. Nameless, they are—root-names like *Firsong* or *Mistvein*—eternal usufructs, neither plant nor beast, but the hollow's wardens. To glimpse one is omen: a frond-parting silhouette, eyes milky as quartz-veins, extending a probing liana toward the intruder, testing for alliance or absorption. They speak in rustles, or not at all; their lives the ultimate communion, bodies bloating slowly with hypertrophic tissues until they bud forth new limbs of wood and leaf.

Here, in this cleft-cathedral, the living are the lived-in: a metropolis where citizens *are* the walls, the streets, the very fog's freight. To dwell is to root, to grow or be overgrown; the plants hunger with quiet inevitability, weaving intruders into their verdant polity, one tendril-kiss at a time. Stand still too long, and feel the moss climb your boots, the vines assay your warmth—invitation or inexorable claim.
