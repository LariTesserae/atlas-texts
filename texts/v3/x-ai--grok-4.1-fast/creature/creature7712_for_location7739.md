In the colossal basin's engineered hush, where the drowned city's bones cradle an ocean's weight, life endures not as fleeting pulse or predatory gleam, but as a patient, pervasive verdancy—a congress of rooted empires, chemotrophic kinships woven into the ruins' very grain. These are no transient weeds scavenging margins; they are the sovereigns, the slow conquerors, their dominion absolute in the cold dark, drawing breath from the seeping alchemy of stone and silt. They live as a continuum, a breathing lattice that transmutes the mausoleum into maternity, where every frond and filament claims ancestry in the builders' forgotten lime and copper-laced mortar.

At the thicket's crown, the kelp-analogs reign tallest, titans of leathery resilience: *Abyssal fronds* (*Macrocystis profundis*, if names must be imposed from surface taxonomies), blades spanning twenty meters from holdfast to frayed apex, each a ribbed sail harvesting the faintest nutrient veils. Their stanchions—stems girth-thick as a human torso—anchor into fractured colonnades, pneumatocysts bloating and deflating in glacial rhythm to buoy the canopy against the water's crush. Here, in the near-total gloom, they photosynthesize not with stolen sun but through bacteriochlorophyll symbiotes, partners embedded in blade tissues, sipping hydrogen sulfide from geothermal seeps and ferrous ions dissolved from bronze relics. A single frond elongates a millimeter per lunar tide, its growth etched in micro-ridges, a biography in cellulose; colonies span hectares, interlocking like siege ladders against the ziggurats' humps, parting only for the sinuous aqueduct-channels where brines concentrate into fertile rills.

Beneath this vaulted overstory, the fern-hydrophytes sprawl in tiered profusion—*Necroferns* (*Pteridophyta mortuorum*), their rhizomes delving deep into porous substrate, questing after nitrates leached from eons-old organics entombed in the slabs. Fronds unfurl in fractal density, pinnae overlapping like chainmail, each leaflet a veined lancet dimpled for diffusive exchange in the stagnant flow. They propagate asexually, spores germinating in situ amid the calcareous filigree, forming clonal groves that mantle boulevards-turned-plains. A necrofern's lifecycle spans centuries: fiddleheads uncurling over decades, mature sporangia swelling bulbous with progeny, then senescence yielding to successors without decay—tissues calcifying seamlessly into the architecture, blurring the line where plant ends and ruin resumes. Their symbiotes, sulfur-oxidizing archaea, pulse faintly bioluminescent in extremis, betraying hidden vigor when vents burp methane, but otherwise they merge into the black hush.

Encasing all, the coralline algae form the living rind—*Crimson ossifiers* (*Corallina abyssi*), not polyps but true plants, their calcite skeletons precipitating in high-relief whorls and branches, turning mesas into labyrinthine reefs of rigid intricacy. They carpet every surface: flagstones etched with their tracery, aqueduct lips bearded in red filigree, mine-shaft mouths fretted into gothic portals. Growth accretes at a micron per season, building reliefs centimeters deep over generations, harbors for epiphytes while secreting alkaloids that deter all but the hardiest kin. In hypersaline dead zones, they thin to crusts, enduring where fronds falter, their photosynthetic engines tuned to the dimmest red-shifts filtering from above.

Threading this matrix, the vinous explorers—*Seagrass lianas* (*Posidonia tendrilis*)—probe and bind: runners slithering from ledge to ledge, rhizoids piercing mortar fissures like burrowing roots, nodes swelling into clonal hubs. They form aerial webs (aerial in this aqueous realm), tendrils coiling around petrified timbers, hauling nutrients upslope against the basin's sink. Bulbous sporophytes dangle as fruiting orbs, gravid with gemmules that drift on rare gyres, lodging in rubble to sprout new enclaves. Their economy is parasitic-symbiont: tapping the kelp's detritus while hosting methanogens that belch bubbles in anaerobic pockets, faint *pops* marking territory in the silence.

Deeper in the weave, the microbial underlife teems unseen, the true progenitors: consortia of *thioautotrophs* and *methanotrophs*, biofilms smearing every submerged facet, mats thickening in reservoir domes where brines stratify. They oxidize the ruins' dissolved bounty—ammoniac from decayed reeds, phosphorous from calcined shells—fixing carbon into the plants' foodweb, a chemotrophic engine humming without oxygen's spark. Fungi-analogs, chitinous mycelia (*Hyphomyces hadalis*), infiltrate the necroferns' vascular cores, trading minerals for sugars in mycorrhizal pacts spanning square kilometers, their fruiting bodies rare sporocarps bloating in thermoclinal eddies, dispersing zoospores on upwellings.

No beasts trespass this realm; predation is obsolete in such paucity of flux. Competition manifests in subtle wars: lianas throttling frond-bases, corallines overgrowing fern-rhizomes, kelp pneumatocysts shading sporophytes below. Reproduction is broadcast restraint—spores released in synchronized pulses with lunar thermoclines, settling as probabilistic colonists. Mortality feeds the cycle: senescent blades sloughing into silt, enriching the water column for kin; uprooted holdfasts tumbling into trenches, seeding abyssal mineshafts.

These dwellers are the city's heirs, reshaping the drowned expanse in their image: roots heaving flagstones into hills, tendrils reopening aqueducts as verdant flumes, calcifications sealing the builders' cuneiform in living script. They know no haste— a frond's sway spans hours, a vine's advance years—embodying the basin's tyranny of time, where motion is diffusion, conquest incremental, silence the medium of existence. In this green mausoleum, life is the structure itself, emergent from extinction, patient and unyielding, a vegetal polity ruling the bedrock's final cradle.
