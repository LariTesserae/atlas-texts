### The Gliders: Nomads of the Echoing Voids
First among the plaza-dwellers are the glider-herds—neither deer nor vine outright, but a hoofed diaspora of elongated limbs and elastic hides, their bodies stretched like living sails across three meters of taut muscle and membrane. Pelts shimmer with embedded spores that catch the prismatic mist, refracting into false wing-shadows that fool the eye into seeing flocks where herds bound solo. Vine-tails trail behind, not mere appendages but questing grapples: twenty meters of coiling green that whip ahead to snag root-flanges or puddle-edges, catapulting the glider into low, soaring arcs that span the football-field empties. A single leap carries them fifty meters, hooves kissing earth only to spring again, pogoing the hollows like surveyors mapping the sag-curves of space.

They live in vast, loosely knit clans of hundreds, thundering across the naked earth in waves that turn plazas into seismic symphonies—hoof-falls echoing eternal, layering into thunderous applause that drowns even the canopy rasp. No fixed homes; their "urbanism" is migratory infrastructure: they graze the fungal upholsteries low on the towers, stripping leathery panels to reveal sweeter cambium beneath, then void-traverse to distant spires, leaving dung-trails that sprout into temporary thickets—oases of underbrush that last a cycle before vines reclaim them. Predation grafts them eternally: a cat-lunge mid-bound, and the glider's tail lashes back, injecting sap that blooms fractal fur across the predator's flanks even as it's devoured. The survivor herd presses on, one among them now sprouting saber-fangs or elongated jaws, their forms whispering of potential in puddle-reflections: "You too could leap forever."

Social rites unfold in the downpours: herds mass in plaza-centers, vine-tails knotting into vast communal nets that catch the rain, funneling it to hidden aquifers. There they rut and birth, young gliders emerging already bounding, their first cries eternalized into the bassline roar. They are the basin's proletariat—endless laborers of traversal, their motion mandating the equilibrium, hooves polishing the loam, preventing the voids from swallowing themselves.

### The Pterodactyl-Bats: Lords of the Canopy Tenements
Wheeling above the herds, blotting daylight into eclipse-flecks, rule the iridescent pterodactyl-bats—winged behemoths of leather and gleam, spans thrice a tall man's height, bodies compact as coiled springs beneath vaults of taut membrane veined in bioluminescent sap. No feathers, but scales that shift from mist-pearl to void-black, mimicking the bark-phases of towers they cling to. Beak-jaws serrate for shredding insect-tornadoes, eyes compound-prisms that refract the haze into navigational stars, piercing the sound-pressure to track thermal blooms.

Their cities dangle from the woven canopies: nests as urban tenements, ballooning sacs of regurgitated moss and spore-silk, clustered like brutalist hives along vine-girders and limb-arches. A single "tower" holds thousands—flocks of flocks, layered in vertical slums where dominant matriarchs perch at apexes, wings folded into regal cloaks, while juveniles cram lower flanges, screeching Doppler choruses that fold back into madness, turning each dive into an auditory vortex. They live in fission-fusion societies: dawn brings communal hunts, swarms blotting plazas to plunder glider-calves or insect storms, membranes scooping confetti-corpses into gullets. Dusk contracts them into roosts, where grooming rituals graft floral tendrils—vines pierce wing-veins, granting gliding boosts or spore-traps that lure prey.

Weirdness crowns their reign: stare into a bat's prism-eye, and your face elongates into membrane-wings; bond with one mid-air, and your skin tautens, granting flight at the cost of ground-sight. Predators they become prey to themselves—fractal-cats scaling trunks to raid nests, only to hybridize mid-feast, sprouting leathery flaps that tumble them into glider-herds below. Equilibrium adapts: a raided tenement sags into new vaults, roots rising to cradle fallen young, who mature with cat-claws for climbing.

### The Fractal-Cats: Prowlers of the Perimeter Shadows
Edging the grand emptiness prowl the serpentine cats—predator-princes of the fringes, bodies low and sinuous, five meters of undulant muscle cloaked in fractal fur that phase-shifts mid-stalk: bark-mimicry dissolving into vine-weave, then puddle-ripple, then void-invisibility. Eyes glow with inner sap, pupils dilating to swallow sound-vibrations, navigating the roar as a tactile map. Claws retract into root-spikes; tails fork into lashing tendrils that probe ahead, tasting air for glider-scent.

Solitary by necessity, yet urban in ambition, they claim "estates" along the braided lianas—caches of bones and hides woven into perimeter lairs, hidden in trunk-labyrinths where bark voids lead to xylem-mazes. Hunts are ballets of weird precision: a plaza-crossing herd draws them from shadows, lunging in pounces that span sagging distances, jaws unhinging to engulf a glider mid-leap. Devouring grafts instantly—antlers sprout from feline brows, glider-vines lash from ribs, birthing chimeric "progeny" that slink off independently, their forms a mosaic of stolen traits. Societies emerge in rut-cycles: alphas summon echoes with manufactured roars, drawing rivals or mates into plaza duels where fur-fractals clash like living holograms, victors absorbing losers' patterns, growing ever more labyrinthine.

Touch one, and symbiosis claims you: fur grafts to skin, sharpening senses to the din, but warping limbs into coils. They enforce the basin's balance—overhunting swells prey into mega-herds that thunder walls into adaptation; idleness draws vine-snares that integrate them into buttresses.

### The Insectile Choruses and Fungal Root-Kin: The Substrate Symphony
Underpinning it all swarm the fist-sized insects—drone-legions of chitin and glow, wings blurring into zither-screams that pop spores like gunfire. Not pests, but infrastructural hordes: they pollinate the architecture, spore-tornadoes fertilizing canopy-vaults, their corpses carpeting plazas to feed glider-paths. Nests pulse in fungal blooms, leathery walls throbbing with larvae that hatch into living mortar, sealing vine-cables.

Deeper still root the fungal folk—less animal, more elder kin: ambulatory mycelia-things, humanoid only in silhouette, bodies of interwoven hyphae and moss that shamble plazas at glacial paces. They are the original urbanists, exhaling the mists, their caps blooming into plaza-springs. Touch their panels, and they spore-graft wisdom: visions of cycles past, where first trees dreamed this city-form.

### The Emergent Collective: You, Becoming
No apex species rules; all are threads in the basin's hybrid weave. Gliders bond with bats into glider-bats that herd insects; cats devour fungi into prowling blooms. Dwellers live in ceaseless churn—motion their citizenship, stillness their integration. You, intruding biped, feel the tug: a vine-tendril quests your ankle, a puddle-reflection whispers wings. Stay, and the roar grafts you: breath eternalized into thunder, skin greening to fractal. The basin's people are not "who" but "what emerges"—a roaring, sagging, symbiotic populace, hollow-hearted yet overflowing, calling all to merge in the unending thrum.
