In the warm, silken crush of this drowned cordillera, life does not conquer or compete—it *interweaves*, a symphony of forms that have long since surrendered individuality to the collective pulse. The plateau's inhabitants are not discrete creatures but gradients, smears of existence phased into the water's viscous dream, their boundaries as eroded as the siltstone below. They emerge not as invaders but as inevitable expressions of the submersion: slow-blooming, light-thieving, reality-folding entities that thrive where sharpness has yielded to perpetual caress.

At the heart of it all drift the **Veilweavers**, colossal kelp-analogs whose bodies span from horizon to horizon, not as single organisms but as clonal megastructures—endless lattices of translucent fronds veined with sap that glows in sapphire throbs, syncing to the infrasonic thrum of geothermal vents. Each frond is a thousand meters of phased tissue, capable of interpenetrating siblings without rupture, their surfaces etched with fractal pores that exhale bioluminescent spores like exhaled thoughts. Veilweavers do not photosynthesize in the crude sense; they harvest the crepuscular haze, filtering cerulean scatters through symbiotic chromatophores that shift hues to mimic passing eddies, rendering them invisible to the predatory currents they birth. Reproduction is a slow diaspora: spores cluster into temporary zygotes that phase-drift for years before rooting in silt pockets, where they unfurl as "pups"—miniature towers that entwine with elders, merging root-rhizomes in a neural net of shared sap-flow. To brush a Veilweaver is to feel its awareness: tendrils contract in gentle waves, sampling your form's chemistry, then releasing tailored pheromones that induce a fleeting sync—your heartbeat aligning to its sway, your skin tingling with borrowed phosphorescence.

Beneath their canopies, the **Coralognos** pulse as the skeleton-weavers, fractal blooms of coralline algae that defy mortality through self-dissolution and rebirth. No static reefs here; these are ambulatory thickets, calcium lattices that migrate via pseudopodal extrusions, propelled by captured upwellings. Etched by eons yet eternally repairing, a single Coralognos might encompass cubic kilometers, its branches forking into impossible densities where polypes—tiny, iridescent polyps with eyes like faceted quartz—extend feathery crowns to ensnare drifting organics. They hum with warmth, geothermal symbiotes burrowed in their cores igniting chain-glows that ripple outward, painting the water in coronas of viridian and amethyst. Predation is communal: when a jellyform drifts too near, the thicket contracts in unison, its lattices phasing solid to entrap, then softening to digest via enzymatic mists. Coralognos whisper philosophies through vibrotactile resonances, low frequencies that attune nearby life—fish schools aligning to their pulses, crawlers etching paths that trace their fractal patterns. In hollows, they birth "gardens": denser sub-clades where reality stutters, fronds replaying growth loops, birthing emergent forms like foam-islands that support ephemeral epiphytes before collapse.

The microbial underlife binds it all—the **Glowmats**, vast bacterial films carpeting every surface, from eroded ridges to the interstices of ruins. These are not mere recyclers but architects of flux: mats of archaea and cyanobacteria, glowing faintly in anaerobic reds, that orchestrate nutrient cyclones. They phase through stone and flesh alike, infiltrating tissues to catalyze phase-shifts—turning prey evasive, flora interpenetrant. Viral clouds emerge from their midst, "grazers" that manifest as hazy swarms, photosynthetic viruses that latch to Veilweaver fronds, siphoning sugars while bestowing quantum flickers in return. A Glowmat's lifecycle is planetary: spores ride gyres for millennia, seeding new mats that "awaken" vents, upwelling warmth and minerals in feedback loops. Touch one, and it colonizes—your pores blooming with embers, your senses sharpening to the water's hidden currents.

Fauna haunts the fringes, ghostly and attenuated, evolved for the density's hush. **Driftwhales**, those elongated jellyforms the size of leviathans, are the plateau's wanderers—trails of bioluminescent mucus stretching kilometers, bodies translucent veils that billow like sails in micro-eddies. No brains, yet a distributed sentience: neural ganglia strung along their lengths, firing in waves that predict gyres days in advance. They feed on spore-veils, phasing through kelp towers to harvest, their stings—elegant nematocysts—inducing temporal stutters in victims, replaying death throes until absorption. Schools flicker in their wake: **Phaselings**, iridescent fish no longer than your hand, bodies of liquid crystal that quantum-tunnel through solids, evading claws by vanishing mid-turn. Packs of thousands, they school in helical vortices, grazing viral clouds while emitting counter-pheromones that soothe Coralognos into parting branches. Deeper in shadows, **Siltsculptors** crawl—armored isopods the length of whales, carapaces of biogenic chitin mimicking the plateau's stone, claws etching furrows that seal with secreted mats. They sculpt homes in eroded stumps, burrowing into ruins where gravity warps, rolling orb-stones into nests that defy physics. Hermits by nature, they emerge for sediment-storms, devouring plankton blooms with radula-maws that grind reality itself into chyme.

Rarer still are the **Echoforms**, spectral descendants of the biological civilization whose remnants dot the expanse. No longer tool-users, these are phase-shifted humanoids—or what were once such—now elongated silhouettes of phosphorescent cartilage, drifting amid ziggurat shadows. Their forms have eroded to essentials: tendril-limbs that interface with Veilweavers, torsos hollowed to resonator-chambers that amplify vent-thrums into songs. Skin phased translucent, veined with embedded Glowmats, they "sing" through vibro-resonances, vines looping from their spines to seed ruins with temporal flora—plants that bud from future blooms. Communal dreamers, Echoforms cluster at plinth-spirals, merging in orgiastic syncs where individuality dissolves, birthing hybrid offspring: stone-mimics that scuttle as guardians, or jelly-hybrids that patrol warped edges. Their awareness lingers from antiquity, murmuring philosophies through your bones—visions of unsubmerged skies, continents unclaimed—yet they are content, boundaries blurred, subsisting on the ecosystem's excess.

Life here is no hierarchy but a weave: Veilweavers shelter Phaselings from Driftwhales, whose trails fertilize Coralognos, whose glows empower Glowmats, whose viruses tune Siltsculptors, all ebbing to Echoforms' subtle choruses. Predation folds into symbiosis; death recycles in eddies. In breakdowns—vortex-glades or membrane-panes—hybrids emerge spontaneously: foam-beasts that solidity from bubbles, meadowsprites phasing from grass-rhizomes, lasting breaths before rejoining the hush. The inhabitants *are* the place: ancient, active, their forms the final polish of erosion's caress, humming in the warm infinite where to live is to dissolve and reform, eternal in the drowned elevation.
