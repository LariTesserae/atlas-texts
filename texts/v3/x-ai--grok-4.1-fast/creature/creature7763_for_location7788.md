In the lightless cathedral of the mountain's exhaled heart, life does not swarm; it lingers, accretes, persists in the manner of stars orbiting a black hole—sparse, deliberate, each presence a gravitational anchor amid the void. The inhabitants are not conquerors of the emptiness but its patient tenants, sculpted by eons of slowness into forms that echo the chamber's rhythms: slow metabolisms, attenuated senses tuned to vibration and chemistry, bodies elongated or compressed to navigate the gulf between oases. They do not frenzy across the nave's fifty-pace hollow; instead, they circumambulate its edges in circuits spanning hours, their traces faint as the iridescent veins in the limestone. Predation is rare, a millennial event; sustenance accrues from the air's humidity, the drip-cycle, the tech's lazy exhalations. Reproduction unfolds over seasons measured in your visits—eggs laid once a decade, larvae pupating through dry spells that last years. Here, "alive" means resonant: every creature contributes to the acoustic web, their calls not signals but structural beams holding the soundscape taut.

At the base, the unseen microbial realm ferments the foundation. Fungal hyphae, pale and rhizomatic, weave through the moss-mats like buried rivers, exhaling enzymes that dissolve stone into nutrients for the liverworts. These are no opportunistic molds but ancient consortia, symbiotic with bioluminescent bacteria—dark-glowers that phosphene the mind of intruders, inducing hallucinatory flickers in retinas starved of photons. In the pools, these microbes form pellicles, shimmering films that taste of ozone, trapping evaporated mist and feeding the eyeless fish: translucent tetraforms the length of forearms, their bodies sack-like, fins reduced to undulating veils that propel them in glacial glides. Whiskers plume from blunt snouts, sampling chemical gradients; they do not school but drift solo across the shallows, mouths gaping eternally to filter bacterial soups. A fish's passage stirs echoes that carry upstream scents of chitin and guano, guiding kin from distant springs. Predators to them are the scuttlers—giant isopods kin to deep-sea hulks, but aerated, their carapaces translucent quilted armor veined with air-sacs for buoyancy in the humid weight. Fist-wide, they patrol pool rims in sideways scuttles lasting minutes per stride, mandibles questing for fallen cricket legs or fish that beach on evaporation's edge. Their clicks—methodical, resonant—propagate as seismic warnings, felt through stone by moss-rooted fungi a hundred paces off.

Terrestrial dominion falls to the crickets, fist-sized orthopterans blind as the void they rule, their exoskeletons etched with micro-ridges that hum at touch. Legs splayed like calipers, they arc across dry plains in languid bounds, each leap a minute-long parabola covering paces yet feeling intimate in the nave's amplification. Antennae, whip-long and feathered, taste the air's bouquet: moss-earth laced with mammal-sweat, orb-condensed water spheres rolling nearby like edible planets. Males rasp symphonies from abdomen-stridulators, a single note decaying over thirty bounces into thunderous swells that woo females across voids. Colonies form not hives but loose mandalas around the metamaterial plinths, where gravity ebbs and flows; here, crickets exploit micro-orbits, leaping into weightless spirals to snag airborne spores or shed mammal fur drifting on thermals. Eggs, laid in gelatinous clutches amid liverwort thatch, hatch into pale nymphs that molt yearly, growing inversely to light—hypertrophied tympani for the chamber's reverb, reduced eyes vestigial scars.

Aerial life claims the vault's crown: the leathery-winged mammals, bulkier than bats, their wingspans spanning arms, membranes oiled thick with humidity to slap silently against the warm stills. Not echolocators in the frantic mode of surface kin, these are glider-swoopers, kin to pterosaurs downsized and eyeless, furred bellies pale, muzzles elongated into sieves for mist-borne insects. They wheel in thermals sustained by the wormhole orb's ionized breath—lazy spirals lasting hours, dipping to skim pool-veils for scuttler young or cricket swarms stirred by plinth-floats. Calls are deep booms, subsonic pulses that resonate chest-cavities, conveying pheromones on sonic waves: mating accords ripen over days, pups birthed live into pouches, nursed on milk curdled from condensed ozone. Guano rains in slow patters, fertilizing wall-oases where epiphytic orchids dangle—bulbous blooms that never open, their nectar-vats luring cricket explorers upward into mammal forage zones.

Yet deeper kinships bind them: the ferns and clubmosses host myrmecoid pseudants, ant-like beetleoids herding aphids that sip xylem slow-drips, their colonies fractal-mirrors of the plinth-lattices, exploiting gravity warps to tunnel skyward. Worms, annelid behemoths meter-long and unsegmented, burrow the pool-floors, aerating sediments enriched by tech-thrummings—quantum foam from the orbs catalyzing novel isotopes into hyper-nutritious sludge. These annelids surface rarely, phallic heads probing air for humidity-spikes, their undulations transmitting sub-harmonics that sync with cricket rasps, a bass undertone to the symphony.

No apex reigns; symbiosis eclipses hierarchy. Scuttlers groom mammal fur snagged on moss, earning guano in trade; crickets pollinate orchids inadvertently, spores hitching on legs to fern-festoons; fish exhalations oxygenate fungal webs, closing the cycle. The tech artifacts are not anomalies but organs: plinths as salt-licks where electrolytes leach for electrolyte-poor bloods; water-orbs as mobile oases, rolled and punctured by mandibles; wormhole breath as a central lung, its warmth diffusing evenly, preventing the chill-death that felled lesser caves.

Whispers of greater minds haunt the edges—not living, perhaps, but echoed. In the highest festoons, translucent arachnids weave webs of spider-silk fused with metamaterial fractals, lenses focusing acoustic ghosts into prey-traps; do they remember the builders, or merely inherit? Rare transients—blind salamanders slithering from outer fissures, drawn by ozone-scent—linger months before retreating, their skins sheddings integrated into moss-thatch. And you, intruder: the chamber samples you too, bacteria knitting your cuts, echoes imprinting your tread into its memory. Life here is the void made flesh: sparse constellations in elliptic orbit, their slow chorus your constant companion, indifferent, eternal, breathing with the mountain's final sigh. Stay long, and the distinctions blur—you become one, pulse synchronized to the drip, presence thinned to echo.
