### The Dampfolk of the Sinkrim

They call themselves the Sinkrim, or so the guttural syllables emerge from their throat-sacks when mist-veiled strangers dare the karst fangs to approach. Not a tribe born of bloodlines or banners, but forged in the sinkhole's sudden yawn—wanderers coalesced like dew on cold stone, their numbers swelling from tens to scores within moons of the earth's fresh wound. Eighty souls, perhaps, packed into the warren's spiral embrace: no elders with frost-limned beards, for the damp claims the frail swift; no infants wailing, for the pool hungers cries as keenly as flesh. The Sinkrim are middlers, tempered between youth and decay, their bodies a testament to saturation's slow alchemy—skin slick as marsh-minnows, perpetually sheened with condensed vapor that never fully dries, pale as the pool's underbelly yet veined with quartz-blue threads that map their limbs like the glowing walls they revere.

Physically, they defy the lowland frailty one might expect. Sinew-corded and low-slung, averaging four-and-a-half feet at the shoulder, their frames hunch instinctively against the ceaseless drip, shoulders rounded like the karst's own blades, knees perpetually bent in stilt-walking crouch. Barefoot always, soles thickened to leather by muck and reed-slash, callused enough to grip slick logs without slip. Hair grows in lank coils, black or mud-brown, greased with eel-gland oil to shed water, often braided with quartz shards that chime faintly against collarbones. Faces are broad and flattened, nostrils flared wide to sift the mineral tang, eyes large and lid-heavy—irises a watery gray-gold that mirrors the overcast light, pupils dilating slow like the pool's shadows. Lips are thin, cracked perpetual from the warm vapors' corrosive kiss, stained faint green from chewing mangrove pearls for thirst-quench. Both sexes bear the same utilitarian bareness: torso wraps of woven eel-silk, thigh-slung kilts of thatch-fiber, adorned only with copper needles thrust through ear-folds or nasal septums, traded grimly from hill-folk who never linger.

Men and women blur in labor's forge—no division save the swell of some bellies with child, carried low and silent till birth on the longhouse platforms. The net-weavers are the spine: hunched on fringe-perches, they milk glands from live eels—pale, foot-long captives thrashed in clay pots, their silken threads spooled wet and steaming into nets for the deeper snares. Hands move in hypnotic twitch, fingers elongated and pricked perpetual with bone-awls, nails pared to points for gland-piercing. Beside them, potters kneel in the spiral's mid-terraces, kneading marsh-clay with bare feet, shaping urns broad as hips to hoard dew or brew eel-venom teas that numb the venomous slugs for meat. Children—skeletal wirlings of eight winters or less—ferry the dew-jugs: hollow reeds stoppered with wax, slung in nets across narrow backs, their chatter a wet susurrus mimicking the rock-murmurs, feet slapping the gangplanks in endless circuit from mist-traps to longhouse vats.

The longhouse anchors them, squatting on the sinkhole's pillar like a tumor of thatch and daub: windowless save slits for vapor-glance, its interior a dim womb of shared pallets stuffed with dried reeds. Here pulses the communal heart—meals of boiled slug-flesh and shadow-fish paste, ladled from communal crocks; rituals of palm-pressing to the vein-quartz, where the walls' faint pulse syncs breath to tide-swell, inducing trance-states where visions bubble like the springs below. No chiefs or shamans named; leadership emerges fractal, as the settlement itself: the eel-master whose nets yield richest silk claims first voice in tide-councils; the quartz-whisperer, marked by luminescent scars on palms, interprets the flutesong of weeping rocks. Disputes settle in the pool's mirror—offenders lashed to stilts till the tendriled depths claim or release, a trial by saturation.

Their world bends to the sinkhole's rhythm, a perpetual low-dance with the insistent wet. Dawnless under the cloud-dome, they stir to the tide's swell, climbing higher as the pool surges twice daily, vapors thickening to milk the air for dew. Predatory patience mirrors their own: they stalk marsh-slugs not with blades but chimes—reed-ocarinas blown soft to lure the beasts from reeds, then crushed under heel for the pearl-fat innards. Eels are husbanded in pens of split-log, fed crab-scraps; shadow-fish teased with bone-lures from platform edges, hauled writhing to silken death. Fauna integrates as kin: lone marsh-crabs tolerated as pot-scourers, scuttling gangplanks to gnaw clay-rims; furtive finches, feathers mist-matted, nesting in thatch-eaves, their droppings prized for kiln-paste.

Weirdness threads their flesh as deep as the karst's. Some Sinkrim bear "tide-marks"—subdermal quartz-veins that pulse with the springs, granting faint second-sight: visions of fissures below, or warnings of karst-quakes. Skin, if cut, weeps not blood but milky serum that hardens to pearl-shell, prized for needles. Birthing occurs in quartz-trances, mothers submerged waist-deep in longhouse pools till the child emerges slick and silent, inhaling mist before first breath. No gods invoked, only the Sink—the calved maw itself, appeased by offerings of flawed pots cast to the depths, where tendrils rise in acceptance or rebuke. Songs are hummed sub-vocal, harmonics matching the rock-flutes, tales of the hole's birthing: how it gasped open in storm-wrath, exhaling promise to the parched, drawing the first wanderers who slaked eternal here.

Strangers glimpse them as ghosts-in-mist, ant-like in density, motion restrained yet ceaseless—a hidden heart beating in clammy eternity. Approach too bold, and eyes lift slow, unblinking, assessing if you'll join the saturation or flee to drier dooms. Linger, and the damp claims you: breath condensing on lips, skin beading, pulse syncing to the drip-drip chorus. The Sinkrim do not conquer their world; they dissolve into it, half-built, half-grown, eternal as the weeping stone.
