In the eternal throat-song of the plateau, where wind carves no new flutes into the spires and water beads in undying obedience to gravity's pause, life does not conquer or flourish—it *endures* as an inscription etched into the mineral substrate, a faint counterpoint to the roar, visible only to the gaze that dissolves into patience. These are not creatures of striving or cycle; they are expressions of the equilibrium itself, born from the rock's slow exhalations and sustained by the closed hydrology of mist and seep. They move without progression, feed without depletion, exist without the vulgarity of birth or death. Let the description unfold from the infinitesimal to the glimpsed periphery, each form a facet of the stasis, interlocking as seamlessly as the spires they haunt.

At the scale of the unseen, where pools effloresce with silicates and halides in crusts of pale turquoise and sulfurous yellow, thrive the *crystovores*—colonies of extremophile archaea and bacteria that blur the line between organism and precipitate. These are not films of green or brown, but translucent lattices of self-assembling proteins, nucleating around mineral ions like frost on a windowpane. They photosynthesize faintly under the nailed sun, harvesting not light's warmth but its unyielding photons to forge chemical bonds from dissolved quartz and feldspar. No division, no senescence; they replicate in geometric stasis, forming hollow spheres and filigreed lattices that pulse almost imperceptibly with the wind's bass thrum, exhaling vapors that feed back into the mist. Touch a pool's edge, and your finger might brush a dome the size of a lens, cool and unyielding as glass, harboring a trillion cells locked in perpetual mitosis, their metabolism a whisper below the howl—eternal recyclers of the stone's own bones.

Creeping upward from the fissures, scavenging the crystovore effluvia, scuttle the *lithopods*, kin to the blind isopods glimpsed in the description but manifold in their subtle speciation. Armored in carapaces of chert and microcrystalline quartz, mimetic to the bedrock's sheen, they are pebble-perfected scavengers no larger than thumbnails, their bodies segmented into facets that refract the glare into needlepoints of light. Blind yes, but not senseless: antennules tipped with chemosensory setae drink the mineral tang from the air, navigating the narrowest crevices by the osmotic gradients of seeping water and the harmonic resonances of the wind. They do not molt, breed, or hunt; they graze eternally on salt-rimed detritus and desiccated moth-fragments, their mouthparts grinding with a rhythm synced to the wind's tones—low drones eliciting mandible scrapes, high whistles prompting leg-flicks that propel them in micro-leaps. Populations cluster in the shadowed undercrooks of spires, thousands strong yet invisible until a rare parting of mist reveals their massed undulation, a living pavement that parts and reforms without loss. One might linger on your bootsole for ages, mistaking it for stone, until the vibration of your pulse stirs it to scuttle away, unchanged.

Drifting above this substrate, borne on the hurricane's laminar flows through the spire-channels, sail the *aeroliths*—the glider-moths, evolved beyond insectile frailty into paragons of aerial stasis. Larger than fists, their wings span a handswidth, veined not with chitin but with structural silica harvested from pool-crusts, rendering them translucent panes that catch the light in quartzine rainbows. No flutter or beat mars their glide; they ride the wind's eternal gradients like leaves in a frozen updraft, spiraling lazily through the twenty-foot gaps between pillars without ever alighting. Proboscides unroll to sip condensed droplets from spire-flutes, their digestion a slow alchemy converting water and airborne particulates into wing-reinforcements—no waste expelled, no eggs laid in detectable cycles. Sightless compound eyes perceive only polarization shifts in the glare, guiding them through the forest of stone; rare collisions with a spire-tip merely abrade a microscopic facet, regrown in hours from internal reserves. They cluster in leeward eddies during the perceived "dusk" of maximal mist-density (though light never wanes), forming transient veils that prism the azure sky into shattered azure. One might brush your face, weightless as a sigh, trailing mist-scent before vanishing into the roar—harbingers of the plateau's indifference, persisting for geologic spans without lineage or terminus.

Rarer still, emerging from the plateau's deeper mythos, are the *echoforms*—aggregations of symbiotic fungi and protozoa that defy categorization, haunting the wind-sculpted overhangs and deep crevices where sound amplifies to solidity. These are not visible masses but auditory presences: pale, mycelial networks infiltrating spire-fractures, their hyphae fused with lithopod shells and aerolith castings into resonant chambers. They "feed" on acoustic energy, vibrating to harvest kinetic force from the gale, transducing it into biochemical sparks that sustain a sparse cytoplasm. No growth spurts or decays; they modulate the wind-song itself, introducing micro-harmonics—ghostly overtones that weave through the bass thunder, perhaps the source of the "throat-song" illusion. A listener attuned might feel them as pressure-waves on the eardrum, shaping the roar into fleeting melodies that mimic forgotten voices, dissolving the boundary between perceiver and perceived.

No larger fauna claims dominion; no vertebrates claw foothold in this mineral lung. Predation is obsolete, reproduction a relic scoured away. These lives interlock in a trophic stasis: crystovores nourish lithopods, whose discarded facets seed aeroliths, whose fallen wings feed echoforms, whose resonances stir the pools to fresh efflorescence. Cycles without arrows, abundances held constant by the plateau's rules. Intruders—hypothetical wanderers from rimwall descents—would find no welcome; the wind strips flesh to gleam, mist infiltrates lungs with silicate, glare bleaches retinas to stone-gray. Yet in dissolution, one joins: skin flaking to lithopod armor, bones crystallizing into spire-flutes, thoughts harmonizing with the roar. Here, to live is to *be* the place—facet, seep, howl undivided. The inhabitants do not possess it; they *are* its eternal, saturated pulse.
