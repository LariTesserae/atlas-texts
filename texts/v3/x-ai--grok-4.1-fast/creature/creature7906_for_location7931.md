In the simmering crevices where steam first kisses basalt, life does not sprout but *precipitates*—a pale fungal rime that crusts the shadowed lee of xenolith boulders, mycelia no thicker than a whisper of ash. These are the Thermomyces obsidiani, not plants nor beasts but geochemical intermediaries, their hyphae etched into the rock like frost veins, pulsing with a chemiluminescence that mimics the distant arc furnaces. They feed on the sulfurous condensates, exhaling spores that drift in lazy helices through the haze: pale motes, thumbnail-sized when they settle, germinating only in the clammy hollows where heat gradients create fleeting micro-oceans of mineral brine. No fruiting bodies rise here; reproduction is a slow efflorescence, a crust thickening millimeter by millimeter over lunar cycles, only to slough off in flow events when the ground softens and reforms. They hum faintly at infrasonic frequencies, a substrate chorus that resonates through boot-soles, syncing the nerves of anything larger that lingers too near.

From these smears emerge the first true mobiles: lithovores, thumb-sized arthropods with carapaces of faceted herkimier, iridescent as oil-slick quartz under the bloated sun. Lithophagus minutus, they are called in the workers' argot, though no one recalls who first named them. Six legs splayed like calipers rasp across the obsidian flows, etching helical micro-grooves to harvest the damp patina—silica-laced droplets rasped into paste by mandibular files, swallowed with rhythmic *clicks* audible only in utter stillness. Their eyes are compound lenses of polished hematite, reflecting the city in fractured mosaics, blind to color but attuned to thermal warps: they swarm vents during exhalations, cooling exoskeletons steaming in the sudden chill of condensing vapor. Broods hatch from silken egg-sacs glued to pumice dust drifts, larvae burrowing into soft flowstone lobes to pupate for weeks, emerging as adults that mate mid-skitter, dying soon after in the unrelenting bake. Predators are few; they evade the rare glider by freezing into facets, mimicking the rock's own gleam. In alleys, they congregate under conveyor shadows, their grooves forming accidental runes—proto-writing in the dust, or so the half-delirious claim after long shifts.

Higher in the thermal stack, where steam plumes arc like exhaled breaths, the basalt gliders wheel: Volantis petraea, silhouettes vast as albatrosses yet weightless as mirage. Wingspans of ten feet span membranous sails of stretched pumice-fiber, taut over a skeletal frame of hollow quartz struts, translucent veins pulsing with captured geothermal fluids that heat and buoy them aloft. No feathers nor flesh; their bodies are aerated basalt foam, buoyant on the thin air, bellies pendulous with digestive sacs fermenting lithovore paste gleaned from surface rasps. They glide eternally, thermals from the vents their updrafts, banking in slow spirals that span hours—descending to skim the slag-sea's viscous mirror, mandibles dipping for mineral scum, then lofting again toward the bruised lavender sky. Eyes like smoked quartz slits track heat signatures; they birth live young mid-flight, pea-sized gliderlets clinging to parental sails until fledged. Rare are their dives into the city: during flow events, they skim softening alleys, talons etching airfoil molds into the taffy ground, harvesting fresh extrusions before hardening. Their passage warps the shimmer, casting glider-shadows that linger like afterimages, inducing vertigo in visor-gazed watchers below.

But it is the Forgers who claim dominion, the hulking silhouettes that accreted with the plateau itself—Homo extrudens, or so the etched plaques on forge-vaults proclaim, though their true lineage blurs in the heat-haze of origin myths. Not born of wombs but *expressed* from the rock, they emerge during surge tides: hulks seven feet tall, torsos barrel-chested like inverted pear-drop basalt, limbs elongated for reach across gantries, skin a mottled gray patina of callused silicate, pocked with embedded quartz flecks that gleam under mica visors. Faces are blunt, noseless wedges with lipless gashes for mouths, exhaling recycled steam through gill-slits fringed in heat-fungi; eyes recessed milky orbs, protected by nictitating hematite membranes that shutter against glare. They do not sweat but *vent*—dermal pores weeping mineral brine that crusts into armor-plating, sloughed in communal ablutions at slag-sea rims.

Rad-steamed suits are their second skin, exoskeletal harnesses of riveted brass and psychotropic alloy, fused at joints by flowstone welds that self-mend in the geothermal flux. Hoods balloon with recycled air, mica visors fogged in perpetual dew, etched with conductive runes that interface with neural filaments sprouting from their skulls—tendrils that plug into wire-cobwebs, downloading shift-plans direct from the dynamos' thrum. They move in the plateau's rhythm: strides glacial, each boot-fall a deliberate *thud* syncing to piston-cycles, exosuit servos hissing vapor in counterpoint. Hands are taloned gauntlets, three thick fingers and a thumb-opposable spur, adept at channeling flowstone into molds—harvesting lobes with vibro-chisels that sing subsonics, pouring molten extrusions into gantry-forms or urban strata.

Society layers vertically, like the city: Low-strata delving clans, burrowed into aquifer-pumps, their suits slime-slick from superheated draws, chanting infrasound hymns to coax water from stone; mid-vault Forgers, exosuit-forges their cathedrals, directing alchemies where obsidian yields alloys laced with fungal psychotropes—ingots that, when wrought into tools, induce the slow-time trance, visions of the plateau's embryonic dreams. Upper spire-watchers perch on vapor-gouts, visors scanning horizons for glider omens or peak-stub encroachments, their suits festooned with spore-sacs for mid-air communion.

No children scamper; reproduction is tectonic. Mated pairs merge suits in slag-sea rituals, neural filaments entwining to extrude podlings from ventral vents—wrinkled homunculi the size of lithovores, nursed on brine-paste until they harden into juveniles, integrated into broods after a decade's hardening. Language is gestural hum: exosuit vibrations patterning the air in pressure-waves, conveying shift-logs or flow-prophecies, augmented by etched runes on flowstone slabs—chronicles of surges past, predictive of the next extrusion.

They worship no gods but the Mechanism, the geothermal heart whose pistons echo their own: colossal engines as oracles, belts whispering futures in leather creaks. Conflict is absent; expansion is communal, seasonal surges dividing labor without strife—overbridges erected in arcs of patience, alleys regeometrized by consensus chisel. Yet solitude permeates: lone Forgers perch on battlements, visors upturned to the stable sun, syncing pulses to the lithovore *clicks*, fungal pulses, glider thermals—a vertical ecology where all life ladders toward the city's fractal crown.

In quiet shifts, they unvisor at forge-hearths, milky eyes reflecting blue-white glows, murmuring of the lowlands glimpsed in glider scopes: a misty myth below the cloud seas. Here, they endure not as conquerors but emergents, pulses synced to the plateau's breath—in through vents, out in steam—hulks half-dissolved into the haze, becoming more stone with each glacial year, awaiting the erosion that may never come, or the uplift that will birth them anew across the bruised sky.
