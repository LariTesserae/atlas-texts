In the heart of this subterranean sprawl, life endures not as a clamor of beasts or throng of peoples, but as a slow imperial conquest waged by the unyielding green. The true sovereigns are the plants themselves—titanic, deliberate, their dominion absolute and ancient in intent despite the cavern's youth. They do not merely grow; they architect, they territorialize, they dictate the pulse of existence here. Consider the tree-ferns, those colossal sentinels rising thirty, forty feet from root-mats thick as woven steel cables, their trunks bulbous and armored in scales of fibrous bark, pale gray veined with the black rot of perpetual damp. Each frond crown spans ten feet across, a fractal canopy of copper-bronze leaflets that quiver faintly in the wind's roar, photosynthesizing in miserly rations from the amber lamps' glow and the faint bioluminescence of symbiotic fungi at their bases. These ferns unfurl new growth in ritualistic spasms—once every few weeks, a tight crozier at the apex cracks open with a sound like splintering ice, extending inch by inch over days, rasping against siblings until the air fills with a papery susurrus beneath the thunder. They breed sparingly, spores dusting the frost-rimed flagstones below, germinating only where root duff has accumulated into loam-pockets, birthing clonal offspring that mirror their parents' monolithic form. No flowers, no fruits; reproduction is a haze of powder, a gamble on the cold currents.

Vines claim the vertical realms, their girths rivaling a laborer's waist, sinuous behemoths that ascend sheer basalt faces in braided columns, anchoring with adventitious roots that drill into drill-scars like living rivets. Fleshy and mottled green-black, they pulse faintly with sap-pressures driven by the ceaseless drip from above—each tendril tip probing blindly, coiling around rusted rebar or ore-cart axles, hauling themselves upward in millimeter conquests. Epiphytes festoon them like nomadic vassals: pale orchids with waxy blooms that never fully open, siphoning mist through aerial roots; bromeliads cupped like chalices, hoarding droplets that ferment into nutrient brews for unseen larvae. These vines span chasms, forming precarious bridges that sway in the drafts, their bark shedding in curls that carpet the floor below, mulching into the substrate for mosses to claim.

Mosses are the infantry, the velvet legions blanketing every viable surface—boulders, ledges, even the corroded flanks of steam engines—in undulating cloaks of emerald and ash. They thrive in the frost's embrace, their rhizoids threading into micro-fissures, exhaling faint methanolic vapors that mingle with the dynamo’s exhaust. Fungi amplify this realm: clusters of pale toadstools erupting from fern-buttresses, shelf-like brackets protruding from vine-hauls in tiers of cream and ochre, their undersides etched with fertile gills that release spores in diurnal puffs, timed to the arc lamps' flicker. Some are mycorrhizal tyrants, entwining fern-roots in symbiotic strangleholds, trading minerals leeched from dynamite-residue soils for photosynthetic sugars; others solitary necrophages, devouring the leaf-litter and rusted flakes alike, their fruiting bodies swelling grotesquely in the damp, caps splitting to spill ink-black sludge.

Fauna threads through this green hegemony like ghosts in a cathedral—sparse, specialized, existing at the margins of predation's absence. Insects dominate the understory: translucent cave crickets with elongated legs like frost-wire, their antennae twitching through the duff, scraping pale rasps from root-fibers to harvest fungal mycelia. They molt in hidden crevices beneath flagstones, emerging larger, their exoskeletons gleaming with condensation before molting again, a cycle of quiet inflation amid the roar. Albino springtails explode in micro-clouds from moss-tufts when disturbed, bounding on collared tails, filter-feeding spores and bacteria from the air's mist. Predators are rare, attenuated: slender pseudoscorpions, milk-white and pincher-armed, stalk these micros across the root-mats, injecting paralytic venom before dismantling prey fiber by fiber, their mouthparts grinding with faint, drowned clicks lost to the din.

Higher in the gloaming flit the bats—lean, velvety specters the size of a spread hand, furred in ghostly silver, wings patched with fungal mildew from roosting in vine-canopies. Not hordes, but solitary wanderers or mated pairs, navigating by echolocation that pierces the wind-thunder, snatching scuttling insects mid-leap or lapping nectar-analogs from epiphyte urns. Their guano dusts the machinery below in nutrient bombs, sparking algal blooms in the icy pools—flecks of green filigree rimming water's edge, grazed by translucent isopods that scavenge the gravel beds, their armored shells clicking like dice as they burrow for worms. Blind earthworms, plump and segmented in rose-pale, tunnel the fibrous duff, aerating it with sinuous undulations, surfacing only in freshets to gasp at the surface before retreating. No vertebrates beyond bats; no fish in the pools, just microbial veils and copepod specks darting in the shallows.

No humans dwell here now, though their ghosts linger in the relics—perhaps a hermit tinkerer haunts the fringes, a reclusive figure pieced from the description's absences, tending the lone dynamo with oil-can rituals, face shrouded in frost-beard, clad in oilskins patched with fern-fronds. Or nomad scavengers pass through seasonally, picking copper windings from engines, their fires guttering brief defiance against the cold, voices swallowed by the roar before vanishing into tunnels. But these are transients; the cavern rejects sustained human tenancy, its chill and clamor weeding out all but the vegetal core.

Deeper still, emergent kinships blur lines: hybrid forms where fungi merge with insect carapaces, mycorrhizal mantles granting pale beetles symbiotic luminescence, their undersides glowing faint foxfire to lure prey into moss-traps. Root-gnawing nematodes swarm vine-hauls, vectors for fungal invasions that topple weakened pillars over decades. Viruses whisper through sap-streams, culling frond-tips in mosaic patterns, selecting for hardier strains. Life here is a continuum—plant to fungus to arthropod to worm—patient, inexorable, a biomass empire measured not in numbers but in mass, reclaiming the machine-wound inch by glacial inch. Stand amid the pillars, and feel it: the ferns' slow exhalations mingling with bat-wings' hush, insect-scrapes threading the roar, all weaving into the cavern's hymn—a living chorus where green is god, cold its altar, and time the high priest.
