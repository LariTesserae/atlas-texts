In the shadowed cradle of this cirque, where stone still remembers the earth's fevered dreams, life clings and contends in forms both familiar and estranged, woven into the mist's endless exhale. They are the dwellers of the hush, these beings whose rhythms pulse beneath the basin's brittle silence—not conquerors, but supplicants, tenants in a realm that tolerates their fire and their flesh. Foremost among them are the Cirque-folk, two-score souls scattered across the boulder-flats in their vigilant scatter of hovels, kin-groups bound by blood and the shared glyph-warding that etches their lintels like whispered prayers. Pale as the fog they breathe, their skin gleams with a perpetual oil-slick from rendered crawler-fat, stretched taut over frames honed lean by the thin air's bite—limbs corded like twisted roots, torsos broad to hoard what warmth the fumarole gifts. Eyes like polished obsidian, wide and unblinking, track the half-seen shifts in the gloom; hair falls in matted coils, braided with beetle-chitin beads that clatter softer than dewfall. They move as ghosts among their own works, footfalls placed with the precision of hound-hunters, cloaked in frond-woven tunics that blend with the fern-pockets, faces half-masked by nets of woven reed to filter the floaters' sparking whips.

These folk, who call themselves the Veilborn in their guttural murmurs—tongues shaped by the cirque's damp hush, words exhaled like contrails in the chill—emerge with the grayglow's peak, when the overcast thins to let slate-light sift through. Dawn is their tending: women with callused palms kneel in the lush groves, fingers delving into spongy moss to unearth tuber-knots swollen fat on fumarole-seeps, roots that taste of earth-smoke and hidden minerals, pulsing faintly with the same bioluminescent lichen that rims the central vent. Men patrol the scree-paths, flint-axes slung at hips—blades chipped to screaming edges from the cirque's fresh fractures—testing wards carved into low cairns, glyphs that hum faintly when hounds phase near, a vibration felt in the teeth. Children, small as the fist-sized floaters they mimic in play, dart between boulders on sound-muffling pads of moss-wrapped feet, practicing the net-flips that snare drifting orbs for the evening stew. No shouts mark their labors; communication flows in hand-glyphs, low throat-rumbles that mimic the wind's keen, ensuring the basin's silence remains unbroken lest it rouse the deeper predators from the mist-veils.

Yet the Veilborn do not dwell alone; they are but one thread in the cirque's thrumming web, outnumbered tenfold by the native strangeness that claims this aerie as birthright. Closest kin to their hearths are the spectral hounds—packs of five or seven, sleek as dissolving smoke, their pelts a roil of vapor that shifts from pearl-gray to stone-shadow, edges feathering into nothingness. Larger than mountain foxes, with muzzles elongated to vapor-jaws lined in teeth like frost-needles, they pad the interstitial plains on paws that drink vibration, leaving no print, no sound. Born from the fumarole's warmer exhalations, they whelp in crevices rimed with lichen, pups emerging as wisps that solidify over moonless cycles, learning to phase through slabs thinner than a handspan—slipping into stone as if it were mist, re-emerging with the wriggle of ensnared crawlers clutched in jaws that phase shut like condensing fog. The Veilborn revere and revile them: offerings of bloated floaters left at pack-trails to draw hunts away from tuber-plots, while axes gleam ever-ready for the rare hound that hungers for warmer prey.

Higher in the churn dwell the arboreal crawlers, ambulatory vines birthed from shrub-groves where roots drink deep from aquifers. Six-limbed grotesques, torsos thick as a child's thigh armored in bark-scales veined with sap that glows faint green in the underglow, they propel via fringed tendrils—dozens uncoiling like lure-threads from a fisher's net, quivering to mimic dew-drips and ensnare the iridescent beetles that scuttle the fern-fronds. Their hunts are a slow seduction: tendrils arch and pulse, drawing prey into a maw hidden beneath bark-flaps, where mandibles grind silently to pulp. Reproduction blooms in grotesque profusion—egg-sacs swelling like tubers amid the moss, bursting to release hatchlings that climb walls via sucker-pores, evading the thread-like predators that uncoil from adjacent fronds like animated veins, whip-thin hunters with barbed tips that inject paralytics distilled from mist-condensates.

Commanding the air-currents are the floaters, fist-sized orbs of quivering jelly buoyed by internal gas-bladders that inflate with thermal updrafts from the fumarole. Translucent husks veined in azure, trailing nematocyst whips that spark blue-silent against wing or carapace, they cluster in drifts of twenty or more, adhering to sheer granite via inverse adhesion—bodies repelled upward by the rock's mineral charge, clinging like inverted dew until hunger bids them loose. Predated upon by the featherless gliders—leathery pteroids the size of hawks, their hides stretched taut over cartilage frames, eyes like smoked quartz gleaming from membranous cowls—they wheel in lazy spirals, dipping soundlessly to snatch floaters mid-drift, beaks serrated for puncturing bladders and guzzling the helium-sweet innards. Gliders nest in arête-crevices, laying clutches of leathery ovoids that hatch into wind-riding fledglings, their first flights a desperate evasion of hound-packs below.

Micro-life saturates the seams: iridescent beetles, jewel-backed with elytra that shimmer false rainbows to dazzle thread-predators; the threads themselves, nematode-worms coiled in moss-veins, erupting to lasso beetle-legs; fungi that pulse in dim symbiotes with moss, dissolving husks into nutrient-veils. Even the vegetation stirs sentient—ferns retracting fronds in ripple-waves when crawlers near, shrubs exhaling mild toxins that haze the air and deter glider-stoops, roots questing like tendrils to throttle intruding beetle-nests.

Interdependence binds them all in silent frenzy: Veilborn nets harvest floaters to bait crawler-traps, whose silk yields thread for nets anew; hound-scat fertilizes tuber-soil, glider-feathers fletch child-darts for beetle-hunts; decay cycles whirl ceaselessly, beetle-husks mulching moss that cushions human steps. At the fumarole's heart, convergence reigns—warm exhalations drawing floaters into dense clouds, crawlers draping the rim with tendril-veils, hounds circling the azure glow, gliders stooping from the fog, Veilborn murmuring rites as they tap the steam for cooking-pits. Here, in the cirque's bounded pulse, they live—human and other, predator and prey—in a hush that amplifies every evasion, every quiet kill. They evolve as one: a Veilborn child might dream of phasing like a hound, a crawler-tendril mimic the curve of a flung net, mist weaving their fates into the stone's young indifference. The cirque endures, its dwellers its breath, thriving in the perpetual half-light where strangeness is sustenance, silence the grandest roar.
