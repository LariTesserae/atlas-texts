In the hush of this eternal shelf, where the granite breathes damp equilibrium, they dwell—not as intruders but as extensions of the stone itself, their forms and rhythms carved from the same unyielding patience. They are the Endurers, a people whose lineage traces back to the glacier's gouge, their bodies low and broad, hunched like the prostrate shrubs, skin weathered to a uniform slate-gray patina that shifts subtly with the mist's caress, rough-textured as lichen-flecked basalt, beaded eternally with condensation that neither evaporates nor drips. No taller than a child of the lowlands, they move with the creep of erosion—deliberate, infinitesimal steps that span hours, feet splayed wide on the gravel paths, soles callused into the shape of the packed stone, leaving no print but a faint warmth that lingers like hearth-glow.

Their numbers match the hovels: two dozen souls, or perhaps fewer, for they blur at the edges, merging with shadows in the wide expanses between dwellings. Men and women indistinguishable save for the subtle swell of hips or the angle of shoulders, all hairless and unadorned, scalps gleaming wetly under the diffused light, eyes pale as the alpine grass—milky quartz orbs that fix on nothing, seeing the haze's infinite layers rather than horizons. Breath fogs from nostrils flared wide to capture the thin air, each exhalation a measured plume that joins the mist, inhalation drawing in the scent of crushed moss and verdigris as if tasting the mountain's marrow. They do not speak; their communication is the *plink* of a tool set down, the faint *hiss* of a shared ember stirred, gestures as slow as the seep from fissures—fingers extended like bone awls, tracing patterns in the dew that evaporate into memory.

Life unfolds in suspended rituals, enacted without haste or end. One pauses at a communal slab, her hands—nails chipped to flint edges—grinding seeds from the wiry grass tufts into meal that never diminishes, the stone pestle circling in eternal arcs, muscles taut but unstrained, veins threading blue beneath the gray skin like quartz inclusions. Nearby, a figure shapes clay from the fractured basins, thumbs pressing ridges that harden instantly in the chill, forming vessels brimmed with unboiled seep-water, set aside mid-form to await a purpose forever deferred. Within the hovels, they cluster around the eternal embers—naked forms radiating faint heat, backs to the walls, knees drawn to chests, eyelids half-mast in a trance that blurs wakefulness and stone-sleep. No coupling disrupts the stasis, yet bellies swell periodically with the damp's gift, births silent and unheralded, the newborn emerging slick and still, eyes opening to the haze as if remembering the womb's equilibrium.

Their sustenance is the essence: they sip from the pools, tongues lapping the mineral tang, chew the tough sedge cushions raw, fibers grinding between molars worn flat as the plateau's shelf. Flesh sustains without waste—no excrement mars the paths, bodies metabolizing all into the gray patina that thickens over centuries, turning elders into near-statues, seated at thresholds with tools clutched mid-motion, chests rising once per "day" in rhythm with the mist's arrival. Fauna defers to them as they to it; the ptarmigan pecks unmolested at their leavings, ravens drop lichen from on high as unwitting tribute. They hunt not, for need dissolves in the balance—copper blades green and unused, adzes hung like relics.

Emotions etch faintly: a serenity carved deep, brows smoothed by millennia, lips parted in perpetual near-smile, as if beholding the sun's pale disc through eternal veils. No fear of the ragged drop-off, for they perch there in contemplation, legs dangling over fog, balance innate as the rounded edges. Joy flickers in the stir of embers, a communal *creak* of timber as they shift in unison. Sorrow? Buried in the unyielding stone, perhaps, or transcended—the hamlet their ossuary and cradle, deaths as imperceptible as births, forms reclining into walls, merging until only the warmth remains.

They are the monument's pulse: sparse, implied in every tool's pause, every dew-bead's pattern, every hearth's glow. Wander among them, and they acknowledge without startle—a slow tilt of head, a hand extended to share unboiled water from a pot, inviting you into the inertia. Touch their skin, and it yields like damp moss, cool yet vital, promising endurance without end. Here, they are fully themselves—humanity distilled to stone's whisper, lives enacted in the unhurried now, where change is the myth of lesser altitudes, and the mountain's flank beats with their unchanging hearts.
