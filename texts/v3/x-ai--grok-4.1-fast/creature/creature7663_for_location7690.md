In the breathing hollow of the Burrowmarket, life does not merely endure—it architects itself into guilds and wards, a teeming commonwealth of the sightless and the subtle, where every scuttle and slither writes the law of the damp dark. They are the Troglodytes of the sump, not a single folk but a congress of kin: arthropod herdsmen, glider-priests, salamander-oracles, and rodent-scribes, their bodies pale as pearl-fungus or slick as root-sap, forged in the cold forge of eternal twilight. No upright walkers claim dominion here; the builders—those vanished hominids of bone-awl and resin-mortar—have dissolved into the weave, their husks feeding the mycelium that now binds the walls, their echoes rippling in the memory-pools like half-remembered hymns. What lives now is post-human, post-plant even: a feral parliament pulsing in symbiosis with the living town, their numbers swelling to fill the voids, turning sparse alleys into thoroughfares of furred flanks and chitinous clatter.

Foremost among them swarm the Isopod Cattle-Lords, squat barrel-beasts the length of a man's forearm, their segmented shells etched with iridescent scars from ancient molts, rolling in thunderous herds across fungal pastures that carpet the plaza. They graze in unison, mandibles shearing bioluminescent spores with a wet schick-schick, their guild-wards clustered around the central sump like bellows expanding and contracting. Dumb in seeming, yet cunning in herd-mind: a pheromone congress elects the lead-roll, the alpha-isopod whose armored curve parts the dew-moss first. They trade not with voice but with cast-off exuviae—empty shells bartered to web-weavers for silken lassos, or crushed into powder for glider-nests. Disturb their graze, and they unfurl en masse, a thousand legs questing blindly, expelling clouds of defensive spores that sting like frostbite and glow azure to summon predators-of-predators.

Skittering in their wake come the Pseudoscorpion Shepherds, fist-sized arachnids with pedipalps like ivory tongs, herding the cattle through the vine-alleys with pincer-nips and venom-tugs. Vermilion-eyed despite the dark, they navigate by vibration and chemoscent, their guild perching on prop-root buttresses overlooking the pools. Masters of the micro-canyon paths, they etch toll-routes into the talus with acid-etches, demanding tribute: a beetle-carcass dropped from a glider's dive, or a moth-wing plucked from the clouds. Their young cluster in brood-sacs woven from fern-fronds, pulsing with shared heartbeats, and in the quiet hours, they "sing" subsonically—pedipalps clacking in rhythms that sync the isopod herds to the drip-plink of the ceiling. Fierce barterers, they duel over sump-rights with lightning grabs, victors claiming the choicest dew-pools where reflections whisper of the old builders, granting visions that guide migrations through the root-tunnels.

From the rafters descend the Glider-Bats, furred specters with wing-membranes tacky as tree-frog pads, launching in silent parabolas to cull beetle-swarms mid-flight. Naked-tailed and keeled, their bodies hum with stored cold-fire from fungal meals, echoloc pulses weaving through the hush like invisible nets. Their guild-roosts hollow the polliwog-fern trunks, aqueducts diverted to mist their leathery young, who dangle head-down, jaws agape for regurgitated payloads. As the priestly caste, they orchestrate the cascades: a diver's kill dropped precisely into a web-pit draws the thread-legged ambushers, whose struggles summon salamanders from below, the cycle's bounty redistributed via carcass-markets where pheromones advertise "fresh kill: one glider-beetle, uncrushed." Lone gliders patrol the smoke-holes, tasting the rare updraft for lunar cues that quicken their biolum-lures—throat-glows flaring to hypnotize rodent-kin into sacrificial drifts.

Beneath the squelch lurk the Blind Salamanders of the Oracle Pools, undulant sovereigns of the subsurface, their milk-white bodies coiling five feet in sinuous loops, jaws yawning to engulf larval fish or fallen comrades. Gilled and lunged, they prophesy in the memory-waters, effigies forming in their lens-pools: ghostly builders hammering flint, or future quakes cracking the schist. Their guild lairs thread the aquifer, blind eyespots pulsing to the sap-flow of the root-mats, and they spawn in ritual frenzies post-molt, gelatinous wriggles bartered upward as "live-tribute" to scorpions for safe passage over pool-edges. Predatory yet symbiotic, they cull the weak, their waste enriching the moss that feeds all; a salamander's belch ripples vibrations that warn of raptor-insect raids, scythe-limbs flashing from ambush.

Weaving through it all dart the Rodent-Scribes, sleek as wet otters, hairless hides gleaming with dew, their whisker-arrays mapping the gloom finer than any light. Pack-runners of the root-tunnels, they nip fern-sprouts for communal silos, evading raptor-insects with corkscrew dodges and false-trail urine-marks. Their guild burrows mimic the huts—dome-nests of gnawed osier, etched with claw-scratches that record the trades: tallies of carcasses swapped, molt-cycles predicted. Nimble thieves, they pilfer pitcher-plant nectars for guild-feasts, their chitter suppressed to whispers that blend with the hush. Hybrids of cunning, they "read" the warped reflections, whispering interpretations to glider-priests during parleys at the sump-plaza.

Lesser legions fill the gaps: Wingless Moth Clouds, spectral pollen-bearers drifting guild-to-guild with spore-messages, preyed on by Thread-Legged Weavers—spindly horrors with hypodermic fangs, their vine-webs strung like market-stalls laden with desiccated prizes. Raptor-Insects stalk the edges, scythe-limbs mimicking thorn-thatch, while larval fish silver the pools in desperate schools, feeding the cycle's base. All hybrid in the weirdness: arthropods dream in afterimages, glimpsing builder-ghosts; salamanders hybridize with isopod spawn, birthing chitin-gilled whelps; time blurs their motions into lingering trails, so a glider's dive seems eternal, a scorpion's grasp prophetic.

This is their agora, the Burrowmarket's breath: guilds convening at dusk-cycles marked by fungal blooms, bartering in pheromone script across the plaza, a hush broken by the slurp of deals sealed—carcass exchanged for web-shield, spore for vision. Predation is partnership, scarcity sacrament; they pulse as one, the town their carapace, the dark their cloister. Intrude, and the hush thickens, eyespots flare unseen, the sump-plaza empties to ambush-readiness. They watch not with sight, but with the world's own wound-flesh, eternal dwellers of the seep.
