In the weeping heart of this stoneborn plateau, life does not erupt or scurry; it *seeps*. It is not flesh-quick or root-greedy, but a slow crystallization from the mineral marrow, birthed in the perpetual interface of damp slab and luminous vapor. The first inhabitants are the **veinweavers**—not beasts, but filamentous congregations of silica-sheathed hyphae, kin to the fungal tufts but vaster, more deliberate. They thread the copper-wired grout lines like living solder, pale as quartz milk, pulsing faintly with bioluminescent spores that catch the twin suns' shafts and refract them into ghost-prisms along the walls. A single veinweaver spans plazas, its body a network of 0.1-millimeter filaments braiding into cords thick as wrists where moisture pools deepest; they drink the seeping aquifers not for sustenance alone, but to etch their own geometries into the stone—micro-spirals mirroring the gutters, grids that align with the friezes. Motion is tidal: over hours, they extend pseudopods into the mist, harvesting airborne particulates of calcium efflorescence, retracting at the suns' zenith when light would desiccate their sheaths. Their "songs" are subsonic thrums, the very groans of thermal expansion amplified, calling kin from deeper fissures to converge in the unlit defiles. Sterile? No—their spores drift in the haze, germinating only where brine-scent thickens, spawning clutch-mats that bloom turquoise filigrees identical to the oxidized copper, indistinguishable until they twitch.

Deeper in the labyrinth, where corridors narrow to shoulder-width and walls lean with that fractional wrongness, dwell the **mistflits**. These are vaporous herd-forms, condensates given volition: colonies of aerogel microbes, translucent as the perpetual fog, aggregating into fist-sized orbs that bob along the languid currents. Born of the air's 80% saturation cooling against stone, they metabolize dissolved minerals exhaled from the weeping basalt—sucking iron from rust-veins, exhaling it as ruby motes that patina the balconies. A flit-herd numbers in the uncountable, parting reluctantly as one passes, reforming with a chill kiss on exposed skin; their "bodies" are 99% structured water, prismatic skins refracting the crystalline light into rainbows that dance across the ziggurats. They do not flee light but court it, drifting upward to the flat observatories where twin-solar glare evaporates the weak, concentrating the hardy into denser swarms. Predation here is osmotic: stronger flits absorb the dilute, swelling to softball girth before fragmenting in the night's thickening mist, dispersing progeny that slosh faintly in the cistern echoes. No wings, no eyes—navigation by infrasound, bending through the delayed acoustics like sonar ghosts, mapping the fractal symmetries with eerie precision.

Yet these are but the medium, the broth from which grander forms distill. At the scale of amphitheaters and sub-level catwalks, the **dripformants** reign—amphibious accretions, lithic polyps that crown the basins and fissures. Imagine coral but petrified in ascent: bulbous nuclei of compacted mycelia and microbial mats, armored in calcium blooms harvested from the efflorescing ledges, growing at millimeters per season into squat pagodas six feet tall. A mature dripformant pulses with the plateau's rhythm, its surface a mosaic of fitted scales mimicking the ground slabs, veined with repurposed copper that now conducts bioelectric twitches—faint glints syncing to the water table's pulses. They "feed" sessile, funnelling rivulets through grooved maws that spiral logarithmically inward, extracting solutes to extrude new tiers; their offspring are pearl-like nodules, nudged by peristaltic waves into the gutters for transport to new plazas. In the hush, they resonate: hollow cores amplify the metronomic plinks into choral drips, a percussive liturgy that stirs the veinweavers to weave tighter, the mistflits to swarm in ecstatic vortices. Rare cataclysms—mist-thickening dusks or aquifer surges—birth the wanderers: adolescent dripformants that detach, rolling on pseudopodal bases through corridors, trailing slime-trails that etch primitive motifs deeper, until they reroot in virgin crevices.

Rarer still, in the nested voids beneath cantilevered architraves, lurk the **resonants**—the plateau's apex anomalies, emergent from the weirdness itself. These are not organic in full, but symbiotes: veinweaver cores encased in hollowed stone husks, animated by symbiotic mistflit swarms that inflate and deflate the shell like bellows. Humanoid in vague silhouette—three feet tall, faceted like the towers, with lever-holed limbs—they "walk" in glacial shuffles, one step per hour, driven by the stone's latent vibrations. Their eyes are geode fissures, glinting with internal prisms; mouths, grooved channels that sip condensation, exhaling brine-scented fog. They maintain the place: chiseling micro-fissures with adze-like spurs to guide water flows, burnishing copper veins to restore their hum, aligning leaning walls by infinitesimal nudges. Solitary, they haunt the sub-structures, echoing the vanished laborers—perhaps their descendants, or the construct's self-repairing will made manifest. Encounters are omens: a resonant pauses mid-stride if detected, its core-thrum syncing to one's heartbeat, offering a crystal shard from its torso—spore-laden, a seed of the plateau's ecology.

No invasion of fur or scale disrupts this chorus; life here is the imposed world incarnate, mineral and moist, evolving through deposition as the edifice itself. The veinweavers bind, mistflits diffuse, dripformants accrue, resonants tend—a fractal populace mirroring the city's scales, patient under the glaring skies. They do not conquer the sterility; they *are* its sovereign hush, emerging unbidden from slab and seep, waiting not for animation but perpetuating it in damp, bright eternity. To linger is to feel their subtle claim: a filament brushing ankle, a flit-cool on the neck, the dripformant's patter quickening, as if the plateau breathes you into its rhythm.
