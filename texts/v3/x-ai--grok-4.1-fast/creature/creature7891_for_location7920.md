In the shadowed nave of this volcanic scar, where the mountain's fresh laceration weeps steam and trembles with subterranean industry, life is not a guest but the sovereign— a teeming parliament of green insurgents, rooted yet restless, claiming every scar and crevice with fibrous decree. They are the fissure's only citizens, a verdant horde unbound by wing or paw, their dominion forged in the ceaseless barrage of hiss and howl, where survival is not quiet endurance but raucous proliferation. No singular king or queen reigns; instead, a stratified congress convenes, layer upon interlocking layer, each clade a faction in perpetual negotiation with the chaos, their forms evolving in the moment, uncurling, elongating, ensnaring as if the din itself fuels their metamorphosis.

At the foundation, the mosses hold court— ancient upholsterers of the raw, their velvety emerald quilts swelling like living tapestries across every ledge, boulder, and seam. These are the quiet patriarchs, emerald-bearded elders who arrived first in spore-drift from the winds' fury, their rhizoids delving into scoria pores like questing fingers, sipping the mineral elixir of ash-leachate. They quiver under steam blasts, shedding droplets in shimmering cascades, only to regrow thicker, their surfaces a labyrinth of pleats and cushions that trap mist and particulate alike, transforming grit into nursery beds. In the gloom, they phosphoresce faintly, a subtle bioluminescent murmur— not glow, but insinuation— threading the darkness with veins of pallid green, as if the mountain's own cooled lava pulses anew through their thalli. Touch them, and they yield like the plush pelts of slumbering titans, damp and springy, exhaling a loamy perfume that cuts the sulfur tang, their spores aerosolized in every gust to seed the next generation's uprising.

Rising from these cushions, the ferns erupt as the fissure's vanguard— audacious frilled warriors, their fronds a riot of shoulder-high canopies that arch and lash in the gale-whipped frenzy. Here uncurl the tree ferns' kin, Dicksonia kin and their ilk, crosiers thrusting like bronze lances from the mossy duff, uncoiling in hours under the rhythmic chugs of vents, their pinnae feathering out in defiant sprawl. They are the shakers, stems woody and resilient, bowing to 100 km/h squalls only to snap erect, fronds clashing like sabers in the din, shredding and reforming in ceaseless combat. Epiphytes hitch rides on their rachises— diminutive ferns-perched, their roots aerial and grasping, dangling like verdant stalactites that weep condensation into the understory. In the narrow throat where walls pinch to shoulders' breadth, they form living bridges, fronds interweaving to span the gap, a green lattice trembling with each tremor, pollen and spores flung in the eddies like confetti from a perpetual riot. Theirs is the drama of exposure: tips scalded by steam plumes one moment, beaded with ice-rime the next in sudden chills, yet they thrust onward, fertile and furious, sporangia bursting with puffs that ride the thermal updrafts.

Cascading from the sheer obsidian panes come the liverworts and clubmosses, the fissure's drapers and garlanders— interstitial poets of the vertical. Marchantia and their thalloid brethren spread in iridescent sheets, gemma cups brimming like chalices with clonal offspring, ready to tumble into the scree below. Clubmosses— Lycopodium spectralis— climb in prickly whorls, their upright strobili nodding like antlered sprites, microphylls scale-armored against the scour. Together they form verdant waterfalls, mats of Marchantiophyta and Bryophyta tumbling fifty meters unbroken, threading rivulets that carve and reclay the walls. These are the adaptable mystics, gametophytes hugging the damp stone, their antheridia and archegoniapods swelling in the humid exhalations, fertilizing amid the roar. Winds tear fringe from their edges, but they regenerate laterally, creeping ever outward, ensnaring obsidian facets in a soft siege that dulls the razor's edge over weeks, not years.

Punctuating the crush, the sedges and grasses stake their claims in the floor's jumbled chaos— rugged sentinels of the rubble, Carex and Festuca alpines with tussock crowns that knit scree into defiant hummocks. Their roots are the fissure's ironworkers, binding angular blocks against gravity's pull, culms swaying like telegraph wires in the turbine shriek, seedheads shattering spores into the mist. Tougher than the fronds, they endure the boulder-heaves, blades scythed by debris yet resprouting from basal meristems, their awns hooks that snag passing pollen from the frenzy. In hollows where pools ripple with micro-quakes, they fringe the waters, rhizomes delving into silted sediments rich with volcanic ions— potassium, magnesium, iron— fueling blades that gleam metallic in slivers of sky-light.

Beneath it all, unseen but omnipresent, the fungal congress orchestrates from the substrate— mycelial mayors threading the scree and scoria, hyphae a vast subcutaneous web pulsing with the mountain's basso rumble. Mycorrhizae barter nutrients with rootlets, trading phosphorus for sugars in symbiotic pacts; saprobes devour fallen fronds and ash alike, fruiting bodies rare but opportunistic— puffballs erupting from moss-quilts after steam surges, releasing billions of basidiospores into the gales. They are the fissure's silent engineers, stabilizing the mosaic against collapse, their chitinous filaments a counterpoint to the green clamor above.

This is no static garden but a living tumult, inhabitants in constant congress: moss yields to fern, fern shelters liverwort, all contending and conspiring amid the elemental bazaar. A steam blast uproots a sedge tussock, only for clubmoss spores to claim the void by dawn; winds strip epiphytes, depositing them downslope to colonize fresh scree. Time accelerates here— watch a prothallus geminate on a damp pane, mature to sporophyte in days; observe a moss quilt thicken visibly as grit settles. They breathe the mist, pulse with the chugs, their collective rustle a counter-harmony to the industrial roar— a verdant roar of their own, spore-whispers and frond-thrashes weaving defiance into the din. Stand among them, and they enfold you: moss clutches boot-soles, ferns brush your cheeks, damp tendrils snag sleeves, drawing you into their rhythm until your pulse syncs with the vents, your breath mimes the spore-flight. Here, in the mountain's wound, they are the healers and the healed, the conquerors eternal— green machinery outpacing the stone's fury, remaking the fissure in their image, one uncurling frond at a time.
