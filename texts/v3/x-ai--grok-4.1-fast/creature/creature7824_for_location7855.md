Not the fragile moss of sunlit glades, these are armored phalanxes: tufted hummocks dense as sodden velvet, probing with rhizoids into cracked concrete and rusted welds, halting the grind of flywheels not with force but with the sheer mass of their advance—a millimeter per dawn, inexorable as tide. They thrive in the steam-belch and drip, sporangia swelling like pearl blisters under the fungal glow, releasing clouds of invisible progeny that settle and root in the perpetual damp. Clustered around boiler-houses, they fur the brick like living insulation, muffling the deep-throated roars into a contented purr, their surfaces pebbled with gem-like water droplets that quiver with each vibration. From the ventilator grates and workbench clutter burst the **fern-heralds**, sentinels of arching elegance amid the industrial ossuary. Their fronds uncoil in crozier fists, spiraling open to form vaulted parasols over half-assembled valves and stamped widgets—fronds feathered in fine pinnae that splay like peacock tails, edges crisped by the mineral tang in the air. They favor the edges of the marsh, roots delving through paver gaps to sip from the cold shallows, fronds nodding in the languid convection currents, brushing against conveyor belts to dislodge rust-flakes that nourish the detritus below. These aerial aristocrats sway gently on their fibrous moorings, tendrils questing downward to snag passing vapor, their presence turning the ceiling into a suspended jungle—drips cascading from leaf-tip to leaf-tip in silver chains, irrigating the realm below.
