In this ferroconcrete womb, life clings not in abundance but in exquisite, attenuated thrift—forms pared to essences by the dual blades of heat and shadow, each organism a specialist in the vault's unyielding niche, sustained by the slow alchemy of rust, drip, and subterranean pulse. No dominion of predator or prey disrupts the equipoise; instead, a scattered congress of adapted oddities persists, their cycles synced to the chamber's own rhythms: the hourly hiss of vents, the millimeter creep of corrosion, the faint convection that stirs vapors like a mechanical breath. They are the heirs of the builders' toxins—engineered, mutated, or opportunistic—thriving in the dense interlace where machine and moisture conspire.

**The Lichen Veil (vegetation dominant at 1.1, engineered lineage)**  
At the base of all, the pale lichen tufts reign as territorial lords of the seams, not sprawling carpets but miserly fiefdoms: each clump a fist-sized rosette of desiccated fronds, silvery-threaded with phosphor veins that kindle to a wan teal glow (peaking at 0.3 lux in their brief circadian flare, every 28 hours). Seeded perhaps as bioremediators—air-scrubbers to metabolize the ozone tang and mineral fumes—they have speciated into a dozen micro-variants, their hyphae infiltrating weep-holes like neural filaments, drawing sustenance from the tepid oil-pools below. The *Graphidermus pallor* variant, most common, unfurls lobed thalli edged in crystalline rime, photosynthesizing feebly off the lichen-glow of kin; it exhales a faint ozone-sweetness that feeds the fungi below. Rarer is the *Ventarix symbiota*, threading copper fittings where verdigris blooms—its glow pulses in sync with steam vents, as if tasting the cycle, trading minerals for the vapor's humid kiss. They reproduce by sporangial burst: a single plink of condensation triggers spore-release, dust-motes drifting on convection currents to seed distant cracks, germinating over years into new outposts. Predated upon sparingly, they shed desiccated fronds as millipede forage, their decay accelerating the very corrosion they colonize.

**The Millipede Legions (fauna primary, *Myriapodum iridex*, abundance 0.9)**  
Pallid millipedes are the vault's restless nomads, each a living pearl-string: 40-60 segments armored in nacreous chitin that shimmers oil-slick hues—mother-of-pearl blues fracturing the gloom as they scuttle. No larger than a human thumb, they swarm in loose phalanxes of dozens, navigating the cabling undergrowth with antennae that quiver to the subsonic thrum of idling engines. Heat-adapted to 3.0+, they thermoregulate by coiling into crevices during peak fever, emerging at "dusk" (post-vent cycles) to graze lichen-fronds and lap oily shallows, their mandibles grinding crystalline efflorescences into a mineral paste. Iridescence serves dual purpose: camouflage amid the rust-patina, and a lure for sac-insect emergents, whose dim compound eyes fixate on the gleam. They mate in tangled writhes atop catwalk flanges, extruding silken egg-clusters into insulation sheaths—hatching after months into velvety miniatures that molt 17 times before maturity. Their waste, a gritty slurry, fertilizes fungi; in turn, they vector spores across the vault, unwitting pollinators in this mechanical meadow. Rare mass-migrations occur when a pool overtops (every 3-7 years), driving hordes into squeezes between vats, their multitudes clicking like castanets (briefly spiking sound to 1.4).

**Sac-Insects and Their Stillborn Broods (*Opalinum pupara*, abundance 0.7)**  
In the warm crevices of exhaust manifolds and piston housings, translucent sac-insects pupate eternally—gelid ovoids the size of quail eggs, walls veined with milk-white capillaries that pulse lazily against the heat's throb. Larvae enter as microfilariae, inhaled via vents or wafted on spore-currents, gorging on bioluminescent fungi before encysting: the sac hardens to a rubbery husk, semi-permeable, sipping manifold vapors for hydration while enzymes liquefy the host-flesh within (though no true host; they parasitize the manifold's rust-scurf). Wingless adults never fully emerge—doomed by lightlessness, they fracture the sac after 9 months, spilling vestigial forms that writhe briefly before desiccating, their chitin husks littering like spent casings. These "ghostwings" attract millipedes, perpetuating the cycle. A weirder caste, the *Vaporiform* variant, swells sacs with ferment-syrup trapped from lower vats, exhaling it as lures—sweet ghosts that draw millipedes into manifold maws, where heat desiccates the prey for fungal uptake. Reproduction is parthenogenic: sacs bud daughters internally, a clonal dynasty sustained by the vault's unchanging chemistry.

**Fungal Symbionts and Microbial Haze (*Mycelium ferruginea* network, abundance 1.2)**  
Woven deeper into the substrate, bioluminescent fungi form a subsurface realm: vein-threads no thicker than capillaries, pulsing crimson once per "dusk-cycle" (synced to lichen flares), mapping pipe-joints and weld-pocks in labyrinthine filigree. *Ferrumycetes* dominates, its mycelia infiltrating rust-lace to mine iron oxides, exhaling ferrous spores that hasten patina-blooms while sustaining the lichen above. In water pockets, they fruit into toadstool nubs—pale caps fringed with gill-ruffles, biolumescing to guide millipedes. The microbial haze completes the base trophic layer: airborne consortia of thermophiles and acetogens, thriving in the 2.8 heat, metabolizing vent-vapors into syrup-ghosts and ozone. Invisible but omnipresent (density 2.6 per cubic meter), they haze the air with a faint particulate shimmer, respiring the chamber's recirculated breath—without them, oxygen dips to lethal lows.

**Rarer Wanderers and Apex Anomalies (fauna outliers, abundance 0.4)**  
Episodic visitors punctuate the stasis: *Thermovorms*—blind, heat-seeking annelids two handspans long—erupt from floor-slags during seismic groans (once per decade), wriggling through slag-pits to devour sac-husks before burrowing back. Airborne *Dust-moths*, fragile as tissue, flit on convection drafts from upper conduits, ovipositing in insulation before starving— their scales dusting catwalks with phosphor powder. The apex rarity: *Forge-leeches*, thumb-thick slugs with heat-gills like furnace bellows, clinging to generator housings; they suck mineral-laced oils, swelling gravid before bursting into spore-clouds that seed new lichen. No larger fauna endures; the dense packing and heat cull all but these.

**The Living Machine-Echo**  
Yet life blurs into artifice: catwalks sigh organically under rare footfalls (human? scavenger droid?), load-sensors woven with fungal nerves flexing in memory; vats harbor slurry-cultures, half-alive brews fermenting eons-old syrups, bubbling with yeast-analogs that scent the vents. The engines themselves harbor endosymbionts—biofilms of heat-bacteria colonizing crank-journals, their metabolic hum subtly modulating the thrum. In this pocket-ecology, inhabitants are not conquerors but commensals, attuned to the slow: a millipede pauses at a drip's plink, lichen flares to a fungal pulse, sacs swell to vapor-sigh. Survival is attunement—ignore the rhythms, and the vault claims you as another patina bloom. Here, life is the erosion made flesh: sparse, iridescent, eternally patient.
