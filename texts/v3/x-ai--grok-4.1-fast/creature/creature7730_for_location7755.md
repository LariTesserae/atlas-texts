In the forge-breath heat of this subterranean nave, life clings, swarms, and surges not as conquerors but as inevitable tenants—opportunists etched into every crevice, every drip, every shuddering gear. They are the true citizenry, a proletariat of flesh and chitin bootstrapping their empire from the castoffs of stone and steam, their rhythms syncing to the chamber's ponderous pulse. Humans flicker at the edges, pale phantoms amid the teeming tide, but it is the beasts that own the depths, their multitudes a living fog that makes the air hum with intent.

The workers come first to mind, though they are few—dozens, maybe a hundred in the haze—gnarled figures birthed from the same raw rock, their skins etched with soot-grime and steam-blisters, lungs blackened like the pools they wade. They are the Stokers, the Bolt-Men, the Vein-Crackers: squat and barrel-chested, clad in oil-slicked leathers and aprons reinforced with chainmail scraps scavenged from the looms. Oilcloth caps perch on sweat-matted scalps, visors of hammered tin shielding eyes gone milky from arc-welder glare. They move in terse clusters, three or four to a scaffold, hoarse shouts—"Tighten that flywheel, ye slag!"—barely piercing the din, hands callused to leather from wrench-twists and hammer-blows. No children here, no elders; they breed sparingly in the upper warrens, sending down the hale for shifts that blur into days, fueled by lukewarm gruel ladled from vats steaming with fungal mash. Sleep claims them in hammocks slung from catwalk undersides, swaying to the engine-thrum, dreams laced with valve-hisses. They worship no gods, only the ore-veins—prizing glittering quartz hauls as talismans, tattooing vein-maps on forearms slick with machine oil. Yet they are harried lords, forever shoveling rat-droppings from gear-boxes, swatting cricket-swarms from lamps, their hammers pausing to crush cockroach under heel. They expand the town grudgingly, riveting new shacks into fractures, but the fauna mocks their labors, reclaiming every idle bolt overnight.

Above them, the bats rule the ceiling-veil—thousands strong, a leathern cyclone of *flap-screech-flap*, their bodies no larger than fists but fused into a single, writhing organism. Fur-matted, fang-toothed, eyes like currants gleaming in sodium-glow, they roost in limestone bosses, guano raining in pattering veils that nourish the moss-fringes below. Mating flights spiral at dusk-cycles (though no true day penetrates), males pulsing throat-sacs with ultrasonic trills that vibrate your teeth, females wheeling to select amid aerial dogfights. They dive in black arrows for the insect clouds, sonar-pinging off struts and stalactite-stubs, guts bloating on cricket-wings and moth-scales. Pups dangle hairless from teats, mewling through fur, while elders molt in corners, wings frayed to tatters by pipe-condensate. Noisy tyrants, they foul the catwalks with droppings that corrode iron, driving workers to curse and flare torches—but the bats endure, their swarms a perpetual eclipse.

Lower still, the rats command the horizontals—pink-eyed legions, sleek as wet otters, bodies elongated for beam-sprints and puddle-wades. Not scavengers but sovereigns, they surge in pink-gray tides across flagstones and rail-tracks, *skitter-scritch-skitter*, tails whipping like live wires. Fur glossy from oil-baths, whiskers quivering to steam-currents, they gnaw cable-sheaths for copper-flecks, rear litters in rust-hollows twenty strong, pink nubs nursing amid hoarded ore-shards. Packs form hierarchies in the shadows: alphas with notched ears leading raids on ore-carts, betas wrestling in ultrasonic squeaks over prime sump-nests. They devour blindfish from pools, crack cockroach shells for marrow, even nip at Stoker ankles when hunger peaks—fever-bites swelling legs to tree-trunks. Their migrations trace the water's creep, abandoning drowned warrens for fresh fractures, empires shifting millimeters with each puddle's swell. In quiet lulls, they groom in furry huddles, eyes reflecting lamp-flickers like underworld stars.

The floors and walls birth the arthropod hordes, a chitinous underclass turned overwhelming by the heat's alchemy. Cave crickets, palm-wide and balloon-bellied, perch in crevice-choruses, legs rasping *zidd-zidd-zidd* in waves that sync to piston-clangs—a symphony of heat-spawned lust, luring mates across open spans. Antennae whip the steam for pheromones, ovipositors stabbing moss-patches to hatch nymphs that molt daily, exoskeletons cracking like eggshells. Cockroaches patrol the fringes, fist-hefty bruisers with shells iridescent as weld-sparks—midnight-blue and copper-veined, scuttling in phalanxes along shack-bases, mandibles shredding fern-tufts and belt-rubber alike. Unkillable, they survive steam-blasts flattened to pancakes, regenerating from nymphs birthed live in oil-sumps, a thousand gleaming young erupting hourly. Spiders weave the spans—thumb-sized orb-weavers draping dewy nets between struts, silks pearled with condensation, ensnaring crickets mid-rasp; trapdoor lurkers in moss-banks, fangs injecting fever-venom that liquifies prey from within. Millipedes coil in detritus drifts, centipede-legs a hundredfold, secreting iridescent slime that corrodes boot-soles, feasting on larvae hatched from vent-moths.

Water claims its court: pale blindfish, eel-slender and translucent, flop in mirror-pools, gill-flaps gasping humid air when levels drop, jaws grinding detritus and dropped rivets. They spawn in gelatinous clumps amid puddle-edges, fry darting like silver needles until rats pluck them. Lizards skitter the machine-legs—emaciated geckos with suction-pads clinging to rivets, tongues flicking for moth-wings; broader skinks burrowing under flagstones, tails regenerating from cat-nips. From steam-vents hatch the moths, powder-winged and pale, fluttering into bat-jaws; larvae writhe in gear-grease, fattening on electrical shorts.

No kings among them, only collectives—cycles interlocking like the belts overhead. Bats thin cricket swarms, rats cull roach nymphs, workers poison rat-nests (ineffectually, as survivors breed vengeful kin), spiders snare all. Predation is casual, abundant; corpses fuel the next wave, detritus layered inches deep, shoveled futilely by Stokers. Fever blooms in bites—rashes weeping pus like cave-drips, forcing quarantines in shack-corners—but survivors toughen, antibodies forging from the miasma. They adapt ceaselessly: rats growing oil-resistant fur, crickets tuning rasps to evade bat-pings, bats roosting higher as stalactites lengthen. The town swells, and so do they—new fractures birthing niche-empires, shacks infilled with nests. Linger, and you feel it: not chaos, but congress—a parliament of the fecund, pulsing in the heat, indifferent to the hammers above. This is their cathedral, the machines mere altars to their endless rite.
