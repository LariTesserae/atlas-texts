### The Bone-Weavers and Stone-Breathers

In the perpetual amber hush of this embryonic underworld, life clings and thrusts with the tenacity of roots splitting bedrock. They are not conquerors but coaxers, these dwellers—five thousand souls who have traded the sun's tyranny for the stone's indifferent embrace. Call them the Lithari, if names must be pinned like pegs to walls, though they whisper among themselves as *karn-veth*, the "vein-folk," blood pulsing through the earth's hidden arteries. Their skin gleams with a perpetual pallor, not the milk-white of shut-ins but a subtle pearlescence, honed by generations in the misted glow: cheeks dusted with limestone flour that cakes into fine masks during workshifts, eyes wide and reflective like polished quartz, adapted to parse shadows within shadows. Hair grows thick and wiry, often bound in coils with bone clasps or dusted with ochre pigments scraped from vein-walls—blacks shot through with fungal silver, braids heavy with embedded quartz shards that chime softly in the damp drafts.

The miners lead the rhythm, broad-shouldered figures with hands callused into paddles, fingers splayed from years gripping bronze-tipped picks that spark like captive stars against schist. They move in crews of ten, rotating through the chisel-choirs: *plink-thud-plink*, the heartbeat of expansion, widening a tunnel mouth or squaring a new gallery. Their tunics—algae-woven, oiled against the clammy seep—hang heavy with tool-slings, pouches bulging with flint wedges and cordage spun from cricket-silk. Faces bear the scars of close calls: a puckered line from a settling ceiling, a milky eye from quartz dust, yet their laughter rasps low during rests, tales swapped around hearth-glow of the time Old Vrenna wrestled a cave-in back into place with her bare mallet. Women and men alike delve, bodies lean but dense with muscle earned hauling rubble-sledges, their breaths syncing with the stone's groans—inhale on the upswing, exhale as chisel bites.

Crafters claim the forge-quarters and fungal galleries, a subtler tribe within the crush. Smiths, soot-streaked wraiths with arms corded like flowstone draperies, hunch over anvils of hollowed basalt, hammers dancing a subdued tattoo on ingots pried from shallow lodes—bronze blooming ruddy from ore-flesh, tempered in stream-water chilled to the bone. Their chants are wordless, rhythmic hums that soothe the metal's rage, invoking the old pacts with vein-spirits: "Yield, sister-stone, as we yield sweat." Potters wheel clay foot-pushed in alcoves, their children apprentices daubing beehive kilns, faces smudged with the ghosts of fired pots. Weavers perch in loft-nooks, bone needles flashing through algae-fiber, crafting cloaks that mimic the moss-scrim—camouflage for the rare surface-raids, bartered wood and gossip hauled back through shafts on rope-hoists.

Families layer the verticality: warrens stacked like termite combs, lofts curtained with hide and reed, where newborns wail muffled against the drip-plink symphony. Elders hold council in lantern-circles, their skins mapped with vein-blue tattoos recounting the Great Settling—when the first clans fled surface wars into this raw womb. Children scramble the walkways like pale crickets, legs skinny and sure-footed over abyss-drops, practicing tosses of flint-knives or herding penned insects with reed prods. Meals congeal around communal hearths: smoked cave-fish mashed with fungal paste, cricket-legs roasted crisp, slurped from reed bowls amid the sizzle of peat-smoke. Bonds form in the crush—marriages sealed by shared mallet-strikes, feuds over water-claims settled by elder arbitration, the air thick with the musk of unwashed bodies, mineral sweat, and the faint, sweet rot of fermenting algae-bread.

Yet the Lithari are not alone; the stone exhales its own brood, outliers woven into the human tapestry. Bats reign the high vaults—colonies of a hundred thousand, fur-matted husks with wings like frayed leather, leathery snouts twitching in roost-clusters that blot ceilings like living stalactites. *Squeak-flutter-whoosh*, they unravel at "dusk" (lamplight dimming), wheeling silent on hunts for the cricket-swarms that tick-hop from moss-tufts. Wing-membranes translucent in torch-glow, veined with the same pale blue as miners' tattoos, they drop guano-rain—prized fertilizer shoveled into farm-terraces, its acrid tang fueling fungal booms. Harvesters mimic their flights in reed-cradles, swaying from chains to pluck phosphorescent caps that glow like fallen stars, weaving wicks that burn slow and true.

Lower still, the translucent shrimp pulse in stream-pools, armored ghosts no longer than a thumb, blind eyes like milky pearls, legs flickering in blind frenzy. Fisher-children net them with basket-traps, their chitin-crunch a staple crunch under teeth, shells ground into lime-mortar for hovel-walls. Cave crickets, larger kin—pale ovals with legs like ivory splints—infest the moss-ledges, hopping erratic bursts that mimic distant mallets. Pens thicken them fat on fungi-scraps, their *tick-tick* a domestic underscore, silenced only in stew-pots. Even the flora lives, in its sessile way: moss-clans in emerald quilts, fronds quivering under drip-kisses; lichens, spectral crusts that elders scrape for dyes, whispering of symbiosis with the rock itself; fungi towers, pale sentinels blooming weekly in caps that bruise blue when plucked, their spores a hazy under-breath inhaled by all.

Deeper, in the forge-domes where steam seeps from hot-springs, outliers emerge stranger: blind albino worms coiling in clay-pockets, harvested for bait; quartz-mites, pinprick herds grazing mineral veins, crunched for luck-charms. Rivalries flicker—miners cursing bat-droppings that slick walkways, children tormenting crickets till wings shred—but equilibrium holds, a predator-prey waltz subdued by necessity. The Lithari speak of *the Becoming*, where human chisel meets stone's yield, birthing hybrids: a pick-handle fused with stalactite-grain, a child's tooth set in bronze like a gem. They are not static; they evolve mid-breath—tattoos mapping new tunnels, songs lengthening with each gallery claimed. Vigilance defines them: sentries post shafts against flash-floods, prophets divining quakes from drip-rhythms. In this cool, damp bastion, life is not endured but exhaled—dense, layered, forcing form from void, each soul a chisel-bite in the endless mid-birth. The cavern breathes them as they breathe it, blurring builder into built, until the line dissolves in torchlit mist.
