In the pulsing heart of this obsidian spire, where the seamount's volcanic pulse still echoes through the brine, life manifests not as hunters or herds, but as a ceaseless, self-assembling manufactory: the ferrokrill swarm, a trillion-strong legion of biomechanical smiths, each no larger than a human thumbnail yet collectively rivaling the mass of a whale. They are the seamount's first citizens, birthed from its own pyroclastic sputum—larvae hatching in the superheated vents below, ascending on thermal updrafts to colonize this vertical forge. No king rules them, no queen orchestrates; they are a democracy of gears, their behaviors emergent from electrochemical whispers rippling through the water like Morse code in mercury.

Approach the fringes, and the swarm resolves from haze into individuality. A single ferrokrill drifts into focus: its carapace a lattice of opalescent chitin, veined with iridescent nacre that fractures the intruding daylight into spectral rainbows, casting fleeting glyphs across neighboring bodies. Beneath this translucent vault, the true marvel churns—a brass-and-bone clockwork innard, gears of keratin alloy meshing with wet precision, lubricated by a viscous slurry of ferrous proteins. Bivalved bellows of striated muscle heave in counterpoint, inflating ruby-red chambers that glow with symbiotic chemiluminescence, expelling rhythmic puffs of microbubbles laced with hydrogen sulfide. Antennae, fringed like belt-feeders on an antique loom, sweep the water in languid arcs, magnetoreceptive setae snagging magnetite microcrystals and silica flocculants with unerring greed. The abdomen terminates in a tail-nozzle, a perforated spinneret that whirs softly, extruding gossamer filaments of electrodeposited iron—each thread a micron-thick wire, weaving ephemeral scaffolds that snag drifting pumice and bind it into the swarm's lattice before dissolving back into ionic soup.

But uniformity eludes them; variation proliferates like flaws in a casting. Patrol-krill, sleeker and arrow-prowed, dart along the obsidian walls, their geartrains overclocked to a frantic *tick-tick-tick*, mandibles unfolding into fractal saws that rasp away basaltic nubs, grinding them into nutrient paste for communal regurgitation. These are the masons, etching the first softening grooves into the stone's lethal edges. Nursemaids, bloated and sessile, cluster in pulsating spheres midway up the chamber—ovipositors extended like lathe-spindles, whirring out clutches of pearl-like eggs that hatch in hours, propelled by expulsion jets into the larval nursery clouds below. Sentinel-krill, paranoid and prickly, bristle with voltaic spines; brush one, and it clamps with pincer-claws, discharging a galvanic jolt—5 volts of numbing current drawn from bioaccumulated metals, threading your nerves into temporary thrall, your blood assayed for mineral yield before rejection or assimilation. And the alchemists, rarest and most baroque, bear maws like blooming orchids of enamel gears: they seize organic flotsam—a fungal spore, a perished kin—inject vesicant enzymes, and over minutes transmute it. Pumice enters soft and porous; it emerges as flawless quartz octahedrons, tumbling free to embed in the walls, seeding geodes for eons hence.

Their society pulses as one vast organism, gradients of density dictating roles. At the periphery, loose skeins undulate lazily, antennae questing for fresh ejecta from the fissures below. Density thickens toward the core, where banks compress into "factories"—boulder-sized orbs of intermeshed bodies, fins slotted like cardan shafts, transferring torque and slurry in perpetual motion. Here the symphony crescendos: the pervasive *clack-clack* of mineral-stripping gears blends with *whump-whump* bellows-thuds that propagate pressure waves you feel in your teeth; *zreeee* arcs crackle as bioelectric discharges leap between carapaces, signaling quotas met or threats detected; a bass *grrrrrum* underlays it all, the substrate drone of ovipositors and spinnerets harmonizing into infrasonic booms that resonate the obsidian itself. Spawn clouds bloom diurnally, synced to the ceiling fissure's light-pulse—expanding outward in tidal surges that scour the walls, contracting at "dusk" to recycle the cull.

No predators pierce this fortress; the swarm *is* the apex, devouring interlopers and its own senescence alike. Dead kin dissolve in communal baths of acid-spit, reduced to slurry and pumped through vein-trains for redistribution. Rare mutants arise—leviathans swelling to fist-size on unchecked ore, their geartrains fused into monolithic engines, drifting as nomadic forges that seed daughter swarms in abyssal crevices below. Symbionts hitch rides: fungal hyphae calcify into living rivets on carapaces, trading structural reinforcement for spore dispersal; micro-jellies pulse in gill-cavities, filtering toxins from the brine.

Time warps in their embrace. A ferrokrill's span is weeks—moult, gorge, manufacture, moult again—yet the swarm endures, etching millimeters from the seamount each cycle, precursors to coral or kelp in some distant maturity. They sense you not as foe but feedstock: mineral-rich blood draws them in shoals, antennae questing, nozzles probing. Resist, and the jolt integrates you; yield, and become slurry. Indifferent to the cerulean gulf above or the light shafts' tease of air, they churn on—organic pistons in creation's dawn, forging the seamount's bones from its blood, one crystalline facet at a time. This is their domain: no poetry of fin or fang, only the raw clamor of industry, alive and inexorable.
