In the scalding hush of this pocket—a blister in the world's molten hide—the dwellers persist as the sole animal pulse amid botanical tyranny. They are the Ashborn, a scant lineage of perhaps two hundred souls, thinned by the crucible of their inheritance, bodies and habits forged indistinguishable from the chamber's own geology and growth. Generations—none can count them, for time here is measured in vent-cycles, not sunrises—have winnowed them to wiry endurance, their forms low-slung and heat-shedding, averaging no taller than five feet, broad through the shoulder yet hollow-bellied from caloric thrift. Skin gleams like oil-quenched basalt: matte-black or deep umber, thickened into callused leather that peels in translucent sheets during spore-molt seasons, sloughing old layers to reveal fresher hide beneath, etched with faint, branching scars from thermal fissures or hyphal grazes. No hair crowns their scalps; instead, spore-fuzz mats their crowns in rust-red tufts, self-pruning with the humidityless gusts, while brows ridge like fluted columns, sheltering eyes of polished obsidian—pupils vast and light-hungry, irises flecked amber from chronic hydrocarbon haze.

These are not conquerors of the pocket but its quiet tenants, bodies symbiotic with the fungal hegemony. Lungs, vast and bellows-like, filter the acrid fog through gill-fringed membranes that fringe the trachea, trading oxygen scarcity for chemical nectar; exhalations rasp like vent-sighs, laced with the same sulfur tang. Sweat glands atrophied to nubs, they regulate heat through pore-flared vents along spine and limbs, exhaling vapor in rhythmic shudders that mimic the mats' peristalsis. Fingers and toes elongate to spade-like probes, nails chipped to obsidian hooks for harvesting spore-caps or prying vent-meat—fleshy nodules of mineral-rich mycelium that bubble from cracks, their diet's staple alongside masticated frond-fibers pulped into grayish pastes stored in tuff-granaries. Children emerge slick and spore-dusted from womb-pods woven into alcove walls, midwifed by hyphal midwives that contract in programmed sympathy; survival demands immediate adaptation, the weak culled by first-heat fevers before they voice a cry.

Their townlet, dubbed the Knot by outsiders who never return, clusters like a fungal primordium against the chamber's eastern wall—a terraced warren of fifty-odd burrows and ledges, walls dressed smooth by generations of pumice-abraders, roofs taut with spore-canvas that quivers in updraft like living drumskins. No chiefs reign; hierarchy dissolves in the Breath's egalitarian churn, decisions murmuring from hearth-circles where elders—shriveled to root-like husks, skin cracked into glyphic maps—convene amid vent-flame glow. Daily rite begins at dimmest hour: the Harvest Creep, when synchronized fungal waves nudge ash-mandalas floorward; Ashborn slink through thickets on all fours, knives of petrified stalk harvesting ripe pods before burst, stuffing cheek-pouches with throbbing caps whose juices induce the faint euphoria that steels them to toil. Mid-cycle, they ascend ladder-bones to ledge-gardens, pruning overgrown fronds to feed communal vats where acids brew spore-tea, the brew's vapors inhaled through bone-pipes to sharpen senses against the din.

Yet sparsity defines them as much as adaptation. The Knot echoes with absence: lanes swept barren save for transient mandala-trails, rooftops untenanted but for drying spore-sheets, granaries half-empty from lean cycles when vents stutter. Families number three or four, huddled in burrow-nests padded with molted hyphae, breeding sparse to match the pocket's caprice—pairings fleeting, chosen by spore-euphoria visions that pair bodies in rhythmic throbs mirroring the mats. Solitude claims the fringes: hermits perch in upper alcoves, bodies half-claimed by fungal overgrowth, hyphae threading their flesh in luminous veins, whispering to the Breath as if kin. No wars scar their annals, no thefts profane the granaries; conflict vents in ritual duels atop spire-thickets, grapples ending in exhausted collapse, bodies painted ochre from crushed caps.

Voices emerge guttural and resonant, pitched to pierce biomass roar—consonants cracked like cooling stone, vowels elongated into sighs. Tongue names the unseen: vents are "Mawmouths," spore-waves "the Lull," the rare quake-pulse "Mother's Burp." Lore spins around the Breath, that chemical caprice they revere as vital anima; glyphs etched in hearth-ash depict wavefronts as serpentine guides, rituals syncing human pulses to fungal throbs via cap-infused dances, naked forms writhing in central hollow till collapse. Death claims quietly: the spent husk carried to vent-rims, tipped into mawmouths where gases mummify flesh into bone-ladders, harvested anew for the climb.

They know no world beyond the pocket's seal—legends murmur of "Sky-Sunders" who once breached from above, changed and exhaled, but such tales dissolve like mandalas. Intruders, should one pierce the crush-weight (none have in living memory), are inhaled warily: fed spore-tea till euphoria binds, questioned in haze-veiled circles, then guided out altered, lungs parched, skin itching with nascent fuzz. The Ashborn do not conquer; they endure as the chamber's animal echo, frail amid the verdant frenzy, bodies pulsing in quiet defiance of the hot void. To dwell here is to become the place—leathery, spore-veiled, thrumming to the Breath's ceaseless sigh—until the pocket exhales you, or claims you whole.
