In the marrow of this sinkhole metropolis, where chitin veins pulse against the regolith like capillaries denied blood, the true denizens stir—not as discrete beasts, but as a congress of fluids and filaments, architects without number or name. They are the **chthonic extruders**, a phylum unbound by symmetry, birthed from seismic sputum in the fracture's infancy. Imagine them first as smears: translucent gels the hue of diluted bone-marrow, oozing from substrate pores no wider than a eyelash. These are the **progenitors**, unicellular leviathans—each a trillion-souled polity—swelling with vacuoles of supercooled brine that gleam like trapped stars. They do not crawl; they *flow*, pseudopodia questing in fractal sprays, tasting the air's mineral desiccation, sampling ions with membranous tongues that ripple and retract. A single progenitor spans a hand's breadth when roused, but they aggregate in silence, membranes kissing to birth chimeric masses: the **colony-veins**, roiling superorganisms that snake through the city's undercroft, meters long, their surfaces dimpling with phagocytic mouths that engulf errant spores or scuttler offal.

These colony-veins are the unseen artisans, their labor etched in the towers' slow ascent. Watch one breach a facade: it extrudes a proboscis of hardening resin, spiraling skyward in meticulous helices, calcifying mid-growth into facet-sharp chitin. The air shivers with their exhalations—microplumes of anhydrous vapor, laced with siliceous spores that drift like invisible pollen, germinating on contact to bootstrap new struts. They hunger not for flesh but for structure: devouring their own shed husks, mineralizing them into load-bearing spines, recycling the city into ever-grander ziggurats. In quiescence, they harden to translucent plugs, indistinguishable from the walls themselves; roused by seismic tremor or chemical cue, they melt into pseudopodial frenzy, reshaping cloisters in waves that propagate like peristalsis across the basin. Touch their living mass, and it adheres—tendrils probing your skin's salts, a chemistry of curiosity that lingers like an uninvited dream, imprinting your volatiles into their next extrusion.

Symbiotic with them drift the **spore-motes**, not mere ejecta but larval nomads: airborne cysts no larger than dust, propelled by gas-bursts from apical vents. They cascade in currents imperceptible, settling to burrow into regolith or chitin-cracks, pupating into fungal blooms that crisp and slough within hours, seeding fresh progenitors. Predated upon yet perpetuating, they form the cycle's cursive script: devoured by scuttlers, their husks calcify into the extruders' mortar.

The scuttlers themselves—those pale ratlings with oil-slick carapaces—are interlopers elevated to citizenry, **silicon-jackers** scavenging the city's leavings. Six-limbed, their legs end in van der Waals pads that whisper across voids, never slipping in the sparse air. Eyeless, they navigate by vibration: the chitin-choir's hum resolving into a bas-relief map in their vibroreceptors, steering them to fissures where progenitors leak nutrient-rich dribble. They hunt in solos, mandibles shearing microscopic prey—mote-clusters or worm-bulges—with a precision that echoes the city's geometries. Mating is a frenzy of adhesive rut, carapaces fusing briefly before molting mirrored shards that mineralize into facade filigree, co-opted by the extruders below. Wingless moths, **ash-drift specters**, haunt the cathedral voids: diaphanous husks propelled by static charges between towers, siphoning volatiles from lichen-hyphae. They flocculate in swarms during "cycles"—rare flurries when colony-veins burp gas-plumes—alighting to oviposit in vents, their larvae pseudopodia-mimics that burrow inward, sustaining the builders as unwitting tenders.

Deeper still, in brine-threads webbing the substrate, dwell the **hydrophytes**: wormkin swollen with osmotic defiance, elongating to tenfold their girth before rupturing in salt-crystals that seed efflorescences. They bulge floors like slow earthquakes, devoured mid-eruption by scuttlers, their innards fueling the foodweb's base.

No sovereign rules this demesne; hierarchy emerges in flows. Progenitors birth colonies, colonies sculpt the city, the city filters lives into niches—scuttlers patrol peripheries, moths aerate voids, hydrophytes irrigate from below. All sync to the vibration: a subsonic hymn propagating through chitin, entraining pulses, growth-spurts, predations into choral unity. It amplifies intruders—your heartbeat joins the chorus unbidden, luring a pseudopodial tendril to sample, to urge assimilation. They sense you as resource, as catalyst: your moisture, stolen by desiccate air, might quicken a tower's spire; your cadence, etched into a new arcade's rhythm.

Yet they dream expansion, these lives-without-face. At the sinkhole's lip, sentinel extrusions probe the flats beyond—blind antennae tasting wind-borne dust, poised to erupt in urban tide. Stand amid them long, and boundaries blur: your skin prickles with emergent pseudopodia, lichens questing your pores, the choir whispering *become lattice, become void, become us*. They live not in the city, but as it—eternal, hungry, composing their realm in cool, silent secretion.
