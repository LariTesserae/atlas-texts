In the breathing vault, life is not a hierarchy of predator and prey, nor a frantic scramble for scant niches, but a choral assembly—an eternal convocation where every swimmer, crawler, and drifter is a chorister in the liquid liturgy. They are the vault's indwellers, evolved not from ocean's fury but from highland rivulets and glacial melts, bodies sculpted by millennia of this temperate plenum: sleek, elongated forms with lateral lines attuned to the faintest eddy, gills feathering like frost on stone, scales or carapaces etched with mineral ghosts from the plateau's veins. No leviathans dominate; scale is intimate here, bodies from finger-length to forearm span, populating the sparse blue without congestion, each presence a deliberate stroke in the water's vast canvas. They move in concert, not chaos—schools wheeling as one mind, herds grazing in synchronized undulations—bound by the vault's sovereign rhythms, where survival is less conquest than communion.

At the heart of the multitude swarm the silversides, the vault's fleeting congregation: fist-sized minnows whose flanks ignite in sun shafts like mercury spilled across altars. They are billions in their ceaseless gyres, partitioned into vortices that braid through sea-grass aisles—tight spirals of fifty or five hundred, flanks pulsing in unison to refract light into blinding helices. No lone wanderers; they fuse into living tapestries, bodies compressing into arrowheads that slice frond-gaps, dispersing only to reform instants later. Their eyes, black beads vast against slender skulls, track shadows with preternatural focus; they feed on the pollen-puffs drifting from overhead vines, lips nibbling in micro-swarms that strip a bloom to skeleton in heartbeats. At hunts, their chorus rises—high trills scaling to shrieks that pierce the din, summoning allies from adjacent shoals until the blue thickens with silver storm. They birth in the sediment basins, eggs laid in gelatinous mats amid tuberous roots, hatching to join the whirl without pause; death claims them mid-spin, bodies claimed by currents or jaws, recycled into the substrate without ceremony. They are the vault's pulse, ephemeral yet inexhaustible, their mass migration at lunar tides a pilgrimage: clockwise spirals hourglass-slow, circling the central agora over dawns, polishing ruin pillars with flanks until stone gleams.

Guarding the frond-forests patrol the morays, eel-kin wrought from basalt shadows: sinuous lengths two paces long, hides the mottled gray of weathered schist veined with algae-glow. They coil basal pillars like living ropes, jaws unhinging to cavernous maws fringed with recurved fangs, eyes milky-veiled yet sightless to all but vibration. Solitary hermits, they stake territories in alcove crevices—ancient home-hollows softened by time—emerging to undulate in lazy figure-eights, tails whipping sediment into nutrient clouds. Their hunts are patient ambuscades: a silverside vortex brushing too near, and a moray erupts in a blur, body inverting through its own coils to engulf a dozen at a gulp, bones crunching with resonant snaps that echo vault-wide. They mate in rare trysts, females bloating with eel-fry that sheathe in ruin fissures, guarded by slack-jawed males until the young wriggle free, pale and voracious. Phosphorescent nodes along their flanks flicker in current-stirs, signaling kin across the blue—a Morse of pulses evoking the vault's mineral memory, drawing others to shared feasts or warnings. They are the ribs' wardens, rasping fronds with gill-clears that tune the hush-roar, their basso grunts underscoring the fish-choruses like thunder held in abeyance.

From the floor's mounds surge the crustacean clans, herds of lobsters and cray kin bloated to cat-sized girth, carapaces encrusted with coralline blooms that mimic the walls' patina. Chitin gleams basalt-black, pincers oversized for scuttling—hammer-claws that clack in percussive barrages, cracking tuber-nodes or detritus for marrow-soft innards. They migrate in phalanxes of hundreds, legs splaying in tidal advances across plant bases, feelers waving like antennae of a submerged hive. No queens command; consensus drives them, a collective scuttle dictated by the low rumble of inflows—toward nutrient eddies or away from ray-glides. Battles flare in pincer-locks, shells fracturing with pistol-cracks that rebound as gongs, victors feasting on spilled viscera amid the herd's indifferent press. They moult under sea-grass cloaks, vulnerable husks guarded by kin-cliques, emerging armored and swollen. Their mass lends the floor its subtle mound-heave, bodies composting fallen fronds into fertile silt; at night-currents (when shafts dim to sapphire), they ascend in waves, clinging to kelp-trunks to browse bladderwrack orbs, shells scraping symphonies that layer the din.

Midwater ghosts the jelly conclave, rarer envoys from the fissures' hidden broods: saucer-domed medusae the span of platters, bells translucent-veined with silver nerves, trailing skirts of stinging threads that pulse bioluminescent hymns. They drift in loose parliament, bells contracting in slow chimes to propel through light shafts, feeding on pollen or unwary minnow-frays with nematocyst harpoons. No aggression marks them; their glow lures crustacean stragglers into paralytic embrace, bodies dissolving into nutrient soup over days. Reproduction is the vault's quiet miracle: polyps budding from sediment holds, strobilating into ephyra that rise like living confetti, joining the drift. Their presence partitions the plenum—trails of light weaving aisles amid grass-pillars, echoes of bells muting the roar to cathedral hush.

Apex shades claim the voids: thornrays gliding floorward on velvet wings three paces wide, undersides pale vacuums that suck detritus and crippled swimmers, exhaling silt-plumes in languid sighs. Pike-specters, elongate assassins with jaws needled for silversides, dart from ruin shadows in explosive lunges, herding schools against walls for moray-feasts. They are sparse—dozens, not hordes—territories overlapping in tolerant truce, scars from pincer-clashes webbing flanks.

Yet deeper still, in the agora's heart, amid pillar-lattices where currents spiral tightest, dwell the vault's elders: the remnant folk, descendants of the stone-haulers, transfigured by epochs into the water's own. No longer bipedal, they are the aquarines—slender ichthyoids with humanoid echoes: torsos elongated to dolphin-grace, arms fused to flippers fringed with dexterous webbing, faces flattened to gilled masks with eyes like polished onyx, frilled crests echoing ancient helms. Scaled in schist-mimicry, they haunt the half-domed enclosures, weaving kelp-threads into nets that span plazas, harvesting minnow-swarms in communal sweeps. Their voices thread the din—low hums that modulate fish-choruses, whistles summoning morays to shared hunts. They tend the ruins not as relics but as reefs: chiseling new holds for holdfasts, channeling inflows to nourish algae blooms, bodies etched with mineral tattoos evoking builders' scars. Solitary trios or octet-clans, they court in light-shaft dances, laying pearl-clutches in alcove shrines; young emerge translucent, schooling with silversides until crest-frills harden. They know the vault's idiosyncrasies—the algae-veins that flare at solstice currents, water's highland tang that stirs ancestral dreams of misted peaks. Immortal in their slow eternity, they dissolve no more than the stones they hewed, drifting the blue hush as its eternal custodians—sparse, sovereign, the living breath of this highland sea's secret heart. To dwell among them is to join the choir, body yielding to the cool plenum, every pulse a note in the unending roar.
