In the hollow's perpetual hush, life stirs not as clamor but as a verdant pulse, inhabitants woven from the same damp threads as the place itself—hybrids of root and rivet, spore and sprocket, their forms emerging from the interstices where iron yields to ivy and rust feeds filament. They are the Verdigrised, a loose kin of being born of the basin's alchemy: neither beast nor botany alone, but symbiotic amalgams that have fermented over decades in the cool condensate, their bodies a living patina of leaf-vein circuitry and fungal-forged plating. Sparse in number, they drift through the green lattice like mist-wraiths, their presence felt more in the subtle shifts—a tendril uncoiling to adjust a gear, a low hum syncing with the waterwheel's thud—than seen outright, for they shun the open plazas, favoring the shadowed vaults of workshop-naves and the root-woven undercrofts.

Foremost among them are the Loomweavers, colossal arborforms that anchor the hollow's core. Picture a Verdigrised elder: trunk-like torso of buttressed banyan-wood, fluted with corrodant veins where sap mingles with leaked machine-oil, granting a faint, iridescent sheen that glows under mist-veiled lanterns. From their crowns sprout manipulator-fronds—long, prehensile lianas tipped with calliper-claws of hardened thorn and salvaged steel, dexterous enough to thread a needle or torque a piston. Their "faces" are knotty oculi: clusters of dew-lensed photoreceptors, like compound eyes of moss-agate, flickering with the pulse of bioluminescent sap drawn from breached sumps. They move seldom, rooted to the forge-house's heart where the central steam-vein bubbles eternally; instead, they loom as living looms, weaving vine-cables across rail-girders to channel the waterwheel's torque into irrigation-spindles. A Loomweaver "breathes" through gill-slits along its bark-plating, inhaling the ozone of sparking cables to catalyze spore-release, their exhalations seeding the mist with propagules that drift to claim new iron. They commune in infrasound throbs—low creaks that resonate through the clay, coordinating the hollow's hydraulic rhythm, ensuring no vine overruns a flywheel, no root severs a vital seep. Elders bear the patina of centuries: flanks encrusted with lichen-dials that track the dilating days, one arm perhaps sacrificed to become a trellis for its own spore-kin.

Flitting at their fringes are the Siltscutters, the hollow's mobile tenders—slighter kin, the size of a stooped human, their forms a scurrying fusion of rodent agility and clockwork finesse. Elongated bodies of woven bramble and frayed conveyor belting, armored in overlapping scales of mica-flecked bark and oxidized tin. Limbs end in scissor-paws: one set for snipping questing tendrils that stray too wild, the other for etching micro-runes into stone paths, channeling rivulets to parched moss-beds. Their heads are hooded by cowls of fern-spoor, housing a proboscis-siphon that drinks from condensation lenses, filtering it through internal alembics—gut-chambers of symbiotic fungi that distill nectar laced with gear-lubricant, fueling their ceaseless patrols. Eyes like cabling-knots glow faint blue during arc-storms, navigating the gloom; tails, whipcord vines trailing copper filaments, trail behind to mend frayed root-bridges or spark fungal blooms into light. Siltcutters are the basin's scavengers and sentinels, their chatter a staccato of leaf-rustles and metallic clicks, warning of overgrowth or alerting Loomweavers to a stalled piston. Packs of three or four form temporary swarms, gnawing paths through bramble-thickets to access inverted barrows, harvesting the "fruits" of decay: spore-pods swollen with sump-oil, or cog-nuts sprouted from gear-sprouts. They reproduce by budding— a Siltcutter curls into a silt-nest, extruding a pupa of bindweed and wire that hatches in hours, imprinted on the wheel's heartbeat.

Higher in the canopies dwell the Mistmoths, ethereal transients that blur the line between fauna and epiphyte. Wingspans of frayed liana-silk stretched over balsa-frame ribs, veined with conductive fibers that harvest static from sighing winds. Bodies are puffballs of mycelial down, dusted with pollen-sparks, their abdomens distended sacs that bloat with transpired mist, venting it as fine veils to thicken the basin's chill. Antennae like tuning-forks quiver to the chunk-thud of distant machinery, guiding them to blooms heavy with nectar—lianas pendulous with dew-drips laced with boiler-seep, sucked through siphons that process it into luminous eggs. A clutch hatches mid-flight, larvae tumbling to latch onto rust-flakes, metamorphosing into new moths over days of sap-fed wriggle. They are the hollow's pollinators and illuminators, their wings tracing glyphs of light in the overcast noon, cross-pollinating epiphyte gardens across warehouse-girders. Lone corvids—ink-black outliers with beak-tips filed to key-bits—sometimes perch among them, trading caws for gleaned wire-shards, their own nests lined with thimble-ferns, a rare bridge to the outer hummocks.

Deeper still, in the underloam, thrive the Mycorusts—subterranean myrmidons, fungal hives fused with the rail-sidings' underbelly. Colonies of threadlike pseudopods, plated in chitinous rust-flakes, tunnel through silted berms, their networks pulsing with electrolytic sap drawn from root-hair electrodes. They wage the micro-wars: devouring aphid-herds that overrun leaf-veins, exhaling counter-spores that lacewings amplify. Surfacing rarely, they erupt as toadstools with cap-eyes, dispensing worker-drones—beetle-sized crawlers with mandibles of sheared bolt-threads—to mend the Loomweavers' lesser wounds.

These Verdigrised do not conquer; they curate. Their society is one of harmonic decay, where a frayed belt becomes a birthing-web, a stalled engine a nursery for sprouts. Conflicts arise in scarcity—a drought-veil thinning the mist might spark Siltcutter raids on Mycorust caches—but resolve in the wheel's turning, moisture's return. They sense intruders through vibra-tendrils strung across the thorn-hedge, responding not with fang but finesse: lianas looping to gently entwine, guiding the wayward back to the perimeter's rise. To dwell among them is to attune to the hollow's reverie—feel the plink of droplets sync with your pulse, watch a vine etch your shadow into moss before dawn claims it anew. They are the hollow's quiet sovereigns, embodiments of its rule: moisture moves all, machinery feeds the green, and in the balance of rust and root, eternity settles verdant and vast.
