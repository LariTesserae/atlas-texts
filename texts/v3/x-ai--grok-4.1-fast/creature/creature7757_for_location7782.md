In the throat of this stone-behemoth, life is not partitioned into beasts and bugs, but woven into a singular, seething congress—a parliament of the damp and the darting, where every scuttle and flutter votes in the endless referendum of survival. They are the Inhabitants, nameless in their multitudes yet sovereign in aggregate, heirs to the vault's imposed geometry, tenants who have outlasted the masons' ghosts. No king crowns this realm, no priest intones its laws; instead, dominion fractures and reforms in the perpetual churn, each species a faction in the greater body politic of predation and proliferation.

Foremost among them wheel the **Murderflocks**, the bats—not millions as idle count might claim, but a living firmament, a leathern canopy that blackens the dome's heights in ceaseless gyre. Born of guano-piled ledges crusted inches thick, their young pup blindly from pink nubs into furred fury within hours, eyes sealed shut forever to the world beyond echolocation's whip-crack. Each is a speck of night: wingspans no broader than a child's palm, membranes veined like delta maps of forgotten rivers, sniffing the air's thermals for the heat-plumes of gnats or the wingbeats of rivals. They hunt in orchestrated chaos—vortices tightening like maelstroms when prey clouds rise, collisions yielding only fluff and blood-mist that rains to the meres below. At zenith, they fractal into those eerie glyphs: spirals resolving into spirals, predatory runes that script the vault's dreams across the gloom—talons raking, beaks parting in silent shrieks. The elders, frayed at the edges, cling to the lattice-jaws overhead, fur matted with spores, imparting lore through ultrasonic hymns that only their kin comprehend: warnings of tide-rises, mappings of new slime-trails blooming verdant. They are the vault's sentinels, their rustle-shush the baseline thrum from which all other symphonies deviate.

Beneath their aerial dominion, the meres harbor the **Silverscales**, a dynasty of the submerged, eyeless fish no longer than a finger yet legion in their silvered hordes. Evolved from some surface minnow's foolhardy spill epochs past, they glide as one mind through the handspan depths—bodies tapered to knives, flanks pulsing iridescence that flares in warning or courtship amid the shafts of miserly light. Jaws gnash ceaselessly on planktonic soup strained from the mist, gill-flaps wheezing in metronomic rhythm. The boldest surge for fallen bats, scales rasping stone as they thresh; the frailest school in trembling spheres, evading the crustacean claws that pinch from below. Their world is vibration: the plash of doom-drops from above, the suck of drains during ebb-tide, the quake of rat-paws on slabs. They spawn in frenzy twice daily with the flood, eggs scattering like aborted stars into the murk, devoured or hatched in equal measure—a silver calculus of continuation.

Armored vassals to the waters crawl the **Clackers**, those tick-armored crustaceans, squat and implacable, their carapaces etched with barnacle-scars from ancestral seas. Legs splayed in sextets, they skitter sideways across drowned flagstones, chelae clicking like castanets in territorial duels or the evisceration of drowned insects. No larger than thumbnails, yet they build empires in the weeds: burrows plugged with pebble-mortar under overhangs, nurseries where pale larvae molt in gelatinous clutches. Predators in miniature, they swarm newt-tadpoles, cracking exoskeletons with hydraulic snaps that echo like musket-fire. When tides recede, they venture shoreward, trailing slime-ropes that phosphoresce faintly, beacons for lacewings plunging from the haze.

Undulating through this submerged melee glide the **Frillnecks**, translucent newts with gills fanning like blood-red coronets, bodies limned in vein-glow against the meres' tea-dark. Blind as the fish yet tactile virtuosi, their skin secretes a mucus veiling toxins that blister Clacker-mouths and Silverscale-gills alike. They court in hypnotic undulations, males trailing luminous spores that draw mates through the chill, spawning gelatinous strings anchored to submerged carvings—those eroded faces whose agape mouths now serve as nurseries. Juveniles, legged and lunged, scramble to ledges during floods, joining the frill-less adults in raids on ledge-lichens, tongues flicking like live wires to harvest spores.

The air's dominion fractures into the **Chirrmasters**, cave-crickets grotesque in their longevity—legs elongated to compass-needles, carapaces bowed like lutes strung with chitin-strands. Perched on every buttress and filigree'd wall, they rasp ceaseless nocturnes, amplified to gales by the vault's cruel parabola: solos cascading into chorales that drown all but the bats' pips. Preyed upon by all, they persist through armor and evasion, leaping parabolic arcs that defy the eye, ovipositors probing cracks to plant broods in moss-wombs. Beside them hum the **Gnatclouds**, ephemeral billions birthing from meres' edges, wings a frenzied blur preyed by whirring **Lacewings**—delicate assassins with raptorial arms folding midges mid-flight—and the mite-drifts, fungal parasites that hitch rides on bats, sowing decay in fur-tufts.

Ledges host the **Paleprinces**, rats sleek as oiled shadows, fur silvered by mineral mist, eyes garnet-bright with feral cunning. Not scavengers alone but raiders supreme: packs tumbling fledgling bats from nests, nipping lizard-throats, disemboweling crickets in crunching orgies. Litters burrowed in guano-pockets swell weekly, pink nubs nursing on milk laced with venom-tolerance from scavenged newt-flesh. Hierarchies form in whisker-flicks and tail-lashings, alpha sows commanding the choicest lichen-patches, their squeals knifing the din to assert borders.

Webs veil the crevices, spun by **Dewspinners**, spiders whose orbs tremble like harps in the drafts—bodies plump on gnat-hauls, abdomens ballooning gravid with silk-sacs. Cave-swallows, those darting sprites with scimitar-wings, shear through veils beaks agape, fledglings fattened on web-trapped mites. Wall-clingers, the **Pulse-throats**, lizards flat as pamphlets, scale in olive-drab, throats inflating balloon-like to herald hunts, forked tongues lashing midges from mist-veils.

Yet beyond faction lies symbiosis' underweave: guano feeds moss, moss harbors mites, mites ride bats, bats' droppings fatten fish—a cycle etched in the stones themselves. And in rare lulls, when drips sync to crystal-song and bats etch sigils, something greater stirs: the **Congregation**, a fleeting sentience where echolocative maps overlap into cartographies of rite, where carvings seem to shift under slime, whispering invitations in near-words—"enter, partake, persist." No singular godform claims throne, but the vault's ecology pulses as one organism, adaptive and eternal, watching intruders with a thousand eyes, waiting to enfold them in its humming embrace. They are the true masons now, scoring the stone with trails and nests, commanding its breath as their own. To dwell here is to join the swarm or feed it; there are no exiles, only the devoured and the devourers.
