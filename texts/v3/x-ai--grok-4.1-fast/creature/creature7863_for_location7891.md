In the marrow of this plateau's unyielding shelf, where the earth's fresh wound exhales its chill breath, lives a people forged from the same impatient stone—broad and unbreaking, their forms a testament to the air's thin insistence and the wind's ceaseless honing. They are the Highfolk, or so they name themselves in guttural syllables that rasp like flint on schist, a lineage birthed not from myth but from the slow crawl of ancestors up scree-choked passes, those who outlasted the falls and frosts to claim this aerie. No kings crown them, no scribes tally their epochs; they are a swarm of kin-groups, bloodlines braided into clans that sprawl across the Settlement's leagues like the rivers' mercury veins—each district a warren of loyalties, marked by totems of carved horn lashed to lintels: aurochs skulls for the herders of the central flats, raven feathers for the scavengers of the rim-vales, marmot pelts for the burrow-diggers of the hillside warrens.

Their bodies bear the plateau's signature: chests barrel-wide to hoard the sparse air, lungs bellows that draw deliberate, fogging plumes with every measured stride; limbs thick as the alpine pines they fell, corded with sinew from hauling stone or wrestling lowing beasts; faces broad and wind-sculpted, cheekbones high as arêtes, noses flattened against the bite, eyes narrowed to slits of slate-gray or amber, crinkled at the corners from peering through veils of mist and sleet. Skin ruddy as rusting iron, weathered to leather by the damp cold that never dries, etched with fine cracks like quartz veins; beards thick and frost-matted on men, framing mouths that chew tirelessly on salt-meat or pipe lichen-smoke; women with hair plaited into ropes woven with wool tufts and bone beads, hoods framing brows furrowed by the loom's rhythm or the whetstone's rasp. Children swarm like marmots from the lofts—gap-toothed, feral-limbed, their laughter a muffled burble that punctuates the hush, scampering across rooftops where goats nibble sedge, learning early the grip of ice-rimmed ledges and the weight of a herding crook.

Clad in the land's own shed, they move as extensions of herd and stone: cloaks of layered wool from fat-tailed sheep, oiled against the clamminess, heavy with the scent of lanolin and hearth-dung; tunics of aurochs hide, seams stitched with sinew thread, panels reinforced with horn scales where wind knives deepest; boots upturned at the toes from bog-tanned leather, packed with moss for warmth, thudding soft on cobbled lanes worn to their contours over generations. Tools hang from belts like talismans—flint adzes for shaping, bronze needles for mending, whet-flints that sing against blades; no metal glints unused, for the smiths' weak alloys demand thrift, crucibles nursed like infants over peat fires that glow through chinks in turf walls.

Life pulses in their dense communion, a vertical crush where privacy yields to survival's press: families stack in the warrens, lofts tiered like ziggurats within ziggurats, elders wheezing tales by tallow wick while below, youths mend nets from twisted sedge-fiber for snaring ptarmigan in the high cirques. Meals are communal vats—stew of sour milk curdled with herd-blood, chunks of smoked aurochs flank slow-simmered over dung coals, ladled into horn bowls passed hand-to-hand in circles around fire-pits that scar every courtyard. Voices murmur low, conserving breath for the labor: grunts of barter in the vale-markets, where stalls groan under piles of cheese-wheels wrapped in birchbark, bundles of raw wool traded bead-for-bead; songs rise rarely, a droning chant to pace the looms or mark a calving, their tones flat as the flats themselves, harmonies woven from wind-whispers and herd-lows.

Yet they are not solitary in this frost-rimed throng; the beasts that sustain them are kin as much as cattle, their lives entwined in a vast, migratory weave. Greatest among them roam the aurochs-kin—massive, hump-shouldered brutes the Highfolk call *thunderhides*, taller than three men at the withers, their wool a tangled mat of black and dun that sheds in clumps to carpet lanes, horns curving like crescent moons from skull-bosses broad as shields. Penned in corrals of lashed pine that span leagues, they low in rumbling choruses that vibrate the turf-roofs, milked by hands callused to their kicks, driven in seasonal circuits by crook-wielding herders who read the melt-rills' flow like oracles. Beside them bleat the sheep-folk, squat and wool-swollen, tails fat as haunches storing the graze they wrench from lichen-mottles; flocks cascade down terraced pens, shorn twice yearly with shears of chipped obsidian, their bleats a constant undersong to the Settlement's hush.

Wilder shadows prowl the fringes: wolf-packs tamed to heel, lean-flanked grays with eyes like polished quartz, scavenging offal from the culls or patrolling against rim-raiders, their howls muted to whines in deference to human fires; hounds bulkier still, bred from those wolves for the chase, brindled coats matted with sleet as they nose through alleys for vermin. Overhead, ravens command the skies in vast, wheeling parliaments—glossy-black arbiters with beaks like flints, croaking negotiations over carcasses, feathers traded as omens by the seers who read their flights against the cloud-tempests. Beneathfoot burrow the marmots, plump tunnel-dwellers with fur like sun-bleached moss, whistling alarms from rock-piles, their fat hoarded for lamps and their pelts sewn into mittens; ptarmigan ghost the sparse sedges, white as fresh frost, snared for feasts; hares bound in cryptic zigzags across the flats, their tracks a lacework in the slush.

This menagerie and multitude endures as one organism, the plateau's beating heart: dung pats fuel the hearths that warm the warrens, hides clothe the herders who guard the herds against ice-cracks and sudden squalls; failed calves feed the stew-pots when famine's shadow lengthens over a lean cycle, bones boiled to broth that binds clan to clan. Predators culled become cloaks, milk sours to sustain the young through the marrow-freeze; even the lichens scraped from stone nourish the goats that clamber the rooftops, their milk strengthening the broad chests of the next generation. Conflicts simmer low—feuds over prime corrals settled by ritual wrestling on the flats, where broad hands grapple under raven-witness; shamans invoke the peaks with offerings of blood and wool, beseeching the weather's patience.

In the hush of their persistence, ambitions curl inward like frost-rimed ferns: a herder dreams not of conquest but of a larger pen for his thunderhides; a potter envisions urns glazed rarer by the damp mists; lovers steal moments in shadowed alcoves, breath mingling hot against the cold, birthing more lives to layer the stack. Children grow broad and deliberate, aping the elders' measured gait, learning to listen for the ice's crack or the herd's uneasy shift. Elders settle into lofts, eyes misted like the horizons, murmuring of ancestors who first tasted the thin air and deemed it home. Here, in this sodden, sharp-edged vastness, the Highfolk and their beast-kin live not despite the plateau but because of it—a dense, patient proliferation, ruddy faces turned to the pale sun, hands ever shaping stone and sinew into one more wall against the sky's vast indifference. As you thread their lanes, a broad-shouldered figure brushes past, murmuring *'cold welcomes, wayfarer,'* her eyes appraising your thin-blooded frame, while behind her a thunderhide lows, and ravens croak approval from the slate-roofs above.
