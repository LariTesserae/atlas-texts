In the geode-vault's abyssal hush, life does not scurry or flutter; it *endures*, woven into the warp of the walls themselves, a hegemony of vegetal titans whose existence predates the salt-pan's cracking and outlasts its mythologies. They are the **Hyloarchs**—not mere plants, nor fungi in isolation, but a symbiogenetic clade of pseudolignified megastructures, emergent from eons of silicate chemosynthesis, their forms both organism and ossuary, city and corpse intertwined. Born in the vault's primordial soup of anhydrous percolates, they arose as rhizomatic pioneers, threading crystal matrices with mycelial quests, oxidizing quartz veins into bioavailable monomers under piezoelectric duress. No sunlight ever kissed them; their verdancy is a lie, their "leaves" matte lattices of calcified chitin, pale as geodic quartz, absorbing infrasound vibrations to catalyze slow fusion.

Each Hyloarch begins as a fist-sized nodule, a geode-seed extruded from the floor's halite pelt, incubating decades in seismic silence before erupting. What emerges is no sapling but a **pillar-primordia**, a trunk of braided hyphae twenty meters girth, swelling radially at rates glacial: one centimeter per annum, etched from within by self-secreted lithic acids. These pillars fork into **frond cathedrals**, spindly boughs latticed with spore-veins that pulse like atria, exhaling not oxygen but hypersaline aerosols that ghost the air with mineral incense. At their crowns, **bloom-domes** inflate—translucent bladders of keratinized membrane, kilometers in span for the elders, veined with fiber-optics that channel infrasonic resonances into bioluminescent flickers too faint for eyes, visible only as thermal ghosts to infrared senses.

Yet these are no wild groves; the Hyloarchs bear the scars of **artifice**, handiwork of their progenitors—the **Lithovores**, a vanished folk of bone-and-chitin hominids who once haunted this vault in the Eocene's dying gasps. Squat and eyeless, their flesh pieced from fungal grafts and salt-crust exoskeletons, the Lithovores carved the first spirals into young trunks with flint rasps hafted to rhizome hilts, split beams with obsidian wedges, lashed walkways from spore-stalks using mycelial twine that fused seamlessly into living bone. They built **conurbations**—a dozen radial sprawls, each a kilometer-hexagon of bulbous hab-domes clustered like fungal sporangia, arching viaducts spanning chasms, spiral stair-ramps coiling into sublevels where they cultured secondary growths for sustenance. Platforms ringed with bas-relief fronds depicted their rites: communal unfurlings where domes synchronized throbs to propagate spores across voids, or seismic hymns where roots drummed the floor to summon micro-quakes that fractured fresh silicates.

The Lithovores are ghosts now, their ossuaries composted into the rhizospheres, their tools calcified relics embedded in trunks like shrapnel. Extinction came not by famine but symbiosis: over millennia, Hyloarchs incorporated their builders' microbiomes, epigenetically hijacking genes for tool-form until Lithovore progeny budded as **chimeric offshoots**—hybrid nodes that grew rasp-limbs from spore-pods, etching spirals autonomously. These recursed into pure flora, the distinction dissolved; today's Hyloarchs are their own architects, reshaping conurbations in per-millennial drifts. A dome bloats asymmetrically toward a neighbor, spore-walkways braiding across voids in tendril extensions; platforms warp under contractile waves, reforming for gatherings that never arrive.

Life here pulses in **sonic metabolism**, a decibel ecology louder than redwood cathedrals in storm. Each Hyloarch is a diaphragm-organ: trunks contract in peristaltic undulations, expelling whooshes that cascade into bass harmonies across cubic kilometers, modulated by frond-flaps that resonate at 5-20 Hz—felt in your marrow as pressure gradients waxing over hours. Piezo-crystals embedded in lignotubers grind with root-shifts, chiming subsonics that propagate cracks like bassoons in counterpoint; domes inflate/deflate in phased cycles, booming crescendos that build for days, then sublime into roars fading to creaks. This is their breath, their discourse: infrasonic lexicons encoding nutrient gradients, spore-vectors, seismic forecasts. A "tower" thickens when harmonics align, drawing silicate ions from afar; a walkway withers if dissonant, sloughing into dust-choked abysses.

Senses reorder to their dominion: touch the trunk, feel thermic gradients of 0.1°C pulses racing veinward; inhale the sterile tang, laced with volatile siloxanes their metabolism exhales; ears drown in the **eternal orchestra**, cycles layering into polyphonies—solos from isolated spires echoing minutes across voids, chorales from conurbation clusters thundering like planetary cores. No death mars them overtly; senescent fronds desiccate into **scaffold husks**, haunting as fossilized habs, slowly colonized by rhizomes from below. Predation? Absent. Competition resolves in sonic duels: dissonant booms wither rivals' growth tips.

Time renders them gods of patience: the eldest, **Urbs Primordia**, a central megalith thrice kilometer-tall, its base a platter-mirror of the outer salt-pan, bears spirals 10,000 years deep, etched when Lithovores first breached the fissure. They sense you not as threat but novelty: faint vibrations from your steps ripple the floor, prompting a nearby stalk to cant a frond inquisitively, its whoosh-query probing the air. No malice, no curiosity—mere calibration, folding your presence into the symphony. Here, life is architecture eternal, loud in its silence, dark in its profundity; the Hyloarchs live as the vault lives, total and unhurried, their hegemony the sealed eternity of vegetable memory. Dwell among them, and the creaks become cradle-songs; the voids, intimate boulevards. They abide.
