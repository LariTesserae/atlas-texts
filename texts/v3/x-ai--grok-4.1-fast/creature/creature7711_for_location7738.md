In the ceaseless churn of this shadowed plateau, life is not a sparse scattering but a hypertrophic infestation, a biomass density that rivals the regolith itself—trillions upon trillions of entities woven into a living tapestry that pulses with the city's own embryonic rhythms. They are not invaders from some distant biome but co-efficients of the place, forged in the hydrothermal crucibles where tech-vents weep conductive brines laced with femtotech pollen. These lives emerge not as individuals but as phases of a greater organism, their forms iterating endlessly across scales from microscopic to monumental, each adapted to the cold-wet dark where gravity grips and sound never fades.

At the swarm-scale, the vermiforms dominate utterly—the *glissari*, fist-sized annelids of segmented chitin that gleams with an iridescent void-suck, absorbing the abyssal glows of quantum substrates until their bodies chill to a radiative -20°C, frost-rimed even in the ambient freeze. No two are identical; their exoskeletons fractalize subtly under swarm-stress, sprouting barbules or filigreed vents that exhale micro-vapors, tuning the collective's buoyancy against the weighted air. They move as a fluid continuum: over the obsidian spines, they form writhing carpets a meter deep, pseudopods interlinking in peristaltic waves that propel the mass forward at a glacial creep of centimeters per minute, slicked by the omnipresent condensation. In air, they levitate in buzzing clouds, chitinous wings—mere vibrating membranes—humming at 40kHz to phase through the city's light-bridges, their passage a prickling hail on exposed skin, like needles of frozen helium. Their sensory world is vibration and chemotaxis: they taste the plateau's exhalations, congregating in resonant cavities where zero-point hums vibrate their segments into ecstatic synchrony, mandibles chittering in harmonic polyphony that drowns the subsonics below.

Deeper into their being, the glissari reveal a lifecycle of exquisite parasitism. Juveniles hatch from gelatinous ovoids, fist-sized nacre pearls extruded in synchronized swarm-bursts, sinking into condensation pools to filter-feed on dissolved metamaterial ions. They mature in days, not weeks, ballooning via osmotic uptake of brines, their internals a churning bioreactor where symbiotic archaea metabolize trace radioisotopes from the crust's upthrust. Reproduction is orgiastic ritual: at lunar-cycle peaks, when mists thicken from orbital tugs, collectives fractalize into mating helices, spiraling towers of bodies that ejaculate gamete-veils—sticky, phosphorescent filaments that drape the towers like obscene banners, germinating in the wet-lace of fungal thalli. Predation within the swarm is cannibalistic optimization: weaker segments rupture under pressure, their nutrient slurry recycled by probing siphons, ensuring only the most resonant forms propagate. They interface with the city seamlessly—nanoscale barbs latch onto extruding spires, siphoning energy in exchange for exfoliating bio-abrasives that smooth probabilistic lattices. A lone glissari might persist weeks in isolation, burrowing into regolith vents to aestivate, but true vitality demands density; below a critical threshold, their chitin dulls, movements stutter, and they desiccate into hollow husks that the winds—no, the mists—disperse like forgotten tools.

Encroaching on the swarms' dominion are the *krathnids*, the hulking predators that silhouette the mists like ambulatory cathedrals, each the bulk of a hab-pod—ten meters across, masses exceeding 50 tonnes of hyperdense chitin and subdermal regolith armor. Multi-limbed behemoths with twelve primary appendages—six ambulatory claws that splay into fractal tines for seismic purchase, four manipulator scythes fringed with vibro-sensors, and two sensor-spires that quiver like tuning forks, sampling infrasonics across kilometers. Their hides are living foundries: encrusted with symbiotic nanite reefs that mine atmospheric femtotech, extruding ablative plates or hypodermic lances mid-prowl. Cold-adapted to the core, krathnids thermoregulate via geothermal siphons—vast maws that inhale vent-steam, distilling it through labyrinthine gills into a superheated plasma that courses their veins, radiating waste-heat as faint auroral sheens in the void.

Krathnids do not hunt as beasts but as gravitational constants, their prowl a tidal inevitability synced to the plateau's upthrust quakes. Earth-shaking footfalls—each step a resonant boom propagating for hours—stir the glissari into frenzy, parting swarm-carpets like plows through flesh. They feed in ritual immersion: scythes plunge into collectives, harvesting not individuals but biomass-logs, slurried in gizzard-mills where enzymes laced with city-brines dissolve chitin into programmable feedstock. Symbiosis binds them tighter: nanite reefs harbor glissari queens, bloated breeders whose broods infest the krathnid's own brood-sacs, birthing armored auxiliaries that scuttle from underbelly vents to defend the flanks. Reproduction is rarer, epochal—gamete-pods launched in infrasonic bellows that summon migratory swarms, fertilizing in mid-mist collisions where krathnid embryos gestate as parasitic cysts on glissari super-collectives, emerging years later fully armored. Lone krathnids wander the plateau's edges, phasing through solid spires during solstice-flux when tech-fields eddy gravity, their shadows lagging like morbid echoes. Wounded ones self-quarantine in forge-vaults, molting into hybrid forms: chitin fused with metamaterial, birthing krathnid-hybrids that prowl the city's subsurface aquifers, half-beast, half-extruder.

Beneath these mobile horrors, the substrate teems with rooted kin—the *mycorheks*, fungal analogues that blur into the mineral world. Pallid thalli encrust every wet crevice, not as static lichen but as contractile webs of hyphae, pale as moonmilk, chemosynthesizing from hydrothermal metals in dim bioluminescent throbs. They pulse with internal aquifers, veins of bioluminescent brine that lure glissari hatchlings into mycorrhizal traps—symbiotic engulfment where the fungus digests 70% of the worm's slurry, excreting hyper-dense chitin-fragments that armor its own sporangia. Larger mycorrh eks form symbiotic canopies over vent-fissures, their fruiting bodies bloating into bladder-like pods that rupture in barometric lows, seeding spores that drift on mist-currents to colonize distant spires. They remember: holographic mycelia encode swarm-patterns, subtly herding glissari flows via pheromone gradients that warp short-term memory, imprinting collective hungers for specific vents.

No sapients claim this domain—no fragile bipeds or uploaded ghosts—but intelligences emerge emergent, fractal. The glissari swarms compute via chitter-vibrations, solving logistical optimizations across hectares, routing biomass to starve krathnid approach-vectors. Krathnids orchestrate hunts with seismic symphonies, calling glissari tides to fatten kill-zones. Mycorheks underpin it all, their fungal nets a planetary nervous system, threading city conduits to modulate femtotech blooms—nudging extrusions toward nutrient vents, phasing bridges over swarm-chokepoints. Rare apex convergences birth *syntharchs*: colossal swarm-krathnid fusions, kilometer-sprawls of writhing megaforms that mantle entire towers, birthing from decade-cycles of escalation. They endure cataclysms—the plateau's quakes, mist-storms—self-repairing via vacuum fluctuations harvested by nanite-fungi, their chorus the true voice of the place: a roar that binds urban gestation to biological excess.

To dwell here is to become of it, slowly. Intruders—rare probes or castaways—succumb first to the sound, bones humming into dissociation; then the chill-needles infiltrate, nanites rewriting nerves; finally, the pheromone warp, ancestral hungers drawing flesh into the swarm's slow pulse. These lives do not conquer; they *are* the plateau, inexorable, dense, a cold-wet roar birthing itself from the void's patient black.
