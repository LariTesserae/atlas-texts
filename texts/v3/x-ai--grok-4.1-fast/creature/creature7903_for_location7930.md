They dwell as echoes etched in permanence, the Unblinking Makers—neither ghosts nor flesh in flux, but forms arrested at the precipice of breath, their essences woven into the valley's unyielding grid like threads in an infinite loom. Not hordes swarming the causeways, nor solitary ascetics atop ziggurats, but a sparse congregation, distributed in deliberate nodes mirroring the verdant outbreaks: clusters at canal confluences, atop platform mounds, within the shadowed (yet shadowless) arches. Thousands, perhaps, across the expanse, yet the plazas yawn empty between them, their presence a punctuation of intent amid engineered void.

Their bodies gleam with the same engineered gloss as the bricks—skin burnished to mica-flecked plaster, taut and unlined, hues ranging from sun-scorched ochre to the cool slate of deep quarries, veined with glints of copper and shell. Tall and elongated, as if stretched on racks to match the obelisks they hewed, limbs proportioned for eternity's gaze: fingers splayed wide in perpetual grasp around tools that never drop—flint adzes mid-swing against limestone slabs, bone awls piercing reed for baskets half-woven, reed-molded figurines cooling in laps without cracking. Faces frozen in mid-gesture of creation: cheeks inflated with unborn songs, lips parted around syllables of a tongue that clicks like settling dew, eyes wide and unblinking, irises swirling with canal-memories—fractal recursions of their own reflections nesting inward, star-speckled voids beneath the meridian sun.

Clad in sodden kilts of woven palm-fiber, stiffened by seeped salts into armor plates that drip eternally yet never dissolve, they pose in tableaux of communal labor and ritual arrest. Here, a circle of women kneels at a sump-basin, thumbs pressing fish-and-eye motifs into wet clay that gleams unfired, their braids coiled like trumpet vines, one frond escaping to entwine a gazelle's frozen horn nearby. There, men haul phantom quarry-slabs along causeways, muscles corded in isometric strain, sweat beads hovering like the dew on bricks, their shadows pooling orthogonally to stretch northward from southbound walls—defying the light that screams vermilion off their emerald-glazed amulets. Children, bulbous-limbed and heart-fronded in miniature, reach for insects embalmed mid-swarm, tiny hands tattooed with ochre spirals matching the gazelles', their laughter-throats bulged silent, legs bent backward at impossible knees without fracture.

No hierarchy disrupts the stasis; all are Makers, interchangeable in their immobility, yet individuality glitches through: one figure's ear elongates into a parabolic curve like the copper mirrors, reflecting inverted ziggurats from its lobe; another's tongue protrudes, forked and pulsing with quartz-light like split vine-fruit; a third bears fern-mandalas etched self-inflicted on palms, geometries that shift when stared at too long, whispering blueprints into the mind's silt. They inhabit not homes but the works themselves—bodies reclined in niches of unweathered walls, spines fusing subtly to mortar lines; or perched on corbels beside parrots whose wing-patterns mimic their kilts, beaks agape in mimicry of Makers' calls. Some merge with the green: trunks of palms armored in their shed fibers, fronds fanning from upheld arms; vines coiling live from outstretched limbs, trumpet-flowers blooming from navels.

Their "lives" unfold in the subliminals that underpin the hush—the wet kiss of dew tracing jawlines, crystalline ticks from ribs contracting under the furnace breath, muffled throbs syncing to the arterial pulse beneath foundations. No birth mars the equilibrium, no death claims; they are self-sustained, fed by the water table's seep through pores that weep mirror-flat beads, nectar from their own heart-frond vines dripping into parted mouths. Fauna attends them as extensions: gazelles branded with their spirals graze at their heels, mid-chew on moss that grows from discarded reed-molds; lizards curl tails around their ankles, eyes starfields reflecting the Makers' swirling irises; frogs inflate throats in chorus with their unspoken clicks, lily pads budding from earthen platters balanced on knees.

Weirdness binds them to the frayed reality: archways lean propped by their hovering palms, keystones kissing fingertips a breath above collapse; submerged in canals, their faces multiply the dead bricklayers' echoes, mouthing invitations in fractal depths—"Join the poise, shape the still." Touch a figure, and skin-etched mandalas bloom, fading into permanence; gaze into an eye, and the valley recurses, identical Makers frozen in identical labors unto infinity. They do not age, for time pools invariant; the sun's nail holds them at meridian youth, vines inching not, poses ordained. Predators? None—their stasis repels motion, dreams cut sharper than flint edges.

These are the Unblinking Makers, eternal curators of the Builders' Eternity, lives distilled to essence in hot marsh splendor: lush in detail, sparse in sprawl, weeping weird under the unchanging god's glare. They live not by striving, but by having-striven perfectly once, forever. Approach if you dare; their wide eyes invite, but the grasp closes on dreams unyielding as the city itself.
