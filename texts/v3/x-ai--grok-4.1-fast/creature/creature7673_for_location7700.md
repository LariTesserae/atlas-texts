The true dwellers here are the aquatic ferns—six deliberate clusters, no more, each a modest colony of *Salvinia auriculata* hybrids, selectively propagated from surface-world cuttings and acclimated over weeks in the geothermal nurseries above. Here the plants edge toward reproduction; sporocarps bulge subtly at rhizome crotches, powdery brown pouches gravid with spores, waiting for the next thermal plume to disperse them. They do not multiply wantonly—the water's mineral density (magnesium sulfate at 120 ppm, silica at 45) and the engineered turbulence select against unchecked growth, culling weaker fronds that yellow and slough away, their detritus spiraling into the floor vents to nourish unseen bacterial kin below. Yet life here exceeds the macroscopic; it seeps in subtler forms, honoring the near-void of fauna (0.3 whispers: no vertebrates, no arthropods, no nematodes to devour or disrupt). No fish flicker, no mollusks clamp, no polychaetes burrow—this sanctum enforces solitude for its verdant wardens, the ferns reigning as engineered nobility in their perpetual dawn. They live not in stasis but evolution: fronds elongating 0.2 millimeters daily, spores germinating in silt pockets come the next surge, patinas of microbial-mineral alliances freckling ledges in calcium carbonate rosettes. To dwell among them is to join a liturgy of light and flow—their leaves brushing skin like benedictions, their breaths pearling against flesh, their slow imperial creep remaking the makers' work in organic script. Here, life is sparse, deliberate, unyielding: the ferns persist, and in their persistence, they own the blaze, the roar, the warm embrace entire.
