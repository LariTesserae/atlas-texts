They live in layers, these dwellers of Echelon Spire, accreted over generations like the ferroconcrete megablocks themselves—8 million souls and twice that in non-humans, their presences overlapping in the humid crush, voices and footfalls threading the mechanical chorus without ever drowning it. Humans predominate, but not the frail lowlanders from the fog-shrouded vales below; these are high-spirans, broad-shouldered and barrel-chested from cradle to pyre, lungs expanded by the crisp bite of oxygenated drafts, skins weathered to a perpetual tan under the unrelenting sodium-gold glare. Their eyes squint habitually against the brightness, irises darkened by gene-selects for glare resistance, brows furrowed into permanent ridges from scanning horizons of stacked habs and spiraling ramps. Clothing clings damply: oil-stained coveralls of waxed synth-canvas for the forge-tenders, loose tunics of woven epiphyte fiber for plaza-dwellers, all patched with hydroponic leaf-matter and edged in rust-fleck embroidery, practical garb that molds to the eternal moisture.

At the industrial hearts, the mechkin labor in their thousands—families of hereditary gearwrights, faces smudged with coal-dust halos, hands callused thick as tire rubber from wrenching hissing valves and feeding belts into forges that stamp girders hot and relentless. Men and women alike, indistinguishable in their grease-slicked vigor, they hum work-chants synced to piston throbs, children apprenticed young at conveyor webs, sorting ore simulants with nimble fingers while parrots perch on their shoulders, pecking at ration-crums. Their laughter booms basso across turbine halls, echoing off radome vaults, a counterpoint to the generators' hourly rumble; they breed sturdy, five or six per hab-unit, sleeping in hammocks slung from exhaust struts, dreaming of quota surpluses that earn extra mist-cycles for rooftop pastures.

Higher up the verdant strata, the greenhands tend the agroscape—lithe figures in vine-wrapped harnesses, scaling hydroponic spires with sucker-cup boots that grip wet alloy. Their skin gleams with algal sheen, hair cropped short or braided with bromeliad fronds, bodies tattooed in fractal patterns mimicking the cliff-veins below. Women with child-slings balanced on hips prune fern-canopies, murmuring to geneered canids that herd synth-bovines across warehouse roofs, the beasts lowing in damp harmony; men mist epiphytes from capillary rigs, coaxing orchid blooms that yield nectar for communal vats. These folk speak in rustles, voices soft as leaf-shifts, bartering yields in plaza-markets where rodents dart underfoot, gleaning fallen pods. Non-humans mingle here freely: servo-drones the size of hawks, their carapaces furred with lichen for camouflage, pollinating alongside insect clouds; or hulking recycler-golems, vaguely humanoid stacks of treaded limbs and funnel maws, shambling through compost drifts to mulch rust-flakes into fungal beds, their vocalizers intoning quota tallies in gravelly binar.

Amid the plateaus' open expanses, families sprawl in repose—high-spirans of all castes picnicking under dripping awnings, platters heaped with synth-bovine curds, fern-wrapped tubers, and parrot-egged custards flavored with canal-algae. Elders with oxygen-masks woven into beard-beards recount erosion epochs, children chasing iridescent flocks whose cries pierce the fan-whines, while courting youths tangle in vine-thickets, their trysts lit by bioluminescent flares during flush rains. The uncounted non-humans thicken the weave: bio-stabilized parrots in rainbow swarms, bartering shiny trinkets for fruit-cores; geneered canids with matted pelts and pheromone glands tuned to herd without bite, packs led by alpha-bitches whose eyes glow faint phosphor in dusk-fades; synth-bovines docile as damp earth, hides quilted with epiphyte grafts, milk-vats auto-tapped by drone-milker swarms. Rodents proliferate in conduits, sleek-furred opportunists with whisker-sensors for steam-vents, breeding in clans that till walkway soils; birds of prey—eagle-kin with wing-clips for updraft loyalty—perch on parapets, culling vermin excesses under cull-drone oversight.

Deeper in the undercrofts, the unseen throng pulses: scavenger-clans of tunnel-dwellers, pale and wiry, navigating monorail shadows with echo-clickers, harvesting slag-nuggets for personal forges; they trade with fungal tenders, symbiotic folk whose skin hosts glowing mycelia blooms, bartering spores for conduit-maps. Non-humans dominate here—feral servo-swarms, insectile clusters reassembling from shed parts, scavenging valve-drips into self-repair; or elder golems, half-rusted sentinels from founding eras, their joints weeping lubricant as they whisper maintenance litanies to passing mechkin. No castes clash overtly; pheromonal diffusers enforce calm, diffusing tensions into zephyrs, while cull-drones prune outliers—overbreeders, quota-saboteurs—with quiet laser-flicks, their husks composted into green strata.

Sentients blur at edges: chimeric hybrids from old gene-vats, human torsos grafted to canid limbs for mid-level prowls, herding with pack-mates; or neural-uplinked parrots, vocabularies laced with forge-slang, serving as aerial couriers between habs. Full non-humans include the watcher-arrays—immobile radome-minds, vast neural lattices pulsing under summit caps, overseeing air-cycles and micro-grav poise, their "voices" threading public vocasters in serene directives. And the beasts: not mere fauna, but kin—packs of canids claiming plaza territories, dens in perforated decking where pups nurse amid rust-filagree; synth-bovine herds trampling rooftop pastures into loam, their low-moos syncing with generator rumbles; insect mega-colonies in exhaust vents, queens enthroned in wax-hexes, workers ferrying pollen across kilometers.

All acclimated to the deliberate rhythms: they ration during drought pockets, bodies thinning to wiry efficiency before flush rains swell them anew; prune vegetation in generational phases, hands gnarled from shears passed down; oil mechanisms during maintenance epochs, festivals of clang and steam where mechkin wed amid hissing pistons. Lovers fog the chill parapets with breath-shared warmth; elders dissolve into recycler vats upon death, atoms reborn in girder-stamps or vine-sap. Conflicts simmer low—resource squabbles diffused by communal quotas, enforced by golem-arbiters—yielding to the spire's inexorable harmony. They are the lifeblood: damp-skinned, loud-voiced, green-veined, their multitudes a slow pulse in the warm haze, enduring as the patinaed concrete, feral as the parrots' cries, engineered as the zephyrs that bear them aloft. Here, living is not conquest but accretion—layer upon verdant layer, soul to servo, in eternal, overlapping poise.
