In the Lumivoid's eternal cradle, life does not cluster or teem; it haunts. It is the shadow cast by glow upon void, the shiver in the frond's vein, the echo that lingers after a root snaps. These are not beasts of burly herds or buzzing swarms, but specters etched into the architecture of absence—forms refined by cold's forge, instability's whim, and the relentless pulse of plant-flesh that both sustains and starves them. They are the voids' children, born not from soil or womb but from the dripping ethers, the quaking rifts, the bioluminescent afterbirth of cataclysms. Sparse as stars in daylight, they traverse megameters of green lattice and black drop, their existences measured in migrations across frond-plains or skitters along moss-bridges, forever chasing the flare of nectar or the sigh of decaying bloom.

Foremost among them drift the **mote-veils**, colossal nebulae of symbiotes no larger than frost-flakes yet collectively vast as thunderheads. Born in the hovering mists where gravity falters, these are fungal spores entwined with algal filaments and microbial predators, forming living fogs that condense and disperse like breaths of the cavern itself. They pulse with synchronized teal glows, harvesting condensation beads mid-air, their collective intelligence emerging in subtle drifts: veils that part for safer crossings or coil into predatory funnels to ensnare phospher-worms. Touch one, and it adheres like rime-silk, feeding on warmth before dissolving into harmless vapor—guardians of the air, weaving the dampness into veils that cloak travelers from wind-vortexes, or luring prey with false auroras.

Crawling from the root-webs emerge the **skitterlords**, kin to the spectral millipedes but exalted in their dominion. These are arboreal titans, bodies segmented into a hundred chitinous coils, each pair of legs tipped with luminous barbs that hook into vine-cathedrals like climbing stars. Thirty meters from mandible to tail-spike, their carapaces are translucent lattices veined with stolen bioluminescence, flickering in hypnotic codes that signal mates across chasm-spans or warn of upheavals. They do not devour leaves whole but milk the sap-veins with needle-tongues, excreting a hardening resin that reinforces bridges against quakes. Solitary save in rare convergences at colossal blooms, they duel with pincer-whips that crack obsidian, their molts piling into spire-nests where larvae hatch amid the glow. In the hush, their legs whisper like frost on glass, a thousand points etching trails that fungi claim as new mycelium.

Wings claim the glow-shafts: the **veilwings**, translucent bats evolved beyond membrane to symbiotic sails of fungal parchment, stretched between bone-spars that hum with embedded spores. Wingspans rival the millipedes' lengths, their bodies mere knots of sinew and heat-glands, furred in frost-moss that insulates against the chill. They navigate not by echo but by tasting light-winds, flitting in loose flocks that spiral through plant-vaults, sipping nectar from frond-dew or snatching mote-veils in fang-nets. Their guano rains as luminous pellets, seeding algal blooms that flare violet—cycles of light they shepherd. Rare is their call, a subsonic thrum that resonates root-fibers, summoning winds or lulling skitterlords to sleep; in vortex-storms, they cluster into living kites, riding currents to seed new realms.

Undulating across the cavern plains come the **phosherim**, herds of worms not mere in number but in sublime procession. Each a serpent of ten meters, blind and armored in iridescent slime that hardens into glowing causeways behind them, their bodies writhe in unison, propelled by ripple-waves of muscle beneath scales that shift teal-to-violet with mood or poison. They migrate in sinuous rivers of hundreds, following pollen-tides from fungal towers, burrowing into root-nests to pupate amid decay. Parasites ride them—tiny crustacean remoras that glow warnings of instability, picking at wounds from calving cliffs. When quakes heave the floor, phosherim coil into living dams, channeling ghost-rains into aquifers; their deaths birth worm-mounds, fractal hills where new generations erupt, slime-trails persisting as eternal lanterns.

Deeper in the ribs of this green leviathan lurk the **rift-hounds**, quadrupeds of predatory grace, forged from the ancient fauna's bolder echoes. Lean as shadows, with pelts of woven algal filaments that camouflage against moss-veils, their eyes are void-black lenses reflecting glows in prismatic halos. Paws splay wide with frost-claws for basalt grip, maws lined with inward-curving fangs that shear vine-flesh. They hunt in pairs, bounding across frond-lattices with gravity-defying leaps, ambushing veilwings at bloom-perches or harrying phosherim tails. Their howls are rare, compressed into infrasound bursts that shatter rime-ice, calling kin from abyssal drifts. Nests hide in obsidian crevices, lined with skitterlord molts, where pups nurse on regurgitated sap, their growth accelerated to match the Lumivoid's frenzy—full hunters in weeks.

Yet amid these, whispers persist of higher echoes: the **frondkin**, last frail heirs of the vanished makers. Not the robust primitives of quartz-obelisks and bone-huts, but their attenuated descendants, warped by the churn into symbiotic wraiths. Humanoid in silhouette yet elongated, their skins are pallid tapestries of moss and fungal mycelium, glowing faintly with borrowed azure, limbs stretched to five meters for spanning voids. Eyes vast and milky, adapted to eternal brilliance, they navigate by root-vibrations felt through bone-conduits. Naked save for frond-cloaks that photosymbiose, they subsist on sap-siphons and worm-slime, wielding tools of living vine: whips that extend like tongues, blades grown from spore-pits in palms. Nomadic clans of dozens trek the lattice, etching spirals anew on heaving obelisks—not worship, but maps of stable paths amid the quake. Their voices are hums, plant-melodies that soothe aggressive flora or summon mote-veils as shields; in gatherings at flare-tides, they dance on moss-bridges, seeding hybrids from their own spore-sacs—children that bud from ribs, emerging slick and luminous.

Rarer still are the **voidsingers**, enigmas phased between flesh and dream, perhaps evolved from rift-hound packs or frondkin outcasts, or birthed wholesale from the whispers at chasm-edges. Ethereal bipeds with forms that flicker like misaligned shadows—torsos of translucent membrane stretched over bioluminescent cores, limbs dissolving into tendril-fringes that phase through fronds. They haunt the gravity-hiccups, where mists orb into rains, sustaining on pure ether and the echoes of absent voices. Singers do not hunt; they harmonize with the Lumivoid's instability, their keening flutes—carved from stalactite shards—triggering controlled quakes to unearth aquifers or flare light-waves to scatter predators. Solitary or in duets, they perch on basalt spires, casting shadows that birth illusions: phantom herds or builder-ghosts to mislead intruders. Touch a singer, and it unravels into vapor, reforming elsewhere—immortals of the seams, dreaming the cavern's surfacing.

These lives interlace in fragile web: mote-veils feeding veilwings, whose guano nourishes phosherim; skitterlords bridging for frondkin treks; rift-hounds culling the weak to sustain migrations; voidsingers weaving the chaos into fragile order. Yet the Lumivoid devours as it births—vortexes claiming herds, flares blinding flocks, upheavals entombing nests. Survivors adapt, mutate, emerge sharper: a millipede hybrid with bat-wings, a frondkin-veil that thinks in clouds. In this cold genesis, they are the pulse beneath the green, ghosts proving the void alive, forever remaking themselves in the luminous storm.
