Elevation bites the air thin, but heat defies it—geothermal fury boils beneath, radiating a swampy swelter that turns breath to steam and skin to slick sheen. Water doesn't pool in friendly streams; it's emergent, insidious—a high water table just subsurface, bubbling through cracks like the earth's own labored respiration, turning the basalt slabs treacherous with algal slicks that aren't quite algae, more like mineral precipitates mimicking life.

No green mars this expanse. Vegetation clings to absence at 0.2— a pre-life mineral world reclaimed by nothing organic. Obsidian panes and pumice drifts dominate, weathered to a settled patina (erosion 1.5), their surfaces etched with millennial rain-scars and heat-fissures, balanced in their stark equilibrium. Sulfur crystals crust the low spots, refracting light into blinding prisms; iron oxides stain the rockfaces rusty red, as if the plateau bleeds slow. Fungal tufts? None. Lichens? Barely ghosts. This is a world post-flora, or never floraed—a chemosynthetic domain where energy flows from heat and minerals, not sun and soil.

Bright light floods it all—2.6 harsh, unfiltered glare from a bloated sun low on the horizon, doubled by reflective rock-glints and steam rainbows. Emptiness reigns between presences—vast pumice flats, yawning gaps where nothing dares cluster.

Yet presences stir. Fauna thrives abundantly (2.1), a bizarre menagerie adapted to this sterile inferno—strange at 1.5, not monstrous but profoundly *off*. Leathery flyers with translucent wings like mica sheets glide silently on thermals, their bodies bloated with heat-stored fluids, beaks probing fissures for brine-crustaceans that scuttle like animated quartz pebbles. Ground-dwellers: packs of low-slung hexapods, shells fused from ingested obsidian, mandibles clicking mutely as they herd in the mist, exhaling faint vapor plumes. No roars, no chirps—soundscape at 0.1 hushes all to near-silence, broken only by the wet *thup* of condensing droplets or the distant, muffled *whoomp* of a geyser priming. Erosion's balance holds it all—forms settled, not crumbling, yet ever reshaped by vapor's subtle scour.

Chimneys vent superheated mist, not smoke, blending with natural vapors. Organized, yet estranged: buildings clustered in loose nodes around powerhouse-cores, plazas of fused pumice tile vast and empty, inhabited by crews in rad-suits who move like specters. No bustling streets; it's a settlement in equilibrium with the wild mineral chaos, machines harvesting the plateau's endless heat without dominating it. Drones flit mute among the flyers, cataloging fauna migrations for... reasons unspoken.

Stand here, and the place unfolds: heat presses like a living weight, mist beads on your lashes, bright light fractures into a thousand glares off wet obsidian. Silence amplifies the inner roar of your pulse against the outer hush—the wet *plink* of a droplet from an overhanging pipe, the faint scrape of hexapod legs on basalt. A geyser distant hisses awake, plume blooming slow and spectral; a flyer wheels overhead, its shadow a fleeting ripple. This high, hot, weeping mineral city-perch exists in quiet industry, fauna weaving through its spars, a world where life sips from rock and steam, balanced on the knife-edge of emergence. The plateau breathes; you feel it in your bones.
