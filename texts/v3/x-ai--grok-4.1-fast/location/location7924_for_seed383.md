The chasm unfurls below the crustal lid of the world, a regional hollow ten miles across and thrice that in length, its floor eroded to a primordial undulation—vast basins and subtle swells planed flat by eons of subterranean dissolution, where once-raging aquifers have gnawed stone into a final, somnolent repose. No jaggedness remains; the bedrock gleams as if polished by the patient breath of deep time, a mineral expanse of quartz-shot basalt and marble veins, slick with the damp kiss of condensation. Pockets of water gleam here and there—shallow meres fed by intermittent seeps from ceiling fissures high above, where cool air (hovering at a perpetual chill just above freezing) draws perpetual dew from the stone. Occasional rains drift down in silent veils, born from thermal vents that belch steam in lazy plumes, pooling in eroded cisterns before vanishing into gravel-throated sinks.

Light pours unrelenting, bright as a sub-surface dawn: fields of radiant crystals carpet the basins, their facets refracting a cold, actinic glare from phosphorescent lattices embedded in the walls—strata of fluorite and selenite that pulse faintly, casting hard shadows across the emptiness. No sun pierces this underworld, yet visibility stretches crisp to the hazy limits of the chasm, illuminating a sparse dominion where presences huddle amid yawning voids.

Scattered across this open mineral waste rise the bones of an urbanity wrought by primitive craft: town-scale enclaves of hand-hewn ziggurats and cyclopean arches, assembled from megablocks of local stone dragged by muscle and sinew, lashed with rawhide and bone pegs. No metal mars the scene—tools were flint chisels and obsidian blades, their makers long extinct or devolved into the fauna they half-resemble. These settlements cluster in threes or fives, kilometers apart, their terraced flanks pocked with burrow-halls and smoke-vents, layered in crude infrastrata of viaducts channeling seep-water to basal reservoirs. Erosion has softened their edges over geological spans, blurring sharp angles into ancient swells, yet their forms persist, organized ghosts of intent amid the sparse grid of the chasm floor—much emptiness between, balanced with the quiet insistence of presence.

Life here defies the vegetal void; no greensward claims the stone, no fungal fronds or algal mats persist beyond ghostly smears. This is a post-phytotic realm, mineral-skeletal, where ecology orbits the abundant fauna in ceaseless, turbulent churn. Creatures abound, a mid-weight profusion: swarms of thumb-sized crystal-hoppers that skitter in silent avalanches across luminous dunes, their carapaces chiming mutedly like shattering glass but swallowed by the hush. Winged scarabs the size of hawks wheel in vortexes, tracing chaotic spirals through the bright air, colliding in frenzied matings that birth clouds of larvae tumbling into the meres. Burrowers—blind, eel-thick worms with chitin mandibles—erupt from gravel in hyperactive surges, excavating and refilling tunnels in minutes, their passages collapsing in slow-motion cascades that reshape the terrain. Lemurkin glide between ziggurat spires on membrane sails, packs migrating in bazaar-like frenzy from one enclave to the next, dismantling crude totems with stone teeth before rebuilding elsewhere. Larger herds of quadrupedal grazers—scaled behemoths that gnaw crystal edges for mineral milk—stampede in unpredictable waves, their hooves pulverizing dunes into fresh silt, stirring meres into frothy chaos. The air thrums with this anthill-energy: constant creation and destruction, tunnels born and breached, crystal shards flung in glittering storms, water pockets swelling and bursting under the press of bodies. Yet sound betrays none of it—only the barest hush: a distant *plink* of condensation, the velvet rustle of a million tiny limbs, wing-whispers fading into silence, the subterranean world in turbulent ballet upon a stage of quiet.

Mildly unusual laws bend reality here, woven into the fabric: crystals grow and shatter in rhythmic cycles, extruding blades that impale the unwary before subliming to vapor; water defies gravity in places, climbing walls in thin films to nourish hidden aquifers; fauna exhibit faint telemetries, flocks veering as one to avoid cave-flutters, a collective instinct humming beneath the sparsity. The primitive builders' remnants—carved totems depicting these beasts as progenitors—hint at a settled finality, their urban sprawl now a scaffold for the endless, dynamic churn. To stand amid it is to feel the chasm breathe: cool dampness beading on skin, brightness etching every fleeing shadow, the ground subtly shifting underfoot as a nearby burrow collapses in voiceless exuberance, remaking itself anew. The place endures, ancient and alive in its quiet cataclysm.
