The basin cradles itself at the lip of the world, a shallow scoop in the high spine of nameless peaks—perhaps two hundred paces across at its widest, hemmed by sheer walls that rise no more than thrice the height of a tall tree, forming a natural cloister of stone. Elevation presses the air thin yet warm, a perpetual sauna-balm that clings to the skin like a lover's breath, heated from unseen geothermal stirrings below. Mist condenses ceaselessly from this warmth, beading on every surface, trickling in lazy rivulets that never quite rush, pooling instead in the basin's cracked floor where the water table kisses the surface like a hidden spring dreaming upward.

The terrain is a single, settled form: an ancient caldera, long since eroded into equilibrium, its rims smoothed by uncounted millennia of wind-whittled patience. No jagged edges scar the basalt and granite; instead, fluted columns and bulbous outcrops emerge like the vertebrae of some colossal, slumbering beast, their surfaces polished to a waxen sheen by persistent damp. Water weeps from fissures in these rocks—not in falls or streams, but in translucent membranes, sheets that shimmer and shift with glacial slowness, refracting normal daylight into a diffused glow that paints the air with subtle veils of pearl and aquamarine. The ground beneath is a mosaic of moss-paved slabs, tilted just enough to guide the seepage toward the center, where a sole, lens-shaped pool gathers, its surface unbroken save for the faintest ripples from condensing droplets.

Vegetation thrives here in lush defiance of the sparseness, each specimen a verdant monument claiming vast territories of emptiness. Massive ferns unfurl in solitary clusters, their fronds arching twenty feet high like cathedral vaults, frilled with droplets that tremble but never fall hastily. Scattered among them, pitcher-plants the size of rain barrels lurk, their hooded mouths eternally half-filled with nectar-laced water, veins of iridescent sap pulsing faintly beneath translucent skins—strange, for they seem to *draw* the mist inward, inflating and deflating over hours, as if respiring the air itself. Vines, thick as thighs, drape the stone walls in isolated tapestries, their leaves broad and heart-shaped, exhaling a sweet, fungal perfume that thickens the warmth. Yet between these giants yawn airy voids: bare stone expanses, mist-shrouded gaps where nothing grows, balancing the lushness with eloquent absence. No undergrowth chokes the floor; the ecology is one of deliberate isolation, plants rooted meters apart, their root systems delving deep into the weeping substrate without rivalry.

No animals stir this sanctum—its silence is profound, broken only by the ghost-whispers of dripping water and the occasional, distant *plink* of a droplet surrendering from a fern-tip. The air hums with its own subdued rhythm, a low-frequency throb from the rocks themselves, too faint to name as sound, more a vibration felt in the chest. Light filters evenly from a diffused overcast sky, pooling in the mist without harsh shadows, illuminating the subtle strangeness: the water in the central pool *remembers* its ripples, patterns from hours past etched faintly on its surface like interference from a dream, fading only across languid days. The ferns' fronds bend not with wind—for there is none—but with the slow swell of their sap, curling open at dawn's warmth and reclining by dusk over spans of quiet hours. Erosion's mastery reveals itself in balanced impossibilities: a thirty-foot pillar leans at an angle that defies gravity, propped by a single vine whose grip has weathered deeper than the stone around it.

This place obeys unhurried rules. Time dilates; a single cycle of mist-rise and pool-swelling might span a full day, with changes so gradual they evade the eye unless one lingers for weeks. The primitive lingers in the natural: hand-worn hollows in the rocks, perhaps accidental or ancestral, where water gathers for hypothetical sipping, but no tools mar the scene—only the untouched craft of aeons. Strangeness accrues in the cohesion: enter, and the warmth seeps into your bones, urging torpor; drink from the pool, and visions of ancient seepages flicker unbidden, memories not your own. The basin is a lung of the mountain, exhaling equilibrium, inviting surrender to its slow, wet pulse. It has always been, and in its sparseness, it is whole.
