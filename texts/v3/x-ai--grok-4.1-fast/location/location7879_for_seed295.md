The plateau hangs at the roof of the world, a vast, undulating expanse where the air thins to a perpetual hush broken only by the roar from within. This is no bare mountain crown of stone and ice; every inch, every curve, every crevice is the work of uncounted generations of weavers—flesh-and-chitin architects whose bodies secrete the very ground you stand upon. The terrain rises in slow, eroded swells like the back of some colossal beast long settled into its form: towers of compacted earth and resin, weathered smooth by millennia of wind and their own patient expansions, honeycombed with tunnels that breathe as one. No primordial rock peeks through; the substrate is theirs alone, a living mortar of saliva-hardened soil, fungal mycelium, and shed exoskeletons, layered in dense strata that speak of eons in balance—erosion met by ceaseless, gradual repair.

Dampness clings everywhere, a subtle mist born of condensation in the depths and sporadic seepages from hidden aquifers tapped by root-like probes. Water pools in shallow, lens-shaped depressions on the plateau's broader flats, fed by occasional highland squalls that patter down like distant applause before vanishing into the porous skin. Rivulets trace sinuous paths along the contours, not raging rivers but patient trickles that carve no new wounds, only polishing the established forms. The air is warm, a humid embrace hovering at the edge of swelter, generated from below by the metabolic furnace of the teeming masses—heat rising through vents and shafts like breath from lungs.

Vegetation erupts in lush defiance of the altitude, not sparse alpine scrub but a verdant pelt of mosses, lichens, and ferns that cascade from every protrusion. These are no wild invaders; they are cultivated, intertwined with the structure itself—symbiotic gardens tended by the weavers' secretions, their spores dispersed in deliberate clouds. Broad-leaved prothallia unfurl in the damper hollows, epiphytic bromeliads perch on spire-tips like jeweled crowns, and fungal blooms pulse with bioluminescent veins, casting faint, erratic glimmers in the gloom. It's dense, this green abundance: layers upon layers, vines threading through chambers, root mats binding the walls, a saturation that blurs the line between builder and built.

Darkness rules here, a thick, velvety obscurity pierced only by the wan glow of phosphorescent fungi and the rare shaft of clouded daylight filtering through disguised skylights—fissures artfully masked by overgrowth. The plateau spans a regional sweep, open and multi-featured: a central "valley" basin ringed by spires that climb two hundred meters, flanked by terraced slopes descending to sheer drop-offs where the structure meets the abyss. You could wander for days, from the echoing grand halls (cavernous voids for assembly, floored in woven silk) to labyrinthine suburbs of spindle tunnels, never seeing the horizon but sensing its vastness in the echoing calls.

And the fauna—oh, the fauna is the pulse, the purpose, the overwhelming tide. They are everywhere, a dense saturation of life brushing against itself: trillions of weavers (wingless termites the size of your thumb, pale and purposeful) marching in columns that snake across floors and walls, their smaller kin scuttling in the seams. Predatory beetles patrol the fringes, iridescent wasps dart through airways, spiders drape silken veils over doorways, and burrowing worms aerate the depths. Bats wheel in the higher vaults, gleaning from the aerial plankton of shed wings and spores; blind cave crickets leap through understories, their antennae tasting the humid dark. It's layered, near-touching multiplicity—bodies piled in nurseries, swarms clotting vents, a high-saturation ecology where every volume hums with presences.

The sound is deafening, a ceaseless symphony that drowns the self: the rustle-click-scrape of mandibles grinding cellulose into paste, the thunderous rust of wings from scouting flights, the chittering choruses of nymphs in vast hatcheries, the low moan of air rushing through flues as pheromonal signals propagate. It builds in waves, not frantic but relentless, a wall of noise from slow-cycling shifts—dawn processions at the faint light-crack, dusk congregations in the core—changing across hours and days with patient rhythms.

The rules of this world are woven into its flesh: all is biological, grown not hewn. No metal gleams, no silicon whirs; tools are mandibles and spinnerets, machines are glands and instincts. The weavers expand the plateau incrementally, a millimeter at a time, their queens in buried citadels laying eggs in rhythms tied to the moon's veiled phases. Predators are culled by communal venom sprays; waste is recycled into fertilizer for the gardens. Conflicts resolve in ritual combats, victors claiming tunnels through chemical consensus. Time moves slowly here—colonies rise and fall over centuries, weathering storms that batter the spires but never unmake them. The structure endures, in balance with its own weight, its warmth sustaining the cold heights above, its darkness a cradle for the endless labor.

Stand still, and the floor quivers faintly underfoot, a respiration syncing with your own. Brush a wall, and it yields like living clay, already reforming behind your touch. This is their city, their mountain, their world—total, ancient, alive.
