They weep a viscous primer of minerals, precursors to sediment, dribbling into the all-encompassing water that fills every crevice, every pore. The liquid here is not mere ocean but a hyperdense brine, cold as the void between stars—hovering just above freezing, it clings with the weight of lead syrup, pressing against your skin (or whatever passes for it in this realm) in a constant, intimate embrace that borders on suffocation. Visibility frays at the edges; dimness reigns, pierced only by the sporadic pulse of pallid chemiluminescence from microbial blooms that speckle the stone like failing embers in fog.

This microcosm teems without clamor. Sound is absent, or nearly so—only the barest susurrus of microcurrents slithering through the chamber, a hush like breath held in infinite suspension, punctuated by the occasional velvet *pop* of a gas vacuole imploding in the depths. No waves crash, no wind howls; dynamism unfolds in slow, predatory ballets and metabolic churns. Water cycles eternally within: cold upflows from nascent fissures below bubble faintly with dissolved iron and methane, swirling in lazy helices that birth and dissolve ephemeral eddies, carrying nutrient flakes that ignite brief phosphorescent trails. The brine stratifies in invisible layers—denser at the nadir, where silt gathers in embryonic dunes just beginning to hint at future relief; lighter aloft, where it quivers against the ceiling's pocked dome.

Erosion's embryonic hand ensures nothing endures: walls soften incrementally under acidic secretions, forcing constant reconstruction, a Sisyphean urbanism where collapse breeds innovation. Intruders feel it first as disorientation—the brine's density warps proprioception, making limbs heavy, senses smeared; linger too long and the cold osmosis draws heat from core to fringe, inviting symbiosis or dissolution into the throng.

Yet it thrives, this hidden polis of the depths. Currents ferry migrants from unseen vents: a clutch of barnacle-lanterns affix to the eaves, igniting new districts with their rhythmic flares; a rogue medusa-bat queen births parthenogenic hordes that claim a flank, erecting membranous arcades overnight. All in near-silence, in cold submersion, in the raw birthing of geology that might one day seal this pocket away forever—or birth siblings in adjacent fissures. To dwell here is to be enveloped in the city's breath, a guest amid the dense press of lives weaving their strange, ceaseless city from the void's own wet clay.
