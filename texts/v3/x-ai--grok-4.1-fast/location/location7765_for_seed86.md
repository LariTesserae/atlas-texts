The cavern sprawls eternally beneath the earth's unyielding crust, a vast, unbroken expanse of dim twilight where time has congealed into permanence. No dawn pierces this realm, no dusk descends; the air hangs cool and unchanging at a steady chill that seeps into stone and flesh alike, neither warming nor cooling across eons. Shadows pool in infinite gradations, lit faintly by the bioluminescent pulse of scattered phosphorescent fungi clinging to jagged ceilings—mere specks in the gloom, casting a hazy, perpetual gloaming that reveals outlines but devours details beyond arm's reach.

The terrain unfolds as a young labyrinth of sharp-edged fractures, as if the rock itself were born yesterday from some subterranean quake, unweathered and defiant. Immense chambers stretch regionally wide—a town-sized vault here giving way to a valley-like chasm there—linked by sinuous tunnels that twist without collapse or shift. Formations rise fresh and angular: knife-blade stalactites dagger downward from vaults hundreds of feet high, their tips glistening with fresh condensation; stalagmites thrust up in sparse clusters, faceted like uncooked crystals, their surfaces etched only by the faintest scoring from drips that have fallen unchangingly since formation. No smooth polish from wind or flood mars these features; edges remain razor-keen, walls sheer and uncrumbled, floors a fractured mosaic of slabs and boulders scattered amid open expanses of dusty gravel. Pockets of dampness persist in equilibrium—shallow pools of mineral-laced water mirror the faint glow from above, fed by intermittent condensation that beads eternally on overhangs and trickles in steady, metronomic rivulets down sheer faces. No rivers rage or dry up; moisture clings in balanced interstitial films, soaking mossy patches without flood or famine.

Vegetation is a whisper amid the sparseness: threadbare carpets of pale lichens and wiry mosses hug the dampest ledges, their fronds unmoving in the still air, sparse enough that vast expanses of bare limestone gleam between them. No trees claw for light, no vines entwine; only these low, fungal kin persist, their dim chemiluminescence a fragile counterpoint to the stone's gray palate—quartz veins flickering coldly, limestone veined with calcite in milky scars, all materials raw and primordial, untouched by tool or tide.

Yet life overruns this stasis in overwhelming profusion, a teeming equilibrium of fauna that fills the voids without altering them. Bats dominate by the millions, their leathery wings a perpetual storm blotting the faint glow as they wheel in ceaseless, circular flocks through the open vaults—never landing, never roosting, an endless migration tracing the same aerial circuits. Their echolocation shrieks compound into a deafening roar, bouncing off the vast acoustics to thunder like a ceaseless gale: piercing squeaks layering into a wall of sound that vibrates the stone, mingles with the percussive drip-plink of water echoing like distant rifle shots, and swells with the frantic buzz of swarming cave crickets whose legs scrape chitinous symphonies on rocks. Insects carpet the floors in shifting mosaics—blind beetles scuttling in rigid patrols, millipedes coiling through gravel in undulating waves, cave weta clinging to stalactites with legs that twitch in rhythmic frenzy. Spiders drape silks in taut veils across tunnels, their bodies pulsing as they harvest the aerial plankton of mites and springtails. Rodents—shrew-like creatures with oversized ears—dart in scattered bursts across open floors, their footfalls lost in the din, while translucent cave fish glide motionless schools in the shallow pools, gills fanning eternally. Predators lurk in sparse balance: sleek cave scorpions with stingers arched, prowling the fringes; blind snakes slithering in sinuous lines that never deviate. No dominance shifts, no extinctions loom; populations hold in frozen abundance, every niche saturated yet densities low—creatures scattered across the immense scale, granting the eye long vistas of emptiness pierced by sudden, swarming multitudes.

This world obeys immutable rules of subsurface eternity: gravity pulls unyieldingly, but no quakes rumble; air circulates in faint, laminar flows from unseen fissures, cool and damp-laden, bearing the mineral tang of wet stone and guano without breeze or gust. No seasons cycle, no migrations end; the fauna's clamor sustains a loud, invariant symphony—bat wings clapping like thunderclaps, cricket chirps rasping in octave swells, water percussion underpinning all—vibrating the cavern walls in perpetual resonance. Primitive instincts rule the beasts: no crafted nests mar the stone, no tools shape the flows; only natural burrows honey the bases of boulders, woven from detritus by mandibles and legs. Touch a surface, and the sharpness bites; breathe deep, and the cool damp fills lungs with echoes of ammonia and earth. Here, existence is a stable tableau, vast and echoing, alive with unceasing motion that circles without progress, a dim orchestra playing to empty halls forever.
