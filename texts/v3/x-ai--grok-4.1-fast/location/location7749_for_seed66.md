The plateau sprawls outward from the world's axial heights, a continental crown of eroded basalt and obsidian, where the spine of the planet pierces the thinning sky. Elevations here crest at five miles above the forgotten lowlands, but the air clings heavy and warm—geothermal vents threading the rock like buried arteries, exhaling steam that never quite dissipates. It's a warmth that permeates stone and steel alike, fostering a constant humidity that beads on every surface: water table high and treacherous, seeping through fissures in lazy rivulets, pooling in the lee of weathered buttresses, condensing from the fog into a perpetual post-deluge sheen. No true rain falls often, but the atmosphere weeps—vapors from below rising to kiss the chill drafts aloft, birthing ground mists that swallow the bases of the towers and turn the horizon into a blurred infinity.

This is a city without end, urban sprawl calcified into permanence across a thousand miles of highland. Call it the Forgeplains, or the Swarmcrown, or nothing at all—names erode like the land itself. Established over epochs, its forms are settled into equilibrium: ziggurats of quarried tuff and fused slag, their edges rounded by winds that have howled for millennia; viaducts of riveted iron, patinaed green and weeping rust into the cracks below; smokestacks piercing the overcast like the spines of slumbering leviathans, their output a haze that feeds back into the mist. Infrastructure layers upon itself in dense profusion—factories abutting hab-domes abutting forge-halls, all interwoven with catwalks, sluices, and conveyor spines that pulse with mechanical rhythm. Industrial tech dominates: belching steam engines the size of city blocks, crankshafts churning ore into alloys under electric arcs that snap like lightning trapped in cages; pneumatic hammers pounding in symphony; assembly lines birthing anonymous components for inscrutable purposes. Yet it's all grown organic in its weathering, balanced against the erosion—no aggressive expansion, just slow accretion, forms honed by time into their ideal states.

No green mars the palette. Vegetation is a ghost, absent or primordial at best—perhaps faint fungal crusts in shadowed crevices, mineral blooms mimicking life in silica fractals. This is a mineral world, pre- or post-phyto, where biology surrendered to rock and mechanism long ago. The ecology belongs instead to the fauna: overwhelming, omnipresent, a dense saturation of life that presses against every volume. They are the Aerids—winged, chitinous multitudes, fist-sized to crow-sized, their exoskeletons iridescent with metallic sheens from the forges' fallout. Billions—no, trillions—per square mile, a living fog denser than the water vapor. Swarms cascade from hive-spires embedded in the urban matrix, near-touching in their flows: scouting parties weaving through girder forests, maintenance crews stripping corrosion from turbine blades with mandibles that click like shears, colossal breeders pulsing eggs into geothermal nurseries. Larger specimens patrol the viaducts—six-limbed glide-beasts with leathery spans, herding the smaller ones; burrowing colossi tunneling aquifers for the city's cooling systems. They touch without collision, layered in behavioral strata: outer swarms for aerial labor, mid-layers for scavenging, depths for raw material processing. The city lives because they do; they are the infrastructure's blood, evolved or engineered to interface with the machines. Overwhelming not in ferocity but in sheer, inexorable *presence*—step into the streets and the air thickens with wings brushing skin, bodies parting like fluid around an intruder before reforming.

Light is mundane, daylight filtered through the perpetual overcast: a diffused normalcy, gray-gold at zenith, casting long shadows that crawl slowly across the expanse. Weirdness lurks at the edges—subtle, like the Aerids' faint bioluminescence syncing to factory rhythms, or the way water pools refract not quite true spectra—but nothing ruptures the familiar. Sounds define the place: loud, unrelenting, a cacophony woven into the fabric. Deep thrums of engines vibrate the stone underfoot; clanging pistons and venting steam hiss in counterpoint; Aerid multitudes produce their own roar—a chittering, buzzing drone like a trillion tuning forks, swelling to crescendo in migratory pulses, echoing off canyon-walls of tiered habs. No silence exists; even in the rare lulls, the geothermal murmur persists, water dripping into abyssal shafts.

Dynamics unfold in geological patience. Change is slow, gradients weak: swarms migrate across seasons measured in years, following heat blooms that shift by degrees per decade; factories cycle output in lunar-long phases, building up slag dunes that erode back into feedstock over centuries. The plateau's weather is its own—highland cyclones birthing in the distance, but here motion is minimal, fluxes subdued. Erosion holds court: winds sculpt facades into fluted grandeur, water undermines foundations only to deposit them elsewhere, all in balance. Rules of this world? Matter persists; the Aerids demand tribute of heat and ore, punishing neglect with blockages that resolve in ritualistic swarms; altitude taxes the lungs, but warmth rewards with vigor. Wander far enough and the city's edge dissolves into raw mesa—unbuilt wilds where Aerid hives dot the stone like stars, hinting this urban vastness is but one organ in a planetary body.

Deeper in, the mist parts to reveal a grand foundry basin: a crater-mile wide, walls carved into terraced workshops where Aerids spiral in vortexes around molten pours. Heat warps the air here, water flashing to steam on contact with cooling blooms of alloy. A glide-beast alights nearby, mandibles parting to regurgitate a payload of sorted quartz—its eyes compound mirrors reflecting the electric blue of arc-welders. The swarm parts for it, reforms; the din peaks, then evens. This place endures, a slow-breathing engine of warm stone and endless life, vast as a continent, dense as a hive-mind, wet with the world's exhaled breath.
