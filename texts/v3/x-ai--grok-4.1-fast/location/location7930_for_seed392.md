The valley sprawls under an unblinking sun, a vast basin of engineered flatness at the humid cradle of the world, where the air hangs thick and heavy like breath held forever. No raw earth pokes through; every inch is hewn or piled by hands wielding flint knives, bone chisels, and woven reed molds—mud bricks fired in communal pits, slabs of limestone dragged from unseen quarries, stacked into ziggurats and causeways that bite sharp against the sky. This is a city-forged plain, a regional expanse the size of a drowned kingdom, its edges lost in heat-shimmer where identical walls repeat into infinity, young and unweathered, their corners crisp as if laid yesterday, defying the wet that seeps eternally from below.

Water defines the stillness without motion: not rivers or rains, but a perpetual seep from the constructed underbelly. The brick-paved floors, laid in herringbone patterns of ochre and slate, weep condensation in glistening beads that pool in geometric sumps—shallow basins carved with thumb-pressed motifs of fish and eyes. Canals of mortared stone slice the expanse at right angles, their surfaces mirror-flat, holding water so still it reflects the blinding azure overhead without a ripple, as if the liquid were poured from molds and left to solidify in place. Humidity clings like a second skin, hot and equatorial, turning breath to vapor, clothes to sodden rags; the temperature hovers at the edge of endurance, a furnace breath from the sun that never arcs or sets, bathing everything in merciless, shadowless glare. Light bounces feral from polished shell inlays on walls, from mica-flecked plaster, amplifying the brightness until colors scream—vermilion clays, emerald glazes from crushed beetles, whites so pure they ache the eye.

Vegetation erupts in lush defiance of the sparseness, not a jungle blanket but deliberate invasions: thick-leaved vines with waxy, heart-shaped fronds coil up obelisks like living ropes, their roots prying at grout lines without crumbling the mortar, blooming trumpet flowers that droop heavy with nectar but never wilt or fall. Pockets of ferns unfurl in the lee of buttresses, sword-sized blades glossy and unmoving, fed by the water table that bubbles just beneath the slabs. Palms with fronds like vast feathers crown platform mounds, their trunks bulbous and armored in fibrous sheaths, casting no sway in the windless air. Scattered, yes—great voids of open paving stretch between these green outbreaks, balancing the lush with emptiness, as if the builders invited the wild to punctuate their dominion, then froze it in equilibrium.

Fauna teems in silent abundance, a menagerie held in perfect stasis. Herds of long-necked gazelles, their hides dappled in primitive tattoos of ochre spirals (branded by the city's anonymous crafters?), stand frozen mid-graze on mossy ledges, jaws parted around leaves that will never be swallowed. Birds with iridescent plumes—parrots mimicking the canal geometries in their wing patterns—perch immobile on corbels, beaks agape in eternal calls that produce no sound. Insects swarm in glass-clear hives of woven grass suspended from arches, wings blurred into stasis, legs posed in impossible balances; frogs bulge from lily pads in rooftop cisterns, throats inflated but throats silent. Lizards skitter frozen across walls, tails curled mid-flick, eyes fixed on flies embalmed in mid-air. Abundant, yet sparse in distribution—clustered at verdant nodes, leaving the plazas ghostly empty, their presence a quiet frenzy locked in time's vise.

Here reality frays at the seams, weirdness woven into the primitive craft like flaws in flawed stone. Archways lean at angles that should collapse but hover, defying gravity's pull, their keystones floating a finger's breadth above the void. Shadows, cast sharp by the relentless light, twist orthogonally—stretching east-west on north-south walls, pooling in corners as liquid ink that refuses to dry. Mirrors of burnished copper, hand-hammered into parabolic curves, reflect not the viewer but inverted echoes of the valley itself, recursing into fractal depths where identical cities nest eternally. Water in the canals holds memories: gaze long, and submerged faces of long-dead bricklayers stare back, mouthing words in a language of clicks too faint for sound. Vegetation bleeds surreal—vines bear fruit that pulses with inner light, splitting to reveal geodes of quartz instead of seeds; ferns whisper geometries when touched, etching mandalas into skin that fade but never heal. Fauna glitches the eye: a gazelle's leg bends backward at the knee without breaking stride (such as it is), birds' eyes reflect starfields under daytime sun, insects hum harmonics that bend light into prismatic halos, visible but soundless.

Sounds? A hush dominates, mostly quiet as a tomb's antechamber. No wind sighs through the fronds, no water laps, no beasts low or cry. Only faint subliminals persist: the wet kiss of dew on brick, a crystalline tick from contracting stones under heat, the muffled throb of something vast and arterial beneath the foundations—perhaps the heart of the world, pumping stasis. Equilibrium reigns supreme; nothing shifts, gradients erased. The sun nails the meridian, vines inch not toward light, fauna holds poses ordained at creation. Time pools here, invariant, a frozen jewel in the lowlands' swelter—cross a causeway today, and it greets you unchanged eons hence. This is the Builders' Eternity, a hot, bright marsh of monuments where life and artifice entwine in lush, sparse splendor, weeping wet and weird, silent under the gaze of an unchanging god. Step careful; the sharp edges cut dreams as easily as flesh.
