The land stretches out like an endless fabrication, a colossal expanse of low, undulating plains that cradle the horizon in every direction, baked under a relentless sun that hangs high and unyielding in a sky of uniform blue. This is no mere valley or basin—it's a continental sheet, vast enough that heat shimmers rise in waves across horizons lost to distance, birthing mirages of phantom spires and their own localized thermals, where faint clouds congeal and dissipate without ever committing to rain. Elevation hugs the sea level, a shallow depression pooling warmth like a solar furnace, where the air thickens to a humid shroud, temperature soaring into the high nineties Fahrenheit by midday, cooling only fractionally under the weight of night. Sweat beads instantly on skin, evaporates almost as quick, leaving a salty film; the ground itself exhales moisture in erratic pulses, damp but never drenched—pockets of slick condensation gather in the crevices of its making, fed by sporadic mists that roll in from unseen oceans or the infrequent drizzle that patters down like a half-hearted afterthought, lasting minutes before the sun reasserts its dominance.

Everything here is *made*, not born of tectonic whim or sedimentary patience, but constructed in a raw, embryonic frenzy of biological assembly. No naked rock pokes through; no primordial crust lies exposed. The terrain is the work of unseen architects—trillions upon trillions of microbes and fungi, a mycelial hegemony that has woven itself into a living substrate across this entire supercontinent. Imagine stromatolites bloated to planetary ambition: bulbous domes and sinuous ridges, some mere feet high, others swelling to low hills that crest at twenty or thirty meters, all extruded from calcium carbonate secretions, chitinous filaments, and gelatinous biofilms. These forms are *emerging*, still in their pre-formation adolescence—surfaces glisten with fresh precipitations of mineral crust, edges fraying into pseudopods that probe outward, inching across the void at a pace perceptible only over human lifetimes. The geology is under construction: where growth stalls in the heat, vast flats of compacted hyphae lie exposed, pale and fibrous like sun-bleached bone meal, cracking faintly underfoot into hexagonal plates that slowly curl and reform as subsurface networks pulse with nutrient flows. No erosion scars this place; wind and rare rains merely polish the nascent curves, while the builders themselves consolidate their work, layer by accretive layer.

Sparsity reigns amid the totality. The constructed plane is a sparse lattice—eighty percent emptiness, twenty percent presence—where domes and ridges stand isolated by kilometers of barren expanse, connected only by thread-thin rhizomes buried inches below the crust. Step from one mound to the next, and you're traversing a desert of pale, matte expanse, pocked here and there by fist-sized blisters of fresh secretion, or shallow dew-traps where moisture beads and reflects the normal daylight in prismatic sheens. Vegetation clings in the thinnest veneer: sparse tussocks of heat-tolerant algae mats, emerald smears no thicker than velvet on the shadier flanks of domes, or wiry lichens that etch fractal patterns into the fungal caps, thriving on the intermittent damp but never overwhelming the builders. No trees, no shrubs—just these faint green accents, wilted by day, subtly plumping with evening condensation.

The atmosphere hangs heavy, a soup of biogenic volatiles—earthy geosmin from fungal breath, sharp ozone tang from microbial photosynthesis, undercut by the mineral bite of calcifying excretions. Air moves in languid convection cells, stirred by the land's own heat engine: thermals rising from sunward slopes spawn dust devils that whirl fungal spores into lazy vortices, visible as faint golden hazes against the sky. No animal life stirs this realm; it is a world ordained for the infinitesimal empires below—fauna absent, leaving the ecology to a silent procession of bacteria, yeast, and mycelia in perpetual, unseen churn. Yet it is profoundly *active*, dynamic in its biological clockwork: domes throb faintly with peristaltic contractions, pumping fluids through capillary veins visible as branching blue rivulets beneath translucent skins; biofilms ripple as they photosynthesize, exhaling oxygen in micro-bubbles that burst with a wet *plink*; distant ridges groan softly as they extend, chitin cracking like distant thunder muffled by distance. Weather cycles imprint their rhythm—midday bake followed by twilight cooling that triggers spore releases, coating the air in a saccharine mist; nights bring a hush of dewfall, where condensation trickles into runnels, carving temporary micro-cañons destined to be overgrown by dawn.

Sounds are a whisper in the vastness: profound quietude, broken only by the *drip-drip* of gathering moisture from overhangs, the *schlurp* of expanding biofilms, or the remote *creak* of a ridge birthing a new lobe, propagating across the emptiness like echoes in a cathedral of flesh and stone. No wind howls, no leaves rustle—only the substrate's own subdued orchestra, felt more in the soles of boots than heard in ears. Touch it, and the rules of this world reveal themselves: the surface yields like firm gelatin under pressure, then rebounds with elastic memory; cut into a dome, and it weeps a viscous slurry that hardens to porcelain sheen within hours; linger too long, and symbiotic spores infiltrate fabrics, minds wandering to fungal logics of slow expansion. Here, time dilates—human pace frantic against the patient accrual of biomass; permanence is illusion, all locked in eternal construction. Wander far enough, and the curve of the planet hints at infinity, this living carpet unrolling toward poles unseen, a biosphere bootstrapping its own geology from damp heat and sparse ambition, indifferent to intruders, inexorably claiming the void.
