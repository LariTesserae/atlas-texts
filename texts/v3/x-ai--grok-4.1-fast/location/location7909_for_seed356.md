In the heart of an unmoving night, where no dawn has ever crested and no dusk has fallen—because time itself pools here like condensation on stone—a vastness unfolds. This is the Low Expanse, a continental basin the size of forgotten worlds, cradled low in the planet's belly, its edges jagged with the fresh wounds of emergence. Barely eight-tenths up from the hypothetical sea level that birthed it, the terrain recalls youth: sharp scarps of basalt and obsidian slice skyward in unworn blades, uneroded flanges protruding like the first teeth of a colossus mid-bite. Rain—or what passes for it in this weeping equilibrium—licks these faces ceaselessly, carving no grooves yet, for change is forbidden. The air hangs warm, a humid broth at fifteen degrees shy of fever, thick with vapor that never fully precipitates, never fully clears. Water is everywhere imminent: subsurface aquifers bulge the soil to a perpetual squelch, seeping through fissures in crystalline sheets; hollows brim with mirror-black pools that neither rise nor recede; fronds weep droplets in suspended arcs, and the very atmosphere condenses on your skin like a lover's breath turned possessive.

But sight? There is none. Light scores a mere tenth here—utter darkness, profound and structural, as if the Expanse were sunk beneath an infinite canopy of its own making or shrouded in a global pall of volcanic soot frozen mid-spew. You perceive only by the press of warm dampness, the faint resistance of air too saturated to stir. No stars pierce; no bioluminescence flickers. Shapes emerge from inference alone.

And yet, life—no, *plant*—claims totality. Vegetation at two-and-six-tenths: not mere coverage, but *substrate*, *architecture*, *ontology*. The Expanse is plant-flesh from horizonless horizon to horizonless horizon, a dense lattice (one-and-five-tenths crowded) where every cubic meter pulses with layered presences. Stalks thicken to boles the girth of ancient sequoia, but sharper-edged, their young bark unweathered, pleated into fluted columns that interlock without gap. Vines—millimetric to meter-thick—braid into suspension bridges spanning ravines, woven so tightly they bear their own weight and more, forming vaults that multiply the darkness beneath. Canopies stack in tiers: understory ferns unfurl like accordion bellows, mid-layers erupt in frond-walls of razor grass sheened with perpetual dew, overtopped by crown trees whose leaves merge into a seamless thatch, blotting even hypothetical photons. Roots delve shallow in the waterlogged marl, but laterally sprawl in mycorrhizal webs vast as cities, threading the soil into a living mortar. Mosses carpet every surface, not fluffy but plated, enameled in metallic sheens—coppers, irons, chromes extruded from sap—that glint only in imagination. Fungi? No, these are the plants' underbelly, chemosynthetic kin thriving on methane seeps and hydrothermal whispers from below, their fruiting bodies inflating like bellows to process gases into biomass. No gradients mar this greenblack mass: height varies but equilibrium holds; no tallest tree topples, no vine overtakes. It's a pure-plant ecosystem in stasis, where competition resolved eons ago into symbiosis-as-law. Ecology dictates: plants *are* the ecology, self-nourishing, self-sculpting, a biomass monolith where spores settle and germinate in preordained slots, filling voids before they form.

No animals stir this hegemony—fauna a null tenth, absent as ghosts in vacuum. No skitter, no wingbeat, no spoor trail. The silence enforces it: sounds a miserly two-tenths, a profound hush broken only by the softest inevitabilities—the plink of condensate from leaf-tip to pool, the subsonic gurgle of sap surging through xylem veins, the velvet hush of stem-swell against stem. Dynamic? Two-tenths: never changing, a frozen equilibrium where forces balance to nullity. Winds do not gust; mists do not drift; growth halts at perfection. This is thermodynamic nirvana, a landscape time-invariant, as if the Expanse were a snapshot etched in warm amber.

Yet strangeness blooms at two-and-two-tenths, the weirdness woven deepest in the "tech"—industrial at two-and-one-tenths, but untouched, unbuilt (one-tenth null). No factories of iron, no smokestacks belch; the plants *themselves* industrialize. Behold the bioforge: colossal stems pulse with hydraulic swells, pistons of pressurized sap driving bellows-fronds that exhale spores in metered clouds—each puff a production run, factories of pollen processing atmospheric volatiles into structural monomers. Root-mats churn substrate like conveyor belts, grinding regolith into nutrient paste, extruding it upward as fresh rhizomes in endless, silent assembly. Here, a thicket of tubular reeds acts as electrolytic cells, splitting water via bio-redox gradients, hydrogen vesicles bloating and bursting to propel spore-pods kilometers distant in micro-jets, all scaled to continental output: quadrillions of tons of biomass "refined" annually, yet waste zero, equilibrium absolute. Vast groves hum faintly—a collective infrasound like idling engines—where catalytic leaves distill hydrocarbons from peat, fueling contractile fibers that ratchet vines into tensioned scaffolds, erecting architecture before collapse can threaten. It's mechanized at planetary pitch: engines of cellulose, turbines of twisting lianas harnessing thermal updrafts in the warm dark, electrified by piezoelectric crystals in straining bark that spark silent currents through the lattice. No artifacts mar the wild; this is *emergent industry*, evolution's machineries writ vast, a self-replicating economy of pure phytomechanics, churning without progress, producing without purpose beyond stasis.

Wade deeper—the water yields ankle-soft, then knee— and the density envelops: tendrils brush, fronds enclose, the air itself textured with sporangial down. Warmth seeps from geothermal youth below, the basin's fresh formation tapping mantle heat. Sharp outcrops loom, unclimbed ramparts dripping, their facets etched with vine-scars that never deepen. Pools reflect nothing, but palpate alive with algal blooms—filaments threading water into living filters, purifying in industrial throughput. The scale asserts itself: weather of its own, but static—perpetual microconvection cells stirring vapor lazily over square thousands of kilometers, cloud-banks immobile as if nailed. No seasons cycle; the young geology holds its lines, unrounded. Here, in the darkwarmwet heart, you are intruder in equilibrium, the plants' vast industry indifferent, their quiet labors eternal. The Low Expanse endures, a continental forge of flesh, whispering its nullity into infinity.
