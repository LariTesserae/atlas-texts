The cavern breathes. Not with wind—there is no true wind here, only the subtle convection of heat rising from the humid floor—but with a living rhythm, a pulse of motion too faint to shatter the quiet. It is a single chamber, vast yet intimate, its ceiling lost in a haze twenty paces overhead, like the vaulted roof of some colossal temple long surrendered to roots and decay. Walls curve inward from a diameter no wider than a village green, enclosing this underworld grove in a womb of weathered stone. The rock is ancient, smoothed by eons of dripping dissolution: fluted columns of limestone rise in pale, rippled spires, their surfaces etched with the delicate filigree of past floods, now balanced in eternal poise—no fresh collapses, no raw fractures, just the settled patina of a place that has forgotten upheaval.

Heat presses close, a heavy blanket woven from the earth's core, thick and unrelenting at near-fever pitch. Your skin gleams with it, beads of sweat mingling with the air's own dampness. Moisture haunts the edges: not a raging river, but insidious veils of condensation that weep from every crevice, pooling in shallow basins amid the roots. Occasional rivulets trace the walls, fat droplets plinking into darkness below with a sound so soft it's more memory than noise—a sporadic *plink... plink...* swallowed by the vault. Mists rise lazily from these pockets, curling through the dense green heart like exhaled breath, intermittent as a sigh, keeping the air saturated yet never flooding the stone.

Lushness erupts everywhere the dim light touches. No barren cave this; vegetation claims every inch in stratified fury. From the floor, a carpet of mosses unfurls, thigh-deep in places, their fronds quivering with trapped vapor, bioluminescent spores flickering like scattered embers—blue-greens and faint violets, just enough glow (dim as perpetual twilight) to paint the chamber in ghostly hues without banishing shadow. Vines festoon the pillars, thick as arms, twisting upward in emerald ropes studded with broad, heart-shaped leaves that lap at the drips. Ferns crowd shoulder-to-shoulder, their fronds overlapping in canopies that layer the space: low creepers snag at ankles, mid-height brambles form thorny curtains, and higher still, epiphytic orchids dangle from cracks, petals furled against the heat. It's a jungle compressed into cathedral scale, dense as a thicket in a bottle—branches near-touching, leaves brushing leaves, a high-saturation press of life where no path persists for long.

Life teems silently within it. Fauna abound, a hidden multitude in constant, subdued motion. Beetles the size of fists scuttle through the moss, their carapaces iridescent under the glow, mandibles clicking faintly as they shear fungal caps. Bats—sleek, furred shadows—dart in whispering flights from ledge to ledge, echolocating in pulses too high for ears, snatching midges from the mist. Smaller things layer the air: translucent larvae wriggling on vines, pulsing with inner light; millipedes coiling through root tangles, legs whispering like dry leaves; blind salamanders gliding through the shallow pools, their gills feathering the water's surface with the barest ripple. Predators prowl the understory—a sinuous lizard with heat-sensing pits, uncoiling to snap at a fleeing roach; arboreal scorpions dropping from fronds onto softer prey. The ecosystem churns actively, predator tracing prey in endless cycles, spores drifting to germinate on dung, fronds uncurling to photosynthesize the scant glow from phosphorescent fungi—but all in near-silence. No roars, no crashes; only the hush of wingbeats, the sigh of scales on leaf, the muffled *crunch* of chitin under claw. Quiet reigns, broken only by those distant water notes.

Here and there, amid the green profusion, faint scars of the built intrude—scattered, half-claimed by the wild. A low plinth of worked stone protrudes from the moss, its edges softened by root-wedging, inscribed with faded glyphs now illegible under lichen. Opposite, vine-draped arches frame a dry alcove, their keystones cracked but holding, as if some forgotten rite once echoed here. No tools mar the scene, no machines hum; these are relics eroded into the natural order, biological tendrils reclaiming what hands once shaped. The place feels mildly askew from pure cavern—those glyphs hint at ritual purpose, the arches suggesting deliberate framing of some central pool—but it bends nothing toward the unnatural. It simply *is*, hot and damp and densely alive, a subterranean grove in quiet equilibrium, where every breath stirs the mist and every drip feeds the green. Step deeper, and the fronds close behind, the glow dims to suggestion, the life presses nearer still.
