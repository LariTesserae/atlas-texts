The air hits you first—thick, warm, and heavy with moisture, like stepping into the lung of some vast, breathing organism. It's a lowland cradle, barely risen above the unseen seep of groundwater, where the earth itself exhales in languid puffs of mist. This is no wild expanse but a bounded sanctum: a single vast hall, cathedral-huge yet intimately confined, its walls curving inward like the ribs of a forgotten behemoth. Measure it in strides from nave to apse—perhaps two hundred paces end to end, fifty across at the nave's swell—roofed by a lattice of iron-framed glass panes, fogged and streaked with perpetual condensation. Beyond the glass, the marsh-edge world lurks: flat, reed-fringed waters lapping at the foundation pilings, but inside, the true dominion unfolds.

Vegetation claims totality here, not as intruder but as sovereign architect. Vines as thick as thighs coil up wrought-iron columns, their leaves broad and waxy, dripping with dew that never fully dries. Palms arch overhead in layered canopies, fronds interlocking to form living vaults that filter the normal daylight into a perpetual emerald gloam—shafts of sun piercing through like stained-glass lancets. Ferns carpet the flagstone floors in dense, shoulder-high thickets, their rhizomes prying at grout lines softened by decades of humidity. Banyan-like figs have taken root in the cracks of brick walls, their aerial roots cascading down like structural cables, weaving into the scaffolding of catwalks and gantries. Every surface is substrate: rusting steel beams swathed in mosses that pulse faintly with captured warmth; concrete plinths hollowed into planters by root systems that mirror the hall's own geometry. It's a pure-plant ecosystem in balance, plants filling the volume without waste—trunks for pillars, tendrils for wiring, blossoms punctuating the green mass like forgotten lanterns. No bare earth shows; the ground is a living mat, saturated, yielding underfoot with the squelch of sphagnum and leaf litter.

Yet this is urban bone beneath the green flesh—industrial city writ small, a self-contained settlement scaled to one humming organ of the greater machine. The structure is a relic of the steam age, established and weathered into equilibrium: brick arches etched with lichen, ironwork pitted to a stable patina, no fresh scars of expansion or collapse. Factories in microcosm—boilers squat amid root tangles, their riveted plates furred with epiphytes; flywheels the size of millstones idle in shallow pools, algae-slick and veined by lianas. Conveyor belts, long arrested, serve as espalier for climbing orchids, their leather belts cracked but intact, looped through gears that gleam dully under verdigris. Pulleys and belts dangle from rafters, festooned with air plants; a central forge, cold now, hosts a grove of philodendrons sprouting from its ash-clogged hearth. Pipes snake along walls, some still faintly warm, sweating beads that nourish bromeliads cupped like chalices. This is organized construction layered into the biomass: infrastructure density without sprawl, town-scale ambition compressed into this room-grove, where human intent and vegetal persistence have eroded into symbiosis. No new builds mar the scene; it's in its own state, forms settled by time's patient chisel.

Warmth permeates—tropical languor at 80 degrees Fahrenheit, the air a condensing blanket that beads on every leaf and beam. Water is omnipresent, not raging but insistent: post-rain patter from the roof, marsh vapors rising through floor grates, springs welling in low spots to form mirror pools that reflect the canopy. Rivulets trace iron filigree, weeping from joints long sealed by plant gums. The water table ghosts the surface; step wrong, and your boot sinks into muck that bubbles slowly, releasing peat-scent.

Sounds dominate, loud and relentless yet unhurried—a chorus of industrial respiration tuned to slow cadences. Deep bass throbs from a bank of engines in the apse, pistons cycling with glacial deliberation, exhaling steam in sighs that hiss through valves half-choked by moss. Drip-drip symphonies cascade from heights, amplified by the vaulted acoustics into a percussive rain. Leaves rustle in faint convection currents, fronds clapping softly; somewhere, a fan blade whirs unevenly, grinding against overgrowth. Echoes layer the din—metallic clanks from settling chains, the glug of water in pipes, a low-frequency hum from dynamos that vibrate the flagstones. No silence intrudes; it's a dense auditory saturation, voices of mechanism and growth braided together.

Ecology is sparse beyond the green tide: fauna rare, ghosts in the plenty. A lone gecko skitters across a beam, its pads whispering; high above, a bird—perhaps a finch—trills once, then vanishes into frond shadows. Insects hum faintly, but no swarms; the biomass sustains itself, plants as total trophic base. No predators stalk; this world-rule is equilibrium, where succession has plateaued. Motion is minimal, dynamic subdued: steam pressure builds over hours, vines inch forward seasonally, condensation cycles from glass to soil in diurnal loops. Change accrues gradually—rust advances microns per month, roots thicken by fractions yearly. No tempests, no migrations; patience governs, weak gradients driving eternal slow flux.

Stand here, and the place reveals itself deeper: a micro-city of the verdant machine, where factory and jungle have weathered into one enduring form. The air tastes of ferrous tang and chlorophyll, warm breath drawing you inward, the loud heart beating slow. It holds you, this small wet world—complete, dense, alive in its settled hush.
