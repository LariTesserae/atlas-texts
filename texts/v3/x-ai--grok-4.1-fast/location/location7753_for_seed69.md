The air hits you first—thick, warm, and sopping, like breath from a fevered giant. You're high up, lungs straining against the thinness even as the heat wraps around you, a humid embrace that beads sweat on your skin before it mixes with the constant mist. Water is everywhere, not in rivers or seas but as a living presence: the ground underfoot is a saturated sponge of black peat and pulverized stone, sucking at your boots with each step, water table so near it seeps up through cracks in the eroded rock. This is no fresh deluge; the erosion here is ancient, equilibrated, forms sculpted over eons into sinuous balance—towering karst spires of limestone and basalt, their flanks wept by perpetual condensation, fluted and pocked where acidic rains have etched them smooth. But high, impossibly high: a vast plateau spanning continents, thrust up from some primordial upwelling, its edges dropping into abyssal chasms you sense more than see, winds howling faintly from those voids like distant accusations.

Darkness absolute, a blackness that devours even the memory of light. No stars pierce it, no bioluminescence flickers; this world has rules of its own, a cosmic occlusion perhaps, or a vault of lightless cloud-metal suspended eternally overhead, trapping heat below in this high crucible. You navigate by other means: the press of dense life against your body, the cacophony that assaults your ears, the tactile map of the terrain unfolding beneath probing fingers or staff.

Sound dominates, loud and unrelenting, a symphony of industrial fury interwoven with biological frenzy. Deep throbs pulse from scattered relics of mechanized ambition—vast, half-buried factories the size of cathedrals, their rusted hulks overgrown yet humming with residual life. Piston-whines and gear-grinds echo from within, automated even in abandonment, churning out god-knows-what from vats of viscous slurry fed by geothermal vents cracking the plateau's flesh. Steam hisses in geysers, electrified arcs crackle blindly through flooded conduits, sparks unseen but felt as ozone tang and static prickles on damp skin. These are not ruins but feral machines, scattered like the bones of a fallen empire: a lone smokestack belching chemical vapors into the mist, a conveyor belt rattling under creepers, its load of stamped metal ingots tumbling into moss-choked sumps. Industrial on a continental scale, but sparse, faint marks amid the wild—single smokeries, ore-crushers tilting on eroded plinths, their forges cold but dynamos whirring on inertia alone.

Yet the vegetation claims primacy, lush and stratified, a dense tangle pressing in from all sides. Vines like armored cables festoon the karst pillars, their leaves broad and leathery, dripping condensate that patters endlessly. Ferns unfurl in layered canopies overhead, impossible to see but felt as a rustling roof, roots drinking from the weeping rocks. Mosses carpet everything, thick as rugs, exhaling a fungal reek—earthy, metallic, alive. Bamboos creak and snap in the warm currents, groves of them clattering like bones in wind, while thorned lianas snare the unwary, their barbs greased with sap that burns like acid. It's a highland jungle-marsh, equatorial in warmth despite the altitude, waters condensing from the saturated air to pool in eroded basins, feeding a riot that layers three-deep: ground-huggers, mid-canopy climbers, aerial epiphytes dangling like nooses.

Life teems, abundant and ravenous, a dense press of presences in the black. Fauna everywhere—scales slithering over your calves, leathery wings batting past your face in flocks, the scuffle of claws on stone. Pack-hunters yip and screech in choruses, their bodies heat-radiating hulks barreling through underbrush, pursuing prey that thrashes and bleats. Something vast lumbers nearby, its footfalls quaking the peat, exhalations a guttural bellow that vibrates your ribs. Insects drone in swarms, wings a buzzing haze that clings to sweat-slick skin, their bites itching promises of fever. Strange they are, these beasts: predators with multiple mouths that click in ultrasonic harmonies, prey that mimics machinery with whirring appendages, echoing the factories' ghosts. Bioluminescence denied by the world's rule—no glow betrays them—but their movements stir thermals you feel, scents of musk and blood painting the air.

Dynamic, ceaseless: processes layer upon one another in this vastness. Rains drum sporadically, not sheets but veils condensing from the warm aloft, pattering on leaves before sluicing into sinkholes that burble and glorp. Predation cycles whirl—chases crashing through thickets, kills marked by wet snaps and slurps. Vegetation surges visibly by touch: tendrils coiling shut on trespassers, fruits splitting with pops to scatter spore-clouds that tickle the throat. The factories inject chaos too: a sudden flare of pressure as a boiler vents, scalding mist blasting outward; conveyor rhythms syncing oddly with herd migrations, herding beasts into grinders by accident or ancient design. Erosion persists in micro-motion, rocks groaning as they settle further into form, water carving fresh flutes overnight.

Weirdness seeps in, unbidden. Echoes don't decay right—shouts return layered, distorted, as if the plateau breathes them back warped. Waters in the sumps hum at infrasound frequencies, drawing fauna to drink and dissolve. Machines exhibit cunning: a drill-rig pivots blindly toward footsteps, probes extending like feelers. The darkness itself thickens in patches, tactile as velvet fog, muffling sound while amplifying vibration. Time dilates here amid the vastness; a night's trek feels like weeks, the plateau's scale birthing microclimates—warmer hollows steamy as saunas, ridge-crests lashed by chill updrafts. Balance holds, though: lushness tempers the industrial scars, fauna preys on machine-spawned vermin, water weathers all to equilibrium.

Deeper in, the place reveals more: cave-mouths exhaling warm drafts riddled with root-mats, their interiors factories' underbellies where pistons churn blind. Flocks wheel overhead, vast as storms, their downbeats a gale. A riverine pulse grows—waters rushing unseen through canyons, foam hissing against boulders. Touch a spire's flank: it's etched with faint reliefs, not natural but machined glyphs, repeating fractally. The ecology pulses as one organism, industrial and organic fused in the dark, warm wet vastness—a highland abyss in perpetual industrious night, where sound is sight and motion is god.
