The plateau sprawls under a vast, unfiltered sky pierced by twin suns that blaze with relentless clarity, their light refracting through a perpetual veil of condensing vapor into sharp, crystalline shafts that illuminate every crevice without mercy. This is no natural highland; the entire expanse—stretching across a regional bowl some miles wide, cradled by jagged escarpments that drop into unseen abysses—is a monolithic edifice of hand-hewn stone, assembled with the crude precision of mallet and chisel. Every square inch pulses with artificiality: no bare earth, no primordial bedrock protrudes. The "ground" is a seamless mosaic of fitted slabs, some veined with primitive copper wiring that faintly glints but serves no evident purpose, laid down as if by legions of laborers who vanished upon completion. This young construct bears the rawness of emergence—edges knife-sharp, corners unweathered, surfaces etched only by the merest patina of moisture rather than eons of wind or ice.

Cool air hangs heavy, laced with the mineral tang of wet stone, temperatures hovering in a brisk chill that draws moisture from the very atmosphere. Water defines the rhythm here: not raging rivers or pounding storms, but an insidious, constant seepage. The stones weep. Porous basalt and limestone blocks, laboriously quarried and stacked into sheer walls that rise a hundred feet or more, draw condensation from the damp air like living sponges. Rivulets trace deliberate channels—hand-grooved gutters that snake across facades, pooling in shallow basins carved into plazas that are themselves vast, interlocking platforms. The water table lurks inches below the slabbed surface, bubbling up through intentional fissures in slow, patient pulses, as if the plateau exhales. Mist gathers in the narrow defiles between structures, born of the cool exhalation against the bright heat, drifting in languid currents that shift over hours, not minutes. Post-rain without rain: the air is saturated, 80% humidity that beads on every edge, drips from overhangs in metronomic plinks, and fills the voids with a hazy luminescence.

Density saturates the scene—no vistas of emptiness, no breathing room. The built environment packs itself into a hypertrophied warren: cyclopean blocks form labyrinthine corridors just wide enough for a single figure, abutted by faceted towers that interlock without mortar, their surfaces incised with rudimentary friezes of geometric motifs—spirals and grids that hint at primitive toolcraft, perhaps etched with flint or early bronze awls. Balconies protrude without railings, architraves cantilever impossibly over voids filled not with air but with nested sub-structures: sub-levels of catwalks and buttresses, all crammed into continuity. It's a vertical city compressed horizontally, multi-featured across the scale of valleys—central ziggurats capped by flat observatories give way to terraced amphitheaters, then radiating grids of micro-chambers, every plane occupied by some facet of construction. No soil clings; no lichen stains the stone. This is a mineral realm, pre-life in its sterility, hostile to root or spore. Vegetation is an absence: the scant 0.3 whispers of fungal tufts in the deepest, unlit recesses—pale, threadlike mycelia probing grout lines, quenched by the ceaseless damp into dormancy.

Fauna echoes that void: nothing stirs. No skitter of insect, no avian cry. The ecology is null, a constructed sterility where biology was never invited. Bright light floods it all, banishing shadows to razor-thin slivers between blocks, highlighting the primitive tech's hallmarks—lever-holed stones, rope-burn scars on ledges, tool-marks like the bite of adzes everywhere. Yet a subtle weirdness infuses the whole: geometries strain against intuition. Walls lean inward at angles that defy gravity by fractions of degrees, stable yet wrong; water channels curve in logarithmic spirals that guide flows with unnatural efficiency, as if anticipating the slow gradients of evaporation and recharge. Echoes in the corridors carry a fractional delay, sounds bending oddly, suggesting the stone resonates with latent vibration—perhaps the remnant hum of some forgotten quarrying rite.

Mostly quiet reigns, broken only by the environment's own murmurs: the patient drip of condensation, amplified in vaulted spaces into a cavernous patter; the faint slosh of water shifting in subsurface cisterns over long cycles; a low, subsonic groan from thermal expansion as the cool stone warms infinitesimally under the suns' gaze. Dynamic is glacial—mist thickens toward dusk over hours, water levels rise and fall with tidal irrelevance unbound to moon or sea, surfaces patina with mineral crusts across seasons uncounted. Time here is patient, the young-built expanse evolving through deposition rather than decay: calcium blooms efflorescing on ledges, copper veins oxidizing to turquoise filigree. To stand amid this is to inhabit a machine of stillness, a high altar of wet stone under glaring skies, where the world is not given but imposed, sharp and saturated, waiting for whatever hands—or lacks thereof—first animated it. As you move, the mist parts reluctantly, revealing further layers of the packed infinity, each slab a testament to labor long past, now sovereign in its damp, bright hush. Deeper in, the air grows thicker with brine-scent, hinting at aquifers engineered below; the weirdness swells as symmetries repeat at impossible scales, a fractal city whispering of builders who saw patterns in the void we call empty.
