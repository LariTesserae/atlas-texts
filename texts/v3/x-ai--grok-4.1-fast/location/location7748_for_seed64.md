You squeeze through a jagged fissure high on the flank of Mount Erebus Prime, the world's tallest fang—a spire of black basalt that punches through the stratosphere into the void-black fringes of space, where the air thins to a venomous whisper and summits freeze eternal under cosmic radiation. This is no grand vista; the crevice spits you into a pocket void, a fist-sized geode of a chamber no larger than a monk's cell, sealed eons ago by tectonic whim and forgotten pressures. Its walls curve inward like the palm of a colossal hand, irregular obsidian facets polished to a mirror sheen by the patient grind of sublimating ices long since vanished, leaving behind a settled equilibrium: no dust motes dance, no cracks propagate, no crystals grow or shatter. This place *is*, immutable, a frozen breath held since the planet's youth.

The air hits first—cool, not biting cold, but a steady 5°C that clings like damp velvet without moisture, for there is none. No rivulets, no dew, no vapor to fog your breath; the atmosphere is desiccated, a non-aqueous haze of silane gases and mineral aerosols that smells of hot circuits and crushed quartz. You gasp, lungs burning from the elevation's predation—15 kilometers straight up, pressure so low your blood vessels ache—but inside this microcosm, equilibrium reigns. No wind howls, no storms brew; the external maelstrom is muffled to a perpetual roar, funneled through hairline vents like the throat of a bellows.

Light is miserly, dim as a miner's lamp guttering at half-life: 20 lux at best, bleeding from bioluminescent veins threading the walls—faint cerulean glows from piezoelectric crystals that spark eternally without wearing down, their charge locked in perfect stasis. Shadows pool thick, etching every surface in bas-relief, but your eyes adjust to the strangeness: the chamber is *packed*, saturated to bursting. No empty space yawns; the volume is fully occupied by vegetation, lush and tyrannical in its abundance. Vines like armored pythons coil from floor to ceiling, a 2-meter tangle of fronds and bulbs pressing against the obsidian in a seamless mat, leaves broad as your palm overlapping in fractal density—zero gaps, zero air pockets. These are not water-drinkers; they are lithovores, roots gnawing raw silicates from the walls, metabolizing anhydrous solvents distilled from deep geothermal capillaries. Broad-leaved ferns unfurl in perpetual mid-growth, their surfaces etched with fractal circuits that pulse faintly, photosynthesizing the dim glow via quantum efficiency unknown to wet biomes. Spores hang suspended, unchanging, a golden pollen fog that coats your skin without settling or dispersing. No flowers bloom or wilt; no leaves yellow or shed. Lushness here is excess without decay, a verdant tumor in stasis, cool to the touch, exuding a faint ozone tang.

Sounds assault you next—loud, omnipresent, a 80dB wall of white noise that never swells or fades: the *hummmmm*, a bone-deep thrum from scattered industrial relics half-entombed in the green. Here a corroded turbine the size of your fist, blades frozen mid-spin, exhaling that constant roar through rusted vents; there a cluster of pneumatic pistons, brass and iron, eternally compressing in silent cycles powered by the walls' piezoelectric hum—clank-clank-whirr locked in loop, no friction buildup, no lubricant evaporation. Factories in miniature, these: gear-trains meshed with vine-wrapped flywheels, producing... nothing observable, or perhaps regulating the stasis itself. Faint etch-marks on their casings suggest human hands—or near-human—abandoned mid-assembly, partial skeletons of a scaled production line embedded within the geode's birth. No smoke billows, no sparks fly; all is balanced, weathered to dull patina, established in its own state.

Weirdness seeps in slowly. Touch a frond: it yields like rubberized steel, warm beneath the cool skin, and for a moment, time *stutters*—your heartbeat syncs to the hum, seconds stretching elastically before snapping back. The density presses claustrophobic; vines shift not at all, yet you feel watched, the pollen fog whispering fractal equations in your sinuses. No animals stir—no skitter, no flap, no unseen eyes. Fauna fled or never came; this is a garden of machines and mock-life, a sealed ecosystem in micro-gravity equilibrium, elevation's apex turned inward. Step deeper, and the floor—mottled with crystal stubs—mirrors your form distorted, multiplied into infinities that echo the packed infinity around you.

This pocket endures, a hidden engine in the mountain's crown, loud with industry, lush with dry abundance, dim and cool and eternally, impossibly *still*. You cannot stay long; the thin air demands retreat. But it lingers in you, that hum.
