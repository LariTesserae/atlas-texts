The depths here are absolute, a colossal basin of engineered silence swallowed whole by an endless, primordial sea. This is no natural ocean floor; every grain of silt, every jutting ledge, every cavernous hollow was hewn from stone by hands wielding flint knives and hammered copper long before the floods claimed it all. The builders—gone to dust in some cataclysmic submersion eons past—crafted a continental sprawl at the planet's lowest cradle, a low-elevation expanse stretching horizons unseen, vast enough that currents eddy in their own sluggish gyres, birthing faint, localized tides that lap against the ruins over centuries rather than moments. Elevation sinks here to the bedrock of the world, or what passes for it: a fabricated substrate of cyclopean slabs, mortared with lime from calcined shells, now softened and pocked by the patient erosion of water's eternal whisper.

Cold permeates everything, a bone-deep chill hovering just above freezing, the kind that numbs flesh before it kills, water thick and heavy like congealing oil. Visibility claws for scraps in the near-total darkness—light: a rumor from fissures far above, diluted to gloom by fathoms of overlying sea. Phosphorescence is absent; no eerie glows betray life. Instead, the black is broken only by the faintest silt-shimmer when rare particulates drift past, or the imagined gleam of a tool-forged edge catching a stray photon from the surface world's oblivion.

The terrain unfolds in layers of ancient exhaustion, erosion's masterpiece at its endgame: not jagged ruins, but a near-flattened tableau, primordial in its stability, settled into a final, weathered form after millennia of tidal scouring. Spires that once pierced toward drowned skies have worn to squat mesas, their surfaces polished to a glassy sheen by ceaseless abrasion. Labyrinthine streets—wide boulevards flanked by toppled colonnades—have merged into a single, undulant plain, where walls blend seamlessly into floors, arches into arches, all fused by calcareous deposits and the slow accretion of precipitated minerals. The built environment is total, omnipresent: no wild seafloor intrudes. Vast aqueducts, now collapsed into sinuous channels, snake across the expanse, directing the water's languid flow. Domed reservoirs, cracked but intact, pool denser brines in their bowels. Terraced ziggurats, eroded to mere humps, step down into abyssal trenches engineered for forgotten industry—mineshafts that plumb deeper artificial bedrock, their mouths choked with rubble.

Yet amid this constructed desolation blooms lushness, defiant and dense. Vegetation claims every crevice, a verdant saturation verging on overcrowd: fronds of kelp-like macroalgae unfurl in thickets twenty meters tall, their blades broad and leathery, adapted to harvest scant nutrients from the water column. Fern-like hydrophytes, rooted in the porous mortar of ancient walls, form layered canopies that interlock and overlap, stems as thick as thighs weaving through perforated stonework. Coralline algae—crimson and calcified—encrust every surface in high-relief filigree, turning sheer faces into textured reefs without a single animal polyp. Vines of sea-grass analogs trail from ledge to ledge, their runners probing the dense matrix of growth, while bulbous sporophytes swell with spores, bobbing gently in the weak currents. This greenery thrives not on sun but on the chemical bounty seeping from the ruins—dissolved metals, nitrates from decayed organics long ago—fueled by bacterial symbiotes in a cold, chemotrophic economy. Density presses in: plants near-touching, layered ten deep in places, a saturated jungle where a hand's breadth of water might thread through twenty fronds, stems, and tendrils. No space unwon; the built world is a trellis for this verdant conquest.

Ecology hums in absence: no fauna stirs. No darting fish, no scuttling crabs, no leviathans gliding through the dark. The silence is near-absolute, soundscape a muffled hush—mostly quiet, broken only by the occasional groan of settling stone, the distant *crack* of a frost-heave in the cold substrate, or the protracted *swish* of a current parting the kelp. Bubbles rise infrequently from geothermal vents engineered into the foundations, their pop a solitary punctuation lost in the vastness. Dynamic unfolds in glacial patience: growth inches forward over decades, a frond elongating a finger's width per year; sediments settle in seasonal veils, reshaping contours across generations; weak gradients drive imperceptible drifts, minimal flux where tempests above might rage unseen.

Rules of this world bind tight to its making. Buoyancy governs motion—sinking is swift, ascent a labor against the weight of water. The primitive tech lingers in artifacts: obsidian blades wedged in crevices, bronze hooks corroded to verdigris, clay tablets etched with cuneiform now softened to illegibility, half-buried in algal mats. Tools speak of an age before iron: hand-adzed timbers petrified into the architecture, woven reed filters calcified into aqueduct walls. No machines hum, no forges glow; the makers' epoch is entombed, their environment now a self-sustaining mausoleum where water is both tomb and womb. To dwell here demands adaptation: breath held or gills contrived, warmth hoarded against the cold, light conjured from memory. Currents map invisible paths, the slow bleed of brine lenses creates hidden hypersaline dead zones where even plants thin. Time dilates; a day passes unmarked, epochs inscribed in the incremental etch of a single groove.

Deeper still, the basin reveals its scale's tyranny—a continental depression rivaling ancient seas, where weather brews internally: thermoclines that shift over lunar cycles, generating their own upwellings that fertilize the lushness from afar. Edge the periphery, and the ruins climb imperceptibly into steeper declivities, walls of hand-laid megaliths marching into denser darkness. Centerward, the great plaza endures, a flattened acropolis now a kelp-choked arena the size of lost empires, its flagstones buckled into low hills by root-pressures eons in building. Here, in the heart's black hush, the place settles deeper into itself—ancient, constructed, alive with green stillness, a drowned monument to hands that shaped a world now reshaping them in turn.
