The plane stretches without horizon, a continental wound eroded to its final, flattened sigh—once mountains, now a low cradle of primordial silt, pocked with shallow basins where water never fully recedes. It's warm here, a humid breath that clings like fever-sweat, air thick with condensing vapor that beads on your skin and drips from nowhere. Elevation barely rises; you're wading knee-deep in the aftermath of endless rains, the water table so high it seeps through cracks in your soles, turning every step into a reluctant slurp. Marshes bleed into flooded flats, weeping stones ooze ferrous tears that stain the murk rust-red, and the atmosphere itself weeps—fine mists that rise from the ground, curl into ephemeral pillars, then collapse back into the sopping earth.

But it's packed, this vastness, saturated to bursting. No voids, no breaths of empty air; the landscape is a seamless crush of presences, a living thrombus clotting the world's floor. Sparse vegetation dominates by sheer insistence: wiry reeds and thorn-mats, blackened by damp and age, interlock in a dense, horizontal snarl that covers every square meter without gap. Their roots plunge into the mud, braiding with fungal hyphae and calcified rhizomes into a continental mycelial net, pulsing faintly with bioluminescent throbs in the dimness. Leaves are rare, tattered flags on stems that twist together so tightly they form quivering walls, horizons of verdant razor-wire that undulate in the warm gusts. No trees, no canopy—just this low, endless pelt, eroded smooth over eons, flattened to a skin that hides the ancient bones beneath.

Light is dim, a perpetual gloaming filtered through bruise-purple clouds that roil at scale-of-its-own-physics altitudes, birthing localized weathers: sudden squalls that drench one hectare while parching the next. The sun, if it exists, is a diffused smear behind veils of condensing fog, casting everything in submarine twilight where shadows pool like oil. Visibility frays at the edges; forms blur into suggestion, and in the distance, mirages of solidity warp the packed mats into hovering barges.

Ecology is sparse but ravenous. Fauna appears rarely, and when it does, it's cataclysmic: hulking silhouettes like uprooted mangroves with lamprey mouths, slithering through the reed-walls on cilia-feet, or vast, translucent medusae that balloon from the shallows, trailing nerve-tentacles that snag on thorns and scream. They move seldom, conserving in the warmth, but their rare passages leave furrows that heal instantly, the mud knitting with vegetal slurries. Predation is ambient—microbes in the water devour flesh mid-stride, spores that bloom carnivorous when shadows deepen.

Reality breaks here, unspools at the seams with a wet rip. This is no stable end-state; erosion has worn the world to its oldest form, but dynamics deny rest. Turbulence reigns: the land is Solaris-storm incarnate, a hyperactive chaos where creation devours destruction in ceaseless bazaar-frenzy. Reed-mats heave like breathing lungs, splitting to birth geysers of scalding slurry that arc hundreds of meters before splattering back, fusing debris into new growths. Water surfaces boil without heat, erupting into fractal spires of liquid glass that shatter into vapor-swarms, reforming as hovering droplets that pelt the flats like mercury rain. The mycelial net convulses, roots erupting skyward in thorny cathedrals that collapse after heartbeats, pulverizing into loam that sprouts inverse flora—vines that grow downward into mirrored sub-worlds glimpsed through cracks. Time frays: petrified remnants of scattersome builds emerge half-digested—crumbling ziggurats of coral-bone, single obelisks etched with glyphs that shift under gaze, faint walls of eroded brick meshed into the thorns like fossilized veins. No tech mars this; all is biological-only, overgrown and reverted, the ancients' marks biological now, pulsing with sap or burrowing worms.

And the sound—deafening, omnipresent, a roar that colonizes your skull. It's the world's throat gargling: subterranean rivers thundering through primordial caverns, bursting in muffled detonations that ripple the flats. Reed-walls rasp in warm winds, a billion chitinous clicks amplified into white-noise hurricanes. Geysers shriek like flayed skins, water-smacks echo impossibly across the vastness, folding back on themselves in reality-warped choruses. Fauna's rare bellows boom planet-scale, infrasound that vibrates marrow; the mats themselves groan in tensile fury, roots grinding like tectonic plates. No silence exists; even stillness hums with the packed presences' friction—fungal pops, vapor hisses, the endless slosh of weeping earth. Your own breath drowns; thoughts fragment in the din.

Rules bend here: physics is suggestion, not law. Density forbids passage without violence—you tear through the mats, but they reseal behind, claiming shed cells as new tissue. Water defies level; it climbs thorns in anti-gravity sheens, pools in mid-air before crashing. Erosion continues in fast-forward: a reed withers to dust in minutes, its minerals sucked into neighbors, flattening anew. Scale warps perception; walk hours, and the storm you flee circles back, birthed from your wake. Life persists in the chaos, sparse but omnipacked, warm-wet cradle birthing its own unraveling. The plane endures, ancient and newborn, a vast scream woven into flesh.
