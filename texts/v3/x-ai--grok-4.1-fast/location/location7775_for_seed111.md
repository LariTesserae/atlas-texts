In the splintered crown of the world, where the mountain's final thrust against the sky has rent a single, cathedral-vaulted crevice—a gash no wider than a titan's outstretched arms, yet plunging a hundred strides deep into the unweathered heart of naked stone—this place exhales its own reluctant breath. It is a throat of the peak, freshly calved from some tectonic cough mere ages ago, its walls unsoftened by wind or flood: blades of granite and schist jut like the ribs of a godling slain in birth, razor-edged and milk-pale in the gloom, veined with quartz that catches no light because there is so little to catch. Elevation strangles the air here, thin and cool as the inside of a glacier's sigh, pressing a damp chill into your lungs with every labored inhale—condensation beads eternal on the overhangs, trickling in sluggish rivulets down the sheer faces, pooling in cupped hollows where the water's murmur joins the greater din. No rivers rage, only these intermittent weepings, fostering pockets of slick moss in the crevices, sparse as forgotten oaths: brittle fronds of liverwort, ghostly pale and threadbare, clinging to fissures like the last hairs of an old scalp.

Darkness reigns, absolute save for the faintest phosphorescent smears—fungi that pulse a sickly blue-green, too feeble to pierce more than arm's reach, leaving the chamber's bounds a void that presses close. The scale is intimate, bounded: the crevice floors out in a jumbled scree of house-sized boulders, ringed by walls that converge overhead into a jagged slit of starless sky, choked by perpetual cloud-veils that shed their mist like slow tears. No wind howls unchecked; change crawls here, patient as the mountain's own growth—the dampness waxes and wanes over days uncounted, lichens inch forward in geologic time, crystals nucleate one molecule at a time. Yet density saturates every inch: the air thrums thick with *presences*, layered and near-touching, a menagerie pressed into every crevice, every ledge, every shadowed fold until the stone seems alive with furred and chitinous multitudes.

They are the fauna's kingdom, overwhelming in their sheer saturation—creatures the size of hounds to thumb-joints, eyeless and pale or armored in iridescent shell, evolved in this abyssal nursery where light never tutored sight. Swarms of *skritchlings*—scuttling isopods the length of forearms, their carapaces etched with primitive glyphs like tally-marks from bone-shards they clutch as tools—clamber in dense carpets over the walls, mandibles clicking to etch crude burrows or fashion barbs from shed antlers. Larger kin lumber below: *lumberhulks*, bearish hulks with skin like wet velvet, too vast for the space yet contorting slowly through boulder-gaps, their hides encrusted with symbiotic *glowticks* that flicker erratically, casting no true illumination but teasing the outlines of fangs and claws. Above, in the rafters of stone, *flitterkin* flocks whirl in murmuration—bat-like with membranous wings stretched translucent, beaks hooked for gleaning skritchling eggs—while *coilworms* dangle from silken threads thicker than rope, their bodies undulating in hypnotic slow-motion, unspooling to snag passing prey with barbs that weep paralytic slime.

Sound is the overlord here, loud and unrelenting, a cacophony born of density's friction: the *skree-skree* of claws on stone echoes like a thousand knives sharpening in unison, *lumberhulk* bellows rumble from barrel-chests, guttural and drawn-out, vibrating the scree underfoot; *flitterkin* wings rasp in frantic hurricanes during their rare, ponderous mating spirals; coilworms *thrum* with inner vibrations that resonate through the damp air like distant thunder dragged low. No silence survives; even the water's patter amplifies into a percussive underbeat, and the mountain itself groans occasionally, a slow shift of stone protesting the weight of its menagerie.

Weirdness coils through it all, defying the eye's weak protests: these beasts are not mere animals but a living strangeness, where hierarchies blur—skritchlings harvest glowticks to "trade" luminescent nodules to lumberhulks in ritual exchanges, bartering light for passage; coilworms birth live young that scuttle off as new skritchlings, closing a cycle that feels like conspiracy. Time dilates in the thin air, movements languid: a lumberhulk might take an hour to cross the chamber's floor, pondering each step; flitterkin spirals unfold over minutes, not heartbeats. No fire burns, no metal gleams—tools are flint chips wedged into carapace-grooves, bone needles for stitching hides into crude slings—but survival is craft, etched into every interaction. Touch a wall, and it pulses faintly under your palm, alive with the slow crawl of hidden veins; breathe deep, and the cool damp carries scents of musk and ozone, mineral and molt.

This crevice is a world unto itself, untouched by hand or forge, where the peak's extremity has birthed a teeming darkness—raw, sharp, and densely woven, its slow pulse the heartbeat of something ancient yet newborn. Stand still too long, and the fauna senses the intruder: a skritchling probes your boot with a shard-tool, testing; a coilworm descends, curious. The mountain watches, damp and patient, as the chorus swells.
