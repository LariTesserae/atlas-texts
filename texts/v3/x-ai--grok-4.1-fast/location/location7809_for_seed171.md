It's a post-deluge world without the deluge's memory: the ground drinks greedily, holding moisture inches below the skin, birthing a thousand shallow mires where the substrate quivers like unset jelly.

But the rock doesn't endure solitude. Vegetation claims it all, a total saturation, packed to the brink of implosion. No bare earth persists; plants are the architecture, the substrate, the very grammar of existence here. High elevation thins the oxygen, but these flora thrive on it, hyper-efficient photosynthesizers hacking the dim light: a perpetual gloaming filters through layered canopies, 1.3 on the scale of luminescence, where a shrouded sun bleeds indigo and silver through perpetual overcast. Coolness permeates—10-15°C, a damp chill that beads on leaves and soaks through to the bone, mist coiling in slow rivers between stalks.

It's not wind, though gales born of the plateau's own microclimate whip across its vastness, stirring the canopy into symphonies of thrash and snap. No: the cacophony is vital, intrinsic. Growth is dynamic to the point of frenzy—stems lengthen meters per hour, tendrils quest and coil in minutes, entire thickets bloom and senesce in orchestrated waves. This is an ecosystem in perpetual motion, biological clocks ticking at human-scale speeds: waves of expansion ripple outward from nucleation points, petals unfurl like parachutes, roots deliquesce older growth to recycle nutrients on-site. No predators, no prey—just plant-on-plant Darwinism, allelopathy in gaseous volleys, faster than eyes can track.
