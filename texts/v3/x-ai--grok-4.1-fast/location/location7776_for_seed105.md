The elevation here asserts itself not in air but in verticality: sheer cliffs and needle-spires of basalt and schist erupt from the silty floor below, soaring two hundred, three hundred meters toward a ceiling that isn't there, lost in the uniform dark. Currents have planed the rocks smooth in places, gouging fluted columns and undercut arches, while mineral crusts—ochre iron oxides and pale silica blooms—cake the surfaces in weathered patinas, evidence of a world in its settled form, neither raw nor ruined, but patiently sculpted by the ceaseless flow.

Cold permeates everything, a bone-deep frigidity hovering near freezing, where the water thickens almost to syrup in the gloom, carrying the faint, briny tang of dissolved minerals and decaying fronds. Visibility claws at mere meters; light is a rumor, a scant 0.5 on the scale of illumination—eternal darkness broken only by the sporadic, sickly phosphorescence of the vegetation itself. These plants dominate the ecology, lush yet sparse, their presences scattered across the expanse like solitary sentinels in a vast hall of emptiness. Imagine kelp-analogs scaled to giants: towering stipes of translucent cartilage, thirty meters tall, anchored to rocky outcrops with rhizome-claws that burrow deep into the sediment. Broad phylloids unfurl from their crowns, veined with luminous filaments that pulse a dim emerald or bruised violet, casting wavering shadows on the substrate. Bladder-like pneumatocysts bob and drift, trapping microscopic particulates in the current, while secondary shoots sprout in fractal whorls, heavy with gelatinous spores that drift lazily before bursting in silent clouds. Yet density remains low; between these growths stretch barren expanses of rippled silt and bare stone, balanced with emptiness, the lushness a patchwork amid the void—no crowding, no understory tangle, just isolated groves swaying in isolation.

No animal life stirs. Not a fin flicks, not a mandible snaps. The ecology is botanical monarchy, ruled by these silently propagating forms, their life cycles a slow, inexorable rhythm: spores germinate on current-borne whim, mature fronds senesce and slough in fibrous tatters, nutrients cycle through the water column in endless recirculation. But dynamic forces animate it all—powerful tidal currents, born from some distant oceanic pulse or geothermal heartbeat, surge across the plateau in cycles of inflow and ebb, loud at 1.5 on the sonorous scale. It's active, this place: water boils from vents in geyseric fits, silt clouds billow and settle in waves, plants undulate in synchronized swells, spores eject in bursts timed to current peaks, erosion gnaws incrementally at ledges while mineral deposition counters it elsewhere. Processes overlap—thermo-convection drives micro-vortices that spin detritus into ephemeral cyclones, osmotic gradients draw nutrients from abyssal depths upward, fostering localized blooms that fade as quickly as they flare.

Scattered amid this are the built remnants, primitive at their core, tech-level echoes of hand-wrought craft from a pre-flood epoch. Faint marks on the landscape: low cairns of hand-stacked basalt, no taller than thrice human height, their stones fitted without mortar, now crusted in calcareous algae and tilted by current-scour. Occasional artifacts protrude—flint-like blades embedded in silt, their edges dulled; woven nets of fibrous kelp-stipe, petrified into rigid lattices draped over outcrops; single-room hovels of dry-laid schist, walls bowed inward, roofs collapsed into skeletal frames overgrown with frond-clusters that seem to claim them as natural extensions. Tools lie abandoned: hammers of horn-like coral, adzes hafted with sinew long rotted to ghosts, all simple manual craft, pre-metal, scattered singly across the plateaus like forgotten thoughts. No villages, no roads—just partial remains, integrated into the weathered balance, as if the builders were kin to the plants, transient presences dissolved back into the medium.

Echoes defy physics: sounds loop in cavernous reverberations, the *boom* of a distant surge arriving as a chorus from all directions, as if the plateau is hollow, an acoustic resonator amplifying the void. This place is alive in its stillness, a high-sea necropolis where the flood's logic inverted elevation—peaks now plumb the depths, darkness crowns the heights, and the lush voids sing their loud, lonely processes into eternity, evolving ever so slightly with each tidal breath. Drift deeper into a ravine, and the chorus swells; ascend a spire's flank, and the emptiness sharpens. It becomes more itself the longer you submerge, pulling contradictions into coherence: cold fire from vents, dark luminosity, sparse abundance, a world balanced on the knife-edge of submersion.
