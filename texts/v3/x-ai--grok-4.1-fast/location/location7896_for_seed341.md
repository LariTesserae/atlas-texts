The air hits first—a thick, sodden slap of heat, like stepping into the lung of some vast, breathing beast. It's a low hollow, barely a depression in the endless coastal flats, where the elevation sinks just enough to trap the sea's exhaled breath: water everywhere, not in rivers or ponds but in the bones of the place. The ground is a mosaic of cracked pavers and submerged slabs, water bubbling up through fissures like the earth's own sweat, high water table pressing from below. Post-deluge vibes linger eternally here; rains come in sheets from a sky that's never quite cleared, leaving everything slick, condensing mist that beads on every surface and drips in relentless patters. Weeping walls, yes—ferrous-red bricks of the old factories ooze rivulets that carve faint, established channels, erosion's patient work over decades, smoothing edges without toppling the structures. The water's warm, almost bath-hot, pooling ankle-deep in the open spaces between buildings, steaming faintly where it meets hotter iron.

Scale it small: this is no sprawling metropolis but a hamlet-clustered core, a single block's worth of industry hemmed by its own decay, boundaries marked by sagging chain-link fences half-drowned in murk and vine. Urban in intent—rows of two- and three-story warehouses, their facades stamped with faded company insignias (long-bankrupt syndicates of steam-barons)—but sparse, oh so sparse. Vast gaps yawn between them: empty lots the size of ballrooms, choked not with rubble but with lush, improbable greenery. Vegetation thrusts up defiant: broad-leaved ferns unfurl like elephant ears from cracks, their fronds heavy with dew; vinous creepers dangle from rusted gantries, blooming in fat, pallid flowers that pulse faintly; clusters of mangroves hunker at the edges, roots clawing into the shallows like arthritic fingers. Lush, yes, but scattered—each plant a solitary sovereign in its plot, ruling yards of emptiness, the density low enough that you can see forever through the haze, from one dripping girder to the next.

Hotter than a forge-heart, the temperature coils around 90-100°F, humidity cranked to suffocation; breath fogs your vision, clothes plaster to skin. Dim light filters through perpetual overcast, a bruised-gray dome that smothers midday to twilight gloom—perhaps the condensing atmosphere itself, thick with vapor from the ceaseless boil of geothermal vents beneath, or factory exhaust that never dissipates. Shadows pool deep in the alleys, making the scene feel intimate, cloistered, like a vast cathedral nave abandoned mid-rite.

Industrial tech thrums here, scaled for a town's needs but frozen in amber: belt-driven engines chug in open sheds, pistons slick with condensate slamming in hypnotic rhythm; flywheels the size of wagons spin behind chain barriers, their whoops and groans amplified by the enclosure. Electrified arc-lamps buzz overhead on sagging poles, casting erratic flickers that barely pierce the steam—wires festooned with moss, sparks occasionally leaping to ignite brief, phosphorescent flares in the mist. Factories churn without workers: automated looms clatter in windowless halls, weaving fibers from god-knows-what (seaweed? fungal strands?), extruders vomiting coils of pipe onto flooded floors where they float and drift. No people evident—just the machines, inherited from some bygone labor peak, now self-sustaining in a weird equilibrium.

Sounds? Loud, a cacophony that assaults from all directions, dynamic as a storm in perpetual motion. Steam whistles shriek every few minutes from pressure valves; water gurgles and splashes as pumps cycle brine from below, process after process—boil, condense, expel—cycling without cease. Gears grind, belts slap, anvils ring from hidden forges where metal shapes itself in response to heat-warps. The vegetation rustles too, fronds slapping wetly in thermals rising from grates, while unseen vents hiss ultrasonic whines that set teeth on edge. No animal calls pierce this—fauna near-absent, as if the noise or the chemistry repels life; perhaps a lone lizard flickers once across a beam, then vanishes forever. Ecology skewed mechanical: plants photosynthesize sluggishly in the dim, drawing minerals from the mineral-saturated floods; machines "feed" on geothermal steam and tidal surges, their corrosion balanced by the same erosion that sculpts the bricks.

But strangeness seeps in, elevating the weird: the water glows subtly after dark, a bioluminescent slurry from deep aquifers, painting paths in milky trails that shift like living ink. Shadows don't quite align—cast by lamps, they lag a beat behind the light, as if time frays at the edges. Machines exhibit faint agency: a conveyor pauses to "nudge" a fallen frond back into place, or steam vents pulse in sync with the mangrove breaths (tidal? organic?). Rules of this world bend: heat warps distance, so crossing an empty lot feels longer than it looks; water defies level, climbing walls in thin sheets via capillary sorcery; sounds propagate oddly, whispers from one factory booming in another across the gap. It's active, alive in its inertia—cycles of flood and ebb, rust and regrowth, machine-heartbeat and vine-creep, all in a hot, dim, loud ballet of settled strangeness. Stand here long, and the place reveals more: a vine tendril coiling experimentally toward your boot, a distant piston cough that mimics laughter, the mist parting just enough to hint at... something watching from the farthest empty lot, before the steam swallows it again.
