The cavern sprawls like a buried city-state, a regional expanse of jagged subterranean vaults hewn from limestone and basalt mere epochs ago—sharp-edged walls and fresh fractures speak of recent excavation, unweathered by time, their faces still dusted with the pale grit of drill scars and dynamite blasts. This is no primordial underworld but a young wound in the earth's crust, a valley-sized hollow perhaps a mile across and half that in depth, its ceiling a vaulted dome pocked with stalactite nubs just beginning to weep mineral tears. Air hangs cold, bone-deep, the kind that numbs fingertips after minutes and turns breath to fleeting ghosts; frost rims the edges of stone ledges, and metal surfaces gleam with rime. Dimness rules, a perpetual gloaming pierced only faintly by the amber glow of scattered arc lamps and sodium-vapor bulbs—relics of industry, strung on sagging cables that sway imperceptibly in the draft. Shadows pool thick in the recesses, swallowing details beyond arm's reach, though the vast openness of the space denies true claustrophobia; you sense its scale in the echoes, the way your voice would fragment across the gulf.

Dampness permeates without flooding: condensation beads on every surface, slicking rocks and foliage alike, while intermittent trickles snake down sheer faces from unseen aquifers above. Pockets of standing water gleam in hollows—shallow pools fringed with ice, fed by slow-dripping fissures or the occasional rivulet that murmurs briefly before vanishing into gravel beds. No raging rivers here, just the patient seep of moisture, enough to nurture life without drowning it.

Plants claim dominion, a pure vegetal hegemony that has surged into the void with relentless vigor. They are not a dense jungle but sparse titans—immense, architectural forms rising from the sparse-soiled floor like the pillars of some verdant basilica. Towering tree-ferns, thirty feet high with fronds like hammered copper, arch their crowns toward the faint light, their trunks swollen and buttressed as if engineered for load-bearing. Vines thicker than thighs coil up sheer cliffs, weaving living cables that span chasms, dripping with epiphytes that clutch moisture from the air. Mosses carpet boulders in velvet cloaks, and clusters of pale fungi—half-plant in their sessile ambition—form buttresses at the base of root systems. These flora fill the space not through multiplicity but through sheer mass: each specimen is a world unto itself, its leaves rasping faintly as they unfurl in imperceptibly slow cycles, growing inches per season in this chill. They form the substrate, the architecture—the ground yields to fibrous root-mats, walls to clinging tendrils, even the ceiling sprouts hanging gardens of aerial orchids. Ecology bends to them: rare fauna ekes a sparse living—pale insects scuttling in the duff, the occasional bat flitting ghostlike through the dim, or blind worms tunneling root-wrack—but predation is minimal, survival a quiet attrition. No birdsong, no rustle of herds; life is vegetal, patient, dominant.

Scattered amid this green vastness lie the marks of human endeavor, faint and half-reclaimed. Industrial tech lingers at a mid-tier hum—rusted steam engines the size of cottages, their pistons frozen mid-stroke, boilers cold but for the faint hiss of residual pressure. Factories reduced to skeletal husks: brick shells with collapsed roofs, conveyor belts dangling like vines from mezzanines, overgrown with ferns that pry apart corroded gears. A single intact dynamo whirs in the distance, its belts slapping rhythmically, powering the few lamps that sputter on. Partial remains dot the terrain—a toppled crane arm bridging a pool, ore carts derailed amid root tangles, faint rail lines vanishing into plant-choked tunnels. These artifacts are occasional, balanced with emptiness; wide plazas of cracked flagstone open between them, hemmed by plant-monoliths, where one might walk for minutes seeing only stone, frost, and green arches.

And the sound—deafening, omnipresent, a roar that presses the ears like physical weight. It is the cavern's voice: the ceaseless thunder of subterranean winds funneled through shafts, amplified by the vaulted acoustics; the guttural grind of idling machinery echoing from afar, pistons and turbines moaning in low register; the percussion of condensation falls magnified to hammer-strikes, water pattering into pools with cannon-crack resonance. No silence exists here—stepping feels like wading through white noise, voices lost, thoughts fragmented. Yet it is a slow dynamic: changes unfold across hours or days—the wind's pitch shifts gradually with crustal breaths, frost thickens imperceptibly toward glaze, plants extend fronds in millimeter increments. No tempests rage, no migrations sweep through; time dilates, processes patient as root descent.

In this world, rules bend to the cold's economy: fire sputters weakly, demanding constant fuel; light is rationed, hoarded in bulbs or reflected off wet stone; travel favors the open expanses between plant-pillars, where footing is sure on gravel or flag. Moisture sustains but chills; industry persists in dormancy, awaiting warmer sparks. It is a place of stark balances—vast yet sparse, alive yet hushed beneath the roar, young and raw, where green life slowly reclaims the machine's sharp legacy. Stand at its heart, and the dim expanse unfolds in layers: a fern-trunked colonnade framing a rusted silo, icicle-draped arches over a misty pool, the roar weaving it all into one relentless hymn.
