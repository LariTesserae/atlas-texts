### Physical Environment Preparation

#### Altitude and Climate Adaptation
This basin sits at high elevation in young, jagged mountains—likely 10,000-14,000 feet based on the "crisp" air despite geothermal warmth. Expect thin oxygen levels causing potential altitude sickness (headaches, nausea, fatigue). **Acclimatize beforehand**: Spend 1-2 days at similar altitudes (e.g., 8,000+ ft) via hikes or hyperbaric chambers. Bring portable oxygen concentrator or acetazolamide (Diamox) for prophylaxis (125mg twice daily, starting 24 hours prior). The air is humid (near 100%) and perpetually warm (60-75°F/15-24°C), like a high-altitude steam room—no cold snaps, but the crispness can chill sweat-soaked skin.

- **Clothing**: Layer breathable, moisture-wicking synthetics (merino wool base layer, Gore-Tex mid-layer). Quick-dry pants/shorts, long-sleeve shirt for UV/mist protection. Pack 2-3 changes; everything will stay damp. Waterproof overpants/jacket (no ponchos—they snag on rocks). Wide-brim hat or buff for constant diffuse light; polarized sunglasses despite no direct sun (reduces glare on wet rock). Sturdy, high-traction boots (Vibram soles, ankle support) with gaiters to block gravel/moss slurry. Spare laces—wet rocks make slips inevitable. Gloves: lightweight, grippy for slick holds.
- **Foot Care**: Expect spongy, waterlogged ground. Talcum powder/socks changes prevent blisters from eternal dampness.

#### Terrain and Mobility
Vast (10-mile-wide) valley with sheer cliffs, boulder fields, marshes, and slick schist/limestone slabs. No trails; everything weeps water, creating perpetual ice-like slipperiness without the freeze. **Mobility gear**:
- Trekking poles (2, adjustable, with carbide tips and rubber feet for marshy bits).
- Microspikes or lightweight crampons for exfoliating slabs.
- 30-50m climbing rope, harness, carabiners, helmet—for cirques/arêtes if exploring edges.
- Knee/elbow pads; the "endless drip" means frequent low crawls or slides.
- Pack light (20-30L daypack): hip belt for stability on uneven ground.

#### Hydration and Geothermal Hazards
Water is ubiquitous—seeps, pools, fog condensing on skin—but geothermal influence means potential mineral contaminants (sulfur, heavy metals). **Test first**: Bring water test kit (pH, bacteria, minerals) or LifeStraw/UV purifier. Boil via portable stove (geothermal steam could work, but risky). Carry 4-6L collapsible bottles; humidity hydrates somewhat, but exertion demands more. Watch for **hot vents**: Steam hisses signal scalding fissures—thermal imaging camera or probe stick to test ground temp before stepping. Insulated boot insoles; no barefoot exploration.

### Sustenance and Health

#### Food
No fauna, static vegetation (ferns/mosses/shrubs at "lush maturity"—likely tough/fibrous, indigestible, or mildly toxic in stasis). No fruits/roots evolve. **Self-sufficient for 3-7 days**:
- High-energy, no-cook: Energy bars (2,000-3,000 kcal/day), nuts, jerky, electrolyte gels (humidity causes sweat loss).
- Lightweight stove (esbit fuel tabs) + dehydrated meals.
- Multivitamins; iodine for thyroid (geothermal iodine possible).

#### Medical Kit (Comprehensive)
- Altitude meds (as above), painkillers (ibuprofen for headaches/slips).
- Antihistamines (mold/moss spores in fog), antifungals (damp-induced athlete's foot).
- Wound care: Antibiotic ointment, bandages (cuts from sharp basalt common).
- Blister kit, tweezers (splinters from reeds/thatch).
- Epinephrine auto-injector (rare allergy to exotic botanicals).
- Hypothermia blanket (unlikely but crisp air + wetness).
- Sanitation: Trowel, biodegradable wipes, hand sanitizer—marshes for catholes, but waterlogged soil slows decomposition.

#### Time and Biological Clock
Endless noon light, no shadows/seasons. Jet-lag-like disruption. **Circadian tools**: Headlamp (red mode for night simulation), mechanical watch/alarm (electronics might glitch in stasis?), blue-light blockers. Set arbitrary "days" (12h explore/12h rest). Melatonin for sleep rhythm.

### Navigation and Safety

#### Orientation
Basin is hemmed by peaks—hard to get truly lost, but fog/haze cloaks distances. No wind/current for drift cues. **Essentials**:
- GPS device (Garmin inReach satellite communicator—SOS/tracking, offline maps).
- Compass (geothermal magmas might interfere; backup sun-compass app or star charts if "night" simulated).
- Topo maps (pre-printed; mark seeps/huts).
- Whistle, signal mirror (diffuse light still reflects off wet surfaces).
- Leave cairns/ribbon markers—stasis might preserve them eternally.

#### Shelter and Exploration Base
Huts: Wattle-and-daub/thatch, stable but damp/mossy. Check for collapses (generations' rubble). **Bring**:
- Ultralight tent (2-person, with footprint) + tarp for vestibule.
- Sleeping pad (R-value 4+ for stone floors), silk liner (damp-proof).
- Base in central plaza: Flat stones, aqueduct water access.

#### The Stasis Factor
Time "dilates"—you age, place doesn't. No erosion/tools rust/moss overgrows your path. **Protect gear**: Silicone spray for metal (humidity corrosion), sealed dry bags. Exit plan: Timer/portals might warp subjective time; log entries hourly. Psychological anchor: Journal, photo log—document glyphs/copper to ground reality.

### Psychological and Sensory Preparation

#### The Unsettling Stillness
Absolute silence (only plinks/glurps), no life sounds, vast emptiness amid "deliberate sparseness." Eerie diorama vibe—like a video game paused mid-frame. **Prep**:
- Noise-cancelling headphones with ambient/nature tracks for breaks (ease sensory deprivation).
- Meditation apps or mindfulness training: Focus on water rhythms to attune.
- Solo wilderness experience: Prior 3-5 day solo hikes to build isolation tolerance.
- Mantras: "This is equilibrium, not abandonment."

#### Sensory Overload/Underload
Constant mist on skin, dripping everywhere, unchanging light—claustrophobic openness. **Mitigate**: Facial mist towel, eye drops (fog irritation). Scent neutralizers (earthy/mossy forever).

#### Social Void
Empty town screams "people were here." Loneliness amplified. **Combat**: Pre-record voice memos from loved ones. Bring book/music for "campfire" evenings (humming to self breaks hush).
