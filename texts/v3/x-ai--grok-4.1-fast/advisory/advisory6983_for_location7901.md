### Physical Fitness and Acclimatization

Focus on cardiovascular strength, as standing still invites trampling or predation—your survival hinges on perpetual movement across vast, open expanses.

Mentally steel yourself with exposure to extreme noise—blast recordings of stampeding elephants, jet engines, and wind tunnels at 100-110 dB for hours daily (the described "2.7" soundscape suggests 70-90 dB baseline with peaks exceeding 120 dB from herds or flocks). Consult a doctor for a full checkup: get vaccinations for tetanus, and screen for altitude sickness susceptibility (e.g., via Lake Louise criteria). Carry a prescription for acetazolamide (Diamox) to aid acclimatization, and practice hyperventilation techniques for oxygen boosts mid-stride.

### Clothing and Personal Protection
Layer for balmy, shirtsleeve warmth (15-25°C) with persistent dampness and wind-whips:
- **Base Layer**: Moisture-wicking synthetics (merino wool or advanced polyester like Polartec) to manage constant mist condensation. Avoid cotton—it retains water and chills on wind exposure.
- **Mid-Layer**: Lightweight, breathable fleece or softshell for the simmer-warmth without overheating during motion.
- **Outer Layer**: Gore-Tex or equivalent fully waterproof/breathable rain shell and pants with pit zips for ventilation. Seal seams; the fine, specter-like rains will soak through lesser gear.
- **Footwear**: High-traction mountaineering boots with aggressive, self-cleaning lugs (e.g., Vibram Megagrip) and integrated gaiters to repel mud from fungal blooms or dung. Ankle support is critical for fissures and scarps—add aftermarket rock plates for puncture resistance against root prybars or insect legs. Carry two pairs plus waterproof booties for overnight rests.
- **Head/Neck/Hands**: Wide-brim hat or buff for haze-glare (golden sunlight is unrelenting), UV-protective sunglasses with side shields (mirage-shimmer distorts depth perception). Noise-suppressing earplugs (high NRR 33+ dB, foam or custom-molded) mandatory—insert during flock overflights or herd passages. Thick gloves with reinforced palms for gripping slick vines or basalt; consider armored knuckles for brush-offs from questing lianas.
- **Full-Body Armor**: Custom moto-cross or adventure-race underlayer (kevlar/D3O pads on torso, limbs, joints) to blunt megaherbivore brushes, predator bounds, or falls. A deployable spike-proof overcoat (e.g., modern chainmail hybrid) for close encounters—fauna ignores the small but collides indifferently.

Camouflage in muted earth tones (basalt gray, moss green) to blend into the rock-plant void; avoid bright colors that draw aerial swarms.
