### Physiological and Acclimatization Preparation

Visiting this plateau demands treating your body like a fragile interloper in a realm that resents intruders. The air is thin and cold—think 4,000-6,000 meters elevation equivalent, with oxygen levels dipping to 12-15%—so hypoxia will hit fast: headaches, dizziness, fatigue, and "plateau brain" fog within hours if unacclimatized. Start with 2-4 weeks of high-altitude simulation: sleep in a hypoxic tent or train at real mountains (e.g., above 3,500m). Build cardiovascular endurance via stair climbers or hikes with a weighted pack; aim for 10-15km daily at altitude. Hydrate obsessively—dehydration amplifies thin-air woes—and preload with iron-rich foods (spinach, liver) to boost red blood cell production (get a blood test pre-trip; consider doctor-prescribed acetazolamide for AMS prevention). Cold exposure training: ice baths or outdoor winter camping to toughen skin and circulation against bone-deep chill that ignores layers. Test for spore/moss allergies via exposure to similar molds; carry an EpiPen. Mentally, simulate silence: spend days in sensory deprivation tanks to prep for the auditory void—no birds, insects, or rustling leaves, just your breath echoing like a thunderclap.

### Clothing and Personal Protection

Layer like your life depends on it—because the damp chill (0-5°C days, -10°C nights) seeps through everything, turning sweat to ice. Base layer: merino wool or synthetic thermals (wicking, no cotton). Mid-layers: fleece or down puffy (packable, 800+ fill power). Outer shell: Gore-Tex hardshell with full hood, pit zips, and taped seams—mist beads and pools, so taped boots and gaiters essential. Overpants for ground dampness. Gloves: insulated liners + waterproof shells; balaclava and goggles for wind-whipped mists (UV protection too, as pale sun glare bounces off polished stone). Footwear: insulated mountaineering boots (B3 rating, crampon-compatible for ice) with microspikes for slick granite slabs. Weirdness-proofing: add a "reality cloak" mimic—taffeta or silk poncho treated with hydrophobic spray (like NeverWet) to shed hovering droplets and phasing mists; iridescent fabric to blend with glowing mosses. Reflective strips for visibility in fog-beasts. No skin exposure—UV through overcast burns, and frost runes might "etch" unprotected flesh symbolically.
