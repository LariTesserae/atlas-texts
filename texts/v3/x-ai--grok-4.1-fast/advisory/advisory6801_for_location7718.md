### Physical and Physiological Preparation

This plateau's elevation—likely 3,000-5,000 meters based on the thinned air and breath-stealing vastness—demands altitude acclimatization as your top priority. An ordinary human without prior exposure risks acute mountain sickness (AMS): headaches, nausea, dizziness, fatigue, and worse like pulmonary/high-altitude edema. **Spend 1-2 weeks pre-adapting:** Ascend gradually in a similar environment (e.g., Andean altiplano or Tibetan plateau simulations). Use acetazolamide (Diamox) starting 24 hours before "arrival" at 125-250mg twice daily to speed acclimatization by acidifying blood and stimulating breathing. Pack a pulse oximeter to monitor SpO2 (aim for >85%; below 80% means descend or oxygen). Hyperventilate consciously in the first days—deep breaths every few minutes—to counter hypoxia. Hydrate aggressively: 4-6 liters/day minimum, as dry-ish cold air (despite mist) dehydrates via insensible losses. Urine should be clear; dark means trouble.

The pervasive chill (hovering 5-12°C/41-54°F, "1.3 temperate chill") seeps into bones without freezing outright, amplified by constant dampness (100% humidity feels clammy). Expect wind chill dropping effective temps to 0°C on exposed ridges. **Layer for moisture-wicking and modularity:** Base layer merino wool or synthetics (e.g., Patagonia Capilene) to pull sweat; mid-layer fleece or wool for insulation; outer Gore-Tex hardshell with pit zips for venting. Pack wool hat, balaclava, heavy gloves (waterproof mittens over liners), and gaiters. Footwear: Insulated, waterproof boots with aggressive lugs (e.g., La Sportiva Trango) for slippery mud-stone; spare insoles for wet feet. Change socks daily to prevent trench foot—dampness breeds fungal hell. A compact sleeping bag rated to -10°C (e.g., Western Mountaineering) and inflatable pad (R-value 4+) for any overnight; the ground stays cool even in "summer."

Water is omnipresent but suspect: seeps carry minerals, bacteria from human density. **Purification kit essential:** LifeStraw or Sawyer Squeeze for volume, plus chlorine dioxide tablets/UV pen for giardia/cryptosporidium in cisterns. Boil if possible (small stove/gas canister). Avoid untreated pools—ferrous mud hints iron bacteria. Food: High-carb, high-fat for cold/energy (nuts, jerky, energy gels); electrolytes (Nuun tabs) for hyponatremia risk. Pack a 7-10 day supply (dehydrated meals like Mountain House) as foraging is barren—sparse sedges/mosses offer zilch calorically. Iodine deficiency possible in lithic soils; multivitamin with iodine/selenium.

### Gear and Mobility Essentials

No wheels, no metal-heavy tech—this is a foot-and-fiber world. Travel light (15-20kg pack max) to navigate warrens, roof-streets, and terrace-steps. **Navigation:** GPS watch (e.g., Garmin Fenix) with offline topo maps pre-loaded for the plateau's curve-bending horizons (true north drifts visually). Compass/altimeter backup; star charts for overcast nights. The fractal city defies grids—mark alignments with ziggurats/aqueducts. Fractal patterns mean self-similar navigation: alleys mirror plazas.

**Core kit:**
- **Multi-tool:** Leatherman with stone-compatible bits (no flashy steel; wrap in cloth to dull shine).
- **Light:** Headlamp (Petzl Reactik, 400+ lumens) + spares; plateau dims early, city shadows swallow alleys.
- **Fire:** Ferro rod + fatwood/stormproof matches; peat-smoke hints tinder abundance, but dampness kills sparks.
- **Shelter:** Emergency bivy (Sol or SOL) + 20m paracord for tarps/roof-hopping; structures abut but privacy nil.
- **Medical:** Comprehensive kit—ibuprofen/acetaminophen (altitude headaches), loperamide (diarrhea), ciprofloxacin (infections), epinephrine auto-injector (rare allergies), blister kit, tweezers for splinters/thorns.
- **Misc:** 50m climbing webbing for terrace scrambles; binoculars for hawk-spotting distant districts; notebook/pen for sketching layouts (paper survives damp better than electronics).

Blend or stand out strategically: Dress in neutral earth-tones (mud-brown woolens, fiber-mimic tunics). Hide modern gear in a hand-woven reed satchel. Barefoot paths mean callused locals; your boots mark you outsider, but ankles snap on glassy earth.
