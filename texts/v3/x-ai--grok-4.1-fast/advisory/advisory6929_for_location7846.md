### Physiological Preparation: Surviving the Thin, Sweltering Air

Echolost's 3-mile elevation means oxygen is scarce—about half sea-level pressure—causing immediate hypoxia, headaches, dizziness, and pulmonary edema if unprepared. The constant blood-heat humidity (near 37°C/98.6°F with 2/3 saturation) accelerates dehydration despite the omnipresent water, as sweat evaporates slowly in the steam, leading to electrolyte imbalances and heat exhaustion within minutes.

- **Acclimatization**: Spend 1-2 weeks pre-trip at high-altitude simulators (e.g., 10,000-15,000 ft chambers) or real mountains like the Andes or Himalayas. Use hypoxic training tents at home to build red blood cell count. Take acetazolamide (Diamox) starting 24-48 hours before arrival to speed adaptation by acidifying blood and stimulating breathing.
  
- **Oxygen Support**: Carry a portable oxygen concentrator (e.g., battery-powered unit delivering 5-10 L/min) with extra tanks or rechargeable batteries (solar-compatible, given constant daylight). A personal oxygen mask or nasal cannula with demand-valve regulator for on-demand flow. For redundancy, include nitrox tanks (higher O2 scuba mixes) and a small compressor if multiverse tech allows.

- **Hydration and Thermoregulation**: Despite water everywhere, it's stagnant and potentially laced with geothermal minerals or plant exudates—boil or use UV/chemical purifiers (e.g., SteriPEN or Aquamira drops). Pack 5-7 liters of your own electrolytes (e.g., LMNT or Nuun tablets) daily, plus desiccated coconut water powder. Wear a phase-change cooling vest (absorbs heat via melting wax inserts) under moisture-wicking base layers (merino wool or synthetics like Coolmax). A wide-brim hat, neck gaiter, and UV-protective arm sleeves (UPF 50+) combat the unrelenting equatorial glare. Avoid cotton—it retains moisture and chafes in the swelter.

- **Medical Kit Essentials**: High-altitude meds (dexamethasone for edema, ibuprofen for headaches), anti-nausea (ondansetron), broad-spectrum antibiotics (for fungal infections from humid cuts), and electrolyte IV kits for emergencies. Include a pulse oximeter to monitor SpO2 (aim for >85%) and a portable hyperbaric bag for descent simulation if symptoms hit.
