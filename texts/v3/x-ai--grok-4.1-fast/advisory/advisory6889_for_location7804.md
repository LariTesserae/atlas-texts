### Physiological and Environmental Preparations

#### Breathing and Dive Systems
This chamber is **100% flooded with motionless, high-density water** (2.7 immersion scale), offering no air pocket whatsoever—equivalent to being fully suspended in a solid gel-like medium. As an ordinary human, you cannot survive more than 1-2 minutes without artificial breathing apparatus. Prepare for extended submersion (at least 30-60 minutes minimum, longer if exploring the machine's nooks):

- **Primary: Closed-Circuit Rebreather (CCR)**. Preferred over open-circuit SCUBA due to zero bubble production (bubbles would disrupt the "frozen equilibrium," potentially signaling instability). Models like the rEvo or Poseidon MKVI with trimix diluent (helium-oxygen-nitrogen blend: 18/45 for narcosis resistance at estimated 20-30m depth equivalent). Scrubber canister (Sofnolime 797) rated for 4-6 hours; bailout bottle (2L O2 + 3L trimix) strapped to chest.
- **Backup: Twinset SCUBA** (two 12L steel tanks, 232 bar, trimix fill) with balanced regulators (Apeks DST), octopus, and SPG. Total gas: 4,800L for safety margin.
- **Dive Computer**: Shearwater Perdix 2 with multi-gas, gradient factors (30/85 for conservative deco), and depth gauge calibrated for the chamber's uniform pressure (no gradients, but assume 2-3 ATA from description).
- **Mouthpiece Comfort**: Custom ortho for long dives; anti-fog mask treatment.

**Why CCR?** The stasis (dynamic 0.2) means no currents to disperse bubbles; they might linger, creating false motion in this "time-invariant" space, psychologically disorienting you.

#### Thermal Protection
Water temperature is a persistent **deep-spring chill (0.7 scale, ~10-12°C/50-54°F)**—not hypothermic immediately, but prolonged exposure (30+ min) risks core temp drop below 35°C without insulation. The water's "unyielding weight" conducts heat away efficiently.

- **Drysuit**: Trilaminate (e.g., Waterproof D1X) with heavy undergarments (thinsulate 400g/m² torso, 200g limbs). P-valve catheter for extended stays (no surfacing). Argon inflation for buoyancy tweaks via integrated wing (42L Apeks shear).
- **Neoprene Hood, Gloves, Socks**: 6.5mm hooded vest, 5mm gloves (palm-reinforced for machine handling), 7mm booties with rock boots over.
- **Thermal Layers**: Merino base + vapor barrier liner to prevent sweat chill during gear-up.

**Test Protocol**: Pre-dive cold soak in 12°C tub for 20 min to acclimate; hydrate with warm electrolytes.

#### Buoyancy and Propulsion
Zero currents, perfect stasis—motion feels "yielded nothing," like pushing through dense syrup. The small scale (1.1, 50 paces/40m diameter) demands precision to avoid bumping stalactites, algae, or the machine.

- **Wing/BCD**: 30-40L rear-mounted with corrugated hose for fine control.
- **Fins**: Long-blade jet fins (e.g., OMS) for efficient, low-kick propulsion without silt disturbance (floor cobbles are smoothed but fragile).
- **Weight**: 12-16kg trimix-adjusted (calculate via RBH app); ditchable belt for emergencies.
- **SMB/DSMB**: Finger-spool deployable surface marker for exit signaling (if portaling out isn't instant).

#### Visibility and Illumination
Diffuse, shadowless light (1.7, eternal "stained glass twilight") provides excellent clarity to the water's "glassy limits," but backup is essential if the chamber's source fails (unlikely, but multiverse anomalies).

- **Primary Mask**: Low-volume frameless (Atomic Aquatics) with wide field-of-view.
- **Lights**: 
  - Primary: Bigblue VL18000P (18k beam, 180° flood) on helmet mount.
  - Backup: 4x UK Cyalume chemlights (green, 12hr) and Petzl e+LITE headlamp (waterproofed).
