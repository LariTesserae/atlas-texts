### Essential Diving Equipment
Visiting this submerged industrial chamber demands professional-grade technical diving gear rated for deep, cold-water penetration diving in a high-biomass, low-visibility environment. Treat it like a 100-200+ meter deep wreck dive in the open ocean, but with zero air pockets, constant currents, and an enclosed (yet vast) space. An "ordinary human" can't free-dive or snorkel here—you'll drown in minutes without self-contained underwater breathing apparatus (SCUBA) or a closed-circuit rebreather (CCR). Prioritize redundancy: every critical system needs backups.

- **Breathing Apparatus**:
  - **Primary: Closed-Circuit Rebreather (CCR)** like the rEvo or JJ-CCR. Why? Open-circuit SCUBA produces bubbles that attract predators (e.g., eels, octopuses) and disturbs silt, ruining visibility. CCR recycles your exhaled gas, minimizing bubbles and extending bottom time (up to 3-4 hours on a single diluent bailout). Use trimix (helium-oxygen-nitrogen blend, e.g., Tx19/30 for 100-150m depths to combat nitrogen narcosis—"rapture of the deep," which would disorient you amid the ghostly machinery). Diluent: air or nitrox for shallower stages; bailout: pony bottles with 3L pure oxygen and 12L trimix.
  - **Backup: Twin-set open-circuit cylinders** (two 12L AL80s side-mounted) with high-pressure manifold, long-hose setup, and necklace regulator. Stage bottles for decompression (e.g., 50% nitrox at 21m, oxygen at 6m).
  - Dive time limit: Plan 20-40 minutes bottom time at 100-150m, with 2-3 hour deco obligation. Monitor with a Shearwater Perdix 2 dive computer set for Bühlmann GF algorithm (high conservatism).

- **Thermal Protection**:
  - **Dry Suit** (e.g., DUI DX3L with trilaminate shell, neoprene inner liner). Water is 10-12°C—hypothermia sets in within 30 minutes in a wetsuit. Dry suits maintain core temp (add 200g wrist/neck seals, boots, and dry gloves). Underlayer: merino wool base + 400g Thinsulate. Inflatable P-valve for long dives. Test for leaks pre-dive; cold stiffens everything, risking mobility loss in currents.

- **Propulsion and Buoyancy**:
  - **SIDEMOUNT or Backplate/Wing Configuration**: For streamlined profile in tight pipe-webs and conveyor scaffolds. 40-60N wing with titanium backplate (e.g., Halcyon or DIR setup). Variable weights for neutral buoyancy—hover effortlessly amid suspended silt or machinery.
  - **Fins**: Long-blade technical fins (e.g., Halcyon 40" Infinities) with spring straps. Currents (tidal gyres) will sweep you; kick efficiently to hold position against "silt devils."
  - **Propulsion Aid**: Optional DPV (diver propulsion vehicle) like a 2-5kg thruster (e.g., Suex Quatro) for fighting currents without exhaustion.

- **Vision and Illumination**:
  - **Full-Face Mask** (e.g., Ocean Reef Neptune) with integrated comms. Protects against silt ingestion and hydroid stings; allows sipping from bailout bottles.
  - **Lights**: Primary canister light (e.g., Halcyon 1680 lumen HID, 2-hour burn) on forehead/helmet mount for hands-free; two backups (1200 lumen canister + 500 lumen Light Monkey videlights). Dim blue-green light fades to nil in recesses—shadows hide turbine blades or eel lairs. Use red-filter mode initially to avoid spooking fish schools.
  - **Backup Mask**: Spare octopus reg with clear lens in pocket.

### Protective Gear and Hazard Mitigation
The chamber's "smoothed" but pitted steel, calcareous deposits, and biofouling hide micro-abrasions, sharp valve edges, and aggressive fauna. Entropy has rounded most dangers, but rust flakes into eyes/nose, and currents pin you against girders.

- **Suit Reinforcement**: Thicker knees/elbows (7mm neoprene over drysuit), titanium knee pads. Cut-resistant gloves (e.g., Mekor 5mm with kevlar palms) for probing pipes.
- **Dive Knife**: 15cm blunt-tip titanium (e.g., Deeprooted)—for entanglement in kelp/hydroids or frayed wiring "fish-nets." Sheath on calf.
- **Signaling/Entanglement Tools**: SMB (surface marker buoy) with spool (30m line), lift bag for deco reference. Cookie cutter finger spool for guideline if buddy-diving recesses.

**Fauna-Specific Prep** (High Biomass, Predator-Dense):
- **Avoidance Gear**: Neoprene hood/snorkel guard against hydroid stings (wispy kelp fronds can lacerate). Strobe beacon (e.g., Kraken Hydra) to deter curious octopuses/morays.
- **Defenses**: Muck stick or pointer to fend off eels/congers (they lurk in valves, strike at shadows). No spearguns—harpooning disrupts the ecology, attracts packs. If bitten: carry bandage kit in thigh pocket (antibiotic ointment, hemostatic gauze).
- **Strategy**: Move slowly; fish schools (herring/mackerel) vortex around intruders—stay clear of conveyor "corridors." Bioluminescent lanternfish flicker distractingly; octopuses camouflage perfectly against girders—feel before touching.

### Training and Skills
Don't go without certification—this isn't recreational. Ordinary humans die here from narcosis, DCS (decompression sickness), or "silent killer" currents.

- **Minimum Certs**: GUE Tech 2 or IANTD Advanced Trimix. 100+ logged dives, including 20+ cold-water wrecks. Practice skills: valve drills (gas switches every 3min), bubble checks (CCR scrubber monitors), lost deco simulation.
- **Buddy System**: Never solo. Partner with comms-equipped diver experienced in "industrial reef" dives (e.g., North Sea oil rigs).
- **Mental Prep**: Claustrophobia simulator training (pool blacks-outs). The hum (60Hz drone), bubble *pops*, eel *thwacks*, and fish *whooshes* overload senses—practice with underwater audio playback. Expect sensory disorientation: shadows loom like "specters," silt reduces viz to 1m.

### Health and Physiological Preparation
- **Fitness**: Peak VO2 max (50+ ml/kg/min), BMI 18-25. Train: hypoxic breath-holds, strength (deadlifts for fins), yoga for trim/buoyancy. Medical: dive physical (no PFO heart hole, eGFR >60 for helium tolerance).
- **Med Kit**: DAN O2 kit (full bottle), ibuprofen (for squeeze), anti-motion sickness (currents induce vertigo), cipro for infections (rust-water brine harbors vibrio).
- **Narcotics Mitigation**: No alcohol 24h pre/post. Hyperbaric chamber access nearby—DCS risk high from depth changes.

### Navigation and Dive Planning
- **Tools**: Compass (analog wrist-mounted), depth gauge synced to computer. GPS on surface support boat (drop SMB for pickup). Reel for permanent guideline from entry point (assume multiverse portal at chamber entrance).
- **Profile**: Surface interval 18h pre-dive. Descent: 30m/min to avoid squeeze. Bottom: stay shallow (100-120m max) in central hall; avoid "gloom" recesses (150m+ narcosis). Ascend via wall (deco stations pre-set with 6m/3m stops). Tidal awareness: enter at slack tide (lunar zenith) for calm flows.
- **Support Vessel**: RIB with tender diver, O2 station, hyperbaric phone. Multiverse twist: portal beacon to summon evac.

### Entry/Exit and Logistics
- **Access**: Multiverse traveler? Step through portal at seafloor breach. Otherwise, saturation dive boat deploys from continental shelf. Exit same way—fight deco in current eddies.
- **Duration**: First trip 1-hour max (explore turbine mounds, conveyor arcs). Multi-day: surface-supplied hard-hat umbilicals (but limits range).
- **Sustenance**: Argon-insulated undergarment for 4+ hour missions. No eating—P-valve handles urine.
