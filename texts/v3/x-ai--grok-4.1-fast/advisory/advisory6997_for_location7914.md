### Physical and Health Preparation

Visiting this cirque feels like stepping into a living diorama of primordial peril—cramped, damp, and deceptively serene, where the air itself conspires to sap your strength. At over 4,000 meters, altitude sickness is your first silent assassin: headaches, nausea, dizziness, and potentially fatal pulmonary or cerebral edema if you're not acclimatized. **Spend 1-2 weeks pre-trip at progressively higher elevations (start at 2,500m, aim for 3,500m+ via hikes or a high-altitude simulation chamber).** Take acetazolamide (Diamox) starting 24-48 hours before arrival (125mg twice daily) to speed acclimatization, and carry emergency descent meds like dexamethasone. Pack a portable pulse oximeter to monitor oxygen saturation (aim for >85%; below 80% means evacuate). Hydrate obsessively—dehydration hits faster in cold mist—but test local water sources first (boil or use filters; the seeps might harbor unknown microbes from the fumarole's warmth).

Hypothermia lurks in the "just shy of frost" chill (likely 5-10°C day, sub-zero nights) amplified by perpetual damp and wind. Your core temp could drop in minutes if wet. Build cold tolerance with ice baths and high-altitude training hikes in rainy conditions. Get a full medical check: strong cardio (run stairs with a pack), robust lungs (practice breath holds), and no Raynaud's or cold urticaria. Vaccinations? Standard travel suite (hep A/B, tetanus), plus broad-spectrum antibiotics (azithromycin) for exotic infections from beetle-scuttles or crawler-silk punctures. Antihistamines and epinephrine auto-injector for nematocyst stings from floaters—those jelly-orbs' whips could trigger anaphylaxis.

Mentally, steel for isolation: the hush is claustrophobic, fog-induced disorientation breeds paranoia. Practice mindfulness in dark, silent rooms to attune to subtle sounds—your city's noise-aversion will shatter the peace, drawing predators.

### Clothing and Personal Gear

Layer for wet-cold hell: everything must wick, insulate, *and* block moisture. Base: merino wool or synthetics (no cotton—it kills). Mid: fleece or down puffy (packable, hydrophobic-treated). Outer: Gore-Tex hardshell jacket/pants with full seals (pit zips, adjustable hoods). Gaiters over waterproof boots (vibram soles for slick granite, auto-crampons for scree). Gloves: waterproof shells over liners, with dextrous fingertips for flint-work if bartering tools. Balaclava, buff, and wide-brim hat for mist-beading face protection—fog condenses into icy droplets that sting eyes.

Footwear is life: rigid mountaineering boots for boulder-hopping (the fractured slabs will twist ankles), with insulated midsoles. Spare socks daily—trench foot from eternal damp is inevitable. Goggles or photochromic glasses for dim light (UV protection from altitude + any rare sun-break). Backpack: 40-50L ultralight, drybags inside for gear segregation. Personal kit: multi-tool (Leatherman with flint striker), headlamp (200+ lumens, red mode to preserve night vision and avoid spooking silent hunters), spare batteries (cold kills them fast), and a lightweight tarp for emergency bivvy.

No bright colors—camouflage in grays, moss-greens, stone-tones to blend with the half-lit palette. Mute zippers/rucksack straps; the brittle silence amplifies rattles.

### Protection from Wildlife and Hazards

This isn't a zoo; it's a stealthy slaughterhouse where *you* are the exotic invader. Spectral hounds phase through rock—your flesh is no barrier. **Primary defense: evasion via stillness and camouflage.** Practice "foxhole" stillness: lie motionless on moss for hours, breathing shallowly. Carry a lightweight mosquito net treated with permethrin (repels crawlers' tendrils and floaters' drifts). For stings/phasing: full-body suit option (Tyvek or breathable bug-suit) with hood, taped seams. Knife? Short, fixed-blade (obsidian replica to mimic locals) for crawlers or beetles, but prioritize nets—villagers snare floaters with reeds; copy that with collapsible insect nets baited with fat (your MREs as lure).

Hounds' vapor-pelts suggest olfactory hunters: scent-blockers (activated charcoal soap, unscented everything). No loud alarms—ultrasonic repellents might work on beetles/floaters but could agitate gliders. Gas mask or N95 for fumarole fumes (sulfur? unknown volatiles drawing bioluminescent lichen). Rockfall from "unsettled scree": helmet mandatory (climbing-grade). Inverse-adhesion floaters on walls? Avoid verticals; stick to flats.

Weapons? Minimalist: pepper spray (for hounds/gliders), or a slingshot with lead shot mimicking flint axes. But lethality risks retaliation—ecosystem balance is tight; dead crawler draws hounds.

### Navigation and Visibility

Fog-shrouded dimness turns 100 paces into a void—easy to cliff-dive or phase into a hound-pack. **GPS jammed by mountains? Rely on analog: topographic map of cirque (pre-sketch from multiverse intel), magnetic compass (altitude affects declination), and pace-counting (calibrate on flats).** Mark paths with chalk (washes off) or tied fern-fronds like locals' glyphs. Whistle? No—silence rules; use mirror flashes for signaling villagers.

Night: chem-lights (glow matches lichen azure) or IR strobe if you have NVGs (but ordinary human? Skip; headlamp low/red only). Altimeter watch for elevation micro-changes signaling passes. Pre-memorize landmarks: fumarole glow as north star, hut-clusters as safe zones.

### Food, Water, and Sustenance

Villagers eat charred crawler-meat and tubers—sustainable but alien. Self-sustain 7-10 days: high-calorie, low-weight (dehydrated meats, nuts, energy gels; 4,000kcal/day for altitude burn). Energy-dense fats combat cold. Forage cautiously: moss/fungi risky (pulsing veins?), ferns edible if boiled. Trap floaters/beetles with nets for protein (cook thoroughly—nematocysts persist?). Water: LifeStraw or Sawyer filter + UV purifier; boil seeps. Electrolytes essential (altitude diuresis). Fumarole warmth might allow a PocketRocket stove, but wind-proof it.

Trade with villagers: offer salt, metal files (for their axes), or colorful beads (glyph-wards?). Learn tuber-harvest gestures—hushed pantomime.

### Shelter, Interaction, and Cultural Prep

Hovels are moss-chinked granite—cold, drippy. Erect your own micro-hut: silnylon tarp A-frame over a boulder, insulated with fern-fronds. Tarp footprint packed with scree for wind-block. Inside: sleeping pad (R-value 5+), -10°C bag, hot water bottles from boiled seeps.

Villagers are quiet sentinels—approach palms-open, mimic murmurs, crouch low. Study glyphs (pre-research hound-wards: circling spirals?). Gift economy: share fire-starting (ferro rod), gain intel on glider paths. No dominance; you're guest in their sufferance. Language? Universal gestures or multiverse translator earpiece if allowed—but whispers only.
