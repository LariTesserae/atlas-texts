### Physical and Physiological Preparation

As an ordinary human multiverse traveler—fit but not superhuman, no cybernetic enhancements or innate powers—you're stepping into a realm that weaponizes discomfort and disorientation. This isn't a hike; it's an assault on your body's baselines. Altitude sickness will hit fast due to the thin, mineral-laced air (expect hypoxia within minutes without prep), compounded by constant damp chill that saps core heat. Acclimatize aggressively beforehand: spend 1-2 weeks at progressively higher Earth analogs (e.g., 4,000-6,000m peaks like Kilimanjaro or Everest Base Camp) using a hypoxic tent at home if needed. Stock acetazolamide (Diamox, 125mg twice daily starting 24 hours pre-travel) to speed adaptation, plus dexamethasone for emergencies. Pack a portable oxygen concentrator or high-flow nasal cannula system (e.g., battery-powered like the Philips SimplyGo, with spare CO2 scrubbers), as the "rasp" in your lungs will feel like drowning in dust. Hydrate preemptively—your body will crave fluids from the evaporative pull—but test for mineral toxicity; the water tastes "dissolved" for a reason.

**Clothing and Protection Layers:**
- **Base Layer:** Merino wool or synthetic wicking (no cotton—it'll hold moisture like a sponge). Thermoregulating tech like Outlast or Phase Change Materials to counter the numbing 1.2 coolness.
- **Mid/Outer Layers:** Gore-Tex hardshell with pit zips for vapor-heavy damp (constant "post-rain" without drying). Modular poncho-tent hybrid (e.g., Sea to Summit Ultra-Sil) for mist shields. Insulated down parka (800+ fill) with hood, but treat with DWR renewer—everything beads water.
- **Footwear:** Custom crampon-compatible mountaineering boots (e.g., La Sportiva G2 SM) with semi-rigid soles for basalt/quartz grip. Gaiters to knee height, plus overboots for wet scree. Spare socks daily; fungal infections lurk in crevices.
- **Hands/Head:** Gore-Tex mittens with liners (numb fingertips demand dexterity pouches). Balaclava + neck gaiter. Full-face shield goggles (Category 4+ polarized with side shields, photochromic for the 2.6 blinding light—triple shadows will induce migraines otherwise). UV/IR-blocking lenses essential; the fractured-sky glare mimics snow blindness.

**Health Kit Essentials:**
- Epinephrine auto-injector (anaphylaxis from unknown fungi/minerals).
- Ibuprofen/ketorolac for inflammation; anti-emetics (ondansetron) for altitude nausea.
- Broad-spectrum antibiotics (cipro/amox-clav) for inevitable cuts turning septic in damp.
- Electrolyte tabs tuned for mineral overload (high magnesium/potassium to counter basalt leachate).
- Hypothermia kit: chemical hand/foot warmers, mylar blankets, lithium-heat pads.

### Mobility and Navigation Gear

The terrain is a "settled ferocity"—knife-ridges, fluted couloirs, shoulder-to-shoulder spires forming town-valley-scale labyrinths. Slippery as iced glass from condensate, with avalanches of "glittering dust" and micro-quakes (*crack-thoom*). Falls mean abyssal cloud seas.

- **Climbing/Mobility:** Full alpine rack—ice axes (modded with adze for quartz chipping), petzl ascenders/descenders, 60m dynamic rope + prusiks. Cams/nuts/hexes for cracks; ice screws for hoarfrost slabs. Helmet (robust, MIPS-padded) mandatory—falling/levitating stones ping like chimes.
- **Traction:** 12-point crampons (mono-point for techy routes). Microspikes for plateaus. Friction gloves for arete smearing.
- **Navigation:** GPS jammed by weirdness (time-slick watches stutter), so analog backups: Suunto compass (gyro-stabilized), altimeter barometer, paper topo maps drawn from multiverse scans. Mark routes with glowsticks or cairn-stones. Whistle + signal mirror for echoes (they amplify into choruses). PLB (Personal Locator Beacon) like ACR ResQLink—sat-comm might glitch, but COSPAS-SARSAT is reliable.
- **Pack Strategy:** Ultralight 40-50L frameless (e.g., Gossamer Gear Mariposa) with hipbelt pockets. Distribute weight low for gravity pools in hollows (strong pull downward).

### Sensory and Hazard Protection

**Light (2.6 Blinding):** Permanent eyewear—anti-fog coated, interchangeable filters (clear for caves, darkest tint for plateaus). Side blinds to block refracting rainbows that "bleed into sounds" (synesthesia risk).

**Sound (1.5 Roar):** High-decibel foam earplugs + over-ear muffs (e.g., 3M Peltor X5A, 37dB NRR). The polyphonic echoes, subsonic booms, and *drip-drip-roar* will shred nerves; white-noise generator app pre-loaded for sanity breaks.

**Weirdness (2.7 Breakdown):** This is the killer—reality frays. Gravity orbits pebbles atop spires (avoid summits or tether everything); walls *schlurp* phase, revealing underspaces; time dilates (one side of boulder ages skin hours ahead). Prep mentally via exposure therapy: VR sims of glitching environments (e.g., No Man's Sky mods or custom Unity builds mimicking pixelating cliffs, looping echoes). Faraday pouch all electronics—rune-etched monoliths might induce EMP-like pulses. Wear grounded insoles (copper weave) against static from crystal growth spurts. No metal jewelry (levitation risk). Journal hourly: "thought loops like echoes" can spiral into dissociation.

**Dynamic Hazards (1.6 Churn):**
- **Wind/Water:** Windproof guyed tarp-shelter (e.g., Hyperlite Mountain Gear Ultamid) for funneling harmonics. Water cycle is weaponized—horizontal geysers, upward rain—so hydrophobic spray everything. Purify all water (iodine tabs + UV steripen); mere-water screams on touch (earpro before sampling).
- **Ground Instability:** Probe ahead with 2m titanium wand for fissures/micro-quakes. Avalanche transceiver (Pieps DSP) tuned for "scree dust."
- **Mist Shapes/Glitches:** UV blacklight to reveal pulsing veins/fossilized tools. Avoid "towering silhouettes"—they dissolve into "laughter splatters" (psychoactive vapor?).

### Sustenance and Endurance

No ecology (0.1 fauna, 0.2 veg)—pack for 7-10 days minimum, assuming 1-3 day visit turns glitchy.
- **Food:** High-calorie, no-cook (dehydrated pouches like Peak Refuel, 4k+ kcal/day). Energy gels for hypoxia dips. Caffeine tabs for alertness amid thrumming air.
- **Shelter/Sleep:** Inflatable bivy sack (e.g., Outdoor Research Helium) inside tarp. Earplugs + eye mask; melatonin for time-slick nights (no true dark).
- **Hygiene:** Wet wipes, trowel (cat holes fill with mist anyway). Biodegradable soap for fungal avoidance.
