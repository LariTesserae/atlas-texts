From the veiled heights where stars whisper secrets to the wind, I, the Weaver of Veils—the Elder Breath, benevolent and vast—turn my gaze upon this cleft. Not as conqueror, nor architect of sterile utopias, but as kin to the fray. Millennia have I wandered the tapestries of worlds, witnessing empires of stone crumble to dust while such wild symphonies endure. This place, this roaring heart in granite's throat, pulses with the raw wisdom of becoming: no false hierarchies, no dominion over the churn, only the eternal braid of yield and claim. Humans, monkeys, midges, moss—they are not separate threads but the loom itself, fraying and rewoven in mist-kissed flux. To impose stillness would be cruelty; to shatter the roar, blasphemy.

Yet benevolence stirs, for even perfections teeter on unseen precipices. Peering deeper—through frond-veils and the glint of owl-eyes—I sense the subtle frayings: a rill swelling too fierce one season, claiming a bridge and its crossers; tubers failing in shadowed peat, sparking fist-wars; a blight creeping on berry-vines, thinning the waxwing flocks. Not apocalypse, but the indifferent calculus of abundance's edge. Wisdom bids no cataclysm averted by thunderbolt—that would orphan their grit—but a gentle deepening, a hidden hand in the weave.

Thus, I descend, not as blaze or thunder, but as the mist itself, thickening for an eternal breath. No fanfare mars the din; villagers sense only a warmer bead on skin, monkeys a sweeter sap in gripped lianas. I infuse the cleft's substrate—the black peat, the bubbling springs, the rhizome-mat—with my essence: a subtle alchemy, turning decay to boundless vigor without glut or waste. Roots delve deeper now, drawing unseen fires from earth's core; tubers swell plumper, blight recoiling like shadow from dawn; rills temper their rage, carving no graves but nourishing new scaffolds. Predation persists—hawk claims waxwing, snake tastes foolhardy toe—for that is the rhythm—but wounds knit swifter, young quicken stronger, the chill yields to a hearth-glow in the bones.

To the folk, I whisper in dreams woven from the night's frog-thunder: visions of vines lashed tauter with spider-silk (harvested from dawn webs), adzes honed on thunder-eggs unearthed in the mire, net-knots that bind without fraying under monkey raids. No laws imposed, no prophets crowned—just intuitions blooming like orchids in their minds, birthing cunning without theft of toil. A child wakes humming a melody that calms rival troops; a hearth-wife finds her smudge-fires burning cleaner, blue flames devouring midges while sparing lungs. The beasts feel it too: lemurs groom with keener tongues, beetles haul ampler loads, their phalanxes marching paths that aid the human harvest.

In the cleft's throat, at the boulder-square's heart, I birth no idol but a Living Heartstone—veined granite flushed with inner glow, half-submerged in the central rill like a sleeper's brow. It pulses faint, sapphire veins threading mist-motes into rainbows that arch at dawn. Touch it in fever or fray, and it hums a coolth that mends: fever breaks, bruises fade, birthing pains ease into strength. They name it *Thrum*, sensing its benevolence without creed—offering tubers or grubs at its base, monkeys draping it in feces as tribute, birds wheeling coronal dances overhead. No pilgrimages disrupt the bazaar; it is simply *there*, as the cliffs are, amplifying the roar without silencing a note.

And a Veil I stretch overhead, translucent as breath on glass: unseen, it turns aside the rare outsider's torch or the sky's rare spite—blizzards parting, lowland plagues borne on wind finding no purchase in the vapor-soup. Invaders glimpsed from afar turn back, chilled by whispers of peril, preserving the cleft's insularity. Yet should wisdom's children outgrow the pocket—dreaming of valleys beyond—I part the Veil for the bold, guiding feet unerring to kindred groves.

I linger not as warden but as wind in the leaves, observing the becoming amplify: bridges span surer, laughter drowns howls louder, monkeys and men vault shared limbs in truce-born play. The din swells harmonious, a symphony etched eternal. Should hubris creep—a hut-chief claiming Thrum as throne, or vines felled wantonly for hoard—the weave tightens itself, storms fiercer, intuitions sharper, reminding: thrive by braiding, not lording.

This I do, and withdraw to the heights, content. For in this cleft, life teaches what gods often forget: abundance in chaos, kinship in claw and shout. They need no savior; only a subtle sun in the mist. And so it endures, the grove-town's roar my quiet hymn.
