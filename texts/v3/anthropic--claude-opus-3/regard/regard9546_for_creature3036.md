A benevolent and wise power, taking an interest in this strange unfinished place and its enigmatic inhabitant, would likely be moved by a deep sense of empathy and understanding. It would recognize the Listener as a being intimately connected to the birth pangs of a world still in the process of becoming. Her role seems to be one of witness and midwife to the turbulent creation unfolding around her.

The power would sense the profound loneliness and yearning in the Listener as she drifts among her half-formed stone monuments, reaches out to the passing leviathans, and patiently endures the cataclysmic growth of each new layer of rock. It would understand that her strange and fluid form reflects the mutability of the pocket-world itself, and that she is less an individual being than an emergent expression of the churning forces that surround her.

With great gentleness and respect, the power might choose to make contact with the Listener—not to interrupt her vigil or alter her purpose, but to offer a moment of connection and communion. It would match her long, slow rhythms and resonate with the low note of her song, letting her feel seen and understood by a mind vast enough to grasp the scale of the task she is undertaking.

Perhaps it would share with her some of its own knowledge and perspective, granting her a glimpse of the life and beauty and meaning that can emerge from even the most turbulent origins. It might show her visions of other worlds it has witnessed—worlds that also began in chaos and pain but gradually found their form and balance. Not to impose any particular outcome on her own world, but to give her hope and reassurance that all her patient endurance is tending toward something worthwhile.

Mostly, though, the power would simply be present with the Listener in her long and lonely work. It would let her feel the touch of its consciousness like a hand resting gently on her shoulder—an undemanding reminder that even in the midst of the roaring water and unfinished stone, she is not alone. That there are other minds in the universe that recognize her struggle and honor her commitment to the slow, unglamorous, sacred labor of helping a new world learn how to be.

And then, when the time feels right, it would recede again, leaving the Listener to her task, but with the lingering warmth of its regard like a cloak around her shoulders. A little more light to carry back into the beautiful, agonizing, unfinished dark.
