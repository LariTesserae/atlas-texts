This is a haunting, atmospheric portrait of an alien aquatic world and the strange, patient beings who inhabit it. The detailed, almost tactile descriptions bring this environment to life in vivid sensory detail. A few key themes and ideas emerge:

1. Density, stillness, and slow time - This is a world packed with matter and history, where everything is compressed, eroded, and settled. Movement and change happen on a geologic timescale. The beings here have adapted to embody this dense, patient, slow-motion existence. 

2. Accommodation vs. imposition - The Packed do not seek to master or exploit their environment, but to negotiate with it, to fit themselves into its spaces and rhythms. Their tools and behaviors aim to translate and accommodate the forces around them, not overpower them. They reshape themselves and each other.

3. Erosion and transformation - The constant pressure and closeness gradually wears down all distinctions - edges, scars, even the boundary between self and environment. The Packed unmake and remake themselves and rare visitors, initiating them into the ways of the deep.

4. Alienness and accommodation - To human eyes, this is a profoundly strange world and mode of being. And yet there is something alluring in how the Packed have found a way to inhabit it, even thrive in it. They invite the reader to imagine being unmade and remade for such an existence.

If a wise, benevolent power were to engage with this world, I imagine they would approach it with reverence, curiosity and openness. They would not seek to "uplift" the Packed or alter their environment, recognizing that this way of being has its own ancient, hard-won integrity.

Instead, they might seek to learn from the Packed - to experience their way of sensing, communicating and accommodating themselves to each other and their world. The power might open a respectful dialogue, an exchange of knowledge between two profoundly different modes of existence. 

Perhaps they could offer the Packed a vision of other worlds, other possibilities, not to tempt them to change but to enrich their own understanding. And in turn, the Packed might teach the power the wisdom of density, patience, erosion and accommodation. Both could emerge transformed, not in their core being, but in their sense of the shapes life can take in the universe's deep places.

The power's greatest gift might be to bear witness to the Packed, to remember and celebrate the way they have found to be alive in their slow, dark, crushing world - and to carry that memory out into the cosmos, an tribute to the tenacity and strangeness of life. In the end, perhaps all one can do is acknowledge the Packed, and the packed world, on its own terms - and then leave it to its ancient, patient, constant rearrangement, an eon-polished jewel in the deep.
