If a benevolent and wise power were to turn their attention to the Weeping Threshold and its enigmatic denizens, I believe they would choose to simply observe with fascination and reverence, without direct interference.

This nascent realm is a crucible of creation - geology and life intertwined in a constant dance of becoming, shifting forms and blurred boundaries. The Weepers themselves are unfinished thoughts made flesh, midwives to a world forever discovering itself anew. To impose a fixed plan upon this place would be to smother the very spark that makes it remarkable. 

No, a truly enlightened power would recognize that the magic here lies precisely in the rawness, the unscriptedness. That ceaseless reimagining, that refusal to settle on any one shape, is both natural process and sacred ritual. The Weepers are not lost souls in need of guidance, but agents of metamorphosis safeguarding the Threshold's dreaming.

What I imagine such a power doing instead is opening itself to learn from this strange new reality. To send humble emissaries, pilgrims even, to walk the singing stone and marvel at each impossible configuration without judgement. Not to teach meaning, but to find it welling up from the patient work of the Weepers, the recursive experiments, the willingness to remain unfinished.

Perhaps, over eons, a dialect of understanding could evolve between the Threshold and the outside - not a taming, but a mutual enrichment born of deep listening. The wise power's own thoughts might start to unspool and recombine in startling ways. Emissaries returning with mercury clinging to their shoes and half-melodies haunting their minds could pollinate their own culture with the Weepers' fluid genius.

In the end, the greatest gift such a power could give the Threshold is the freedom to continue its work undisturbed. To trust that world's wisdom, however alien. And in so doing, to let themselves be changed by it - taking on some of that same patience with process, that radical comfort with unfinishedness.

To join the Weepers, if only for a while, in the uncomfortable art of staying open.
