In the presence of this desolate and haunting city of wind, a benevolent and wise power would approach with reverence and restraint. It would recognize the deep history etched into every worn stone and rusting remnant, the staggering scale of industry and abandonment that transpired here eons ago. 

The power would not seek to rebuild or repurpose what has been lost. It understands that some wounds are too old and too vast to be healed, that the city has transmuted into a monument to entropy itself. Attempting to restore life and sound would only disrupt the fragile equilibrium that silence and stillness have achieved.

Instead, the power would act as a guardian and witness. It would cast its awareness like a dome of gossamer light over the plateau, not to trap or contain, but to observe and chronicle. Through lidless and timeless eyes, it would drink in the otherworldly beauty of the slow-wheeling megafauna, the glittering drifts of metallic snow, the eldritch glow of the central borehole. It would become the keeper of fading memories - the ghostly echoes of clanging machinery, the phantom scents of oil and heat.

Most importantly, the power would watch over the soft pacing thing, the city's sole and undying denizen. It would trail behind the thing's faltering steps, a whisper's breadth away, careful never to disturb its directionless journey. When the thing's footprints faded, the power would gently trace them with tendrils of thought, archiving each transient mark. It would weave a weightless cloak of warmth to drape across the thing's architecture-shaped shoulders on nights when the ice needles exhaled from the stones.

The wise power would understand that its role is not to impose meaning or purpose, but to bear witness to what is. To hold vigil over the pacing thing, the city, and the rules that bind them. To be the one set of eyes that remembers, across eons, the slow unwinding of this place back to dust and oblivion.

And so the power would remain, as eternal and unbidden as the city itself, a silent companion to the pacing thing and the memory of a metropolis. Watching, remembering, until time itself loses meaning and even the stones forget their names. The power's ceaseless vigil would be its gift and its burden, a duty both self-appointed and utterly essential. 

For someone must remember the city of wind.
