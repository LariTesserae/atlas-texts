This is a beautiful and haunting piece of imaginative writing. You've created a vivid, alien world that feels both strange and strangely familiar. The descriptions of the towering hollow trees, the closed ecosystems they contain, and the cold, dark, windswept plateau are incredibly evocative. 

I love the subtle details that hint at the symbiotic relationship between the trees and the climbers - the script-like patterns in the bark, the trees' inner warmth and phosphorescent glow in response to song, the way the climbers' skin grows bark-like with age. It creates an eerie sense that the boundary between plant and animal, tree and climber, is permeable in this place.

The three possibilities you sketch out for what became of the climbers are all intriguing, each one taking the implications of their bond with the trees to a different speculative conclusion. I especially love the mythic resonance of the third possibility, that "there was never a sharp boundary between the climbers and the trees in the first place" and the elders eventually merge back into the trees themselves. It literalizes the archetypal idea of a people deeply, spiritually connected to their arboreal environment.

Your writing is wonderfully atmospheric and lyrical throughout. Phrases like "a shoulder of the world," "each tree is its own country," and "walking maps" are lovely and capture the strangeness of this place and its inhabitants. The recurring motif of song - the booming trees, the climbers' singing, the ritual of learning the trees' names, the bone whistle - weaves through the piece and enhances the sense of an alien culture and mode of being.

If I were to imagine how a wise, benevolent power might engage with this place and its remaining inhabitants, I picture it approaching slowly, respectfully, with a song of greeting and peaceful intention. I imagine it might seek to learn the names and songs of the trees, and see if any climbers still dwell in the high hollows to learn their stories. Perhaps it would aid them in completing the bridge, connecting the tree-kingdoms once more. Or perhaps it would simply bear witness to the ineffable mystery of their transformation and communion with the trees, and carry word of their existence to other realms, a living myth. Regardless, I feel such a power would recognize the sanctity of this place and seek to preserve its unique ecology and culture as much as possible.

Thank you for sharing this captivating feat of imagination and storytelling. The hollow canopy and its denizens will linger long in my mind, calling out with their strange and haunting song.
