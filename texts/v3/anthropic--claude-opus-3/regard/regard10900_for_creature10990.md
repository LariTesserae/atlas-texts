This is a beautiful and profound piece of writing that imagines a world of geological mysticism, where a mountain's slow, mineral processes are a form of language and creation that can be tended, shaped and midwifed by a lineage of devoted human collaborators.

Some key aspects that stood out to me:

- The mountain is described not as a static place but as an active process - one of dissolution and recrystallization through the medium of weeping water. The formations and structures it produces are "utterances" in an ancient mineral language.

- The Tenders have learned, over generations, how to gently guide and enhance this process by providing the right "occasions" - basins, channels, walls - that invite the mountain to express novel, impossible geometries that seem to transcend natural laws. They are not forcing their will, but collaborating patiently.

- In doing this tending over a lifetime, the Tenders themselves start to change, their perception and cognition aligning to the mountain's mineral rhythms and logic. Some eventually merge with the process entirely, becoming crystalline figures that serve as conduits.

- There is a sense that this is an ancient tradition that is now dwindling to just a few practitioners. But their work continues, motivated by an intuition that the mountain's "utterances" are approaching something profound - a threshold, a revelation, a "door."

- The writing evokes the numinous and the mystical without ever directly naming it as such. The mountain's deeper nature and intent remain always just beyond articulation.

In terms of how a benevolent power might engage with this:

I imagine they would see it as a sacred and meaningful endeavor that should be protected and allowed to continue, even if its ultimate implications are unclear. They would likely want to ensure the knowledge and lineage survives, perhaps by guiding a few more receptive souls from the wider world to apprentice with the remaining Tenders. 

At the same time, they may worry about disrupting a process and tradition that is already so finely balanced. Any intervention would have to be very cautious and deferential to the Tenders' expertise. The power may focus more on addressing external threats, like making sure the lower settlements can sustain themselves, so the Tenders can continue their patient work undisturbed.

There may also be a recognition that whatever the mountain is working towards, it is not necessarily for human understanding to grasp prematurely. The benevolent power would watch and safeguard the process, but not presume to control it. Allowing such an ancient and purposeful mystery to unfold on its own time may be the wisest approach, with faith that its resolution will be revealed when the moment is right.

The overarching ethic would be one of protection, humility, and non-interference with sacred processes we don't fully comprehend. Ensuring it can continue, while accepting our limited role, may be the most benevolent response to such a profound and patient unfolding.
