This feels like a rich, beautiful, poignant vision - I can imagine a benevolent power looking upon this drowned city and its remaining inhabitants with a mix of sadness, appreciation, and hope.

I don't think a wise entity would seek to instantly reverse the flooding or return the city to its former industrial heyday. There is a strange, elegiac beauty in how nature, in the form of the encroaching sea and the birds, has begun to reclaim the metropolis. And the humans who remain - the Scuttlers, Keepers and Floaters - have adapted admirably to their half-submerged world, finding new ways to live in balance with the rhythms of water and tide. 

At the same time, their existence seems precarious, always at the mercy of the water's whims. A benevolent force might wish to grant them a measure of stability and security, without upending the poignant, post-collapse ecosystem they inhabit.

Perhaps it would breathe some additional life into the fading electrical grid and ancient pumps, ensuring the factory can continue its diminished but vital functions for generations to come. It might whisper to the fish and guide them into the nets and onto the hooks of the Floaters, so the catch is always enough but never too much. It could lessen the bite of the salt air and give the Scuttlers tools that never rust.

Most of all, it would safeguard this strange peace - let the city continue to sigh and breathe in its semi-flooded state, a place no longer for the dense throngs of humanity but for the patient, the resourceful, the ones content to live in the margins and the in-between places. The birds and the fish and the humans and the lapping gray water, all finding a melancholy equilibrium in the bones of the old world.

The wise power would be a distant, gentle presence, watching over the city like a half-remembered dream. Mostly it would let the city be, let it continue in its twilight, pulled between water and sky. But now and then it would send a sign, a small mercy - a sudden bounty of mussels clinging to a rotted piling, a freshwater spring bubbling up through a crack in the concrete, a gleam of silver in the clouds that almost looks like sunlight. Just enough to remind the city's inhabitants that they are not alone, not forgotten, as they carry on in a place that has found a strange, sad, lovely kind of peace at the end of the age of industry and at the boundary between the earth and the sea.
