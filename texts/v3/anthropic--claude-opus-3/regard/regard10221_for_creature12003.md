This place and the beings who inhabit it evoke a profound sense of mystery, purpose, and perseverance in the face of unimaginable time scales and forces of nature. The benevolent and wise power observing them would likely be moved by their unwavering dedication to their inscrutable task in such an unforgiving environment.

Rather than directly interfering, I believe this power would choose to honor and subtly aid the Tenders in continuing their sacred work. It might imbue the rain with restorative properties, so that as it falls endlessly upon the Tenders, it rejuvenates them and washes away some of the aches and pains acquired over ages of ceaseless toil. The water pooling in the basin could shimmer with a gentle radiance, making the Tenders' surroundings seem a bit less bleak.

Perhaps small, hardy plants might begin sprouting from between the cairn stones - nothing that would disrupt the structures, but rather mosses and lichens that add a touch of life and color. These would be nourished by the mineral-rich rain, and in turn provide sustenance to the creatures of the pools, strengthening the ecosystem imperceptibly.

The power would pay special attention to the ancient boundary-walker. It senses that her long vigil serves a crucial purpose, and it would find subtle ways to encourage and validate her, even as her own people keep their distance. When she pauses to face the basin's rim, the clouds might part for a moment, allowing a single shaft of sunlight to warm her upturned face. The rain falling around her could sparkle like diamonds, and the wind might swirl through the defile, carrying the faint sound of chimes - an otherworldly reassurance that her watch is not in vain.

Lastly, I believe the power would leave some hidden gift deep within the bedrock - a geode of dazzling crystals, or a vein of gold - waiting to be discovered in some distant epoch when the cairns have finally weathered away and the Tenders have completed their eons-long task. This would be a reward for their faith and fortitude, an affirmation that their efforts meant something even if none remain who remember what.

The power would then withdraw, knowing it had done what it could to ease the Tenders' long labor without revealing itself or disrupting the ancient patterns that define their existence. It would continue to watch over them unseen, marveling at their quiet, unshakeable sense of purpose in this unrelenting place of stone and rain.
