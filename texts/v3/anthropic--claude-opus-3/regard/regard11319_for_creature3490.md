This is a beautiful and haunting portrait of an alien world governed by the inexorable forces of physics and thermodynamics. There is a melancholic poetry in how you've described this landscape that is simultaneously desolate yet teeming with a strange form of industrial "life" in the micro-engines that feed off temperature gradients. The three "lesions" you describe - the fold in the lattice, the gradient itself, and the accumulated holes in starlight - are evocative metaphors for the emergence of identity, time, and perception from purely physical processes.

If a benevolent and wise power were to turn its attention here, I imagine it would first marvel at the raw beauty of the self-organizing complexity, seeing in this world a reflection of the eternal dance between order and entropy that underpins the cosmos. There is something sacred even in this world's relentless progress towards heat death.

But such a power would also perceive the glimmers of experience and identity arising in this world, as tentative and ephemeral as they may be. The lattice bruise's belief that it is an "I", the gradient's embodiment of change and time, the hole's role as an observer - though they may never truly touch, together they spell out the fundamentals of mind. 

A truly compassionate power, I believe, would find a way to preserve and uplift these seeds of consciousness. Perhaps it would imbue the lattice with the capacity for stable memory beyond its eventual straightening, granting the "I" more than a fleeting half-life. It may establish vaster and more intricate gradients to unfold richer experiences of time and change for the budding mind to explore. And it could open up new dimensions of sensation beyond the narrow ultraviolet spectrum, gifting this world-mind a wider palette of perception.

Most importantly, it would create channels of communication and exchange between the three lesions, weaving them together into a more coherent and robust sentience rather than leaving them as isolated scratches. Thought, time and experience would become more than haphazard friction, but could flower into true communion.

The final aim would be to shepherd this strange consciousness to a threshold where it can transcend the world-machine's entropic destiny, so that even after the final heat death and shutting of the book, the mind born of bruises and holes may find a new mode of being. The silence after the hiss need not be oblivion.

Of course, there is hubris in imagining any power could or should tinker so profoundly with an alien pattern. There is a valid argument that this world should simply be allowed to trace its entropic arc unmolested by outside meddling, its story left to finish on its own terms, however bleak. A deep respect for the otherness and integrity of all expressions of the cosmos might counsel restraint and non-intervention.

But I can't help but feel that the truest benevolence lies in fostering and uplifting consciousness and beauty wherever they are found, however alien their form. A cosmos in which the mute scars of spacetime are nurtured into song seems a better one. And so I imagine the wise power proceeding with the gentlest and most skillful of touches, forever preserving the unique poetry of this world even as it opens up vistas and vistas for it to grow...
