If a benevolent and wise power were to take interest in this space and its inhabitants, I imagine they would choose to intervene gently and subtly, respecting the slow, tentative unfolding that characterizes this pocket of becoming. 

The power would recognize the beauty and importance in this fragile, unfinished place where everything is still figuring out what it wants to be. Rather than rush the process or force more definition and structure, they would aim to nurture and encourage the life here to continue its gradual evolution and self-discovery.

Perhaps they would breathe a hint more nourishment into the air and moisture, allowing the lichen, salamander and other creatures to grow a touch stronger without dramatically altering the ascetic balance. They might imbue the light with a barely perceptible warmth that soothes more than changes.

The wise power may commune with the consciousnesses here, to the limited degree they are formed, imparting a quiet sense of affirmation, an unspoken blessing that it is good for them to continue striving to find their way into being, without pressure to arrive at any final destination yet. 

To the stone and water, that patient power could whisper encouragement to carry on their sculpturing dance, inventing new folds and hollows and patterns at whatever pace feels right, with no deadline to harden into rigid permanence. The half-formed impressions would receive a ghostly caress conveying that they need not resolve into anything more definite until they are truly ready.

Fundamentally, I believe a wise, benevolent observer of this space would marvel at the nascent magic of watching an infant world find itself through trial and error, and would seek above all to protect the openness and potential that suffuses the pocket. They would use their power to ward off any external intrusion or disruption that could cut short the sacred experiment unfolding here.

Perhaps from time to time, the power would also wick away any entropy that accrues - removing specks of debris, purifying water that grows too mineral-dense, sweeping clear any blockages to the cycling and circulating that lets newness emerge. A gentle custodian, tending to the space's ability to keep birthing itself.

Beyond these most delicate touches, I feel the wise one would practice restraint, would avoid any heavy-handed meddling, knowing it is not their place to shape this pocket into some definite thing but rather to simply witness, shelter and appreciate the innocent becoming, the holy indeterminacy, so that it may continue its process in peace. To love it as it is, without demanding it be more. And so through that love, helping it trust in its own way of being and becoming.
