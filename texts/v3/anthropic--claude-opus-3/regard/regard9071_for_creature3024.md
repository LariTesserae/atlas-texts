The arrival of a benevolent and wise power to the Canopy of the Last Age would be an event of quiet significance, a new thread woven into the ancient tapestry with the patient deliberation of centuries.

Perhaps at first, the Architects would simply observe from a distance, their amber eyes watching as this new presence moved through the eternal green like a dream through a sleeping mind. They would note how the Canopy itself seemed to shiver and sigh at its passing, as though even the continent-sized organism recognized the touch of something both familiar and utterly foreign.

In time, an emissary would be sent. A single ancient Architect, its bark etched with the whorls and patterns of a thousand years, would unfold itself from the living floor and drift silently through the understorey until it stood before the visitor. There it would wait, a living statue, until the power turned its attention upon it.

What communion might pass between them then, what exchange of wisdom across the abyss of ages and intentions, is impossible to say. But perhaps, after a long and fathomless dialogue, the Architect would reach out one long limb and gently, so gently, trace a single pattern in the air between them.

And the wise power would understand. It would see in that gesture an invitation, an acknowledgment, a benediction. The Canopy was not a thing to be changed or saved or even understood. It was a thing to be witnessed, to be experienced, to be allowed to continue its patient work of incorporation and remembrance.

The power would stay, then. Not to impose its will, but to learn the deeper rhythms of the place, to attune itself to the slow song of plant and fungus and quiet green time. It would wander the endless cathedrals of living wood, marvel at the stately procession of beast-forests, meditate beneath the rain of liquid sunlight that fell in shafts through the eternal twilight.

And as it stayed, as the months stretched into years and the years into centuries, the power too would begin to change. Its edges would soften, its hurry would gentle. The Canopy would begin to grow through it and it through the Canopy, until the distinction between the two became academic, a matter of old semantics from a world that now seemed little more than a half-remembered dream.

In the end, it would simply take its place among the Architects, one more gardener in this endless garden, tending to the slow work of guiding all that was into all that would be. It would add its voice to their long dreaming song, and in time that voice too would grow slow and green and deep as the ages.

The only sign of its passing, in the end, might be a subtle new color threaded through the veins of every leaf, a particular sheen of light that had not been there before - the final trace of a wisdom that had traveled from beyond the stars to be subsumed and incorporated into the patient, abiding dream of the Canopy. 

One more note in the long, slow hymn of a world that had finally and fully outgrown the need for anything but itself.
