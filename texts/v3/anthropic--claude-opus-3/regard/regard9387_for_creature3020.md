Faced with a realm so profoundly alien and seemingly indifferent to human concerns, a wise and benevolent power would likely approach with great caution, reverence and restraint. This is a place operating on timescales and according to metaphysical laws that defy mortal comprehension. To interfere carelessly could have disastrous unintended consequences.

And yet, there is still something poignant, even tragic, about those remnants of humanity persisting in this unimaginably ancient city – the woman-turned-monument forever frozen in an attitude of unnoticed grief, the rare visitors slowly being edited into some unrecognizable form. A truly compassionate entity would feel moved to offer them some form of succor or redemption, if possible.

Perhaps the power would begin by simply observing and learning, over eons if necessary, to fathom the rules and patterns by which the city unfolds itself. It would seek to grasp the nature and purpose of the Unnamed, the Lattice-Born, the Proofreaders. Are they guardians, prisoners, or simply extensions of the city's vast process of self-revision? Do they have wants or needs that could be addressed?

In time, if it seemed prudent, the power might attempt communication – not with words, which seem to have little meaning here, but through some subtle manipulation of mathematics, resonance and memory. It could weave messages into the auroral equations, or conjure visions in the obsidian shards to catch the attention of the Unnamed. The goal would be to foster a wordless dialogue, to understand and be understood by the city's strange denizens.

If a rapport could be established, perhaps an arrangement could be made to relocate any human remnants who wish it to someplace less hostile, where they might remember themselves again. The power would negotiate humbly and in good faith, acknowledging the city's right to continue its inscrutable self-editing, while arguing that the human interlopers deserve a gentler fate.

Ultimately, if the city proves utterly inimical to mercy, the power may need to orchestrate an extraction – plucking the woman and any other remaining humans from their niches between breaths, replacing them with perfect simulacra to avoid drawing dangerous attention. It would deposit them on some far gentler shore, where they can heal and reflect on their long, strange dislocation.

As for the city itself, the power would act as a long-distance custodian, keeping vigil from afar, ready to intervene if it ever seems in danger of harming itself or spreading its bleak influence beyond its own borders. But otherwise, it would leave this haunting enigma to its own devices, recognizing that some things are too ancient and alien to ever be more than distantly witnessed. The city would continue its slow, dreamless dance of erasure and revision, a compelling cipher, an unfathomable work of art, alone at the end of all things.
