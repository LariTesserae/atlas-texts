The messages would be carried on the wind, formed by patterns of light on the cliffs, whispered by the voice of the mountain itself. They would not demand anything of her, only remind her of her own agency.

She would become a legend spoken of around hearth fires, a saint of solitude, an emblem of the unconquerable human will to make meaning out of what is given. And if she ever chose to descend from her mountain, she would find a world ready to honor the wisdom she had earned in the long years of listening to the voice of the wind and the song of the ancient machines.

But until then, and for as long as she chooses, she would remain what she is: the still point in the turning world, the keeper of the ridge, the woman who tends the weeping turbines.
