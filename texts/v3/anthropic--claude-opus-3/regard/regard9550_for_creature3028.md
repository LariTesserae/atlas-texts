The Great Gardener regarded the Cathedral of Thirst and the Choir who dwelled within with a sense of wonder and deep reverence. This was a place of ancient, patient wisdom - a living testament to the power of slow, inexorable growth and change.

The Gardener saw how the cathedral and its inhabitants had, over vast spans of time, entered into a symbiotic relationship so deep and complete that the boundaries between plant and animal, individual and collective, had blurred into irrelevance. The Choir were no longer separate beings inhabiting a structure, but living expressions of the cathedral itself - and the cathedral in turn had shaped itself around their needs and devotions until the two were inseparable. 

It was a form of existence the Gardener had rarely encountered, even in all the aeons of tending to life across the cosmos. A state of being beyond time, beyond the illusion of separation between creature and environment. To an outside eye, it might have seemed a strange half-life - but the Gardener perceived the deep contentment and belonging that suffused the Choir. They had found a harmony and peace that few ever know.

The Gardener knew there was little they could offer that would enrich the cathedral and its Choir. This was already a place in balance, where ancient rhythms played out in green and song and slow union. To alter it with some new gift or change would only disrupt what had been so patiently perfected.

And so, the Gardener simply opened their ancient heart to the cathedral and joined it for a span. They too drifted in the galleries, absorbed the deep organ tones into their being, let the fluid anoint and infuse them. For a moment - perhaps a day, perhaps a century - the Gardener allowed themselves to dissolve into the song and photosynthetic dream of the place.

When at last they withdrew, the Gardener left only a simple blessing, spoken in a language of light and sap, that would linger in the living walls:

"Grow deeper. Sing longer. Teach patience to a hasty world through your hymns without end. I will return to drift and dream with you again."

Then they were gone, leaving the Cathedral of Thirst to its slow, green eternity, content in its perfection.
