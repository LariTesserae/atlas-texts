A benevolent and wise power, observing this remarkable world of plunging canyons, relentless currents, and the extraordinary anchor-kin who have made it their home, would likely choose to intervene only minimally, if at all. The reasons for this are multifold:

1. Respect for the anchor-kin's autonomy and resilience: The anchor-kin have evolved to not merely survive, but to thrive in this uniquely challenging environment. They have developed a complex society and ingenious adaptations that allow them to harness the very forces that threaten to destroy them. To interfere with their way of life, even with the best of intentions, would be to undermine their hard-won agency and resilience.

2. Recognition of the anchor-kin's symbiotic relationship with their environment: The anchor-kin do not simply inhabit the rusting remnants of heavy industry; they have formed a profoundly intimate and symbiotic relationship with these structures. They are actively working to replace the dying metal with living, organic materials, fusing their own biology with the machinery. This process is a testament to their adaptability and ingenuity, and should be allowed to unfold naturally.

3. Appreciation for the beauty and complexity of the ecosystem: The drowned cordillera, with its violent currents, lush kelp forests, and teeming marine life, is a unique and intricate ecosystem. The anchor-kin play a crucial role in maintaining the balance of this system, orchestrating the flow of nutrients and energy through their hunting and harvesting practices. To disrupt this balance, even with the intention of easing the anchor-kin's struggle, could have unintended and far-reaching consequences.

4. Trust in the anchor-kin's ability to shape their own future: The anchor-kin are not passive victims of their environment, but active agents in shaping their own destiny. They are undertaking a monumental, multi-generational task of replacing the dying machines with living structures. This process is a testament to their foresight, unity, and determination. A wise power would trust in the anchor-kin's ability to forge their own path forward.

That being said, the benevolent power might choose to offer subtle, indirect forms of assistance that do not undermine the anchor-kin's autonomy or disrupt the delicate balance of their world. This could take the form of:

- Gently guiding the evolution of the kelp and other marine life to be even stronger and more resilient, better able to withstand the intense currents and provide materials for the anchor-kin's construction efforts.

- Subtly influencing the currents and tides to create more pockets of stillness and respite, giving the anchor-kin more opportunities to rest, gather, and tend to their young.

- Imbuing the anchor-kin's mucus secretions with additional protective and healing properties, to better shield them from the constant abrasion and injury of their environment.

- Seeding the anchor-kin's collective consciousness with flashes of inspiration and insight, to help them develop even more ingenious solutions to the challenges they face.

Ultimately, the wise power would recognize that the anchor-kin and their world are a testament to the indomitable power of life to adapt, to endure, and to find beauty and meaning in even the most hostile of circumstances. To honor this, the power would watch with compassion and awe, intervening only in the most subtle and respectful of ways, and trusting in the anchor-kin's resilience and wisdom to guide them through the storms ahead.
