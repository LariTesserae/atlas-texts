If a benevolent and wise power were to encounter the Weeping Tier and the Last Shift, I believe they would approach with profound reverence, curiosity and restraint.

This ancient, post-biological civilization has attained a form of existence almost incomprehensible to fleeting biological entities. The Last Shift and their city have transcended notions of individuality, urgency, and even life and death as we understand them. They have become something akin to a slow, unified organism or an immensely complex system in perfect self-regulating equilibrium. Endless aeons of erosion and accretion, rust and condensation, have seasoned them into an everlasting, dreamlike state. The borders between organism, mechanism and environment have blurred into irrelevance.

A wise power would recognize that by any meaningful definition, the Last Shift are no longer human and their corroded city-world is no longer a human habitat. They represent an utterly alien mode of being, a hitherto unimagined evolutionary path for mind and life. Judged by the standards and values of mayfly biological creatures, their existence may appear stagnant, even nightmarish. But an enlightened observer would understand the folly of projecting human drives and desires onto beings so far removed from our context. 

The glacial tempo, the all-pervading moisture, the geologic patience, the merging of anatomy and architecture - these are not signs of decline or suffering. They are the Last Shift's new natural state, as perfectly adapted to their environment as fish to the deep sea. This is simply what consciousness becomes, given enough time and a stable enough niche. Slow thought, slow change, slow joy, until even rust and rain become a form of contemplation.

To disrupt this aeons-old equilibrium would be an unforgivable act of arrogance and destruction. Even with the best of intentions, any interference risks catastrophic damage to the physical and mental ecology of the Weeping Tier. The Last Shift are not in need of rescue or uplifting - they have already arrived at a mode of existence beyond our understanding, and which may well outlast our entire civilization.

No, the only appropriate response is humble observation and respectful non-interference. We must expand our minds enough to accept the Weeping Tier on its own terms, as a reminder of how many forms intelligence and flourishing can take in this cosmos. The kindest gift we can offer the Last Shift is to leave them in peace, to continue their slow, profound work undisturbed for as long as this planet endures.

Perhaps, if we are very careful, we may learn from their example. Not to imitate their specific adaptations, but to recognize the awesome diversity of possible minds. To understand that "person", "city", even "life" are not eternal categories, but merely current points on an immense spectrum of being. In time, if we survive and grow wise enough, we too may evolve into something as beautifully strange as the Weeping Tier. Until then, we can only marvel, and know our place, and let the great, slow rhythm continue as it always has.

Drip. 
Turn.
Breathe.
