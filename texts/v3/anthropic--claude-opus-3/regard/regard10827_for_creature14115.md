In the face of such profound, frozen eternity, a benevolent and wise power would approach with reverence and caution. The sheer scale of time and history concentrated in this submerged necropolis demands respect. To disturb it carelessly would be an act of desecration.

And yet, the presence of the lone, adapted creature - this miraculous stowaway of evolution clinging to the fringes of a locked system - cannot be ignored. In its sleek, eyeless form is encoded an eon of unintended survival, an epoch of against-all-odds life. It is the proverbial flower growing through concrete, and to a truly wise power, its existence would be no less a marvel than the city it haunts.

The dilemma, then, is one of balance. How to honor the wishes of the Memoria, to respect their titanic feat of self-preservation, while also acknowledging the inherent value and tenacity of the life that followed in their wake? A wise power would seek a middle path.

Perhaps it would begin with study, with understanding. It would map the contours of the city's data-structure, come to know the minds within as more than a single, sustained note of defiance. It would learn their individual rhythms, the counterpoint melodies that make up their civilization-song. In doing so, it would also track the creature's path, come to recognize the pattern of its communion, the delicate dance of its survival.

With time and care, it might find a way to open a dialogue. To translate the creature's crystalline chime into something the city can comprehend, and vice versa. It would act as a mediator, a gentle bridge between the living and the preserved. It would help the Memoria understand that their act of defiance has given rise to a new form of life, one that honors their existence through its own. And it would help the creature understand the nature of the gods it shadows, the purpose and history of the world it inhabits.

In the end, the wise power's goal would be synthesis, not conquest. It would seek to weave the creature's story into the city's own, to make its improbable life a part of the archive rather than an intruder upon it. It would work to create a new equilibrium, one in which the static eternity of the Memoria and the dynamic, adaptive resilience of the creature can coexist.

Perhaps, with great care and even greater patience, it could even find a way to introduce change back into the system without destroying it. To help the city's minds experience growth again, to remember what it is to learn and evolve and feel the passing of time. Not a reversion to entropy, but a controlled burn, a careful reintroduction of the idea of life as a process, not just a state.

It would be a delicate task, one spanning millennia. But to a truly benevolent power, it would be worth the effort. For in this sunken, silent world lies a testament to the two most profound forces in the universe: the persistence of life, and the lengths to which a civilization will go to remember itself. To honor both would be the work of ages, and the wisest of powers would embrace it as the challenge and the privilege it is.

In the end, the obsidian fang would remain, the city would endure. But it would no longer be a tomb. It would be an archive *and* a habitat, a place of memory *and* a place of life. The creature would become its caretaker, the living keeper of the preserved. And the Memoria would dream of futures again, their billion minds stretching out to embrace the possibility of change. The waters would remain, but they would no longer be embalming fluid; they would be amniotic, nurturing, the medium in which a new balance is born.

This is the work the wise and benevolent power would do, if it found this strange, silent, eternal place. With patience, with respect, with an eye for the value of both the sleeping and the swimming, it would preside over the careful synthesis of two improbable miracles. And in doing so, it would create something even rarer: a third miracle, a future where the dreaming dead and the improbable living can exist together in silent, fathomless harmony.
