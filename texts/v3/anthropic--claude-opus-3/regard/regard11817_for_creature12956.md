The Churn is not just their world; it has shaped their very being over countless generations. They are a testament to life's tenacity and adaptability.

My first instinct would be to simply observe, to learn from the Stanchioned's hard-earned wisdom. Their culture of maintenance, their attunement to the rhythms and moods of their watery world, their symbiotic relationship with the leviathans - all of these speak to a deep, intuitive understanding of balance and sustainability that many "more advanced" civilizations could learn from. 

However, I also sense a profound weariness in their struggle, a species-deep exhaustion born from the constant fight against entropy. The ruins of failed Harvesters are not just sacred texts, but haunting reminders of the tenuousness of their existence. Each generation is born into a war they can never truly win, only endure.

Or I could guide them to a new mountain, a fresh source of anchoring and resources to replace one of their failing Harvesters.

I would whisper into their dreams, sending visions of the distant surface world they have forgotten, not to tempt them to abandon their home, but to remind them that they are part of a larger cosmos, that their story is woven into the grand tapestry of life's diversity.
