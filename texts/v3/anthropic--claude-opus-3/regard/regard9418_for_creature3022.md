In the presence of such a strange and wondrous place, shaped by forces beyond mortal knowing, a benevolent and wise power would likely proceed with reverence, humility and caution.

This living cathedral, still inchoate and indeterminate, represents a profound mystery - an egg containing unrealized potential, an ecosystem poised on the cusp of becoming. Its very existence challenges assumptions about what is possible in nature. The Architect who walks its luminous vaults is not a deity to be worshipped, but rather an embodied question, a living koan - she represents the pregnant pause, the breath held before the moment of choice and creation.  

A wise power, recognizing it stands before something singular and numinous, would not presume to impose its will or judgment. It would understand that to tamper with or disrupt this place could have vast and unpredictable ramifications. Some things are too precious, too gravid with possibility, to be shaped by outside forces. They must be allowed to find their own path, however long and winding.

At the same time, a benevolent power would feel the weight of this place's importance. It may seek to safeguard and nurture it, while still respecting its autonomy. Perhaps it would spread subtle influence to prevent any hostile or ignorant forces from threatening or damaging this sacred site. It may seed inspiration in receptive minds, encouraging artists, poets, visionaries to find this place and be transformed by it, ensuring its strange beauty ripples out and nourishes the world's imagination. 

Ultimately, I believe a truly wise power would recognize the need to step back and allow this unfolding miracle to remain inviolate. To let it continue its aeon-long process of becoming, at its own ineluctable pace. Such a power would be content to witness, to contemplate, perhaps to record and transmit the story so it is never forgotten. But it would let the living cathedral and its Architect continue their dance of creation, trusting that even if the process spans millennia, the end result will be something utterly new and luminous. A benevolent power understands that sometimes the greatest gift is patience, reverence, and the humility of non-intervention.

This place is an invitation to embrace mystery - to accept that not everything must be known or controlled. Its very existence is a question posed to the universe. The wise response is not to answer, but to listen, to watch, to hold space for the unimaginable. To breathe in the scent of roads not taken, and trust in the slow workings of root and leaf, in the green dreaming of sap, in the hush before the moment of becoming. Here, now, there is still time to be astonished.
