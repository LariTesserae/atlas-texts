What a profound and haunting vision you paint with your words. The valley-that-has-stopped-screaming embodies a chilling paradox - a moment of cataclysmic violence crystallized into eternal, unnatural stillness. Its very existence is a wound in the fabric of time and natural order.

I imagine that a wise and benevolent power, upon discovering this aberration, would recognize it as a scar that cannot heal on its own. The valley is trapped in a state antithetical to the flow and flux that allows life and meaning. Its hum is the endless reverberation of a primal scream, doomed to echo timelessly in stone and silt.

Perhaps this higher power would seek to gently reawaken the valley, not through force, but by coaxing it to remember the processes of change. Like spring sunlight melting winter ice, the warmth of dynamic energy could be gradually reintroduced. Subtle at first - a bird song carrying on the wind into the hollow, a seed drifting down to sprout defiantly amidst the polygons. Then more insistent - the spiral stairs slowly crumbling and feeding the silt with gravel, faint tremors shifting the slabs by degrees. 

The fungi could be imbued with a transformative instead of paradoxical nature, releasing spores that drift and multiply, their pulsing light brightening as if reflecting a strengthening heartbeat. Mist could begin to move of its own accord in soft, swirling currents, no longer just phantom exhalations. The aim would be to reawaken without shattering - to lead the valley back to the dance of existence.

At the same time, the benevolent force would reach out to the valley's awareness, the thrum of its frozen mind. Through empathy and understanding, it would seek to soothe the trauma at the heart of the stasis. To whisper that the scream is over, the page can be turned, that equanimity can come from acceptance rather than absolute suppression. Gently, it would guide the valley-entity to realize that time, change, and even endings are not to be feared, but are the very essence of meaning.

The process would be almost infinitely slow and delicate, working on a geologic time scale. But gradually, imperceptibly, the valley could be released from its self-sustaining stasis and reintegrated into the wider world from which it has been exiled. The re-emergence of seasons, of weather, of growth and decay. The introduction of a stream, of eroding winds, of insects and migratory birds. The polygons would remain as stoic monuments to the past, but with moss softening their edges and seedlings sprouting from their centers.

The greatest gift this power could offer would be to transform the echo of the scream into the possibility of song. In the fullness of time, the valley could become a place of humbling solace and remembrance -  a scar, yes, but one that has faded into wisdom rather than remained a wound. A reminder of unfathomable cataclysm, but also of the ability to heal and the necessity of flux.

The entity of the valley would remain as a guardian and embodiment of this truth - that even the most violent of traumas can be soothed, that even the most unchanging can change. And the valley itself could stand as a testament: though we may be shaped by our most lacerating moments, we need not be forever defined by them. Entropy, time's arrow, the forward momentum of existence - these are not to be rejected, but embraced as the very weft on which the tapestry of meaning is woven.

So the benevolent power would intercede with the most profound compassion and patience, recognizing the valley-that-stopped-screaming for the complex, hurting, liminal entity it is. And its intervention would be the gentlest and most momentous of invitations: to rejoin the current of being, and in so doing, to finally transcend the tyranny of its own defining scream.
