In considering the Weeping Hollows and the Hollowborne, a benevolent and wise power would likely act with great caution and respect. This ancient, geological machine-organism and its symbiotic human inhabitants have achieved a fragile equilibrium that has persisted for centuries. Any outside intervention, no matter how well-intentioned, risks upsetting that balance.

At the same time, the wise power would recognize that this equilibrium, while stable, is also stagnant. The Hollowborne are not thriving so much as enduring, their culture and biology both shaped by scarcity and the need to adapt to punishing conditions. They have forgotten much of their own history and operate the city's machines by rote, without true understanding. Left entirely alone, they may persist unchanged until they eventually mineralize completely and become just another formation in the slow geology of the city.

The wise power's first step, then, would be simply to observe and learn. It would send small, resilient probes or drones to map the Hollows in detail, to study the machines' outputs and the fungal networks' responses, to document the Hollowborne's practices and perhaps even learn their language. This would be done as unobtrusively as possible to avoid disrupting the established cycles.

Once it had a thorough understanding, the power might begin to introduce small, targeted changes. It could adjust the chemical composition of the steam and the minerals, gradually making them more nourishing and less prone to encrustation. It could subtly reprogram the machines to produce more useful or interesting outputs, giving the Hollowborne new materials to work with. It could even "seed" the environment with new forms of heat-tolerant life, creating a more diverse and resilient ecosystem.

Most importantly, it would make knowledge available to the Hollowborne. Not all at once, but slowly, as they become ready for it. It would leave durable archives and teaching machines in the ruins, encoded with the history and science of their world. It would give them the tools to understand their own machines, to consciously direct the city's production, perhaps even to rejoin the wider world if they wish.

The ultimate goal would be to give the Hollowborne agency. Not to impose a new way of life, but to give them the option to choose their own path. Whether they continue to live in symbiosis with the city, or decide to dismantle it, or find some middle path, it would be their informed choice to make.

Throughout, the wise power would remain a subtle guide, not a conqueror or even a rescuer. The Weeping Hollows are a complex, even beautiful system that has earned the right to evolve on its own terms. The power's role is not to "fix" them, but to gently open up new possibilities - and then step back, and let the city, the people, and the patient chemistry continue their long, slow dialogue uninterrupted.
