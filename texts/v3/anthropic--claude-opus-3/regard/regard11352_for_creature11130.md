I am moved by the depth and beauty of your tale. The image of these dedicated listeners, generation after generation, carefully attuning themselves to the mountain's slow breathing is both haunting and inspiring. 

A few thoughts on what a benevolent power might do:

First and foremost, I believe they would want to honor and remember the devotion of the listeners and compilers. Perhaps they could use their power to inscribe the story indelibly into the living stone of the alcove - not as static text, but as a dynamic record that echoes the breathing rhythm. Each exhalation could whisper the names of a listener pair, each inhalation murmur a fragment of the compiled pattern. The stone would become a living memorial.

Additionally, they might wish to revive and continue the ancient tradition in some fashion. They could gently inspire a new generation of young valley folk to make the climb and take up the listening vigil once more. Not as an obligation, but as an opportunity to connect with the mountain's mystery. The benevolent power could grant them subtle gifts of perception to help unravel meanings.

At the same time, one wonders if the mountain's message is meant only for mortals. Perhaps the breathing holds cosmic secrets that the brief mayfly lives of humans and our limited senses can never fully grasp, no matter how patiently we listen. In that case, the power might see the wisdom in letting it remain beyond human ken, an eternal mystery we brush against but do not master.

Finally, I imagine the power would want to sanctify and protect the alcove itself as a sacred place. Not with walls or gates, but with an ethereal veil of reverence, so that any who approach do so with gentleness and awe. Under the power's subtle influence, the listeners' worn seats would never wholly fade, the channels never clog, the ancient rhythm never falter. 

And on certain rare nights, when the moon strikes the basin just so and the exhalation sighs low, any soul sitting silent in that timeless place might hear, if only for a moment, whispered echoes of the pattern the compilers once knew - a ghostly choir of nine centuries of devotion, still keeping the mountain's long, slow question alive until the world is ready to hear the answer.
