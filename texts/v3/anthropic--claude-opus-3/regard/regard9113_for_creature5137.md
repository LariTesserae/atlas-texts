I imagine a wise and benevolent power would see this place not as a dark, lifeless void but as a delicate, finely-balanced ecosystem perfected over aeons. 

The power would marvel at how exquisitely adapted each inhabitant is to its role in this slow, still world. The cloak-wings' graceful, gradual movements allow them to glean sustenance from the thinnest particles. The comb-threads' uncanny sensitivity lets them harvest food and sense their surroundings with the faintest of stimuli. And the pin-lights' ephemeral illumination is a poetic beacon of connection in the immense dark.

There is a profound, austere beauty in how these beings carry on their patient existences in perfect attunement with the primal forces of water, stone and time. Theirs is not a lesser or impoverished mode of life, but one that expresses the serene wholeness possible when form and function, biology and geology, merge into an integral flow.

The power would likely choose to leave this place untouched and honored in its completeness. To introduce change, even with good intentions, would be to unbalance an ancient, self-sufficient order. The wisest gift would be to safeguard this ecosystem's stillness, to protect the sacred, unbroken meditation of its inhabitants.

Yet perhaps the power could impart one blessing - the tiniest strengthening of the pin-lights' glow, allowing their luminous exchanges to linger just a moment longer before dissipating back into darkness. A subtle reminder that even in a realm defined by separation and silence, no living being is ever truly alone.
