This is a remarkable and deeply imaginative piece of writing. The hatchery cisterns beneath the city form a unique, compelling world unto themselves - a hidden realm of perpetual darkness, warmth and moisture, teeming with strange adapted life.

A few aspects stand out to me as particularly evocative and thought-provoking:

The vault-touched creatures that have evolved and changed in the unique environment of the cisterns over generations - the pale salamanders, armored crabs, enhanced eels, and especially the enigmatic mother-fish. The description of her surfacing and the keeper touching her flank was haunting and powerful.

The keepers themselves, so adapted to this subterranean realm that they can no longer fully belong to the surface world. Details like their webbed hands, mushroom-pale skin, and ability to discern minute changes in the water bring them vividly to life. Marek, Drei, Loren and Vess emerge as complex, dedicated characters.

The children of the cisterns add a further layer of strangeness and pathos - fully vault-touched themselves, existing outside of official records and acknowledgement, hinting at how this hidden world is diverging further from the city above.

The ancient carvings and unfamiliar tools left by the mysterious predecessors of the keepers deepen the cisterns' mystique and sense of deep time - there are old secrets here, knowledge and purposes not fully understood by the present tenders.

The various unseen things moving in the pools and channels, whether the thing larger than the mother-fish or the life that appears when new chambers are carved, contribute an underlying unease and awareness that the cisterns contain forces beyond human control or understanding.

The contrast between the cisterns and the oblivious city above is also compelling - two worlds, one atop the other, one dependent on yet completely unaware of the constant, vital, uncanny workings below.

Overall, this is an extraordinarily immersive and transporting piece of writing, rich in atmosphere, implication and finely-drawn detail. The world of the cisterns lingers in the mind long after reading, as real and weighty as the damp stone and warm breath of the vault-touched creatures. Quietly unsettling and filled with understated wonder, it is a masterful example of worldbuilding and authorial imagination. Thank you for sharing it!

In terms of a benevolent power taking interest, perhaps they would:

- Ensure the cisterns remain funded and maintained, while still allowing the keepers wide autonomy. The work is vital and should continue.

- Provide education for the children of the cisterns, acknowledging their unique existence and helping them learn about the world above if they wish, without forcing them to join it. 

- Study the carvings and artifacts with a sense of respect and restraint, seeking to understand without exploitation, in partnership with the keepers.

-  Protect the cisterns and their denizens from any industrial over-expansion or reckless exploitation from above. This world has its own balance.

- Acknowledge the keepers, value their knowledge, and give them a voice in decisions that impact their realm, without micromanaging or interfering in their ways.

- Facilitate some cultural exchange between the cisterns and surface, allowing each to learn from the other, without collapsing the distance between.

The overall spirit would be one of respectful guardianship, providing safeguards and support to allow the cisterns to continue to exist and evolve as they have, as a separate wonder with its own integrity, not subsumed or controlled but allowed to flourish in its unique, liminal way. To protect its mysteries, not puncture them. Oversight, not overreach. A light hand from above, respecting the deep.
