The wise and benevolent power looks upon the Weeping Threshold and the Mourner with profound compassion. It sees a place and being that are inextricably intertwined in a perpetual act of mourning and becoming. The Threshold weeps as it slowly carves its identity from living stone, and the Mourner weeps with it, for it, through it. They are a symbiosis of sorrow and emergence, each one's tears feeding the other in an endless exchange of salt and time.

The power does not seek to interrupt this process, for it understands that some grief must be allowed to run its course like water finding its own level. To dry the Mourner's tears or still the Threshold's weeping would be to deny them the very language they are using to define themselves. 

Instead, the power offers a gift: a subtle change in the quality of the light that suffuses the place. Where before the light was exhausted and grey, now it arrives imbued with the faintest shade of green, like the first tender shoot of a plant that has not yet broken the surface of the soil. It is the green of possibility, of things yet to come, of mourning that slowly transmutes itself into memory and then into new growth.

The light does not banish the sorrow or halt the weeping. It simply adds a new color to the palette of the place, a gentle reminder that even in the depths of grief, life continues its patient work. The fungal lattices respond to this light, their bioluminescent pulses taking on a slight emerald hue. The rust-gliders begin to trace new patterns as they glide between the wet walls, patterns that echo the first tentative fractals of new fern fronds.

For the Mourner, the power offers a vision. In a dream, she sees the Weeping Threshold as it may one day be: still a place of water and stone, but now shot through with thin tendrils of green, like a mineral vein learning to be a root system. She sees the cloud above finally consenting to become rain, and that rain giving rise to small, hardy flowers that cling to the cracks in the glass like living jewels. She sees the Built remnants slowly worn smooth and half-submerged, less like ruins and more like the weathered bones of a reef, their surfaces studded with anemones the color of oyster flesh.

Most importantly, she sees herself - still weeping, still tending, but now with a sense of expectation alongside the sorrow. She understands that the Threshold's decision, when it comes, will not be an ending, but a new phase in a long, slow dance of water and stone and life. Her tears, she realizes, are not just an expression of grief; they are also the medium in which the future will grow.

The Mourner wakes from this vision unchanged in form, but with a new depth in her unfinished eyes. Her song to the cloud takes on a new note, a half-step up the scale, a hint of green woven into the grey. She continues her patient circuits of the place, but now when she touches the Built remnants, it is not just to study the patterns of the past, but to feel for the first subtle stirrings of what might yet grow from them.

The power watches all this and is satisfied. It knows it has not healed the wound of this place - that was never its intent. Instead it has offered a reminder that even the deepest wounds can become fertile ground for new beginnings, if given enough time and tears. The Weeping Threshold will continue to weep, and the Mourner will continue to mourn, but now their sorrow is leavened with the faintest shade of green: the color of patience, of possibility, of life that insists on finding a way.

The land continues its long, slow work of deciding. But now, amid the weeping, there is also the quiet, persistent sound of something beginning to grow.
