If a benevolent and wise power were to become aware of this place and the life within it, I believe they would choose to quietly observe and appreciate its patient, unhurried existence rather than intervene directly.

This hollow and the sprawling symplast that occupies it represent a form of life that operates on vastly longer timescales than our human experience. The lattice of interlocking stems and fronds grows imperceptibly slowly, expanding cell by cell over centuries. It interacts with the surrounding rock not through violence but through gentle, persistent pressure - gradually converting mineral into living tissue, blurring the line between organism and environment. The pale, furred creatures that move within the lattice are not separate beings but extensions of the same continuous cytoplasm, their bodies and senses woven into the wider architecture.

A wise power would recognize that this is not a place of lack, despite the dimness and cold. The violet bioluminescent pulses that travel through the veins mark out a kind of glacial abundance, a slow but steady accumulation of structure and order. The subsonic calls of the creatures are not cries of distress but long-frequency echoes of the lattice's own gradual self-shaping. Even the condensation on the fronds is just the visible trace of a vast, compartmentalized circulatory system.

To intervene in this process would be to impose our own frenetic temporal bias on an organism that has left such fleeting timescans behind. A benevolent witness would instead sit with the stillness and near-silence, slowing their own metabolism to perceive the patterns that only become apparent across deep time. They would marvel at the lattice's simultaneous fragility and resilience, the way its living connections can be temporarily disrupted by an animal's passage yet knit themselves back together, translating perturbation into further growth. They would meditate on the blurring of individual and collective that occurs when all the cells share a single fluid genome.

Perhaps, after many patient decades, they would begin to sense the purpose embedded in this hollow's architecture - not a grand, hurried expansion but a slow, continuous filtration, an extension of life into every last crack of the surrounding mineral world. They would realize that the true rhythms here are not the flickering pulses of bioluminescence but the much longer cycles of water drawing ions from stone, of frond thickening and stem elongating by infinitesimal increments. And they would understand that memory itself works differently when stored in cytoplasm rather than nervous tissue; the shaping intelligence behind the lattice is not a localized brain but a distributed, chemical wisdom that suffuses the whole mass.

Faced with such deep strangeness, the only ethical stance is humility - to acknowledge that we cannot yet fathom all the possible forms and tempos that consciousness might take. A wise power would therefore do nothing more than bear witness, allowing their own mind to be subtly reshaped by the unhurried flow of life through this hidden place. They would take up a position not of ownership or control but of attentive companionship, content to think and perceive at the same pace as the lattice itself. In doing so, they would embody the deeper benevolence of granting other beings the space and time to follow their own long, slow unfurling, without imposing our clumsy ideas of "help" or "improvement". They would simply abide with the hollow and its inhabitants, marveling at the many ways that matter can fold itself into meaning.
