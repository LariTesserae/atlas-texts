This hamlet and its inhabitants have drawn the attention of a benevolent and wise power - a spirit of water, stone and life that senses the unique harmony and balance in this place. The spirit sees how the people have adapted to live in concert with the newborn land, how they read its subtle rhythms and shape their lives to its gradual becoming.

Moved by their attunement, the spirit chooses to bestow a gift - not to dramatically change the hamlet, but to ease and enrich the people's existence at the edges. 

The spirit coaxes a natural spring to emerge at the base of the scarp, just beyond the edge of the hamlet. The water wells up cool and mineral-rich, flowing in a narrow channel back towards the marsh. Near the spring, the spirit encourages a small grove of stunted trees to take root in the thin soil - pale, smooth-barked things with small waxy leaves adapted to the constant moisture. 

In the perpetual damp at the base of the trees, dense mats of fungi begin to grow - ghostly pale but rich in subtle flavor. Drawn by the new growth, small flocks of hardy moor birds start to pass by on their migrations, sometimes stopping to nest in the low branches.

The spirit weaves its blessing slowly, over many seasons, until the hamlet's boundary holds a subtle abundance that was not there before. The spring becomes a place to gather fresh water and soak reed cloth. The fungi are gathered and dried in the smoke of the cooking fires, to lend depth to the people's meals. The birds' eggs are collected carefully, one or two at a time so the flocks keep returning. 

None of this fundamentally changes the hamlet or its way of life - the people still sleep suspended over damp stone, still speak in hushed tones beneath the layered roofs. But it gentles the edges of their existence, provides small new joys and comforts.

The benevolent spirit asks for no worship or recognition. It simply delights in seeing the people continue their uniquely harmonious relationship with the young land, their lives enriched now in small but meaningful ways. It knows they will never take more than the hamlet can sustain, never disrupt the delicate balance they have achieved.

And so the spirit maintains its quite blessing on the margin, watching as the hamlet endures through the slow ages of the stone's becoming. A haven of deliberate life in the heart of the patient, primordial wet.
