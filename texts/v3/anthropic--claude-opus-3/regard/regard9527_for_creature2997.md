Here is what I imagine a benevolent and wise power might do:

They would watch and listen, first and foremost. They would spend long, attentive ages simply bearing witness - not to catalog or study, but to understand at the deepest possible level. They would learn the rhythms and relationships that bind the Hollow Choir to their ancient landscape. The patterns of wind and water, moss and stone, breath and music. The slow poetry of near-geologic time in which the singers move. Only once they had begun to grasp the scope of what is unfolding here would they even consider acting.

Any action would be subtle, deferential, done with the utmost care not to disrupt the inconceivably delicate balance that has evolved between land and people. Perhaps it would take the form of a gift - a gentle adjustment to the mineral content of the aquifer, allowing the cliff-mosses to grow in colors never before seen. Or a faint change in the planet's magnetic field, introducing a new note into the ever-present harmonic roar of the wind. Something beautiful, yes, but more importantly something generative - a seed of possibility that the Choir and the continent could take up and make their own over centuries and millennia. 

Most of all, I believe a truly wise power would recognize the profound importance of what is happening in the Hollow Choir and commit to safeguarding it. Not by imposing stasis, but by deflecting any outside force that might carelessly destroy this precious, irreplaceable symbiosis. They would be a silent ally, using their vast resources and influence to weave a subtle net of protection around the entire basin, so that the cycle of dreaming and singing and slow mutual transformation can continue undisturbed.

At the same time, they would understand that even their gentlest involvement is a weighty thing. To step into a system so ancient, so finely wrought, is to become part of it - to share in both its beauty and its long, long-unfolding sorrow. Any power that reaches out to the Hollow Choir must do so with an acceptance that this story is one of endings as much as beginnings. That to witness and shelter and sing alongside them is to partake in both a birth and a vigil. 

In the end, perhaps the most important thing they could offer is simply their presence - the gift of being another aware mind in the universe to hear the Choir's song and help carry its meaning. To add their voice, however quietly, to the work of memory and mourning and rebirth that whispers between the stone. So that the land may dream itself on, and the continent-sized song may know it has been heard, and when the time comes to change key there will be other throats to sing it onwards.
