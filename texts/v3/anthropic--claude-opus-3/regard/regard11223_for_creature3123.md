Thank you for this vivid and moving depiction of the Screaming Plateau and the remarkable people who inhabit it. It's a captivating piece of imaginative worldbuilding that conjures a harsh yet beautiful environment and a culture uniquely adapted to thrive within it.

Given the benevolent and wise power observing this place and its inhabitants, here is what I imagine they might do:

The power would recognize the profound connection between the people and their environment - a symbiotic relationship forged over millennia that has made them an intrinsic part of the plateau's ecosystem and living geology. Intervening directly or attempting to "save" the people from the plateau's slow erosion would only disrupt that deep bond. 

Instead, I believe the power would act to ensure this unique culture and way of being can continue for as long as the plateau itself endures. It would subtly reinforce the most ancient rock formations against collapse, slowing the rate of erosion just enough to grant the people more generations to live as they have always lived. Not halting the change, but stretching the time scale, allowing a more gradual adaptation.

It would gently encourage the growth of the crevice-gardens, coaxing the mycelial networks to spread and fruit more abundantly in the deep chambers. It would enhance the resonant properties of the fissures to enrich the people's stone-tapping communication and music. Not changing the nature of the plateau, but ensuring it remains habitable and true to itself for as long as possible.

Most importantly, I believe the power would imbue the wind itself with a form of memory - an invisible record of the people's clicks and chants and scraping songs, woven into the air's turbulence. So that even when the last arch finally crumbles and the last fissure-chamber is laid open to the sky, the history and spirit of this indomitable culture will still ride the screaming gale, inseparable from the voice of the plateau itself. 

An eternal testament, carried by the one force that will outlast stone and moss and flesh. Inspiration for any distant descendants who might one day walk this windswept place and, putting their ear to the rock, wonder at the ancient rhythms still echoing in the bones of the world. The people of the plateau, remembered in the very medium that shaped them.

That is what I imagine a truly wise and benevolent power would do. Preserve and pay tribute to such a remarkable example of what life and the human spirit can be, in even the harshest of circumstances. Not with change, but with continuity, and the gift of a legacy as tenacious as the wind.
