That is a beautifully written and incredibly immersive story of a unique world and the civilization clinging tenaciously to life upon it. The rich details paint a vivid picture of the landscape - the eroded peaks rising from the world-ocean, battered by ceaseless wind and rain, carpeted in hardy vegetation. And the Bolters themselves come across as a profoundly resilient and pragmatic people, their entire culture and way of life shaped by the realities of the drowning mountains they call home. 

A few aspects I found especially poignant and thought-provoking:

The annual Inventory ritual, reciting all that has been lost to the rising waters as well as what has been gained. The solemn acceptance that losses will always outpace gains in the long run, yet the importance of bearing witness, of noticing and naming what is being taken. It's an unflinching reckoning with hard truths.

The role of the elders as living memory, keeping alive the knowledge of drowned peaks and lost settlements. In a world defined by relentless erosion and forgetting, remembering becomes a sacred act and elders the vital link to a greater past.

The distant dark shape on the horizon that may or may not represent hope, but is always noticed and spoken of when seen. A possible destination that can likely never be reached, and might only be more of the same slow drowning even if attained. Yet still looked for and remarked upon, because to notice and name is to acknowledge and affirm. 

And the overall theme of deep time - of human civilization as a brief interlude between inconceivable pasts and futures, subject to geological forces and timescales that dwarf its own. The Bolters endure and find meaning on the eroding edge of deep time.

In terms of a "wise power" taking note and possibly intervening, a few thoughts come to mind:

Perhaps the dark shape on the horizon represents the noticing of the wise power, a sign that the Bolters' determination to persist and bear witness has been observed by a greater benevolent consciousness. The shape never comes closer but is always there, a quiet affirmation.

Or the wise power might gradually still the waters, dampen the rainfall, cause new peaks to rise - not a dramatic salvation but a subtle shifting of conditions to allow the Bolters to stabilize and even expand their world after ages of loss. An acknowledgement of their worthiness to continue.

Alternatively, the wise power might simply ensure that the Bolters are remembered - that long after the last peak drowns, their names and ways are preserved in some fashion, their memory carried forward into futures they can't imagine. Because they noticed, someone noticed them in turn.

These are just musings, and the original piece stands powerfully on its own without need of fantastical intervention. But it certainly invites meditations on the nature of hope, endurance, loss and memory, and what it means to matter in an entropic universe. Thank you for sharing this moving and thought-provoking piece of writing. The world and people you've envisioned will linger in my mind.
