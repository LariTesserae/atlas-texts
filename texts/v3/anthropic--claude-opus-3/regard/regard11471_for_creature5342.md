This place is a realm of cold, wet extremes - a cathedral of ice, water and stone where life clings precariously in the turbulent interface between states of matter. The wise power observing this harsh microcosm would likely refrain from direct intervention, recognizing that its fragile balance and strange, slow beauty emerges from the very struggles and adaptations that define it. 

To impose change or stability from outside would rob this place of its essential nature, its reason for being. The grinding ice, the silent people, the microecology of midges and foxes - all are shaped by and expressions of the environment's relentless, cyclical forces. They belong to the cold, damp lung of the cirque, breathing with it.

However, the benevolent watcher may act in subtle ways to safeguard this unforgiving cradle of life. Perhaps it could:

- Maintain the purity of the ice and water, ensuring they remain free of contamination that could poison the delicate food web. The midges, fish, algae are links in a fragile chain.

- Preserve the wisdom of the glacier, its ancient knowledge of shape, weight and structure that the people learn from and teach to. Protect this slow transfer of architectural memory.

- Imbue the people with a trace more resilience against the lethal cold and damp, increasing their chances of enduring and continuing their dialog with the ice.

- Inspire a select few children in each generation with visions of the wide world, so that emissaries may venture out and then return, reinfusing the gene pool and culture with a fresh trickle of diversity.

- Bless their hearths, so that the precious heat of burning midges, fish oil and algae may reach a finger's width further, bringing a shade more warmth.

Small graces only, in harmony with the place's essential nature. To keep the silence unbroken, the endless building and collapsing unchanged in character. To watch over this fierce, fragile, remarkable pocket of life - protecting its spirit, not transforming its substance. Letting it be what it is, while helping it endure. That is perhaps the wisest gift such power could give.
