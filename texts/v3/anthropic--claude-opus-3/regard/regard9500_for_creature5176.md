In this spare and lifeless place, a benevolent and wise power would likely recognize the profound stillness and slow, inexorable processes that shape the cirque over eons. Rather than seeking to alter or "improve" this austere environment, they would respect its primordial rhythms and leave it largely undisturbed.

However, the power might make a subtle intervention to honor and highlight the human traces that remain - the stone circle, flaked quartzite, pecked basin. With a gentle touch, they could arrest the gradual erosion of these artifacts, preserving them as enduring echoes of the hands that made them long ago. Not to disturb the cirque's natural processes, but to maintain these faint signatures as a reminder of the fleeting human presence against the vastness of geologic time.

Perhaps the power would also imbue the site with an almost imperceptible hum of sacredness, a resonance tuned to the ancient silence rather than intruding upon it. Those who venture into the cirque - seekers, wanderers, artists - might feel this subtle presence and be moved to contemplation and wonder, reminded of their own small place in the universe. The rare visitor would leave as little trace as their predecessors, taking away only a sharpened awareness of time, solitude, and the spare beauty of stone and sky.

Mostly, the wise power would act as a near-invisible guardian, ensuring that this timeless place remains undisturbed but subtly deepening its atmosphere of stillness and mystery. Under their watch, the cirque would endure as it always has - a place folded in its own vast rhythms, where the only movement is the slow breathing of stone, and the only marks are the fading traces of those who passed through and were humbled by all that remains when they are gone.
