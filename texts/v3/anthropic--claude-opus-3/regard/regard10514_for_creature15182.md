In contemplating this fledgling city being birthed from the living rock, I sense that a benevolent and wise power would approach with a profound sense of reverence and restraint. This is a sacred act of creation, a delicate dance between the mountain's unhurried patience and the gentle, persistent ingenuity of the people shaping it into a home.

The wise power would not seek to impose its will or vision upon this place. It would recognize the deep wisdom already at work - in the way the people read the stone, finding the natural paths of water and root, coaxing forth chambers and passageways in a rhythm untethered from the turning of the sun. There is an ancient knowing in their hands, a primal bond with the bones of the earth itself. To disrupt that would be hubris.

Instead, the power would offer itself as a humble ally, a subtle guide working in harmony with what is already unfolding. It might whisper to the mountain, easing the way for those pale tendrils seeking soil, ensuring pockets of nourishment nestle within reach of each new-cut room. It could gently gather the moisture that films the walls, spinning it into slender rivulets that feed the basins steadily, so thirst is never a pressing concern. 

When the diggers sleep, curled against the warm stone, the power might drift through their dreams, seeding visions of how the next chambers could unfold, where a larger vault might serve the community, where a narrower passage could spiral up to bring a shaft of daylight down. But these would remain mere suggestions, ephemeral as the mist on waking, leaving the true choices in human hands.

Above all, the power would act as a warden, an unseen protector holding vigil over this hypogeal genesis. It would shore up the weight of the mountain's patience, ensuring no sudden shift or fracture imperils the delicate labor below. It would keep a mindful eye on the surface world, warding away any threat or intrusion that might shatter the hush, flood the chambers with unmeant light. 

The greatest gift such a power could offer would be the gift of time - the slow, measureless time of stone, root, and water. Time for the city to find its form organically, for the people to learn the mountain's language at their own pace, to make this place their own through the work of hands and the low murmur of lungs. In that unrushed obscurity, something unique and precious is being born - a way of being human in deep intimacy with the earth. That is a birth the wise power would shelter, not shape, allowing it to unfold at the pace of dripping water, curling root, and the human heart learning to beat in time with the deep stone. The power's role is not to create, but to create the conditions for creation - to hold the space, safeguard the time, and let the mountain and its children find each other in the dark.
