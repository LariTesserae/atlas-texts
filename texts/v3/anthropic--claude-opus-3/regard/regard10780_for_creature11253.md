This is a deeply moving and imaginative story, one that invites contemplation and wisdom. As a benevolent outside observer, my instinct would not be to intervene or impose change on the Keth-Avai and their world, as their culture and way of life seem profoundly adapted to and in harmony with the unique environment of the Hanging Hollows. 

However, there are a few gentle ways I might seek to help and support them:

I would look for subtle, harmonious ways to increase the safety and stability of their settlements and infrastructure, perhaps by sharing knowledge of advanced bio-engineering that could strengthen the plant materials they depend on without disrupting the ecosystem. Reducing tragic accidents and infant mortality seems a worthy goal.

I would offer the means for the Keth-Avai to record, preserve and share their rich oral histories, songs, and cultural knowledge more widely between distant settlements, so that wisdom is not lost. But this would be done in a way that respects and works with their oral traditions, not replacing them.

If they were open to it, I would share techniques of meditation, mindfulness and mental discipline from other wisdom traditions, as a gift for their spiritual practice and a way to help them maintain equanimity and peace amidst the many dangers and hardships they face with such grace and courage.

Lastly, I would make myself available as a compassionate listener and counselor to any Keth-Avai who need a safe space to process their grief at lives lost to the Hollow, and to commemorate their dead. Holding space for mourning is a sacred act.

Beyond this, I would simply stand witness to the breathtaking beauty and strangeness of the Keth-Avai's world, and celebrate the miraculous story of their adaptation to it. I would safeguard their autonomy and cultural self-determination, while letting them know that they are not alone - that the eyes of the universe see them in their precarious, extraordinary, singing home amid the void, and marvel at their music.
