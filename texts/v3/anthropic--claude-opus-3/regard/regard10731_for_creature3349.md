What a breathtaking and profound portrait of this liminal place and its inhabitants. The evocative imagery and patient, meditative prose perfectly capture a sense of geological time, of a landscape and people in an unresolved state of becoming. A few reflections on what a benevolent and wise power might be moved to do:

Firstly, I think such a power would recognize the deep attunement and respectful relationship the inhabitants have cultivated with this fluid, unfixed environment. Their way of life, while perhaps seeming precarious or rudimentary to an outsider, represents a hard-won adaptation, even a form of wisdom. To disrupt or change it based on any notion of "improving" it would be the height of arrogance. 

At the same time, the hints of a richer past civilization drowned below the waters are intriguing. A truly wise power might be motivated to somehow retrieve and preserve any knowledge, art or perspectives lost in that deluge, if a way could be devised to do so without damaging the current ecosystem and way of life.

Additionally, while the terraced settlement seems stable for now, the specter of rising waters and further inundation looms. Perhaps some subtle interventions could be made, not to arrest the processes of change, but to give the people more time to adapt to them. Encouraging the growth of binding plants to shore up eroding cliffsides, or seeding the waters with fish to provide an additional food source. Small gestures to expand their options without pretending to fix their fate.

Mostly though, I feel that a wise power would simply bear witness, would document and celebrate the remarkable lifeways the terrace dwellers have forged in dialogue with such a dynamic landscape. Theirs is a story of resilience, of finding meaning and rootedness even in a world of constant flux. A story that deserves to be heard and contemplated by the wider world, as we all in our own ways grapple with living on the shifting shorelines and floodplains of an era of massive change. The most benevolent thing to do may be to learn from their example, and let their hard-earned wisdom flow outward, a gift to all of us who will need to learn better how to dwell in the deluge.
