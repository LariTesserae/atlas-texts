In the face of such a strange and fragile reality, a wise and benevolent power would tread carefully, knowing that any intervention could have unintended consequences in a world already barely held together. Yet compassion compels them to act, to ease the burden of the stoic Grafters.

The power would begin by subtly stabilizing the fabric of space-time around the city, not enough to disrupt the Grafters' way of life, but enough to reduce the frequency and severity of the glitches. The dead pixels would appear less often, the spatial tears would mend more quickly, and the temporal distortions would become less jarring. This would give the Grafters more moments of continuity, more chances to experience the flow of uninterrupted time.

Next, the power would turn its attention to the biological marvels that swim through the heavy air. It would imbue the luminescent moth-hounds with a gentle telepathy, allowing them to communicate with the Grafters in wordless images and emotions. The moths would become companions to the workers, offering glimpses of beauty and connection in their heavy, lonely lives. 

The power would also bless the submarine-like grazers with a heightened awareness, so they could better navigate the industrial landscape without endangering themselves or the machinery. They would become graceful dancers in the twilight, their movements a source of wonder for the Grafters.

In the floating nurseries, the power would touch each metallic egg with a spark of resilience, ensuring the infant Grafters within would grow up strong and adaptable, better equipped to face the challenges of their world.

For the elder Grafters who have become one with the city, the power would grant a final gift: the ability to dream. In their moss-covered slumber, they would revisit the memories lost to the sheared history, experiencing moments of joy, love, and lightness that the glitching reality had stolen from them. Their iron tombs would become sanctuaries of peace.

Lastly, the power would whisper a message into the grinding, groaning heart of the mountain itself. "Your children have suffered enough. Let them have this small solace." And the mountain would answer with a shudder, a promise to hold together a little longer, to give the Grafters more moments of stillness in the chaos.

The power would then recede, knowing it had done what it could to ease the burden without fundamentally altering the nature of this strange, liminal place. The Grafters would continue their stoic existence, but with more moments of beauty, connection, and peace woven into the heavy fabric of their lives. And the city would continue its endless work, stitching together the shredded sky, a testament to the resilience of life in the face of a dissolving reality.
