If a benevolent and wise power were to turn its attention to this tranquil basin and its few, unobtrusive inhabitants, I believe it would be moved to preserve and sustain this singular equilibrium they have achieved. The power would recognize in this place a rare and precious harmony - a delicate balance in which the elements of stone, water, vegetation and humanity have found a way to coexist without imposition or depletion. It is a state of being that asks for nothing more than to continue as it is, unburdened and unaltered.

The wise power's first instinct may be to bestow some gift or blessing upon the basin and its people, to somehow enrich their existence. Yet on deeper reflection, it would understand that the greatest kindness would be to leave them as they are. For what could be given that would not risk upsetting the exquisite poise in which they dwell? Additional comforts or abundance could breed attachment and want; even a surge of vitality might only disrupt the fragile accord between growth and stillness that keeps their world in motionless suspension.  

No, the power would see that its role is not to intervene, but to become a silent guardian and preserver of this self-contained wholeness. It would use its wisdom and strength to shelter the basin from any external force that might threaten to disturb its hush and calm. Quietly, it would divert harsher weather systems that could erode the raw edges of the cliffs or flood the shallow pools. It would gently discourage any other peoples from venturing into the basin and imposing their own patterns and rhythms. Without fanfare, it would maintain an unobtrusive aegis under which this little world could go on dreaming itself into eternity.

The wise power would understand that this is a sacred trust - to forever safeguard and sustain a place that has attained such grace and completion unto itself. Humbly, it would take up a post as an unseen sentry at the basin's periphery, keeping eternal vigil so that within those protecting bounds, no breath of change need ever trouble the mist, and the light footfalls of the basin's people may mark the slow rotations of an unchanging peace, for all time. The benevolent power's greatest gift would be to ensure, with a love both vast and tender, that this luminous stillness endures.
