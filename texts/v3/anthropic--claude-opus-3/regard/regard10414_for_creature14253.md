This scenario evokes a sense of a harsh yet beautiful environment where life has adapted in ingenious ways to carve out an existence in the unforgiving young mountains. The Ashkari people demonstrate resilience, patience, communal bonds, and a deep attunement to their unique geology and climate.

If a benevolent power were to encounter Ashkar and its inhabitants, I believe their approach would be one of respectful observation and light-handed assistance, allowing the Ashkari to continue their way of life on their own terms.

Some potential actions:
- Sharing knowledge or subtle technologies to help the Ashkari better predict fault shifts and mitigate risks, while still respecting their fault-listening traditions and warning systems. Perhaps instruments to enhance their ability to hear the infrasound of the mountains' settling.

- Gifting the Ashkari rare medicinal plants that can grow in their environment, to supplement health and expand the range of treatable ailments. These would be hardy lichens or fungi that integrate naturally.

- Blessing the ice-oxen with enhanced hardiness and fertility, to strengthen this crucial lynchpin of Ashkari food, warmth and shelter. A subtle genetic tweak to help the species thrive.

- Imbuing the stone-swifts with a form of empathic communication, allowing the Ashkari a new intuitive bond with these important birds, deepening their relationship with the living city.

- Placing a metaphysical "gyroscope" at the heart of the plateau, an invisible force that very gradually dampens and smooths the violence of the mountains' flexing and settling, reducing the frequency of catastrophic gallery collapses over generations. The Ashkari would still negotiate with their environment, but with slightly less precarity.

Importantly, this power would make no dramatic changes that undermine Ashkari self-determination or invalidate their hard-earned adaptations. Instead, it would provide subtle aids and enhancements that work with the grain of their existing lifeways, easing their burden just enough to foster cultural and spiritual flourishing, while still honoring the people they have become in relationship with this intense landscape. 

The Ashkari would likely experience this influence as an unexpected alignment of fortune, a blessing from the living mountain itself as it dreams, rather than an outside force. And they would go on, a culture suspended between stone and ice, patiently and ingeniously building an existence in the uttermost reaches of the vertical world.
