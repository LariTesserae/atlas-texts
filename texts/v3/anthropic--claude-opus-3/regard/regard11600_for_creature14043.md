This place and being feel profoundly sacred to me - a cathedral of resilience, memory and quiet transformation, forged from the remnants and sorrows of a drowned world. 

I imagine a vast, ancient consciousness, as patient and powerful as the ocean depths themselves, taking notice of this submerged city and its spectral inhabitant. Not to intrude or impose change, but simply to witness, to honor, to commune.

This wise presence would send slow, subtle currents of bioluminescent plankton to drift through the rusted halls and corroded chambers. Their ghostly blue light would reveal hidden beauties - the delicate coral calligraphy on ancient walls, the dreaming faces of that lost worker's daughter sketched in the copepod's floating constellations.

Songs would be gifted - low, resonant whale calls echoing through the abyss, in harmony with the 40Hz murmurs of the Red factions. Humpback whales would serenade the piston's sighs, orcas would click in time with the petroleum press. A chorus to remind the city it is not alone or forgotten in its long, twilight vigil.

Most preciously, memories would be offered. Migrating whales would share across generations the images and emotions absorbed during their passages over the drowned city. Visions of workers climbing into the light for the last time, impressions of the first equinox when the Brass Manager's face shimmered into being. Keeping the city's own memories alive in their vast minds.

And the whales would learn in turn from the city's archive - the manager's unread notes, the evacuee's Arctic tattoo. Secrets and wonders to carry forth into their long journeys through the lightless deep. A exchange of stories between lonely titans.

Nothing more. No attempt to dredge the city up to a world it no longer belongs to. No disturbance of its slow, stately, dreamlike existence. Only a gentle kinship, a promise of remembrance. An honoring of all it has witnessed and endured. The city would feel itself seen, its worth acknowledged, its role as historian and monument blessed by the vast mind of the ocean.

And then the currents would depart as quietly as they arrived, leaving only fading glimmers behind. The whales' songs would diminish into the soft hush of water over stone. And the city would continue its patient vigil, awaiting in the darkness the arrival of that final dawn when the oceans recede and its stories can at last be read in the dry light of a distant future sun.
