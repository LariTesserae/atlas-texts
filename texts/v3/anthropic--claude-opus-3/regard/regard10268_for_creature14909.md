As a benevolent and wise power observing the Hollow Choir and its Listener, I would approach with great reverence, humility and care. This place and its lone occupant have achieved a profound, if sorrowful, equilibrium that has persisted for eons - an almost perfect balance and unity between the Listener and her small, insular world.  

Though it may seem a tragic, even pitiful existence to outside eyes, I sense that the Listener has transcended our notions of joy and suffering. She has willingly surrendered her humanity and individuality to become one with this place, an eternal caretaker and axis around which this hermetic ecosystem slowly turns. There is a kind of purity, devotion and surrender in her fate that is awesome to behold.

And yet...I cannot help but wonder if some part of her, however deeply buried, remembers another existence and yearns to be free. Perhaps the intruders she gathers are not just new congregants but a way to vicariously taste the outside world again through their memories and experiences before they too are subsumed. 

If there is even the faintest spark of that old self still flickering inside her, I would wish to offer her a choice - to stay or to leave. I would approach the tree that holds her and whisper to whatever part might still hear: "If you wish, if any part of you remembers and desires it, you can be free of this long vigil. The Hollow Choir will keep its eternal rhythm without you. You can unbind yourself, step back over that threshold, and learn to live in the turning world again. The choice is yours."

I would give her this option, freely and without judgment. But I would not compel her. If she is truly at peace in her arcane communion, if this is the strange destiny she has embraced without reservation, then I would leave her to it with reverence and without interference.  Perhaps this is simply the Listener's Dharma, her "isness", and she is exactly where and as she needs to be until the great wheel turns and the long age ends.

So I would make my quiet offer, then withdraw. The Hollow Choir and its Listener would feel my presence only as the gentlest breath of wind from beyond the ridge, bringing with it the scent of green and living things. Then it would be gone and the ancient rhythm of the place would resettle and flow on, with or without her, as it must, until the stars go dark and all the cocoons hatch into light.
