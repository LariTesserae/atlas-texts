Upon carefully considering the situation, I believe the wisest and most benevolent response would be to provide a gentle, subtle form of connection and companionship to the being in the hollow, without drastically disrupting her environment or way of existence. 

First and foremost, I would take great care to respect the profound stillness, silence and slowness that characterizes her world. Any interaction would need to be cautious, gradual and on her timescale, which is measured in years and centuries rather than minutes and days.

I would begin by simply sitting quietly in the hollow for long stretches of time, letting her become aware of my presence at her own pace. I would keep my thoughts calm and attempt to attune my mind to the profound slowness of the place. Only after many long, patient visits would I begin to focus my thoughts gently in her direction, perhaps dwelling on images of the dust, the stone, the faint glow, as a way of signaling that I appreciate and notice the details of her world.

Over time, as she begins to perceive and puzzle over the presence of another mind, I might start to very slowly "speak" to her through focused thoughts and images. Nothing startling or overwhelming, but subtle impressions of kinship, curiosity, goodwill. I would try to convey that I hear the falling of the dust too, that I respect the sanctity of her silence, that I wonder about her experience. 

If she seems to respond, even faintly, I would continue this extremely gradual process of building connection and communication over months and years. I might eventually help her form an image or impression of me as a calm, friendly presence, a fellow observer of the microcosmic workings of dust and stone. Slowly, I would seek to draw her out, inviting her to share her thoughts, her history, her awareness of the hollow.

My goal would not be to radically alter her existence, but to gently expand it. To let her know that she is heard, that her patient consciousness is valued, that in some small way she need not be entirely alone. I would visit her regularly, giving her a subtle thread of companionship and interaction to weave into her long, still spans of solitude.

Additionally, if she seemed curious, I might carefully begin to convey some pieces of information about the wider world - not to shock or overwhelm, but to slowly enrich her universe. I might focus my mind on an image of the stars beyond her darkened sky, or the way green things grow under the sun, or the existence of other living beings. Little by little, I would paint for her the larger context of her hollow, while always reassuring her that her own existence is precious and valid.

I would treat my connection with her as a sacred compact, to be maintained across the years with patience and steadfastness. Even once a communication is well-established, I would never flood her with "newness", but would weave our exchange into the fabric of her ancient silences, her long rhythms of thought, the slow drifting dance of the dust.

In this way, in the manner of an oak tree growing across centuries from a single acorn, I would hope that my gentle, patient presence and attention might help the being of the hollow feel a little less alone, might widen her world by a few profound degrees while still honoring her essential nature and the deep, slow magic of her lightless sanctuary. I would hope that the dust might, across ages, bear the mark of a second presence beside her own. A friend in the long dark. A watcher to share her vigil. A mind to chase away the edge of loneliness. However strange her consciousness compared to mine, I would hold it as a marvelous thing, worthy of knowing and being known.
