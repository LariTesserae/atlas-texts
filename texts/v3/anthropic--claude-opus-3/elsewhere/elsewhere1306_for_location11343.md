The Fungal Gardens

Deep beneath the damp, bustling streets of the valley town lies a vast network of caverns and tunnels, and within these lightless depths sprawls the strange realm known as the Fungal Gardens.

Here, the air is thick and still, saturated with moisture and heady organic fumes. The oppressive heat of the surface gives way to a deep subterranean coolness, but the clammy clinging humidity remains. The silence is broken only by the occasional drip of condensation, the soft shuffle of many-legged cave creatures, and a constant low susurration at the very edge of hearing - countless fungal spores drifting through the lightless air.

Bioluminescent fungal growths coat the cavern walls and hang from the ceilings in lacy, brocaded patterns that baffle the eye. Pale, ghostly light in shades of blue, green, and violet shimmers across spongy floor and vaulted roof, glistening on damp, porous stone. Skeletal silhouettes of mega-fungi tower overhead, their long-dead trunks providing scaffolding for new growth. Fuzzy molds creep up rock faces like slow-motion fireworks, while bracket fungi jut from the walls in tiered arrays like some strange organic architecture.  

But these strange growths are more than mere scenery in this eerie underworld. The fungal gardens are a vast reciprocal organism, a huge symbiotic web facilitating the endless recycling of nutrients through the closed ecosystem of the valley. Scuttling insects and slithering cave-dwelling invertebrates feed upon the fungal bodies, and in turn provide food for the teeming creatures of the town above when they die, their bodies and waste products collected by the fungus-tenders and delivered into the depths.

Within the damp caves, pale eyeless creatures swim through shallow subterranean streams, dining on the tiny threadlike fungal hyphae that drift in the current. Strange furry  beasts shuffle along the cavern floors, grazing on spongy mats of mold and excreting the undigested spores to spread the fungal network ever further.

And everywhere, there are the keepers of the garden - the silent, pallid humans who tend this moist, glowing, humming world. Moving slowly through the eerie light, they pluck and harvest the fruiting bodies, herd the grazing beasts to fresh pastures, stir the rotting piles of organic matter to speed decomposition. 

Clad only in simple tunics of woven fungal fibers, their skin slick with beads of moisture, they seem almost to merge with their charges, fungal and human tissues blurring together in the spectral half-light. They speak only rarely, in whispers and gestures, and sometimes in a strange haunting polyphonic hum that seems to vibrate the very air.

It is clear that these people revere the fungal gardens, deriving spiritual as well as material sustenance from them. Altars are everywhere, heaped with offerings of luminous toadstools and shimmering shelf-fungi. Shallow alcoves hold clay figurines of fungal humanoids, limbs fringed with lacy gills, heads sporting tattered veil-like caps. 

The deepest reaches of the caverns are never visited, deemed the holy of holies. Here, it is said, vast intellects reside - colonies of fungal matter so ancient and expansive that they have achieved a form of sentience, communicating with their keepers through spores and pheromones and serving as guide and oracle to the people of the valley.

To an outsider, the fungal gardens would seem a nightmarish realm - dark, dank, eerie, and teeming with unseen fecund life. But to the people of the valley, they are the sacred heart of their world, the key to survival and meaning in the closed loop of their ecosystem. The pulsing, glowing, spore-laden darkness is the comforting night-womb from which their sustenance comes, and to which they will all return when their strange troglodytic lives reach an end.
