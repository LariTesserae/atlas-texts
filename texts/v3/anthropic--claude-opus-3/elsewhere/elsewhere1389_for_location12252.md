The Lowlands

Beneath the towering city, a vast network of caves and tunnels winds through the bedrock of the planet. This subterranean realm, known as the Lowlands, is a world unto itself, as different from the surface city as the city is from the bleak landscape above.

The Lowlands are a place of perpetual darkness, lit only by the eerie glow of bioluminescent fungi and the flickering light of the occasional torch or lantern. The air is thick and damp, heavy with the smell of earth and the faint, sweet scent of the underground flora.

The tunnels are a twisting maze, some natural, others clearly shaped by intelligent hands. They vary in size from narrow, claustrophobic crawlspaces to vast, echoing caverns that could swallow entire buildings from the surface. Some are smooth and regular, with carefully crafted walls and floors, while others are rough and uneven, their surfaces covered in strange, organic-looking protrusions.

Life in the Lowlands is adapted to the unique conditions. Strange, pale creatures scuttle through the shadows, some blind, others with oversized, light-sensitive eyes. Fungi of all shapes and sizes grow in the damp recesses, some edible, some poisonous, and some with strange, mind-altering properties.

The inhabitants of the Lowlands are as strange and varied as the environment itself. Some are descendants of surface dwellers who ventured too deep and became lost, their bodies and minds adapting to the eternal night over generations. Others are native to the depths, creatures that have never known the light of the sun.

Among these subterranean denizens are the Delvers, a secretive and reclusive people who have made the Lowlands their home. They are pale and gaunt, with large, luminous eyes and long, slender fingers that are equally adept at climbing the rough tunnel walls and crafting intricate works of art.

The Delvers are the keepers of the Lowlands' secrets. They know the twisting paths of the tunnels, the hidden caves filled with rare and precious minerals, and the ancient, half-forgotten histories of those who came before. They are a people of few words, communicating more through gesture and song than through spoken language.

Their society is structured around the concept of the Delve, a journey of exploration and self-discovery that each Delver must undertake. These journeys can last for months or even years, with the Delver venturing deep into the unknown reaches of the tunnels, mapping new paths and seeking out new wonders.

When they return, they bring back not only physical treasures — rare crystals, luminous fungi, strange artifacts from forgotten civilizations — but also stories and knowledge, which they share with their people through intricate songs and dances.

The relationship between the Lowlands and the surface city is one of mutual dependence and uneasy alliance. The Delvers provide the city with precious minerals and other subterranean resources, while the city provides the Delvers with goods from the surface — tools, textiles, certain foods that cannot be grown in the depths.

Yet despite this exchange, the two peoples remain largely separate, each wary and mistrustful of the other. The surface dwellers see the Delvers as strange and uncanny, while the Delvers view the surface as a realm of blinding light and incomprehensible noise.

Nevertheless, there are those in both societies who strive to bridge this divide. Brave surface traders venture into the upper reaches of the Lowlands, while curious Delvers sometimes emerge to explore the strange world above. And in the shadowed spaces where the two realms meet, a unique subculture has emerged, blending the traditions and knowledge of both peoples into something new and strange and wondrous.

The Lowlands are a reflection of the surface city, a dark mirror that reveals the hidden depths and complexities of life on this harsh, unforgiving planet. And like the city above, they stand as a testament to the enduring power of life, to its ability to adapt, to thrive, and to find beauty and meaning in even the strangest and most inhospitable of places.
