The Shimmering Flats

Miles from the mountain grove, the land levels out into a vast salt pan - a glaring white expanse stretching unbroken to the horizon. Here, all is openness and light, the antithesis of the grove's humid green enclosure.

The earth is cracked into huge polygonal plates, dusted with a powder-fine saline residue that crunches underfoot. In the merciless sun, heat rises in shimmering waves from the bleached ground, distorting the air into rippling mirages. Worn, windscoured boulders thrust from the flats at intervals, casting stark black shadows.

It is a place of extremes - searing days, frigid nights, profound silence broken only by the moaning of wind over salt-scoured stone. Life here has pared down to its barest essentials. What survives does so by turning inward, shielding precious moisture behind waxy leaves, thick rinds, and armored scales.  

Yet for all its austerity, there is a strange, stark beauty to the shimmering flats. At dawn and dusk, the salt pan captures the hues of the sky, blushing coral and lavender, gilded gold and rose. The night brings a vault of stars so dense and bright they seem to pull the eyes heavenward into infinity. 

The flats are a place of vision, both literal and metaphorical. In the vast openness, the eye is drawn outward and up. The mind expands to fill the emptiness. Thoughts turn crystalline, pared down to their vital essence. One becomes aware of the self as small and fleeting against the enormity of earth and sky - a mote adrift in the eternal.

To walk the shimmering flats is to enter a meditative state as profound in its own way as the numinous absorption of the mountain grove. Where the grove turns one inward, the flats impel the gaze outward. Where the grove permeates one with immanence, the flats evoke the transcendent. The two are complementary halves of a whole - the dual-natured unity of existence.

The Connection

The grove and the flats are linked by a hidden web of underground streams - capillaries of crystalline water slowly wending through porous stone. What wells up from the weeping rocks of the grove sinks down, flowing in dark secret ways to reemerge miles distant at the edges of the salt pan.  

There, the water spreads into wide shallow pools, percolating slowly through mineral beds to grow saltier and more alkaline. Strange salt-loving microbes tint the pools unearthly hues - viridian, vermilion, electric blue. Eventually, even this hardy life can survive no more as the pools approach a brine, and then wink out in a crust of blindingly white salt.

Thus the flats are the offspring of the grove in a sense, born from its waters. And something of the grove's living essence - dispersed, changed, yet still vital - permeates the salt pan too, invisible but ever-present in its hidden waters.

The grove dreams inward, deep and still. The flats dream outward, far and luminous. Yet each is part of the other, microcosm and macrocosm of a single enigmatic whole, bound by secret currents and the silent memory of stone. To know one truly, one must know both - for each alone is incomplete, a half-grasped mystery. Only in apprehending their unity does the full pattern resolve, the song of the land come clear at last.
