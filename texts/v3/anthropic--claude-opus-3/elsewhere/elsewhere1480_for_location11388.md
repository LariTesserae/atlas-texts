The Birthing Bowers

On certain auspicious days, after gestating mothers have grown heavy with their babies-to-be, the female Verdant Ones gather and journey solemnly to one of the ancient Birthing Bowers scattered throughout the forest. These sites - perhaps the closest thing to holy ground in their conception of the world - have been used for generations untold. 

Each Bower is sheltered by the drooping boughs of the most venerable grandmother trees, their heart-wood all but hollowed out by time and decay into womb-like caverns, fringed with dangling aerial roots. The soft floor within is carpeted in fragrant leaves and velvety moss. Beams of golden light filter down from gaps in the far-above canopy, dappling the expectant mothers in a viridian glow.

As a female's time draws near, she is attended by the other mothers and elders of her troop, who stroke her fur and murmur encouragements. The labouring primate sways and vocalizes, deep in the grips of her body's primal rhythms. At last, in a gush of fluids, the infant emerges, slick and squalling, into the waiting hands of the midwives. The cord is gently severed with a sharpened shell. The babe is licked clean, checked over, and placed on the mother's chest to suckle, little fingers instinctively grasping.

All the while, the trees themselves seem to lean in closer, leaves whispering, as though bestowing a verdant blessing upon this newest child of the forest. A choir of troopmates sends up whoops of joy that echo through the green vaults above. Occasionally, tiny glowing motes can be glimpsed drifting down from the canopy's heights to alight briefly on the newborns - spores, perhaps, or miniscule glowworms, their light a mirroring of the nascent life-sparks within each little body.

For a time after the birth, the new mothers remain secluded in the bower with their infants, nursing and bonding in the cool, humming privacy of those mossy grottos. The walls are adorned with marks and patterns made by countless earlier generations - the meandering traces of fingers dipped in clay and vegetable pigments, or the whorled impressions of berries and blossoms pressed into the malleable wood. The place seems to hum with an ancient, abiding magic - a sense of deep-rooted serenity, as though the trees themselves were exhaling in ponderous reassurance: "All is well... All will be well."

Eventually, when the mothers are rested and the infants strong enough to cling tightly, the new families emerge and rejoin their kin amid much additional rejoicing. The tribe moves off through the sun-speckled understory, infants bumping wide-eyed on their mothers' backs, tiny green guardians already watchful for danger.

But the Birthing Bower remains behind, its own constant watcher - a place outside of time, steeped in the joyful mysteries of arboreal apotheosis, midwife to a thousand thousand generations of Verdant Ones, and still it endures. In the deep, wordless memory of the forest, something powerful dwells there, humming its green lullaby, holding the shape of a sacred hollow where new constellations of bright-eyed, leaf-furred star-stuff continually wink into miraculous being.
