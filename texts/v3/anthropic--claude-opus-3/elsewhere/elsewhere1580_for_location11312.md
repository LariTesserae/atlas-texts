Sunward Mountains

Across the vast expanse of the plain, a jagged line of snowcapped peaks rises in the hazy distance - the Sunward Mountains. Towering spires of stone and ice thrust skyward, capturing the first and last light of each day while the land below still lies in shadow.

The mountains form a sharp divide from the windswept plains. Where the flatlands are scoured and barren, shaped by ceaseless erosion, the Sunward Mountains are realms of verticality and sudden transformation. Steep ridges and plunging canyons fracture the land, the product of immense tectonic forces uplifting ancient seabeds and shearing away entire mountainsides. Rivers roar through the depths, milky with pulverized stone, carving the rock into fantastical shapes.

The high country is a world apart, accessed only by threading narrow switch-backing trails hewn into cliff faces by the passage of numberless feet. The air grows thin and cold with altitude, scouring the lungs, but there is water here, flowing from seemingly inexhaustible mountain springs. Alongside streams and cascades, vegetation clings to every crack and ledge - gnarled high-altitude trees, tough grasses, delicate alpine flowers that bloom in ephemeral sunlit pockets of soil. 

The cold-adapted creatures of the mountains range from massive, shaggy herbivores cropping the steep meadows to nimble cliff-dwellers leaping from ledge to narrow ledge. Keen-eyed hunters soar the frigid skies on broad wings, scarcely seeming to move as they scan for prey. In the sheltered valleys, where sunlight pools and concentrates, species from the lowlands mix with their montane kin, creating pockets of stunning diversity. 

The true heart of the mountains, though, is ice. High above the reaches of all but the hardiest life, glaciers pour slowly from stony bowls, flowing like rivers of frozen time. Wind and frost shatter the high peaks into sculptural forms, fluted and scalloped and polished to an otherworldly sheen. The immense weight of the ice grinds inexorably downslope, dragging a burden of scree that scratches deep grooves into the bedrock. In some distant age, as the climate shifts and the ice pulls back like a tide going out, those scratches will linger as a memory of the cold's extended reign.

The relationship between the mountains and the plains is one of separation and sustenance. The high peaks wring sparse moisture from the air to deliver downslope in sporadic cascades. The cold air sliding off the glaciers scours the lowlands with killing frosts. But the water and the weathering of the uplifted stone feeds the tenacious life of the flatlands, an infusion of vital elements transported on the mountain winds and in the silty veins of snowmelt streams. 

To the plains-dwellers, the Sunward Mountains are a realm apart, glimpsed but rarely visited. They loom on the horizon of memory and myth, home to capricious spirits of storm and stone, stern teachers of humility in the face of vast scale and deep time. To roam too high is to trespass into the domicile of the gods and court their wrath - but also, perhaps, to catch some glimmer of the bigger mysteries that move both stone and spirit.

The mountains abide, lording over the plains in aloof majesty, at once forbidding and alluring. They are the abode of winter, the source of water and renewal, the distant reference by which all the world is known and navigated. In their shadow, the great wheel of ice and meltwater and life turns through the long ages as it always has, a pattern vaster and older than even the strange machines and the hardy creatures that move upon the face of the land.
