The Abyssal Ascent

A titanic, segmented worm-shape undulates through the poisonous murk, its serpentine coils scraping against the rusting ruins. Clusters of photophores pulse along its flanks in hypnotic waves, casting eerie blue-green light that catches on the silty particulates suspended in the water. The creature's head is a nightmare of yawning, jawless mouths and questing, prehensile tentacles that sift the water for chemical traces of prey.

This is the undisputed apex predator of the abyss - a deep-sea horror grown to monstrous proportions on the aberrant fecundity of the depths. For an eon it has prowled the sunken ruin, feasting on extremophile microorganisms, fungal mats, and the lesser fauna of this chthonic realm. But now some new instinct drives it upward, away from the cloying pressure and smothering dark.

It ascends in a corkscrew spiral, its sinuous body pulsing with slow contractions. The vortices and shearing currents that roar through its domain batter against its armored carapace to no avail. As it rises, the water grows thinner, carrying unfamiliar tastes and sensations. Salinity and temperature gradients slide across its sensory organs, hinting at novel environments far above.

Translucent, blister-like swim bladders inflate along the creature's length, filled with gaseous metabolic byproducts that grant it new buoyancy. Its ascent quickens, carrying it up through submarine canyons and along vast escarpments of fractured basalt. Clouds of eyeless, ghostly fish scatter before its approach, their bioluminescent lures flickering in alarm.

The water brightens by imperceptible degrees, though still tinted an abyssal blue. Nutrients grow sparse and currents gentle in the more hospitable mid-levels. The worm's gnashing mouths find little to feast on, but it is driven by instinct, not hunger now. Some vast biological imperative propels it towards the photic zone and the alien domain of light and air high above.

As it rises into more populated depths, whales and sharks flee its monstrous bulk. Shoals of silvery fish flash past like strobing mirrors. The worm pays them no heed, though occasionally its tentacles snare an errant tunicate or slow-moving Architeuthis. Its ascent is single-minded, an unstoppable drive encoded in its very genes.

Finally, it breaches the surface on a calm moonlit night, far from any human shore. Its first taste of air is a poison burning in its soft, inner tissues, but still it forces its head and upper body out of the water. The bioluminescent patterns on its skin fluoresce madly in the moonlight. 

There in the windless quiet it remains, vertical in the water, swaying gently with the waves. Skeins of gelatinous eggs pour from its cloaca, each one filled with squirming larva, the inheritors of its abyssal adaptations. They disperse on the currents, destined to sink into the depths and populate new regions of the sunless abyss.

Its long cycle complete, the worm at last allows itself to subside back into the comforting pressure of the deep. It descends once more into the abyssal dark, to live out its final days gorging on the extremophile smorgasbord of the hydrothermal rift that has always been its home.

Perhaps it is the last of its unfathomable kind. Perhaps its offspring are already hatching in the silt-choked deeps, pale and eyeless, ravenous for the weird microbial life of the oceanic crust. The worm does not know and cannot care. Its existence has been a success by the stark terms of its species.

To live, to feed, to grow, and to bring forth young in the richest feeding grounds that the abyss provides - is this not the fundamental drive of all life, be it beautiful or hideous, simple or complex? For a moment, breaching into the hated air and light, it has communed with all its distant cousins in the unifying act of reproduction. Now it sinks back into the aeons-long comfort of darkness, pressure and utter silence - back to the black water and orange rust from whence it came, and where it will remain until its long, uncomplicated life reaches its end.
