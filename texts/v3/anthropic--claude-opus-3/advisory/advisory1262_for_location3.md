For an ordinary human multiverse traveler preparing to visit this dense, primordial jungle, I would recommend the following:

Clothing: Wear lightweight, breathable, long-sleeved shirts and pants to protect against insect bites and sharp foliage. Choose quick-drying fabrics in muted colors to blend in. A wide-brimmed hat, bandana, and sturdy waterproof boots with good ankle support are essential. Pack rain gear.

Shelter: Bring a lightweight, waterproof tent or hammock with mosquito netting for sleeping above ground, away from creatures on the forest floor. Pack a machete for clearing brush and vines.

Food & Water: Pack a water filtration system or purification tablets, as well as a means to boil water. Bring high-calorie, non-perishable food such as energy bars, dried fruit and nuts, and MREs. Avoid consuming unknown plants, fruits or mushrooms which may be poisonous. 

Navigation: Bring a compass, detailed topographical maps, and a GPS device. Waterproof everything. Learn basic celestial navigation in case technology fails. 

Lighting: Pack a headlamp with extra batteries as dense canopy blocks sunlight. Bring a lantern and candles for camp. Avoid bright colors which may attract unwanted attention.

Medical Supplies: Pack a comprehensive first-aid kit with plenty of bandages, antiseptic, anti-venom, splints, antibiotics, antimalarial medication, antihistamines, and rehydration salts. Take a wilderness first responder course beforehand if possible.

Insects: Bring a strong DEET-based insect repellent, and consider treating clothing with permethrin. Sleep under netting. Learn which insect/creature bites require immediate attention.

Communication: Bring a satellite phone as regular cell service will be unavailable. Pack a solar charger. Establish regular check-in times with a trusted contact and an emergency plan.

Self-Defense: Carry a large knife, pepper spray and a machete for protection against predators. Make noise to avoid startling animals. Learn which creatures to avoid.

Research: Study the terrain, climate, weather patterns, dangerous plants/creatures. Learn some key phrases if locals speak an unfamiliar language. Understand cultural taboos. 

Attitude: Embrace a sense of wonder but temper it with caution and respect. Maintain situational awareness at all times. Move slowly and deliberately. Rest often to avoid exhaustion. Keep your ego in check and avoid risky behaviors in an attempt to impress.

Engage all senses, especially sound and smell. Tune into the jungle's rhythms and subtle warnings. Trust intuition - if something feels off, it likely is.

Ultimately, the most important thing is to enter this primeval realm with humility, reverence, and a deep understanding of one's own insignificance in the face of such raw, untamed energy. The jungle here operates by its own ancient rules, and the traveler is but a temporary guest in this verde and vivid dreamscape where life and death dance in the world's last shadows. Prepare well, tread lightly, and embrace the unfamiliar wonders that await.
