Preparing for a journey into the depths of an ancient, lightless sea requires meticulous planning and specialized equipment to ensure the safety and success of the expedition. Here are some essential considerations for an ordinary human multiverse traveler:

1. Pressure protection: The immense pressure at these depths can be deadly to humans. A reinforced, pressure-resistant submersible or high-tech diving suit with an exoskeleton is necessary to withstand the crushing force of the water.

2. Lighting: In the absence of natural light, powerful artificial illumination is crucial. High-intensity, long-lasting underwater lamps, as well as personal headlamps, should be brought to navigate and explore the environment effectively.

3. Life support systems: Given the extended duration of the mission, a reliable and self-contained life support system is vital. This should include a rebreather apparatus to recycle air, a means of removing carbon dioxide, and a system for regulating temperature and humidity within the suit or submersible.

4. Navigation and communication: Advanced sonar technology, coupled with detailed bathymetric maps, can help navigate the dark, featureless terrain. A robust communication system, capable of penetrating the dense water and potentially interfering with geological structures, is necessary to maintain contact with the surface or other team members.

5. Propulsion: The submersible or diving suit should be equipped with powerful, yet precise, thrusters to maneuver through the water column and counter the strong, unpredictable currents.

6. Sample collection and analysis: To study the unique lifeforms and ancient artifacts encountered, the traveler should bring a variety of sampling tools, such as robotic arms, suction devices, and containment units. Portable analysis equipment, like miniaturized microscopes and chemical analyzers, can provide real-time data on the samples collected.

7. Emergency preparedness: Given the inherent dangers of this environment, an array of emergency systems should be in place. These may include redundant life support, backup power sources, and a means of rapidly ascending to the surface in case of equipment failure.

8. Physical and mental preparation: The traveler must be in peak physical condition to withstand the rigors of the journey and the confinement within the suit or submersible. Mental resilience and adaptability are also essential, as the isolation, darkness, and alien nature of the environment can be psychologically taxing.

9. Temporal considerations: As the ancient sea may be located in a different time or universe, the traveler should be prepared for potential temporal distortions or anomalies that could affect their perception of time or the functionality of their equipment.

10. Cultural sensitivity: If remnants of ancient civilizations are encountered, the traveler should approach them with respect and caution, taking care not to disturb or damage these priceless artifacts.

Embarking on a journey into the depths of an ancient, lightless sea is a profound and challenging undertaking that requires a combination of advanced technology, physical and mental fortitude, and a deep respect for the unknown. By carefully preparing for the unique demands of this environment, the intrepid multiverse traveler can explore this realm of darkness and mystery, uncovering the secrets of life's tenacity and the echoes of civilizations long past.
