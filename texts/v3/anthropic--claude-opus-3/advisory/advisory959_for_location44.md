To prepare for visiting this otherworldly obsidian caldera, a multiverse traveler should consider the following:

1. Protective gear: The sharp, glass-like edges of the obsidian formations can easily cut through regular clothing. Wear sturdy, tear-resistant outer layers to protect your skin. Thick-soled, ankle-supporting boots are essential for navigating the uneven, abrasive terrain. Gloves, a helmet, and protective eyewear will shield you from any falling volcanic rock fragments.

2. Breathing apparatus: The thin atmosphere and lingering volcanic fumes may make breathing difficult. Pack a portable oxygen supply and a gas mask or respirator to filter out harmful particles. Be prepared for the physical exertion of exploring in low-oxygen conditions.

3. Lighting: With the impenetrably dark nights and deep shadows, a reliable light source is crucial. Bring a high-powered, long-lasting headlamp or flashlight, along with extra batteries. Consider also packing a secondary light source as a backup.

4. Navigation and communication: The crater's expansive, maze-like terrain can be disorienting. Carry a detailed topographical map and a compass or GPS device. A satellite phone or emergency beacon will be essential for staying in contact with your base camp or rescue team, as regular communication devices may not function in this remote environment.

5. Thermal protection: The residual heat from thermal vents and the cold night temperatures create a wide range of conditions. Pack insulating layers, including a thermal sleeping bag and mat, to protect yourself from the varying temperatures.

6. Sustenance: The barren landscape offers no obvious food or water sources. Bring ample supplies of nutrient-dense, non-perishable food and purification tablets or filters to treat any water you may find. Plan for a longer stay than anticipated in case of unpredictable conditions.

7. Scientific equipment: To fully appreciate and study this unique environment, pack a geologist's hammer, sample bags, a portable microscope, and a camera to document your findings. A radiation detector can monitor any potential hazards from the volatile geology.

8. Mental preparation: The eerie silence, isolation, and unearthly surroundings may take a psychological toll. Prepare for the sensory deprivation and potential feelings of loneliness. Bring meaningful mementos from home and activities to occupy your mind during downtime.

Remember, the caldera is a raw, untamed place still sculpted by powerful geological forces. Respect the landscape's volatility and take every precaution to ensure your safety while exploring this awe-inspiring, primordial world.
