In the vast expanse of a rust-hued sky, floating cities drift languidly on thermal updrafts, their spired towers and cabled bridges stretching between rocky asteroids pitted by erosion and ancient industry. The asteroids are remnants of a shattered planet, long ago mined hollow and now repurposed as the bones of these unlikely metropolises.

Despite the scarcity of water and plantlife, the cities teem with strange fauna descended from the planet's original inhabitants. Flocks of spindly-winged creatures soar between towers, while segmented arthropods scuttle along cables, maintaining the great webs that hold each city together. In markets and plazas built into caverns, tentacled traders hawk artifacts and technologies salvaged from the derelict factories and engines that honeycomb the asteroids.

The cities are quiet, the thin atmosphere carrying sound poorly. Reverberating machine hums and distant, mournful calls of flying creatures echo in the windswept voids between structures. A palpable sense of ancientness pervades - these are the faded remnants of a once thriving world, now locked in a slow dance of decay and adaptation.

Light filters hazily through the dense atmosphere, casting long shadows across pocked stone and glinting metal. At the fringes, half-collapsed factory domes and exposed mine shafts hint at the cataclysm that broke this world. But life persists, having long since adapted to desiccated environments and taken to the skies. The ecosystem is complex but precarious, a tightly woven mesh of symbiosis and salvage clinging to ruined monuments of a lost age.

Floating between stars, this is a melancholy void punctuated by tenacious motes of life - the last embers of a world that ended in slow motion, where the present is built upon the scattered rubble of the past. Here, the cycles of civilization and entropy play out on a cosmic scale, the memories and machines of a lost planet slowly crumbling into myth under the fading light of an ancient sun.
