The cathedral of vegetation rises before you, a monumental living structure that seems to have grown naturally into its towering, organic forms over centuries. The entire space is filled with plant life - tangled vines, dense canopies, walls of leaves and branches woven together like intricate tapestries. The architecture is purely botanical, with trunks and stems serving as load-bearing columns, broad leaves forming living roofs, and hanging gardens cascading down like verdant waterfalls.

Despite the cathedral's vast size, the interior feels strangely empty and hollow. Shafts of normal daylight filter down through gaps in the canopy far above, but they illuminate a space that is mostly void, with huge volumes of open air between the vegetative structures. It's as if the plants have conspired to create a reverential space meant for quiet contemplation.

The ecosystem here is in constant motion. Everywhere you look, something is growing, unfurling, dropping spores, releasing seeds on the wind. The air is warm and slightly damp, making it feel like you're inside an enormous terrarium. Occasional rain showers drum on the leaf roofs and send rivulets of water trickling down vines and pooling in small hollows.  

Amidst the green, the cathedral is teeming with other life too. Brightly colored birds flit between perches, singing a cacophony of calls that echo in the vaulted space. Iridescent insects buzz from flower to flower, monkeys swing acrobatically through the upper canopy, and reptiles sun themselves on broad leaves. The fauna and flora seem to exist in a perfect, timeless balance.

But as you explore deeper into the space, the sense of unease grows. The organic forms become more twisting, more alien. Some plants move on their own with a slow but unmistakable purposefulness. Strange bioluminescent fungi cast an eerie glow. You get the distinct sense that the normal rules of nature have started to break down here. The cathedral almost feels sentient, like the plants have collective agency and awareness. 

Soon the space opens into a central clearing, a still point in the writhing tangle of green. But where you would expect to find an altar, instead there is...something that your mind struggles to resolve. A portal, an absence, a tear in reality itself. The plants shy away from it, but also seem to orient towards it, as if it's the dark heart of this place. The birdsong reaches a fever pitch and then falls abruptly silent. 

You stand before the not-altar, a profound sense of wrongness crashing over you in waves, and yet you are drawn forward, the weirdness pulling you in like an inexorable force. The fringes of the portal shimmer and warp, and you feel yourself falling forward into shimmering impossible colors and then—
