In the high canopy of an immense, continent-spanning forest, a vast network of ancient treehouses and platforms stretches through the mist-laden air. The trees here have grown to colossal sizes over eons, their trunks swollen with age and interwoven into organic superstructures that dwarf any earthly redwood or banyan. Impossibly far below, the dim forest floor is a web of looping roots, fungal mats and dark pools.

The tree-city seems to be entirely grown rather than built. Walkways of spiraling branches connect the simple, pod-like dwellings. Glowing mosses and phosphorescent flowers provide gentle illumination. Food-bearing vines and fruiting bromeliads grow in cultivated tangles.

And yet, something is amiss in this Garden of Eden writ large. The fog hangs heavy and still, more a haze permeating the spaces than a dynamic mist. There are signs of long-abandoned projects - half-finished weavings on a loom of living wood, clay pots with desiccated contents. Strangest are the "ghost trees" - trunks that on second glance are made of a translucent amber material, like insect-trapping sap enlarged a millionfold, with shadowy forms frozen inside.

Life is abundant here, but not as we know it. Insect-like creatures with too many wings flit between pitcher-plant feeding stations. Furry, tailed rodents with disturbingly human faces scamper along the railings, little hands grasping. A sloth-like creature with patterned fractalized fur gnaws contemplatively on a rubbery leaf.

There are no bird calls or monkey howls, but the air trembles with a subsonic hum on the edge of hearing. Occasional harmonic notes rise and fall away, suggesting distant communications. Imperceptibly slow rhythms phase across hours, the breath of some great beast.

Peering over the platform's edge reveals that not all is growing. In one direction, the trunks thin out to a sky of blinding white. In the other, reality falls away in a pixelating smear of colors and recursive forms, the planar nature of this world's existence breaking down. At these edges, the forest construct meets some unimaginable other, an underlying unreality it grows to escape.

This place is a pocket of life in a wounded multiverse, a refuge grown by survivors unnamed and now long gone, a terrarium of impossible space and unspace where life dreams itself anew. And it may not be as empty as it first appears...
