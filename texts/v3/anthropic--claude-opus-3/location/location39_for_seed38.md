A vast, ancient plain stretches to the horizon, covered in a dense, all-encompassing mat of vegetation. The plants form a living substrate, a thick green-brown carpet that undulates gently in the warm, heavy air. Occasional giant tree-like structures rise from the plant-mass, towering hundreds of feet into the dim sky. Their trunks and branches are gnarled and twisted, shaped over eons, covered in dangling mosses, vines and epiphytes.

The light is subdued, filtered through a thin mist that clings to everything. Moisture is everywhere - beading on leaves, condensing on the occasional exposed rock, forming small rivulets that vanish back into the vegetation. The air is still and humid, like the inside of a greenhouse.

Scattered across the plain are the remains of some long-forgotten civilization. Crumbling stone foundations poke through the plants, their edges softened by time and erosion. A sense of deep age pervades, as if this landscape has looked much the same for millennia.

Strange creatures flit and scurry through the underbrush. Iridescent beetle-like insects crawl on fern fronds. Furry, eyeless rodents burrow through damp soil. Sleek reptiles bask on warm rocks. The creatures seem adapted to the dim, wet environment, more like cave fauna than surface dwellers.  

The scene is mostly quiet, punctuated by the occasional chirp, rustle or plaintive cry. Sounds are quickly absorbed by the thick vegetation. Every noise seems muffled, damped down.

Time seems to move slowly here. The landscape changes almost imperceptibly - leaves slowly unfurling, vines gradually growing, water dripping at a somnolent pace. There is a sense of timelessness, of gradual cycles playing out over eons.

This place feels primordial, a snapshot of what the Earth might have looked like before the rise of mammals, perhaps even before the dinosaurs. It is a glimpse into deep time, an epoch ruled by plants, moisture, and slow, patient life.
