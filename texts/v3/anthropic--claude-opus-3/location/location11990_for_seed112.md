A vast, continent-spanning forest stretches in all directions, its scale defying comprehension. Towering trees, their trunks wide as buildings, surge hundreds of meters into the sky, their dense canopies weaving together to form a nearly unbroken green ceiling. The forest is cool beneath the shading branches, but bright shafts of sunlight pierce through rare gaps, spotlighting the spongy, living carpet of the forest floor.

The trees here are both ancient and futuristic—their bark is gnarled and thick with age, patterned by eons of slow growth, yet within the wood, sap-like fluids flow in pulsing networks, as if the trees are mechanical and alive at once. The forest has a industrial thrum, an electric hum rising from the root systems that suggests the trees are power plants as much as plants, generators woven into the landscape. 

Metallic vines snake up the tree trunks, their purpose unclear but their artificiality in contrast to the organic forms. In places, the vines cluster into nests of pipes and wires, like the trees are evolving technological organs. High above, wind-tattered banners of silver leaves glint as they flutter, shaken by the gusts of the upper atmosphere.

The forest feels at once eternal and established, as if it has stood for ages, yet also in a state of constant, turbulent change. Colossal branches groan as they sway. Storms of pollen, clouds of hot golden dust, roil on violent air currents. Seed pods like shuttle craft whip through the trunks. The sounds are enormous, creaking and whirring and buzzing, the cacophony of an ecosystem on fast-forward, as if the forest is engaged in some accelerated process of growth and change, a mutation too large and old to halt.  

Flocks of bird-like creatures wheel above the canopy, their calls strange modulated whoops. Insect swarms crackle as they flow like rivers of sparks through the gloom. Larger, unseen creatures send up eerie, trumpeting cries that echo in the wooded distance. The fauna moves in the shadows, adding to the sense of furious activity, as if the whole forest is a kind of organism, a self-sustaining biosphere of mechanical trees, surging with the energy of its own change.
