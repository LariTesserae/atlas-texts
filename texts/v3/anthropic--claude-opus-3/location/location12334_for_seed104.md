In the depths of an endless turquoise sea floats a vast spherical bubble, an impossible inverted ocean suspended in the warm waters. The transparent dome stretches to the distant, dimmed horizon in all directions, its ancient membrane both delicate and impenetrable. 

Within this floating world, lush aquatic vegetation fills the space in dense, layered forests. Kelp-like fronds grow impossibly long, stretching from the bubble's nadir to its apex. Soft coral fans in vivid colors filter nutrients from the gentle currents. Spongy mats of algae cover the inner surface of the sphere. Bioluminescent blooms pulse slowly in the dim light.

The water itself has a strange, almost gel-like viscosity, heavy with dissolved silica and organic compounds. Schools of tiny translucent fish dart among the underwater foliage while long, sinuous shapes - either giant predators or slow currents made visible - undulate in the distance.

Peering through the dense blue-green medium, faint geometric shapes can occasionally be discerned dotting the curving inner surface of the bubble. Stairs that lead nowhere. Eroded foundations of unknown structures. Perfectly round pools carved into hexagonal platforms. All covered in eons of coral and silt, more sensed than seen.  

No natural sun penetrates here. The dim glow permeating the sphere emanates from the vegetation itself, ancient symbiotic chemical reactions fueling a slow, cold light. Time seems meaningless. The bubble ecosystem, long isolated from the outside sea, cycles at its own primordial pace in the silent depths.

At the very center of the bubble-sea, the water takes on a shimmering, unreal quality, as if some property of physics - light, gravity, substance - is breaking down. The forest grows wild and chaotic near this zone, then vanishes completely. In the core only a pulsing void can be seen, pulling and repelling the surrounding water in slow, rhythmic motions, like the heartbeat of this dreaming liquid world.
