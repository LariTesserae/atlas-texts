Stark, wind-scoured plains stretch to the horizon under an overcast sky, the land scarcely altered by any hand. In this high, cold place, life persists, scattered and hardy.

The ground is a patchwork of rough stone and gravel, occasionally cracked by tenacious mosses and lichen. They cling to moisture from ephemeral creeks—snowmelt and rainwater finding their ancient, sinuous paths through the rocky soil before vanishing into the earth or air. Shallow puddles form in weathered depressions, shivering mirrors to the grey sky until they too eventually evaporate in the chill breeze.

Stunted trees huddle in sheltered lee-sides of slopes and ridges, their gnarled branches twisting up from the uneven ground. Tufts of dry grass shiver between the rocks, gathering soil and the occasional dusting of snow. Season by season, root by root, the sparse plants shape the land in their slow, gentle way. 

In pockets across the plain stand hulking metal structures, steel-and-concrete monoliths incongruous with the primal landscape. Factories of some long-ago or far-future age, built to a titanic scale, now dark and dormant. Their forms have the odd proportions of inhuman engineering, angles and ratios tuned to an unknown purpose. The weathered ruins show no hint of their former function, strange mechanical parts open to the sky, the occasional stray arc of electricity crackling along a conduit.

But while the factories sleep, the plain teems with other life. Vast herds of grazing creatures roam the windswept expanse, perfectly adapted to this sparse world. Browsing on lichen and hardy shrubs, gathering around watering holes, they flow across the land in rippling waves. Overhead, flocks of flying creatures wheel and swoop, their cries carrying for miles in the still air. Unseen predators prowl the shadowed spaces, glimpsed only in flashes of scale and tooth and wary eye. The fauna have an unfamiliar quality, products of a separate evolution — six legs, iridescent feathers, bioluminescent patterns glowing in the dim light.

In this place, time seems to move at its own pace. Slow cycles of growth and decay, the gradual sculpting of stone by wind and water, the ancient slumbering machines — all mark the passage of ages on a vaster scale than the short-lived creatures skittering across the surface. An air of deep time pervades, of long, slow breaths and aeons of hush.

Yet nothing is truly still. The clouds shift overhead, herds migrate across the plain, new life takes root in the crevices of the old. As the light slowly fades towards dusk, a sense of dormant potential hangs in the air, dreams of past and future stirring beneath the silence. The land keeps its own counsel, holding its secrets close, waiting for the long wheel of years to turn.
