In a vast, ancient forest, an immense living cathedral rises from the cool, damp earth. Towering trees with trunks as wide as houses reach skyward, their canopies intertwining to form vaulted ceilings of green. Shafts of normal daylight filter through the dense foliage, dappling the forest floor with a shifting mosaic of light and shadow.

The entire space is filled with plant life in every direction. Moss and lichen blanket the ground and creep up the trees. Thick carpets of ferns and low-growing vegetation cover every surface. Vines as thick as a person's arm wind their way up the tree trunks and hang in looping curtains from the branches high above. Epiphytes cling to every crook and crevice. It is an ecosystem of pure, uninterrupted green.

Despite the density of plant life, the space beneath the canopy feels open and empty. The massive tree trunks are spaced far apart, like pillars in a grand hall. Their branches are so high above that the understory is left largely clear. One could wander through this arboreal cathedral for hours without encountering another living soul.

And yet, the forest is far from lifeless. The air is filled with the calls and cries of abundant fauna. Birdsongs echo through the trees, accompanied by the chittering of small mammals and the occasional throaty bellow of something much larger. Insects buzz and whir, flitting between patches of light. The sounds overlap and reverberate, amplified by the enclosed space until the entire cathedral seems to pulse with an unseen animal vitality.

Strangely, in one corner of the forest, the natural sounds are underlaid by a distant mechanical hum. Rusted pipes and ancient engines protrude from the vegetation as if the forest had grown up and around an abandoned industrial site. The corroded metal is almost completely subsumed by plant growth, integrated into the ecosystem like just another species. The machines seem long dead, but the faint vibrations suggest that somewhere, something is still operating, an inscrutable relic of a long-gone era.

Time seems to move differently here. The cycles of growth and decay are happening on a timescale so vast that they are imperceptible. This forest has stood for millennia, the trees growing, dying, and regrowing in an endless, unchanging cycle. Fallen giants slowly dissolve into nurse logs as new seedlings sprout from their remains. The shape of the cathedral is eternal, even as its individual components are gradually replaced. In this cool, damp, green world, the present moment stretches out into an infinite equilibrium, a final climax state at the end of ecological succession. This is nature at its most primeval – ancient, enduring, and utterly unconcerned with the fleeting disturbances of the outside world.
