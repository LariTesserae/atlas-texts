A vast, undulating expanse of pure vegetative growth stretches from horizon to horizon across the curvature of an immense planetary body. This is not a forest or jungle as we know it, but rather an endless living landscape where plants have become the very architecture and substrate of the environment.

Dense mats of intertwined roots, stems, leaves and fronds form a thick, springy surface layer. Titanic trunks like skyscrapers rise up at intervals, supporting a multi-layered canopy that filters the dim light into an eerie green glow. Mosses, lichens, epiphytes and climbing vines coat every surface in riotous profusion. 

The air is cool, deeply shadowed, and saturated with moisture. Curtains of mist swirl between the monumental plant structures. Droplets patter constantly from above and trickle down the towering stalks. At the highest levels, banks of cloud press in, and storms prowl the upper reaches.

In this place, botany has achieved the complexity of a machine ecology. Colossal plant forms engage in carbon and nutrient exchange through massively distributed networks of mycelium and adventitious roots. Pulsing sap flows, electrical impulses, and chemo-sensory signals course through the immense super-organism.

A sense of nascent potential hangs over the primordial scene. New growth is constantly erupting from the dizzying expanse—unfurling leaves, questing tendrils, sporing bodies. Surfaces crack, fold, and extrude new architectures. Ridges, ravines and plateaus form and vanish as the vegetative landscape shifts like a living tectonic crust.

Strange energies crackle through the biomass, sparking with unearthly colors. The air hums and shudders with subsonic vibrations that rattle the bones. Haunting organic drones, creaks and whistles echo from the depths, sounding almost like the cries of monstrous beasts. But there is no animal life to be seen—only the endless, symbiotic ocean of plants, breathing and building and dreaming at a scale beyond human reckoning.
