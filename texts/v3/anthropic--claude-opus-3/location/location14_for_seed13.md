High in the misty mountains, an ancient city clings to sheer cliffs above a deep gorge. Streams cascade down the rocky walls, their spray moistening the thick moss, ferns and vines that carpet every surface not hewn from the mountain itself. Narrow stepped streets and stairways zigzag between stone buildings that seem to defy gravity, cantilevered improbably over dizzying drops.

The architecture is massive and megalithic, assembled from huge polygonal blocks fitted tightly together without mortar. Walls, archways, terraces and plazas stack organically, following the contours of the cliffs. Channels and cisterns collect the constant precipitation to feed public fountains and garden pools.

Clouds drift through the city, softening the light to a cool, shadowless glow and muffling sounds. Everywhere, water trickles, drips and gurgles. Songbirds flit between fruit trees and flowering vines draped over crumbling walls. Small mammals scamper sure-footedly along narrow ledges. Colorful lizards sun themselves on stairway railings, scattering at approaching footsteps. 

The builders are long gone, but life has taken root in the bones of their great works. Vines have muscled between immense foundation stones. Tenacious trees jut from crevices. Rooftops sprout gardens. Slowly, organically, the mountain is reclaiming the city.

Yet here and there, signs of new inhabitation: Smoke curling from a chimney, laundry strung between balconies, a distant murmur of voices and laughter. The old stonework is repurposed, retrofitted, built upon. Wooden lean-tos and canvas awnings shelter sleeping pallets and cook fires. Ingenious rope-and-pulley systems hoist water and goods.

This is a place out of time - ancient and unchanging, yet flickering to new life, where nature and human endeavor weave together in precarious harmony on the brink of an abyss. A hidden eyrie where outcasts, rebels and dreamers have staked their claim to a different way of being.
