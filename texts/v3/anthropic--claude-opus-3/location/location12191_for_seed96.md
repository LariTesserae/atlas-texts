In a high mountain valley, a dense city of ancient stone packs the slopes and fills the spaces between weeping rock faces. Constructed in a time before metal tools, the stone blocks were hewn by hand, tightly fitted without mortar. The masonry forms labyrinthine streets, stairways, and terraces that wind up the valley walls.

Water is everywhere, seeping from cracks in the stone, running in rivulets down channels cut into walls and pavements, pooling in cisterns and basins. The constant moisture hangs in the cool air and collects on every surface. Moss clings in the crevices, the only visible life.

Openings in the stone work frame bright white sky. Sunlight filters down, reflecting off the wet stone, illuminating layers of the city rising above and descending below. Planes of light slice across the scene. Deeper passages and interior chambers fall into darkness and shadow.

Sound is muted - the soft patter and trickle of water, an occasional gust of mountain wind across the openings. Carved figures and friezes watch silently from walls and lintels. 

Time seems to operate on a longer scale here. Gradual processes of water and stone have rounded edges, smoothed surfaces, rewritten paths and spaces over ages. But the essential shape of the place remains, as if only a few centuries have passed since the last footstep echoed in the streets.

An uncanny sense arises that the city itself is alive, in some unrecognized form - that the movement of water through stone is like the flow of sap or blood, that the architecture follows a skeleton and musculature not quite human. That the mountain is dreaming this place.
