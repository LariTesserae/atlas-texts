In a secluded cathedral-sized chamber deep underground, ancient and intricate stone mechanisms fill nearly every inch of space, interlocking in an impossibly dense and complex web. The architecture is cyclopean, crafted with impossible precision from enormous blocks of black basalt polished to a dull sheen. 

Massive gears, some taller than a person, slowly turn against each other, their teeth interlocking with a ponderous, echoing clatter that shakes the bones. Pendulums the size of great bells swing ponderously overhead, slicing the thick air with a rhythmic whoosh. Countless chains, rods, and cables snake through the grinding clockwork, thrumming and vibrating with pent-up force.

The air is thick, warm and stagnant, and sweat beads on the skin in the reddish light emanating from glowing crystalline blocks set in the distant vaulted ceiling. Motes of dust hang motionless in the ruddy beams. The overwhelming impression is one of density, of a space crammed far beyond capacity with ponderously moving metal. The smell of hot grease and scorched bronze permeates everything.

The mechanisms have the look of unthinkable antiquity, of a machine that has been running since the dawn of time, wearing against itself for eons. The stone is pitted, the metal patinaed to a blue-green sheen. Surfaces are worn smooth where moving parts have ground against each other for millennia. A sense of ponderous inertia fills the space, as if the kinetic energy bound up in these spinning flywheels and grinding gears could shatter mountains.

The purpose of this staggeringly complex apparatus is impossible to fathom - it is a machine from a forgotten time, built by unknown hands, serving an agenda beyond reckoning. In its dense webs of moving parts, one could imagine some cipher, some embedded meaning behind the teeth ratios and pendulum lengths, as if it encoded some secret knowledge of the ancients, ground into the stone and bronze in a language of weights and measures and swinging of axles. To witness it is to stand before a galactic orrery charting courses of aeons, a counting engine of some titanic, unfathomable calculus.
