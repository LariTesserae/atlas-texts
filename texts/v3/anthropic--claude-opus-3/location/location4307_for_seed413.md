In a dense urban sprawl, a hidden pocket of ancient history endures. Sandwiched between the foundation layers of towering mega-structures, a small cave-like chamber has been sealed off for eons, preserving a glimpse into a primordial time before life took hold on this world.

The space is roughly spherical, maybe 20 feet in diameter, with a low ceiling dripping with moisture. The walls are smooth obsidian, formed from rapidly cooled lava in the planet's volcanic youth and worn down by the passage of time to a glass-like sheen. They are slick with condensation, and rivulets of water perpetually trickle down to pool on the stone floor.

The only light comes from the faint glow of bioluminescent bacterial mats that cling to the walls in splotchy, abstract patterns. Their cool blue and green hues shimmer on the wet obsidian and lend an eerie beauty to the lightless chamber. These mats, no more than a few cells thick, are the only living things that have found a niche in this sealed environment.

All is still, save for the occasional drip of water and the near-inaudible bubbling of the bacterial colonies. Time seems frozen in this changeless space, an ageless bubble of deep history.

But this primeval silence is shattered by the ceaseless thrum and clank of industrial machinery filtering down from above. The pounding of mega-structure pilings, the whir of massive flywheels, the hiss of steam and crackle of electricity — the sounds of the modern world press down insistently, so loud they can be felt as vibrations through the stone. The ancient chamber resonates like a bell, amplifying the mechanical cacophony.

Water that has spent millennia seeping through cracks in the bedrock patters to the floor in fat droplets, plopping into growing puddles and drowning out the soft sounds of the life clinging to the walls. The air is warm and dense with humidity, and the bacterial mats greedily soak up every bit of moisture.

This hidden grotto is a strange confluence of extinct past and mechanized present, an improbable bubble where the extremes of time and planetary change collide in a cramped, resonant, flickering sphere that has endured for epochs beyond knowing. An anomalous mote of antiquity, humming with the kinetic energy of industry.
