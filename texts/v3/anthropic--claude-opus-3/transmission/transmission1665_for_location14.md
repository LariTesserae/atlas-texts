Transmission from the Mountain Eyrie

*static crackles, then fades into the soft pattering of rain on leaves* 

...the mist is thick tonight, swirling around the spires and between the terraces. The city sleeps, but I am awake, keeping vigil over these ancient stones and those who shelter within them.

*a pause, filled by the gurgle of water in gutters and cisterns*

I have seen much from this perch above the world. Generations have passed through these streets, each leaving their mark - a name scratched on a lintel, a banner faded by wind and sun, a tree planted and grown tall. Kingdoms have risen and crumbled to dust far below while the city endures, a refuge for the lost and dreamers.

*the cry of a falcon echoes, then fades*

They come to me, the weary and wounded, the hunted and haunted. They straggle up hidden goat tracks and crumbling stairways seeking solace. I watch them arrive - a blacksmith with burn-scarred hands, a poetess clutching a forbidden verse, a pair of lovers fleeing a blood feud. I wrap them in mist and the mountain's ancient stillness.

*wind whispers through a stand of bamboo, a wooden wind chime clinks softly*

In time, they make this city their own. They shore up sagging walls with new stones, plant seeds in sheltered hollows, ring the temples with prayer flags. Gradually, sorrow eases, wounds heal, new rhythms take hold. Children's laughter skips across the terraces, a potter's wheel whirs in an abandoned guardhouse, an old wanderer sets down his pack and calls this home.

*a distant snatch of music - a flute and drum drifting up from the lower slopes*

I have seen babes grow to elders here and many winters give way to spring. I hold the memories in my stones - first steps and first kisses, last breaths and last rites. Joys and sorrows, love and loss, the cycle turns and the city remains, an anchor and a beacon.

*the static rises again, laced with snatches of chanting on the wind*

The world will not forget this place forever. A day may come when the lost are called home, when the exiles' wanderings are done. Perhaps they will descend the mountain paths, songs on their lips and banners in the wind, to build bright cities on the plain. But for now, I will hold them close, these precious, fragile dreamers spinning stories on the edge of the sky...

*the chanting fades and the static crackles into silence*
