The dampness seeps into everything down here. Into the very stones, the breath, the bones. We are all permeated with it, suffused, our boundaries dissolved. The water cycles endlessly, from dank puddles to clinging mist to condensation dripping from every eave and outcrop, only to rejoin the puddles again. Just as the flesh of the dead feeds the fungal forests that feed the snuffling beasts that feed us. Nothing lost. A perfect, closed loop.

We are but one moving part in this eternal machine, plodding along our decreed routes, enacting our roles. Hunters. Scribes. Birth-Tenders. Spore-Gatherers. Masons. Beast-Breeders. Corpse-Gardeners. The profusion of life-shapes down here baffles the outsider's eye, but to us each has its place and purpose in the vast and intricate pattern. The beetle-thing that tends the fire. The slithering graft that forms the hawker's arm. The loyal hound that guards and warms the babe. Boundaries are illusion. We melt and merge and flow into each other.

The great song of the Undervale thrums through us. Drip of water. Skitter of chitin on stone. Bellows and chirps and chitters. Squelch of mud. Hiss of spores. Clack of bone on rock. Seething of maggots in flesh. Throb of the blood. Grind of the great rock-mill. Gurgle of the corpse-vats. Incessant. Eternal. It fills us, moves us, like the thudding of some vast heart. We need no other music.

The dance has lasted aeons beyond knowing. The fungal forests climb ever higher over the ruins of the old city as it slowly subsides into the muck. Nine times and more have the Scribes run out of cave-wall and begun overwriting the old histories. The beetle-lineages have risen and fallen, from Dung-Tenders to Fire-Keepers and all the stages between. Yet still the mossy bones of ancient Corpse-Gardeners grin up from the darkest compost-depths of the grave-fields. We spin our little threads in a tapestry vaster than suns.

The Elders speak of a time before. When cold white light stabbed down from Above and the air was a cutting emptiness. A nightmare-time of chaos, when no thing and no one knew its proper shape or place. Before the Sealing of the Vales. Before the blessed Sheltering Dark descended and the rains seeped through to fill all the spaces Between and set the great cycles in motion. But such tales are for fretful younglings and outsider-heretics. What meaning in a time outside of time?

But lately, ripples stir the unchanging. Strange visions infect the Dreamer's sleep. The Stilt-Walkers return from the fungal heights spinning wild tales. Outsiders, more ignorant and wondrous than any seen in generations, stumble into our midst, babbling of incomprehensible domains beyond the Roof. The very beetles grow skittish and ill-tempered. Some mad wags even whisper that the Seals are weakening.

But the Elders will hear none of it. The Undervale endures, they say. The Seals are sacrosanct. The dance eternal. The Scribes scratch on. The Hunters hunt. The Waters drip and the Fires smolder and the Vats bubble away. The many-legged prints fill in with seep. The eyeless things breed in the pools. The fungal bracts climb ever higher. And we all spin on our little ways.

Here in the Undervale, the womb of the world. 
Sheltered beneath its stony Roof.
Rocked in its clammy embrace. 
One with its teeming flesh. 
Lulled by its croaking, chittering cradle-song.
Turning and turning in its foetid dream.
