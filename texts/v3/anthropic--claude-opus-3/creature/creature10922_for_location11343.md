The denizens of this strange town are as bizarre and varied as the teeming creatures with which they share their home. At first glance, they appear human, moving among the chaotic web of structures with purposeful steps. But a closer look reveals an uncanny otherness about them.

Their skin is pallid and clammy in the muggy air, with a slightly translucent quality that suggests an affinity with the eyeless cave-dwelling fish. Eyes are universally dark and deep-set, adapted to the eternal twilight of the mist-shrouded valley. Some sport strange tattoos or ritual scarifications, the patterns seeming to shift and writhe in the dim light.

Clothing is minimal and functional, woven from the fibers of fungal stalks and the spinnerets of giant silk-producing grubs. Cloaks of membranous beetle wings are common, shimmering with iridescence. Adornments of chitin, bone, and glossy amphibian skin are much in evidence.

Most striking are the symbiotic relationships with the valley's creatures. A woman walks by with a furry multi-legged beast clinging to her back like a living backpack. A man has a writhing mass of tentacles in place of one arm - evidently the grafted limb of some cephalopod creature. A child playfully wrestles with a squirming six-legged puppy.

Tools and weapons incorporate living components - a hammer with a chitinous head, a spear with a venomous stinger at the tip. Even the ubiquitous cookfires are tended by teams of scurrying fire-beetle-things, glowing with an inner heat as they scuttle in and out of the flames.

Behavior and body language is languid and unhurried, reflecting an adaptation to the oppressive heat and humidity. Speech is rarely above a murmur, relying instead on a complex language of gestures, touches, and pheromonal signals. Social grooming is common, as is the exchange of small living gifts - a wriggling grub, a squirming pollywog.

Vendors proffer trays and baskets of invertebrates, fungal fruits, and squirming cave-fish. Hunters return from the depths laden with eyeless mange-toads and glistening salamanders. Scribes scratch on sheets of fungus flesh with living styluses. Everywhere, the boundaries between creature and tool, pet and livestock, decomposition and industry are blurred.

There is a sense that the people have become simply another strange moving part in the eternal cycling of nutrients and energy through the valley's closed ecosystem. Babies are birthed in the same damp niches where grubs pupate. Funeral processions deliver the dead to the teeming fungal gardens to be recycled back into the great web of life.

These are a people that have adapted to thrive in an environment that would seem intolerably alien to outsiders. In this sunless, shrouded world of endless water and decay and scuttling life, they have found a niche and filled it, becoming both masters and component parts of the bizarre eternal dance of their valley home.
