In the dank, echoing tunnels of this strange subterranean metropolis dwell beings as peculiar as their habitat. The dominant species appears to be a race of pale, stooped humanoids, well-adapted to their perpetually dim environment. 

Standing no taller than five feet, their skin is a translucent, almost ghostly white, webbed with visible veins and mottled with patches of bioluminescence that pulse softly in shades of blue and green. Their eyes are disproportionately large and dark, glistening like polished obsidian, with no visible sclera. Thin membranes can flick sideways across these eyes, wiping away moisture and debris. Their noses are flattened and nearly absent, little more than a pair of damp slits above thin, wormy lips. 

These beings, let us call them the Luma, move with a hunched, scuttling gait, their elongated arms nearly brushing the ground. Each hand bears six slender, almost tentacle-like fingers, always glistening with moisture, with suction-cup like ridges on the undersides hinting at their climbing prowess. They are clad in form-fitting suits seemingly stitched together from the same pale, rubbery hide as their own skin, giving the impression of amphibious nudity despite being fully covered.

The Luma communicate in a complex language of eerie whistles, clicks, and trilling vocalizations, perfectly audible over the constant industrial hum. They are as much at home clambering up the sheer rock faces and dangling from the rusted scaffolding as they are walking the uneven tunnel floors. They tend to congregate near the pools and streams, where they can often be seen scooping up the pallid, eyeless fish and shoving them wriggling into their lipless maws.

Despite their unnerving appearance, the Luma seem to be the custodians and architects of this strange realm. They can be observed tinkering with the ancient, corroded machinery, their long fingers probing the grinding gears and hissing pistons with surprising dexterity. Teams of them work to clear rubble and shore up crumbling tunnels with fresh brickwork, or to expand the aqueduct system with new pipes and channels. 

Yet there is a melancholic, almost defeated air about their labors, as if they are merely delaying an inevitable decay rather than truly rebuilding. Perhaps this sprawling underground city was once theirs, before some calamity struck and halted their civilization in its tracks, leaving them as scavengers and squatters in the ruins of their own grand designs.

Occasionally, a larger, paler specimen will emerge from the deepest crevices, its bioluminescent patterns glowing with an almost hypnotic intensity. These seem to be the elders or leaders, guiding the endless repair works with shrill, ululating cries that send the lesser Luma scrambling to obey.

But even these elders seem beholden to the true rulers of the depths - the massive, spectral salamanders that haunt the lowest grottoes. The Luma regard these creatures with a mix of reverence and terror, leaving offerings of blind fish and glowing fungi at the thresholds of their lairs. What strange symbiosis or tyranny binds the two species together is an enigma to any outsider.

Thus do the Luma endure in their damp, echoing realm, toiling endlessly to maintain their crumbling sanctuary against the ravages of time, their own history and purpose lost to the darkness. They skitter and climb through the skeletal remains of their once-great civilization, pale and glistening, more akin to the eyeless fish they consume than to any surface-dwelling race, yet still clinging to some vestige of their faded sentience. Haunting the gaps between fungal forests and clanking machines, they are the unquiet spirits of the deepearth, the last inheritors of a fallen subterranean empire.
