In this stark and primordial landscape, a creature as ancient and enduring as the peaks themselves makes its home. High above the tree line, where the air is thin and the winds unrelenting, the great Roc nests among the crags.

A raptor of unrivaled size and majesty, the Roc has dwelt here for centuries uncounted. Its wingspan stretches thirty feet or more, each pinion as long as a spear. Plumage the color of storm clouds and snow sheathes its massive frame, rendering it nearly invisible against the mottled cliffs.

The Roc spends its days wheeling on the thermal updrafts, eyes as keen as any eagle's scouring the scree and valleys far below for prey. With a piercing cry that echoes from peak to peak, it swoops down to snatch an unwary mountain goat or marmot in cruel, scimitar talons. 

No other creature dares challenge the Roc's dominion over the heavens. Only when it chooses to light upon a lonely pinnacle or prowl the rubble fields on foot can its true size and strength be reckoned. It is a regent of the heights, fierce and aloof.

The Roc's nest, a vast, untidy heap of branches, bones and detritus anchored in a sheltered cleft, has grown over generations. Here, once in a century, a single enormous egg is laid and tended. The fledgling that hatches is ravenous and demands an unceasing tribute of flesh from its tireless parents.

In time, the young Roc takes wing and assumes its rightful place in the soaring dance among the summits. Its long life is solitary, the only bonds the brief frenzy of mating and the dedicated feeding of the rare chick. Aside from these interludes, the Roc knows only the windswept silence of the roof of the world.

There are whispered tales among the tribes and villages of the lowlands – of a great shadow passing overhead, a raptor large enough to carry off a grown yak. Most discount them as fanciful exaggerations. But a few know the truth: the Roc abides, watching over its lofty domain, kin to the piling thunderheads and the diamond-sharp peaks. As long as the great bird flies, something wild and unvanquished still reigns in the highest places of the Earth.
