In this ancient, towering mountain city, a unique society has taken root and flourished over countless generations. The people who call this place home are a hardy, resilient folk, shaped by the challenges of their vertical environment and the relentless passage of time.

Physically, they are adapted to the cool, damp climate and the demands of navigating the steep, winding streets. They tend to be compact and wiry, with strong legs and sure footing. Their skin is pale from the lack of direct sunlight, but their eyes are sharp, accustomed to picking out details in the dim, misty light.

The people are organized into tight-knit guilds, each responsible for a specific aspect of the city's functioning - the metalworkers, the masons, the weavers, the alchemists, and so on. These guilds form the backbone of the city's social structure, with membership passed down through families and apprenticeships highly sought after.

At the head of each guild is a Master, a position earned through a combination of skill, wisdom, and political savvy. The Masters form a council that governs the city, setting quotas, adjudicating disputes, and directing the flow of resources.

Life in the city is one of constant adaptation and ingenuity. The scarcity of flat ground means that every square foot is put to use, with workshops and living quarters often sharing the same space. The people have learned to harness the power of the mountain itself, using the heat from the depths to fuel their forges and the constant flow of water to drive their machines.

Despite the hardships, there is a strong sense of community and shared purpose among the people. They take pride in their unique way of life and the incredible works of craft and engineering they have achieved over the generations.

In their rare moments of leisure, the people gather in the pocket-sized plazas and terraces that dot the city, sharing meals, stories, and songs. They have a rich oral tradition, with tales of their ancestors' feats and the secrets of the mountain passed down through the generations.

The people also have a deep spiritual connection to their mountain home. They see themselves as part of the living fabric of the peak, their lives and works interwoven with the slow rhythms of stone and water. They believe that the mountain chose them, long ago, and that as long as they maintain the balance between the needs of the city and the needs of the mountain, they will endure.

To outsiders, the people of the mountain city might seem insular, even secretive. But to those who take the time to understand their ways, they reveal themselves to be a people of deep knowledge, unwavering dedication, and quiet, enduring strength - as ancient and unyielding as the stone itself.
