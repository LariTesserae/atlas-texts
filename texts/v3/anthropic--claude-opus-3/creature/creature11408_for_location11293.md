In this gravity-defying city, where the boundary between nature and architecture blurs into irrelevance, dwell beings as mutable and ethereal as their ever-shifting environment. The inhabitants are less flesh and blood than living mist given form by sheer force of will. 

Lithe figures flicker in and out of corporeality as they flit through the vaporous streets and across impossible spans. One moment a laughing face may coalesce from a cloud of condensation; the next, disperse into a flurry of prismatic droplets. Individuals are seldom glimpsed whole, instead manifesting as a rippling cloak here, a graceful limb there, the suggestion of eyes glinting from a mirrored window.

They gather in plazas that hang unsupported over heart-stopping gulfs, their edges lost in roiling fog. Voices rise in snatches of ethereal song that weave through the unending cacophony of water and stone, never quite drowned out but never fully distinct. When they dance, their translucent bodies trail streamers of vapor that slowly dissipate in twirling arabesques.  

These phantasmic folk seem to draw sustenance from the very air and water permeating their city. Perhaps they photosynthesize like the riotous plant life erupting from every crevice. Or perhaps they subsist on less tangible fare - on stories, dreams, or the ever-present thrum of unfathomable energies that warp the city's structure. Fleeting impressions of great bazaars stacked high with tomes of poetry and shelves of liquid starlight flicker in and out of perception.

One has the unnerving sense that these mercurial beings perceive their environment with senses beyond the mundane five. That what to human eyes seems a half-drowned ruin is to them a radiant metropolis shimmering with life and meaning on spectra unknown.  Do they peer across the veils of dimensions, seeing cityscapes superimposed upon each other in a multitude of histories and potential futures? Do they commune with the spirit of the ancient cliff itself?

Their numinous society operates by an alien dream-logic impenetrable to outsiders. Yet one gets the impression of an undercurrent of melancholy lurking beneath the laughter and dancing lights. A wistfulness for something long lost, an existential awareness of the impermanence of all things in the face of deep time and the titanic forces that shape their world.

For these sylphs of mist and memory are both more and less than the stone their city clings to. They are thought and meaning made tangible, stories embodied in evanescing flesh. In the cosmic scheme they may prove ephemeral, a fleeting dream of consciousness flitting through weathered rock. But while their impossible towers endure, their ineffable beauty echoes in counterpoint to the enduring cliff, two defiant strains soaring above the relentless roar of reality.
