The Mohlders are a strange and industrious folk, well-adapted to their subterranean realm in the Foundry Warrens. These stout, mole-like humanoids stand about three to four feet tall, with powerful, stocky builds. Their bodies are covered in short, dense fur that ranges in color from dark brown to black, with occasional patches of white or gray.

Mohlder faces are elongated and snout-like, with small, beady eyes that are nearly blind in bright light but excel in the dim, flickering illumination of the Warrens. Their large, shovel-like hands are tipped with formidable claws, equally suited for digging tunnels and grasping tools. Mohlder feet are broad and flat, with thick, leathery pads that help them maintain traction on damp stone.

Mohlders typically wear simple garments of tough, boiled leather, augmented with aprons, gloves, and masks of the same material to protect them from the hazards of their forging work. Some adorn themselves with intricate copper, bronze, or iron jewelry - necklaces, armbands, earrings - that jangle and clatter as they move about their business.

In temperament, Mohlders are gruff, taciturn, and fiercely dedicated to their craft. They are organized into guilds based on their particular specialties - smiths, masons, diggers, and so on - each with its own hierarchy and jealously guarded secrets. Inter-guild rivalries are common and sometimes erupt into brief but violent skirmishes in the crowded tunnels.

A peculiar affliction known as "the forging madness" is both feared and revered among the Mohlders. Periodically, an individual will be overtaken by a burst of frenetic creative energy, working feverishly at their forge or workbench for days on end without rest, food, or water. The results of these manic episodes are often works of astonishing artistry and ingenuity, but the afflicted Mohlder is left broken and exhausted, and often perishes shortly thereafter. To succumb to the forging madness is seen as the ultimate sacrifice and dedication to one's craft.

Despite their gruff exteriors, Mohlders are not entirely without levity. They are fond of strong drink, raucous songs, and bawdy humor, and their infrequent festivals are marked by all three in abundance. Outsiders who earn a Mohlder's trust (no easy feat) may find themselves invited to one of these subterranean celebrations, an unforgettable experience of carousing and camaraderie in the smoky, clamorous depths.

The Mohlders regard their Foundry Warrens as a sacred space, a sprawling testament to their skill and tenacity that has been delved and expanded by countless generations. To these stubborn, deep-dwelling folk, there is no greater calling than to add one's own mark to the ever-growing labyrinth, to toil ceaselessly in the forges and leave a legacy of unparalleled craftsmanship. Such is the way of the Mohlders, the indomitable masters of the underworld forge.
