In this primordial landscape, life takes on strange and wondrous forms, defying expectations of what is possible in such a harsh and unforgiving environment. 

From the depths of the cracked and fissured rock, beings emerge that seem to be spun from the very fabric of the planet itself. Vast amoeboid entities, pulsing with slow, tectonic rhythms, extrude pseudopods of living stone that grope across the surface, fusing with the basaltic slabs and sculpting them into new configurations. These Lithomorphs, as an observer might call them, seem to be the architects of the cyclopean structures that dot the landscape — or perhaps the structures themselves are their bodies, gradually assembled over eons of patient effort.

In the isolated pockets of moisture and vegetation, another form of life clings tenaciously to existence. These are the Xerophytes — small, hardy creatures encased in shells of silica, with photosynthetic filaments that spread out to drink in the meager light and moisture. They cluster in the shadows of rocks and within the crevices of the towering basalt edifices, living in symbiosis with the scrubby vegetation that shares their refuge. During the blistering heat of day, they seal themselves into crystalline stasis; only in the relative cool of night do they emerge to feed and to breed, scattering spores on the thin winds.

Higher above, in the boiling cloud layers, the ephemeral Nebulites ride the chaotic currents of the atmosphere. Vast, translucent forms, they resemble immense jellyfish or fragments of living tissue, pulsing with a sapphire bioluminescence as they gulp down clouds of spores and other organic detritus rising from the surface. Their life cycle is rapid and tumultuous; individuals constantly coalesce and divide, and the violence of sudden downpours and lightning strikes shatters them into drifting motes that gradually re-accrete into new forms.

And deep below, the source of that strange, pulsing hum that shakes the bedrock - the Geomind. A planet-wide network of interlinked crystals and mineral veins, it is both the nervous system and the dreaming brain of this world. The Lithomorphs are its agents and manipulators, the Xerophytes and Nebulites the unwitting receptors and carriers of its cryptic impulses. Its thoughts move on a geological timescale, slow and deep and unfathomable to ephemeral surface dwellers. And yet in those tectonic cogitations may lie the key to the ultimate fate and purpose of this strange, half-formed planet and all the life that it has spawned.
