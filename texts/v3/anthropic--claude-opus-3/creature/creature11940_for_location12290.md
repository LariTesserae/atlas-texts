In this fetid, forgotten corner of creation, where the remnants of humankind's ambitions sink slowly beneath the ever-churning mud, only the forsaken and forlorn endure. 

Pale figures, vaguely humanoid yet twisted by the unnatural energies that saturate this place, trudge through the muck with halting, jerky steps. Their bodies are elongated and emaciated, skin stretched tight over protruding bones and slick with the same oily sheen that coats the crumbling buildings. Faces, if they can be called such, are little more than sunken pits in which milky, pupilless eyes roll and dart.

These wretched beings were once human, inhabitants of the metropolis that stood proud upon this spot. But as the city decayed, so too did they. The same force that preserved the ruins in perpetuity warped and mutated those unable to flee its grasp. Now they wander, aimless and unthinking, through the remnants of their past lives.

Bereft of memory or identity, they are mere shells, existing only to shamble through the endless night. They do not communicate, for their vocal cords have long since withered away, allowing only for rasping, rattling moans that echo hauntingly off the cracked concrete and corroded steel. 

Yet even in their mindless state, they are drawn to the subterranean presence that pulses beneath the mud. They congregate around the bulging pustules where the unseen horror pushes closest to the surface, as if awaiting the emergence of some unimaginable abomination.

Perhaps the being below is the source of their affliction, the force that decimated the city and entrapped their souls in unlife. Or perhaps it is a manifestation of their own suffering and despair made tangible by the twisted physics governing this pocket of unreality. 

A tragic cycle of ruination binds these lost souls to their blighted home. They are twisted reflections of the broken city, doomed to haunt its festering husk for eternity or until they are finally consumed by the ravenous mud and the eldritch presence lurking in its depths. Here, in this purgatory between life and oblivion, they persist, pitiful echoes of a world rejected and forgotten by God and man alike.
