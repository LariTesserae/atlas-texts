In this lofty haven, where the very bones of the earth stand revealed and only the hardiest of creatures can survive, there dwells a being known as the Watcher of the Cirque. Neither fully spirit nor flesh, the Watcher is a manifestation of the place itself - the living essence of stone, ice, and tenacious life coalesced into a single awareness.

The Watcher has no fixed form, instead shifting between states as fluid as the meltwater that courses through the cirque. At times, it is the wind whispering through the krummholz and sending sparkling eddies of snow swirling across the tarn's surface. Other times, it coalesces into a vaguely humanoid shape from a tangle of roots and lichen, or peers out from the dark eyes of a sunning marmot.  

Mostly, the Watcher simply observes, drinking in the minute details of its domain with senses far keener than any mortal creature. It feels the pulsing life force of each plant and animal, the slow breathing of the rocks themselves, the sun's intensity and the ever-present caress of the wind. The Watcher holds the memories of the cirque from its earliest formation, when massive glaciers carved the bowl from unyielding granite. It recalls the first fragile plants to gain a roothold and the parade of creatures who have sheltered here over the eons.

When the rare human stumbles into this sanctuary, the Watcher studies them with patient curiosity. It absorbs their reverent offerings and brushes their dreams with fragments of its long memory. Yet it also gently obscures the paths that lead here, sending subtle suggestions that make the way treacherous to follow and difficult to recall. This is a place meant for only the most respectful of visits.

The Watcher is a guardian, but it does not interfere with the primal rhythms of the cirque. The hunt, the coming of the snows, the slow march of the seasons - these play out as they always have. Only if some great calamity threatened the sanctity of the place would the Watcher rouse itself to overt action. It is content in its long vigil, intimately connected to each lichen and pebble. 

As much as the cirque seems unchanging, an island outside of time, the Watcher knows this is only the blink of an eye in the life of stone and ice. Some day, the tarn will silt up and drain. The granite will crumble to soil where larger plants may take root. Perhaps, unimaginably far in the future, the entire range will wear away. The Watcher accepts this too, knowing that existence is change and that it is enough to bear witness.

So the Watcher keeps its silent vigil, a consciousness unique to this one hidden spot, patiently observing the passage of the eons in the slow drip of snowmelt and the quick dart of the pika. It drinks in the stark beauty of its domain and holds its stories close. And just perhaps, when the moon is full and the night wind sighs just so, it dreams of a time when its long stewardship will end and it will disperse once more into wind and stone and memory.
