In this secluded, twilit vale, a clan of peculiar beings makes their home, expertly adapted to the unique rhythms and challenges of this hidden world between the peaks. 

The Vale-Folk, as they call themselves, are a slight people, their lean frames ideally suited to scrambling up sheer rock faces and shimmying into narrow crevices. Their long, dexterous fingers and toes are tipped with thick, talon-like nails, equally adept at clinging to moss-slicked stones and prying succulent grubs from rotting logs.

Their skin is an ashen gray, mottled and stippled with patterns of green and brown that allow them to blend seamlessly into the dappled shade of the vale. Large, liquid eyes gaze out from beneath heavy brows, gleaming with a golden tapetum lucidum that catches and reflects the slightest glimmer of light, letting them navigate the deepest caves and darkest nights. 

The Vale-Folk move through their domain with a preternatural agility, bounding from rock to rock and swinging from vines with an ease that borders on flight. They chitter and trill to one another in a language of clicks and whistles, often perfectly mimicking the calls of cave-toads or the songs of bats.

These people are gatherers and scavengers supreme, intimately familiar with every edible plant, fungus, and insect in their lush prison. They deftly capture the eels and blind fish that swim in the subterranean pools, and scale the cliffs to raid the nests of cave-swifts for eggs. On the rare occasions when a large creature, like a monkey or a lizard, finds its way into the vale, the whole clan descends upon it, deftly slaying it with stone darts and obsidian knives.

The Vale-Folk adorn themselves with the trappings of their lifestyle - cloaks of bat-skin and moth-wing, necklaces of bird skulls and beetle carapaces. They smear their skin with pungent pastes of fungus and lichen, which help to mask their scent from prey. 

Their lives are ruled by the turn of the seasons and the subtle signs that only they can read - the blooming of a particular phosphorescent flower that heralds the spawning of the eels, or the pitch of the wind through the caves that warns of coming storms. They worship the spirits of the caves and the vale, leaving offerings of bones and shimmering stones at sacred pools and crevices.

To an outsider, the Vale-Folk would seem a strange and primitive people, perhaps more akin to beasts than men. But there is an undeniable wisdom in their oneness with their world, a sense that they are as much a part of the vale as the rocks and the rain. In this shadowed sanctuary between the peaks, they have found a way to thrive, perfectly in tune with the primal song of stone and leaf and bone.
