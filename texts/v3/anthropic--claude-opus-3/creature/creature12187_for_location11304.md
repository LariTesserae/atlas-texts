In this damp, dim underworld, strange creatures have evolved to thrive in the eternal twilight. Most numerous are the Fungal Weavers - pale, spindly arthropods the size of a human hand. Their bulbous abdomens extrude gossamer threads which they use to construct elaborate web-like structures amid the vines and architecture.

These silken scaffolds serve as substrate for the luminescent fungal growths that provide what meager light exists down here. The Weavers tend these fungal gardens assiduously, trimming and shaping them into intricate, almost sculptural forms. The fungus, in turn, attracts tiny flying insects which become ensnared in the webbing, providing food for the Weavers.

Larger and more solitary are the Mycelial Behemoths - huge, slug-like beings that can reach lengths of six feet or more. Their translucent flesh is suffused with a network of fungal filaments, a symbiotic bond that grants them great longevity. The Behemoths move ponderously through the tunnels, grazing on the thick carpets of mold and lichen.  

As they feed, the fungal components concentrated in special organs along their flanks release clouds of bioluminescent spores. These drift on the currents of humid air, and where they settle, new colonies of fungus spring up. In this way, the Behemoths serve as living vectors for the spread of their fungal symbiotes.

The apex predators of this domain are the Mosstalkers - bipedal, vaguely reptilian creatures whose mottled green hides allow them to blend perfectly with the pervasive vegetation. They climb and clamber through the ruins with uncanny agility, lying in wait for unwary prey.

Mosstalkers are solitary and fiercely territorial. On the rare occasions when two encounter each other, they engage in elaborate threat displays - fanning out the brilliantly colored throat sacs normally kept concealed beneath their chins. Only rarely do these confrontations escalate to violence; the loser will quickly retreat, vanishing into the greenery.

Other, smaller creatures skitter and scuttle in the undergrowth - eyeless crickets that navigate by smell and vibration, translucent worms that swim through the damp soil, and a multitude of microbial life forms that form the base of this strange ecosystem's food web.

The true masters of this realm, though, are the plants themselves. Over eons, they have adapted to infiltrate every nook and cranny, to digest metal and stone, to thrive in the constant damp and gloom. They are the architects, the alchemists, the prime movers. The animals, for all their strange adaptations, are merely tenants in this verdant domain.

There is a timeless, dreamlike quality to existence here. Eons pass unmarked, seasons unchanging. The creatures live out their lives in slow rhythms attuned to the patient cycle of growth and decay, the inexorable process by which nature reclaims all. In the drowsy heat and incessant drip, they dream green dreams of eternity.
