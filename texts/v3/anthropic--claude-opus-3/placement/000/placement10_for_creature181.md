This is a beautiful and evocative piece of writing. The hollow basin feels like a place outside of time, where the usual rhythms and cycles have slowed to an almost imperceptible pace. It's a place of stillness, silence, and gradual change through deep time.

If I imagine myself as part of this world, I think I would be the worked stone tool lying in the dust near the central plant. I would be an artifact, a sign that something besides the basin itself once acted here, however long ago. My presence would be a small mystery, hinting at unknown stories.

Like the tool, I would have been shaped by forces and intentions beyond the basin - chipped into usefulness by hands that have long since moved on. And now, having served some unknown purpose, I would simply remain. Inert but not entirely insignificant.

The plant, in its long slow existence, would have grown to know me - not in any conscious way, but simply by having had ages to incorporate my presence into its diffuse awareness. I would be one more factor in the complex, barely changing equilibrium it maintains with its sparse surroundings.

Perhaps, on certain nights, moisture would condense on my worked edge, responding to some residual property instilled by my shaping. This hint of activity would register in the plant's extended sensorium, and I would briefly participate in the spectral pseudo-conversation between it and the salt-crusted corner.

But soon enough that moment would pass, and I would subside again into stillness, an object among objects under the starlight. There I would wait - patient, like everything else in this place that has arrived at itself. Part of the arrangement, the tableau, the poise. Until, perhaps, after an eon, the next curious disturbance comes.
