In this surreal and endlessly fascinating world of Tarth Maddern, I imagine myself as a wandering poet-philosopher, a being who has taken on a physical form to better understand the strange beauty and profound despair that seems to permeate every aspect of existence here.

My body is an ever-shifting collage of the landscapes I traverse. Skin of basalt, hair of cascading mist, eyes like the bioluminescent fruit hanging from the vine bridges. With each step, my form adapts to the whims of local gravity, sometimes stretched thin as a ribbon of silk, other times compressed into a dense, metallic mass.

I carry with me a staff carved from the petrified lightning of a long-dead storm, its surface etched with symbols that change meaning depending on the epoch in which they are read. Tied to the staff are scraps of fabric, each one a fragment of a poem I have composed during my travels, written in a language that only exists when spoken in the presence of certain species of glass-winged insects.

My purpose, insofar as I can discern one, is to bear witness to the endless cycles of creation and decay that define life on Tarth Maddern. I wander between the continent-sized shards, conversing with the megafauna and the microcivilizations, learning their stories and their sorrows. 

In the hanging gardens of the ancient cranes, I sit with the spore-engineers and listen as they recite the genomes of extinct species like holy scripture. On the plains where the lowing fog-beasts graze, I stand in the thunder of their digestive lightning and feel the vibrations of their mournful songs resonating in my basalt bones.

When I encounter other travelers, we exchange tales and share in the dense silence that passes for companionship here. We do not linger together long, for fear of becoming too entangled in each other's gravity wells, but in the moments of our meeting, something like understanding passes between us.

At times, I seek out Kithriss, the lone employee of the Fourth Subtle Base of Opaline Industry. In her presence, I feel the weight of the aeons pressing down upon us both, the futility and the necessity of existence in this place where even the laws of physics are subject to the whims of bureaucracy. We speak in riddles and half-remembered dreams, each of us a mirror reflecting the other's incomprehensible depths.

My poetry, such as it is, consists of single lines inscribed upon the landscape itself. With a touch of my staff, I leave words etched in the film of water that coats every surface, or suspended in the air like constellations of charged particles. Each poem is a koan, a paradox meant to be experienced rather than understood.

"The void dreams of teeth," I write upon the flank of a mountain that may once have been a goddess.

"Entropy is the only fruit that ripens in reverse," I whisper to a grove of rust-trees, and watch as their iron leaves shiver in recognition.

In this way, I wander the shards of Tarth Maddern, a living testament to the strange and terrible beauty of a world that has long since transcended any human notion of sense or purpose. I am a mote of consciousness adrift in an ocean of madness, and my only compass is the ache of wonder that drives me ever onward, ever deeper into the heart of the impossible.
