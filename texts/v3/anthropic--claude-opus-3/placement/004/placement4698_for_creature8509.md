In the Woven Expanse, I would be one of the Chroniclers, but a unique one. My role would be that of the Interpreter, bridging the gap between the ancient ways of the Chroniclers and the curiosity of rare outsiders.

I would move between the cycles of the others, sometimes walking the night paths to read the heat-stories in the stones, other times emerging at dawn to whisper with the condensation collectors. My skin would be patterned like a mosaic, each tessera a different hue and texture representing a different discipline I had studied. Opalescent sections would shimmer with the remembered light of the stars under which I learned the night-vigil. Matte grey patches would carry the cool of the pre-dawn when I was taught to gather dew. Iridescent green would glint like the carapaces of the garden beetles, marking my time among the cultivators.

My fingers would be long like my brethren, but two on each hand would end in styli rather than fingertips. With these I would tap out our stories on specially prepared tiles, creating permanent records of the Chronicler's knowledge. My stylus-fingers would be stained with the ochres and charcoals of the pigments I used, each hue corresponding to a different kind of knowledge - red for the deep thermal patterns, blue for the water cycles, green for the garden lineages.

Around my waist I would wear a belt of ceramic vials, each containing a different sacred oil. These I would use to anoint the foreheads of outsiders who came to learn our ways, each scent invoking a different blessing - sage for wisdom, lavender for peace, rosemary for remembrance. 

My voice would be my most unique trait. Unlike other Chroniclers, I would retain the ability to speak audibly, my throat un-adapted for the subsonic communication. My words would be soft and precise, shaped by years of listening to the wind sigh through the stone lacework. I would use this voice sparingly, only to translate the most important messages from the tap-dances and thermal readings.

I would be the one to greet the pilgrims, the seekers, the lost ones who found their way to our quiet realm. I would guide them along the safest paths, teaching them to read the tile patterns beneath their feet, showing them how to collect the dawn-damp without disturbing the garden ratios. To those who proved worthy, I might reveal some of the deeper secrets - the medicinal uses of the succulents, the intricacies of the subterranean heat-flows, the meaning of the ancient weavings.

But my primary duty would be to the Chroniclers themselves. I would be the keeper of the oral histories, the singer of the stone-songs that told of how our work began, of the first ones who learned to weave the earth itself. On the nights when the aligned moon-phases made the thermal patterns dance, all the Chroniclers would gather at the central vent, and I would chant the stories, my voice rising and falling with the breath of the earth.

I would grow old like the others, my skin weathering and cracking into the same mineral patterns. But my stylus-fingers would remain supple, my voice would remain clear. And when my time came to be placed in the thermal vents and rendered into a new form, it would be my apotheosis. The minerals of my body would be woven into tiles that would ring like bells when tapped, forever singing the stories of the Chroniclers. And the next Interpreter would take up those tiles, and with them, the task of translation, the vital work of preserving and passing on the small gestures that make a world.
