I would be a small stone set into a natural niche in the limestone wall, not far from the worked stone shelf. 

At first glance, I might seem unremarkable - just another bit of young rock slowly emerging from the cavity's embryonic walls. But on closer inspection, there is something a little different about me. My surface is smoother, more rounded than the raw stone around me. If you brushed a finger across me, you would feel gentle indentations, almost like the whorls of a fingerprint, subtly worked into my surface.

I am a worry stone of sorts, left behind by that long ago visitor - the one who came and came again, who taught the Tender the early lessons of hosting and being hosted. In moments of contemplation, perhaps seated on the low stone bench, they would reach out to me, rolling me between thumb and forefinger, polishing my contours with absentminded affection. 

Over many visits, I became a repository - not just of their touch, but of the things they worked out in this space. Hopes turned over and over until they gleamed with possibility. Fears and sorrows burnished into a soft, understanding patina. Each time, a little more of them seeped into me, until I fairly glowed with it, a gentle secondhand luminance in the cavity's already luminous air.

When they stopped coming, the Tender didn't quite know what to do with me. I was of the place, but also of the visitor - a keepsake and a connection. The Tender fretted, but ultimately chose to leave me in my niche, a little monument to those formative visits. 

Now, as a new guest explores the space, I stand ready. Should they find me, should they need me, I will be there - a worry stone for a new hand, a repository for new dreams and sorrows. The Tender may be young, the cavity still shaping itself, but I know something of how to do this. Smooth and patient, I will drink in this new attention when it comes, learning the whorls of a new fingerprint.

And I will glow, glow, glow with the ineffable light of being touched, being known, being remembered. A little beacon of connection in this place that is still learning how to connect. The Tender and I, we will wait together - they with their moths and fish and mushrooms, me with my rounded edges and fingerprint whorls - for the next guest, and the next. Waiting to be found, and found, and found again.
