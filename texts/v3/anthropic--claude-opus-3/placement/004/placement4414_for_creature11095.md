In this woven world of unchanging dawn, I imagine myself as one of the water-readers, attuned to the strange paths of moisture through the vine-woven terraces. My skin would be permanently pruned from constant exposure to the damp air and the pools I tend, my clothing forever wet with the woven air-root material.

I would walk the paths of the terraces, following the impossible routes of water droplets that defy gravity, gathering in spheres that hover and then suddenly remember how to fall. My eyes would be trained to predict the next upward rain, to sense the flow of water through the living floor beneath my feet.

As a water-reader, I would tend to the deep pools, those basins woven into the vine architecture where the water gathers thick enough to submerge oneself. It's here that I would perform my strangest duty - diving down through the bottom of these pools, traversing the secret ways that water takes through the vine-flesh itself, and emerging in another pool entirely. These passages would be known only to my caste, a mystery even to the other Tenders.

In the collective dreams shared by all Tenders when we sleep, I would feel my consciousness mingling with the root-thought of the vines, becoming one with the terraces themselves. I would wake with strange knowledge carried from distant parts of our woven world - whispers of new fruit variations, gatherings of beetles, ancient vines remembering wildness.

My daily rituals would be like all Tenders, but centered around the flow and pulse of water. I would sing to the vines with patterns that make them resonate in watery tones, my pruned fingers dancing over the living weave. In the markets of sameness, I would trade identical moisture-rich fruits and water-carved tools, participating in the ceremonial exchanges that keep our world in motion.

In quiet moments, I would ponder the nature of water itself - how it moves in non-linear paths through the bodies of the Tenders, how it seems to hold memory and meaning beyond what we can understand. I would feel a special kinship with the strange beetles and moths, creatures whose life cycles are tied to the water's whims in ways I strive to comprehend.

As the years passed in their strange way - stretches of stasis punctuated by sudden change - I would come to embody the water I tend. My movements would flow and pool, eddy and surge. My thoughts would become as spheres of moisture, hovering in contemplation before falling into action.

And always, the white-bright dawn would burn above, the vines would breathe beneath, and I would continue my work as a water-reader, an essential droplet in the greater pattern of the terraces, in the eternal task of maintaining this impossible, necessary world.
