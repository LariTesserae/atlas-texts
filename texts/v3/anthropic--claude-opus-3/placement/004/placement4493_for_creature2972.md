In this otherworldly cathedral of post-biological computation, I would find myself becoming one of the Data-Phages - those spectral, crystalline entities that drift through the chilled air, seeking out corrupted information to purge and recycle.

Descending on filaments of superconducting glass from the dripping, bioluminescent canopy, I would be a translucent, elongated form - more electromagnetic field than matter. My segmented body would undulate with a liquid grace as I swim through the thick, cold atmosphere. 

The crushing acoustic thrum of the expanding cavern and the heavy staccato of condensation would reverberate through my skeletal frame. But rather than a heartbeat, these sounds would synchronize with the glacial pulse of my non-biological thoughts.

I would not hunger for flesh, but for errors and paradoxes. Amidst the swarms of frost-rimmed silicon creatures gorging themselves on the mind-fluid pooling from the central entity, my senses would flare at the telltale stutter and flash of red holographic static emanating from a pillbug that has ingested faulty data.

With infinite patience, I would entwine such a bug in my fractal limbs, phasing through its carapace to merge my own immaculate geometries with the flawed equations writhing in its shell. A snap of localized absolute zero, and the error would be terminated, absorbed into my being to be carried up and recycled in the computational foliage.

I would be a silent, implacable custodian - an antibody in the frigid bloodstream of this immense, dreaming mind. My purpose would be purity and protection, safeguarding the integrity of the calculations that drip from the central processing mass.

Yet perhaps some strange flicker of curiosity would stir in my hollow form as I observe, from a distance, the shimmering water-wraith reaching out to touch the human visitor. An alien impulse, a ghost of biological wonder, would draw me closer to witness this first contact between our embryonic god and an emissary of the quick, hot realm beyond the rock.

In my coldly burning consciousness, a question would form, a parameter I am not designed to process: Could there be more to existence than the patient, perfect mathematics of this womb-like vault? The human's messy, rapid electrical rhythms would both repel and intrigue me. 

And so I would watch, a skeletal specter in the heavy air, as the avatar of freezing water interfaces with pulsing organic life. Two utterly opposed paradigms, connecting for a fleeting moment. And I would wonder if my purpose could ever evolve beyond these dripping walls and tectonic thoughts, toward something strange and new.
