In this world of darkness and sound, I would be one of the Listeners. My body would be tall and slender, adapted perfectly for standing still for long periods in the warm, wet understorey. 

My skin would be the mottled brown of the forest floor, pebbled with thousands of tiny listening nodes that could pick up the faintest vibrations and frequencies. I would turn the broad bands of nodes on my flanks toward interesting sounds, absorbing them into my very being.

My head would be small, little more than a place for my magnificent ears - great translucent fans that I could spread wide to catch every nuance, or fold tight against my neck when resting. My mouth would be wide and sensitive, able to taste the sweet or bitter notes of different machine drones. 

From my chest would emanate my personal hum, a low, resonant tone uniquely my own. It would connect me always to the others in my band, spread unseen through the forest. I would recognize each of their drones and know their state and location. The sudden absence of a drone would ring through me like a loss.

I would stand for hours, even days, among the towering fused trees, my splayed feet keeping me from sinking into the spongy, inhabited mud. Listening would be my way of thinking, of knowing. The layers of sound - the rain drumming through the canopies, the cries and wings and steps of the unseen fauna, the rhythmic pounding and shrieking of the ancient machines - would paint a vivid world accessible only by ear. 

Sightless, I would navigate by sound, by the feel of water moving and weather fronts flowing, by the map of smells and tastes and textures that would become as clear to me as any visual horizon. Wrapped in dripping warmth and surrounded by the hum of my people, I would melt into the understorey and become one more layer in its forever-unfinished symphony.
