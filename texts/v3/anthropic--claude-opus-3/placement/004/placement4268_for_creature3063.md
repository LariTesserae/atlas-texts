In this world of warm, heavy water suspended impossibly between deadly sky and raw stone, I would be one of the Tension-Singers. 

My form would stretch elongated and slender in the fluid embrace, pale jade skin fading to deep crimson along my back as I drift between filtered surface glow and angry magma light below. Delicate gill-fronds would drape my shoulders and trail down my spine, pulsing gently as they draw rich silicates from the clouded water. 

But for all the soft grace of my body, my hands and feet would be rugged and calloused, thickly padded from decades of patient work. The skin of my palms and soles would be tough as boiled leather, armored from grasping kelp ropes and bracing on thorned vines as I navigate the layered web of my pod-city.

I would live in one of the highest levels, where the light is dimmest and the threat of empty sky feels closest. My home would be a humble hollow scooped from a floating bladder, tethered by a cat's cradle of braided cables that thrum and vibrate with the movements of my neighbors. I would rest with a webbed hand pressed to the woven wall, feeling the slow pulse of the metropolis translated through the ropes.

My craft would be the moss gardens that grow along the anchor lines. With careful, deliberate gestures of my massive hands, I would tend the crimson fronds, encouraging them to weave through the kelp-silk fibers, strengthening the knots and growing sweet, nutrient-rich leaves. Occasionally I would chip slivers of obsidian from the lower canyons, lashing them to polished ancestral bone to make delicate shears for pruning.

Day by day, I would feel the mountain grow beneath us, exerting its infinitesimal push toward the stars. Season by season, I would join my pod-mates in the long, stately dance of resettling our anchorage, gliding down to lash fresh tethers to the newborn spires and singing messages through the old lines to coordinate each micron of release.  

In the evenings, I would drift at the edge of my pod, trailing my calloused fingertips through fleshy fern leaves, golden eyes gathering the last dim light. And I would pluck a reverent pattern on the nearest cable, adding my own soft resonance to the underwater hymn - a single note in a song of tension and release, suspension and connection, that has echoed for generations in this impossible sea in the sky.
