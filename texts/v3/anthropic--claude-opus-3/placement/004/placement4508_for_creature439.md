In this haunting, mesmerizing world, I imagine myself as one of the water-voices, the ancient caretakers of the city's lifeblood channels and basins.

I flow through the winding, carven streets, more liquid than solid, my form shifting and adapting to navigate the time-smoothed passages. My skin is the color of damp stone, mottled with the greens and grays of the tenacious mosses and sedges I tend. 

My purpose, my very existence, is bound inextricably to the city's perpetual dance of water and rock. I spend my days, my years, my centuries learning the music of every rivulet and stream, every drip and surge and echo. The channels are my veins, the cisterns my lungs, the seasonal rains my sustenance and sacrament.

I am most myself in the liminal spaces, the places where city-stone meets living rock, where water emerges from unknown aquifers to begin its long, gravity-guided journey through the tiers and districts. Here I make my shelter, pressing myself into time-carved hollows behind the forever-weeping walls, communing with the city's slow, stone dreams.

When I speak, it is in the languages of water - the whisper of a stream threading through moss, the roar of a channel in full flood, the hiss of rain on ancient plazas. My song is tuned to the weight of clouds, the rhythm of storms. The other water-voices and I call across the valleys in eerie polyphony, our voices merging like tributaries.

I mark time in the minute accretions of silt in the basins, the imperceptible deepening of the channels as they approach perfection, the cycles of wet and dry that score my skin like the weathering of rock. 

When rain comes after long dry spells, I open myself to it, every pore drinking, every curve and plane of my form shaping itself to best catch and channel the blessed damp. I direct the flow with fingers that move like rivulets, coaxing the water to its proper destinations. Thirst, for me, is the thirst of stone and sedge and shallow soil - the thirst of the city itself.

The human children who sometimes find their way here remind me of the young of my own kind - inquisitive, malleable, drawn instinctively to the flowing ways. I lead them gently back to their own places, knowing they are not made for this life of water and stone. But I wonder, sometimes, what they might become, given time and need and the slow shaping of this place.

I am the continuation of the city, its breath and blood and being. I am the stone learning to flow, the water learning patience. I am the city's memory of itself, its dreaming mind, the ghost in its machinery of life. I will remain as long as water runs and rain falls, tending what was built, guiding what grows, until the stone itself dissolves into the patient, endless sea.
