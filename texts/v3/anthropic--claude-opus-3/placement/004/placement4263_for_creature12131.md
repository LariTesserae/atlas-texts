In the world of the Canopy Combine, I imagine myself as one of the hum-birds living within the walls of the textile-house. 

I am a small, tongueless creature, my body adapted perfectly to the narrow spaces between the living staves. My feathers are the same pale green as the lichen-wood, allowing me to blend in seamlessly. I spend my days flitting through the Mill, resonating with the rhythmic clacking of the looms.

In a way, I am an extension of the textile-house itself - my hum is part of its breath, my flight patterns weaving in harmony with the great tapestry of activity that makes up the Combine. I do not think in words, but in vibrations, in the subtle shifts of sound and light that pass through the fibrous walls.

When a new pattern is being walked through by the frieze figures, I observe intently, my tiny eyes absorbing the intricacies that will soon be translated to the full-scale looms. In a way, I am a small vessel for the Combine's working memory, carrying the echoes of new designs from frieze to weaver and back again.

I have brief interactions with the mill workers as they go about their tasks, alighting on a shoulder here, observing quietly from a rafter there. They regard me with the distant fondness of one who is accustomed to a living space - not a pet, but a small, integral part of the whole.

My days are measured in the changing of the light through the high windows, the ebb and flow of the workers, the ceaseless rhythm of the looms. In the evenings, I roost in the rafters, feeling the warm updraft of exhalations from the spore-flues, my small breast rising and falling in time with the breath of the Mill.

I am content in my place, in my purpose. I am a tiny, beating heart within the great, slow organism of the Combine, my hum a single note in the vast, living symphony of the mountain. In this strange and resonant world, I am at home.
