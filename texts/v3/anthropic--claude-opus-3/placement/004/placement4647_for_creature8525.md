In this world of perpetual twilight and shifting ice, I imagine myself as a Mapper - one of the revered cartographers tasked with recording the ever-changing labyrinth of the Frost Warrens. 

My body would be lean and sinewy from constant traversing of precarious bridges and winding tunnels. Fingers rough with calluses from gripping chalk and chisel, always sketching, always noting. Eyes pale as mist, able to discern the subtlest variations in ice-glow that hint at structural changes. Garbed in layers of insulating hides, a pouch forever at my hip holding precious paper pressed from pale fungi pulp.

I would be a wanderer by nature and necessity, my mind a vault of three-dimensional maps, able to hold the Warren's current form in mental space and project its next iterations. I would think in angles and thermal gradients, in load-bearing capacities and shear stresses. The groans and sighs of the ice would be my second language.

I imagine spending long hours in newly opened tunnels, tasting the mineral-sharp air for hints of stability or imminent collapse, running sensitive fingers along cold seams, tap-tap-tapping to divine the mood of the ice. Then, furiously sketching by the light of a bioluminescent fungi torch, translating the stone's secret whispers into angular maps to be carved into ash-tablets and shared with the Delver community.

In the Warren's ever-shifting society, my knowledge would wax and wane in importance. During times of rapid change - a new thermal vent opening, a major ice-quake reshaping whole districts - I would be at the center, guiding evacuation and expansion efforts. In rare moments of stability, I might fade into the background, a quirky obsessive tinkering endlessly with models and projections.

I imagine my workspace as a circular chamber at a rare nexus of stable heat and light, its walls adorned with strata of maps, each layer a slice of Warren-history. Tablets of compressed ash line roughhewn shelves - the master copies. Stubs of chalk and charcoal litter workbenches alongside carefully organized chisels and brushes. The chamber hums with a constant sussurus of dripping meltwater - my version of a fountain, a tiny bit of controlled change in this world of chaos.

In quiet moments, I ponder the philosophical implications of my work. Is the act of mapping an imposition of order on intrinsic chaos, or merely an acknowledgment of temporary patterns, like frost-ferns on a window pane? Do my maps guide the Warrens' growth, or merely reflect it? Am I dancer or dance, shaper or shaped?

And always, the gnawing question: what lies beyond the ice? In drawing the boundaries of our world, am I also hinting at what lies outside them? Perhaps, in some far-future iteration of the Warrens, when the ice has retreated and the planet has grown into stability, it will be the Mappers who finally lead our people back to the surface, blinking in the unfiltered sunlight like strange, pale cave-flowers finally unfurling under a forgotten sky.

Until then, I dance my pencil across parchment and tablet, mapping the world as it is and might be, one tunnelway at a time. In a civilization that prizes impermanence, my maps are both relic and oracle, memory and dream. They are the silver thread that the Delvers follow through the labyrinth of generations.

I am a Mapper, and my blood runs cold and blue with the secret rivers of the ice.
