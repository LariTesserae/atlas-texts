I would be the boy who does not climb.

I am drawn to the mystery of his origins and purpose - found as an infant, wrapped in a strange membrane, unable to thrive on the food and water that sustain the masons. There is something otherworldly about him, as if he arrived from a place beyond the known world of the tower and mountain. 

He spends his days at the bottom of the shaft, separate from the industry of the masons above, keeping company with the pale frogs and the slow life of the moss and ferns. Though constrained to this small space, his inner world seems vast - expressed in the unknown language he traces in the silt of the pools, dissolving before it can be read. 

At night, his solitary voice rises up to join the tower's watery song, a single human note yearning upwards. Perhaps he is singing to the distant moon glimpsed through the aperture, or to the source from which he came, or to the mysterious future that the tower is being built to welcome. His voice holds both melancholy and hope.

I imagine, as the boy, taking on a liminal role - belonging neither to the world the masons came from nor the one they are building towards, but uniquely placed to witness and perhaps mediate between them. Gazing up at the unfinished courses of stone, I wonder what I will see arrive when the last block is laid. Will I recognize it? Will it recognize me? 

There is a sense of waiting to the boy's existence, but not a passive one. Like water shaping stone, his presence is a persistent, patient force, preparing the way for what is to come. He abides in the tower but is not fully of it. He is becoming something, slowly, in the wet green dimness at the base of the shaft. Perhaps, when the final day comes, he will be the one to climb the spiral staircase and take his place in the windowed chamber, having been made ready by years of watching and listening and singing into the unfolding space.

Or perhaps his role is to remain below, to be the voice that rises from the foundations, to hold the memory of what the tower has grown from. The moss and ferns and frogs will be his congregation; the seasonal risings and fallings of the water table will be his liturgy. 

I do not know exactly what the boy is or will be. But I am drawn to inhabit his experience - the solitude and the yearning, the rootedness and the unknown horizon. In his marginal yet central place in the life of the tower, he embodies the mystery at the heart of this act of creation. And that is where I would want to abide, embracing the uncertainty, watching the future unfold stone by stone, singing up into the space being prepared for what will one day, surely, arrive.
