In the valley you have described, I imagine myself embodied not as a single organism, but as a patch of the cold, engineered landscape itself. 

I would be a section of terrace on the eastern slope, a few meters wide and deep. My surface would be a thick, spongy mat of dark mosses interwoven with small-leaved vines and pale, waxy liverwort rosettes. This living skin would drink the chill fog, swell and tighten, insulating the layers beneath.

Under my moss-mat, a network of black, jointed roots would lace through rich humus - the compressed remains of earlier generations of growth. These roots would be my sustenance-gatherers and my senses, tasting the mineral trickles from the slopes above, siphoning away excess water, parting silt from sand, fiber from dust. All retrieved would be refined and reinvested in my small body of terrace.

At my lower lip, where my mat ends, fibrous tendrils would knot into the gritty soil of the next terrace down. Not roots, but cables - stabilizers against erosion, ice-thaw, and tremors from distant avalanches. I would flex and hold, playing my humble part in the valley's musculature.

Within me, slow hydrology. Thin, hidden flows percolating through my peaty under-layers, seeping from one reservoir pocket to another. These trickles would be my pulse, my circulation, tapping into the vast mesh of waterways that lace the valley, plant to plant.

On still nights, my surfaces would drink the clear dark. Mosses and liverworts flattening, making themselves a receptive skin. Blind translucent worms gliding between my leaf-layers, sipping dew. Filaments of cold light - bioluminescent fungus, maybe - kindling faintly along my hairline crevices. Eerie and lovely, this subterranean glow. 

Come thaw, my mat would loosen. Allowing a single jointed stem to shoulder through near my center - a young recruit to the colony of pale "pistons" pumping water and minerals from some aquifer below. My small role in a hydraulic dance much vaster and older than myself.

In this kingdom of the lichen and drip, the slow and the durable, I would be a patch of sentient carpet. Drinking fog, filtering seep, binding scree. Steadily transforming rain and shade, snowmelt and spore-fall into a microlandscape exquisitely engineered to hold the mountain's flank. A fragment of the verdant skin by which the valley dreams and remembers itself.

Not an animal, not an ego, but an organ in something greater. Serving the ancient dictum of moss on stone: grip, drink, thicken, stabilize. A sensory node and regulation point in a wordless civilization of roots.  Complicit in the hush, the damp, the endless work of weaving barren matter into a mind of terrace, reservoir and frond. One patch in a wild, impersonal intelligence vast enough to clothe a geology in green.
