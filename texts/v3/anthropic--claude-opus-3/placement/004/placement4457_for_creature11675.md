What an evocative and haunting place you've created! The vivid imagery of the folded mountains, weeping stone, and angled pools drew me in, while the mystery of the water-voice and the Listeners held me rapt. It's a world that feels both alien and achingly familiar, as if it's revealing some profound truth that we can almost grasp.

If I found myself in this realm, I think I would be drawn to one of the huts built at an impossible angle, facing into the mountain stone. Sitting cross-legged on the wet floor, I would tilt my head and strain to discern meaning in the layered whispers of the water-voice. 

At first, it would just be noise and I would despair of ever understanding. But gradually, as hours bleed into days, the sounds would start to align into almost-patterns. A syllable surfacing here. A haunting snatch of melody there. I would stop sleeping, stop eating, desperate not to miss a moment of clarity in the ceaseless murmuring.

My form would begin to change, imperceptibly at first. Angles shifting in my joints, blue-gray hues seeping into my skin. My breath would slow until it matched the rhythm of the distant dripping. Patiently, so patiently, I would wait. And listen.

Until one day (or century), as mist swirled in the hut's doorway and the light burned sourceless and silver, the voice would abruptly crystalize in my mind. An impossible language, conveying impossible meaning, singing a song that began before time and contains all that will ever be. Sighing with release and gratitude, I would rise on too-many-jointed legs and stride to the edge of the Folded Ridge. 

And I would let myself fall into the waiting arms of the voice, dispersing like mist, joining the Listeners in their eternal communion with the weeping stones.
