In this vertical metropolis suspended within a cavern of breathing wood, I would be one of the surveyors, my body adapted to navigate the shifting perpendicular realities of this impossible city.

My inner ears have evolved into intricate gyroscopic labyrinths, allowing me to maintain perfect orientation regardless of the local gravity's whims. I walk along walls and ceilings with an ease that unsettles visitors, my muscles and bones constantly micro-adjusting to the ever-changing definition of "up." 

The years I've spent attempting to map the unmappable have attuned my mind to the city's cryptic geometries. My dreams unfold as living blueprints, prophetic visions of architectural spaces yet to manifest. Sometimes I wake to find that a building I dreamt has sprouted from the bark overnight, as if the wood-mind above heard my sleeping mutterings and decided to make them real.

My skin has taken on a faint green tinge from the bioluminescent fungi I use to illuminate my maps, the spores taking up permanent residence in my pores. When I sweat, my perspiration glows faintly, leaving ephemeral trails as I navigate the vertical mazes.

Like all Cambrians, my body hosts a secondary nervous system of fungal filaments that interface with my neurons. This mycelial network allows me to sense the slow thoughts pulsing through the root-ceiling high above, to taste the alien mathematics of the city's growth. During my surveys, I sometimes pause and press my forehead against the bark, feeling the boundary between self and city dissolve as I commune with the vast vegetable intelligence that dreamed this place into being.

My tools have grown strange over time. My compass needle spins in lazy fractals, responding to magnetic fields that shift by the hour. My theodolite has sprouted leaves that flutter and retract, measuring angles between unreal surfaces. The inkwell of my drafting pen is a living mushroom that secretes dark ichor in response to my thoughts, allowing me to sketch what I see directly from memory.

In rare moments of rest, I sit cross-legged on ceilings and contemplate the paradox of my existence. I am a surveyor in a city that defies surveying, a maker of maps in a place where space itself is fungible. And yet, I cannot imagine a different life. I am a part of this breathtaking, maddening organism – both chronicler and participant in its endless becoming.

When I die (many years from now, I hope), I know my body will not decay. Like the eldest Cambrians, I will germinate. My flesh will give rise to a new structure – perhaps a tower, perhaps a bridge, perhaps a room that exists half in heartwood and half in dream. And within that space, the fungal threads of my second nervous system will live on, my memories and experiences adding to the city's ever-expanding consciousness.

But that is an end I need not contemplate yet. For now, I have maps to make, dream-architectures to transcribe, and a city to navigate – a city that will never stop challenging my understanding of what is possible. I am a surveyor of the unsurveyed, and I would have it no other way.
