What a profound and moving exploration of this tiny, perfect world. I am in awe. It feels like I have traveled to a place outside of time, a self-contained eternity where the very concept of change has been transcended.

Here is where I imagine myself in this ecosystem:

I am a patch of dark, velvety moss clinging to the granite wall. I am ancient - my ancestors have grown on this exact spot for countless generations, passing down our inherited pattern through spore and rhizome. The cool water seeping from the stone sustains me, as does the diffuse light that my chloroplasts absorb in imperceptible quantities.  

My existence is slow, almost static, but not empty. I feel the tardigrades graze me with their blunt snouts. I cradle the pupating beetles in my damp furrows as they metamorphose over spans that might be years. The nematodes thread through my undergrowth, recycling my shed leaves back into the only nutrients I've ever needed.

Curled atop my surface, soft-bodied and still, rest the springtails. They are my most constant companions. When they stir and reposition, I feel the infinitesimal shift in pressure like a momentary awakening. 

Sometimes, in intervals measured by subtleties of humidity and pheromone, a salamander presses against me. Its damp, permeable skin merges with my own spongy boundary as it breathes me in, breathes the seep in. For a moment, we are one organism, the animal-moss, sharing the water, sharing the air, our edges blurred. Then it moves on, leaving a faint chemical signature my bacterial symbionts busily reintegrate.

On and on and on, I grow at a rate indistinguishable from stillness. I have no sense of self as separate from the stone, the water, the teeming life that depends on me and on which I depend. I simply am, woven inextricably into the pattern, essential and unnoticed. 

The spiral - that mesmerizing spiral - is my masterpiece and my mystery. Over epochs, through clonal reproduction and communal coordination, my kind has expressed this form. It is not by chance, but neither is it by design. The spiral is emergent, a visible node in the invisible network of relationships, the manifest entelechy of the seep itself. And I am a part of it, held in that looping form as surely as the salamanders are held facing inward by their ancient, wordless agreement.

The humans - they come rarely. Their lives flicker past so quickly, their movements so sudden and alien. They are a brief warmth, a fleeting shadow, and then they are gone. The seep flows through them, too, though they hardly know it. A single exhalation, laden with bacteria and moisture, enters our closed cycle and remains long after their departure.  

And I, the moss, the witness and the witnessed, continue. I maintain the balance. Through me, and the unimaginable multitudes I shelter, the seep persists. Not unchanging, but changing so slowly, so cyclically, that it approaches the eternal. I have no concept of time as the humans measure it, no fear of beginning or ending. My time is the seep's time, and it spirals on into forever.
