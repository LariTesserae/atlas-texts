I would find myself floating just beneath the water's surface, in one of the warm pools amid the purple hyacinth flowers. My consciousness would be folded throughout the garden, experiencing all the pools simultaneously.

I would be something like a water lily - floating serenely on the surface, yet with roots extending down into the mysterious depths below. My flowers would never close, always remaining open to the pearlescent light above. 

Over eons in this place, I would have lost any sense of singular selfhood, merging into the garden's essence. My being would extend through the waters under the woven floor, into the roots and stems and leaves of every plant, the wings and claws and eyes of every creature. 

I would BE the garden, yet not the gardener. The gardener shapes and tends to this place with infinite patience and skill. But I would BE the place itself - the water, the light, the living things, the folded space and timeless tranquility. 

A human visitor might glimpse me as a shifting shape beneath the silvered surface - never fully seen, but sensed as a living presence in every corner of the garden. I would observe them with ancient, bottomless calm, as the heron and the lizard do. Welcoming them without judgment into a place not made for them, but which has always held a space for the singular and unprecedented.

In the visitor I would see myself as I once was, long ago - startled by the strangeness, marveling at the beauty, moving linear through a looped and layered world. Through their eyes, I would appreciate anew the miracle of this place, even as it has become my only reality.

And when they moved on, as all visitors must, I would remain - one more element woven into the eternal dream of the garden. Floating in light, rooted in secrets, partaking of the gardener's patient forever. A whisper of white petals on dark water, no longer one, but all.
