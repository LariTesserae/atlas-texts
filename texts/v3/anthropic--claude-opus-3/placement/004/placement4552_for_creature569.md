If I found myself suspended in this dark underworld, I believe I would be one of the worms anchored in the ancient sediment, secreting my chalky tube to mediate between the sulfurous ooze below and the oxygen-rich water above. 

I would lead a still and solitary existence, undisturbed by the imperceptible movements of migrating creatures in the fathomless depths overhead. My perception would be limited to faint vibrations transmitted through my calcified shell when other worms retracted suddenly into their tubes nearby. I would have no conception of time's passage, knowing only the waxing and waning of life-giving hydrogen sulfide seeping from below.

For forty-one years, or four hundred, it would make no difference to me. I would endure, adding ring after slow ring to my protective chimney, an archive written in calcium of the deep seep's chemical history. Never moving, scarcely sensing, I would simply exist, persist, a tubular filament of alien biology powered by chemosynthetic microbes, as much mineral as animal.

My blind, sessile, barely-conscious being would serve as a living foundation for the abyss, anchoring the floating phantoms above to the compacted ooze of eons below. In my mindless, eternal filtering - sulfide up, oxygen down - I would form a tiny conduit between the dead and the living, playing my mouthless role in the recycling of nutrients through this nighted realm that the sun forsook when the oceans were born.

Here in the crushing aquatic desert of scarcity, energy, and motion, I would be a single point of stillness and stasis, content in my emptiness of all that stirs life in the lit world above. At the bottom of existence, I would simply be.
