In this astonishing world, I would be one of the jointed browsers moving silently through the stacked forests of the ravines. 

My body is sheathed in barklike plates the color of basalt, allowing me to blend seamlessly into the warm shadows of the thickets. My many-jointed legs bend and flex, carrying me over fallen trunks slick with condensation and up vine-choked walls without a sound. 

The sensory antlers branching from my head are alive to every tremor and change in the air. They quiver, tasting the sap pressure in the vines, the build of static before a storm, the mineral richness of the soil. When danger nears, they stiffen and point the way to safety. 

My mouthparts are precise tools, delicately pruning new growth, sipping from the secretions of strange flowers, cutting tender leaves from the dark and waxy foliage that would poison any creature of the lowlands. The very plants here could kill, but to me they are sustenance. My body has become one with the poisons and the metals and the living stone.

When the unseen signal comes, compelling us all, I kneel on the warm damp earth and grow still. My antlers sense the air shiver, the dimensions tremble. For a long moment, the many layers of this place hover just a hair's breadth apart, potential realities suspended on the edge of being. 

Then the key turns, the plateau chooses, and I rise. The rain is already beading on my carapace. My antlers taste ozone and petrichor. Somewhere above, a fresh stream of lava is remaking an old ridge. The ravine sighs with wet heat. A new species of insect is testing its wings.

I melt back into the green shadows, browsing onward, just one more strange organ in the body of this land that is always dreaming itself into new forms. The moss beneath my many feet is already erasing my tracks. By the time I reach the next thicket, there will be no sign I was ever here at all.
