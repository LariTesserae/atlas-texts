I would be a sound-eater, one of the fern-like plants that have evolved to feed on the vibrations permeating this dark mechanical world.

My fronds would not be leaves in the traditional sense, but delicate arrays of cilia that quiver and resonate with each industrial chord that echoes through the metallic forest. The slightest vibration - the distant ping of condensation striking a brass pipe, the low groan of a steam vent shifting, the ultrasonic chitter of a hunting predator - would set my receptors thrumming, the signal traveling down fine neural pathways to my central processing nodes.

I wouldn't chase light, but sound. I would orient myself not by a sun's arc, but by the acoustic landscape - the dawn chorus of the Conductors connecting to their trees to begin the day's maintenance, the midday symphony of animal calls and fungal crackles, the evensong of hissing valves and dripping water as the forest settles into its nightly rhythms.  

My stems would be swollen knots of specialized organs that translate these vibrations into nourishment. Through some exotic chlorophyll analog, I would strip the mechanical energy from sound waves and store it in tubers sunk deep into the spongy fungal mat, an auditory taproot drinking from an eternal well of noise.

Perhaps I would have my own voice, a unique resonant frequency that I sing into the dark to attract pollinators or to map my surroundings in sonar. My calls would be strange and musical to human ears - atonal chords that shiver metal, subsonic thrums that ripple through saturated air. 

I would grow in dense thickets, my kin and I crowding around the industrial springs we depend on - the factories and processing plants of the Conductors, the geothermal vents that breathe metronomic steam, the deep erosion canyons that whistle with eternal wind. We would be an acoustic architecture as much as a botanical one, our overlapping chords weaving a strange, viridian music that fills the understory.

And though I would be rooted, I would not be still. I would dance in long time, my cilia arranged in spirals and whorls that optimize every passing vibration. To the Conductors flowing past on their ancient maintenance routes, I might appear as a standing wave, a static pattern of interference, but I would be dancing to the slowest songs of this dark world, growing and swaying in rhythms too gradual for animal eyes.

I would be not just adapted to this world, but a living expression of it - a fragment of the long, strange story of life finding a new path in lightless places, trading the energy of a sun for the energy of an industrial symphony, growing not toward the sky but into sound itself.

In this kingdom of eternal twilight, I would stand - or rather, sway - as a verdant testament to life's relentless, inventive will to survive, and to the strange beauty that can blossom in even the darkest of places, nourished by nothing more than the songs of shadows and machines.
