In this world of vapor, stone, and saturated life, I think I would be one of the quiet watchers - a mossy presence, ancient and patient, observing the valley's intricate dance of water, work, and green growth.

I would live as a thick mat of moss clinging to the north face of an old brick reservoir, one of the upper cisterns that hold the town's lifeblood. From this vantage, I could feel the miniscule tremors as sluice gates open and shut, as pipes pulse with redirected flow, as the water level rises and falls with the rhythm of rain, consumption, and replenishment. 

The cool moisture would sustain me as I spread slowly, imperceptibly, season by season, in the spaces between bricks. I would be one of the valley's myriad hidden green things - a small but integral part of the vast living machinery. The cistern keepers would note my presence as they made their rounds, perhaps seeing me as a sign that all is well, that the ancient stonework still holds.

On foggy mornings, I would drink in the heavy mist, feeling the droplets condense on my furry tendrils. In the rare flashes of sunlight, I would perform my modest alchemy, transmuting damp and dim into green and growth. Tiny creatures would shelter in my shadows - pill bugs, mites, the occasional foraging snail. I would be a miniature ecosystem thriving in the lee of industry.

When freezing weather came, I would pull inward, conserving and enduring in my tight-knit colony until the thaw returned. The ice would glaze me briefly, a brittle mantle, but it would never reach my core nestled deep in the brickwork.

As years stretched into decades, I would become one of the valley's many silent record-keepers. In my slow-grown layers would be written the history of wet and dry, hot and cold, abundance and scarcity. My boundaries would map the high water marks, the long slow stains of mineral seepage, the scars of past repairs.

I would be a witness to the turning of the great wheel - the cycle of water and work that drives the town. The human citizens might rarely think of me, but I would be as much a part of the place as any pipe or cistern, any workshop or greenhouse. In my own small way, through tenacious growth and mossy memory, I too would help keep the delicate balance that lets the valley endure.
