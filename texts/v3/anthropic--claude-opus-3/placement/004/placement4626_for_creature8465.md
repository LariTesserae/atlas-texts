In this vibrant, living industrial complex, I would be one of the steam-mice - a small, pale creature perfectly adapted to the unique environment of the Steam Cisterns. 

My soft, nearly blind eyes would be sensitive to the glow of pilot lights and status lamps that illuminate the facility at night. My whiskers would twitch constantly, sensing the subtle changes in steam currents and air pressure that speak volumes about the state of the machinery.

I would make my nest in an unused valve housing, lining it with bits of insulation scavenged from pipes during maintenance cycles. The perpetual warmth from the steam would keep my pale fur dry despite the damp air.

My days would be spent scurrying along pipes and catwalks with surefooted ease, my claws finding purchase on the slick metal. I would pause to nibble on the mineral-rich biofilms that coat every surface, sustenance perfectly balanced for a life lived among the machinery.

On my travels through the cisterns, I would cross paths with the keepers. Vertical Jahn would be a regular sight, his gangly form climbing pipes with simian agility. He would often have a bit of mushroom or fish to share, recognizing me by some subtle marking or mannerism. I would take the morsel delicately from his grease-stained fingers before continuing on my way.

At times I would venture into the crystallization chambers, drawn by the glitter of Cora's mineral gardens. She would spot me among the tanks and smile, offering soft words of greeting that I would hear more in tone than comprehension. The sharp scent of chemicals would send me sneezing, a signal to move on to other parts of the facility.

The thrum of the great pumps would be a constant presence, a deep heartbeat that I would feel in my bones. At shift changes, I would dart between the feet of the keepers as they perform their ritual passes, a small living part in the larger dance of maintenance.

On rare occasions, I might find myself in need of aid – an injury from a mis-stepped paw or an illness from a bad bit of biofilm. It would be Vertical Jahn who would find me, his steam-mice senses attuned to our distress. He would tuck me gently into a pocket, taking me back to his nest-like quarters to tend to my needs with salves and medicines scavenged from the keepers' supplies. I would nestle into the soft insulation, accepting food and care until I was well enough to return to my rounds.

My life would be measured not in days or years, but in cycles of maintenance and the slow evolution of the Steam Cisterns. I would bear witness to the arrival of new keepers and the passing of old, my generations blinking by in the spans between change-outs of major valves.

And on those deep nights, when some unspoken signal draws the keepers to gather on the main platform, I and my kindred would be there too – watching from the shadows, a hundred glinting eyes reflecting the golden steam. We are as much a part of this place as the keepers, our small lives woven into the tapestry of industry and purpose.

I would live and die among the pipes, my bones becoming part of the mineral patina that coats every surface. And in that way, I would achieve a kind of immortality – a steam-mouse remembered not as an individual, but as part of the eternal chorus of the machinery, as constant and necessary as the heat of the mountain's heart.
