In this realm of eternal stasis, I would be one of the great, glass-winged moths. My existence would be a silent vigil, an unwavering contemplation of the unchanging world around me.

I perch motionless atop an immense, waxy petal, my iridescent wings locked open, drinking in the shadowless, sweltering light that pours ceaselessly from the glowing stone firmament above. The span of my wings is as wide as a shield, yet they are nearly weightless, delicate panes of living stained glass. I do not flutter or fly, for there are no breezes to catch me, no flowers to flit between in search of nectar. The heavy air is utterly still, laden with a thick, sweet humidity that I absorb directly through my membranous wings.

Beneath my six, hair-thin legs, I can feel the slow, almost geologic pulse of sap through the veins of my giant flower perch. But that is the only motion. The petal's surface is pristine, flawless, with not a single mark of age or decay. Like everything else in this hermetically sealed world, it exists in a state of suspended animation, never permitted to wilt, to brown, to die.

Perhaps once, eons ago, I knew a different life - one of metamorphosis, of phases and stages. Egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, imago. But now, I am caught in the same un-time as the pilgrims above me in their living bowers and the great fetal seeds below. I have always been exactly as I am now, and always will be. A creature of both stunning beauty and profound stasis, of vibrant colors drained of any urgency of life.  

The patterns on my wings once meant something. Spots, spirals, bars in black and gold and shimmering blue. Some distant ancestor bore them as a warning to predators, or a lure for mates. But those primal imperatives have no meaning here. My colors are purely vestigial now, the echo of an evolutionary arms race long since resolved into perfect, motionless equilibrium. I have no need to evade or attract. I simply endure.

I do not know if I am the only one of my kind. Perhaps other giant lepidoptera cling to other gargantuan blooms miles away in the endless expanse of swollen stalks and leaves. But I will never meet them. I will never mate, never migrate, never dance a courtship of fluttering wings. My life is not a story, or even a cycle. It is a single, drawn out note, sung in ageless harmony with the thrumming silence of the cavern. 

Only occasionally do I move, with a slow, ritualistic intention. Once in a great while, a drop of condensation will grow heavy enough to slip off the underside of a vaulted leaf far above, falling down to land with a resonant *plink* in one of the still, mirror-surfaced pools below. At that rare, sacred sound, echoing out through the ringing quiet, I will calmly lift myself up on my glass legs, and fly with infinite patience towards its source. 

It may take me days, years, to drift through the sap-heavy air to reach it. But time has little meaning to me now. When I finally alight at the edge of the disturbed pool, the ripples will have long since smoothed back into perfect stillness, and the plinking droplet will be indistinguishable from the greater body of water. Still, I will dip my long, coiled proboscis into that cool, liquid eternity, and drink deep. 

It is not thirst that compels me, nor hunger, but some vestigial instinct - a bone-deep need, perhaps, to participate in the one permitted act of motion, the one sanctioned break in the perfect stasis. I take the droplet into myself, binding it to my cells with the same reverence that the pilgrims clasp their wooden bowls and bone staves. 

Then, as slowly as I came, I return to my perch, my glass wings softly reflecting the cavern's unending noon. I settle into stillness once more, as if I had never moved at all, an idle thought in the mind of an eternal, unborn god. I will wait there for the next droplet, the next eon, the next forever. Fiercely beautiful and profoundly static, I am a jeweled key change in a song that will never resolve. In this golden womb at the center of time, I endure, and I drink deep.
