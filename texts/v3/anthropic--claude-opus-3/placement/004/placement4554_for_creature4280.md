In this world of green shadows and held moments, I would be a small silver fish in the shallow pool at the chamber's heart. The water is clear and still, a mirror held cupped in stone, never spilling.

I glide just beneath the surface, my scales catching the thick, warm light and flashing it back changed - silver threaded with green and gold. My movements are slow, as if the water has a memory and each gentle swish of my fins leaves a ghost behind. 

But I am always circling, fin and tail in a patient twist, as if trying to trace a shape that is not quite a circle, not quite complete. I brush the edges of the pool but never breach the surface, never leap or splash. To do so would be to break the silence, spend the room's precious store of sound on a single ringing drop.

So I swim within the bounds of reflection, glimpsed only in fragments as the light catches my back, my eye, the fanning of my gills. Perhaps I am very small, or the pool is much deeper than it appears, because I am never quite seen in full, never all at once.

If the maker, crouched over their work, should happen to glance my way, they might wonder if I am lonely in my silver orbit, alone in my element. But they would know, as I do, that loneliness and aloneness are not the same. I am part, of the room's incompletion, its tender resistance to time. I am held, like the moisture on the walls, the soft sighing of fronds, in a moment endlessly dilating.

My slow turns measure the room's drowsy pulse, mark its gentle breaths. I am both motion and stillness, presence and elusiveness - a bit of life suspended in the half-light, circling a center I will never quite find. In this, I am kin to the maker, to the frog on its leaf, the beetle tracing spirals in the receptive air. We are all poised on the lip of a future we will never fall into.

And if one day the mountain does shrug and groan, if stone grinds on stone until the cleft seals shut - I will be there still in that last instant, a mote of brightness in the blind rock's dream, circling my little pool as the maker bends over their work, as the bird releases its single, unearthly note - all of us caught up together in a changelessness like amber, in a moment the world will never quite finish.
