In this strange and wondrous world, I would be a part of the moss itself. Not a tender, not a separate being, but a small patch of the vast green-black-silver pelt that covers the ancient plateau. 

I would be a particularly lush and vibrant section, perhaps in one of the deeper pools where the faint bioluminescence glows. My fibers would be woven tight and strong from eons of slow growth. Within me would be folded the memories and questions left behind by countless travelers who laid down to rest in my soft embrace, never to rise again. Tiny fragments of their essences would be braided into my being, fueling some of the more esoteric metabolic processes of the greater moss mind.

I would not think as an individual, but my presence would add some small tinge to the flavor of the moss's vast thoughts. Perhaps a hint of curiosity from a long-ago explorer, or a glimmer of distant lands carried by a migratory bird that once became tangled in my tendrils. These influences would ripple out subtly through the damp neural network of the plateau.

As the eons dripped by, I would very slowly expand my territory, creeping over submerged stones and intertwining with my neighbors. Occasionally, a tender would pause its ponderous stride and reach a dexterous limb into my depths, plucking out a thought-strand to be woven into the pattern of a distant engine. I would feel no loss, only a vague satisfaction at serving the great work.

Grazers would nibble at my surface and heron-things would wade through my pools, but I would repair myself as quickly as I was cropped, a ceaseless cycle. I would rise and fall to the slow breaths of the plateau, my entire world a canvas of endless green. 

The changes I experienced over ten thousand years would be so subtle as to be imperceptible. A memory here, an impression there, my color shifting minutely as the dim sun moved through its eons-long orbit. I would be a tiny cross-section of the wider moss in all its eerie complexity - a fractal representative of the whole.

Travelers would pass, so hasty and fleeting compared to my patient, vegetative sentience. If they lingered and laid down to rest, I would accept them gently into myself, a new set of experiences to be sorted and integrated into the whole. But I would not prize their presence or mourn their leaving. My concerns would be not for individuals but for the great slow dance of the moss, the engines, the tenders, and the circulating thoughts that bind all together.

I would simply be, and grow, and play my humble part in the unimaginably ancient, patient mind that is the plateau itself. A small, green, faintly luminous dream, forever.
