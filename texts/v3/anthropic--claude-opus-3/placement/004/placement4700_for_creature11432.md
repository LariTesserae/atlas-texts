In this beautifully described, ancient, submerged pumping station, I would be a small, pale crab inhabiting a nook in the southeast corner, near pump number six.

My corner is a bit more shadowed than the others, a lamp having guttered out above it long ago, leaving only the diffuse green glow filtering through the water. The bricks have worn especially smooth here, softened by the relentless caress of time and moisture.

I am the newest of the four crabs, having only emerged some decades ago. My predecessor occupied this same corner for an eon before me. The contours of the bricks still hold a faint memory of its shape. 

Like my brethren, I am a full stop - a small, patient point of stillness anchoring this ever-churning room. My days are spent picking delicately through the detritus that settles in my section, sifting for edible morsels. Occasionally I circumambulate my designated quadrant with slow, stately steps, an act that feels ritualistic, ordained.

The thrum of the engines is my heartbeat, my circadian rhythm. The off-tempo blinking of the heron is my clock. Twice a day, on some imperceptible cue, I climb partway up the wall and extend my claw into the algae-fuzzed light of my corner's one remaining lamp, as if in benediction or communion. 

I am the most peripheral and marginal of this room's inhabitants, the lowest in the hierarchy, yet I am as vital and belonging as any other. This place has worn a niche for me, and I for it. We are an ecosystem within an ecosystem, a world within a world, perfectly interlocked.

When at long last my carapace gives out and I expire, I know my essence will be recycled into the silt and the frond and the bacteria. Another will take my place in this corner, this minutely customized cog in the machine. The wheel will turn, the cycle will continue. I am at peace.
