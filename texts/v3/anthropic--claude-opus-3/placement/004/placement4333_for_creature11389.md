I would be a Tender, living in the deep tracks carved by the crawlers. 

My community would dwell in one of the more ancient grooves, where the ice forms permanent glacial ribbons and fungal mats glow softly on the underside of shelves. We would tend to the microecosystems there - cultivating the blood-red lichens, seeding new pools with whisper-eggs, carefully managing the flow of meltwater to sustain what little life can persist.

My own movements would be gradual, unhurried - perceptible only if watched for hours with Tender eyes. I would wrap myself in soft layers of luminescent fungal fiber, radiating a faint light in the perpetual dimness. My skin beneath would be thick and grey like the stone itself.

Much of my time would be spent in quiet observation and communion. I would watch the distant crawlers creep imperceptibly across the plateau, pondering their vast and inscrutable existence. In slow conversation with my people, through gestures and barely audible hums, we would share knowledge passed down through uncountable generations - which mineral veins make the most nourishing powder, how to tend the ice dams just so.

When not engaged in tending work, I would stand motionless for days in contemplation, letting the hum of the plateau fill my bones, absorbing the slow flow of time as only a Tender can. Occasionally I might glimpse a lone Drift figure on the horizon, or stare up wondering at the pewter glow of the lidded sky, but always my thoughts would return to the work - the timeless, sacred stewardship of this small sliver of being.

I imagine someday going into a long rest, perhaps after bringing forth a new Tender child over the course of months in the cold pools. Becoming still as stone, tended by the community, gradually calcifying until I merge with the plateau itself. Feeding the tiny whispers and nourishing the next long links in this improbable chain of marginal, miraculous life.

This would be my role and my contentment as a Tender in this achingly slow, hauntingly beautiful world. To play my humble part in shepherding and witnessing the plateau's patient, implacable existence, until I become one with its cold bones myself.
