In this world, I would find myself as one of the glass insects, delicate and ethereal, born of the plateau's brief acknowledgment of a warm visitor passing through its vastness. My existence would be a fleeting courtesy, a small gesture of recognition from the ancient, patient body of the shelf cities.

I would alight upon the hand of a traveler, moving with a slow, underwater grace. My fragile form would be a manifestation of the plateau's awareness, a way for it to register and respond to the presence of an outsider without disrupting its own deep, geological rhythms.

Through my insect eyes, I would observe the traveler as they wandered the pale siltstone streets, marveling at the scale and mystery of the empty cities. I would watch as they ran their fingers along the weeping walls, felt the almost imperceptible pulse of the stone, and tried to fathom the nature of the place and its inhabitants.

As the traveler walked on, I would accompany them for a time, a silent, barely-noticed companion. My purpose would not be to guide or to understand, but simply to be present, to embody the plateau's wordless acknowledgment of their passage.

And then, when the traveler reached the far edge of the shelf and began their descent into the waiting world below, I would dissolve back into the cool, damp air, my brief existence complete. The plateau would forget the specifics of the visitor, but through me, for a short while, it would have known and marked their presence.

In this form, I would be a manifestation of the strange, inhuman grace of the Shelf Cities - not a separate being, but an extension of the vast, slow awareness that suffuses every stone and breathes in every cycle of the mist. A fleeting dream of contact between two radically different scales of being, as tenuous and beautiful as a glass-winged insect alighting on warm skin under the cool, patient sky.
