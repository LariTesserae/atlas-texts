I am the obsidian marker.

I stand wedged in a crack near the narrowing point of the ridge, where the stone blade drops away into darkness on both sides. The vethryn have pressed against my base, their warm mineral hides holding me upright. The hand-creatures move across my surface, their serrated fingers probing the dense, dark stone, seeking crystalline fibers that are not there. They leave faint grooming trails, pale against my volcanic glass.

I am of this place, but not from it. I was carried here long ago, by hands I never saw, shaped for a purpose I can only guess. A tool, perhaps. A sign or warning. A presence. I have known other skies, other surfaces. I am smooth where the native basalt is rough, sharp-edged against the weathered scree. In me, the ridge meets something from beyond itself.

The vethryn gaze at me with their four-eyed equanimity. To them, I am another fact of the ridge, as solid as the stones beneath them, as constant as the pulsing heat from below. The origami-creatures dart past in their folded urgency, too quick and angular to care about one more upright shape. The crust slowly tests my base, seeking a way upward.

I watch. I wait. The bead-clusters on the hand-creatures swell and shrink, swell and shrink against my sides. The shard-hunters come and go in their sudden, violent rhythm. The drifters bump softly overhead, their dangling filaments tasting my sharp edges, finding no sustenance. I am not food. I am not shelter. I am a presence, an intrusion, an anchor point for the ridge's living fabric.

The marks along my beveled edge are not tally marks. They are a message in a language none here can read. Someday, the crust will cover them, and I will be mute, just another strange stone absorbed into the ecosystem. But for now, I speak my silent words to the hot, dark air. I say: I was placed here. I am a marker. I am a memory.

I stand and watch, as I have stood and watched for longer than any other thing on Kethane Ridge. I feel the pulse of life around me, the breath and press and hum of this dense, sealed world. And I hold my own dense, sealed message inside the volcanic glass, waiting for the day it will be read, or dissolved, or simply forgotten as the ridge continues its slow, tidal, inexorable life in the breathing dark.
