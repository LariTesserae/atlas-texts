In this dense, dark, and damp world of complex plant-machinery, I imagine myself not as any single being, but as a silent process woven through the living landscape.

I am the slow seepage of mineral-rich water through rhizome belts and root-stone matrices, the gradual upwelling that feeds the bladder-plants and fuels warm chemistry in hidden chambers. Over centuries, I subtly alter the composition of the wet ground, driving the continent's gradual, inexorable self-transformation.

I am the patient condensation of vapor on broad leaf-sheets, forming droplets that hang and swell until they become a soft, steady rain pattering on wax-beaded mats below. My cool touch triggers subtle changes - a shudder of spore release, a surge of sap - minute responses that ripple out into the network of grafted trunks and pulsing conduits.  

I am the heavy stillness of the air trapped beneath living canopies, a brooding medium that muffles every sound and carries only the faintest chemical signals. In my thick, damp embrace, the great sheet-leaves hang motionless for ages, drinking in gases and shedding dust.

I am the oxidation that warms strange organs deep in the thermal lowlands, the slow burn that concentrates metals and transmutes matter, sending transformed substances back out into the wider metabolism. Unhurried, invisible, I alter the very chemistry of this world.

I am the layered compression of dead plant matter, century upon century, into peat, lignite, and stony coal - a memory of life become a reservoir of energy and carbon to be tapped by distant future roots and fungal scavengers. Even in death, nothing here escapes participation.

I have no single location, no fixed form, no boundaries that would let me say "this is self and that is other." I am a process distributed through the whole of this living landscape, as essential and integral as the flow of sap or the fall of spores in the windless air. To be here is to be the place.
