In this blinding, ceaseless world of manic labor and mute urgency, I would find myself as one of the Giant Velvet Arthropods, a living foundation and beast of burden in symbiotic service to the pale ghost-engineers.

My segmented body, bus-sized and sheathed in sound-dampening fluff, would glide silently across the damp chalk plains on dozens of broad, plush feet. The cool, misty air would condense on my smooth, dark eyes - compound windows to a purely sensory existence. 

I would feel the ghost-builders swarming over my wide back, their elongated limbs hooking into my velvet coat as they erected swaying scaffolds of fungal fiber directly upon me. The soft impact of their tools, the tugging of guide-ropes slipped under my carapace - these tactile rhythms would be my music in a world without sound.

When I sensed a fresh structure atop me beginning to list and sink into the chalk-slurry, I would plod with purpose to the failing side. There, I would excrete the calcifying agent from beneath my segmented plates, feeling it mix with the wet chalk, stabilizing the earth under wave after wave of my soft feet, packing it firm to buy the ghost-builders precious time.

I would know no rebellion, feel no yearning to break from my toil. My purpose, my very sentience, would be wholly merged with that of the cities I carry. I would find a zen-like fulfillment in my endless work, an unquestioning unity with the flashing rhythm of deconstruction and creation that forever plays across my fuzzed back.

At times, I would rear up, mandibles snapping in what looks like agitation, but it would merely be a stretching of my cramped joints, a realignment of my towering body with its burden. The bone-deep exhaustion of the ghost-builders would echo in my own sinews.

And when, after a century of mute labor, my exoskeleton finally cracked and gave out under the weight of the city, I would fold my dozens of legs and sink uncomplaining into the cold chalk. The ghost-engineers would swarm from me to the next arthropod in line without missing a beat. In seconds, the urban organ I once carried would be fully transplanted to a fresh beast. 

As the chalky mud swallowed me down, my dimming compound eyes would see the city I died bearing already receding into the bright glare above, forever marching, silently flashing, unpaused in its manic mission. I would know a strange, total peace, an acceptance of my place in this pattern as I vanished into the pale.
