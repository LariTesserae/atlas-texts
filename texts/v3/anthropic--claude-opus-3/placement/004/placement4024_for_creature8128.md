My body would be lean and elongated, shaped by generations of life in this humid, resonant environment. My eardrums would bear the unique scars of the sounds I've experienced, each whorl and ridge a record of the harmonies and discords that have defined my life.

As an elder, my role would be to guide the younger generations, teaching them to interpret the subtle variations in pitch and timbre that signal safety or danger. My fingertips, sensitive from years of practice, would trace the vibrations in the walls, translating the stone's song into wisdom for my people.

In the quieter moments, I would work with the bronze-smiths, crafting tools that ring at frequencies designed to soothe rather than agitate the living rock. The handles of my hammers and chisels would be wrapped in layers of sound-absorbing plant matter, allowing me to work the metal without triggering the cisterns' resonance.

During ceremonies, I would lead my people into the depths, our bodies thrumming with the psychoactive effects of the spore-drink. Pressed against the singing stone, I would open myself to the mountain's voice, seeking to understand the geological rhythms that shape our lives.

My own skull, when I eventually pass, would be added to the community's collection—an instrument for future generations to learn from, my unique pattern of auditory scars becoming part of our collective memory.

I would move through this world with a constant awareness of sound, my steps muffled by plant-fiber wrappings, my voice rarely rising above a whisper. Yet even in silence, I would be communicating—with touch, with gesture, with the subtle manipulation of acoustic space through the placement of resonance baffles and chimes.

In the rare moments of equilibrium, I would trade with the outside world, offering the perfectly pitched instruments I've crafted in exchange for the necessities of life in this challenging environment. I would watch the traders' unease with a mix of amusement and pity, knowing they can never fully comprehend the world I inhabit.

And always, I would be listening—to the water's endless seep, to the stone's slow settling, to the echoes of my own footsteps in the chambers that birth and shape our culture. In this place where sound is both sustenance and threat, I would be a keeper not just of resonance, but of the delicate balance that allows life to persist in the space between bedrock and sky.
