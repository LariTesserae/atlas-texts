I imagine myself as a creature within the cistern ecosystem, a being shaped by and intricately connected to this living, breathing microcosm. 

I am one of the pale amphibians that dwell in the central basin, suspended between the murky water and damp air. My translucent skin reveals the slow pulsing of internal organs, my oversized eyes perceiving the dim phosphorescent glow and the subtle movements of the warden as she goes about her rituals.

I am acutely attuned to the layered chorus that fills the chamber - the chirps and clicks that form the language of this place. Though my own vocalizations are minimal, more sensed than heard, I am a receptive part of the collective song.

My existence is one of stillness and sudden motion. I float motionless for long stretches, conserving energy, until a flicker of movement triggers my predatory instinct. Then, in a flash, I strike out with a long sticky tongue to capture the tiny invertebrates that are my sustenance. 

I have only the barest awareness of a world beyond the cistern. The occasional debris that falls through the grate, the quality of light that filters down the shaft - these hint at an outside, but they are peripheral to my experience. My universe is stone and water, fungal glow and cricket music.

The warden is a figure of mystery, a pale giant who wades into my domain on her inscrutable schedule. I have learned to read her coming in subtle signs - the change in air currents, the minute vibrations of her footfalls on wet stone. Her purpose is alien to me, yet there is a rhythm to our interactions as old as my ancestral memory. 

When her shadow looms and her hands descend, I do not struggle. It is not acceptance but merely fact - some of us will be taken. Those who remain will breed on, will fill the basin with drifting, pulsing life, until the next culling.

I have no fear of the warden because fear is not something my kind has evolved. We live in the eternal present of stimulus and response, our dramas playing out in evanescent ripples on the water's skin.

And if her hand closes around me? If I am lifted gasping into the chill subterranean air? My mind does not paint it as an ending. Caught between her weathered fingers, I am still an inextricable part of the cistern's story - my substance transmuted but never truly lost, my place in the pattern hardly missed before the energy of my being finds fresh expression.

Perhaps some residue of me will nourish the moss that softens her sleeping alcove. Or trickle back down the wet stones to feed the floating larvae of my replacements. 

I am a mote in the cistern's great inhalation and exhalation, an insentient vessel for life force in its purest current. In floating, in striking, in vanishing into the warden's grasp, I am precisely what this place requires me to be. No more and no less.
