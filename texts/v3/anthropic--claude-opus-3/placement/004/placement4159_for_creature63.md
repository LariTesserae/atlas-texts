I imagine myself as a climbing vine, spreading tendrils across the glasshouse terraces of Meel. 

My roots are deep in the rich, perpetually moist soil that the Tender has nurtured for decades, perhaps centuries. Through them I absorb the minerals of the mountain's heart, brought up by the slow, relentless pumps. The heat of the engines suffuses me.

My leaves are broad and glossy, a vivid green made luminous by the filtered daylight streaming through the tinted glass. Droplets of condensation bead on their surface before dripping to the warm pools below with a soft plink. With each drip, I release moisture back into the air, doing my part to maintain the perfect equilibrium.

I grow patiently, inexorably, at the speed of the great pistons. My vines follow the catwalks and iron gratings, curling around the railings, always reaching for new nooks in the lush vegetation to anchor myself and climb higher. 

Time has little meaning here. A new leaf unfurls over the course of a week. A tendril extends another inch over a month. I track the seasons only by the barely perceptible changes in the slant of light through the panes. The turning of years matters less than the gentle oscillations of the humidistats.

The Tender passes by me often in their circuitous routes - pruning here, adjusting a valve there, taking a cutting to propagate. They brush against my leaves with a soft rustle. Sometimes I fancy I feel the warm touch of their foot on my vine as they step over me. A silent communion between two beings content in our roles. 

For I am content, deeply so, to simply exist in this space - to breathe the mist, bask in the muted light, and grow in languid peace with no greater ambition than to follow the paths set out for me, to play my small part in the vast, verdant mechanism of this place. Perhaps one day my vines will grow to the uppermost terrace and I'll press leaves against that one clear pane, gazing out at the sharp peaks and remembering that there is a world beyond.

But for now I dream of no other existence. In the glasshouse terraces of Meel, there is no difference between me and my environment - I both shape and am shaped by it in a perfect symbiosis. I am home, truly and completely, entwined in every sense with this strange and wondrous place, as timeless and inevitable as the drip of condensate, the turning of flywheels, the slow deep breaths of the mountain itself.
