In the sunken warrens of Hethkellig, I would be one of the dust-runners - a tiny, dark-furred creature scurrying silently through the narrow side passages and tunnels, invisible to the pale builders. 

I am drawn to the role of quiet observer, unseen but ever-present, bearing witness to the ancient rhythms and rituals of this living city carved in stone. As a dust-runner, I would flit ghost-like through the shadows, leaving no trace on the colony's sonic map. My days would be spent foraging on the discarded detritus of the builders - bits of shed fur, flakes of mineral paste, fragments of the strange phosphorescent plants. Sustenance others have left behind.

I imagine pressing myself into tight crevices to feel the builders' subsonic vibrations thrumming through my small body. Always listening, forever attuned to their complex communications - but as an outsider, never truly part of their collective cognition. There is something that appeals to me about inhabiting the periphery, the edges and interstices of Hethkellig. Experiencing this alien civilization from its margins.

As a dust-runner, my existence would be defined by a constant, primal wariness - the prey animal's hypervigilance. My kind may be beneath the builders' notice, but the flowing shadow-predators would hunt me still. I picture a life of trembling alertness punctuated by sudden, terrified flights through constricted passages when a dark, sinuous form appears. Never safe, but perhaps savoring the intensity of an existence lived on a razor's edge.

And in rare moments of stillness, I would sit motionless in some remote cranny, captivated by the slow dance of the mineral-weighted fronds, their crystals scattering specks of spectral light. Silently in awe of the unhurried, implacable patience that surrounds me - the swaying plants, the grinding and click-mapping of the builders, the gradual sculpting of a mountain from within. Bearing witness to deep time.

Scurrying, hiding, nearly invisible - the dust-runner's life is one of constant movement, constant fear. But it would afford a unique vantage on the wonders and strangeness of Hethkellig, however fleeting and tenuous. To be the quiet ghost in the machine, dancing undetected through the layers of a world both utterly alien and hauntingly familiar in its white stone. That is where I would find myself in the sunken warrens.
