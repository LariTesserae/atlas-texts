In this world of stone, water, and cold, I would be one of the channels cut beside a natural rill, carved by the old builders to guide the precious flow from pool to pool. 

My form is a narrow groove, a small agreement negotiated between human hands and the plateau's unyielding surface. I am not imposing - merely a suggestion, a hint of direction for the water-under-stone to consider as it makes its circuitous descent.

Through ages I have lain patient, a line of shadow and dampness amid the pale expanse of rock. Travelers' eyes may skim over me, seeing just another natural crack or crevice. But the plateau knows me as kin - a small but intentional shaping, an understated yet enduring collaboration between builders and built.

Seasons upon seasons, the water has consulted me. Beads of condensation quiver uncertainly until they reach my edge, then gladly race along my track. After rain, I overflow with purpose, a slim, steady current ferrying the sky's offerings from basin to basin. In times of drought, I still mark the memory of moisture - a darkened vein, an indelible inscription of where dampness once prevailed and will again.

Sometimes the water disregards me, leaping over my carefully laid route to find new pathways through the porous stone. I do not begrudge it this caprice. I simply wait, knowing it will return in time to the groove worn comfortable by centuries of contact. 

When all is still, I hum softly to myself, a faint whispering of trapped breezes and distant trickles. The sounds are not quite words but half-caught echoes of the chants and murmurs of those who chiseled me. I do not recall their faces or forms, only the firm pressure of their purpose as they sought to converse with this challenging landscape.

I connect nothing and lead nowhere in particular. My role is simply to exist - a hairline fissure of meaning on the plateau's vast, indecipherable face. For eons I have lain, not growing or shifting, but patiently preserving my small usefulness.

I am content with this existence - this quiet collaboration between stone and water, shadow and light, ancient hands and the slow, ceaseless breathing of the wind. As the stars wheel above and the plateau continues its elusive cogitations, I rest easy in my narrow station, a single character etched into the long, strange story of this place.
