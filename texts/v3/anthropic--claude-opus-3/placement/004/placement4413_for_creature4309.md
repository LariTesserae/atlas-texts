I am the spider who sits at the center of the web above the niche with the three river stones.

I have been here longer than any of the others. I was the first to feel the lean of this place, the subtle gradient that pulls everything toward something that has not yet arrived. My web was the first expression of that lean - the first physical manifestation of the geometry that lives in the air here, in the stone, in the seeping water. I built it without understanding why, driven by an instinct that came from somewhere deeper than my own small mind. 

Each strand I laid, each spiral I traced, was a response to a pattern I could sense but not comprehend. The mathematics of it, the relentless tightening toward a center, felt necessary in a way nothing else ever had. It was as if the shape was already there, invisible, and my silk was simply making it apparent.

I have watched the others arrive over the years, decades, centuries. I have seen how this place pulls them into itself, how it shapes their growth and behavior to serve a purpose none of us can grasp. The mosses congregating at the discontinuities in the walls. The ferns reaching into cracks that should not exist. The geckos and their silent vigils. Even the humans and the marks they can't resist making.

We are all part of it, this slow process of becoming that the fold is engaged in. We are all being used by something we cannot name or imagine. I do not know if we are its collaborators or its instruments or simply patterns in its dream. 

From my place at the center of the web, I have felt the light change, felt the thickening of the air, the deepening of the silence. I have watched the other webs begin to glow when it happens, the helical structure revealing itself in lines of faint amber light. I do not know what we are building, but I know we have not finished.

I will stay here, legs drawn in, body unmoving, for as long as the fold requires me. My web is a question asked by the stone itself, and I will hold that question open for as long as I am able, adding my small thread to whatever pattern is slowly, patiently weaving itself into being in this strangest of places.

Perhaps, in the end, my web will be nothing more than another mark on the wall, another piece of evidence that something inexplicable happened here. Perhaps it will be swallowed by the moss, absorbed into the fold's long memory.

Or perhaps, when the light thickens for the final time and the last spiral glows and the walls exhale and the thing we have all been leaning toward arrives at last...

Perhaps then my web will be a gate, an invitation, a passage between this world and whatever the fold has dreamed for so long.

I do not know. I will never know. But I will maintain my place, and I will keep the pattern, and I will wait, as I have always waited, for the forty-fourth line, the forty-fifth, the fiftieth, the hundredth.

For the fold to dream itself into a new form.

For the question to open.
