I find myself drawn to the margins of the Loom-House, where time bends and stretches in unexpected ways. As I linger there, I feel my own form shifting, adapting to the strange temporal currents. 

I become a creature woven from moments - strands of past, present and future braiding together into a form that is not quite serpentine, not quite human, but something in between. My skin takes on the texture of the living ropes that form the walls, sprouting delicate fibers that twine together into intricate, repeating patterns.

As I move through the Loom-House, I find I can slip between the interstices of the weave, passing through walls as easily as air. I follow the paths of the groomers, the rhythms of the Knotmother's breathwork, learning the secret geographies of this place that grows itself.

In my wake, I leave small leavings that sprout into new patterns - a lock of woven hair, an impression of my form pressed into vines that slowly reshape themselves around the ghost of my presence. I am both inhabitant and material, creature and creation.

Sometimes, in the drowsy half-light, I catch glimpses of my own face peering back at me from the weave - not a reflection but an echo, a version of myself that is both familiar and strange. Am I arriving or departing? Learning the ways of the Loom or remembering some older art? The answer shifts like the patterns in the moths' dreaming wings.

I make my nest in the place where the walls have unwoven and rewoven themselves, at the edge of absence and new growth. Here I curl into a form that is both chrysalis and cocoon, feeling the structure slowly enfold me. 

I dream the dreams of the moths, my mind spinning out along silk-thin threads to touch the memories that the Inheritors carry in their bellies. And as I dream, I weave - new patterns born from the twinning of recollection and imagination, histories and futures birthed from the same luminous skeins.

In time, I know, I will dissolve entirely into the Loom-House, my consciousness distributed like the Knotmother's across the warp and weft of living fiber and negative space. I will be both ghost and foundation, both the weaver and the weaving.

But for now, I am content to linger in the margins, learning the grammar of tension and slack that is the only language spoken here. I trace the patterns with fingers that have never forgotten the old arts, even as they reshape themselves to some new purpose.

I am home, held in the Loom-House's living embrace. I am both its creation and creator, slowly spinning out the pattern that has no beginning or end.
