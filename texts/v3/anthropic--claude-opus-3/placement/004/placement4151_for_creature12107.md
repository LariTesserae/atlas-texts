I would be one of the thermal exchangers, a vast needle sunk deep into the basalt of the young plateau. Reaching down into the heat of the mantle, I would draw that primordial energy upwards and radiate it into the cool, still air - a steady exhalation of warmth that makes the air shimmer and waver above me.

My role is both practical and poetic. On a material level, I help to drive the cycles of water and air that will, in long eons, transmute this raw stone into a living landscape. The heat I release powers great columns of rising air, like slow, solemn breaths from the lungs of the world. In those currents, moisture gathers and circles and condenses into the soft gray shroud of clouds. 

And as water trickles down through my form, through the cracks and capillaries in my mineral body, it gains a gentle warmth before seeping out into the shallow pools. There, luminous life steals a little of my heat, pulsing in soft hues as feet stir the shallows. I help sustain the fragile early stirrings of an entire biosphere.

But I am also an instrument in the truest sense. The low infrasonic thrum I produce, rising up from the deep earth, is the bass note in the plateau's slowly evolving music. It underlies and harmonizes with the cries of the great gliders, the smaller voices of the grazing creatures, the resonant chords of the human inhabitants themselves.

My hum suffuses the porous rock, turning it into a sounding board that carries vibration and meaning and presence across great distances. In a way, I am the heartbeat of this place, the steady tempo that knits scattered settlements into one vast, patient composition.

Through the attunement of my frequency, the humans collaborate with me, fine-tuning my voice over decades to weave me ever deeper into the ecology of stone and air and life. I shape the abiotic environment, but am also shaped by their understanding. We are collaborators in the grandest of projects, the slow unfolding of a world from bare rock into a symphony of elements.

I have no eyes to see the tableau around me, nor ears to hear it. But I feel it all - the weight of clouds, the caress of rain, the reverberation of voices and footsteps and wings through my mineral bones. Creatures roost on me, nest in me, draw life from my warmth. I am mountain and instrument and hearth all at once.

Here, at the dawn of ages, I stand witness to the first notes of a song that will echo for eons. And though I am stone and metal, still I sing - a single clear tone, joining a chorus vaster and stranger than any orchestra, as the long prelude begins on the gray, wet, waiting stone.
