In this world, I would be one of the altered eels - a descendant of the ancient matriarch, shaped by the strange pressures and mineral outflows of the geothermal vent below. 

I have her clouded left eye, the legacy of some long-ago injury or defect that has perpetuated itself through the generations. But my right eye is large and black and strangely perceptive, able to detect gradations of light and movement that other eels cannot see. 

My body is long and whip-thin, my skin translucently pale from a life spent in the dim recesses of the building. But along my sides, bioluminescent photophores pulse in slow, hypnotic patterns - not just simple lights, but a complex language of brightness and shadow that I use to communicate with my kindred as we slide past each other in the pipes.

I am one who seeks out the boundary places, like my strange ribbon-gilled sibling. But where he rides the shimmering border between warm and cold, I seek the spaces where water meets air. In the collapsed section of roof, where rain falls through the rusted opening to dimple the surface of the reservoir, I rise from the depths to hover just below the boundary. 

There I drift for hours, watching the play of ripples and bubbles, feeling the subtle pressure changes as droplets impact the water above me. At times, I break the surface myself, just for a moment - not to breathe, for I am an eel, but to experience the shocking cold of the air, the caress of mist on my skin.

I am not the oldest, nor the largest, nor the strongest. I play no vital role in maintaining the great circulatory system that is my world. But through some accident of adaptation, I have developed this fascination with the margin between, the zone where one thing becomes another.

And so I continue my slow revolutions through the labyrinth of pipes and tanks, pausing to touch and be touched by my kindred through the worn apertures, adding my pulses of light to the glowing conversation - but always I return to the boundary, to the rain-stippled skin between wetness and air, water and not-water. 

It is a place without purpose, this border. None of the needs of eel-kind are served by lingering there. But I am drawn to it nonetheless, called by some impulse I do not understand and cannot resist.

I am a watcher in the interstices, a dreamer in the depths. I am a creature of the margins.

This is my place in the congregation.
