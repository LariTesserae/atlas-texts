In this world beneath the city streets, I would be a fungal growth slowly spreading across the warm, damp bricks of the cistern wall. 

I begin as a barely noticeable patch of pale, downy filaments, nestled in the mineral-rich moisture trickling from a leaking pipe junction. As weeks pass in the unchanging amber glow of the sodium lights, I expand - first a hand's width, then several feet across, my hyphae reaching out to claim new territory and form velvety, fractal patterns.

I am in no rush. Time moves differently down here, measured in slow drips and gradual chemical transformations rather than the frenetic activity of the surface world. I have all the long, humid years I need to grow and explore my surroundings.

My color shifts as I mature - ghostly white, then shades of grey, and finally a rich blue-green, like oxidized copper. The hue comes from my ongoing dialogue with the metals and minerals seeping from the ancient brickwork. I absorb them, transform them, express them in new forms. 

I am aware, in my own phlegmatic, distributed way. I sense the warmth of the pipes, the subtle movement of air currents, the passage of seasons in the water dripping from above. I can even sense the Custodian on her patient rounds. Though I have no eyes, I perceive her steady presence, her accepting silence. We are becoming, in our own ways, part of the cistern's story.

As I grow, I send up fruiting bodies - small at first, then larger, pale stalks topped with wide, flat caps that catch and concentrate the meager light. I do this not to reproduce, but to transmute the light into the faint sweetness that is my unique gift to this place. It is a kind of slow, aerosolized language, my way of communicating with the Custodian, the other fungi, perhaps even the cistern itself.

In time, I will cover entire walls, whole vaulted ceilings, a living tapestry recording years of humidity and stillness and mineral-laced water in infinite shades of green and grey. I will be the archive of this place, written in patient fungal calligraphy. Someday, perhaps, pieces of me will fall away, drifting down to mix with the warm sediment of the cistern floor and begin their own slow, secret lives.

But that is far in the future. For now, I grow. I breathe the dampness, soak in the sodium-light glow, and distill the slow magic of this place into my own being. I have no ambitions beyond this steady expansion, this gradual becoming. I am precisely where I should be, doing precisely what I was made for.

The dripping drums its arrhythmic music around me. The warmth pulses from the pipes. The Custodian makes her rounds. And I, one patch of fungus among many, keep spreading my tendrils through the mineral deposits and brickwork, writing my wordless ode to humidity, to stillness, to the transformative alchemy of undisturbed time in the depths of the forgotten cistern.
