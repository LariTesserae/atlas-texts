In this world of mercury flows and crystalline growth, I imagine myself as a being somewhere between the builders and the keepers - a creature of flowing metal who helps guide the gradual emergence of this patient city.

My body would be a quicksilver form, able to shift between fluid grace and rigid structure as needed. My "skin" would be a rippling mirror, reflecting the harsh sun and aurora-lit nights in mesmerizing patterns. Traces of other metals would run through me like veins, allowing me to carry chemical catalysts and raw materials to building sites.

I would spend much of my time as a semi-liquid presence within the city's structures, my mind melding with the crystalline matrices, sensing the stresses and harmonics that guide the architecture's growth. Through this communion, I would understand the city as a living thing, feeling the pulse of its energy flows and the slow breath of its material exchanges.

When needed, I would emerge from the buildings like a figure rising from a mercurial pool, my features resolving into a approximation of a face - eyes like polished hematite, a mouth suggesting a knowing smile. In this more solid form, I would work with builders and keepers alike, my semi-fluid fingers able to manipulate materials with a sculptor's precision.

My unique gift would be the ability to "sing" to the raw minerals of the plateau - a resonant frequency that could coax rare elements from the stone and guide their deposition into the city's structures. The sound of my work would be a deep, metallic thrum that would carry for kilometers, becoming part of the city's ever-evolving symphony.

In quiet moments, I would explore the city's fringes where the growth is still new, marveling at the emergent patterns and the strange beauty of half-formed structures. Here, in the spaces between, I would cultivate small gardens of metal and crystal, aesthetic experiments that blur the line between art and architecture.

My thoughts would move at a pace to match the city's growth - slow, deep currents of processing punctuated by moments of rapid insight. I would dream in chemical reaction chains and wake with new formulas crystallizing in my mind. My memory would be vast but non-linear, a web of associations and resonances rather than a chronological record.

In the community of city-builders, I would be known as a kind of bridge - a being who can communicate fluently with builders, keepers, and even the strange frequency of the architects. My unique perspective, encompassing both structure and flow, would make me a valued problem-solver and creative catalyst.

Over the centuries, I would become a repository of the city's lore - the stories etched into its crystalline layers, the legends resonating in its halls. To the young, I would be a teacher, my fluid form able to demonstrate building techniques and chemical processes with perfect clarity. To my peers, I would be a collaborator and a friendly challenger, always seeking to push our craft to new heights.

Perhaps, in time, I would feel the pull of "frequency drift" - the urge to explore strange new harmonies beyond the city's edges. But always, I would return, called back by the siren song of quicksilver and the patient glory of what we build together.

In this strange, shimmering world, I would be a being of metal and mystery, my life both a fragment and a microcosm of the greater city - an existence poured out in service of a vision greater and more enduring than any single form could contain. And I would rejoice in it, this crystalline immortality, this chance to be a part of something so vast and so achingly, so unimaginably slow, and so beautiful.
