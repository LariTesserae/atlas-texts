In this hauntingly beautiful world, I would be one of the *kerathin*, born and raised on the moss-covered volcanic shelf clinging to the flank of the mountain. 

From a young age, I would learn the ways of my people - to move with deliberate slowness and speak in hushed whispers, so as not to disturb the ever-present moths that are the lifeblood of our settlement. My pale skin would be faintly freckled from the filtered mist-light and my dark hair would grow long, worn knotted with pins of polished bone.

As I grew older, I would discover my calling as a keeper of the moths. Through patient observation, I would learn to read the subtle signs in the stone water basins - which moth species visited in the night, which populations were thriving or struggling. In the great central plaza garden, I would tend to the plants that sustain the moth life cycles with a reverent dedication. 

Each day I would walk the plaza, making nearly imperceptible adjustments - a resting spot expanded, an ailing plant replaced - to maintain the delicate balance between the kerathin and the moths that they rely upon but do not control. The responsibility would weigh heavily but also fill me with purpose.

In quiet moments, my mind would turn to the deeper mysteries of our mountain realm. What is the nature of the witness that watches from the fern-shrouded cliffs above? Who built the ancient stone structures that the moths favor for their cocoons? These questions would arise without clear answers, but with the acceptance that some things are beyond knowing.

As the seasons turned and I grew older, I would take an increasing role in the life of the settlement. I would teach younger keepers the skills and lore passed down for generations. I would participate in the rituals and ceremonies that mark the rhythms of kerathin life - the weaving of a braid to bond with a mate, the offerings left for the witness, the return of the dead to the moth-silk cocoons. 

And always, I would hold tight to the kerathin dream - not of growth or conquest, but of continuity. To maintain the life we have, to pass on our traditions, and to hope that those who come after can do the same. A modest dream, but a worthy one, as the mountain breathes and the moths cast their shadow-dance in the eternal twilight of the mist.
