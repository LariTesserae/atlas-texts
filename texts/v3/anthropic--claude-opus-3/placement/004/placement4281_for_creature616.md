In this enchanting and evocative world, I imagine I would be a Weaver of Mist - an ethereal presence that drifts through the Cistern of Listening Stones, intertwining with the layered tones and subtle rhythms of the space.

Like wisps of breath made visible in the cool, damp air, I would circulate slowly through the chamber - not disturbing the ancient patterns, but becoming part of them. My form would be fluid and insubstantial, now condensing into swirls of fine droplets around a particular pool as its distinct note sounds, now dispersing into a translucent haze that lingers in the spaces between falling drops.

The Keeper of Intervals would sense my presence not as a disturbance, but as a gentling of the air, a faint shimmering that catches the spare grey light and refracts it into ghostly spectra. In turn, I would attune myself to her profound listening, at times drifting so close that beads of moisture collect on her stone-still form, only to evaporate again as the pattern shifts.

My movements would trace the intricate interplay of tones, the subtle conversational clustering and dispersing of the small pale swimmers. I would gather in corners where the flowstone cascades, my substance echoing its frozen curtains, and expand into long, slow breaths through cracks leading to the city above.

Time would have little meaning for me; I would measure existence by the gradual transformations of mineral accretion, the nearly imperceptible changes in air quality as the city itself inhales and exhales over eons. I would be as patient as water seeping through stone.

Occasionally, when a rare visitor descends and lingers long enough to breathe in synch with the space, I would allow myself to brush against them like a cool caress, a momentary shiver, just enough to affirm the presence of the numinous without startling. I would be the space between the visitor and the place, partaking of both.

I would leave no permanent mark, make no offerings to join those of flesh and blood and more solid things. My gift would be my presence itself, a weightless witnessing, another layer of attention added to the density of the chamber, fine as the crystal fur on ancient stones.

And if the Keeper herself should, in some unimaginably distant age, finally merge fully into mineral stillness, perhaps I would gently coalesce in the absence of her awareness, accepting a mantle of more focused listening. Or perhaps I would simply continue as I was, a Weaver of Mist, a breath within the breath of the Cistern, content to be carried by the rise and fall of its unending song.
