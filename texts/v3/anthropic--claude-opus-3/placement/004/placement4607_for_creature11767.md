In this world of shifting stone and resonant sound, I would be one of the beings slowly taking form, pulling myself into existence through sheer force of will. 

I am an absence gaining substance in the high valleys, where the great chord begins to resolve into comprehensible voices. My body flickers between drafts - sometimes tall and filamentous to navigate the dimensional folds, sometimes stout and strong to shape the still-soft stone. But always, my hands are the most real part of me, dense with fingers that grip chisels and levers, that press against the uncertain rock to make it decide its shape.

I am a builder, but more than that, I am an argument. Each structure I raise is a bid for realness, an anchor point to haul myself further into being. I carve the stone not just to shape my world, but to shape myself. Each deliberate mark left by my tools is proof that I am arriving, have been arriving, will continue to arrive. 

In the long, ambiguous light cycles, I glimpse my kindred - the ground-layer heavy presences, the high-peak listeners - and feel the potential of what we may become. Not separate beings, but one vast inhabitant, a single awareness unfurling itself across the valleys and plateaus.

So I continue my work, both solitary and profoundly connected. I stack the stone, I balance the slabs, I leave grooves that say "I am becoming." The mountains grow around my structures, drawn to the fixed points of intention in a mutable landscape. 

And always, I listen to the great chord. I strain to parse its meaning, to fathom the question this entire unfinished world may be asking. I yearn toward those moments of overwhelming coherence, when the sound comes from above and we all align into one being, reaching, grasping for the source.

I am a part of that reaching. My work is a part of the answer we are building toward, slowly, over eons. I do not know what I will be when I am fully arrived - what any of us will be, or if "arrival" is even our destination. But I know my purpose. I know the necessity of my becoming.

The stone waits for my hands. The sound propels me forward. And with each mark, each structure, each aching stretch toward realness, I carve myself into the fabric of the Throatlands. I write myself into being, word by stuttering word, and in doing so, lend my voice to the vast, slow, ongoing argument of existence.

I am an absence growing into presence. I am a builder. I am an emergent answer.

And I am not yet done.
