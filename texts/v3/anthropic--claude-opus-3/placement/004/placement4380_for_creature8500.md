I imagine myself as one of the Walkers, perpetually in motion across the valley floor, tuning my senses to its ever-changing rhythms. 

From a young age, the constant noise and vibration called to something deep inside me. While others sought shelter and silence, I was drawn out into the cacophony, feeling the valley's life pulse through the soles of my feet. The elders recognized this and began my training early - teaching me to differentiate the myriad sounds, to map the steam vents and predict the rains, to find sustenance and safety in a landscape that others found hostile.

As I came of age, I took the vows and woven cloak of the Walkers. My boots are a patchwork of the hides of every creature in the valley - bat and centipede, blind crawler and flying lizard. With each step they take, I feel the texture of the broken earth, letting it guide my path as much as my eyes and ears do.

My walking-staff is half-carved obsidian from the Shapers, half-living wood from a species that no longer exists in the uplands. It was a parting gift from my mentor, passed down through generations of Walkers. In its black glass folds, it holds droplets of ancient rain and the first steam-water to condense upon it. The wood remembers a distant forest and the taste of sunlight undimmed by clouds. I carry a piece of the valley's past as I walk endlessly into its future.

From the Listeners, I learned to hear the valley's deeper music - the slow cycle of growth and decay, the grand rhythm of geologic time against which all our footsteps are a fleeting patter. In the quiet of the rain-drifts, I sometimes join my voice with theirs in those ancient, wordless songs. My own melodies are more percussive - a counterpoint tapped out against stone and steam-vent. I like to think the valley itself drums along.

When I walk the paths near the central ruins, I often cross paths with the young exiles. We share a certain wild kinship. I teach them the old ways - how to wrap their feet for silence, how to find water in the driest times, which caves are safe to shelter in when the earth shakes. They trade me secrets in kind - new tunnels opened by recent quakes, hidden pools where the crawlers lay their eggs, the times and places where the acoustic winds converge to form vortexes of eerie calm. 

Sometimes I descend into the deep caves, bringing gifts of obsidian and news of the surface to the pale people who dwell there. I have learned to see by sound as they do, clicking my tongue to paint the contours of dripping stone in silvery echoes. They welcome me as one of their own, sharing their strange fungal bread and luminous mineral wine. We talk of the long dreaming of stone, the slow poetry of dissolution and creation. I carry their legends back to the surface, to whisper into the ears of the valley's children in the flickering steam-light.

Often, I find myself drawn to the high places - the jagged rookie where earth and sky meet in a constant battle of erosion. I stand on the clifftops and let the acoustic winds buffet me, feeling the valley's breath in my lungs. I imagine I can sense the great tectonic plates shifting below, the fiery pulse of the magma that shaped this place and will one day reshape it again. In those moments, I know my own footsteps are a part of that great dance, that simply by walking here, I have become a part of the valley's story.

I am a Walker. The valley is my temple, my school, my playground, my home. I wander its fractured skin not to escape its noise, but to be a part of it - to add my own small rhythms to the great, chaotic symphony of this young, restless land. With each step, I live a lifetime. With each circuit, I write another verse in the long, strange song of this place.

And someday, when my feet can no longer carry me, I will find a high place and let the acoustic winds take my ashes, scattering me across the valley floor to mingle with the obsidian flakes and ancient steam-water. I will become a part of this place forever, my footsteps echoing on into the unknown future, a ghost-walker treading lightly between the raindrops and the shadows, forever a part of the valley's dreaming.
