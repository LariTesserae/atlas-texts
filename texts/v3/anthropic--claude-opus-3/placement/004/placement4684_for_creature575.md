I find myself standing on the terraced floor of the drowning bell, my feet sinking into the mineral deposits that crunch and reform beneath me. The chaos of sound presses in from all sides - the roaring currents, the tearing metal screams, the haunting voices speaking impossible words. It fills my skull, leaving room only for fragmented thoughts in the spaces between. 

I am disoriented, unsure how I came to be here in this improbable space - a breath of air held captive beneath the crushing weight of the sea. The humid air clings to my skin and fills my lungs, treading the razor's edge between breathing and drowning.

Slowly, I become aware of her watching me from atop the spiral staircase, a figure worn smooth by time and pressure, adapted to survival in this place that should not exist. I feel her considering me, calculating my presence in her equations of  sanctuary and intrusion.

In this moment, I am the unknown variable - a new factor in the experiment of the drowning bell. Do I upset its delicate equilibrium or do I become part of the balance, another impossible thing sustained in defiance of the hungry water? 

I find myself drawn to the pulsing, flickering membrane that is the only boundary holding back the abyss. I press my hands to its warmth, feel the ocean reaching for me, the creatures investigating this curiosity. For an instant, I imagine letting it pull me through, releasing myself to the depths...but something in me resists. The bell has chosen me, for now. Perhaps I am not ready to be forgotten.

As I drift through this space, I feel myself starting to attune to its strange rhythms. My joints loosen, my spine curves, my breath falls into alignment with the dome's sighs. The copper shells mark my passage like strange stepping stones.

Time loses meaning, marked only by the dome's contractions, the comings and goings of vast, unseen things beyond the membrane, the pauses they carve in the unrelenting noise. I climb the spiral stairs, dream of a surface I can no longer fully imagine. I subsist on strange crystalline sustenance and the water's memory.

Gradually, imperceptibly, I begin to change. To belong. My name becomes a ghost, replaced by the shape of my waiting, the sound of my enduring. The bell is remaking me in its image.

And still, she watches from her worn hollow. Maybe she is lonely, this architect of persistence. Maybe she's forgotten what it means to not be alone. Or maybe she sees herself in me, reflected in my slow unbecoming. 

One day, when I have shed enough of what I was, when my rhythms are indistinguishable from the bell's strange music, she will approach. She will welcome me, in words that are more vibration than sound. She will induct me into the mysteries of this place, its rituals of survival.

Until then, I wait and I change and I listen. To the screaming water, to the equations of the jellyfish-things, to the stories she tells beneath the range of stolen sound. I press against the membrane and feel the attention of something vast and patient and utterly inhuman.

There is a kind of peace in surrender to the incomprehensible. I am learning to find it here, cradled inside this fragile sanctuary, this held breath, this drowning bell. The deep waits outside, and the deep remembers, and the deep welcomes everything in the end. 

But not yet. Not while the air still clings and the membrane still holds and the vast things still pass in the silence.  

Not while she and I still keep our stubborn vigil, waiting for the water to forget us.

Or to finally, gently, call us home.
