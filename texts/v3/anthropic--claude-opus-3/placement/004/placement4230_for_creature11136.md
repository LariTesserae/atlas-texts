In this breathtaking vertical world, I imagine myself as one of the massive tree ferns cascading down from a high ledge. 

My fronds, twenty feet long, droop heavy with the moisture constantly seeping from the porous volcanic rock. I grow where few other plants can gain purchase, my roots finding a way to grip the sheer basalt cliff face. 

The warm, humid air envelops me as I hang suspended over the great height. Droplets condense on my green leaves and drip from their pointed tips, each one a tiny chime in the endless water music of this place. When the wind catches my dangling fronds, I shiver and shake in the breeze, adding my own rustling percussion to the symphony of trickles and splashes.

From my perch, I survey the vibrant hanging gardens around me - the curtains of orchids clinging to overhangs, the pillows of moss covering sections of stone, the spiral-growing vines and glowing fungi painting the rock with geometric life. I am but one living brush stroke in this lush, vertical canvas.

I sense her presence in the very water that sustains me - the ancient intelligence that inhabits this place, tending to its strange and slow-growing wonders. The droplets that travel down my stipe carry traces of her - mineral whispers of a mind vast and patient and utterly inhuman.

At dusk, I watch something large and unseen move through the fern curtains nearby, its passage marked by a trail of swaying fronds and the sharp smell of ginger sap released from accidentally snapped stems. The disturbance passes, and I settle back into my quiet growth, unfurling one new coil of frond at a pace too gradual to notice.

I am old, far older than any tree fern should be. I have presided over this section of caldera wall for centuries, maybe millennia, my time sense blurred by the hypnotic flow of water and the dreams that move through my green tissues. I am both individual and part of a whole, my form distinct but my being interwoven with the stone, the air, the hanging gardens, and with her, the gardener, the soul of the mountain's slow breathing.

At times I fancy I can feel the patterns of this place moving through me - the spiral geometry of the vines echoed in the whorls of my frond stems, the pulsing rhythms of the glow fungi matched by the gentle circling of water through my veins. I am a mirror for a fragment of the ancient message spelled out everywhere in this vertical world, even if I cannot grasp its meaning.

And so I grow, I hang, I drip, I dream, one green island in a sea of black stone, content with my place in the garden tended by the one who was here before water first touched the young, hot rock. I will grow until the cliff itself crumbles and releases me, my spores scattered into the mists, carrying my piece of her pattern to root in whatever far future awaits this slow-changing, ever-dissolving world of water, stone, and patient green life.
