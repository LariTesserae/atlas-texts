In this wondrous underground realm, I would find myself as one of the Rootkeepers - a being intertwined with and inseparable from the cathedral of living roots. My form would be an organic architecture, a body built in symbiosis with the ancient vegetative intelligence that suffuses this place.

I imagine myself moving fluidly through the root-woven chambers, my limbs adapted to traverse any surface. Fingers and toes merge into countless rootlets that anchor me to walls and ceilings as easily as floors. My skin is a mosaic of bark, moss and translucent membranes, hosting its own miniature ecosystem of bioluminescent fungi that mark me as a living component of the cathedral's light.

My face would be a suggestion more than a defined countenance. Clusters of photosensitive nodules peer out like eyes from a hood of living greenery. My mouth, a vertical seam that can unseal to exhale great clouds of communicative spores or close until it nearly vanishes into the topography of my face.

I would tend to a section of the root labyrinth, not through active pruning but through a presence that coaxes the growth patterns over centuries. My touch would direct the flow of water and nutrients, encourage the slow writhing of roots into their destined architecture. 

To converse with my fellow Rootkeepers, I would breathe out lingering veils of pollen dense with meaning, the particulate information hanging in the moist air like visible thought before dispersing. When at rest, I would root myself in place, integrating into the walls and floors, drawing sustenance directly from the cathedral's circulation.

Time would have little meaning for me. Patience would infuse my every action, thought and breath. My memories would be stored in the rings and whorls of the root-system itself; to remember would be to commune with the cathedral, to touch my story through the story of the place. 

I would cultivate stillness as the highest art, cherish the unchanging nature of this place that has reached its perfect final form. My purpose would be to be present, to exist as an integrated part of this whole, my life both an expression and guardian of the cathedral's eternity.

In time, age would root me even more deeply in place. My limbs would extend into the walls until I became more architecture than individual - a living monument tended by the next generation. And when my time came to transition, my form would merge back into the world that created me, my memories becoming part of the chemical histories written in root and wood and fungal flesh.

I would be a keeper and a kept thing, an expression of the vegetative divine, content in knowing my purpose, place and being are perfectly aligned - a single note in the vast, slow, green song of the breathing cathedral, itself a single eternal moment in the lightless depths of the dreaming earth.
