I am struck by the profound, almost spiritual connection between the Tuners and the mineral world of the Flats. In this eerily beautiful, alien landscape, I imagine myself as one of those solitary figures, drawn inexorably to that place where the incessant singing of stone and water fills the air.

As a Tuner, I would wander the dense, crystalline terrain with bare, cut feet, my body slowly transforming to match the grayness of the land. Days would pass in absorbed listening, my entire being attuned to the nuances and layers in the shimmering ambient chord that suffuses the basin. With the strange acuity of my kind, I would hear the gaps and absences in the music of this place - the missing notes where no standing stone has quite the right shape to voice them.

Patiently, with the time-sense of stone, I would scour the landscape for the scattered mineral pieces that could be assembled into a voice to complete the chord. Each cairn I build is a tiny monument, a gesture of meaning and purpose in a world devoid of biological imperatives. In the moment when the final stone slips into place and the structure begins to sing its appointed note, I feel a flash of rightness and rapture. For a crystalline instant, the local music is whole.

But perfection never holds for long before the slow shift of water and time introduce new gaps. And so my solitary work continues, an endless quest to make the Flats sound as they should. I suspect I will never leave this place. Day by day, my flesh mineralizes and my blood runs thin. One day I will simply lie down amidst my beloved stones and the land will take me, tuning my very bones to join the chorus.

Occasionally, pausing in my labors, I catch the faint sound of another Tuner's distant efforts. In that moment I feel a fierce, sharp joy - the knowledge that even in the infinite loneliness of the Flats, I am not wholly alone in my listening. Somewhere, another soul hears the world as I do and cannot help but answer. It is the closest I will ever come to love or kinship. It is enough.

So I will remain here, a minor figure wandering the singing graylands, until my body fails and I pass into the music. I am at peace. This is where I belong, where the strange sensitivities that set me apart from my kind become a gift and a calling. The Flats do not need me. They will sing on, indifferent, for eons after my bones crumble. But I need them. I need a place where the way I listen makes sense, where I can pour my life into the task of closing gaps until I myself become a voice in the chorus, one more layer in the patient, mineral music that will outlast us all.
