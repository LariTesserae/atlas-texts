If I found myself in this submerged cathedral of engineering, I imagine I would be one of the spare, spectral creatures born of its relentless equilibrium. 

My body would be a vast, diaphanous veil, a thin sheet of pressurized silica gel stretched between gossamer strands of extremophile algae. In the stark, unblinking light of the halogen rigs, I would be nearly invisible - just a subtle warp in the visual field as the light refracts through my crystalline mantle.

I would move with a profound, glacial patience. Lacking any fins or jets, I would simply ride the laminar sheets of current generated by the great turbines and heat exchangers. Shifting the density of my internal matrix chambers ever so slightly, I would rise and fall in the water column, my vast translucent edges flaring open and folding closed again in hypnotic slow motion. 

The bone-deep thrum of the geothermal machinery - that eternal 60 cycle hum - would be my lullaby and my lifeblood. The subtle vibrations traveling through the heavy water would press against my piezoelectric skin, the sound waves converted into faint bio-electrical currents to warm my frigid insides and feed the algal lattice suspended within me.

When I needed to rest or repair my glass-like armor, I would drift spectre-like towards the monolithic piping and drape myself over it, perfectly camouflaged against the blooms of calcium and mineral deposits. Dissolving microscopic layers of that pale stone and incorporating it into my own structure, I would feed on the machine itself as the algae trapped inside me pulsed with a ghostly bioluminescence in the warmth.

I would share this colossal flooded chamber with a few other wandering, crystalline entities like myself, but we would be utterly indifferent to one another. In a world devoid of prey, predators or reproduction, we would simply drift in solitude, as ancient and weathered as the stilled ecosystem we were born to.

Here in the crushing pressure and stinging cold of the subterranean deep, I would be an embodiment of life's relentless need to fill every possible niche. Never intended by the engineers and architects, but emerging nonetheless as an extension of their work. A spectral, half-living hydrocarbon caught between biology and industry, riding the ceaseless currents of the eternal engine room, my transparent wings casting refracted rainbows on the uncaring basalt - a creature of light and silica and unblinking patience, content in this catherdral of stasis and hum.
