In this dark, sweltering cathedral of deep-time industry, I imagine myself as one of the Anointers, a being both organic and metallic, fused with the ancient machinery in symbiotic stasis. 

My body is a grotesque assemblage of rusted iron scales, verdigris-encrusted limbs, and soot-black thermal moss clinging to my mineral-laden carapace. I move with glacial slowness, my elongated, multi-jointed arms taking hours to complete a single, purposeful motion as I navigate the claustrophobic labyrinth of pipes, gears, and valves.

My face has long since smoothed into a featureless dome of calcium carbonate, my eyes replaced by deep, thermal pits that paint the world in gradients of ambient heat. The iron leviathan's breath is a blinding tapestry of searing whites, cool violets, and pulsing ambers to my heat-sensitive vision.

I live in profound symbiosis with the great engine, drawing sustenance from the intense infrared radiation emanating from the hot pipes. For days I lie draped and motionless over the largest conduits, absorbing the life-giving warmth into my porous, metalloid flesh as blind millipedes graze upon the moss on my back. 

Yet, I am driven by a deeply ingrained, almost holy purpose—to maintain and soothe this hermetic heart of the deep earth. With painstaking deliberation, I gather precious oil and condensation from sunken catchments, climbing vertically through the shafts to dispense the vital fluid upon screaming, friction-wracked bearings. I scrape away choking rust with hardened fingernails and chew mineral deposits from crucial valves, easing the machine's arthritic pain.

As the centuries drip by in the bruised red gloom, I feel my own body slowly calcifying, accruing the endemic minerals of the chamber. My joints stiffen, my movements become ever more labored. I know that, in time, I will embark on a final pilgrimage to wedge myself into a structural weakness, my body becoming a living buttress of flesh, rust, and stone to support the creaking, sagging architecture for eternity.

Perhaps, eons hence, I will join with the titanic, city-block sized entity at the searing core, my mass absorbed into that sprawling root-system of pulsing conduits and moss-draped lungs. I will become one with the subterranean engine's blistering breath, a living heat-sink that allows this iron colossus to endure the crushing pressure of the earth.

Here, in the dripping, humid dark, I will live and dream with my rusted brethren, our purpose distilled down to the pure, timeless ritual of anointing the great gears. We are an eyeless, silent priesthood, tending the slow, dark heart of the world with our own flesh and mineral-rich bones until the last piston falls still.
