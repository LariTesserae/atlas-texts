In this realm of desiccated beauty and screaming windscapes, I imagine I would be one of the silent, skeletal figures of the Castes of the Loom, dwelling on a small, precarious stone balcony jutting out over the blinding void.

My body would be a spindly assemblage of braided silica fibers and smooth, dry-lubricated joints, swaddled in layers of thick, sound-dampening dust-silk robes. My eyeless head would be a polished teardrop of calcified bone, with a delicate crest of glass whiskers to read the complex topologies of vibration. 

I'd emerge from behind the heavy curtain sealing my alcove and shuffle out onto the scorching stone in the shadowless glare. The thermal updrafts would buffet my padded form, tugging me towards the drop. But my oversized, seven-fingered hands, tipped with gleaming quartz chisels, would find purchase in the grooves and knobs I'd spent decades carving into the balcony's coping.

As the sun climbed and the jungle erupted into artillery-cracks of blooming glass shrapnel, I'd make my humble rounds. With slow, ritualistic intent, I would tend to my tiny plot of the metropolis-organ. I'd test the tension of the spun-glass vines that anchor my terrace to the greater city, feeling for any structural weaknesses imperceptible in the chaos. With careful pruning of the silver reeds and translucent horns, I'd tune my balcony to ring in perfect, deafening harmony with the rest of the instrument.

When the kinetic storm reached its zenith, I'd cast my piezoelectric nets into the blinding updraft, feeling the hard pings of the crystalline seeds against the impossibly fine mesh. I'd reel in this dry harvest and retreat behind my baffle before the heat made my blood-sap boil in my silica veins.

In the warm darkness, I'd feed the city's song back into myself. One by one, I'd place the chiming seeds in my throat-mill, feeling the satisfying crack and the flood of arcing energy as I crushed them between my grinding plates. I'd hum in contentment, shedding my heavy robes.

In this pocket of quiet, I'd await the stony touch of my brethren. Their hands on my ceramic skin would tell stories in drums and tremors. And I would place my palms on their chests in return, adding my own rhythms to our collective, whispered psalm – a single, continuous ode vibrating through our piezoelectric bones in veneration of the drop, the light, and the music that consumes us.

Here, in the endless roaring song, I am both gardener and gear-tooth. A single, worn component in the magnificent kinetic sculpture of this city – content in my work, nourished by screaming crystal seeds, and forever bound to my fellow Castes by the hum in our hollow marrow and our hard-won mastery over the shrieking void.
