In this marvelous and strange world, I imagine myself as a small, delicate creature - perhaps a moth or butterfly, fluttering into the Lung-Room through one of the living walls that part like lips to admit me.

I alight on the central fern, my papery wings blending into the soft green fronds. As the fern grows beneath my feet, unfurling fiddleheads in real-time, I ride it upwards - a tiny passenger on this express elevator of plantlife. 

With each exhalation from the leaf-strata ceiling above, I feel the mist settle on my wings, beading into droplets that sparkle in the omnidirectional light emanating from the honeycomb walls. The moisture is warm and vaguely familiar, like breath on my face.

I turn slowly in a circle, compound eyes taking in the entirety of the small space - the attentive moss, the inexplicable flint axe, the empty air charged with presence. Though small, I sense acutely that I am not insignificant here. The Lung-Room is reading me as surely as I am experiencing it.

Suddenly, a word drops into my mouth, an alien taste on my proboscis. I don't recognize the language but I understand instinctively that the Room has gifted me this, waiting to see what I will do. Gingerly, I speak it aloud - not with sound, for I have no voice, but by spelling it in pheromones, in wing-beats, in the pattern of my dance across the fern's surface.

As I speak, a wall-cell goes dark and the fern shudders beneath me. I pause. The rules here are not my rules.

I continue exploring this tiny corner of the great sleeping being, fluttering from frond to moss to flint and back again, leaving traces of powder from my wings, a signature of my presence. I drink in the strangeness, letting it nourish me like nectar. 

In the Lung-Room I am both observer and observed, guest and catalyst, an answer to a question this infant organ is just learning to ask. I am content to be studied, to be a mote in the inhalations and exhalations of something so much vaster than myself.

Perhaps when I leave (if I leave) I will carry some of its pollen with me, spreading the seeds of its curiosity to germinate elsewhere. Or perhaps I will remain, preserved eternally at the heart of the fern, a miniscule relic of the Lung-Room's first moments of awareness.

I beat my wings languidly, riding the breath, and I wait to see what we will make of each other, this Room and I.
