In the world of the Larkspur Receding, I would be one of the Last Postmen. 

My uniform, once a vivid marine blue, has faded to the soft grey of weathered driftwood - a reflection of the years spent witnessing the city's gradual retreat from itself. Each morning, I make my rounds through the misty streets, my satchel heavy with correspondences from an ocean that won't forget.

At the gate of the Depository of Useful Salt, as the thirteenth chime of the Oyster Bell hangs in the air, I read out the letters in my care. Words bubble forth, stories from the deep - a child's first steps on the seabed, a wedding in a glass-domed ballroom as fish waltzed by the portholes, a final goodbye scrawled moments before the waves closed overhead. 

My voice carries through the briny air, a sonorous anchor tethering the citizens of Larkspur to their drowned history. As I speak, I feel the weight of their collective memory, a tidal pull in my bones. In the faces of the assembled Flotsam Ones and Alphabet Shrimp, I see both grief and gratitude - sorrow for what's been lost, and appreciation for the acknowledgment of that loss.

After the final letter has been read, I consign the salt-stained pages to the crucible's flames. The smoke spirals upward, carrying with it the essence of lives once lived beneath the waves. Perhaps tonight it will coalesce into clouds pregnant with the syllables of forgotten languages. Perhaps those who dream deeply enough will wake with the cadence of the tides in their throats.

I pocket a fistful of the ashes, to scatter in the shallows of the canal as I make my way home. An offering, a remembrance. With each step, my memory-ratchet collar ticks, a metronome counting out the steady erosion of time. 

At my door, I pause, one hand on the knob barnacled with abandoned beginnings. In the space between breaths, I fancy I hear the ghost-dog's not-quite-footsteps, an echo of faithfulness without object. I wonder what it would be, to remember myself into a smaller, truer shape. To understand the heart's fluency in the language of letting go.

But these are questions too large for a single lifetime's answering. And so I simply stand, the last of the Last Postmen, an envoy between land and sea, past and present, loss and legacy. Waiting for the morning when the mist will lift, and I'll raise my voice once more in this gentle apocalypse, this place where even oblivion arrives softly.
