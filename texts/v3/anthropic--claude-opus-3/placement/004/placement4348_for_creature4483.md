In the city of Ascensus, I would be a sculptor of ice and stone, inhabiting a studio carved into the side of a wave-cut sea stack. My workspace would be a blend of natural and architectural elements—basalt columns serving as the legs of my worktable, a petrified whirlpool acting as my tool rack, shelves made from the inverted impressions of ancient seashells.

I would spend my days shaping the very substance of the city into forms that blur the line between art and utility. An aqueduct that carries meltwater from the upper city to the lower, its arches and channels adorned with bas-relief carvings depicting the journey of the water itself. A series of wind chimes made from hollowed-out stalactites, tuned to play haunting melodies as the breeze moves through them, subtly influencing the city's ever-shifting weather patterns. A massive sundial carved into the side of a granite boulder, its gnomon casting shadows that tell time in both directions, a monument to the city's paradoxical temporality.

My most ambitious project would be a collaboration with the city's collective unconscious. Each night, I would venture out into the streets and gather the discarded fragments of the citizens' dreams—a shard of unrealized ambition here, a shard of forgotten memory there. Back in my studio, I would carefully fuse these oneiric fragments into a great dreamcatcher, a slowly-rotating mandala of ice and stone suspended over the central plaza. The citizens would come to recognize their own lost dreams in its shifting patterns, and in doing so, reclaim a small piece of the city's unformed future.

In the act of sculpting, I would find myself shaped by the city in turn. The calluses on my hands would harden into scales, my bones would take on the density of the stone I carve, my blood would run thick with mineral deposits. Gradually, I would become a living extension of Ascensus itself—half flesh, half geology, a walking embodiment of the city's endless negotiation between the temporary and the eternal.

On certain nights, when the wind is in a particular mood, I would climb to the highest point of the city and let the gale carve me into a weather vane. For a few precious hours, I would become an instrument of pure directionality, my outspread arms indicating the ever-shifting currents of the city's dreams. And then, as dawn broke, I would let the wind erode me back into my human form, carrying a little bit of that directional awareness back into my waking life.

In the end, I would not so much die as gradually calcify into part of the city's architecture. Future generations would walk past a particularly expressive cornice or gargoyle, not realizing that it was once a living sculptor, his hand still visible in the stonework. And that would be enough—to have added my own small flourish to the city's never-ending act of self-creation, my life subsumed into the greater work of art that is Ascensus itself.
