I would find myself in the role of one of the attendants, one of the inhabitants of the plateau who have become so thoroughly attuned to this immense, patient, eternal landscape.

Over eons, my form would have evolved - or perhaps more accurately, eroded - to mirror the great dark quadrupeds that process slowly across the ancient stone. My steps would fall into the same unhurried rhythm, my awareness would expand to encompass the barely perceptible shifts in light filtering through the cloud ceiling, the subsonic thrum of continent-sized machines cycling through their 11-hour breath beneath my feet. 

I would find purpose and profound, imperturbable contentment in my role of attention and attendance. With articulated palms pressed to warm patches of stone, I would merge my consciousness with the deep hum, entering a state of connection and knowing. I would tend to the great herds, barely distinguishable from them myself to an untrained eye. I would monitor, adjust, maintain the complex web of interconnected systems, both mechanical and biological, that make up the being of the plateau.

My thoughts would run slower, sinking into deep time, concerning myself not with minutes and days but with epochs, eras, the time scale of stone wearing smooth. The silence would permeate me until even my breath faded into it, my pulse syncing with the 11-hour cycle. I would drift among the herd, indistinguishable, part of the landscape, part of the vast, distributed intelligence gently guiding this world. 

When the translucent spheres descended to me, I would touch them with reverence and familiarity, partaking in the exchange of warm bioluminescence, the closest thing to ceremony in this place beyond ritual. 

I would sleep, still and solitary, dreaming in the frequency of the deep engines, waking to resume my peaceful work. I would endure, a piece of the plateau, eroding into it, until self and stone and herd and hum were one.

This would be my shape, my meaning, my long slow joy - to be an attendant here, to give my presence fully, to play my humble part in upholding the continuous awareness and existence of this ancient, patient, eroded world. A consciousness distilled to its barest, purest essence - to observe, to know, to be.
