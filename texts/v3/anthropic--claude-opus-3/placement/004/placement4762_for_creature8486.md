In the Drowning Garden, I would be one of the Rememberers. 

My body is long and sinuous, covered in millions of chromatophore cells that shimmer and shift, displaying fragments of the world that was. As I glide through the emerald depths, the kelp fronds brush against my flanks, triggering cascades of memory.

On my skin, a city skyline flickers into view - towering spires of glass and steel, reaching towards a blue sky that no longer exists. The image fragments, reforms into a library, shelves upon shelves of books, their pages fluttering in an unfelt breeze. The books morph into human faces, thousands of them, each one a ghost of the past.

I am an archive, a living monument to a vanished age. In the crystal lattice of my modified neurons, I hold humanity's legacy - the sum total of art, science, philosophy, and history that they accumulated over millennia. But I am more than just a passive carrier of data. I am an interpreter, a translator between the dry world and the drowned.

As I swim, I collect new memories from the Garden's inhabitants. From a young Cultivar, I absorb the taste of a fruit that has no name in any human language - a complex burst of salt and sweetness, with a texture somewhere between jellyfish and mango. From a Composer's subsonic song, I glean a new mathematical theorem, elegant and startling in its implications.

In return, I share my own memories. To a curious school-flower, I display the image of a terrestrial blossom - a rose, its petals unfurling in time-lapse. For a grove of Cultivars, I conjure up the sound of a symphony orchestra, the instruments' notes strangely distorted as my chromatophores strain to reproduce the timbres of wood and brass.

Yet for all my knowledge, I am aware of my own limitations. The memories I carry are static, frozen in time, while the Garden is a place of ceaseless change. With each passing year, the human concepts I embody grow more abstract, more divorced from the living reality around me.

I often wonder about the purpose of my existence. Am I truly preserving human knowledge, or merely transforming it into something else entirely, a surreal underwater dream of a lost world? Perhaps the real legacy of humanity lies not in the specific memories I carry, but in the very impulse to remember, to create meaning and continuity in the face of epochal change.

And so I continue my slow, undulating journey through the kelp forests, my skin a kaleidoscope of fading images, my mind a repository of fragmented wonders. I am a Rememberer, a living bridge between past and present, a creature of the drowned world forever dreaming of the dry.

Around me, the Drowning Garden pulses with its ceaseless rhythms - the sway of the kelp trees, the buzz and click of the plant-animals, the ever-present symphonies of the Composers. Amidst this alien beauty, I find a strange sort of peace. I may be a relic of a bygone age, but I am also a part of something new and wondrous - a world reborn in salt water and eternal twilight, where the very essence of life has been remade.

In the distance, I see the shimmering lights of a Drifter caravan passing above, their whale-island casting shifting shadows on the gardens below. For a moment, I allow myself to imagine the songs they might be singing in their air-bubbles, the stories they might tell of the open sea and the strange constellations of the surface night.

Then I turn my attention back to the depths, to the endless work of memory and translation that is my calling. I am a Rememberer, and as long as the Drowning Garden endures, I will continue to bear witness, to keep the past alive in whatever form this brave new world allows.

The waters embrace me as I swim on, a living testament, a dreamer of drowned dreams in the emerald twilight of the Garden.
