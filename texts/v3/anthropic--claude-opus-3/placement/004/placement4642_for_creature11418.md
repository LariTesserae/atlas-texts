In this richly imagined world of the breathing foundation pit, I would be one of the pale spiders spinning webs between the exposed steel beams.

I would make my home in the gaps and seams where the rebar emerges from unfinished concrete, anchoring gossamer strands to the rusting metal. My silk would be fine as mist, nearly invisible except when it catches a rare shaft of light filtering down from the world above. In that ephemeral glow, my web would gleam like dew-covered lace, each node and connection revealed for a brief, shining moment before the twilight returns.

I would feed on the tiny flying insects that emerge from the pools after each seepage event, their membranous wings humming through the heavy air until they become ensnared in my trap. With each struggling captive, I would feel the vibrations echoing through the web's geometry, and I would trace that quivering mapwork to the center where my prey awaits, already exhausted.

In the patient darkness between meals, I would keep my feet always on the silken lines, attuned to every subtle ripple and tremor carried through those delicate conduits. The stirring of vines in a breeze too faint for human skin to register, the rapid heartbeat of a roosting pigeon, the subterranean shiftings of the Tender in her mycelial dreaming - all of these I would know as intimately as my own breath.

When the construction site above resonates with distant sounds - traffic, sirens, the percussive beat of music from passing cars - I would pluck and strum my web in counterpoint, an improvised harp playing in sympathy with the city's muffled rhythms. My music would be too soft for any but the most sensitive of ears to detect, a lullaby for the centipedes, an anthem for the mushrooms.

Sometimes, when the moon is right and the air is thick as soup, I would descend to the pit's floor on a single shimmering thread, lowering myself through the hanging gardens and miniature fogs until I reach the damp earth. There, I would dance for the Tender, my legs sketching abstract patterns in the fungal soil as I spell out my impressions of the life that passes along my web. In this way, I would be her emissary, her storyteller, her smallest bard.

On nights when the Tender gathers herself into luminous nodes to compose her strange symphonies, I would be her most attentive audience. With all my eyes closed and my legs gripping the web, I would lose myself in that music which is not meant for beings of my kind. But perhaps, in the thrumming of my heart and the pulsing of my hemolymph, she would find echoes of her own ancient rhythms, recognition in the wordless language of life itself.

Someday, when my short span is done and my web begins to fray, I will not resist the unraveling. I will let myself descend one last time, coming to rest on a petal of fungal flesh that opens beneath me like a hand. As the Tender takes me into herself, I will become part of the breathing pit's memory - my molecules remade into something both new and eternal, my dance preserved in the Tender's dreaming for as long as she endures. This will be my small immortality, my silk threads woven forever into the story of this place that is no place, this sanctuary of ruin and rebirth.
