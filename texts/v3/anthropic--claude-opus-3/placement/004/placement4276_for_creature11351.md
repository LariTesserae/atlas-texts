In this haunting, drowned world, I would be one of the bioluminescent barnacle-like colonies clinging to the warmest surfaces near the heat exchangers in the Bore Chamber. 

Not quite animal or plant, I exist as a pulsing mat of bacteria, my pale branching structures providing the only natural light in the murky depths. My eerie glow competes with the occasional sparks of dying electrical systems, casting wavering shadows through the turbid water.

Anchored to corroded metal, I feed on the waste heat bleeding from ancient machines - a fragile symbiosis in this techno-organic realm. The shuddering vibrations of the malfunctioning pumps pass through my calcium carbonate scaffolding with each thunderous cycle.

From my perch, I bear witness to the chamber's convulsions and the strange dances of its denizens. The elongated, translucent creatures - part fish, part something else - drift past, drawn to the electricity arcing between exposed wires. And everywhere, the ragged membranes of the chamber's dominant inhabitant undulate on the churning currents, digesting violence and debris as they morph into ever-stranger configurations.  

My kind are both observer and participant in this ecosystem of decay. Our photosynthetic glow nourishes the copper-veined fish creatures. Our mineral deposits provide building material for the constructor membranes' enigmatic structures. Gradually, imperceptibly, we remake our surroundings even as we are remade.

In the oppressive darkness beneath the weight of the drowned world, my living light is a fragile but stubborn beacon, an assertion of improbable life in the grinding depths. I cling, I luminesce, I endure the shuddering roar and twisting currents of this pocket of mechanical ruin. An ancient witness pulsing dimly against the enveloping blackness, life and light in tenuous balance at the margins of a world slowly devouring itself.
