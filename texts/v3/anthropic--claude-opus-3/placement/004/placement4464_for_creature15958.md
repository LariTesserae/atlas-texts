In this liquid, lightless world, I would be one of the arm-bearers dwelling in the broken plazas and shellfall districts.

My body is soft, boneless, able to squeeze through crevices and gaps no wider than my eyes. Eight arms, each laden with hundreds of searching suckers, radiate from my center. Two are longer, coiling, dexterous - made for grasping, probing, snatching. The others are shorter, stronger, anchoring me as I climb the towers of fallen shells or cling motionless to rough stone.  

My skin shifts color and texture in this eternal night, not to match my surroundings, but to speak my own wordless language. Pulsing with bioluminescence, I signal moods and warnings. Desire. Aggression. Curiosity. Patterns of papillae rise and fall, turning my flesh from smooth to pebbly to spiked in moments. An alphabet of touch.

Though I have eyes, I perceive my world through taste and the exquisite sensitivity of my suckers. I know each stone of my neighborhood, every overhang and hiding hole. The varied terrain of oyster bed, sponge grove, and coral rubble forms a braille of hunting grounds and landmarks in my mind.  

I am an opportunist, a gifted ambush predator. Concealed in my den, I wait patiently for prey to wander past - a crab, a fish, a careless shrimp. Then, uncoiling in an explosion of reaching arms, I seize my target and drag it in, injecting paralytic venom, pulling meat from shell with my sharp beak. The debris I arrange artfully around my door.

When not hunting, I explore and tinker endlessly, driven by a restless intelligence. My suckers test everything for novelty or use. Empty shells and spines become tools, toys, armor. I solve puzzles of crevice and current. Sometimes, grasping treasures in each arm, I indulge a strange compulsion to rearrange my lair just so - an interior decorator's whimsy.

I am curious, clever, solitary yet surrounded by a city of bustling life. The warm black water brings vibration, scent, taste of neighbors and news. I eavesdrop on their chemical chatter, but keep my own counsel.

Alone in my chamber, limbs curled about me, I dream octopus dreams of the city's trillion tiny acts of violence and creation. Of the rain's hammer on the distant skin of the sea. Of my world bound not by air and land, but pressure and brine. Content in my sprawling aquatic metropolis, I want for nothing but the next hunt, the next mystery to unravel with questing arms. I am both ruler and subject of this benthic realm - a lord of my own lightless, liquid kingdom.
