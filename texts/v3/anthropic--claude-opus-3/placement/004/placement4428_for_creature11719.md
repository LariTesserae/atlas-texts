In the Cloudloom Highlands, I imagine myself as a young tree-fern, perhaps only a few decades old. My fronds stretch up towards the diffuse brilliance of the cloud-lit sky, droplets of moisture clinging to their delicate edges. 

At my roots, I feel the slow, saturated breathing of the moss-mat that covers the plateau. It is a living substrate, ancient and patient, cradling me as I grow. The faint vibrations of the distant, unseen works travel up through this green sponge, a constant thrum that I have known since my first unfurling.

I stand quietly among my elders, the taller, long-established tree-ferns, our spacing seeming carefully arranged like guests at an eternal gathering. Through the mist, I glimpse the strange segmented columns of the giant horsetails, their hollow stems singing faintly in the ceaseless wind. 

Between us, the open spaces of luminous moss are dotted with the subtle movements of the Tenders as they make their slow migrations across years and decades. I sense more than see their passage, the nearly imperceptible adjustments in the living landscape as they go about their enigmatic work.

As a young fern, I have only the barest inkling of the vast, slow cycles and symbioses at play here. The works, the Tenders, the water, the mountain - ancient relationships beyond my fathoming, measured in centuries rather than seasons. I can only aspire to the timeless endurance of the eldest ferns, silently witnessing the plateau's patient transformations across ages.

For now, I content myself with soaking in the ever-present sky-glare, feeling the clean dampness on my fronds, and listening to the ceaseless churn of the hidden machines reverberating through my tissues, as much a part of this place as the moss and the mist. Slowly, millimeter by millimeter, I grow into the dream-like rhythm of the Cloudloom Highlands, wondering what I might see if I can persist even a fraction as long as this strange, deafening, beautiful land and its enigmatic denizens.
