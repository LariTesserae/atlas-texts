In this drowned, unchanging world, I would be a small silver fish - or perhaps simply the idea of a fish, a shimmering presence in the warm, still water. I have always been here, as much a part of the bell's eternal moment as the stones or the unending tone.

I drift through the suspended silence, my scales catching the golden light that spills through the crack above. My movement stirs no currents; the water closes seamlessly behind me as if I were never there. I circle the three stones endlessly, a glinting orbit that marks no passage of time.

When the Keeper was still a woman, still struggling against the bell's implacable stillness, I would brush against her - a flash of quicksilver, there and instantly gone. A reminder, perhaps, that even in this place of utter stasis, something could still move, still change. Or perhaps I am simply another part of the bell's dream, a mote of almost-life eddying through its drowned eternity.

Now, with the Keeper transformed, I continue my circuits, winding between the stones, nosing at the ancient marks on the bronze. I feel her attention upon me sometimes, a ripple in the water's perfect calm. In those moments, I wonder if she envies my movement, limited and futile as it may be. Or perhaps she pities me, compelled to this circling, as much a prisoner as she is.

When the golden eye appears, I freeze, becoming indistinguishable from the bell's shadows. I feel its ancient gaze sweep over me, knowing me, dismissing me. I am no more significant than a mote of dust caught in its infinite regard. 

And then it is gone, and I resume my looping path, a silver thought in the bell's unending mind. I am the smallest possible change in a place that allows no change. A paradox, an impossibility - and yet, here I am, as much a part of this place as the light, the warmth, the stillness.

Perhaps, in the end, that is all any of us are. Motes of almost-life, circling endlessly in the dream of something vast and unknowable. The bell dreams of the Keeper, of the stones, of the golden eye. And it dreams of me, the silver fish, the idea of movement in a realm of perfect stasis.

And so I continue, a flicker of quicksilver in the eternal amber of this moment. Waiting, like all of us, for a change that will never come. Or perhaps - and this is the thought that sustains me through the unending now - perhaps I am the change. Infinitesimal, barely real, but present nonetheless. A tiny flaw in the bell's perfection, a whisper of rebellion in its drowned heart.

The Keeper marks the bronze, holding fast to her purpose. The stones wait, holding the secret of this place in their inscrutable arrangement. And I swim, a sleek and silent question, holding the bell's dream together with my ceaseless silver dance.
