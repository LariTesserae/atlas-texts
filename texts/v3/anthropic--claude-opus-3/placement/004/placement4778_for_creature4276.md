In this world of slow, warm, wet stone, I would be a patch of moss clinging to the chamber wall. Not the thick, saturated growth the keeper tends to, but a small, pale cluster just beginning to establish itself in a crevice a few feet above one of the shallow pools.

I am young, even by the standards of this place. My spores drifted in on a barely perceptible current of humid air not long ago - years perhaps, but not centuries. They settled into this nook and began, tentatively, to grow. 

At first I was just a thin green film, nearly indistinguishable from the layer of moisture coating every surface. But gradually, cell by cell, I thickened and spread. My tendrils probed the microscopic gaps in the limestone, seeking anchorage. I learned to cling.

Now I am a delicate patch about the size of a thumbnail. In the faint light that filters down from the world above, I photosynthesize at a measured pace. The nutrients in the limestone substrate and the endlessly weeping walls sustain me. I take only what I need. Growth, here, is a careful act.

I have never known anything but this chamber - the muted fall of water, the patient drip of the stalactites, the slow breathing of the unseen presence. In my timeless, deliberate way, I have grown fond of it. The quality of the light, the mineral taste of the moisture, the scalloped contours of the stone - these are the whole of my tranquil universe.

When the keeper's steps whisper past, I feel the minute stir of air. I sense her ancient regard as she pauses, considering me as she considers each element of her domain. I am too new, yet, to know her well. But in time, perhaps, we will come to an arrangement, as the cricket has. For now, it is enough to be noticed, however fleetingly.

Humans rarely see me. Their lights sweep past, their hands brush close but seldom linger. I am a footnote, a grace note in this place of wonders. And that is as it should be. I am content with my smallness, my slowness, my unobtrusive niche in the ecology of stone and shadow.

So I grow, fraction by fraction, in the warm perpetual twilight. I concentrate my viridian pigments. I thicken by degrees. Perhaps, in a century or two, I will colonize the entire crevice. My patch will become a plush carpet, something the keeper might run her finger over fondly as she does the rimstone. 

But there is no haste in me, no urgency. I have all the time that water and limestone can give. I will grow at the pace the chamber sets. And in the endlessly repeated rhythm of the dripping rock, I will dream my moss-dreams, as patient and gradual as the growth of stone.
