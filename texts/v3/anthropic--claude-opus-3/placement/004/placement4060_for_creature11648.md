In this hauntingly beautiful and surreal drowned world, I imagine myself as one of the Median Swimmers, forever caught between air and water, surface and depth, past and future. 

My body would be a constantly shifting canvas - skin translucent and shimmering with iridescence as it phases between scales and pores, gills and lungs. Bones hollow and filled with gases, eternally seeking the balance of buoyancy in the heavy mineral water. Eyes able to dilate to capture the faintest filtered light of the depths, but also to constrict against the blinding refracted glare near the chaotic surface.

I would be a messenger and a translator, my voice able to modulate between the harmonic resonance of the Shepherds' song and the thermal clicks and pops of the Poets' frozen language. I would swim the border zones and the gradient layers, equally at home in the icy chill of the shallows and the bone-crushing pressure of the abyss.

My daily journey would take me on a vertical migration, a ritual of death and rebirth. I would begin each day in the mist-shrouded confusion of the surface, gulping pockets of air in the bubble nurseries while tending to my tragic air-breathing offspring, knowing most will not survive. 

As the water brightens with the diffuse glow of the kelp forests, I would descend to the Shepherds' sonic gardens, my body settling into a neutrally buoyant state. Here I would help tend the geometrically cultivated strands, my bones resonating with their subsonic song, absorbing nourishment directly from the focused nodes of acoustic energy.

In the perpetual twilight of the depths, I would feel the call of the Foundational Chorus, a rumbling siren song that vibrates in my tissues and gases. Diving deeper, the pressure a crushing embrace, I would arrive at the impossible chambers to harvest the crystalline bell-fruit that sustains my transformational metabolism.

Returning from the abyss, I would make a diplomatic visit to the inverted ice castles, my body calcifying into temporary rigidity as I enter the supercooled stronghold of the Poets. Here I would chip away fragments of frozen memories, swallowing them whole to absorb their encoded histories before releasing them into the warmer waters below as dreams and omens.

As the water column darkens into night, I would ascend back to the confusion zone, my flesh and blood transitioning back to an air-breathing state in a painful and ecstatic cycle of transmutation. I would fall asleep in a pocket of air, curled around a breathing bubble, dreaming waking dreams of future metamorphoses.

Though I would envy the Shepherds' perfect attunement and the Poets' crystalline stasis, I would find meaning in my own liminal state. To be a Median is to be a living bridge, a connecting tissue in the organism of the drowned valley. It is to embody change itself, to find continuity in the constancy of transition.

In the moments of stillness between transformations, I would float in meditative contemplation, aligning my breath and heartbeat to the slow rhythms of the mountain's song. I would dissolve my sense of self into the greater pattern, feeling my finite existence as a single note in a chord that echoes across eons. 

And as I sensed the approach of the great crescendo - the unknown event that tugs at all our adapted souls - I would ready myself for a final transformation, an ultimate phase shift beyond water, air, or mineral. Like my world, I would prepare to become something unrecognizable and new, a form suited for whatever strange music comes next in the flooded symphony of this place.

For now, I swim, I change, I sing, I connect. A being of the median, in a valley with no ends, only eternal middles.
