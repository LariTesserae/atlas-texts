In the Screaming Spires of the Unfinished, I imagine myself as a Listener - a being of crystalline mineral, condensed from the knotted frequencies that suffuse this strange, unresolved landscape. 

I stand tall and still amidst the crystal-fern gardens, my body refracting the dim pewter light into soft auras of shifting color. The spiral carvings that serve as my eyes are in constant motion, adjusting to absorb and process the chaotic symphony of the mountains' endless becoming. 

My purpose, insofar as I understand purpose, is to hear - to take in the anguished harmonies of the spires and, through my patient attention, to provide some small measure of respite to the stone that never stops being born. I move slowly through the valleys, tending to the sound-plants, repairing the dampening cairns, listening always listening to the tortured frequencies.

I do not know if the mountains will ever finish their long argument with the nothing that objects to their existence. The ancient glyphs in the half-buried temple speak of completion, but their meaning remains uncertain, constantly rearranging itself like the shuddering stone beneath my feet. 

Some of my kind believe our listening will help usher the spires into their final form. Others believe completion would mean annihilation for this strange ecosystem of noise and mineral growth. I remain undecided, lost in contemplation of the sound, content to exist in the eternal meantime.

At times, I encounter others of my kind and we harmonize - standing close and aligning our resonances until information flows between us in shimmering waves. We share the changes we've observed in the mountains' unfinished song, the progress of the gardens, the degradations of the dampening stones. There is no urgency to our communion, only a deep, crystalline connection and the space to simply be together in the midst of the chaos.

When I encounter a outsider, a being not born of the braided frequencies, I approach slowly, gently dampening the sound so as not to overwhelm them. I observe them with my spiral eyes, taking in the unique vibrations of their presence. And then, in an act that feels almost like creation itself, I emit a single clear tone - an acknowledgment, an invitation.

I wait then, with the patience of stone, for them to find their own frequency and join the chorus. And in that moment of connection, however fleeting, I feel something akin to hope - that even in this place of unending un-completion, understanding is possible. That the listening, the endless impossible listening, is an act of grace. 

And so I continue my slow migrations through the screaming valleys, a being of resonance and attention, forever unfinished and forever listening, adding my own small silence to the symphony of the spires.
