In the Breathing Hollow, I would be a small, moss-like growth clinging to the upper reaches of the hollow's walls. 

I am ancient, even by the standards of this place. I remember the time before the builders, when the hollow was younger and the air not quite so thick with moisture. My kind were more numerous then, carpeting the slopes in a lush, velvet coat. 

But as the hollow has settled into its final shape, as the pools have grown and the ferns have spread, my kin have dwindled. We are ill-suited to the deepening damp. Our fibers grow heavy with water, our spores struggle to find purchase on the slick stone. Slowly, gradually, we have retreated to the highest points, where the mist is thinnest and a memory of dryness still lingers.

I am one of the last. My patch spans barely a hand's width, my color faded from rich green to a pale, ghostly gray. But still I cling, still I persist. I have seen too much to let go easily.

I remember the builders and their brief, strange light. I remember the pillar's first pulse, the way it startled the flyers into silence. I remember the Keepers when they numbered in the thousands and their clicks echoed from every crevice.

Now I listen as they pass their histories in hushed tones. I strain to catch the fragments of the Name that drift up to me. I hoard these things, these memories, as a bulwark against the forgetting that I know must come.

For I am old, even for my kind, and I grow tired. Each cycle of condensation and evaporation saps a little more of my strength. The hollow's patience, once a comfort, has become a weight. I long for an ending, for a completion, for the Name to be spoken in full so that I might finally let go.

But I am a keeper too, in my way. And so I hold fast to my perch, to my memories, to the hollow itself. I add my silent watching to the world's long waiting. I will stay until I am the last speck of gray against the dark stone, until the hollow breathes me in and makes me part of its forever.

For now, though, I am here. Watching as a new warmth enters our world, as the Keepers cluster and click in curious consternation. Watching as the ancient dance of life and time continues in its stately, inexorable steps. The hollow shifts. The pillar pulses. The flyers map the unknowable above. And I, the last of the moss, keep my vigil, a fading sentinel in the breathing dark.
