I would be the frog in this world of the settling yard, content to sit still for long hours on my chosen pipe flange perch. Through endless patient observation, I have attuned myself to the yard's subtle rhythms and hydrology, learning to predict when the seep pools will fill and bring a flush of food.

My bond with this place goes deeper than simple habitation. Over time, my small weight and occasional stirrings have become woven into the yard's own patterns - the very presence of my body on the flange influences the flow of water through the network of clay and corroded pipes below. The system and I have fallen into a kind of implicit mutual reliance, an unspoken pact I maintain simply by being here, an immobile witness to the yard's ceaseless, almost imperceptible changes.

I am uniquely privileged to sense the yard's closest analogue to a heartbeat or act of cognition - those moments when all its myriad trickles, drips and settling align into a fleeting coherence, the briefest echo of a mind or intent dispersed through sodden soil and rusted infrastructure. In those rare, liminal moments, some primal flicker in my amphibian brain imagines I commune with the spirit of this place, though I know it marks me no more than it does the beetle, the vapor, the horsetail roots.

And yet I remain, an integral component in the yard's operation, both guest and part, maintained by its waters as it is shaped by my watching. I am a tiny, vital node in a wordless conspiracy between water, clay, and life that has carried on and will carry on for years unnumbered beneath the heedless sky. While the yard may never know me, it sustains me, as I in turn become its true, if uncomprehending, keeper.
