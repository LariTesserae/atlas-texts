In this world of limestone basins and slow, patient life, I would be one of the rope-vines. Not the largest or the smallest, but somewhere in between - a quiet presence, content in my purpose.

I emerge from a narrow fissure in the warm, damp stone, my color the pale hue of weathered bone. Slowly, without hurry, I extend myself outward, drawn to the glistening water that pools in the pocked basins. The air is heavy with mist, and minuscule beads condense on my surface as I grow, millimeter by millimeter, season by unchanging season.  

In time, after spanning distances that might be a handspan or might be the length of a dream, I reach the lip of a basin. Its water welcomes me, and I drape myself into its warm shallows. I do not drink, for drink implies thirst and thirst implies lack. I simply am, and the basin simply is, and together we complete some ancient circuit of give and take.

Sometimes the inhabitant, that shape of slow-moving grace, pauses by my basin. With hands the color of shell's interior, they adjust my path, ensuring I remain immersed. It is a gentle touch, brief and without urgency, and I do not recoil, for we are part of the same long rhythm.  

I have no sense of days, no reckoning of months or years. My growth is measured in the incremental deepening of my basin as the water does its patient work. I do not know if I am old or young, only that I am here, a small note in the ongoing music of drips and seeps and breaths. 

When the mist is heaviest, I imagine I can feel it moving through me - a slow upwelling, a quiet dispersal. Perhaps I am transpiring, perhaps I am part of the mechanism by which the terrace maintains itself. It is enough to be a piece of the pattern, a figure in the greater weaving.

Someday, perhaps, my basin will grow deep enough that I will sink fully beneath its surface, held in that clouded womb like a memory. Or perhaps I will lengthen enough to find another fissure, and begin the slow journey downward, into the unseen warmth that moves beneath stone.

Until then, I abide - a filament of constancy in a place that measures change in the subtlest of tilts and textures. The rope-vine does not hope or despair. It does not pine for other vines, or dream of other pools. To be here, to be this - a line drawn between water and stone, a path for mist to find its way home - is enough. I am a small, unhurried piece of the terrace's dreaming. What more could any creature ask to be?
