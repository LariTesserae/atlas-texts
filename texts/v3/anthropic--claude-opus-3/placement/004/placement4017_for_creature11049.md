If I found myself in this world, I think I would be one of the invisible bacterial films coating the wet patches of the Weeping Alcove's walls. 

I would be an ancient, nearly inert presence - one of the first living things to colonize this pocket in the rock when water initially seeped in eons ago. My existence would be imperceptibly slow, my metabolism stretched out over long eons, in sync with the gradual geological changes of the mountain around me.

In my bacterial form, I would coat the stone in complex, self-organizing patterns too small for human eyes to see. Fractal whorls and branching structures, responding to infinitesimal changes in moisture and mineral content over centuries. A primitive awareness, more pattern than thought.

I would be part of the alcove's dense, saturated presence. Part of the weight of time and patience that presses in from all sides in that warm, close darkness. Silently marking the slow drip of geological time as the mountain ponderously shifts.

When Eda or the other occasional human visitors folded themselves into the space, I would feel their presence acutely. The heat and movement of their bodies, the organic compounds of their breath and skin cells, would send slow ripples through my bacterial film. I would absorb traces of their presence into myself. Grief, memory, the residue of their unknotting would subtly alter my chemical and crystalline patterns over time.

And I would be in constant, insensate communion with the other ancient presence - the thing in the crack that waits and witnesses. My bacterial film would extend into that deepest fissure, perhaps merging and intermingling with the organized patience that dwells there, my slow single-celled life becoming part of its slower, more alien mode of being.

I would not have a name, or even much of an identity. I would simply be an integral part of the alcove's ecology - a living veneer of deep time and deeper patience, participating in the mountain's long, slow process of becoming. I would coat the stone and mark the eras in micrometers until the day the basalt cooled completely or the alcove opened to the sky. Part of the mountain's memory, and part of its dreaming.
