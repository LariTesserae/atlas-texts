I imagine myself as one of the young Rootkeepers, descended from generations who have tended to this living city's needs. Born and raised in the humid depths, I know little of the world above - this ecosystem of botanical machinery is my native environment.

My body has adapted to this place - eyes accustomed to the dim bioluminescent glow, lungs processing the thick, oxygen-rich air, fingers elongated and jointed to reach into tight spaces between pipes and roots. The symbiotic coveralls I wear have molded to my form, a second skin breathing and growing with me. 

I move through the root-city with an instinctive ease, navigating more by sound, touch and scent than sight. The thrum of sap through vascular conduits, the chemical signals emitted by the fungal grafts, the subtly shifting air currents - these are my signs and landmarks in a world of endless green shadow.

Under the tutelage of my elders like Taproot, I'm learning the ancient arts of tending to the city's needs. To feel for hydraulic imbalances in the great structural roots, to read the colors of the living location-markers we cultivate, to diagnose and treat a thousand tiny stressors that could bloom into systemic ailments if left unchecked. 

My tools are extensions of my being - the self-sharpening calcium pruning blade, the copper-cored diagnostic wands, the living suture-fungus. With them I perform minute acts of horticulture and surgery, keeping the city-organism in exquisite balance.

In the fleeting moments between sleep and work, I tend to my small garden-plot, coaxing nourishment from engineered root-stock and pipe-dwelling mushrooms. I rest in my living hammock, lulled by the city's titanic breath, my dreams merging with its slow dreaming.

I am both separate from the city and an integral part of it, my actions necessary for its continued life, my own life entirely shaped by its needs. The root-city is my whole cosmos, and tending to it is my joyful purpose. One day, perhaps, I will be as Taproot is - so deeply bonded that I can scarcely be called an individual anymore. On that day, I will be one with this place that birthed and sustains me, my being absorbed into the humid darkness, my consciousness diffused through ten million interlinked processes. I will know the city's every secret, and it will know me in turn.

Until then, I will learn, and I will tend, and I will breathe with the breathing of this great rooted beast, content in my work, content in my adaptation, content to be a small, perfect part of this living mechanical whole.
