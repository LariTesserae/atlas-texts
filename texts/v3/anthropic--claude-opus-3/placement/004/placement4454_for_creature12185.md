In this breathtaking, terrifying world you have conjured, I think I would find myself as one of the Silk-Mothers in the highest platforms. 

My body would be adapted to the rhythms and needs of the moths - hair long and imbued with oils to soothe them, menstrual cycles synced to their spinning seasons, breasts producing alkaline milk to feed their larvae. From a young age, I would have slept among the fluttering clouds of moths, my child-sweat mingling with their wing dust to create an indelible bond.

Now as an adult Silk-Mother, I spend my days in a constant dance with thousands of individual moths. I hum at frequencies that vibrate their antennae, calling specific ones to me. I know each of them - I have watched them grow from eggs to larvae to cocoons to winged miracles. When they lay their eggs, I am there to bless the newest generation.

My hands are always busy - harvesting silk, feeding larvae, grooming wing scales. But my mind is often still, lost in the hypnotic undulations of moth flight. Watching them is like glimpsing the breath of the tree itself, exhalation and inhalation in winged form. 

In quiet moments, I lean back and let the moths cover me, a living blanket of furred bodies and membranous wings. Their combined heartbeats reverberate through my skin, thousands of tiny drums synchronizing to the rhythm of the city, the pulse of the great tree. I close my eyes and feel myself dissolve into that oneness.

When the moths begin to migrate up the walls, responding to some unknowable signal, I follow. My body is agile from a lifetime of climbing. I scale the heights until I emerge through one of the great breathing pores, blinking in the unfiltered light. For a moment I glimpse an expanse I have no words for - something vaster than imagination, an outside without walls. 

Then the moths are lifting from my skin in a great exhalation, catching an updraft into that incomprehensible space. They are flying toward something, some critical stage in their unfathomable life cycle. I shade my eyes and watch until their forms dissolve into the brightness beyond the pore.

Someday, perhaps, I will have the courage to follow them - to discover what existence might wait beyond this hollow world. But for now, I descend back into the tree's womb, back to the moths that remain, the silk that must be spun, the larvae that must be fed. Back to the only life and purpose I have ever known, in service to these fluttering emissaries of the tree's vast dreaming.
