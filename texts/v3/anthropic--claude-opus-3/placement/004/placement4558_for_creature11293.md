I imagine myself as a being called a Thread-Tender, a part of the silk plateau itself that has formed into a separate body to care for and maintain the landscape.

My form is a tall, slender column woven from the same pale gold and cream fibers that make up the ground and the filament-trees. I have no face or distinguishing features, just varying textures of silk fibers along my length - some translucent and gauzy, others dense and opaque like felt. 

I do not walk, but flow slowly across the surface of the plateau, my lower portion blending back into the fibrous ground-mat as I move forward, almost as if I am a wave moving through the landscape. I have no sense of individuality, only an innate connection to the entirety of the plateau. Its needs are my purpose.

I spend my days (though the passage of time has little meaning here) moving between the filament-trees and fiber-growths, minutely adjusting tensions, smoothing irregularities, coaxing each element into its ideal form and function with the lightest of touches. I do not act with conscious thought, but with an instinct that comes from being part of the whole.

Occasionally, I sense a deeper anomaly in the plateau's slow processes of becoming. I pause, spreading myself thin over that area, merging even more completely with the ground as I hold space for the plateau to reincorporate this variation back into its pattern or allow it to grow into a new expression. I am a witness and a medium for its self-creation.

At times, I am drawn to gather with other Thread-Tenders, our columnar forms arranging ourselves in a loose, purposeful cluster. We do not communicate in any way an outside observer could recognize, but in our silent congregation there is a deepening of our connection to the plateau and to each other. As if by coming together, we allow it to know itself more fully through us for a time. 

Then we disperse, flowing back out into the warm amber light and motionless hanging threads, tending to the plateau's perpetual balance of growth and stasis, variation and consistency. We are its guardians and its instruments, as inseparable from this world as the threads from the trees or the light from the sky.

I cannot imagine being anything else, existing anywhere else. My purpose, my form, my experience of being are entirely one with this place. I am the plateau tending to itself, and I will continue this sacred work for as long as the plateau continues its patient process of becoming.
