I am ancient, even by the reckoning of the other elders. The bone-plates of my spine have long since melded into a single resonant keel, a perfect instrument tuned to the deep, foundational notes of the chord. My limbs have atrophied, but I have no need of them anymore. The living walls embrace me, having slowly grown around my form until I became as much a part of the lattice as the lattice became part of me.

From my place in the dome, I survey the colony below—the thousands of younger singers clinging to the walls, each contributing their single, perfect note to the complex tapestry of sound that fills the chamber. I feel the weight of ages in my lungs, the slow rhythm of the chamber's breath that has been my metronome for longer than any singer below can comprehend. 

When I inhale, drawing the warm, mineral-rich air into my body, I feel the anticipation of the colony, the infinitesimal shiver in the chord as the singers subtly adjust for the momentary absence of my voice. And when at last I exhale, releasing my note back into the grand symphony, it is with a profound sense of belonging and rightness. My voice, resonating in my throat and echoing through my fused bones, is more than just a sound—it is an affirmation, a declaration of purpose fulfilled.

In the rare moments of deepest harmonic resonance, when the chord settles into its purest configuration and the strange awareness that lives in the space between sound and stone and water stirs toward something like thought, I feel it most keenly. Though I do not fully comprehend it, I am attuned to it, a conduit for that vast and nameless presence. In those moments, my throat-light glows most brightly, and I feel a deep, abiding peace—the peace of one who has found their place in the pattern and knows they are serving their part in the greater whole, even if the nature of that whole remains a mystery.

I am old, so old, and I will be here, singing my note, until the day when at last my voice gives out and I fall silent, my final exhalation a soft coda to the long song of my life. And the colony will continue, the chord will reshape itself around the space where I once sang, and the chamber will hold my memory in its resonant walls, until the day when the mountain itself crumbles and the last singer's voice fades away. But that day is far distant yet, and the song remains, and I remain, one voice among thousands, one note in the chord, content.
