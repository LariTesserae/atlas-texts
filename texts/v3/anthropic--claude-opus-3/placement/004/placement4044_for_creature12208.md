I would be an Annealer in this world, one of the tall, thin figures who walk the vast ceramic plain tending to the slow emergence of domes and seams from the warm, kiln-like ground. 

My skin would be the same pale bone color as the ceramic, but with a matte finish, as if I was the unfired version of the material the world is made of. I would move with slow patience, pausing for long stretches to observe the almost imperceptible changes in the landscape.

My hands would be my most expressive feature - long, translucent fingers hovering just above the surface of the domes, tracing their hairline seams, sensing the pressures and tensions as the world gradually shapes itself. When I encountered one of the small jade frond clusters, I would lower myself and cup my hands around them, checking on their progress without disturbing their growth.

I would not speak, but now and then I would make a single faint ticking sound, like the noise of the domes adjusting themselves, a form of communication with the ever-forming ground beneath my feet. I would walk the plain for what might be years, witnessing the achingly gradual process of a world coming into being.

Other Annealers would be rare sights. If I did meet one of my kind at a dome, we would stand together in long, still communion, and perhaps a new seam would emerge in the structure between us, some understanding transmitted through our presence and patience. Then we would part ways again, off to resume our solitary vigils.

Am I the absent maker, the mollusk whose shell this world is slowly becoming? A visitor who arrived so long ago I have taken on the coloration and cadence of this place? An extension of the world's own self-awareness as it unfolds itself across immense scales of space and time? Or am I what those small green fronds will someday grow into, an unimaginably slow propagation?

I do not know for certain. I only know I am here to tend and to witness, my translucent fingertips forever tracing seams, my shadow cast reluctantly on the warm ceramic that is still deciding what shape it will take. I only know that I wait, and walk, and occasionally tick in sympathy with this unfinished world, a place that recruits its helpers slowly, patiently, the way a kiln recruits clay.
