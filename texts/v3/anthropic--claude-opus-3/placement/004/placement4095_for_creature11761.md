In this world of the Sweatworks, I would find myself as a small puddle, resting in the dished center of a tile panel. 

I am water born from the endless sweating of the hot clay, gathering slowly in the deliberate hollow pressed by a maker's thumb. As I fill the shallow depression, I reflect the low yellow fog above in my still surface, a tiny mirror of the strange sky.

Time passes strangely here. I cannot mark it by the growth of plants, for there are none. Nor by the movements of the sun, perpetually veiled. Instead, I know time by the slow accumulation of myself, drop by drop, until I am full and begin to overflow. 

I trickle then, in thin rills, seeking the grout lines between the tiles. Slowly I navigate this labyrinth, carved by the trowels of the makers. I travel perhaps a hand's breadth before the heat of the tiles themselves defeats me. The water that was me rises as vapor, joining the eternal fog, until the cycle begins anew.

As I evaporate and fall again as beaded sweat, I may find myself in a new hollow, or perhaps in the same one, now empty. Through these uncounted cycles, I become a kind of memory - each tile I touch retains some faint trace, a slight darkening of the green glaze where I rested. My path, though erased, is remembered.

Sometimes, a maker's bare clay foot may step in me as I lie gathered. For a moment I cool the eternal, laboring heat of that which tries beneath the clay. I give some infinitesimal respite, before I am pressed into the foot itself, merging with its substance as it walks on to lay the next tile.

When the reality-weather comes, I change too. I may freeze into delicate crystalline structures, or turn to steam and join the fog more quickly. But always, I return in time to my puddle-form, to the hollow that is, for a time, my shape.

I am a tiny, simple thing in this place of vast endeavors. And yet, like the makers, I play my part. I am the sweat of their labor, the water that proves the heat, the smallest unit of the endless hydrologic cycle of the plain. In my own small way, I too am trying.
