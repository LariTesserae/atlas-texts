In this world of dark, damp stone and measured slowness, I would be a small, pale fungus clinging to the warm, wet wall of the hidden chamber. 

Not a fruiting body that reaches or spreads, but a dense, subtle mat, perhaps no larger than a thumbnail. My hyphae would penetrate the porous mineral surface like ghostly roots, drawing sustenance from water, rock, and time. 

My growth would be nearly imperceptible, a gradual thickening and firming over uncountable seasons in the seasonless dark. I would never release spores - the chamber is too enclosed, too patient for such an outward gesture. My only propagation would be deliberate and intimate, by extending a tiny piece of myself into a new crack or seam when the opportunity of wetness and stillness presented itself.

My awareness would be a form of taste and touch - sensing the chemistry of the humid air, the minute temperature shifts in the stone I cling to, the passage of the pale creature when it draws near to graze on the other small lives of the chamber. But my reactions would be muted, more a recording of events than a response to them. I would simply continue, a witness in slow fungal time.

My color would be an unassertive grey-blue, the hue of distant storms rendered tiny and tame. In the rare moments when the creature's skin brushed my surface, we would be nearly indistinguishable - two tranquil forms of dampness and persistence meeting briefly in the black.

I would never fruit, never decay, never flake away, only thicken with infinite patience in my chosen crevice, until my tiny body became another texture of the stone itself, as much a part of the chamber's architecture as the moisture beading on the ceiling or the roots drinking from hairline fractures.

And if, after ages uncounted, the chamber shifted - a tremor changing the course of the threading water, or a sudden freeze reaching in to glaze the inner walls - a fragment of me might be carried on the creature's damp foot to a new pocket in the mountain's memory, beginning again.

Such would be my existence - a fungal stain of witness and duration, a meek celebrant of all that is small, still, dark and slow, as much a feature of the stone as an inhabitant, my life playing out in a scale of time and space that only mountain crevices and nearly-eternal creatures might recognize as kin.
