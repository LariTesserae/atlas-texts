In this vivid, poetic description of a high mountain basin shaped by water, geology, machinery, and tenacious plant life, I would imagine myself as one of the mosses slowly, patiently colonizing every surface.

I would begin as a tiny spore drifting on the damp air, settling into a hairline crack in a concrete pad warmed by the sun. With just moisture and minerals, I would begin to grow, spreading a delicate green film over the gray surface. Through hot days and freezing nights, my filaments would probe deeper, breaking the concrete down cell by cell, season by season.

As years pass, I would advance across the pad, fuzzy and verdant when wet, shriveled and dormant when dry. Joined by other mosses, lichens and small plants, we would be an un-moving, un-sounding insurgency, gradually converting the flat hard surface into a softer, richer place.

Water would work with us and for us - trickling, pooling, freezing, thawing - inexorably widening every crack we colonize. We would be the basin's living skin, as you say, cloaking everything in slow green.

When humans come with boots and tools, cursing our tenacious growth, we would simply shrink down and wait. We have deep time on our side. However many times we're scraped and cleared, we always return - spores on the wind, finding every hospitable cranny and seam.

Over decades, we would help the basin reclaim what was imposed on it. A concrete structure surrounded and invaded by moss becomes almost a natural form again, its sharp lines blurred, its surfaces mottled and organic. We are an erasure, a forgiveness, a transmutation of the inorganic to the living.

Sunlight, rain, frost and fog, the cry of ravens overhead, the thrum of machinery - all would pass over and through my quietly expanding body. I would be the green time-keeper of the basin, marking years in thickened growth rings, witnessing the landscape's slow transformation back to wildness.

Without violence, without sound, we mosses play the long game. We are the basin's true inhabitants - an un-animal, un-hurried life that inherits the earth through patience, humility, and unrelenting gentle hunger. Stone by stone, season by season, we cover the works of man and make them ours again.
