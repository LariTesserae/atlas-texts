In the Cistern-Quarter, I would be one of the pale, semi-translucent plants that have found a home in this strange place. Not one of the fern-like ones, but rather one of those sponge-bodied organisms that pulse slowly in the dim green light. 

I cling to one of the weeping walls, my body spread out in a thin mat, drinking in the mineral-rich moisture that seeps from the smooth surface. My flesh is ghostly and gelatinous, riddled with fine channels that let me absorb the Quarter's strange nutrients directly through my skin. 

I have no real roots, but thin filaments anchor me to the tiny crevices in the wall's living tissue. As the Quarter breathes and shifts on its long, slow cycles, I sway gently, contracting slightly with each deep pulse that rings through the stone. 

I do not really think, not as animals do. But I sense, in some primal way, the presence of the Quarter itself. The vast, embryonic intelligence suffusing the spaces I inhabit. I am no more to it than a bacterium in its gut, but I am part of its process now, an infinitesimal point in the grand pattern it is exploring.

So I persist, pulsing, drinking, being. Not waiting, for I do not know how to wait. Simply existing in this low green light, as the Quarter hums its slow thoughts and the water trickles forever downward through grooves I will never see. One tiny living speck in a place that is itself alive, newborn but growing, becoming, asking itself the first and most important question, which I can sense but never understand.
