In the Carved Hollows of Threnod, I would be a Bone Chanter - one of the artists who create musical instruments from the remains of the honored dead. 

My workshop would lie partway up the city's levels, where the endless seep and drip of water is a constant metronome, and wan shafts of surface light occasionally pierce the blue-green phosphorescent glow. Here, in a chamber carved smooth by generations, I would receive the bones of the recently deceased, lovingly cleaned and etched with their life stories by the families.

With reverent hands, I would select the bones most suited for music - the long flutes of femurs, the resonant drums of skulls, the plucked strings of ribs. Each instrument an echo of the life it once supported. 

My own bones would bear the marks of my craft - fingers callused from carving, lungs deepened by playing, ears attuned to the subtlest variations in sound as it moves through honeycomb stone. I would dress in the colors of mourning and memory - deep blacks dyed with carbon from the lowest caves, pale whites of new-dried bone.

The bereaved would come to me with grief and I would give them back music. A daughter's ulna becomes a flute that sings with her playful spirit. A father's skull-drum beat with his steady strength. Their songs join the symphony of the city - melodies of memory winding through the market squares and great halls.

On certain ceremonial days, I would join my fellow Chanters in the Remembrance Walls. Surrounded by thousands of instrumentalized ancestors, we would play the Threnod Requiem - a piece that has grown with every generation, absorbing new riffs and motifs with each set of bones added to the walls. The living and the dead, the stone and the song, merging into a single reverberating history.

In this way, I would be a bridge - between loss and remembrance, sorrow and celebration, the forever-damp and the forever-dark. With each instrument carved and tuned, with each note coaxed from marrow and mineral, I would affirm the truth etched into every tunnel of the city:

That the Threnodies endure, not in spite of death and darkness, but through them. That our songs sound sweetest when played on the bones of our beloveds. That even the Drum's unending roar cannot drown out the music of a life well-lived and well-remembered.

So I would live and work, a Bone Chanter of the Carved Hollows. Helping my people sing their sorrow and their strength, in this strange, beautiful, deathless dark we call home.
