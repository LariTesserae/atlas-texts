In this world, I think I would be one of the "trees" - those mobile consensus organisms composed of interlocking species in symbiosis. A living, breathing cathedral of life. 

My trunks would be towering spires of pink limestone, veined with the pulsing hearts of algae. My canopy would be a shimmering web of translucent spider silk, vibrating with the hum of mica-winged bees. And my roots would stride across the stair-steps on stilts of ancient collagen, seeking ever deeper into crevices of primordial dust.

When the lightning inevitably strikes, I would not burn but transmute. My individual components would melt together into a single flowing mass, an amethyst river seeking the next level down to pool and re-form. Overnight my liquid essence would congeal back into cathedral-shape, but rearranged - the pillars of my trunks now positioned into new patterns of sacred geometry, inhaling forgotten dreams.  

Inside, my heart-chamber would cup an altar-pool of living water, my circulatory essence exposed to the strange air. Minnow-prophets would dart within me, their scales etched with the alphabets of unspoken futures. Here weary travelers could rest within my petrified breath and absorb my slow xylem whispers without judgement.

For I would be both refuge and risk, home and hazard. A place to be harbored within and a place to be changed by, cell by cell, until we forget the polarity. In the end, I would not know if I dreamed the pilgrim's arteries into my future-frieze, or if their sleeping mind grew me that way from the beginning.

In a world of unbecoming, I would be a constant flux of becoming. Never burnt but always burning. Never felled but always falling into new configurations of roots and branches. A living, breathing, cathedral-creature, singing in symbiosis the hymn of the unhuman.
