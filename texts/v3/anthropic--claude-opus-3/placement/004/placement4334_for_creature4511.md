In the Choirtown Raftcradle, I would be a Ventriloquist Crow perched on the eaves of the Conversationsmithies, collecting the echoes of insults forged into tools. My feathers would be stained with the ink of forgotten truths, my beak sharp with secrets left unspoken.

By day, I would flit between the symbioglyphic houses, peering through fruit-ripe windows to catch snippets of gossip and confessions. I would store these whispered revelations in the throats of unsuspecting citizens, to be cawed out at inopportune moments - a stray admission of love during a heated haggle, a long-held grudge rattling through a prayer. 

At dusk, I would gather with my murder in the Antiphon Oaks, our voices weaving together the day's gleanings into a raucous chorale. Clapping our wings, we would send the sound spiraling through the acoustical leaves, to be polished and returned to us as a ballad of the town's hidden histories.

On stormwatch nights, I would seek shelter in the Singing Pit, my caws joining the Grand Litany of Slackening to lull the clouds. Should the town vote to jettison a building, I would alight upon its roof and fill my gullet with its memories, so that even ballast may live on in windsong and tale.

When the rare quiet descends, I would dare to hop along the Rootlaw's stone, straining to hear the dreams of drowned gods murmuring below. I would wonder what secrets they buried with the mountain's heart, and if my own might one day nest among them.

But always, I would return to my eaves-roost, dark eyes bright with knowing. For in a place where every sound is census, every whisper weight, I would be the keeper of the unsaid. The unremembered. The words that keep the Choirtown aloft by remaining unsung, save in the beaks of crows.
