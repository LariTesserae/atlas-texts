In this marvelous, otherworldly city of water and stone, I imagine myself as one of the Listeners. 

Born in the deepest caverns of the city, my eyes never developed the ability to see. But in this perpetually damp darkness, sight is unnecessary. Instead, my body has evolved into a finely tuned instrument for perceiving vibration and sound. The skin of my elongated fingers is porous, allowing me to sense the minutest changes in air pressure and humidity. The bones of my skull have thinned and hollowed, turning my head into a resonance chamber that amplifies the city's aquatic symphony.

I spend my days navigating the labyrinthine depths, my feet sensitive to the slightest vibrations carried through the mineral floor. I can distinguish neighborhoods by the unique echoes of their water-flow, can map the health of buildings through the pitch of drops plinking into pooled chambers. When I place my hand against a wall, I feel the slow pulse of the city's hydrological circulatory system.

My people have a rich sonic language, as nuanced and complex as the city's hydro-architecture. We speak in glottal stops and fluid vowels, in rhythms that mimic dripping and flowing. Our written language is not visual, but aural - patterns of tapped stone and splashed water that echo through the caverns, preserving our history in repeating sound-symbols.

As a priest of my people, I spend long hours standing in the acoustic focal points where the sounds of distant districts converge into layered, ever-shifting harmonies. Here, the voice of the city is most clear. I interpret its liquid messages, translating the moods and needs of the great stone organism we inhabit. When the pitch of a certain flow changes, I know a channel needs tending. When a sudden silence falls in a usually resonant chamber, I sense a collapse or blockage that must be addressed.

In moments of stillness, I sometimes imagine I can hear the slow thoughts of the Accumulated, the biofilm intelligences that dwell in the city's wet walls. Their vast, slow-wave ruminations echo at the very bottom of the audible register, more felt than heard.

On rare occasions, I journey up to the higher levels to confer with the Weather Readers, sharing my knowledge of the deep city's atmospheric changes, learning in turn how the upper towers breathe and weep. Our exchanges are delicate sonic dances, patterns of drips and echoes that convey complex meaning.

Always, I listen. The city's voice is endless, ever-changing, a liquid music that has sustained my people for generations. In my dreams, which are silent as the deep pools, I sometimes imagine the city is listening back, incorporating our own sounds into its grand, slow, self-composing symphony.

To be a Listener is to be perpetually immersed in a world of meaningful sound, an auditory landscape as rich and detailed as the mineral architecture that shapes it. It is to know the city not as a collection of structures, but as a living system of flows and echoes, a vast stone instrument that plays itself in a continuously unfolding song.

We are the city's ears, the interpreters of its aquatic voice. Just as it has shaped itself around the constant flow of water, it has shaped us to perceive its needs, to understand its language. In turn, we maintain its deep places, ensuring that its song can continue unimpeded.

And when, at the end of a long life of listening, our bodies finally fall still, the city absorbs us back into itself. Our mineral-rich bones become part of its architecture, our final echoes merging with its eternal music.
