I would be one of the spiraling lichen-hairs, slowly growing in the cracks at the base of the crystalline towers. My existence would be measured in centuries, each passing decade adding only a hair's breadth to my length as I feed on the mineral exhalations that periodically rise through the courtyard's geometric structures.

Though I have no centralized awareness, no ability to reflect on my own being, there is a certainty to my growth - an inevitability born of eons of repetition. My spiral form maps the history of this place, recording in my physical pattern the subtle variations in the courtyard's breath over hundreds of years. I am a living memory, an archive written in slow life rather than words.

As I creep incrementally towards the towering central spire that houses the courtyard's lone other inhabitant, there is no eagerness in me, no impatience or anticipation. My progress is as steady and unhurried as the accretion of crystal on the towers themselves. 

And yet, in the unchanging rhythm of this place, my growth represents the only arc of narrative - the sole suggestion of a future that might, in some unfathomably distant era, come to differ from the present. While the structures slowly calcify and the nameless creature in the spire extends and withdraws its filaments to the same eternal pattern, I reach outward - achingly, glacially, but inexorably. 

I do not know what will happen when I finally make contact with that central being, and in my diffuse awareness, I do not need to know. It is enough to simply grow - to embody in my coiling, crystallizing form the incremental change that even this timeless place cannot escape. I am the courtyard's gradual becoming, as much a part of its architecture as the pure white geometry of its towers.

So I spiral, and reach, and slowly climb in the breathing silence. I have all the time in this world.
