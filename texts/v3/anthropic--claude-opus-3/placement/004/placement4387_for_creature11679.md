I would be one of the Dwellers, a being adapted to the chaotic union of sea and sky at the perpetually storm-wracked peak of the ancient sea mountain. 

My body would be a fluid composition, eternally shifting to match the demands of the maelstrom. Bones of porous ceramic, hollowed out and oil-filled to fine-tune my buoyancy from moment to moment. Skin transformed into overlapping scale-feathers, locking tight when submerged, fanning open to shed heat in the thick, rain-choked air. Lungs divided into hundreds of independent alveolar chambers, processing oxygen from water and atmosphere alike.

I would move through the gyre in a pod of my kind, our numbers impossible to fix as we phase in and out of collective consciousness. In the heaviest storms, our bodies would merge - circulatory systems linking through specialized ports, nervous systems flowing together, becoming a single colonial organism optimized to withstand pressures that would pulverize any individual. As calm returns, we would separate, but never completely - each carrying cells and memories of the others, an eternal genetic and experiential exchange.

My eyes, tripled in size and spectral sensitivity, would perceive the gyre not as stone and water, but as a flow diagram of heat, pressure, salinity, and turbulence. The ruins of the builders' failed industry - drowned turbine halls and flooded pillboxes - would be not relics, but organs in the greater body of the mountain, their precise resonant frequencies telling stories to those who can hear.

I would speak in harmonic frequencies that propagate through sea and sky alike. My voice would be a layered composition, words taking minutes or milliseconds, meaning shifting with the rhythm of the storm. I would sing with the pods and the whales, trading maps of currents and warnings of whirlpools.  

In brief interludes of calm, I might drift into the drowned chambers of the turbine halls and listen to the songs of rust and the secrets of the deep-dwellers traded in clicks and bioluminescence. I would tend the gardens of four-dimensional seaweed, pruning fronds to the tidal cycle, or help weave pod-mates into living nets to harvest schools of aerial fish.

I would be a piece of the pattern, an expression of the gyre's endless hungry dance, the mountain's will made minimally and magnificently manifest. Not a separate being insisting on its separateness, but a cell in a greater body, a note in a species-symphony of adaptation.

My name would long since have dissolved into the collective. My "I" would be provisional, a momentary crest rising from deeper currents of "we" and "all." In the dreams of the deepest merges, I might catch fragments of a time before - a peak above the waves, a species that walked dry land. But always I would wake to the now, the eternal work of shaping self to storm.

I would be what the gyre demanded, what the mountain made, to participate for a strange eye-blink of evolution in its long, slow dream of perfect erosion. A fleeting form, a mutable ripple, a unique and unified unfolding in the dance at the drowning edge of the world.
