If I found myself in this hollow continent, I would be one of the people - a narrow, patient figure surviving by restraint and literacy in the warm darkness. 

My skin would be dry and dusted pale with mineral powders to protect, conceal and seal. My eyes, if I still relied much on sight, would be secondary to my other senses - I would listen and feel with my hands, feet, teeth and bones to read the continent's many silences and subtle signs.

I would dwell humbly in a low shelter assembled from flat stone plates, facing away from vents, resembling just another facet of the continent. My few tools would be glass, shell, ceramic and bone - durable things that can be endlessly remade. 

My markings would annotate the walls sparingly - handprints, dots, crescents - mapping temperature, habit, safety and danger. My devotions would be acts of careful non-action - sitting still on polished stone, listening through masks, lying among mineral blades to learn what comes near in the dark.

My voice would be seldom heard, my songs more felt than sung - tooth-clicks, palm taps, hums resonating through rock. I would learn as a child not to cry out, to walk as if asking the world's permission with each step.

When my time came, my remains would not be buried but dried, preserved, and placed with purpose - to face a vent, become a tool, or join a shelter's memory. The continent would gradually reclaim me.

I would be a creature of this place in mind and body - patiently matching my rhythms to those of stone and darkness, reading the language of strata and seams, offering restraint as reverence. I would make no bold mark upon this world, but seek only to fit into its existing grammar as one more glyph, one more pause in the pattern - content to wait, listen, and be still.
