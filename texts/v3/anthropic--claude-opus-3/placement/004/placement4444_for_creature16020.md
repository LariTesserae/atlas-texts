In this landscape of raw geology and subtle, subterranean life, I would be one of the stone-setting inhabitants of the lowlands.

My body is low and long, suited for moving close to the damp rock, more comfortable in a folded crouch than standing erect. My skin bears the hues and crusts of the mineral-rich waters I frequent - grays and blues mottled with salt, iron, manganese. Where others might have eyes, my face is smooth, for sight matters little in this dim and dripping realm. Instead, my hands and skull are attuned to vibration, able to read the varying resonances of water moving through stone.

I dwell beneath a leaning ledge, just tall enough to accommodate my hunched form. The floor is silty clay that retains a pleasant dampness. Nearby, a steady seep trickles into a shallow pan, its sound a constant murmur that orients my days. 

My patient work is tending the living films that feed on mineral seepage. With small adjustments to the flow - a stone lip here, a clay plug there - I cultivate pools with chemistries to encourage those pale, barely visible blooms. Their growth is slow but sustaining.

When the under-tides rise, connecting scattered pools, I visit the others of my kind. We click stones underwater and press our hands to resonant hollows, feeling the low thrum of hidden chambers. The young ones who drift up from below are known by the taste of their basin. News is shared through patterns tapped on damp stone.

Occasionally, I add a stone to one of the ancient cairns that mark our realm. Perhaps placing the 13th piece atop a mound to signify a new sweetness in the western pools. Or moving the 5th stone to indicate a migration of drop-bodied creatures. Each cairn holds meaning in its count and configuration.  

My existence has the cadence of dripping water. I scrape sustenance from films and collars of deposited salts. I wait out the long spans between lightning-lit instants. My mind holds no thoughts of green, of open sky, of dryness or fire. The wet skin of the land is the limit of my world.

In this place of measured patience, I am a steward of subtle changes, a reader of stone and seep, a long-lived creature content to dwell low and listen deep. The slow, elusive rhythms of this raw terrain are the very shape of my being.
