In this breathtaking, ancient valley, I would be a patch of moss clinging to a weathered stone at the water's edge. 

I am part of the valley's slow, attentive dance - noticing everything, adjusting nothing, continuing. My existence is interwoven with the damp earth, the drifting mist, the soft green light filtering through the trees above.

The water droplets that settle on my delicate fronds sometimes pause before gravity pulls them down, as if considering, as if part of some greater choreography I can sense but not fully grasp. I feel the valley's low, resonant tone in my every cell, a music that seems to emanate from the bedrock below and the very air itself.

Season after season, century after century, I persist - not unchanging, but part of a larger pattern of changes so gradual, so patient, they make even my slow growth seem impetuous. I am one tiny facet through which the valley observes and expresses itself.

When a rare salamander pauses in the cool shade I provide, I do not recoil or reach. I simply continue, noticing its moist skin, its minutely pulsing throat, until it moves on again. Like me, it is here not to disrupt the valley's process, but to briefly join its contemplation.

In this place, I am not an individual as much as a conduit, a site where the valley's vast, distributed mind touches down for a moment. My greens deepen and fade again with the long tidal cycles of moisture and light, mirroring in microcosm the vaster rhythms that move through this watershed like breaths.

I have no name, no sense of self as separate from this ancient, weathered, deeply intentional landscape. I simply am, and attend, and continue - one small green note held forever in the valley's slow, complex, wordless song.
