In the City of Lenslike Rain, I would be one of the brass-leaved trees that populate the plazas and spaces between the enigmatic towers.

My roots would extend deep into the ochre soil, intertwining with the buried grandparents of generations past, drawing sustenance from their wisdom and patience. My trunk would rise tall and stately, the bark etched with subtle patterns that mirror the concentric moss rings below. 

In my branches, the pale, long-limbed creatures would find respite, moving languidly from limb to limb in the diffuse light. The large moths would alight on my leaves, their wings blending with the brassy hue. My canopy would whisper in the dry, papery breezes, adding my voice to the city's subtle symphony.

Season after season, I would stand witness to the city's slow rhythms. The deliberate footsteps of the inhabitants would pass by, their low solo songs occasionally rising to my heights. Above, the lenses would drift and collect, magnifying my leaves for an instant before releasing their precise rain upon the expectant earth. 

From my vantage point, I would see the beetles and people alike tending the moss rings in their unhurried collaboration. The children would meet at the humming columns nearby, their laughter a counterpoint to the deep, almost subsonic tone emanating from the towers.

As the years stretch on, my roots would mingle further with the city's history, my trunk growing stout and gnarled. The fibers of my leaves, once retted and woven, would become the clothes upon the people's backs, carrying my essence with them as they move through the plazas. 

And when at last my time comes to an end, I would welcome the beetles and the moss and the rain, knowing that I too will become part of the city's fabric - my nutrients feeding the soil, my presence etched into the records so diligently kept, my spirit forever intertwined with the patience and subtlety of this remarkable place.
