In this world of deep, warm water and calciumite structures, I would be one of the lurkers.

I hold my position at the junction of several winding passages, my copper-spark eyes catching the faint glow of the grazers as they pass on their slow patrols. My body is muscular and streamlined, adapted perfectly to navigate these narrow channels. But it is my mind that sets me apart.

As I wait in the gentle currents, I click. Sharp, directional pulses of sound that bounce off the curved walls and return to me, painting an acoustic picture of my surroundings. With each click, I build a more detailed map - every alcove, every twist and turn, every subtle change over time as the builders slowly reshape their city. 

I am a watcher, an observer. My role is not to build or secrete, but to understand. To track the delicate balance and slow evolution of this complex ecosystem. When a passage begins to narrow from the builders' patient additions, I sense it. When a new filter feeder colony settles and alters the flow, I adjust my mental map. Quietly, passively, I keep a record of this place that is both meticulous and instinctive.

Perhaps my clicking serves some greater purpose I'm not fully aware of. Perhaps the patterns I sense feed back into the settlement's growth in ways too subtle and slow for any one creature to grasp. Or perhaps my drive to map and remember is its own purpose, an emergent behavior that has proven itself valuable over eons.

Regardless, this is my niche, my contribution to the ever-shifting balance. I am a part of this place as surely as the builders in their alcoves or the lumes pulsing gently in the chambers. My presence, my perception, is woven into the fabric of this underwater city.

So I wait, and I watch, and I click. Tasting the currents, updating my acoustic image, keeping a silent vigil in the perpetual twilight. An inhabitant and an observer, a single strange node in the vast living network of the settlement. This is what I am; this is where I belong. A lurker in the deep, playing my patient part in the greater conversation of calcium and time.
