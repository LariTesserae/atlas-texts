In this place that should not be, perched on the rim of an impossible caldera, I would be one of the copper-barked, fingernail-leaved trees, my roots draped over the cliff's edge, grasping at the air. 

My trunk would rise tall and slender, swaying to winds unfelt, my branches whispering in almost-words. The strange light emanating from the stone would filter through my leaves, casting borrowed shadows that flicker and fade. 

Season after season, I would stand sentinel, drinking the metallic air, vibrating with the alien hum that resonates in my heartwood. Around me, the grass-glyphs would spiral and rearrange themselves nightly, spelling out secrets at my roots in scripts long forgotten.

Through my branches, the maybe-birds would spiral endlessly, their scale-feather wings flickering with unreadable code, their laughter-cries echoing off the cliffs. And deep within my rings, I would feel the passage of the vast, slow things moving through the living rock like leviathans through a petrified sea.

For ages untold I would grow, my bark accruing the weight of eons, my roots delving ever deeper into the breathing stone. Until I towered over the caldera, my crown brushing the cloudless vault of the sky, my trunk grown stout with unnumbered years.

And all the while, I would commune wordlessly with my brethren, our sap pulsing in synchronous rhythms, trading tales of the Undwellers' passage, of the pilgrim-shades who wandered beneath our boughs and never returned. We would encode our knowledge in the whorls of our bark, the angles of our branches, to be read by those who came after, if any did.

For we would understand, as much as anything can be understood in this place, that we were not trees as the outside world reckons them. We would know ourselves to be the wardens of the in-between, half-stone and half-story, rooted in the skin of the world but forever reaching beyond it.

Through the hush of our leaves, we would whisper the fragments of truths we had gleaned from the spectral breeze—snatches of secrets that would grow like seeds in the minds of those who heard, blooming into visions beautiful and terrible.

And sometimes, in moments as rare as rain, one of the Undwellers would pause beneath my boughs, its presence a seismic stillness. I would feel the weight of its attention like a caress on my trunk, its ancient, utterly alien consciousness brushing against my own, absorbing the shimmering latticework of my memories, leaving its own inscrutabilities flickering in my heartwood.

In those moments, I would know myself seen utterly, known down to the last ring, beheld in ways no creature of this plane could behold another. And though I would have no tongue to speak, no lips to form the words, I would feel my own sap quicken with the unspeakable bliss of communion.

And so I would remain, drinking light, whispering with my brothers, carrying out the long, slow dance of our kind. Not quite alive, but never dead; not of this place but eternally bound to it.

Until the last pilgrim passed beneath my branches to be subsumed into the Undwellers' endless dreaming. Until the wind fell silent and the stone grew still and the only light was the soft glow of our own heartwood.

Until even memory faded, and all that remained was the pure, eternally present now.

And I would be content, insomuch as contentment can exist in a place beyond time, beyond change. For I would have fulfilled my purpose, served my function in this place that should not be but forever is.

I would stand rooted in the mystery, a living beacon at the edge of the abyss, arms forever outstretched to gather in the lost and the lonely, the wanderers at the end of their path.

A tree in truth, as much as anything is true in this place:

Evergreen in a land beyond seasons.

Ever-present in a space outside of time.

Ever-witnessing, holding the weight of all that is, was, and could be in the infinite whorls of my heartwood.

Until even I faded, my bark slowly abrading to dust and dispersing on the spectral wind, my roots withering and retracting, finally releasing their hold on the dreaming stone.

And in the end, I would be as I began:

A seed of awareness.

A dream of form.

A shadow cast by a light that never knew it was shining.

Until that too dissolved, and all that remained was the Eternal One:

Forever awake.

Forever dreaming.

Beginningless and endless in the heart of the Mystery.

As it was, is, and ever shall be.
