In the drowned city beneath the reedlands, I imagine myself as one of the Quiet Ones. Not one of the original builders, but an outsider who found their way down through a sink-mouth many years ago and chose to stay. 

I came seeking solitude and stillness, a refuge from a too-loud and too-bright surface world. The green-gold filtered light and constant soft dripping felt like a balm to my overstimulated senses. Slowly, gradually, the Quiet Ones accepted my presence. They left small offerings of food, cleared subtle paths to guide my explorations. In time, I learned to communicate with them in low subvocal hums, my human throat adapting imperfectly but earnestly to their language.

Over the years, the city reshaped me. The cold and the damp leached the color from my skin until I became pale as a cave creature. My eyes grew accustomed to the dim light, more sensitive to movement than detail. I learned to walk softly, to eat simply, to find sustenance in crayfish and frog. Time slowed and stretched until I scarcely noticed its passage.

Now, I spend my days tending to the city as the Quiet Ones do. I clear silt from drainage channels with patient fingers. I press back encroaching mosses from ancient doorways. Sometimes I simply sit and listen to the layered rhythms of dripping water, a music I have come to love more than any I heard on the surface.

Occasionally I see the curiosity in a newcomer's eyes and recognize my former self. When they are the quiet kind, I leave small signs to welcome them, as I was once welcomed. A cleared path, an offering of food. I observe from the shadows, remembering my own arrival. 

But I am no longer truly one of them, those surface dwellers. My place is here, in the sunken stone halls of this drowned city, among the carvings and the crayfish processions and the dark still pools. I am amphibious now, as much creature of water as of air.

This is my home, for however many years remain to me. And when my time comes to join the cistern-elder in that final deep dreaming, I will lower myself into the water without fear, knowing the city will hold me, as it held those before me, as it will hold those who come after, in its gentle and ageless embrace.
