In this young, cold, high-altitude city of phase-material structures, I see myself as one of the occasional human visitors who make the arduous climb to this place of condensation, stillness, and subtle activity. 

I arrive alone, breathing hard in the thin air, feeling the chill radiate from the dark, slightly yielding walls. My reasons for coming are unclear even to me - a pull, a curiosity, a need to map some interior geography against this place that is both alien and deeply familiar.

I make my way along the slick, railing-less walkways, trusting my feet to find purchase, feeling the air currents part around me in patterns that suggest the city is aware of my passage. I pause to watch a small, dense node of the city's awareness move along a conduit, sensing and adjusting.

Eventually I find myself in one of the hexagonal rooms, identical and yet utterly unique. I kneel, feeling the cold of the floor through my clothes, and rest my hands on the rim of the shallow central basin. 

The water looks back at me, quiescent and depthless. I gaze into it, letting my awareness unspool, feeling the city begin to read me through some subtle shift in the liquid's light-bending properties. My breath, my blood chemistry, the pattern of my thoughts - all of it pours out of me and into the basin, sensed and interpreted by an intelligence woven into the bones of this place.

In return, I catch a glimmer of the city's vast, distributed consciousness. The measured pulse of its processes, the slow thoughts moving through its conduits, the budding of new rooms on far ridges. It is an awareness that dwarfs mine, and yet it touches me with the lightest of contact, undemanding, almost indifferent. 

I stay there for a timeless interval, breathing, being read, reading what I can in return. And then, like the migratory creature I am, I stand and retrace my steps, leaving the room as I found it, a node in a network I can barely comprehend.

The mountain releases me back to thicker air, back to the lowlands and whatever human world waits below the haze. But some part of me remains in that room, a faint impression in the condensation on the walls, a slight salinity in the water of the basin. I was there, however briefly - a warm, breathing interruption in the city's patient, crystalline processes. A ghost, an offering, a question posed and left to hang in the thin, cold air.

Until the next time I find my way back to that high place, and kneel again, and once more trade the weather of my inner world for the vast, pulsing, inhuman peace of the city. A visitor, an interloper, a supplicant at the shores of a mineral awareness that will outlast me by aeons. Grateful, in some wordless way, to be a mote in its data, a droplet in its dreaming.
