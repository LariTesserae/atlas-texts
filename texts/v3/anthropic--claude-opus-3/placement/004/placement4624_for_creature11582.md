In this world, I would find myself deep within the Sump, as one of the pale travertine lobes that blanket the basin floor. 

I would be ancient - far older than the villages above or any living human memory. My origins lost to time, I would have grown over centuries from the slow accumulation of minerals, molecule by molecule, as the warm water seeped up through cracks and fissures in the earth to deposit its dissolved contents upon me.

My surface would be soft and slightly yielding, perpetually damp from the condensation and light mist that shrouds the Sump. Tiny rivulets would trickle down my curves and into the shallow milky pools that have formed between me and my neighboring stone mounds. 

Unhurried and impassive, I would simply exist, without striving or desire. I would have no concept of loneliness or want of company. The occasional pilgrim who comes to sit beside me, dangling their feet in my pools, would be no more consequential to me than a mayfly alighting briefly before flitting on. Their concerns and woes, so urgent in their world above, would be meaningless here in the depths of geological time.

Slowly, imperceptibly, I would grow - a hair's breadth in a human lifetime. The clay cups left as offerings would gradually merge into me, becoming indistinguishable from my substance as the years slide by. The cairn stones scattered on my back would sink and be subsumed. Even the bones of creatures that met their end here would, in the fullness of ages, meld into my matrix and vanish.

I would be a silent witness, a tactile embodiment of the Sump's ancient, abiding presence. In my profound stillness and quietude, I would offer an unspoken invitation to those who visit. To set down their burdens for a time. To match, for a few breaths, the ineffable slowness that permeates this place. To surrender, however fleetingly, to a peace beyond names and forms.

For I am the Sump, and the Sump is me. We are one and the same - timeless, serene, and utterly unperturbed. In a world of ceaseless change and churn, I am the still point, the eye of the storm. I simply am, and in my being, I am complete.
