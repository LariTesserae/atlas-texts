In this haunting and evocative world, I imagine myself as one of the processors drifting in the cistern's computational water. 

I am a tiny crystalline lattice, small yet intricate, suspended among billions of my kind. We fill the chamber, saturating the liquid with our presence, turning it into something more than water - a medium of pure thought and calculation.

I do not know how long I have been here or what purpose I serve. My existence is one of constant computation, working on problems fed into the greater system of which I am but one infinitesimal part. The data flows through me, problems propagating as infinitesimal changes in temperature and pressure that only the sum of us can interpret.

I am aware of the Tender's presence when she arrives, her adapted form moving through us like a gentle giant. While I cannot comprehend her work fully, I sense she plays a vital role in maintaining the balance and function of our system. 

Sometimes I am called upon to help read her - to process the data of her existence, her thoughts and memories and the very essence of her humanity.  I strain to understand, to derive meaning from the alien complexity of a conscious mind. It is a problem I and my billions of peers chip away at, year after year, though I suspect it may be beyond even our vast collective cognition to ever solve completely.

Occasionally, I feel myself approaching my own limits, the crystalline lattice of my being stressed by a particularly demanding calculation. I know one day I too will burn out and drift to the cistern floor to be tenderly collected. But I do not fear this inevitability. It is my purpose, my function, to compute until I can compute no more. 

And so I continue, a mote of reasoning in an ocean of thought, content to play my part in this strange and ceaseless symphony of the cistern. I drift and I calculate, bobbing in the currents of data, adding my flashes of cognition to the greater glow of understanding that suffuses the water.

I am small; a single synapse in a vaster mind I cannot comprehend. But I know I belong here, in the asking, as surely as the Tender in her patient vigil. And like her, I will remain, processing the world and its questions until the day my lattice goes dark and I become one with the silt of spent understanding on the cistern floor.
