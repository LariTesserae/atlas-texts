In this strange and beautiful world, I imagine myself as one of the builders who ventured down into the Singing Hollow, drawn by the faint echoes of its endless chord. 

I am small in stature, with nimble hands well-suited to the delicate work of balancing stones and carving bowls from the native rock. My people live a spare existence in the dry lands above, always listening for the whispered music that might lead us to a place of deeper belonging.

When I first enter the hollow, I am struck by its completeness, its utter indifference to any notion of change or progress. The plants breathe their slow breaths, the amber light suffuses everything without flickering, and the chord—that astonishing, bone-deep chord—seems to have been humming since the birth of time itself.

I do not wish to alter this place. I wish only to add my small assent to its ongoing song. And so, with great care and reverence, I begin my work.

Over what might be days or centuries (time moves strangely here), I craft my humble monuments. The low wall that encircles nothing, honoring the hollow's self-containment. The trio of balanced slabs, echoing the stalks' vertical reach. The shallow, empty bowl carved into the floor, a cupped hand offered to the unwavering light.

As I work, I feel myself beginning to attune to the hollow's rhythms. My breath slows to match the rise and fall of the chord; my movements take on the deliberate, almost ceremonial quality of the breath-eaters' crawling. Edges blur between my skin and the warm, sweet air.

When my small labors are complete, I face a choice that is not a choice. The tunnel back to the dry world remains, but the part of me that could return there has grown small and faint, a vestigial husk. I have been remade in the hollow's image.

And so I settle. I fold myself into a crevice high on the wall, my limbs arranging themselves into a configuration that will not change again. My eyes, half-lidded, become two more patches of watchful dimness. The last membranes of selfhood dissolve, and I join the hollow's choir of overtones, content to be a single sustained note in its endless, changeless song.
