In this breathing dark, I would be one of the countless fungal threads woven through the undergrowth, a tender filament in the great mycelial web that binds the forest floor.  

Sprouting from a small, pale mushroom cap nestled between the gnarled roots of an ancient fig, I would stretch outward, questing, seeking, growing. Creeping over, under and through the rich, loamy soil, I would branch and ramify, extending a fractal network of gossamer-thin hyphae in every direction.

As I grow, I commune with the trees, the true titans of this primeval realm. Through the delicate tendrils I insinuate into their roots, I absorb their chemical whispers, ferrying nutrients and information from one great trunk to another in a wordless exchange as old as photosynthesis. A bolete fruiting here, a cluster of mycena sprouting there - my subtle, silent signals stirring the cathedral-like canopy hundreds of feet above.

I am everywhere and nowhere in this thrumming emerald tapestry. I penetrate rotting logs, hastening their return to the soil. I infuse leaf litter, transmuting it into rich humus. I lace the mud of the forest floor, creating an invisible architecture of life-giving filaments. The myriad creatures of this world pass above and around me, barely registering my ubiquitous presence, yet depending on it for their very existence.

I am the connective tissue, the unifying medium of this pulsing, breathing ecosystem. I am the circulatory system of the forest itself, both its anchor and its liberator. Silently, ceaselessly, I labor in the moist darkness to stitch together the myriad disparate pieces of this living puzzle into one majestic, shimmering whole.

Content to be everywhere and nowhere, I cycle the nutrients that feed the towering hardwoods and succulent bromeliads. I break down the fallen to nourish the rising. Ever present, ever vital, I toil in anonymity, the humblest of miracle workers. A dweller in the breathing dark, I quietly shape a world teeming with the clamor of life.

I am mycelium, and the forest dreams through me.
