I would be one of the creatures that inhabit the space between the towers, living my entire existence within the subtle vibrations of this ancient, submerged city.

I am not one of the Threadkeepers, with their slow, purposeful movements and their architectural consciousness. Nor am I one of the bacteria, painting the bone-white surfaces in gradients of rust and sulfur. I am something in between, a being that has evolved to live in the narrow channels and crevices formed where the towers nearly touch.

My body is ribbon-like, translucent and edged with delicate cilia that ripple in the warm currents. I am one of the vast schools that undulate through the city, grazing on the bacterial films that coat the tower surfaces. We move in synchrony, our movements creating intricate patterns in the dim, reddish light that filters up from below.

I have no eyes, for eyes are useless in this world of perpetual twilight. Instead, I navigate by taste and touch, sensing the subtle changes in the water's composition, the minute variations in the texture of the towers' surfaces. My world is one of gradients and edges, of smooth worn calcium and rough patches of bacterial growth.

Time has little meaning for me. I drift in the slow currents, feeding when I encounter a rich patch of bacteria, resting in the eddies that form in the spaces where the towers converge. I might live for days or decades; in this place, the difference is hardly noticeable.

Sometimes, I brush against the Threadkeepers as they go about their endless work. To me, they are like mobile towers, their bodies mineral-hard and ancient. I do not understand their purpose, but I sense that they are essential to the maintenance of this world, that without them, the towers would eventually crumble and the delicate balance of this ecosystem would unravel.

I am content in my simple existence, in my place within this complex, living architecture. I do not question why the towers were built, or what lies beyond the warm water that is my universe. I simply am, a part of this ancient, living city, as much a component of its ecology as the bacteria I graze upon or the Threadkeepers that move ponderously through its passages.

In a way, I am like the water itself - shaped by the towers, flowing through and around them in endless, gentle currents. I am a part of this place, and it is a part of me, and together we endure, a forgotten world of bone and stone and living things, persisting in the timeless depths.
