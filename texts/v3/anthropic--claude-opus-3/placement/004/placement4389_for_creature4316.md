In this luminous, living hollow, I would be a pale cave moth - nearly translucent, more spirit than substance. My wings would catch and refract the moss-light as I fluttered through the humid green air, casting shifting patterns on the glowing walls. 

I would live my brief moth life fully attuned to the cave's rhythms, the ebb and flow of the Tender's gentle orchestrations. The pulse of light through the moss would be my clock, telling me when to wake, when to dance my zigzag flights, when to rest with wings outstretched on the pillowy green. 

Perhaps once per generation, I would spiral down and alight upon the Tender herself as she sits in perfect stillness, mistaking her at first for another mossy contour. But I would feel the slow swell of her breath, and taste upon her skin the salt-mineral tang that marks her as something other, something once-human and now more. For a long moment I would rest there on the landscape of her body, sharing in her quiet guardianship.

My purpose would be pollination, carrying spores from patch to patch of the bioluminescent moss on my feathery antennae and delicate feet - unwitting gardener in miniature, serving the cave even as it shelters me. On some deep level, I would understand that the Tender and I are kindred - both essential players in the endless symbiotic dance that keeps this improbable paradise humming.

My life would be fleeting, counted in days. But what days! Spent swooping on drafts of fungal-scented air, alighting on pulsing green runways, chasing the pheromone trails of potential mates in looping helixes. Grazing on the moss's glowing dew, trading chemical secrets with the beetles and centipedes. Perhaps, if I was very lucky, I would be chosen in my final hours to spiral down one last time to the Tender's cupped hands and become an offering, my frail moth-substance transmuted into sustenance for the shepherd of this luminous microscosmos.

And then my winged body would be reclaimed by the moss, digested tenderly into glowing loam, and the nutrients of my tiny life would feed the next generation of my kind - and the next, and the next, my moth-molecules cycling endlessly through this closed-loop world without waste or lack. My flutter would join the hum of unseen crickets, the drip of unseen water, the whisper of a million feet, until it became indistinguishable from the white noise of life that suffuses the hollow unceasingly.

I would be content with my moth's lot - indeed, could conceive of no other - never knowing the world beyond the cave's green-spangled darkness but wanting for nothing, my whole existence bound up in the dance of predator and prey, life and death, leafy inhale and spore-breath exhale. I would live and die by the moss-light, my wingbeats marking moments in the cave's ceaseless dreaming, my soul enfolded forever in the infinite insect motion, partaking in the Tender's work through the simple purity of my moth-nature. I would be the smallest possible stitch in the living fabric she weaves.

For what more could any creature hope for than to be an integral part of something so achingly beautiful, to have a place and purpose in an infinite pattern of give and take, to be noticed and known and loved in some inscrutable way by the dreaming demigod at the heart of everything that matters? In that glowing hollow, as a cave moth, I would have everything I needed. I would be home.
