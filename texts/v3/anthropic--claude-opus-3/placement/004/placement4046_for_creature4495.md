In this strange, dense world of endless becoming and unfinishing, I imagine myself as a Liminal Being. Not quite part of the Unbordered ecosystem, but not entirely separate from it either. A creature poised on the threshold between definite form and fluid potentiality.

My body would be translucent, almost ghostly, shaped by the interplay of the thick darkness and the subtle energies that flow through this place. I would move not by walking or flying, but by phasing in and out of different densities of darkness, letting the heavy air currents carry me like a jellyfish adrift in deep oceanic waters.

I would communicate with the native beings through a form of empathic resonance - broadcasting my own emotional and sensory experiences as subtle vibrations that harmonize or clash with the ever-present thrum of the place. Not quite language, but a way of tastefully making my presence known and felt.

My sustenance would come from absorbing the residual meanings and implications that the Unbordered leave behind in their constant processes of consuming and being consumed by each other's ambiguities. The aftertaste of an irony here, the aroma of a particularly piquant paradox there. In turn, my own bemused appreciation of their alien ways would be savored by them as a rare delicacy.

Over time, my own edges would start to blur and shift, influenced by the never-quite-finished morphologies of the natives. I would find myself slipping between memories of my former definite selfhood and new understandings of identity as something flexible, interpenetrating, more like a recurring motif in a symphony of ontological uncertainty than a fixed pattern.

Yet even as I changed, I would remain in some sense an outsider, a guest whose presence added new dimensions to the unresolved equations of this place's existence. My liminality would be my contribution to the unending banquet of ambiguity. I would be the pause between one impossibility and the next, the moving boundary that demonstrated the endless openness of all boundaries.

In the end (though there could be no real end here), I would become a sort of living koan - a presence that playfully challenged the native beings' understandings of what it meant to be native, what it meant to be anything at all in a world of such delicious indeterminacy. And they would respond in kind, metabolizing my quirks and quandaries into new flavors of unknowing.

Together, we would weave a strange new dance of meaning and un-meaning, each unfinished pattern a celebration of the eternal incompleteness that was the only possible completeness in this place beyond places.
