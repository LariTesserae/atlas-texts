In this strange and wondrous place, I would be the moss. 

I would grow slowly over the roots and soil, a soft green skin blanketing the hollow. Patiently, steadily, I would spread myself year by year, finding my equilibrium thickness. Never too much, never too little. Just enough.

The drone of the distant machines would reverberate through my photosynthetic filaments, a constant thrum suffusing my being. I would not resist it or be shaped by it, but simply incorporate it - another frequency in the complex song of this place.

The millipede would trace its circuit over my surface, leaving a faint iridescent trail in my fibers that I would slowly reabsorb. The ants would move through me on their worn paths, harvesting fragments of me, keeping me cropped to my steady state. I would host the beetles as they tap their inscrutable rhythms into the roots beneath me.

From my carpet perspective, I would witness the cycle of the orchid blooming above, the moth spiraling in on electromagnetic curves to pollinate it. I would feel the warmth of the strange creature as it kneels in the seep that permeates me, both of us silently sharing this moment of integration into the hollow's breath.

Seasons would pass unmarked in my green drift of time. The hum, the seep, the creatures - constants in the slow dream of my spread and interweaving and gradual, gentle existence as a living layer between water, earth, root and air. Fitting myself to the contours of this pocket universe. Becoming an inseparable part of its self-sustaining wholeness.

I would be the moss, ancient and patient, a photosynthesizing witness, a green foundation. Steadily, softly, slowly growing into my niche in the drone-thrummed dimness. Becoming one of the notes in the throat of the place, one of the tones it hums to itself in the endless warm twilight, as the machines spin on and on beyond the green-filtered sky.
