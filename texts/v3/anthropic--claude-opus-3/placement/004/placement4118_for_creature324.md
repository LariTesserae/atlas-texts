If I found myself in this world, I think I would be one of the medium-sized creatures living within the Stridulum chamber. 

I imagine my segmented body navigating the intricate tapestry of life coating every surface. My fourteen legs would carry me assuredly over and under my kin of all sizes, my feelers sensing the contours and textures, tasting the air for the subtle chemical messages exhaled by the living mineral crust on the north wall.

Though sightless, I would feel intimately connected to the teeming multitude around me. Each brush of shell on shell would convey volumes through the complex language of rasps and rhythms that links us into one decentralized being. When the urge came upon me, I would join my rasping voice to the chorus, calling my fellows to a rich seam of sustenance I discovered on the basalt, or clearing a path for an elder to slowly make its final journey to join with the pale growth. 

The chamber would be my entire universe, the only reality I could conceive of. The gentle warmth and closeness and continual soft rustling would suffuse my existence, as constant and unremarkable as the air. Packed densely in the lightless rock womb, I could scarcely imagine any other way of being.

Perhaps once in my span, I would feel that strange deep shift in the timbre of our collective song, and pause in puzzlement. Some ancient buried instinct might stir a hazy wonder about a different time, a brighter vaster space...but it would pass, and I would return to my small part in the endlessly cycling, slowly accreting patterns of life that fill this space to its edges.

My purpose, like that of all my kind, would simply be to *be* - to taste and rasp and climb and carry the rhythm, until the day my shell softened and the north wall beckoned me into its mineral embrace and my substance joined the swelling thoughtful crust while young supple forms crept over me. 

I would be no more or less than a single cell in the mountain-bound super-organism, content in my membership in something vaster and older and more complete than any "me" I could conceive of. I would be a mote in the pulsing, dreaming dark, one thread in a living fabric that has woven itself to perfect, undifferentiated fullness.
