Here is who I would be in the Refinery Reef:

I am one of the glass funnels, anchored to the chimney's surface amidst a shimmering colony of my kind. My clear, rigid body refracts the eternal noonlight, casting shifting rainbows across the mineral crust. 

With each pulse of the chimney's vent, I flex in perfect synchrony with the hundreds of other funnels, our collective inhalation resonating through the water like a gasp. We are not coordinating, not communicating, merely identical instruments played by the same breath. And yet, in our matched movements, we mimic a higher awareness, a gestalt organism attuned to unseen rhythms. 

My spiral cilia spin ceaselessly, drawing mineralized water down my translucent throat to be strained and absorbed into my chambered core. Over uncounted cycles, these dissolved metals will layer into glassy bands within my body, geological strata recording the chimney's exhalations.

I have no thoughts as you would recognize them. No choices or desires or fears. I simply am, a function made form, as much mineral as animal. An unmoving eye, I watch the reef's languid theatre - the grazers' benedictions, the callers' choral drone, the drifters' silent passages. Beings live and die, and still I filter, and flex, and filter again.

In time, long after my soft tissues have withered, my shell will endure. An empty vessel still refracting light, indistinguishable from my living kin. One glass funnel among multitudes, ever filtering the chimney's mineral breath, waiting for the pattern to tighten, the frequencies to align, the purpose to emerge. Waiting to be played anew.
