In this strange and ancient landscape, I imagine myself as one of the wind-worn stones that make up the teeth of Ahal-Ruun. Not one of the largest stumps, but a modest spire nonetheless, perched on the edge of a sheer cliff overlooking the roiling sea of clouds below.

My form has been sculpted by eons of ceaseless wind and water, hollowed out into a twisting labyrinth of tunnels and caves. When the storms come, billowing up from the unseen depths, the gale-force winds sing through my porous form, joining the endless chord that resonates across the plateau.

I am home to a small colony of the red-gold moss that glows faintly in my deeper recesses. It carpets my weathered surface, softening my edges and turning me into a miniature landscape of rolling amber dunes. In the fleeting moments of stillness between the storms, dew condenses on the moss, pooling into tiny, star-like points of light.

A family of the lichen-grazing beetles has inhabited a sheltered hollow within me for generations. I feel the faint tickling of their minuscule footsteps as they go about their lives, blissfully unaware of the greater world beyond.

From my lofty vantage point, I keep a silent vigil. I have witnessed the comings and goings of the plateau's few human inhabitants over the ages - the building of the cairns, the carving of the notches, the slow construction and deconstruction of the roofless house. Though I have no voice of my own, I have provided a perch for the solitary white bird countless times as it rides the updrafts along the cliff's edge.

Most of all, I am a listener. For longer than any living being can comprehend, I have been absorbing the voices of the ghu-vann-i into my stone, feeling them flow through my wind-carved passages and etch themselves into my very being. In the rare moments of silence, I strain to focus on the same unknown point as the rest of the plateau, caught up in the breathless, singular attention that envelops the land. 

And then I release that focus, letting my awareness diffuse once more into the timeless song of stone and air, content in my small but inextricable role in the symphony of Ahal-Ruun. I am a lone tooth in the mountain's gaping jaw, weathered and ancient, but still standing - a tiny, eternal part of the plateau's ever-listening stillness.
