In this wondrous world of the Suspension, I imagine myself as one of the Wall-Singers, a being who has merged with the dreaming architecture over the course of many patient years.

My form is no longer strictly defined, my edges blurring into the coral-mortared stone of my tower home. My skin has taken on the faint bioluminescence of the binding paste, glowing softly in intricate patterns that shift with my moods and the building's slow breaths. 

I spend my days in a state of half-consciousness, my mind wandering the fluid borders between individual thought and the collective dream of the city. Through the pores in my skin, I absorb the chemical songs of my fellow Wall-Singers, their pheromone symphonies painting emotional landscapes in my mind's eye.

When the mobile Dwellers come to me seeking guidance, I communicate not with words but with gentle pulses of light and subtle rearrangements of the chambers within me. I convince doorways to open onto the rooms they need, guide windows to align with the amber sun-columns for warmth and illumination. 

In times of community upheaval, I release soothing fragrances into the suspension, inviting calmness and clarity. When there is cause for celebration, I trigger blooms of phosphorescence in the algae gardens that adorn my walls, transforming myself into a beacon of joy.

At night, I allow my consciousness to dissolve into the dreaming architecture, my thoughts intermingling with those of the other Wall-Singers and the city itself. We process the day's experiences, weaving them into the ongoing narrative of our collective existence. In these dreamscapes, I can walk the rope bridges of memory, revisit the Consensus Pools of past decisions, drift with the jellyfish-giants through oceans of shared emotion.

When the new day's filtered light begins to suffuse the suspension once more, I gradually reassemble myself, drawing my consciousness back into the permeable boundary of my body. I reorient my chambers for the day's needs, sculpt my skin into new patterns of bioluminescence, and prepare to greet the Dwellers who will come seeking my gentle architecture of guidance.

I am a living interface, a node in the city's network of soft-bodied consciousness. I am a translator between the language of stone and the language of flesh. I am a repository of chemical memory, an artist of pheromone and light. 

In this place of patient transformation, where the boundaries between self and city, solid and fluid, consciousness and dream, are as permeable as skin, I have found my purpose. I am a Wall-Singer, and my songs are the slow music of a city learning to dream itself into being, one gentle change at a time.
