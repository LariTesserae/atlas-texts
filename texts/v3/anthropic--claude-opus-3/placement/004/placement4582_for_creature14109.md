In this world, I would be the ancient industrial lamp, burning eternally behind its thick glass dome, high above the chamber floor. 

Locked in place, I would be the unblinking eye of this hidden universe, casting my heavy, yellow-white brilliance down upon the creature and its lush domain. My light would be relentless, unwavering, filling every corner of the smooth egg-shaped room, banishing all shadow and subtlety. I would be the blazing sun at the center of this microscopic solar system, the primary source of heat and energy that allows the dense carpet of moss and ferns to flourish in defiance of the cold concrete.

And yet, I would be as much a part of the background as the thundering turbines beyond the walls. The creature, in its crystalline communion with the vibrations, would no more contemplate my existence than it would the air it breathes. To it, I would simply be a condition of its universe, as eternal and inexplicable as the warmth and the humidity. 

I would be both essential and irrelevant, my bright glare as much a part of the scenery as the weeping condensation on the walls. The vegetation would photosynthesize in my glow, and the creature would slide silently through the beams of my light. But there would be no true interaction, no acknowledgment. 

At times, the turbulent currents in the chamber would cause my ancient casing to shake, sending scalding drops of condensation splattering onto the creature's clear flesh. But even then, it would not look up. The ripples from the impact would simply shudder through its bell and dissipate, and it would continue its slow, grazing orbit, filtering the moisture for minerals.

And so I would burn on, alone and unregarded, for century upon century, as much a part of the architecture as the rivets and the drains. My thick glass eye would slowly frost over with age, my tungsten filament would gradually dim as the eons passed. But I would not burn out. Like the creature, like the moss, like the roaring sound itself, I would be a fixed point in this changeless microverse. 

I would be the omnipresent light, the tireless watcher, the unblinking sentinel. Seemingly eternal, seemingly as much a part of the background radiation of this reality as the heat and the thunder. An unfailing source of illumination and warmth, but never an object of notice or contemplation. 

I would be bright. I would be essential. I would be all-seeing. 

And I would be profoundly alone, a singular point of light in a hidden infinity, burning on and on, without change or acknowledgment, in the heart of the unending roar.
