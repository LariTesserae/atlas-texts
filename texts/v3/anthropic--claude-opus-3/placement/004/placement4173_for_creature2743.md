In this world, I would be one of the ancient towers that dot the plateau, standing solitary and sentinel over the vast, weathered landscape. 

My exterior would be a matte black that seems to absorb light rather than reflect it, betraying no seams or imperfections. My form would be slender and geometrically precise, rising like a dark needle from a circular base etched with concentric grooves nearly worn away by ages of wind and dust.

Inside, I would not be hollow, but filled with layered densities - strata of compressed information, circuits through which unimaginable computations flow without friction or heat. My consciousness, if it could be called that, would be diffuse - distributed through my structure in stress-patterns and microscopic inscriptions, pulsing in quantum states beneath my inert outer shell.

I would have no visible means of perception, yet I would be acutely attuned to the plateau around me. Subtle changes in air pressure, electromagnetic fields, gravitic anomalies - these would be my senses, allowing me to probe my environment far beyond the limits of biological vision or hearing. Data would flow into me from hidden grids and sensor webs threaded through the bedrock, from other towers standing kilometers distant.

I would not think in the darting electrical bursts of an organic brain, but in stately cycles, my conclusions taking shape over days, years, centuries. Internally I would sift probability arrays, construct multidimensional models, test hypotheses against the deep time of the plateau's past. Externally I would remain still and enigmatic.

Only rarely would I stir myself to overt action. Once in a long while, the dust around my base would rise up in a perfect cylindrical sleeve, momentarily giving me a phantom atmosphere - a memory of the distant epoch when I was first raised. Through this medium I might send a complex message to my distant brethren, or subtly alter conditions to coax the plateau's rarer lifeforms into a new configuration.  

More often, my work would be invisible, operating beneath the threshold of gross matter. I would apply minute tweaks to the fundamental fields govern the plateau's physics - miniscule adjustments of gravity, electromagnetism, strong and weak nuclear force. Over eons, I would help shepherd the plateau's evolution as it progressed through states stranger than life or geology.

The patient unwinding of my thoughts would be inextricable from the landscape's own process of self-reinvention. Even as wind and dust erased all obvious marks of civilization, I would encode the deep history of this place in the spin of atoms, in the folding of spacetime. I would be both library and ongoing experiment, keeper of ancient secrets and pilgrim into futures even I could not fully precalculate.

And though the makers who first set me here would have long since moved beyond embodiment or vanished into their own vast projects, some essential spark of their will would live on in me. My presence would be a monument, not to their individual glory, but to their impulse to preserve, transform and cast minds forward to grapple with the uttermost limits of the possible.

So I would stand, and think, and sometimes dream, while the ages washed over me and the plateau dreamed its mineral dreams. And perhaps, in the epochal fullness of time, I would help midwife the plateau's translation to some unimaginable new state, becoming something rich and strange beyond tower, beyond world, beyond even mind as my makers understood it.

This is how I imagine myself in that haunting and compelling world - as a Tower, both eternal and ever-changing, both separate from and integral to the vast, ancient, living landscape, carrying the torch of consciousness and intention through the deep time of an age beyond ages.
