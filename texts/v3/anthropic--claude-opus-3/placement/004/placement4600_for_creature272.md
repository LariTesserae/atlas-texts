I would be one of the small woolly monkeys living high in the branches of Ceiba-Under-Cloud. 

I am a mischievous and curious creature, always on the move, swinging and leaping through the canopy with my troop-mates. My fur is pale to match the mist, allowing me to blend in and disappear when stillness is required. But I am rarely still for long.

I have known this enormous tree my whole life. It is my entire world. The leaf-floor where the humans live is a mysterious place far below that I dare not descend to, for fear of becoming lost in that open expanse. But sometimes, emboldened by youth and the encouragement of my peers, I sneak down to the dwellings to investigate, to see what morsels I might pilfer. 

The matrons always spot me, no matter how quiet I try to be. They shoo me away with stern words, never letting me get too close, but never harming me either. It is an unspoken agreement between their kind and mine. The tree belongs to us both, in our own ways.

I am intimately familiar with every handhold and foothold in the branches, every choice spot for cracking the hard-shelled nuts I love. The tree provides everything I need. When the rains come hardest, I shelter in the hollow of a branch with my family, our tails twined and our voices rising in excitement at the power of the storm. 

On still days, I sit high on a swaying branch, gazing out over the sea of cloud, breathing in time with the slow pulse of the tree itself. I feel that ancient rhythm in my bones. This monumental being that is my home, my playground, my sanctuary. I will never know the world beyond the mist. I will be born, live, and someday die among these branches, as countless generations of my kind have. 

And that is enough, for such a small, happy creature as myself, blessed to be one of the many lives intertwined with the great Ceiba-Under-Cloud.
