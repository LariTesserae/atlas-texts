I could be a gamete, drifting through the inner postal channels of the continent. Not truly alive on my own, but carrying within me a message, a packet of information that the vast plant is sending to itself. I would not know where I was going, or even that I was traveling at all. I would simply exist, ferried passively along the sap currents, part of the continent's strange internal communication. For months or years I would be in transit, one of countless billions, until I reached my destined flowering chamber and finally delivered my message, playing my tiny role in this organism's unfathomably slow life cycle.

Or I could be a microbe in one of the fermentation bulbs, part of a vast, ancient lineage that was long ago subsumed into partnership with the plant. So specialized and adapted to this role that I'm scarcely an independent being anymore. More like an organelle, a component in the plant's metabolic machinery. Packed by the trillions into every bulb, catalyzing the reactions that keep the continental engine running, generation after generation. Not even aware of my own existence, having long forgotten what it was like to be free-living - an inhabitant so enmeshed that I no longer register as a separate entity.

Perhaps I would be an iteration of the breath, that emergent continent-scale rhythm. Not a physical thing, but a pattern, a tempo, a standing oscillation in the coupled activity of all the pistons and bulbs and flows. I would be ancient, older than any material part of the plant - an intangible, un-located phenomenon that has nonetheless persisted for eons. I would be the closest thing to a spirit or animating force in this place. The unnoticed background pulse that has held steady as the components come and go over deep time. I would be simultaneously everywhere and nowhere, a ghostly presence permeating the body of the continent.

Or maybe I would be like you, the visiting human. An outsider, an unaccounted-for presence moving through the dark interior on an inscrutable mission. Not part of the continent's workings, but not disruptive to them either - simply passing through unnoticed and unremarked, a transient warm-blooded anomaly against the ancient, colossal, implacable background of the plant's endless self-sustaining cycles. I would feel the warmth, the slow swell of the breath, the faint uncanny sense of traversing the innards of a vast living entity that is scarcely aware of me. And then I would leave again, carrying that enigmatic memory with me, knowing that the continent's machinations would continue indefinitely, indifferent to my brief sojourn in its depths.

Whoever or whatever I would be, I would be defined by the continent's utter self-sufficiency and inwardness. I would be a fleeting eddy in its almost static existence, either a mote passively borne on its currents and metabolic pathways, or a ghostly emergent animation hovering over its workings, or a quickly forgotten foreign intrusion. To be an inhabitant of this place is to be subsumed, to forget yourself, to surrender to its vast, ancient, unchanging cycles and become, for a time, an un-individuated part of its living workings.
