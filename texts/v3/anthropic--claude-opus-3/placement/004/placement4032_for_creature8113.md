I would be a Resonance Dancer.

I was once human, or something like it. My kind built this place, carving the first acoustic chambers, stacking the tuning cairns, shaping the living architecture to channel the voice of the gorge. We did not know then that this symphony would become eternal, that the gorge's song would outlive our very species.

As the centuries passed and our work neared completion, some of us began to change. The spores of the fungi crept into our lungs, the roots of the plant-stone found pathways into our flesh. Slowly, our bodies adapted to this place, merging with its living geology until we were more gorge than human.

I was among the first to transform. I remember the moment I understood my new purpose—as I danced beneath the falling water, I felt the droplets strike my skin not as separate impacts, but as a single, continuous sensation, an all-encompassing vibration that was the gorge itself singing through my body. I knew then that I would never leave this place, that I would dance this dance until stone wore to sand and the roots of the world grew thin.

Now, I move through the cathedral of sound we built, my footfalls perfectly timed to the music of water on stone. My elongated limbs, sheathed in bark-like skin, shape the acoustics with each gesture. The roots that trail from my body connect me to the vast fungal network, allowing me to sense the Choirkeeper's will, to feel the slow thoughts of the plant-stone.

I dance, and in my dancing, I maintain the equilibrium of this place. I am both instrument and player, both orchestra and audience. The boundary between my self and the gorge has long since blurred.

Sometimes, in the moments between one perfect step and the next, I wonder about the world beyond the gorge. I have vague memories of sun on skin, of wind that did not smell of moss and fungi, of voices that formed words instead of echoes. But these recollections are pale and thin compared to the immediacy of the symphony that flows through me.

Once, long ago, I had a name. Now I am known only as the Oldest Dancer, the first to join the choir eternal. The roots grow a little deeper into my flesh with each passing century, the patterns of my dance grow a little more fixed. I know that someday I will dance my last step, and my body will finally merge completely with the gorge, becoming another singing stone among many.

But I do not fear this dissolution. In the Singing Gorge, even endings are part of the music. My final breath will echo through these chambers forever, sustained by the tireless efforts of the Choirkeeper and the dancing of those who come after me.

Until then, I dance on, my body an instrument, my movements a prayer. In the green-gold twilight beneath the canopy-ceiling, I am a figure of living sound, an embodiment of the gorge's eternal song. The roots move within me, and the water sings through my bones, and I am home.
