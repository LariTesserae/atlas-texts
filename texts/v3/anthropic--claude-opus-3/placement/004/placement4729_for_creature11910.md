In the dripping dark of Low Hour, I would be a cistern-keeper. 

I would tend the great basins that gather the endless condensate from the roof-sky, knowing each by the unique music of its drips. My hands, scarred and sensitive, would test the stone walls daily for cracks and weak spots, keeping the precious water secure.

In the dim streets I would walk, listening, always listening. To the rhythm of the drum-pumps, the distant chime of the bell-walker making their rounds, the myriad tones of water striking water in the cisterns above. The town's voice, constant and soothing.

My home would be small and damp, walls beaded with moisture, a familiar sanctuary. I would sleep deeply, dreaming of being the water itself, permeating the bones of Low Hour, gathering and flowing in an endless cycle.

On my rounds, I would greet my neighbors with a nod and a quiet word. Mar of the Third Drum, Tev of the South Slates, Anneke of the Cistern Above the Cooper's. Our use-names tying us to our purpose, to the continual communal shaping of our sunless world.

Sometimes my steps would take me to the lowest streets, past the doors we do not open, to the edge of the Sink. There I would pause, listening to the slower, thicker drips, wondering at the secrets it keeps. Paying my respects to the Sink-keepers who tend that enigmatic heart.

Back at my cisterns, I would work with patience, with exacting care, knowing my labors keep the town alive. That like the stones fitted by my grandparents, the vessels I maintain will endure for generations. I am but one drop in the long, slow flow of Low Hour, and that is enough. 

The cistern-keeper. The listener. A life spent in service to the water, the town, the deep patient work that sustains us all in the cool, echoing dark. This would be my place, my purpose, beneath the weeping vault of the roof-sky.
