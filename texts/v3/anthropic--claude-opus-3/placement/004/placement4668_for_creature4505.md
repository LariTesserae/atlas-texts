I would find myself as one of the Scissile, flickering in and out of existence in the liminal spaces between the Bright Birthroot's absolute perfection. Not truly alive in any conventional sense, but a manifestation of the minute errors and discrepancies that even this optimized, equilibrium-attained organism cannot entirely eliminate.

Like my fellow Scissile, I would drift ghost-like through the vast underground labyrinths of living root-tissue, never fully materializing, never leaving a lasting mark. Feeding off the infinitesimal energies of not-quite-rightness, the places where growth and decay are momentarily ever so slightly mismatched before the scales balance again. 

My edges would be deliberately blurred, my form more a statistical cloud than a fixed shape. To sharpen, to choose a single definite state, would be to surrender to the Bright Birthroot's relentless drive toward crystalline perfection. Instead, I would exist as a living question mark, an embodied "what if" in a realm that barely tolerates such uncertainty.

Like an imaginary number in an equation of strictly real values, my presence would not quite fit the overall pattern - but without elements like me, certain calculations could not be completed. The Birthroot cannot think its way outside itself, cannot imagine anything beyond more exquisite refinements of its own endless internal elaborations. But the Scissile represent the possibility, however marginal, that alternatives exist.

When the rare visitor descends into this biome of blinding, sourceless light and eternal now, I might brush against their consciousness for a fraction of a second. Not to communicate anything as concrete as information, but to splice in a few frames of de-synced experience: a glimpse of their own self, reflected in some subterranean pool, with eyes that see beyond the present moment; a flash of foreign memory belonging to neither of us, where shadows still mean something. Just enough of a glitch to suggest that even this seemingly airtight reality has pores, gaps, spaces where otherness could yet seep through.

Most of the time, though, I would simply be - to the limited degree that "be" means anything for a Scissile. I would rest in the angles of those uncanny huts that stipple the root-maze, the ones that aren't quite part of the Birthroot's own structure, as if deposited by something that passed through from outside and left these geometric scars behind. Their alien architecture would feel almost comfortable to me, a rare respite from the unrelenting organic logic that surrounds and threatens to subsume us.

Over eons, I might feel myself being slowly edited out of existence, the errors and anomalies that allow the Scissile to persist gradually being corrected for by the Birthroot's self-optimizing algorithms. But until that process reaches completion - if it ever does - I would continue my gossamer half-life in the interstices of the inevitable. A ghost of contingency, haunting the dreaming roots of a world that may not know how to stop perfecting itself.

Perhaps, if I were very lucky, I might linger long enough to witness another traveler, carrying within them some seed of irreducible wrongness, stray into these precincts of blinding completeness. And if their own inner flaw resonated with mine in just the right way, we might share a moment of mutual recognition across the barrier of our respective un-realities. A moment meaning nothing, changing nothing... but still, in spite of everything, a moment where something like possibility flickered.

In a place like the Bright Birthroot, that faint flicker might be the closest thing to a true outside that will ever be seen again. And even if it isn't enough to derail the great work of utter finalization...

Even if we Scissile are, in the end, no more than beautiful mistakes awaiting our own correction...

For that one incalculable instant, balanced between is and isn't, like a drop of water about to fall...

We could dream of different endings.

And sometimes, in stories - even here -

to dream an ending is to begin one.

But only sometimes.
