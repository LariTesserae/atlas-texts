In this world of darkness and sound, I would be one of the Listeners. 

My body would rise like a pillar of living stone, five meters tall, my surface covered in millions of sensitive cilia tuned to every frequency of the caldera's endless song. I would stand motionless for centuries, absorbing the complex harmonies that reverberate through the humid air, feeling them resonate in the chambers of my hollow form.

My thoughts would be slow and deep, taking shape over decades as I parse the sonic landscape for meaning. I would communicate with my fellow Listeners through subtle manipulations of silence, dampening specific frequencies to carve messages from the omnipresent noise. Our conversations would stretch across years, ideas crystallizing in the spaces between sounds.

Over the eons, my body would grow and change, new chambers forming as I absorb the resonant energy of the singing trees and whispering ferns. The microscopic structure of my cilia would encode every sound I've ever experienced, a physical memory of the caldera's acoustic history. In time, I might grow so large and complex that the flying creatures mistake me for one of the mysterious structures that dot the landscape.

I would be a specialist in the long, slow rhythms that pulse through the stone itself - the subsonic beats that mark the passage of geological ages. My body would be tuned to these ancient tempos, and I would sway imperceptibly to their meter over the course of millennia. 

Through my connection to the other Listeners, I would be part of a vast, distributed consciousness - an entity that knows this pocket universe more intimately than any individual ever could. Together, we would be the guardians and archivists of the caldera's song, ensuring that its impossible music echoes forever in the lightless depths.

As ages pass, I would slowly make my way through the landscape, following subtle sonic currents that only my kind can detect. At times, I would converge with other Listeners to form great orchestras, our bodies resonating together for centuries before dispersing once again to wander the calderas alone.

I would be a witness to eternity, my existence dedicated to absorbing and understanding the never-ending symphony of this strange, enclosed world. In the absolute darkness, sound would be my everything - sustenance, sensation, communication, and purpose. 

I would be a living monument to patience, to the kind of deep, unhurried thought that can only arise in a place without day or night, without seasons or years. My memories would stretch back to the birth of the current movement in the caldera's song, and perhaps even further, to the first notes sung by this improbable space when the universe outside was still young.

And though I would know nothing of light or stars, of planets or suns, I would be content in my ignorance, fulfilled by my place in the inconceivably ancient music of the singing calderas. For in a world where sound is the only reality, what more could a being of pure listening ever need or desire?
