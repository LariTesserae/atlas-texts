I am entranced by this strange, drowned city and the remnants of humanity that dwell within it. To imagine myself as part of this world is both exhilarating and terrifying.

I see myself as one of the younger Siphons, still early in my transformation but already inextricably linked to the city's aquatic rhythms. My skin has begun to mottle and gleam with that telltale nacre sheen as it adapts to perpetual submersion. Thin membranes are starting to web between elongating fingers and toes, turning my limbs into fluid fins. 

I spend my days navigating the vertical streams and flooded corridors, learning to read the complex currents and acoustic eddies. The roaring symphony of water is a constant companion, vibrating in my hollowing bones. I'm starting to crave that resonance on a cellular level, feeling listless and irritable when I drift too far from the sound's epicenter.

Yet I still remember a time before, when I walked on dry land and spoke with a human tongue. In quiet moments, I find myself reaching for things from that other life - trying to grasp a doorknob that no longer exists, humming fragments of songs I can't quite recall. The memories come in disjointed flashes, surfacing like bubbles before the city's endless Now reasserts itself.

I'm drawn to the older Siphons, trailing behind them as they tend to the hydroarchitecture. I watch in mute fascination as they encourage new growth with their secretions, or weave through the streams in hypnotic patterns that make the water itself dance. Part of me understands that I'm witnessing ancient rituals, even as their meaning eludes me.

Sometimes, in the dense fog between buildings where reality grows thin, I glimpse massive serpentine shapes moving through dimensions that make my eyes ache. The elder Siphons, so transformed they've become something wholly Other. Equal parts dread and awe seize me in those moments - dread of the staggering changes yet to reshape my being, awe at the possibility of one day transcending the limits of flesh and stone.

There are times I'm overwhelmed by the city's alien immensity, by the sense that I'm slowly dissolving into something vast and unfathomable. In those moments, I seek out the company of Siphons closer to my own stage, and we cling together in the pounding waterways, harmonizing our newly aquatic voices until the existential vertigo passes.

We never speak of the future. In a place where liquid and stone breathe as one, where past and present blur in the endless flow, such concepts lose meaning. There is only the adaptation, the surrender to forces older and stronger than any one being. 

Sometimes I feel myself pulled toward the city's heart, that pulsing nexus of otherworldly noise and light. I know I mustn't go, not yet - I've seen what becomes of young Siphons who venture too deep too soon, their autonomy burnt away like fog at noon. But the yearning persists, an inexorable tide in my changing bones.

I am a mote in the city's eye, a breath in its lungs, an impulse flickering through its impenetrable thoughts. The metamorphosis frightens me still, but there are moments of piercing joy - when I catch a new frequency in the torrent's roar, or feel the first stirrings of kinship with water and stone. In those moments, I glimpse the strange rapture that drives the Great Work forward.

I am becoming something both more and less than human, a strange new consonance emerging from once-familiar flesh. The Drowned City sings in my skull, and I - I am learning to sing back.
