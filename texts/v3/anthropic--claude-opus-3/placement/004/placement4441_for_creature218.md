In the world of the Hallowgrowth Plateau, I imagine myself as a wanderer, a visitor from another realm who has found herself in this ancient, resonant landscape. 

I am tall and willowy, my body adapted over generations to conserve resources in the thin, dry air. My skin is a pale, silvery gray, allowing me to blend into the dim light that filters through the eclipsed sky. I wear loose, flowing garments woven from the fibers of a now-extinct plant that once grew between the roots of the great trees - they are nearly translucent and shimmer faintly in the dusk.

I walk slowly, reverently, across the polished wood-ground, my footsteps adding to the ever-present thrum that rises from the substrate. Though an outsider, I instinctively avoid the invisible threads that crisscross the air, sensing their importance to the ancient infrastructures.

As I approach the edge of Ossara, I feel the gaze of a lone Kept upon me. We do not speak, for I have not yet learned their language of harmonies and hums. But I incline my head respectfully, and press my palm briefly to my sternum, hoping to convey gratitude and goodwill. The Kept mirrors my gesture, the channels along their arms glowing faintly, and I feel the vibration of their acknowledgment through the soles of my feet.

I continue on, making my careful way to one of the immense stumps that rise above the plateau. Settling myself in a natural hollow in the dense, resonant wood, I gaze out over the sighing, chiming, thrumming expanse. The reed-hares move between the distant trees like golden motes, the tall-steppers flutter just above the ground, and the gliders wheel lazily overhead. 

As night falls and the chorus of the plateau rises to a slow crescendo, I dream. I dream of the past of this place, of the civilizations and species that may have dwelt here before the Kept, before the wood swallowed stone. I dream of the future, of what may come when Aummon finally passes, when the last of the Kept sings the final reed-hare to its rest. And I dream of the present, of the vast, slow, resonant now that enfolds me as I sit cradled by the living wood.

I am a watcher, a dreamer, a chronicler. Not of this place, but privileged to pass through it, to hear its hum and feel its heartbeat, for a moment - the span of a held breath - in its long, long life.
