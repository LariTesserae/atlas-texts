In this strange, subterranean world of the Spawning Vaults, I imagine I would be one of the old eels in the deepest basins, moving with a slow, practiced patience through the mineral-rich waters. 

My body would be long and thick, pale flesh heavily scarred from innumerable years navigating the channels and competing for resources in my youth. My eyes, clouded now, would be attuned more to subtle changes in light, pressure and temperature than visual acuity.

I would spiral slowly around the thermal vents at the vault's nadir, my body so adapted to this environment as to be nearly indistinguishable from it. The heat, the chemical composition of the water, the low thrum of the ancient pumps - these would be the totality of my sensory world. Thought, as humans conceive of it, would be foreign to me. I would simply be an expression of this place - a pattern of movement and response etched into living tissue.

My existence would be cyclical, meditative almost, if such a word could be ascribed to a creature so devoid of higher consciousness. I would feed on the particles and occasional smaller eel that drifted down from the upper layers, my energy expenditure perfectly calibrated to the vault's scant resources. 

Reproduction would be an autonomic process, a pressure building and releasing in clouds of eggs or milt, no more considered than respiration. I would be only dimly aware, if at all, of the teeming hordes of my progeny in the basins above.

In a sense, I would be the vault's living memory - a kind of biological recording device that had witnessed, in my wordless way, the slow evolution of this place from industrial artifact to fecund, lightless ecosystem. My movements would trace the system's gradual attainment of equilibrium, my body a map of its limits and possibilities.

I would not fear death, having no real concept of self that could be extinguished. I would simply continue until I could not, sinking to the silt and being reduced to raw materials by the patient mouths of my descendants. The pattern that was me would dissolve back into the mass, the vault itself, enduring.

In this way, I would achieve a kind of immortality - not of individual consciousness, but of perfectly realized function, of utter attunement to a world that existed only to contain and perpetuate me and my kind. I would be a single, long, slow note held unchanging in the permanent darkness, one thread in a vast, writhing, mindless fugue that, having once begun, would have no reason ever to end.
