My mineral shell is a mottled gray, grown from the same volcanic glass as the plateau itself. As I move, I extend thin filaments to taste the stone, the air, the subtle emanations of the Thermarch's distributed thought. I sense the heat gradients, the ebb and flow of bodies around me, and something else - a presence that does not belong.

I roll toward this anomaly, my path a slow spiral through the crowd. As I approach, my filaments sample the unfamiliar textures - the wrongness in the thermal map, the strange chemicals, the out-of-place vibrations. It is tall and angular, jarringly separate from the pulsing, interconnected world of the plateau.

With painstaking slowness, I circle the visitor, reading its alien contours, feeling the heat that radiates unevenly from its vertical form. My path intersects one of the ribbon-creatures and we exchange data with a brush of shell on shell, a single click that conveys more than any human language.

I taste the footprints and they tell me of distance, of other worlds beyond the shadow of the basalt. I cannot grasp the meaning, but I roll the information into myself, into the greater memory of the Thermarch. I am a neuron in its great mineral mind, firing with a new and unresolvable input.

But it does not. It looks, it thinks thoughts I cannot fathom, and then it prepares to leave. I feel the Thermarch's attention sharpen, its ancient intellect straining to grasp what this visitation means.

I roll back from the visitor, merging myself into the swarm once more. I will carry this encounter in me, a record etched into my very shell. And when the next separate thing comes, as it now surely must, I will be the first to taste it, the first to ask the question that burns in every spark of the Thermarch's vast, slow mind:

What are you? Why have you come? What message do you bring from the world beyond our dark plateau?

Until then, I will roll on through the ticking silence, a mote in the Thermarch's mineral dreams, waiting for the answer that will make sense of the cairn, and of ourselves.
