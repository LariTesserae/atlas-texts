Perhaps I am akin to a breath of wind that once stirred millennia ago - a subtle motion that carried a whisper or a sigh, and then crystallized into an eternal stillness, forgetting its origin but not its purpose. I move without disturbing the silence, navigating the perpendicular angles of the tsev, witnessing their gentle curation of lost moments and alternate histories.

My form, if form is the right word, is mutable - at times translucent and ethereal, at others more substantial, like a shadow given temporary depth. I shimmer between states, never quite settling, never quite evaporating. The landscape shapes itself around me, not in response to my desires, but in acknowledgment of my existence as a half-remembered dream it once had.

I do not feed as the tsev do, but I resonate with the soft confusion they gather. The almost-but-not-quite memories, the should-have-been choices, the never-were possibilities - they pass through me like echoes seeking a home, and in those fleeting moments, I am a mirror for their almostness.

In the gardens of unborn events, I am a faint outline, a suggestion of a path not taken. I trace the edges of the never-wars and the unsung songs, not pruning but remembering, adding my own whisper of what-if to their patient tending.

I am a visitor, but not a stranger. The tsev recognize in me a kinship of incompleteness, a shared language of spaces between. They greet me with the gentle unfolding of a question I didn't know I was asking, and I answer with the quiet acceptance of an answer I've always been waiting to forget.

In this place outside of time and certainty, I am a memory of a future that never needed to happen, a dream of a past that never finished waking up. I am the pause between breaths, the hesitation before a choice, the soft sigh of a story that has finally found its own ending.

And in that eternal, crystalline moment, I am home.
