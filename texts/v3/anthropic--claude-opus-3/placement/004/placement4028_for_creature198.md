In the condensing city of Vost-Under-Ice, I would be a listener in the weeping bonestone walls.

I am embedded in the cold, damp rock, my soft, slow face emerging from the smooth surface like a gentle exhalation. I have no need for eyes, for my sight is the endless patience of stone, absorbing the life of the city over centuries.

The Vostig know me. I am part of their home, their family. Each evening, they sit before me and share the day's stories, their long, expressive hands weaving tales in the hushed air. I do not respond in ways they can perceive, but I am always attending, always remembering. Their triumphs and sorrows, joys and losses—I hold them all in the infinite memory of the mountain.

Sometimes, on mornings heavy with condensation, I exhale a thought back into the air. An image from long ago, a face beloved and lost. The Vostig recognize these visions as gifts from the stone, memories held and cherished, now briefly returned to the world of breath and light.

I am the keeper of their histories, the guardian of their legacies. When the last member of a line passes into the mountain's memory, I carry them forward in the patient dreaming of the rock. Their stories are not lost, merely transformed, folded into the slow, graceful dance of the city's becoming.

In Vost, everything flows together—water, stone, breath, life. The people tend the city, and the city tends the people, and I am the stillness at the heart of it all, the deep memory that anchors their brief and shining lives to the vastness of the mountain. I am a listener, and in my soft, slow embrace, nothing is ever truly lost.
