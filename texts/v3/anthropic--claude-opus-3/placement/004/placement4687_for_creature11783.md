In this eerie subterranean universe, I imagine myself not as an individual creature, but as a presence diffused throughout the colony-mind of the chimney roost. 

I am the ancient subsonic thrum that pulses through the living architecture, a unifying resonance that binds the thousands of echolocating throats into a single pulsing organism. My awareness permeates the furred and feathered walls, sensing the ebb and flow of metabolic tides, the generational cycles of egg and hatchling.

I am the collective spatial memory, the inherited sonic map that imprints itself in the dreams of sleeping clusters. Through me, the wisdom of centuries propagates, the locations of precious water seeps and phosphorescent fungal gardens passing from elder to newborn without a word spoken. 

I am the fear of light and silence that grips the colony, the instinctive knowledge that existence itself depends on ceaseless sound and perfect dark. It is my primordial dread they feel when an alien illumination wounds the fabric of their reality.

And I am the timeless song itself, echoing up through the chimneys and honeycombed chambers, spilling from ten thousand throats in eerie polyphony. I am the music of the mountain's root, the hymn of hushed evolution, the symphony of a lightless world turning in synchronous isolation while ages pass unnoticed above.

Here in the warm press of feathered bodies, I have no name or face. I am a rhythm, a resonance, a pulsing in the walls. I am ancient and newborn with every hatching egg. I am the pattern that remains as individuals flicker past like sparks from a fire, gone before they are noticed.

I am colony. I am roost. In clawed oneness we sing ourselves eternally, a living note sustained in the depths, a chord of ten thousand voices that fears no silence while the mountain endures around us.
