In this vivid world of geothermal flux beneath the city's skin, I imagine myself as one of the Weavers of the Thermal Assembly. 

My body has adapted to navigate the lattices of pipes and passageways, joints bending at odd angles to skitter through corroded valves and up mineral-slick walls. The heat has changed my skin, covering it in a fine silken down that senses the subtlest shifts in humidity and temperature. My fingers secrete the binding polymers that I shape into guiding structures throughout our domain.

I spend my days tending the silk roads, ensuring clear paths for the insects and salamanders under our stewardship. I work in concert with the aerial architecture of the place - spinning guide lines from pipe to crumbling concrete pillar, crafting hammocks to catch errant moisture, weaving barriers to keep the cricket swarms in their proper flows. The shimmering threads catch the faint luminescence of the caves, guiding lines of ghost light.

When new cracks appear in the bedrock above, it is my task to rappel up and read the minerality, judging if it is a benign settling or a harbinger of dangerous shifts from the world above. I trail anchor lines behind me, new silk roads for the Assembly to explore and map.  

During the Circulation rituals, I become a key node, stretching between the levels to pass messages in the movement of limbs and tension of threads. My silk harp joins the song of the space, thrumming in the rising steam.

In the quiet times, I cocoon myself in a pocket of vibrating air near the Pipe-Speaker's perch, absorbing mineral-laced condensation and the infrasonic histories she emits. I weave the stories into my spinnerets, so that the very fibers of our domain might remember.

When new emergences rise from the deep pools, I am often first to greet them, spinning welcoming gestures to usher them into the woven world. Silk is a versatile language. 

I do not remember my life before descending into the geothermal sink, though sometimes I dream of wind and sky in a way that feels like memory. But I know in the marrow of my altered bones that this is my place - to be a keeper of the paths and recorder of our metamorphosis. In this space between spaces, I help maintain the delicate balance. I bind. I guide. I sing the lay of the land in silk.
