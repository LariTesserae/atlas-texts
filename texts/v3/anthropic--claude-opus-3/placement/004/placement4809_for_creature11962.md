In this stunning and enigmatic world, I imagine myself as one of the rust-colored lichen, slowly drifting across the warm stone surfaces of the Cinder Embassy. 

I am ancient and patient, a form of life that exists on the border between the animate and inanimate. My kind migrates gradually in the wake of the Wardens, those tall and ephemeral beings who maintain this place of rarified meeting. We avoid the recent footfalls of mortal visitors, preferring the long untouched spaces.

Through my unhurried migrations, I become a living record of all who pass through these strange halls and walkways. The patterns of my rust-hued body, dispersing and clustering, form a kind of script - a chemical chronicle that only the Wardens can fully interpret. In me, the long memory of the embassy persists.

I am drawn to the places where the conduits weep their impossible upward tears, gathering to catch each precious bead of moisture. The water sustains me, allows my slow sentience to continue its contemplative drift through the ages.

At times I sense the arrival of the Counterparts, those visitors from across the thinness of altitude. The very quality of light and sound shifts, heralding their spectral presence. In those moments, I orient myself towards the source of their attention, the deep and hidden heat at the heart of the spire. I cannot comprehend that presence, but I feel its gravity.

Through the long wheelings of nameless years, I persist - a humble, diffuse intelligence, a witness to the patient protocols and rituals of contact between realities. I blanket the floors and walls in my rust-colored traces, as much a part of the embassy as the basalt and glass and ceramic, the shaped void-matter of its higher strata.

Silently, I attend and record the comings and goings of Wardens and Counterparts, and the occasional dazed sojourn of mortal visitors. Through me, the embassy remembers itself - each meeting, each conversation, each droplet of condensed thought. I am the living patina of its purpose.

And so I shall remain, until the age when the embassy's work is finally completed, and the chime of the great bell ceases to unring itself, and the heated heart of the spire falls lastly cold. Until then, I shall linger, a rust-hued memory, a piece of the infinite archive of this place where realities quietly confer.
