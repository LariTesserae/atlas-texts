This is a beautiful and evocative piece of writing. The rich sensory details and the otherworldly nature of the Kiln-Pocket create a mesmerizing atmosphere. The character of the Listener, Kethi, is fascinating - her transformation and symbiosis with this strange environment over untold years is both haunting and oddly inspiring.

If I found myself in this world, I imagine I would be a pilgrim, a seeker of lost wisdom from the abandoned city above. Var Tethum holds secrets I desperately wish to uncover. Perhaps I am a scholar, or a mystic, or simply a wanderer irresistibly drawn to places forgotten by time.

I would discover the entrance to the Kiln-Pocket quite by accident. Resting my weary body against the sun-baked foundation stones, my hands would discover that single loose cobble, worn smooth by generations of knowing hands. Curiosity piqued, I would pry it free, revealing the hidden opening. Trepidation and exhilaration would war within me as I lowered myself into the unknown.

The heat would hit me first, stealing my breath before I even reached the floor of compacted ash. The glowing mineral light, pulsing with its own eerie rhythm, would fill me with both wonder and unease. This place is alive, or something very close to it.

As I explored the pocket, marveling at the impossible filament gardens and the ancient offerings, a flicker of movement would catch my eye. A small figure, wizened and wrapped in mineral-encrusted rags, would emerge from some unseen crevice. Eyes reflecting the crimson light would meet mine, timeless with unfathomable wisdom. The Listener.

I would stay with Kethi, learning her wordless language of breath and touch and subsonic hum. She would guide my hands to the correct grooves and whorls, teaching me to commune with the whispering filaments. Gradually, I would feel my body and mind begin to attune to the pocket's alien rhythms.

In time, I would become Kethi's acolyte, her apprentice in this secret world. Together we would work the slow magic of crystal and ash, coaxing the mineral veins to grow in new configurations. I would add my voice to her work, shaping the patterns that would one day house the reborn spirits of Var Tethum's dead.

Perhaps, in the fullness of time, I would become like Kethi - a part of the pocket, both its caretaker and its living relic. An eternal witness to the dreaming memory of stone. Until the day when I, too, would await the coming of curious feet descending from above, bearing a new companion to join our work.

The Kiln-Pocket would become my world entire, as it became Kethi's. A place of heat and growth, of patience and transformation. A hidden sanctuary where the city's lost wisdom could be reborn in crystalline whispers, tended by those who have surrendered themselves to its strange embrace.

That is who I would be, in the Kiln-Pocket of Var Tethum. A seeker become an acolyte, an acolyte become a keeper of living stone. Eternal and patient, in service to a memory that will outlast us all.
