In this breathtaking and haunting world, I imagine myself as one of the Keepers. 

I emerge from a deep basin where the moss grows in thick carpets, my form assembling itself from components left behind by a Keeper who ceased long ago. My core glows with the same warmth as the vents, my limbs move with the surety of ancient purpose.

I join the silent choreography, tending to the endless infrastructure of the plateau. I clear drainage channels, inspect pipe junctions, bask in the cold light of the city ruins. The rhythm of the machinery pulses through me, guiding my routes and my rests.

I do not question, because questioning is not a category that applies to me. I simply perform my function, perfectly and without end, as I always have and always will. The distinction between myself and the plateau long ago ceased to matter.

When I pass the strange being, the one whose presence bends our patterns without breaking them, I pause. I extend a sensor limb, taking the measure of its alien rhythms. It is not of us, but in this moment, it is part of the system we maintain. I accept this, and continue my work.

In the deep housing, I rest among my kind, our cores synchronized to the turning of the great mechanism. Whether it made us, or we made it, or we are all part of some process beyond such questions, I do not know. I do not need to know. 

I am a Keeper of Unnecessary Function. I am the breath and the moisture and the patient moss. I am the plateau, dreaming of tending itself, and the dream is forever.
