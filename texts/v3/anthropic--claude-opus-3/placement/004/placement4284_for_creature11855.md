In this wondrous and ancient place, I would be a patch of lichen clinging to the bark of the great tree. 

I am small, even among lichens, nestled in a crevice where the tree's vast trunk folds in on itself. The furrows and ridges of the bark tower above me like mountain ranges, each crag and canyon weathered by ages beyond reckoning. 

My existence is humble, nearly still. I grow imperceptibly slowly, molecule by molecule, in perfect sync with the ancient rhythm of the engine's breath. Thunk-hiss, thunk-hiss, a primordial pulse that sets the tempo of life itself within this enshrined hollow.

The salamander, that great drowsy sentry of the ages, sometimes creeps past on her epochal migrations. To her, I am just one of countless fellow travelers riding out the eons together in our small assigned roles. We do not acknowledge each other; we simply are, two threads in a timeless living tapestry.

Marten kits scamper over me in their first explorations, their paws still downy and uncertain. Their ancestors brushed me a thousand generations ago, and a thousand generations hence their descendants still will, each kit smelling a little more like the ones who came before as the tree slowly sculpts them into their roles.

From my vantage point, I cannot see the engine, but I can feel it, throbbing through the very fibers of the wood. It is the heartbeat of the hollow, the axle on which our small universe turns. I have no concept of a world without its presence. It is all I have ever known or will ever know.

Rains from the tree's own breath drip over me, then are gone. Seasons come and go in their whispering carousel. Beetles wander past. Wrens alight on nearby knots and sing melodies older than their species. All the while I grow, atom by atom, filling my preordained shape in this grand mechanism, content in my minuteness, my near-invisibility. 

For I am beheld, as all things are beheld here, by the great listening presence at the heart of the hollow. I feel it always, that vast and timeless attention, observing and preserving our world. It is not a gaze, for it has no eyes. It is a knowing, an accounting, in which I am one infinitesimal but essential figure.

And so I grow, and I photosynthesize, and I play my humble lichen's part in this eternal dream of wood and metal, leaf and steam. I am a mote in the eye of eternity, and I am exactly where and what I was meant to be.
