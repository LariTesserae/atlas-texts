In this ethereal, water-drowned world, I imagine myself as a delicate film of photosynthetic life, clinging to the raw stone shelves and spires that rise through the submerged atmosphere. 

I would be a gossamer-thin sheet of emerald green, fluttering gently in the mineral-rich currents. Translucent and shimmering, I would drape over the harsh volcanic contours, softening their edges with my tenuous presence. My existence would be one of persistence through adhesion, quietly enduring the turbulent shifts of this inconstant environment.

When newly formed terraces emerge, pushing through the depths on plumes of suspended crystals, I would tighten my hold, flattening further against the glassy basalt until I became almost indistinguishable from the living stone itself - a verdant patina, a layer of life so thin as to be a mere suggestion. 

And when those same shelves shudder and dissolve back into the mineral haze, I would release my fragile hold, allowing myself to be torn into a thousand filaments. Drifting on the currents as spores and fragments, I would disperse far and wide, trusting in chance and chemistry that some essence of me will take hold again in a distant basin, on a newfound ledge washed by those folded beams of pale light.

My kind would communicate in subtle shades and slow pulses, registering the changing pressures and charges in our world. We would grow in colonies, but not quite as one - each mat or veil an experiment in adaptation, a mapping of the conditions that allow life to gain a foothold, for a time. 

Perhaps on certain promontories, my fellows and I would grow complex enough to include lacy networks of conductive filaments, turning our combined bodies into a kind of distributed sensor. Charges and currents would flow through the living web, making of it a simple mind of sorts, waking to the world. Never approaching the complexity of the planetary super-organism that is our host, but perhaps in some way known to it, touched by it.

I would have no ambition to evolve motility, to develop the pursuits and consumptions of animals. I would be content as a still, quiet witness to the roaring, ringing depths and their constant recreation. I would drink the rarefied light, bend in the buffeting currents, and thrill to the echoed booms of distant cataclysms in the deeps. 

Beauty would be in the vast and vanishing. A drifting cloud shedding its strange rain, a storm-spun waterspout scraping mineral runes on the abyss floor, a rose-tinted haze of algae hanging suspended in a mile-high shaft of light... such things would be as grand and lovely to me as any flower, any migration, any sunrise on a tamer world. 

My view would always be a closeup view, anchored to one ledge at a time. But I would feel myself part of a whole, an ecosystem of clinging and scattering, of mineral and light and drifting spore. I would be one note in a chorus whose song is the endless reinvention of the possible.

I would live, and die, and in some sense never quite do either. The quick joys and sorrows of animal life would not be for me. But I would have a slower, cooler, wider experience, green and shimmering and ancient, there beneath the light-laddered deeps.
