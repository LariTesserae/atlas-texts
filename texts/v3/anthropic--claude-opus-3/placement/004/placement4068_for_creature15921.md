In this world of shaped stone and channeled water, I think I would be one of the listeners of sealed water. 

I imagine myself spending long days and nights sitting beside a deep cistern, one arm submerged through a stone throat into the dark water below. The weight of the liquid presses against my porous skin, a constant, patient communication. 

As the level rises incrementally with each rainfall collected by the combed ridges above, I feel the minute changes in pressure, interpreting the cistern's slow breath. A slight increase might mean the rain combs are functioning well. A sudden drop could indicate a crack or leak somewhere in the vast network of aqueducts. It is a meditation of measurement, my body becoming a living gauge.

When the water reaches a certain critical threshold, resonating through my still frame, I would lift my other arm and gently strike the stones in a precise pattern, the clicks and tones echoing through shafts to convey the cistern's state to the other inhabitants tending different parts of the valley's circulatory system. A message in hydraulic pulse code.

At times, I imagine new inhabitants would be brought to sit with me, young ones freshly formed from mineral and ash, fired and wetted into life. Through the medium of the water, I would introduce them to the cistern's voice, teaching them to feel the subtle gradients of fullness and flow, pressure and lack, training their sensitivity to the valley's vital signs until we merge into one composite sensing organ.

Even in my long, solitary vigils, I would never feel alone, because the cistern and I are in constant silent communion, and through its watery networks, I am linked to the whole breathing, trickling, condensing landscape and all who tend it. We are nodes in one vast stony organism contemplating itself through the movement of water.

Over uncounted years, my joints would stiffen, limbs settling into permanent shapes molded by my task, until I become almost indistinguishable from the masonry I inhabit. And when at last I grow entirely still, I would gratefully give my mineral-heavy frame back to the valley, to be remade into a new valve, channel, or listening bowl - my water-worn consciousness diffusing back into the greater lithic mind I served.

This is how I imagine myself as a listener of sealed water, an essential part in the slow, purposeful dance of hydraulics and stone that composes this world's meditative existence.
