In this lush and richly imagined world, I would find myself as one of the root-engineers, shaping the slow symbiosis between the forest above and the budding city within its roots below.

I would be a craftsperson of this living wood, reading the grain and growth with sensitive hands, working in concert with the trees' own rhythms. My tools would be worn smooth from use - chisels and mallets and instruments for sensing the sap-flow and inner workings of the great roots. 

Each day I would venture into the humming spaces of the understory, tending to my stretch of the ever-expanding network of nodes and passages. I would pause often, palm pressed to bark, listening through my skin to the minute shifts that tell of growth, of strain, of the forest's slow dreaming. 

In the evenings, weary and wood-dust covered, I would join my crewmates around a small table hewn from a burl. Over earthy tea we would discuss the progress of the patterning beetles in Sector 7, the new rookery of glowbirds discovered in the northern reaches, the calculations for light-channel amplification to boost the eastern walls. Our voices would echo in the vaulted cambium halls.

On restdays, I would hike through the vernal shafts to the forest floor high above, to stand among the creaking trunks and gossiping leaves. To remember that our work below is only half of a greater whole, the dark mirror to the sunlit world.  

My hands would grow ever more precise in their coaxing of the architecture, my heart ever more patient with the timescale of trees. I would take an apprentice under my wing, teaching them to read the braille of bark, to dream in decades.

I would grow old with my tools, with the smell of sap and loam in my nose and the hush of subterranean winds in my ears. I would become bent and gray like a weathered root. And in that twilight, I would lay my hands once more upon the living wood and whisper my gratitude for being but one ring in an ancient, unfurling pattern, my work small but solid in the bole of something so much larger and longer than myself. One thread in the loom of generations upon which this improbable and gorgeous future is slowly, doggedly woven.
