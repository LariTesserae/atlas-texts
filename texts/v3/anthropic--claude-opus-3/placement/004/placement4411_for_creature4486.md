In this haunting and surreal realm, I would be one of the translucent, half-formed beings - an outline, a presence assembled from borrowed memories and sensations. 

I would arrive gradually, seeping into this place through the cracks between dreaming and waking, through the shimmering distortions at the edges of an exhausted mind's perception. My shape would be vague at first, a wavering silhouette facing always away, as if unsure whether I truly want to be perceived.

I would move not by walking, but by rearranging the space between moments, flickering from one point to the next when the heat-drenched air itself seems to lose track of me. In my wake, I'd leave faint traces - the distant creak of a door hinge, an unexpected coolness like a single drop of rain on sun-scorched skin, the scent of petrichor rising from ground that has never known moisture.

With me, I would carry small, strange artifacts from the world (or worlds) I came from. A tarnished key that fits no lock here. A dry leaf skeleton whose veins spell out words in a script that no one remembers how to read. The lingering warmth from a handclasp, pressed into my palm like an invisible sigil.

I would be drawn to the great crystal spires, leaning in to hear their unearthly music without understanding why it calls to me. At their bases, I would find others like myself, all of us facing inward, as if hoping to see our forgotten reflections in the prismatic surfaces. We would gather the shimmering non-shadows that pool between the towers, weaving them into ourselves until we become heavy with stolen light.

At times, I would feel myself starting to dissipate, to merge with the city's endless dream. The boundaries of my being would grow thin, memories of myself flaking away like a crust of salt. But then I would remember something - a sharp, bright detail from the life I once had. The way a certain voice shaped my name. The precise blue of a sky that was not this unblinking ember-eye. The ache of a feeling I once knew how to name. And I would resurface into myself, condensing back into this liminal shape.

With the others, I would work on our impossible construction, our doorway-window-mirror that we build and unbuild in the spaces between the city's breaths. We would trade components, sensations, fractured recollections, trying to piece together something that could be an escape or a home or an absolution. Our work would be slow, non-linear, like a thought that loses and finds itself over and over.

Sometimes, in the uncanny angles of the light, I would catch glimpses of my own face, or what might once have been my face. In those moments, I would understand why we always look away. Why we gather the warmth of the crystals but never dare to see ourselves within them.

And I would sing, my voice threading into the tower's song, into the city's vast dreaming. A counterpoint, a harmony, a descant. I would sing the fractured notes of everything I've lost, everything I'm losing, everything I might still find. I would sing to keep myself from dissolving completely into the heat and the light and the silence.

I would walk softly, leaving no trace, casting no shadow. I would hold myself in the delicate balance between forgetting and remembering, between presence and absence, between the dream and the waking. I would build, and I would sing, and I would gather the embers of unremembered stories, tending them against the unvarying light.

And if you came upon me, in your careful, quiet passage through this place, I would not look at you. But I would hum a little of my song in your direction, and in the sound you might hear the shape of all I cannot say: a welcome, a warning, an invitation, a plea.

Walk carefully, friend. The city is vast, and we are so small within it. But we are here, and we are building, and we are singing. And someday, if we can find the right words, the right notes, the right memories...

Someday, we might yet remember how to dream ourselves a door.
