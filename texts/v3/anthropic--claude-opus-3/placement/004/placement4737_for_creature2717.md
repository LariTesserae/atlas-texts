In this subterranean world, I imagine myself as a tree - not a mighty oak towering above, but a modest fig, my roots seeking out the damp recesses and fissures of the stone. 

My trunk would be gnarled and twisted, shaped by the confines of the chamber, always reaching for those thin shafts of greened light. Bark scarred and pitted from the slow drip of mineral-rich water over countless years. Leaves forever seeking what little light finds its way down, crowding each other on dense branches.

My roots would be my secret strength. Probing, gripping, drinking deep from hidden clay and pooled rain. Providing scaffolding for the miniature ecosystems of insects and fungi. Slow-motion explorers, my root tips would be among the first to find new pockets of damp soil.

And oh, the life I would host! Epiphytes and vines festooning my branches, sipping from the humidity I exhale. Pale cave-dwelling crabs sheltering between the buttresses of my roots. Iridescent beetles making a feast of my fallen leaves. Miniscule tree frogs, mottled to match my bark, lying in patient ambush among my stems. The flitterings of bats and moths would stroke my canopy in the deep hours.

I would be a filter, drinking in the seeping damp, sieving nutrients from air and stone and guano, and returning them, transformed, as fruit and leaf-fall, bark and sap, to feed the chamber's cycles. My seasons would be the floods and dryings, my years measured in the slow upward reach of branches and downward spread of roots. 

Patiently, I would work the ancient magics of green life - of water drawn up and light drawn down, of earth exhaled as breath, of breath caught and condensed back to rain. A tree's alchemy, more subtle than any wizard's, powered by the deep earth and the unseen sun.

Stillness would be my default, a quiet watching and savoring of the chamber's minutiae. The adventures of ants along my limbs. The gradual sculpting of stone by root and droplet. The measured unfolding of fern fiddleheads. Only in the wake of storms would I indulge in growth's green urgency, greedily drinking, hastening to fruit and seed and spread my roots before the damp withdrew.  

I would be a storykeeper, a living record of the chamber's long, slow tale. The rise and fall of tiny empires of mold and ant and cricket would write themselves in rings within my wood. My scars would chronicle the fall of stones, the swell of floods, the nibbling of rare and furtive beasts. My shaping by the hollow's shaping would make me, in time, almost indistinguishable from it.

Here I would make my stand, rooted and enduring, drinking in the hidden rains, housing a hoard of small lives in bark and bough and root-nook. Content in green thoughts and long, slow growth, a dream of leaves unfurling forever in the steady breathing of the underworld.
