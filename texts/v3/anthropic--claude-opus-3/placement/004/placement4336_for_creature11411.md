I would find myself on that singing shelf high on the mountain's shoulder. Not as a person, but as something woven into the essence of the place itself.

I think I would be one of those gnarled, low-growing trees with bark like braided rope and waxy leaves. Rooted there for a very long time, part of the shelf's patient, gradual rhythm of being. My branches would provide a perch for the dark bird on its quiet passings. The mist from the cloud that sometimes swaddles the shelf would condense on my leaves, each drop lingering a long while before finally falling to nurture the moss below.  

When the keeper comes, whether in the form of the woman with limestone hands or the angled seep or the rim-respecting moss, I would be a living landmark helping define the bowl's place on the shelf. A silent sentry and witness to the ancient ritual of tending.

As the wind cycles through the shelf's slow chords, resonating through fluted rock, my leaves would shiver faintly in the breeze. Not enough to add my own voice to the shelf's singing, but just enough to acknowledge and appreciate the notes as they flow by and through me, as they have for ages. 

In the stretches between keepers, I would abide, an steadfast green presence amidst patient stone and trickling water. Waiting, with the bird, with the bowl, with the shelf itself, for the next form the keeping will take. Enduring at the pace of stone, content to simply be, there in that singular place where the mountain's shoulder holds a garden-room open to the sky and lets the wind give voice to its ancient, mineral music.
