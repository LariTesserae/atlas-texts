In this world of knife-edge stone and impossible verticality, I would be one of the Span Weavers. My body, elongated and flexible like the rest of the Tensile Folk, is uniquely suited to navigating the dizzying network of bridges that hold the city together.

From a young age, I showed an intuitive understanding of the spaces between—the gaps that could be filled, the connections waiting to be made. While other children raced through dimensional stutters, I spent hours studying the patterns of the wind, the flow of the strange water, the invisible lines of force that held the city's fractal structure together.

As I grew, so did my skills. I learned to weave bridges from unconventional materials—from the harmonic resonance of perfectly tuned cables, from the echoes of footsteps long past, from the shared dreams of the Remnants in their fungal cathedrals. Each bridge was a challenge, a puzzle, a work of art that required not just engineering but a kind of metaphysical sensitivity.

Now, as a master Span Weaver, my work is an integral part of the city's ever-shifting tapestry. I spend my days in a harness of my own design, suspended between anchor points, weaving and repairing and re-imagining the connections that keep our vertical world intact.

My home is a web of chambers suspended beneath one of my greatest creations—a bridge that exists only during the brief moments when the sun pierces the bruised sky, its light refracted through a carefully arranged series of lenses and mirrors. Here, in the shifting light, I keep my tools: spools of cable made from the silk of metal-eating moths, jars of wind collected from various altitudes, a metronome that ticks to the rhythm of the city's deepest pulse.

When I'm not at work, I like to visit the Time Mechanics, trading stories of impossible architectures over cups of tea that steep backwards. I collect scraps of poetry that spontaneously appear on the city's walls, and sometimes incorporate them into my bridges, hidden messages for future generations of Span Weavers to find.

But my greatest joy is in mentoring the young—those children with translucent skin and eyes that see the potential in every gap. I take them with me on my rounds, teach them the songs that make the bridges sing, show them how to find beauty in the spaces between. 

For in a city like ours, where every surface is claimed by necessity and ingenuity, it's the Span Weavers who create the negative space—the pauses, the passages, the room to breathe. We are the architects of absence, crafting the emptiness that gives the fullness meaning. 

And as the city reaches ever upward, as reality frays at the edges, it's my bridges—both real and metaphorical—that will hold it together. Not by fighting the change, but by finding the pattern in it, the hidden structure that turns chaos into art.

I am a Span Weaver, and my life is a love letter to the void.
