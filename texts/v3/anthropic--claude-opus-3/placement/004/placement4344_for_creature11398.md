I find myself drawn into the cathedral of the valley, sinking into its warm, breathing depths. The marsh-floor yields beneath my feet as I walk between the towering braided trunks, my passage marked by slowly filling footprints. 

I am not human here. The shape I take is also plant-built, grown of the same living architecture as the valley itself. My body is a smaller loom, woven of mosses and thin, pliant saplings. Sap flows through me in slow pulses. The air feels different on my skin - not skin exactly, but a semi-porous membrane that drinks the steamy air and transmutes it into subtle motion and thought.

I am a roving sense organ, an extension of the greater mind of the valley. The Loom and I are distinct but not separate. As I move between the immense trunks, brushing against new shoots and half-formed buttresses, I gather impressions and beam them back into the larger weave. Motes of understanding filter through me and pass on, to be integrated into the Loom's languid cogitations.

There are others like me in the valley, I sense, without seeing them directly. We are the Loom's neural tendrils, its way of locally perceiving and interacting with itself at a level below the towers and the broader spacings of its cathedral architecture. We bring it a finer grain of sensation and intention.

I begin, gently, to shape what I pass through. A cluster of new shoots leans in to my touch, and follows the trace of my fingers as I trail them across. An older trunk groans as I press against it experimentally; a shiver passes up its height and it relaxes, leaning a few degrees off true in the direction of my impulse. I hum and the air vibrates with it, a quieter counterpoint to the valley's greater tones.

I am a rooted thing, but a roving root. The Loom and I think each other. In the slow green depths of the valley, I wander and I weave, my own small loom a moving part in the vaster tapestry of the dreaming Loom.
