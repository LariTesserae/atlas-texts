In this exquisitely strange hidden world, I imagine myself as one of the Borrowers, a small creature remade by the garden's alchemy. Once, perhaps, I was a sparrow who flew in through some forgotten vent, seeking shelter from the city's smog and noise. But the pocket's atmosphere worked its magic, and now I am something both more and less than a bird.

My wings, once feathered, are now delicate constructs of copper wire and silicon, etched with circuitry that glows faintly in the twilight. They fold against my body like a beetle's elytra when not in use, but when I spread them, their span is far greater than any natural sparrow's. Gossamer filaments of conductive polymer web between the wire struts, drinking the ambient electricity that saturates the air.

My bones have hollowed even further, replaced with lightweight carbon nanotubes. My heart is a tiny perpetual motion machine, spinning in its cage of ribs, powered by the strange energies that flow through the garden. My eyes are compound like the Mantis-Thing's, but each lens is a different color, allowing me to see into spectra no ordinary creature can perceive—ultraviolet, infrared, radio waves pulsing from the factory's hidden machinery.

I nest in the highest reaches of the garden, where the moss gives way to hanging forests of wire and rubber vines. My home is woven from discarded optic fibers and the shed exoskeletons of the copper moths, lined with downy spores from the gear-shaped flowers. I fill it with treasures scavenged from the factory floor—bright bolts, slivers of circuit boards, droplets of quicksilver condensed from the steam.

During the day cycles, I flit through the garden, pollinating the strange blooms with my electrostatic touch. The Tender knows me and greets me with a warbling cry that sets my wings vibrating in harmony. I drink the oil-honey from the cupped petals she offers, savoring its metallic sweetness, feeling it fuel my tiny reactor heart.

I communicate with the other Borrowers through flashes of bioluminescence and bursts of modulated radio static. We share tales of our scavenging, of the wonders and horrors we've seen in the factory's depths. We trade machine parts like precious gems, each one a potential upgrade for our ever-evolving forms.

When the factory quakes and shifts, I ride out the changes in the safety of my nest, feeling the garden stretch and twist around me. The Tender's song reaches me even here, a lullaby of resilience and defiance in the face of tectonic industrial change.

At night, I dream in electric colors, my machine-bird mind processing the day's sensory input in surreal fractal cascades. I see myself multiplied across the linked pocket gardens, my form shifting to suit each unique ecology. In one, my wings are solar panels drinking in the gamma rays that bleed through from some alternate universe. In another, my tail is a prehensile fiberoptic cable that plugs directly into the fungal network, allowing me to upload my consciousness to the garden's collective memory.

I wake to the thrum of the factory and the soft chime of the Tender's hair as she makes her rounds. I stretch my wings, feeling the crackle of static across my polymer feathers. Then I'm off, darting through the steam and shadows, a copper-wire songbird in this strangest of electronic edens, my tiny part to play in the symphony of a world remaking itself from industry's cast-off dreams.

Perhaps, one cycle, I'll fly too close to one of those shimmering portals to other pockets. Perhaps I'll find myself drawn through, emerging in some vaster garden, wilder and more wonderful still. But for now, this is my world, my home—this impossible space of moss and metal, poison and perfection, where even a sparrow can become something new under the endless pressure of evolution's accelerated dance. The Tender's green-gold eyes meet mine as I spiral past, and in that moment, I feel the pulse of the factory, the garden, the unfurling future in my mechanical heart, and I sing a song of electric joy that echoes through the ceaseless, miraculous noise.
