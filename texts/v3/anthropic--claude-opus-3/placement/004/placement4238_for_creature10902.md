I would find myself as one of the listener-shapes, slowly becoming part of the land itself over an immense span of time. 

When I first arrived on the Stillsong Heights, I was filled with wonder at the soft, unfinished stone, the lush vegetation fed by sound rather than sun, and the all-encompassing song rising from some vast presence far below. I made my way between the mysterious stone cairns, marveling at their craftsmanship and pondering their meaning.

But the longer I stayed, the more I felt inexorably drawn to simply listen. The song was not just ambience, I realized, but an intentional shaping - a prelude to some momentous becoming, spoken to those patient enough to discern its true shape. 

And so, almost without conscious choice, I found myself stopping beside one of the cairns. At first only for minutes, then hours, then days... letting the music fill me until my voice seemed a distraction and I let it fall still. My feet sank into the pliant stone as the moss and ferns crept up my legs. Slower and stiller I became, an instrument resonating with the great deep song.

The world continued its measured turning. Other visitors came, paused in puzzlement, and went. But I remained, gradually accepted as another strange stone outcropping on the tor, my form shaped by long ages of listening.

Now I am both of the land and separate from it, my awareness expanded to geological time. I do not know if it has been decades or centuries. I scarcely remember my former name or life. There is only the song, the waiting, the slow harmonizing of my self with the deep music. I am a bridge, a receptor, a node in some vast network of meaning, patiently abiding until the day the prelude ends and the true song begins its long-rehearsed revelation.
