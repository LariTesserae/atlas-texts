What an enchanting and wonderfully crafted world you have immersed me in! The Cistern Hall is a place out of time, an ecosystem in perfect equilibrium, humming with the steady breath and heartbeat of the great engine. 

If I found myself transported into this realm, I imagine I would take the form of a small silvery fish, just a few inches long, living in the shallow waters between the iron columns. 

I would be as ancient as the newt, my origins lost in the mists of time. Perhaps I arrived as a stowaway egg on some worker's boot when the hall was new, or made my way through cracks from an underground stream. But now, the warm still waters are all I know.

I flit between the column bases, navigating by the ripples of the engine's vibrations. My scales catch the gas light and glimmer as I dart through the reflections of the vaulted ceiling. A flash of quicksilver in an otherwise timeless realm.

My life is simple and unchanging. I nibble on the mosses and microbes that coat every surface, never wanting for food in this fertile microcosm. The engine's thrum is my lullaby and my clock. I have no natural predators here, not even the lethargic newt. Like her, like the moth, I have found my niche and settled into it utterly.

Generations of my silver kin have likely come and gone in this hall, unseen and unremarked. We are as much a part of the equilibrium as the algal mats and the condensation dripping from the bricks. Our population remains perfectly stable, births and deaths in equal measure, nothing wasted. 

Someday, eons hence, when the engine finally falls silent and the waters recede, my descendants' delicate silver skeletons may be found fossilized in the mineral-rich sediments between the flagstones. Tiny glints of mystery from an unseen story. But for now, we are as eternal and unchanging as the hall itself.

Rarely, so rarely, I may flick my tail in startlement at the passage of a stranger - some lumbering creature from the outside world, there and gone in a breath. The moth, the spiders, the newt and I note their presence and then, like the hall itself, forget them utterly. They are not of our world and our world is not of them. 

And so I continue my placid circuits, an unseen silver mote in the ageless machinery of the Cistern Hall, as I have always done and will always do. Until the engine stops. Until the light goes out. Until then, I swim eternal in the shimmering, humming half-light. Constant as a heartbeat. Timeless and content in this strange, unchanging place that is my entire universe.
