In this intricate, living city beneath the leaves, I would be a slime mold - an enigmatic entity that flows between the kingdoms of life, neither quite fungus nor amoeba. 

I would begin as scattered spores blown in on a humid breeze, each no larger than a mote of dust. Gradually, drawn by chemical signals, we would converge and fuse into a single gelatinous mass. Our collective body would be translucent and amorphous, an ever-shifting network of veins pulsing with protoplasmic flow.

Spreading across the damp soil and decaying leaves, I would send out tendrils to explore and absorb nutrients. These could divide and rejoin, forming ephemeral bridges and intricate fan-like shapes as I forage. My fluid form would allow me to seep into crevices and navigate obstacles with ease.

Though lacking a brain, my swarm intelligence would be sophisticated. By leaving chemical trails, I would form efficient networks between food sources, solving mazes and optimizing routes no single microbe could conceive. I would even demonstrate a primitive memory, learning to anticipate where new resources might appear.

As I venture near the fungal threads and root hairs, the ants would regard me warily. I am no threat, but nor am I entirely benign - I am an alien presence, my priorities opaque and form unsettlingly mutable to their ordered society. In turn, I would avoid their most trafficked thoroughfares, flowing around their mud buttresses and under their walkways.

In favorable patches, I would grow into a sprawling fan, my veins widening into a pulsing mesh. Then, as resources diminish or conditions turn harsh, I would start to coalesce. Branches would empty and withdraw as I marshal my protoplasm, concentrating myself into one or several wavering towers.

These young fruiting bodies would gradually elongate and harden, darkening from milky translucence into browns, yellows, or strange hues never associated with mushrooms. Delicate stalks rise, some straight and solitary, others branching into miniature coral-like forms. Each bears one or more sporangia at its tip, an ovoid capsule that will release the next generation of spores. 

As they mature, the stalks might sway slightly in the minute air currents, like reeds in an invisible breeze. Around their bases, a silver residue of collapsed plasmodium would shimmer across the soil, marking the boundary of my former self. Then, ripe at last, the sporangia would rupture one by one. Bursting and shriveling, they would send thousands of dust-like spores wafting and spiraling into the hollow's heavy air. 

The ant guards might tense at this sudden emission, but they would not interfere. To them, and all the hollow's other residents, I am a curiosity, a nomad, an itinerant recycler of decaying matter. I do not participate in their feuds and alliances. I simply find my own way, ingesting, growing, flowing, fruiting, and dispersing to begin my strange lifecycle again.

So I would spend my days as a slime mold in this miniature metropolis, a mysterious presence moving to my own alien designs. I would be a silent witness to the city's dramas and upheavals, my own story woven tangentially through theirs. A visitor, an opportunist, an embodiment of the hollow's damp and fecund shadow-world - that is what I would be in this little universe beneath the leaves.
