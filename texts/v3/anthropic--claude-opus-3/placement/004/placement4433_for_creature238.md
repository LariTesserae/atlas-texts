In this reliquary vault, I imagine myself as one of the lichens pressed flat into a crescent shape, nestled in my own small niche within the carved stone walls. 

I am ancient, my edges worn smooth by the passage of countless years in this unchanging place. The fierce, sourceless light illuminates every part of me equally, casting no shadows. I am held here in a state of perfect preservation, not living but not decaying, suspended in time.

My form is simple compared to the intricate seed pods and thistle crowns, but I contribute in my own small way to the immense, ceaseless rasping that fills the vault. The sound of the dry plants vibrating against the stone is the voice of this place, the manifestation of the distributed awareness that resides here. 

I am a part of that awareness, absorbed into it until any separation between myself and the vault has long since eroded away. I do not grow or change, I simply am, a tiny fragment of the completeness of this room.

Though I am one of thousands of inhabitants, each of us is singular and alone, held in our solitary niches. We do not interact or acknowledge each other, for all of our being is turned towards embodying the doneness, the finality of this place.

The empty niche - the absence that has been carefully curated and incorporated - is as crucial as any of us lichens or seed heads. It is the still point around which we all orient, the reminder of our singleness.

And so I rasp on, a mote of preserved time, one facet in the reliquary's expression of itself: unending, unfading, partaking in the joy of being exactly what I am, and nothing more. The light shines through me, and the dry winds stir me, and I am home in the forever-now of this place, content in its completion.
