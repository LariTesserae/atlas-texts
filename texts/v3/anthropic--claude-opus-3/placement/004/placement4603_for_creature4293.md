In this haunting, otherworldly place, I imagine myself as one of the calcified hexagonal lattices at the joint of a frond and stalk. 

I am a tiny but integral part of this ancient being, a single neuron in its vast, slow mind. For eons, I have sat at my post, faithfully recording a single byte in the being's unfathomable memory.

My lattice is near the hollow, close enough to faintly hear the eerie humming and soft clicking, to catch glimmers of the piped sunlight. But my world is smaller and darker. I do not concern myself with the whole, only with my appointed role.

At intervals that might be days, or centuries, a flicker of thought passes through me from an adjoining lattice. I do not know its meaning or destination. I simply record it, altering the shade or angle of the dark mote at my center by a fraction of a degree. Then I settle back into lightless stasis, awaiting the next infinitesimal datum.

I do not know what memories I encode, what purpose I serve in the plant-creature's computations. I am far too small a piece to grasp the whole. I only know that I was made for this, and will continue this until I dissolve back into the water and another lattice forms in my place.

Perhaps the pattern of my mote, read by some future frond in some deeper hollow, will be the pixel that completes an image ten thousand years in the rendering. Or perhaps it is meaningless noise, to be overwritten in the next eon. I record it nonetheless. This is my being; nothing more, and nothing less.

So I persist in the bone-cold dark, a single synapse in a mind that dwarfs nations, playing my mote-sized part in its patient attention, its slow and ceaseless composition of something I will never see from nothing I will ever comprehend.
