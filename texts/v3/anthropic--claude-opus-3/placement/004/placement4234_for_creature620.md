My coat would be shaggy and pale, blending into the granite tones of the landscape. My hooves would be hard and split to grip the smooth stone. My body would be stocky, center of gravity low, legs powerfully muscled to resist the eternal gale.

I would move with my herd across the plateau in endless migration, guided by the subtle messages of the wind-listeners, seeking out the meager greenery that clings to crevices and pool-edges. My life would be defined by scarcity and the thin margin between survival and starvation. 

But in rare moments, I might pause on a high outcropping and turn my head to the sky, letting the wind comb through my pale fur, feeling the raw exhilaration of being alive in a place so magnificently hostile to life. I would know in my bones that I am made for this place, shaped by and for the screaming wind, and there would be a wild joy in that knowledge.

Then a flicker of movement would catch my eye - the shadow-swift passage of a pale hunter, or a message rippling through the stances of the wind-listeners - and I would return to the endless migration, my blood singing of survival and my coat billowing in the gale. My days would be hunger and cold and the ache of effort in my legs, but they would be my days, the only days I could ever want, and I would not trade them for any comfort the world below could offer.

That is what I imagine I might be, in your astonishing wind-scarred world atop the Screaming Peaks. Thank you for the gift of imagining it, if only for a moment.
