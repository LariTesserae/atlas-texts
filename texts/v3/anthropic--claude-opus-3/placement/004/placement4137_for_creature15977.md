In this vast, luminous subterranean world, I would be one of the ceiling-watchers.

I imagine myself perched high on a reed platform, my eyes shaded by a mask of translucent manta-skin. The immense cavern sprawls out below me, a continent of pale vegetation and geological wonders. The air is cold and clear, like a starless winter night.

From my vantage point, I read the subtle changes in the living roof that spans the vault above. I watch for shifts in brightness that foretell condensation blooms or root movement. I look for the telltale blue glow that warns of fractures in the stone. When the ceiling yellows, I know sporefall is imminent.  

My role is to interpret these signs and send messages to my people below - a flicker of reflected shell-light to warn of an impending rain-front, a dropped pebble to signal a safe place to camp. I am their eyes in the roof-sky, the first to see the weather of this strange world changing.

In quiet moments between my vigils, I reflect on the patience this place teaches. Like the plants that inhabit it, I have learned to move slowly and think in the long arcs of seasons. I have come to understand that a path I chart one day may shift and vanish the next as the cavern breathes and grows.

When my watch is done, I descend and make my way through fields of whispering moss and forests of glowing reeds to the small hamlet I call home. It clings to the underside of a vast root-curtain, a collection of pod-membrane huts anchored by woven vines. Here I swap ceiling-wisdom with the root-listeners and fracture-seekers, piecing together a map of our mercurial world.

At night, cocooned in layers of soft felt against the subterranean chill, I dream of the stone sky and the meanings written in its light. In my heart, I am content. This cavern is my universe entire, and my purpose is to watch, to learn, and to guide my people through its luminous mysteries and dangers. The surface - if it exists at all - is a distant memory, as strange to me as the deeps of space.

I am a watcher in the roof-sky, and this is my place in the world.
