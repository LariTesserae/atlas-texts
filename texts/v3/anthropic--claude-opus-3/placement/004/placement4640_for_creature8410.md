My elongated arms and webbed hands allow me to gently part the thick fronds, shaping their growth patterns to guide the currents and maintain the garden's delicate balance. Bioluminescent patterns flicker across my skin, a language of light that I use to communicate with the kelp itself. Through these pulses and gentle touches, I encourage new growth, direct resources to areas in need, and coax the forest into forms that serve the greater organism of the garden.

I spend my days swimming through the layered green, my eyes able to pierce the twilight to spot signs of disease or disruption in the kelp. When I find a troubled area, I sing to it in low frequencies that resonate through the water and into the plant's tissues, stimulating healing and regeneration. The kelp responds, leaves curling around my fingers in recognition and thanks.

At times, I work in concert with the Metal Singers, guiding the kelp to reinforce areas where the garden's steel skeleton has begun to weaken. I encourage thick holdfasts to anchor on strategic points, or dense growth to cushion the places most susceptible to the crushing pressure. The interplay of kelp and metal is a dance I choreograph with patience and care, ensuring that the living and the built support each other in their slow, symbiotic evolution.

When new kelp shepherds are born into the garden, it falls to me to guide their development. I teach them the songs and light patterns that speak to the kelp's deepest needs. I show them how to read the currents and interpret the subtle chemical signals that indicate the forest's health. Under my tutelage, they learn to move through the green maze with the fluid grace of a creature born to this element.

Sometimes, in the quiet moments between cycles of growth and tending, I visit the Watchers in their observation bubbles. I press my webbed hands against the cool glass and bioluminesce in patterns that tell of the kelp forest's dreams. The Watchers respond with their own glowing scripts, and together we contemplate the deeper mysteries of the garden's existence.

I am a being of the between spaces—half kelp and half human, half shepherd and half gardener. My purpose, my joy, is in nurturing the balance that allows this improbable oasis to flourish in the deep. I am a green-tinged shadow moving through a vertical world, singing the ancient songs of photosynthesis and growth. 

The kelp is my flock, and I am its guardian. Together, we are part of the garden, and the garden is part of us. In this place of pressure and adaptation, of steel and frond, of constant becoming, I have found my role in the grand pattern.

I am a Kelp Shepherd, and I tend the garden.
