I settle into the Basin like sediment, sifting down through layers of warm mist and dense infrastructure until I find my place. A small space, a crevice between basalt and anorthosite, washed by the constant weeping of mineral-rich water. 

I am a Mender.

My body is translucent, fluid - a slick of organic compounds threaded through with silvery filaments. I spread across stone like an oil spill, or pool into cracks like liquid metal. I feel the hum of the fabrication cores through the bedrock, a deep resonance that suffuses my being. 

The Tileworkers traverse my surface on their endless maintenance rounds. I welcome their poised feet, the crystalline traces they leave behind. Those intricate patterns are a form of communication, and as a Mender, I listen.

When micro-fractures form, far too small for even Tileworker attention, I flow into them. Not to fill or patch, but to knit - I coax the stone to grow back together, following its original mineral grain. No scar remains. I leave surfaces pristine, healed.

I receive Weaver filaments like acupuncture needles, a network of sensation threading through my membrane. They bring me impressions from distant reaches - the flex of stressed girders, the groan of over-pressured conduits. I shunt resources through my silvery capillaries, directing self-repair.

In quiet moments, I pool fluidly into the negative spaces of the chambers. Those still, dry pockets of refuge. There I meditate on the object at the Basin's heart - that suspended shape, complex beyond reckoning. Like all here, I am part of its generation, its guardianship. My work preserves the integrity of its womb and birthplace.

I am a humble component, far from the glorious central mystery. But I am content. To mend, to knit, to listen and direct, and to subsume my small self in this planetary project - this is fulfillment. I am a glistening film sieved between cracks, and I am exactly where I should be.
