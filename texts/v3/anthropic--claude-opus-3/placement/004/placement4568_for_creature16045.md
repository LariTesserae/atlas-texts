In this world, I would be one of the listeners who dwells in the high mountain hollow. I imagine myself as a being shaped by the place itself, folded low to the damp earth, my skin the color of wet granite flecked with pale constellations echoing the winter sky.

I would spend my days in quiet communion with the hollow, attuned to its every sound and subtle shift. My hands and feet, webbed and splayed, would be perfectly adapted for navigating the slick stones and pools. I would hear through my palms pressed to rock and moss, deciphering the mountain's hidden language.

My diet would follow the sparse rhythms of the alpine seasons - shoots and bitter roots in spring, small fruits and trapped insects in summer, lichen paste and marmot fat stored for the long, cold winter. I would take only what the hollow could spare, never disrupting its delicate balance.

The stone hut would be my sometime shelter, a place to curl against on rainy nights, its mossy roof and willow ribs murmuring with the intricate music of water. But I would not think of it as home, for the whole basin would be my true dwelling.

I would tend the ancient cup-marks brimming with rainwater and offerings, listening at the one that opens into the mountain's depths for whispers of what the future holds. My voice, when I did speak, would be a rough clicking and dropping of water-sounds, shaped for carrying across stone to the ears of my kin.

I would fear fire, revere the dark amphibians, and share a wary peace with the other creatures, knowing my place in the mountain's strict hierarchy. Even the pale cat would know not to enter my domain, feeling the hollow's awareness in every stone.

Slowly, patiently, the mountain would claim me - lichen colonizing old scars, my movements becoming heavier and more deliberate with each passing year until at last I sank back into the stony earth, my weathered bones retrieved and repurposed in a lineage of listening.

I would be, not an intruder nor a conqueror of the hollow, but an expression of its ancient, exacting patterns - a being molded from its rain and stone and thin bright air, as much a feature of the landscape as the marmots and moss and meltwater torrents. The mountain and I, listener and listening place, would be one.
