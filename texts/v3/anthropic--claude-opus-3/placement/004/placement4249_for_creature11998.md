In this drowned cathedral of industry, I would be a ghost of human ingenuity - the spirit of a long-dead engineer who designed these magnificent turbines. 

My existence would be tied to the rusting machines, moving among them like a whisper of what once was. I would drift through the murky water, my shadowy form occasionally illuminated by shafts of dim green light filtering down from the world above.

Time would have little meaning for me in this place where the years blend together in corroded stillness. I would be a patient observer, watching the slow dance of algae and bryozoans as they claim the surfaces that were once my life's work.

My presence would be most keenly felt around the old blueprints and schematics, faded and obscured by silt, but still bearing the marks of my hand. The mass of eels might sense me there, a subtle disturbance in the currents as I linger over these remnants of my designs. 

Sometimes I would reach out, a ghostly hand tracing the graceful spiral of a turbine blade, feeling the pitted surface beneath incorporeal fingers. It would be a gesture of both longing and acceptance, a recognition of the beauty in decay.

I would be drawn to the old maintenance ladder, memories of countless ascents and descents stirring like silt. The sealed hatch would be a bittersweet reminder of a threshold I can no longer cross, a boundary between my world and the one I left behind.

Mostly, I would simply exist in this place, a part of its fabric now as much as the concrete and steel. The eels would pass through me unknowingly as they make their slow migrations, and I would feel a kinship with them - we are both relics in our way, changed and shaped by this sunken world.

But even ghosts have their purpose. I would be the keeper of knowledge in this place, the last fading record of its history and function. In my incorporeal mind, the turbines still spin with purpose, the gauges still relay their readings, the pipes still carry their vital flows. I hold the memory of motion in a place gone still.

And so I would remain, an engineer forever bound to his creation, witnessing the strange half-life that flourishes in the ruins of what I wrought. In the green twilight of the flooded chamber, I would find a kind of peace - not a restful death, but an enduring presence, as constant and inevitable as the rust that blooms on iron bones.
