In the Long Hollow, I would be one of the pale walkers. 

I am quiet and still, blending into the grey stone walls between shafts of dusty light. My proportions are a little off, not quite suited for standing upright or moving on all fours, but perfectly adapted to navigating the uneven, broken floor of the hollow. The cool damp air and unchanging dimness are all I've ever known.

I move at the hollow's pace - slowly, patiently, without haste. I listen more than I call out, attuned to the constant rustling and chirping of the roosting birds overhead that form the living ceiling of my world. Their sounds are my language, telling me when a slab is about to fall from above or when something unusual stirs.

I subsist on what the hollow provides - eggs from the lower ledges, handfuls of beetles, the occasional eyeless fish scooped from a still pool. I do not accumulate possessions or lair in a den. I simply exist, moment to moment, in harmony with this timeless, self-contained ecosystem. 

On rare occasions, I pass another of my kind in the dim distance. We acknowledge each other with a mutual pause and stillness, then continue on our separate paths. Breeding is a rare necessity, not a ceremony. We are solitary but not lonely, separate but connected in our unified existence as extensions of the hollow itself.

At times I pause by the slab holding the ancient flaked tools, relics from when my ancestors were still people who visited the upper world. My broad hands with their claws, suited now for prying eggs and sifting beetle drifts, half-remember the feel of grasping those tools. But it is a distant memory. 

I am no longer a person waiting to return to the surface. I simply am this place, as much as the dripping water and shedding stone. A visitor passing by might glimpse my pale form and feel a watchful gaze, but I would take no more note of them than the hollow takes of a single falling slab. Their footsteps would echo then fade from thought, absorbed into the cacophony of fluttering wings and back into the still, damp air.

I am the pale walker, and the hollow and I are one.
