The pages of long-forgotten engineering manuals and botanical folios are interwoven with my fibrous musculature, granting me a deep, instinctual understanding of the caldera's delicate balance.

I move slowly, spending long hours in quiet contemplation beside the hissing exhaust manifolds, watching the play of emerald light through the shafts of steam. My purpose is to decipher the secrets of this place, to understand the language spoken in the pulsing of raw voltage through the copper-vines and the slow, tectonic conversations of the engine-trees as they flex their roots.

Like the other creatures of the grove, I do not eat or sleep. My sustenance comes from basking in the electromagnetic aura of the great dynamos, absorbing the stray electrons through the metallic ink of the diagrams etched into my folio-skin. I "breathe" by fanning my pages slowly, creating tiny vortices in the humid air that draw the ambient ozone over the sensitive spores of my exposed endpapers.

When I walk, it is with the gait of a heavy tome, my resinous limbs bending like the covers of an ancient codex. The Galvanic Drifters are drawn to me, settling on my pages like living bookmarks, leaving behind glowing spores and fragments of pollen that slowly stain my cellulose in blooms of bioluminescent annotation.

I cannot speak, but I can read the history of the grove in the rings of the ironwood trees, the sedimentary layers of compacted leaves, and the carbon scoring on the ceramic insulators. Through the electrochemical networks of the Root-Weavers, I can send my insights pulsing into the depths of the earth, adding to the slow, recursive memory of the living caldera.

When at last my bindings grow brittle and my sap-lacquered joints begin to stiffen, I will not fear. I will make my way to the base of a great engine-tree and lean against its conductive bark. There, I will let my pages fall open one last time, releasing my spores into the rich, loamy air.

The grove will absorb the knowledge held in my folio-body, my cellulose and lignin breaking down to become part of the humic foundation of the next generation. And perhaps, in time, a new Amber Acolyte will sprout from the spongy floor, its first leaves unfurling like the pages of a newborn book, ready to resume the long, patient study of this strange and wondrous world.
