In the Drowned Canopy, I imagine myself as a young Shaper apprentice named Kel, just beginning to learn the ways of shaping the living wood that forms our entire world.

I cling to my mother's back as she swims powerfully through the thick amber water, her four arms working in perfect coordination as she pulls us along the carved handholds. I match my breath to hers, sucking in the oxygen-rich water through my mouth and expelling it through my nostril valves in time with her strokes. 

As she works, carving channels into a swollen trunk with her boring worms, I press my small palms against the wood, feeling the vibrations of other Shapers moving through distant chambers, their locations and activities translated through texture and rhythm. An elder is re-shaping a major junction to account for this season's growth, his deep pulses resonating confidence. A team of apprentices my age are clearing a blocked filtration channel, their quick rhythms buzzing with focused energy.

My mother pauses and reaches back, guiding my primary hands to a section of trunk. I feel the pattern - whorls and ridges I don't yet fully understand, but which she's been teaching me to read. "Feel here," she pulses. "The density change. What does it tell you?"

I press my sensitive fingerpads to the wood, tracing the minute shifts in texture. The fibers are swirling in an eddy pattern, tight and stressed. "Disrupted flow," I click hesitantly. "Pressure building. Without correction, it will…" I struggle to remember the term. "Delaminate?"

I feel her affirming rumble through my chest. "Good. And what must we do?"

I consider, running through the protocols she's drilled into me. "Relieve the pressure, then restructure the growth to restore efficient flow." I point to a spot just upstream of the eddy. "A bleed channel here, with a lateral brace to direct new growth."

She reaches back and squeezes my leg, a pulse of pride and affection. "You've been paying attention." She hands me a smaller bore-worm from her wrist pouch. "Go ahead. Start the channel, and I'll check your work."

Carefully, I press the bore-worm to the point I've selected and flex my forearm. The creature awakens and begins to chew into the wood, its diamond teeth rasping as it eats a narrow tunnel. I feel the wood changing immediately, the pressure easing as sap begins to flow from the hole.

It's a small change in a vast world, but as I work, I feel myself as part of something immense - an ancient living machine that breathing and pulsing around me. And not just a part of it, but a shaper of it. My actions, multiplied by generations, keep our world alive.

As I carve, I hum softly, etching my own personal rhythm into the wood. Someday, another apprentice will feel the traces of my work, and know that Kel was here, learning the sacred Pattern, doing their small part in the never-ending shaping of the Drowned Canopy.
