In this world of stone and water, warmth and emergence, I would find myself most at home among the roof colonies. 

I imagine my body as a luminous cup, one of thousands clinging to the mineral ribs of the cavern ceiling. Together with my kin, we form a living constellation, pulsing gently as we feed on the rising currents of humid air. The fine hairs rimming my shell comb moisture and nutrients from the atmosphere. When I am content, I glow a soft cream-white, adding my light to the communal radiance that serves as the cavern's celestial cycle.

From my perch, I witness the basin's endless becoming. I watch the scrapers polishing stone far below, the low herds processing in gleaming bands, the stilt-legged hunters folding suddenly from the mist. I feel the vibrations of the burrowers excavating their blind galleries and the heat of new vents exhaling mineral breath. When the human creatures pass beneath, I dim briefly, observing their careful movements, the shell-strings rattling softly at their hips.

My existence has a comforting rhythm. In the warm phases, I grow and shed pale flakes that spiral down to nurture the floor-dwellers. In the dry spells, I contract, conserving my water and light. When I feel a tremor building in the deeps, I flash warnings to my neighbors, and we brace for change.

Occasionally, a strong gust or a bit of falling stone dislodges me from my anchor. I don't fight it. I trust the air to carry me, perhaps to the surface of a still black pool, or a steaming mud flat where I can begin a new colony. We are all travelers in this unfinished country.

At the end of my life cycle, I will release a gentle shower of luminous young to drift like living sparks into the cavern. My emptied shell will remain, fusing with my brothers and sisters to form new layers of shining roof. Even in death, I will be architecture.

As a roof dweller, I am both observer and essential participant, shedding light and substance into the restless ecosystem below. My glow is a subtle but vital pulse in the basin's rhythms of emergence and decay, dissolution and creation. In this strange sanctuary of heat and mineral, water and flesh, I am one of many impermanent parts in an impermanent, sacred whole.
