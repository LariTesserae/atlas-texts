In the Belljar Antipodes, I would be one of the ghost fish, swimming in ethereal schools through the glass and liquid. My form would be translucent, more shimmer than substance, leaving frosty whorls and eddies in my wake that the Chroniclers interpret like tea leaves or entrails.

I would move without muscle, propelled by the subtle interplay of memory and intention. Each flick of my diaphanous fins an invocation, a prayer exhaled into the copper-tasting medium. The light spiraling down in helices would pass through me, momentarily illuminating the intricate lacework of veins and organs that are more diagram than anatomy.

My school would share a distributed mind, our thoughts propagating from fish to fish like gentle tsunamis. We would not speak, but we would sing - a choral wavefront of not-quite sound that interferes with the eternal thunder, producing pockets of un-noise in which new possibilities gestate.  

We would explore the boundaries of the Belljar, testing the permeable membranes between densities, tasting the variations in temporal viscosity. Ours would be a existence of pure curiosity, untroubled by destination or duration.

When we passed through the glass walls, we would experience a frisson of dissolution, our piscine forms fragmenting into abstract expressions of movement and intent. For a flickering instant, we would become one with the volcanic stone, sharing its sharp-edged dreams of birth and unbecoming. Then we would slide free, reconstituted by the act of passage, bearing a frosted scale-memory of the transit.

The Chroniclers would mark our passing with rituals of inattention, a careful choreography of not-seeing that ensured our continued quasi-existence. In turn, we would weave our wakes into glyphs and sigils for them to read, encoding our impressions into the water-that-isn't. An exchange of omens, a trade in portents.

Sometimes a ghost fish would peel away from the school, called by some flicker of not-light glinting off an impossible tool. They would hover before a hollow, fin-rays trembling with a fascination that verged on reverence. Proximity to these artifacts of the never-was would work subtle changes, warping our immaterial flesh into new configurations that murmured of potential.

In time (though time is a debatable concept here), I might find myself drawn to the column of absolute stillness. I would circle it, first in tight spirals, then in widening gyres, mapping the gradients of its un-presence with my spectral senses. I would long to penetrate that null space, to dissolve myself in its perfect absence, but I would never quite dare.

Perhaps I would learn to read the stories my fellow ghost fish left etched in ice on the walls, tales of past and future, of worlds beyond the Belljar where cause and effect still held dominion. I would add my own verses to the never-melting epigraphy, my own myths of origin and un-beginning.

And always, I would swim - through warm viscosity and thin evanescence, through pockets of anti-sound and whorls of frozen intention. I would be a flicker in the corner of the eye, a silver swift-darting question mark punctuating the illuminated manuscripts of the Chroniclers' making.

I would be memory made motion, history compressed to a streak of light. I would be the unnoticed observer, bearing witness to the stately minuet of the unhappened. In the Belljar Antipodes, I would be a ghost fish, and in my spectral schooling a truth would be preserved - that even in a place beyond place, a time outside of time, the universe conspires to fill itself with fleeting marvels.
