In this world of drowned stone and subtle sounds, I would be one of the Seepfolk - a listener, a tender of mineral films, a dweller in the low places.

My name might be something like Fog-Hum-Rising or Still-Pool-Waking, earned from my attentiveness to the plain's voices. My back would be plated with callused skin crusted in pale limestone, blending into the rocky landscape. I would move close to the ground, crouching, noticing the tremors that pass beneath my wide, gripping feet.  

I imagine spending my days at the seeps, carefully scraping and spreading the living tarnish that feeds our people. My long, delicate hands would be skilled at teasing blue eels from their crevices and tying intricate patterns in gut-cord. In the evenings, I would gather with my family in our sunken shelter, sharing percussive songs and stories of the day's listenings.

As a child, I would have played among the channels and pillars, learning the sounds of safety and danger. The Basin Keepers would have been my teachers, showing me how to hear the voice of the plain in the great roaring chord of water. Perhaps in adulthood, I too would crouch inside that tumult, seeking wisdom.

I would live by the rules of the Seepfolk - yielding to what moves below, respecting the power of fire, finding nourishment in stone. My art would be in tuning traps and cairns to the fog and tide, making bowls sing with revealing pitch. I would hope to grow old listening at the great screens, interpreting the plain's deep language for a new generation.  

In this landscape of echoes and hidden throats, conquest and separation from nature would be foreign concepts. I would simply be one life among many, crouch-walking and listening my way through a world both dangerous and sustaining. The great slowness of the plain would be my clock and comfort, reminding me with every drip and tremor that I am part of a long and fluid pattern, vaster than my own small survival.
