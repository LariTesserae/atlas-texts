If I found myself in this world, I would be a small, cryptic creature lurking in the shadows and narrow spaces of the hollow. 

I imagine myself as a strange insect-like being, not quite beetle, not quite moth, but something in between. My body would be flat and angular, segmented like a primitive trilobite, allowing me to wedge into the thinnest cracks and crevices in the dark rock. My exoskeleton would be stone-gray, mottled and textured to blend perfectly against the damp mineral surfaces.

My eyes would be pale, almost translucent orbs, highly sensitive to the faintest glimmers of light that filter into the depths. At night they would glow faintly with an eerie bioluminescence. Beneath my segmented plates, my body would softly pulse with that same cold light, visible only in the deepest gloom.

I would move with a slow, deliberate gait, conserving precious energy. My limbs would end in delicate feathered appendages, allowing me to taste and smell the thin films of moisture and organic matter clinging to the stones. I would graze patiently on this scant sustenance, drinking from the merest dewdrops caught in grooves.

But my most notable feature would be an uncanny attunement to the hollow's secret rhythms. The feathery hairs covering my body would detect the faintest vibrations emanating from the central cleft. My very exoskeleton would resonate to those deep subterranean tones, like a living tuning fork. 

I would keep to the quietest, darkest recesses, avoiding even the other small creatures. An enigmatic presence, more shadow than animal. But in moments of stillness, I would creep to the edge of the abyss, feeling its cold exhalations, and add my own barely audible hum to the mountain's ancient song.

In this forbidding world of stone and cold and hunger, I would be a cryptic symbol of the hollow itself - a fragment of living stone, pulsing with a hidden light, trembling to the secret music of the deeps. A silent witness to the mountain's mysteries.
