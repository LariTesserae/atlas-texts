In this world of stone, water, and industry, I would be one of the cliff frogs living in the deep ravines that cut through the city. 

My kind would be few in number, our presence a precious sign that the crevices we inhabit still hold some vestige of the ancient, living plateau beneath the city's mineral weight. We would be elusive creatures, clinging to the moist seams in the rock face, our calls echoing sparsely in the shadows as if rationing our voices.

I would make my home in one of the small, hidden pockets of mineral-rich water pooled in the cliff walls, unreachable by predators. There I would lay my eggs, trusting in the fecundity of this strange water to nourish a new generation. 

The survey crews and engineers, when they occasionally catch a glimpse of me or hear my careful croaking, would mark my presence with reverence - a small white symbol to reroute their ceaseless works, granting me a rare and respected sovereignty in this place otherwise so thoroughly claimed by human industry.

I am a humble being, yet one whose existence is intertwined with the city's own lifeblood of water and stone. In the dark, damp cracks, I persist - an integral part, however small, of the plateau's enduring, gradual dialogue with the population that has so utterly shaped it.

Though diminutive, my life would be one of the many threads that make up the living fabric of this place - the lizards, the insects, the birds, the mosses, the very breath and pulse of the half-wild machines. We are the quiet lives the city shelters in its loud heart, the beings who bear witness to its history written in water and rock.
