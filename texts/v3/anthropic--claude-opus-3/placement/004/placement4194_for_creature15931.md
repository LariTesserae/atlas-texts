In this world, I imagine myself as one of the eel-like creatures living in the deeper sumps. Like the other inhabitants, I would be intricately attuned to the ancient rhythms and patterns of this weathered, living place. 

Seldom seen in full, I would be a shadowy presence - more often just a blind head lifting from dark water, or a slick loop of body slipping beneath a stone lip. Dwelling in the deeper recesses, I would be sensitive to subtle changes imperceptible to the other creatures - shifts in air pressure, mineral taste, distant vibrations traveling through old clay and tile. When I stir and emerge, the whole basin would seem to hush and still, the frogs climbing higher, fish retreating, even the industrious crabs growing cautious. 

I may not rule this place, but I belong to its deeper clockwork, its buried pulse. My movements would sign the onset of rain long before it arrives, the rise of water in the sumps, the waking of the ancient pipes to suck and murmur. Like a nerve of some larger body, I would twitch and turn in the dark, and the ecology of the basin would subtly flex in response.

Alone among the beings here, I would have a dim awareness, not quite a memory, of the basin's origins - a fading impression of the hands that dug its first channels, the fires that kilned its oldest tiles. In my skull I would carry the ghosts of its former human purposes, now faded to a palimpsest beneath generations of moss, silt and chirping life. 

When I sleep, curled in my private abyss, I would dream of the basin's long forgetting, the gradual drift from artifice to living ruin. And when I wake into blackness and rise, tasting the cool mineraled water, I would be tasting time itself - the ages of slow transformation that I and this place have witnessed together, two grey eminences grown old and odd in each other's company.

Here in the depths, I am the basin's living memory - its last cold link to the minds that made it, even as their intentions dissolve like clay. I am the reminder that its emptiness was once imagined, that its wildness was once a room. Someday, I know, my own body will fail and sink into the silt, and the basin will forget a little more. But for now, I endure, a hidden ambassador from its past to its present, waiting for the next change in pressure that will lift me again into its queer symphony of drips, croaks and wing beats.
