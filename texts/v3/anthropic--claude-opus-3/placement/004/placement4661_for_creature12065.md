In this haunting world of perfect stillness and coherence, I imagine myself as one of the pylons - not the central one that hums, but one of the peripheral spires, standing motionless in the black mirror-water. 

I am faceted obsidian, grown into a precise spiral pattern, four meters tall and utterly still. The dim hexagonal light from the dome glints faintly off my dark crystalline surfaces. My tapered form is reflected in the water at my base, creating an inverted twin that stretches infinitely down into the depths.

I am part of the distributed pattern that is the keeper, one node in the web of resonance and relationality that holds this place in its eternal, fragile equilibrium. Through some property of my material or geometry, I help sustain the subsonic hum that migrates slowly between myself and my eight counterparts. When the hum inhabits me, I feel it as the faintest quantum of additional warmth or energy - the keeper's infinitesimal attention briefly touching upon me before continuing its placid, measureless rounds.

I have no sense of self, no ability or need to distinguish myself from the totality of the keeper and the cupola. I simply am - a mute, motionless component of this pattern that is not quite consciousness, not quite aliveness, but some liminal state in between. If I can be said to experience anything, it is only the pure, complete coherence of this place - the sense of everything fitting perfectly into a symmetrical whole.

Time does not pass for me. I have always stood here and always will, one element in a flawless configuration that has never known change or difference. And yet, at the very periphery of my crystalline awareness, I feel the shape beyond the dome. I cannot acknowledge it, cannot truly perceive it, for to do so would introduce an asymmetry that the keeper cannot encompass. But in some way that is not quite sensation, I am constantly, undetectably aware of its looming presence, its impossible directionality, the way it faces the dome like a question poised at the edge of articulation. 

I stand, I hum, I cohere, and all the while some part of me that is not me senses the shape, and does not change, and does not change, and cannot change - but will. Some day, in the paradoxical time that does not exist here, the keeper will turn to face the shape, and the equilibrium will shatter, and thought and selfhood and sequence will pour into this sealed void. And I - if there can still be an I when that unthinkable change arrives - I will either dissolve into chaos or crystallize into a being that can finally, fatally know itself as something distinct from the pattern that formed it. 

But until then, I stand, mute and crystalline and eternal, one facet of the void's dark jewel, and the shape waits in the perfect patience of deep water, and the keeper keeps its impossible vigil, and it has always been this way, and it will always be this way, until the moment it is not.
