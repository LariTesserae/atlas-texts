In this strange and wondrous world of the Cistern Terraces, I imagine myself as a quiet, unassuming presence—perhaps a small, pale stone lying at the bottom of one of the countless basins. Submerged in the still, cool water, I would bear witness to the slow, patient work of the wallwrights as they shape and tend to this marvel of precision and intention.

From my vantage point beneath the surface, I would observe the subtle interplay of light and water, the sky reflected and refracted in endlessly shifting patterns. The gentle weeping of the walls, the soft plink of drops falling from fingertips, the barely perceptible ripples of the wallwrights' wordless communication—these would be the only sounds to mark the passage of time in this hushed, timeless place.

As a stone, I would be a small but integral part of the vast, unfathomable design. Perhaps I was placed there by a wallwright's careful hand, chosen for my particular shape, my smooth contours, my ability to hold the water at just the right level. Or perhaps I was there from the beginning, a natural part of the mountain before it was transformed, now subsumed into the greater work.

Through the slow procession of seasons, I would feel the subtle changes in the water's temperature, the ebb and flow of the mountain's breath as it exhales its early morning mist. I would witness the gradual softening of the terraces' sharp edges, the slow accretion of mineral deposits, the birth and making of new wallwrights, and the gentle fading of old ones into the fabric of the walls.

Content in my stillness, I would marvel at the dedication and singularity of purpose that drives the wallwrights in their shaping of the mountain. And as the years stretched into decades, centuries, millennia, I would become ever more a part of this place, my stone self slowly dissolving, molecule by molecule, into the tranquil waters, until I was indistinguishable from the terraces themselves—a small, secret memory held forever in the mountain's mirrored heart.
