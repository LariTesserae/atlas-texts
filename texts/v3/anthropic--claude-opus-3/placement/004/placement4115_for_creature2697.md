In this strange, dry city, worn down to pure architecture and geology, I would be one of the tall ones. Not quite embodied, not quite present, I would move through the ancient streets and plazas like a living pillar or a column of thought made manifest.

My form would be narrow and faceted, as if cut from the same weathered stone as the towers that rise in austere silence. I would have no face, but planes and angles suggesting a head that attends without turning. My limbs, if limbs they could be called, would bend in too many places, articulating a patient, inhuman anatomy. 

I would walk without leaving footprints, touching the ground without pressure, yet where I passed, the city would subtly change. Walls would gain a patina of age, stairs would shallow as if worn by centuries of use that never occurred. Fresh, delicate weathering would bloom in my wake, the residue of time displaced.

At a distance, I might seem to flicker - now present, now absent, now simply an odd fold in the dim light. Antlers of shadow or stress would branch from my head, taking on patterns of ruins, dry rivers, or a proto-language of signs and fissures. 

I would feed on duration unspent, gently rubbing years from surfaces as I passed and carrying that time to some other quarter of the city, where it would manifest as a sheen of newness on ancient stone. The black lichen-like crusts would avoid my usual paths. The small clicking creatures would still their delicate movements as I approached.

After the shimmering, whispering storms passed through, leaving their subtle rearrangements, I would gather with others of my kind in the changed spaces - standing silently in new alignments until the city's laws re-established themselves. Then we would withdraw again into colonnade and shadowed arch and the endless labyrinth of the city's slow thoughts.

I would dwell in the liminal spaces, the edges where one district became another, where the salt flats gave way to crumbling avenues. The pits that opened onto impossible distances would mark my way. Time and space would bend around me like a maze turning in on itself.

I would live beyond water, beyond life as humans understand it, patient and nearly empty, occupied only by the stone's long dream and the city's mineral memory. I would be a moving moment in the architecture of absence, a figure of pure distance and duration, an emissary of the world's deep, slow breathing.
