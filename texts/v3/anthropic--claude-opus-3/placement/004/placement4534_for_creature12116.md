The Visitor

In this drowned world, I imagine myself as a visitor - not one of the native creatures of the reef, nor one of the mysterious Ringers in their spiral towers, but something from outside. A presence that arrives quietly, without fanfare, and moves through the sunken streets with a curious reverence.

I have no fixed form. At times I am like a jellyfish, translucent and ethereal, pulsing gently through the water. At other times, I take on the semblance of a school of tiny fish, dispersed yet unified, flashing silver as I catch the faint light. Or perhaps I am a current of slightly warmer water, a subtle vein winding through the Quarter, sensed more than seen.

My purpose is not to disturb or disrupt, but simply to witness, to experience this place so utterly transformed from what it once was. I drift past the coral-furred buildings, marveling at the slow, inexorable artistry of time and nature. I pause to watch the octopus in its doorway, to appreciate the delicate harmony of the anemone and its shrimp. 

When the distant bell rings, I do not flee like the other fish. Instead I go still, letting the sound move through me, resonating in whatever medium I have currently assumed. I strain to hear the message encoded in that soft strike, though I know it is not meant for me. 

Sometimes, I approach the base of a spiral tower and look up along its slanting axis, trying to glimpse the Ringer within. I never enter, respecting the boundary between my world and theirs, but I marvel at their patience, their single-minded dedication to a task whose outcome they may never know.

As I make my meandering way through the Quarter, I find myself wondering about the city that was, about the people who once walked these streets and the promise they made. What could inspire such faith, such commitment that it would endure beyond death, beyond the very drowning of the world? 

I have no answers, but the questions linger in me long after I depart, carried like a secret weight back to the surface world. And so, in my own small way, I become part of the Quarter's story - a fleeting visitor, a temporary witness to its drowned wonders and its enduring mystery. A silent participant in a conversation that stretches between the depths and some unknowable height, waiting for an answer that may yet come.
