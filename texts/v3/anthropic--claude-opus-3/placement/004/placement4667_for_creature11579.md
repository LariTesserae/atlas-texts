In this vivid, primordial world of living rock and steaming fissures, I would be one of the fungal-animal colonial beings, emerging from the interaction of mineral and moisture. Not quite a single organism, but an amorphous, pulsing essence that flows as an iridescent film across the slick basalt when dormant, then coalesces into translucent pillars to feed on temperature gradients from the hissing vents.

My form would constantly shift between liquid and solid states - dispersing into a near-invisible sheen to absorb the scalding rain and percolating groundwater, then slowly rising into undulating columns of clear gel that refract the brutal sunlight into dancing spectra. Internally, I would be a kaleidoscope of suspended flecks - trapped insects, algal cells, silica granules - all churning hypnotically in my viscous matrix. 

With no fixed boundaries, I would meld seamlessly with others of my kind, our essences mingling until we became a single pulsing entity draped over the steaming rocks. Through subsonic vibrations thrumming in the bedrock, we would sing to each other of geothermal shifts deep in the earth, of new mineral-rich seeps, of the slow dance between scalding steam and cool mist that sustains us.

Indifferent to other life, I would engulf any small creatures that ventured too close - not to feed, but simply because I could, my form flowing around and through them like sentient ooze. At night I would glow with stolen bioluminescence, emitting an ethereal blue-green light as I thinned myself to a diaphanous film to drink in the different energies of cooling stone and humid air.

Inhospitable gases would be my sustenance, my strange and permeable body breaking down sulfurous compounds and heavy metal ions into forms more palatable for the tenacious vegetation. I would be an intermediary between geological and biological realms - a living filter that rendered extremes into a livable balance, encouraging the first rusty-green lichen crusts and delicate fern spores.

Over eons, as the primal land breathed and bucked with volcanic forces, my kind would gradually shift too - perhaps elaborating into more complex forms as true soil developed, or maybe simplifying down to our essence as the steam vents cooled and went dormant. But always, we would be singing our resonant tones through the bedrock, an indelible part of this dynamic, newborn land - the living ooze that flowed between cracks, pulsed in translucent glory, and shaped a hostile terrain into a cradle for new, explosive life.
