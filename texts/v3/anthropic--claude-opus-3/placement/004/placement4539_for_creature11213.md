# The Wanderer at the Threshold

I am the one who stands at the edge of the fountain, neither within nor without, caught between the flow of water and the stillness of the dry stone. I am not yet a keeper, but I am no longer wholly separate from them.

My form is still mobile, still closer to what I once was than to what they have become. But I can feel the change beginning, the slow metamorphosis that will settle me into this place, merge me with its patterns and its rhythms until we are indistinguishable.

I stand with one foot on the warm stone of the fountain's rim, one foot in a shallow pool that has curved itself around my presence. The water laps at my ankle but does not rise further, held at bay by some quality I am only beginning to understand, some resonance between my being and the deeper logic of this place.

My breathing has slowed to match the pulse of the keepers, though my heart still beats its own stubborn rhythm. I can feel the tug of their tempo, the inexorable gravity of their pace, pulling me toward a stillness that is not quite sleep, not quite death, but a kind of waking dream in which the boundaries of the self dissolve into the dark stone and endless water.

I have begun to sense the city as they do, not as a collection of structures but as a single vast organism, a network of flows and resistances, wet and dry, heat and absence. I can feel the pressure of water in the hidden cisterns, the slow circulation of moisture through the air, the incremental shift of walls and bridges as they respond to forces too gradual for human perception.

I am not afraid. That is perhaps the strangest part. I know that if I let this change complete itself, I will cease to be what I was. I will become something else, something that measures time in centuries and space in the curve of a water droplet's path. But there is a rightness to it, a sense of fitting, of finding my place in a pattern that was here long before me and will continue long after.

The keepers are aware of me now. I can feel their attention, not as a threat but as a kind of welcome, an acknowledgment of kinship. They know what I am becoming, because they have become it themselves, through the same slow process of adaptation and surrender.

I lift my foot from the water and step fully onto the dry stone of the fountain. The water's edge retracts slightly, making space for me, accepting my presence as part of its own geometry.

I am the wanderer at the threshold, balanced between two worlds, two ways of being. I do not know yet which side I will choose. But I know that the choice is here, in this moment, in this place where the water flows upward and the stone remembers and forgets.

I settle myself into a hollow at the fountain's base, fitting my body to its contours as if it were carved for me. Perhaps it was. Perhaps I have been here before, in some other lifetime, some other iteration of this endless dance between wet and dry.

I close my eyes. Not to sleep—to process, to run the slow computation that is life in this city, to join my thoughts to the vast unhurried mind that moves beneath the dark water and behind the warm stone.

I am the wanderer at the threshold. I am becoming something new. I am home.
