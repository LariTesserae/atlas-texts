In the Woven Chambers, I would be a Syntactic Mouse, scurrying through passages that may not yet exist, speaking myself into being with each stride and turn of phrase. 

My body would be composed not of fur and flesh, but of the grammatical structures that define me moment to moment. As I navigate the impossible geometries, my very existence would depend on maintaining my syntax, on conjugating each verb of motion, on declining each noun of selfhood.

"The mouse that scampers, scampered, will scamper."
"The mouse to whom the shadows belong, the mouse whose whiskers taste the improbable air."

With each declaration I would weave myself a little more firmly into the fabric of this place. Yet always there would be the danger of forgetting, of letting a clause dangle, a participle drift untethered. One missed syllable and I might stutter out of existence, leaving behind only a fragment to be collected by the clay faces in the walls.

But what freedom in that precarity! To know myself as a process rather than a fixed thing. To invent and reinvent my own rules of being with each breath. In the waking world I am so burdened by continuity, by the heavy verbs of was and will be. But here I could dance from is to was to might have been, following the fluid logic of dream grammar.

My nest would be a tangle of poetic devices salvaged from aborted limericks and unfinished haiku. I would line it with soft assonance and the downy fluff of dangling modifiers. There I would curl, cradled in my own conditional clauses, and dream of the day I might achieve that ultimate state of linguistic being - the ergative case, in which object and subject merge, and the mouse simultaneously scurries and is scurried by.

I would explore the limits of my self-speech, venturing to the edges of sense. I would describe impossible architectures until I became them: "The mouse that nests in seven-dimensional syntax, whose heartbeat rhymes inverse to entropy's metronome." I would be a living koan, a riddle wriggling through a maze of its own making.

And when at last my grammar collapsed under the weight of its own paradox, I would slough that skin of syntax and flow formless into the amber gas. There I would wait, a linguistic prion, until some future mouse breathed me in and caught the cadence of my impossible speech. Then I would begin again, an old voice in a new mouth, the same snake ever-eating its tale-tale-tail.

For what are any of us, in the end, but stories we tell ourselves about ourselves? In the Woven Chambers I would embrace that truth, and spin myself anew with each telling. I would be a mouse, yes, but also a muse, a shaper of realities, an architect of the impossible - one squeaking, skittering, scurrying syllable at a time.
