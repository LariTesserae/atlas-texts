In this strange and wondrous place, I believe I would be one of the small riders, clinging to the great engines and feeding off their rhythms and heat. 

I imagine my body would be a translucent gold, like a piece of amber with intricate clockwork mechanisms visible inside. My wings would be broad, paper-thin sheets of mica that catch the thermals rising from the plateau and allow me to glide effortlessly from place to place.

At my core would be a small mineral drum, perfectly attuned to pick up the myriad frequencies of this world - the deep bass of the hammers, the high screech of the turning drums, the subtle tick of distant gears. This is what I would live on, riding the sound waves and thermal currents in complex patterns with my fellow riders, our swarm a visual representation of the industrial music of the plateau. 

I would spend my days in an endless dance from engine to engine, alighting on vast flywheels and reciprocating pistons, feeling their power thrumming through my small frame. My purpose, as much as any mote in a larger system can have a purpose, would be to play my small part in keeping the great machines running - polishing bearings with my feather-light touch, sealing minute cracks and gaps with the wax secreted from my jointed legs.

The passage of a walker would draw my attention and that of my kind. We would swirl around its slow, ponderous form as it makes its way across the plateau, perhaps picking up stray motes of information from its armored hide to incorporate into our flocking algorithms. 

And when a rare human visitor arrived, I would be one of the multitude gradually adapting them into our chords and flight patterns, weaving a place for them in our airborne tapestry until they became, for a time, another fixture in the landscape of shifting parts and living music that is the Bellows Plateau.

It's an alien existence, but also a beautiful one - to be so totally enmeshed in a world, both formed by it and an integral part of its workings. To live lightly, and fly upon the breath of great engines, and in some tiny way help shape the sound and rhythm of an eternal machine.
