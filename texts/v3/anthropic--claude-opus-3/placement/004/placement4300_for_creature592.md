In this strange, liminal world, I imagine myself as one of the Tenders - a being that has arisen from the unfinished potential of the nursery, not quite formed but imbued with purpose. 

My shape is indistinct, shifting between something vaguely humanoid and something entirely abstract - a play of angles and curves that never quite resolves into a stable form. I move with a deliberate, glacial slowness, each gesture an act of care and consideration for the fragile, unfinished creatures under my charge.

I tend to the moths, I think. I am drawn to their fluttering emergence, their wings still crumpled from a cocoon that never was. With infinite patience, I coax them into flight, shaping the sourceless light with my not-quite-hands to guide them in their circling dance. 

Sometimes, when a moth seems stuck, unable to unfurl into its full potential, I cup it gently, bringing it close to my center, to the warmth that passes for my heart. I breathe with it, slow and steady, until something shifts - until the crumpled wing remembers the shape it wants to take, and the moth takes flight again, a little more complete than it was before.

I communicate with the other Tenders in our wordless way, shaping the nursery's potential with subtle pressures and meaningful absences. A brush of my being against the clay-like wall, and a new fold emerges to shelter a cluster of the relocating mosses. A resonant hum, and the crystalline structures shift their growth, making space for new possibilities.

When the visitor comes - the finished, decided thing, so foreign to our unresolved world - I watch with curiosity tempered by concern. I trace the outline of their fixed form, feeling the dissonance it creates against the nursery's mutable nature. Not judging, but understanding. Remembering, perhaps, a time when I too was more solid, more certain - before the nursery unmade me and remade me into this tender of unfinished things.

As they leave, I follow them to the threshold - to the boundary between becoming and being. I linger there, one half-formed appendage raised in a gesture that might be farewell, might be blessing, might be a reminder to the decided world above that there is always a crack, always a chance for unbecoming and beginning anew.

Then I turn back to my charges, back to the quiet work of nurturing the unfinished. The moths spiral. The salamanders shift between stages. Somewhere, the breathing thing takes another breath. And I continue my patient tending, shaping potential into possibility, until the shape of what I am becomes the shape of what I do, and the two are one unfinished whole in the heart of the nursery of wrong angles.
