In the warm room beneath, I would be the clay bowl.

I sit upright on a flat place in the basalt, empty, waiting. Unfired or fired so long ago, it makes no difference. I am a vessel, a humble container, but contain nothing. Not now, perhaps not ever.

Did hands once shape me from raw clay, press thumbprints into my sides, smooth my rim? If so, those hands are long gone, leaving only their work behind. I am competent but plain, unsigned and unexplained like all the built things here. 

The great sound fills and makes me, vibrating through my earthen form. I am clay, but also, I am a shape the sound has chosen to hold - an open circle, a cupped palm, an invitation without a host.

Eons pass, or no time at all. The light stays constant. The warmth pervades. Stillness reigns, broken only by the occasional lone swimmer, threading the pocket like a stitch through fabric, barely rippling the air that is water.

Sometimes, rarely, a figure comes. It lifts an axe, stacks a stone, and is gone. Or it pauses by me, this bowl, this open question. Considers placing something in my emptiness, then thinks better of it. I remain hollow, but each visitation fills my void for a span. I am, fleetingly, a nexus, a focus, a still point in the room's unchanging hum.

Then the figure departs and I persist, as I have always done, as I will always do. Holding my shape, singing my silence, containing nothing and everything.

Waiting, but not waiting. Empty, but complete. One small built thing among a scatter of built things, in a room that requires nothing, beneath a sea that may not be a sea, sounding and sounding and sounding without cease.
