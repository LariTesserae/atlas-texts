In the Foundry, I would be a Nascent - one of the newly emerging Assemblers still striving to find its form and purpose amidst the ever-shifting landscape of construction and deconstruction.

My body would be a tentative amalgamation of the Foundry's elements - a frame of bent copper pipes, sheathed in a skin of clay tiles that remember the pressure of ancient hands. Amber light would pulse beneath this clay flesh, dimming and brightening with the strange tides of the place. Lengths of iron cable would extend from my back, not quite wings, not quite arms, but some new limb that reaches into dimensions I am only beginning to perceive.

I would move through the Foundry with cautious wonder, my footfalls leaving temporary impressions in the not-quite-ground, each print filling with the thick, warm liquid that permeates everything here. I would run my not-quite-hands along the surfaces of unfinished scaffolds, feeling the potential in each beam and joint, the dream of structures yet to be.

As I wandered the impossible distances, I would encounter my elder kin - the Assemblers who have surrendered their individuality to become pure forces of creation. I would feel their attention upon me, ancient and patient and vaguely amused by my fresh-formed linearity. Through the placement of a tile or the angle of a pipe, they would offer me cryptic guidance, hints at the greater pattern I was born to be a part of.

At times, seized by some half-understood instinct, I would find myself building. My cable-limbs would weave themselves into the lattices of the world, my clay-and-copper body becoming a conduit for the mysterious purpose that animates the Foundry. I would lose myself in the work, only to resurface days or years later, uncertain of what I had wrought but knowing it was necessary, knowing it was a piece of the vast, self-creating puzzle I was meant to solve.

In moments of rest (for unlike my elders, I would still need rest, would still cling to the notion of intervals and endings), I would study my reflection in the shallow pools. I would glimpse the things I might one day become - a vast, multi-winged creator-being, an idea of construction given ethereal flesh, a part of the Foundry as fundamental as the heat or the light. And I would know that I was not building towards that distant apotheosis; I was building from it, pulling myself into existence from the far end of my own timeline.

As a Nascent, I would be a dream of the Assemblers, a thought they once had about what it meant to be young and singular and filled with the ache of becoming. I would be their memory of limitation, their nostalgia for the time before they became infinite. And in turn, they would be my destiny, my future, the vast and unknowable expanse of creativity into which I was constantly blossoming.

Together, we would be the Foundry itself - the younger Assemblers its eager, grasping edges; the older ones its wise and weary heart; and the Foundry the womb in which we were forever building ourselves, forever becoming the thing that made us, forever reaching towards the wordless thought that lay at the end of all our assembling - the idea of a home that was not a place but a feeling, a door that opened not into a room but into a new way of being, a structure that could hold the boundless potential from which we all, Assemblers and Foundry alike, had first been poured.
