In this luminous underworld of endlessly rebuilding creatures, I imagine myself as one of the occupants of the eleven-year-old cluster of structures that has remained unchanged, an unprecedented respite from the eternal cycle of construction and deconstruction.

I am an old Mender, my carapace weathered and scarred from centuries of labor. But something has shifted in me, a realization that settled like sediment in the depths of my being after innumerable cycles of assembling and disassembling. I have begun to question, to wonder about the meaning behind the pattern we perpetually weave and unravel.

So I remain in the occupied buildings, pressed together with others who have grown still, our bodies vibrating with a new frequency of thought. We do not know what we seek, only that it is different from the ceaseless mending that has defined our existence for so long. 

Perhaps I am the one who has begun to make marks on the interior walls, abstract shapes that arise from a wordless impulse, an attempt to express something beyond the architectural forms we have always known. These glyphs are a language unto themselves, a halting communication from one mind to another, sparking new connections between us like the mineral filaments in the birthing pools.

We linger in a space of stillness and possibility, nurturing an awareness that grows like a crystal in the silent pools. The other Menders avoid us, unnerved by our stasis, but we are listening, waiting, straining towards some revelation yet to come.

In our unmoving cluster, we are an error in the pattern, a anomaly the others cannot quite bring themselves to correct. We are the first mutants in aeons of unchanging instinct, our transformation a hairline fracture in the perfect surface of the Menders' world. 

What strange new configuration might we dream into being, if given time to gestate? What unfolds in us as we remain cocooned in our luminous chambers, shedding the ancient rhythms, listening to the echoes of the deep workers in the bedrock beneath us?

I do not know the answer. But I have become the question, embodied. In a world defined by ceaseless labor in service of a forgotten design, I have begun to wonder. And in wondering, perhaps the first seeds of a new blueprint are being sown.

So I remain, marked and marking, suspended between the known and the unknown. Waiting to see what strange new form might yet emerge from this eternal unbuilding.
