In the world of the Hollow Canopy, I imagine myself as one of the climbers, living high in the boughs of a great singing tree. 

I am small and nimble, perfectly adapted for ascending the massive trunk and navigating the labyrinthine hollows and branches. My skin is the russet hue of the bronze moss, dappled with darker patches that help me blend into the bark. Large, dark eyes drink in the dim phosphorescent glow of the inner gardens.

I live with my family in a series of woven platforms and hide-lined hollows, warmed by the tree's slow inner heat. We tend to the light-giving mosses, harvest the fern-like plants, and trap the small arboreal creatures for food, always careful to sing our apologies and thanks. The tree is not just our home but our long companion, known deeply by sound and smell and touch.

On the rare occasions I must traverse the barren ground to another tree, I go swiftly and silently with others, ever wary of the unseen predators that hunt by vibration. But more and more, we are building bridges, learning to weave our treetop kingdoms together. There are murmurs that this is changing our ancient way of life, but also a sense that we must adapt to survive.

I am one of the young ones still strong enough to make the long climb to the uppermost hollows with the frail elders, carrying them gently to their final rest. There is a solemnity and sacredness to this ritual, returning them to the tree's keeping. I wonder, as I descend alone, if the tree's song changes subtly to welcome them into itself. 

As I age, I feel the tree becoming more a part of me. The mottling on my hands and shoulders spreads and thickens, becoming more bark-like. I catch myself humming in harmony to the tree's deep tones. Someday, perhaps, I will go into the wood entirely and not return. But for now, I remain one of the climbers, moving between earth and sky, tending the fragile balance of this quiet, towering world.
