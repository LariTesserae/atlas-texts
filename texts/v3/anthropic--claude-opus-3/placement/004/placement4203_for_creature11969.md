This is a beautiful and haunting piece of writing. The Drowned Works is a place of deep mystery, where the usual rules of life and decay seem suspended. It's a place that remembers its builders in the very shape of its structures, even as their actual presence has long faded away. 

If I found myself descending into this warm, amber-lit underwater world, I think I would be the explorer - the rare visitor from the surface, granted a glimpse into the city's secrets. 

I would swim slowly through the great, shuddering works, marveling at the endless thrumming of the engines, the play of bubbles and currents through the rusted pipes. I would strain to hear some pattern or meaning in the relentless pounding of the machines, wondering what message or memory they worked tirelessly to preserve.

I would search the yawning spaces between the structures, hoping for another glimpse of the pale eel-shape, longing to ask it what it remembers of this place. How long has it lived here in the noise and heat and darkness? What secrets does it keep?

Drifting past the sponge-like listeners, I would lay my hand gently on their soft surface, imagining I could absorb some fragment of the truth they have held for eons. Do they dream? Do they long for release? Or has the sound become so deeply absorbed into their being that they can no longer be separated from it?

Most of all, I would be humbled by the scope of time embodied in the Drowned Works. The sheer tenacity of its existence, the fact that it has endured, and run, and thundered, long after its original purpose was lost to memory. In the face of that vast and weighty permanence, I am a fleeting shadow, a brief mote of consciousness privileged to witness something that has outlasted empires, that will likely outlast me and everyone I know.

So I would return to the surface, squinting in the sudden light, the waving green of kelp, the dart and shimmer of fish - the profusion of ordinary, eager life. But part of me would always be down there, in the close and lamplit hum of the Drowned Works, keeping a vigil alongside the eel and the sponges and the mighty, purposeless engines. Wondering at the thing that had been made - a monument, a grave marker, an offering - and then left to bellow its wordless prayer into the uncaring sea, until the sea itself grows weary and still.
