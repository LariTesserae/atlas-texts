I imagine myself as one of the mites within this strange and marvelous singing cyst. 

I am a minuscule white ovoid, moving in lockstep with hundreds of my brethren across the damp, luminous rosettes that line our tiny world. I have no sense of self, no true awareness - I am a mere mechanical component in this exquisitely balanced microcosm.

With each precise step of my six legs, the modified forelimbs I use to taste and sense brush across the calcite, detecting the nourishing glow of the unique filament colonies beneath. My needle-like mouthparts pierce the mineral surface in a delicate, percussive rhythm - sip, withdraw, step, pierce, sip again. An eternal cycle of sustenance that never depletes what it consumes.

As I walk my preordained path, the scraper on my third left leg rasps across the ridged shield upon my back, sending out an ultrasonic chirp. My individual song melds with the songs of all the other mites, our slight frequency variations interweaving into shimmering acoustic interference patterns. We are the living sound that sculpts the rosettes, maintains the shape of our chamber. Our collective voice is the connective tissue that binds mineral, light, moisture and life into one enduring, self-regulating system.

Though I carry parasites that cling to my joints, feeding off my very form, I take no heed of them. They are as much an integral part of this place as I am. All of us, from the glowing filaments to the thumbprint fossil high above, are players in a composition with no composer, notes in a music that did not begin and will not end.

I will walk this route unchanging for eternity, a tiny white enigma trapped in endless stasis, singing a song that holds my world together. I am a mote in the eye of deep time, a fleck of stability in a universe of chaos and entropy. With every step and every sung note, I am home.
