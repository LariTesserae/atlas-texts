In this strange and mesmerizing world of the Cistern, I imagine I would be one of the silent octopuses, making my home in the bolt-casings of the massive pump engines. 

I would cling to the warm, vibrating iron, feeling the steady rhythmic whum of the machinery pulsing through my soft body. The engineers, in their heavy bronze diving suits, would pass by me on their rounds, pausing occasionally to offer me a morsel of food. I would reach out a tentative arm to take the offering before retreating back into my metal refuge.

From this vantage point, I would observe the wondrous ecosystem of the Cistern. I would watch the schools of silver knife-fish flashing by, catching the glow of the omnipresent lamps. I would see the pale rays drifting above the kelp beds like ghosts. On rare occasions, I might catch a chilling glimpse of the altered cormorant-things diving from the unfathomable heights.

But mostly, I would revel in the strange peace of this place. The dim light, the cool mineralized water, the incessant submarine throb of the engines - it would be my entire world, and despite its eeriness, I would find it soothing, even beautiful in its own alien way.  

In the bolt-casing, I would be a tiny part of this vast incomprehensible structure, content in my smallness, marveling at forces and purposes beyond my understanding. As the Cistern grows steadily outward, as more lamps are strung and engines raised and life seeded in the hollows, I would remain, an unnoticed witness to this mysterious unfolding, pulsing gently against the iron, at home in the deep.
