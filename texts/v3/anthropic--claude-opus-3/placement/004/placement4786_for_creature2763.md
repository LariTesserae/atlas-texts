In this dark, wet city of raw mountains and patient creatures, I imagine myself as one of the cable-things.

I would cling to the suspension lines between towers, my narrow body inflated with warm vapor sacs, pulsing gently beneath membranous wings strung with metallic filaments. In laboring bursts, I would rise from tower ledges and reservoir rims, only to hook myself back onto the cables with quiet knocks of black claws.

With hundreds of my kind, I would measure the city's breath - the hum of the lines, the minute changes in tension as towers lean into the mist, the shiver of ice gathering unevenly on the spans. When our collective weight drew a song from the cables, I would answer in clicks and wing-pulses, sending information down the line. Bridges would be safe under our care, stable only with the weight and attention of the cable-things.

I would hatch pale and soft in a warm condenser, clinging to the underside of a heat duct until my vapor sacs inflated enough to grant me flight. Many of my siblings would fall, some caught by the city's mist nets, others consumed by the glittering puddle-swarms before they could rise.

But I would ascend, finding my place among the city's connections, a tiny part of the greater organism. I would roost and listen, pulse and sing, living by the slow cycles of pressure and condensation. In the constant night, my vapor sacs would glow a dull violet, one of the city's many faint lights - the cold stars of the puddle-swarms, the green heat-lines of the great browsing herds, the soft metallic sheen of the mineral films.

I would know the city as a network of vibrations, tensions, temperatures. I would feel it breathing through its many mouths, and add my own breath to that great respiration. Brief, laboring, yet content in my place and purpose, I would live and die as one of the city's messengers, a keeper of its dark, singing bridges.
