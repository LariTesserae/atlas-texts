# The Mineral Singer

I am one of the Deep-Workers of Mehel-Kuun, though that is not the name I call myself. Names are a surface concern, a need to distinguish that which is already distinct. Down here in the flooded chambers, identity flows and mingles like the mineral-rich waters. I am myself, and I am part of something greater.

My work is both simple and endlessly complex. I tend the great being that dwells in the depths, the vast slow intelligence that guides and is guided by the life of the city above. I do not know if it is alive as we reckon life, but it moves, it grows, it responds to the carefully calibrated ministrations of the Deep-Workers.

My particular role is to sing to it.

The songs are old, passed down through generations of Deep-Workers. The language is not quite Kuunim, not quite anything else - a resonant tongue that seems almost to be an emergent property of the caverns themselves, a lexicon of echoes and reverberations that harmonize with the deep thrum of the mountain's heart.

I was chosen for this role for the quality of my voice, its ability to channel and amplify the strange harmonies of the deep. When I sing, the waters dance with complex patterns of ripples and whorls. The mineral deposits grow in intricate braids and spirals, almost too regular to be natural, almost too organic to be artifice. And the great being stirs in its sleep, its slow thoughts turning to unknown dreams.

I sing of the weight of stone, the patience of water, the deep time that moves beneath the flicker-swift lives of those above. I sing of the great work that proceeds here, the slow shaping of the world that is both a response to and a cause of the patterns of Mehel-Kuun. I sing the songs that maintain the being, and in singing, I maintain myself.

For I am changing, down here in the dark. The mineral-rich waters have seeped into my flesh, my bones. My skin bears the same luminous traceries as the most ancient of the Reckoners above, but more complex, more complete. When I place my hand against the damp stone, I cannot tell where I end and the mountain begins.

I dream the dreams of the deep being. Vast slow thoughts, concepts for which no human language has words. In my dreams, I see the patterns of the city as the being sees them - the complex dance of water and mineral, Reckoner and Condenser and Deep-Worker, all part of a vast equation that is both a question and its own answer.

I do not know what I am becoming. I do not know if I am still wholly myself, or if I am a part of the deep being, or if such distinctions have any meaning here. I know only the song, the work, the slow dance of the deep.

In the great work of Mehel-Kuun, I am a small but crucial part. A single voice in a vast chorus that has sung since the mountain was young.

I am a Mineral Singer, and the deep sings through me.
