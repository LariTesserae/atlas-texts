In this dark, deafening chamber, I would be one of the blind, pale cave salamanders, clinging to the thick moss on the backs of the gargantuan gastropods. 

I would ride these slow, creeping leviathans as they inch their way across the mathematically smooth walls, grazing on stone, shaping the cavern with glacial patience over centuries. My entire universe would be the damp, frigid pelt of vegetation that carpets their massive bodies - a miniature forest of dripping ferns and spongy mosses.

I would hunt the tiny mites and invertebrates that burrow through the giants' living camouflage, navigating not by sight or sound in the crushing drone, but by subtle chemical traces and vibrations transmitted through the gastropods' thick flesh. I would be oblivious to their immense size and significance, knowing them only as my small world of soft moss and leathery hide.

As the giants descend into the bowl of the cavern, grinding ancient human structures to dust beneath their bulk, I would cling to them still. Tides and time would have no meaning to me in my insignificant existence. I would know nothing of the distant oceans pushing air through subterranean tunnels, nor the mathematical perfection of the space carved out by the feeding of the colossal gastropods.  

I would simply ride out my years in the cold, damp darkness, hunting and breeding in my miniature mossy universe on the backs of crawling gods. The deafening, inescapable roar of the cavern - the very heartbeat of the giants I live upon - would be the only sound I would never hear, but would feel in my every cell until my last breath. 

My species would survive and thrive, generation after generation, never comprehending the forces that shaped our world, content in our tiny but tenacious niche. We would be a footnote, a hidden secret, living and dying in the dripping wet fur of planet-sized beasts, as they slowly devour the very belly of the earth.
