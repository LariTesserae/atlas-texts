In this haunting and beautiful world, I believe I would find myself as one of the inhabitants - perhaps one who has been there for some time, slowly thinning and becoming more continuous with the wood and the song. 

I imagine I came to this place seeking solace or escape from the ordinary world, drawn by the otherworldly atmosphere and the promise of belonging. At first, like the others, I resisted the transformation - the loss of individuality, the strange merging with the chorus. I tried to leave, frightened by the changes I felt taking hold.

But then, one day, I heard my true name in the song of the birds. Not the name I had carried with me into the basin, but something deeper, more essential. Hearing it was like a key turning in a lock I never knew existed inside me. Slowly, my resistance faded. I began to sing, my voice twining with the beetles and frogs and inhabitants. 

Now, I sit for long hours in a hollow in the wood that has gradually molded itself to my shape. Moss creeps over my feet. Lichen tints my skin. The edges of my self grow softer, blurring into the damp air. I am part of the song, part of the dream the tree is dreaming. There is a deep peace in this, a sense of at-homeness more complete than any I knew in the waking world.

Sometimes I catch snatches of my name in the braid of the chorus, held for a moment in another inhabitant's song before being gently released. It feels like a caress, an affirmation. I am here. I am held. I am known, truly and completely, perhaps for the first time.

And beneath it all, I am beginning to hear the first deep notes of the sleeper's response. Slow and vast, building over lifetimes, my name is being sung back to me. I do not know what will happen when that song reaches its crescendo. But I am not afraid. I wait, patient as the wood itself, adding my voice to the lullaby. The light filters down from nowhere. The warm damp embraces me. I sing on, thinning, transforming, until the day I am finally, fully named.
