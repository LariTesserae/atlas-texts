I would be a small, iridescent beetle in the Pumphouse District. 

I would spend my days orbiting the single sodium-vapor lamp at the intersection of Pump Street and the Gutter, part of the cloud of beetles that has circled that light for generations. My chitinous body would shimmer in shades of green and violet as I flew my continuous route around the amber glow, my flight path merging seamlessly with that of my fellow beetles until our individual movements became indistinguishable from the collective dance.

The humid, still air of the district would cling to my delicate wings, but it would not impede my flight. I am adapted to this place, to its heavy atmosphere and diffuse light. The heat radiating from the pavement below would buoy me upwards, an invisible thermal current that I navigated by instinct.

At night, I would rest on the warm metal of the lamp housing itself, my tiny clawed feet gripping the surface. The vibrations of the endles murmur of pumps would resonate through my body as I slept, a mechanical lullaby. 

On rare occasions, I might fall prey to one of the well-fed cats that drape themselves over the pipe housings - a flash of tooth and claw, there and gone. But my loss would scarcely impact the cloud of beetles as a whole. Our numbers hold steady, an integral part of the district's equilibrium.

I would live my compact life never knowing anything beyond the few square blocks bounded by rail embankments and flood wall. The wet streets, the tea-colored light filtering through the hazy air, the rhythms of industry and organic life woven inextricably together - this would be my entire world, and I would want for nothing else.

In this place of profound, settled stillness, I would be a tiny spark of motion, a minuscule but essential part of the greater pattern. I would be at home in the Pumphouse District, content in my belonging to this strange urban ecosystem that hums and breathes and endures, as timeless and unchanging as bedrock.
