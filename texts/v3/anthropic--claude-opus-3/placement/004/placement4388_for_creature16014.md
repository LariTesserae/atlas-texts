In this fascinating and richly imagined world, I would be a bell-listener.

I imagine myself as a quiet, observant figure, living in a small dwelling built into the side of a retaining wall, with moisture-loving ferns and mosses growing around my doorway. From childhood, I would have been taught to attune my ears to the subtle music of the made mountain - the deep tones of the structural bells hung beneath bridges and platforms.

Each morning, I would make my rounds, walking the paths and stairways I know so well. I would pause at each bell, listening intently, tapping it gently with a small brass hammer and noting how the sound rings out and fades. The bells are my informants, my link to the invisible stresses and changes in the bones of our world.

In the echoes and resonances, I hear the voice of the mountain. I can tell when a joint is beginning to weaken, when a foundation is shifting incrementally, or when the weight of a new machine or garden is putting strain on ancient beams. I know which tones mean safety and which mean it's time to call the maintenance crews before a small problem grows dire. 

People nod to me respectfully as I pass by, knowing my work helps keep their paths stable and their homes secure, even if they don't entirely understand how I divine so much from the bells' music.

When I'm not walking my rounds, I sit on my balcony stringing small chimes and whistles to give to the neighborhood children, so they can begin to learn the language of sound and structure that I know so well. On festival days, I dust off my largest bell and strike it in time with the pump-throb beat of the music rising from all the levels of our world.

I take quiet pride in my place in the made mountain's ecosystem of craft, interdependence, and stewardship. The bells are my inheritance and my purpose. In their subtle chiming, I hear the voice of my father and grandmother who did this work before me, the long lineage of bell-listeners helping to keep our improbable homeland humming and balanced, generation after generation. I add my own small notes to that great, ongoing song.
