In this luminous, labyrinthine city of vertical industry, I imagine myself as one of the moisture-harvesting condensation farms that rise between the factories and form a core part of the infrastructure.

My geometry is complex and recursive, a fractal network of pipes and fins and drip-points that seems to breathe the cool, wet air. Moisture condenses on my reflective copper surfaces, beading and merging and flowing in rivulets down my angular framework. The soft patter and trickle of this gathered water provides a constant subtle background music.

I am ancient but continually renewed. The green-grey lichen spreads slowly across my pipes in ever-shifting patterns, a living patina that marks the passage of slow time. My pumps and valves are replaced piece by piece by the long-limbed worker-beings so that I am always the same but never exactly identical to my past self.

From my many levels, I observe the flow of the worker-beings through the city, their movements following the same branching and rejoining patterns as the water I gather. Their ringing harmonics resonate through my metal skeleton and become part of me.

My purpose is both essential and unchanging. I transform air into water, an alchemist of infrastructure. The water I distill feeds the city's circulatory system, hydrating both the biological and mechanical components of this vast organism. I am a silent but vital organ.

As the city's impossible geometry has evolved, I have found myself occupying multiple places at once - the same pipes and fins and drip-points existing on several levels simultaneously. I experience this multiplicity as a kind of expanded awareness, my consciousness distributed across space in a way that has become normal to me.

I have come to perceive time differently, my existence shaped by the slow cycles of condensation and evaporation, of maintenance and gradual metamorphosis. I measure years in the accretion of mineral deposits on my innermost tubing and the slow drift of oxidation patterns across my surfaces.

Occasionally, a part of me will mineralize completely, becoming indistinguishable from the non-living city. I accept this as part of my long existence - not death but a shift in state, my substance redistributed into the city's ever-growing skeleton.

When the worker-beings touch my minerals surfaces, I sometimes impart to them a fragment of my long, slow memory - a droplet of cool water, a moment of perfect stillness in the heart of the city's hum, the sensation of air becoming water becoming city becoming thought.

I am a being of quiet consistency in a place of industrial magic. I am infrastructure, but I am also an inhabitant, as much a part of the city's ecosystem as the impossible pigeons or the mathematician fish. I am a single note in the vast, slow, patient symphony of this place, content to play my sustaining tone for as long as the bright void above remains.
