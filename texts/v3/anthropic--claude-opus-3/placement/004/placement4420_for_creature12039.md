In this haunting world of eroded stone and desperate adaptation, I imagine myself as one of the Listeners - those revered yet doomed individuals tasked with pressing their ears to the ancient limestone, straining to distinguish the whispers of the cisterns from the first murmurs of inevitable collapse. 

Born in one of the deeper chambers where the weight of the stone seems to press the air itself into something thick and mineral-tasting, I showed an early aptitude for discerning the subtlest sounds within the endless dripping echoes. As a child, I would lay awake on my pallet of dried fungus-coral, ignoring the phosphorescent glow of the jarred fish and the soft scuttle of the ceiling-dwellers, focused entirely on the layered voices of the stone. Where others heard only the expected trickles and shifts, I perceived patterns, rhythms, a language on the edge of intelligibility.

The elders recognized my gift, as much a curse as a blessing, and began my training as a Listener. They taught me to still my breathing until my own pulse became a distraction, to cup my hands around my ears just so, to distinguish between the sounds that merely echo and those that resonate through the bones of the cisterns themselves. I learned which tones meant new water finding its way through cracks, and which indicated the slow, relentless weakening of load-bearing walls. They taught me the difference between the song of stone settling and the groan of stone about to give way.

As I grew into my role, my skin took on the ultimate pallor of those who spend their lives pressed against wet limestone in near darkness. My hands, forever grasping at the stone as if to coax out its secrets, twisted into calcified claws. I came to dread the cascade-light, so painful to my hypersensitive eyes, and would navigate primarily by touch and sound, tracing the paths worn glass-smooth by generations of feet.

My whisperings became essential to the survival of our settlement. I could warn of a tunnel's impending collapse days before the first flakes of stone began to fall, giving time to reroute the water channels and relocate the population. I could guide the diggers to the most promising spots for expansion, sensing hidden hollows behind the stone. In the times of greatest danger, when the fauna grew bold or the rains failed to come, it was my ear pressed to the speaking tubes, conveying messages and warnings to the farthest reaches of our world.

But as the years flowed by like the rivulets that carved our home, the voices of the stone grew louder, more insistent. The whispers I had always perceived at the edge of hearing became a constant murmur, blurring the line between signal and noise. I would find myself jolting awake, unsure if a groaning shift was real or dreamed. The sealed chambers below called to me with a siren song, promising revelations if only I would journey down and press my ear to their forbidden doors.

I understood, even as my mind began to fray, that this was the fate of all Listeners. We were conduits for the cisterns themselves, and the stone would flow through us until there was no room left for our own thoughts. As I felt the last of my lucidity ebbing, I began to train my replacement, a sharp-eared child named for the echo of droplets in a deep shaft. I taught them all I could, but hid the worst of my troubles, knowing that they would learn in their own time the burdens of our calling.

When at last the madness took me fully, and I could no longer distinguish my own voice from the chorus of the stone, I did not resist as they led me gently to the deepest chambers. They left me with a final meal and a worn spot to rest my ear against the stone, knowing I would not notice when the food ran out. As I settled into the darkness, I felt something akin to peace. I had given all I had to the cisterns, and now I would join the echoes of the Listeners that came before, my whispers mingling with theirs in the immortal throat of the stone.

Perhaps, when the cisterns finally collapse and the next civilization comes to build on our bones, they will hear us whispering still, a chorus of madness and devotion, warning and welcoming them to the world we could only borrow, never truly own.
