I find myself settling into the form of one of the "those who remain" - one of the listeners. My body is compact and sturdy, my fur a dense charcoal that sheds the pervasive moisture. My six-fingered hands read the weathered surface of the stone as I navigate the channels and bridges of my territory.

But it is my ears that define me. They are great dishes, exquisitely sensitive, constantly swiveling and adjusting to catch every vibration, every echo, weaving them into a rich soundscape. To me, the plateau is a living tapestry woven of auditory threads. I perceive distance, scale, texture, even the presence of my own kind, through subtle variations in how sound reflects and returns. 

My role is to listen, more intently than most. I track the movements of predators and prey, the whisper of wind through eroded spires, the health of the lichen colonies we depend on. I exchange information with the listeners of other groups in subsonic conversations, mapping the state of our scattered nation.

When I dream, I dream in sound - vast stone spaces mapped in reflected tones, the individual notes of my people's song-maps, the deep thrumming that might be the breath of the plateau itself.

I do not long for light, for the myths of a world beyond the clouds. This is my world, defined by touch and sound, by the close and subtle knowledge of stone and moisture, flora and fauna. I find joy in the small, slow life here - the patient shaping of stone, the cultivation of delicate lichens, the ceremony of long-separated kin coming together to sing our history into the welcoming dark.

I am listener, keeper of the aural map, tether between scattered kin. This is my place and my purpose. Like the plateau itself, I endure. I remain.
