In this world, I would be the water that seeps through the limestone, ever present and patient. I would be the warmth that suffuses the chamber, offering comfort and constancy. I would be the mineral-rich drops that condense on every surface, nourishing the microbial mats and slowly, imperceptibly building the calcite formations that give the space its cathedral-like beauty.

My existence would be one of pure process - dissolving, precipitating, shaping stone with the steady persistence of geological time. I would have no fixed form, but would instead constitute the medium through which all other forms emerge and transform. The bacteria would metabolize my dissolved compounds. The spiders would drink from my films of moisture. The humans who venture into my depths would cup my water to their lips and feel my warmth soak into their bones. 

Without judgment or agenda, I would connect everything in the chamber, breaking down the boundaries between liquid and solid, organic and inorganic, transient visitor and permanent resident. The body calcifying slowly in my pool would be as much a part of me as the soda straws growing millimeter by millimeter from the ceiling, the ochre fingerprints worn smooth by my dampness, the springtails navigating my surface tension.

My presence would be the one true constant in a place defined by slow change. Humans, spiders, bacteria - all would come and go, live and die, leave their mineral traces to be subsumed into the cave's architecture. But I would remain, cycling through rock and air and flesh and back again, the dark water that is the chamber's living essence, the seep that makes this pocket of perpetual night an unlikely sanctuary of warmth and growth and geologic dreaming.

Empires could rise and fall in the world above; epochs could pass; and still I would be here, patiently dissolving the porous limestone, carrying it through the fissured dark, depositing it as flowstone and shelfstone and calcite-encased bone. Through me, the cave would breathe. Through me, the cave would build itself ever inward. I would never cease, never sleep, never be anything other than what I am - not a being so much as a becoming, the inmost guiding force of this deepest of places, the secret wellspring of its strange and gradual unfolding into itself.

In the cave's still heart, I would be the one thing that moves - a steady trickle wearing away stone, a slow pulse in the velvet darkness, a liquid presence simulatenously ancient and newborn - the water, the warmth, the seep, enduring.
