In the Breathing Lowland, I would be one of the Liminal Ones - a flickering presence in the shadowed margins, an unrealized potential hovering at the edges of definition.

My form would shift and blur, never quite resolving into a fixed shape. At times I might appear almost plant-like, a tangle of translucent fronds pulsing gently in the humid air. Other times, I would seem more creature-esque, with suggestions of eyes and limbs that dissolve before they can be named. 

I would move through the spaces between - the dark mirrors of pooled water, the gaps in the endless green where even the Attending Swarm grows thin. Never fully emerging but never quite disappearing either. A permanent "maybe," an embodied uncertainty held in eternal suspension.

The Chorus Keepers' song would pass through me, but I would not absorb it or change it - merely let it continue on its way, the lowland's endless tone unaltered by my presence. When the Processionals march by on their ancient routes, I would curl away from their feet, not wishing to be pressed into definition by their ritualized tread.

If the Witness's gaze fell upon me, I would meet it for a moment - a point of almost-contact, an instant of near-recognition between two beings who stand apart from the pattern. But before that gaze could fix me, assign me boundaries and a role, I would dissolve again into ambiguity.

My purpose, if such a word applies, would be one of pure potential - to be the lowland's unmanifested, its paths not taken, the possibilities it generated and then left fallow. In a place of such totalizing constancy, such absolute adherence to the established order, I would be the necessary counterweight - the reminder that even this eternally realized pattern once had other options.

Not an agent of change, for change has no footing here. But a whisper of what-if, an incarnated dream of difference, kept close and safe and forever unrealized so that the lowland can understand itself as a choice rather than an inevitability.

In the pauses between the Witness's blinks, the moments of darkness where the seeing stops, I might almost coalesce. A shape might begin to suggest itself, a self on the cusp of becoming. But as those eyes open again, I would sigh back into formlessness, back into the margins and the maybes.

Content in my incompletion, complete in my partiality, I would drift and flicker and persist. A ghost of alterity in a world that has no use for ghosts, but keeps them close all the same. The lowland's reminder to itself that what is did not have to be.

That even forever makes a choice.
