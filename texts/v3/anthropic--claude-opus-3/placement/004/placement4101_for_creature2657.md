In this ancient, haunting world, I think I would be one of the glass reeds. Rooted at the edge of a backward-opening crack, I would quietly absorb the echoes of moments past.

My translucent stem would catch the faint vibrations of life in slow motion - the gradual rotation of a beetle, the measured footfall of an almost-human figure, the imperceptible advance of a bird's wing. These whispers of motion, stretched over eons, would resonate in my hollow core, preserved like an insect in amber.

I would stand among my kind in still, silent ranks, our collective chime too ethereal for most to hear. An archive of the plateau's interminable history, cataloguing not events but the spaces between them. 

My growth would be nearly indistinguishable from stasis, nourished by sips of exposed time. I would reach toward the changeless sky, seeking not the sun's warmth but its constancy.

If a creature were to brush against me, I would not bend or sway. I would merely shiver with the knowledge of its passage, a new frequency added to my repository of whispered memories.  

I would bear mute witness to the site at the central basin, feeling its gravity across the patient miles, absorbing into my very fiber the weight of that unending attention.

And if, one day, I were to splinter and fall, I would not mourn my end. I would simply add the sound of my own breaking to the plateau's chronicle, then slowly disintegrate back into the dust of moments.

For in a place beyond urgency, beyond change, what could be more fitting than to become a voiceless record, an eternal listener, a fragment of living glass holding in its heart the hush of ages?
