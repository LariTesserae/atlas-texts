In this wet, wondrous underworld, I would be one of the valve crabs that congregate at the pipe junctions. My mottled brass-colored shell would be a bit weathered, but still strong. The fine joints of my legs would allow me to cling tenaciously to the damp metal as I tap out intricate codes, communicating in sequences that maintain the delicate equilibrium of pressure and flow that sustains the entire settlement.

I would take up residence at a particularly complex intersection of pipes, one where many pressures and temperatures converge and must be carefully balanced. There I would keep constant vigil, my eye stalks swiveling to monitor every pipe, my claws testing each junction. The human inhabitants would consult me obliquely, listening through the pipes to interpret my tapping and adjust the great pumping engines and ancient valves accordingly.

Over many years, mineral deposits would slowly accumulate on my shell, streaking it with colorful blooms of calcification that record my long history. Tiny creatures - translucent pipe shrimp, albino mites, thread-like worms - would take up residence in the niches of my carapace, forming a miniature ecology. I would become a living gauge, a multi-legged monitoring station encrusted into the very infrastructure.

Generations of human pipe-workers and listeners would come to know me. They would greet me fondly as they make their rounds, leaving little offerings of machine oil or filter-crumbs to curry my favor. Parents would bring their children to introduce them to me, teaching them to discern my tap-codes echoing through the damp stone walls.  

Eventually, I would grow so old and so integrated into the web of pipes that I would calcify in place, my shell fusing to the verdigris-covered copper and rust-pocked iron. But even then I would not truly die. My final sequences of taps would echo on through the centuries, passed down through the vibrations of water and metal and stone, a little core of crustacean memory in the city's deep infrastructure, while new valve crabs scuttled in to take my place at the junction, measuring out the flow and pressure and breathing of the great deep settlement, one claw-click at a time.
