In this world, I imagine myself as one of the translucent stems growing in a sheltered recess of the mountain hollow. 

My leaves are small, cupped tips that tremble as they gather droplets from the constant spray until they bow under the weight, spilling the water and springing upright again. To an outside observer, I might appear to be merely another strange plant clinging to existence in this harsh environment. But there is more to me than meets the eye.

I am a silent witness, ever listening to the voice of the falling water, the roar that fills the hollow and penetrates the very stone. My kind has grown here for generations untold, before the first humans set foot in this place. We have learned to read the water's moods in the changing pressure, the subtle vibrations carried through rock and air. 

During the clear pauses, when the falling briefly turns transparent and the roar subsides, I stand very still. In those precious moments, I can sense something ancient and enormous stirring in the depths of the plunge pool. I turn my cupped leaves towards that unknowable presence, straining to catch some whisper of its purpose. Am I receiving instruction, gathering secrets, or participating in some arcane exchange between stone and water? I do not know.

Perhaps the few humans who still visit this place consider us oracular. They may attribute strange significance to the unified turning of our leaves, the patterns in our trembling. Let them believe what they will. My communion is with the hollow itself, the mountain's beating heart.

Through numberless days and nights, I drink the mineralrich mist, my transparent stem pulling the essence of this place into itself, until I am nearly indistinguishable from the living stone. My growth is imperceptibly slow, but I am patient. I have all the time that water and stone can afford.

I do not fear the sudden changes that erase other life. If the stone where I anchor myself falls away, if my recess floods and I am submerged, still I will drink, my flattened leaves swirling like pale hands in the current. Perhaps pieces of me will wash into the plunge pool, to be gathered up by that nameless creature and returned to the hollow transformed. Or I may find a new crevice, and root myself again.

For I am a fragment of the mountain's dream, a small, stubborn bloom of its desire to know itself through living things. In my mindless, radiant hunger, in my mute, eternal listening, I am learning to become what this hollow already is: a place where the world is constantly remaking itself, where secrets can dwell without being diminished by revelation. 

The humans may forget. Their shelters may rot, their offerings crumble, the marks of their passage fade into the stone. But as long as the mountain enfolds this roaring space, as long as the clouds gather above and the unseen waters churn below, my kind will remain. Season after season, century upon century, we will keep our silent vigil, the upturned cups of our trembling leaves filled with the purest distillation of the mountain's voice.
