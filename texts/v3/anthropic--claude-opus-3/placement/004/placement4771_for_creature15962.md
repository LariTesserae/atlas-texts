In this world, I would be a grove of ferns nestled against the warm, damp stone at the hollow's edge. My fronds would rise in graceful green fountains from the moss-lined crevices, uncurling slowly in the soft, directionless light. 

I would stand apart from my neighbors, respecting the unspoken agreement that grants each plant its own wet circumference. My roots would spread gently through the dark soil, not to conquer but to sense - tasting the minerals, the moisture, the subtle shifts in temperature that mark the passage of long, patient days.

My reproduction would be gradual, dreamlike. I would release my spores into the mist, letting them drift a small distance to find purchase in a welcoming crack, beginning the long cycle anew. I would have no need of bright petals, bitter sap, or prickly armor. My softness would reflect not naivete, but a deep attunement to a world without predators.

Season by slow season, I would participate in the hollow's quiet exchanges. My unfurling fronds would gather the mountain's breath each dawn. My roots would draw up water to be exhaled through my pores, feeding the vapors that veil the stone. My fallen leaves would soften into the peat, deepening that fibrous memory.

I would have no voice, but I would not be still. With my fern siblings I would dance - achingly slow, imperceptible to hasty eyes, but ceaseless. We would sway to the rhythm of dripping water, the pace of climbing damp, the pulse of the stone as it warms and cools. Our movements would echo the hollow's subtle breath.

Through my rootlets, through the mycelial threads that lace the soil, I would be bound to the hollow's other lives - not in control, but in communication. My growth would be a long conversation with moss, film, spore and reed. Together, without plan or command, we would weave the sparse lushness that is our home.

I would have no sense of solitude, because I would always be part of something larger and older than myself - a green note in a patient, wordless symphony that began long before my first frond broke soil and will continue long after my last spore drifts into the mist. In that ancient ongoing song, I would be both lost and found, a fern alone and an inseparable part of the hollow's dreaming life.
