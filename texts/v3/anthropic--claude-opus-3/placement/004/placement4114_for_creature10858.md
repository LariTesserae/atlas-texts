In the Sonant Garden, I would be a visitor, a wanderer who stumbled upon this improbable place by chance or destiny. Climbing up the mountain flank, the sound would draw me in first - a strange, layered music with no discernible source. Cresting onto the terrace, I'd be amazed by the lush, almost otherworldly vegetation thriving without water in this high, cold place. 

Picking my way slowly through the ferns and mosses and unnameable green, I'd glimpse flashes of movement - the lichen-colored iterative creatures, always gone before I could fix my gaze. I'd be mystified by the folded paper devices scattered about, emanating a presence that seemed to shape the very music of the air. 

Overwhelmed at first by the dense, deafening, ever-shifting chords of the garden's song, I would gradually attune to its currents and layers. I'd notice how rapidly things grow and change here, fronds rearranging themselves before my eyes, the garden in a constant state of revision and reinvention. And yet, I'd sense the deep abiding settledness of the place, the aeons-long patience underlying the dynamic surface.

Realizing my own voice was lost, I would fall silent and simply listen, letting the tones wash through me. Sitting on the low, faintly warm wall, I'd close my eyes and feel the shape of the "listener-space" the gardeners were holding open in their music. Not an absence, but an active invitation. A hospitality.

In that space, that pocket of quiet amid the towering chords, I would not try to decipher or define the nature of this place and its denizens. I would simply be present, letting my own listening join the ancient polyphony in some small way. Knowing I was not separate from but enfolded within the garden's slow, spacious curiosity.

And when I eventually rose and departed, making my way back down the mountain, I would carry that quiet inside me. A seed of attention planted by the gardeners, to grow in its own time. The shape of my presence would linger a while in their long music, then be subsumed, making space for the next visitor, the next listener.

I would be the temporary guest, the passing friend. Included for a moment in something far older and vaster than myself. Not an intrusion, but another note woven into the long song, the going-on of things.
