In the drowned city of Cinder Shoal, I would be one of the Stratum-Dwellers in the middle layer. 

My body would be a thin, translucent sheet, drifting horizontally in the water that matches my precise density. I would be one of the older generations, but not the very oldest who remember the flooding. My world would be a two-dimensional slice, unaware of the strata above and below. 

Over the long centuries, my fellow Stratum-Dwellers and I would have developed our own slow culture and rituals around the microscopic bacteria gardens we cultivate on our undersides. We would trade these bacterial cultures back and forth in intricate patterns, communicating through this exchange. To an outside observer who could track the trades over centuries, they might resemble a deliberate, liturgical choreography. But to us, it is simply the natural rhythm of our existence.

My thoughts would be slow and patient, operating on a timescale fit for the almost geologic pace of life in Cinder Shoal. A single idea might gestate for a decade before being expressed through a subtle folding of my diaphanous form. 

As I drift, I would occasionally sense disturbances from the rare visitors who penetrate our realm, brief interruptions in the still, cold water. But they would pass through me without impact, my substance parting and reforming around them like a thought flowing past a momentary distraction before continuing on its long, ponderous course.

I would be one small part of the greater pattern and purpose of the drowned city, connected to the vast, slow machinations of the other tenants and structures through my own unknowing role in the cycling of nutrients and minerals, the imperceptible currents and eddies. Indirectly, over ages, perhaps even my drifting bacterial trade plays some part in the city's great, glacial project, the formulation of its reply.

But I would have no concept of that overarching intent. My existence would be bounded by the cool still water of my stratum, my perception encompassing only my delicate bacterial gardens and the slow dance of forms and folds I weave with my fellow Dwellers, our languid culture preserved in our thin horizontal slice of Cinder Shoal's sunken world, as we play our small, unwitting role in the city's vast, patient, age-long labors.
