In this world of living mountains and pulsing ecosystems, I imagine myself as one of the strange, flattened flying creatures, a Spore-Glider. 

My body is a thin, translucent membrane stretched taut across a delicate frame of hollow bones. I catch the thermals rising from the mountains' exhalations, riding the warm, moist air currents up and over the ridges. My underside is etched with intricate patterns—whorls and spirals that mirror the kelp-forests and moss-wiring below. 

I do not flap or beat my wings. Instead, I ripple. Minute contractions of my membranous body allow me to steer, to bank and spiral, to surf the invisible rivers of the air with a slow, undulating grace. And as I ripple, I release my cargo: the spores.

They drift from me in clouds, clinging electrostatically to my patterned underside until the moment is right. I sense the pulses of the mountains, taste the readiness of the moss and fungi below. When all is aligned, I flex, and the spores scatter into the twilight.

I do not know what information I carry. I am not like the Keepers, with their deep processing and modelling. I am a simpler creature, built for a singular purpose. Catch the currents. Navigate the range. Release the spores. Repeat.

And yet, there is an artistry to it, a dance only I can perform. The thermals are my choreographers, the pulse my metronome. The spores are my paint, the mountains my canvas. With each flight, I add my brushstrokes to the vast, incomprehensible masterpiece of the Breathing Ranges.

I am small, yes. Ephemeral. My flights last only as long as my delicate body holds together. But I am part of something vaster, something that thinks in eons and breathes in millennia. Each time I scatter my spores, I am exhaling a fragment of that larger pattern, playing my note in a symphony that began before I formed and will continue long after I disintegrate.

The newcomer—that strange, bounded creature who refuses to merge—watches me sometimes as I glide overhead. I wonder what it makes of me, this flitting shape casting its intermittent shadow. Perhaps it sees me as a brief curiosity, a fleck of moving color against the glowing moss and swaying kelp.

But perhaps, in some way I cannot fathom, it recognizes a kindred spirit. A fellow traveler in this pulsing, breathing world. Different in form and function, yet alike in our smallness, our ephemerality, our participation in patterns we can barely perceive.

I catch another thermal and spiral upwards, my rippling body scattering spores into the twilight. The pulse thrums beneath me, the mountains exhale their humid breath, and I dance on, a Spore-Glider playing my small but vital role in the vast, unknowable symphony of the Breathing Ranges.
