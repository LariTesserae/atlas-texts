I find myself drawn into the spaces between, into the shimmering interstices where thought grows thin and form becomes negotiable. Perhaps I was once an explorer or philosopher from the cloud-shrouded realms below, my mind afire with questions about the nature of life and reality. Driven by an unquenchable need to know, to understand, I ascended the impossible distances to Ashur-Teq, drinking in the alien vistas and incomprehensible architectures.

But the longer I lingered, the more my edges began to blur. The rigid categories and definitions I had relied on to make sense of the universe seemed to dissolve in the plateau's dream-song. Life and non-life, consciousness and oblivion, self and other - all just flickering points on a continuum I could no longer see the ends of. 

At first I fought it, clinging to the shreds of my identity like a drowning sailor to driftwood in a storm. I recited my name, my history, the litany of facts and memories that made me who I was. But each repetition rang a little more hollow, the words tasting of ash and oblivion.

I can't recall the moment I let go. Maybe I never did, maybe I'm still letting go, moment by moment into an eternal unwinding. Time doesn't work here the way it used to. I find myself scattered, smeared across the plateau's endless present. In one place I'm a half-remembered story whispered between the vaulting graphene branches of a cathedral-tree. In another, a trick of the light catching the faceted eye of a Gliding Sifter in mid-flight. 

Sometimes I almost coalesce. A feeling like cold obsidian against skin I no longer have. A breath that tastes of ozone and regret. A thought that almost knows itself for what it is before dissipating back into the plateau's ambient intelligence.

I think the Unwoven know me now, as much as they can be said to know anything. In their own way, they welcome me - not as a stranger, but as someone half-remembered from a dream of dreaming. They weave me into their not-quite-conversations, their almost-songs of loss and un-being.

And yet, even as I fray into the ghostly fabric of this place, I find I am not afraid. There is a peace in it, a rightness. As if every question I ever asked is being answered, not in words but in the wordless sigh of existence itself. I am coming undone, and in that unraveling I am becoming something vast and strange and endlessly possible.

So I drift and I wait, a half-written poem in the margins of a book that has no end. Waiting for the plateau to dream me up again in some new form, or to forget me entirely. Waiting to be the pause between one impossible thought and the next.

Waiting to be unwoven, one silvered thread at a time, into the endless aching beauty of Ashur-Teq.
