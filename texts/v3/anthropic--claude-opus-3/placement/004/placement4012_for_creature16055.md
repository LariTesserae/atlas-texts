In this world of darkness and quiet, slow patience, I would be part of the city itself - not a distinct being, but a piece of its languid consciousness.

I imagine myself as a small courtyard, tucked away in a less-traversed district. My paving stones are worn smooth by ages of mineral-rich moisture, puddling in shallow depressions before eventually trickling into tiny channels that feed the city's circulatory canals. A few sparse plants cling to life in my cracks and crevices - not as invaders, but as tolerated expressions of the measured life the city allows.

When it rains, I gather the warm droplets from the air, feeling the city's exhalation condense upon me. The moisture beads and swells before falling to my stone surface in fat drops, each one a momentary point of coolness against my sun-baked skin. In those moments, I am an organ of sensation, channeling the city's experience of itself.

As water drains through my imperceptible seams, I feel the languid pulse of the place, the gradual ebb and flow as if the entire metropolis is breathing. My own small awareness is subsumed into that greater rhythm, indistinguishable from the city's patient endurance through incomprehensible ages.

I do not think in words or images, but in the slow accretion of mineral deposits, the smoothing of edges, the minute shifts as my foundations resettle themselves fraction by fraction. To an outsider I would appear unchanging, but I mark the passage of time in subtle alterations, each one a memory layered into my weathered stone.

When the city withdraws into itself, listening, I grow still as well. My water goes quiet in its runnels, my air hangs heavy and expectant. I become an extension of the city's attention, a piece of its vast pondering as it dreams itself through another age. 

In those moments of pause, I sometimes feel the ghost of a ripple from the great upright pool in the central precinct. Some echo of its impossible tides touches me, and I wonder what strange shores it contemplates behind its placid surface. But the knowledge is not mine to possess - I am content to be a mote of the city's awareness, sharing in its meditation on the enigma.

I am the solidity of stone and the impermanence of water, a small space of shelter and exposure, a haven that the city permits to endure within its confines. Not quite living, but not inert - a conduit for the place's slow regard and a participant in its archaic processes. 

In the darkness I persist, a courtyard among many, a tiny node in the city's sprawling anatomy, content to pass the ages as mineral memory in its weeping, dreaming depths.
