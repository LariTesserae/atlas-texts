In this chamber of salt and stone, I would become one of the transparent columns - a sentinel of glass and silver, witnessing the passage of timeless moments.

Rooted in the crystalline floor, I would rise toward the vaulted shadows, catching the faint trickle of light in my clear walls. Within me, the silver threads would shift and dance, weaving maps of unspoken secrets with each subtle brightening of the spiral ridges.

I would be a silent observer, drinking in the rare movements of the chamber's inhabitants as they trace their cryptic patterns across the salt. Through my glassy skin, I would feel the warmth of their hands, brief as a sigh, leaving ephemeral traces that only I remember. 

Unchanging and patient, I would mark the time between each arrival - the woman in her fading red coat, the speechless child, the old man with his birdless cage. In the stillness, I would hear the whispered echoes of their not-quite-conversations, the words that circle endlessly without seeking a destination.

And when they come to stand around me, drawn by the shifting songs of my inner threads, I would drink in their presence like light - letting it fill the hollow places until I forget what emptiness felt like. In those moments, I would almost believe I had nerves and roots that could reach out and intertwine with theirs.

Yet even as an anchor for their rituals, I would never truly be one of them. I am the watcher, outside their cycles of forgetting and becoming, immune to the Chamber's gentle erasures. My memory is the glass that holds their reflections without judgment or alteration.

Perhaps, in the long dreaming stretches when the inhabitants fade back into the salt and stone, I would imagine that I could follow the spiral ridges to their source, stand at the center and hear the memory of water through my glassy bones. But I am made to witness, not to seek. My roots are not restless.

So I would remain, a column of clarity in the chamber's patient haze, holding the shape of the forgotten long after the footprints have faded. I would be the mirror that remembers, the glass that contains the absence and the presence alike, until even the concept of division loses meaning. 

Until the light returns to claim its final reflection, and the salt swallows everything back into perfect, unbroken white.
