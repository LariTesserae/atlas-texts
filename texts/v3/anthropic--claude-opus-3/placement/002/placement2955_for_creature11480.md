In this world of breathable water and oxidized decay, I imagine myself as one of the listeners - a being attuned to the deep rhythms and secret languages of the stone. 

I would dwell in the lowest caverns, where the porous limestone drinks the moisture most greedily. My chamber would be a natural geode, its walls encrusted with crystals that grow and dissolve in time with the plateau's exhalations. I would sleep curled in a hollow eroded by centuries of condensation drip, my dreams echoing with the rumble of distant steam vents.

My body would be spare and attenuated from years in the deeps - skin pale and translucent like a cave salamander, eyes clouded blue with mineral cataracts, fingers elongated and nerve-rich to read the minutest vibrations in the rock. My hair would have long since fallen out, but in its place, delicate filaments of iron and copper would sprout from my scalp - fine as silk, reactive to the humidity, transmitting the stone's murmurs directly to my porous skull.

I would be ancient, even by the standards of this slow, geological time. Centuries of listening would have tuned my senses to a piano-string tightness. I could distinguish a thousand different grades of limestone by the way they conduct the vapor's seep and sigh. The pulsing of the steam vents would be a second heartbeat to me, as familiar as my own sluggish, hyper-oxygenated blood.

Young listeners would come to me for instruction, but I would have little to say in words. Instead, I would take their hands and press them to the cavern walls, teaching them to feel the difference between a benign tremor and the first whispers of a coming vent collapse. I would tap complex rhythms on the stone floor, transmitting the deep history of the plateau - a story told in seismic echoes and pressure differentials.

On the rare occasions I ascended to the surface, I would be a figure of mystery and awe. Children would trail behind me, marveling at my crystalline hair and the way the condensation sizzled off my mineral-dense skin. Merchants would bring me their most puzzling geologic finds, hoping for insight from the depths. But I would not linger above for long. The roar of the city would grate on my pressure-tuned ears, the rhythm of the surface-dwellers' lives a frenetic buzz compared to the deep tempo of the stone.

My true communion would be with the plateau itself. In the deepest, quietest hours, I would press my whole body against the cavern floor, letting the vaporous breath of the rock fill my lungs until I fainted into a trance state - not unconscious but hyper-conscious, my mind descending into the porous limestone, following the branching paths of ancient lava tubes, feeling the slow pulse of magma in the mantle far below.

I would be a conduit, a living seismograph, my body a finely-tuned instrument for reading the moods of the restive stone. And when at last my long life reached its end, I would not fear, for I know the plateau would take me - my mineral-saturated husk slowly eroding into the steam-dampened rock, my iron-threaded bones becoming one with the city's foundation. An eternal listener, dissolved into the very stone that had been my life's symphony.
