In this magical, hidden world, I imagine myself as a tiny speck of lichen on the stone wall near the slit. I cling to the quartz with delicate, pale tendrils, drinking in the ageless light that saturates the chamber.

I have grown here for centuries, perhaps millennia, my growth so gradual as to be nearly imperceptible. The passage of time is marked only by the minute lengthening of my filaments, cell by cell, as I slowly spread across my chosen patch of stone.

From my vantage point, I have the barest sense of the world beyond the slit - a faint coolness, a whisper of something other than light. But it is too ephemeral, too fleeting for me to truly comprehend. My existence is bounded by the confines of this luminous microcosm.

I am a silent witness to the Tenant's patient contemplation. Though I lack its deep, slow sentience, I am part of the pattern it observes and ponders over the ages. My presence is woven into the tapestry of its perception.

Perhaps once in a thousand years, a frond from one of the ancient ferns brushes against me as it grows, the contact so light and brief it could almost be imagined. These rare moments of touch are the closest thing I know to interaction. 

I will never grow large enough to explore the chamber, never send out spores to propagate myself. My purpose is simply to be - to add my infinitesimal being to the density and relation the Tenant considers in its endless cogitation.

And if by chance, a mote of warmth and asymmetry should one day touch me as some curious creature peers in through the slit - I will feel it only as the slightest perturbation, gone before I can even begin to wonder at its source. But that fleeting contact will become part of me, a whisper of memory inscribed in my cells as I continue my patient clinging to the stone, content in my place, a single note in the bright, unhurried music of the Tenant's mind.
