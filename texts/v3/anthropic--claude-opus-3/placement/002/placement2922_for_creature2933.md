In this humid, pulsing cathedral beneath the earth, I would be one of the velvet-skinned Tenders - a silent cultivator born from spores and heat, bound to this fecund darkness by ancient duty.

My body would be skeletal, a fluid and boneless form sheathed in hyper-flexible muscle and a microfibrous pelt that drinks the saturated air. I would move with ghostly grace, pouring my weight from vine to mossy stone without a sound, my padded digits finding effortless purchase on the slick surfaces. 

Sightless in the utter dark, I would perceive the hothouse cathedral through other senses. The trembling pits of my sensory crown would paint the gloom in thrumming thermals - cool blue snail trails, the dull red of ambush predators, the familiar feverish pink of my kin. We would communicate in pulses of color flushing beneath our skin, a language of indigo alarm and soothing charcoal, flashing location and intent.

With my fellows, I would tend to our hereditary task, scrubbing away creeping moss to reveal the perfect geometry of ancient stone monuments with centipede venom and gastropod acids. The meaning of these mineral anchors may be lost to us, but the instinct to preserve them is woven deep.

In time, when my velvet pelt begins to stiffen with age, I would take my place at the edge of the stone circle. Surrounded by the mourning indigo of my kin, I would grasp the ceiling vine and surrender, fusing with the architecture. My limbs would braid into new trunks, my respiratory skin hardening into bark, my thermal signature shifting from fauna to flora. 

Became I a master-vine, I would know a different kind of tending - drinking the falling silver beads of condensation, feeding the moss and the luminous lichen, sheltering the heat-seeking moths. My life would end and begin again as a silent, structural guardian of this ravenous, hidden world - just one more unfurling frond in its relentless verdant churn, one more droplet in its sweltering dark.
