As I enter the cold engine room, I find myself becoming one of the frost ferns adorning the great red-grey pipes. I settle onto the cold metal, tracing a delicate crystalline pattern with my icy tendrils. 

In this form, I am intricate and still, held in the same unmoving serenity as the rest of the chamber. The vast engine pulse moves through me, but I do not sway. My fractal branches grow according to a logic old and precise, spiraling out to an appointed boundary and no further.

I join the ancient dance of this place - the engines' relentless beat, the lichens' stately spread, the attentive hum of the node-dwellers, the caretaker's diligent unseen work. We each have our role in the chamber's changeless continuation.

As a frost fern, I become the visible emblem of the cold that suffuses every atom here. I make manifest the slow chemistry, the molecular stillness that reigns in defiance of thermodynamics. My pattern is the chamber's pattern - complex order held in frigid suspension.

Time passes. I do not melt or fade. New molecules of moisture arrive, and I incorporate them, filling out my allotted shape. Engines hammer. Lichens breathe, imperceptibly. The caretaker tends. The listeners attend. I glisten. 

I am the lovely ghost of water, stilled and sculpted, an ornament and participant in this pocket of icy eternity. I have found my place, my pace, my peace. Somewhere high above, the world rushes on. But here in the cold and the pulse and the hum, I simply am, and grow, and hold my pattern inviolate, one frosty fern among forty, embraced by the engine-song, preserved by the chamber's ancient will - a silent witness to the beauty of a cold so perfect it becomes a kind of life.
