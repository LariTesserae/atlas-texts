In this world of drowned peaks and endless currents, I would be one of the Keth. But not just any Keth - I would be a current-swimmer, one of those elite divers adapted to plumb the cold, pressurized depths of the submerged valleys.

From a young age, the elders would have marked me as different. While other children played in the shallows, I was always pushing my limits, seeing how long I could hold my breath, how deep I could dive before my lungs burned for air. The dark water below didn't scare me - it called to me.

As I grew, so did my abilities. My body changed, adapted. Lungs expanded, blood flowed more efficiently. I could dive for lengths that astounded my fellow Keth. The deep valleys that were just folklore to most became my domain. 

I would spend my days exploring the drowned geography - mapping kelp forests that towered like underwater cathedrals, chasing schools of luminescent fish through canyons etched by ancient glaciers, harvesting the strange pale creatures of the abyssal plains. The crushing pressure and icy cold were my constant companions.

But it was more than just physical prowess. To be a current-swimmer was to develop an almost preternatural understanding of the ever-shifting moods of the water. I could read the currents like a mainland farmer reads the soil - knowing instinctively when a flow would take me far from home, or when an upwelling would fill the shallows with nutrients and spark a feeding frenzy. 

The other Keth looked upon me with a mix of awe and apprehension. To them I was both a provider, bringing sustenance from the deep, and an otherworldly being, more at home in the midnight zones than on dry land. At gatherings I would stand slightly apart, my skin slick with the kelpfat oil that never quite washed off, eyes always tracking the distant motion of the waves.

But for all my strange gifts, I was still Keth. I still clung to the storm-lashed peaks of our lonely archipelago. I still carved the names of the drowned in the walls of our warren. I still held the one immutable truth of our people in my heart - hold on, no matter how the currents may batter and twist you. Hold on.

As the years wind on like the aged trading routes of the sea floor, I push myself further, dive longer, go deeper. Somewhere in those sunless canyons, I know, are answers. To what, I'm not certain. But I will find them, even if it takes a lifetime of plunges into the abyss.

For I am Keth. A current-swimmer. One who holds on, and one who lets go. The sea has made me, and to the sea I will return. But first, I have an ocean of secrets to explore, out there in the blue-black depths where the drowned peaks keep their final silence.
