In this strange and wondrous place, I imagine myself as one of the **Listeners**.

Stretched out between the waxy stems and fleshy leaves in the middle terraces, I would be still and silent, my elongated body becoming one with the vegetation that grows over and through me. Salt crystals would form along my limbs as I remain motionless for days, weeks, years.

My clustered eyes would track the sun's slow arc across the sky, not out of need, but because it is the only discernible movement in this realm of profound stillness. When a falling salt-bead, a patrolling Tender, or a rare human visitor crossed my field of vision, I would simply note their presence, recording the shape of their passage in whatever passes for my memory.

I would listen, endlessly, for sounds or vibrations beyond human perception. Perhaps for the returning footsteps of the long-gone carvers, or for seismic whispers from distant pillars. Or perhaps I would listen simply to listen, my purpose distilled down to pure awareness, a meditation beyond understanding.

Feeding would be an almost incidental process for me, my mouthparts working slowly, constantly, at the waxy leaves. I would require very little, my metabolism slowed to match the pillar's own glacial pace.

Only rarely, driven by some enigmatic internal prompt, would I uproot myself from my vegetative trellis and relocate to a new position. An event noteworthy only for its infrequency, never remarked upon by my fellow Listeners.

And at dusk, when the violet glow of the Dreamers pulsed overhead, I would turn my eyes upward to watch, participating in some unspoken communion that affirms the pillar's profound and ancient wholeness. 

As a Listener, I would be a witness to the pillar's silent mysteries, a living embodiment of patience and stillness, so integrated into this eerie edifice as to be indistinguishable from it. A life both profoundly limited and strangely expansive in its unwavering commitment to simply observe and endure in this hushed, dreamlike, almost eternal place.
