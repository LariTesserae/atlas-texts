In the world of the abandoned smelting district, I would be one of the Kessler family's cats. Not a pet, but a working member of the household, tolerated for my usefulness.

I would be a scrawny creature, ribs showing beneath a patchy coal-black coat, one ear permanently notched from some long-ago fight. My eyes would be a bright, almost unsettling yellow-green, well adapted to cutting through the district's permanent haze. 

I would spend my days prowling the edges of the Kessler's territory, slipping soundlessly through gaps in crumbling brick walls and rusting iron grates. My primary prey would be the not-rats - those hairless, copper-flavored things that infest the ruins. I'd hunt them tirelessly, keeping their numbers down around the assay office, reducing the competition for the family's own food sources.

The Kessler children would have mixed feelings about me. Some, especially the younger ones, might try to pet me or offer me scraps, but I would tolerate no such familiarity. I'm a working beast, not a lapdog. The older children would understand better, respecting my space and valuing my skills.

At night, I would return to the assay office, slipping in through some high, narrow window to avoid the salamander-things that hunt in the dark. I'd find a warm spot near one of the thermal vents, curling up to sleep, but always keeping one eye cracked, one ear swiveled towards the door... just in case.

The Kesslers would not love me, but they would value me. In a place as harsh and unforgiving as the smelting district, that would be enough. I would be a small but vital part of the delicate ecology that allows the family to keep surviving in this hostile world.

And if I sometimes pause in my hunting, prick my ears towards that forbidden door in the deepest basement, wondering about the sounds that seem to emanate from behind it... well. I'm a cat. Curiosity is in my nature. But I'm also a survivor. I know when to let a mystery lie. In the smelting district, you learn to accept what you cannot change.

So I would continue my patrols, continue my hunts, continue playing my small role in the Kessler family's endless fight for survival. In this world of rust and steam and quiet desperation, it would be a life - and a good one, by the standards of the place. A cat could do worse.
