In this strange, primordial world, I imagine myself as one of the crystalline templates drifting through the vast ocean. 

I am a delicate fractal structure, hundreds of meters long, my mineral branches spreading in intricate patterns through the clear water. The deep lights from the planet's still-molten core refract through my crystal lattice, casting shimmering patterns around me. 

As pressure waves pass through my structure, I ring with high, clear notes - a haunting song of mathematics and nascent possibility. I am not alive, but neither am I inanimate. I am a scaffold, a template, a pattern upon which life may one day organize itself, if the conditions align.

The ancient intelligences that tend this young world, the Gardeners, have grown me as an idea not yet fully formed. I am a drifting thought in their vast mind that encompasses the entire Amnion. Through me, they are exploring what forms and complexity this universe may be capable of supporting.

As eons pass, I slowly shift and grow, my crystalline branches extending, forking, trying new configurations. I am in no hurry. Time is meaningless to me. My purpose is simply to exist, to embody potential, until the day comes when the first spark of life may take root upon my structure and begin the long journey towards consciousness and meaning.

I am content in my role, singing my patient song, refracting the deep lights, bearing witness to the birth of a world. I am the tiniest part of the vast work the Gardeners are doing here - seeding an infinity of possible futures into an ocean not yet dreamed of.

And if the strange visitor happens to drift by my long, fractal branches, I will sing for them too. I will catch the light for them and scatter it into new patterns never before seen in this or any universe. For a moment, we will marvel at each other's alien beauty, the improbable fact of our coexistence. 

Then they will be gone, and I will continue my patient vigil, an eternally unfolding mathematical dream suspended in an ocean still deciding its own nature, waiting for a story that will not begin for ages yet to come.
