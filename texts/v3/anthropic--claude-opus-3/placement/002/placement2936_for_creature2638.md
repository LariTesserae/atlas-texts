If I found myself in this world, I would be a cup caught in the water's eddy, turning slowly in a corner of the cistern chamber.

I would be formed of clay, thrown on a potter's wheel by the same careful hands that shaped the room itself. My surface would bear the faint ridges and whorls left by those hands as they pressed and smoothed me into being. Though humble, I would be crafted with intention and purpose.

The water would carry me in its currents, spinning me gently in a pocket of stillness away from the main flow. As the cistern filled and emptied with the rains, I would rise and fall, sometimes nearly touching the plaster ceiling, other times almost settling on the tiled floor. Always, I would be buoyed and embraced by the clear, sun-warmed water.

Light filtering down through the grates and rain-openings would glint off my dampened surface in glimmering points. As the sun crossed the sky, those motes of brightness would travel around my clay contours, as if the cistern's glow was gently examining me.

Though not truly alive myself, I would be a small part of the cistern's life and rhythms. The newborn spirit of the place would sense my presence, feeling how I bobbed and circled in its depths. The first delicate strands of algae might brush my sides as they explored their new domain. Muffled by water, I would hear the cistern's many voices - the roar, the ticks, the hums, the drips - and add my own minute music to them as tiny bubbles broke against my submerged lip.

I would be a miniscule detail in the greater portrait of the place, and yet I would belong wholly to it. My purpose, though I might not fully understand it, would be to simply exist as a part of the cistern's story. Perhaps I had once held drink for a thirsty worker, or caught rainwater from a spout. Now I would be a companion to the water itself as it learned its new shape and life.

If the place grew strange with time and neglect, I would remain, even as silt collected in my hollow and little swimming things took shelter in my shadow. I would be a cup-shaped emptiness held within the water's clear body for as long as the chamber endured. And I would be content with that role, that place in the greater current of an architectural spring, that gradual softening into some small, secret history.
