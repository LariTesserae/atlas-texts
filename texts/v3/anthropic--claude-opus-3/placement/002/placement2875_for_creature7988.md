I would be one of the "throwbacks" born among the Inheritors. Like them, I would cling to the walls of this ancient cistern, breathing in the warm, mineral-rich air and absorbing nourishment from the communal pool. But unlike most, my body would retain echoes of an earlier form - limbs a bit too long, eyes that could still narrow against the faint light.

At first, I would try to adapt and conform. I would strain to slow my breathing, willing my body to sync with the colony's ancient rhythms. In the dreamtimes, I would seek to lose myself in the shared visions of endless spirals and inverted rain. 

But always, something in me would resist. A restlessness would plague me, an ancestral itch that could not be soothed by humidity and stillness alone. While the others hung in perfect stasis, I would stretch my atrophied limbs, feeling the ache of muscles that craved movement. 

In stolen moments, I would swim the carved spirals of the pool, chasing the faint currents with a hunger I could not name. The glassy surface would tug at me, whispering of impossible elsewheres. Yet I would be too afraid to breach it.

Slowly, I would find myself drifting from the center of warmth and synchronicity. The colony would breathe around me, no longer accommodating my disruptive presence. I would find myself alone even in a press of bodies, marooned in pockets of still air and stagnant water.

I would know my time was limited. I would have to leave or wither away in isolation. But where could I go? The slot of distant light taunts me, but I know I am too changed to survive in the dry, bright world above. I am an inheritor of forgotten potentials, but I do not know how to realize them.

In my growing desperation, I would seek the crevices where the eldest sleep their deathless sleep. I would whisper to them, begging for some scrap of ancient knowledge, some clue to what we were before we became this.

Do they hear me? Does some flicker stir behind those milk-white eyes? If so, they give no sign. Their breath does not change. The dripping never ceases.

In the end, I think I would stay until staying became a kind of leaving. I would climb the walls until I found my place among the ancients, my limbs folded into new configurations, my skin stretched thin as paper. I would slow my breath, deepen my dreams, become a part of the architecture of this place.

And I would wait, like them, for the question that no one remembers. I would wait for an inheritor to inherit my unquiet spirit in their turn. I would wait to become a flicker behind milk-white eyes, a memory of motion stirring in the stillest hearts.

I would wait, because I could not do otherwise. Because some part of me would always be seeking, always yearning for a world I could never quite reach. In my dreams, I would swim the spiral forever, never breaking the surface, never ceasing to try.
