I imagine myself as a solitary bat, tucked up in one of the corbels of the Sumphouse vault. 

I spend my days drowsing in the warm, humid dimness, lulled by the constant music of dripping water, rustling creatures, and chiming crickets that echo hauntingly in the ancient chamber. The scents of damp brick, copper, and fig sap infuse my dreams.

As dusk approaches, I rouse myself and drop from my perch, stretching my leathery wings. I swoop lazily through the shafts of golden evening light that pierce the gloom, riding the currents of moist, warm air that circulate through the room. The tips of my wings brush the trailing roots of the great fig tree as I pass.

From my vantage point in flight, I take in the patterned mosaic floor below, now broken and rearranged by time and nature into abstract swirls and eddies, like the flow of subterranean rivers charted on an ancient map. I see the shimmering pools and cisterns, each one ringing a different chiming note as the endless drops fall from the ceiling. Copper, stone, clay - a symphony of subtle sounds.

I dip down to skim the surface of the broad pool, snatching a quick drink, then spiral up again to my roost. In the dusky green air, I pass the caretaker as she makes her occasional pilgrimage, lantern in hand. She nods to me - a fellow dweller in this hushed, hidden world. 

I settle back onto my corbel to sleep again, wings wrapped around me. I am a small part of this place, but one that belongs. Not a builder or tender, but an inhabitant, another thread in the ancient tapestry of the Sumphouse's ecosystem, woven over generations in brick, water, root and wing. As long as these walls stand, as long as the water falls and the warmth rises, this is my home.
