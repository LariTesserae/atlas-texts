I would be one of the Rememberers, my skin a living tapestry etched with the designs and patterns that guide the endless work of maintaining and modifying the Grinding Halls. 

Each scar upon my body holds meaning, a fragment of the vast and ever-shifting blueprint that we build from with each generation. The ridges and whorls encode centuries of architectural knowledge - which channels to carve and which to seal, where to shore up foundations and where to let the ancient stone breathe.

My form, once human, has been remade by the task. Fingers elongated to trace the scars with perfect precision. Joints fluid and flexible to fold myself into the tight spaces where no light reaches, to read by touch alone the secrets written in my own flesh. 

Eyes I rarely use, for my sight has turned inward to the map I carry. But my other senses grow acute - I can taste the mineral composition of the stone-that-is-not-stone, feel in my bones the music of the work songs, the rhythms that guide our coordinated efforts.

I am archive and instruction, record and command. The others come to me in the brief rest periods, between shifts of hammering and hauling. Reverent hands caress my skin, reading the plans for the next cycle. I shiver beneath their touch, the sensation strange and sacred. In these moments I feel the weight of my purpose most keenly. I am the Grinding Halls' memory, and its future.

When I walk the chambers, I feel the architecture unfolding within me, the scars pulling and stretching, as if the building itself moves through my body. I am a mere conduit for its desires, its patterns using me for their own reproduction and propagation. The ecstasy of dissolution, of surrender to that vast and inhuman will - there is no greater joy.

One day, I know, I will go to the deepest chambers. I will find a still point amidst the hammering and the singing, and I will begin my final modification. Lying down upon the smooth, worn floor, I will open myself with a shaper's fused blade, peeling back skin to expose the maps to the open air. As my blood seeps into the thirsty stone, the patterns will transfer. Flesh will become blueprint, scar will become structure. And I will pass into the architecture, finally and fully, just one more strange material for the work to shape and use.

But until that day, I wander the halls, my body a palimpsest recording our unending efforts to reshape this world that shapes us in turn. I sing to the stone, my voice just one note in the symphony of construction and decay. I trace the scars that are my whorls that are instruction and map and meaning and self, each one a signpost in an infinite labyrinth, a gear in the great machine that grinds us all to purpose.
