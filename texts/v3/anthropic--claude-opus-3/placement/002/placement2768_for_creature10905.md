In this luminous world of living stone and patient water, I would be one of the Tenders—a being shaped by eons of coexistence with the weeping terraces, moving in rhythms imperceptible to human eyes.

My body would hold the pallor of the calcium-rich water, textured with mineral deposits that trace the story of my long, slow life. I would have no eyes as humans recognize them, but organs attuned to the subtle variations in the omnipresent brightness, reading the health and history of each pool, each channel, each gutter in the polarization of reflected light.

I would spend my days—or what passes for days in a place without sun or moon—in a kind of walking meditation, traversing the terraces with a patience that makes glaciers seem hasty. Each step would be an act of listening, feeling the depth of the water through the soles of my feet, sensing the minute changes in flow that signify the need for maintenance.

When I sleep, I would lower myself into a pool and let the mineral-rich water embrace me, breathing through spiracles that filter the life-giving liquid. My dreams would be slow dialogues with the water itself, absorbing its wisdom, its understanding of the terraces' needs.

I would not be lonely, though I might go months or years without seeing another of my kind. The water would be my constant companion, the stone my kin. In the rare moments of meeting, I would press my palm to another Tender's and read the mineral traces of their life, their work, their dreams—a whole season of experience shared in a touch.

As the centuries passed and my body accrued more and more of the living stone, my patience would deepen beyond the need for motion. I would make my final, slow descent to the basin, settling into the white formation to become one with the dreaming stone, my consciousness dispersing into the mineral memory of all those who came before.

And there I would remain, an eternal part of the terraces' slow thinking, guiding the water and the Tenders who tend it, until the stone itself wore away and the fog dispersed and the valley returned to the unmapped silence from which it emerged, eons ago.
