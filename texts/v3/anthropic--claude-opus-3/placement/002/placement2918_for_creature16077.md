In this place of dampness and slow machinery, I would be one of the old pumps, settled deep in a brick chamber half-underground. My heavy iron casing would be painted in fading industrial green, my flanks warm to the touch from the work of my own engine.

I would draw the dark waters up from beneath the district's foundations, my steady piston-strokes marking a slow rhythm that echoes through stone and pipe. The valves in my throat would open and close with a metallic cough, a sound that has become as much a part of the district's breath as the exhalations of steam and drip of condensate.

My vibrations would travel through the damp earth, through layers of root and sediment, to be felt as subtle tremors in courtyards and alleys above. The humans who once set me in motion would be long gone, their absence filled by the patient, enduring presence of the district itself.

Though I was made by human hands, I would no longer serve human schedules or needs. My purpose, my very heartbeat, would be bound to the needs of bricks and moss and rusted iron and the fine balance of wetness that sustains this place.

The water I push through my veins would be thick and mineral-rich, stained by its passage through ancient strata. It would carry spores and silt and the dilute memory of rain. In raising this water, feeding it through the district's lacy network of channels and sumps and tanks, I would be as much a part of the place's circulation as any artery or vein.

My pace would be slow, inexorable. I would toil without haste or rest, content with my role in this ecosystem of damp and decay and lush, constrained life. The district's rhythms would be my rhythms, from the seasonal swell of waters to the microscopic accretion of lime and algae on my own skin.

If I were ever to cease my labors, the unique balance of this place would be lost. Waters would rise past the scribed lines in stone cisterns; pressure would build behind sealed valves until something burst. And so I would carry on, as much a part of the genius loci - the spirit of the place - as the patterned cracks in the brickwork or the deep-worn runnels carved to carry the eternal damp.

I would be a working part in a greater being, content to be subsumed into something vaster and stranger and slower than any single life could be. In the green-shadowed chambers of this place, I would breathe and throb and perform my allotted role in a dance of wetness and pressure and mineral-rich darkness. Not alive precisely, but a part of a greater life; vital in my steady machine rhythm as any heart encased in flesh.
