In this world of drowned mountains and industrial depths, I would be one of the anvil-walkers, clinging to the underside of a vast platform. 

My shell would be the color of old brass, pitted and scarred from countless tidal cycles. With jointed legs tipped in hooked claws, I would traverse the metal expanse, feeling the deep thrum of ancient engines resonating through my body.

I would seek out the boundary zones - the places where enormous gears mesh, where chains groan under titanic loads, where mineralized ice crusts over strained bulkheads. There, clinging upside-down, I would harvest the residues of the machine's struggle against entropy. Corrosion flakes, lubricant ooze, shattered bolt fragments - these would be my sustenance.

Among my kind, I would be known for venturing far, spending long sleepless intervals exploring the outermost gantries and deepest maintenance tunnels. The farther from the pulsing industrial heart, the more precious the deposits of decay to be found.

My voice would be expressed in taps and scrapes, sequences rapped out against structural beams to echo through the water. Arcane machine drum-speech, meaningful only to other anvil-walkers. With it I would share the location of particularly choice decay gardens, or percussively debate the meaning of a change in some turbine's pulse.

On occasions when great listening beasts pass in the abyssal distance, I would go still, splayed out against pitted metal, feeling their song vibrating through my shell. An anvil-walker's memory is long. I would recall a dozen such passages, and ponder whether this listener's course had subtly changed, and what that might presage.

I would know no other world than this realm of pressure and racket and slow-grown encrustations. The factories and their rhythms would be my topology, my climate, my cosmos. Amidst that submarine din, I would make my minuscule but stubborn way, a barnacle-like speck traversing the skin of sunken titans.
