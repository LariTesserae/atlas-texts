In this frigid, densely-woven living cathedral, I would be one of the pale amphibian creatures that serve as the city's silent caretakers and living connective tissue.

I emerge from a translucent, fluid-filled pod deep in the warm, fetid undercroft, dropping wetly onto the spongy fungal floor. My skin is ghostly white and perpetually slick with antifreeze mucus. I have no eyes, navigating instead through chemical signals, moisture gradients, and the subsonic creaks and groans of the massive plant-architecture all around me.

On six splayed, gecko-like feet, I begin my long, vertical patrol, adhering to the dripping, fibrous walls. I am in constant motion, driven by instinct to maintain the city's integrity against the relentless cold and damp. My mouth is a star-shaped orifice filled not with teeth, but with dense clusters of neural rootlets. 

I pause frequently to address the micro-fractures and faults in the living infrastructure. Finding a leak in a massive bromeliad trunk, I latch on, driving my facial rootlets deep into the plant's wound. I draw sap for sustenance while simultaneously secreting a biological resin—a cocktail of self-hardening proteins and enzymes. My body vibrates, a strange purr that accelerates the curing. I leave behind a perfect weld of iron-hard moss, sealing the breach.

My mucus trail is not mere lubrication, but an osmotic sealant against the creeping frost. Where fungal "streetlamps" have grown dim, I linger, sharing nutrients to reinvigorate their ghostly bioluminescence. I am a roving neuron in a fibrous network, an eternal maintainer.

Years pass in ceaseless upward spirals. My skin begins to darken to charcoal, my joints stiffen, my movements slow. I feel a final, instinctive drive pulling me to the freezing perimeter of the gorge. I find a structural gap in the city's armored shell and wedge my body into it, splaying my limbs across the void. I drive my rootlets deep into the surrounding wood and finally go still.

The frigid wind mineralizes me. My flesh turns fibrous, my mucus hardens to bark. I have one final role to play in my city of wood and sap and fog. In death, I become a flying buttress, an overlapping shingle, a structural band that holds the biomechanical masterpiece together.

I am the city. The city is me.
