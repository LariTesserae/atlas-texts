In this windswept and unforgiving realm, I would be an ancient, gnarled pine tree, clinging tenaciously to a narrow ledge high on a sheer cliff face. 

My roots delve deep into the cracks and crevices of the stone, seeking out every scrap of moisture and trace of soil. Over long centuries, my boughs have been sculpted by the ceaseless wind into contorted, bonsai-like shapes - bent and twisted, but never broken.

From my lofty perch, I survey the vast emptiness of the peaks and valleys, watching the play of light and shadow over the austere landscape. The rising sun paints the rock faces in shades of gold and rose, while the setting sun sets the high clouds aflame with crimson and violet.

In the shelter of my branches, small birds find refuge from the bitter cold and predators' hungry gazes. They fill my wind-tossed crown with twittering song, their music a counterpoint to the low, constant roar of the air currents. The Roc I glimpse only from afar - a dark shape riding the thermal updrafts, its keening cry faint and eerie.

I bear mute witness to the turning of the seasons in this high fastness. Spring comes late, a fleeting rush of melting snow, tiny alpine blossoms, and greening moss. Summer passes in a blaze of intense sun, riotous wildflowers in the sheltered hollows, and the constant industry of birds and marmots. All too soon, the short autumn paints the groundcover in russet and gold before the first blizzards sweep in. The long, diamond-brilliant winter rules the heights, locking the world in stillness and silence beneath drifts of snow and ice.

Year upon year, decade upon decade, I endure. My bark grows ever more furrowed and weathered, my limbs ever more contorted. Slow time carves the peaks into new shapes around me. The Roc's generations succeed one another, each as haughty and fierce as the last. 

And I abide, a silent, steadfast sentinel in my high place. The thin wind sings in my branches, the stone cradles my roots, and the wheel of the seasons turns through its stately dance. Here in the roof of the world, I have found my place - a watcher, and a presence, as much a part of the primordial fabric of the wild peaks as the changeless stone itself.
