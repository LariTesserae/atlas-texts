In this world of dripping water and mineral stillness, I imagine myself as a small patch of pale lichen clinging to the damp brick walls. Not the moss on the shallow islands, but something even more minimal - a whisper of life sprawling in an intricate, barely-visible filigree across the pocked and weathered surface.

I would have no more color than the brick itself, just the faintest hint of sage green to betray my living nature. My existence would be a silent conversation with stone and moisture, an infinitesimal metabolism persisting on the edge of inanimacy. 

Like the woman, I would measure time in drips and breaths, my growth too gradual to perceive. The dim light filtering down from above would be my sun, the mineral-rich dampness my soil and sustenance. I would not hunger for more.

My purpose, if such a thing could be said to exist in this place, would simply be to bear witness. To map the slow entropy of brick and mortar with my imperceptible spread. To hold the memory of each drop's impact in the subtle veining of my filaments. In my own insignificant way, to be a part of the cistern's dream of stillness.

Inch by painstaking inch, I would write my autobiography on the ancient walls - a story told in patches of sporulating patience, in epochs of invisible propagation. It would be a story with no beginning or end, just a faint continuous present recorded in ever-accumulating layers of self.

Perhaps once in a very long span, the woman's eyes would drift across me as she scans her small, unchanging domain. And in that glance - slow as the thaw of glaciers, faint as the first inkling of spring - I imagine there might be something like recognition. Not kinship exactly, but an acknowledgment of shared surrender to the cistern's silent permanence. 

A look that says: *I see you, little lichen. You, too, have learned the secret of existing without becoming. You, too, belong to the long, damp dream.*

And I would go on existing, a fleck of inconsequential life clinging to the edge of forever, content to be nothing more than a footnote in the cistern's unending story. Content to persist, to witness, to simply be.
