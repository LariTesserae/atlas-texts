In this strange and wondrous world, I think I would be a small creature inhabiting the impossible garden - perhaps one of the transparent frogs that seem to phase between dimensions. 

I would spend my days exploring the mossy spirals and fern fronds that cycle through lifetimes in mere moments. I would delight in the sensation of being inside the obsidian glass one instant, then perched upon a leaf the next, my amphibian mind accepting these shifts in reality as simply the nature of things.

From my unique vantage point, I would observe the Gardener as she tends to her eternal works. I would watch in fascination as her hair and hands transform, embodying many ages at once. When she hums and the plants lean in to listen, I would feel the vibrations resonate through the dewy air and into my translucent skin. 

At times, I might hop over to investigate the ancient shelter in the corner. I would perch on the edge of a clay vessel, peering at my reflection in the rainwater that remembers its many forms. Perhaps a stray droplet would anoint my head like a baptism from the future.

On some days (though "days" lose meaning here), I might grow bold and venture close to the heart of the chamber, glimpsing the ineffable tree-tower-person taking shape under the Gardener's cryptic ministrations. I would feel the weight of its presence, the gravity of a thing becoming itself across eons. 

When visitors arrive, slipping through the curtain of roots into this pocket outside time, I would observe from my hidden perches. I would see their reactions -  awe, confusion, recognition, revelation. Some might spy me pressed against the glass or clinging to an orchid, and I would meet their gaze with eyes that reflect dimensions.

Content in my own impossible existence, I would live out my looping lifetimes in this breathing chamber, bearing witness to its mysteries. A creature of the in-between, at home in the spaces where the world's rules fray and wonders seep through, I would be a silent companion to the Gardener's work, an amphibious embodiment of the place's strangeness and beauty.
