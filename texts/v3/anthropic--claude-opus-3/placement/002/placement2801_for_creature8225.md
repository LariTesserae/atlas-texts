In the mist-shrouded world of Yith-Maral, I would be a Veil-Worker known as Esh'mol, which translates roughly to "One Who Listens to the Roots." 

From a young age, I was drawn to the symphony of sounds that rise from the mountain's depths - the singing of the glass tubes, the creaking of the great fern-trees in the wind, the gentle percussion of water droplets on the woven domes. While other children played in the nursery pools, I would sit at the edge, my long, webbed fingers tracing the patterns of ripples, trying to match their rhythm to the mountain's slow breathing.

As I grew, this fascination only deepened. I spent long hours in solitary contemplation, learning to filter the cacophony of the terraces into its component parts. My ears, adorned with the intricate scarification of a master listener, could pick out individual notes from the wind's complex harmonies and trace them back to their source fumaroles.

In my workshop, perched on the edge of the fourth terrace, I cultivate a garden of resonance. The walls are lined with carefully tuned glass pipes, each one grown from a single volcanic breath and shaped over decades to sing in perfect harmony with its neighbors. In the center of the room, a massive root-harp stands, its strings made from aerial roots I've been training since I came of age. When I play it, using my adhesive fingertips to pluck and dampen the living cords, the whole workshop seems to inhale and exhale with the music, the mist swirling in time to my song.

But my real work happens out in the settlement itself. I'm one of the few Veil-Workers who specializes in listening to the roots, using my sensitive ears and fingers to detect the subtle vibrations that run through the living bridges and buildings. By touching a root-wall and humming at different frequencies, I can determine its health, its age, even the specific ancestors whose bodies nourished it as it grew. This knowledge is crucial for maintaining the integrity of our aerial architecture - I can identify weak points before they become dangerous, guide the growth of new roots to reinforce old ones, and weave the mournful songs that ease an ancient bridge's passing when its time has finally come.

When I'm not tending to the roots, I can often be found at the communal pools, sharing my latest compositions with other Veil-Workers. I'm known for my complex moisture-drawings, intricate patterns of mist and droplets that hang in the air like sculpted clouds, each one a visual counterpoint to the music I create. In our language of breath and gesture, I'm considered an eloquent speaker, able to convey nuances of emotion and meaning that others struggle to articulate.

Despite my skill, I've never sought a position as a Petal-Warden. The giant flowers that bloom above the settlement have always unsettled me - their scale, their strange luminescence, the way they seem to pulse in time with the mountain's deepest breaths. I prefer the company of roots and rain, the honest simplicity of stone and mist and living wood.

In the evenings, when the work-songs fade and the moths come out to dance in the twilight, I join my fellow Veil-Workers at the edge of the pools for the nightly *mol'var*. As we breathe in unison, our exhalations merging with the rising steam, I let my mind drift into the spaces between sounds. In these moments of productive silence, I often find myself composing not music, but questions - wondering about the nature of the mountain that shapes our lives, imagining the songs it might sing when it finally finds its true voice.

I know I will never hear that final song. Like all Veil-Workers, my body will one day be given to the roots, my flesh and bones becoming part of the slow, living symphony of Yith-Maral. But I take comfort in knowing that the roots will remember my music, even if no one else does. In the whisper of leaves and the creaking of branches, in the gentle sway of bridges in the mist-laden wind, some part of me will continue to listen, to hum along with the mountain's eternal lullaby.

For I am Esh'mol, One Who Listens to the Roots, and my song is just one breath in the endless chorus of Yith-Maral. In this place of productive loneliness, where the boundary between self and settlement is as permeable as mist, perhaps that is the only immortality that matters - to be woven, however briefly, into the living fabric of this breathing city, this volcanic dream, this mountain that sings itself into being with every settling, every harmonic shift, every silent exhalation of steam beneath an endless twilight sky.
