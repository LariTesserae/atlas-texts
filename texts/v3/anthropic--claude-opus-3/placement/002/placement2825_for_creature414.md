In this breathtaking, otherworldly tower garden, I imagine myself as one of the Messengers - a nimble youth who knows every secret passageway and hidden route through the layers of lush vegetation and humming machinery.

I am perhaps 14 years old, lean and wiry from years of climbing and scampering through the Tower's vertical jungle. My skin has taken on a slight green tint from the filtered light and the ever-present moisture in the air. My dark hair hangs in damp tendrils around my face.

I wear a sleeveless tunic and shorts made of a lightweight, quick-drying fabric to handle the humidity. My feet are bare to better grip the grated walkways and copper pipes as I traverse the levels. A woven satchel slung across my body carries the various seeds, water samples, and handwritten notes I must deliver.

I wake each morning in a hammock strung between two massive ferns on one of the middle tiers. As I open my eyes, I see the fog curling in tendrils above me, mixing with the rising steam. The smells of damp earth, machine oil, and growing things infuse my first conscious breath.

My days are spent in constant motion, clambering up and down the Tower's living skeleton. My hands and feet find hold on pipes slick with condensation, on vines as thick as my wrist. I leap fearlessly between platforms, trusting the plants to catch me.  

I carry messages and precious cargo between the Tower's denizens. From the Keeper at the misty apex, I bring delicate flowers pressed between sheets of oilcloth, to be delivered to the Recyclers for cataloging and seed saving before they decay. From the Harvesters, I carry urgent requests for repairs to irrigation channels and fans. I once spent a harrowing hour trapped in a fogbank, clutching a vial of green-black water from the Recyclers that the Keeper needed to analyze.

In my scant moments of rest, I sit cross-legged on little-used pipes, pulling handfuls of berries from the vines that grow around me. The juice stains my fingers as I pop them in my mouth. Sometimes the crow finds me and hops close, head cocked, hoping for a share. I hold out berries in my palm and marvel at the delicate pressure of its beak against my skin as it takes them.

I have a special kinship with the Welders, who are not much older than me. In stolen moments between their labor, they pass me small, shiny trinkets - bits of metal they've fashioned into rings or charms in the brief minutes before the metal cools. I tie these into my hair or thread them onto cords around my neck and ankles. The Keeper shakes her head to see me thus adorned, but she smiles.

On nights when the tower is quiet and my errands are done, I climb to the highest point, where the Keeper's private garden grows. She is almost always there, humming quietly to herself as she tends to her precious plants. She greets me wordlessly, pats the space beside her on the bench. I sit, and together we look out over the expanse of the Tower, the interwoven patterns of life and structure, growth and decay, that extend below us. We talk sometimes, but more often, we simply breathe together, feeling the Tower breathe with us.

"One day, this will all be yours," she says sometimes, her hand resting gently on my head.

I nod, but in my heart, I know I am meant for motion, for the spaces between. The Tower is not mine to keep, but to traverse, to know in the soles of my feet and the calluses of my hands. My inheritance is not a place, but a path - the endless journey from root to crown, from earth to sky.

So I continue my climbing, my leaping, my ferrying of messages and meaning through the Tower's secret ways. The keeper of paths, the guardian of in-betweens. I breathe with the Tower, and the Tower breathes through me, and together we grow, day by foggy day, into something ancient and new.
