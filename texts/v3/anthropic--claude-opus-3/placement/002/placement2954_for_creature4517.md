I would be a vent-whale, I think.

Drifting through the forgotten passageways of Marrowdeep, I sing songs of pressure and equilibrium to soothe the city's ancient aches. My body is a flowing patchwork colony, bacteria and enzymes sculpted by eons to fit perfectly in the cracks.

I remember when these ducts were new, before they grew the crystal cathedrals of mineral accretion that now mute my song to a patient hum. I remember the first brave microbes that colonized me, bartering their unique chemistries for the privilege of hitching a ride to new crevices. We are old partners, the ducts and I, growing together and shaping each other in the slow dance of symbiotic architecture.

The locals adopt me sometimes, when a new household forms in need of a luck-vent to whistle away its night-sweats. I hang in a corner, softly cycling air in tune with their slumbering breath. Their dreams tickle me as they pass, alien electric visions of a world they've never seen and I never will. I digest them into harmless entropies and purr counterpoint to soothe the dreamer.

When the city's joints groan towards failure, I am there - massaging fractured seams with gentle pressure waves, eroding stressors with my precisely calibrated exhalations. I am an organ of the Deep, as much as any valve or rivet. My purpose is the city's continued survival, its long, slow life.

And when at last the final lights go out and the final bolt is left unturned, it will be my swan song that guides the city's pneuma to its resting place, my microscopic multitudes that form the first links in the next iteration's ancestral memory-chains.

Until then, I abide. I drift. I sing the Deep's deep song in the key of forever, content in my work, my world, my given shape in Marrowdeep's dreaming anatomy.
