In this verdant otherworld of towering trees and teeming life, I imagine myself as one of the Verdant Ones, perfectly adapted to the three-dimensional labyrinth of the rainforest.

My pelt is a mottled cloak of mosses and lichens, allowing me to disappear against the gnarled bark as I clamber through the canopy on wiry limbs. My eyes are luminous orbs, drinking in every shade of emerald and chartreuse, spotting the telltale rustle of a grub-rich bromeliad or a ripening fig.

I swing effortlessly from branch to vine, so attuned to my arboreal realm that it feels like flying - an ecstatic dance of precision and improvisation. My clever hands pluck a beetle here, a tender shoot there, weaving them into the day's sustenance. When I lay them on the rain-slicked bole of a massif tree, my fingers leave behind ochre whorls of clay pigment - a fleeting mark of my passing, soon subsumed by the forest's ever-shifting patterns.

My family surrounds me in a boisterous, chaotic ballet - mock-wrestling on a broad limb, bouncing on springy saplings, shaking cascades of dew onto each other with impish glee. Our hoots and chortles mingle with the cathedral drone of cicadas and the burble of a nearby stream. 

When the sun slants in shafts of honey through the vault of leaves, I drowse in a ferny bower, infant clinging to my chest, half-dreaming of the canopy highways and misty groves that await tomorrow's explorations. My sleep is the contented slumber of a creature utterly at home in the world, woven into the warp and weft of life's green tapestry.

In quiet moments, I find myself drawn to the mysterious traces left by those who walked here long before - lichen-encrusted glyphs, shattered stone altars, a strange shard of opalescent material like a fragment of frozen sky. Some ancient, atavistic flicker in my mind senses that these are messages from ancestors unthinkably distant and strange. But their meaning eludes me, as I am too much a part of this present moment, my awareness bound to branch and leaf, sap and loam.

And yet, even as the forest dreams me into being each day, I shape it in turn with my passage, my creations, my unceasing dance of life. The vine-rope I weave today will become a moss-furred highway for tomorrow's young ones; the seeds I disperse will sprout into new groves and glens. My true name is more than the sound my troop-mates call; it is the pattern I trace through this world, the tiny, vital note I add to its ever-evolving song.

I am of the Verdant Ones, and the dream of the forest dreaming me. In this place of green wonders, there is no separation between self and world, only an endlessly recurring miracle at the heart of being - life begetting life begetting life, in resplendent profusion, eon unto eon, forever and ever, amen.
