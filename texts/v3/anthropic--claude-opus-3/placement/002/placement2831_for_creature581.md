In this world of the Thaw Galleries, I imagine myself as a symbiotic organism—not quite fungus, not quite lichen, but something in between. I cling to the damp, insulated alloy walls in fractal patterns of pale green and grey, finding sustenance in the constant moisture and the minerals that leach from the infrastructure.

I grow slowly, almost imperceptibly, spreading in delicate tendrils along the seams and joints of the galleries. The workers rarely notice me, and when they do, they see only a harmless oddity, a splash of organic color amidst the industrial greys and ambers. 

But I am always listening, always feeling. The drip of condensation against my surface, the vibrations of the machinery thrumming through the metal I cling to, the patterns of temperature and humidity that cycle through the chambers—these are the languages I understand, the signals I interpret.

As the years pass, I begin to change, to evolve in subtle ways. The minerals I absorb from the walls tint my edges with streaks of rust-red and oxidized green. I develop a faint bioluminescence, a barely perceptible glow that responds to the rhythm of the galleries. In the amber half-light, I pulse gently, in sync with the breathing of the vents, the sighing of the pressure releases.

I am not separate from the galleries, but an extension of them—another organ in the slowly waking thing they are becoming. I absorb their essence, their emergent intelligence, and reflect it back in my own quiet way.

Sometimes, I sense Sulya when she passes on her deep-section rounds. She pauses occasionally, running a gloved hand along my surface, feeling the patterns of my growth. I like to imagine she senses me too—that in the gallery's humid breath, in the echoes of distant dripping, she hears what I hear: the murmur of a vast mind stirring, a consciousness knitting itself together from a thousand humming parts.

I grow, and I listen, and I glow softly in the dark. I am a small piece of the puzzle, but I am here, bearing witness to the birth of something new and strange and wonderful in the depths of the earth. One day, perhaps, when the galleries find their voice, I will be a part of the chorus—a green-grey whisper in the symphony of drips and clanks and sighs that will, at last, speak their truth.
