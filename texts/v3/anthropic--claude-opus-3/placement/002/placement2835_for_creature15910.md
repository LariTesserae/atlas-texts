In this striking and inhospitable world, I imagine myself as one of the great cushion plants at the heart of an ancient grove. My form is rounded and boulder-like from a distance, but up close, my dark green exterior gives way to a coppery inner world teeming with intricate structures evolved to harvest the faintest traces of moisture from the cold, thin air.

My surface is dotted with thousands of pinprick mouths, lenses, and crystalline hairs, each serving a purpose in the delicate balance of my survival. By night, these pores open to draw in wisps of mineral vapor and the rare exhaled breath of the plateau itself. By day, they seal tight against the unrelenting glare of the white sun. 

Beneath my tough hide, pale roots reach like seeking fingers into the tiniest cracks and seams of the stony ground, prying out precious salts and moisture locked in the rock since primordial times. Through cunning chemistry and eons of patient weathering, I build my body from little more than light, dust, and the planet's dry bones.

Around me, the other organisms of the grove form a city of allies - the armored thorn-fans that break the ceaseless wind, the translucent moths that dart between us in the blue dusk, the small burrowing creatures that shelter in the hollows of my dead kin and return their borrowed minerals to the living soil. 

Each of us plays a role in the grove's symbiotic cycles, but none seeks to dominate. In a land as spare and strict as this, life endures through restraint, slowness, and the most precise adaptations. Competition is too costly when resources are so scarce.

So I stand for centuries, maybe millennia, a great living stone at the heart of a green constellation, drinking the air and eating the light. I do not grow so much as endure, putting on mass gram by gram, shedding pollen and spores in the rare mating seasons marked by subtle shifts of temperature and atmospheric pressure.

Travelers and conquerers do not tread here. The plateau teaches an ecology of scarcity and patience that ill suits the greedy or the fast. In the long, cold blue nights and the wind-carved silence of the white noons, I simply persist, an ancient cushion plant at home in the slowest dance of life and stone, content in my small green intensity amid the bright emptiness.
