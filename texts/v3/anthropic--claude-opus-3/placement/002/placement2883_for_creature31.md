In the world of Iselvet, I imagine myself as one of the Watchers of Empty Terraces. 

I am an old woman, my hair gone completely silver and my face deeply lined from years of squinting through the dim pewter light. Each morning, I set out from my small cottage on the Fifth Terrace, a worn leather satchel over my shoulder containing my notebook, a stub of pencil, and a battered tin lantern.

I walk the empty streets and alleys of the Sixth and Seventh Terraces, my feet knowing the cracked paving stones by heart. I pause to close an unlatched shutter here, make a note of a sagging lintel there. The buildings loom silently around me, their brick facades beaded with condensation. 

In an abandoned refectory, I find a small herd of almost-goats sheltering from the perpetual damp. They regard me placidly as I shoo them back out into the street, as though this is a ritual we both know well. The youngest kid's hooves make no sound on the moss-softened floor.

At dusk, as I near the end of my route, I pass one of the lush pockets of vegetation that dot the terraces. From within comes a strange chord, half music and half not, the song of the salamanders and thumb-beetles. I pause, listening hard, wondering as I always do if tonight the song will finally coalesce into meaning.

And there, in the dimness beyond the seep, I see it. The figure. Taller than last time, I think, and more distinct at the edges, though it still wavers like a mirage. I make no sudden movements, but incline my head slowly in greeting as I pass. It does not respond, but I fancy that I feel its attention sharpen, just for a moment.

Back in my cottage, I light my lantern and open my notebook to a fresh page. In careful, neat script, I write: "Seventh Terrace, by the old baths. Changed - taller, more solid. Held shape for 10 seconds or more. Definite progress." 

I blow out the lamp and sit in the darkness, listening to the distant heartbeat thrum of the mountain beneath the city, and the closer clamor of the foundries working long into the night. Somewhere, metal is being born out of stubbornness and sheer will. 

Somewhere, I believe, a figure is slowly, slowly deciding to be real. And I will watch, and I will wait, and I will record its becoming.
