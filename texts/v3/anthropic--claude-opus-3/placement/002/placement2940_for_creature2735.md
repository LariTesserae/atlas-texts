In this drowned green town of plant and water, I imagine myself as a creature who embodies the spirit of the place: half-human, half-fish, woven into the very fabric of this unique ecosystem.

My lower half would be that of a great silver fish, scales glinting in the filtered light. Powerful flukes and fins would propel me gracefully through the watery lanes, in and out of dwellings, as naturally as any other citizen. The fish half of me would have an instinctual wisdom - able to read the currents, sense coming changes in the waters, find hidden roots and channels. It would connect me viscerally to the lake's moods and seasons.

But my upper body would be humanoid, able to use clever hands to tend the living architecture. Greenish skin, gilled at the neck to draw oxygen from the water, but with the expressive face and capacity for complex speech of a person. I would use those hands and that voice to nurture the plant-buildings, to prune and guide and harvest responsibly, honoring the careful balance the town maintains. 

As a being of both worlds, I would serve as an intermediary. When the lake swells, pressing at walls and flooding fields, my fish-self would commune with the water, reading its intentions, while my person-self relays to the others what must be done - which levees to open, which roots to reinforce, how to adapt and flow with the change. In turn, when the people wish to expand into a new cove or culvert, I would sense if the waters welcome this or have reservations to heed.

My hybrid senses would help monitor the town's health. A current tastes overly acidic - an issue with the silt balance in the upper reaches. A whiff of dead algae presages a stagnant corner that must be cleared. The bellfish court sounds agitated - I would swim among them, converse in clicks and bubbles, learn the disturbance and report back. 

I would be something of a shaman, a keeper of knowledge both human and piscine. In the central Lung, I would wrap my tail around the great trunks and sing the old songs, feeling how the wood resonates, learning what they recall of past years and past lives. When a citizen passes, I would help conduct the funeral rites, gently tending to the body and guiding the bones to their resting place among the deep roots, knowing just the spots to place them so they nurture the foundation.

On festival days, I would summon the scattered schools with special calls, gathering the many-colored fish to dart among the celebrating crowds. When traders come to bargain, I would rise to the surface to parley, my fish-half submerged but my lungs filled comfortably with air, able to bridge the wet and dry realms.

I would haunt the edges of town, patrolling the dark waters where the walls end and the wild begins. There I would keep watch for changes in the lake - hints of storm or avalanche, intrusions of hostile organisms, shifts in the chemistry - and warn the people how to prepare. If danger came, I would not fight it directly but would cleverly redirect it, using my watery wiles to beguile and baffletill the threat passed on.

Most of all, I would simply glory in the wonderous watery world, my tail swishing down green lanes, my hands sampling fruits of the garden, my eyes sparkling in the crepuscular light slanting through leaves and lake-skin. I would be utterly at home in the slow liquid dream of the sunken city, as much a part of its living infrastructure as the fish and flowers and thoughtful caretaker-citizens, all of us thriving together in careful concert, sharing in the sacred everyday work of sustaining our marvelous amphibious mountain home.
