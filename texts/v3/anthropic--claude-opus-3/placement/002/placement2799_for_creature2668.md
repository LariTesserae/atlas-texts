In this mountain town woven from stone, ice, and the constant flow of water, I imagine myself as one of the blind fish threading through submerged channels beneath the marsh-edge.

I am a slim, translucent ribbon, pale as old bone except for the dark seed of my heart visible through my ribs. My eyes are vestigial, hidden beneath soft scale-less skin, for in the mineral-rich waters I navigate by taste, vibration and the brush of currents against my lateral line. 

I swim in the dark veins that run between and beneath the town's foundations. From hand-width streams under doorway thresholds, to flooded burrows abandoned by marmots, to cracks in the bedrock itself - these are my dim roads, known by feel and flow. Sometimes I emerge briefly into icy pools or cisterns, flashing silver, before vanishing again into watery tunnels too narrow for anything but an eel to follow.

The humans rarely see me directly, only the faint flicker of my shadow beneath a frozen surface or the swirl of silt from my passing. But they know I am there, tasting the mineral traces in their floor-water, sensing the drumming of feet overhead, a silent custodian of the town's circulation. When channels grow clogged with debris, I nose it loose. When foundations shift and water seeks new routes, I find them first. 

On certain days, one of the basin people scoops me gently from a spring-pool with a hollowed gourd. Held up to the light, my transparent body is like a slice of living ice, my organs like beads of black roe. The human thanks me by the name their grandmothers gave my kind, older than any one tongue, and returns me to the water to continue my cold vigil.

For I am a small, secret part of what keeps this improbable town alive and ever-changing. In my blindness, I see how the flow of water is its heartbeat, rearranging stone and ice, eroding and rebuilding. My seemingly fragile form belies the ancientness of my lineage here, a swimming memory of how life persists in this place between frozen and fluid, between airless dark and brilliant white cold. 

I am a whisper of the marsh's dream, a shimmering thread in the tapestry of the town, my minute awareness bound into the patterns of wet and bright and chill and breath and stone that make this basin a miraculous, tenacious presence where the mountain's own bones show through its skin. In my blindness, I witness.
