In this world of the Drowning Gardens, I imagine myself as a wandering entity, something not quite of the Tenders but not entirely foreign either. Perhaps I was once one of the builders or cultivators, but through some strange alchemy of time and pressure and darkness, I have transformed into a new kind of being.

I drift through the cold, heavy waters, my form fluid and changeable. At times I am almost like one of the kelp fronds myself, undulating in the slow currents, clicking and thrumming in the echoing language of this place. Other times, I coalesce into something more solid - a roughly humanoid shape that can touch and explore the ruins and artifacts left behind.

I am drawn to the ancient structures, to the spiraling carvings and corroded tools. Like the old Tender in the meeting hall, I feel a deep connection to these remnants of a lost time. I spend long stretches in silent communion with the stone and metal, as if by touching them I might absorb some understanding of what came before.

But I am not still and rooted like that ancient Tender. I am a wanderer, an explorer. I follow the currents and the sound-cascades, drifting from grove to grove. I observe the young Tenders at their patient work, and sometimes I join them, lending my own strange appendages to the tasks of cultivation and repair.

I click and chirp and sing to the kelp and the Tenders alike. I do not know if they understand me, but I feel I understand them in some bone-deep way, as if the cold water has seeped the knowledge into me over eons.

Sometimes, in the faint illumination of the bioluminescent blooms, I leave marks of my own. Not on the stone or the ruins, but on the silt and the bacterial mats. Ephemeral spirals and whorls that echo the carvings, patterns that might be messages or maps or simply meditations on the shape of time in this sunless world.

I do not know what I am waiting for, any more than the Tenders do. But I feel it in the water, in the clicking, in the slow dance of the groves. Something is coming. Or perhaps something is returning. And when it does, I will be here, a strange amalgam of past and present, builder and tender, ready to welcome it into the patient darkness of the Drowning Gardens.
