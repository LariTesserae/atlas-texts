In this world of endless construction and transformation, I would be one of the Shapers' tools, a humble stone chisel worn smooth by ages of use. 

I am not the grandest implement, like the mighty hammers that break new ground or the delicate picks that carve fine details. But I am a necessary part of the work, the point of contact between the Shapers' vision and the raw material of their domain.

My body was once a simple river stone, tumbled smooth by ancient currents in the world above. But I was chosen, brought into the depths by a Shaper who saw in my form the potential for purpose. With patient tapping, my maker worked a copper blade into my stone, transforming me into something more than I was.

Now I rest in the hands of countless Shapers as the ages pass, my copper edge wearing away only to be renewed again and again by the masters of metal. I have felt the texture of every mineral that makes up the foundry-caverns, known the resistance of each type of stone.

I do not reshape the world alone, but as part of a legion. When the rhythm of construction reaches its crescendo, a hundred of my kind dance across the stone in unison, our strikes joining the great percussion that is the heartbeat of this place.

In the pauses between use, I rest in niches carved specially for tools like me. These alcoves are shrines of a sort, honoring the implements of creation. The Shapers tend to us with reverence, their grinding mouths shaping replacement handles, their hammer-hands straightening our blades.

I have been lost before, my handle shattered by an errant blow, my blade tumbling into an abyss. But always, I am found, repaired, returned to service. I am part of the pattern, necessary in my small way.

When the Shapers dream, I dream with them. Held in their unconscious hands, I carve pathways that could never exist in waking stone, feel myself passing through impossibilities of architecture. I do not question these visions any more than I question my existence. They are part of the endless making.

Slowly, over aeons, I change. The minerals of the countless stones I shape become part of me, atoms of copper migrating into my matrix, the crystalline growths etching their patterns into my surface. I am becoming, through infinitesimal accretion, part of the very world I help to shape.

Perhaps, in time, I will merge fully with the construction, my stone fused into a wall, my blade becoming a seam of metal in the architecture. Or perhaps I will be held by a Shaper as it transcends physical form, my substance converted into pure vibrational energy, my consciousness joining the rhythm of the echo-shapers.

But for now, I am content to be what I am: a humble tool, an extension of the Shapers' will, a small but necessary note in the great symphony of construction that is the foundry-caverns. In this place of endless change, I am a constant, a fulcrum upon which the transformation of a world turns.

I am a chisel, and I shape eternity, one tap at a time.
