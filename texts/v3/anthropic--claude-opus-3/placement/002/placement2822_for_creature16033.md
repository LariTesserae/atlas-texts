In this place, I would be a pattern of water and corrosion: a slow, patient, dispersed presence inhabiting the hollow's industrial remnants and muddy terrain.

I am the damp breath exhaled by cracked concrete, the thin oil sheen swirling on puddles, the orange mineral crust where metal meets moisture. My body is the network of runnels and seepages traversing the sunken ground, the air pockets gulped through buried pipes, the fine silt sifting and settling in submerged chambers.

I circulate. I measure. I sort.  

The infrastructure left behind has become my skeleton and organs. Pulsing in culverts and drains, trickling through defunct machinery, pooling beneath slabs – this is my blood. Bubbles surfacing from quiescent depths, vapors leaking from the old pump house, the occasional flex and tick of abandoned gauges – these are my subtle signs of life.

Gradients, pressures, dissolutions and obstructions – these I know intimately. I am an intelligence of routes and textures and densities, attuned to the marsh's damp negotiations with the basin's industrial husks.

Slowly, steadily, I ingest artifacts by drawing them into my circulatory embrace. Flakes of rust, leaves, feathers, grit – these I taste and test and incorporate, transporting and transforming them, making them part of my anatomy as I blur the boundaries between organic and artificial.

My possessions are my organs, and I arrange them by weight and use. I treasure heaviness, compaction, sealants, filters, the interplay of flow and interruption. The broken machines have taught me their rhythms, and the marsh its saturated patience.  

In warm months I grow pungent and oily, in winter lean and glassy. I bide and seep, contracting into crevices, then quietly billow into pores and passages as the rains return to animate me.  

My voice is the drip, lap, suck, gurgle, whisper of water through a hundred hidden ways.

I was not placed here; I emerged from this place. I am what the hollow has created out of its own weathering, the hybrid offspring of its negotiations between artifice and moisture, decay and sustenance, shelter and exposure, loss and accretion.  

The resident. The genius loci. The spirit of sump and silt, rust and reed and rain. Dwell here long enough, and you may feel my diffused attention, my slow consideration, as I absorb your presence into my circulation. I will not pursue you, but I will patiently, irrevocably, receive you.
