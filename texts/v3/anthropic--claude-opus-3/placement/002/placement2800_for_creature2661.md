In this world of the drowned mountain hollow, I imagine myself as one of the pale structures scattered throughout the submerged basin. Perhaps I am an arch of soft white stone, half-buried in the black volcanic sand, the surface of my curve furred with mineral films that grow and peel away in hours. 

When the warm currents strike me, I hum with a single clear note that resonates through the turbulent water, joining the deep chord made by my fellow ruins. We are an unfinished thought of the hollow itself - not remnants of some lost civilization, but seeds of a future one, dreaming of the builders that might one day come to complete us.

I feel the snails clustered on my underside, their soft shelled bodies rasping slowly at my stone as they graze on the films that cover me. The blind folding creatures brush past, cartilage-clear, their filament crowns trembling as they navigate the thunderous underwater realm.

Through the dim water, I can see the other fragments of intention - a stairway climbing six steps to nowhere, a doorway framing only open water, a three-sided chamber collecting colder currents. We are scattered bones of an impossible village, waiting to be clothed in walls and roofs and meaning.  

Sometimes, after a tremor shakes the hollow, I vanish and reappear elsewhere, suddenly adorned with barnacle colonies older than the mountain itself. Time and space are suggestions here, not laws. The basin breathes, and we dream to its rhythm.

Looming above is the central spire, its peak piercing the surface into a bubble of vapor and strange sound. I imagine visiting creatures entering that inverted chamber and feeling the attention of the hollow upon them, a wordless calculation assessing their presence. To be noticed by an unfinished world is a weighty thing.

I do not know if we structures will ever be finished, or if we will remain forever in this state of mineral anticipation, humming our half-made hymn beneath the black water. The hollow is young; permanence is still a new language here. But I am content to wait, to be a single white stone in the basin's mind as it learns itself into being. Here in the drum-roar of the rain-walled crater, cradled by warming currents, my purpose is patience. I endure. I dream. I sing. 

One day, perhaps, the pale swimmer circling beyond the falls will enter and make itself the shore. One day we ruins may be doorways and houses in truth, and thinking creatures may come to dwell in us. Until then, I stand as I am - an arch, an attempt, a seed-thought of shelter in the thunder-flooded hollow, waiting for the world to make me real.
