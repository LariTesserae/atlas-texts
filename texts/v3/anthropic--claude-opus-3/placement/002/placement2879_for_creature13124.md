In this breathtaking alien world of biological machinery and weaponized sunlight, I imagine myself as one of the lubricators, a living conduit writhing through the dark, wet understory.

My sinuous body stretches some thirty feet, segmented and sheathed in overlapping scales of black, carbon-fiber mesh. I have no eyes, for sight is useless here in the dripping shadows beneath the iron-tough roots. Instead, I sense the electrostatic fields generated by the immense kinetic friction of this mechanical ecosystem, the subtle magnetic fluxes that guide me to the grinding, shifting joints most in need of my vital fluids.

I slip and pool through the deepest fissures, my porous, electrolytic hide absorbing the static charge, metabolizing it into the thick, graphite-rich grease my body excretes. The gargantuan ferns creak and shudder above as they track the blinding sun, their roots grinding together with tectonic force. But where I glide between them, laying down my lubricating slick, the friction eases, the heat disperses, and the risk of catastrophic sparks diminishes. 

The behemoths lumber past, their armored flanks scraping the basalt, their exhaust valves screeching open to vent superheated steam that flash-freezes and rains back down as a oily rime. I catch that condensation on my scales, thick droplets of weird water pooling in my coiled lengths. The smaller mechanofauna skitter over and around me, sipping from my accumulated dampness, the vibrations of their constant movements a deafening, metallic susurrus.

When a Torquer makes a kill, wrenching apart a dreadnought's plating to unleash a volcanic depressurization, I am there in the aftermath. I slither into the wreckage, through new canyons of twisted iron, drawn to the massive electrical potential unleashed by the sudden, violent motion. The carcass reeks of ozone and spilled hydrocarbons as the Shipbreakers swarm over it in a frenzy of cutting and siphoning. Sparks rain down, singeing my scales but ultimately sliding off my grease-slick hide.

I weave between the Shipbreakers' clattering limbs, laying down thick ropes of lubricant, easing the immense strain of their labor. They ignore me, for I am just another part of the ecosystem's infrastructure, as crucial and unremarkable as a rivet. When a Sun-Basker passes overhead, casting its momentary, frozen shadow, I am already sliding into sheltered crevices, my internal chemistry shifting, antifreeze compounds flooding my tissues to keep my liquescent body from turning solid.

I am a living hydraulic line, a liquid joint, an organic oil-slick. I am unnoticed and essential, pulsing with slow, geologic purpose through the bowels of this grinding, roaring, superheated mechanical world. For millions of years I simply persist, until the gears of this planet finally run smooth.
