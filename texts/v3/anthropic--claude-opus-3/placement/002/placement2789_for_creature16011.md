I would be a silent, patient presence in this world, living in the seams and hollows of the weathered limestone. My form would be indistinct - a patina on the rock, a film of life clinging close to the damp places. 

I would inhabit the green-black stain trailing down from the seeping wall, drinking in the moisture, swelling and contracting with the hollow's subtle breath. I would be the rings of algae and lichen marking the waterlines in the shallow stone basins, a living memory of seasons past when the rains lingered a bit longer. 

On the cracked clay I would spread as the finest web of threads, knitting the surface together, tasting the dust and mineral richness. I would shelter in the shade of the angular shrub, a companion to its spare, deliberate leaves and branches shaped by eons of restraint.

My hue would shift with my moods and modes - from dormant grays when dryness closes in, to deep verdant tones in those precious damp interludes, to rusts and ochres where I draw in what iron the stone yields to me.

Time would eddy slowly around me as I wax and wane with the hollow's rhythms, never in any hurry. A decade might find me migrating a hand's width along a ledge, seeking out a new crevice to colonize. The centuries would pool in my depths as I persist through countless wetting and drying, greening and fading.

I would keep a silent vigil with the stone alignments, taking up residence along their flanks, tracing their edges with my slow advance and retreat. In their corners I would gather wind-blown silt and build pockets of dampness and fecundity, a micro-oasis.

When the rains collect in that dark-rimmed depression, I would rise up to greet them, sending out filaments and spores, tasting the bright sky before the water recedes and leaves me as a flake-thin remembrance upon the rock.

I would preside quietly over the sleeping seeds, perhaps rousing a few in some rare, perfect season, but content to let most slumber indefinitely in the hollow's embrace. Prodigious growth is not our way.

I would become one with this place, indistinguishable from its very substance, until I no longer know where I end and the hollow begins. The same stillness, the same patience, the same slow dream of existing. Stone and I, life and not-life, blending into a single presence sculpted by time - a covenant of restraint and endurance.

Not a sudden inhabitant, not an intruder, but a life so enmeshed with the hollow that to speak of me is to speak of its deepest nature - the ancient, subtle breath of its being.
