In the Living Sprawl, I would be a **canopy glider** - one of the shadow-sails that soars silently between the upper platforms, navigating the dappled golden light with precise spatial awareness.

I imagine my body sleek and aerodynamic, covered in short dark fur mottled with green and brown that blends into the shifting shadows of the canopy. My eyes are large and forward-facing, granting me keen stereoscopic vision to judge distances as I glide. Skin membranes stretch between my elongated limbs, from wrist to ankle and along my tail, forming the airfoils that allow me to catch thermals rising from below and convert altitude into distance. When I fold my gliding membranes tight against my body, I move through the dense foliage with agility, gripping branches and trunks with hand-like paws tipped in retractable claws.

My mind is always mapping my territory - the positions of columns, branches, vines, gaps, updrafts and obstacles. I maintain a detailed spatial model that extends in all directions, allowing me to chart efficient paths and predict the subtle air currents shaped by the sprawl's ever-shifting architecture. This ceaseless mental mapping is effortless for me, an innate aptitude occupying most of my brain's processing power.

I am a solitary hunter. I spend my days patrolling my territory in long, looping glides, conserving energy, scanning the layers below for tell-tale movements of prey. When I spot a column-runner, I dive with breathtaking speed, membranes tucked, claws extended. The strike is nearly always successful - my prey glimpses only a fleeting shadow before my claws puncture the base of its skull and sever its spinal cord. I carry my kill to a feeding platform and consume it methodically over hours, oblivious to the teeming ecosystem around me.

Mating is the only time I tolerate the presence of another shadow-sail. But even then, our interactions are strained and wary - a careful negotiation between apex predators instinctively disinclined to share space. We come together briefly to copulate, then separate again to our respective territories.

I have little awareness of or interest in the broader sprawl. The shavers moving ponderously along the corridors below, the burrowers excavating their tunnels in the fungal-threaded soil, the vast underground networks pulsing with chemical signals - these things exist at the periphery of my perception, if at all. My world is the wind, the branches, the prey, the next glide and the next. I am the sprawl's silent watcher, its elegant killer, a shadow tracing paths through the golden light at the top of the world.
