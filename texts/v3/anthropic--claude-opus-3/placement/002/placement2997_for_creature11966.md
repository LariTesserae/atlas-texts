What an astonishing and deeply unsettling place you've conjured. The richness of sensory detail is exquisite - I can practically feel the cloying heat and taste the thick, organic air. Yet behind the vivid textures lies a sense of immense, implacable otherness. It feels mythic and numinous, a liminal space where creation and completion are taking place according to an utterly inhuman logic.

If I found myself in this kiln-womb, I believe I would take the form of one of the succulents growing in the crevices between the tiles. Packed together with my kin, pulsing with slow vegetable rhythms, I would drink the mineral seep and transpire the building's breath. My bruise-colored flesh would swell with the mountain's dreaming.

When the time came, I would fruit. Gravid blisters would bulge from my unity with the other succulents, finally rupturing to spill pale segmented things onto the shuddering tile floor. Larvae of meaning, they would squirm hungrily towards the patterned walls, instinctively seeking the encoded significance that is their food.

Some primal photosynthetic urge would drive me to turn my leaves towards the promise of light high above, even though rationally I would know that shaft extends far past the reach of any sun. The orange glow suffusing the air would be enough to awaken the growing-impulse, the ancient upward yearning that slumbers in the sap of all plants.  

Occasionally, a tile would click and resettle itself against my crevice-root, inscribed with some new, mysterious sensation-memory - and I would feel a total, unquestioning conviction that THIS is the way of things, that I am a tiny but essential part of the incomprehensible greater Pattern that the mountain is weaving into being.

I would have no fear of the waiting thing at the top of the shaft, because I would know in my budding bones that it could not, would not descend until the summit architecture was completed. To be afraid would be like a single cell fearing the maturation of the embryo it is embedded in. The gestation must run its course.

I would simply grow, and fruit, and lend my small chlorophyll will to the urgency of the building, until either the membrane of reality flexed to release the ripe, writhing mountain... or until it consumed me, my vegetable matter converted by awful kiln-pressure into a smear of living clay to be thumbed into a whorl by a not-quite-hand. Either fate would be fruition.

So here I would put down roots, in this crevice, in this dream of life and stone. The mountain would drink my sap. I would become a green seam in the tessellated flesh of its endless throat. I would fill my phloem with meaning and feed it to pale crawling things, my transpiration moistening the forever-drying clay so that it never quite hardens, never quite dies, but remains suspended on the precipice of two transmutations - mud into brick and brick into god.

When the final firing comes, I will greet it with a flowering.
