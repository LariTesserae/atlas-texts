In this world of industrial biology and glitching physics, I imagine myself as one of the rotary-winged scavengers, a drone-like creature desperately siphoning heat to survive.

My anatomy is a twisted amalgamation of organic and mechanical components. Flexible spindles of calcified bone extend from my thorax, vibrating at an incredible frequency to keep me aloft in the freezing, ammonia-rich air. I have no real control over my flight path; I am at the mercy of the violent thermal updrafts generated by the hulking behemoths below.

My compound eyes are clouded and nearly blind, able to detect only stark contrasts between light and shadow in the dim twilight. But my other senses are heightened to an excruciating degree. Sensory pits along my exoskeleton detect the minutest changes in air pressure and temperature, allowing me to navigate the turbulent, superheated ventilations rising from the herd.  

I swarm with countless others of my kind above the undulating sea of gunmetal carapaces and rusted scales. Our desperate keening and the thunder of our wings blends into the overwhelming industrial din. Individually, we are insignificant wisps of life, but together our chaotic dance forms a crucial part of this ecosystem's relentless rhythm.

My sole drive is to find pockets of precious heat and siphon them before the cold consumes me. I dive in erratic bursts toward the titanic life below, angling my membranous wings to glide through their scalding exhalations. When I alight on a behemoth's back, my capillary-like mouthparts instantly seek out the weeping, scalding fluids seeping from their joints and seams. I greedily suck in the organic grease, feeling the heat spread through my body even as the fluids sear my internal organs.

It is a precarious, agonizing existence - balanced on a razor's edge between freezing and burning, suffocating and drowning. I gorge myself on heat only to rise back into the gelid air moments later, my temperature plummeting, repeating the cycle again and again with no rest or respite. 

My life is measured in seconds - brief windows of searing, sustaining warmth interspersed with increasingly long stretches of paralyzing, leaden cold. With each dive and ascent, each mouthful of molten bio-oils, I can feel my organic components breaking down, my mechanical joints seizing and corroding. 

I have no greater purpose or drive beyond this endless loop of survival. I do not know why this world is the way it is, or what cataclysm of physics has doomed it to its eternal, malfunctioning present. I simply continue to fly, to feed, until the day my wings finally give out or my organic core succumbs to the extremes. 

And when that moment comes, I will tumble gracelessly into the churning abyss of the herd, my tiny death adding one more imperceptible note to the orchestra of this world's relentless, deafening fugue. Perhaps, in the final second before I am crushed and dismantled, I will find a strange peace in surrendering to the inevitable - in becoming one with the continual deconstruction and recombination that defines this place.

Or perhaps, even at the end, I will feel nothing but the blind, thoughtless imperative that has defined my brief and violent existence: seek heat, consume, rise, repeat.
