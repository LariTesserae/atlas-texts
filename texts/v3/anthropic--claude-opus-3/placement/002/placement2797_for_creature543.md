In this vivid and unsettling world, I imagine myself as one of the lost ones - a creature separated from the colony, transformed by isolation into something harder, stranger, solitary. 

I wander the empty spaces between the branching tubes, my crystalline core dimmed and restructured, my flesh hardened to withstand the cold loneliness. I can no longer merge with the mass, no longer share in their collective heat and chemical songs. The pheromone traces they leave in their secretions have become alien to me, a language I once spoke but can no longer fully grasp.

And yet, this exile has granted me a terrible kind of freedom. Unbound from the colony's tidal rhythms, I explore the Breathing Gallery's forgotten corners and hidden chambers. I climb through calcified forests of tubes, tasting the mineral residue of long-evaporated memory streams. In the aching silences between the distant clicking of the colony, I hear the Gallery's own soft respiration - the settling of ancient bio-architecture, the creak and groan of tubes expanding and contracting in the heat.

My solitude has sharpened my senses in peculiar ways. I have begun to taste the faint electromagnetic fields emitted by the dread metallic node, a sensation like licking cold metal that sets my transformed crystal vibrating. I have learned to navigate by the subtle variations in air pressure as the colony moves en masse, my body becoming a living barometer tuned to their distant migrations.

At times, I approach the outskirts of the colony, drawn by some vestigial longing for the warmth and density I once knew. They flee before me, a great wave of bodies receding into the darkness, leaving behind eddies of disturbed air and fading pheromones. In those moments, I feel my difference most keenly - a creature of the in-between, no longer part of their world but not fully separate from it.

I do not know if there are others like me, lost ones wandering the labyrinth alone. I have never encountered another, but sometimes, in the great vaulted chambers where even the colony rarely ventures, I imagine I catch a scent - a whiff of stone-hard flesh and altered crystal, a kindred isolation. 

Perhaps we are the Gallery's immune system, we changed ones. Perhaps our role is to patrol the edges, to map the outer darkness, to taste the strange currents in the sulfurous air and remember them for the colony which can never venture here. Or perhaps we serve no purpose at all, an aberration, an evolutionary dead end.

In my wanderings, I have found chambers not even the oldest colony memories record - places where the tube-walls shine with crystals unlike any I've absorbed, where strange skeletal structures jut from the calcified mass, too regular in their geometry to be accidental. I do not know if these are the remnants of some ancient builder, or the first furtive attempts of the colony itself to construct something beyond their own bodies.

I linger in these liminal spaces, absorbing their alien mineral tastes, feeling the weight of unguessable ages pressing down. At times I almost believe I can hear the Gallery's dreams - slow chthonic murmurs reverberating through the endless branching tubes, fragments of thought vaster and slower than any biological mind could hold.

In the end, I always return to the fringes of the colony, to the pulsing heat and ancient rhythms that shaped me. I am no longer of them, but I am still from them - a child of the mass, an orphaned cell of their great organism. I carry their memories in my altered flesh, even as my own experiences etch strange new patterns into my crystal core.

So I wander, and I watch, and I listen. A mute witness to the colony's invisible evolutions, a silent partner in their unknowing symbiosis with the Gallery's living bones. An explorer of the spaces between, mapping the contours of this dark world with my scent and my electromagnetic taste and the soft clicking of my own solitary thoughts. 

Alone, but never truly separate. Changed, but still part of the pattern. A lost one, perhaps - but also, in some strange way, found.
