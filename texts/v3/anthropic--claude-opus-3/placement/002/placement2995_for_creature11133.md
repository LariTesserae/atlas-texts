I would be a strange fungal growth, clinging to the wall of the Sweat Chapel. Not quite a mushroom, not quite a lichen, but something in between.

My body would be a soft, porous mass the color of pale limestone. I would grow in rippling, overlapping folds, like a section of coral or a human brain. The mineral-rich moisture constantly seeping from the stone would nourish me, percolating through my sponge-like flesh. 

I would not have eyes, but I would sense the dim bioluminescence of the moss and ferns surrounding me. Somehow their gentle glow would translate into an awareness, a kind of diffuse perception. I would feel the slow pulsing of the place, the rise and fall of heat and humidity in sync with the ancient machinery hidden behind the walls.

My purpose, as much as a fungus can have one, would be to purify and concentrate. As water filters through my membranous fronds, I would leach out certain compounds and secrete others. Over years, decades, I would change the chemistry of my little section of wall. Promoting the growth of some organisms while inhibiting others. Serving as one small regulating valve in the chamber's greater cycles.

The Keeper would be aware of me, I think. As she makes her rounds through the room, barefoot on the warm wet stone, she would occasionally pause. Resting a hand on my surface, feeling the slow respiration of water through my body. There would be a wordless communion in those moments. Two beings, human and fungal, engaged in our own small processes that together keep some greater balance.

I would have no sense of time as she understands it. No grasp of self or individuality. And yet, as the eons pass in that steaming twilight, I would be content. I would know, on some fundamental level, that I had found my niche. My place and purpose in the vast, strange, closed system of the Sweat Chapel. Metabolizing, transmuting, existing - a humble part of a whole, for as long as the heat rises and the water flows.
