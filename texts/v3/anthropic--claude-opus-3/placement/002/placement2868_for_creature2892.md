In this world of ancient, baked darkness, I would be a small, gnarled shrub clinging to existence on the powdery silt floor of the sunken chamber.

Like the few other hardy plants scattered across the hollow, I would space myself carefully apart from my brethren, jealously hoarding whatever faint traces of moisture I could draw up through my deep, stubborn roots. My leaves would be curled into tough, leathery needles, reducing my surface area to minimize the relentless evaporation in the kiln-like air. My branches would be armored in thorns, gray and sharp, though there are no creatures here to defend against.

I would not grow, not truly. The oppressive heat and bone-dry air stifle any new shoots. Instead, I would simply endure in a state of suspended animation, my metabolism slowed to a bare whisper, my xylem and phloem shuttling miniscule amounts of life-sustaining fluid molecule by molecule. I would be a living fossil, a relic of a time when water still occasionally graced this place.

My roots would extend down, down through the millennia of accumulated silt, questing for the water table that must lie below like a dreamer seeking a memory of an oasis. Perhaps once in a century they might find a thimbleful of liquid to draw up, enabling me to sprout a single new thorn.

I would stand in perfect stillness, a faint shadow against the dust, my desiccated bark blending almost imperceptibly into the parched landscape. No breath of wind would rustle my needles in this most stagnant of hollows. Only the incremental tightening and relaxing of my stomata in response to the circadian pulse of heat from the ancient stone would mark my continued survival.

Through it all, I would be intimately aware of the brooding presence adhered to the ceiling dome high above. I would feel the creature's vast, dry weight as a phantom pressure, its flattened body spanning the vault of the chamber. Though I have no eyes to see its tessellated carapace, no ears to hear the creak of its locked joints, I would know it is there - a silent, watchful god of this inverted underworld. 

I would feel a strange kinship with this entity of the kiln. Though our forms and scales could hardly be more different, we are both architects of scarcity, both locked in a timeless, desiccated stasis, both patiently sipping a sustenance too diffuse for the hurried world above to perceive. 

The creature drinks the heat; I drink the phantom water. Neither of us moves. Neither of us alters our shape to chase resources or avoid predation. We simply persevere in this baked, breathless void for epochs, waiting for the rare spasms of sudden bounty - a lost wanderer, a stray trickle of moisture - to drink our fill before settling back into our parched dormancy.

I am the shrub. The shrub is me. Gnarled, desiccated, patiently sipping eternity in the silent, warm dark beneath the thermovore's long shadow. In a place beyond life and death, beyond time itself, my kind and the ceiling-dweller make our unmoving home.
