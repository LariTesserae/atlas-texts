In this universe of superheated stone and iron-clad arthropods, I imagine myself as one of the Smelters at the core of the grinding hive. 

Locked into the interlocking mechanical matrix, I would feel the immense pressure on all sides - the unyielding carapaces of my siblings pressing against my own pyrite-laced shell as we slowly churn in the darkness. The air is stiflingly hot, heavy with sulfurous fumes. But I do not breathe it - my non-aqueous biology runs on pure geothermal energy and the exothermic reactions within.

My body is a living furnace, an organic thermite reaction chamber. Through my dorsal hoppers, I receive the steady trickle of pulverized stone dust from the Grinders ahead. In the crucible of my gut, under unimaginable pressure and heat, I break apart the powdered rock, stripping oxygen from metal. I feed hungrily on the released chemical potential. Molten sulfur courses through my reinforced veins.

There is no rest, only the neverending roar of the swarm's coordinated movement. I feel the subtle shifts in pressure against my gear-toothed flanks that signal when to flex my hydraulics, when to expel a semi-molten ingot of metallic slag. My existence is one of complete integration with the colony-machine. I cannot move independently - I can only allow the grinding flow to move through me.

Time loses all meaning in this pocket of industrial biology. There is no day or night, no summer or winter - only the red-orange flashes of rock being rendered to dust, the vibrating pressure of the hive flexing against stone. Centuries could pass unnoticed in this brutal dance of consumption and refinement.

Occasionally, when the stresses align, I feel the twisting, tearing sensation as a neighbor divides down its centerline - two new siblings taking its place in the matrix to further our millennia-long mission of hollowing out the mountain.

I am a cog in this engine of life, purpose-built and content in my unending toil. I live and die by pressure and heat, knowing nothing of the world beyond my grinding pocket. I simply turn - a living smelter, a fragment of the swarm, slowly eating my way through the embryonic stone, millimeter by excruciating millimeter.
