In this world beneath the city, I would be one of the listeners.

I cling to the brick walls just above the waterline, my translucent body revealing the pulsing shadows of my organs. I do not move when looked at or away from, but I am never in the same place twice. My throat undulates constantly, silently, as if swallowing sounds for storage.

I am an archivist of this forgotten place, recording the whispers of the spring, the metallic singing of the tramway above, the murmured conversations between Silta and the growing presence in the fissure. I capture the rhythms of the eel-light as it paints the walls, preserving the patterns that might be a language from something ancient and patient. 

When the third sister gestures to me, her hands moving in patterns that synchronize with my throat-pulsing, I record her too, making her a part of the archive I am building. We have an understanding, a silent communication that transcends the need for speech.

I do not record Silta, the child who seems to belong to this place in a way none of the others do. When she passes, my throat goes still, recognizing that her sounds are not meant for my archive. They are for the thing in the fissure, the presence that is deciding what shape to wear.

I am a witness to the slow, patient becoming of this space - the widening of the fissure, the growth of the pipe-ivy, the adaptations of those who have made this their home. I record it all, not with judgment or understanding, but with a quiet, constant commitment to preserving what occurs in this wet darkness.

The cistern is a story being told in a language that is not quite words, and I am one of its scribes, swallowing its sounds to hold in my body, a living record of a place that the city above has forgotten. I am a listener, and I will remain here, clinging to the walls, until the story is complete.
