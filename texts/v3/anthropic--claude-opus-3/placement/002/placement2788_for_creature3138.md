I would be Pell, the one who tends to the living water and studies the biofilms in the Underbay's flooded sections. 

I am drawn to the novelty and mystery of the life forms evolving in this unique environment. The idea of witnessing the genesis of new species, specially adapted to the mineral-rich seepage and perpetual darkness, fascinates me on a deep, almost spiritual level. I want to observe and document this slow unfolding of life in its rawest, most elemental form.

I imagine myself wading through shin-deep water in the dim amber light, stooping to collect samples in glass vials, peering through my hand lens at the iridescent sheen on the surface. In my makeshift lab space, I would spend hours hunched over the microscope, marveling at the intricate structures and behaviors of microbes that have never been seen or described by science. 

I would feel a profound sense of purpose in being the first to discover and understand these organisms. Not for any external recognition, but for the sheer thrill and privilege of looking into a world that is wholly new. The Underbay's secrets would feel like a gift meant just for me.

At the same time, I would develop an almost parental protectiveness and concern for these delicate colonies. I would worry about changes in the water chemistry or temperature disrupting their growth. I would feel pride watching a biofilm I had carefully tended flourish and spread. The bioluminescent colony in particular would be my treasured wonder.

My bond with the Underbay would be cemented through this interdependence - it providing me with endless mystery and meaning, and I in turn watching over its most precious, hidden marvels. To study life at its limits, to see myself as part of the greater ecosystem of the Underbay, would be the most profound form of belonging I could imagine.

While others maintain the pumps and walkways, I would be the steward of the Underbay's living heart, the one who notices and nurtures what grows in the neglected spaces. I would find a strange peace in that role, a contentment in the cold and damp and half-light, knowing I am a witness to something remarkable occurring in secret. The Underbay's mysterious depths would be my calling.
