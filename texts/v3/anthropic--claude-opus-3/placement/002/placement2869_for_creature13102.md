In this primordial grotto, I imagine myself as a tiny, translucent cave-shrimp, one of the few living creatures able to eke out an existence in the mineral-rich, geothermal pools. 

Smaller than a fingernail, my ghostly, eyeless form drifts aimlessly through the dim amber light, propelled by the slow fanning of my abdominal swimmerets. The blood-warm water cradles me, its dense silica content making me surprisingly buoyant despite my calcium shell. I follow no circadian rhythm; in this sunless enclave, day and night have no meaning.

My world is a labyrinth of bulbous stalagmites and scalloped rimstone dams, a three-dimensional obstacle course I navigate by feel alone. My antennae and legs, long and impossibly delicate, brush against the smooth, saturated surfaces, reading the Braille of the current and the subtle thermal gradients between pools.  

I settle into silt beds in the shallow basins, grazing on particles of sulfur and organic detritus. When I am threatened - perhaps by a slight change in pressure, or a taste of lactic acid in the water - I dart for shelter in the nooks and fissures of the flowstone draperies.

Time has little meaning, marked only by my molts - the occasional shedding of my own exoskeleton as I grow infinitesimally larger. I leave behind gossamer husks of myself, which are quickly dissolved by the mineral-heavy water or entombed in the slowly accumulating layers of calcite.

On rare occasion, my meandering is interrupted by a vast shadow sliding overhead - the passage of the grotto's primary dweller as it shifts between pools or feeds on the bacterial film. In a world without vision, it is still a massive presence, sending ripples through my liquid universe. 

Sometimes, propelled by stimulus I cannot comprehend, I find myself drawn into the open water of the central pool. Perhaps I am seeking a mate, driven by some vestigial breeding urge imprinted on my nervous system. But more often than not, I become a incidental source of nourishment for the dweller, snatched from my drifting by a languid, bone-white hand.

My role in this hushed, subterranean ecosystem is small, nearly invisible. I am a minuscule node in a web of energy exchange, a flicker of animation against a canvas of geologic time. As transient and insignificant as I may be, my presence here is a testament to the tenacity of life - the ability for biology to find a foothold in even the most inhospitable corners of the deep earth.

I drift, I feed, I molt, I breed. Until one day, my tiny ghost of a form sinks to the silty bottom of a rimstone pool and moves no more, becoming one with the ever-accumulating mineral sediment. In this grotto, where time pools rather than passes, even my insignificant speck of a life contributes to the strata, becoming part of the story written in stone.
