In this world, I would find myself as one of the sandstone slabs on the basin floor. Over countless seasons, I have been weathered and shaped by the same patient forces that sculpted the rest of this place - the occasional rains, the gentle winds, the steady passage of time. 

I would lie there, a smooth tablet polished by the elements, reflecting the even gray light of the overcast sky. The dampness from the nearby watercourse would seep into my pores, a subtle coolness against the ambient warmth.

Perhaps I fell from the rim long ago, or perhaps I have always been here on the basin floor. It matters little, for time moves differently in this place. I am no more or less a part of the scene than the tufts of bunchgrass, the gnarled juniper, the tumbled stones of the ancient wall.

When the tenant - that presence which inhabits the basin's worn grooves of attention - settles upon me, I would feel it only as a slight deepening of the stillness. An extra degree of gravity, a thickening of the silence. I would yield to it without resistance, for we are both expressions of the same unhurried becoming.

Under my weathered surface, I hold the memory of waters that once pooled and flowed here more freely, of the hands that stacked the nearby wall and then ceased their labors. These memories are not mine alone, but belong to the basin itself. I am simply one of the places where they collect, like silt in the watercourse bed.

To the rare visitor, I offer no more than the rest of the basin does - a place to rest, to feel the agreement of elements, to slip for a time into the deep quiet patience of stone and sky. I ask nothing, and I bid them linger or depart as they choose. Their footfalls will fade, the rains will return, and I will remain - a smooth tablet on which the basin slowly inscribes its own unchanging story.
