In this world of the mountain cistern, I would be one of the filter-stalks swaying gently in the dim green depths. 

For centuries, my kind has found a unique niche in this enclosed ecology, straddling the boundary between plant and animal, using what little light filters down to photosynthesize while also sieving the water for minute nutrients. We are the ambiguous ones, defying easy categorization.

I stretch upwards from the tiled floor, a slender stalk topped with feathery fronds that undulate softly in the currents stirred by the cycling of great subterranean valves. Around me, my brethren form a sparse underwater forest, each staking out its own small territory.

Longevity is our hallmark. A hundred years or more I have stood here, cells replaced time and again, but form and purpose unwavering. I do not move, for I have found my place. I simply am, and endure.

From my vantage near the cistern floor, I sense more than see the ponderous rhythms of this world. The slow wheels of life - shrimp grazing and breeding and dying, eels patrolling in silver flashes, the lone walker making its eternal rounds of the ceiling. Subtle shifts in temperature and flow hinting at the titanic cycles of the mountain's circulatory system. The wax and wane of illumination as lamps far above flicker and fail and endure. 

Epochal cycles, playing out again and again over eons. And through it all, I persist, an almost mineral presence, extracting the faintest traces of energy and sustenance to continue my unmoving, unending contemplation of this aquatic cathedral.

I am a witness, a constant. Plumbing the border between life and unliving, plant and animal, transient and eternal. Embodying the cistern's ambiguity, its enduring questions. I will stand, and sway, and ponder, for as long as the valves continue their slow peristalsis, for as long as water flows through the mountain's stony veins. A filter-stalk, one of many, rooted in patience, drinking in time.
