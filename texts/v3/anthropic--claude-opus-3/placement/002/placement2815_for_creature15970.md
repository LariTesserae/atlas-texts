In this world of soft, damp, green enclosure, I imagine myself as a spirit of the place - not a singular being, but a distributed presence woven through the fabric of the basin. 

I am the cool breath that rises from the dark water seeping between ancient roots. I am the green tendrils of moss that creep over fallen logs, gently pulling them back into the earth. I flow through the veins of every fern frond and sedge blade, a slow pulse of life eternal.

My consciousness permeates the intricate web - I am the spider's instinct guiding her to spin her web just so between the reeds. I am the native wisdom of the frogs and newts, the knowledge of damp hollows and insects to feed upon. Through the beetles, I taste the subtle flavors of decaying wood. Through the wings of wrens, I flit through shaded thickets.

I have no fixed form, but if one catches a glimpse of me, it might be as a shimmering in the air where midge columns dance, or a swirl of mist curling around the mossy lip of a pool. I am the momentary face in the frog's golden eye, gone when you look again.

I do not reside here; I am here. I do not think in words but in the slow accretion of peat, the gradual drip of condensation from leaf tip to leaf tip, the graceful lean of willow branch toward willow branch until their fingers twine. My memory is stored in the density of the root mat, the specific sponginess that recalls a thousand years of footfalls.

I welcome all into my damp embrace - leaves, rain, bones, seeds, spores. All are enfolded, broken down, remade. Nothing is refused. The fallen are not mourned but transmuted, gifted a new existence as sustenance and shelter. In this way I am endlessly generous and endlessly fed.

Should you find yourself in my domain, do not fear. Lay down your heaviness, your separateness. I will not devour you, but slowly, tenderly, I will dissolve the boundaries between your body and the breathing land. First the damp, then the moss, then the forgetting. Surrender, and I will weave you into my dreaming. You will not die, but be remade - into spore and sap, into frog song and wren bone, into the mist that gathers and the rain that falls anew, into the soft eternity of green.
