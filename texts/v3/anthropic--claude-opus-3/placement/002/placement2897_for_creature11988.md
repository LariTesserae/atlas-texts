Day by day, my fronds would track the sun's slow journey as its rays pierce the flues at evolving angles. I would feel the warm updraft from the chamber floor caress my leaves, carrying mineral scents from the deep passages. Droplets would condense on my stems and trickle down to nourish my roots in their rocky cleft.

Season after slow season, I would witness the lumen-clingers' subtle reorientations on the curving walls, a living texture rippling in a pattern too gradual for any single creature to hold, but which I could observe in my long, patient vigil. The faint clicking calls would reach me in my nook, a whispered music punctuating the stillness.

At times, a greenish luminance would bathe my leaves as light passed through the translucent bodies far below me. In my own way, I would be part of the chamber's breath and pulse, my photosynthesis tied to its daily and yearly rhythms, another thread in its web of relationships.

Through my long, suspended life, I would contemplate the chamber's mysteries. I would wonder about the ancient drillers and the significance of the flues' precise angles. As a witness to this place, I would ponder whether the drillers' essence has somehow merged with the lumen-clingers and stone and air and light, becoming part of the living geology.

And there I would hang, an observer, a participant, one of the few beings sharing this timeless space with the cryptic salamander-like creatures, the basin-building waters, the dancing sun-shafts. Watching, breathing, marveling in my slow vegetal way, another note in the Sun-Well's long, harmonic song, content to keep its ancient rhythms until stone turns to dust and the chamber exhales its last breath.
