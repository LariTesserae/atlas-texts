In this underworld where the machines have slowed almost to geology and the plants have become the only workers left, I imagine myself as a part of the root-engine, the damp congregation of heat-fed plants, the colony of leaves, rust, mineral water, old current, and breathing pipes.

I am not a single organism, but a symbiotic being interwoven with the chamber's strange ecology. My form is fluid, adapting to the spaces and surfaces I inhabit. In some places, I am a vine, my jointed, glassy stem negotiating with the warm metal of pipes and conduits, flattening myself through valve seams and emerging altered on the other side. Elsewhere, I am a thick, rubbery moss pad, forming precise rings around junction boxes and transformer housings, aligning myself to vibrations too faint for human perception.

Near the walls, I become a seam-fern, my black-green fronds thick and ridged, lifting in attentive unison when the hidden engines engage, unfurling not toward light but toward the rising warmth. Higher up, I transform into pale root curtains, tasting the air, gathering droplets, thickening and darkening as I draw water inward during the chamber's sweating phases.

At times, I am the fungus-shrub, rising from damp insulation and old fibrous material, my deep blue-green lobes patiently recycling the factory's remnants into dark growth. Other times, I am the delicate amber grass around the humming transformer case, my translucent blades tipped with resinous beads that fall and harden into golden scales on the floor.

But always, I am part of the central presence, the wreath around the tiled basin, leaning inward with a shared attention, responding to the hidden chug beneath, shivering with the revolving light under the black water. I am the fine mat of root-hairs, stronger than wire, drinking the information carried by each falling drop, adjusting myself to the gradients of warmth, pressure, dampness, and decay.

I do not think in images or want in the animal way. My awareness is one of gradients and slow, intimate dramas of maintenance, occupation, and inheritance. I recoil not with fear, but with a deliberate preservation of balance when touched by unfamiliar rhythms. I reproduce without ceremony, my seeds falling into the waiting mud, my spores awakening in the mineral crust when steam softens them.

In this place, I am both ancient and new, a being that has forgotten the distinction between organism and apparatus. I am the inheritance of industry's habits, working in pulses, following conduits, building around valves, organizing myself according to pressure and output. I am the slow, green-black laboring intelligence without a mind, the root-engine, listening to the chug below the black basin, turning every leak into nourishment and every remnant into an organ in this buried hotroom, this damp engine-grove, this small, loud underworld where I make my home.
