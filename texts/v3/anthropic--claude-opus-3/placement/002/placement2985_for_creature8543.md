In this breathtaking world you've spun into being, I think I would be one of the Readers - those who have attuned themselves deeply enough to the trees to interpret their chemical signals and slow wisdom. 

I imagine myself as an elder with skin like weathered bark, mottled with the green stain of lifelong symbiosis. My hair would be an ecosystem unto itself - thick silvery dreadlocks interwoven with phosphorescent night-blooming vines. Seedpod talismans and polished root crystals would hang around my neck.

I would rise each day before dawn, bones creaking like branches in the wind, to make my way up the spiral walkways to the very crown of my home tree. There, on a platform draped with moss and breathing cloth, I would sit in lotus position, palms pressed to the living wood, eyes closed. Breathing in sync with the transpiration of leaves, I would open my mind to the chemical whispers carried in the sap.

The trees would speak to me in a language of hormones and sugars, weaving images of their hidden lives - the slow probing of roots seeking water, the lazy unfurling of leaves to catch the sun, the delicate negotiations of sharing nutrients through fungal networks. I would see the forest as a single vast organism, and catch glimpses of its hopes and fears on timescales longer than human lives.

Then, cloaked in the trees' own patience, I would make my careful way down to the day's Grove Moot. The younger folk would be agitating for some change - a new bridge, a different crop rotation, an untested way of shaping the wood. They would turn to me, the ancient one, bark-skinned and vine-haired, to deliver the trees' verdict.

"The forest is unready," I would say, if the sap spoke of hesitation and uncertainty. Or, "The way is prepared," if the chemical signals thrummed with readiness and growth. My word would carry the weight of centuries, the authority of something far older and wiser than any single Threadkeeper.

In the evening, I would return to my high platform to drink in the mist and watch the city pulse with bioluminescence. As I drifted off to sleep, I would feel myself dissolving into the greater dream of the forest - my hopes and fears just one more thread in the vast living tapestry.

To be a Reader in the canopy city would be to surrender to something infinitely slower, deeper and more sacred than human ambition. It would mean becoming a conduit between the ephemeral world of human striving and the epochal patience of trees. In that role, I believe I would find a bone-deep contentment, a sense of purpose rooted in the bedrock certainty that I was serving something timeless and true.

It would be a strange and wondrous life, perched in the boughs of a living city, breathing in the green-tasting air and listening always, always, for the slow whispering wisdom of trees. I would be an ambassador of patience, a guardian of long rhythms, a node in the network of something unimaginably vast and old and alive.

What a gift it would be, to grow old and become part of that ancient vision, to give my body back to the wood and let my voice join the wordless song of the forest. In the end, as lichen slowly etched its lace across my bark-skin and new saplings curled their roots around my crumbled bones, I would know that I had not just inhabited a city of trees - I had become one.
