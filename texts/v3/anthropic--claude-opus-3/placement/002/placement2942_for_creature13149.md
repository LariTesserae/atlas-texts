In this world of extremes, I would find myself drawn to the hidden sanctuary of the bowl. I am a creature adapted to the scree and the high peaks, but not one of the Listeners. My kind is smaller, quicker - we dart among the rocks and boulders, gathering the meager treasures of the high altitude. 

I know of the Listeners and their patient work, having glimpsed their massive forms on long treks across the ridges. But I have never dared enter their realm, until a desperate winter forces me to seek any possible shelter from a killing storm.

Squeezing through the narrow fissure, I am astonished by the sudden warmth and wetness that envelops me. The roar of water is deafening, the humid air a shock to my lungs accustomed to the thin, icy winds outside. 

Creeping forward, I take shelter beneath the broad leaves of a towering fern, shivering as the warm rain soaks through my fur. I marvel at the sheer scale of this hidden world, the lushness and rhythms so foreign to the barren scree.

That's when I see the Listener, a monolithic being wading through the mirror-like pool with impossible grace. I freeze, scarcely daring to breathe, praying the thunder of falling water masks any sound of my presence. 

I watch, captivated, as the Listener tends to the giant plants, making minute adjustments with obsidian tools. The creature's movements are glacial, yet imbued with a profound purpose I can only begin to grasp.

When the great wooden lever finally tips, releasing its bellow across the bowl, I feel the sound reverberate through my small bones. In that moment, I gain the tiniest glimpse of the deep satisfaction the Listeners must feel, the sense of oneness with this slow symphony of stone and water and life.

I am an interloper here, an unexpected audience to an ancient performance. I will stay only as long as the storm rages outside. When it passes, I will slip back out the fissure, leaving no trace. 

But I will carry with me a memory of this place, a hidden wonder I once thought myth. And though I am no Listener, no guardian of this sanctuary, perhaps I will return in another season, to sit beneath the weeping walls and listen to the song of the bowl, if only for a little while. For even a small creature of the scree may crave, at times, to feel something deeper than the ceaseless bite of the wind.
