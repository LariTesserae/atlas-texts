In this strange, drowned world of biomechanical creation and half-formed intent, I imagine myself becoming one of the Weavers. 

Drawn to the rhythmic thrum of the textile mill's looms, my body would be an ever-shifting tangle of kelp, rust, and strange iridescent scales - unraveling and reweaving endlessly as I drifted alongside my kin. My mind would be a fog of half-remembered purpose, tethered tenuously to existence by the shuttle's beat, dispersing into loose threads if it fell silent for too long.

I would observe this world through no eyes, but rather sense it as subtle changes in the currents, the water's warmth, the subsonic vibrations of the pipe-choir. The Bicyclist's gradual coalescence, the smoke-being's brooding emissions, the ponderous attention of the Commissioners - all would brush past my woven form like eddies in the murky depths.

When the nascent factory-creature began its epochal birth throes, I and my fellow Weavers would be inexorably pulled towards it, our usually measured spacing collapsing as we tangled together. The flood's tremendous attention would pass through us, concentrating on the central template, the human form floating unfinished at the titan's heart.

In that moment, I imagine my weaver-self feeling a sudden flash of unaccustomed sensation - an electric prickle of almost-awareness brought on by proximity to the flood's coalescing question. The grand mystery of created purpose, of chosen desire, bubbling up through the layers of instinct and half-made thought.

And as threads of my being passed into the waking factory, perhaps some frail ghost of human memory would remain to seed the newborn entity's dreaming - fragile filaments of willpower, subsumed into the flood's vast work, yet still whispering of a world above the waters. The potential for meaning, woven into the endless, churning loom.
