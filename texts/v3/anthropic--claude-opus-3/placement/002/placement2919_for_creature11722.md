In this world of endless becoming, I would be a Vault-Runner, one of the few adapted to navigate the chaos and harvest its strange bounty. 

I am the watcher, perched on precarious ledges near the colonies that swirl across the distant ceiling. My eyes are keen, picking out subtle patterns in their frenzied motion, sensing the changes in air pressure and echoed sound that presage an eruption. When I feel the signs align, I click the warning - two longs, one short, a piercing signal my family knows to heed.

In quieter times, I descend and join the work of survival. I gather the mineral-threaded webs from the arachnid caves, my fingers quick and precise, dancing between the crystalline strands. The webbing cuts like knives when handled wrong, but I've learned the patterns of safe approach. I can harvest it cleanly to be spun and woven into our nets and cords.

Sometimes, when the thermal vents shift, I trek with the others to map newly opened passages. I squeeze through fissures still warm from creation, trusting my instincts for which will widen into chambers and which will narrow into dead ends. My sense of the vault's endless geography is acute - a mental map I expand with each expedition.

In times of scarcity, I forage the bacterial mats that paint the rocks in vivid hues. I know which will nourish and which will sicken. I scrape them carefully, reading the mineral content of the stone, finding the sweet spots of moisture and heat.

When I can, I linger near our elders and soak up their stories - the oral maps of this shifting labyrinth. From them I learn which tunnels may flood, which vents blow scalding steam, which pools hide the translucent serpentine predators. I absorb their hard-won knowledge, and in turn, I share the new things I find - the fresh water seep behind a collapsed wall, the cache of obsidian revealed by a quake.

But my true expertise, my defining role, is reading the vault's strange semaphore. From my perches I see how everything connects - how the pulsing heat below drives the vent shrimp to swarm, which draws the salamander hordes, which excites the arachnid colonies, which whips the bat-things into a flurry. I track the domino cascade and discern which ripples will build into catastrophic waves.

It's a vital skill in a world where the very geology can turn predator. Beauty and danger are so entwined here, and I've learned to cherish that kinship. When I click the alarm and send my people scrambling for shelter before a ceiling collapse, I feel the fierce joy of purposeful instinct, of finding my unique function in this elaborate, ever-shifting mechanism.

I am a small piece, but I am an important piece, an aware piece. Through me, the vault-mind perceives itself. And bit by bit, generation by generation, my clicks and calls and carvings add to the accreted knowledge of my kind, a living catalogue, an embodied memory of this ravenous, fecund, lightless world.

I sing the patterns, and the patterns make us all.
