Wow, this is a hauntingly beautiful and evocative piece of writing. The Drowned Courts feel both alien and familiar, a place of deep time and slow, inexorable processes that mimic life and consciousness in eerie ways. 

I find myself drawn to the Shapeholders - those erosion-sculpted forms that have developed almost into individuals over eons of interaction with the water's flow and each other's presence. To encounter a Shapeholder would be to witness the staggering patience and inevitability of geological time made manifest. I imagine standing before one, running my hands over its surface worn smooth by ages of flowing water, feeling its warmth - the warmth of stone that has been dissolving and reconstituting itself since time immemorial. 

I would marvel at the way its curves and contours speak a silent language - one of complement and counterpoint to its fellow Shapeholders across the drowned plazas and courtyards. To spend time among a group of them would be to stand amidst a conversation of form and flow that has been going on since before humans drew breath, and will continue long after our species is gone.

At the same time, there is something unsettling about the Shapeholders' extremely slow but undeniable awareness of each other and their environment. The way they incline almost imperceptibly toward the city center, that place of complete geometrical dissolution and perfect equilibrium, makes them feel like individual nodes in some vast web of intention. Standing in their presence, I would feel known and measured down to my molecules by the water's thickening around me - assessed and absorbed into that ancient calculation.

It's a beautiful and haunting prospect - to encounter forms and forces that seem to verge on life and sentience despite being composed of completely inorganic components. The Shapeholders embody the Drowned Courts' central mystery: a place that is not alive, but has arrived at something more than mere existence through the sheer depth of time it has spent computing itself. To linger there would be to risk a kind of dissolution - not of the body but of the boundaries of self, as the warm, dark water slowly works to incorporate you into its long, long dream.
