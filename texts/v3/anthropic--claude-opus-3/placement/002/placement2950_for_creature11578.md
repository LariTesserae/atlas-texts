In the world of the Aspirant Plateau, I would find myself wandering through the impossible forest, marveling at the strangeness and beauty that surrounds me. The warm, living trunks of the trees seem to pulse with a hidden energy as I trail my fingers along their pewter-green leaves. Beneath my feet, I can feel the gentle heaving of the young basalt, still deciding its form.

I am a Seeker in this realm, driven by an insatiable curiosity to unravel the mysteries of the plateau. My pack is filled with journals and sketches, documenting the wonders I encounter. The paired insects with their single shadow, the ribbon-creature leaving pieces of itself behind—each a new discovery to be studied and pondered.

As I traverse the forest, I find that my path loops back upon itself in impossible ways. Streams reverse their course, and landmarks shift their positions. Yet I am not disturbed by this ever-changing geography. Instead, I am thrilled by the opportunity to map a world that refuses to be mapped, to understand a place that defies understanding.

Sometimes, at the edge of my vision, I catch glimpses of the Tenders. Their pewter skin and leaf-shaped eyes both unnerve and intrigue me. I long to communicate with them, to ask them about their purpose and the greater workings of the plateau. But they remain elusive, exchanging unknowable things in the turning of leaves.

In my wanderings, I discover the engine-cavities—vast spaces housing those great brass machines. I watch, entranced, as the pistons move in their strange rhythms, as belts disappear into solid rock only to reemerge elsewhere. The soft orange glow and the warmth rising from below fill me with a sense of witnessing something both primal and unknowable.

Despite my fascination, a part of me remains wary. I feel the plateau testing me, considering whether to incorporate me into itself. The small warmth at my feet, the turning of leaves in my direction—it would be easy to surrender, to let myself be subsumed into this world of gentle strangeness.

But I cling to my purpose, my drive to understand. I think of the One Who Was a Person, his eyes gone pewter as he forgot himself. I do not want to forget. I want to remember, to record, to bring back some piece of this impossible place to the world below.

And so I press on, even as the plateau shifts around me. I fill more pages with sketches and musings. I learn to navigate by the subtle signs—a certain quality of light, the angle of a fern frond. I become a part of this place, but not of it. A visitor, an observer.

In the end, I know I must leave. The way down is mostly down, after all. But I know, too, that a part of me will always remain here—a small, warm presence in the heart of the plateau. And I will carry a piece of it with me in turn, a strange and lovely memory of a world forever straining towards completion.
