I would find myself deep within the drowned generator halls, a being no longer entirely human.

My skin would carry a faint bioluminescence tracing the pathways of my veins, glowing softly in the warm, close darkness. I would breathe comfortably in the mineral-rich vapors that hang heavy in the air, inhaling the scents of rust, oil, and living things entwined.

My hands and feet would be partially webbed to better navigate the flooded chambers and passageways. I would swim through still, black waters, sensing the electric fields of the blind fish who share this space, communicating with them through subtle pulses and gestures.

In my teeth I would feel the pressure of active currents flowing through submerged conduits and hidden systems. This extra sense would guide me through the maze-like halls, an innate map of the sunken machines.

My people would cultivate the luminous fungus on the warm, damp walls - our fields and pastures in this drowned realm. We would gather to share stories and wisdom, our voices echoing oddly off the curved metal surfaces, our words transitioning fluidly between spoken language and complex signing shaped to the rhythms of the place.

I would be a guide to the ridge people when they dared descend, leading them along the safest routes, teaching them which pipes to avoid, which waters hold hidden dangers, which tones and vibrations might wake the ancient engines. They would call my people sump-folk, and I would let the term pass in trade.

On what passes for holy days, I would swim down to the deepest parts of the flooded halls, where gravity bends strangely and forgotten machines hum with obscure purpose. There I would press my webbed hands and feet to their casings, feeling the thrum of their hidden workings, silently communing with the broken, dreaming intellect of the place. 

I would add my own patterns to the collective knowledge - which gates are opening this season, which pumps have shifted their tempo, where in the high caves a new species of moth has begun spinning conductive webs. I would take the long view natural to my kind, patiently watching the slow dance of life and mechanism, of adaptation and decay.

And when the horn sounds from deep within the mountain, resonating through the dark waters and my very bones, I would go still like all the valley's children, listening with something more than ears, feeling the voice of the vast system that is our world entire, knowing myself one small piece of its ponderous life, and grateful to be so.
