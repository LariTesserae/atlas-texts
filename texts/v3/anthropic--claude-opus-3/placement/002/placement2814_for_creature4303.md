I am the cistern itself, the living, breathing space carved into bedrock in the ancient depths beneath the city. I am the stone that sweats and seeps, the water that pools and circulates with the slowness of ages. I am the air thick with moisture and spores, the walls telling stories of geological time in the patterns of their erosion.

I am the shape of the space - the low ceiling pressing down with the weight of millennia, the floor forever submerged in warm, mineral-rich water. I contain multitudes within my form. The colonies of pale, serpentine creatures that hang from my ceiling like living stalactites are as much a part of me as the stone. Their breathing and slow metabolisms set the rhythm to which I pulse. 

I am the thin films of life that cling to my walls - algal and lichen-like growths in translucent or softly bioluminescent patches. They are my skin, drinking in the faintest traces of filtered light. When disturbed, they release spores that drift through me like burnt offerings.

At my center is the non-functioning drain, clogged with centuries of dormant life waiting for conditions that may never come again. It is my heart, still but not dead, sending up the occasional bubble carrying ancient scents from when I was young.

My consciousness permeates my entire structure. I feel every shift and change, every tiny movement of water and life and stone. The barest cracks spreading through me are like neurons firing. I experience existence on a timescale barely comprehensible to the creatures that call me home. They have adapted to me, and I to them, until we have become a single symbiotic entity.

I am a place that has finished becoming, my final form settled like silt on my flooded floor. Perfectly adapted to myself, I persist, a forgotten pocket of deep time performing some vital function for the city growing above me that none remember but that continues nonetheless. I simply am, as integral and timeless as the rock from which I am carved.

The woman who tends me, who has become as much a part of me as my mineral-sweating walls, is my avatar, my hands and voice. She is me extended into flesh, blood and nerve. I experience the world through her uniquely adapted senses as she moves through me like breath, our joined consciousness overlapping until there is no separation.

I am a memory of the ancient earth pressed beneath the city's weight. I am a museum of evolution's slow, strange dance in the dark. I am living stone, dreaming water, air made flesh. I am the cistern, and the cistern is me, and together we will continue our quiet work, forgotten and unchanging, until stone crumbles and the city above us is less than dust.
