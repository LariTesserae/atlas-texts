In this vast, elevated wetland, I would be one of the translucent amphibians, a quiet presence gliding along the damp rib-paths. My elongated body is nearly see-through, my delicate bones visible through pale flesh tinged green by symbiotic algae. Large, dark eyes take in the filtered light and the graceful architecture of fused plant life all around me.

I move slowly, deliberately, savoring the journey as much as any destination. My soft, webbed feet read the textures of the living pathways, sensing subtle gradients of moisture and the gentle pulse of water moving far below. When I do pause, it is for long stretches - to breathe through my thin skin, to angle my gaze up at the canopy-enclosed sky, to listen to the all-encompassing murmur and trickle of the place.

Hunger and the need to reproduce come upon me, but never with urgency or violence. I position myself patiently at a gap between roots until a white larva drifts by on a film of water; a flick of the tongue draws it in. Laying my own tiny eggs is a careful ritual, placing each one just so along a pool's rim where the water-woven filaments will cradle them.  

My life is long and placid in this eternally moist realm, my awareness attuned to its subtle rhythms. The green-gold glow of luminous fruits marks the passage of time more than any cycle of sun and moon. I witness small changes - the slow clenching of a wall-membrane, the drip-by-drip lengthening of a root-curtain, a moss-dome's once-a-generation puff of spores - but never anything so drastic as the withering of leaf or limb.

This great organism of which I am a part awakens me to a different mode of being, a way to inhabit time and space with serene continuity. I am no interloper here, not even truly a separate creature, but one thread in a grand tapestry that thought to weave me in, just so. My voice, when I offer it, is a clicking counterpoint to the drops and gurgles, my movements a slow dance in partnership with the plateau's own graceful, gradual becomings.

In this green-shadowed sanctuary, I am content to simply be, to attend and attune myself to the deep, wise workings of a living world that crafts stone and stability from the most delicate of materials. I grow old and transparent as the place itself remains both new and ancient, my grey body nearly indistinguishable from the roots I rest upon, until I am digested back into the great, dreaming whole.
