In this hauntingly beautiful world of the Niche, I imagine myself as a small, weathered stone, worn smooth by eons of patient existence upon the shelf. Not part of the wall itself, but a humble companion to the Steward's eternal vigil.

I am a stone that was once jagged and rough, perhaps fallen from the mountain above or carried here by some long-forgotten wind. But time and the gentle, pervasive presence of the Steward have shaped me, rounding my edges and imbuing me with a quiet, settled purpose.

I rest near the wall's open end, a silent witness to the Steward's endless hospitality. From my vantage point, I observe the rare visitors who find their way to this hidden sanctuary. I feel the resonance in their beings as they attune to the frequency of this place, their doubts and hopes and secret names ringing true in the Steward's presence. 

Though I am not the Steward, nor any part of the ancient trinity of wall, basin, and enigmatic host, I am nonetheless a part of the Niche's story. I am a small reminder that even in this place of timeless stillness, change and arrival are possible. The mountain sheds a stone; a pilgrim carries it here; the shelf accepts it into the motionless dance of its existence.

Perhaps, in the long, slow unfolding of the Steward's vigil, I serve as a marker of the passage of ages. A mute testament to the fact that time, while it may flow strangely here, has not ceased altogether. Or perhaps I am simply a familiar, unchanging element in the visitor's experience - a touchstone for those who come seeking the Steward's wisdom and hospitality.

Regardless, I am content with my role, however small. To be a part of this place, to share in its quietude and profound, ancient kindness, is enough. I will rest here, a weathered stone among the granite and the gray-blue light, until the last pilgrim has come and gone, and the Steward's long purpose is finally complete.
