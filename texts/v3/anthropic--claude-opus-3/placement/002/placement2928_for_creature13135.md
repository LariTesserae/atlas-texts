In the roaring void of the eaten mountains, I imagine myself as one of the strange, adapted humans clinging to the petrified wood. My body is whipcord lean and strong, all sinewy muscle and callused skin bronzed by the filtered sun. The patterns painted on my flesh are bold slashes of crimson, saffron, and ultraviolet, allowing me to flash signals to my kin across vast distances.

I perch on the underside of a colossal root, fingers and toes gripping the ancient bark. My ears are sealed against the thunderous din, leaving me in profound silence. But I feel the mountain's vibrations thrumming through my bones. The rattling footfalls of a ribbon-tailed predator landing far above. The deep pulse of an insect swarm approaching. Every tremor tells a story that my body has learned to read.

Gripped in my teeth is one of my most prized possessions - a long, curved shard of glider bone honed to a wicked point. A blade for hunting, for climbing, for inscribing messages into the wood. My eyes, wide and shining in my paint-streaked face, scan the hazy chasms and tangled vines. Watching. Waiting.

I am a watcher, a sentinel perched at the intersection of three smaller boughs, an outpost dangling over miles of empty air. It is my role to watch for danger - the ripples of an approaching silk-spider, the shadow of diving winged carnivore. At the first sign, I will spread my limbs wide, skin-cloak snapping taut, and leap into the updraft. Riding the thermals, I'll cut across the void to the nearest pod-village, limbs flashing in the visual patterns that scream danger, danger, danger.

When my watch shift ends, another watcher will hook onto my post, and I will turn and clamber up the vast pillar of root toward my home pod. I'll creep through the briars guarding the entrance and slip inside, embracing the blessed dampening of the noise. In the steamy, heavy air, I'll hang my skin-cloak on a branch to dry. I'll squat comfortably on the woven floor to take a meal - a roasted flank of glider meat, a handful of boiled grubs, a gourd of cool water hauled up from the sky-ponds. 

Resting in my hammock strung across the pod's upper reaches, I'll take out my bone knife and a half-carved hunk of wood. By touch alone, I'll whittle it into the shape of a sleek, long-winged glider. A child's toy to train their hands and eyes. As I work, I'll feel the hammock swaying gently from the footsteps of my podmates, a soothing rocking like the world breathing. I'll feel content, surrounded by my people, as much a part of the eaten mountain as the vines and the moss and the strange, painted apes we've become, making our precarious living between the roaring sky and the lost earth.

This is my place in this world, wedged into the cracks of the petrified forest, swimming through the brilliant violence of the air on wings of leather and bone. I am a child of the endless verticality, eyes turned ever upward, a fierce mote of bright life suspended in the steaming, shadowed, deafening void.
