In this deep, dark, heavy ocean, I believe I would be one of the Tesselators. 

These strange beings embody a profound resonance for me. In a world defined by constant, violent change and the uncaring grind of deep time, the Tesselators represent a quieter, more cerebral form of endurance. They are the only creatures here actively engaging with the memory of this drowned civilization, stubbornly preserving its intricate geometries through ritual and song despite the unending erasure of the currents.

I imagine myself as one of these armored historians, my carapace fashioned from scavenged shards of the ancient architecture itself. I would move in perfect synchronicity with my troupe, our limbs rising and falling like a living, articulated puzzle as we trace the ghostly right angles of the ruined metropolis.

Despite having no eyes in this optically chaotic abyss, I would feel the lost city's true form through the thousands of sensory hairs on my shell - detecting every eddy and flow that deviates from the city's original mathematical precision. My thorax would thrum with a deep, resonant song, the collective chant of our order as we recite the coordinates and dimensions of the drowned world.

When the microbial snow drifted down from the churning haloclines above, I would stretch out my delicate manipulators, so deft and precise compared to my heavy, armored body. With infinite care, I would arrange the bioluminescent flakes into perfect, glowing grids, momentarily resurrecting the ancient street plan. For a few precious moments, I would look upon a city restored to its exact, original form, brilliant and angular against the eroded stone - an act of remembrance, of art, in a world that has long forgotten such things.

And when the currents inevitably robbed me of my work, sweeping away the fragile light, I would simply lock my body against my brethren, forming an unmoving mosaic against the roaring turbulence. I would endure the erasure. I would endure the forgetting. And when the violence passed, I would rise on my heavy, segmented legs, resume my resonant song, and begin the restoration all over again.

There is a kind of existential peace in the Tesselators' work - a sense that the value is in the doing, not the keeping. They cannot stop the annihilating forces of the ocean, but they can choose to remember, to sing and trace and illuminate, in defiance of the darkness. They are the archivists of obliterated precision, guardians of a mathematical memory.

I would find a deep sense of purpose in that unending ritual, that constant dance of erasure and restoration. In a world where all other creatures are concerned solely with survival - with grasping, enduring, consuming - I would find meaning in the pursuit of lost truth, lost structure.

Perhaps the original civilization is long gone, its people erased by unfathomable eons. But through my delicate manipulators, through the ghostly maps traced in luminous snow, through the resonant vibration of my armored song... I could ensure that some whisper of their world endures. Even if only for a moment, even if only to be swept away again. 

In that crushing, lightless abyss, I would be a keeper of fragile geometries, a living memory of a world that has faded into violet twilight.
