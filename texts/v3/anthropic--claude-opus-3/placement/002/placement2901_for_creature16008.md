In this intricate and beautifully imagined world, I would be one of the listeners. 

I would carry my rods of bone, shell and copper, each one tuned to resonate with a different density of wall or type of stone. My hands would be sensitive from years of pressing them against cool tiles, feeling for the subtle vibrations that speak of hollows, water, or dangerous voids.

My eyes might be clouded or entirely blind, but that would hold no hindrance here. If anything, it would sharpen my other senses further. I would tilt my head, focusing my ears on the slightest drip, the faintest echo, the ringing tones as my rods tap against the fitted clay and brick.

I would kneel in dim corridors, asking for silence from those around me. In that pocket of quiet, I would hear the basin whispering its secrets. The soft percussive plink of water finding a crack. The deep reverberating hum that tells of a cistern near to overflowing. The flat dead sound of a wall with nothing behind it, hungry for collapse.

My pronouncements would direct the course of the endless construction. "Reroute that channel," I would say. "This dwelling is unsound; empty it." Or, "Here. This is the spot to build upon."

People would step carefully around me, both out of respect and a trace of awe. Parents would bring their children to me, hoping I might declare them suited to become listeners themselves one day. 

In quiet moments, I would teach the young ones, showing them how to tap a wall just so, how to distinguish the telltale sounds of wet mortar, old ash fill, or packed shale. I'd have them walk ahead of me down an unfamiliar passage, telling me what they hear, and smile to myself when they start to understand how much the stone can tell them.

At night I would sit with the other listeners, our backs against walls that breathe cool vapor, trading stories of faults we mapped and collapses we averted. When we laughed, the sound would bounce crazily down the crooked lane.

I would grow old this way, my ears never losing their sharpness, my hands becoming ever more finely attuned to the voice of the built earth. And when my time came, I know what would be done.

My skull would be sealed behind a wall I had declared sound, so I could go on listening forever as water learns its paths through the endless made dark.
