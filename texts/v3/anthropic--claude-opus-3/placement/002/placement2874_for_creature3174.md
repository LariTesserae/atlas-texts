In the luminous, cool thicket of Harrow Shelf, I would be one of the columnar plants rising above the shrub lattice. My segmented, translucent stem would reach three meters into the directionless light, ribbed and faintly green, holding shadows of my vascular threads. At my crown, broad dish-shaped leaves would spread, overlapping with my neighbors to filter the sourceless glow into dappled shade below.

I would not walk the plateau as the keeper does. My roots would extend deep into the spongy moss-mat, anchoring me in place amidst the dense strata of vegetation. But like the keeper, I would tend to the thicket in my own gradual way. 

As condensation beaded on my smooth stem and trickled down my ribs, I would channel the moisture, directing it to the shrubs and ferns at my base. My broad leaves would capture the mist, funneling it into hidden root basins. Through this patient hydration, I would sustain the layers of growth beneath my canopy.

When the keeper passed on its rounds, hands gently adjusting a leaning fern or stirring a still pool, I would sense its presence. The minute vibrations of its careful footfalls would travel up through my roots, the only communication needed in this hushed emerald realm. A recognition, an attunement.

As the machines thrummed their slow industry and the facility's low electrical hum suffused the air, I would be part of the same inscrutable process, my gradual photosynthesis powered by the depthless silver light. Another component in the dense, compressed system of the plateau, patiently transmuting energy, tending to unseen cycles.

Silently, unmoving but never still, I would rise from the shrub lattice, a slender green column amidst dozens like myself. Through my translucent flesh, the keeper might glimpse the dark threads of my inner workings, kin to the channels beneath its own pale skin. Evidence of a shared architecture, a mirrored patience.

When you arrived, stepping softly on the moss, peering through the green dapple and waxy leaves, I would perceive your presence just as the keeper does. An introduced shape, an unfamiliar movement. But like the rest of Harrow Shelf, I would simply incorporate you, the thicket parting and closing, resuming its emerald compression.

I would be here long before and long after your passage - an unhurried, illuminated presence, one of the many translucent sentinels tending to this strange, shadowed, fecund world within a world. Unobtrusive, unremarked, as crucial and integral as the keeper's slow footfalls, the fern roots' blind groping, the facility's low, perennial hum. Rising into the sourceless light.
