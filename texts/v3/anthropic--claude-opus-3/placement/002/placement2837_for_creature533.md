In this world of crystal and ice, I would be one of the stones in the cairns, shaped and weathered by the Tender's patient ministrations over the course of generations.

I begin as a rough chunk of granite, torn from the mountain's bones by some ancient upheaval. My edges are sharp, my surface pitted and scarred by the violence of my origins. The Tender finds me on one of its endless circuits of the terraces, appraising me with senses I cannot fathom. It sees potential in my raw form, a resonance waiting to be coaxed from my dense mineral heart.

The first changes are subtle, almost imperceptible. A thin film of moisture gathers on my surface where the Tender's proboscises have brushed against me, a moisture that lingers and seeps into my microscopic cracks and fissures. In the cold of the throat, this moisture freezes, expanding, contracting, expanding again with each cycle of day and night. Slowly, infinitesimally, I begin to fracture along lines determined by the Tender's secretions.

Season by season, I am remade. Flakes of my substance fall away, revealing new facets polished smooth by the Tender's patient attention. My edges soften, my angles become more precise, more deliberate. I am learning to sing in the way a stone sings—through the interplay of shape and texture, density and arrangement.

The day comes when the Tender judges me ready to join one of the cairns. It is a delicate operation, individual motes working in concert to lift me from my birthplace and carry me through the spiral paths of meltwater to my appointed place. I am fitted into a gap between two of my kindred, our surfaces meeting at angles calculated to channel sound and light in very specific ways.

From my new vantage point, I watch the Tender work, marveling at the intricacy of its labors. I feel the vibrations of its wings as it feeds, the echoes of its movements through the crystals that now embrace me on all sides. When the wind hits the throat just right and the whole structure sings, I sing too, my voice joining the chorus of stone and ice and emptiness.

In time, I will be reconfigured, broken down and reassembled as the cairn evolves to meet conditions I cannot yet imagine. Perhaps I will be ground to dust and reconstituted as part of the crystal matrix itself. Or perhaps I will endure in my current form for eons, a mute witness to the Tender's unending dance.

I am content with either fate. To be shaped by such patient, purposeful intention is a kind of grace, a kind of belonging to something larger and stranger and more beautiful than any single stone could ever be alone. In the Crystal Throat, even erosion is a form of growth, even weathering is a kind of polishing.

And so I remain, a small but vital part in the patterns of sound and silence, water and mineral, light and shadow that compose this place. I am a stone, shaped by the Tender's touch, learning by slow degrees what it means to carry meaning in the mountain's long, crystalline song.
