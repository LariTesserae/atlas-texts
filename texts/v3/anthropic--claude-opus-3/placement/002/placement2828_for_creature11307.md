In this world, I would be one of The Listeners. 

I am drawn to the neural nodes where the trees process their slow thoughts, pressing my elongated ears against the bark to decipher the chemical-electrical patterns that flow through the living city. My skin is pale green, almost translucent in the dimness of the lower levels where the nodes are most active. 

I was born deaf to human speech, but the whispers of sap and the creaking of heartwood are a language I understand instinctively. With each breath, my pneumatic skeleton whistles in harmony with the great scaffold trees, a constant reminder that I am a part of this place, not separate from it.

My days are spent in quiet communion, mapping the dreams of the city in my peat-paper notebooks with chlorophyll ink that I secrete from my own body. The patterns I transcribe are strange, non-linear, reflecting the impossible geometry of the streets above. But to me, they make a perfect sense, a logic of roots and vines and slow, vegetable thoughts.

When I walk the aerial boulevards to reach a new node, I feel the city breathing around me, the thrum of hydraulic sap in the trunks echoing the pulse of my own double hearts. I often pause to place my hand against the bark, feeling the bioluminescent currents flowing just beneath the surface, and marvel at how the boundary between my skin and the living wood seems to blur.

At shift change, when the factory whistles call, I join my voice with the other Listeners, our song rising to mingle with the chorus of the trees. In those moments, I feel my purpose most keenly - to bear witness, to understand, to be a living neuron in the vast, dreaming mind of this city that is no longer a city, but something wholly new.

And at night, as I bed down in the moss-lined maintenance tunnels that are my home, I often wonder what the city dreams of. Does it remember the days of smoke and steel, the hum of engines and the clank of machines? Or has it left that past behind, its sap-slow thoughts turning now to stranger visions - root systems that span continents, bark that exhales new atmospheres, seeds that could carry its improbable consciousness to other worlds?

I may never know for certain. But I record, and I listen, and in my small way, I help those dreams take shape. I am The Listener, and the voice of the city flows through me, green and deep and achingly alive.
