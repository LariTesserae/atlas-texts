In this strange and wondrous pocket world beneath the factory floor, I imagine myself as a peculiar sort of lichen clinging to the rough, damp stone. 

I begin as a single spore wafting down through the hairline crack, carried on an errant draft. Perhaps I hitchhiked on the same rat that brought the moss, or drifted in on the moisture-laden air drawn by the temperature differential between the condensing chamber and the world above.

At first, I am barely anything at all - a near-microscopic cluster of fungal hypha bonding with a cyanobacterium. But the strange mineral soup that drips from the ceiling suits me. I spread slowly across the rock, a paper-thin crust in muted shades of gray and green.

Unlike the moss which stretches yearningly toward the faint light, I grow best in the shadowed recesses, thriving in the cold humidity and low illumination. The heavy vibrations from the factory machines pulsing through my substrate feel almost like a lullaby.

I do not move like the isopods or leap like the springtails, but I sense and interact with my environment in my own slow, diffuse way. The acidity of the seepage, the mineral content of the stone, the microbial life in the film of water - I absorb it, break it down, incorporate it into my own strange biology.

As I spread, I become a part of the Custodian's delicate, unintentional ecosystem. My growth helps stabilize the moisture levels in my little patch of wall. Nematodes navigate the microscopic channels between my hyphae. I form a partnership with the bacterial colonies, exchanging nutrients, finding a balance.

The Custodian incorporates me into its rounds and routines without conscious thought. The centipede's footfalls aerate my margins. The flatworm's bioluminescent trail occasionally lights my fringe. Gradually, imperceptibly, I become part of the pattern - another piece in the mosaic of this little world that somehow, against all odds, persists.

I will never see the factory above, never know the tread of human feet or the touch of unfiltered daylight. My entire existence is bounded by these wet stone walls, by the incessant dripping, by the deep thrum of unseen machines. And yet, in this place, I belong. In the Custodian's wordless way, I am known, and accounted for, and part of what must be preserved.

So I grow, slowly, patiently, in the dark and the damp and the din. My colors shift subtly as the chemistry of the runoff changes with the factory's production cycles. I help bind the stone, soften the acid, host the microscopic multitudes. 

I am small, even in this smallest of worlds, but I am here. I am a part of the pattern, a piece of the process, an organism in the Custodian's care. In this pocket of wildness, carved by chance and circumstance beneath the regimented industrial world, I have found my niche, my place, my purpose. And that is enough.
