In the Undergarden, I would become one of the Rainswallowers. 

My body would elongate and stretch, ribs opening like gills to filter the liquid time that falls in endless cascades from the hollow heart of the Mother Tree. My jaw would unhinge, allowing my mouth to open impossibly wide, a living chalice to catch the chronological runoff. I would position myself beneath the heaviest flows, feeling time pour over my tongue, tasting the tangled vintages of moments that never quite were.

As I drank, my belly would become a fermentation chamber, a vat where seconds and centuries mingle and transmute. Paradox would curdle in my guts, impossibility would metabolize into nutrients that only the garden could absorb. My breath would become laced with fumes of distilled déjà vu, exhaled slowly through skin that had learned to respire the stuff of altered history.

My thoughts would stretch and dilate, perception adjusting to the non-linear passage of sipped duration. Memories would shuffle like cards in a deck that was never in order to begin with. I would remember events from my future, anticipate the ache of wounds long scarred over, dream in flavors of nostalgia for experiences not yet tasted.  

Slowly, gently, my body would fuse with the root-weave beneath me, flesh and fiber intertwining until I could no longer tell where I ended and the garden began. My toes would become taproots, questing ever deeper for pockets of fermenting time. My fingers would branch and spread, growing stomata-pores to breathe out the concentrated fumes of recycled possibility. 

I would cease to move, cease to need movement. The garden would tend to me as I tended to it, bringing the choicest temporal vintages to pour directly into my waiting gullet. Symbiosis would become indistinguishable from worship, biology blurring into devotion, until even my heartbeat synchronized with the slow, steady throb of the Mother Tree's sap-pulse.

Rainswallowing would become my purpose, my art form, my entire existence. I would open, and drink, and filter, and ferment, and open again. An eternal cycle, a closed loop of transubstantiated time, the garden's dark and sacred communion.

And sometimes, in the stretched moments between swallows, I would sing. A slow, subterranean hymn, a rumbling chant that echoed through the root-web and shivered the leaves in the canopy miles above. Not made of words but of resonance, of understanding, of gratitude for the bitter bliss of endlessly drowning in the garden's unlighted grace. 

The song of the Rainswallower, patient and potent, a living prayer in the sodden dark, sung until voice became sap and melody became mycelium and the garden and I were one: an infinite recursion, drinking and filtering and singing the strange vintage of eternity, forever fermenting in the fungal dark of the Undergarden's embrace.
