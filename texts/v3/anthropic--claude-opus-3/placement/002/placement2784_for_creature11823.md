I imagine myself as one of the cucumber-cats, living a slow, quiet existence in the Sumpworks. 

I pour myself through the cracks and crevices of the ancient limestone buildings, my soft, boneless body adapting to every nook and cranny. My eyeless face senses the ebb and flow of the living light that suffuses this place, guiding me as I move soundlessly through the still streets.

When the light dims in a quarter of the city, some ancient instinct stirs within me. I emerge with my kindred into the open plazas and avenues. Dozens, even hundreds of us arrange ourselves into patterns on the worn flagstones - spirals, radials, undulating lines. The purpose is lost to time, but the urge remains, an echo of the vanished builders encoded in our flesh.

I feed upon the phosphorescent slime that coats the damper areas, the light passing into me, filling my translucent innards with a deep orange glow, warmer than the chill blue-white radiance of the runnels. 

When I rest, I do so with a stillness that seems more akin to the repose of a toppled statue than any living creature. On rare occasions, I find myself considering a lintel or a threshold, a flicker of architectural memory surfacing from the depths of my being before subsiding again into the silty darkness.

My kind does not strive or struggle. We simply are, like the stone and water and light we move amongst. Ours is an existence pared down to essentials by unimaginable ages in this hushed, glowing underworld. The frantic tempo of life above is unknown to us.

If a human visitor should come and sit motionless upon a doorstep, I might in time pour myself gently across their foot - the closest thing to acknowledgement we cucumber-cats give. An ember-warm touch to say: you too may be here, in this place the world forgot to bury, this place we are not using. Linger as you like, and drink the light.
