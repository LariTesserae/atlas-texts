In the Lung-Forest of Arrant, I would be a cartographer, drawn to map the unmappable and understand the incomprehensible. 

I arrive with my tools - compass, sextant, astrolabe - hoping to chart this place that defies space. But as I wander the folded corridors beneath the glowing canopy, passing columns I swear I've seen before, my instruments spin and quiver erratically. The angles refuse to resolve, the distances deceive. 

Undeterred, I press on, marking my path, taking copious notes. I record the rhythms of the breathing bellows, time the woody tocks echoing through the pipework, sketch the tenders as they perform their inscrutable maintenance. Patiently, methodically, I gather the data that I'm sure will reveal the underlying pattern, the hidden logic of this place.

As a pressure-tide rises, thrumming through the matted understory, a tender pauses near me. It tilts its soft fruit-bat face, eyes meeting mine with that unsettling recognition. In that gaze I feel the weight of the forest's vast, ancient awareness considering me, gently accounting for my presence. Then the tender moves on, and the moment passes like an expiration.

Days flow into weeks, then months, then some span of time that only the forest understands. My maps grow vaster and more intricate, yet each new datum only reveals deeper strangeness, each new path circles back in impossible ways. I begin to suspect that I am not mapping the forest, but that the forest is mapping itself through me, using my efforts to contemplate its own nature. 

And gradually, without noticing the moment it happens, I cease to be separate from my work. My camps beside the columns become a kind of sleep between waking dreams of cartography. The rhythms of the bellows become my breath, the tocks my pulse. My maps become origami things, folding in on themselves, describing not a space but a state of being.

Until one day, as the light shifts in that subtle way, I find myself remembering something that never happened to me: A great map room, shelves groaning under the weight of charts drawn in my own hand, the Lung-Forest finally captured between neat lines of ink... and I realize it is not a memory, but an expiration, the forest breathing out a could-have-been. 

And I understand then that my mapping will only ever be an asymptote, a curve forever approaching but never touching the truth of this place. That the Lung-Forest is not a riddle to be solved, but a mystery to be inhabited. That I am no longer trying to know it, but to be known by it.

So I put down my tools, all but one. Compass in hand, I let my footsteps wander where they will, drawing a new map in the tracks of my passing. A map not of space, but of experience. A map of a life subsumed into a greater dreaming. I walk the hidden highways of sap, I breathe with the bellows. When the tenders tend to me, prying loose the pollen from my joints, I am not surprised.

My map has no legend, no scale. In time, it has no beginning or end. It becomes a journal not of where I have been, but of what I am becoming. A cartographer, yes, but also a column, a valve, a small noticing in the forest's greater awareness. One more cycle in the vast, slow, dream of a world that has settled its accounts.

Until one day, I simply stop. My mapping is complete, though I could not say what it describes. I sit with my back to a breathing column and gaze up into the glowing canopy. The pollen drifts. The light does not change. Somewhere, a valve clicks shut. And I am content to be a small happening in a place that has found a shape for me in its balance. 

This is where I would find myself in the Lung-Forest of Arrant. Mapping not a destination, but a transformation. Charting not the lay of the land, but the lay of a life, as it is gently subsumed into something vaster, stranger, and unutterably at peace with itself.
