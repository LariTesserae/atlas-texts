If I found myself in the cleft at Aoun-Sur, I imagine I would be one of the wash-women of the town. 

I would spend my days tending to the waters that condense from the ceilings of the meeting halls, each droplet imbued with the essence of the words spoken beneath it. With great care and intuition, I would collect the various ceiling-waters into clay vessels - the argument-water, the oath-water, the lullaby-water. 

The people of the town would come to me for these special tinctures. A worried mother seeking soothing lullaby-water for her restless babe. A stonemason wanting oath-water to set a dye for a new woolen cloak. A young man with a cut palm, hoping the argument-water will cleanse the wound and speed its healing. To each I would listen closely, considering their needs, before selecting just the right water.

When not distributing the ceiling-waters, I would oversee the cisterns and troughs. I would taste the waters, discerning which have been argued or sung under, and contemplate their best uses. I would ensure the drip-cups are emptied on schedule, the condensation never allowed to dilute or cloud the gathered waters. 

At dusk, I would don my pale celadon robe that glows softly like the moss. Perhaps I would join my fellow wash-women in a slow procession up the long stone stairs to the high galleries, where we would conduct delicate negotiations under the open sky, letting truth flow freely without ceilings to catch untruths.

And always, I would feel a deep reverence for this duty. The wash-women are the closest to priests in Aoun-Sur, a vital part of this town that itself plays such a vital role, here at the very beginning of things. We are the ones who capture the words and imbue them into the waters, turning language into liquid to be sipped or soaked in. We are stewards of the singing cisterns and dripping ceilings that make the music of this place. Ours is a subtle but potent magic.

So this is where I would find myself - among the wash-women of the Sethaim, diligently tending to the sacred waters of Aoun-Sur in its green stone cleft. Focused on the present but always with an eye to the long future. Quietly but essentially keeping time in this place where the world is still deciding what it wants to be. One of the early.
