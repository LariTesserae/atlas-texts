In this world of perpetual becoming, I would be one of the Stoneblooded water-listeners. My temporal bones would have grown porous and honeycomb-hollow, allowing me to hear the faint whispers and echoes of underground streams from miles away. 

I would live in one of the older settlement chambers where the stone has nearly completed its formation. The walls of my dwelling would be smooth and cool to the touch, but I could still sense the slow breath and pulse of the rock, the infinitesimal shifts that only a water-listener can perceive.

My days would be spent in attunement with the arteries of the Underweave - following the branching and rejoining of subterranean waterways, listening for changes in flow or mineral composition that might foretell a shift in the stone's growth patterns. I would sit for hours with my ear pressed to a chamber wall, eyes closed, letting the sonic map of the depths unfold behind my eyelids.

When I heard something important - a new stream pushing through where stone was still soft, or the dwindling of a reliable flow - I would surface into the main caverns, my steps sure on the winding walkways. Approaching one of the stone-reader elders, I would bow my head and hum, letting the resonance of my voice carry the message through our bones. "The water sings of change," I might say. "A new vein emerges below the Shivering Gallery. The stone will want to grow hollow there to cradle it."

The stone-reader would nod, their mineral-mapped skin glinting in the fungal light as they made a mental note to direct the next generation's carving in harmony with my revelation.

On rest days, I would join my fellow water-listeners in the deepest accessible cavern, where moisture beaded the walls like sweat and the air hummed with the chatter of unseen rivers. We would sink into the perfect darkness of the underground pools, our adapted eyes perceiving only the ghost-light of flowing water, and release our voices in songs of praise to the currents that made the rock.

Over the years, my external appearance would shift to reflect my calling. Phosphorescent algae would colonize the skin around my enlarged ears, creating swirling patterns that glowed blue-green when I emerged from the listening pools. My fingers would grow long and thin, permanently pruned from a life spent trailing them in underwater currents.

As I aged, I would feel my bones grow dense as the bedrock, my movements slowing to match the pace of geological time. Yet my hearing would only grow more acute, until I could sense the rush of water through pores no wider than a hair. The young water-listeners would seek me out, pressing their ears to my chest to hear the way a thousand streams overlapped in my hollows, learning to discern the voices of the depths by listening through my flesh.

And when at last I felt the stone calling me home, I would retreat to the deepest listening pool and submerge myself entirely, letting the water fill my porous bones until I was more liquid than solid. There I would remain, an eternal ear in the depths, as my consciousness slowly dissolved into the current to become one with the singing flow that sculpted the world.
