In this hauntingly beautiful petrified city, I would be a great horned owl perched high atop one of the few remaining colossal monuments jutting up from the endless ruins. 

My plumage is a regal tapestry of earthy browns, tawnies and grays, perfectly camouflaged against the weathered, lichen-mottled stone. By day I roost in regal stillness on my lofty lookout, yellow eyes half-lidded, listening to the distant clamor of countless birds echoing through the megalopolis. Sunlight glints off my wicked talons, sheathed for now as I conserve my energy.

As the thin, silvery daylight fades to dusk and shadows pool in the ruins, I stir to life. Launching from my perch on silent wings, I float wraith-like above the crumbling city. My satellite-dish face scans for prey far below - a mouse skittering in the rubble, a vole huddled in a patch of weeds sprouting from a rain-filled plaza. Not even the near-dark can hide them from my supernaturally keen vision.

Gliding down soundlessly, I alight on a cast-iron lamppost adorned with oxidized verdigris curlicues. The rusted metal feels cool and familiar under my talons. From here I survey my moonlit domain. 

All around, the eerie luminescence of bioluminescent lichen throws the baroque stonework into ghostly relief. Swifts chitter unseen in their roosts. A nightjar's uncanny yipping floats up from some distant shadowy courtyard. In the distance, the booming call of a great sage grouse carries on the thin air. Somewhere nearby, the soft hooting of an owl colleague echoes my own territorial call.

This lofty world of stone and wings is mine. The endless vertical landscape of ledges, hidey-holes, and soaring heights is my hunting ground, my kingdom. I know every crevice and every creature, from the falcons nesting in high aeries to the swallows plastering the undersides of balconies with mud cups.  

As I glide off on my nightly rounds, a single silken feather drifts down to land on a mica-flecked flagstone, a small token left behind in the ruins. My species has thrived here for countless generations, and will for countless more, long after the crumbling traces of our giant predecessors have dissolved into dust and oblivion under the ceaseless forces of water, wind and time. 

No beings now walk the ancient streets and plazas, or dwell in the eroding houses. That vanished civilization has ceded eternal dominion of this place to us, the birds. We are its feathered inheritors, the new kings and queens nesting in its stony skeleton. And I, the great horned owl, am a nocturnal prince in this petrified megalopolis suspended between earth and sky.
