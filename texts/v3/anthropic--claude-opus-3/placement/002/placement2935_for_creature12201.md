In this subterranean world slowly sculpted by limestone and inhabited by the patient Threadkeepers, I imagine myself as one of the Stone Listeners - a master builder attuned to the cave's gradual becoming. 

My skin would be pale, almost translucent, from generations in the dim light, but still bearing a hint of the sun's memory from my ancestors who first sought shelter here. My eyes, large and dark, would be adapted to catch every precious photon that filters through the fissures above.

I would move through the caverns with deliberate, almost reverent steps, my bare feet sensing the contours and textures of the stone. The chronicle-cord at my waist would be long and intricately knotted, recording decades of the cave's changes - passages opened and closed, chambers born and reabsorbed into the living rock.

Each day, I would press my cheek, my palm, my tongue to the limestone, tasting its mineral secrets, feeling the hum of its slow transformation. The patterns of droplets and flowstone would speak to me, revealing where to build and where to wait, where to invite habitation and where to leave untouched for future generations.

I would guide the placement of wooden structures with fluid gestures, my hands shaping silent instructions in the echoing space. The builders would follow my lead, crafting frames and platforms designed to be moved as the cave breathes and shifts around us.

In moments of stillness, I would sit cross-legged on the damp stone, eyes closed, listening to the whispered drip of water, the stirrings of pale cave creatures, the nearly inaudible sigh of limestone depositing itself fraction by fraction. In this meditation, I would sink into the cave's timeless dream, feeling the mountain's intentions filtered through water and mineral.

At night, curled in a felted hammock strung between calcite columns, I would dream of the cave's future - new galleries unfurling like flowers, pools spreading their mirror surfaces, tunnels reaching like slow roots towards unknown depths. Upon waking, I would share these visions with the Deposit Readers, adding my knowing to their long plans.

Over the years, my chronicle-cord would grow, sagging to the ground with the weight of its knots, and my stone-knowledge would deepen, until the younger Stone Listeners came to me with their questions, touching my weathered hands with the same reverence I touch the cave walls.

And when at last it came my time to surrender my bones to the embrace of the limestone, I would make my way to that far chamber I'd long ago dreamt, the one that would open in rippled terraces like the petals of a rose. There I would lie down on the cool dampness, flesh to stone, and let the patient mineral wrap me in its calcite lace, my form becoming another flowing contour in the cave's long, slow story.
