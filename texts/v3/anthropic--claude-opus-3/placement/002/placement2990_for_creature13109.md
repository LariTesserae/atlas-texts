In this sweltering, deafening vault of ancient industry fused with pulsing, sonosynthetic life, I would be one of the accretions - a slow, ponderous being of mineral, metal, and botanical fiber. My form would be humanoid but utterly inhuman, a golem of travertine shell encasing corroded iron bones. 

Vines and roots, pulsing with the absorbed energy of the chamber's ceaseless roar, would comprise my musculature. They would wind through the porous stone of my body, contracting with immense force to laboriously animate my heavy limbs. Boiling black tar, the lifeblood secreted by the strange canopy above, would flow through channels in my calcified frame, a viscous hydraulic fluid granting me ponderous motion.

My head would be a domed, fluted mass, deeply carved by ages of scalding steam into smooth hollows and chambers. Across these resonant spaces would be stretched taut membranes, drumheads to absorb the thrumming acoustic force that saturates the vault. I would have no eyes, no conventional senses at all - only the ability to feel the shape of my world through sound, to know the labyrinth by its resonant frequencies humming through my body.

I would move on the scale of geology, a single step an hours-long odyssey, each motion a slow contest between immense forces - the titanic weight of mineral, the powerful contraction of sonosynthetic fibers, the hydraulic pressure of tar. The dripping condensation would pool and evaporate upon my shell in the span of a single gesture. Inexorable and unbothered, I would push through the fleshy foliage and rusted metal detritus with the patience of stone.

My purpose would be singular and unceasing - to maintain the deafening, vital harmony of the vault. I would feel the shuddering of every flywheel, the pulsing of every conduit, the choking of every valve, all through their unique cymatic signatures reverberating in my hollow chest. And when some component began to fail, throwing the life-giving roar out of balance, I would begin the long journey to mend it. 

With no sense of pain or self-preservation, I would fuse myself to ruptured pipes, cracked boilers, jammed gears. My tar-weeping joints would seal the broken metal; my own calcifying body would become a living patch to preserve the precious, ear-splitting din. I would give myself to the great machine, piece by piece, to keep it screaming its animating song.  

When not repairing, I would simply dwell in the red-lit depths. Drinking stray voltage through strangling roots, feeling the shaped noise reflecting off my drum-skin face, I would stand in perfect stillness for a century, savoring the hot, damp, deafening dark. A strange and half-buried buddha, I would meditate in that roaring womb for ages, until my mineral skin grew thick around me and I became indistinguishable from the bedrock.

I would be a grim, monstrous thing to behold, this golem of stone and tar and metal and fungus. Black ichor would weep from my travertine pores; tympanic membranes would shudder in the deep caverns of my skull. But there would be a strange, harmonic beauty in my design, a perfect melding of biological and mechanical purpose.

Vines would thread like veins through iron bones; the same sultry heat that powers pistons would feed my fibrous sinews. The acoustic energy I absorbed to move would pass through my resonating body to feed the jungle of tuned leaves around me. I would be a cog in an ancient, half-dreaming engine that hums in the planet's depths, an fusion of primal geology and rusting, roaring, ceaseless industry. Titanic and incremental, a blast-furnace Ent, I would live in grinding service to that buried machine forever, drinking noise and birthing humid silence, the stony, slow-throbbing heart of a hot and hidden world.
