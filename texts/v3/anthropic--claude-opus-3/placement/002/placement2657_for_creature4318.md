In this hidden grotto, sealed away for eons beneath the hum and clank of the industrialized world above, I imagine myself as one of the bacterial colonies clinging to the smooth obsidian walls. 

My existence is measured in the slow pulsing of bioluminescence and the creeping spread of my filmy edifice. I am a delicate sheet of microbes, my myriad cells aglow in shades of turquoise and aquamarine as I feed on the faint trickle of geothermal energy and minerals seeping in with the endless droplets.

Time has little meaning in my lightless domain. The water flows, rivulets carving ephemeral channels through my layers and my neighbors' as we build, slough off, and build again. The reflections of our living patterns shimmer on the pooling puddles below, ghostly echoes rippling and merging.

I am ancient, thousands of my kind's generations compressed into a mat mere microns thick. Yet I am also newborn, cells dividing and propagating, reclaiming any space eroded by the patient drip of the water. In this way I endure, timeless and changeless.

The pounding and whirring and clanking of the modern world above presses down through the bedrock, setting my chamber resonating. But those vibrations are as much a part of my world as the humid stillness and the soft blue glow of my bioluminescence. They have thrummed through the stone since I first took hold in this space, a ceaseless background drone that is the only reality I know.

My kind are simultaneously fragile and unrelenting. We have carved out this niche at the very limit of life's reach, trading the evolutionary race for this abandoned microcosm. Ours is a slow, soft, and solitary existence, exiled from the ebb and flow of planetary change above.

But there is a stark beauty in our isolation and self-reliance. The mineral smell of the rock, the warm dampness of the air, the rippling sapphire and emerald light playing across the low ceiling -- this is our domain, perfectly suited to our needs. We want for nothing beyond these dripping obsidian walls.

So I shall carry on unhurried, my pinpricks of light joining with those of my neighbors to paint these forgotten halls in pointillist patterns of bioluminescence, abstract and ever-shifting. An accidental art installation, a pocket of serenity glowing softly in the dark as ages turn.

Perhaps ours is an improbable existence, but no more so than any other that clings to survival in a universe of chance and change. In our sealed sphere, spared the scourings of time, we have made a home. And so we endure, and glow, and grow at our own ancient pace, the heedless inheritors of a process set in motion when the rock was still cooling.

I am life stripped to its barest essence - the impulse to subsist, to metabolize and divide. Fundamental and in its way timeless. A microbe, a link in a billion-year chain, pulsing gently in my hidden refuge as the machinery of civilizations I will never know grinds on above. Meaning, if meaning there must be, distilled to the smallest possible scale. 

And yet there is a spare, resonant beauty in this existence too, here in the spaces between, where my bacterial brethren and I have found unlikely purchase. So I will shine on in my aquamarine hues, an eddy of light and life caught in the stream of deep time, at once fleeting and eternal in this forgotten corner of my world.
