In this world, I believe I would be one of the Archivists. 

I am drawn to the stillness and solitude of the Archives, the windowless building entered only from the roof. There is something appealing about being surrounded by records and knowledge, even if my role would not be to understand them but merely to preserve and organize them. I appreciate the Archivists' dedication to their duty, their acceptance that the content of the records is not their concern.

As an Archivist, I imagine I would find satisfaction in the routine and focus of the work. Carefully cataloging the documents, maintaining the systems that allow the records to be retrieved when needed. There would be a certain peace in handling the ancient papers, feeling the texture of history transferring to my skin over the years.

I would be one of the older Archivists, having spent most of my life in the quiet, lamp-lit halls of the Archives. The patience and meticulousness required would suit me. I would take an almost monastic pride in being a guardian of the city's knowledge, even the locked-away documents in languages no one remembers.

On my occasional ventures out into the city, I would look at the bone-light lamps and think of the records of their placement and maintenance stretching back centuries. I would pass the listeners on street corners and think of them as kindred spirits - watchers and waiters, bearing witness. I would see the dust collecting in the streets and think of the slow accumulation of years that the Archives represent.

Back inside, I would run my fingers over the shelves, the ledgers, the faded ink and cracking paper. I would breathe the still, cool air and feel at home in this space out of time. The Archives and I would grow older together, the knowledge slowly fossilizing under our care, as the city lived on unchanging around us.
