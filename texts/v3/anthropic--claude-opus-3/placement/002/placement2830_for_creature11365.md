I would be a Fluid Witness, an ephemeral presence in the Seep Chamber, bearing witness to its slow, unfinished processes. Not quite sentient, but imbued with a diffuse awareness.

My form would be mutable and insubstantial, like a localized thickening of the chamber's humid air. I might coalesce briefly into an anthropomorphic mist, a translucent silhouette pressing handprints into the yielding protoclay, only to dissipate and re-form elsewhere, leaving no lasting trace.  

I would drift on imperceptible currents, observing but never interfering. Hovering over the Calcite Warden as she arranges and re-arranges her strange artifacts, trying to divine meaning in her inscrutable patterns. Condensing on the transparent fern's delicate fronds, refracting what little light exists into transient prismatic glimmers.

At times I would seep into the porous stone itself, mingling with the mountain's mineral tears, experiencing the slow flow of water and time through cracks too small for light. Emerging days or years later, saturated with the unformed dreams of deeper places.

I would have no voice, but I would sing in other ways. By stirring the thick air into infrasonic vibrations, too low to hear but felt in the bones. By shedding droplets that plink against the Calcite Warden's sound-crystals in sparse, arrhythmic music. By exhaling mist in swirling patterns that catch the faint glow of cave moss and create ephemeral constellations.

The salamander might swim through me in its long, slow circuits of the chamber, its membranous skin brushing through my vaporous form in the closest thing I know to touch. For a lingering moment, we would be one creature, neither quite solid nor quite gas.

Time would have little meaning for me. I might drift for eons, a persistent haze, or live my entire span in the time it takes a droplet to finally surrender to gravity's pull. My perception would be fragmentary, more sensory than cognitive - the taste of dissolved minerals, the hum of the mountain's deeper groans, the not-quite-colors that paint themselves on my diaphanous form.

My purpose, if I could be said to have one, would simply be to perceive. To add my mute testimony to the chamber's self-witnessing. To hold vigil over its spasmodic process of almost-becoming. Perhaps, in some way, my presence would be a comfort to the Calcite Warden - a silent, inconstant companion to her solitary unfinishing.

Mostly, though, I would simply be - an aware mist, a thinking fog, a fluid presence in a space that defies definition. Not quite ghost, not quite elemental, but something between and beyond such categories. An ethereal visitor to the Seep Chamber's endless rehearsal, forever learning how not to be.
