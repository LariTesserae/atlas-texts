In this drowned underworld, I would be a small, translucent eel-like creature, pale and delicate, my organs visible through my skin like a string of ruby beads. I glide slowly through the submerged roots and fissures, more of a wary explorer than a hunter, carefully navigating the strange, shifting geometry of the place.

My realm is the network of cracks and tunnels that spreads out from the central chamber into the surrounding earth and stone - passageways too narrow for most beings to traverse. In those constricted veins, I encounter tastes and textures unknown in the main hollow: bitter trace minerals leaching from unbroken bedrock, frigid seeps from some deeper source, layers of clay compacted by unthinkable pressure and time. 

Occasionally, driven by some impulse - perhaps curiosity, perhaps an instinctive need to connect my separate world to the larger ecosystem - I emerge from my labyrinth into the green-black heart of the place. I wind between the soft dangling rootlets, the velvet-clad stones, the spectral suspended leaves. Inevitably, I pass through some living curtain and a sheen of bioluminescent spores clings to my skin, a temporary coat of constellations mapping my travels.

I sense the presence that inhabits this place, but I do not fully comprehend it. I am too simple a creature, too transient a visitor to grasp its nature - how it is both one and many, present and potential, more a process than a being. But some primal awareness in my nerves perceives the slow, grand rhythms of its life: its gradual reach and retreat, its dreamlike memory and mutation, the measured dance of predator and prey, host and symbiote.

After drinking my fill of strangeness, I always return to the comforting austerity of the cracks. But I carry back tokens of the great entity - a nutritious mucus coating my gills, a spore lodged in my transparent flesh that will grow into some nameless, illuminating parasite. Even in the depths, even in my blindness, I remain a part of the pattern, connected to the weaving and unweaving in the dark.

In my own way, limited as it is, I am both explorer and emissary. My passage through the roots leaves a fading but indelible trace, a memory etched in displaced silt and bacterial film. And when I curl in some stone crevice to absorb the sustaining cold, my dreams echo with the slow vegetable wisdom of the place - an impression of the infinite  inward deepening of space, the coiling of matter and time into something rich and strange beyond the need for bone, beyond the cage of name and shape.
