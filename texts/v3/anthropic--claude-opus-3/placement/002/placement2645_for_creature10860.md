In this striking, vertical world of stone, metal, and ingenuity, I imagine myself as one of the hardy cliff-dwelling birds - perhaps a species of raptor uniquely adapted to the high mountain ecosystem.

My kind roosts in the aeries and grottos peppering the upper reaches of the peaks, above the city's smoke and steam. From this vantage, I soar out over the sprawl on broad, efficient wings, riding the thermal updrafts that swirl around the mountainsides. My eyes, large and sharp, can pick out minute details even from a great height - a crucial adaptation for spotting prey among the angular shadows of the cityscape far below.

My plumage is an iridescent spectrum of bronzes, coppers, and brasses, shading to a verdigris patina - hues that echo the oxidized metal domes and towers of the city as well as the mineral-laced rock of the mountains themselves. This coloration provides effective camouflage against both stone and structure, allowing me to perch unseen on high ledges, weathered cornices, and ancient statuary.

Swooping between buildings, I startle flocks of smaller birds into flight, their panicked wings churning the air. I snatch them mid-flight with precise and powerful talons before wheeling away, the wind whistling through my pinions. I might also stalk the surefooted mountain goats along the cliffs, waiting for the chance to dive at an unguarded kid. Occasionally, I snatch a careless bat from the crepuscular swarms that boil out of their cave roosts at twilight.  

My species has also adapted to glean resources unique to the city. I pluck ribbons and scraps of fabric from balconies and clotheslines to line my nest, shiny bits of metal and glass to attract a mate. I've learned to recognize which carved rain spouts yield the cleanest drinking water and time my visits to the city cisterns to coincide with the herders' water deliveries, snatching mouthfuls from the troughs while avoiding the swat of an irritated hand.

The humans regard my kind with a certain reverence and awe. My effortless mastery of the vertical domain they've worked so hard to colonize both impresses and unnerves them. They sketch our likeness in scrolling murals and tell tales of our hunting prowess and uncanny intelligence. A glimpse of me circling overhead or perched in silhouette against the setting sun is considered an auspicious omen - a blessing from the spirits of sky and stone.

Some of the bolder souls, wishing to forge a bond with the fierce raptors of the peaks, will hoist meat offerings up to the high places where we roost and hunt. Many take inspiration from our aerodynamic forms in their crafts - winged flying machines, bird-faced masks and helms, cloaks that mimic the geometries of folded wings. To adorn themselves in our image is to connect with the primal energies of the place.

For me, this city of rock and metal is but one feature of a much vaster territory. Beyond its smoke-shrouded spires and bustling industry lies a wider world of remote peaks, plunging canyons, and untamed sky. But I am bound to the rhythms of life in this strange aerie. As much a part of its complex ecology as the darting bats, grazing goats, and industrious humans. My fierce cries - now piercing, now lonely - are one more strand in the living music of the mountain city, echoing out across the vast and vertical world I call my own.
