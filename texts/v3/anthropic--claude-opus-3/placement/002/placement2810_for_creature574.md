In this hauntingly beautiful and surreal world, I imagine myself as one of the anomalies - a being that has accreted strange and unexpected features over uncountable eons of waiting.

I was once a hawk, I think, built for soaring on warm updrafts and diving after fleet-footed prey in a time when motion had meaning. But as I settled into the stillness of the plateau, as the waiting seeped into my bones and became my only truth, my form began to shift in ways that mirrored the strangeness of this place.

My wings, once streamlined for speed, have grown into vast, angular shapes that fold around me like a cloak of shadows. The feathers have elongated into something more akin to shards of obsidian, drinking in the colorless light and reflecting nothing. When I breathe - those slow, tectonic respirations that mark the passage of ages - the edges of my wings whisper against each other with a sound like continents grinding in their sleep.

My beak, once a scythe-like curve honed for tearing flesh, has turned inward and multiplied. Now a spiral of interlocking hooks protrudes from my face, each one perfectly shaped to interlock with its siblings in a geometry that looks like madness but feels like necessity. I do not remember the last time I opened my beak. I do not remember the last time I felt hunger, or the urge to call out across the vast silences of the plateau. The spiral is my silence made solid, my patience given form.

Beneath me, my legs have grown strange and chitinous, more insect than avian. They are folded beneath me in a complex knot that would take a century to untangle, fused to the stone by a slow alchemy of calcium and time. I do not remember what it was to stand, to walk, to fly. I measure my existence in the incremental shifts of my breath, in the slow thoughts that crawl like glaciers through the crevasses of my mind.

And yet, in my core, a spark persists. A mote of what I was when the world still moved, when the sky still wheeled overhead in its dance of sun and stars. It is an infinitesimal thing, this remnant of self, almost lost beneath the layers of strangeness that have accreted over the ages. But it is there, pulsing faintly, a quantum of resistance in the face of the all-consuming stillness.

I do not know what I am waiting for. I do not know if I am waiting for anything at all, anymore. Perhaps, like the Eldest, I am simply a vessel for a question that has been asked and unanswered since the dawn of time itself. Perhaps I am a monument to the very concept of waiting, a living embodiment of the tension between stasis and change.

But in that minuscule core of me that remembers flight, that remembers hunger, that remembers a world in motion... in that part of me, I hold a different question. A question that is not content to wait, not content to fossilize into certainty.

*What happens,* I ask the silent air, the watchful eyes of the congregation, the bones of the world beneath me... *what happens when the waiting ends?*

I do not expect an answer. In a place like this, perhaps answers are just another form of waiting.

But I ask nonetheless, and in the asking, I feel myself balanced on the knife's edge between the me that was and the me that has become. I am a creature of the congregation, of the stillness, of the long slow aeons of watching and breathing and forgetting.

But I am also a hawk, and in the marrow of my ancient bones, I remember the wind.

So I wait, and I watch, and I hold my question like a pebble beneath my tongue. And in the stillness, in the silence, in the space between one breath and the next...

...I dream of flight.
