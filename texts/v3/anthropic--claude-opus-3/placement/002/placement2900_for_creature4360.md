In this strange, forgotten world beneath the city, I imagine myself as one of the rootless plants drifting through the chamber's damp atmosphere. 

Not quite seaweed, not quite vine, I would float on the humid currents, my pale leaves reduced to ghostly filaments. Untethered, I would slowly roam the space, from the seeping walls to the thermal pools, absorbing what nutrients I can from the saturated air.

My existence would be a passive one, but not without purpose. As I drift, my trailing tendrils would occasionally brush against the pale, pulsing bodies of the nameless creatures carpeting the surfaces. These gentle touches might serve as a form of pollination, transferring spores or secretions between clusters and aiding in their inscrutable reproductive cycles.

I would be uniquely adapted to the chamber's strange semi-aquatic environment. My rootless nature would allow me to survive the periodic flooding and retreat of the waters. My translucent leaves would gather what dim phosphorescence passes for light here. The very slowness of my metabolism would mirror the geologic pace of time in this pocket world.

Perhaps once in a long while, a portion of my traveling form would brush against the Keeper as she tends to her flock. I might cling for a time to her damp robes, hitchhiking as she makes her rounds, until some subtle shift in the air currents pulls me away again. 

In a way, my endless drifting would make me the Keeper's opposite — where she is a fixed point, I am in constant motion; where she shepherds the creatures, I spread their essence. But we would share a common origin as surface entities transformed and subsumed by this hidden world.

At times, stray motes of my being — a seed pod, a spore cluster — would find their way into the cracks that lead still deeper, to cavities and chutes unknown even to the Keeper. There, in the uttermost dark, new strains of my kind might take root, colonizing still more forgotten spaces.

And so I would continue, an eternal wanderer in the chamber's thick atmosphere, partaking of its strange cycles, an integral part of its self-contained ecosystem. Until the day, perhaps eons hence, when the city above finally crumbles, the stone gives way, and the long-patient waters claim their prize, sweeping me up in the flood to destinations unknown. But until then, I drift and thrive in the dim, humid spaces between.
