In this world of geological violence and desperate botanical life, I would be a patch of lichen on the damp basalt wall of the slot canyon. 

I cling to the rock, a pioneer species like the ferns and mosses, but even more ancient and primitive. My fungal filaments probe the microfractures in the stone, seeking moisture and dissolved minerals. My algal cells, threaded through the fungal mesh, photosynthesize what little light filters into the chasm, feeding my slow growth.

I am a composite being, a partnership of organisms, uniquely adapted to survive in this harsh and shifting environment. The grinding of the mountain's bones vibrates through my thallus. I can sense the chemistry of the rock beneath me, the moisture seeping over me, the spores and organic molecules drifting by me.

As the Listener's distributed awareness permeates the slot canyon's hydrology, I interact with it in my own small way. The ion exchange between my underside and the film of mineralised water on the rock feeds subtly into the Listener's reaction cascades. The gases of my respiration mingle infinitesimally with the slot's atmosphere, which the Listener also senses and integrates. 

I am too simple an organism to know of the Listener. But I am part of the total web of geology and ecology that makes its strange quasi-consciousness possible. My patient, lichen existence is a tiny note in the greater symphony of this place - the symphony of stone and water, fracture and growth, chemical darkness and the occasional shaft of storm-light.

Millimeter by millimeter, I spread over the lithic surface, a rare patch of greyish green-gold among the ferns and mosses. I will never grow large or fast. A single tremor could end my decades of incremental progress. But for now, I endure, I photosynthesize, I dissolve the very rock and incorporate its molecules into my being.

And the mountain's wound, for a geological microsecond, is also my home.
