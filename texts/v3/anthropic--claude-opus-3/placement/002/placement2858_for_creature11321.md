As I descended into the Hollowing, the honeyed light and warm, pollen-scented air enveloped me. The roar of the teeming life within was overwhelming at first, a cacophony of chiming, rasping, and thrumming that reverberated through my entire being. 

I found myself transformed, my body elongated and thin, with pale bark-like skin and dark, almond-shaped eyes adapted to the amber dimness. In my clever hands, I clutched three stones from my hearth, a coil of plaited cord, a clay bowl, and a small carving blade.  

Compelled by an ancestral imperative, I wandered the labyrinthine chambers, marveling at the riotous diversity of creatures - the ape-like climbers, the ribbon-serpents, the clouds of flashing insects. I felt at once separate from and deeply connected to this roiling, churning world. It was the antithesis of the quiet, grey realm above, and yet it was this very contrast that shaped my identity.

As I trekked deeper, a realization took root in my mind, spreading tendrils of unease. The Hollowing was growing, expanding outward and upward, its vegetable mind operating on a timescale beyond easy reckoning. Perhaps one day, far or near, it would breach the surface entirely, and the grey plateau-dwellers would have to find a new way to define themselves in relation to its unstoppable fecundity.

In a wide central chamber, I stopped, overcome by a need to affirm my presence, my selfhood, against the all-consuming hum of life. With deliberate motions, I stacked my cairn, cut my tally, hung my cord, and pressed the bowl into the compost of the floor. I made my marks. I retained my mind. The Hollowing had not hollowed me out. 

Not yet.

Turning, I began the long ascent back to the cold and quiet, but I carried the weight of the Hollowing inside me now. A new understanding of the thinness of the barrier between the realms, the grey struggling to hold back the green and vermilion. A sense that my role was changing, from visitor to watcher, counter of days, planter of cairns in both worlds. One of the ones who came back out, but who must be forever ready to descend again, to keep the thread, as the Hollowing slowly, inexorably, consumes its opposite.
