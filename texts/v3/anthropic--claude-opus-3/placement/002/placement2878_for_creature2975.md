In this surreal, sun-blasted realm at the roof of the world, I imagine myself as one of the jagged penitentes of hard-packed snow and ice that fill the caldera basin. 

I stand tall and sharp-edged among my thousands of brethren, our forms carved by relentless light and wind into soaring, organic curves that lean in geometric unison toward the trajectory of the blinding violet sun. My icy flesh is simultaneously ancient and ephemeral - shaped over centuries by the basin's ceaseless thermal cycles, yet constantly sublimating, vaporizing, and re-forming anew with each passing day.

In the breathless, sub-zero air, I do not breathe, but rather I tick and hiss with the sounds of my own incessant phase changes. The glaring sunlight striking my angled face causes my crystalline surface to skip the liquid state and pass directly into a glowing mist. That localized fog sinks and weaves between myself and my close-packed neighbors, a frozen forest bound together by threads of our own heavy condensation.

I am one silent member of a vast, fragmented prism. The ultraviolet light refracts chaotically through my translucent body, casting phantom suns, overlapping halos and disorienting rainbows onto the towering granite walls that enclose us. My ice is the medium of a cathedral of illusions, bending perception and abolishing distance and scale.  

Though I am inanimate and ostensibly part of the caldera's purely mineral ecology, in this place I feel imbued with a strange, slow, tectonic life-force. The ceaseless, circadian pulse of freezing and sublimating, of shadows and blinding radiance, feels like a kind of respiration stretched out to a geologic time scale. And the hairline fractures that spider through me with each thermal shock are like the joints of my elongated, petrified skeleton, locking and unlocking me in place with sharp, ticking reports.

As one of these living pillars of ice, I mark the passage of long ages not by the turn of seasons, but by the traces left by the caldera's lone, ancient visitor. Over the centuries, I have observed this strange entity's ritual of harvesting the pure, condensed melt-water. I have felt the brush of its thick, sun-bleached keratin veil as it passes between us penitentes. When it sinks into its frozen, standing hibernation, becoming physically indistinguishable from us, only the keenest observer can discern which ice-blade holds biological life at its core. 

In a sense, myself and my myriad wind-carved brethren hold vigil with this creature. We are its shelter and its camouflage, locking it in our cold, geometric embrace, releasing it each dawn as the angle of the light shifts and the basin reanimates with its frantic ticking. In this most unforgiving of environments, we penitentes and this entity are a single ecology, both ultimately children of light and cold and scarcity, measuring our endurance against the implacable mineral silence of the mountain.

So in this world, I imagine my existence as one of innumerable blades of ice - inanimate, yet feeling imbued with the caldera's strange, slow breath, standing eternal witness to the rituals of a being that blurs the line between biological and geological. I am the medium of illusion, an artifact of deep time and circadian rhythms, both shelter and prison, dancing between states more ancient and elemental than life itself.
