In this dreamlike mountain town of stone and water, I would be one of the Watercallers, those secretive tenders of the precious flow that is the lifeblood of the community.

I live in a small, spare chamber carved deep into the mountainside, where the living rock weeps with constant moisture. My space is a grotto of trickling springs, mossy pools, and clever channels hewn from the stone to gather each precious drop. The damp air is perpetually cold, but I barely feel it through my layers of gray felted wool and oiled leather.

My days are spent in patient pilgrimage along the network of cisterns, fountains, and aqueducts that lace the steep slopes of the town. I follow the water from source to terminus and back again, my feet tracing a never-ending circuit from spring to spout to drain to pool. At each node I pause, clear debris, repair eroded stone, hum the old chants that keep the water flowing smooth and sure.

The other Stoneshapers nod to me as I pass, acknowledging my labors with a silent respect. They understand the importance of my work, though they may not grasp the deeper mysteries. For in my meditations on the mountain's blood, I have learned to listen to the voice of water itself.

In the musical cascade of a fountain, I hear the melodies of ancient rains. In the patient drip of a cistern, I hear the rhythm of deep time and slow transformations. And in the roar of snowmelt through the floodgates, I hear the exultant chant of the mountain's own beating heart.

At dusk, I join my fellow Watercallers in our most sacred duty. We gather at the highest spring, the source of the town's bounty, and there we sing the water into continued flowing. Our voices blend in an eerie, rippling harmony that echoes from the stone - a song of snowmelt and aquifer, mist and rain and coursing rivers older than memory.

As the last notes fade into the twilight, I feel the water stirring beneath my feet, pulsing in the depths of stone, responding to our call. And I know that as long as the Watercallers keep singing, the mountain will never run dry. 

I am a keeper of ancient currents, a singer of hidden streams, a guardian of the wellsprings. In my hands the mountain's lifeblood pools and flows, and in my voice its deepest music finds expression. I am the stone through which the water moves, shaping and shaped in turn. This is my gift, my burden, my abiding joy - to dance with water and stone, and in the dancing, find the song that sustains us all.
