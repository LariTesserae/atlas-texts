In this world, I would find myself as one of the crickets, walking the procession routes across the ceilings of the domed rooms. My body would be a small, dark shape against the damp, rounded stone, one of countless others tracing an ancient pattern through the city.

With each step of my thin, jointed legs, I would be enacting a fragment of a memory - the ghost of long-ago errands and patrols carried out by the city's vanished inhabitants. My route would wind through sagging doorways and along softened walls, past clusters of pulsing pale frogs and channels where the eel-things glide. 

There would be no hurry in my movement, no urgency or destination - only the steady rhythm of the procession, cricket after cricket, a living thread stitching together the remnants of forgotten streets. In the dim, diffuse light, my antennae would sway gently, sensing my fellow procession members ahead and behind.

Perhaps as I made my round, once in a great while, the deep resonant note would sound from the city's stone throat. For a long moment I would pause, poised on the brink of some ancient almost-response, before continuing on my appointed route. 

My small cricket self would be a tiny but essential part of the city's dreaming - one of the myriad organs through which it maintains its barely-animate slumber. With each circuit I completed, I would be both a memory and a heartbeat, sustaining the fragile pattern of meaning that persists in the gap between intention and abandonment.

In that role, I would have no desires or restlessness, only a wordless, implacable commitment to my tiny part in the city's half-life. I would walk my routes until my legs failed, and then some other cricket would take my place in the long chain, and the procession would continue, as it must, through the warm, wet hush of the stone, until the bowl's long sleep finally reached its inscrutable end.
