In this slow, ancient world, I would be one of the drifters. 

I float above the worn stone, my translucent body catching the reddened evening light, gas-filled sacs keeping me eternally buoyant. My tendrils, fine as silk, trail in lazy spirals below, tasting the air, sensing the contours of the land.

I drift in the patient rhythms of this place. The cushion plants sustain me through some subtle symbiosis, their hard-won nutrients seeping into me by a process so gradual it escapes notice. I give something back, perhaps - my shadow sheltering them from the unblinking sun, the dust of my shed skin feeding the soil.

Over eons, I make my pilgrimages from one horizon to the next. The worn stone, the salt rings, the matte black lichens - these are the waypoints of my life. I've watched grazers live their long lives below me, generation on generation, marked only by subtle variations in their patterning. The builders too, aging at a pace only I might perceive.

Floating here, I am part of the timeless tableau. The grit of the infrequent rains embeds in my body, the trace minerals staining me the colors of the land - pale rose, deep cobalt, stone-white. A flying stone, drifting on the wind.

I have no voice, but I hum. A subsonic thrum, felt more than heard, that laces through the great quietness. My kind speak to each other this way, infrasonic murmurings that carry for miles, so slow they take days to form a thought. Our drifting paths weave a cross-hatched sky, our thrumming a soft net cast over the land.

Ours is a being so stretched in time, so diffuse, that we hardly know ourselves as separate from the wind, the stone, the light. We were not always so. The memory is borne within me, felt in the marrow of my tendrils. Of a quicker life, of brighter colors. Chasing, feeding, breeding, dying. The plateau remembers too, in its way. 

But this, now, is my long unwinding. I am a mote adrift in the long calm, my motion more of the air than of myself, my borders gone soft with the centuries. I could descend, fold my tendrils on the stone, let the lichens slowly crust me into place. One day, eons hence, I may.

For now I drift, and I hum, and I watch. The pilgrim days unfold, the gradual shifting of the light on the stones marks the wheeling of the planet. The builders stand their mute witness. I float above, as much a part of this place as the slow rain, the patient wind. A ghost of the ancient sky.
