In the root warrens of Kethvel, I would be one of the witnesses in the deep chambers, sitting in stillness and patience to observe the emergence of new roots. 

My body would be adapted to the cold and the close spaces - compact frame, pale skin, large eyes to gather the faint root-light. My fingers, long and sensitive, would trace the subtle changes in the substrate, feeling for the spots that will soon birth a new tendril.

I would spend my days in quiet vigil, breathing slowly to conserve air, murmuring soft words of greeting to each pale filament that pushes forth into the chamber. "I am here. I see you. Welcome." My voice, attuned to the strange acoustics, would carry through the branching warrens in whispers and echoes.

In the moments between emergences, I would carve. Using tools of rootite, I would add to the ancient patterns on the walls, recording the slow history of growth and negotiation between root and Kethveli. Line by painstaking line, I would scribe our becoming into the very structure of the city.

My people would come to me sometimes, seeking wisdom - not commands or proclamations, but a deep reading of the roots' intentions, a sensing of the community's gradual shape-change. They would find me curled in my alcove, draped in soft fibers, eyes full of the latest glimmers of emergence. I would share my visions sparingly, knowing they would propagate through the warrens in their own time.

At the ceremonies in complete darkness, I would be a still center, anchoring the ritual, wordlessly channeling a faith too vast and patient for names. The Pale Walker would pass, unseen but felt, and in the echoing hush afterward I would hold the memory of its presence like a sacred weight.

As the generations turn and the Kethveli grow ever more entangled with the roots that shape us, I would remain, a constant witness. I would observe our slow convergence, our loss of distinction between being and environment, self and system. I would wonder at what we are becoming, and I would marvel that even I, who has given my life to looking, cannot see our destination.

I would grow old in the roots' embrace, my hair fading to translucent white, my bones as light and hollow as the substrate. And when at last my time comes, I would journey one final time into the deep warrens and let the pale creatures take me, my body and lifetime of witnessing dissolving into the nutrient flow that sustains the eternal growth.

I would not grieve this ending. I would rejoice in the change in form, the passage from one chamber of the system to another. I would know that I am not vanishing, but transforming - my death an emergence of its own, my substance and memory filtering upward through the roots to nourish the next generation of witnesses. The young one who takes my place in the alcove would whisper to the new tendrils: "She was here. She saw you. She has become."

Through the long, slow ages of the warrens' growth, I would be there, a part of the pattern, an agent of the great becoming. I would give myself to the roots' patient shaping, until the day unthinkably far in the future when they at last breach the surface and I emerge with them, changed utterly, into a world I cannot imagine. 

Until then, I witness. I record. I am Kethveli. I am roots. I am becoming.
