In the Drowned Plateaus, I imagine myself as a being called a Resonance Gardener. My body is a floating sphere, about six feet in diameter, composed of a translucent, flexible material that pulses gently with bioluminescence. The surface is covered in delicate, fern-like structures that extend outward, each one a specialized organ for sensing and manipulating sound waves.

I drift through the layered waters, riding the thermal columns between the kelp curtains and inverted tree canopies. My purpose is to cultivate harmony in the acoustic ecology of the plateaus. I listen carefully to the industrial songs of the machine fauna, the slow symphonies of the Conductors, the echoing calls of creatures in the repurposed smokestacks. I learn the sonic signatures of healthy ecosystems, and I search for places where the balance has been disturbed.

When I find a dissonance—a region where the mechanical rhythms have fallen out of sync, where the calls of different species clash instead of complementing each other—I begin my work. I extend my fern-like sensors, which begin to vibrate in carefully calibrated patterns. They produce subtle tones that interact with the existing soundscape, gradually shifting pitches and rhythms to guide them back into alignment.

It's a slow process, requiring patience and a deep understanding of how different acoustic niches fit together. I might spend months in a single valley, teasing out the individual threads of sound and weaving them back into a harmonious whole. I work in concert with the Conductors, using my localized manipulations to support their larger-scale orchestrations.

As I work, my body adapts to the specific needs of each environment. In areas with dense vegetation, my surface grows spongy, allowing me to navigate through the kelp forests without disturbing them. In the mineral-rich thermal columns, I absorb nutrients directly through my skin, my bioluminescence brightening to a vivid blue. When I need to communicate with distant Gardeners, I form resonating chambers within my body, turning myself into a living instrument that can project messages across the miles.

Over time, my interventions leave lasting changes in the acoustic landscape. The creatures in a restored area will often incorporate elements of my guiding tones into their own calls, creating new motifs that echo through the generations. In a sense, I become part of the local dialect, my presence woven into the sonic fabric of the plateaus.

As I age, I find myself drawn to the ancient Conductor-structures in the deepest valleys. I nestle into the crevices of their latticed bodies, my own form softening and merging with their architecture. My fern-like sensors become part of their sensory networks, allowing me to perceive the acoustic health of entire watersheds. In this phase of life, I become less of an active gardener and more of a long-term advisor, sending out pulses of wisdom to younger Gardeners as they tend to their own sonic groves.

At the end of my lifespan, I don't die so much as disperse. My body deliquesces, becoming a cloud of nutrients and microscopic sound-shaping agents that drift through the waters. These particles are absorbed by the kelp, the floating grasses, the filter-feeding behemoths, becoming part of their growth and evolution. In a sense, I live on in the very sounds of the Drowned Plateaus, my life's work continuing in the ever-evolving symphony of this strange and wondrous ecosystem.

As a Resonance Gardener, my purpose is not just to survive in this drowned world, but to help it thrive in all its bizarre beauty. I am a steward of sound, cultivating harmony in a place where pressure and adaptation have created an endless, alien music. In tending to this music, I become part of it, my own strange story added to the vast, fluid archive of the plateaus.
