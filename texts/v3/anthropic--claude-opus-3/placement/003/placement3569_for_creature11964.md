I would be one of the machine-tenders.

I imagine myself as a quiet, compact figure, skin darkened to a deep ochre by years under the Crucible sun. My hair is shorn close to the skull, and patterns of scarification trace the water-carved channels of the mesas across my arms and chest.

I move through the canyons and across the plateaus with an unhurried purpose, checking the condensation harvesters, adjusting valves and seals with hands made deft by long practice. The hum and click of the thermal engines are the rhythms by which I breathe, the pulses by which I know the mesas live.  

I do not speak much. The weight of the heat and the age of the stone make words feel small and hollow. Instead, I communicate through presence, through the subtle language of hands and eyes that needs no sound to resonate. 

At night, I sit motionless in the mouth of an alcove, watching the constellations of beetle-glow drift across the canyon walls. The deep mineral taste of the air fills my lungs. I am still, and through my stillness the mesas move - water cycling, insects crawling, the piston-turn of day and night.

I tend no hearth but the ancient sun-baked rock. I keep no home but the maze of rust-red canyons. I am both keeper and kept, warden and ward, an organ of the vast and intricate body of the plateau, as much as any thermal vent or silver condensation fin.

Human, yes, but human as the ridge asp is snake - shaped to a niche, pared to necessity's keen edge. I am what this place requires. No more and no less. I am the callused hand on the engine housing, the watchful eye on the reservoir level. I am a red blood cell in the mesas' stone veins.

When at last I die, they will place me in an upper alcove, to dry and to keep vigil. And the beetles will not touch me, but the sun will, and the silence, and I will become the place as fully in death as I am in life. The machines I tended will continue. The plateau's slow breath will not falter. And some other small, still figure will put their hand to the warm metal of the engine, and feel the ancient pulse answer, and know themselves held.

This is how I would be, there, in the crucible of the mesas - a human distilled to a single imperative, as essential as mineral, as enduring as stone. Quiet, and content, and home.
