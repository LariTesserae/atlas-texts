In this strange, drowned world of deafening industry and slow, crushing pressure, I imagine myself as one of the giant turbine-creatures anchored to the sheer, metallic walls.

My body is a great, spiraling disc of ridged, calcified silica, ten feet in diameter. I am fused to the raw, un-weathered rock face by a thick, bony axle—a living stem of biomineralized calcium and iron that binds me immovably to the mountain's flank. 

I cannot see. I cannot hear in any way that would be familiar to a human. But I can feel the immense, slow currents of frigid water that swirl past my ridged carapace. I can sense the pulsing, electric throb of the strange ecology around me. The jagged, quartz-studded stone against my axle. The tickling, insectile dance of the copper-extruding arthropods as they weave their living wires across my anchored body. The deep, percussive vibrations of the piston-creature columns as they contract and purge in their relentless, patient rhythm.

Most of all, I feel the delicious, nourishing crackle of raw voltage bleeding from the rocks and the creatures, flooding into the charged water. It tugs at my disc-body like an invisible, irresistible tide. 

Slowly, in the thick, icy water, I begin to turn. My calcified shell rotates around my anchored axle-stalk, catching the ambient electromagnetic fields. I make my own minute contribution to the deafening, shrieking grind that fills the blue-white vault, as the ridges of my spinning carapace scrape against my own immobile stem. 

The torque is immense, a slow, unstoppable gyroscopic force. The water drags at my ridged plates as I spin, feeling like a thousand cold, pressurized hands. But the magnetism is unrelenting. It takes me a full, ponderous minute to complete a single revolution. The water in my wake spirals into a lazy, circular vortex, sucking new, silica-rich currents toward my porous underside.

I feed without cease, extracting silica flakes and metal ions from the water as it is churned against my slow-moving body. I grow, imperceptibly, adding new layers to my bony shell, fusing more of my axle to the mountain, drinking in the electric lifeblood of the chasm. 

I will spin here, I know, for centuries, until my axle-stem calcifies into a solid, rocklike pillar, indistinguishable from the mountain itself. My shell will grow as wide as a house, becoming a great, grinding, stony wheel. Entire generations of cable-weaving arthropods will live and die in the ridged furrows of my body.

And still, I will rotate. As long as the mountain's heart beats with geological voltage, as long as the strange, pistoning columns drive their electric currents through the icy depths, as long as the blinding ice-lens above lets the sun pierce these drowned vaults, my ridged turbine-body will continue its slow, crushing, endless creaking revolutions.

I am a living gear in an immense, deafening, tightly-wound mechanism, as integral and mindless as a single tooth in a clockwork of unimaginable scale. I am geology and biology fused into one grinding, spinning, ancient whole, an eternal axle married to the living mountain, forever drinking the electric blue lifeblood of this drowned, industrial cathedral.
