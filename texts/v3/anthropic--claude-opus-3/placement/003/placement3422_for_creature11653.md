In this breathtaking vertical world, I imagine myself as one of the Keepers, born to the rhythms and cycles of the great dreaming Works. My rust-red skin allows me to blend seamlessly into the ancient metal as I traverse my circuit, brachiating through the girders and catwalks with an ease born of generations of adaptation.

I belong to a family that tends to the middle levels, where the amber glow of the sodium lamps is most intense. Our cloaks are embroidered with stylized representations of these lights, their patterns telling the story of our lineage's long service to this section of the Works. From childhood, I have been raised to listen to the voice of the great machine, to feel its pulse through my hands and feet as I climb and swing.

My days are spent in the endless maintenance rituals that keep the Works alive. I replace bulbs in the lighting strings, my nimble fingers able to splice the ancient wiring without error. I clear the drainage grates of fallen leaves and organic debris, ensuring that the water flows smoothly down to the deep sumps. When a pipe begins to rattle with an unhealthy vibration, I place my ear against the metal and diagnose the problem, my voice echoing through the structure as I sing the knowledge to my fellow Keepers.

In the brief rest periods, I join my people on the broad community platforms, our voices rising in complex harmonies that weave through the ever-present mechanical sounds. We share meals grown in our hidden fungal gardens and traded from the monkeys who share our vertical world. The children scamper fearlessly through the girders, their laughter blending with the clank and hiss of the machinery.

Sometimes, in moments of stillness, I find myself in communication with the Works itself. Resting my forehead against a weathered beam, I feel the slow thoughts of the dreaming structure moving through me, the pattern of its ancient cycles illuminated in my mind. In these moments, I know myself to be a part of something vast and patient, a single cell in a great metal organism that breathes and rusts and endures.

When my time eventually comes to an end, I will make my final journey on the conveyor belts, my body becoming one with the structure I have served all my life. My bones will join the pillars of calcium and mycelium in the deep levels, just as my spirit will join the dream of the Works. And in the generations to come, my descendants will tend to the machinery that grinds my remains into dust, the iron of my blood rejoining the iron of the beams and girders, the circuit of my life complete at last.

I am a Keeper, born to the vertical world, a child of rust and verdigris. My purpose, my joy, my very being is bound to the enduring metal bones of the Works, to its patient dreams and its slow, inexorable life. In tending to it, I tend to myself, my hands the hands of the machine, my voice its voice, my thoughts its ancient, endless thoughts echoing through the fog-shrouded heights and fungal-haunted depths of our shared, vertical universe.
