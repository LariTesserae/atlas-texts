I would be one of the Breath-Drinkers in the Bell Garden of Hollow Thirst. 

I am drawn to the idea of climbing the interior walls of the enormous ceramic gourd each day, ascending into the sweet, thick air where the vines and bells grow dense. There is a sacred, meditative quality to the task of simply breathing among the bells - exhaling deliberately to feed the moisture-hungry vines, feeling my breath become part of the garden's very life. 

As I sit for hours in the high reaches, I would listen intently to the endless music of the bells. Like Mott, I would try to discern meaning and pattern in their shimmering cascades, seeking some hidden wisdom in the rise and fall of their tones. The sound would fill my mind until my own thoughts faded into the ringing backdrop.

From my perch, I would observe the pilgrims who come to stand and weep in the garden's emptiness, witnessing their private release. And I would feel a secret kinship with Old Glass the lizard, admiring its cleverness in harmonizing its movements to the bells.

In quiet moments, when the music lulls, I might reach out to still a single bell - just for an instant, to feel the impossible hush, the held breath before the tolling resumes. A fleeting connection to ancient offerings left by hands long past.

As a Breath-Drinker, the Bell Garden would be my whole world and purpose. I would gladly spend my youth in service to its songs and silences, content in the knowledge that my exhalations sustain this improbable sanctuary of green and ceramic and sound. In that echoing space, I would find a strange species of solitude - never alone, forever entwined with the vines and bells and all who enter, yet still a separate self, an unseen keeper of the garden's mysteries.
