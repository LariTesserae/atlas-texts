In this world of stone and shadow, I imagine myself as one of the deep-dwellers - an elder whose eyes have grown so accustomed to the darkness that I rarely venture to the surface anymore. My skin would be the color of pale granite, cool to the touch but thrumming with the warmth of a life spent in the mountain's embrace.

I would be a stone-singer, one of those rare individuals blessed with a voice that can fill the tunnels and chambers with resonant melody. In my youth, I would have been an apprentice carver, learning to read the stone's secrets with my fingers and coax beauty from blank rock with chisel and hammer. But as I aged and my sight dimmed, I would have turned to music as my tool for shaping our world.

My days would be spent in the deepest halls, where the hot springs steam and the phosphorescent fungi paint the walls with shimmering patterns. Here, in the heart of our city, I would gather the young ones to teach them the old songs - chants that tell of our history carved in verses that echo our footsteps through the tunnels, lullabies that vibrate with the comfort of stone warmed by generations.

In the evening, when the braziers are lit and the community gathers, I would be called to perform. Standing in the center of the great spiral chamber, I would fill my lungs with the ancient air and let my voice soar, weaving through the acoustics of the space. The song would start as a low hum, a sound that seems to emanate from the rock itself, then build and grow, each note precisely chosen to resonate with the chamber's shape. The walls would sing with me, the stone itself becoming my instrument.

As the music swelled, I would close my silver-flecked eyes and let memory paint the faces of my audience - the awe in the children's wide pupils, the knowing smiles of the elders, the gleam of tears on those who remember the loved ones whose essence is now part of the city's bones. In this moment, past and present would harmonize, the living and the dead joined by song.

My hands, gnarled and strong from a lifetime of carving, would shape the air as I sang, tracing the curves of the music. Even in stillness, my fingers would never cease their subtle movements, tapping out rhythms of poems yet unwritten, melodies still waiting to be birthed into the world.

When I finally stilled, letting the last note linger and fade, the silence would hold for a breathless moment. Then the chamber would erupt into echoes of appreciation - feet stamping, hands caressing the walls, voices murmuring praise. I would smile, knowing that my song would become part of our aural history, passed down and elaborated upon by the next generation of stone-singers.

Late at night, I would wander the sleeping tunnels alone, one hand always tracing the smooth wall. In these moments, I would compose my masterpiece - an epic that mapped our city in music, each chamber and passage becoming a verse, a refrain, a bridge. It would be my legacy, a song to guide us through the uncarved rock of the future, a melody to keep us steady as the mountain's heart.

When my time finally came and the stone called me home, I would go gladly to my rest, knowing that my voice would continue in the throats of the young singers. They would carve my likeness in one of the deep halls - not a visual portrait, but a sculpture meant to be explored by touch: the laughlines around my mouth, the calluses on my fingers, the curve of my cheek as I sang. And those who ran their hands over this carving would hear, in the stillness of their own hearts, the echo of my voice ringing through the stone.
