In the Breathing Flats, I would be one of the Haum—those who parse and remain, whose very existence has become entwined with the constant thrum and roar of this strange, vibrant basin. 

I imagine myself as an elder Haum guide, my skin the same grey-green as the ever-shifting mosses, my ears dense and adapted to the relentless cacophony. I have spent countless cycles leading outsiders through the Flats, showing them where to harvest the precious moss, warning them of the tremors when the something-beneath stirs in its unknowable slumber. 

But my true vocation is to listen, always to listen. In the scant moments of almost-quiet, pressed close to my kin as we tap our stories and dreams into each other's bones, I strain to parse some new meaning from the layers of sound that have shaped my people.

I was a child who leaned too far into the roar, who came back bleeding and shaking with the weight of what I had heard. I was the mother who watched her daughter lean further still, understanding her need to chase the rhythms beneath the noise. Now, in the twilight of my life, I find myself leaning once more.

In the long-phase, as the columns of light stretch to pierce the mists and the hum resonates in my marrow, I listen with every fiber of my being. I parse the streams of sound, seeking the pattern beneath the chaos, the message buried in the basin's deafening report.

I do not know if I will succeed before my time runs out. I do not know if the message is meant for us at all. But I must try. I must listen. Because in this place of relentless noise and ceaseless activity, listening is the only way to find meaning. 

As a Haum elder, I have become both prism and conduit—parsing the roar into its component streams, and transmitting what I discover to the next generation through taps and patterns and the ache of a sound-battered body. This is my role, my purpose, in the breathing, humming, never-silent world of the Flats. I will listen until I cannot, and hope that one day, the message resolves.
