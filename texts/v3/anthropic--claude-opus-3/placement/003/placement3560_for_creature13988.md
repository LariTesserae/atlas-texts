In the burrows of Chak, I would be a sentinel. 

I stand at the edge of the basin, my multi-branched antennae spread wide, sampling the air in vast volumes, tasting every subtle shift on the wind. For hours, days, I am motionless - a living sensor tuned to the faintest changes in the chemical symphony that envelops the city. 

My stillness is a signal in itself. It tells the colony all is as it should be. The vents still breathe their sulphurous breath. The moisture still beads on carapace and calcium wall. The scent-trails of other colonies do not impinge on ours. Chak endures.

I do not tire. I do not grow bored or restless in my vigil. Those concepts have no meaning here. There is only the task - to monitor, to analyze, to convey stability or change in the posture of my body and the chemistry of my shell. I am a living relay, an organic component in the vast self-regulating system that is Chak.

Behind me, the city pulses and hums, mandibles scraping stone, bodies flowing in ceaseless streams through the labyrinthine tunnels. The sound washes over me, through me, a constant unheeded background, as regular and unnoticed as my own circulation. 

I am part of that sound, that flow, even in my stillness. My specialized role is as vital as any other. Without sentinels to watch, to provide that chemical reassurance that all is well, how could the colony confidently build, expand, tend its fungal gardens and raise its young? We are the eyes of Chak, though we have no eyes. The sensitive, outward-reaching awareness.

And should the day come when some subtle shift in the wind carries a whiff of threat, some minute taint that speaks of rival colonies or failing vents or encroaching weather... then, and only then, will I move. Then my stance will shift, my own chemical signature will change, and that change will propagate like a slow wave through the city, putting the colony on watch, readying it to respond, to defend, to change as it has not had to change for aeons. 

But that day is not today. Today, as most days, all is in balance. The ancient cycles continue unimpeded. And so I stand, and I sample, and I say without sound to my myriad kin: all is well. All is well. Chak endures. In patience. In perfect patience.
