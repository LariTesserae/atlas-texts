I would find myself being a part of the interconnected web of life in the Boiler Ridge, perhaps as one of the climbing mammals in the canopy, the "tank-eaters". 

I imagine myself as one of these slow, deliberate creatures, my damp, algae-tinged fur blending into the dripping epiphyte-laden branches as I make my nightly rounds. With nimble paws, I reach my long snout into the recesses of the bromeliad tanks, fishing out trapped insects and frogs, pruning back leaves to keep the microcosmic pools healthy and thriving.

Though not quick or flashy, I am an integral part of this steaming, pulsing ecosystem. My foraging resets and maintains the bromeliad tanks that stud the forest, preventing stagnation. I am a gardener, tending to my lush patch of canopy.

At dawn, I curl up to sleep in the damp duff collected in the crooks of the great tree ferns, my algae-colored fur camouflaging me among the fronds. Even in slumber, I am attuned to the rhythms of the ridge - the thrum and hiss of the geysers, the slow piston-knock echoing up from the depths. 

My kind navigate around the timing of the vents, our loose troops of three or four communicating in quiet coughs as we skirt the steaming openings in sync with their ancient schedule. We are a piece in the complex clockwork of the Boiler Ridge - not a cog, but something softer, more organic, yet no less essential to the functioning of the whole.

Here in the eternally dripping green bower of the canopy, I am a humble but inextricable filament woven into the living fabric of this self-sustaining, self-regulating realm. A small, steady pulse in the greater circulatory system of the ridge, as much a part of its respiratory cycle as the exhaling vents and ghosting steam. My leafy domain is both my charge and my universe, my habits and appetites and instincts all shaped by and shaping these misty, ferny heights.
