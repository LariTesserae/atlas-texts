In this buried, industrial underworld, I imagine myself as a small, resilient creature adapted to the harsh environment. Not quite mammalian, my body would be low and narrow, built for squeezing through tight spaces and clinging to pipes and ladders. 

My skin would be mostly bare, with patches of coarse, mineral-stiff hair along my spine and flanks, stained orange at the tips by iron dust. On my belly, pale and nearly translucent skin would reveal a network of blue-gray veins and old scars from encounters with scalding steam and jagged metal. My ears would be sealed, vestigial flaps, and my eyes cloudy beads beneath the skin, sensing only major changes in light.

But my true "sight" would come from an array of long, delicate whiskers sprouting in fans from my muzzle, brow, wrists, elbows and ribs. Glass-fine and heat-curled, some broken short and sealed with my own amber resin, some blackened at the tips, these living sensors would detect the subtlest changes in air current, vibration, moisture and electrical charge. They would trace the surfaces of railings and grates, discern the hush of steam behind metal plates, and differentiate between dormant and dangerous pipes. My whiskers would be my map through the mechanical labyrinth.

I would subsist on the strange life eking out an existence here. Lapping drops of condensation and warm mineral seeps. Scraping dark fungus from beneath drip pans with flat front teeth. Plucking pale, squirming larvae from stagnant puddles. Snatching near-invisible insects off gauge faces with a swift flick of my pale, paper-colored tongue. In lean times, I would make due with bits of softened gasket, degraded rubber and old adhesive - meager rations that would leave me restless and ill.

My nest would be tucked away in an inspection alcove behind a tank, where radiant heat permeates the walls evenly and errant drips are limited to times of pressure changes. This refuge would be lined with the cast-off debris of the place that has become my world - curls of peeled labels, chips of paint, tufts of insulation, metal shavings, husks of insects, my own shed fur, flakes of rust. Prized finds like washers, a broken bit of porcelain, a mug handle, would be carefully gathered - not for any practical purpose, but for the simple rightness of their shape, the way they hold warmth, and the scent of safe passage that clings to them.

I might not be alone. Another set of prints in the rust mud, a brush of whiskers in a chance encounter beneath the drizzle of a broken pipe - subtle signs of others, potential mates. Communication would be in touches, in tastes, in the exchange of knowledge about shifts in our perilous environment rather than any audible language. Sound would be too risky, the way it travels along the metal surfaces, potentially attracting unknown dangers.

If there were young, they would enter existence in perfect darkness, learning the world first through heat and texture and subtle vibration, their eyes never opening, never needed. The crucial knowledge would be what the pipes and gauges and machines telegraph through their every tick and tremor and change in temperature - the wordless language of this place that is our entire universe.

I would not grasp the concepts of the long-gone creators and workers - the notions of pumps and furnaces and pressure valves, of ledgers and shifts and quotas would be as meaningless to me as the illegible remains of chalk marks and pressure charts. But on a level beneath thought, in my blood and nerves and whiskers, I would understand the patterns and the demands of this interconnected, faltering, but still breathing mechanical ecosystem.

I would be both an interloper and an integral part - a mote in the occluded eye of this vast, slumbering, subterranean beast of iron and brick and ceramic and rubber. Crawling through its veins, sheltering in its pores, drinking its strange secretions, I would be its unintended offspring, its unlooked-for symbiont, spawned from its slow decay but also participating in and furthering the cycles of its weary, but oddly persistent, life.
