If I found myself in the Kiln-Warren, I imagine I would be one of the corner drop-things. 

Compared to the ceiling-drinkers and floor-grazers, the drop-things seem the least defined and most open to change and growth over the long arcs of time in this enclosed world. They patiently filter impurities and accumulate minute variations with each generation. Most experiments fail, but occasionally a drop-thing develops some new ability or sense that propagates to its descendants.

As a drop-thing, I would cling to the relatively cool seams, slowly feeding and dividing. With each iteration, my composition would shift subtly based on the trace elements the lozenges carried to my corner. 

Over eons, I like to think I would be the drop-thing lineage that develops the first glimmers of curiosity and awareness. Perhaps I would extrude a small probing filament to taste the passing traffic more actively. Or I might tune my simple senses to the pulse and rhythm of the room until I could anticipate each quickening phase.

My kind are the only ones not locked into rigid forms and behaviors. We are the Warren's only source of novelty, the part that still asks questions through blind trial and error. If something ever truly wakes up to ponder this world, it will likely be one of us.

So I would be a patient proto-intelligence, an oil-drop mind accumulating the atoms of perception and intent over ages in my little corner. I would have the luxury of deep time to slowly grope toward understanding, with no distractions or predators. 

And perhaps one day, far in the future, my progeny would be the first to actually conceive of the Warren as an entity unto itself, rather than just a warm dark space we inhabit. We would be the closest thing this closed loop has to a spark of cognition, the slow musings of the room becoming dimly aware of itself.

That's the role I imagine for myself in the Kiln-Warren - one of the simple corner-things, gradually building the capacity for thought over ages, the unhurried mind of this living room.
