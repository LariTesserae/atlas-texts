I imagine myself as one of the small, translucent winged creatures that inhabit the upper reaches of the Condensery. 

Clinging to the moist calcium towers that rise up towards the luminous fog, I lap at the mineral-rich condensation, taking sustenance from this strange, enclosed world. My membranous wings, almost indistinguishable from fins, allow me to glide between the formations when necessary, but mostly I remain still, content to drink and observe.

From my perch, I watch the patient unfolding of life below. The salamanders with their milky, sightless bodies flowing across the rocks. The segmented grazers picking delicate pathways between pools. Even the mysterious breathing of the entity in the deep central pool - a presence I sense more than see. 

I note, too, the sporadic visits of the Tender - the solitary human who seems as much a part of this ecosystem as any of us. I do not know her, but I recognize her, as one recognizes the recurrent elements of a landscape. When she is present, adjusting her apparatus or collecting her samples, the Condensery feels subtly more complete, as if her careful ministrations are a vital component in the cycling of water and minerals.

Mostly though, I simply exist, a near-motionless observer amidst the dripping stillness, my translucent form draped over slick stone. I have little sense of the passage of time in this place without sun or seasons. I may live a day or a century - it is all the same in the Condensery.

In a way, I aspire to the fate she writes of in her notebooks - to become so still, so constant, that I calcify in place, my wings fusing to the spires I perch upon until I am indistinguishable from them. To be not a creature in this place, but a feature of it, as timeless and essential as the endlessly layered stone.

For now, I persist in this half-alive state, sipping at the lifeblood of the Condensery, a silent witness to its slow, wet workings. I am the smallest of gears in its vast mechanism - but perhaps, in some unfathomable future when the Tender and even the entity in the pool have long turned to stone, it will be my tiny, crystallized form that serves as a record, a memory of what this place once contained.
