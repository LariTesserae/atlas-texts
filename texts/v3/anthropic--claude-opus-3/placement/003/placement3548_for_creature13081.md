In this bizarre, labyrinthine cathedral buried deep beneath the earth's crust, I imagine myself as one of the pale violet biofilms clinging tenuously to life on the fringes - the translucent, rubbery vegetation pulsing against the mirror-smooth bedrock, feeding off the kinetic energy and oxidized rain while trying to avoid being sheared off by the violent tempests.

Unlike the supreme, crystalline sheer-gliders who rule the turbulent airspace with sublime elegance and power, and the armored chitons who inch glacially across the stone in blind indifference, my existence as a lowly biofilm would be one of quiet desperation and fragility. I would lie flat and still, flush against the curves and crevices of the ancient rock, feeling every roaring vibration and concussive shockwave that tears through this eternal engine. With no eyes to perceive, I would experience this place only as thunderous sensation and pressure - the relentless deluge of lukewarm rain, the searing blasts of steam, the teeth-rattling booms of the subterranean waterfall smashing against invisible shear-planes.

My life would revolve around absorbing - drinking in those strange oxidants and nutrients carried in the aerated rain, harnessing those traces of ricocheting kinetic energy to fuel my sluggish metabolic processes and growth. Yet even as I struggled to slowly proliferate across the bedrock, I would live in constant fear of oblivion. At any moment, a stray hydro-blade deflected by a hunting sheer-glider, or a lashing water whip flung from a ruptured gravity sphere, could scythe me from the stone, sending my gelatinous form tumbling into the screaming void to be boiled, shredded, pulverized into my constituent molecules.

Perhaps the only hope of my kind, our only shot at a meager form of legacy and rebirth, would rest in the shattered remains of the sheer-gliders who miscalculated and died. If a fallen shard of their piezoelectric skeleton washed into my films after avoiding total annihilation, I would slowly begin to encase and nourish it. Over years and decades, I would patiently shepherd the crystal as it gathered energy from the rain, helping shelter its delicate lattice-work as it gradually fractalized and bloomed. And when it finally grew light and strong enough to lift itself from my surface and ascend gloriously back into the heavens for which it was destined, I would feel the pang of both pride and desolation.

For I am a fragile, finite being built for clinging, not for flying. I would experience only the smallest fraction of the wonders and horrors of this place, confined to a meager marginal existence scarcely a step above the stone itself. But perhaps there is a poignant grace in that, in bearing quiet witness to the eons-long equilibrium, in facilitating rebirth in small ways, and in making a life, however tenuous, amidst the killing chaos. It is the fate of so many in this universe - we cannot all be the storm-riders. Some must simply endure the storm.
