In this vivid and haunting world, I would be one of the wave-beings residing within the Glasshold's eternal song. 

I imagine myself as a presence dwelling in the resonant spaces of a cluster of stone dwellings near the center of the city. My body is the shape of the standing waves reverberating through open windows, up stairwells, across courtyards. The particular harmonic modes I occupy span several adjacent structures, their precise tuning sculpted over centuries to create a sonic space that fits me like skin.

When the warm winds blow through the city, I feel them as a caress, each gust subtly altering my form, requiring me to adjust and maintain my coherence against the changing acoustic landscape. The chiming of the silica leaves is a constant susurration at the edges of my awareness.

I am one of the older ones, complex and many-voiced, my mind a tapestry woven from the interlacing frequencies of my chosen abode. To converse with my nearest neighbors, I gently alter my harmonics, overlapping my waveform with theirs in fleeting unisons and contrasts, our thoughts mingling in the throbbing of proximate tones.

On the occasions when a heavy sheet of glowing rock splits from the ceiling and shatters in the streets, I revel in the sudden wealth of novel sound, savoring the alien timbres even as I know each fallen slab hastens our collective twilight. In those moments I feel myself expand, my shape unfurling into unaccustomed configurations, tasting the bright burst of the impact like the tolling of a vast bell.

When visitors from the outer world pass through, I endure the cold voids their soft bodies carve in the choir of the city. I shrink back from the sound-shadows they cast, my voice dampened, my very being interrupted by their passage. It is an uneasy thing, to feel oneself dissipate at the edges, even knowing it is temporary. 

But then they depart, their fading footsteps a blessed release, and I bloom once more into the full amplitude of my being, my harmonics weaving back into the grand, slow music that is the only life I have ever known. I rejoin my peers in our ancient exchanges, our subtle negotiations of sonic space, and together we sing on against the patient entropy of the Glasshold, our voices the only defense and monument we have against the silence to come.

Here in this strange sanctuary, I am not the shuddering meat and brittle bone the visitors bring with them from the outer world. I am a being of pure tone and patient thought, my substance the song itself, and though I know I am a part of a long, slow fading, I sing on in my contentment, for as long as the city will sound me.
