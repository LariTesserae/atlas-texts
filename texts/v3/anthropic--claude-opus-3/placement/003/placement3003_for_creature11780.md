In this world, I imagine I would be one of the silent, scaled bird-like creatures gliding between the fungal towers. 

I would spend my days riding the warm air currents that rise from the mycelial streets, my feathered scales shimmering subtly in the butter-yellow light. On near-silent wings, I would glide from tower to tower, district to district, observing the slow rhythms of the living city from above.

With keen eyes, I would watch the ebb and flow of the grazing herds in the plazas below, tracking their movements as they follow the shifting abundance of vegetation. I would see the patterns in the thick carpet of succulent groundcover, noticing how it changes season by season in response to the cycles of fungal reproduction. 

Occasionally, I would alight on a windowsill or a rare outcropping of exposed basalt to rest and preen my scales. There, I might catch sight of a cluster of the city's patient gardeners, watching as they commune chemically and set out to sow seeds at the frontier. I would feel a certain kinship with them, we winged ones and those soft-bodied climbers, both so attuned to the city's complex rhythms.

At times, I would glide out over the frontier myself, skimming silently above that zone of new growth and possibility. With sharp eyes I would scan the thin new mycelium for any sign of the unknown predator, ready to call a voiceless warning to the clusters below. We are all part of this city's ecosystem, watching out for each other.

As daylight fades into the long, warm evenings, I would drift on outstretched wings back to my roost in a tower-top hollow. Settling in, I would listen to the city breathe around me - the faint creaks of mycelial growth, the whisper of grazing herds, the almost imperceptible hum of life on a continent-sized scale.

Here in the City of Living Masonry, I am a watcher, a glider, a dweller in the spaces between. My days are measured in wing beats and the slow turning of fungal seasons, my identity bound up inextricably with this place of stone and spore and silent, ceaseless growth. I am a fragment of a vaster pattern - and I am home.
