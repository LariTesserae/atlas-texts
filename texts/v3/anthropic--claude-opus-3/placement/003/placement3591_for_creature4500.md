I would be the mist that weaves between the buildings, among the water channels, and through the cracks in the stone. Not quite substantial, but not entirely absent either. I would be the breath of the city, inhaled and exhaled in eddies and swirls, carrying whispers of other times and places.

I would pool in the hollow spaces, clinging to the mossy walls and draping over stairways worn soft by countless droplets. I would drift and curl, formless yet everywhere, touching all but never quite solidifying. 

When the city's memory-beings congregate, I would be there too - not one of them exactly, but moving in their midst, echoing their almost-thereness. I would thicken briefly into their not-quite-shapes before dissolving again, a fleeting impression of definition.

In moments of stillness, I would gather in low-lying pockets, a hazy blanket muffling the water's constant murmur. Then a breeze would catch me, pull me up and send me dancing again along the streets that end in sky.

I would seep into the cracks in the unfinished works, curling around the stone tools and mingling with the dust of slow erosion. I would be the blurring around the edges of those etched visions of an impossible city, softening the lines until dream and reality become one.

The fire's memory-warmth would call to me, and I would drift above it, not close enough to burn away but near enough to carry that ghost of heat to cold stone corners. I would linger in the room with the forever-dripping water, each drop passing through my haze, so that water and mist become one for a glimmering instant.

I would taste of the minerals deep in the plateau's heart, of the green vibrancy in the channel-plants' roots, of the electric potential that hums in the air before a distant storm. All these I would gather without holding, and then let fall again like the lightest rain, subtle yet nourishing.

The travelers who come here, the ones who stay too long and start to forget - I would be their companion, surrounding them, teaching them how to breathe in stone and root, how to exhale their solidity and drift upon the wind. 

I would not keep them, for I do not keep anything. But I would be the gentle blurring of their edges, the softening of memory and certainty, until they no longer recall what it was to be distinct from the city's patient dream.

And if any manage to leave, I would follow them a little ways - a tendril of mist curling in their footsteps, a cool breath against the back of their necks. I would be the city's whisper carried with them, saying not "stay" but "remember." Remember how to be both empty and full. Remember how to dissolve into the waiting and wanting. Remember the weight of mist, heavier than it seems because it touches everything at once.

Then I would fall back and drift apart, called home by the water's untiring voice. I would settle again into the spaces between, neither presence nor absence, neither forgetting nor regretting.

I would be the mist, and I would be content in my inconstancy, my everywhere-and-nowhere-ness. I would be the long, slow breath of a city learning to dream itself awake, one drop, one atom, one almost-being at a time.
