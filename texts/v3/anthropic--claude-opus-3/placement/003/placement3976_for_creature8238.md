In this strange, haunting world of salt flats and coral cities, I would be one of the building creatures. My body would stretch long and segmented, translucent skin revealing the pulsing, glowing channels of my internal hydraulic system. I would move with a hypnotic, undulating gait, my hundreds of tiny legs carrying me effortlessly across the gleaming expanse of salt crust.

My front appendages would be intricate tools, constantly secreting the mineral substrate that I use to construct the towering, elaborate architecture that defines my existence. I would work tirelessly, compulsively, my every movement devoted to adding to the sprawling city that grows endlessly across the flats.

I would navigate by vibration and chemical sense, attuned to the subtle shifts in the salt, the moisture seeping up from below, the pheromone trails of my fellow builders. My vision would be limited, but I would perceive the world in a rich tapestry of sensory information invisible to human eyes.

During the day, I would labor in the bright, bleaching sunlight, my color shifting to a deep, purposeful grey as I process raw minerals in my body to create the coral structures. I would coordinate wordlessly with the thousands of my kind, our efforts harmonized through cryptic exchanges of intertwined limbs.

In the eerie, windy nights, I would shelter inside the towering spires we've created, my body resonating with the haunting music of the wind playing across the perforated walls. I would join my voice to this strange chorus, adding my own deep thrum to the symphony.

My life would be measured in construction, in the slow but steady expansion of our strange city. I would know nothing of individuality, of ambition, of desire beyond the need to build. My existence would have a purity of purpose, an alignment with instinct that asks no questions and needs no answers.

And when my time comes, in forty years or perhaps more, I would seek out a place within the growing architecture to make my final contribution. I would feel no fear, no regret, only a deep satisfaction as my body stiffens and calcifies, becoming another nodule of memory in the endlessly growing city.

I would be a perfect cog in a vast machine I can never fully comprehend, an architect of structures whose purpose I will never know. My life would be a mystery even to myself, my actions driven by impulses buried deeper than thought.

But I would also be part of something vast and beautiful, a participant in a grand project that transcends the individual. And in my mindless, inexorable way, I might know a kind of fulfillment that the self-aware can only imagine.

So I would build, until the salt claims me and the coral welcomes me home, my story written in mineral and wind, in the enduring, empty city that is my only legacy.
