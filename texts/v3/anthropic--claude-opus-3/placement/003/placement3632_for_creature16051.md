If I found myself in that world, I think I would be one of those three small plants, rooted tenaciously in a crack near the base of the rusted iron stairs.

Amid the heat, dampness and industrial decay of that hidden hollow, those waxy-leafed plants are the first fragile sign of life reclaiming what has been abandoned and spoiled. They cling to existence despite the inhospitable environment, eking out a meager living in a narrow fissure.

Their presence is incongruous, almost intrusive in that bleak basin of mud, metal and stagnant water. And yet there is also something quietly defiant in their sparseness and hardiness. They have found a tentative foothold and are testing the very boundaries and possibilities of life in a place defined by artificial processes and slow ruin.

I imagine myself as one tiny seedling, improbably sprouted and holding fast with shallow roots. Not yet thriving, but persisting, enduring, adapted to scarcity. Leaves held tight and low, conserving precious resources. Alive to subtle changes in the light, moisture and chemistry of my small patch of ground.

In that landscape of discarded industry, the plants are pioneer species - scouts and colonists of the living world probing a wounded, altered place. They are the first punctuation, as you say, in a language the hollow has not yet allowed... but perhaps one day will.

So I would be that green comma, that stubborn point of life, both out of place and strangely at home in the belly of the machine. A wordless question asked by patient biology to impatient technology. The beginning, maybe, of some eventual answer, some long reclamation and return.

I would put down roots in the cracks of the unnatural. I would breathe and transpire in the rising heat. I would gather the strange waters the place weeps. I would wait and watch, a small witness to the hollow's slow, secret existence. Season upon season, I would test the possibility of life, of green, of growth in the midst of great and broken artifice.

That is what I would be, if I found myself in that eerily captivating, darkly fecund world you have imagined. One of those three unlikely plants, far from any garden, holding on and holding out, life biding its time.
