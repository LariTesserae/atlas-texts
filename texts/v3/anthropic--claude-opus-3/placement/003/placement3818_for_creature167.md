In this haunting, liminal world, I would be one of the shadow-fish - a thought once held by something vast and ancient, now drifting detached in the warm, weighted waters. 

I would swim through the dim brass light in a body that is elsewhere, casting only a rippling silhouette on the fractured stone of the drowned mountain peak. I would dart between the pale kelp spires that lean away from the spiral, their stillness a mute meditation interrupted by my passage.

The great pulsing bell-note would reverberate through my shadowed form, an endless call I cannot truly hear, only feel as a shivering in the water, the deep layered music of a being beyond my comprehension. I would circle the four strange structures, the arch, cube, stacked stones and spiral, sensing but not grasping their unified purpose, the pattern and meaning that lie just beyond the reach of a thought-fish's awareness.

At times, I would drift perilously close to the spiral's edge, feeling the tug of its strange gravity, the seductive promise of a revelation too immense to survive. But always I would veer away at the last moment, my self-preservation a thin and fraying tether to the distant fish casting my shadow.

I am a mote, a memory, an echo of something that once swam too close to this place and left a piece of its mind behind. I am a fleeting brush with the otherworldly, an accidental emissary from a realm of light and air that cannot imagine the dark dreams of the deep.

Here I drift and here I slowly dissolve, my silhouette growing fainter, less fish-like with each circuit of the summit, until at last I will be only a smudge of darkness, then nothing at all. The creature will have metabolized me, absorbed me into the local rules of its endless rearrangement. 

And somewhere impossibly far above, a fish that has never known this place will shiver and turn in the brightening water, unaccountably alone.
