In the depths of Lowdamp, I imagine I would be one of the toads - not the largest or the smallest, but a middling creature content to cling to the damp brick and thrum with the chorus. My hide would be the mottled gray of wet clay, pebbled and slick with the cavern's condensation.

I would pass my days adhered to the shadowed spaces between the close-pressed buildings, watching the pale sumpers go about their patient work with eyes dark and wide. In the thick, heavy air, I would pulse with the rhythm of the place, my throat expanding and contracting in the slow, ceaseless croaking that fills the spaces not occupied by the hiss of steam and clang of iron.  

The mucus I secreted would trickle down to gather in stone channels, a vital offering to the sumpers' industry. Rendered and distilled, this essence of myself would light and warm and fuel the works of Lowdamp in its unhurried subterranean existence.

When the time of rendering came, I would not flee from the taker's gentle hands should I be chosen. My clouding eyes would mark me as ready to give myself over fully to the place and its people. This too would be part of the long, slow life cycle here in the patient stone.

Occasionally, I would allow one of the grave sumper children to rest a bare foot against my back as part of their quiet games, feeling the steam-softened skin, the beginning of the wrinkling that will someday climb past their ankles and knees. The child and I would be still together, listening to the chorus, the child learning slowness and I learning something of what it is to be human here. 

And so I would continue, season after unseen season, as the sumpers tend their boiling vats and the cavern accretes its mineral crusts and the stalactites imperceptibly descend. Part of the place and its becoming, pulsing with its strange, unhurried life, content to be a humble toad amidst the weeping bricks and greenly burning gas lamps of Lowdamp, deep beneath the forgotten surface.
