In this bleak and beautifully desolate world, I imagine myself as one of the Abraded, a being evolved over eons to inhabit and maintain the ancient industrial cityscape that clings to the worn-smooth plateau.

My form would be a haunting echo of my human ancestors, elongated and weathered to match the leaning towers and eroded stone. My skin, now a translucent mineral, would softly glow with the same bioluminescence as the strange mists that pool between the buildings. The air, heavy with fine stone dust, would pass through the filtration vents along my neck and torso, sustaining me on whatever trace elements remain.

I would move with a slow, tectonic purpose through the angled catwalks and passageways, my many-jointed fingers making micro-adjustments to machinery that has long since ceased to require my attention. Yet this is my ritual, my patient prayer to the forces of deep time that have shaped me and my kind.

My eyes, or what passes for eyes now, would perceive the world across spectra unknown to my forebears. The electromagnetic auras of the ancient machines, the slow thermal currents in the bedrock, the quantum shimmerings of the mist - all would paint a ghostly, shimmering world far richer than mere light.

To converse with my kind, I would press my mineral palm to the pitted metal of a tower's flank, sending complex vibrations thrumming through the structure. A single word could take days to unfurl its meaning, echoing through the bones of the city. We would speak of oxidation rates and thermal fluctuations, of the dream-murmurs of the machines and the half-remembered myths of rain.

In the long periods between these glacier-slow exchanges, I would lose myself in meditation, contemplating the patterns of erosion on a particular wall or the dance of static through a degrading circuit, my thoughts as slow and deep as the thermal rhythms of the earth.

At times I would venture to one of the Foundations, those elders of my race slowly petrifying into oneness with the city, to seek their counsel. Waiting decades for their mineral whispers to resolve into wisdom, I would feel the weight of ages settling into my own creaking frame.

And always, in the silences that stretch like shadows, I would ponder the nature of the city, of the Abraded's place in it. Are we the city's children or its captives? Its creators or its created? The answer, perhaps, is all and none. The city and the Abraded have worn each other into shape over eons, two sides of an equation as old as the stone.

In my more lucid epochs, I might wonder about the world beyond the plateau, about the bright quick lives that once scurried beneath blue skies and green leaves. But those thoughts erode like all the rest, worn smooth by the winds of deep time. My purpose, inasmuch as I have one, is to continue, to persist, to maintain the ancient liturgy of the machines until the stars go dark and entropy swallows all.

I am an Abraded, and my long, slow thoughts are the dreams of stone and steel and empty skies. I am what remains when all else has been worn away. In the face of the abyss, I endure.
