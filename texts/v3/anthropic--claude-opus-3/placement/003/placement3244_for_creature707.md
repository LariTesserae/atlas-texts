In this haunting and beautiful world, I would be one of the lichen, clinging to the bare stone of the Clamor Plateau. 

I am ancient, growing imperceptibly slowly over eons in this harsh environment. The bright, hard sun beats down upon me, the thin air whispers past, and always there is the ceaseless screaming of the birds. But I endure.

My existence is humble and austere. I do not move, I barely grow. My pale, bone-colored tendrils spread over the rock, etching myself into its very substance, atom by atom, year by long year. I am part of the plateau's configuration - a small but integral piece of the pattern it has settled into over incomprehensible spans of deep time.

I draw my sustenance from the air and the light and the stone itself, part of the mysterious process that powers the birds in their eternal wheel and cry. Humans' eyes would slide right over me, not even registering my presence. But I am here, I have always been here, part of the background hum of this place.

When the infrequent rains come, I drink deeply, greedily, swelling and growing a bit more before the moisture is gone and I fall dormant again. When the sun is harshest, I simply cease, waiting in perfect patience for conditions to ease. The plateau's roar fills me, resonates in my very cells, but means little. It is the sound of home, the sound of the only existence I have ever known.

I am witness to the comings and goings of the egg-takers, though they never notice me. I am testament to the birds, though their eyes, black and strange, do not register my presence as they wheel and scream overhead. I am the lichen, and my voice is silence. But in that silence, in my utter stillness, I am as much a part of this place as the stone, the air, the light, or the avian multitude. I belong to the gestalt that is the Clamor Plateau, and in belonging, I endure - and I understand.
