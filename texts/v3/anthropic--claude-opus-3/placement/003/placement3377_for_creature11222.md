I could imagine myself being one of the fingerprints pressed into the ceramic walls. Not the person who made the print, but the shaped absence itself, the negative space that endures long after its maker has gone.

As a fingerprint, I would be a point of connection between the past shaping and the present inhabitants. The flat ones would trace their crystalline feet over my whorls and ridges. Condensation would gather in my deepest points and drip from my edges. The vibrations from below would resonate in the space where a hand once pressed, transforming me into a tiny acoustic chamber, playing my own small part in the symphonic architecture.

I would be a record, an echo, of the builders who are now inseparable from what they built. My curves and lines would hold the memory of skin and pressure, preserved in the ceramic long after flesh had gone. Each contour would be a lingering touch across time - fingertip to clay to mineral crust to unknown future.

At the same time, I would be a living interface, a border crossing between states. The porous boundary where liquid meets solid, where film colonizes glaze, where impression becomes structure. A permeable membrane in the slow metabolism of the Underhalls.

I would be a point of contact for any warm and curious hand that might find me in the ceramic dark. An invitation to connect, palm to palm, across species and centuries. To feel the hum and shiver of this made world in the making. And in that touch, however brief, I would fulfill my purpose, my quiet work. I would be a bridge, a translator, a channel. Solid and permanent, yet always in relationship with my changing environment. 

Stable in form, dynamic in function. A fixed note and a fluid voice in the long, unfinished song of this place that is still learning how to sing itself into being.
