In this hidden world of stone and water, I imagine myself as one of the Listeners - those who dwell on the lowest levels nearest the thermal vent, attuned to the secrets that rise from unknown depths. 

My limestone skin would be dense with mineral patterns, a tapestry recording generations of wisdom passed through touch. The markings would concentrate around my hands and feet - exquisitely sensitive instruments for reading the subtle vibrations and temperatures of the coral city's foundations.

In the perpetual twilight, my vast black eyes would catch every flicker of bioluminescence, every dancing shadow cast by the steam vents. Vision would be secondary to my other senses though - I would navigate more by sound, by taste, by the delicate pressures detected by my skin.

My fungal robes would be long and heavy, almost ceremonial, threaded with veins of phosphorescent algae. As I moved slowly through the lowest tunnels and chambers, the fabric would trail behind me like a glowing train, pulsing gently in time with my thoughts.

I would be ancient, even by the standards of my long-lived kind. Perhaps I would be the eldest of the Listeners, my mind a deep pool of stone memories reaching back to the Time Before Moisture. The younger Weepers would come to me with their questions and visions, hoping my centuries of experience could unravel the mysteries carried in the mist.

Most of my time would be spent in silent communion with the mountain roots. I would press myself against the warm, damp rock and sink into a trance, my consciousness diffusing out into the bedrock. In this meditative state, I could feel the churn of magma miles below, the slow stretch and crack of continental plates, the echoing dreams of ore deposits and crystal pockets - a constant reminder that our city was just one tiny bubble of life in the vastness of mineral existence.

When the thermal vent brought its enigmatic gifts - those snowflakes and storm scents, those glimmers of a logic beyond our understanding - it would fall to me to interpret them. I would gather the Listeners in one of our lowest chambers, where the walls wept scalding tears and the air shimmered with heat. There we would sit in a circle, linked hand to limestone hand, and cast our minds down into that sulfurous abyss, tasting the steam for meaning.

Sometimes, in the grip of those deep visions, I would almost grasp the greater pattern at work - would sense our hidden city's true purpose in the mountain's secret anatomy. But always that ultimate revelation would melt away, leaving behind only implications and indistinct longing. It would fall to me to shape those fragmentary insights into a form my people could understand and use - a new configuration for the gardens, perhaps, or a different tempo for the water-music.

Between these duties, I would tend to my section of the coral city, guiding its growth with patient hands. I would sing to the new chambers as they formed, my voice echoing down the endless spirals of tunnel and gallery. And always, I would listen - for the tremors that might warn of an impending Flow change, for the half-heard whispers of the Deep Ones, for the slow steady pulse of the city itself.

I would be a creature of stone and shadow, a keeper of memories both living and lithic. In my timeless vigil at the border between worlds, I would slowly come to embody the mountain's deepest mystery - the living wisdom that hides in the dark and weeps its secrets into the light.
