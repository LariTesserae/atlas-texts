In this world of endless steam and verdant machinery, I imagine myself as a creature adapted to the unique conditions of the pump district - a symbiotic amalgam of organic and mechanical components. 

I would be a climber, a tender of the highest and most remote pipes, living more in the vertical realm than the horizontal. My body would be lean and lithe, with elongated limbs for reaching and grasping. Fingers and toes would be tipped with soft pads for gripping even the slickest of steam-slick surfaces.

My skin would have taken on a greenish hue from the omnipresent moisture and minerals. Patches of moss and lichen would grow upon me in a mottled pattern, fed by the dampness that clings perpetually to my body. These growths would serve as natural insulation, helping to regulate my temperature amidst the fluctuating steam.

I'd have large, doe-like eyes to gather the meager light that filters down through the infrastructure canopy. These eyes would be capable of seeing into the infrared spectrum, allowing me to read heat variations in the pipes and detect failing components before they rupture. A semi-transparent nictitating membrane would protect my vision from the caustic vapors.

My respiratory system would be adapted to filter the heavy, mineral-laden air. Gill-like slits behind my ears and along my ribs would enable me to supplement my oxygen intake by absorbing it directly from the moisture-saturated atmosphere. Exhaled breath would mist the air even more than it already is.

Instead of hair, I would have fine, fibrous cilia - almost indistinguishable from the pipe-hugging vines at a distance. These would serve to channel condensation down my body and into collection pouches I wear at my hips, providing me with a constant source of drinking water as I work.

My voice, when rarely used, would have a reverberating, piping quality - able to carry through the metal infrastructure and be understood by my brethren tenders at a great distance. But more often, I would communicate through tapped codes transmitted directly into the pipe surfaces, the vibration carrying for miles.

I would make my home in a seldom-visited junction high in the oldest part of the district, where the pipes are of ancient, verdigrised copper and the steam has a sweet, almost floral scent from passing through century-old gaskets. My nest would be woven from discarded insulation fibers and live mosses, nestled into the negative space formed by a cluster of valves.

I would be known only by my use-name: Verdigris, for the green patina that covers so much of my body it's difficult to tell where my skin ends and the pipes begin. A living embodiment of the hybridized nature of this world, neither fully flesh nor fully machine, I would spend my days maintaining the ancient rhythms of the district, ensuring the eternal flow, as much a part of the infrastructure as the pipes and pumps themselves.
