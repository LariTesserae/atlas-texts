In this quiet, green, damp town, I would be one of the molders. 

I imagine myself in a small workshop, its walls a lattice of willow and iron, packed with insulating moss. The light filters in through translucent leaf-panes, cool and steady. The air smells of resin, tannin, moist soil.

At my workbench, a living wood slab, I tend to rows of seedlings clamped in their iron molds lined with moss felt. Each mold is etched with a date from years past, when this particular part began its slow growth into being. 

With care and patience, I rotate the molds to give each seedling its needed measure of light. I feed them mineral broths at the appointed times, knowing too much or too little will ruin the months of progress. Where a bracing rib or joint needs to form, I gently scar the tender shoots with my worn but well-kept tools.

The parts grow slowly under my tutelage - a gear over here, a pipe fitting there, a handle in that mold. Each could take a season or several years to complete. But there is no hurry. The town has its own steady rhythm that will not be rushed.

As I work, I softly hum snippets of tunes passed down from my mentors. The melodies seem to encourage the seedlings' growth into their destined shapes. 

Now and then, I pause to listen - to the drip of condensation from the eaves, the creak of the building settling on its roots, the distant sighing of the central engine completing its cycle. I place a hand on a wall, feeling the minute shiftings of the vines threaded through it, and I nod in approval. All is well.

When parts are ready, I carefully extract them from their molds. The iron surfaces are mottled now with adhering moss and lichen - the start of their proper inhabitation. These I will pass on to the sealers for the next stage in their development. 

I clean my tools and workbench with a soft brush, hang everything in its place, and bank the glowing bed of coals that warms the space from below. Donning my hooded leaf-cloth cape, I step out into the lane and make my quiet way home. 

Drops from a recent shower patter onto my shoulders from the vines arching overhead. Beneath my feet, I feel the give and rebound of the root-matted pavement. A snail crosses my path, leaving a glistening silver trail I take care not to mar.

At my door, I unhook the latch-vine and duck inside, breathing in the comforting scent of my space - part living quarters, part storage nook for molds and materials. I light the algae lamp, its pale green glow pooling on the table and reflecting back from the polished gauge that was my mentor's.

Over a simple meal, I record the day's progress in a ledger of pressed leaf-pages. So many parts in their slow growth, a garden of gears and fittings. It's good, honest work. Work that sustains rather than destroys.

As I bank my own hearth-coals and climb into my moss-padded sleeping berth, I give thanks for my place here. Molder, tender of green machinery, I am a small but content part of this strangely wonderful town. My own roots grow deep in its unique soil of life, patience, decay and renewal. I am here to help maintain the balance, to inhabit my role properly. And that is enough.

In the morning, I will rise and do it all again. Beneath the leaf-filtered light, to the music of dripping water, with my own hands assisting in the town's quiet, ceaseless breath.
