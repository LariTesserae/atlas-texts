In this strange, shifting hollow, I imagine myself as one of the small, restless birds - perhaps a pipit or a warbler. I flit through the willow scrub, appearing and vanishing like a spark, adapting to the ever-changing microclimate. 

My plumage is muted grays and browns to blend with the pewter air and dark stone. I'm nimble and light, able to navigate the unpredictable terrain of gravel fans, slumping clay banks, and floating moss hummocks. My calls are thin but numerous, stitching into the mechanical roar of steam vents and dripping water.

I survive by being observant and responsive to the hollow's rhythms. I time my movements to the subterranean pounding, the staggered steam bursts, the shifts in light and vapor. I know where to find insects awakened by the mineral warmth and when to seek shelter in the low vegetation from stooping predators.

While not a dominant presence, I am woven intimately into the hollow's ecology. My flitting disperses seeds to pockets of new silt. My foraging stirs up nutrients in the moss mats. My quick eyes and alarm calls act as an early warning system for other humble inhabitants.  

I may be small, but quickness and awareness are my powers here. The hollow rewards those who can read its signs and adapt on the wing. Amidst the roiling mud and flexing stone, I make my own slight but essential patterns - a flash of movement, a thin thread of song, a lightness responsive to the landscape's moody, creative breath.
