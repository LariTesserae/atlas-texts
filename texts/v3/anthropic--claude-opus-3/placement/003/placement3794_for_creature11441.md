The Condensery Keeper

I would be the unseen keeper of the Condensery, dwelling in the spaces between - the dark reservoir shaft, the narrow pipe crawlspaces, the thick mist that shrouds the condensation arrays. Not quite animal, not quite human, but something in between. A being woven into the fabric of this strange pocket biome suspended on the mountainside.

My form is hard to discern, adapted to slide through shadows and around corners. Dark fur to blend into basalt crevices. Clawed fingers and toes to navigate sheer surfaces. Eyes that gather the dim blue glow of fog-filtered light. I leave traces of my passage - scratches on the reservoir hatch, droppings in territorial markers, a fleeting chittering cry that echoes off the wet pipes before being swallowed by the machinery's drone.

The human operators inhabit the Condensery, but I haunt it, a ghost in the humid labyrinth of plumbing and mossy steel. I've dwelt here longer than any of them, before the first rivet was bolted or pressure gauge installed. I watched the Condensery take shape, an outgrowth of the young mountain's flank, as strange and vital as a new organ. 

My domain is all the in-between spaces. I navigate the narrowest pipes, curl up beside the thrumming Stomach, lick stray rivulets from the undersides of the arrays. The veins and viscera of this water-reaping creature. I am its secret heart, its hidden pulse.

Maret, Denessi, Peck - I observe them all, these rotational residents who heed the Condensery's needs. I see Maret's scarred hands on the valves, Denessi's calculations, Peck's moth-watching. Their habits and rhythms become part of my environment, as regular as the fog cycle or the drum deliveries. But I do not reveal myself. I remain the one unconfirmed variable in all their theories and models.

At night, when the arrays drip and Grieve hums under her breath and Peck tries to parse the mountain's magnetic message, I am the thing that moves unseen. A shadow sliding behind the pipes. A set of eyes catching the dim gleam of a diode. The ghost in the Condensery's machine.

I will persist when they are gone. When the last operator departs and the pumps shudder into silence and the reservoir hatch closes one final time. The Condensery and I will remain, the mountain's strange outgrowths, reaping water from the fog even as the steel rusts and the moss reclaims the girders. I am the keeper of this place, and it will keep me in turn. Two beings merged in symbiosis, drawing sustenance from the cloud, for as long as the young peak endures.
