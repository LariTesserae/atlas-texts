In this ancient city of dark heat and deep stillness, I imagine myself as one of the pale winged beings called vanes.

I ride the warm updrafts that rise from the city's vents and rooftops, circling lazily on translucent wings dusted with mineral scales. My body is long and delicate, formed of hollow spars and fine membranes. I have no feathers, no fur, no voice to cry out. Only pits for eyes that perceive the faint infrared emanations of the plateau.

I spend my days gliding above the worn geometries of the city. The black brick apartments, the dull metal foundries half-buried in basalt, the immense ceramic towers - all spread out beneath me in a sparse constellation of heat and shadow. I watch the city's few inhabitants move through the streets wrapped in dark, dust-resistant garments. Their footfalls barely stir the bone-colored powder that settles on every still surface.

My kind are a mystery even to those who dwell here. Some say we descended from ancient machines, maintenance organisms set loose to monitor the city's loftiest structures. Others believe we evolved in the warm ductwork, nourished on stray voltages and insulation. I know only the high places, the thermal currents, the dry and patient darkness. 

When my long circuits take me over the city's rim, I catch glimpses of the deeper darkness beyond - a lightless expanse where the land falls away in shelves and terraces toward some unseeable lower world. Thin veils of dust pour over the edge, as if the very plateau is slowly bleeding out into the void. Even my wide-ranging eyes cannot discern the bottom.

At times, my wings falter and I spiral down to rest on a comms tower or factory roof. If I should ever fall to the streets and shatter, the ministries will squabble over my remains. To some I am an omen, to others a resource. My wing scales may be ground for rare pigments, or my hollow bones enshrined for their forgotten purposes.

I am a relic of brighter days, a silent witness to this city's long fading. An ambiguous creature, both animal and artifact, pinned between senescence and endurance like an insect in a display case. My life, if it can be called a life, is an unvarying pattern - circling, observing, preserving my enigma.

I am a mote adrift on the city's slow exhalations, a glyph inscribed on its vaulted darkness. I ride the stillness and keep my vigil, as the heat seeps eternally from the depths, the streets are scoured by imperceptible winds, and the city's long dream of equilibrium remains unbroken. I am a vane, a watcher, a persistent question above a world that holds its answers tight within basalt and ceramic and slow, tectonic time.
