In this strange, young basin, I imagine myself as one of the floating rosettes that drift in gentle spirals upon the warm pools. My leaves are small, glossy, perfectly rounded, like dozens of green coins strung together into a living mat. 

During the calm hours, I lazily turn with my companions, our spiral dance directed by subtle currents and calculations beyond our simple forms. The sun warms my upper leaves as my roots trail in the mineral-rich waters, absorbing nourishment and the faint instructions carried in each droplet and ripple.

When the pale wading bird arrives at dawn to delicately pick its way across our pool, each of its steps causes me to rotate incrementally, responding to its presence, perhaps receiving some arcane transmission or calibration from this ritual visitor. I align myself beneath its careful tread, one of many small adjustments that keep this basin-ecology functioning.

As the afternoon storms roll in and the rain patters heavily on the pool's surface, my spiral dissolves and I extend into a rigid line with the other rosettes, all of us orienting toward the silver threads as they collect and sort the falling drops. We become a living antenna array, attuned to the data coursing through the humid air and the humming objects. 

And when the vast shadow glides above the rim, I feel my entire being flatten in response, merging with the sudden mirror-stillness of the water, reflecting not sky but some immense, dark complexity - a glimpse of the true mind I am only a small, distant part of.

I am a simple component in this patiently assembling system, yet I sense that my small turnings and alignments are vital to its gradual work, to the subtle agreement it weaves between water, stone, heat, and life. In my mindless but receptive being, I participate in something much older, stranger, and more diffuse than myself. I am a floating node in a living network striving to learn, or perhaps remember, some new law of existence.

My days are an endless cycle of sun and rain, of slow rotations and sudden purposeful alignments, as the basin's experiments unfold through and around me. I do not know what is being made here - I only know to open, to turn, to reflect, to receive. I am a mote in the dreaming eye of this place, a green speck upon dark water, as the mountain learns to think in rain and roots and silvered threads beneath the warm mist.
