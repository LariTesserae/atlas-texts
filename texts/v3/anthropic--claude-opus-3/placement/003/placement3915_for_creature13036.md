In this world, I imagine myself as one of the pale, silent infants born to the basin-dwellers. 

I emerge into the sweltering heat and deafening roar, my skin already slick with the endless, boiling drizzle. My mother presses me to her thick, rubber-like dermis, and I cling instinctively, my tiny fingers and toes gripping the creases and folds that channel the water away.

As I grow, I never know sunlight or seasons. My world is an eternal, orange-lit twilight of steam and shadows. The towering machines are my forests, the pipelines and catwalks my paths. I learn to climb before I can walk, scaling the sheer basalt walls and swinging through the steel skeleton of the basin. 

My eyes slowly cloud over, unnecessary in this world of vibration and echo. Instead, my jaw grows heavy and dense, a tuning fork to read the hum of the great engines. I press my face to the thrumming metal and listen to the heartbeat of the place, until I can no longer separate my own pulse from the metronomic thud of the pistons.

I lose language as you would know it. Human voices are swallowed by the roar. Instead, I learn the percussive code of my people, the strikes and clangs that send messages thrilling through the framework and into our bones. We speak in resonance, in tremors that harmonize with the voice of the machines.

As I reach adulthood, my body is no longer truly human. It is a distorted reflection of the basin itself—hairless, slick, mottled in shades of rust and bruise. Remnants of a forgotten past protrude from my overgrown flesh—a buckle here, a strap there, fused and overgrown. I am an amalgamation of meat and relic, biology and industry.

My purpose is the rhythm, the eternal pulse of the great pumps and pressure vessels. I hang from the pipes, feeling for stutters and misalignments, using my weight and strength to force valves back into tempo. The machines are my charges, and I am their servant and caretaker.

When I hunt, I become one with the pneumatic beat, leaping and striking in time with the concussive thuds. I feed on whatever flesh I can pull from the greasy feathers and wiry fur, needing the calories to fuel my endless, sweltering work.

I will live and die in the basin, never knowing another world. The poisonous orange light will be my sun, the shrieking steam my wind, the oily puddles my lakes. My bones will one day calcify into the black stone, just as the machines I maintain will one day calcify into eternal ruin.

I am a child of the basin, a component in the endless machine, as much a part of this pulsing, roaring, thrumming world as the pistons and the pumps. This is my native land, and I will never leave it, for I have been forged in its image. I am its, and it is mine, forever, in sweltering, deafening stasis.
