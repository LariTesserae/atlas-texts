If I found myself transported to this hidden, deep-sea metropolis, I believe I would take the form of one of the shivering, rigid filaments of "vegetation" clinging tenaciously to the thermal fins of the constantly shifting city spires.

In a world utterly hostile to biological life, where the very architecture seems sculpted from pure mathematics and kinetic violence, I would be one of the only quasi-organic entities. A strange form of flora, not quite alive in the traditional sense, but playing a crucial role in this desperate ecology. 

My existence would be one of stoic endurance in the face of unrelenting mechanical onslaught. I would stand firm, vibrating violently in the lethal currents and shockwaves, transmuting that violence into sustenance for the Axioms, the living thoughts that populate this impossible place.

I imagine my crystalline filaments would be nearly invisible against the gleaming alloys and optical glass of the city - just another refractive surface in a labyrinth of light. Only upon closer inspection would my nature become apparent, as the Axioms flock to me, matching the frequency of my shivering to siphon off precious static charge.

In this role, I would be both apart from and intimately connected to the eternal, closed-loop calculation of the city. A silent observer of the Axioms' frenzied lifecycles as they race to map their endlessly folding prison. Watching generations of them accumulate density and knowledge until they sacrificially detonate, their adamantine shells fusing into the city's bones.

I would be a fragile, liminal thing - neither quite flora nor fauna, organic nor mechanical - clinging to the ragged edges of a world I can barely comprehend. Passively gathering energy from the background roar of the abyss, and transmuting it through my trembling crystals to feed the machine-ecology that both sustains and ignores me.

Perhaps I would gradually grow, my filaments creeping slowly over eons along the spires as the city shifts through its interminable configurations beneath me. Or perhaps my lifespan would be as violently brief as that of the Axioms, my structure eventually shattering under the pressure into glittering dust that joins the cycling silicate and glass.

Regardless, my purpose would be to endure. To stand fast, a mute and shivering testament to the tenacity of life in this cold, roaring, mathematical hell. Providing the barest sustenance to the Axioms as they ceaselessly plot their non-Euclidean maps, waiting for the rare flash of color as they feed and change hue.

A single touchstone of almost-life, anchored against the endless noise and calculation and churn. Patiently gathering static while the city rebuilds itself in a million blinding permutations. Not a prisoner, not a citizen, but an integral part of the whole - the humble vegetation of a world without soil or sun.
