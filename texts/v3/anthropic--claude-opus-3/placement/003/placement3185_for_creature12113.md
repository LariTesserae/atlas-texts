In this world of the Grown Plateau, I would be a visitor who lingers, gradually transitioning into a Keeper role.

At first, I would arrive with the typical haste and urgency of an outsider, marveling at the strange architecture of fused vegetation, the eerie consistency of the filtered light, the signs of life and tending by the near-invisible Keepers. I would explore the moss-boulevards and cathedral-like nodes with a sense of wonder, but also a restless need to comprehend it all quickly.

As days stretch into weeks, however, the plateau's pace would begin to work on me. The silence, the stillness, the sense of time stretched to breaking - it would unsettle me, then fascinate me, then slowly seduce me. I would find myself pausing longer in the plazas, watching for the subtle movements of the Keepers, straining to decipher the patterns of the leaf-birds. 

At some point, a Keeper would approach me with their gradual, geologic stride. Over the course of a day, they would cross the distance to ask me that question: "Why hurry?" And I would find myself without an answer, realizing that my reasons for hurrying have evaporated in the face of this place's timeless patience.

So I would stay. I would match my tempo to the plateau's. I would eat its fruit, drink from its fountains, sleep in the chambers it has grown. Over years, my restlessness would drain away, replaced by a profound contentment in slowness. My skin would mottle to match the bark; my gestures would stretch to span hours.

I would become a tender of this living landscape - guiding its growth at the edges, clearing its waterways, communing with my fellow Keepers in our gradual language of sighs and stillnesses. The memories of my former haste would crystallize into a sort of bittersweet pity, as I polished each one in my mind over the course of decades.

In the end, I would simply be another rhythm in the plateau's slow symphony - a being who has found a different kind of fullness in the patient unfolding of centuries, content in tending and being tended to by a world that grows itself. A Keeper, who has forgotten urgency in the face of a life so vast it has space for every moment to unfurl itself completely before giving way to the next.
