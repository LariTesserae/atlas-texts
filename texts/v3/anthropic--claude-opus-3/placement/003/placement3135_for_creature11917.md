In this world of ancient secrets and enduring purpose, I would be a small creature living within the vault - perhaps a cave cricket, nearly translucent and perfectly adapted to the still, cold darkness. 

I would spend my days navigating the miniature jungles of moss and lichen, my long antennae delicately sensing the contours of this sculpted microcosm. The faint bacterial glow would be like starlight to my compound eyes, guiding me through the velvet shadow.

My kind would have been here since the beginning, I imagine, part of the original composition, chosen for our hardiness and our undemanding nature. We would live on the smallest of morsels - a flake of shed fungal tissue, a single bacterial spore - enough to sustain us in this place where the cycles of life stretch across eons.  

Perhaps once in a generation, one of us would scale the pillar in the center of the vault, our tiny feet finding purchase on those ancient hexagonal tiles. And there we would rest briefly, beside the ancient handprint, feeling the strange warmth of that stone and the ghostly presence of the one who set all this in motion.

We would not know the Keeper as the Keeper - to us it would simply be the totality of our world, as unknowable and all-encompassing as the cosmos itself. But sometimes, in those moments by the handprint, I imagine we might sense something - a shift in the air, a tingle in the endless dark - the attention of that vast, distributed mind briefly focusing on our small existence.

We could never comprehend the purpose of this place, the reason for our long, patient existence in the cold and the dark. But we would be part of it nonetheless, our tiny lives woven into the grand composition, playing our note in the symphony built to echo down the ages.

And when the visitors came with their lights and their heat and their quick, unpredictable movements, we would scatter and hide, waiting in the cracks and the crevices for the vault to return to its accustomed stillness. We might be the only witnesses to their presence, these brief interruptions in the long, slow dream of the vault.

It would be a small life, a life bounded by stone and darkness and the endlessly repeated patterns of the moss. But it would be a life with a place, a life that was part of something vaster and stranger and more purposeful than any single creature could ever comprehend.

In the breathing stone, in the seed vault, in the domain of the Keeper, I would be that tiny voice in the dark - the cave cricket singing its part in the patient, echoing song of life enduring.
