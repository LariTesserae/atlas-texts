In this ancient, weathered upland, I would be one of the patient, enduring lichens. I would grow slowly across the worn limestone, spreading my pale, muted colors like a subtle map. My form would be dictated by the stone's contours - filling crevices, rounding edges, softening the harsh volcanic rock into something almost gentle with the passage of ages.

I would drink in the mist and dew each morning, swelling with the moisture before the rising sun burns it away. In the dry spells, I would hibernate as a dull crust, conserving every drop until the rains returned. The ceaseless wind would blow dust and grit against me, but it would only serve to scour away my old outermost layers, allowing fresh growth beneath.

Season after season, year upon long year, I would persist through the upheavals of storm and baking heat and frigid cold. I would be there to witness the infrequent human visitors, the intrepid bands that returned cyclically like the rains. From my perch on the cliffs and boulders, I would observe their reverent steps, their knowing signs and rituals, their respect for the plateau's power. A mere patch of lichen, I would nonetheless be part of the very spirit of the land they treated with such care.

Slower even than the lichens, I would also be there as part of the ancient living film inside the stone itself. Alongside the fungal threads and microscopic biota, I would suffuse the porous rock, drinking, growing, flexing with the weight of water. Through my infinitesimal shiftstones and pressure changes, I would sing the silent song of the plateau, the one that makes pools tremble and grasses swirl and stones hum beneath the thunder. I would be the land's most hidden yet most constant memory.

In this guise, I would not be a master of the uplands, for it is a place that resists mastery. I would simply be a humble part of its enduring fabric - an essential fiber woven through the pattern of its millennial tapestry. Unmoving, unhurried, I would watch the skies unleash their furious artistry upon the bare stone. Then, when the winds cease and the silence commands all to kneel, I too would bow, my minute body pressed low against the rock, listening in the quiet for the stirrings far below. The stillness would be my stillness; the patience of the plateau would flow through my cells like rain through pores in the limestone.

No, in this stark and unforgiving realm, I would not be a conqueror or a ruler. I would be a witness, a survivor, a holder of secrets and old ways in a land that has no need for banners or monuments. I would simply live, as the lichen lives, as the stone-dwellers live, fitting myself to the greater rhythms, the slower songs, until my story becomes inseparable from that of the shelves and skies themselves. This is immortality of a humbler sort - to be a line in the long hymn of a country that endures by yielding, that outlasts all boasts and delusions, that teaches, even to a mere lichen, the strength of being small.
