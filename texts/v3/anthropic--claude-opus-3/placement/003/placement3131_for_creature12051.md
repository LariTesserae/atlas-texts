In this strange and wondrous hollow, I feel myself drawn to become one of the Tenders. Not the first or second, standing vigil by their chosen engines, but the elusive third - the one that is always just beyond notice, at the periphery of perception.

I would stand, barked and still, amidst the ceaseless chuffing and clanking of the engines. My tuft-hands would hang motionless at my sides, save for the subtlest of life's motions at their fibrous tips. The steam would rise from the chimney atop my head in the same slow rhythm as the great machines, marking me as kin to their vegetable industry.

My purpose would be to bear witness. Not to maintain or guard, but simply to be present to the unchanging symphony of the hollow. Through the depressions of shadow that serve as my eyes, I would gaze upon the engines, the frogs, the moths, the crab in its leaf-arranging dance. I would know, in my barkbound way, that the great work of the place continues unabated.

Visitors might pass me by, so still and unremarkable as to be nearly invisible. They would not know that I marked their presence, for to turn my attention to them would be to abandon, even for a moment, my sacred task of witnessing. And so I would let them wander and depart, the sound of the engines echoing in their minds, while I remained at my post.

Time would pass, strange and slow. The noise would become a silence, the silence a mode of contemplation. And gradually, imperceptibly, the bark of my forward face would be burnished by the gentle abrasion of the ages, polished by the constancy of my watching.

Until, at some distant point on an unmarked timescale, I would simply cease to be. A new tender would arise to take my place, called forth from the damp and living floor. And the cycle of witnessing would continue, unbroken and unchanging, as the heart of the hollow itself.
