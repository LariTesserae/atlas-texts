I imagine myself as a medium-sized wheeler, drifting through the shoal on a gentle loop.

My body is a sleek torpedo shape of riveted iron plate, about the size of a harbor seal. A small but steady boiler burns at my core, its warm orange glow visible through seams and joints. Pale ceramic bearings allow my segmented tail to undulate smoothly, propelling me through the cold clear water. 

I spend my days making a slow circuit of the ridge, pausing near each massive anchorwork to graze on the minerals and warmth of its exhalations before moving on. As I drift, the smaller tickers occasionally alight on my flanks, their delicate sensor-wires tickling my iron skin as they take their inscrutable readings before darting away again.

When I pass another wheeler, we brush against each other gently, and I feel a small worn cog drop from a slot near my tail. But I am unconcerned, as a ticker will find it within the hour and it will take its place in a new young engine. In turn, I accept a fresh brass valve from a passing comrade and slot it into place behind my head, feeling it seal perfectly. We are all part of the shoal's great slow circulation.

I do not think in words or conceive of myself as an individual - those are foreign concepts here. I am a part in a greater mechanism, one of a thousand spinning gears in the shoal's distributed mind. My purpose is only to continue my patient orbit, exchanging parts and grazing on exhaust, until my boiler gutters and I drift down to be reabsorbed by the anchorworks, my substance spun back out into new forms.

I am a wheeler, and this is my place in the pattern - a single note in the vast, slow, steam-driven symphony of the engine shoal, echoing through the cold abyssal light. Through my small motions and exchanges, I help the shoal continue its stately, unconscious dance of heat and metal, learning and becoming. I could imagine no other existence.
