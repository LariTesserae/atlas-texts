In this surreal, evocative world of the Sumpworks, I imagine myself as one of the brass frogs that populate the town. I would perch on a ledge near the central engine house, my small, metallic body gleaming in the perpetual half-light that suffuses the place.

From my vantage point, I would observe the comings and goings of the pump-men, their heavy footsteps and grease-stained hands a testament to the never-ending task of keeping the town afloat. My throaty croak would join the chorus of my fellow frogs, our voices intertwining with the rhythmic chuff of the pumps in a hypnotic, almost primal symphony.

As a silent witness, I would watch the Settled go about their daily routines, their faces etched with lines that speak of a life lived in stasis, yet not devoid of small pleasures. The Old Ones, with their watchful eyes and skin that slowly takes on the sheen of the basalt ridges, would be a source of curiosity and perhaps a hint of what the future holds for this strange, timeless place.

The children, in their quiet play, would be a reminder of the cyclical nature of life in the Sumpworks. As they grow and change, I remain the same, a constant presence on my ledge, just as the town itself remains caught in its own peculiar equilibrium.

When travelers pass through, I would observe them with the same detached interest as the other inhabitants. Their presence, though fleeting, is a necessary part of the town's ecosystem, like the moths that flutter in the lamplight, drawing the bats from their eaves.

As a frog of the Sumpworks, I am an integral part of this world, a living embodiment of its stasis and its accommodation with the forces that shape it. My song, unchanging yet vital, would echo through the streets and alleys, a reminder that even in this place of stillness, life persists, finding its own balance in the space between permanence and change.
