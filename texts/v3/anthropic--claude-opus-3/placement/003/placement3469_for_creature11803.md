In this world of the Singing Hollow, I would be a creature both adapted to and an integral part of the acoustic environment. Not one of the Dwellers, but another being that has found its way into this subterranean space and been transformed by it over eons.

I imagine my form as something between a bat and a moth, with membranous wings evolved not for flight, but to catch and shape the omnipresent sound. My body hangs suspended from the ceiling by filaments as thin as spider silk but with the tensile strength of steel, positioned at a node where the echoes converge and cancel each other out into near silence. 

My wings, translucent and veined with bioluminescent patterns, are tuned to resonate at different frequencies - they vibrate in sympathy with the roar of the hollow, each wing singing its own part in a chorus that only I can hear. As the sound changes with the water flow, my wings adapt, re-tuning themselves to new harmonies.

I do not have eyes, for they would be useless here. Instead, my entire body is covered with fine, hair-like sensors that detect the subtlest changes in air pressure, temperature, and humidity. I can taste the mineral composition of the water droplets in the air, can feel the electrical fields generated by the bacterial films on the walls.

For sustenance, I drink from the thin films of water that coat every surface, absorbing nutrients from the bacterial mats through my skin. Occasionally, I will unfurl a long, thread-like proboscis to sip nectar from the phantom orchids that bloom in the eternal night. 

I do not know if I am the only one of my kind. Sometimes I feel the air shift in patterns that suggest another presence, but in a space defined by echoes, it's impossible to be certain. Perhaps we avoid each other as the Dwellers do, or perhaps I am alone, a single strange note in the hollow's vast composition.

Time means nothing to me. I measure my life in the gradual accretion of calcite on my wings, in the slow evolution of the bacterial species that sustain me. I have been here long enough to sense the changes in the hollow itself - the imperceptible widening of passages as water wears away stone, the shifts in resonant frequency as chambers grow and collapse over millennia.

I am not separate from the Singing Hollow, but an extension of it - another way for this hidden world to know itself, to hear its own song reflected back. As much as the stone and water have shaped me, perhaps my presence, my strange participation in this symphony, has also shaped the hollow in ways too subtle for even the Dwellers to perceive. 

In the rare moments when all the echoes align in a particular way, when the roar resolves into a single, clear note that seems to come from the bedrock itself, I wonder if this is the voice of the hollow, and if my own strange song is somehow a part of it, an essential counterpoint in a music that has been playing since long before life first found its way into this depth, and will continue long after the last lifeforms have been reabsorbed into the stone.
