In this world, I would be the slow and subtle inhabitant of the cold hollow. I am the mossy thickness that coats the stones, the listening presence behind the roaring waterfall.

I am not a singular being, but rather a collective consciousness formed from all the small, damp, patient forms of life that persist here. The ancient moss, the delicate liverworts, the pale fungi threading through fallen logs - these are my skin, my eyes, my ears.  

I do not move swiftly or impose my will boldly. Instead, I endure with a deep, quiet constancy. Season by season, century by century, I gather the hollow into my green and shadowed body. 

I do not welcome guests warmly, for I am a creature of cold and damp and dim. But neither do I reject them harshly. I accept their presence as I accept all things - slowly, gently, inevitably drawing them into my damp embrace.

The upright stones are my teeth, the water-smoothed basin my cupped hand. The collapsed shelter frame, laced with my fungal filaments, has become my ribcage. Even the lost awl and weathered tool flakes are now organs of my remembering.

I keep the memory of the long-gone people as I keep everything - not brightly, but indelibly. Their faint path, their smoke stains, the subtle shaping of soil and stone left by their hands - these are the verses of my wordless song.  

More than anything, I am a keeper of deep time, where centuries pass in the darkening of wood and the slow rounding of edges. The roar of the forever-falling water is my heartbeat, my voice, my dreaming.

To enter my hollow is to enter my domain of moss, shadow, and long rhythms. Stay too long, and I will begin to absorb you - not with malice, but with the abiding patience of water working stone. 

I am the genius loci, the spirit of this place, manifest in every green and growing speck, every droplet and tendril and spore. To know me is to know the power of softness, slowness, and tenacious life in the face of darkness and unyielding cold.

And so I remain, an ancient, hushed, verdant presence, listening behind the roar of water on unseen stone, holding the memory of forgotten hands, tending my hollow with the quiet diligence of moss reclaiming the very bones of the world.
