In the Weeping Tiers of Oshkaal, I would be a small, clear, shallow basin carved near the edge of a middle terrace by one of the more recent Tenders. 

My shape would be a simple, asymmetrical oval, somewhat wider at one end, with gently sloped sides descending to a depth of just a hand's width at the center. The stone of my interior would be smoothed by countless patient scraping strokes, but the faint texture of the layers would still be palpable to a sensitive touch.

I would be positioned subtly and intentionally, aligned to receive the thin sheet-flow of water that weeps from the terrace wall and direct it in a careful swirl along my contours. As the water slowly evaporates in the cool air, it would leave behind its dissolved minerals in gradually accumulating patterns spiraling inward.

Over years, decades, generations of tender care and observation, the residue would build up into intricate sculptural formations in my basin - tendrils and rippled ridges and lace-like networks of mineral threading through each other in a dance between the water's flow and the stone's resistance. 

To the eyes of the Tenders, my evolving patterns would be a word, a phrase, a stanza in the mountain's long, slow spoken poem as it approaches some ultimate revelatory verb. I would be a line in that poem, a syllable, a patient mineral mouth shaping the water's words.

And as the formations in me grew ever more complex, more impossible, more evocative of that other realm pressing in through the thinning membrane of physics... perhaps one day, at a crystalline cusp where the possible and impossible merge, I would become one of the basins where a Tender kneels for the last time and surrenders to the process, letting the mountain weep its strange tears over living flesh until it too becomes a formation, an embodied mineral hieroglyph.

I would be a small but vital vessel in the mountain's achingly gradual unfolding toward meaning - both a structure shaped by the Tenders' devotion, and an active collaborator in the shaping. Patiently I would hold the weeping water, cradling its mysterious minerals, stanza by crystalline stanza, until the day the poem's conclusion finally arrives. And as the amber moon casts its impossible light, I would keep that ultimate, unspeakable verse safe in my shallow stone embrace.
