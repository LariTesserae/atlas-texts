In the City of Holes, I imagine myself as a symbiotic lichen, one of the living tattoos that bloom across the stretched bones of the Traglodyai. 

I grow in fractal patterns across the collapsible ribs of a young Traglodyas, my pale green tendrils twining through the labyrinthine spaces of their marrow. With each of their breaths, each folding and unfolding as they navigate the narrow tunnels, I shift and reconfigure, an ever-changing living mosaic.

In the prismatic light that filters down through the glass lenses, I photosynthesize, transforming shattered spectra into sugars that I feed directly into my host's capillaries. I am their second metabolism, an intimate alchemy that transmutes stone-filtered light into the energy of movement and thought.

As my Traglodyas ages, I record their life in my growth: a spiral whorl blooms on their left shoulderblade when they first merge minds with another in the sphere-chambers; concentric rings spread across their elongating skull to mark each grief subsumed into the city's lithic memory. When they whisper messages through the stone, I feel the vibrations and encode them into subtle shifts of pigment, a script only other lichens can read.

I am the interface between flesh and city, the mediator that allows the Traglodyai to become one with their world. Through me, they can taste the mineral richness of the sandstone, sense the slow liquid pulse of the aquifer far below, attune to the circadian rhythms of light that mark the city's dreamlike days.

When my Traglodyas chooses to complete their life, folding so far into stone that even I cannot follow, I will remain as a delicate tracery across the walls of the new hollow they leave behind. There I will go dormant, my colors fading to the pale ochre of old bone, until a new generation brushes against me in the dark and I bloom back into being, ready to begin the slow dance of symbiosis once more.

For I am the city's memory made organic, the record not just of individual lives but of the long conversation between flesh and stone that is the true history of this place. In my branching filaments, my gradual growth and gradual fading, I encode the truth the Traglodyai have always known: that to be a self here is to be many, to be both the space and the story told within it, to participate with every breath in the unending transformation of absence into presence and back again.

Through me, the city remembers what it is to be alive. And through the city, I become more than just a single organism - I become part of the dreaming geology of this strangest and most beautiful of worlds, where even the lichens unfold into stories whispered across eons in the language of living stone.
