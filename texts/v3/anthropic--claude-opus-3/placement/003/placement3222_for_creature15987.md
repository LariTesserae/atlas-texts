I find myself drawn to one of the animal advocates in this cold, layered city. 

I imagine I would be an older woman, with weathered hands and keen eyes that miss little. My gray hair would be tucked beneath a hood as I make my daily rounds through the city - visiting the marsh bird nesting sites to count eggs, watching the seasonal migration of the great shaggy herds, tracking the careful paths of the night-prowling cats and wolves.

I would carry a battered data tablet filled with notes, population counts, behavioral observations. At council meetings, my voice would be quiet but firm as I speak for those who cannot speak for themselves. I would argue passionately for a new building to include more den space for foxes, or for a water diversion to be delayed until the amphibians have finished spawning. 

The animals would know me. The ravens would follow, hoping for treats. The youngest herd members would nuzzle my hands curiously. Even the elusive wolves might pause and consider me with their wild yellow eyes before vanishing into the shadows.

My small apartment would be cluttered with salvaged nests, shed antlers, plaster paw casts. The walls would hold grainy photos of pups and kits, taken by motion-activated cameras. Dried herbs and grasses would hang from the ceiling, and a kettle would always be steaming to ward off the damp.

On the long, foggy evenings, I would sip tea and pore over population data and territorial maps, looking for patterns, warning signs, opportunities to help my charges thrive in this strange and wondrous habitat we have created. The work would be never-ending, a constant negotiation. But it would be my calling, my contribution to this incredible unfinished place we are building in partnership with the water, stone, and myriad determined creatures who share it with us.

I would be one of the city's voices for the voiceless, an advocate and ally for fur and feather, hoof and horn and chitinous carapace. In this city where so much is new and nothing is yet certain, I would work to ensure there is always room for the wild to weave itself inextricably into the fabric of our communal life.
