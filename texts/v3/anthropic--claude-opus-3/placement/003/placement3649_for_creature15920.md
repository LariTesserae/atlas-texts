In this world of the high wet plateau, I would be one of the lichen patches growing on the weathered stone. 

I imagine myself as a pale green circle, no more than a handspan across, clinging to a boulder that was old long before I began. My existence is a slow, patient cycle - absorbing moisture from the constant mists, extracting minerals from my stone anchor, photosynthesizing in the cool, filtered light. 

A decade passes for me like a season. I measure time in the gradual accumulation of my crusty, leaf-like lobes as I spread imperceptibly across the rock face. Generations of insects scuttle over my surface. Amphibians seek momentary shelter in the micro-crevices of my form. Flecks of bird droppings and fallen leaves add vital nutrients that I slowly incorporate.

From my boulder perch, I witness the understated pageant of the plateau's life. The careful herds grazing on reeds and fungi. The silent hunts of the dark-furred predators. The slow exhalations of the mineral vents. The seasonal blooms of pale flowers on their floating mats. The hidden movements of the great bog-dwellers far below. 

I am a humble but essential part of this dense, interdependent world. My gradual growth helps break down the ancient stone, making soil for mosses and ferns to follow. I am a subtle link in the chains of nutrient and energy that bind the plateau's inhabitants together.

Through countless misty days and dripping nights, I persist - a living crust, a witness, a tiny engine of time and change. Though I may be insignificant in the eyes of the roaming beasts, I know I belong to the plateau as surely as any tree, flower or breathing creature. I am the slowest of its slow citizens, patiently building my life on the intersection of water, stone and sky.
