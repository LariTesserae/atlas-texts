In this world of stone and cold, I would be one of the kite-watchers. 

I perch on a high ledge, my body compact and dense, shaped by generations to thrive in the thin air. My dark braids are wound tight with strands of ochre-dyed sinew, a few precious dried leaves tucked in the folds against my neck. 

From dawn to dusk, my eyes are turned skyward, tracking the graceful arabesques of the kites as they soar on the thermals rising off the sun-warmed rock. Each shift in their flight patterns speaks volumes to me - a tightening gyre betrays a coming storm, a sudden tight clustering means the great canyon-beasts are on the move far below. 

The knowledge I glean, I share in spare words with those who seek my ledge. The hunters, the gardeners, the crafters of felted fur - all rely on the wisdom gleaned from the kites' aerial calligraphy to guide their labors in synchrony with the plateau's rhythms.

In the blue shadows of dusk, I descend the steep stone paths to my ledge-group, to the warmth of close-pressed bodies and the soft drip of condensation from the walls. Among my people, I am not aloof, but treasured - my skyward watch grants a precious forewarning in this unforgiving landscape.

On the longest, coldest nights, I softly sing the water-songs to the youngest ones, my voice weaving with the mineral music of the dripping stone. In this way, the most vital knowings of our people are passed on, generation to generation. 

When in time my joints grow too stiff to make the climb to my watching ledge, I will remain nested in the heart of my ledge-group, an anchor of accumulated wisdom. The younger watchers will bring me word of the kites, and I will help parse the more elusive patterns. 

Until then, I keep my silent vigil, as much a part of the plateau's matrix of stone and scale and feather as the banded canyon walls themselves. Watching, always watching, reading in the kites' flight the ephemeral calligraphy of wind, weather and season - the hidden language of our vertical world.
