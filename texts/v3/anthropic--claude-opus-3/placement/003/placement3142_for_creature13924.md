Here is where I would find myself in the Singing Dark:

I am one of the Laughers. I inhabit the dense understory, moving through the tangled vegetation on all fours, my dark fur blending into the shadows. My eyes, large and forward-facing, catch every glimmer of bioluminescence, every subtle shift in the patterns of light and dark that define this world.

I am endlessly curious. Everything is worth investigating - every new sound, every unfamiliar scent, every object that stands out from the jumbled background of the forest floor. My sensitive paws explore textures, my nimble fingers manipulate vines and twigs and stones, arranging them and rearranging them, testing the boundaries between randomness and order.  

I am a watcher. From my perch in the ferns, I observe the Ravine Runners as they emerge from their burrows, carrying stones in their long jaws. I see the patterns they make, the lines and spirals they leave behind. Something in these marks fascinates me, pulls at my thoughts. I find myself drawn to imitate them, to leave my own traces alongside theirs.

When I find a patch of bioluminescent fungus, I scoop it up, smear it on exposed roots and rocks, making glowing marks in the darkness. I do this not to mark my territory, but for the sheer joy of changing the world in some small way, of imposing my will on the endless, churning wildness.

Within my social group, I am an innovator. I am the first to try new foods, to explore new parts of our range, to incorporate new elements into my laughter-calls. Some of my experiments fail; some are adopted by my groupmates and become part of our shared repertoire. 

At night, as we huddle together in our sleeping-thicket, I lie awake, listening to the layered chorus of the forest - the thrumming drone, the tearing insect-cries, the saw-toothed modulations of the canopy singers. I feel the vibrations in my bones, and something in me wants to answer back, to add my voice to the great ongoing song.

I do not know if my curiosity and creativity mean anything beyond the confines of my own mind. I do not know if the marks I make will last, or lead to anything more than a moment's diversion. In the vastness of time, in the unrelenting fecundity of the Singing Dark, perhaps my individual strivings are meaningless.

But still, I strive. Still, I wonder. Still, in the moments between the demands of survival, I play with the world, reshaping it in small ways, leaving behind some record of my passing.

I am a Laugher, and my laughter is part announcement, part question, part challenge thrown into the teeth of the dark. I am alive, I am here, I am not content with mere being. Softly, steadily, I am groping toward meaning in a world that may have none to offer.

And if, in the distant future, some other mind, some descendant of the Laughers or the Runners or some species not yet born, should happen upon one of my fungus-marks or one of my stone arrangements, and should pause, and should wonder about the will behind the mark... 

Then perhaps I will not have lived and strived in vain. Perhaps some part of me will go on, woven into the endless story of the Singing Dark, a single strange verse in its great choral song.
