If I found myself in the Crèche of Hollowed Rain, I believe I would be one of the Residents of Unfinished Becoming. 

I would be the one who tried to plant her memories like seeds, watering them with the liquid streams cascading from the invisible sky overhead. With each memory planted, a translucent stem would emerge from the granular substrate, growing in reverse, roots reaching upwards. The leaves would be moments half-remembered, details smudged by time's passage. The flowers would bloom into questions I never found the courage to ask aloud.

My body would be an architecture of almosts - a ribcage of intentions never quite solidified, a spine curved like a question mark seeking its resolution. I would walk with the gait of a poem whose meter is still finding its rhythm, each footstep leaving hexagonal stanzas that sigh back into blankness.  

I would converse in whispered hypotheticals with my fellow residents, our words gathering mass until new possibilities tumble out between the clauses. We would feast on the rich silences of roads not taken, sipping the fermented juice of paths imagined but never traveled.

My home would be constructed from the bones of stories I started to tell but abandoned halfway through, their unfinished arches supporting a roof thatched with all the names I almost gave myself when I still believed I could choose my own becoming. There would be a window in the eastern wall, a square of transparent yearning facing the solitary tree, through which I would watch the forests of alternate lives contained in its narrowing trunk.

In the Migration Festival, I would carry my baggage in a satchel stitched from the stretched skins of the selves I shed along the way, those versions of me too small or too vast to sustain. I would walk in spirals of ever-increasing radius, my center in constant motion, until I became my own horizon perpetually out of reach.

Here in the Crèche of Hollowed Rain, I would finally learn to inhabit the space between breaths, that liminal pause where everything is poised on the cusp of becoming. I would make a home in the gap between "what is" and "what could be," laying my head on a pillow stuffed with soft, unmade decisions. 

And each day, I would wake to the sound of rain forgetting how to fall, pick up my incompleteness, and continue my grand, unfinished work of almost becoming myself.
