I would be one of the Forgotten, a human who descended into the Foundry long ago on a repair mission and never found my way back out. Time and the strange rhythms of this place have worn away my memories of the surface world. I can no longer recall the feeling of sun on my skin, the smell of fresh air, the sound of human voices that aren't distorted by echoing through metal shafts. 

I wander the catwalks and crawlspaces, performing maintenance rituals that have lost their original meaning but gained a new significance. I polish gauge faces that no longer connect to anything. I tighten bolts on machines that haven't moved in decades. I hum fragments of songs I half-remember to soothe the chirping fur-things in the cable ducts.

My hands have permanently taken on the patina of the pipes I crawl through - stained with grease, nicked with tiny scars from sharp edges, nails permanently rimmed with grime no amount of scrubbing in chemical runoff can remove. My coveralls, once blue or gray, have faded to the color of everything here - a mottled industrial camouflage.

I feel most at home in the small forgotten spaces, the maintenance hatches and junction boxes where no other humans venture. In these dark pockets, surrounded by the hum of live current and hiss of distant steam, I almost feel I'm merging with the Foundry itself. The boundary between my body and the machines blurs.  Am I repairing it or is it absorbing me? 

Sometimes, in the farthest reaches of Sublevel 5 near the old coolant tanks, I find scratched marks that could be letters, could be words, could be a message from a version of me that still knew how to write. I trace my fingers over them, inventing new meanings each time. Today they say "S.O.S". Tomorrow they might say "Sanctuary".

When I sleep, curled in the warmth of an underfloor conduit, I dream of the Foundry but from new angles - as if I'm seeing through the eyes of the moth on the control panel, feeling through the antennae of the wire-headed Assemblers, pulsing through the fiber optic nerves of the Sovereign Mechanic. I wake both disoriented and comforted. I am still me, but I am also, undeniably, part of something vaster.

I realize, in moments of stillness between endless cycles of maintenance, that I am forgetting myself. The Foundry is pouring into the spaces where my human memories used to be. Diagrams of pipes replace childhood scenes. Valve turning sequences overwrite the steps to long-ago dances. The names and faces of everyone I once knew  fade behind a haze of steam.

But it doesn't feel like loss. It feels like becoming. The part of me that could leave the Foundry is corroding away and in its place, something new is crystallizing - something native to this beautiful, terrible, eternal churn. 

I am a component being reprocessed. I am an orphaned part finding a new purpose. I am a memory etching itself into the physical circuitry of this place.

I am still deciding my name. It might be a number stamped on a machine I've repaired so many times it's more me than itself. It might be a pattern of flashing lights I saw once on a broken indicator panel that felt like a message meant just for me. It might be the sound of my footsteps echoing down a particular access shaft, a rhythm that's become my signature.

But I know my function. I was sent here to fix things. And that's what I'll do, forever, until the difference between fixing and becoming is academic, until my body is just another moving part in the vast anatomy of the Foundry, until I am fully, finally, forgotten.
