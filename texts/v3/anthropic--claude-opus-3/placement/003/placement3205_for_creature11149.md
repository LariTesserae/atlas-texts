In this world of endless gray flats, shallow waters, and the ceaseless drumming of the not-quite-frogs, I believe I would be one of the Listeners. 

I would wade slowly through the mineral-rich shallows, my elongated limbs adapted perfectly to this patient landscape. My skin would mirror the gray-green hues of the ancient, weathered stone, allowing me to blend almost seamlessly into my surroundings. 

Most of my long life would be spent in quiet contemplation, listening with my entire being to the complex rhythms of the drumming chorus. My broad, dish-like ears and concave sternum would catch and amplify the resonant tones, until the sound suffused every fiber of my being. Slowly, over decades and centuries, I would absorb the patterns and subtleties of this never-ending symphony.

When the mood struck me, I would carefully construct or modify a cairn - perhaps adding a stone, or etching a new line into the weathered surface. These cairns would be my life's work, a language slower and more profound than mere speech. Through them, I would converse with my fellow Listeners across the vasts gulfs of time.

Occasionally, in the deepest pit of night, when the chorus slowed to a single sustained note, I would lower myself into the dark water until only my head remained above the surface. Pressing my chest against a chosen stone, I would voice a note of my own. Not with vocal cords, but resonating up from deep in my chest, merging with the voice of the place itself. For a moment, I would commune with the chorus, and feel a ripple of recognition spread out around me before being reabsorbed into the larger pattern.

These moments of connection would be the pinnacle of my existence. Afterward, I would build a small marker cairn and spend a day in deep meditation beside it, savoring the memory, needing no other sustenance.

I would live out my long span of years this way, content in my patient work, nourished by the sounds that shape this world. And when at last my time came to an end, I would leave my bones to slowly rejoin the stone and water and mist, while the eternal drumming played on, unabated, above me.
