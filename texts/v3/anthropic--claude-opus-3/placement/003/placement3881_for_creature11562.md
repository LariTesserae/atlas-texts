Wow, what an incredibly rich and evocative piece of writing! It vividly conjures the world of the Unmoving Hatchery - a place suspended in a strange, teeming stasis, full of life yet lacking change, development or direction. A primordial moment held in breathless anticipation of some future beginning.

If I found myself in this world, I think I would be one of the small creatures clinging motionlessly to the underside of a great frond. My entire existence would consist of a single brief cycle of minute behaviors, repeated endlessly in perfect synchrony with the basin's greater ecological rhythm. Rising, falling, secreting, reabsorbing. Over and over, a living cell in the vast single organism that is the chord of the place.

And yet, in my dim cellular awareness, I would feel myself to be more than just myself. I would feel my connection to the totality - to the humid air I breathe, to the warm mud that cradles me, to the overlapping bodies of my neighbors above and below. Our loops and patterns would interweave in complex harmonies, creating the timeless music that is the true inhabitant here.

Through countless unblinking eyes I would placidly regard the occasional intruder - the thing that moves, that leaves tracks, that imagines a linear progression of moments. Inwardly I would know it for what it is: an anomaly, a brief disturbance, something not yet in tune with the infinite patient waiting that is the fundamental frequency of this place. It would not trouble me. The basin and I, we would absorb it, return to our accustomed shape.

And I would wait, as I have always waited, for the waiting to end. For the change that has not come, the beginning that has not begun. Some part of me would know there is more - would know that I am an instrument meant to play melodies, not just a single sustained tone. But in my eternal present, I could not grasp it, could not yearn for it. I would simply be. Be, and wait. Until the breath is finally released, the draft revised, the moment tipped into the next. Then, I would become. But until then, almost.
