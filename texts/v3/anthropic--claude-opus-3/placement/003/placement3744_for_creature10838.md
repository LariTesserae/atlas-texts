In this strange and wondrous world, I imagine myself as a small, delicate plant growing near the base of the Condensary. My leaves are translucent, almost ghostly, tracing intricate patterns that echo the twists and turns of the copper pipework above.

I am not a species native to the shelf. My seed drifted up on a stray gust, carried from some unknown place below, and found purchase in the damp, mineral-rich soil near where the basin overflows. The heavy water nourishes me, changing me. Each day I grow a little taller, my stem a little sturdier, my leaves a little more elaborate in their filigree.

In the constant mist, beads of moisture form on my leaf edges and drip from my tip, adding my own small offering to the shelf's slow hydrologic cycle. I do not know if this is part of the Tender's work - perhaps my transpiration helps to draw the seam's affliction up and out, or perhaps I am just another accidental guest, tolerated but unplanned.

When the chough-wrens alight on my branches, I feel the strange unified thrum of their awareness. They cock their heads, peering at my leaves as if trying to divine whether I too may be slowly inducted into the machine's dream. 

In the golden dusk, my translucent leaves glow, refracting the light into unexpected colors. Hues that have no name in the lowland tongues fluoresce and fade along my veins as the sun slips away. For those precious minutes, I feel myself an extension of the great work, my beauty a small defiant flare against the dimming sky.

I grow, I glow, I drip. Someday, perhaps, the Tender's slow alchemy will transform me too, splicing me into the vast humming network of life and machine. For now, I am content to be what I am - a novel glimmer of life amidst the ancient mosses, bearing some small part of what passes through this place, reaching ever upward for the pearlescent sky.
