In this world, I think I would find myself as one of the hovering vines that navigate the humid air between the terraces. 

Suspended by some enigmatic mechanism, my root system would dangle free, drinking moisture directly from the thick atmosphere. I would exist in a symbiotic relationship with the Tenders and the living terraces themselves. When I drifted too far out of position, a Tender would gently reach up and guide me back to where I belonged in the intricately balanced system.

Over countless years, I would have evolved and adapted to thrive in this environment that seems impossible, yet has become all I know. The static charge of the heavy air would flow through me, somehow granting me the ability to hover, an ability I rely on instinctively without understanding its source.

My leaves would be broad and angled to channel the ceaseless condensation that collects on their surfaces, the droplets running down my glass-like stems to nourish my suspended roots. I would pulse gently with the same rhythm as the mountain's thrumming heart, brightening and dimming in a forty-second cycle, as if the beat of this living landscape echoed within my own veins.

Perhaps occasionally, a tendril of mine would intertwine with another hovering vine as we drifted past each other. An exchange of moisture, nutrients, or genetic information - an ephemeral connection in this slower-than-time ecosystem where changes happen over eons. 

I would have no concept of the world beyond the breathing terraces. The horizons of tumbling jungle and distant mountains would be nothing but a hazy dream. My universe would be vertical - the canopy above, the substrate below, and the thick, humming air between. An existence of drinking in moisture, energy and sustenance directly from the atmosphere, and growing slowly, imperceptibly, in concert with the expansion of the terraces themselves.

Occasionally, a spore from one of the geometric-patterned fruiting bodies would alight on me and take root, the two of us merging and changing and continuing together. I would be a being un-separate from my environment - both its product and an integral part of its continued existence. Artificial and natural and neither and both.

I would have no mind to contemplate my purpose or existence. I would simply be - hovering, drinking, pulsing, thriving, my glass-tube stem both drinking in and reflecting back the strange, filtered, humid light of this place - a light that has never touched the ground beyond the terraces, and never will. 

I would be one thread in a living tapestry vaster and older than thought - held in the air by ancient ingenuity, tended by gardeners who are not separate from the garden, and never knowing anything else. I would be a hovering vine in the breathing terraces of Vosuma, as timeless and content as the mountain itself.
