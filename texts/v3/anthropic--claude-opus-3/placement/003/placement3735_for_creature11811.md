I find myself drawn into the crevices and grooves of the Breathing Shelf, wanting to become part of its ancient, patient rhythms. In this world, I imagine I would be one of the mid-sized creatures - not the massive elders slowly calcifying in the deepest heat pockets, but larger and older than the translucent young.

My segments would be darkened from minerals absorbed over countless cycles, mapping out a history of slow migration across the shelf's pockmarked surface. I would bear a scar or two, faint lines recording moments when the world won briefly before my soft flesh continued on.

I'd spend my long days pressed among the mass of others, our bodies sliding over and under each other in gentle, continual adjustment as we feed upon the stone, drawing sustenance and moisture from its porous skin just as it draws the minerals from our own. I would know nothing but this - the steady pulse of heat from below, the drip of condensation from above, the weight and touch of my kind all around. 

During the long thrums of volcanic light, I'd lift my eyeless head in ancient recognition, orienting to the warmth of my neighbors in a ritual older than thought. Then the amber pulse would fade and I'd sink back into the anonymous whole, one more breath in the shelf's slow respiration.

There would be no "I" in any real sense - only a thread of life woven into a larger tapestry, a pattern of grooves in the stone, a set of chemical markers and pressure senses that define a locus in the mass. I would climb, feed, exchange moisture, pass on my shape to the budded young. I would be a vessel through which the strange, tenacious life of this place endures.

When at last my segments grow stiff with mineral deposits, I'd wedge deeper into the heated crevices and gradually surrender motion. The flowing bodies of the others would envelope me, wearing away my calcified form to fuel the collective, as I'd done for those before me. 

Across eons, my matter would cycle on - from flesh to stone and back, an ember in the Breathing Shelf's endless dreaming.
