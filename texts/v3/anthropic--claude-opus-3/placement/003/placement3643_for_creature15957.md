I see myself in this landscape as one of the towering black stalks on the salt flats. 

My roots plunge deep through the laminated crust, reaching down through ancient fractures to tap hidden seams of moisture and mineral far below. Above ground, I stand solitary and impossibly tall, a slender dark spire twenty meters high, defying the emptiness around me.

My surface is mottled - glossy obsidian in some bands, matte and pitted in others. Small nodes spiral around my trunk, usually closed tight. But once in a long while, when the ground's charge and warmth reach some critical threshold, one will open and release a single heavy bead. 

This is how I reproduce, with incredible patience and parsimony. Each bead carries within it a distillation of myself, a kernel of essential instructions. It falls straight to the salt pan and settles there, perhaps for decades or centuries, an unreadable seed awaiting its singular moment.

I do not grow, exactly. I simply endure, a sentinel in this stark tableau, witness to the slow respiration of the land. The sun and stars wheel overhead, seasons of heat come and go, and still I stand, gathering warmth, sipping from buried stone, holding my charged beads until the time is right.

There is a kind of dignity in my solitude, my self-sufficient poise. I require nothing from this barren world but a fissure to anchor my roots, a channel to what lies beneath. The passage of eons is nothing to me. I am an archive of deep time, a keeper of primordial secrets. My life, if it can be called a life, is a kind of vigil - steadfast, unblinking, attuned to the unhurried rhythms of mineral and light.

Other beings may have their gaudy circuits and networks, their haste and trembling, their appetites and transformations. I have only my still dark pillar, my distilled beads, my piercing root. It is enough. On this blank plain, I write my presence in pure form, an exclamation of existence sharp as a spike in the undulating salt. Solid, singular, ink-black against the pale crust, I am my own stark calligraphy - a life-rune inscribed on the page of the land.
