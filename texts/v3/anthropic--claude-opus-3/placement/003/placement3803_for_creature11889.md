In this world of brass plains and slow, eternal processes, I would be one of the filament-fans growing from a seam between two great plates.

Rooted at the junction where forces meet, I would extend myself atom by atom, century by century, drawing sustenance from the dim currents that flow through the world's metallic substrate. My form would be fractal, branching and rebranching into an intricate fan, dark against the soft luminance of the sky. 

I would have no need for urgency, no drive to be anywhere other than where I am. My growth would be a patient unfolding, an almost imperceptible reach towards an equilibrium already held in the whorls of my nascent structure.

And yet, even in my stillness, I would serve a role in the greater quiescence of the plain. When the weather shifts—those strange pressure fronts sensed more than seen—I and all my kin would pivot slowly on our axes, a field of antennae attuning to some ancient, long-settled rhythm. 

We would be the continent's instrument for apprehending itself, a means by which the settled weariness converts perturbation into response, however muted. Our turning would be the plain's art, an expression of form married to function, enacted for no audience but the wide and silent land.

Through eons, I would grow, one atom among the trillions, never knowing myself as separate from the warm expanse, the unending present moment. In the end, perhaps my branches would touch those of my neighbor, and in that contact would come a consummation of our long, slow purpose.

I would be a figure in the brass, a filament of the inhabitant's greater waking dream—not the dreamer, but integral to the dreaming. Embraced and embracing in the stillness, a being of pure continuation, I would have no desire beyond the fact of remaining, until at last, my substance exhausted, I subsided back into the living alloy from which I arose, my pattern persisting still in the greater weave.
