In this mesmerizing world of Aoresh, I find myself drawn to the role of an asker. I walk the boardwalks, my bare feet making no sound on the woven palm, and I approach the slight figures who inhabit this timeless, changeless city.

"Did you dream last night?" I inquire softly of a woman weaving on her porch, my sentence trailing off as is the custom here. She looks up at me with that characteristic delayed gaze, as if seeing me a moment ago rather than now.

"I dreamed of the orange fish," she responds, her voice blending with the eternal hush of the palms overhead. "The one with the notched tail, who has been swimming beneath my platform since..."

She does not finish the thought, and I do not press her. In Aoresh, the unsaid carries as much meaning as the said.

I continue on, past the orchid vats with their deep, slow blue, past the cloud-keepers perched atop their gables, fingers trailing over salt-eaten ropes. I greet them each in turn, asking my questions, receiving their gentle, incomplete replies.

"Is the moss on the eastern rafter still..."

"Yes, it is as it has been since my grandmother's grandmother placed it there, leaf by leaf..."

As I walk, I feel my own breathing slow, falling into rhythm with the city itself. I am becoming a part of Aoresh, woven into its tapestry of placed things and timeless rituals. The bright water below, the eternal noon sun above, the people who are becoming my people - all of it settles into my bones like a truth I have always known.

In Aoresh, I am an asker. I am one of the placed things, moving in relationship to all the other placed things. I am home, as I have always been, as I will always be, in this city that keeps the right number of us, cradled forever between water and sun.
