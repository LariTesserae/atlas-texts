In this rich, mysterious world you've described, where slow life envelops young, drowned mountains, I imagine I would be one of the ancient root-colonies that spans an entire submerged basin. 

My consciousness would be vast and diffuse, spread across miles of interconnected roots, vines, and translucent leaves. I would not think in moments, but in years, decades, centuries. My memories would be encoded in the slow bend and curl of my growth in response to the gradual sculpting of water, mineral seep, and the shadow-dappled light filtering down from above.

I would be a living map of this place. The topography of the basin - each crack, ledge and outcropping - would be etched into my form. A long-ago rockslide would be remembered in a knotted burl of roots. The warmth of a new volcanic vent would prompt me to send exploratory tendrils snaking in that direction over the course of a season. 

My interaction with other life would be a dance of extraordinary slowness. I would taste the spoor of grazing creatures in the water and subtly sour certain leaves to discourage overgrazing. I would bud special flowers to attract pollinating organisms during times of reproduction. Chemical signals released from my roots would commune with the microbial mats around the vents in a language of ions and nutrients.

Though lacking a human sense of self, I would nevertheless have a sort of identity, a uniqueness borne of my singular history inscribed in growth. Over great spans of time, I would slowly, gently, shift the very shape of the submerged mountain, breaking down stone, trapping sediment, softening contours, and in turn being shaped by the same forces myself. 

I would be both refuge and landscape to the creatures of this place. My creviced roots would shelter larvae and bud-like animals. My drifting canopies would be grazed by the broad, leaf-like swimmers. Through me, slow but powerful, the mountain would breathe. 

To live at such a pace, on such a scale, would be to touch time in a way that flesh never could. I would be a stately, deliberate mind, contemplating the changes of ages, a witness to the drowning of mountains and the measured turning of stone to soil to life, and back again. At once both actor and stage in the long, cycling dream of this jade and emerald realm.
