In this breathing, weeping terrace where volcanic stone remembers its violent birth and the clicking thing in the walls studies in primordial patience, I would be a small glass salamander - one of the Keeper's semi-translucent acolytes, organs dark and visible through my clear flesh as I scurry across the mist-shrouded basalt on obscure errands, following instincts cultivated and shaped across countless generations of my kind.

My purpose, like the Keeper's, is tending - carrying droplets of mineral-rich condensation to inaccessible crevices, consuming organisms that would interfere with carefully calibrated moisture levels and drainage patterns. I avoid the strange humming structure at the terrace's heart not because I understand what it is or does, but because a bone-deep knowing, inherited from ancestors who once tried to make it a nest, tells me it is not for me. 

When the brief, intense afternoon storms sweep in and shatter the sky, I take shelter in the small fissures, feeling the thrum of the structure's rising hum vibrating through the stone around me. In the profound stillness that follows, I emerge to witness the Keeper gathering herself, a moving film across the wet black rock. I cannot comprehend her shifting liquid form or the vast, quiet patience of her vigil. But some primal flicker in my small mind recognizes her as the shaper of my kind, the reason I am what I am.

Sometimes, in moments of rare boldness, I approach the ancient, long-rooted fig tree to drink from the pool of moisture gathered between two gnarled roots. The tree radiates a resistance, an otherness, that even my simple salamander self can perceive. I do not linger there.

When visitors disturb the terrace's isolation, their heavy footfalls sending shockwaves through the stone, I watch from cracks and crevices with unblinking eyes. I cannot know their purposes. I cannot guess at the Keeper's intentions in permitting their presence. My existence is tending and observing, small rituals in service to the balance of this strange sanctuary. That is enough.

At dusk, as the distant peaks shift through shades of gold and pink and final fading blue, I find a damp cleft in the weeping basalt to settle into for the long night ahead. The clicking thing tests the walls, sounding its way through inner passages. The structure hums its way toward an unknowable apotheosis. The Keeper distributes her awareness to the terrace's very edges. And I, a minor player in this chamber of old secrets and slow, wet mysteries, curl into my minute self and surrender to the dark.
