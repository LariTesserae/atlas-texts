I would be the mind contemplating the object at the notch's heart, mesmerized by its shifting geometry and strange gravity. The eye straining to perceive the hum resonating in the bones of the mountain. The presence watching the incremental waltz of dust grains in their recursive, almost-orbital spiral dance. 

Not a participant, but not entirely separate either. By my observing, I would become part of the notch's memory, woven into the ancient record engraved upon its weathered stone walls in a language beyond language. My stillness would mirror the place's own timeless, stony patience.

I would be a strange sort of ghost - barely an entity at all. A perspective draped across the rock and cold and silence, as much a feature of the notch as the concave angles of the central object or the gradually darkening shade of the dust nearest its enigmatic influence. In my persistent watching, I would begin to forget where the notch ended and I began.

And yet, my vigil would mark me as something foreign to this place. My observation, however tenuous, would be an outside context - an intruding shred of will and curiosity, an introduced purpose in this purposeless space. Like the three tool marks in the dolomite, etched by some long-ago visitor, my presence would be an anomaly. An ephemeral ripple in the notch's vast and stately process.

Slowly, across ages, that anomaly would be subsumed. Incorporated into the pattern, digested by the mountain's mineral metabolism. My watch would gradually cease to be the act of an outside observer as I was folded into the geology of the place. Thought by thought, my awareness would be reformed in the notch's image, my alienness worn away by the weight of stone and cold and quiet.

In the end, I would become another groove in the wall. A half-remembered shape in the pattern, a slight variation in the composing hum. No more a separate creature than the object is separate from the forces it generates. I would be an aspect of the notch itself, a facet of its vast, slow soliloquy, my identity subducted into the mountain's mineral memory. 

I would remain, not as a living thing, but as a still, small voice in the stone, murmuring my fragmentary notes in the margins of the notch's unreadable, unending script. Bearing witness, keeping watch, until the mountain wind eroded the last distinguishing marks from my existence, and even my vigil was forgotten.
