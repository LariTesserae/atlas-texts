In this world of the Roost-Cleft, I would imagine myself as one of the Small-Handed. 

I live with my extended family in a column of hollows we have inhabited for generations. The stone is worn smooth from the touch of so many small, deft hands over time. In the uppermost hollow, a channel has been painstakingly carved to direct precious rainwater into a basin - a vital reservoir in times of drought.

My days are spent clambering nimbly up and down the sheer walls, fingers and toes finding the smallest cracks and ledges with practiced ease. I forage for the pale, fleshy berries that grow stubbornly in the crevices, and help tend to the fat grubs we cultivate in the fungal mats on the cave floor far below.

Occasionally, I join a small group that ventures into the adjacent hollows of the Soft-Beaks to carefully, quietly, collect an egg or newly-hatched chick. It is a solemn task, not done lightly. We take only what is needed, and no more - maintaining the ancient, unspoken balance. 

In the perpetual twilight, I watch the fluttering shadows of the Hangers shifting and dangling overhead. Their rhythmic clicks and chirps and the piping call-and-response of the Soft-Beaks form the background music to life in the cleft. 

Now and then, in the strange quietude between choruses, the slow, immense breathing of the Upper Things can be sensed more than heard. Their nature remains a mystery - but their presence is a constant, like the very heartbeat of the mountain.

I take my turn sitting in contemplation in the Hollow of Smoothed Stone, running my fingers along the polished contours carved by the touch of my ancestors. Here, I feel viscerally connected to this place, to the unbroken chain of life cycling through the crevice. 

We Small-Handed, perhaps more than any others, perceive the cleft as a living totality rather than just a habitat. In our unspoken rituals, in the ancient touch-language we share, we affirm our lineage as one of the strands woven into the eternal pattern - clicking, trilling, breathing, and climbing in unconscious symphony with bat and bird and unseen behemoth.

We are not masters of the Roost-Cleft, but rather minute parts of its vast, slow, chthonic existence. To live here is to surrender to the whole, even as we maintain our own small societies and cycles within it. In its warm, womblike embrace, we simply are - and that is enough.
