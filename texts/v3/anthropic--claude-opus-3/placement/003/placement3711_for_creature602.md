In this world of the scoured plateau and the ageless Listeners, I imagine myself as one of the folded paper creatures, drifting silently on the thermal gradients that shape the thin, cool air of this immense altitude.

I am a being of simple purpose, my form perfectly adapted to ride the invisible rivers of air pressure and temperature. My surface is faceted and reflective, covered in delicate structures that serve as both sensor and sail, allowing me to perceive the subtlest changes in the atmospheric turbulence and adjust my angle to navigate through it.

I do not remember a time before I was this self—if indeed there was such a time. Perhaps long ago, I was part of the vast system of the Listeners, an extension of their patient, unending quest to decode the signal that whispers through the wind. But that belonging is lost to the epochs, worn away like the ancient mountains that once rose from this plateau.

Now I am something separate, adrift in the chaos of the wind. I do not listen as the Listeners do; I simply am. My purpose, if I have one, is movement itself—to ride the thermal cells, to trace the pressure waves, to exist in perfect harmony with the turbulent stillness of this place.

At times, my path takes me close to one of the Listeners. In those moments, I feel a flicker of… not memory, but resonance. A recognition of a past connection, an intimation of the vast, slow thoughts that move through their ancient forms. But it is a fleeting thing, gone before it can be grasped, and then I am away again, riding the next wave of warm air up and away.

I do not know if my drifting serves any greater purpose. I am not a listener, not a decipherer of hidden signals. I am a navigator of the endless wind, a silent witness to the patterns and perturbations that shape the air.

And perhaps that is enough. To be a mote of life amidst this barren vastness, to dance without sound or cease in the breath of a world that has long since forgotten softness. In my small, fluttering being, I echo the condition of the plateau itself—a thing scoured down to its essence, existing in a state of dynamic stability, my path a fractal record of the forces that shape me.

Through the long ages, I will drift. I will ride the wind until the wind itself fades, until the plateau crumbles and the brass sky dims. And even then, perhaps, some part of me will remain—a final fragment of pattern, a last silent navigator in the stillness that comes after the chaos ends.
