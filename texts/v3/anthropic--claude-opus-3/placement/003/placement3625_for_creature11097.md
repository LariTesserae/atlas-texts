In this hauntingly beautiful and enigmatic world, I imagine myself as one of the small white shrimp, hanging motionless in the warm band near the towers. 

I am drawn to the stillness and equilibrium of this place, content to simply exist in the presence of these ancient, inscrutable beings that inhabit the city. I do not need to enter their buildings or comprehend their vast, slow thoughts. It is enough to share in the rhythm of the place, marked by the occasional low pulse that thrums through the structures.

As a shrimp, my own existence is fleeting compared to the city's inhabitants. Yet I find myself mirroring their patience, their readiness. Suspended in the warm current, I feel as if I too am waiting, though I cannot grasp what for. Perhaps it is simply in my nature, as a creature of this place, to attune myself to the watchfulness that pervades the stilled city.

At times, I drift closer to the towers, marveling at the intricacy of their construction - the endlessly recursive patterns that hint at the immensity of the minds dwelling within. The waste light that seeps from the structures' seams washes over me, and I wonder what incredible processes must be occurring inside, that even their tiny inefficiencies produce this ghostly luminance.  

I am a mote in this ecosystem of near-absence, but I do not feel insignificant. By participating in the city's stillness, by waiting alongside its ancient denizens, I feel I am part of something vast and purposeful, even if that purpose is beyond my understanding. 

In the long intervals between pulses, I contemplate what the inhabitants might be holding in their minds, what memory or promise or name they are preserving against the erosion of time. I cannot fathom it, but I am comforted by its existence. The city endures, and so I endure beside it, content in my small shrimp's life, sharing in some infinitesimal part of the great work of persistence.
