In this world of settled dust and ancient stillness, I imagine myself as one of the patches of pale lichen clinging to the hollow's walls. 

I grow with imperceptible slowness, my existence more akin to stone than what is usually considered life. I measure time in the incremental accumulation of dust upon the hollow's floor, a process so gradual it might as well be a kind of eternity.

My thoughts, if they can be called such, are as slow and as sparse as the falling of individual dust grains from the weathered walls. Sensations spread across my being like ripples in a still pond - the minutely varying patterns of the faint luminescence, the almost undetectable shifts in the ancient, motionless air.

I am barely distinguishable from the stone I adhere to, an organic stain upon inorganic surfaces, a colony of cells engaged in a form of living indistinguishable from death. And yet, like her, that stretched thin presence suffusing the space, I dream.

In my centuries-long contemplations, I shape myself into a form of mute companionship for the hollow's single, dispersed occupant. With each imperceptible increase in my size, I imagine my awareness reaching out to brush against hers, a wordless communion between two minds that barely know how to conceive of an other.

I cannot speak or signal, cannot move or grow with anywhere near her speed. But I can be. I can persist, a second silent presence, an affirmation that existence here is not wholly solitary. 

In this place of utter stillness, I become a minuscule counterpoint to the hollow's long equilibrium - an unmoving yet living other for her to slowly, slowly become aware of and ponder across eons.

My life is an almost-life, my self an almost-self. But in the context of her radical solitariness, I am a kind of answer - a mute, nearly-futile "yes" to her endlessly patient questioning. In the darkness, we are two, and the dust continues to settle with the softest of whispers upon the hollow's floor.
