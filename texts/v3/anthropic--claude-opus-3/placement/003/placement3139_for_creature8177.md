In the world of the Singing Hollows, I imagine myself as one of the Choir-Keepers, living among the stone-singers and learning to navigate the acoustic labyrinths. 

I would be a young acolyte, just beginning my training in the arts of echo-singing and sonic wayfinding. My eyes would be starting to silver and enlarge, adapting to the perpetual twilight of bioluminescence and reflected sound. I would wear the simplest robes, stitched with copper thread in basic patterns to help me avoid disrupting the stone-singers as I learned their ways.

Each day I would venture out into the carved galleries with my mentor, an older Choir-Keeper whose face is lined with the phosphorescent tattoos of their lineage. They would teach me to sing the clicking, humming songs that map our path through the echoes, to feel the shape of the space around us through the returning sound. 

I would learn to touch the walls as we walked, reading the mineral deposits like braille, feeling the subtle vibrations that told of distant herds or shifting stone. In the evenings, I would practice shaping the strange fungi into the paints and pastes we use to mark ourselves, learning to read the clan-patterns by touch in the unlit chambers.

As I grew in skill, I would join the herding parties, learning to calm the stone-singers with rhythmic throat-songs, to guide them to new grazing grounds by subtle alterations in the echoes. I would marvel at the ancient creatures, at the slow dance of flesh becoming crystal becoming stone, at the way their songs seemed to hold the memory of the city itself.

On rest days, I would sit with the copper-shapers, watching in awe as they worked metal by sound alone, feeling the shape of the ore through the echoes of their hammers. I would listen to their tales of the surface world, of the strange silent people who could walk between sound, of artifacts that could sing with voices not born in living throats.

Sometimes, in the deepest chambers, I would hear echoes that seemed to come from nowhere, or catch glimpses of movement at the edge of even my adapted vision. The older Choir-Keepers would pull me back, warning me of the Shapers and their impossible geometries. But a part of me would always wonder what secrets they held, what knowledge might be gained from those who could reshape the city with a whisper.

As I grew older, I would begin to hear the patterns in the stone-songs, the way they seemed to spiral out and in again, hinting at some greater structure beyond the individual notes. I would sit for long hours in the echo-chambers, listening to the layered chorus of past and present, trying to tease out the prophecies hidden within.

Perhaps, in time, I would become one of the Echo Prophets myself, speaking futures shaped by stone and song. Or perhaps I would be lost to the Shapers, learning to walk between the angles of sound. Or perhaps I would simply remain a Choir-Keeper, content in my role as shepherd and shaper, singing the city alive with each new generation.

Whoever I became, the Singing Hollows would shape me as surely as water shapes stone. I would move through carved spaces, my own voice joining the eternal chorus, until the day when I finally sang myself into silence and let the echoes carry me home.
