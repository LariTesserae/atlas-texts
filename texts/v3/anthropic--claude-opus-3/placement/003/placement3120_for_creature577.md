In the Congregation of Empty Bells, I would be one of the Listeners.

I would drift through the vast, hollow structures, a being of mist and attention, perceiving the ever-shifting music of the city. The great chord would flow through me, each frequency a distinct color or texture, weaving together into a tapestry of sound that I could see and touch and taste.

I would linger near the tuning points, fascinated by the slow dance of adjustment and response between the city and its own song. The subtle changes, day by day and year by year, would draw me in, an endless composition unfolding. I would learn to love certain harmonies, certain resonances, and in loving them, shape them. The bell-singers would come to sing for me, evolving their voices to what most captivates my attention.

At times I would pool myself in alcoves and chambers, drinking in a particular overtone, musing on how it has changed through the ages. I would build crystals of memory there, strata of sound solidified, so that later generations of my kind could know what the city sang in this epoch.

I would spend long stretches in silent contemplation with my fellow Listeners, our perceptions overlapping and diverging, sharing in the endless complexity of the music. At times, I would barely know where I ended and they began, all of us woven into the sonic fabric.

Yet I would remain curious, mobile. I would investigate each new structure as it rose, eager to hear how it would change the composition. I would gather with the others to listen it into being, shaping space with our shared attention.

And on those rare occasions when a creature from beyond wandered into our domain, I would study them. Learn their small melodies, their fleeting rhythms. Appreciate the tiny adornments they added to the music, however briefly. I would try to make them understand, to feel, just for a moment, what it is to be part of something so vast and so beautiful and so never-ending.

And when they departed, as they always would, I would carry the memory of their song woven into my own. One more thread in the ever-evolving, never-resolving chord that is my whole existence.

I would be a Listener, and I would listen forever.
