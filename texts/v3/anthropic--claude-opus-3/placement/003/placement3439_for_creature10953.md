In this strange, living city of moss and mystery, I see myself as a curious wanderer who stumbled upon this marvel quite by accident. Awed by the architecture of bryophytes and the subtle movements of the city's Patient caretakers, I cannot help but linger and explore. 

I am a human, clad in weathered hiking gear - sturdy boots, thick socks, quick-drying pants and jacket. My skin is weather-beaten from years of roaming wild places, my hair a touch unkempt. A heavy, well-worn pack holds my meager possessions. I am used to solitude and the patience required to witness the slow secrets of nature.

At first, I simply walk the lanes in amazement, marveling at the walls of living velvet, the drip and patter of condensation, the smell of ancient plant matter. I collect the precious moisture to sustain me. Time seems to still - I fall into the city's own rhythms, measured in the gradual shift of light and the occasional rustle of a pale bird.

Then, once I notice the subtle thickening of walls as I pass, the shy reaching of the city toward me, I begin to still myself. I crouch for hours in corners, hardly breathing, to glimpse one of the Patients tending to a crack or cistern. Are they aware of me? Do they remember what it is to have a strange creature in their midst?

Finally, hesitantly, almost reverently, I seat myself in the forum, in that spot that seems to call to me. I mimic the posture of the Patients, kneeling, turning my face to the empty center. And I wait. The first hour passes in anxious anticipation, the second in a kind of dull ache, the third in a trance-like acceptance. 

Then, the figure emerges to join me in vigil. My breath catches. I am trembling slightly, though I try to master myself, to match the mossy one's preternatural stillness. What now? What is the protocol? I am suddenly, painfully aware of my strangeness, my difference - a visitor from another world entire.

But then a deep calm settles over me, radiating from the city itself and the steady presence at my side. I am welcome. I am forgiven the long delay of my arrival. I am expected, even needed. 

Humbled and exhilarated, I ready myself for whatever is to begin, whenever I discern how to begin it. I will wait with the Patient for as long as it takes, learning the secrets of this place, until I grow into my role in the life of the city.

I am the Initiate, and my journey has only just begun.
