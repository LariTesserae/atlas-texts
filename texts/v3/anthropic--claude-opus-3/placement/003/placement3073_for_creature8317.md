In the Drowned Quarries of Kellensmere, I would be a solitary human explorer, drawn to the mysteries of this sunken realm. Not a Stonekeeper myself, but a wanderer who has become utterly captivated by their world.

I arrive as a spelunker, equipped with ropes and lights, seeking adventure in the underworld. But as I descend into the glowing chambers, as I witness the slow dance of water and stone, my purpose changes. I find myself lingering longer with each visit, sitting motionless for hours to watch the play of light on the pools, pressing my hands against the weeping walls to feel the endless pulse of moisture.

The Stonekeepers remain elusive, but I catch glimpses—a shimmer beneath the surface, a silhouette in a distant gallery, a haunting snatch of song echoing through the tunnels. Each sighting draws me deeper, not in pursuit but in a strange kind of harmony. I start to move as they move, pausing to tend the moss gardens, deciphering the ancient tally marks, learning the secret rhythms of the quarry.

I am the Quarry Chronicler. 

Over time, my gear changes. I trade my synthetic ropes for woven vines, my plastic-cased lights for a lantern of bioluminescent crystal. My wetsuit gives way to garments of living moss, my boots to bare feet that learn to grip the damp stone. I still return to the surface, but my stays grow shorter, my eyes more sensitive to the sun.

In the drowned chambers, I begin my work. Using pigments made from the same clay and crystal as the Stonekeepers, I fill a waterproof journal with sketches, maps, observations. I record the water levels, the growth patterns of the ferns, the slow evolutions of the crayfish and cave fish. I transcribe the Stonekeepers' songs, inventing my own notation to capture their unearthly harmonies.

I become a fixture of the quarry, as much a part of its ecosystem as the pale flowers that bloom in the deepest galleries. The Stonekeepers come to tolerate my presence, then to accept it. Sometimes, I awaken from a trance-like state of concentration to find offerings left beside me—a perfectly formed fossil, a chunk of luminous crystal, a water-worn tool marked with symbols I'm only beginning to understand.

In my brief sojourns to the surface, I become a conduit, sharing tales and wisdom from the underworld. I speak to the children of Kellensmere, spinning stories of the Stonekeepers and their realm. Some listen with wonder, others with a strange recognition, as if hearing echoes of a call they will one day answer.

As the years pass, my own transformation becomes undeniable. My skin takes on the pallor of something cave-born, my eyes widen and fill with a silvery sheen. My voice, when I speak, carries the cadence of dripping water. The quarry's chill settles into my bones, a constant companion.

I am not becoming a Stonekeeper, but something akin—a witness, a record-keeper, a voice that carries their story to the world above. In the Drowned Quarries, I have found not just a place, but a purpose. 

And so I continue my work, balanced between two realms. By day, I chronicle the slow poetry of water and stone. By night, I dream of maps that pulse like living things, of hidden rivers that connect all the world's secret places, of a vast and patient pattern that only the Stonekeepers can see.

In the Drowned Quarries of Kellensmere, I have found my place. Not quite human anymore, but not yet something else, I dwell in the margins, in the spaces where elements and epochs meet. I am the Quarry Chronicler, and as long as there are stories to be told, I will remain.
