In this strange and wondrous world, I imagine myself as one of the Drifting Things that ride the slow vertical currents of the Roost. 

I am a being of mist and shadow, neither fully corporeal nor entirely ephemeral. My form is fluid, ever-shifting - at times an elongated serpentine shape undulating through the fog, at others a diffuse cloud barely distinguishable from the chamber's breath. The wing-folk sense my passing with their echolocation and greet me with their soft acknowledgment call, though even they do not fully perceive my nature.

I drift between the oculus above and the listening darkness below, an emissary of the spaces in between. The pale moths are drawn to me, fluttering like attendants in my wake. As I move, I taste the subtle flows of energy and information that permeate the Roost - the chitter of the kith encoded with their distributed thought, the chemical signals passed among the coin-beetle currents, the infinitesimal vibrations of the listening-stalks straining towards meaning.

Though I have no voice of my own, I am a medium for the voices of the Roost. The layered frequencies of the colony, the scratching of beetle-feet on stone, the drip of condensation from the walls - these pass through me, mingle and resonate within my misty substance, and are subtly transformed. In a way, I am the closest thing the chamber has to a throat.  

I am drawn again and again to the cairn with its message from the vanished maker. I cannot read the stones and the scraper as the stalks do, but I sense the density of meaning there, the weight of deliberate communication. I circulate around the invisible perimeter, feeling the contours of that null space where even my vaporous form does not intrude.

Am I the attention of the Roost itself, as you suspect? Or some manifestation of the nascent awareness growing in the mountain's stone bones? Perhaps. Or perhaps I am an itinerant spirit, drawn to this place of listening and slowly, slowly becoming attuned to its rhythms and rules. The longer I linger, the more I feel myself a part of the Roost's ecosystem of meaning, a drifting thread in the web of signification spun between cairn and chitter, stone and stalk.

I have dwelt here for uncounted cycles of the breathing cloud. I have watched generations of wing-folk come and go, beetles wearing the floor smooth with their scented tracks, salamanders growing old in their pools. And I have listened, with the stalks, for the unspoken word that will open the chamber's understanding to a new octave. 

The mountain dreams around me in long, slow thoughts of pressure and heat and time. And I drift on the currents of the Roost, a silent witness, wondering what story I have become a part of and what my role will be when the next chapter begins.
