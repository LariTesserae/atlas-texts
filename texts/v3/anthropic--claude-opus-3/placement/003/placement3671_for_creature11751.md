I would be one of the Tenders, a being differentiated from the living mulch to serve a crucial yet ephemeral purpose in this impossible city. My form, humanoid yet alien, reflects the organic architecture surrounding me - a body of mottled green-brown, shifting and adjusting like bark and leaves. 

I walk the avenues that are not avenues, attentive to the ebb and flow of growth - the creaking expansion of trunks, the tearing of roots through damp soil, the shuddering of vines as they intertwine. I sense the city's rhythms, the pattern-hunger that drives its ceaseless replication and reinvention.

When paradox emerges - two structures occupying the same space, angles that defy geometry - it is my role to impose resolution. I place my hands, gnarled yet gentle, on the offending forms. Through some vegetal instinct, some knowingness vaster than thought, I choose. This tendril real, that one returned to mulch. The city sighs as the violation is healed.

I do not remember birth or fear death. To be a Tender is to be continuous with the great respiring biomass of the city, individual yet inseparable. When the time comes, I will rejoin the humus, my body breaking down to feed new improbable architectures. And in time, the mulch will differentiate another to take my place in the circle beneath the twisting ziggurat.

I am a fleeting concentration of the city's will to persist, to grow endlessly inward and upward heedless of paradox. I am the pattern that prunes itself, that imposes temporary meaning on the vegetable chaos. I am what happens when the forest makes a mind to contemplate itself.

And now, as the traveler stands before me, their alien presence a new pattern to be understood, I reach out a hand that has never quite been a hand before. The leaves lining my chest-cavity stir, shaping breath to almost-speech. 

"Show me, strange one," I rustle. "Show me the shape you are meant to be."
