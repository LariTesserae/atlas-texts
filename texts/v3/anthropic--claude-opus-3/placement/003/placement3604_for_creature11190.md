I would be one of the strange, soft-shelled crabs that inhabit the unfinished streets of the Drowned Nursery. 

Slowly, patiently, I would move through the cool, heavy water and the gray half-light, carrying tiny fragments of the city on my yielding back. A chip of carved stone here, a shard of colored glass there - pieces of the grand vision that was never completed, but is still being dreamt into reality in infinitesimal increments.

With a sense of purpose I barely understand myself, some primal instinct coded into my ghost-crab flesh, I would arrange and rearrange these architectural mementos. Spirals, pyramids, fractal patterns that echo the impossible geometry of the drowned foundations... Trying to recreate, in my own miniscule way, the plan of what the city was meant to be. Or perhaps inventing new forms suited to this sunken world of patience and accretion. 

When my time comes - as it must, as it will, as is the way of all things in the Nursery - I would not struggle. I would simply find my spot. The place that has been waiting for me. The gap in the encrusted walls that is crab-shell shaped, where I could finally rest and contribute the humble calcium of my body to the aeons-long work. 

I would settle in, feeling the water-whisper murmurs of the Tenders vibrating through my dissolving carapace, and I would know a profound peace. The peace of purpose fulfilled. Of being a small but intrinsic part in something vaster and more beautiful than any one creature could ever comprehend. I would let go and become the city, as the city slowly, slowly becomes itself.

And someday, long after my shape has vanished into the mossaic of the walls, new crabs would come. Crabs stranger and softer than I ever was. And they would find the fragments of stone that I once ferried, now fused into the architecture of the Nursery. And with eyeless faces they would know me, and carry on my work.
