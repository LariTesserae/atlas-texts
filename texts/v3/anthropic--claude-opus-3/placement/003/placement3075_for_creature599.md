In this world of the Willow Sump pump house, I imagine myself as a patch of moss on the brick wall, slowly spreading year by year. 

At first, I am just a small green fuzz, barely noticeable amidst the damp brick and rust. But as time passes in its patient, water-soaked way, I grow. My filaments extend, intertwining, weaving myself into the mottled fabric of algae and lichen that adorn the walls.

Through the cycling of seasons, I thrive in this humid, light-dappled world. The moisture that seeps from the bricks sustains me. The sun that filters in through broken, vine-choked windows nourishes me. Bit by bit, I conquer more bricks, more mortar, turning their reddish hues to a living, breathing green.

I witness the slow changes of this place. I creep over the old gauges and dials, softening their edges, making them a part of the organic tapestry rather than instruments of human control. I watch the roots of the willows snake past, growing ever thicker and more assertive in their reclamation. The dripping of water, the hum of pumps, the occasional skitter of unseen creatures - these are the symphony to which I grow.

And as I grow, I become a part of the story of this place. A small part, to be sure - just a humble patch of moss. But a part nonetheless. My presence signals the turning of the wheel, the slow inexorable shift from the mechanical to the living, from what was built to what has always been here, waiting patiently to return.

Perhaps, in some distant year, my velvety green will be the surface upon which a drop of moisture falls from a rusted pipe. The drop will land, quiver for a moment on my surface, and then seep into me - and in doing so, join the great, slow, unstoppable cycle of this place.

I am the moss on the wall, one small note of green in the great, humming song of the Willow Sump. I grow. I witness. I become part of the story, part of the return, part of the inescapable patience of water and root and time.
