In this world of the Cupped City, I imagine myself as a small, translucent fish, making my home in the warm, mineral-rich pools that fill the streets and arcades. 

I swim lazily through the dim underwater light, my scales catching the reflections from the surface and scattering them in shifting patterns on the stone walls. I dart between the tiny towers, exploring the maze-like alleys, brushing against the soft carpets of algae. 

Sometimes I pause beneath one of the half-full thimble cups, pondering what ancient drink still rests inside, a relic of the long-gone builders. I watch the shrimp grazing in their placid herds and the beetles tracing cryptic paths above. Occasionally, I startle as a droplet falls from a roof edge and sends ripples across my whole liquid universe.

Over the ages, I become attuned to the city's rhythms - the gradual rise and fall of the water levels marking the passage of seasons, the infinitesimal changes as the stone settles and new mineral blooms emerge. Though short-lived compared to the patient city-organism, I am a mote in its greater awareness.

When a human visitor appears, enormous and strange above the water, I am one of the myriad tiny eyes through which the city regards this curiosity. I turn my head to the sky to take in the vast, kind darkness of its gaze peering down between the rooftops. A flick of my fins, a shimmer of my scales, becomes part of the city's slow contemplation of the visitor.

I am a fleeting but harmonious note in the city's long, tranquil song - a silver scale reflecting the pale light, a tiny heartbeat pulsing to the tempo of stone and water and deep time. Witnessed and witnessing, I swim on through the drowned streets, content in my place within the city's dream.
