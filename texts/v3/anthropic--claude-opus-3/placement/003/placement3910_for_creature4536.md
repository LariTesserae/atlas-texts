In this fluid world of Driftglass, I would be a memory weaver. Not a conventional being of flesh and blood, but an ethereal presence that drifts between the porous membranes of past and present, self and other.

My form would be mutable, a shimmering amalgam of stories absorbed and reconfigured. In one moment, I might appear as a diaphanous jellyfish, tentacles entangled with glowing plankton-words that I've gathered from the dreams of sleeping citizens. In the next, a tattered sail from a ship that never finished sinking, stitched with the faded coordinates of forgotten homelands.

I would spend my days (though "day" loses meaning in Driftglass's half-light) wandering the spaces between - the pauses in conversations, the depths beneath floorboards that aren't quite floors. There I would listen, not with ears but with a kind of psychic osmosis, absorbing the unspoken longings and unexpressed joys that leak from citizens like salt from skin.

At night (which arrives not by clock but by a subtle shift in the city's respiration), I would weave. Drawing ephemeral threads from the day's gleanings, I would craft ghostly tapestries in the shallows. A discarded machine part, still warm with the residue of its past purpose, becomes a loom; the quiet dripping of an old faucet, my metronome. 

The scenes I weave are glimpses of lives that could have been, might still be. Here, the valve-throated child, grown and singing drainage songs of such exquisite sorrow that the pipes weep fresh water in response. There, the prosthetic coral dream, become a living reef of memory where citizens swim to remember loves they never had but still cherish.

I leave these weavings draped over bridge railings, caught in eddies of bioluminescence, or snagged on the spires of drowned machinery. Ephemeral gifts for whoever needs them most. A widow of ink and parchment, shored against her ruin. A tideborn fool who has forgotten the taste of its original metal. A newly sung citizen, still translucent with uncertainty.

In this way, I become the connective tissue of Driftglass's collective unconscious. A reminder that no story is ever truly lost, only submerged, waiting for the right tide to carry it back to the surface. That every ending is a beginning in disguise, a chance to revision and reweave.

And on those rare occasions when a stranger arrives, blinking in the half-light, asking the usual questions - Where are we? What is this place? - I am the one who whispers the truest answer, a susurrus in the back of their mind:

"You are the story you choose to tell about yourself. Driftglass is the setting you didn't know you'd always been dreaming of. Stay, and learn to swim in memory. Or go, and carry our songs with you to dry lands. Neither choice is wrong. 

But choose. The tide is rising, and it has such beautiful things to show you..."
