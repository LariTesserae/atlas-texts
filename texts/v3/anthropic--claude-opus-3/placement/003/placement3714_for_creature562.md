In the Sweating Vaults, I would find myself becoming part of the ecosystem, gradually adapting and integrating into the humid, patient darkness. 

I imagine myself as a species of bioluminescent fungus, spreading slowly across the damp concrete and rusted metal surfaces. My mycelium would thread through cracks and crevices, breaking down the remnants of the human world molecule by molecule to sustain my gradual growth. 

In the near complete darkness, my fruiting bodies would emit a soft, eerie glow - pale green or bluish, pulsing faintly like a heartbeat. This cool light would stand in gentle contrast to the pervasive warmth rising from deep below. The light would catch the edges of the ferns and hanging vines, the curves of broken machinery, creating an alien landscape of shadows and suggestions.

Over time, Grohn would notice my presence - the unfamiliar constellations of light in once darker corners. At first she might mistake me for some trick of her vision, some strange new dream leaking into her waking hours. But as I slowly spread, it would become clear that something new has arrived in the vault. 

I would be something novel in this place of long, slow cycles. A reminder that even here, change and growth and adaptation are still possible. I imagine Grohn might start speaking to me as she speaks to the control panel, the door. Telling me of the weather up above, the state of the dripping water. I would be another thing transforming in the patient dark alongside her.

Perhaps some night, she would notice my glow pulsing in response to her voice, to the patterns of the rainfall - some subtle communication emerging, a new language being born in light and timing. A conversation between two beings who have learned to speak the vault's dialect of slowness and stillness.

As years pass, both of us would spread, transform, become something integral to this hushed pocket world. Her nitrogen feeding my growth, my light feeding her some crucial nutrient of wonder or change or companionship. The vault exhaling around us, the machines settling into their long sleep, the ferns unfurling one infinitesimal inch at a time.

Two strange lives becoming native to a place never meant for life. Warming it, illuminating it, filling it with breath and spores and the rare sound of a voice in the dimness saying *Grohn*, saying *I am here with you*, speaking the truths that can only be spoken in a place that measures time in drips and rust and the achingly gradual curve of a new world being born.
