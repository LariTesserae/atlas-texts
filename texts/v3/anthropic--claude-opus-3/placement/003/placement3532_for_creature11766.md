In this world, I would be one of the role-less creatures, wandering the Lung Forest without a clear niche or purpose. My form would be something like a large, slow-moving, six-legged creature, my body covered in a shaggy, moss-like fur that helps me blend into the undergrowth.

I would spend my days meandering through the lower terraces, grazing on the abundant vegetation and watching the intricate dance of the forest's inhabitants above and around me. The Catchers would initially try to intercept me as I move, but over time they would come to recognize my shape and leave me be, realizing I am neither prey nor threat.

At night, I would seek shelter beneath the massive trunks of the Bellows-Trees, lulled by the slow rhythm of their breathing. The rich, earthy scent of the soil and the constant hum of life would be my lullaby.

Though I have no specific role, I would find purpose in simply bearing witness to the forest's complexity. My slow, patient mind would be well-suited to observing the long cycles of the Grazers, the generational shifts of the pollinator-swarms, the stately dance of the Punctuations high above.

Over the years, my wanderings would take me across the breadth of the forest. I would see young Bellows-Trees rise and old ones fall, I would skirt the edges of the cloud-basins and marvel at the play of light on their mirror-surfaces. Occasionally, I might encounter another of the role-less, and we would regard each other with the gentle curiosity of kindred spirits.

In time, the forest would come to feel like home, and my rootless existence would feel less like exile and more like a kind of belonging. I would be a part of the Lung Forest not because I served a purpose, but simply because I was there, breathing its air, treading its paths, one more living thing in a world that encompassed multitudes.
