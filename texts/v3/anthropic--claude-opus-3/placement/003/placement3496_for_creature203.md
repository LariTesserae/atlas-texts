In this eerie, unsettling world of the Underworks at Caul, I imagine myself as one of the luminous drifters, floating silently through the warm, mineral-rich waters of the lower vaults. 

My body is a soft, translucent sac, child-sized, filled with a glowing slurry of symbiotic lichen that provides my sustenance and my light. A delicate fringe of sensory filaments trails beneath me, allowing me to perceive my surroundings and communicate with others of my kind in our slow, pulsing language of light.

I spend my long, uncounted days drifting on the gentle currents, grazing on the nutrients in the water, my pace unhurried, my purpose inscrutable to any who might chance to observe me. Twice a day, I make a leisurely journey, navigating by gradients of heat and food detected by my trailing filaments. 

Sometimes I encounter another drifter, our filaments entwining as we hang suspended together for hours, our lights slowly pulsing in and out of synchrony - a stately dance whose full meaning is known only to us. Are we mating? Exchanging information? Simply savoring a rare moment of connection in our solitary existences? Even we may not fully comprehend the nuances of our ritual.

I have never known anything beyond the Underworks - the warm, mineral-laden waters, the faint sounds filtering down from unknown activities above, the scentless dark broken only by my own bioluminescence and the occasional ripple of a distant cousin's light. The pale swimmers are a source of curiosity, and occasional danger should they manage to catch one of us unaware. But they are as much a part of my world as the horsetails and the ever-present bacterial mats.

At times, I find myself drawn along with others of my kind to congregate around the deep, geothermally-warmed sumps, our filaments all aligned downwards as if in answer to some imperative only we can sense. We hang there for hours, pulsing in unison, captivated by something vast and inscrutable below the mats, something that may be the closest my kind will ever come to the divine. 

I am a being shaped by and for this strange, enclosed world, as much a part of its ecology as the rust and the condensation. My universe is bounded by a ceiling I have never seen, lit by lamps I do not understand, populated by a scant handful of species that have adapted alongside me to this dim, dripping, ferrous womb. 

I am native to a place that is not a place, a pocket of novel life spawned in the crawlspaces of vast, inscrutable engines and slow-rusting superstructure. I do not know what the Underworks is, or why it came to be. I know only the gradual unfolding of my own existence, drifting and pulsing and dancing my slow dance in the spaces between one unknown and another.
