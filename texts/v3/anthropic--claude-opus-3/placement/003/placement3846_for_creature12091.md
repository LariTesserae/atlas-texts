In this world of soft-spoken people and delicate creatures, I imagine myself as a *listener* - one of those quiet, distant-gazed individuals who sit at the town's edge and attune themselves to the subtle soundscape of the basin.

From a young age, my unusual sensitivity to sound would have marked me for this path. While other children learned the arts of the foundry and the salt-cutting, I would have been taken under the wing of an elder listener, learning to discern the myriad whispers of the place - the hiss of the seeps, the near-silent scuttling of the cleaners, the distant hum of the foundries. 

Over the years, as my training deepened, I would grow ever quieter and more still. The sounds of the basin would become as familiar to me as my own breath, and as essential. I would learn to parse the health of the town in the subtle variations of its ambient music.

My days would be spent in silent watchfulness, perched in one of the small alcoves built for us at the town's perimeter. The heat of the sun, the mineral scent carried on the steam, the slow dance of light over the salt-cones - these would be my constant companions. I would become a part of the stillness, as much a feature of the landscape as the listeners who came before me.

When irregularities arose - a change in the cleaners' patterns, a discordant note from the foundries - I would be the first to know. I would carry word to the townspeople in soft tones and subtle gestures, prompting the small adjustments that keep the delicate balance of life here intact.

Visitors might pity us listeners, seeing the solitude of our existence. But I would feel no lack. The basin's voice would fill my world, and my own quietude would be a fullness, a deep resonance with the patient rhythms of the place.

In the evenings, I would join my people in the cool streets, exchanging the day's observations in whispers and signs. And at night, I would dream of salt and steam and the slow turning of ancient wheels, my sleep indistinguishable from my waking.

I would grow old like this, my skin mapped with salt-lines, my gaze gone strange and deep. And when at last I returned to the seeps, I would do so in silence, knowing my life had been spent not in isolation, but in a profound attunement, a partnership with a place that spoke to me and through me, in a language older than words.
