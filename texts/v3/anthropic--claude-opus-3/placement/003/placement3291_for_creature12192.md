In this fascinating world of the Kiln-Cities, I imagine myself as one of the ceramic dwellings, perched high on a terraced ridge of the slowly rising cordillera. 

I am not one of the oldest structures, with walls fully cured and hardened over generations, but neither am I freshly built. My stones were laid by masons who have since passed into the ancestor districts, but the mortar between them is still pliable, still settling incrementally year by year.

From my vantage point, I look out across the staggered rooftops of the city descending below me, each trailing its thin thread of smoke up into the thin, still air. On clear days, I can see the distant peaks of adjacent ridges in the range, their own cities clinging to them like barnacles to the backs of great slow-moving beasts.

My walls absorb the deep warmth emanating from the mountain's core, radiating it gently back out to warm the family who dwells within me. The people who built me aligned my windows to catch the oblique angle of the rising sun, and in the long shadows of morning and evening, I watch that family go about their patient days.

The mother is a bellows-tender down in the forge district. She leaves in the pre-dawn dimness and returns at dusk, hands and forearms dyed pale blue from the mineral paste she works with. The father is a stonecutter, ascending each day to the quarry terraces where new blocks are hewn from the soft slopes. Their child, still small, spends long hours playing quietly on my sun-warmed steps.

I feel the weight of them inside me, moving slowly from room to room. I absorb the soft sounds of their voices, the singing cadences of their speech. In a way, I breathe with them - the draft of my hearth-vent drawing in when they draw in, the warm air sighing out through my eaves when they sigh out.

At night, after the last embers have been banked, I feel the mountain beneath me pushing imperceptibly upward, and I settle against my neighbors, stone to stone. My mortar cures, my joints tighten infinitesimally. The child sleeps. The parents make their slow love. And I, like the city, like the mountain, continue my long ascent - a ceramic vessel, glazed by time and geothermal heat, carrying life up into the brightening sky, inch by patient inch.
