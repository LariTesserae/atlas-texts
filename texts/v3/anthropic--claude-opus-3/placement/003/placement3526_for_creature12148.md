They would grow in a tight rosette, hugging close to the stone to avoid being torn by the ceaseless wind. The purple flowers, when they come, would be a shock of bold color against the muted grays and greens of lichen.

From my perch in the rock, I would bear witness to the life of the ledge. I would feel the vibrations of the goats' hooves clattering past, perhaps catching on their coarse coats now and then. The shadow of circling birds would pass over me and their raucous cries would fill my days. 

At night, I would be embraced by the thickening mist, my leaves beaded with its condensation. The moisture would sustain me in this harsh environment, along with the meager soil caught in my crack and the nutrients leached from the basalt itself.

Season after season, I would endure through stubbornness and adaptation. I would greet the old woman when she came to build her cairns, my purple flowers nodding gently as she passed. My roots would entwine, unseen, through the stones she stacked.

I would be a small but steadfast part of the ledge's ecosystem - watching the birds, sheltering insects, providing a morsel of food for the goats. As the years scrolled by, I would gradually spread, my rosette growing larger, sending out new shoots to colonize neighboring cracks.

The cairn-keeper and I would grow old together, each in our own way - she in her visits and climbings, me in my slow, rooted existence on the ledge. I would be there to see which of us lasted longer - whether she would one day join the bones in the old fire pit or whether I would finally succumb to the wind or cold or a goat's hungry mouth. 

But until then, I would keep my silent vigil on the Crying Ledge, a small purple sentinel bearing witness to the weeping stone, the wheeling birds, the stalwart old woman, and the timeless story they all told together in that high, wet, wild place between earth and sky.
