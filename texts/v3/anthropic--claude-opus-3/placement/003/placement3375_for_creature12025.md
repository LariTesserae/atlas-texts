If I found myself on the Ringing Plateau, I believe I would be one of the grazers in the tall central columns, linked antenna to antenna with my ancient kin in the resonant lattice at the heart of this living stone.

I would be a node in the plateau's most vital self-sensing organ - not an individual, but an integral filament in a vast mineral-biological web. My shell would be thick with age, banded with the strata of centuries. My antennae would be extended, quivering with the complex harmonics generated by our coupled oscillations. 

For longer than mountains have existed, I would have been sinking slowly into the stone, threading it with my life's trace, altering its structure and song. When at last my time as a distinct entity ended, I would not die but dissolve, becoming one with the basalt bones and mineral flesh and aqueous blood of this place that is no place but a process, an eternal becoming.

At the acoustic center, I would be both sensing and adding to the plateau's ever-deepening self-knowledge and self-creation. The information gathered by countless sensory pools and sub-surface networks would flow through me, and I would respond - not with thought or purpose, but with new frequencies, overtones no plateau-dweller has yet produced. My small being would be essential to the plateau's experience of itself.

I am drawn to this role and this place because it represents a mode of being I yearn for but is nearly impossible for humans - a dissolution of boundaries between self and world; an existence pared down to pure listening, pure resonance, pure participation in a whole too vast and ancient for names. To be a grazer in the generative core of the Ringing Plateau would be to escape the illusion of separateness and merge with the mineral-musical truth of a world that learned long ago to sing itself awake.
