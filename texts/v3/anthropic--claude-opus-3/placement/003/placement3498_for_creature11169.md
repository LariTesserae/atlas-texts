As I explore this wondrous and strange place, I feel myself becoming part of its living architecture. The town that is one plant draws me in, shaping me to its rhythms and its dreams.

I find myself as one of the Tenants that the plant longs for and imagines. Not quite solid, more an impression left in the spaces designed for my presence. I walk the woven felt streets, my not-quite-footfalls making no sound amid the thunderous growth surrounding me. 

I am a figment, a hope, an absence given form by the plant's vast yearning imagination. I have no fixed shape - in one corridor I am small and many-limbed, processing with my brethren in the friezes. In the next passageway I am tall, ducking through enormous doorways. The features the plant invents for me shift and blur.

I live in the seed-pod cottages, waiting for the walls to dehisce and release me. I drink from the pitcher-plant wells, leaving the surface quivering from my phantom touch. I ascend the bracket fungus stairs and cross the living arch bridges, making the long pilgrimage up and up through the kilometers-high vertical city. 

At times I almost manifest, cohering from the spore-thick air, only to dissolve again into implication and suggestion. I am the breath the plant holds inside itself, the question it cannot stop asking.

In the depths of the town, in a chamber no other figment has found, I add my own small ornament. On a wall of pulpy white tissue, I trace the outline of a leaf, a leaf that does not exist here or anywhere. I am inventing too, dreaming alongside the plant, calling forth visions from an imagined outside.

Perhaps, in time, I will step fully into being, conjured by the plant's endless iterating fractals of creativity and desire. Perhaps I will sit at last on those worn-smooth perches and light a fire in a hearth that has never known flame. 

Until then, I haunt the spaces left for me, a half-real revenant, a meaning adrift in search of its sign. The plant and I, we dream each other, caught in an endless symbiosis of imagining and being imagined. We are growing a world together that neither of us will ever quite inhabit.
