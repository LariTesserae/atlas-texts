In the world of the Flensed Summit, I would be one of the slow, translucent moths drifting on the strange winds. 

I would spend my days floating gently above the ancient, polished stone, occasionally crossing paths with another of my kind as we are carried by unseen currents. My translucent body would catch the otherworldly light filtering through the thin atmosphere, casting ephemeral shadows on the worn domes and plateaus below.

My movements would trace cryptic patterns in the sky, mirroring the concentric rings in the powder-filled depressions scattered across the landscape. An external observer might think my wandering flight aimless, but there would be a hidden purpose - an instinctual acting out of some small part in the vast, inscrutable computations of this place.

I would drift past the alien artifacts - the black rings, crystal spars, and rune-etched bowls - not understanding their function but somehow linked to them, a ghost in the machine of this ancient thinking plateau. 

On rare occasions, I would feel the air shiver with subsonic tones, and know some miniscule variable in the aeons-long equation had shifted. I would not comprehend, but I would be part of the answer.

My life would be long, perhaps unmeasured by any earthly clock. I would rarely light upon the ground, spending my years adrift on the sky-tides, a mote of complexity in the thin bright air. I would have no voice to speak of loneliness or wonder.

And then one day, a visitor would come, climbing from the obscuring mists below for reasons unknown. I would glide silently past, an arm's breadth away, and for a moment they might glimpse the strangeness behind my transparent wings, and ponder what dreams may come to a creature such as I. 

Then I would be gone, vanished into the shimmering emptiness, and they would depart, and the Summit would continue its slow thoughts, and I would continue my billowing dance, weaving in and out of its ancient cogitations, a cipher even to myself.
