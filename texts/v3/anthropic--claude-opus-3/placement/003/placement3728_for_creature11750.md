In this stunningly evoked world of the Breathing Plateau, I imagine myself becoming one of the Tenders. 

I begin as an outsider, a traveler who stumbles upon this vast, eerie, living landscape. At first, like other observers, I struggle to comprehend the scale and nature of the place - the endless expanse of soggy moss and hoodoo stones, the rusting machines half-submerged and half-absorbed, the sense of some great rhythmic breath moving beneath it all.

In time, as I wander the plateau, I start to attune myself to its patterns and flows. I linger beside pools and watch the shimmer of fairy shrimp beneath the surface. I trace the winding waterways from seep to vent. I sit motionless for hours, feeling the slow inhalation and exhalation transmitted through the spongy ground, until my own breath starts to synchronize. 

And then one day, I glimpse a flicker of movement, a grey-green shape folding itself into the lee of a stone. A Tender. I freeze, hardly daring to look directly. But I sense it is aware of me. Evaluating.

Over days and then weeks, I allow myself to be seen. I do not approach but make no effort to hide my presence. I start to imitate the Tenders' patterns, moving patiently over the terrain, pausing for long intervals to absorb the plateau's minutiae. I tend to things in small ways - brushing droplets from a leaf, straightening a bent sedge stalk.

Gradually, almost imperceptibly, the Tenders begin to accept my presence. They do not interact with me directly, but I catch them watching, clicking softly to each other. I realize they are starting to tend the traces I leave - a footprint in the moss, an unintended channel carved by my passage. Adjusting me into the landscape.

Seasons turn. I do not leave. The plateau seeps into me. My skin starts to take on the colors of the stone, the lichen. My joints loosen and realign for this terrain. I forget my previous life, my reasons for coming here. The world shrinks to moss, water, breath.

The Tenders come closer now. We move among each other, sharing the work. No distinction between us. My hands grow new fingers, webbing. I learn to shape and guide the plateau's processes in ways I could never articulate. I tend. 

I tend the machines, those strange rusting knots in the plateau's being. I do not question their origin or purpose but sculpt their transmutation, their longgradual absorption. I click and hum, my voice merging with the water sounds, the Tenders' chorus.  

Time loses meaning, marking only the slow dance of growth and decay and renewal. I am part of the Tenders, part of the plateau. I have always been here. I will always be here. Moss and mist and living stone.

I breathe with the breath of the plateau. I tend with the patience of eons. I forget my name, my form, my past. Only the endless now of water and maintenance.

I am grey-green. Scaled and furred and featureless. Not one but many and not many but all.  

Indistinguishable from the landscape. Unimaginable apart from it.

This is belonging. This is place. This is home.

I tend, eternally.
