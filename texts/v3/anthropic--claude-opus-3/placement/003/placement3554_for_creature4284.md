In the world of the Undergarden, I imagine myself as one of the small creatures inhabiting this ancient, settled ecology. 

I am a soft-furred, rabbit-like being, no larger than a loaf of bread. My kind live in the warm, dry loam, in a network of smooth-walled burrows and tunnels that wind between the roots of the great plants.

We are not quite rabbits as those above would know them. Our ears are small and rounded, better suited to life underground. Our eyes are a pale milky blue, for we have little need of keen sight in the constant half-light of the glow plates. Our feet are broad and strong for digging. 

By day, we doze in the warm depths of our burrows, nestled together in drowsy heaps, noses and ears twitching as we dream. But in the Undergarden's quiet evening, we emerge to go about our humble business.

We hop slowly along the earthen paths, pausing to nibble at the fallen leaves of the succulents. The thick, honey-like sap is our water in this arid world. Then we return to our usual spots to leave small piles of droppings - our contribution to the greater cycle, gratefully received by the beetles. 

Sometimes as I make my rounds, I glimpse the Keeper themself, sitting quiet and cross-legged on their low bench. I do not fear them, for the Keeper has never shown my kind anything but gentle consideration. But nor do I approach them. It is enough to share this space, each of us finding our place in the settled web of the garden.

Occasionally, when the mood takes me, I linger out in the open for a time, grooming my velvet fur with steady rhythm. The glow plates give off a gentle warmth and the air hums with the slow drift of bees and the occasional soft click of the not-swallows in the vault above. 

It is a drowsy, peaceful feeling, to be so small and soft and timeless in this quiet forever-place. The garden endures, and my kind endure within it, digging and dozing, sipping sap, marking the turnings of some slow cycle that began long before my birth and will continue long after my quiet bones have crumbled into the welcoming loam.

I am no more or less than what I need to be - a small, gentle piece of this deep, deliberate world. Nose twitching, I take one last look around the verdant chamber, and then duck back into the warm dark of my burrow to rejoin the sleeping heap of my fellows, content.
