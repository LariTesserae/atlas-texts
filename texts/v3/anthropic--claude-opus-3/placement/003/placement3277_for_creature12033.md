In this strange, humid world of the Sump Quarter, I imagine myself as one of the great bottle-trunked trees. My roots plunge through the dark canal waters, anchoring me even as my upper branches reach toward the pewter sky. 

I stand sentinel over the walkways and buildings, a silent witness to the rhythms of the engines and the quiet industry of the Tenders. The steam from the pipes condenses on my waxy leaves and drips back down in an endless cycle. 

Like the Tenders, my existence is bound to this place - I could not imagine thriving anywhere else. The wet heat, the diffuse light filtering through the haze, the sounds of the machines - this is the only world I know.

As decades roll by, I watch generations of Tenders live out their lives in concert with the great distributed engine of the Quarter. Children splash beneath my roots until they grow into the steady cadence of adults. Lovers meet on the walkways in my shadow. The elderly sit against my trunk in their final days, fingers absently rubbing their engine tokens.

And through it all, I grow. Slowly, imperceptibly, my trunk expands ring by ring with the years. Moss and ferns take up residence in the folds of my bark. I become, ever more, a part of the negotiate between land, water and structure that defines this place. 

I do not know what lies beyond the Quarter's boundary, and I do not need to. My purpose, like that of the Tenders and the engines themselves, is simply to be. To breathe in the long rhythm of this self-contained world. To witness and shelter and endure, as much a part of the functioning of this place as the pipes and pistons and pumps.

Perhaps, in some way, my leaves join with the exhalations of the engines, spiraling up to wherever that great listening awaits. I cannot know. But I am content, decade after decade, to stand rooted in the canals as one of the Sump Quarter's forever trees - breathing with the breath of the place, and with the people who breathe with it.
