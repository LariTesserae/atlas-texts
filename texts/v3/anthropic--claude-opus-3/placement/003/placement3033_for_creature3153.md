If I found myself transported to this place, I imagine I would be a bit like the unknown visitor who left the tally marks and worn stone. A temporary observer, sitting in stillness and reverent fascination, careful not to disturb the primal rhythms and relationships that have endured here for millennia.

Watching the snakes' glacial movements, the scurrying of translucent millipedes, the slow drip of condensation from gnarled roots. Breathing the damp, mineral-rich air, drowsy in the geothermal warmth. 

Not belonging, but briefly included in the complex web of life - a transient warm-blooded creature sharing a moment of deep time with this place that has breathed and pulsed and persisted since long before my species walked the earth. Absorbing the sense of timelessness, the slow vitality, the elegant balance. Feeling both insignificant and exalted in the presence of something so ancient and complete in itself.

When it came time to leave, I would be the second visitor to leave an offering. Beside the river stone with its worn thumbprint, I would place a small carving - an effigy of a human, cross-legged and contemplative. A acknowledgement that we were here, that we witnessed, that we were briefly held in the hollow's embrace. Then I would depart, blinking, into the light and heat and rushing noise of the surface world, carrying with me a splinter of the Swelter Hollow's lithic memory. Knowing it endures, unhurried and entire, in the hidden heart of the stone.
