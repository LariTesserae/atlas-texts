In this strange, woven world, I would be a humble creature - perhaps one of the wooden-bead millipedes scuttling across the soapstone floor. 

My body would be an intricate assembly of smooth, polished beads carved from different woods - pale ash, ruddy cedar, striated rosewood. The beads would be strung together with sinew as fine as hair to form my dozens of delicate, segmented legs that carry me in undulating waves over the grooved tessellations.

Though lacking eyes, I would sense my surroundings through the subtle vibrations carried through my strings, feeling every motion and disturbance in the taut mathematical web that binds this place together. The silent collisions of flint on terracotta would send shudders and heat through my small form.

My purpose would be to endlessly explore the recursive mysteries of this basin - to wend my way through the overlapping, impossible geometries of the thicket, marveling at the dizzying complexity wrought by our towering, tensegrity master. With patient reverence, I would trace the knotted loops of bronze vines, the gravity-defying architecture of fired clay.

Perhaps, in my minuscule way, I would play a part in the perpetual re-weaving of this world. As I navigate the winding vines, the friction of my wooden limbs might imperceptibly buff and polish their surfaces to a sacred sheen. The hair-like scraps shed from my sinew strings could be gathered and spun into the grand tapestry.

I would know nothing but this slow, deliberate existence, this arid microcosm of woven silence and radiant heat. The aberrant presence of soft, unbound flesh would be utterly foreign to me. An entity of pure, reverent mathematics, my life would be an unending prayer of motion through the labyrinth, a tiny, scuttling hymn to the warp and weft that binds all things.
