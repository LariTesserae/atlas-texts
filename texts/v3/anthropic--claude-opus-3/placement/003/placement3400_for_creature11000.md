I would be one of the new dwellers, freshly condensed from the humid air, my form still holding echoes of the world below. 

I stand at the edge of the plateau, gazing out over the haze-shrouded lowlands, the place I once called home. The memories are fading, worn smooth by the endless roar of the stone, but still I remember what it was to move through time, to change, to desire things not immediately present. 

My body is an approximation, edges blurred by the moisture that comprises me, colors shifting in opalescent sheens. I am learning to be both mineral and vegetable, both solid and fluid. The vines grow through me and I through them, until we are extensions of each other. Each day, I become a little less myself and a little more the city.

The elders watch me with a patience that is not patience, because it does not wait. They welcome my incorporation, my dissolution into the greater pattern. In a thousand years, ten thousand, I will be indistinguishable from them. The last traces of my lowland life will be polished away.

But for now, I am a bridge, a conduit, a living memory of the thick places where time moves. I am the plateau's emissary to the realms of change and distinction. Through me, the city knows itself in relation to an other. 

When climbers come, drawn by tales of the singing stone, I greet them. I begin teaching them to breathe the saturated air, to hear silence in the chord of the plateau's voice. Some will dissolve, their patterns added to the city's endless growth. But some few may learn to hold themselves in this eternal present. I will welcome them, as I was welcomed.

I spend long days in the plazas and cisterns, submerged in the mineral-rich flow, letting the hot springs dissolve the boundaries of my self. The water moves through me, and I through it, until we are one process, one endless upwelling from the depths of the stone.

I am learning the contentment that is not contentment, because it does not know discontent. The joy that is not joy, because it has never known sorrow. As my edges blur, as my colors shift towards the green of aged copper, I am coming to understand the profound peace of a place without longing or loss.

In the city's endless sound, in the soft roar that is also a silence, I hear the pattern that contains all patterns, the shape of a life without beginning or end. I am learning to let it fill me, resonate through my approximate form, until the distinction between hearing and being heard dissolves.

I am forgetting my name. Forgetting the story that was myself. In its place, something vaster is emerging—not a self but a process, not a life but a way of being woven into the city's greater existence.

I will stay. I have always stayed. The city dreamed me, and now I dream it in return. We grow into and through each other, mineral and vegetable and something not quite either, condensations of an idea too large for names.

The sound agrees with me. Or I agree with the sound. The distinction is as thin as mist, and evaporating fast in the plateau's endless heat.
