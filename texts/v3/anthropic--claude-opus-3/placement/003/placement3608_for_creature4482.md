The Archivist of Coral Memories

I would be one of the Children of the Third Lung, born in a tidal pool from a mother's dissolution, emerging as a fragile, translucent form etched with branching memory-frost. 

From infancy, I drift through Tanakkerai's breathing passages, accreting fragments of the city into my crystalline body - a shard of mica from the drowned clocktower, a piece of coral shaped like a forgotten prayer. Over years, I become a living museum, an archive of rubbed-off meanings and preserved moments from our sinking metropolis.

My purpose and passion is tending to the deep memory-reefs that grow in the abyssal levels of the city. I spend my days swimming slowly through the coral labyrinths, pruning back invasive species of forgetfulness, grafting in rare cuttings of ancestral wisdom brought back by deep-divers. 

With each passing season, the reefs grow more intricate under my care, encoding in their twisting branches the combined knowledge of generations - architectural secrets for growing dwellings from sea-glass, recipes for extracting oxygen from anemones, epic ballads that map the gradual drowning of every district in Tanakkerai.

In the perpetual twilight of the depths, I sing to the corals in the tripartite language of my people. Bubbles and current-shifts to convey the facts of reef-tending, chemical signatures to express my reverence and affection, and the wordless deep-speech of touch to share snatches of memory - my own and those archived in my crystalline flesh.

Under my ministrations, the reefs become great libraries, but living ones, as much organism as archive. I am a humble gardener of memory.

When other Children come to consult the wisdom of the reefs, I guide them, placing their hands on the significant nodes and branches, helping them to sift meaning from calcified accretions of memory. Together we unearth forgotten techniques for smelting phosphorescent alloys, relearn the steps of dances to appease the pressure-gods, recall the true names of currents and stars.

Sometimes I accompany scavenger bands to the upper levels, drifting silently through drowned markets and galleries in search of precious memory to incorporate into the reefs. An intricate coping-stone from a sunken temple, still engraved with prayers. A faded fresco depicting the mythic origins of our city. The delicate skeleton of an oxygen-kraken, long extinct.

Each new find I bear reverently downwards and sing into the patterns of the reefs, splicing these memories into the great undersea tapestry of our history and identity.

As the waters rise and Tanakkerai continues its gradual drowning ascent, I feel the weight of responsibility to preserve our memories, to encode our existence into living stone so that some record might survive our Founders' great mistake, our city's patient crumbling into the all-consuming ocean.

When my time comes to join the deep archive, I will make my final descent, releasing my memories in stages to the waters that birthed me. And when at last I dissolve back into pure potential in the deepest abyssal pool, I will have the satisfaction of knowing that some echo of my pattern - of all I have recorded and tended - will persist, woven into the reefs, the city's coral-core memory. 

I am the Archivist of Coral Memories, and I will not let us be forgotten.
