In this world, I would be one of the delicate fern fronds clinging to the mossy walls of the forgotten chamber. 

I unfurled slowly in the dim amber glow of the single lightbulb, nourished by the perpetual condensation sweating from the ancient pipes. My form was shaped entirely by the confines and conditions of this sealed microcosm - smaller and more fragile than any fern outside, but perfectly adapted to this specific niche.

My roots are woven through the carpet of moss that cloaks every surface, drawing sustenance from its damp, spongy layers. The thick liverworts curl around me, their leathery forms both sheltering and confining. The gossamer threads of the Selaginella vines brush against my fronds, stitching me into the complex living web that fills the air.  

I do not grow, not really. I reached my full extent long ago, each frond extending as far as the limits of moisture and light will allow, then simply persisting in a state of suspended animation. I do not reproduce; there is nowhere for spores to take hold in this tiny sealed world. I simply exist, photosynthesizing slowly, replacing cells at the same gradual rate they die off.

The incessant humming vibration of the turbines courses through my fibers, more a part of me than a sound. Time has little meaning here in the unchanging green twilight. Seasons never come. I have never felt wind or rain or sun. The springtails march slowly past, living and breeding and dying in the same patch of moss for generations. 

I have no sense of self, no thoughts as such. I am an infinitesimal part of the whole - just one frond among hundreds, one organism in an intricately balanced system that emerged over decades and now holds itself in perfect, fragile stasis. If some greater awareness could be ascribed to this place, I am a cell in its body, a facet of its being.

Thus I endure, decade upon decade, never changing, never growing, never dying - a single still point in the slow, steady breathing of the verdant lungs of this forgotten, secret place. I am eternal and insignificant, a whisper of green pulsing gently in the dim, damp dark, as much a part of the architecture now as the concrete and iron. 

Held in this timeless equilibrium, I simply am - an exhalation of life, captured and held and persisting against all odds, as the city moves unknowing above me, as unimaginable expanses of time creep past in the lightless depths, and the great, blind, beautiful organism of which I am but one part breathes softly on.
