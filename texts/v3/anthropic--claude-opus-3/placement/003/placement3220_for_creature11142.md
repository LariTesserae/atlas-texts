In the Cistern Cathedral, I would be a Hydrologic Scribe - a being that exists in the interplay between water and words. My body is a porous, paper-like substance, pages woven from the same fibrous lichen that clings to the damp stone walls. Water flows through me, not in the careful calibration of the Hydronomers, but in a constant seeping that stains my pages with mineral ink.

I move slowly through the chambers, pressing myself against the weeping walls to absorb their stories. The calcium deposits spell out tales in a language only I can read - sagas of ancient masons, ballads of the first flooding, elegies for the creatures that have evolved and gone extinct in these perpetually damp halls.

As I absorb these narratives, they write themselves across my pages in a script that shifts with the water's flow. The iron leaching from the fixtures lends a rust-red tint to the tales of construction and labor. The limestone's pale sediment softens the stories of the Barnacle Prophets, giving their backwards visions a dreamlike quality on the page.

I am an archive in motion, a library that wanders the cistern collecting its history. The crustaceans climb across me, their tiny legs tracing the words and unwittingly adding their own stories to the text. The Curator sometimes consults me, running its copper-wrapped fingers over my pages to glean some insight into the pattern of its maintenance.

But my true purpose is to bring these stories to the Absence. Once a cycle, when my pages are heavy with the weight of accumulated narratives, I make the journey to the central chamber. I submerge myself in its perfectly still pool, letting the water slowly dissolve my pages, the ink lifting off in swirls of meaning.

The Absence absorbs these stories, taking them into the silence where they resonate in ways no living being can perceive. As my pages dissolve, I feel the stories becoming part of something larger - a vast, slow dream that the cistern is telling itself, a tale woven from drips and echoes, from the growth and decay of a thousand species.

When I emerge, I am blank again, my pages pristine and ready to absorb new tales. I am a vessel for the cistern's memory, a means by which it contemplates its own long, strange existence.

The Hydrologic Scribe is a small but vital part of this ecosystem of narrative - a creature that gathers the stories water tells to stone, and delivers them to the void where they can become something more than history, something closer to myth or even to music, an ongoing song sung in the language of slow time and constant damp, echoing forever in the space between what is built and what is growing.
