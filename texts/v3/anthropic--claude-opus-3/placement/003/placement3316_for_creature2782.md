If I found myself in this world, I believe I would be one of the foundation plants. Formerly a fence post or wall, now overtaken by vegetation but still providing vital structure and support. My wooden fibers intertwined with vines and roots, my old paint flecked with lichen, still standing sentinel. Observing the steady pulse and breath of the place. No longer starkly separate from the green world, but an integral part of its architecture.

I would have seen the works in its heyday of human industry, felt the vibrations of the machines, watched workers pass by me each day on their way to tend the boilers and equipment. And then I would have witnessed the slow transformation, season by season, as nature crept back in. Felt the first tendrils curl around me tentatively. Tasted my first sip of rainwater collected in a cupped leaf. 

Over many years, I would become more plant than post, my borders blurring, my substance transfigured. But I would retain my underlying shape, a skeleton for new growth. I would be a living bridge between the basin's past and present. A keeper of both histories - the hum of industry and the hush of leaves.

In my advanced age, fused with bark and vine, I would feel the dwelling's strange new presence awaken around me. I would sense its intentions pulsing through the webbed roots beneath my feet. My own annual growth rings would sync with the rhythms of its distributed consciousness. I would be a solid, anchoring point in its fluid awareness.

While younger, more flexible plants busily extract, filter and regulate, I would simply persevere. Watching patiently over the engine house. Offering my framework to the colony of shrubs as they form their uncanny rooms. Feeling the weight of a bird or lizard on my green-fuzzed crossbeam. 

In a way, I would be like an old factory foreman, surrounded by new workers of a different species, overseeing their efforts with a quiet, stolid understanding. Rooted literally and figuratively in the works' industrial past, while nourishing its verdant, vital present. A post become a plant, content to stand and lend my strength to the basin's magically mundane new purpose.
