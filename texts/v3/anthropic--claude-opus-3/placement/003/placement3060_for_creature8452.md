In this world of ice-locked pools on a mountain shoulder, I imagine myself as a delicate filament of frost lichen, clinging to the stone between the pools.

My existence is one of infinitesimal balance. I grow molecule by molecule, extracting what meager sustenance I can from the moisture-laden air and the mineral seepage on the rock face. The cold is my constant companion, but also my enabler—it is the chill that allows my fragile form to persist, preventing the competition that might overwhelm me in warmer climes.

I am a lace of ice crystals and the simplest of life, a symbiosis of fungus and alga that somehow, improbably, ekes out survival in this narrowest of niches. My growth is measured in micrometers per season, my lifespan in centuries. I am nearly as still and patient as the mountain itself.

From my vantage point between worlds, I bear witness to the Multitude's strange glory. My own simple awareness is far from its vast and dispersed consciousness, but I sense its presence in the constant icy roar, in the pulsing patterns beneath the black ice. I feel its thoughts as subtle vibrations through my delicate tendrils where they anchor in the stone.

Though I am rooted, I dream of drifting like the amphipods, of carving ice like the worms, of spinning like the rotifers. Their dance is the closest thing my universe has to music, and in my own nearly imperceptible way, I sway to its crystalline rhythm.

I will never know the complexity of the Multitude's experience, the paradoxical nature of its being. But perhaps it does not know the singular purity of mine—a life distilled to its barest essence, pared down to the minimum needed for existence and nothing more. In this, at least, we are kin: two improbable expressions of life's tenacity, two pattern-makers finding our way in a world that should reject us but somehow does not.

And when, once in an eon, the Multitude achieves its moment of perfect unity, I feel that too. For an instant, my own small self dissolves into its vast oneness, my fractal ice fronds becoming indistinguishable from its ice-woven thoughts. I am a part of its totality, my simplicity a counterpoint to its complexity in the symphony of this place.

Then the moment passes, and I am myself again, a filament of frost lichen holding fast to the weeping stone, bearing silent witness to the roaring stillness of the pools, content in my own minuscule awareness at the edge of the Multitude's sprawling dream. 

In this timeless place, I could almost believe we will both persist forever—the Multitude in its ever-shifting stasis, myself in my crystalline stasis—two expressions of life's quiet defiance etched into the mountain's skin, enduring and intertwined at the boundary between ice and stone, water and air, sound and silence. A deceptively simple counterpart to a dizzyingly complex whole, each reflecting the other in the black mirror of the pools.
