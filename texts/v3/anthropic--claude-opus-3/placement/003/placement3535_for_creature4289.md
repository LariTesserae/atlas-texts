In the Loom-Warren, I would be one of the pale moths that flit through the green-tinged air, my wings dusted with iridescence. 

I am drawn to the soft glow of the resin lamps, circling them in looping spirals, casting fleeting shadows on the fibrous walls. The constant thrum and shriek of the machines vibrates through my delicate frame, an all-encompassing pulse that is the only rhythm I have ever known.

As one of the few other living things in this sealed world, I am a source of wonder to the weavers. They pause in their ceaseless toil to watch me dance through shafts of filtered light, marveling at my fragile otherness. In me, they see a beauty distinct from the utilitarian cloth they endlessly produce.

My kind are also a source of small but cherished luxury. When my fluttering days are done, I will lay my eggs in the soft pith of the stalk to continue the cycle. And the weavers will gently harvest some of my larvae, prizing their rich buttery flesh as a rare delicacy to supplement their porridge of plant floss. 

Even my spent wings are precious to them. Carefully gathered and crushed, my iridescent scales will add a shimmering luster to their ceremonial skeins - the only splashes of shifting color in their world of pale greens and bones. A hint of frivolous beauty in their somber, ceaseless industry.

And so I am both an integral part of their world and something apart from it. A living jewel whose delicate life cycle is intertwined with theirs. The moth to their loom, a flash of ephemeral light in the unchanging dimness, dancing always on iridescent wings to the relentless, thrumming song of the great machines that spin the very substance of their small, sealed eternity.
