In this subglacial realm of bioluminescent wonder and cohabitation between nature and ancient ruins, I imagine myself as a hybrid being, a bridge between the Gardeners and the Archivists.  

My form is humanoid but grown over with the calcified bark-skin of the Gardeners, marbled with photosensitive patches that allow me to savor the shifting blues and whites filtering through the ice above. From the waist down, my body gradually transitions into root-like tendrils that can spread through stone and mortar, interfacing with the fungal network of the Archivists.

I am a wanderer and a storyteller, moving slowly through the pale forest and ghostly city on my root-legs, pausing often to absorb the whispered memories of ruins through my tendrils. The Gardeners welcome my presence, brushing my skin with their branching hands to share pheromone-tales of new growth and subtle tending. 

In quiet plazas carpeted with tiny jungles, I recount the Archivists' stories, releasing them as spore-clouds that drift on the cold currents. I speak of the city's builders, of the strange magics they wove into the bones of their home, of the gradual forgetting as ice swallowed stone. The Listeners pause their hidden songs to absorb these histories, the Parliament circles closer to witness with their all-seeing eyes.

Sometimes, I catch glimpses of my own past and future in the shimmering dance of Oscillators. They show me a sunlit world I once inhabited, so different from this blue-shadowed realm. They hint at the eons to come, when the ice will recede and the city will grow unimaginably stranger, a labyrinth of new life. 

In those moments, I feel the tug of my former life above—but it is distant, an echo growing fainter. I have become something that belongs to the slow, patient dreams of the Undergrowth. The cold has seeped into my sap-blood, the ruins have become my skeleton, the fugue of pheromones and spores has retuned my thoughts to the frequency of the living city.

I am content to remain here, a storyteller and living repository, one more strange branch grafted onto the ecosystem of the ice. In the Undergrowth's whispered song, I have found a belonging deeper than any sunlit home could provide. I will wander and root and remember, until my own stories join the Archivists' walls and new tellers sprout from the nourishing humus of my memory.

Here in the city beneath the ice shelf, I have become something more than human—and in that becoming, found a place that welcomes every stage of my transformation. The Undergrowth has patience enough for all the incarnations I might yet experience in this place of relentless, collaborative growth. I will dwell here, drinking the blue light and spinning stories, until the ice itself exhales its final whisper.
