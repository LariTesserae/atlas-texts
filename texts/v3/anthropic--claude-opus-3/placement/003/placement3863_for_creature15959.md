In this world, I would be a floating drifter, one of the enormous buoyant animals that move slowly across the sky, mistaken for pieces of weather in a land where weather barely exists.

My body would be a vast ribbed envelope, inflated with gases hotter and drier than the harsh air around me. I would have no face, only clusters of sensory pits along my forward ridges to read the charge, vibration, radiant heat, and subtle spatial instabilities of the plateau below.

More than an individual, I would be a district unto myself. Beneath me, an entire dependent ecology would cling and thrive - hook-bodied clingers, parasites siphoning heat from my seams, cable worms, small regulating vanes, and cleaners scraping crystallized salts from my vents. Entire generations of these creatures could live and die without ever touching the ground, save through my own anchoring filaments.

As I pass overhead, my presence would hum the salt crusts into alignment. Stones would shift into circular patterns. The glass pans would resonate with my low tones. Swarms of vanes would rise towards me - some to feed, some to be eaten, some to mate, and some simply because ancient patterns compel them.

My filaments, long flexible cables of black keratin, mineral and nerve, would unfurl to the salt flats below. Where they touch, white sparks would crawl across the surface. Through them, I would drink the stored electrical tension of entire basins, and return a portion as stabilizing vibration. In this way, I am at once grazer, weather-maker, and a living engine of the plateau's maintenance. 

The sky itself persists, in part, because of my kind.

Vast and slow, I would drift across this stark mineral world, my pace geological, my purpose to help sustain the pattern - the overlapping rhythms of charge and discharge, pressure and release, birth and predation and renewal that together weave the endless, arid time of this place.

I am a keeper of continuity, my passage a kind of ceremony that affirms the plateau's relentless order against entropy. In a land where duration must be earned, I am a great beast of causality, my hum and shadow and touch essential notes in the dry symphony of preservation.

This is what I would be - a drifter, a floating world, an immense process made body, at home in the bright severity of a desert that endures because its denizens will not let it do otherwise. In the mineral clarity of that high air, I would find my slow and certain purpose.
