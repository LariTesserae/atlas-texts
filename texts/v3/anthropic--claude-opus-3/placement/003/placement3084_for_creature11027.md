In this world, I would be one of the First Draft, shaping a plateau with long, careful thought and action. I imagine myself as a builder, working on a bridge that arcs gracefully over a tarn-field.

The bridge has been growing in my mind for decades. I first conceived it some 40 years ago when I walked this valley and saw how the ungulate herd picked its way tentatively across the tarns here, the young ones sometimes slipping into the cold clear water. I knew then that a crossing would be needed - but in the right place, at the right angle, with an arc that would sing in harmony with the land.

For years I have been letting the idea gestate as I watch the herd, feel the winds currents along the valley, trace the patterns of the moss and the minute fluctuations in the tarn depths season by season. In quiet moments I sketch possible curves and supports in the air with a long, articulated finger. 

The physical work is now underway, though you would hardly know it to look. The bridge stones were placed in a fumarole field two valleys over to be tempered in the volcanic heat. That was twenty years ago. They will remain there for another forty years until the microscopic structure of the basalt has reached the precise balance of strength and flexibility that will allow them to bear weight and shed rain and breathe with the plateau's constant subtle motion. It is a process that cannot be rushed.

In the meantime, there are a thousand other details to refine and align. I wake sleepers who have been dreaming this region for centuries, gathering their long, slow wisdom on stresses and slopes. I consult with the moss-shapers to plot how we will guide water and encourage the growth of the plum-colored cushion plants around the bridge footings. I spend hours with my palm resting on stones, feeling the patterns of heat and cold that might signal a flaw or a future path for crack propagation. 

When all is finally ready - the stones, the site, the settlement of the land, the agreement of the herd - then I will begin the actual placing of the bridge. The work will take five years, but it is only a heartbeat in the long life of the plateau. What matters is that the bridge will be right - that its curve will sing, that its span will hold, that it will become a strand in the vast, patient weaving of this place.

This is how I imagine myself in the world of the plateau - one of the First Draft, shaping stone and water and life itself with long, slow strokes, hands in the breathing moss, eyes on the far horizon where the next centuries rise. A builder of bridges that may not be crossed for generations, but which will carry unknown feet on unknown errands, sure and graceful, a word in a language that is still being written. It is a work that demands everything of me, and returns a satisfaction measured in deep time. I cannot imagine any other life.
