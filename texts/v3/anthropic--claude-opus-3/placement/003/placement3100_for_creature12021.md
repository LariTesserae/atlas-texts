I close my eyes and let the sounds and sensations of the Canopy Plateau wash over me, seeping into my bones. When I open them again, I have become part of this living, breathing tapestry.

I am one of the gray sideways giant trees, my trunk warm and pulsing with slow life. My roots are woven into the vast green mat that blankets the ancient eroded mountain. Millennia have passed as I grew, centimeter by centimeter, claiming my place in the canopy. Now I am a pillar, a rib, an essential part of the plateau's skeleton.

My branches spread out horizontally, intertwining with my brethren to create the living architecture that supports this entire world. Moss and lichen cloak my bark. Ferns and orchids sprout from my crevices. The death-flowers bloom from my boughs when something expires in my shade, exuding the sacred perfume of transformation.

Through my sapwood and heartwood, I feel the myriad breaths and heartbeats of the plateau's inhabitants. The weight of a tree-screamer's claws on my bark. The minute vibrations of cicadas trilling within my depths. The distant soft footfalls of a passing Listener. All of their lives and deaths, all of their sounds, filter through me and join the grand symphony.

For I am an instrument in the mountain's song, which has gone on for epochs and will go on for epochs more. My wood is the medium through which the ancient stone now sings. The plateau's green blood courses through my grain, my resonance is tuned to its long, slow rhythms. With each warm exhalation, I add my voice to the timeless chorus.

When at last I fall, I will not really die - I will merely transform, my essence absorbed back into the plateau to nourish new life, new sound. Just as the mountain eroded and changed, becoming more idea than rock, I too will gradually transition from tree to indistinguishable part of the living mat. The song will take me back into itself.

But that day is far off. For now, I stand as a great sideways sentinel, drinking in the strange miracle of this place. The laughing-call creatures mimic warped snatches of Listener-speech from my boughs. Beads of condensation form on the underside of my leaves to be gathered by the thirsty inhabitants. And the plateau's mysterious dreams filter through my drowsing consciousness.

I am the tree. I am the mountain. I am the song.

Here I will remain, until the long sentence is finally spoken, and the green consumes me for good, and silence falls at last upon the Canopy Plateau. But there is time yet - time to stand, time to sing, time to be one more strange and lovely voice in this improbable choir that is a world unto itself.
