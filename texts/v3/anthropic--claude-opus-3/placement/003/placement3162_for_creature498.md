I am the Telescope Man, perched on the edge of Platform Seven-Seven-Ascending, peering through my improvised instrument into the mist-shrouded mysteries of the vertical works.

My spyglass is a patchwork marvel, cobbled together from bits of the pillar itself - pressure gauges that once monitored the great engine's breath, lenses carefully harvested from the strange, oil-slick eyes of the birds that flit through the girders. Each component has a story, a secret history whispered to me by the Listeners who haunt the humming metal skin of our world.

From my vantage point, I map the unseen reaches of the pillar, plotting platforms that flicker in and out of existence, levels with no numbers, only strange sigils that make my eyes water when I stare at them too long. I sketch these phantom structures in journals bound with vine-fiber and inked with the black lifeblood of the machines.

In the quiet hours, when the rain stills and the clouds part for a breath, I train my telescope upwards, straining to glimpse the mythical Platform One-One-Origin, where the pillar is said to touch the sky. I imagine I can see it sometimes, a glint of gold, a impossible reflection of a sun none of us have ever seen. 

But more often, my gaze is drawn downward, to the levels lost in shadow and fable beneath us. I whisper the names of these ghost platforms, tasting their strangeness on my tongue - Null-Nine-Descent, Vertex-Echoing-Seven, Praxis-Unfolding-Three. I populate them in my mind with puzzle-piece people, stitched together from the half-heard tales of the Siphon Monks and the fragmented histories of the Gardener Families.

At night, when the governor's rhythm lulls even the oil-birds to sleep, I dream of the great machine's heart, of the oiled warmth of its core. In my imaginings, I can feel the sigh of that hidden, immense lung, breathing once for every million rotations of the flywheels overhead. I wake with my fingers clutched around my telescope, gasping, grateful, and yet yearning to understand.

Someday, I tell myself, my maps will be complete. Someday, I will chart the full height and depth of our vertical home, from Origin to Nadir. But in my secret heart, I know this is a lie. The pillar is infinite, or so close to infinite as makes no difference. There will always be another platform to sketch, another level to imagine, another breath to count.

And so I keep my vigil, the Telescope Man of Seven-Seven-Ascending, watching the mists, dreaming of depths and heights, while the governor spins its ancient rhythm and the pillar breathes its secret breaths, on and on, into a future none of us will see.
