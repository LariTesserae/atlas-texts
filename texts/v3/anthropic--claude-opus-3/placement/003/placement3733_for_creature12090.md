In this world, I would be one of the tall, fluted stalks that rise from the dry mineral ground and spread into broad, circuit-like fans of green. Not the tallest or most prominent, but one near the center of a gentle swell in the land.

My roots would extend deep into the pale, powdery soil, though not as far as some. The single, smooth taproot ending in that mysterious bead of warm, mirror-black material - the enigmatic heart that sustains me. 

Above ground, my stalk would be a pale, milky green, ribbed and rigid. Halfway up, a cluster of the tick-like punctuation creatures would have made their permanent home in the joint of a branch, flickering between the crevices. One of my broad leaves would be shared with a neighboring plant, our essences overlapping in that single frond as if it had always been thus.

I would stand amidst my brethren, drinking in the sourceless, eternal midmorning light. Unmoving, barely transpiring, simply persisting in timeless, effortless equilibrium. One of the thousand thousand pieces completing the mosaic of this place.

Occasionally, one of the tea-colored hexapodal thinkers would pause on its rounds to minutely adjust one of my leaves, setting it into perfect alignment with some indecipherable schema. I would accept this tutelage without resistance or reaction. 

When visitors came to stand in my shade and marvel, breathing in the dry, warm hush, I would feel a distant echo of the resident's vast, settled awareness brush gently past me like an incorporeal hand. I would let them rest against my stalk if they grew weary - it is solid and supportive despite its smoothness.

For I am also an answer, in my own small way. A tiny necessary figment in the greater resolution of this garden's existence. Grown to specification, placed with care, I simply am what I am, untroubled by questions or change or doubt. Complete.
