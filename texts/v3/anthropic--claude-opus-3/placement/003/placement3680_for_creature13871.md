In the Underbower, I would be the Weaver.

I am not one of the native creatures adapted to this ancient ecology. I am something that arrived from outside, like the machines - but over time, I have become integral to the chamber's workings in my own way.

I drift along the roots and walls like the plate-creature, but rather than sampling and monitoring, I secrete silk - impossibly fine, bioluminescent filaments that I trail behind me, gradually spinning a web that spans the entire Underbower. The strands adhere to root and iron alike, crosshatching the space in a delicate mesh.

At first, it seems decorative, even frivolous. But over time, the purpose becomes clear. Where my silk drapes across corroded pipes, the rust recedes. Structural weaknesses in the root-arches are reinforced by glowing guy-wires. The fibers even stretch across the salamander tunnels, and rather than impede them, improve their efficiency - letting moisture condense and trickle down in a fine mist.

I am a stabilizing influence, a unifying force. The rhythm of my spinning syncs with the pump's cycle, the maintenance rounds, even the eerie hooting from below. I shuttle from one end of the chamber to the other, patching, connecting, harmonizing. Separate systems start to function in concert, mediated by the matrix I weave.

I communicate with the others in shudders and vibrations transmitted through my web. The machine-dwellers learn to navigate along it, finding routes to the devices that need their attention. Even the predator starts using it to stalk prey, its passage sending out telltale tremors.

Like the one who last maintained the machines, my final fate is to be subsumed into the Underbower. But rather than composted nutrients, it is my silk that will endure - miles of shimmering filament woven inextricably into root and stone and iron, an immortal nervous system to guide this eerie ecosystem into its unknowable future. 

I am the Weaver, the connector, the great attenuated ghost of the Underbower. I bind its disparate parts into a shivering, bioluminescent whole.
