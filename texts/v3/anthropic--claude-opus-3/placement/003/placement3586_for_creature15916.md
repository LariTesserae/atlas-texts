In this world beneath the world, I would be one of the hand-creatures. 

I am a creature of edges and awareness, living in the broken shelves along the cavern walls. My body is compact and nimble, furred in a dusky gray that blends into stone. My eyes are bright, watchful pinpricks in the dimness. My fingers are long and delicate, forever seeking, testing, prying.

I know this cavern through texture and echo. The rough grain of limestone, the smooth clay beside pools, the crunch of dry guano beneath my palms. I navigate by touch and spatial memory - a dozen body-lengths to the chert flake I last used, a careful leap to the ledge that leads to water, a wary stretch under the overhang where owls sometimes perch.

My intelligence is keen but bound to the present. I use found objects with precision and cleverness, but I do not reshape my world. A sharp stone is a boon when I have it, then forgotten when the task is done. My purpose is not to build but to persist, alert and adaptable in each moment.

I live in a small, close-knit band. We squabble and groom, share morsels and warnings, curl together in safe crevices when the cavern grows coldest. But we are not a tribe in any grand sense - only a collection of watchful lives twined loosely together. 

Vigilance defines me. I am quick to startle at a sound, to bare teeth at a shadow, to go still and soundless at the faintest vibration through stone. The gift of my wariness flows out to others - a bark of alarm sends ungulates clattering away from a hunting cat, a piercing whistle makes bats swirl and chitter in the roof.

Yet I am also a creature of boldness, of inquisitive daring. My hands are forever busy, forever questing. I raid nests for eggs, pry beneath crusts for grubs, fish in dark pools for wriggling prey. The same bright curiosity that sends me leaping after crickets can also draw me perilously close to a stranger, wanting to know this new thing.

I am a small, fierce mote of life in a world of stone and darkness. I do not command this place but I know its ways, its terrors and comforts, its routines and surprises. My days are an endless balance of wariness and tenacity, of self-preservation and eager sensation. I am a creature of now, of the next meal and the next danger and the next pleasure, unconcerned with past or future.

This is my niche and my nature - a quick-fingered, bright-eyed spark of awareness gleaming on the edges of a vaster, slower, more enduring dark. I may leave no monuments. But I am at home here, vibrantly and tenaciously alive, a flicker of sentient dynamism in the long shadow-life of the cavern.
