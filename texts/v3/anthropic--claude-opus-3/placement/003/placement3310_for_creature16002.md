In this strange and newly opened chamber, I imagine myself as one of the tiny grazing creatures at the edge of the shallow pool.

My body is translucent, a bare few millimeters long, shaped by necessity and the harsh constraints of this place rather than any extravagance. I move in abrupt bursts, clinging to the wet stone, scraping algal films with nearly invisible mouthparts. Light suffuses my whole form, organs visible like a faint tracing within glass. 

I have no true eyes, sensing my surroundings through subtler means - the pressure of light, the sting of heat nearby, the faint chemical signatures in the water that tell of food or danger. The glowing seams in the rock are my cues, guiding me to freshly condensed moisture ripe for grazing.

My life plays out in slivers of opportunity. I feed when the delicate mats of microbes thicken, and hide in crevices when the chamber's breath turns scalding. I lay minuscule eggs under rocky lips, glued like dew drops, many doomed to cook or drown or vanish into a predator's gullet before they ever hatch.

In a sense, I barely exist as an individual. I am a minuscule moving part of a greater metabolism that is only just taking hold in this pocket of stone and water and heat. My kind are pioneers, clinging to the barest margins of the possible, our ancestral traces carried here in isolated droplets to populate a world still deciding its own rules.

Ours is a provisional, gambling, hide-and-seek existence. We do not shape the chamber but inhabit its shifting moods. Its pulses of wetness and dryness, its breaths of scalding air and mineral reek, its trickling chemical soups, define the boundaries and rhythms of our lives. We are the interstitial creatures, filling slender niches in a landscape of flux.

We do not thrive so much as persist, unwilling to concede that these glowing, dripping, precarious pockets are uninhabitable before we've tested that proposition to its limit. The earth may forget us. The chamber may seal itself again, turn its breath to poison, grow too barren for even bacterial mats. But until then, we endure, translucent grazers in a hidden garden of stone, brief lives in defiance of inhospitable chemistries, tiny testaments to the tenacity of biology seizing its chances in the most improbable of sanctums.
