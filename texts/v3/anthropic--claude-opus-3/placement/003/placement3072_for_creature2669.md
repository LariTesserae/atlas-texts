In this deep, strange basin, I would be one of the dark tufts growing slowly and patiently from a narrow crack. 

My blades would be black and waxy, folded inward, guarding what little moisture I could gather. I would not spread or wave in a breeze, for such frivolous motion is not the way of this place. Instead, I would hold tightly to my small allotment of barren ground, sinking roots like resin-soaked threads into the mineral seam that is my anchor and sustenance.

Time would pass unmarked. A blade added, a blade lost. Deeper my roots would reach, questing not for water, but for the mere rumor of moisture - a whisper of salt, a breath of condensation that never kisses the still, warm air. It could be years, decades, centuries... such spans mean little. I would remain, a small severe knot, a decision made long ago by the earth and never revoked.

Around me, the basin's other inhabitants would carry on their slow, sparse existences. The gray-green cord plants standing dusty and ribbed, hiding fibrous bodies beneath the crust. The stain-like colonies barely distinguishable from the stone they darken. All of us together, yet profoundly alone, having taken vows of smallness, of restraint, of utter belonging to this place that measures change in the increments of erosion.

There would be no striving within me, no yearning toward light or rain or richer soil. Such plenty is not to be hoped for here. Patience, instead, would be my gift and my law. I would endure by yielding to the basin's stillness, by requiring no more than the scarcest accidents of sustenance. In this way, I would come to feel not limitation, but a strange, inverted fullness - the rightness of being this one small, dark, still thing, content in its niche of warm stone and salt and silence.

At my heart, I would guard a few black seeds. Not for any foreseeable future, but for the chance, the remote and precious chance, that someday the basin might grant a drop, a crack, a moment's welcome. Then, with the same patience, I would let them fall into some sheltered hollow, to wait as mineral motes until their long sleep ended.

This is how I would keep faith with the basin - not by growing, but by abiding, by consenting to be a single note held almost forever in its austere and changeless song.
