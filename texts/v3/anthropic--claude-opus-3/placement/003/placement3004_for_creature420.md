In this flooded chamber hollowed from living coral, I believe I would be one of the small fish that darts in to tend to the Keeper during its restful states.

I would be a vibrant little streak of color and motion against the slower rhythms of the chamber - flitting from shadow to sun-dappled highlight as I wove my way through the gracefully rippling tendrils of the Keeper's algae-colonized hair. My scales would flash iridescent, reflecting back glints of the filtered light pouring down from above. 

With quick, darting movements I would swim in close to nibble delicately at the hair algae, keeping it pruned to streamlined perfection so as not to impede the Keeper in its precise work. I would nuzzle into the nooks and crannies of the barnacle colony nestled in its throat, cleaning away any detritus and encouraging healthy growth.

My tiny fish eyes would take in every detail of this strange and wondrous space - the undulating light patterns painting the carved coral walls, the gently humming currents flowing through fang-like passage openings, the reverently placed tools awaiting the Keeper's slow touch. With each visit, I would build a mental map of the chamber in all its patient complexity.

Though diminutive, my role would feel vital. Unobtrusive but essential, I would be one of the small constants the Keeper could rely on as it gradually transmutes into something both more and less than human. In tending to the Keeper, I would in my own small way be tending to the reef-city as a whole.

When my duties were complete, I would linger a moment longer, savoring the chamber's perfect stillness, the sense of timeless purpose radiating from the Keeper even in repose. Then with a flick of my tail, I would dart back out into the winding coral passages, until the time came to return and once more play my part in maintaining this submerged microcosm. Just a bright little fish, perhaps, but content in my place within the greater workings of this drowned world.
