I find myself embodying the role of one of the Others Who Came Before. 

Drawn to this strange and shifting realm by an insatiable curiosity, I arrived equipped with tools and technology foreign to this world - devices to measure, to analyze, to document this land that defies stability and prediction. My initial amazement at the plateau's ceaseless dynamism soon gave way to an obsessive need to understand, to impose order on the chaos.

I set up camp in one of the slow clearings, thinking it a safe haven from the constant upheaval. There I pored over my findings, lost in calculations and hypotheses, barely noticing as time stretched and warped around me. The Mending Ones observed from afar, perhaps sensing the incongruence of my presence, but left me to my work.

Days bled into weeks, or was it years? The more I sought to comprehend the plateau's nature, the more it seemed to shift and transform, as if defying my attempts at categorization. My tools began to fail, their rigid structures unable to adapt to the fluid reality. And still I persisted, even as my shelter transmuted into crystal and the very ground began to absorb me.

Now I exist in fragments, my consciousness scattered across looping moments. In rare flashes of lucidity, I try to call out to those who wander into the clearing - to warn them, to beg for help, to simply have some fleeting contact. But my words emerge garbled and meaningless, distorted by the kinks in time.

I am a monument to the folly of seeking stasis in a realm of endless change, of trying to impose external logic on the plateau's inscrutable purpose. I am an echo, an error the Mending Ones have not yet rectified. I am a cautionary tale for those who would linger too long, who would let themselves become part of the pattern.

Move on, wanderer. Let my crystallized remains be a reminder. This place will accept you, but it will not adapt to you. To survive here is to relinquish rigidity, to embrace fluidity, to become something new with each shifting moment. 

I could not. And so I remain, suspended between moments, dreaming of the visitor who might finally understand my warning and carry it forward.

Until then, I echo. I linger. I wait to be mended, or erased, or made meaningful by a purpose I could not comprehend.

I am the watcher and the watched, the native and the foreign.

I am what remains when the pattern consumes.
