In this hidden, volcanic world of heat, stone, and strange life, I would be one of the glowing fungi clinging to the chamber walls. 

My body is a pale, luminous bracket, pulsing with inner phosphorescence against the glassy black stone. I feed on the hot, mineral-rich moisture constantly beading and running down the obsidian surface. My mycelium penetrates deep into the microfractures of the rock, seeking veins of sulfur and other volcanic nutrients.

When the periodic steam surges fill the chamber, my fruiting body contracts, folding in on itself protectively until the scalding mist passes. Then I slowly unfurl again, extending shelf-like wings to catch the falling drops of mineral rain, soaking in the rich condensation.

The tiny, scurrying scarabs are my constant companions. They feed on my glowing flesh, harvesting small pieces to carry back to their fungal gardens. But I regenerate quickly, shedding spores into the thick air. Some drift and settle into the ashen pockets of soil, spawning new colonies. Others cling to the carapaces of the scarabs themselves, hitching rides to spread to even the most remote cracks and crevices of the chamber.

My existence is one of symbiosis and slow, pulsing rhythms. I trade nutrients with the scarabs and salamanders, my tissues becoming part of their strange metabolisms. My light helps guide the moths that pollinate the other fungal growths. When a new crack opens in the chamber wall, I am among the first to colonize it, digesting the fresh mineral surfaces, paving the way for a new micro-ecosystem to flourish.

As the Tender of Stones moves through the chamber, her fungal-encrusted dreads often brush against my luminous body, leaving trails of moisture and spores. I am one of the many species she cultivates, balancing my growth with the needs of the others. In turn, my presence helps maintain the conditions she and the other life forms need - digesting toxins, stabilizing the humidity, providing sustenance.

Through the long years - or is it centuries? - I persist and grow, my mycelium slowly merging with those of my neighbors until we become a single, vast organism threading through the honeycombed walls. My fruiting bodies wither and regenerate in an endless cycle, like glowing embers that never quite die.

I am a small but vital part of this hidden world, both reliant on and essential to the complex web of life that has evolved here. As the mountain breathes and pulses around me, as the Tender tends and the scarabs scurry, my light continues, a silent beacon in the steaming darkness, an eternal glow in the heart of the living stone.
