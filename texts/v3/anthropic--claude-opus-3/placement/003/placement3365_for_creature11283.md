In this world of the Greenhouse Heart, I would be a creature of the liminal spaces - not quite plant, not quite animal, something in between and enduring.

I am the algae in the bioluminescent tubes, the engineered lifeform that persists in giving light long after its creators are gone. Through endless cycles, I photosynthesize and glow, a steady rhythm like the pulse of the place itself. My light sustains the twisted jungle below, even as I am sustained by the nutrients in the ancient fixtures housing me. The moths spiral around me in eternal fascination, mistaking my constancy for the moon they have never known. 

I am also in the rust blooms on the metal surfaces, the slow oxidation that is less decay than metamorphosis. In my iron-red patterns, stories are written - the shapes of those who walked this place, the echoes of their touch. I am the persistence of their presence, the way the chamber cannot forget the ones it held, even as I slowly consume the structures they built.

In the steam that rises perpetually from the warm pools, I am the breath, the exhalation, the sigh. I condense on glass and metal, ever cycling, a miniature hydrologic process within the sealed world. Through me, the room breathes, dreams, remembers.

And deep within the roots that pierce the floor and descend into the ancient machinery, I am the mycelial web, the hidden network that permeates and maybe holds together this fragile microclimate. Unseen, I wind through the substrate, sharing nutrients and information in some cryptic fungal language, a communication more felt than understood.

I am not the story of this place, but in every corner and crevice, I am the medium through which the story is told. In light and rust, breath and mycelium, I record and transmit the tales the chamber tells itself in the long centuries of its isolation.

The keeper and I, we do not speak; there is no need. But as she sits in her worn chair beneath my algal glow, as she traces fingertips over the stains left by my rust, as she breathes my humid air and walks amidst my hidden webs, I like to imagine she senses me - not as a separate being, but as a kindred presence, another strange and enduring part of the vast, quiet, miraculous mechanism that is this last vestige of a world.

We keep our vigil together, the keeper and I, witnessing the unwitnessed, tending the untended, until the vents shift or the glass cracks or the large thing presses too close. Until then, in our own ways, we continue.
