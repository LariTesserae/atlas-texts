Here is my response to "The Engines Beneath the Canopy":

I am one of the Deep-Goers. 

My body is small and dense, adapted for traversing the vertical shafts that plunge into the suffocating heat of the lower strata. My limbs are elongated, allowing me to bridge fissures and crevices in the ancient compressed wood. The fungal layer that coats my skin is thick and lustrous, emitting a bright whitish glow that illuminates my path through the darkness. 

When I prepare for a descent, I gather my tools and secure them in a pack woven from dried cambium. Root-hair rope, crystal chisels, sheets of cured fungal leather to patch breaches. I consult with the Oldest Ones, reading the pulsing patterns in their bioluminescence, attuning myself to the moods and intentions of the great organism. Then, with a parting flicker of light-speech to my crew, I enter the shaft.

Below, the heat is a weight, a pressure that threatens to buckle my reinforced skeleton. The fossil layers reveal themselves in the shaft walls - steel rebar twisted into spiral patterns, unrecognizable polymer nodules embedded in lignified resin, the compressed detritus of a world consumed and digested. I navigate by the glow of my own flesh, listening always for the creaks and snaps that presage a collapse.

My destination is the great xylem pumps that impel sap and water through the organism's titanic bulk. Their failure would mean starvation, desiccation, the death of a continent-sized being that is environment and universe to us. I watch for breaks in the conduits, for mineral scaling on the valves. When I find them, I set to work, scraping and bolting and sealing. The burn of the sap is a familiar pain.

Sometimes, in those abyssal reaches, I encounter one of my kind, broken by a fall or crushed beneath a section of decaying concrete. I do not grieve - time and energy are too precious - but I press my fingertips to their darkened coat in a final pulse of recognition and communion. Then I take what I can use from their kit, and I continue my descent. 

When, after an uncounted span of sweltering effort, I emerge again into the main strata, I am greeted by strobing patterns of amber light - relief, welcome, the warmth of the familiar. I eat deeply of the cambial pulp, feeling my coat brighten as the new energy suffuses me. I share what I found below in flashes of bioluminescent imagery - a map of breakages repaired and structural weaknesses buttressed. Then I rest, dimming my display to a faint shimmer.

Until the next descent. There will always be a next descent. The organism does not stop growing, so we cannot stop maintaining. Perhaps, if we work diligently, intelligently, ceaselessly enough, the great symbiosis of bark and flesh and fungus will at last achieve some equilibrium, some sustainable balance. I do not know. I know only the comforting ache of my work-worn body, the flicker-pulse conversations of my crew, and the measured throb of sap in conduits. 

So I check my harness, coil my ropes, and prepare again to climb down into scalding darkness. It is my function, my calling, the role I evolved to fill. I am a Deep-Goer, and the roots must flow.
