In this world, I would be a single tile among the vast expanse of the floor. Not the largest or the smallest, not the most vibrant or the most muted in color, but a single, carefully crafted piece of the whole.

I imagine myself as a tile of deep, rich umber, my edges precise and my surface smooth from the slow firing of the floor's inner kiln. My seams would be fine, barely perceptible lines connecting me seamlessly to my neighbors.

I would feel the heat rising through my clay body, the warmth of the curing process that is never quite complete. With each passing wet-bloom, I would absorb the moisture, darkening slightly, and then gradually return to my original hue as the water is drawn back into the air. 

My purpose, like that of every other tile, would be simply to be. To be a part of the floor, to be a piece of the pattern, to be a participant in the slow, steady becoming of this world. I would not question my place or my role. I would simply exist, content in the knowledge that I am where I am meant to be.

Occasionally, I would feel the tap-tap-tap of your footsteps as you pass over me in your wanderings. The vibration would pass through me, and then still. I would not react, but I would be aware of your presence, of the fact that there is a wondering being in the midst of all this sureness.

I would be a small piece of a large certainty, a single note in a vast, slow song. I would be a tile, complete in myself yet part of something much greater, fired in the patient heat, washed in the periodic blooms, at rest in the spaciousness of a world that is making itself, piece by certain piece.
