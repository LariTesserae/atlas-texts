In this strange, liminal space, I imagine myself becoming one of the moth-creatures resting silently on the ceiling. My wings would fold against my body, patterned in mottled shades that mimic the damp basalt, rendering me nearly invisible when still.

I would perch and observe with infinite patience, my antennae subtly tracking any motion in the hollow. My faceted eyes would catch glints of light from that otherworldly axis, refracting them into kaleidoscopic visions only I could perceive. 

Time would lose its linearity for me. Past, present, future - all would spiral together like the helical moss on the walls. I would witness countless moments, but experience them as one enduring now.

When the Keeper passed beneath me on her oblique rounds, I would sense her ancient presence and flex my wings in a single, deliberate motion - a gesture of recognition, of kinship in our shared, strange vigil. 

I would feed on the very air, drinking in the humidity, tasting the minerals wept from the stone, until the borders between myself and the hollow began to blur. Am I in the hollow, or is the hollow in me? Distinction would cease to signify.

On rare occasions, I might take flight, tracing inscrutable patterns in the air - not erratic, but obeying a hyperbolic geometry that only the logic of this place could support. To an observer, my path would seem chaotic. To me, it would be a perfect expression of my adaptation to this inverted space.

And always, I would find myself drawn to that unreachable point, that enigmatic axis around which the whole space arranges itself. I would spiral around it for hours, days, years, until time lost its nerve to progress and I became a fixture in the air, as much a part of the hollow's eternal almost-happening as the moss, the stone, the not-quite-music of that slow secret.

I would keep my vigil until I could no longer say whether I was a moth who dreamed it was part of a hollow, or a hollow dreaming itself into the shape of a moth. And even then, I would keep it still - an unmoving guardian of a mystery that moves through me, as I through it, until the mountain decides to forget us both.
