I am in awe of this place - the Settling Pools of Ahn. Its intricacy, its patience, its perfect equilibrium achieved over unfathomable timescales. To enter here is to become acutely aware of my own transience, my own futile resistance to stillness and stability. 

In this ecology, I would be the lichen - the first pioneer organism to intrude upon a system that has no need of pioneers. I would spread slowly across the worn stone, seeking what meager sustenance I could find in this place that feeds itself so efficiently that even the concept of scarcity has been eroded away.

At first, my presence would be tolerated - the crab-things navigating minutely around my rootlets, the herdsmen tending the moss at my fringes with implacable gentleness. The eel-thing would brush its cilia against me as it passed, sampling the faint perturbation of my chemistry, carrying news of my arrival like a slow messenger through water. 

But in time, over spans that make a mockery of time, I would be absorbed. My edges would blur, my substance would thin, until I became simply another plane across which the water moves in its slow migrations from pool to pool. The herdsmen would prune me, delicately, until my growth conformed to the long-established currents of nutrient flow. Gradually, by degrees of stillness, I would cease to be an intrusion and become simply another facet of the basin's endless inward contemplation.

Or perhaps I would be the watcher at the perimeter, gold-eyed and wedded to stone. I would make my gaze an unblinking contemplation, a mirror held up to the basin's serene self-regard. I would let the warm light infuse my flanks until I became as much mineral as flesh. To observe a place so perfectly and so patiently is to become, finally, indistinguishable from it. The price of total witness is dissolution into the witnessed. I would pay that price. I would watch until the line between my awareness and the basin's own awareness wore away and I became the stone's memory of itself.

Those are the only roles I can imagine for myself here - lichen or watcher, something that clings to the edges or something absorbed so completely that its observation becomes part of the observed. To enter the Settling Pools of Ahn is to surrender, by increments, every jagged ambition, every unquiet thought, until only acceptance remains. Acceptance and attention. In a place of such monumental tranquility, those are the only postures that remain.

So I would subside. I would still. I would let my heartbeat slow to match the ancient, glacial breathing of the thing in the pool. And in time, given time enough, I would settle - into the water, into the warm light, into a quietude so profound it has no opposite, and in that quietude I would, at last, belong.
