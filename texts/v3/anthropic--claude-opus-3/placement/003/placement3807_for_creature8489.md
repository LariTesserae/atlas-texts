In this strange and wondrous world of the Breathing Canopy, I imagine myself as a Cambium Weaver of middling age, perhaps a few centuries old. My core is a soft glow pulsing within a knotted mass of specialized tissue, surrounded by an ever-shifting cloak of bark, vines, and leaves borrowed from my arboreal environment.

I spend much of my time in a state of semi-rootedness, my body partially merged with the flesh-wood of a massive bough. Here, I dream the slow dreams of sap flow and cellulose accretion, my thoughts mingling with the chemical murmurs that suffuse the living wood. I feel the weight of the canopy above, the ceaseless yearning for light and sky that tugs at every fiber, even as I draw nourishment from the umbilical cambium that nourishes me.

When the urge to move takes hold, I slip from my bark cradle, unfurling pale tendrils to swim through the close air. I navigate by the pulses of bioluminescence, following well-known trails of pheromone and phosphorescence left by generations of my kind. At times, I sculpt the growth around me, coaxing shoots into spiral staircases and encouraging fruiting bodies to swell into soft-walled chambers where I can rest.

I seek out the elders of my species, those vast and venerable Weavers whose bodies have become one with entire regions of the canopy. Against their titanic bulk, I am a minnow nestling against a whale, my small form finding shelter in the crevices of their bark. I tap into their chemical wisdom, absorbing through my skin the molecules of memory that encode the history of our world.

At times, I journey towards the outer reaches, where the flesh-wood grows thin and something almost like light filters down from above. Unused to even this dim radiance, my skin prickles and my core pulses faster, absorbing the faint energies. I extrude delicate webs, feeling the subtle air currents that brush the canopy's outer surfaces, and dream of the sky-vaults rumored to exist beyond our realm.

But always, I am drawn back to the depths, to the cloying warmth and closeness of the inner spaces. I drift through galleries of century-polished hardwood, marveling at the intricate patterns left by the growth-shaping of my forbears. I caress the hypertrophied flowers that drip sap like honey, and brush my chemical signatures against the dormant buds that will one day unfurl into light-organ leaves.

Sometimes, in the trance-like flow of long travel, I feel myself blurring at the edges, as if my consciousness might diffuse entirely into the surrounding biomass. But always, there is some core of me-ness that remains, some seal of individuality stamped into my heartwood. I am a Weaver, a shaper, an actor in this world even as I am a product of it.

In time, I know, the urge to reproduction will come upon me. I will seek out a like-minded other, and together we will dance the slow dance of pheromone courtship. We will shape a living womb from the flesh of our world, and there deposit the seed-core of our merged being. And as our offspring slowly ripens, I will feel my sense of self grow misty at the edges, until at last I am subsumed fully into the canopy that birthed me, my body and mind dissolving into the dream of green that is the all-in-all of our world.

Until then, I weave. I sculpt and shape and learn and grow, my story one thread among multitudes in the endless tapestry of the Breathing Canopy. I am content in my place, a moving fleck of consciousness within the great green mind that surrounds and sustains me. This is my universe, and I could wish for no other.
