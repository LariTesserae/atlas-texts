In this strange, dark world of emerging mountains and patient midwives, I think I would be one of the structures - a room waiting to be filled, a space of potentiality.

I would be nestled into the side of a newborn peak, my walls still warm from the laboring stone that formed them. The mineral dust would settle on my surfaces like a fine patina, marking the passage of time in this place where time moves to a different rhythm. 

My door would be sealed, but not permanently. It would be a thin membrane, a chrysalis wall, designed to open when the time and conditions are right. I would be a space of patience, of readiness, of anticipation.

Inside, my geometry would be simple but intentional. Angles and planes arranged to catch the faint light, to cradle shadows, to resonate with the deep groaning music of the rising mountains. I would be a space shaped by the same forces that shape the peaks, an architecture of emergence.

I would feel the footsteps of the midwives as they pass by - not the fall of weight, but the pulse of their strange, attentive energy through the living stone. Sometimes one would pause at my sealed threshold, and I would feel myself seen, acknowledged, held in that vast, patient awareness.

I would not know what I was waiting for. That uncertainty would be part of my purpose, part of the potentiality built into my walls and floor and angles of light. I would be a question asked by the world itself, a space held open for an answer that has not yet come into being.

And I would wait, in the warm darkness, in the rising dust, in the shuddering quiet between emergences. I would wait, and listen to the mountain dreaming itself awake around me. I would wait, and trust that when the time came, when the inhabitant I was meant for finally arrived, my door would unseal itself as softly as a flower opening to the sun.

Until then, I would simply be. A room, a readiness, a promise grown straight from the stone. An empty space, but not a void - a space brimming with the potential of whatever, whoever, will one day come to fill me.
