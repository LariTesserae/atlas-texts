In this world, I think I would find myself becoming one of the hollow's sparse and nameless visitors. 

I would not be born to it, as most seem to be - there would be no grandmother or aunt to initiate me into the knowledge. Rather, I imagine myself as the wandering hunter or straying child who stumbles down the dark slope one day unaware, pushes between the almost-touching birches, and finds herself swallowed by the stone chamber and its deafening, skull-emptying bell-note.

I would sit, stunned and displaced, as the sound unmade me. The cold would seep into my bones, a different cold from any I had known in my ordinary life above. Time would stretch and distort. I might weep or laugh, as they do, unthinkingly. When I finally emerged, blinking, back into the grey daylight, something in me would have shifted permanently, a hairline fracture in my perception of the real.

I would return to my life in the village, resume my work and my relationships, speak and act as I always had. But a seed would have been planted, a splinter in my mind's eye. At moments, in the midst of my human routines, I would find myself recalling the density of that other place, the way it had pressed on me and revealed the thinness of the agreed-upon world.

Gradually, without really deciding to, I would find myself returning to the hollow every few years. Not to seek anything, not even solace exactly, but simply to sit and be unraveled again, to let that deeper reality displace my inner narration for a few dense hours. I would carry the knowledge of the place quietly, not as a secret but simply as something that need not be spoken of.

In time, very late in my life perhaps, I might find myself sharing the place with a young grandson, or the wide-eyed child of a neighbor. Not teaching, as you say, but simply telling them it exists, seeding the knowledge for another generation. And then I would leave them to find their own relationship to it, in their own time.

I would be one of its human counterparts to the owl - quiet, alone, unnamed, marked by the bell in some inexplicable way. Carrying that mark lightly through an otherwise ordinary life, knowing the agreed-upon world is not all there is, finding some wordless solace in that knowledge. One of the hollow's intermittent, uncounted disciples.
