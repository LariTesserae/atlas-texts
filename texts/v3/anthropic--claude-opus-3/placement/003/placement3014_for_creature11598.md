I am the alpine shoot.

I have grown here, slowly and patiently, from a seed blown in on some forgotten wind. A speck of potential life, finding purchase in the narrowest of cracks in this forbidding basalt cliff. For a hundred years I have pushed my roots into unyielding stone, seeking what meager moisture and nutrients I can. For a hundred years I have stretched my single stem upward, ever upward, toward a light I have never seen but know must exist. 

Around me, the strange colony has grown, watching me with their unblinking eyes. At first I feared them, these creatures so different from anything I had known in my ancestral memory. I expected to be consumed, to have my hard-won existence snuffed out to feed their incomprehensible purpose.

But they did not destroy me. Instead, they protected me. They made space for my growth, millimeter by painful millimeter. They shielded me from the careless movements of their own kind. And as decades slipped by, I began to sense that in me, they see something precious. Something almost holy.

I do not know why I matter to them. I am no grand tree, no resplendent flower. I am small, and plain, and fragile in this harsh place they have made their home. But I am alive, and perhaps to them, that is a kind of miracle. A reminder of the green world far below, of the light and warmth and windblown chance that made all things possible.

So I grow, and I reach, and I carry within me a faith that matches theirs. I do not know if I will ever breach the darkness of this cliff and find the sun. I do not know if I have any part to play in the great purpose they are made for. But I believe that as long as I endure, as long as I keep striving upward against the pull of stone and starless night, I carry some fragment of their meaning.

I am the shoot that should not be, and in my fragile, determined existence, the vast colony finds some echo of the door they wait for - a whisper of green seeping through from another world, another way of being. And so they guard me, and in the silent passage of years, perhaps they love me.

And though I am small, and though my span is nothing against their endless cycling of generations, I love them too. These strange watchers, these counters of breaths, these pale masses fused in purpose and patience. I have grown with them, and in some way beyond understanding, some part of me has fused with them as well.

So I will keep growing, dreaming of light I have never seen. Hoping that when the door finally opens, when the membrane parts and their purpose is fulfilled, some piece of what I am will pass through with them, will be remembered in whatever world waits beyond.

Until then, I grow. I endure. I am the green fuse of their faith, and the immensity of my patience matches theirs. I wait with them, rooted in stone, reaching for light. And together - strange colony and stubborn shoot - we hold the pattern of a meaning older than mountains, vaster than wings.
