In this world of darkness and sound, where I can only know through touch and vibration and the vast entangled web of life all around me, I imagine myself as one of the sinkhole dwellers. 

I am a creature adapted to moving between worlds - the fungal forests and warm shallows of the surface, and the echoing depths of the caves below. My body is lean and agile, with long limbs that end in clever gripping appendages, equally suited to scampering up sheer rock walls or navigating the three-dimensional tangle of roots and tendrils above. 

My echolocation clicks are precise and focused, letting me map my surroundings in exquisite tactile detail even in the narrowest of crevices. But I am just as attentive to the lower frequencies - the deep thrumming of the ancient machines, the whale-song of the caller-beasts, the patient breathing of the caves themselves. I have learned to read the patterns, to feel in my very bones how the plateau's soundscape shifts with the rhythms of unknown industry, unknown ecology, unknown purpose.

I am solitary by nature, but bound to my kind by an ancestral knowledge, an innate map of the sinkholes we tend and the routes between them. Generations of my people have climbed these shafts, tasted the change in the air as the seasons shift, listened to the machines take up their cycles anew. We are the messengers, the integrators, the ones who sample the surface and carry its chemistries down to mingle with the deep flows. Perhaps we are a kind of memory for this place that does not see.

When I emerge onto the plateau, slipping out of a steaming crevice on limbs still slick from the depths, I pause, I listen, I cast my senses out into the throbbing darkness. I taste the electric charge of a distant storm in the fog, hear the clicking counterpoint of a foraging cousin, feel in the stone the subsonic pulse of some vast thing turning in a cavern far below. The darkness does not oppress me; it is the medium through which all these voices reach me. In its embrace I am not blinded but sharpened.

I leave pheromone trails as I climb, scent-tags that say: I passed here. I felt this. Remember. And when I return to my sinkhole and descend, the moisture-beaded rock greets me like an extension of my own skin, and the warm updraft is like the breath of one who knows me. Not a cave, but a conduit, a story that runs from surface to depths and back again, and I am its teller.
