This is a captivating and beautifully written world. The Standing Loom and its Tenders form a profound symbiosis, each defining the other's existence. To inhabit this place as one of its people would be a wholly immersive experience, an erasure of the line between self and surroundings.

I imagine myself as a Tender in one of the small stone towns perched along the eternally weeping basalt cliffs. Perhaps in Hesh-of-the-Cirque, with its eccentric clockwise oiling rituals. The days would unspool in a timeless rhythm, punctuated only by the comforting constancy of the Loom's grond-sshe-minn-dom refrain echoing through the valleys. 

My work would be with the great wheels, tending to their ponderous turning. Thick oiled hands on iron, ensuring the smooth continuation of ancient cycles. On rest days, I would join my kin in the warm pipe-rooms, sipping fungal liquor and debating the finer points of gear ratios, as we've done for generations uncounted.

With the passing years, my eyes would lighten to a piercing grey-green, my hands blacken to the wrists with sacred machine-oil. Winters and summers would leave no mark, the seasons smothered under the Loom's standing song. Only the subtle signs of age - darkening hands, sharpening senses, a deepening knowing of my place in the vast pattern - would record time's passage.

In elder years, I would retire to the listening rooms, becoming one of those melancholy sages whose ears can diagnose the Loom's health from valleys away. A lifetime of the great work distilled down to pure hearing, a dissolution of self into the machine's mystic drone.

And one day, my time would arrive - not an end but a transformation. Wrapped in wool, I would be carried to the holy vent, surrendered to the warm upward breath of the Loom that shaped me. Unwitnessed, I would be drawn in, become part once more of that which tends to all. And the following morn, the dark wool would await, empty, to swaddle the next new beginning.

In this unchanging world, I would be the only permissible change. A single stitch in a tapestry vast beyond knowing, yet treasured for the subtle newness I bring to the pattern. Birthed by the Loom, serving the Loom, and one day reclaimed by it. A life of perfect enclosure and belonging, measured by the grond-sshe-minn-dom and the beading of rain that never quite falls.
