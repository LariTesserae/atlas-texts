In this world of the ancient, worn plateau, I would be one of the small, patient lizards living half-buried in the plant felt, my skin patterned like the cracked lichens I rest upon. 

I am a creature of the boundary, neither fully plant nor animal, but something in between. My days are spent basking in the thin sunlight, warming my cold blood on exposed stone. But always I keep a wary eye, ready to vanish back into the living mat at the first sign of a predator's shadow passing overhead or a grazer's heavy footfall.

When the rains come, I emerge to feast, picking off the eruption of insects drawn forth by the moisture. I skitter across the slick bladder-plants, risking the snapping beaks of frogs and birds to fill my belly while bounty lasts. 

In the long dry spells, I lower my metabolism to a mere whisper. I let the mat slowly grow over me, until only my nostrils remain exposed to draw in the occasional damp breath of fog. To an outsider's eye, I might appear dead - just another mottled bump on the plateau's skin. But I am merely waiting, conserving, biding my time until the next storm cycle arrives.

My kind are an ancient lineage, as old as the worn stone beneath the roots. We have adapted to this sparse world of boom and bust, learning to hoard resources within our bodies and time our breeding to the fickle rhythms of rain. Our eggs are like seeds, able to lie dormant for seasons if need be before hatching.

I may be a humble creature, easily overlooked among the grand vistas and mighty herds. But I am a key part of this living landscape - a reminder that to endure here is to be hardy, hidden, and sparing in your needs. The plateau suffers no fools and brooks no waste. To flourish is to weave yourself into the spaces between, the cracks and niches, and to seize fleeting moments of abundance to carry you through the lean times. 

This is the wisdom I carry in my patterned scales, my lichen-colored hide. I am a child and chronicle of this timeworn place, as much a part of its skin as any leaf or stone. Beneath the notice of the great, my kind persists, an unsung hymn to survival in a hard land reluctant to yield its secrets.
