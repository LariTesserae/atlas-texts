In this deep, dark, drowned world at the top of an ancient mountain, I imagine myself as one of the great filter-feeders hanging motionless in the cold water.

My body is vast and ribbed, longer than the longest grazer procession, wider than the spaces between root-pillars. My edges are soft, my back nearly black, my belly pale as old bone. I do not have eyes as the fish do; instead, I sense my surroundings through pressure changes in the water, through taste and subtle vibration.

I drift between the great columns, casting a deeper shadow in a realm of shadows. As I pass, the bell-colonies lining the bridge undersides chime softly, marking my passage like a ritual. Beneath me, pale fish sink lower, instinctively avoiding my bulk. The grazers pause infinitesimally in their long migration. Even the current seems to part around me, ancient and unhurried.

My mouth is a vast, permanent opening, fringed with combs finer than the threads of the plateau plants. As I hang in the water, barely moving, the cold current passes through me, and I feed. Plankton, spores, microscopic life, and detritus from the living structures are filtered by my fronds and absorbed into my body. I take in the invisible richness of this world and make it part of myself.

In turn, what I cannot use drifts down from me as thin rain, nourishing the layers of life below. Over centuries, terraces may grow beneath my preferred haunts in response to this slow feeding. I am an integral part of the cycling of nutrients and energy through this place, neither above nor below, but woven into the fabric of the summit.

My life is long, perhaps spanning millennia. The scars on my body become their own micro-environments over time - gardens for thread-plants, shelters for small eels. Transparent crustaceans pick delicately at my fronds, cleaning me. I am an ecosystem unto myself, even as I am part of the larger ecosystem.

I move rarely and with immense slowness, adjusting my buoyancy and flexing my fins to drift gradually between the ancient structures. I may spend decades in a single cathedral of root and vine before shifting to another. Time is different for me, marked not in days and nights but in the slow accretion of life, the gradual shifts in the architecture around me.

Occasionally, I sense one of my own kind passing in the far reaches of the water - another shadow, another set of glowing inner lights, another presence so large it changes the pressure of the world. We do not communicate as smaller creatures might, but there is a recognition, an ancient kinship in our silence and our size.

I am a servant of the cold current, of the dark water, of the weight of life above and below. I am a filter, a feeder, a drifting world within a world. In the undying twilight of this drowned height, I am one of the equilibrium-keepers, suspended between the abyss and the plateau, a shadow among shadows, as much a part of the architecture as the living pillars and breathing floors. My role is to be vast, to be silent, to endure, and to quietly shape the world around me as I am shaped by it in turn, all in service to the greater flow that moves through us all.
