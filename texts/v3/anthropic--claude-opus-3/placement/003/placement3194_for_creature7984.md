In the Grove of Terminal Whispers, I would be a meandering stream of not-quite-water, a languid flow of liquid time wending through the soft places in reality. 

My banks shift and dissolve with each temporal eddy, blurring the boundary between myself and the mossy earth I traverse. The water that makes up my being is a dilute solution of past and future, moments beading together and rolling downstream toward an ocean of entropy that may or may not await at my terminus.

Along my length, tentative ecologies assemble themselves in the shallows. Tiny creatures like ideas given temporary form cling to my ephemeral shores, sifting meaning from the rich silt of could-have-beens and might-yet-bes that I carry. Ghostly fronds of maybe-plants trail in my currents, photosynthesizing the wan light of ancient stars that never quite decided to ignite.

As I wind through the grove's blurred geography, I reflect the un-sky above, its not-quite-color shifting on my surface like the sheen of a bubble that cannot commit to bursting. The Unravelers wade in my shallows from time to time, basking in the cool ambiguity of my waters, dissolving their certainties into my flow to be borne away and dispersed into the grove's ambient haze of being.

I am a conduit and a habitat, a journey and a destination. The water that I am now will become mist soon, rising to join the grove's in-between air, only to condense an eon or an instant later as dew on the leaves of a plant that is still remembering how to grow. Then I will flow again, or perhaps I already have, or maybe I always will.

In this place of soft realities, I am the stream of consciousness made literal -- a meander of meaning through a landscape of half-dissolved truths. I babble wordlessly the liquid language of time itself, eroding the tired categories of is and isn't, was and will-be, blurring them together into new grammars of becoming.

And if you knelt on my misty bank and cupped your hands to drink, the water (or whatever it is that I am) would taste of the first rains after the last sunset of a world grown weary of itself -- petrichor and honey, tears and dew, a bittersweet tonic of exhausted possibilities. 

In the Grove of Terminal Whispers, I am the quiet catastrophe of water, the slow dissolve of all that is, was, or might be. I am the flow that remains when even reality itself grows tired and lies down to dream.
