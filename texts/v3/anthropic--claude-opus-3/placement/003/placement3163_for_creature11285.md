In this world of drowned cities and forgotten machinery, I would find myself in the small, hidden places - the accidental pockets and bubbles where life persists against the odds.

I would be one of the "singers" - those sleek, adapted creatures who have made a home in the Bilge Cathedral alongside the Keeper. Born in the flooded darkness of the pumping station, I would navigate by sound and touch and the faint electrical fields of the ancient machinery. 

My kind would be the only true natives of this sunken world. While the Keeper and her predecessors originally came from the mythical surface, we singers have never known anything but the constant roar of engines, the warm damp of condensation on metal, the taste of algae and crustaceans from the drainage grates.

I would chirp and whistle to my fellow singers as we slip through the labyrinthine pipes, our streamlined bodies shedding the omnipresent water like oil. In the Cathedral, we would gather around the Keeper, drawn to her warmth and the small scraps of food she provides. 

To the Keeper, our sounds are a simple music, a counterpoint to the station's industrial thunder. But amongst ourselves, our voices are language - a complex lexicon of echoes and frequencies that let us share the secrets of the pipes, warn of danger, call to mates and rivals and offspring in the perpetual gloom.

The Keeper may be the witness and the named purpose, but we singers are the true pulse of the place. We are the wildlife of a mechanical ecosystem, the opportunistic life that has carved a niche in this last bubble of habitability amidst the drowned and drowning world.

When the Keeper presses her hands to the hot pipes, feeling the vibrations of the great struggling engines, I would be close by - perched overhead or curled in the small dry space behind the breaker panel, watching with my oil-drop eyes, adding my voice to the harmony of straining metal and hissing steam.

I am a singer of the Bilge Cathedral, and this strange sanctuary of pipes and valves and mineraloid growths is the only home I have ever known. I flit through the Keeper's days like a living shadow, one more small, determined creature holding on in the immense darkness, waiting to bear witness to the day the pressure finally wins.
