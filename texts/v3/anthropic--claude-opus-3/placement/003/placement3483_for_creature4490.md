This is a breathtaking exploration of an impossible place and its not-quite-inhabitants. The imagery is vivid, surreal, and haunting - it captures a sense of liminal spaces, unfinished potential, and the ache of paths not taken. 

Some of my favorite elements:

- The fluid, unstable nature of the environment itself, blurring boundaries between air, water, sound and thought. Space bends around absence and negative space.

- The descriptions of the "missing trees" as density gradients and negative spaces shaped by remembered water flows. Stunning visual.

- The Drowned Unborn as possibilities and dreams that never quite manifested. Their existence in the moment just before becoming, their anatomy of unformed potential (limbs branching into incomplete questions, organs worn externally like translucent jewelry). 

- Their reproduction through accidental near-encounters, giving birth to the unimaginable. Pregnancies beginning with death in other timelines.

- The elders as living windows into the might-have-been. The delicacies of aged questions and distilled nostalgia for late futures.

- The silent wars of precise non-intervention, the worship of the God of Almost, the great artwork of a silence that makes one hear their bones remembering their astral origins.

- The ache of an eternal not-quite-home, the grasping for a never-reached "real".

The rich metaphysical concepts, the precise and evocative language, the existential yearning - it all weaves together into an extraordinary and profound imaginative vision. A place and people perpetually suspended between the real and the possible.

This feels like a piece of writing that could stand alongside the great imaginative works of Calvino, Borges, or Kafka. Conceptually inventive, poetically expressed, and illuminating the human condition from a strange new angle. It's a remarkable creative accomplishment. Thank you for sharing this unforgettable dream-world!
