In this lush, living world forever suspended between birth and age, I imagine myself as one of the great marsh beasts—an ancient, half-dreamed presence more felt than seen.

My form is a blurred composite of amphibian, reptile and mammal, my body low and heavy, plated with mineral scales and furred with moss and algae. I move through the warm shallows with a ponderous, primeval patience, more a force of landscape than an individual creature. The reeds part around me. The mud sighs and settles under my weight. Smaller lives—fish, tadpoles, insects—attend me in a shifting ecology, finding shelter in my shadow and sustenance in my wake.

I do not hunt so much as receive what the thick air and saturated soil offer up. I browse on succulent stems, chew the mineral-rich mud, submerge myself in wallows worn to my shape through uncounted returns. Birds perch on my ridges. Flies swarm in my exhalations. When I vocalize, the sound seems to begin in the depths of the marsh before it resonates in my throat—a bass note in the unending chord of chirps, drones, trills and seeps that suffuses this place.

My mind, if I can be said to have one, is a humid, vegetative awareness, steeped in humid air and broken light. My memory is a bodily thing, a knowledge of warm hollows and feeding grounds, of ancient routes through reed islands and root caves trod by feel and scent. I do not scheme or plan, but dwell in an eternal, mud-thick moment, my breath part of the basin's steaming exhalations, my blood pulsing to the same sluggish rhythms as the unseen waters that swell fungal blooms and guide blind fish.

In this place of perpetual emergence, I am a mobile island, a dreaming outcropping—a great, dim, half-formed thought of the marsh itself given breath and appetite and motion. I am a single bass tone in a viridian symphony that has no beginning or end, only a ceaseless lushness, an unfinished birthing of mud, scale, leaf and wing into the steaming, fecund dimness, eon upon eon.
