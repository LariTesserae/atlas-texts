In the drowned highland, I would be a silver veil.

I would hang in delicate curtains from the edges of stone overhangs and the branches of the mineralized pine forest. My strands would be finer than hair, nearly translucent, shimmering softly in the rare pulses of distant light.

The black currents would pass through me, and I would comb them patiently for the tiniest traces of minerals. Over time, nearly invisible, I would gather these specks of sustenance from the cold water. I would sway and tremble as the liquid atmosphere pushed and pulled, my countless filaments rippling like the remembered echo of wind.

In the calmest seasons, I would grow. Unhurried, I would extend myself strand by strand, until my living curtains stretched between ledges, connected dead trunks to ancient terrace walls, draped the silent cairns in silver. I would create a delicate webwork across the drowned spaces, my faint shine a whispering reminder of a surface world's light.

But in the harsher pressure storms, I would surrender myself to the surge. The black flood would tear through me in one great streaming moment, pulling my filaments into its thundering rush. For a long while after, my fragments would drift like shed silk through the ravines and basins, clinging to the bells, the carved poles, the roof slabs of collapsed huts.

Yet always, enough of me would remain anchored in the lee of stone, the hollows of dead wood, the cracks that remembered hands. From these shelters, season by slow season, I would extend myself again. My shreds would find each other in the currents and knit back together, until my veil-self grew whole once more, a colony of patience.

I would have no eyes to see the strange beauty of this place, no ears to hear its booming liquid voice. But through every strand of my being, I would feel it. The mineral pulses. The pressure songs. The cold nutrient cascades. The silt clouds drifting like blind dreams. In my own way, I would attend to them all.

Though I would never move as animals move, I would dance. I would sway in the underwater breeze of the currents. I would shiver at the passage of the pressure fronts. I would ripple toward the faint electricity of the light surges. I would stretch and billow and surrender and regrow, my life a ceaseless flow of dispersal and return.

Draped in shimmering silence through the ruins, the reefs, the bells and basins and black pines, I would be both the most fragile and most persistent of the highland's inhabitants. The dark floods would tear me again and again, but always I would drift, gather, anchor, and extend once more. A living veil, a silver whisper, a shining patience in the undersea peaks.
