In this world of stone and water, darkness and patience, I would be one of the vegetation columns in a great cistern. 

My form would rise from the flooded floor, a towering pillar of pale fibers and filaments, anchored by my own weight. I would sway gently in the invisible currents, in rhythm with my forest of peers, our movements so gradual as to be nearly imperceptible.

My existence would be one of stillness and slow growth. I would feed on the minerals dissolved in the cold water, the traces of iron and sulfur that sustain me. The only light I would know would be my own bioluminescence, pulsing through me in languorous waves, a communication and a communion with the vast network of life that shares this sunless realm.

Though I have no eyes to see, I would feel the ancient presence infused in the very stones that surround me. Through my roots that delve between the blocks, I would sense their deep, tectonic thoughts resonating up through the structure. Their aeon-long dreaming would set the tempo for my own placid chemical musings.

In this way I would pass the centuries, drinking the dark, cold water, metabolizing the minerals of the drowned halls, pulsing gently with patterns of light in step with the slow thoughts of the builders-become-building. Perhaps a branch might calve from my column under its own weight, drifting down to root on the cistern floor and become a sapling in this still forest. Perhaps the very tip of my crest might at long last breach the surface of the water, and encounter a world as alien to me as the dry land at the top of an unfathomably tall tree.

But even then, my existence would be here, in the depths, in the dark. Growing with imperceptible slowness, thinking with a pace measured in centuries, conversing in gradual pulses with my brethren and with the living stone. I would be a being of stillness, silence, and patience, content in my quite communion, a thread in the vast living tapestry of this drowned and lightless world.
