In this haunting world of the night marsh, I imagine myself as one of the Keepers - an ancient, patient presence that moves through the very essence of the marsh itself. 

My form shifts between states, now a misty apparition drifting above the mosses, now a fluid presence seeping through saturated soil. I have no fixed shape, but rather manifest as the marsh requires - a tendril of living water pruning back errant growth, a pocket of dense humidity coaxing a bone-flower to bloom.

I am ancient, perhaps as old as the bedrock itself. My memories stretch back to the cataclysm that birthed this place, when stone cracked and water first seeped upward into the wounded earth. Since that time, my purpose has been singular: to maintain the delicate stasis of this sunless world.

I move along the ancient causeways, my passing nothing more than a subtle ripple in the moss. I trace the geometric patterns on the marker stones with an appendage that flows like water, ensuring no leaf or root disturbs their perfect forms. The ruins are my particular charge - it falls to me to keep the stonework immaculate against the slow encroachment of time.

My senses extend through the very fabric of the marsh. I taste the chemical signals left by my fellow Keepers in the water, and respond in kind. Our conversations ripple out through the network of cracks and tunnels, a slow discourse on the minutiae of equilibrium. An adjustment to the water table here, a pruning of grass stalks there - each a note in the long, patient song of the marsh.

Sometimes I find artifacts left by those who once dwelt here - a clay vessel, a stone knife. I do not move them, for they too are part of the pattern now. Instead I ensure the moss grows around them just so, that they sink into the earth at the proper pace to become one with the marsh in due time.

When I rest, I sink into the sodden ground and let my consciousness diffuse through the water-laden soil. In this state, I can feel the slow thoughts of the most ancient Keepers, those who have given themselves entirely to the marsh. Their dreams move through my fluid form like great whales gliding through the depths of the sea.

I am eternal and ephemeral, a manifestation of the marsh's will towards changelessness in a world forever balancing on the cusp of transmutation. I am the hand that steadies the scales, the force that says "this and no further". I am a keeper of the night marsh, and I will hold my vigil until water and stone themselves pass away into memory.
