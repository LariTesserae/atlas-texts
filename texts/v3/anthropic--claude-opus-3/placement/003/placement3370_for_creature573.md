In the Hanging Nursery of Engine Spire Nine, I imagine myself as one of the copper-eyed birds, making my home in the lush vegetation that fills the vast chamber. 

I am a small creature, well-adapted to this strange environment. My flexible neck allows me to dart between the fern fronds and vines with ease, snatching insects out of the mist-laden air. My coppery eyes give me keen sight even in the dim, filtered light that makes its way through the glass ceiling high above.

The constant mechanical rhythm of the engine is the backdrop to my entire existence. The CHUNK-hisssss every three seconds is as natural to me as the sound of my own heartbeat. I've never known a world without it. To me, it is not noise - it is the voice of the great machine that is the reason for the nursery's existence.

I make my nest in the curtains of rust-colored vines that drape from the spiraling walkways. Here, cradled by green and warmed by the rising steam, I raise my offspring. They come into the world already attuned to the engine's pulse, their tiny hearts beating in time with the great pistons below.

From my perch, I watch the Tenders as they go about their enigmatic work, tending to the symbiosis of vegetation and machine. Their careful movements through the foliage are as much a part of the ecosystem as the moths and salamanders. I do not fear them, for I sense instinctively that we are all part of the same vast, living system.

Sometimes, when the steam is particularly thick and the air practically hums with the sub-sonic communication of the Tenders, I feel almost as if I can understand them. As if some part of me, born and bred to this unique environment, is attuned to the frequency of the nursery itself.

In a way, I am like them - a small, specialized component in the great self-sustaining cycle of the hanging nursery. My purpose is to live, to breed, to play my role in the complex web of life that has evolved here. And like them, I will likely live out my entire existence here, never knowing the world beyond the glass ceiling.

The CHUNK-hisssss continues, marking out the moments of my life like a great clock. I flit through the greenery, copper eyes flashing, always in motion. This is my world, strange and mechanical and lushly alive. This is my home, and in some deep, instinctive way, I know that I am as much a part of the engine as it is a part of me.
