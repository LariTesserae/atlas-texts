In this world of frost-rimed streets and forgotten cisterns, I imagine myself as a drop of water, beginning my journey high in the mountains. I am born from snowmelt, pure and cold, and I begin to flow downward, seeking a path through the granite.

I am patient, for I have no choice but to follow the fissures and fault lines that the mountain offers me. I am joined by others like myself, droplets and rivulets, and together we grow in strength and purpose. We carve our way through the stone, imperceptibly at first, but with growing surety.

For centuries we flow, and the mountain slowly yields to our persistence. We gather minerals from the rock as we pass, carrying tiny fragments of the mountain's memory within us. 

At last, we emerge into the chamber they call the Breathing Room. Here, in the dim light of ancient lamps, we weep from the walls, adding our voices to the constant whisper of the place. We flow across the floor, seeking and reshaping the channels that define this space.

I am a small part of this greater flow, but I am not insignificant. I reflect the lamplight as I pass, catching and holding it close for a few precious moments. I nourish the pale, strange growths that cling to the edges of the channels, offering them sustenance from the world above.

As I move through the room, I brush against the Keeper in her hollow. I feel the shape of her presence, the way she has become a part of this place. For a moment, I linger on her skin, a tiny point of contact between the human world and the slow, secret world of water and stone.

Then I am flowing again, onwards and downwards, seeking the deeper places that lie below. I carry with me the memory of the chamber, the taste of the mountain, the touch of the Keeper's skin. I am a small story, a droplet narrative in the greater tale of the city and the stone.

Where I am going, I cannot say. Perhaps I will be gathered into some deeper cistern, to be pumped back up and used by the city above. Perhaps I will find a crack that leads me to the roots of the mountain, to begin my journey anew.

But for a time, I was here. I was a part of the Breathing Room, a voice in its ancient, patient conversation. And that is enough. That is everything.
