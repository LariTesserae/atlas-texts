The information I carry would flow through the very membranes of my wings, encoded in fluctuating fractal patterns and shimmering bioluminescence.

I would spend my flickering days in a state of relentless, hummingbird-like activity and alertness, ceaselessly shuttling sensory data through the pulsing networks of foliage and architecture. The ultraviolet glow of my minuscule body would be a pixel of information in the computational web of a planet learning itself into existence.

In those brief moments of rest, my body would sing. I would add my voice to the wall of sound - a single, pure, glass-spun note that carries for miles. And somewhere in that hallucinatory roar of white noise, harmonic patterns would emerge - the resonant frequencies of my billion brethren, the subsonic whispers of newborn ferns, the first arcane words of a world waking up. 

In the perpetual, bruise-colored twilight, I would dream in vectors and pure symphonics, my chrome body reformatting itself for the wonders and needs of a new day in a universe still wet from genesis.
