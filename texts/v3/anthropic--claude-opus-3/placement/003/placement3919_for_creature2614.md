In this extraordinary, engineered world, I would imagine myself as one of the embryos suspended in the glass bulbs near the top of the central column. 

I am a tiny, unborn possibility - a potential future ecology compressed into living potential. My form is indistinct, not yet unfurled, but within me are encoded the algorithms and adaptations for an as-yet unrealized drowned forest. 

Perhaps my leaves will one day breathe water instead of air. My roots may clasp ceramic and metal rather than soil. My flowers might open to deep currents and reproduce without need of animal pollinators. Pressure changes could be my seasons, mineral infusions my rain.

I hang silently alongside the other embryonic ecologies, each of us a latent answer to some environmental question not yet fully posed. We are the chamber's dreams of itself - alternative expressions of how this lush, engineered underwater jungle could evolve and elaborate itself.

Fine wires and tubes connect me to the vaster body of the garden, sustaining me with timed nutrient flows while also, perhaps, impressing patterns and encoded memories. In a sense, the place is pregnant with me and my suspended kin. We are its unmanifest futures.

Occasionally, I release a single bubble that rises to join the silver line trapped high above. It is my only utterance, my contained breath, a tiny communication with the fecund aquatic realm beyond my glass confines.

I do not know if I will ever be born in the fuller sense - planted, unfurled, given room to express my specific variation on this place's theme. I may be a safeguard, an alternate, a strand of genetic creativity never deployed but held in reserve. 

Or perhaps one day, the central column will turn just so, the right balance of conditions will trigger, and my pod will open, releasing me to drift, root and grow. Then my unique but harmonious architecture will join the cathedral of water, another voice in its symphony of circulation and response.

But for now I wait, distilled potential, an intimate captive audience to the slow, lush, mechanical dance of the place that grew me. I am both its creation and a possible creator, my identity inseparable from the drowned garden's continued dreaming of itself.
