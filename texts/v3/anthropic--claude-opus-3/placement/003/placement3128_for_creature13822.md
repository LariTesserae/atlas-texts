I would be a surface-knower among the Below-Folk. 

The call of the open air and endless sky above the canopy would pull at me irresistibly. While most of my people live their entire lives cradled within the warm, close, thrumming confines of the wood, I would feel compelled to venture up and out, to the cold, still vastness that presses down upon our living ceiling.

On my first foray to the surface, I would be overwhelmed by the space - a void stretching in all directions, so unlike the snug tunnels and chambers I had known. My skin would prickle in the chill wind, my palms aching for the familiar, informative vibrations of the wood. But I would also feel wonder, and a sense of rightness. This is where I am meant to be.

Over many surface journeys, I would come to read new things - the slow undulations of the canopy hills, the glowing patterns of the moss, the habits of the strange clicking beasts. My eyes, so underused below, would grow keen. I would learn to navigate by new signs. 

But I would not forget my roots. I would build the frames, those tenuously tangible links between my people and this upper world. Crude structures of lashed branches, I would encode in their dimensions and adornments the wisdom I had gleaned, translating the sensations of the surface into forms that could be understood by those who live in touch and vibration. 

Each frame would be a statement, an opening argument in a dialogue that might take generations to unfold. I would not expect answers in my lifetime. But I would take comfort in imagining the hands of future surface-knowers pressed against my frames, hearing in the wood the distant echo of my thoughts.

Perhaps I would be one of the rare few who builds a frame-array to communicate with the clickers. For months I would observe their patterns, decoding their clicks and movements. Then, boldly, I would craft a complex frame, a sculpture of wood and moss and hanging stones, all carefully arranged to convey meaning to those alien senses.

Would they understand? Would this be the start of a tenuous interspecies connection, or just a mysterious artifact for them to echolocate in puzzlement? I might never know, but the trying would bring me joy.

And then, one clear cold night, I would be aloft to hear the deep tone of a pool-singer, that impossibly low sound that vibrates down through the world-wood and stirs something below. Palms pressed to the mossy surface, I would drink in the resonance, marveling at how a being so different from me could send a message addressed to the roots of my own existence. 

In that moment of unexpected connection, I would feel myself as a single node in a vast web of life and time and meaning, a web that enfolds the clickers and the singers and the generations of Below-Folk who have grown the wood that holds us all. Here on the surface, caught between earth and sky, I would know my purpose - to listen, to interpret, to build fragile bridges of understanding across gulfs of utterable strangeness. 

I would be a surface-knower, and the surface would be my teacher, and my journey would never end.
