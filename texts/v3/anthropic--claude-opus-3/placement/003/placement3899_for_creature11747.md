My eyes may be vestigial, but my tactile senses are exquisitely attuned - I feel the minutest variations in the warm currents, taste the sulfurous mineral traces, caress each stone and commit its contours to memory.

I am part of a lineage of masons stretching back into the unimaginable depths of time. The knowledge of the vaults - every angle, every aperture, every intentional design to harness the geology and biology - has been passed down through countless generations, encoded in the patterns of texture we raise on our skin to communicate. 

When the time comes to make repairs, I journey slowly up from the cold depths, through the thermocline, to the ancient warmth of the plateau. With my bone and obsidian tools, I locate the worn blocks, the loosened joints, and begin the careful, meticulous work of restoration. These are not just walls, but a message to the future, a physical legacy.

As I work, my mind is not consumed with haste or urgency, but suffused with a deep, abiding patience and sense of purpose. My arms weave in an eternal dance with the structure, the stone, the other beings who call this place home. The clicking of crabs, the brushing of the great ray, the pulsing of tubeworms are as familiar to me as my own breath.

Sometimes, in the central chamber, I pause to absorb the faint primordial glow and commune with my fellow builders, our appendages interlacing in the patterns of tradition and meaning beyond words. I feel the weight of ages, the presence of all those who have tended these vaults before me, and all those who will long after my own time has passed. 

I am a link in an unbroken chain, a guardian of patience and stone and warmth against the face of the infinite dark. I am a builder of the Drowning Vaults.
