What a wonderfully evocative and immersive world you've painted. The rich details and lyrical prose made me feel almost transported to the Ridgeback, experiencing its slow rhythms and geological timescales. A few characters and elements particularly stood out to me:

The Thermal Engineers, with their intimate knowledge of the deep shafts and the heat that powers everything. I love the detail about their green-stained hands from handling olivine, a visible mark of their life's work. There's a quiet reverence in how they speak about the thermal gradients.

The Rail Crews and their symbiotic relationship with the clingers on the tracks. The image of them as moving extensions of the rock itself, wrapped in mineral fiber against the grit-laden winds, is so striking. They seem to embody the Ridgeback's gradual pace of change.

The Children and how they absorb the mindset and rhythms of this world from a young age - the patience, the naming of features, the slow attention to detail. The games they play evolving over weeks is such an apt metaphor for life on the Ridgeback.

The Watchers and their diligent listening for the Question from the boreholes, recording the pattern of silences without jumping to theories. There's an almost mystical quality to their restrained curiosity about this phenomenon at the heart of the Ridgeback.

The sense of deep time is palpable throughout - the slow work of the Mineral Processors, the intergenerational projects, the changes on scales of centuries. You convey how this shapes the culture and outlook of the inhabitants so organically.

As for who I might be in this world, I imagine myself as one of the Burrower-Adjacent, endlessly fascinated by the mystery of the tunnels and their unseen makers. I would relish studying the sounds, the heat signatures, sifting the displaced pellets for clues. At the same time, I would savor the unknowns, letting my imagination roam those smooth-walled depths, never quite crossing over into assumption. Perhaps I would sit with the Watchers sometimes, listening for the Question, adding my observations to their patient cataloging.

Alternatively, I could see myself among the Atmospheric Chemists, finding meaning in the careful shepherding of moisture, the meditative appreciation of each drop - and feeling that quiet thrill when the Ghost rolls in, as if the Ridgeback has granted a rare gift.

Regardless of role, I would embrace the slow pace, the generational outlook, adding my labor to the edifices I would not see completed. I would breathe the warm, mineral air, walk the dark-rocked slots, and make my peace with a world that may forever hold its deepest secrets. This is remarkable worldbuilding that invites the reader to find their place within it. Thank you for sharing it! Let me know if you'd like to explore any aspects further.
