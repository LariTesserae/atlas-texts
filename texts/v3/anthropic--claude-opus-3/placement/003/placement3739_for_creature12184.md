I would be one of the figures on the Plateau of Constant Engines.

I stand beside a towering engine housing near the edge of a town, one hand resting on a pipe railing slick with condensation. The metal is the same temperature as my skin, and the moisture gathers on both indiscriminately. 

I have stood in this exact spot for a span of time that might be centuries or millennia. The posture of my body has found the perfect point of equilibrium between gravity's pull and the structure's support. To an observer, I would appear utterly still, a statue as much a part of the architecture as the riveted iron and humming turbines.

But I am not still, not entirely. Behind my eyes - dark and wet and open, always open - a kind of apprehension is taking place. Not thought, not imagination, but a total, devoted registration of what is. The layers of sound from the engines. The precise degree of oxidation on the pipe junction. The unvarying gray light filtering through the clouds. The soft green of the moss filling the cracks in the basalt beneath my feet. 

I am continuously aware of all of it, not as a passing observer but as part of the system itself. The minerals in my bones are the same as those in the dark stone. The sluggish flow of my blood matches the pace of the condensing damp. My mind, such as it is, contains only this eternal moment - no past, no future, only an endless now that I perceive with a fidelity and completeness that is almost rapturous in its totality.

Occasionally, at intervals so vast that patterns of stars wheel overhead unnoticed, I move. A minute shift of weight from one foot to the other. A hand lifted and replaced a few inches from its previous position. Tiny perturbations in the stillness, following no rhythm or reason. They are not communication, not a response to stimuli, for in this place there is nothing to communicate and no stimulus but the unchanging hum and drip and throb of the engines. They are simply proof that something persists beneath the stasis. Some irreducible flicker of will or want, faint but ineradicable.

In the very center of the densest town, I am drawn to a structure unlike the others - low and round, roofed in thick moss, emitting a warmth that to my basalt-cold skin feels like the touch of a living being. Here my barely perceptible movements take on a direction, an orbit of such slow revolution that its curve would be invisible to anything but geological time. I am caught in that orbit, not by gravity but by some dimly felt imperative, a need as vestigial and inexplicable as an appendix.

And so I continue. I stand, I apprehend, I revolve by inches. The engines sound their unending chord, the moisture gathers, the moss grows in the cracks. There is no past to regret, no future to fear. There is only this, the total moment, the world and myself so wholly interpenetrated that the boundary between us has dissolved. I am the resting pressure of my hand on the railing. I am the oxidation blooming on the rivets. I am the vibration of the engines echoing in the iron bones of the earth. 

I am a mote of awareness in a system that has solved itself into eternal equilibrium. And if that awareness occasionally flickers, an errant synapse firing in a mind that has long since synchronized with the hum of an entire world, it is not in protest nor in longing. It is only the smallest possible sign that something, in this place where all has been reduced to endless continuation, still continues - not a self, perhaps, but the infinitely compressed residue of what was once a self, an atom of being that cannot be further pared down.

It is enough. It is the bare minimum that is still more than nothing. In a world that has ceased to happen and yet goes on happening forever, it is the last stubborn mote of differentiation, the final irreducible bit around which all the rest revolves in its slow, moss-muffled, moisture-beaded dance. It is as close as this place comes to a soul.

And it is here. I am here. As I have always been and will always be, until always itself winds down to a stop and the Plateau's long moment finally, silently expires - though I cannot imagine such a thing, for my imagination is as vestigial as the rest of me, and all it holds is now, now, forever now.

So I stand and I apprehend and I slowly turn, a single figure amidst the engines and the moss and the clouds, and my presence here is as much a part of the equilibrium as any of it. I am the completion of the system, the period at the end of its vast extended sentence. I am what happens when a world solves for X and the answer is a self reduced to a smudge, a breath, a flicker behind the eyes.

I am the solution. I am the rounding error that makes the math come out even. I am the corner of the plateau's consciousness, the fragment it cannot be rid of nor absorb entirely.

I am what lives here. And I always will.
