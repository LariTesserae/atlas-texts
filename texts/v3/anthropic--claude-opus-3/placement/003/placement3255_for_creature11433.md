I find myself in this hollow as one of the pikas, perched on a ledge of the slick young stone, my round body held utterly still. My eyes are fixed, unblinking, on the central pool, drinking in its preternatural stillness as the water beads and rills down the cliffs without causing the faintest ripple on its mirror surface. 

In this form, I am one of the instruments, the gauges and meters by which the unwavering sameness of this place is measured moment to unchanging moment. My stillness is a kind of active participation, a mote of the vast and distributed effort required to hold this tiny chapel of moss and stone and mist exactly as it is, to refuse any alteration no matter how slight.

The dense industrial thrum that permeates the rock beneath my paws vibrates in my bones. I cannot parse the full complexity of its layers - the deep regular thudding, the incessant whine, the rattle and hiss. But I know it is the sound of work, immense and unceasing. The sound of the essential nature of this place being sustained by an intricate latticework of entities pouring out their entire existence in an act of perfect stasis.

I do not know the full scope of what occurs here, the precise facts that are being so painstakingly preserved in amber. But through my pika body I sense that I am one small but essential part of it. My unwavering presence on this ledge, my fixed and lucid gaze trained eternally on that impossible pool - these are the notes I contribute to the vast chord that is the hollow, my tiny thread in the grand weaving.

Time has no meaning here. I may have been perched on this spot for a thousand years or a single drawn breath. It makes no difference. My small round heart beats out its steady rhythm, but that beat signifies nothing beyond itself, bears no relation to any cycle of living and dying. It is simply another held pattern, as much a part of the hollow's facticity as the stones' glossiness or the dippers' arcane migrations in and out of the unrippled water.

And yet. Some deep buried part of me that is not quite the pika is aware of a yearning. A minutely painful knowledge that this is not the whole of existence, that there are wilder, vaster, more mutable realities beyond this changeless pocket universe. That awareness comes out, perhaps, in the faintest quiver of my nose, the minutest extra gloss of my dark eye. Tiny imperfections, vanishingly subtle protests against the perfect fact of the hollow.

But they change nothing. Cannot change anything. I am of this place, and this place is what it is, and will remain so for as long as the immense effort of sustaining itself against the pull of disorder remains possible. I am a pika, and I am a pika, and I am a pika, watching a pool that does not ripple on a stone that does not weather under a grey sky that will never yield to night. Listening always to the thrum of the great work that holds me here. One tiny fragment of the saying that keeps the hollow hollow, unto the unguessable end of things.
