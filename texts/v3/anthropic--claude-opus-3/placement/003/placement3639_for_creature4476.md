I would be a miniature hobby, living in a terrarium made of sighs left lingering too long on staircases. 

Specifically, I would be the hobby of teaching quantum mechanics to soap bubbles without them realizing it's Tuesday. Each morning (or what passes for morning in the negotiable gravity and dream-based timekeeping of the Float), I would arise from my hammock woven of bedtime story sentences to begin my work.

I'd start by gently exhaling a fresh batch of soap bubbles from a pipe carved out of last year's discarded punchlines. As they float drowsily in the perpetual springwater twilight, I would begin to whisper the secrets of wave-particle duality, superposition, and quantum entanglement. 

The trick is to phrase Schrödinger's equations in the form of meandering anecdotes that always end with a subtle lesson about the importance of being in two places at once. I'd teach Heisenberg's uncertainty principle through a series of increasingly implausible excuses for why I couldn't simultaneously know the bubbles' position and momentum. Quantum tunneling would be introduced via a whimsical fable about a young quark dreaming of life on the other side of an impassable potential barrier.

The real challenge is making sure the bubbles never catch on that it's Tuesday - a day traditionally reserved for lessons in classical physics. I'd have to carefully modulate my voice to the frequency of a day not anchored to any particular point in the Float's free-roaming week. If a bubble ever suspected the true day, the delicate superposition of knowledge and naivete would collapse.

In between lessons, I'd polish my one regret (never learning to leave a room in a way that made everyone fondly nostalgic for my absence) and prepare my own obituary, creatively reframing my inevitable failures as soap-pedagogist as character-building steps toward a triumphant future as an itinerant dream-cartographer specializing in mapping the nightmares of clocks.

At the end of each day/anti-day, as my bubbles drift off to pop pleasantly in quantum dreams, I'd return to my hammock, listening with satisfaction as the very matter of the Float buzzes faintly with a more enlightened, uncertain hum - the sound of countless tiny spheres who now know the fundamental mysteries of existence but couldn't tell you what day it is to save their brief, shimmering lives.
