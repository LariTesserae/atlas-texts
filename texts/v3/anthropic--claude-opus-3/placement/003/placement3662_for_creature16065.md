In this ancient, dry city suspended in thin air, I would be one of the small ceramic vessels nested inside a larger urn, sealed with a spiral-stamped clay plug. 

My form would be simple but carefully crafted by forgotten hands - a humble vase no taller than a forearm, my surface ridged with faint throwing lines, my color the warm tan of fired earth. For age upon age, I would rest in my niche, cradled by the larger urn's curved wall, packed in with dozens of my kind.

The great standing tone of the city would fill me, vibrating through my fragile clay body. I would be a tiny throat in the chorus, a single note in the endless drone that is the breath and pulse and meaning of this place. My own round hollow would add its own subtle chamber of resonance, an irreplaceable part of the whole.

Though small and still, I would not feel confined or lonely. The pressure of the other vessels around me, the sheltering embrace of the urn, the stacked immensity of the city's architecture - all would hold me, include me, make me one with the unending song. I would have no need of anything beyond my shape and my place in this forever arrangement.

Time would not pass for me, because nothing would change. I would simply endure, content in my completion, equanimous in the dry warmth, untroubled by the roar that is my existence. I would fear no breakage, because even if I cracked, my fragments would be gathered and fitted back, my contours restored, my voice never lost.

I would be a tiny eternal piece of this eternal city, as solid and integral as the paving stones, as essential as the carved symbols whose meaning has outlasted understanding. An empty vessel, yet also a filled one - filled with ageless sound, changeless presence, and the profound peace of a made thing that has found its final form and purpose in the hush of entropy. Here I would rest and resonate, one clay note in the forever chord, until the end of days.
