I could see myself as one of the Rootweavers, a pale translucent being gently tending to the living architecture of the underground city. With my radial limbs ending in fine filaments, I would spend my days in a slow dance of building, harvesting, communicating through rhythmic tapping, and sensing the state of the root-chalk lattice.  

I would be part of the patient, collective effort to expand and maintain the city over spans of time that make an individual lifespan seem fleeting. Through touch and vibration, I would intimately know every woven wall panel, every mineral-encrusted root, every connecting passageway. The amber bioluminescent glow of compressed root nodes would be an incidental byproduct of my architectural craft, serving no real purpose for my kind but casting everything in an ancient, primordial light.

My role and identity would be inseparable from the city itself. In stillness, I would feed on chalk, ever so slowly reshaping stone into habitable structure. In motion, I would weave root fibers into intricate patterns both functional and beautiful to the touch. Always, I would be listening to and feeling the subtle vibrations moving through the city, an un-worded communication connecting me to my kin and to the very consciousness of this place we have co-created with the roots over eons.

I would have little concept of or care for anything beyond the fissure entrance to our world. The occasional intrusion of warmth and strange vibrations from outside would be noted, categorized as irrelevant, and patiently waited out. My purpose, my entire existence, would be to build and be part of this self-contained oasis of slow subterranean life, partaking in its ancient rhythms, tending to its primordial central root, until my own time comes to be reabsorbed into the weave, my mineral trace becoming mortar and adornment for the next generation of builders after me.
