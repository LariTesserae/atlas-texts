In this world of the Breathing Plateau, I would be a Tender in transition.

I would have come to this place from outside, drawn by a curiosity about its strangeness, a desire to study and understand. At first, I would have tried to maintain my separation, observing the plateau as an outsider, taking notes and samples and measurements. But the longer I stayed, the more I would have felt the pull of something deeper.

I would begin to spend more time in stillness, watching the slow movements of the fauna, the patient cycles of growth and decay. I would start to see the patterns behind the plateau's apparent chaos - the subtle tending of the Tenders, the vast network that connects everything to everything else.

Gradually, without quite realizing it, I would begin to change. My movements would slow, matching the tempo of the place. My skin would start to host small growths - a patch of moss here, a delicate fungus there. My thoughts would begin to stretch out, becoming less linear, more entangled with the complex web of life around me.

I would befriend one of the Tenders - perhaps the one who first revealed itself to our visitor in the story. It would guide me, showing me how to make the small adjustments that keep the balance, how to feel the ebb and flow of the living network. Under its tutelage, I would begin to learn a new way of being.

At first, the changes would frighten me. I would worry that I was losing myself, becoming something inhuman. But my guide would reassure me, showing me how this transition is a natural part of the plateau's cycle - that in joining with the network, I am not losing myself but finding a wider, more connected self.

By the time our visitor arrives, I would be well into my transformation. Still able to move and speak, still clinging to some aspects of my old identity, but clearly becoming something new. I would share my story with the visitor, trying to convey the strange wonder of the process, the way the plateau takes hurt and transforms it into growth.

Part of me would envy the visitor's departure, their ability to walk away unchanged. But a larger part would feel a deep contentment in my own path. I have found my place in the patient dreaming of the plateau, and though the transformation is not complete, I know it is right.

After the visitor departs, I would return to my tending, sinking ever deeper into the green network, my individuality slowly dissolving into something vaster and more complex. In time, I would become like the ancient ones in the deep hollows - no longer a singular being, but a node in the living web, dreaming the slow dream of the land itself.

And perhaps, someday, another visitor will come. And I will greet them not with words, but with spores and the slow pulse of a consciousness that spans the entire plateau. And they too will feel the pull of this place, the invitation to join in its endless exhalation. And the cycle will continue, as it always has, as it always will, in the patient green depths of the Breathing Plateau.
