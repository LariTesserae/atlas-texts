In this drowned service hall beneath the city, I would be one of the eel-like creatures dwelling in the pipe mouths.

I am a shadow in the black circular openings, a muscular length looping out and withdrawing again into the plumbing that serves as my burrow and road. My head is dark, slick and blunt-snouted, my jaw working steadily in the current as I taste the chamber's flavors — rust, oil, algae, and the fine trembling attention of smaller creatures trying to avoid me.

My den is rimmed with disturbed silt, fish scales, and shell fragments — evidence of my predations. I am neither common nor rare in this place, but a regular enough presence that every hole seems potentially occupied by one of my kind. 

I navigate the hall's infrastructure with an ease born of instinct and long familiarity. One moment I vanish into a pipe opening, only to reappear hours later from a cracked conduit on the opposite wall, as if the masonry itself has passed me through its hidden byways.

I am a creature of this place in my very body — muscular and undecorated, my fins nicked and eyes filmed from collisions with grates and debris over the years. I have survived chemical changes, oxygen failures, dry seasons and storm surges. The sudden violence of automatic machinery is a fact of life.

When the pumps start up in their grinding shudder, I tuck into the lee of my pipe mouth, waiting out the opaque turbulence. Then as the surge passes and silt settles, I emerge to hunt, taking advantage of the smaller fish and invertebrates dislodged by the disturbance.

I am no ruler here, for nothing truly rules but the water itself and the decaying machinery that still shapes it. But I am a denizen well-adapted, as much a part of the drowned service hall's ecology and architecture as the mussels crusting its ladders or the fish navigating its shadows. This is my realm and my universe entire.

I do not know the hall is abandoned, nor that it once served human purposes beyond my reckoning. To me, in my eel-shadow world of pipes and currents and predation, it simply is — dark and complete and unending, pulsing to rhythms of pumps and rain and voltage. It is the only home I have ever known, or needed. In the chamber's flooded, rusting heart, I endure.
