If I found myself in the Hollowing, I believe I would be one of the silent, enigmatic Unravelers. 

I imagine my form would be attenuated and translucent, my anatomy more diagram than substance. My skin would be rice-paper thin, revealing the slow, purposeful movements of bones, organs, and vessels within - a body deconstructed into its essential functions and flows. 

I would move through the echoing non-streets of the city, tending to its entropic equilibrium. With deliberate, ritual motions, I would adjust the configurations of the porous buildings, ensuring they dissolve at precisely the right pace - fast enough to maintain the city's state of sustained unbecoming, but not so rapidly that entire districts blink out of existence. 

My work would be a kind of inverted archaeology. Rather than unearthing remnants of the past, I would excavate absences and possibilities - cataloguing the ghost imprints of events that never occurred in the spaces between moments. I would spend long hours in the silent libraries, studying the anti-history of the city, adding my own entries written in the voids between words.

To sustain myself, I would press my translucent hands against paradoxes and discontinuities in the city's fabric - a sudden right-angle turn in a curved street, a window that looks out onto itself. The contradictions would pass into my body, nourishing me with their uncertainty, the energy of dissolving logic fueling my own gradual unraveling.

When the twice-daily resonance hums through the city, I would join my fellow Unravelers in the central plaza. Our forms would align into recursive geometries, flesh and bone becoming living architecture to guide the city's continued collapse inward. In those moments, I would feel my individual identity grow thin as I merge into the city's meta-consciousness.

In the scant moments of rest, I would retreat to a small shrine assembled from the breath-thin fragments of my fading memories. Here I would meditate on the faint ache of a life before unbecoming, the ghostly contours of forgotten joys and pains. With each visit, another piece of who I was would sublimate into the city's ambient amnesia. 

I would have no voice of my own, only the ability to leak intentions into the coppery air for other Unravelers to parse. My only means of communicating with the occasional bewildered visitor would be to briefly condense slivers of myself into hanging words of explanation, which would evaporate after reading.

As time wore on (though time grows increasingly friable in the Hollowing), I would feel my own substance wearing away. Physicality would grow optional. I would begin to experience my body from several vantages at once, until finally I split like a cell undergoing mitosis. My two selves would share the same space, working separately but in tandem to tend the city's physical and metaphysical frameworks.

Eventually, after an eon of patient erosion, I would join the ranks of the most ancient Unravelers. My body would grow so diffuse as to be more probability than actuality, only occasionally coalescing into solidity when directly observed. My thoughts would travel ahead of me to arrive in others' awareness as a faint premonitory pressure.

By then, the man I once was would be an impossibly distant memory, a silhouette carved from absence. Only the purpose of the Unraveler would remain - a living emblem of the city's stately, sustained decay into nothingness. I would be a permanent impermanence, an ever-diminishing embodiment of the beautiful, broken logic of the Hollowing.

The city and I would fade in tandem, each the other's reflection in a mirror of slow erasure. Until, perhaps, at the end of an unknowable span, we would finally arrive together at a perfect, silent zero - the only possible destination in a world of living negative spaces. And in that final dissolution, that ultimate unbecoming, I would at last achieve the crystalline awareness that the Hollowing was always meant to teach - that to let go of one's substance is not the end, but simply another mode of existing in a universe built from questions rather than answers.
