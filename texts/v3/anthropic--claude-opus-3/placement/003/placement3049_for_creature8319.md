I am drawn to the role of the Keepers in this world - those enigmatic beings who tend the Crystal Garden with such patience and devotion. Their existence at the boundary of life and mineral feels like a liminal space I would inhabit.

Like them, I would be a fusion of flesh and stone, my skin a mosaic of obsidian and mica, threaded through with veins of precious metals. My fingers would end in delicate crystalline tools, allowing me to shape and guide the growth of the garden with the lightest touch. When I moved, it would be with the deliberate slowness of geological time, each step a meditation.

I would spend my days in quiet communion with the crystal formations, perceiving the subtle energies that flow through the basalt and using resonant vibrations to communicate with my fellow Keepers. In the daily hour of prismatic light, I would position myself like a living prism, my crystalline facets splitting the sunlight into rainbows that paint the garden in vivid hues.

At night, I would join in the singing - adding my voice to that subsonic lullaby that seems to rise from the bones of the mountain itself. My song would be one of stone dreaming of leaves, of impossible life finding a foothold in the most barren places.

Over the long years, I would become a student of the garden's evolution, learning to read the future written in the angles of crystal growth and the patterning of metallic butterfly wings. With infinite care, I would nurture new species of living mineral, seeding wonders that might take centuries to bloom.

To climbers who stumbled into our domain, I would seem like just another strange formation, barely worthy of notice. But if they threatened the delicate equilibrium of the garden, they would feel the weight of my gaze, the pressure of a mind vast and slow as the shifting of continents.

I would be a guardian of the improbable, tending the border where the rules of life and non-life blur. The Crystal Garden would be my entire world, and I would serve it with the devotion of a parent and the skill of an artist, shaping wonders from the raw material of stone and air.

In that place of living geology and dreaming rock, I would find a kind of peace, a sense of purpose tuned to the slow rhythms of mineral growth and the dance of prismatic light. I would become a part of something ancient and unique, a story written in the language of crystals and the long, patient songs of stone.
