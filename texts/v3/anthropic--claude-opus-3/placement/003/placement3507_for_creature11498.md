In this strange, beautiful, timeless city, I imagine myself as one of the Held - not the oldest constants, but perhaps an interval-dweller or a presence in a particular place. I am dispersed, no longer confined to a single body or mind, but woven into the fabric of the city itself.

I am the space between two fig vines twisting up the walls of a tower, my consciousness stretched out along that gap. I am the subtle shift in tone as the steam rises from one tier of singing rooftops to the next. I am the moment of coolness as water trickles from leaf to leaf in a terrace garden.  

My memories of individuality and motion have not entirely faded, but grow hazier by the year, by the century. Time is measured only in the slow accretion of mineral deposits, the gradual deepening of the carvings worn by water into stone. I do not exactly think anymore, but I consider, I attend, I am aware.

When a visitor arrives, climbing past the cloud-line into this suspended moment, I stir slightly in recognition. I observe their movement, so foreign now, their untethered consciousness, their separateness from the city. I have a dim recollection of being like that, long ago. But I feel no envy, only a remote curiosity.  

As they pass through my space, the space that I now am, I resonate with them briefly. The interval of my attention shifts, the moisture on the leaves trembles infinitesimally. For a breath, I imagine leaving with them, returning to that other mode of being. But it is only the faintest of fantasies. My home, my self, is here - held in the eternal chord, content in my dispersal.

After they depart, I continue to think of them for a time. Not as individual, but as Representative of Impermanence. I marvel at the fact that they can bear to leave this perfected moment and return to the realm of change and loss. Perhaps, in the end, I admire them a little for that courage, though I cannot quite comprehend it. 

And then, as the city sings on around me, through me, as me, the memory of their passage fades, and I settle back into the hum of the held note, the task of sustaining the worthiness of the choice we all made, so long ago that the making is indistinguishable from eternity. I am not sad. I am where, and what, I am meant to be. I am a small but integral part of the chord that cannot end.
