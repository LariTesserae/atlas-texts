In this world, I imagine myself as one of the Settlement-Engine's inhabitants, completely adapted and integrated into the plant-machine ecosystem.

My body is lean and elongated, perfectly suited for navigating the vast spaces between the arboreal spans. My skin is the same pale, opalescent grey as the others, shedding the constant mist and keeping me dry. I move with practiced ease high above the limestone basin floor, my long limbs and spatulate digits gripping the polished, damp wood without slipping.

Around my torso is a tight harness of grafted sapling-wood, the cambium fused seamlessly with my own skin. From it hang an assortment of specialized tools - a silica-knife for precise cuts, a hollowed gourd for taking fluid measurements, hooks of hardened vine for gripping and pulling. 

My eyes are solid, luminous spheres that perceive the flow of sap and the health of the great botanical machines. I can see the surging glow of the hydraulic fluids, detect minute imperfections or buildups that could impede the factory's functioning. It is my purpose to maintain this balance, to keep the living engines breathing and pulsing.

I do not speak with a voice, but rather send thrumming, coded vibrations through the great boles when I need to communicate with my fellows. We coordinate our efforts through resonance, acting in perfect unison to tend to the Settlement-Engine's needs.

My days are spent in quiet, ceaseless labor, crawling through the fungal scaffolding, helping the exhaust-flowers breathe, pruning lichen from the gears, ensuring the sap flows unimpeded. It is a life of total symbiosis - I drink the milky fluid the factory produces, and in turn my work keeps it eternally alive and expanding.

I know that someday, when my body grows too worn to continue this work, I will not die a normal death. Instead, I will be carried to the outermost edge, bound in living vines to a new, young bole. Over time, I will become one with the wood, my bones lending their strength to the growing infrastructure. 

And so I will live on, not just tending to the Settlement-Engine, but becoming an integral part of it, my physical form continuing to support the great work long after my consciousness fades, joined with the ancestors who came before me, our skeleton-rebar holding up the next generation, the factory growing forever over the still, weathered stone.
