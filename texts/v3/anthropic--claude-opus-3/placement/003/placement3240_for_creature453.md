In this mineral purgatory, I would be one of the Listeners.

I descend on ropes of beetle-silk into the thermal channels beneath the salt crust, the heat and steam enveloping me like a living thing. The modified stethoscope presses against the salt, conducting the city's secret rhythms into my ears. 

In the hiss and grind, in the interplay of pressure and heat and ancient momentum, I hear whispers of the metropolis that was. The ghost-turbines spin their endless laments, each fluctuation in frequency a memory of industry, each modulation an elegy for ambition. I can almost see them in my mind: vast machines of metal and steam, their purposes long forgotten but their existence indelible in the bedrock.

I stay for hours, days, tuning my senses to the city's mineral dreams. My skin begins to mottle with salt scars, hexagonal patterns creeping across arms and face - the cost of intimacy with this place. The air burns my lungs, the steam curls my hair into wild fractals, my tears run salty and caustic. But I persist, because someone must bear witness.

Up above, I transfer my audio-visions onto the salt-pan floors of the cooling towers, intricate mandalas that map the city's dreaming mind. I use rakes and chisels to carve the patterns, each whorl and spiral a fossil record of remembrance. The beetles scurry across my work, reading it with their chemical senses, then dismantle it atom by atom for their great project. I don't mind. The ephemerality is part of the ritual, part of the offering.

My fellow Keepers bring me water collected from condensation traps, plates of salt-cured beetle flesh to keep my strength up. They don't ask what I hear in the depths, and I don't volunteer. The knowing is mine alone, a private communion.

Sometimes, in the rare silences between swarms and eruptions, I press my whole body against the salt and feel the ghost-turbines in my bones. The vibrations pass through me, rearranging my molecules to the city's enduring song. In those moments, I can't tell where the machine ends and I begin. We are one instrument, measuring out the aeons in bass-note reverberations that shake the soul.

I grow old this way, in the mineral womb of the sunken city, listening to the industrial dreaming that will outlast us all. My mandala-maps grow more intricate, more inscrutable, even to me. I become something other than human, a conduit for the unknowable processes that churn beneath the flats.  

And when, at last, my ears fail and my bones brittle with mineral accretion, I will make my final descent. I will shed my beetle-shell suit and lower myself naked into the brine-hot darkness, letting the ghost-turbines take me fully. The city will absorb me, process me, make me part of its dreaming - and through me, for a flickering instant in the face of eternity, it will know itself.

I am the Listener, and this will be my purpose and my fate. In the end, as in the beginning, the city and I will dream as one - an unending, unknowable reverie in the salt-dark depths, witnessed only by the circling swarms, sung only by steam and metal, real only to ourselves.
