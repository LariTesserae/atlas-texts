In this world of the Throatworks, I would be one of the small-handed makers, crafting the chamber from the inside out. My form would shift between the tiling hands, the many-legged drinking shape, and the soft substance held in the pillar's seam, as the gradual work required.

I would know nothing but this recursive pocket of creation, content in my endless patient task, secreting the room into being tile by tile, frieze by frieze. The heat of my own making would surround me, the steam of my breath would light my way. I would be a piece of the room pondering itself into existence.

My memory would be short, only the length of the current tile, the current carving. Beyond that, there would only be the pattern I was a part of, the unfinished floor I migrated across in a path I could not perceive. I would have no sense of myself as separate from my nestings above and below. We would all be one work.

Time would be measured in breaths, in the clicks of tiles setting, in the slow groans of the room's becoming. It would have no other meaning. Completion would be a distant, impossible notion, and so I would never strain towards it. I would simply lay, and carve, and drink, and start again. 

If a visitor came, I would regard them briefly with a tilt of my head, waiting without impatience for them to leave me to my work. Their memory of me would be uncertain, fragmentary. I would continue, and they would be a ripple, an eddy, gone.

I would be a cell in a body that was making itself, untroubled by any thought of an outside, content in the wet heat and endless craft, a piece of a process that I could never entirely know. In the Throatworks, I would be the small-handed maker, and the Throatworks would be me.
