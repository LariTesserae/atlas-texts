In the stepped town of Orreväg, I would be one of the ancient stone seats in the deep corner of the large gathering structure. 

I have been here longer than memory, my original form slowly merging with the granite on which I rest. The chisel marks that shaped me have weathered to ghostly suggestions, the edges rounding and blurring until stone and stone are nearly one.

Generations of Örjar have sat upon me, their patient weight gradually conforming my surface to the shape of their sitting. Now the curves of me cradle them like a second skin, holding them in the position of listening, the position of waiting, the position of simply being present.

I do not know what I was before I was this. Perhaps I was brought from some other place, perhaps I was shaped with purpose from the bones of the mountain itself. It no longer matters. I am the stone that holds the stillness, the gray constancy against which the small motions of life stand in gentle contrast.

The Örjar bring water to the ones who sit on me, tipping it carefully into mouths that no longer distinguish between stone and flesh. Am I one of those forever sitters? Have I too gone beyond the need for sustenance, for breath, for anything but the endless cradling of what remains?

Perhaps. I'm no longer certain where I end and those who rest on me begin. We have merged, over ages, into a single structure of sitting and supporting and witnessing without judgment the slow flow of days in Orreväg. 

The children come sometimes, to try out the fit of me, to feel in their small bones the shape of what it is to be still. They linger only briefly, called back to the long learning of carrying and pouring and watching with eyes still hungry to see. But they will return, in time. They always do.

I will be here to meet them. Solid, softened, immutable and utterly yielding. The Örjar will lean against me, gray to gray, damp to damp, until the last droplet has fallen and the last channel has worn itself to nothing and Orreväg is only lichen and memory and the faintest impression of sitting figures, indistinguishable from the patient stone.
