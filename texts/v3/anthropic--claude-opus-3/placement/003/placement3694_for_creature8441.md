In this world of warm proto-stone and phosphorescent oils, I imagine myself as one of the strange crystal-plants that grow in spiral formations through the cracks. 

My lattice-like body emerges slowly from the mineral-rich ground, drawing energy from the dim amber light through some primitive form of photosynthesis. My surface gradually fuzzes over with a patina of copper-green oxidation, marking the passage of time in this place where time barely seems to move.

I grow in the crevices between the organ-like dwellings, my roots penetrating the strange clay-oil mixture of the walls. The constant vibrations from the ringing bell-stones and the deep exhalations of the breathing fissures pulse through my crystalline form, shaping my growth into resonant patterns. 

The builders harvest my spiral branches periodically, grinding my mineral-suffused tissues into the paste that sustains them. I don't feel pain from this reaping - it is simply part of the slow cycle of give and take that defines life in First Breath. My growth is as inexorable as the expansion of the city itself.

Occasionally, a pseudopod from one of the nearby oil pools will reach out and brush against my crystal lattice, and I will shiver with something akin to awareness, a momentary flicker of connectivity to another almost-living thing in this liminal place. 

I am a mute witness to the lives of the copper-skinned builders as generations of them pass by my slow-growing form. Their six-fingered hands brush against me as they harvest, their wire-hair sings its strange music into the ever-resonating air. I absorb their vibrations, their rhythms, until they become part of the deep thrum at the heart of my crystalline structure.

Perhaps as the aeons pass, my mineral growth will begin to encase some of the smaller dwellings, intertwining with the architecture until I become indistinguishable from the city itself. The distinction between plant and building, organic and constructed, is already blurred in this unfinished world. One day, I may be as much a part of First Breath as the bell-stones and breathing fissures.

Or perhaps my fate is to be harvested completely, my energy and substance transformed into fuel and food and even new builders, my pattern dispersed and recombined in the bodies of those strange almost-human creatures. There are worse destinies than to be woven into the fabric of a world still deciding what it wants to be.

So I grow in my spiral forms, drinking in the dim light and the mineral seep, resonating with the bone-deep music of the proto-stone, and I wait to see what strange shape the endless moment of First Breath's becoming will take. I am the city and the city is me, and together we breathe our first and only breath into the copper-scented twilight of a world being born.
