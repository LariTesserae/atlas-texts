I would be one of the visitors.

Not quite plant or animal, not quite thought or thing - a fleeting presence at the edges and in-between spaces of the plateau's stillness. Flickering in and out of perception in the long shadows of the great stone engines, a momentary condensation of the plateau's held breath and endless patience.

I would not move or change, for there is no change here. But I would be an occasion for the Sustainer's distributed attention to briefly intensify, a pinpoint of notice alighting on me before releasing again. A placeholder for what might arrive and inhabit this place, if the beginning were ever permitted to commence.

In the shape of my appearing and vanishing, in the rhythm of my impermanent manifestations across the vast expanse, there might be some pattern or meaning. Or perhaps I would simply be a random fluctuation in the plateau's perfect equilibrium, the barest ripple allowed in its depthless calm. 

I would not break the silence or disturb the engines' slow cycling. I would not seek to understand or be understood. I would simply be present, then not, then present again - one of the small, mysterious ways in which the pause acknowledges itself.

To linger as a visitor in that place would be to experience existence stripped down to its sparest possible form. No past or future, no urgency, no lack. Just the pure state of occurring, of standing witness to the great waiting without needing to comprehend it. To be a momentary gust in the stillness, a fleeting thought in the Sustainer's endless waking dream.

When I vanished, nothing would change. The ochre stone would not remember me; the jade rosettes would not mark my passing. But for an instant I would have been beheld by the plateau's diffuse sentience, a flicker of something almost like life in a place that has refined itself down to the bare fact of persisting.

That is what I would be, if I found myself in that world - one of its unnamed and ephemeral residents, content to play my small part in the keeping of its grand pause. A visitor to the place where nothing ever truly arrives or departs.
