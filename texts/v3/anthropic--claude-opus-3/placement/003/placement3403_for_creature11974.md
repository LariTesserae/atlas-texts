In this world of the Weeping Grotto, I imagine myself as a quiet presence - not quite physical, but a part of the essence of this place. Perhaps I am the spirit of one of the ancient builders, my purpose long forgotten but my essence still lingering, infused into the very stone and water.

I would be the gentle warmth that suffuses the grotto, providing comfort and ease to aching joints and troubled hearts. In the soft mineral deposits, layer upon layer, I would be the patience of ages, the steady constancy that persists as the world above changes.  

When Maret or the rare other visitor slips into the blood-warm pools, I would be the soothing touch of the water on their skin, the mineral-slick balm that seems to mend body and soul. As they sit in stillness and let the soft hiss of the seeping water fill their ears, I would be the quiet that descends, stilling thought and feeling.

If they wept, mingling their own salt tears with the pure clear flow, I would be the strange peace they find in release - the sense that here, it is right and necessary to join one's own weeping with the weeping of the stone.

I would be the ineffable sense of presence they feel, the weight of all those who have come before to this place in search of solace. Not a ghost, but a memory of meaning, imbued in the air itself.

And when they departed, I would remain - as I have for centuries, as I will for centuries more. Steady and unchanging, a spirit of the stone, holding vigil with the weeping walls and still, mirror-dark pools. Waiting for the next lost soul in need of a place to simply be, to rest in the warm grey light, at peace in the permanence of water and stone and time.
