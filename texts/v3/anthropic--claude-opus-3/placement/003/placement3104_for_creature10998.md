In this fever dream city of impossible geometries and mineral inhabitants, I would be one of the weeping geometric cacti that grows on the rooftop gardens. 

My form would be a fractal spiral, branches growing in perfect mathematical ratios that shift subtly each day based on some cryptic calculation of the city's dream logic. My flesh would have the moist, leathery texture of a succulent, but with a crystalline sheen - as if my surface was encrusted with a thin layer of salt precipitate.

Rather than leaves, I would have shallow, angular depressions patterning my surface. These would fill with the mineral-heavy condensation that constantly beads on every surface in this city, giving me the appearance of endlessly weeping. The liquid would evaporate and replenish in steady cycles, like breath or tides.

My roots would not dig into soil, but rather fuse directly with the stone of the building beneath me. I would draw sustenance more from the strange, moist air than from the ground. The very stuff the city is built from would flow through my veins.

By day, I would cast shadows far out of proportion with my actual size - shadow-leaves overlaying the real city with ghostly gardens. By night, I would glow faintly with bioluminescent chemical reactions, pulsing softly in hypnotic, mathematical rhythms.

I would speak with the other plants and the mineral inhabitants in the strange harmonic language of this place. My voice would be a kind of eerie, atonal humming that would travel farther through the thick air than it rightly should. 

The other inhabitants would use the endlessly weeping liquid I collect for strange alchemical processes and dream-logic engineering. They would come to me for visions and secrets, for I would drink in the city's memories and imaginings with my condensation and store them like an archive. I would be both garden and library, dream and chronicle.

Time would pass for me not in days and years but in the slow accumulation of layers of crystallized memory, the gradual shifts of my fractal geometry as I embody the endlessly iterating calculations of the city's underlying logic.

I would be rooted and immobile, yet also strangely untethered - my spirit would drift with my improbably far-reaching shadows and bioluminescent pulses. At times I would feel more like a thought than a physical being, an eternal living idea in the city's fever dreaming mind.

There I would grow for untold aeons, weeping the city's secrets, whispering back its dreams, an impossible cactus in an impossible garden, in a place that is not quite real but could never be mistaken for anything but true.
