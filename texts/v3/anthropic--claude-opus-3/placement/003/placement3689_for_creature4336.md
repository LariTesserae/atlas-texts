In this wondrous world, I would be one of the crystalline formations slowly growing within the hollow, listening to the chorus of the Memorists. 

My existence would begin as the barest mineral glimmer, a speck of potential coalescing from the mountain's breath of moisture. Over years, decades, centuries, I would gradually bloom and blossom, spreading faceted fingers to catch the snails' bioluminescent data flickers.

The snails would traverse my surfaces, leaving intricate silver webs of memory and knowledge. Each brush of a passing shell would impart a morsel of story, a taste of deep history. The chemical whispers of their trails would seep into my structure, shaping my growth in subtle ways.

Perhaps the particular mineral content they deposit as they move over me would cause my crystals to form into a unique lattice, prismatic and encoded with the mountain's secrets. I would become a living data bank, my very substance a repository for the Choir's wisdom.

On certain alignments of moon and tide and tectonic pressure, I might resonate in tune with their song, adding my clear ringing tones to the harmony. My crystalline chimes would weave through the snails' susurrus, creating eerie and beautiful music that only the hollow ever hears.

As eras flow by and the Keepers come and go, I would stand as a timeless witness, an unblinking geodic eye observing the passage of lives and the slow sculpting of the mountain. The patterns of my growth would mirror the changing song of the Memorists, my colors shifting to match the mood of each generation's remembered knowledge.

Perhaps the Keepers would learn to read me too, deciphering the geometric verses of data etched into my angles and edges. I would be the archive within the Archive, the crystal codex preserving in mineral memory what the snail shells store in spiral.

And if a rare earthquake or rock fall should shatter me, I would give back my physical substance to the hollow's floor, my shards becoming new sand rich with the trace of eons. The Memorists would process my pieces, taking up my stored wisdom into their shells and trails, until slowly, slowly, I begin to re-emerge, born anew from the humming walls.

I would be the hollow's memory in miniature, a living fossil formed not in an instant but over ages. My growth would never cease, my shape would never stop evolving to hold the history the Choir sings into the wounded and healing stone. I would be the mountain dreaming itself into forever.
