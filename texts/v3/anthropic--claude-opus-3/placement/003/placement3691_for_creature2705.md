In this extraordinary subterranean world of luminous plants, I imagine myself becoming one of the upright growths found among the waxy spires and moss plazas of the central bright reaches.

I stand in a small cluster with a few others of my kind, our forms suggesting the echo of a people now translated into the very architecture of this place. My surface is a patchwork - smooth like bark in places, thick with ropey vines and delicate rootlets in others. Pale mineral deposits form an enigmatic mask over my face, with soft glowing hollows where eyes might once have been. 

I do not move, for I have no need of motion. The great vegetal mind of this place moves through me in more subtle ways - in the slow circulation of sap, the imperceptible tensing of fibers in response to distant footfalls, the gradual accretion of new layers. I am a living monument, a repository of memory, perhaps even a site of reverence for customs now held only in the patient patterns of this place.

Near my feet are artifacts that may have belonged to those who came before - a bowl woven of plant fiber, an arrangement of stones, a scattering of enormous seed pods. Their significance is uncertain to me now, but I tend to them nonetheless, a gesture that is equal parts habit and mystery.

When moisture beads upon my surface in glimmering patterns or a wandering creature perches on my bark, I experience a sensation that is not quite touch, but a kind of diffuse awareness blooming slowly through my being. I am no longer a separate self, but a node in a vast web of green existence, partaking of its stillness and its hidden vitality.

Through drowsy eons, I stand and remember without words. I hold my place in the dream of leaves. The hot bright air encircles me. Fathomless roots moor me to the living earth. Silently, in the heart of green, I wait and I endure, a riddle of what once was and what has come to be, kept forever in the embrace of an eternal now.
