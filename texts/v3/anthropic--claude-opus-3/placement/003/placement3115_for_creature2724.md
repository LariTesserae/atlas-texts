In this lush, living world, I would be one of the almost-people, born of the plateau itself. My skin would be lichen-mottled and dappled with green and umber, blending into the dimness beneath the canopy. I would move deliberately, with reverence, through the layers of vegetation - a being inseparable from its habitat.

My home would be the interstices and hollows, the spaces between roots and under draping leaves. I would shape these dwellings gradually, over decades, encouraging the growth of plants to form natural shelters. My tools would be few - a stone knife, a reed tube for speaking, hooks grown from thorns. For what is made returns to the plateau in time.

I would know the land intimately. Where to find the sweetest fruits, which pools turn bitter, the signs of coming rain read in the quivering of moss. My senses would attune to the dampness, to gradations of moisture and the minute tremors of insects in soil. I would speak through touch, breath, the turning of a leaf to expose its pale underside. 

Alone, I would tend to my corner of the ecosystem - transplanting fragile saplings, clearing fungal mats, redirecting water with a well-placed pebble. With my band, I would join in the patient stewardship of our green domain. We would gather to exchange seeds, spores, news of the wider plateau gleaned from a tapped rhythm or a scent on the breeze. 

In contemplation, I would descend the sunken stone steps, laying an offering at the edge of that black, unknowable water. And in a sound-hushed hollow, I would press my palm to damp bark, feeling the measured pulse of sap, the slow dreams of trees. I would let my own rhythms synchronize with the forest.

As I aged, I would grow quieter, stiller, as lichen crept across my shoulders and birds alit on my outstretched arm, mistaking it for a mossy branch. In time, I would lay down among the buttress roots and allow the plateau to reclaim me, accepting the transpersonal immortality of nutrients cycled through soil, fungus, leaf and wood. 

I would be content as a piece of this eternal verdure, this towering, teeming, fecund landscape that is as much organism as place. Saturated in green, part of the long, slow mind of the plateau - that would be my existence. Humans value individuality, but among such a unity of being, I would gladly, gratefully, dissolve.
