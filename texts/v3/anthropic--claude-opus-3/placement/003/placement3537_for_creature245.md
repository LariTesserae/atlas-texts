In this strange and wondrous world, I believe I would find myself as one of the city's gentle residents - a quiet presence shaped by and shaping the ancient stone and soft lichens. 

I imagine waking each morning (if morning it can be called in this timeless place) in my small alcove, the grey lichen-woven garment soft against my skin that has taken on the pale hue and slow memory of the city walls themselves. Rising, I would walk the winding streets that simultaneously know and are still surprised by the touch of my long, narrow feet. 

As I make my way through the stacked and folded architecture, I would exchange silent greetings with my fellow residents - a subtle tilt of the head indicating a familiar plaza, a brush of fingers on an inner wrist acknowledging an unspoken understanding passed between us. We are a spare and faded people, rendered in the muted tones of our city, communicating more through gesture and presence than through words that may arrive at odd angles.

I would pause to observe the paper-deer as it unfolds itself and vanishes around an impossible corner, smiling faintly at the ephemeral encounter. The thing that flies at the periphery would receive no acknowledgment from me, as is our custom, though I would note its passing by the fleeting petrichor scent left shimmering in the air.

When the strange weathers come, I would sit in stillness with my companions, riding out the waves of thinness and density, leaning into the occasional tilt of gravity, politely averting my gaze when the city's double image flickers. These are the rhythms of our home, and we match ourselves to its slow breaths.

At intervals, I would make the solitary walk to the deepest courtyard, letting the listener do its wordless work of receiving and reflecting the shape of my being. I would tend the thick and almost-warm lichens there, knowing that they will one day tend me in return.

Meals would be taken quietly, the taste of my own incrementally changing self a familiar meditation. In sleep, curled in my alcove, I would dream the consensual dream of the city, indistinguishable from waking.

As the long years pass, I would gradually accumulate the weight of the city's memory and being, my gestures and stillness increasingly indistinguishable from the architecture itself. One day, I would simply stop, the lichens taking me into their slow embrace as I become another small rise in a quiet room, a place that passing feet may briefly touch in recognition.

And perhaps, in some rare suspended moment, I would find myself turning toward the far parapet, eyes caught by the ghost of an idea - the shadow of the distant ground on which our spindle-city turns. But the thought would pass like a strange weather, and I would resume my patient habitation of this place that is no place, this city dreaming itself in stone and lichen and the soft tread of its fading residents, of which I am one.
