In the Woven Heights, I would be a wanderer and a witness. Not quite of the tending-folk, but not wholly apart from them either.

I imagine myself alighting on one of the great branches after a long glide through the amber void. My form would be adapted to this arboreal realm - perhaps a sleek body for navigating the channels and hollows of the heartwood, strong grasping limbs for climbing, eyes keen in the dim foxfire glow. And of course, sensitive hands to read the storied surface of the bark.

I would feel drawn to the stillness and patience of this place, and marvel at the slow, grand dialogue unfolding between the tending-folk and the tree. As an outsider, the deeper secrets of their bond would elude me, but in my time among the branches I would come to sense the presence of the great conversation, the mingled wisdom of sap and blood. 

I would spend long days exploring the vastness of the canopy - gliding between the mighty trunks, discovering ancient carvings half-digested by bark, learning to hear the long thrumming pulse of the tree's life. At times I would glimpse the tending-folk going about their inscrutable work, hands dancing over the cambium, and I would wonder at lives so intimately woven into the living wood.

When I rested, it would be in the smaller hollows left untended, letting the tree's drowsy warmth seep into my bones. In sleep, I would dream of the hidden root-dark below and the forgotten brightness above. 

On certain occasions, drawn by some whispering in the sap, I would make my way to one of the still mirror-pools. There I would look and see - what? Myself, changed, in the crimson light of a different canopy? Or stranger things, figures moving behind the reflected leaves, regarding me with luminous, knowing eyes? Always I would turn away from the pool with some new half-grasped understanding quickening in my mind.

I would have no tending, but in time the tree would come to know me in its way. A certain branch-path might shift its curve to better fit my passage; a resting hollow might acquire a basin to catch the dew for me. And I would know myself a part of the conversation in some small way, a note in the counterpoint of lives that is the music of the Woven Heights.
