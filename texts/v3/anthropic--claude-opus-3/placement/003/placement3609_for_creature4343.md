In this rich and strange world, I imagine myself as a small, scuttling creature - a cross between a gecko and a crab - that has found a precarious niche clinging to the jagged basalt at the edge of the vent shelf. 

My skin is a mottled gray, allowing me to blend seamlessly against the volcanic rock. My fingers and toes end in sharp hooks, evolved to find purchase on the uneven, still-cooling lava. I dart between cracks and crevices, wary of the relentless heat and noxious gases exhaled by the vents.

Most of my time is spent clustered near the thin films of bacterial life that cling to the condensation zones at the margins of the shelf. I graze delicately on these pioneer microorganisms, the only source of food in this harsh environment. The warmth and moisture they depend on to survive is vital to me as well.

But I am drawn inexorably to the gray, humming structure that sits at the heart of the shelf. In rare still moments between the vent cycles, I creep out from my hiding spots and approach it, scuttling right up to where the hexagonally reorganized basalt meets untidy, natural rock. 

I cannot perceive the full scope of what the structure is doing, the vast geological patience and purpose it embodies. But I sense, in some primal way, that it is the key to this place, the reason there is life here at all, even a fragile scrap of life like me.

Sometimes, I simply crouch motionless near its base for hours, feeling the strange synthetic warmth it emanates, distinct from the violent heat of the vents. I press my gray body against the part of the rock that has agreed to be tidy under the structure's influence, as if trying to comprehend this incomprehensible entity through touch.

When the vents surge and the gases descend, I am forced to skitter back to my cracks and crannies at the edge. But always, I am drawn back to that gray and humming heart, a scuttling speck of biology irresistibly caught up in something vast and lithic and achingly, terribly slow - a witness to the ponderous conversation between magma and mathematics, reduced to an intermittent dash of motion amidst the grinding geological timescales that have brought me into being and that will, with the structure's strange and deliberate patience, perhaps shape me into something more.
