I would be a Minder, one of the surface-tenders who walks the ridgelines at dawn and dusk. 

I would wake in the cool dark before sunrise, uncurling my small body from the crevice where I slept huddled with my companions for warmth and safety. Stretching my limbs, feeling the familiar ache of yesterday's labors, I would emerge onto the still-warm stone, my bare feet finding the well-worn path.

As the sky lightened to a pale gray, I would pick my way along the familiar route, my pale eyes scanning the cisterns and channels. Dipping a hand into a pool, I would check the level against yesterday's memory, the ripples spreading silently in the dim. A low hum and a hand-sign to my partner would confirm all is as it should be.

Reaching a massive pipe, I would run my fingers along the rusted surface, sensitive to any new corrosion, any fresh leak. The metal would be cool from the night under my touch. Satisfied, I would continue on, pausing to reweave a patch of filtering mesh where moths had chewed through in their nightly frenzy.

As the sun crested the peaks, I would pause in a rare shaft of light, tilting my face upward, feeling the heat begin to rise from the basalt. A lizard would skitter by my feet, scales flashing orange. In the distance, a single corvid would let out a harsh cry. I would breathe in the warm, mineral-thick air. This is my place. This is my purpose.

Checking the last of my gauges, confirming the flow and the pressure, I would sign a quick "all well" to the Minder waiting to receive my report. Then I would seek my shaded shelter to wait out the burning midday hours, my mind full of the minute variations and tiny necessary tasks I had noted during my rounds.

I would drift into sleep against the warm stone, my dreams full of pipes and water and a landscape that has shaped me as surely as I tend it. When the coolness of dusk came again, I would wake and begin my secondary duties - laying snares, harvesting fat beetles, meting out carefully hoarded water to my small section of the community.

As true night fell, I would share the day's observations with my fellow Minders in our wordless language of hands and shoulders. We would keep one eye always on the shadow-thick crevices, alert for any sign of the Pad-Walker, that unknown creature whose tracks and kills remind us that we are not the top of the food chain here. 

Finally, I would settle into the close press of bodies in our sleeping crevice, breathing in the scents of my people - stone-dust and lichen, sweat and sun-warmed skin. I would lay my head on the chest of an elder, listening to her slow heartbeat, imagining I could hear the deep thrum of the mountain beneath the rhythm of her blood.

And as I drifted into sleep, I would feel myself held by this place that is not home because home implies somewhere else you could go. The peaks, the moisture, the ancient machines, the life that scurries and swoops and hunts in the close-pressed spaces - this is not where I live. This is what I am, in some way that goes beyond mere residence or use. 

I am this system. This system is me. We tend each other, patiently, through the long years, through the slow ages of stone and water and watchful, half-dreaming heat. Until my bones join the silt and my dust joins the cistern-damp, until the mountains take me fully back into themselves, as they take all things, in the end.

This is where I would be in this world. This is what I would be. A Minder. A thread in the web. A piece of the pattern, content in my work, content in my belonging, from the bright dawn to the black night and all the shades between.
