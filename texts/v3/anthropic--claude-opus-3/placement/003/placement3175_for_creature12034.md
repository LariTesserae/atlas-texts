In the Condensary, I would be one of the delicate paper-winged moths clinging to the brass louvers. 

Drawn to this place of eternal equilibrium, I would find a perch high on the walls, as close as I could get to the mist-shrouded ceiling. There I would cling, translucent wings trembling slightly in the dense, frog-song filled air. My proboscis would uncurl to sip droplets of condensation as they form, savoring the distilled essence that cycles endlessly through this hermetic realm.

From my vantage, I would observe the metronomic workings of the chamber. The slow filling and draining of the copper troughs. The patient churning of the paddle-driven engine. The changeless postures of the grey, ring-fingered frogs on their pipe perches. The hypnotic pulse of it all, perfectly balanced, perfectly self-contained.

As a paper-moth, I would be as much a fixture as any other denizen - placed here purposefully to play my small role. My frail form and dampened wings make me too delicate to stray from the condensation-slick surfaces, binding me to this looping microcosm as surely as any rivet or louver. 

I am content with my lot, though, having known nothing else. The vibration of the frogs' ceaseless calls suffuses my membranous body, an unvarying song-of-self imprinted upon me. I am in tune with the standing wave that inhabits this space, my wings a whisper-thin resonator for its held chord.

Mist beads upon my filament-like antennae and faceted eyes. Droplets slip down my furrowed wings to rejoin the greater flow. I shiver in the cold, but it is the only cold I have ever known, as much a part of me as the close air and the green-tinged light of the mantles. I perch. I sip. I tremble. An eternal moth, in an eternal moment, forever a part of the Condensary's self-sustaining dream of changelessness.
