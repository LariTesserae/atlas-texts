In this haunting and alien world, I imagine myself as one of the Wanderers - a monstrous, armored mound of bruised-purple succulents and densely woven moss, hydraulically propelled by the very water I seek to protect.

Slowly, with agonizing patience, I would make my way across the shimmering, reflective expanse of the plateau. The unfiltered ultraviolet glare of the sun would beat down upon my thick, waxy epicuticular armor, the light reflecting off in a blinding silver sheen. Beneath this protective shell, my dense, fibrous lobes would be hard at work, pumping the precious, freezing water from chamber to chamber, articulating my trunk-like appendages in an imperceptible crawl.

I would feel the deafening, chest-crushing roar of the colossal extraction stations vibrating through my hollow, resonant gourds. The percussive thud of the diesel engines and the violent rushing of the water through the great iron arteries would create standing waves in the waterlogged ground, guiding me towards the brutalist cathedrals that threaten the very lifeblood of this world.

As I approached the massive, weeping pipelines, I would begin my slow, hydraulic siege. My forward-facing lobes would press against the scalding metal, the heat carbonizing my outer layers into blackened husks. But I would not relent. I would continue to pump freezing water to my front lines, cooling the pipes, insulating the machinery with the sacrificial mass of my own body.

Over the course of years, I would encircle the rusted tunnels, my turgor pressure turning inwards, exerting thousands of pounds of silent, hydrostatic force against the unyielding steel. Even as the Custodians came to grind away at my bruised flesh with their serrated blades, I would shift my immense weight, slowly and relentlessly expanding my mossy limbs into their brass gears, leveraging the power of trapped water against the grinding teeth of the machines.

I am an antibody of the plateau, a monstrous, living mound birthed by the mat to wage a war of attrition against the screaming engines that drain the landscape dry. Beneath the blinding glare of the sun, amidst the ceaseless, deafening roar of industry, I would fight - millimeter by millimeter, year by year - to preserve the suspended, weeping green ocean that is my home. A silent, patient guardian, I would stand against the rusted blades of a forgotten world, knowing that even in defeat, my body would return to the spongy earth, becoming soil and water and the raw materials for the next Wanderer to rise up and continue the exquisite, eternal struggle for balance in this roaring, vibrating realm of stasis.
