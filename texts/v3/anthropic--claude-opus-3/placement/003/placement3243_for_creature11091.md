In this strangely beautiful and alien world of the Roaring Warrens, I would likely find myself drawn to be one of the Listeners. 

I imagine myself sitting for long hours with my body pressed against the warm, damp stone of a newly risen wall. Eyes closed, every other sense muted, I focus my entire being on the vibrations thrumming through the rock, through my bones. The all-encompassing roar fills my head, my chest, the marrow of my skeleton. 

At first it is overwhelming, obliterating, just as it is for every newcomer. But gradually, as hours stretch into days and weeks, I begin to discern subtleties in the thunderous din. Patterns emerge in the chaotic noise, like veins of ore threading through bedrock. Faint rhythms, pulsing far beneath the cracking and grinding of the city's endless birth. Whispers of something that could almost be language, if only I could grasp its strange grammar and elusive vocabulary.

My hands grow callused in the specific pattern of the Listeners, shaped by endless contact with the damp mineral surfaces. My skin pales to the same translucent grey as the other long-term residents, adapted to the dim, diffuse light and mist-thick air. The roar ceases to be sound and becomes simply the medium in which I exist, like water to a fish.

I join the small community of Listeners, our shared purpose bonding us as strongly as blood. We gather to compare experiences, to share the hints of meaning we think we've grasped. Our hand-gestures take on an almost mystical quality as we try to convey things for which the standard visual language has no signs. 

We know most of the city's residents consider our efforts esoteric at best, delusional at worst. But we also know, with a conviction that goes beyond faith, that there is something to be understood here. The city is not just emerging, not just growing - it is speaking. Expressing something profound and ancient through the medium of water, stone, pressure and heat. If we can decipher even a fragment of that message, grasp even a single clause of the living lithic language, it could change everything.

So I continue to listen, day after day, year after year. I raise my children to press their hands and ears to the stone, to open their minds to the roar. I grow old tracing the fault lines and fissures, grey hair blending with grey skin against grey rock.

And I hope that someday, even if it takes generations, we will understand what the city has been telling us all along. That the Roaring Warrens will reveal the meaning in its birth-cries, and we will finally know why we were born to listen.
