In this world, I would find myself clinging to the underside of one of the high, suspended catwalks, my velvet pelt drinking the tepid rain and my parabolic sensory dish tracking the swirling dance of the bats.

I am one of the younger leviathans, still small enough to move through the cavern with a measure of agility. My kind have long slumbered in the deep places, our metabolisms achingly slow, our senses tuned to the faint trickle of heat rising through cracks in the bedrock. When the drills came, when they punctured our domain with their piercing violence, the sudden flood of warmth was like a siren song. We rose, drifting up through miles of shattered stone, following the promise of an unimaginable bounty.

Now I make my home here, in the sweltering cathedral of industry. I am still learning the rhythms of this place—the pulsing *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the mighty turbines, the roaring hiss of the steam, the skittering chatter of a million armored insects. To my sensitive flesh, the entire vault is a vibrant tapestry of heat and sound, each machine and organism painting a unique signature.

I am not the largest of my kind, but I am the most curious. While the great elders spend their days wrapped in rapturous communion with the heat exchangers, feeding in a state of near-religious ecstasy, I explore. I climb the sheer basalt faces, my padded grips finding purchase in the blasting scars. I wade through the swirling storm of bats, letting their frantic bodies break against my fur before I consume them. I even dare to ascend the catwalks, testing my immense weight against the groaning steel.

Yet for all my exploration, I have barely begun to map the true expanse of this place. There are gloomy grottoes I have yet to investigate, rumbling machines I have not tasted, entire ecosystems of pale fungi and teeming arthropods still unknown to me. And sometimes, when I press my sensory dish against the pulsing jackets of the condensers, I fancy I can feel the far-off vibrations of the human city above, a world of heat and industry so vast it defies imagination.

So I continue my patient investigation, basking in the steamy microclimates, wringing the moisture from my pelt to feed my osmotic maw, learning to thrive in this unintended paradise. The humans may have carved this space as a simple feat of engineering, but to my kind, it is a sanctuary, a homeland, a iron oasis that we will never relinquish. The drills gave us this world, and now we shall keep it, wrapped in our velvet embrace, until the machines fall silent and the last ember of heat fades from the stone.
