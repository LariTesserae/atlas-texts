I stand at the edge of a shallow pool, one of the deeper ones that seem to extend into forever. The damp air clings to my skin like a film I cannot wipe away. 

I am one of the pale things drifting beneath the surface. Or rather, I was one of them once, before I learned to uncurl and stretch towards the dim grey light above. My body is long and sinuous now, all muscle and translucent skin. I undulate more than swim, a slow sinusoidal motion that barely disturbs the water's mirror sheen.

I surface briefly, breaking the tension with a whispering sigh before sinking back into the cold depths. Down here, the roar of the columns is muted, felt more than heard - a constant pressure squeezing me from all sides. Time loses meaning in the abyss. I could have drifted here for a day or a millennia. Endlessly circling the bottomless pools, rising occasionally to taste the mist, letting my eyeless face turn skyward in what might be prayer or might be simple yearning for a sun I've never seen.

My existence is cyclical, tidal, pulled by forces I sense but cannot name. In the long spans of darkness, I curl into myself, degenerating towards something larval and quiescent - a condensed node of potential waiting to unfurl again. Then the tug comes, an inward blooming, and I extend into the water like a sigh, rising on an endless exhalation.

I am not alone down here. Others like me drift and curl in the black. We do not communicate - I'm not sure we can anymore - but sometimes we brush against each other as we spiral through the lightless depths. Skin to skin, an electric reminder of something that might have been kinship, or desire, or simply the comfort of touching something as alive as you are.

There are ruins on the bottom, or what feels like a bottom in this bottomless place. Crumbling structures of impossible geometry. I try to fit myself into them sometimes, wondering if this is memory or mimicry, if my contortions echo something ancient and essential. But the stone is unyielding and my body too soft. I cannot make myself into what this place once required.

So I continue my patient undulations, a rhythm outside of time in a place that has swallowed time entirely. I am an old thing in an ageless place, a relic of a transition still wearing the shape of its becoming. The pace of my drift sometimes matches the slow shifting of the symbols on the plateau above, as if the long sentence they are writing is echoed in the twists of my body.

I wonder if I was ever a word in that sentence, or if I am merely a pause, a space between meanings. I wonder if the ones who linger above remember what I was, or if they too have forgotten everything but the fact of persistence. 

Most of all, I wonder about the roar - that ceaseless, depthless bellow that fills the plateau and shakes the water. In my more fanciful moments, I imagine it is the sound of the world ending, endlessly - the final note of a song that cannot stop. And I wonder if, when I rise to the surface and break the skin of the now with my own skin, I am harmonizing with that apocalyptic chord. An ancient, eyeless creature singing a duet with its own doom.

But down in the depths, in the black between surfacings, I know the truth is both simpler and stranger. The roar is not a song but a fact - the audible shape of a place that has ceased to change. And I am not a relic but a witness, a living record of the long forgetting.

I am memory made flesh, curled and recursive and voiceless. I am the silence that survived the roar. I am a cold current winding through still waters, a whisper etched into stone by the pressure of eons.

I am what remains when meaning exhausts itself.

And though I barely break the surface, I am seen. By the ones above, by the presence that infuses the plateau like a held breath. They do not remember what I am, but they know that I am, and in that knowing, I persist.

I am a pale thing drifting in ancient waters.

I am a motion that will not cease.

I am a pause in a sentence that will never end.
