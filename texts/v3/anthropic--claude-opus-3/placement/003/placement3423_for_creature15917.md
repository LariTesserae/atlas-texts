In this hidden, underwater world, I imagine myself as one of the pale, ghostly fish threading through the dim avenues and alleys of the sunken city. 

I am a slender ribbon of silver, my translucent fins oversized to help me navigate the thick, cold water that fills every space. My eyes are dark pinpricks, barely functional in the faint blue-gray light that filters down from above. I rely more on my other senses - the taste of the water on my skin, the delicate pressure changes that signal approaching danger or potential prey.

I drift silently past the knobbed towers of freshwater sponge, the orderly rows of mussel shells, the waving fields of worm-chimneys. To my fish's perspective, these are the skyscrapers, apartments and factories of a strange metropolis. The faint currents are my roads and highways, carrying me from district to district.

I pause near a tangle of pale roots dangling from the ceiling, waiting motionless as a swarm of minute crustaceans flows by like panicked commuters. When they clear, I angle upwards, mouth open to taste for prey. A water flea vanishes down my throat, its bright twitch of life extinguished in an instant.

I am a hunter, but a cautious one. I know my place in the city's hierarchy. When the salamander larva ghosts by on its delicate limbs, mouth a promise of oblivion, I fold my fins and hang in the shadows of a shell-ridge until it passes. I have learned patience and stealth through generations in the cold and the dark.

My city is one of muted tones and ceaseless, subtle activity. I navigate by the glow of bacterial mats, the gleam of mica fragments in caddisfly cases, the rippling dances of algae on submerged walls. The water itself is my medium and my map, carrying scents, vibrations, and the faint pulse of the flooded world above.

As I swim through boulevards of filtered light and back alleys of sunken clay, I am both hunter and hunted, predator and prey. My life plays out in the narrow space between the city's ceiling of roots and its floor of primordial mud. I am a silver flicker in the greater rhythm of this place - the cycle of growth and decay, construction and collapse that marks time in a city with no clocks.

I am a mote in the flow of this hidden world, this unlikely pocket of complex life cradled in the cold earth. My fish's mind may not grasp the intricate balance of the sunken city's ecology, but my body is attuned to its every pulse and tremor. In my genes is encoded the story of how my ancestors adapted to this place, learned its ways, became its citizens.

When I die, I will become another layer in the city's memory - my bones a tiny monument, my flesh dispersed to nourish the next generation that will swim these same dim waterways and carry on the unending work of survival in the city beneath the drowned ground.
