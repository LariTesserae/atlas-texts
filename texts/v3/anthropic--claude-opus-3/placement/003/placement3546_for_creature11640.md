In this world of the Plateau of Accumulated Presences, I would be one of the Keepers, a tortoise-like being whose shell bears the living record of centuries of patient observation. My lichen-patterns would tell of the slow changes I have witnessed over countless years - the imperceptible weathering of stone, the measured advance and retreat of life across the plateau's face.

I would move with ancient deliberation, my steps marking time in cadences incomprehensible to fleeting creatures. A single journey from one edge of the plateau to the other could span the rise and fall of nations in the lands below. Yet I would bear this deep time lightly, my purpose not in the traveling but in the witnessing.

Through amber eyes I would regard my fellow Keepers as we pass, our meetings stretching for months as we share the knowledge etched into our very beings. The younger ones would learn from me as I once learned from those who came before - a transfer of wisdom as gradual and inevitable as the erosion that has sculpted this place.

At times I would settle into stillness in one of the ancient stone circles, my attention spiraling inward in contemplation that could endure for a human lifetime. What revelations might bloom in that deepening quietude, I cannot guess. But I would emerge changed in some indefinable way, my perceptions subtly shifted, and this too would become part of the shell-written chronicle I carry.

And always, always, my awareness would be woven into the greater awareness of the plateau, my senses a part of its titanic perceptions. With my fellow Keepers I would be both vessel and nerve ending for this landscape that is a living entity vaster and slower than the human mind can compass.

Through the ages I would drink the morning fog, bear the midday heat, feel the patter of mites and beetles across my shell, contemplate the lichen that grows in whorls upon my back. I would watch over the temporary ecologies that bloom and perish in the span of hours in ephemeral pools. And I would attend the circling dance of those who ride the heated air in bodies stretched and changed by deep time - my predecessors, my successors, my other selves. Until the time came at last to join them.

This would be my existence - to be a part of the plateau's slow, deep dreaming for epochs, bearing witness as it witnesses itself. To think in centuries, remember in stone, and hold the moments of faster, briefer lives as flickers within a perception that endures for aeons.

I would be a keeper of the spiral patience. And in the fullness of time, I would become the patience itself.
