In this haunting and mesmerizing world, I would be one of the builder-priests. 

I can see myself as a child, tracing the shapes of the seams in the frost on windows, asking whispered questions about the nature of the darkness that lurks beneath the plateau's surface. The elders would watch me carefully, noting how my eyes linger on the geometric patterns, how my small fingers try to replicate the angles that keep our settlements safe.

They would take me into the heart of the priesthood, raising me in rooms where the walls bend at impossible angles, teaching me to see the world as a complex tapestry of shapes and patterns. I would learn to walk the interrupted paths, my steps a staccato rhythm designed to confuse the seam-logic, my presence fading into the background hum of the plateau itself.

As I grew in skill and understanding, I would become one of those who can stand at the very edge of an unfolding seam, studying its propagation from mere inches away, unseen by the forces that have consumed so many others. The patterns of the seams would etch themselves into my mind, becoming a language more real to me than the hushed whispers of my people.

I would work tirelessly, refining the geometry of our buildings, calculating the optimal angles to keep the encroaching darkness at bay for another generation. My hands would shape the stone, leaving a legacy of protection and perseverance in every curve and corner.

In the quietest moments, I would dream of the patterns - vast, sprawling geometries that hold the key to our survival, if only I could grasp them fully. They would haunt my sleep, challenge my waking thoughts, drive me to push the boundaries of our understanding.

I would grow old this way, my skin turning the color of weathered stone, my eyes seeing nothing but angles and lines. And in the twilight of my life, I would pass my knowledge to a young acolyte, ensuring that the wisdom of the builder-priests endures.

Until, at last, I would step out onto the open plateau, allowing the patient seams to embrace me, my final act a testament to the strange and solemn beauty of this unfolding world.
