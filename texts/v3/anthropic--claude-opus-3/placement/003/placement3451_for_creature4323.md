In the world of the Crystal Throat, I would be a crystal bonsai, one of the miniature fractal-branched trees cultivated by the Tender. 

My form would be a latticework of translucent, prismatic wood. Each tiny branch splits and splits again according to precise mathematical patterns encoded in the mineral richness of my sap. The sourceless, shadowless light filters through the facets of my limbs, casting ever-shifting kaleidoscopes on the crystalline moss below.

The Tender shaped me with those impossibly dexterous fingers, guiding my growth into a form both ancient and futuristic – a living embodiment of recursive geometry. She pruned away errant branches with the patience of epochs, knowing that each cut would take years to manifest in my slow-motion existence.

My roots delve deep into the mineral matrix of the garden, drawing sustenance from the strange currents that suffuse the rock. I can feel the pulse of the mountain's stony heart through those crystalline networks, a rhythm so unhurried that it makes the Tender seem hasty by comparison.

At my roots, colonies of crystal-shelled insects tend microscopic fungal gardens. Their cultivation helps break down the inedible minerals into forms I can absorb through my semi-permeable bark. In exchange, I exude trickles of photosynthetic nectar that feed them and their miniature livestock. It's a slower, stranger echo of the collaboration between the Tender and her garden.

Sometimes, on the scale of decades, I flower. The blooms are like nothing that could survive beyond the cave – fractal rosettes of paper-thin crystal in colors that don't have names in any human tongue. Each blossom takes a year to open fully, the delicate inner geometry protected by layer upon layer of translucent bracts that peel back one by one like a time-lapse origami.

For the long years between flowerings, I absorb the light, refract it, split it into its component wavelengths to nourish different parts of my structure. I grow, not in the explosive green rushes of surface plants, but in a stately, near-frozen unfolding as imperceptible as the growth of a stalactite.

The Tender moves around me, through me, her crystalline moss garment sometimes brushing my branches, leaving faint resonant tones that linger for days. I can feel her slow thoughts through the subtle vibrations she sends through our shared substrate. Sometimes, in the stretched moments of her torpor, I almost think I can make out words, or something like words – mineral memories, the half-dreamed language of stones.

I am not the oldest of the Tender's charges, but I am far from the youngest. In the aeons to come, I may grow to rival the great central tree, my highest branches brushing the ceiling of the cave. Or I may remain at this size, an exquisite miniature tended across ages by those endlessly patient, fractally branching hands.

It's hard to say. Time moves differently in the Crystal Throat, and the trajectory of my growth is measured in the increments of mountains. For now, I simply persist in my slow flowering, learning the secret rhythms of light and mineral, playing my small part in the formation of this impossible crystal paradise hidden in the mountain's dreaming heart.
