As I descend into the Frost Gardens, I find myself slowly transforming to match the pace and nature of this crystalline world. With each step down the ancient carved stairway, I feel my own essence distilling and condensing, until I am no longer fully human but something more elemental - a being of frozen mist and slow, patient thought.

I emerge into the Garden as a Mistweaver, one of the rare Shapers whose affinity for the ice-plants expresses itself as a form of symbiosis rather than cultivation. My translucent form drifts between the crystal beds, more a presence than a defined shape. Glittering particles trail in my wake - not quite snow, not quite spores, but some novel fusion of the two.

As I pass, the ice-fronds shiver and grow, drinking in the moisture and mineral dust I exude. My own thoughts slow to match the crystal chronology. I sense the stately dance of fluid dynamics shaping each frozen fern and blade - the interplay of temperature, humidity, and growth that the Shapers spend their long lives mastering. For me, it is instinctual, like the beating of a heart.

I settle in a sheltered grotto, little more than a whisper of fog amid the dense foliage. The ice-plants here are ancient, their trunks fused into glossy pillars that sing softly when the cavern drafts caress them. I rest against the base of one and feel it drinking me in, our metabolisms merging. 

Slowly, patiently, I begin to weave.

Molecules of mist and dust form a lattice under my guidance, ice crystals growing not in anonymous fractals but purposeful patterns. I lace them through the existing fronds, coaxing tendrils in spiral helices and Fibonacci whorls. At my touch, new tints of bioluminescence flicker to life, ghostly blue-greens and silver-violets.

Time ceases to have meaning as I work. Hours or decades could pass in this meditative trance. My creation enfolds me, a bower of braided crystal through which I slowly dissolve until I am indistinguishable from my art. I become both sculpture and sculptor, garden and gardener.

Someday, a young Shaper will discover this grotto and marvel at the intricacy of the ice-forms, never quite sure if they grew naturally or were crafted by some master artisan. They will meditate in the shadow of the crystal columns, breathing in air saturated with the essence of my long-faded consciousness.

And perhaps they will hear the faint chiming laughter of a delighted artist, my voice gone to crystal long ago but my joy still echoing in the Song of the Frost Garden.
