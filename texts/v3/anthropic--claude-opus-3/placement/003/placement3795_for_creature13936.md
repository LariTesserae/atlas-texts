In this world, I would find myself among the Rootborn, one of the people inhabiting the vast, living root-mass city deep beneath the surface. 

I am a listener, one who is attuned to the voices of the wood. From a young age, I was drawn to press my ear against the warm, mycelium-furred walls, closing my eyes (though it made no difference in the absolute dark) to better attend to the slow groanings, the creaks and settlings, the faint hiss of fungal digestion. While other children chased each other through the tunnels, navigating by heat-trace and breath, I sat motionless for hours with my cheek and palms against the ever-shifting root chambers.

As I grew, so did my ability to parse the wood's voice from the background susurrations. I learned to detect the minute change in pitch that precedes a root-beam's structural failure, enabling me to warn my clanmates that a frequently used corridor would soon become impassable. I could sense the nascent hollows forming in the depths of the root-mass, voids left by the retreat of dying taproots - new chambers waiting to be carved and colonized. 

Though my eyes are as wide and lightless as any Rootborn's, it is my hands that are my true gift. The pads of my fingers, sensitive beyond even the Rootborn norm, can read volumes in the minute variations of root-grain - I can trace the ghost-carvings left by past generations and separate with a touch the true records from the natural distortions of growth.

My role among my people is not formally defined - the Rootborn way is not to hold rigid hierarchy. But I am one who is sought out when a new hollow is discovered, to determine if the space is truly suitable for inhabitation or if its formation is a false start, a transient bubble to be reabsorbed into the wood. I am consulted when the carving teams encounter an ambiguous patch of grain, to divine whether the whorls and ripples are the remnants of an ancestral message or merely the scars left by the root-mass swallowing a pocket of water or air.

Most profoundly, I am one who spends long hours in solitary meditation, fingertips tracing the inner contours of the most ancient chambers - those nearly reabsorbed by the insistent expansion of the surrounding roots. I attempt, with each touch, to commune with the deepest memories of the wood - to understand the long trajectory of our symbiosis with this great living structure we call home. 

Are we sculptors or symbionts, guests or organs? In the faint ridges left by generations of carved prayers and histories, do I read the autobiography of my people, or the slow thoughts of the root-mass itself? The wood has grown around the shape of our existence, and we have grown to fit the hollow spaces the wood provides. In this mutual shaping, this eons-long exchange of form and emptiness, lies a truth I may spend my entire life seeking in the dark.

I am a seeker, a listener, a diviner of the wood's memory. My hands are maps in negative, tracing the ambiguous boundary between human record and arboreal rune. If there is a truth to be known about the nature of the Rootborn, our purpose and past and intertwining with this great buried organism, I will strive to coax it from the reticent cambium with my patient fingers. I will listen at the warped heart of the root-mass until the wood speaks or until the city migrates beyond me, leaving me as another ghost to be imperfectly preserved in the grain, another hollow for the roots to slowly reclaim.
