In the world of the Vertical City of Moths, I imagine myself as one of the Deep Listeners - those attuned to the voice of the living mountain itself.

I dwell on the outermost edges of the city, where the last tendrils of structure merge seamlessly into raw stone. My moth-cloud is sparse out here, many of my bonded insects having been drawn to the deeper stillness of the rock face. Those that remain are the oldest, their wings translucent with age, tracing delicate spirals in the thin air.

My days are spent in a kind of walking meditation, traversing the narrow ledges and hidden paths that lace the cliff. I place my hands and feet with deliberate care, feeling for the minute tremors that run through the stone, the barely perceptible shifts that might presage a more catastrophic movement.

When I sense a change in the mountain's patterns, I send word back to the city through a series of touch-signs relayed by the younger Tenders who bring me supplies. A particular sequence pressed into a palm as food is handed over, a pattern of squeezes on a wrist as a worn tool is replaced - these are enough to convey the necessary information.

But my true work happens in the deep hours of the night, when even the faint hum of the city fades and the moths sleep in motionless clusters. In these moments, I press my body against the cliff face, splaying my hands and feet to make maximum contact. And I listen.

It's a listening not just of the ears, but of the bones, of the breath, of the blood moving through my veins. I attune myself to the mountain's frequencies, letting its subsonic murmurs resonate in my chest cavity. Over time, I've learned to distinguish its different voices - the deep groan of tectonic plates in slow friction, the higher whisper of wind through infinitesimal cracks, the sudden snap of frost shattering stone.

Sometimes, in the deepest communion, I feel the mountain's awareness brush against my own. It's a vast, slow, cool presence, as patient and unhurried as the city itself. I get impressions more than clear thoughts - a sense of great age, of pressures balanced on the knife's edge of time, of a watchfulness that endures on a scale beyond human reckoning.

There are times I've felt it looking through me, examining the fragile threads of organic life that run through its domain. I wonder if it knows why we're here, if our presence is tolerated or welcomed or merely insignificant. I wonder if it dreams, and if so, whether our city and its workings figure into those mineral visions.

When the morning comes, I uncoil myself from the stone, joints popping like ice cracking. The moths stir as the first glimmers of light activate the city's outer edges. Another day of listening begins, another chance to feel for those critical whispers in the rock.

I know one day I'll miss something - we all do, eventually. Even the most attentive Deep Listener cannot catch every sign, predict every shift. And when that day comes, part of the city will fall, torn loose by the mountain's inexorable movements. We'll rebuild, as we always have, weaving new structure into raw stone, spinning new bridge cables across voids.

But until then, I listen. I place my hands and feet, I attune my breath, I let the mountain's voice echo in the chambers of my body. I am a nerve ending of the city, a sensory node pressed against the living rock. I am a Deep Listener, and this is my purpose, my place in the vast, slow, dream-like workings of the Vertical City of Moths.
