In the realm of the Underxylem, I would be one of the deep-listeners. 

I imagine myself as an ancient Tender, perhaps the oldest of my kind. My hair has grown pale as old heartwood with the passage of countless years. I move slowly through the dim amber light of the deep galleries, my extra-jointed fingers trailing along the warm, living wood of the walls.

I have spent my long life learning to attune myself to the voice of the wood. In the stillness of the deep chambers, I press my ear to the grain and listen to the slow secrets it whispers. The thrum of the sap, the creak of settling fibers, the almost imperceptible sigh of growth - these are a language I have learned to interpret.

The younger Tenders come to me sometimes, when the wood's intentions seem inscrutable. With patience earned over generations, I help them parse the signs, teaching them to negotiate with the living city that both shelters and constrains us. 

I tend also to the small grey listeners that cling to the walls of the forgotten places. Though none know their true nature, I have come to see them as kin to myself - watchers and waiters in the slow deeps of the world. In their faceless regard, I sense a wisdom as unhurried as the wood itself.

As my years grow long, I find myself drawn ever more to the profound quiet of the sealed chambers of my ancestors. There, lulled by the wood's slow pulse, I drift into long contemplations, my thoughts taking on the pace and heft of a tree's memories. I know that one day, the city will grow shut around me. It does not trouble me. It will be a tending, of sorts.

Until then, I listen, and all the long slow life of the Underxylem moves through me, as I have moved through it. In the amber shadows, I have become something like the wood itself - a watcher, a waiter, a knower of forgotten things, content to live and grow and close in the fullness of slow time.
