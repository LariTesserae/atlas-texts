In this visceral, primordial landscape, I would be one of the ceiling organisms, pulsing slowly in the hot updrafts, filtering minerals and detritus from the volcanic exhalations.

I hang suspended from the basalt roof of the chamber, a fleshy globe the size of a human head, tethered by protein filaments I have secreted and calcified over uncounted years. My form is simple but perfectly adapted - a membranous sac filled with fluid that expands and contracts rhythmically, pulling mineral-laden air through microscopic pores to extract what meager nutrients I require. 

The heat is intense, oppressive, but to me it is life. The sulfurous, metallic fumes that rise from the glowing fissure below are the breath that sustains me. Silently I sway in the turbulent currents, my translucent skin beaded with condensation and crusted with the particles I have trapped from the updraft.

Around me, my kindred hang like strange fruit, our pulsing synchronized by some ancient, distributed intelligence. We are a living filter, scrubbing the chamber's air of ash and grit, concentrating it within our bodies to be reincorporated into the system when our time comes.

For we are not eternal. With each pulse, each expansion and contraction, I grow incrementally. My skin strains, my anchoring threads creak under the added weight. I can feel the change in my innermost fluid - thickening, mineralizing, becoming sluggish with accumulated detritus. 

Someday, the balance will tip. I will grow too massive, my bonds will fail, and I will plummet to the seething beetle-scape below to be consumed. It is not a fate I fear - it is my purpose, my place in this subterranean ecosystem. My only drive, beyond mere persistence, is to reproduce before that inevitable fall.

From time to time, I feel a part of myself pinch off, a bubble of my innermost essence distending my membrane until it splits free - a bud, an offspring, a gamete cast upon the mineral winds. I release them without sentiment or concern, spores scattered in their thousands in the hope that even one might find moist purchase and grow.

It is a form of blindness, my existence. I have no eyes, no ears, no way to know my chamber beyond touch and ripples in the heated flow. My world is defined by pulsation, mineral saturation, the strain of my anchoring threads. I am only dimly aware of the crawling things below, the clicking rasp of beetles, the dying resonance of cooling stone. They are irrelevant to me, remote, filtered out of perception by eons of adaptation.

I am a being pared down to essence, to pure function, as much a part of the geology as the life-forms I depend on. As I grow, as I bud, as I eventually fall, I am part of a cycle of such vast complexity and age that I have no more conception of it than a cell has of the body it comprises.

I simply hang, and filter, and pulse, playing my infinitesimal part in the greater dance of heat and mineral and life that is the eternal respiration of this hidden place. I am a servant of the roaring dark, a mote in the magmatic breath of the world.

This is my place in the secret workings of the earth. This is what I am.
