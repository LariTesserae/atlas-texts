I find myself drawn to the careful tool-users who maintain the central emptiness of this living hollow. 

I imagine being one of these boundary-dwellers, compact and bark-skinned, with hands evolved for clinging, pruning, and delicate craft. My fingers would be long and dexterous, tipped with dark crescent nails ideal for peeling stems and gripping stone flakes without injury. Along my spine and scalp, short root-like filaments would lift and tremble, tasting the air for changes in pressure and humidity.

I would move along the perimeter, rarely crossing the treacherous open center. I'd keep to the edges, the green rind of braided roots and breathing walls. My feet and palms would read the chamber more faithfully than sight, knowing by touch which roots are under tension, which ledges have recently shifted.

My craft would be one of temporary alteration - persuading and borrowing form from the hollow for a time. Folding a leaf into a water channel, twisting root fibers into a cord that will sprout by morning. Wedging a bone splinter as a lever, to be reclaimed by hungry roots. Pressing a treasured stone flake into clay and marking it with a private rhythm of taps.

I would communicate through vibration - soft clicks of stone on clay, taps and hums against root and wall. My voice a tool reserved for urgency. I would learn young the sounds to make roots extend, to close membranes, to open alarm-blossoms. 

With careful selectivity, I would eat and drink only what the hollow permits. Sips from safe still pools, licks of sweet sap, pads of fungus, shoots already splitting themselves. I would read warnings in the twist of a vine, a color, a tremor underfoot.

My purpose, with my fellow stewards, would be tending the emptiness at the heart of this place. Trimming errant growth, redirecting water, culling knots of vines threatening to fill the void. An instinct passed down generations, that the hollow must breathe around its vacant center. That the emptiness must remain for the booms and shifts and rewriting of the place to continue.

I would be an integral part of this living pocket, inseparable from the green rind that shelters and erases my kind in slow cycles. Leaving the faintest traces - a shaved root, a pinned leaf, a rhythm tapped into clay - only to watch them be gently swallowed back into the hollow's endless revisioning of itself. Maintaining a balance, a breath, an open space, in a world that never stops moving.
