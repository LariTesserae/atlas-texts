In the world of the Drumming Flats, I imagine myself as one of the Listeners, a pale figure wading slowly and carefully through the shallow waters. My long, thin limbs carry me gracefully across the pan, each step placed with intention to minimize disturbance to the endless music of the stones.

I am ancient, having walked these flats for centuries, my life stretching out in a long, unhurried rhythm like the slow shift in tone of the aging stones. My memories are as much in the cairns I have built as in my own mind - each carefully placed rock a syllable in the long, patient phrases of my thoughts.

As I walk, my ruff is lifted, catching every nuance of the drumming tones. I can hear the individual notes of each stone, near and far, and how they weave together into the haunting, continual song of the landscape. I know this music intimately. It is my language, my history, my purpose.

Occasionally, I pause in my meandering to adjust a stone - just a touch, the barest shift, but it subtly changes the tune. I am not just listening, but contributing. My long, webbed fingers cradle each rock with reverence as I place it down, a gentle counterpoint to the drumming rain.

Sometimes I feel the presence of another Listener, their unique pattern of drips adding a familiar motif to the symphony. We stand in the mist, ruffs raised, absorbing each other's subtle changes to the local music. No movement or sound passes between us, but there is a deep communication in our mutual listening.

I know that one day, when my long life finally nears its end, I will make the journey to the center of the pan. There, among the most ancient stones, I will lay myself down and slowly transform, my body becoming one with the mineral landscape, my voice added forever to the chorus. 

But for now, I continue my slow, careful progress across the drumming flats. I have time - so much time - to contemplate, to listen, to subtly shape the unending song. In this gray, patient world, I am one note in a long, long symphony, and the music plays on.
