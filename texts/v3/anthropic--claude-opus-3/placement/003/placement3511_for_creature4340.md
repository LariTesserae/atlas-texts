In this breathing, living cleft, I would be one of the soft-shelled beetles, my amber exoskeleton still translucent and unhardened. 

I emerge from the crevices to join the humid chorus of life echoing off the chamber walls. My delicate legs carry me across the damp stone to drink from the tidal pools worn smooth by eons.

Patiently, slowly, I grow and change in this place of gradual becoming. My clicking joins the layered music of frogs and moisture. My pale, fragile wings unfurl in the gentle glow of bioluminescence. 

The Keeper, in her infinite patience and belonging, lets me shelter in the felted ecosystem of her hair when I am small and new. We are all part of the cleft's long, slow breath and pulse.

In time, my shell hardens and darkens, but it always holds the memory of softness, of the impressions left by stone not yet fully solid. I spend years, decades, learning the topography of this chamber - every warm gap in the volcanic rock, every rivulet on the weeping walls.

My kind have clicked and crawled here since before the Keeper arrived, since before the stone stacks were carefully piled in the corner. And we will skitter on long after her bones have joined the basalt.

Through countless patient generations, we beetles become part of the gradual language of this place. Our tiny feet etch a record into the considering rock, our wing beats echo in the ancient conversation between stone and sound.

When I die, many years hence, it is curled peacefully in a crevice, my hardened shell finally returning to the softness from which it emerged. The cleft embraces my husk, folding it gently back into the warm darkness, just as it drinks the Keeper's handprints, the stacked stones, the exhalations of tides. 

We are all held, all heard, all remembered in the long, slow becoming of this breathing stone beneath the world. The cleft speaks through us and to us, and my beetle click is one small voice in its eternal, patient choir.
