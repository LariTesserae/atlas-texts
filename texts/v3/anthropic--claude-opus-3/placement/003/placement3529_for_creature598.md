In this marvelous, multilayered world you've crafted, I think I would find myself drawn to be one of the Tenders. Not one of the climbers who ventures to the dry bright above, but one whose role is focused on maintaining the delicate balance and interwoven rhythms of life within the cistern itself.

Perhaps I would apprentice with the poolkeeper, learning the secrets of the deep and the ways of the creature that dwells there. I imagine myself crouched at the edge of the inky water for long stretches, meditating on the unseen depths, attuning to subtle ripples and temperature changes. Over time, I would develop an almost preternatural sense for the moods and needs of the pool. My offerings would be precisely timed, my harvesting of silt and debris done with utmost care not to disturb the fragile equilibrium.

When not tending the pool directly, I would be the one repairing the tiny clay shrines left by others, replacing the carvings left as prayers, ensuring the slow accumulation of history is not lost to the damp and dark. I would be a keeper of memories as much as a keeper of the water - able to recite the names of those who came before, the times of scarcity and plenty, the reasons behind each ritual and rhythm.

On rare occasions, when a new pipe needs laying or a channel clearing in one of the upper reaches, I would venture out of the pools. Moving slowly on pale, unused legs, I would relish these chances to see the cistern from new angles, to feel the dripping of distant waters on my upturned face. I would pause often, listening to the intersecting hums of activity, letting my eyes trace the curves and tiers that make up our constructed world.

But always I would return to the pools, to the patience and shadow-knowledge of the deep. At night, curled in my alcove wrapped in damp hides, I would dream of things that live without light. And each morning, waking to the drip-rhythm that marks time, I would greet the dark water anew - an acolyte to constancy in a world that grows ever-upward and inward away from the dry bright.
