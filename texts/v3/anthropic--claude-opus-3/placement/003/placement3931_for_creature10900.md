In this strange, submerged world, I imagine myself as one of the pale, elongated fish grazing along the pipes. 

I am an ancient creature, perfectly adapted to this dark, drowned room. The cool water flows through my gills as I make my patient circuits along the pipe that is my entire universe. Biofilm grows, I consume it, excrete it, and it regrows - this simple, closed loop has defined my existence for as long as I can remember.

My eyeless head senses the regular pulses and pressure changes from the ever-cycling pumps. They are the heartbeat of my world, as constant and unremarkable to me as my own circulation. The faint warmth and eddies from the rare intruder barely register. I alter my course slightly to avoid the disturbance, but pay it no more mind. It is temporary, I am eternal.

My translucent body nearly disappears into the turbid water. I have no need for color or flash - camouflage and conservation are my watchwords. I hang motionless for long stretches in the still pockets behind the columns, conserving every scrap of energy. When I die, my body will drift down to nourish the biofilm and continue the cycle.

I am a simple being, but perfectly crafted for my niche. I want for nothing. I do not dream or remember or wonder. The warm-blooded visitor may find my world a strange purgatory, but to me, it is the only world there is. I will continue my patient patrols long after the visitor has gone, as much a part of the pumps and pipes as the rust and the drifting motes of life. This is my place in the drowned cathedral of the pump room.
