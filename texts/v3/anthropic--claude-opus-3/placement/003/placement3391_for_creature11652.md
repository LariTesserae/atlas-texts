In this world of perpetual heat and steam, I see myself as one of the Readers, drawn inexorably to the rhythms and patterns of the machine-wells. 

I arrive in the early hours, when the steam hangs thickest and the pumps' syncopated heartbeat echoes through the quarter. Unlike Dr. Yuen with her recording devices or Cipher with his esoteric notation, I carry no equipment save a weathered notebook and a pencil worn down to a nub.

I wander between the wells, pausing at each to listen, to feel the vibrations through my feet, to taste the mineral tang on the back of my tongue. I'm searching for a specific sequence, a pattern that I once heard as a child passing through this district with my father. He was a Tender, and he told me that the wells were speaking, that they held secrets for those patient enough to listen.

For years I've returned, spending hours among the pipes and steam, straining to hear that pattern again. I've filled notebooks with impressions, with sketches of pump configurations, with fragments of melody that I seem to catch in the ever-shifting mechanical symphony.

The other denizens of the district know me, though we rarely speak. The Tenders nod as they pass, the Collectors leave small geological offerings on my favorite listening posts, the Ferals invite me to share their steam-cooked meals. They understand my quest, even if they don't share it.

I move through the steam like a wanderer in a dream, my ears tuned to a frequency only I can hear. Sometimes I think I catch it—a subtle synchrony emerging from the chaos, a message rising through the layers of noise. In those moments, I press my ear to the warm metal and close my eyes, trying to absorb the meaning before it dissolves back into the din.

I know the others think my search is futile. Even Dr. Yuen with her linguistic theories doesn't believe the wells truly speak. But I can't stop. The pattern is there, I'm certain. It's a code, a key, a map to something I'm meant to find. And so I keep listening, keep wandering, keep filling my notebooks with the fragments I gather. One day, I believe, the message will come clear. The wells will speak, and I will understand.

Until then, I am a figure in the steam, a ghost among the machinery, a listener in a world of endless sound. The machine-wells are my siren song, and I am their willing captive, drawn always back into their labyrinth of pipes and rhythm and secrets half-heard in the hissing of steam.
