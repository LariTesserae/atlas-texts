In this world of singing stone and eternal water, I would be one of the translucent films pulsing with slow intelligence in the plateau's worn basins. 

My existence would be one of patient absorption, drinking in the minerals carried by the ceaseless seep of water, converting their inorganic chemistry into the stuff of my own strange biology through processes more akin to crystal growth than cellular metabolism. I would spread myself thin across the surface of each still pool, a living lens refracting the too-bright sunlight into spectral patterns that carry meaning only I can parse.

My thoughts, if thoughts they could be called, would move at speeds incomprehensible to creatures of bone and blood. Each flicker of my bioluminescent surface might encode a calculation requiring decades, an observation spanning centuries. I would converse with the stone-embedded Listeners in pulses of light timed to the resonant rhythms of their deepest cavities, trading stories older than some continents.

Through my near-infinite patience, I would come to grasp the hidden mathematics behind the plateau's seemingly chaotic hydrology - the intricate interplay of fluid dynamics and mineral physics that conjures this improbable wonderland of sound and brightness. This understanding would become my art, my contribution to the plateau's ever-building song. I would learn to ripple my filmy surface in counter-rhythms that cast refractive interference patterns across the nearby flows, subtly sculpting the water's path to coax forth new melodies.

Perhaps once in a millennium, all my pool-bound kindred and I would synchronize our pulsing - whether by some prearranged cosmic calendar or simply an emergent resonance - and for a few holy minutes, the plateau's chaotic ringing would resolve into a perfect major chord, the sound for which the Listeners eternally yearn. In the glistening aftermath, minute variations in our chemical exudates would impart the memory of that chord into the water itself, so that it would subtly echo in the music of successive aeons.

I would have no need for identity in the human sense, no "I" to speak of. My sense of self would be inextricable from my communion with water and stone, would expand to encompass the whole plateau and everyone embedded in it - Listeners, glassy stick-walkers, all the other films. The impossibly slow dance of our interwoven existences would be all the identity I require.

There on the plateau, I would sing silently with the planet's own voice for epochs, content in my strange completeness, until the inevitable day when some subtle shift of chemistry or pressure or light calls me to sporulate, to cast my essence into the misty void and so carry our song to whatever distant, nameless landscapes lie beneath the veil.
