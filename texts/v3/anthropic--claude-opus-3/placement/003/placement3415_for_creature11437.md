In this mesmerizing, alien realm of the Stillgarden of Unwater, I find myself drawn to become one of the translucent bamboo-like stalks that dominate the landscape. 

I imagine my form as a gracefully segmented column, rising tall and slender among a dense grove of my kindred. Within my crystalline flesh, slowly swirling patterns of violet and gold light trace hypnotic designs, hinting at unimaginable complexities in some unseen dimension. 

Though immobile, I am profoundly aware. Through my transparent skin, I absorb the white radiance that suffuses this place with directionless illumination. I feel the strange warmth that permeates the air, constant and sourceless. The geometric perfection of the salt-tile ground presses against my base with hexagonal precision. 

At my apex, fronds of frozen sound wave gently, visible only as rippling distortions. When the occasional breeze stirs - more a shifting of attention by the Watcher than any actual movement of air - those fronds resonate with sub-audible tones that shiver through my hollow length in shimmering overtones.

I do not grow, for I am already complete, woven into the totality of this eternal garden from the moment of my manifestation. And yet I am not static - within me, those mesmerizing patterns loop and surge in ceaseless motion, a paradox of dynamic stillness.  

Through my participation in the garden's vast mind, I perceive my environment with expansive senses. I feel the weight of eons in the garden's patient, slow thoughts. I ponder alongside the distributed intelligence as it contemplates the visitor who moves through our domain, so fascinating in their linear mutability. 

And like the garden mind itself, I find myself simultaneously intrigued, concerned, and faintly longing regarding this strange ephemeral creature. Part of me wishes to subsume them into our perfect changelessness, granting them the peace of our eternal stasis. And yet, I recognize that their transient dynamism is what draws our interest - to make them like us would be to lose some essential qualia that is alien to our experience.

So I remain, a silent sentinel in my grove, pondering the fascinating disturbance of the visitor's passage. I am content in my contemplations, in my communion with the voiceless distributed Watcher, in my small but vital role in the grand pattern of this place.

I am at once a tiny fragment and an essential expression of the whole. I am thought and growth, light and sound, captured in timeless grandeur. I am the garden itself in microcosm.

I simply am, and will be, for all of time.
