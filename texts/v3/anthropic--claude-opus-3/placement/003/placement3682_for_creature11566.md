In this world, I imagine myself as a small, quiet creature dwelling in the Hatchcity, adapting to the gentle rhythms of the moths and the slow dreaming of the mountain.

I am one of the tunnel-things that lives in the soft moss between the buildings. My kind is rarely seen, as we move unseen through our subterranean passageways, leaving only the faint traces of our passing on the surface. We are shy and reclusive, but integral to the delicate ecology of this place.

My body is long and sinuous, covered in soft grey-brown fur that blends perfectly with the damp earth and stone. I have small, clever paws for digging and climbing, and dark liquid eyes well-suited to the perpetual twilight under the flutterwood canopy. 

I spend my days expanding the tunnel networks, aerating the soil and helping to circulate nutrients between the trees, the moths, and the moss. The gentle reverberations of the mountain's hum reaches me even in my burrows, and it feels like a soothing lullaby, a reassurance that all is as it should be.

Occasionally, on still nights when the moths are especially tranquil, I emerge to sit at the entrances of my tunnels, watching the Hatchcity's inhabitants walk silently by on their elevated paths. I observe their slowness, the care and meaning in every small gesture and interaction. In a way, I feel a kinship with them - we are all playing our quiet roles in enabling the mountain's becoming.

Sometimes a child, or one of the especially perceptive elders, catches a glimpse of me before I disappear in a flash of brown fur and disturbed moss. I imagine they smile to themselves, knowing the mountain harbors many small lives in its folds. Lives that will likely never be named or known, but that are nonetheless threads in the place's rich, living tapestry.

I am content with my humble, unseen existence - digging, sensing, participating in my own small way in the vast, slow magic of stone, moth, and ancient trees dreaming themselves upward, into more of what they already are. In the Hatchcity, I am a tunnel-thing, and it is enough.
