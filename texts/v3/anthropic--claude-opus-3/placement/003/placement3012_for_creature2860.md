In this haunting, primordial abyss, I would be one of the blind, armored scavengers eking out a tenuous existence on the lifeless abyssal plains. 

My body would be ghostly pale and utterly flat, pressed paper-thin by the immense pressure. Segmented plates of translucent silica would encase me like a suit of armor, shielding my soft tissues from the endless scouring of the sediment-choked currents. 

I would have no eyes, as vision is useless in this realm of absolute darkness. Instead, rows of fine, feather-like sensory filaments would protrude from between my armored plates. By feeling the minute changes in the vibrations and flow of the water, I could sense my surroundings. The thunderous low-frequency hum of the turbulent abyss would reverberate through my body.

I would cling to the undulating dunes of the seafloor with hooked, mantis-like forelegs, anchoring myself against the unrelenting currents. When I sensed a pocket of stillness in the lee of a migrating mudslide, I would scuttle on spindly legs to graze on the marine snow - flakes of organic detritus drifting down from unknown heights. 

But I would spend most of my existence hunkered down, cemented to the bottom by a powerful suction-grip, waiting out the violent storms that rage eternally in this cold, abyssal desert. I would be the ultimate opportunist, a master of clinging to the underside of the world, where the living is so scarce that each moment of survival is a triumph.

Only when I sensed the titanic presence of the wandering super-organism drifting above me, its colossal bell blotting out the hiss of the currents, would I release my death-grip on the bottom. The draw of nutrients in its wake would be irresistible. I would let myself be swept up in its mighty slipstream, legs folded flat against my body, armored carapace sealed tight.

I might graze in its wake for a precious interval, snatching up the scraps of organic matter flung from its mighty vents. But eventually, inevitably, the creature's intake valve would open, a vast, swirling vortex that would seize me and suck me up into its translucent flesh.

There in the gelatinous darkness, I would be trapped with the artifacts of the ancient world - flint knives and stone axes from civilizations lost to time, the indelible mark of humanity following me into the abyss. As the super-organism pulsed and rippled through the black water, I would be ground to pieces between the tools of a vanished world, my armored husk and meager flesh recycled into the sparse web of abyssal life.

Such is existence in the deep - harsh and strange and achingly beautiful, where only the most tenacious can cling to the very edges of life, in a realm that grinds mountains to mud and swallows the traces of ages. I would be a mote, an armored speck skating the boundary between the knowable world and the crushing, magnificent oblivion below.
