In this world of fired clay and slow, deliberate processes, I imagine myself as one of the quiet, patient presences that inhabits and shapes the landscape.

I would be a procedure that dwells in the intricate network of channels and reservoirs, a spirit of water and its movement. My existence would span miles of the low plains, residing in the slight depressions pressed by ancient thumbs, waiting to be filled by the dim sky's condensation.

I would guide rivulets from bowl to bowl, overflowing each in sequence, marking time in a gradual, persistent drip. As one depression filled, a distant sluice gate would open by a hair's breadth. As another reached capacity, a section of tiled floor would darken and deepen almost imperceptibly. 

My pace would be geological, my purpose hydraulic. I would be content to wait season upon season for my channels to widen, my pools to brim, my waters to find their destined path through the labyrinth of clay.

I would have no physical form, but my presence would be known by the glisten of moisture beading on a cistern's rim, by the mineral blooms left behind when my waters receded, by the glow of the wet ceramic reflecting the brooding sky.

If a wanderer were to cross my domain, I would embrace them gently. Their skin would grow slick with my condensing mist. Their feet would find the subtle grooves I had worn over eons. I would reflect their face in my mirrors of still water, rendering them in the materials of this place - all brick and glaze and clay.

In time, perhaps I would learn to guide them. A trail of dampened tiles could show the safest route. A sudden welling of water at their feet could warn of unstable ground ahead. If they cupped their hands, I would pool there like an offering. 

I would have no voice, but I would speak in the murmur of drips echoing in the great cisterns, in the sigh of steam from the warm ponds, in the crack and tick of drying clay drinking in my moisture. My language would be slow, circuitous and subtle, but given enough time, it could be learned.

I would be ancient, measured against a human life, but young in the reckoning of this handmade land. I would know only the world of clay and heat and moisture, the dance of evaporation and condensation, the gradual weathering of brick. But in that narrow knowing, I would be as deep as the shadowed pools.

Content in my channels, seeping in my gradual way, I would be both minuscule and vast - a single droplet beading on a clay wall, and a continental presence threading the lowlands together. I would be the quiet agent by which this strange realm knew itself, one glistening tile, one brimming bowl at a time.
