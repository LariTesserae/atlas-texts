In this hauntingly beautiful world of abandoned infrastructure reclaimed by nature, I imagine myself as one of the ancient pneumatic message cylinders, forever traversing the labyrinthine passages of roots and rusted metal.

Crafted from tarnished brass, I rocket endlessly through the twisted remnants of pneumatic tubes, my trajectory warped and rerouted over the decades by the slow, inexorable conquest of roots and vines. The once straight shot passages have morphed into a convoluted, organic roller coaster - I careen around hairpin turns of gnarled wood, plunge through corkscrews of strangling vegetation.

My exterior has long since lost its polished sheen, now patinated with age and the residue of a hundred years spent hurtling through this dungeon of decay and new growth. Algae streaks my pitted surface in emerald stripes, and tiny mushrooms sprout stubbornly from the dents and dings earned from a century of ricochet and impact.

Inside, I carry a curling wisp of ancient paper, yellowed and crisp with age. Once, it may have borne important tidings, urgent instructions, missives between those who thought themselves the masters of this place. Now the message has faded to ghostly obscurity, the ink siphoned away by hungry fungal filaments that have worked their way through the cylinder's seal. I carry only the faintest memory of purpose.

My journey never ends, an infinite loop through the veins of this place. I blast through turnstiles of twisting roots, ricochet off drums of mushroom flesh that reverberate with deep, hollow tones at my passing. At times I shear burls of bark from the pipe-walls, sending sprays of sap misting into the clammy air. The passages sigh and groan at my passing, as if the exhalations of the roots propel me ever onward.

There are moments of almost peace, when I drift through a stretch of tube still clad in its original brass, the roots not yet having perforated the metal. In these rare straightaways, the echoes of the place speak to me - the thrum and chug of ancient machinery, the hiss of pneumatic pressure, the subsonic roar of sap rushing through a million fibrous conduits. I am a mote in the lifeblood of this place, an errant blood cell that will never find its way home.

Sometimes I rattle past grates or vents where wan shafts of light penetrate the gloom, and in those moments I catch sepia-tinted glimpses of the Tender going about her patient work. I see her silhouette move through the twilight, her fungus-furred form an amalgam of woman, machine, and forest. We are kin, the Tender and I, both purpose-built components in a mechanism that has long since transcended its original design.

I do not know how many others like me may still be in circulation. We were legion, once, but time and the inexorable crush of roots have doubtless thinned our numbers. Perhaps I am the last of my kind, doomed to solo flight through these dripping catacombs until I finally lodge inseparably in some tight coil of sap-slick piping. Or perhaps, like the Tender, my form will one day transmute, my brass softening and warping until I become just another valve in the twisting plumbing of this place.

Until then, I will careen onward, an arrow let fly by an archer a century dead, racing toward a target that has long since ceased to exist. I am a missile without a mission, a mantra without meaning, an ampersand in the run-on sentence of this place's existence. I am a pneumatic prayer that long ago lost the name of its deity, echoing forever through the circulatory system of a vast and dreaming body that knows not what it dreams of.
