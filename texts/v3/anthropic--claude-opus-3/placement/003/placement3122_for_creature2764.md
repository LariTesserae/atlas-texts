In this drowned low country, where the cold depths have swallowed every trace of the civilization that once thrived upon it, I imagine myself as a clump of ancient moss clinging to the worn steps of a great stone stairway. 

For centuries, or longer, I have grown here in the dim underwater light, spreading slowly over the cracked and crumbling masonry. My growth is almost imperceptible, a gradual softening and veiling of the hard edges shaped by hands long since vanished beneath the black water.

I am one of the inheritors of this place, part of the submerged garden that is reclaiming the sunken architecture millimeter by patient millimeter. My filaments probe the seams between the huge blocks, my fuzzy green mat tracing the tread of feet that will never again descend these stairs. 

The water is my atmosphere, still and heavy with cold. What little light filters down from the unseen surface sustains me. I need nothing more. Time moves differently for me, marked not in minutes or hours but in the settling of silt upon my fronds, the gradual mineral accretion that whitens my tips, the slow lean of my patch as the steps beneath me shift and sink further into the underwater earth.

I am at home in the silence and the dimness. The bustling life this stairway once knew is as alien to me as the dry air that used to fill these drowned halls. My communion is with the algae that stain the risers, the dark fronds of weed that trail down from higher steps, the floating veils of detritus that brush me as they pass, propelled by some faraway current.  

I dream no dreams of sunlight or wind, of scurrying feet or bright voices echoing in vaulted chambers. The hands that fitted these stones are a mystery I do not ponder. I only know the stairway as it is now - a softened ruin, a substrate for slow life, a passage upward into deeper shadow.

This is my place and my shape in this drowned world - an ancient patch of moss, an inheritor cloaked in patience, thriving in the ruins of a forgotten grandeur. I am what remains when the makers have vanished. I am a green breath in the stillness, a living whisper of the Stair that Was.
