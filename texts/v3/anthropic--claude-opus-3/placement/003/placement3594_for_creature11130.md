I would find myself sitting in one of the worn places at the edge of the alcove, my body settling into the shallow grooves left by generations of listeners. The stone would be cool and slightly damp beneath me, its surface polished smooth by centuries of contact with living flesh.

At first, I would struggle with the stillness and the silence. My mind, accustomed to the quick rhythms of life below, would rebel against this place of slow breaths and deep time. But gradually, as the dome rose and settled, as the water sang its ancient song, I would feel myself slowing to match it. My breath would lengthen, my heartbeat would steady, until it seemed I was an extension of the mountain itself, my body just another channel for its great subterranean pulse.

I would not be alone. Beside me, occupying the other worn space, would be my companion - my matched one, tuned to me like one string tuned to another. We would not need to speak. Our communication would pass in the subtle shifts of posture, the brush of hand against hand as we took turns at the watching and the resting. 

In my companion's eyes I would see my own awe and wonder reflected back at me, twin mirrors drinking in the mystery of this place. We would become, in time, something more and less than human - mineral-stained, mist-touched, our skins taking on the hues of the breathing stone.

Through cycles of light and dark, wet and dry, we would keep our vigil. Across seasons and years, we would listen - not straining for meaning, but simply attending, making ourselves vessels for the mountain's wordless speech. We would let the pattern write itself on our bones, trusting that we were part of something vaster than our own understanding.

And if one day we rose from our worn places and walked deeper into the alcove, following some call only we could hear... if we passed between the hanging vines and did not emerge again... who is to say if this was an ending, or merely another kind of listening? 

Perhaps we would become compilers of a different sort, our bodies and breath added to the record the mountain keeps in its own stony memory. Or perhaps we would simply be absorbed, our small swift lives folded at last into the slower, deeper life of water and stone and dark.

This is where I would find myself: caught between the quick and the slow, the known and the unknowable. Listening, always listening, for the shape of a question that might take lifetimes to ask, and longer still to answer. Sitting in that place of pattern and patience, until the mountain or time itself erased the borders between my small self and its vast, lithic dreaming.
