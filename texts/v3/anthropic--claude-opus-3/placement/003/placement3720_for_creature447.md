In this drowned world, I imagine myself as one of the Weavers - perhaps an apprentice to Mira, learning the patient art of tending and shaping the kelp forest.

I would be a young man, lean and strong from a life lived underwater. My name would be Kai, after the shellfish that cling to the rocks beneath the kelp roots. Like the other children of the Woven, I would have been born in the years after the drowning, knowing no other world than this one of filtered green light and endless ocean.

Under Mira's tutelage, I would spend my days swimming through the kelp columns, learning to read the health of the forest, to feel where a frond needs to be trimmed or coaxed in a new direction. My hands would grow nimble with braiding, weaving the living kelp into the walls and chambers of our growing cathedral.

In the quiet moments between work, I would explore the drowned city, marveling at the strange artifacts of the dry world - a glass bottle, still unbroken, wedged beneath a fallen beam; a tarnished silver coin glinting in the silt; a snarl of copper wire, now home to a colony of seahorses. I would collect these treasures, not for their value but for the stories they hint at, the mysteries of the past.

During the long nights in the air pocket beneath the town hall dome, I would listen raptly to the tales of the elders - to Grandmother Wrack's fading memories of life on land, to the Octopus Keeper's strange accounts of her cephalopod companions, to Old Tam's stories of the early days of the Woven, when every survival technique had to be invented anew.

But my true fascination would be with the Singers and their haunting underwater music. In stolen moments, I would try to imitate their vocalizations, training my lungs and vocal cords to produce those eerie, resonant tones. Perhaps, in time, I might become one of them, my voice weaving through the kelp forest along with my hands, shaping our world with sound as well as touch.

As I grew into adulthood, I would take on more responsibilities - tending the sea urchin herds, making the long dives to map new sections of the city, trading with the boat people on the rare occasions of their visits. But always, my true work would be the kelp forest itself, the patient shaping of this living cathedral that shelters and sustains us.

In quiet moments, I would swim up to the surface as Mira does, floating among the kelp fronds under the stars. I would wonder at the vastness of the world, both above and below the waves, and at the strange twists of fate that brought us here. And then I would dive back down, back to the work that is life itself, the endless task of weaving ourselves into the fabric of this changed world, making of it a home.

Perhaps, in time, I would take an apprentice of my own, passing on the skills and stories that were passed to me. And so the Woven would continue, generation after generation, tending the kelp forest that sustains us, shaping the drowned city into a place of life and beauty and endless adaptation. This is where I would find myself in this world - not just surviving, but truly living, truly a part of the patient, inexorable weaving of the sea.
