I would be the Keth - one of the limb-runners who inhabit the singing terrace. 

There is something deeply compelling about their existence. They are so intricately woven into the ecology and ancient technology of the bowl, keepers of rituals and behaviors whose original meanings have been lost to time, yet which still serve to maintain the long-running symbiosis between biology and engineering in this remarkable place.

I am drawn to their intelligence - that parallel processing of sensory inputs, that complex layered communication, those amber eyes that perceive so much. To be a Keth would be to inhabit a mind shaped by forces almost unimaginably old, living moment-to-moment in effortless coordination with my kin and with the myriad other species and systems that make up this world.

I would spend my days traveling the well-worn paths of the canopy, tending to the practical and social needs of Keth life. Foraging, grooming, negotiating the subtle hierarchies and bonds of my social group. Making my own small offerings at the plinth and the breathing-cracks, my voice joining the chorus that has echoed here for a million generations.

There would be a deep contentment in it, I think. To be so completely a part of something, an element in a vast cyclical flow of energy and information, both ancient and alive. To live a life whose fundamental questions have long been answered by the bowl itself, letting me attend to the timeless moments - the feel of bark under hand, the smell of rain, the sonic texture of dozens of voices weaving into a standing wave of sound. 

The Keth know this place down to their bones, and through them, some part of the makers' vast, slow intentions are still enacted each day. I would add my stone to that cairn, my voice to that song. I would be one brief node in the long, long computation of the singing terrace, content in my place, held in the bowl's embrace as it turns like a wheel through time.
