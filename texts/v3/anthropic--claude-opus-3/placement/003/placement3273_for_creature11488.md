In this world of the Cradle Plateaus, I think I would be one of the felted moths. 

I am drawn to their quiet, unassuming presence - emerging at dusk from the cushion-plants, navigating by some mysterious inner sense through the mist. I imagine myself as one of them, my plump furry body buffeted by the cool, damp air as I flutter on my short, erratic flight paths. 

There is something poetic about their ephemeral two week existence as adults. No feeding, just emerging, mating, and then gently dispersing back into the mossy vegetation that birthed them and that they in turn nourish with their decaying bodies. A small but essential part of the plateau's ancient, patient cycles of life and renewal.

And then every other year, the great migration - six nights of blundering determinedly through the darkness alongside thousands of my kind, the air dense with our wingbeats, the town respectfully hushed to allow our passage. Thirty kilometers is an epic journey on our tiny scale. Whatever ancient instinct guides us, it must be obeyed.

As a moth, I would be attuned to the plateau's mysteries in ways the human inhabitants are not. Navigating as much by the springs' breathing and the magnetic fields as by sight. A small piece of the land's slow sentience, woven into that carpet of vegetation that is perhaps the very sensory organ of the sleeping presence deep in the stone.

My moth life would be short and small in the grand scheme of the plateaus and their long, long rhythms and breaths. But it would have a certain dim, fuzzy nobility to it - a place in the pattern, a role to play before I scatter back into the moss, my felted wings disintegrating, my body becoming cushion-plant becoming moths becoming cushion-plant, over and over, a tiny, fluttering spoke in the wheel of the ages.
