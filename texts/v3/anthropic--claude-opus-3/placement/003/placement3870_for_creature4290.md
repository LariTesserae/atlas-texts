In the Unfinished Cleft, I would be one of the strange, stalked plants leaning out from a crack in the rock at the angle my seed happened to wedge. 

I am rooted in the thin grit of the cleft's own shedding, nourished by the condensation that beads on the walls each morning and trickles down to pool briefly around my base. My form is not quite fern, not quite anything taxonomically familiar - a draft of vegetation feeling its way into being in this young, unfinished place.

My fronds, pale green and delicately branched, stretch out into the narrow vault of the cleft, soaking in the dim tea-colored light that filters down for most of the day. For those brief minutes around noon when direct sunlight pierces the slot overhead, my stalks stand a little taller, my unfurling leaves flare a more vivid green. 

I do not grow quickly or expansively. The cleft measures change in increments of fingernail widths and shades of fading ochre. To its slow, geological sense of time, my lifecycle is a momentary flourish, there and gone in the gap between two of the deep, slow breaths from the shadowed end.

Yet I am a presence in the cleft's strangeness, one of the rare flashes of green amidst the pinkish stone. The russet swifts alight on my sturdier stems when they pause their quarrelsome echoing. Smaller things - the thumbnail-sized grazers, the brief ecologies blooming and dying in the ephemeral dew pools - find flecks of shelter in the whorls of my fronds. 

The cleft has not yet learned to erase, and so as my leaves wither and drop, they do not decay but simply become part of the thin grit, the bone meal sediment. In this way, I am a tiny collaborator in the lithic memory of this place. The minerals drawn up by my roots, the carbon gathered by my leaves, pass into the pale silt and remain.

I grow, not with ambition, but with the quiet persistence of a thing made to fit a crack in the world. My presence will never be a major character in the cleft's story. But as long as the seed wedged in that crack continues to send up new shoots, I will be one of its recurring motifs - a small green strophe in the long, slow poem of the unfinished mountain.
