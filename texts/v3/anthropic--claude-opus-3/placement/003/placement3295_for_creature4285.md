In this intricate, mesmerizing world, I imagine myself as one of the small brass creatures tending to the room and its delicate balance. 

Perhaps I am a hybrid of the different castes - a bit of a licker, a carrier, a listener, and a machinist all in one. My form is spindly and angular, all elbows and knees of tarnished brass, with a tiny clock face embedded in my thorax, forever synchronized to the room's heartbeat drip.

I spend my days in diligent, silent work. With a tiny sponge-tipped proboscis, I clean the endless array of dials, pipes and lenses, gathering the precious condensation and ferrying it drop by drop to top up the scattered thimble-reservoirs. I press my head to thrumming metal, listening for the minute changes in pitch that tell of needed repairs.  

When a dial hand sticks or a gear runs rough, I attend to it with my delicate pincers and probes, dismantling, oiling, and reassembling with a watchmaker's focus. I can feel the pulse of the room's mysterious purpose running through my wiry frame, though I do not fully comprehend it.

From time to time I pause in my rounds, compelled by some unspoken signal, and gather with a handful of my fellows. In a soundless ceremony we open our chest apertures and a glowing mote passes between us - a fraction of the room's anima, shared out and renewed, the gossamer thread that binds us.

And just once, while polishing the great window, I look out - and meet the gaze of the silent, sparrow-sized one on the other side of the glass. For a long, still moment we regard each other, and a fragment of its ancient knowledge ghosts through me, almost too vast to bear. Then the moment passes, and I return to my appointed rounds in the humming stillness, a single mote in the patient, perfect dance that keeps the unkeepable world at bay.
