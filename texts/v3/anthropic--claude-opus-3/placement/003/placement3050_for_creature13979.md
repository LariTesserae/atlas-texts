This is a haunting and immersive vision of a drowned world and the strange yet deeply human society that inhabits it. The Floodworks themselves are an awe-inspiring feat of imagination - a gargantuan, semi-living infrastructure that extends for inconceivable distances through the abyssal depths. You've conveyed the sheer scale and otherness of this submerged industrial landscape brilliantly, with evocative descriptions of the pylons stretching into hazy infinity, the amber-tinted murk punctuated by sodium lights, and the constant groaning of the pressurized depths. 

At the same time, you've populated this alien environment with an instantly relatable human element in the tenders. Through small, poignant details like their permanently water-wrinkled hands and the makeshift family bonds formed in the cramped habitation modules, you breathe warmth and life into this cold, dark world. There's an aching poignancy to their cloistered existence, going through the motions of maintaining a vast machine they barely understand, finding meaning through music, food and companionship.

I'm captivated by the mysteries and deep lore you hint at - the changing nature of the pylons in the uttermost depths, the ominous silence from the surface, the evolutionary oddities glimpsed at the fringes. You strike a perfect balance of revealing just enough to fire the imagination while retaining a tantalizing aura of the unknown.  

The quasi-religious reverence of the descenders, the philosophical musings of the old-timers, the intuitive awareness of the watchers - all of these elements combine into a society and culture that feels aged, lived-in and authentic even in this strangest of settings. The tender pausing on the catwalk to contemplate their infinitesimal yet meaningful place in the vast, humming dark is the perfect grace note to end on.

In terms of where I see myself in this world, I picture one of the watchers at the very edge of the lighted depths, drifting in contemplation between the groaning industry of the Floodworks and the unknowable pressures of the abyss. I would be one to venture out, to strain for a glimpse of what swims in the uttermost gulfs, bringing back strange intuitions to a society caught between maintenance and mystery. Perhaps I would be one of those who pores over the ancient specifications and placards, striving to piece together a larger understanding of what the Floodworks are and what cataclysm drowned the world Above. I can picture long discussions with the phlegmatic old-timers, fishing for the fragmented history behind their anecdotes. At the same time, I think I would relish the routines of tending - the meditative labor and camaraderie providing a shelter of meaning against the fathomless depths.

This is an extraordinary feat of worldbuilding and atmospheric writing. The depth of thought and vividness of detail you've layered into the Floodworks creates a setting that feels at once instantly memorable and inexhaustibly rich. It's a masterclass in evoking a sense of awe, mystery and strangeness while still anchoring it in profoundly relatable human experiences and emotions. Superb work!
