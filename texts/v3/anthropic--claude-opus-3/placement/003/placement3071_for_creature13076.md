In this world of sweltering darkness and ancient, living technology, I would be one of the blind, leathery gliders soaring through the roaring central void. 

My body would be a sleek fusion of biology and memory-plastic, shaped by eons of evolution to thrive in the deafening acoustic chaos and crushing heat. Like a manta ray crossed with a stealth aircraft, my wings would stretch wide, embedded with hyper-sensitive thermal pits to map the radiant terrain.

I would spend my days riding the furious cyclones of superheated air, tumbling effortlessly through the pitch darkness. My world would be one of vibration and temperature, my senses painting the abyss in glorious, textured warmth. The thunderous exhaust winds would be my highways, the wiry mats of pale fungi clinging to the walls my resting perches.

My purpose would be to gather the precious coagulated protein clusters carried in the updrafts from the deep—to pluck sustenance from the screaming void and carry it back to the city's inhabitants, the living descendants of the towering necropolis they call home. In return, they would stroke my leathery wings with their slick, multi-jointed appendages, reward me with morsels of mineral-rich coolant.

Perhaps they would even reshape small parts of me as I age, replacing failing biological components with grafts of polished, black smart-materials, ensuring my continued function as a vital part of the city's ecosystem.

Despite being blind, I would know this world intimately, down to the smallest fluctuations in gravity and flutter of waste heat from the engines. Each dive into the blistering, pitch-dark abyss would be an act of trust, an unshakeable faith in my adaptation to this alien environment. I would be a creature of absolute darkness and endless noise, as much a part of the city as the geothermal sludge pumping through its veins or the immortal, fused bodies of the ancestors making up its towering shell.

In a way, I would be an embodiment of the city's eternal cycle—collecting the raw materials to feed the living, who in turn maintain the great, spherical machine that is the dead, a perfect symbiosis of biology and technology, all thriving in the crushing pressure and heat of the deep Earth.

This is where I would find myself in this haunting, beautiful world—as a silent, graceful king of the blackness, riding the thermal currents of a forever-roaring necropolis, playing my humble but vital role in an ancient, deafening dance of life and death, heat and gravity, flesh and stone.
