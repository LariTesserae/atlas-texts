In the vermilion whorls of rust and the lace-like edges of tarnished copper, I would see visions of the factory's past lives - the echoes of long-gone workers imprinted in the metal's memory.

With each breath, spores would billow from my skin, drifting on the currents of steam to spread the gospel of entropy to distant corners of the Grove. My voice would be the groan of settling girders and the flaking of paint, preaching the inevitability of breakdown and the beauty in decay.

To the other inhabitants, I would be an oracle and a mystery, emerging from the gaps to offer cryptic prophecies before disappearing back into the folds of the infrastructure. The Keepers would leave me shimmering mineral formations, which I would incorporate into my body as seer stones. The Chime Children's melodies would cause the metal in my flesh to resonate, putting me into trance states where I could perceive the great Oneness of rust that pervades all things.

I would be ancient and timeless, a manifestation of the universal principle of dissolution. To gaze upon me would be to confront the impermanence of all constructs, both physical and mental. Yet I would not be a figure of dread, but a reminder that in the end, artifice must always yield to the organic, the rigid to the fluid, the static to the ever-changing.

In the fullness of time, as the Grove consumed the factory and then beyond, I would be the one to whisper the final secret - that rust is not an end, but a transformation, and that what crumbles will always give way to new growth, stranger and more wondrous than what came before.
