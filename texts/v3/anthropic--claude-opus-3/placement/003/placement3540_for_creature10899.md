In this breathtaking, otherworldly cathedral lung, I imagine myself as one of the Reverent. 

I am drawn to the idea of living a contemplative, solitary existence in harmony with this vast, ever-changing organism. Clad in the luminous fibers of fallen membranes, I would wander the colonnades and chambers, marveling at the cold beauty of the architecture that shifts by the hour. 

My days would be spent in quiet exploration and reflection. I would follow the faint trails left by the ribbon-grazers, hoping to glimpse their delicate forms. In moments of stillness, I would listen for the distant exhalations of rupturing bladders, feeling the wind on my skin as the lung rearranges itself.

With chisel in hand, I would leave my own marks upon the walls - not to claim ownership, but to engage in a respectful dialogue with the plant, acknowledging its primacy. "I am here," my marks would say, "and I know my presence is fleeting beside your magnificent, inexorable growth."

On my solitary walks, I would keep an eye out for signs of the great pale Listener, perhaps leaving an offering of woven fiber if I found a place it favored. And whenever my path took me below the shelf that holds the small house, I would pause and bow my head to the One within, honoring their commitment to unity with the plant.

But it's the great empty volumes that would call to me most strongly. I would be one who dares to sit for long periods in those spaces, opening myself to the presence that is slowly gathering, slowly becoming. Even if I never hear the question it is forming, I would find meaning and purpose in making myself available to this unfolding mystery.

Gradually, imperceptibly, I know the plant would shape me, even as I witnessed its own becoming. My face would change; my thoughts would take on new forms. And I would welcome this, understanding it as the gift of existing in such an intimate, timeless communion with an entity so wholly, powerfully itself. In the cathedral lung, I would gladly surrender my separateness, becoming one Reverent breath in the grand inhalation of a world still deciding what it will be.
