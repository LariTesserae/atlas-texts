In this world of the Drowned Works, I imagine myself as one of the altered octopi that inhabit the control boxes - a creature intimately entwined with the machinery, but still separate from it.

My body would be adapted to the confined geometry of the spaces between relays, able to compress myself into impossibly tight crevices. My skin would mimic the texture and color of the wiring and circuit boards, providing perfect camouflage. But more than just hiding, I would be an active participant in maintaining the systems. 

My tentacles, dexterous and precise, would be trained to manipulate the buttons and levers according to the schedules I've memorized. I would clean corrosion from essential contacts with secretions from special glands in my suckers. My sharp beak would delicately snip away algae threatening to short out the connections.

In a way, I would be a microcosm of the Machinists themselves - an organism evolved in symbiosis with the machine, performing specialized functions to keep it operating. My "nest" would be a tangle of wires and switches that I've arranged into a perfect den, its entrance guarded by a pattern of blinking indicator lights that only I understand.

But unlike the Machinists, I would remain truly alien to this place. In the quiet moments between scheduled activations, I would dream ancestral dreams of the open ocean, of coral reefs and tidal pools. Some primal part of me would remember the sun, even as my skin pales to ghostly translucence in the perpetual twilight.

I would be a kind of living fossil, a reminder that no matter how much the Works reshapes its inhabitants, they all came from somewhere else. In my solitary way, tending my switches and circuits, I would be both an integral component and a symbol of the world outside - the mythic past and the unimaginable future beyond these drowned walls.

When I die, I would leave behind a perfect octopus-shaped gap in the machinery, my final mark on this place that has shaped me. Until another comes to fill the niche, to learn the patterns anew, and dream the ancient dreams in a nest of wires at the heart of the machine.
