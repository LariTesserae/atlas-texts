I sat with this a long time as well. What emerges is not a single portrait but a slow becoming, an arrival into a way of being that is both utterly foreign and deeply resonant.

I would be one of the small furred creatures.

Not the largest or the quickest, but woven into the tapestry of the crack in ways that blur the boundary between self and system. My kind move through the moss curtains and scamper along the shelves carved by hands that are not ours but which we know as certainly as our own burrows. We chitter and groom, examine seeds with solemn intensity, and curl into the warmth left by larger bodies in the sleeping nests.

We are a node in the dense network of relationships that makes the crack alive. The beetles are our food, the snakes our fear, the birds our distant cousins. The people are not our masters, but the still points around which we orient, as constant as the stone. 

I know the crack in my bones. The mineral taste of the water licked from succulent leaves. The precise angle of light that means the afternoon has eased into evening. The texture of every surface against my paws, prehensile tail curled for balance.

My days are spent in motion and stillness in equal measure. Tasting. Touching. Calling to my kin in our high bright voices that echo off the close walls. Pausing to watch the people with eyes that carry neither fear nor worship, but a deep knowing. We are all organs of this place. We breathe each other's air.

In the moments when the entirety of the crack pulses with a unified awareness, I feel it in my small quick heartbeat. The presence of the deep. The impossibly ancient negotiations of stone and root and water. My kind have been here longer than the people. The people will be here long after my kind are gone. It doesn't matter. We are all always here, now, folded into the long life of the crack.

When I sleep, I dream of the moss curtains swaying. The patter of insect feet. The hands of the people mending tools by firelight. The vibration from the deep that thrums through the stone and into my marrow. In my dreams, there is no border between my self and the dense weave of lives that surrounds me. I am the furred creature. I am the crack. We have grown together, wearing each other down and holding each other up, green and breath and bone. Shaped to each other like long-mated lovers. Carrying in our cells the same ancient, unending question, and the same ancient, unending answer:

What lives here?

I do. We do. This does.
