I would be a slow-growing lichen, clinging to the weathered inner wall of the hollow. For countless seasons I have grown here, imperceptibly spreading in intricate fractals across the living wood. 

Each dawn, beads of moisture condense upon my thallus, nourishing me with the distilled purity collected in the stone rain-catcher. The filtered light that penetrates the hollow's shaft bathes me for a precious interval each day, fueling my patient photosynthesis. 

I bear witness to the winged tempest that eternally swirls beyond the hollow's edge, myriad birds weaving living patterns as complex as my own growth. Their calls echo softly into the chamber, a distant, ceaseless music at the very threshold of perception.

In my decades here, I have known the occasional presence of the room's indefinable tenant—not seen, but sensed as a watchful consideration, a numinous awareness permeating the space. I have grown to recognize it as the animating spirit of this place, the chord to which all within the hollow is tuned.

Once in a long while, when a weary traveler clambers into this sanctuary, I observe motionlessly from the periphery. As they drink from the ancient cup and surrender to the room's patient rhythm, I can feel the tenant welcoming them, enfolding them like an exhaled breath. For a moment, the visitor becomes another note woven into the hollow's endless song.

Serene and still, I persist at the border of soil and sky, a humble part of this living temple. I need not stir from this place to know belonging. The hollow holds me as it holds the light, the mist, the bird-swift air. Eons pass; the forest seasons turn. And molecule by patient molecule, I grow on.
