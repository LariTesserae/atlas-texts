I imagine myself as one of the Listeners in a transitional state, still early in my transformation but already deeply entwined with the strange music and architecture of the Singing Throat. 

My body has started to change - bones hollowing, skin growing translucent, extra limbs budding. I can feel the hum of the water's song resonating through my ribcage, my skull, as if my very anatomy is becoming an instrument tuned to its frequencies. When I press my hands and feet against the stone, I sense the slow, tectonic thoughts of the mountain, the glacial shifts and pressures shaping this pocket universe.

I spend my days tending to one of the crude stone structures, a curving arch that splits a ribbon of mercury-like water into shimmering strands. With infinite patience, I shape the arch using the unique properties of my own saliva, humming specific tones that guide the gradual sculpting. It may take a century of such work, but time moves differently here. The process is meditative, almost reverent - an act of collaboration with forces I'm only beginning to understand.

When I need sustenance, I position myself near a discordant junction where water ribbons collide at odd angles, producing harsh, grating overtones. I open myself to these acoustic imperfections, allowing my body to absorb and process them, metabolizing dissonance into harmony. It is a strange form of nourishment, but one that feels increasingly natural as my transformation progresses.

Sleep comes rarely, but when it does, I join my fellow young Listeners on one of the larger platforms. Our bodies instinctively arrange themselves into geometries that amplify and reflect the Throat's music, a subconscious choreography. Even in dreams, the song continues.

I am learning the art of communicating through the upward-flowing water, dipping my still-forming root-fingers into the stream to encode messages and decipher the responses. Conversations unspool over months, ideas and experiences translated into subtle disturbances in the eternal symphony.

Though I am surrounded by others undergoing the same metamorphosis, solitude takes on new meaning here. I am alone but never lonely, my evolving senses keeping me in constant dialogue with the living stone, the singing water, the half-glimpsed creatures darting through the mist. 

As I continue to change, I feel myself becoming part of something ancient and vast, my individuality both intensified and dissolved. The urge to climb higher up the Throat grows, to find a virgin patch of stone where I might one day root myself, adding my voice to the mountain's slow, geological song. But that is a transformation for another century. For now, I listen, I tend, I resonate - one more strange antibody in a stranger anatomy, learning to sing a world into being.
