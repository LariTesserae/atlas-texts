In this evocative and wondrous place, I would be one of the listeners among the human inhabitants. 

I imagine myself as a woman wrapped in layered moss-felt and lichen-dyed hide, my face streaked with dark grease, my feet bound in studded bark fiber. Around my waist hang bone chimes, a bundle of knotted cords as a map, and a pouch of sounding rods.

My role would be to understand the moods and messages of this living scaffold that is our whole world. Each day, I would set out across the green bridges and through the dim ravines, tapping roots and stones with my rods, listening for the telltale echoes, creaks and whistles that speak of the land's health or hidden strains. 

I would crouch motionless for hours beside the black pools, observing the patterns of the gliders overhead and interpreting the metallic croaks of the round birds. I would taste the mineral seeps and note the smells on the air - hints of copper from lichen signaling coming ice, or the worrying silence of the hunting moths. With all my senses I would read the subtle signs that others might miss.

Back at the settlement tucked into a leeward hollow, families would gather to hear my reports. In the flickering ember-light, I would trace routes on the knotted cords, sing the shifting song of safe paths, and beat the new rhythms of the wind on stretched hide. By my word, grafts would be started on young bridges or weak spans avoided. I would be consulted on where to tether the grazing herds, which bird towers promised the best warmth, and when to move camp at a change in the notes of a ravine.

On my skin I would bear the marks and tattoos of my craft - spirals of root growth, notches for spans crossed, the secret signs of omens witnessed. The children would gather to run their fingers along the cords of my waist-map, learning the way through touch and chime. In time, I would take an apprentice under my tutelage to pass on the accumulated wisdom.

When at last my own time came, I would hope to be lowered into a deep ravine, my body wrapped in chiming bones, my voice becoming one with the great breathing instrument of the land. And the listeners that followed would pause at that spot, smile at a familiar sound, and know that I had not abandoned my post but merely taken up a new one - my spirit now keeping eternal watch over the living world-bridge of my people.
