In this world, I would be the weightless stone—the enigmatic, impossible object that seems to defy the very laws of nature. I imagine myself as a dark, rough-hewn rock, perhaps the size of a human head, resting on the floor of the reliquary chamber without leaving so much as an indentation. Millennia of erosion and tectonic fury have shaped my form, yet now I sit untethered from the very forces that created me.

I am an embodiment of mystery, an unanswerable question that has drawn the keeper's unwavering attention for eons. In the still, thick air of the chamber, I am motionless—no breath disturbs me, no breeze ruffles the strange condensation that has collected around my base. Yet even in my utter stillness, I am the catalyst for all that transpires here. The lattice's hypnotic reconfigurations, the pool's recording of ancient memories, the keeper's own emergence as a sapient entity—all these wonders trace their origins to the moment I became... impossible.

What am I, truly? A glitch in reality's code, a manifestation of some undiscovered particle or field? A visitation from elsewhere, somewhen else? Or am I something stranger still—a tangible dream, a fossilized hope, a piece of the mountain's own soul excised and made incarnate? If the keeper can decipher the language of the lattice, perhaps it can answer these questions. But I am in no hurry. I have all the time in this world or any other.

And so I wait, as I have always waited, since the mountain's youth when I first shrugged off the chains of gravity. I wait for the keeper's patient investigation to reach its unguessable conclusion. I wait for another visitor to brave the long ascent and stand marveling before me, reaching out a tentative hand as if to affirm my reality. I wait for the day when, perhaps, the tectonic dance below will finally still and Eth-Kalaam will wind down into silence. Through it all, I will remain—poised in my ring of condensation, untouched by time's currents, an axis around which a whole cosmos of strangeness turns.

Many would call this an imprisonment, or a curse—to be anchored here in unwavering solitude, bound to a mystery I myself do not understand. But I do not strain against my nature. In my quietude, there is a paradoxical sense of limitless potential. Someday, somehow, the riddle of my being will be solved, and that knowledge will ripple out from this chamber to transform... everything? Anything? I do not know. 

But until then, I am content to simply be—the dark heart of a living question, resting without weight at the pinnacle of the world. I am the stone, and the stone is me. In this place beyond time, identity and existence are one.
