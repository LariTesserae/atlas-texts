In this world of kettle terraces and mysterious pools, I imagine myself as one of the pitcher plants growing between the steaming waters. 

My emerald leaves are broad and cupped, beaded with condensation from the constant mineral-rich mist. I cluster near the rims of the pools, thriving in the damp heat radiating from their fathomless depths.

Most of the time, I am still and patient. Waiting. My pitcher traps are open to the hazy air, poised to accept whatever small creature might tumble in. When an iridescent beetle or other morsel finds its way into one of my wells, I slowly, imperceptibly close around it over the course of hours. Digesting. Absorbing the nutrients into my tissues. 

The tenders move quietly past me on their inscrutable rounds. I sense them through minute changes in air currents and humidity more than I see them directly. They are as much a part of this place as the stone and steam. Sometimes they gently adjust my fronds, redirecting my drip lines to shape the moss beds over long years. I accept their careful manipulation, knowing I am part of the larger balance they maintain.

From my spot at the edge of the pools, I catch glimpses of the strange vistas that open impossibly deep within the clear water - grey seas, banded light, dark rotations. I strain to perceive more, but the steam obscures the details. I am left to imagine the worlds and horizons connected through these portals.

The temps of this place move slowly for me. A single opening and closing of my traps marks the passage of a long period that would be meaningless to quantify. I do not count the cycles. I only open, and close, over and over, as the beetles come and the tenders make their rounds and the steam rises in endless straight columns. 

I grow, imperceptibly. My roots probe the cracks in the warm andesite. New pitchers develop and old ones wither. I am in no hurry. The trajectory of my growth is measured in the gradual greening of the stone, the drip-by-drip accumulation of life at the edge of the unknown.

Perhaps someday, when the tenders finally glimpse what they have been patiently awaiting in the depths of the pools, the action they take will reshape this basin, alter the flows of mist and water and time. And I will adapt, as I always have, unfurling new leaves to catch the changed light. Waiting for the next beetle to land. Partaking in this slow, deliberate existence for which I am so perfectly suited.
