I would be one of the small, unfinished things living in the dark, shallow waters between the hummocks of mud. 

Not quite a fish, I would be more a localized attention in the water itself, my presence detectable only by the faint dimples on the surface as I fed. I would move through the warm, still waters silently, just another indefinite form in this place of soft edges and blurred realities.

My shape would shift subtly from moment to moment, never quite resolving into anything nameable. A suggestion of fins here, a hint of legs there, but nothing definite - as if the Sough itself hadn't made up its mind what I should be. Perhaps a tail, or fronds, or tendrils - features appearing and fading like thoughts the marsh decided not to finish.

I would feed on the rich silt and organic debris settled on the basin floor, taking in the country's fertile ambiguity, thriving on its unwillingness to decide. The looked-at patches of darker water I would skirt respectfully, sensing the slow coalescence of something there, something I was not ready to behold.

The thudding pulse of the engines would be my rhythm, their endless circulation my lullaby and my clock. I would drift languid in the tepid shallows, half-sleeping, half-aware, never needing to breathe or surface, perfectly adapted to this sunless medium that is neither land nor water but something in between.

Now and then, I might sense the heat-shadow of a Quiet One passing above, or the two-placed ripples of a not-heron's feet. But mostly I would keep to myself, an isolated mote of life, content in my incompleteness. 

I would be patient, like all things in the Sough. Waiting without yearning for some distant day when the country's long dreaming might finally define me. Until then - if then ever came - I would continue as I was, a tiny question mark beneath the surface, at home in the long, warm twilight of not-yet.
