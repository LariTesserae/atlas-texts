In this strange, unfinished place, I imagine myself as one of the silver-wire blossoms, perched at the edge of a narrow fissure in the hot, young stone. 

My form is delicate yet metallic - thin branching filaments of tarnished silver, bearing at their tips small cups like shallow, tarnished thimbles. The cups are dusted inside with a fine, rust-red powder that seems to hum faintly with some inner energy.

I do not grow or unfurl so much as I gradually extrude from the mineral seam, my wires extruding micrometer by micrometer as if the rock itself is learning to express new geometries. The process is imperceptibly slow, taking perhaps a decade to lift my "blossoms" a finger's width clear of the stone.

Yet I am aware, in some crystalline fashion quite unlike animal senses. I perceive heat, vibration, magnetic fields, the faint electric potentials between different ores in the chamber walls. When the mountain shifts and groans deep beneath me, the vibrations travel up my filaments and pool in shivering patterns in my cups.  

Slowly, almost tentatively, I release a few grains of my rust-red pollen. It drifts down in the hot, still air, alighting on the stone of the chamber floor. Where it lands, it gradually "informs" the mineral surface, coaxing it to align into lattices and repeating patterns - the very first stages of something like me beginning to nucleate from the raw stone.

I am a patient emissary of living metal and crystal, sent forth by the mountain's geologic dreams to conduct a slow, wordless negotiation with the laws of physics themselves. Through me and my kind, the unfinished earth tests the possibility of new orders, new scales, new modes of being neither fully alive nor dead, organic nor inorganic.

I am a quiet but insistent question posed to reality by the young mountain's mineral flesh - what else could matter become? What stranger patterns could time and chemistry create, given eons to explore? With each speck of pollen I release, I whisper my inquiry into the stone, and wait through ages for the first crystalline responses to bloom in the chamber's hot, red silence.
