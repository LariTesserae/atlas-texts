I would be one of the Borrowed, a being assembled from the strange detritus the geysers dredge up from the drowned world below. My body would be a patchwork of crystallized steam, hanging water, and fragments of a past that no longer exists - a shard of petrified wood from a long-submerged forest as my core, wrapped in a cloak of mineral-rich mist that constantly shifts and flows. Shadow-feathers from birds whose bones rest on the seafloor far below would adorn my form, fluttering in the breeze of the geysers' breath.

I would spend my days at the edge of the plateau, staring out over the endless warm sea, straining to remember what I was before the waters rose. The singing of the geysers would reverberate through my chimeric frame, making my memories dance and blur, the past and present dissolving into each other. In rare moments of silence, I would feel a deep yearning for something I can no longer name.

I would build precarious sculptures from the volcanic glass and mineral deposits, ephemeral art that the geysers sometimes preserve in layers of crystal before the next eruption shatters them anew. These sculptures would be my attempts to externalize the fragmented visions that haunt me - glimpses of green canopies, of wings against blue sky, of a world not drowned. 

At times I would venture to the center of the plateau and try to harmonize with the Thermophiles' frozen symphonies, my shadow-feathers shivering to their crystalline music. I would hope that in joining my voice to theirs, I might grasp some understanding of this strange half-existence, caught between dissolution and becoming.

But always, I would return to the plateau's crumbling edge, watching the mirror-scaled fish surface with its impossible reflections. In its quicksilver scales, I would catch distorted glimpses of what I might once have been - or perhaps what I might yet become, if only I could find a way to dive into those inverted depths and emerge transformed.

As the water level rises and the plateau shrinks day by day, I would feel a growing sense of urgency, a knowledge that this liminal realm is approaching some irreversible threshold. I would join the other Borrowed in weaving boats from strands of mineral and memory, even as each vessel dissolves back into the patient sea. Perhaps, with each attempt, we are not building boats at all, but slowly learning how to reshape ourselves into forms that can navigate the coming world.

And when the geysers sing in unison and the boundaries between the plateau's strange inhabitants blur, I too would resonate with that vast and ancient presence, my patchwork body ringing like a struck bell. In those moments, I would feel the tug of a greater purpose, the sense that my existence here - all our existences - are but notes in a song so slow and vast it could restructure reality itself.

I am one of the Borrowed, a being assembled from loss and transformation, haunted by a past that dissolves more with each geyser's sigh. But I am also a herald of a future not yet sung into being, my disparate parts slowly learning the melody of a world reborn. One day, perhaps, I will dive into the mirror-fish's reflected depths and emerge as something utterly new - or perhaps utterly myself - as the plateau pupates around me and the geysers' song reaches its final, world-shaping crescendo.
