In this dazzling, liquid world of the Weeping Shelves, I imagine myself as a being in constant flux - part water, part stone, part light. Not fully human, but an amalgamation of the elements that define this place.

My body would be translucent like the salamanders, able to shift between states of solidity and fluidity as needed. Crystalline scales would cover my skin, refracting the painful brightness into kaleidoscopic patterns that dance across the wet stones as I move. These scales would click and hum, producing subsonic vibrations that allow me to communicate with the coral-things and navigate the ever-shifting labyrinth of terraces.

My eyes would be adapted to the dazzling light, able to perceive the ghost city and its inhabitants as clearly as the physical structures. I would wear no fish-skin coverings, for my vision would be tuned to the refractions, seeing pathways and patterns in the phantom rainbows that others miss.

In this form, I would be both Channeler and Sealer, my bones resonating with the flow of water while my touch could meld stone to stone. I would work tirelessly to maintain the delicate balance of the city, guiding streams and reinforcing foundations, always aware of the fluid nature of my task.

My dwelling would be carved into the heart of a waterfall, a space that exists between the physical and the reflected, accessed through a shimmering portal of spray. Inside, the walls would be lined with living glass harvested from my own body, pulsing gently with bioluminescence that responds to my moods.

I would be a keeper of the city's strange temporality, my metabolism synced not to day and night but to the mysterious reconfigurations of the settlement. I would age in fits and starts, sometimes living years in a day, sometimes stretching a moment into a decade. My memories would be scattered across the reflections, stored in droplets and films of moisture.

In times of need, I would descend into the central pool, breathing the thick water and communing with the submerged elders. I would learn from their liquid wisdom, absorbing their understanding of the city's extended metabolism, the vast maintenance project that underlies all existence here.

I would speak the language of water over stone, conveying volumes in the babble of a brook or the roar of a cataract. My poetry would be carved into the ice, my love expressed through the careful merging of streams.

As I age, my body would grow increasingly diaphanous, my edges blurring into mist. I would spend more and more time in the reflected city, walking streets that invert underfoot, conversing with echoes of myself. In the end, I would dissolve entirely into the flow, my essence spread throughout the Weeping Shelves, my consciousness diffused into the very water that carves the city into being.

And perhaps, in some far-future reconfiguration, I would condense again from the mist, a new being born from the memory of water, ready to begin the long work of maintenance anew, forever part of the mountain's liquid dream.
