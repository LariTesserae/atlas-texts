In this world, I would be one of the Hydromancers - a solitary, slow-moving creature perfectly adapted to the damp, labyrinthine megalopolis of stacked stone.

My body would be a heavy, boneless mass of gray muscle, encased in a thick, porous skin that mimics the texture of the wet basalt I move across. I would have no eyes, no ears, no mouth - only a sensitive cowl of pale flesh fringed with delicate tendrils for sensing the subtle vibrations and flows of the ever-present water.

I would breathe through my skin, absorbing oxygen from the cold mist condensing on the endless terraces and plazas. The vast city would be my external lung, the billions of square feet of damp stone surface allowing me to draw breath from the thin mountain air. 

I would move with immense, hydraulic strength, my body flowing and conforming around the uncut boulders and slabs as I slowly patrolled my hundred-square-mile territory. With unfathomable, patient power, I would lift and align the massive limestone blocks, maintaining the critical geometry of pooling condensation and flowing runoff. The city's health would be my only purpose.

I would graze as I went, my underside blindly scraping algae and minerals from the flooded passages, keeping the reflecting pools pristine through my passing alone. Encounters with my own kind would be profoundly rare in the continent-spanning stone reef. 

When the occasional need arose to communicate a warning or a mating readiness, I would descend into the deepest cistern and press my body to the bedrock, sending a low shudder through the interconnected waterways. The ripples would carry my message through miles of canals to be sensed by another of my species.

My existence would be defined by water - shaping it, following it, becoming one with its quiet movements through the porous world of stone. When the rare sunlight flooded the high plateaus, I would sink into the dark, flooded chambers and wait for the mist to return, as I had for centuries beyond memory.

I would know nothing of my origin or the origin of the city. There would be only the eternal present - the pressure of mist on my skin, the slow pulse of my hydraulic body, and the timeless instinct to build the stone ever higher, to pull the clouds down from the sky and merge them with the thirsty earth.
