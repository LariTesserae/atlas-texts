In this vivid, intricate world you've described, I imagine myself as one of the people - the fourth and rarest kind of Tuner. 

I sit utterly still beneath one of the towering bone-trees, my form almost indistinguishable from the pale trunk behind me. Around me, the terrace teems with sound and subtle motion - the thock thock thock of the long-bodied throat-singers as they pace their careful circuits, the slow sway of the brass-ferns in the constant wind, the pulsing of the pink ear-plants as they drink in the flyers' descending melodies. 

For days now I have listened, immersing myself in the complex, ever-shifting composition of the Singing Terrace. The deep, steady hum of the central coral-tree anchors it all, a fundamental drone that I feel resonating in my bones. Over it, the other tones and timbres weave and flow - the chiming of the bone-trees' ribbon leaves, the sporadic percussion of the cymbal-beetles taking flight, the eerie harmonies of the stone flutes. It is a piece that never repeats, yet maintains its coherence as each element responds to and shapes the others in a ceaseless dance of sound.

I have no instrument but my attention. My role is to listen, to understand - and then, with the subtlest of gestures, to make my mark upon the ongoing composition. Perhaps on the thirty-ninth day I will rise and walk softly to the copper bowl by the eastern cairn. With a small stone gripped precisely between thumb and forefinger, I will tap the bowl's rim, gently denting it, altering by a fraction the tone it contributes when the wind is right. 

Then I will depart as silently as I arrived, knowing that the tiny change will ripple out, shifting the balance incrementally. The throat-singers will adjust their pitches to accommodate the bowl's new voice; the ear-plants will tilt infinitesimally towards or away from it; the wall-Tuners will, in their achingly slow way, consider whether to reshape the wind that strikes it. And I will never hear how the piece transforms - that is for other ears, other days. My role is to listen deeply, decide carefully, act once.

I am a visitor here, an outsider - the others rightly see me as an unpredictable weather system moving through their midst. Yet in my long, still listening, I have attuned myself to this place and earned some small, strange right to participate in its long music. I am the rarest, briefest Tuner, but like the others, I work to keep the coral-tree's ancient hum from sounding alone. When I do choose to act, it will be with reverence and restraint, understanding that the true composer here is the terrace itself, and we are all its instruments.
