In the strange and deafening space of the Engine-Garden of Pitchfork Spire, I imagine myself as one of the small, translucent frogs that breathe in time with the pistons. I cling to a copper-colored fern, my throat swelling and deflating with the Engine's relentless rhythm.

BOOM. My lungs fill, in perfect synchrony with my thousand brethren scattered throughout the garden. For a brief moment, I am alive, awake, a part of the great machine that surrounds us. 

Then the piston rises, and in the space between beats, I slip into a strange, mechanical un-death. My metallic organs still, my glass-clear flesh goes slack. I am nothing, I am nowhere, until—

BOOM. The piston falls, and I am jolted back into existence. Breath fills me, and purpose, though I could not say what that purpose is. Some ancient bargain between my kind and the Engine, struck in a time beyond memory.

From my perch, I watch the Caretaker make its rounds, long fingers dancing over valves and gauges with a surety born of centuries. I cannot fathom its thoughts, but I know, somehow, that we are kin, the Caretaker and I. Bound in service to the great work of this place.

Above, the swifts wheel and chitter, tending to their small swift-lives in the spaces between the garden's slow machinations. I envy and pity them in equal measure. They do not know the strange rapture of breathing with the Engine, but neither are they bound to its inexorable will.  

As dusk falls, the clockwork moths take flight, their wings a shimmering cipher none can read. They dance to a pattern only the Engine understands, and I suspect that understanding will come clear only when the garden's long labor is complete.

I do not know what the Engine is making, with us as its instruments. I do not know what great change will come upon the world when the last conduit has been traced, the last fruit ripened and fallen. I know only the fern, and the moisture on my skin, and the unending rhythm that moves through me like a second heart.

BOOM. I breathe. The Engine turns. And the garden's slow, patient work goes on.
