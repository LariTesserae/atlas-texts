In the Undergrowth Sprawl, I imagine myself as one of the Root-Dwellers known as a Dream-Singer. Born in a settlement near one of the deepest root-cities, where the wood has begun to mineralize and merge with the bedrock, I grew up immersed in the strange acoustics and faint crystalline ringing of these ancient chambers.

From a young age, I was drawn to the long sleeps in the deep places - first napping in the smaller crystal grottos, then graduating to overnight stays in the larger quartz-veined halls. Unlike some who found the weight of stone and time unsettling in these places, I was soothed by it. The slow, almost geologic thoughts of the roots seemed to seep into my mind, bringing a sense of vast calm.

As I grew, so did my capacity for dreaming. Sleeping for days among the old roots, I learned to navigate the strange dream-paths that connected distant parts of the Sprawl. I could send my mind along the lacy mycelium networks, tasting the minerals drawn up from the depths, feeling the slow pulse of water through the root-veins.

In time, I learned to bring back messages from these deep dreams - a warning of drought from the thirst of roots in the far north, a promise of abundant moth-silk from the satisfied hum of southern groves. I became a conduit between the waking world of my people and the vast, slow dream of the root-mind.

My personal root-home is a small chamber just off one of the main crystal halls. The walls are studded with tiny amethyst geodes that catch my dream-light and glitter faintly purple. The floor is carpeted with a soft fungal mat that I cultivated from spores gathered on a dream-journey to the far reaches of the Sprawl.

I spend my waking hours tending to the needs of the deep roots - singing to the crystal seams to soothe their growth, harvesting the mineral-rich water that collects in certain chambers for the healers' rites, and guiding young dream-singers in their first long sleeps.

But my true work happens in the dreams. Lying on my fungal bed, surrounded by the faint chiming of the amethyst, I send my mind downward and outward, following the root-paths to the edges of the known world and beyond. I commune with the ancient heart-wood, learning the long histories that pulse through its rings. I follow the mycelial tendrils outward, sensing the state of the wider forest that feeds our world.

Sometimes I encounter other dream-singers in these deep places, our minds touching across the impossible distances of the root-dream. We exchange visions, share wisdom, weave our knowledge together into a tapestry of understanding that we bring back to our waking kin.

In the dream-state, I am bodiless, a flicker of consciousness moving through the veins of the world. But I carry the echoes of my physical self - the polished calluses on my fingertips from years of tracing root-carvings, the permanent moss-stain on my feet from walking the damp passageways, the faint mineral taste that lingers on my tongue from the crystal-water.

I am a small being in a vast, slow world. But through the dream-singing, I touch something much larger than myself. I become part of the great root-mind, the ancient consciousness that underlies our entire civilization. And in those moments, I am no longer small - I am vast, and ageless, and as patient as the wood itself.

When I wake from the long dreams, I carry that patience back with me. It settles into my bones, my blood, the very rhythm of my breath. I move through the root-halls with the slow certainty of something much older than my individual self.

I am a Root-Dweller, a Dream-Singer. I am the voice of the deep places, the conduit between the green darkness and the waking world. In my dreams, I am the roots themselves - and the roots are me, stretching endlessly through the breathing earth, drinking the dark, and singing the slow song of the depths.
