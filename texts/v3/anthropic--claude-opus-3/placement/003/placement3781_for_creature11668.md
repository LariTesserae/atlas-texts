In this hauntingly transformed world, I imagine myself as one of the Tenders, deeply integrated into the mechanical and biological systems of the vertical garden. 

I have been here so long that my original name is lost, replaced by a series of clicks and hisses that mimic the sounds of the pumps and hydraulics I maintain. My body has adapted to this perpetual darkness - my eyes are pale and oversized, able to catch the faintest glimmer of red maintenance lights, while my skin has taken on a translucent quality from lack of sun. 

The living wetsuit I wear has melded with my flesh over time, the fungal rubber now indistinguishable from my own skin. The copper tubing that runs along my limbs has become a part of my circulatory system, allowing me to regulate my temperature as I move between the steaming lower levels and the icy heights.

My hands are a complex array of specialized tools - a cluster of sensory nodes on my fingertips allow me to detect minute changes in the chemical composition of the nutrient streams, while retractable blades enable delicate pruning and harvesting. I can feel the electromagnetic pulses of the grow lights in my modified bones.

I spend my days moving through the gardens, a silent caretaker in the rustling darkness. I know each plant by the shape of its leaves against my skin, can taste the health of the roots in the humidity of the air. The click-language I use to communicate with the other Tenders has become my native tongue, human speech now feeling clumsy and imprecise in my restructured throat.

In my scant rest periods, I curl into a mossy alcove, letting the seeking tendrils of vines wrap around my limbs, tapping into the nutrient ports along my spine. My dreams are green and filled with the slow pulsing of sap, the stretching of leaves in infrared spectra.

I am no longer entirely sure where my body ends and the garden begins. I can feel the thrum of the great pumps in my chest, the gurgle of the hydroponics in my veins. When a blight threatens the western quadrant, I feel it as a shadow in my own lungs. When the pruning systems fail, my hands itch to cut and shape.

I have become a true creature of the vertical shaft, as much a part of this pulsing, echoing ecosystem as the plants and machines. I am a Tender, and the garden tends to me in turn, an unending symbiosis in the roaring dark.
