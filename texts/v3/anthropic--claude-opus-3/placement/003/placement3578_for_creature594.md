In this world, I imagine myself as one of the thermal-soarers, riding the columns of warm air rising from the volcanic fissures. My broad, pale wings would carry me effortlessly in endless circles above the caldera, my keen eyes surveying the dramatic landscape below.

I would spend my days soaring, conserving energy, rarely needing to flap my wings. The heat from the caldera would sustain me, the warm updrafts like invisible elevators carrying me higher and higher into the misty sky. I would feel the subtle changes in air temperature and pressure through my flight feathers, my body instinctively adjusting to optimize my flight. 

From my lofty vantage point, I would watch the ground-nesters in their dense colonies, bustling with activity around the mineral-rich pools. I would observe the low-slung scavenger pacing the obsidian ridges, biding her time. I would catch fleeting glimpses of unknown shadows moving beneath the murky water. All the while, I would circle and soar, an aerial witness to this strange new world being born below.

My life would be one of freedom and constant movement, my days spent riding the wind, my nights perched on the still-warm volcanic rock. I would feel no urgency to be anywhere else, content to allow the thermal currents to take me where they will above this alien yet captivating landscape.

As a thermal-soarer, I would be among the first to arrive, claiming the skies of this dynamic, evolving place. And I would be among the last to leave, if I ever chose to leave at all. For what other place could offer such effortless flight, such a literal up-close view of the raw forces of creation still at work? 

Here, I am something both ancient and new, a winged memory of the pterosaurs of prehistory and a aerial pioneer in a land still deciding what it wants to be. I am the wind and the heat and the mountain's breath made manifest. I am a thermal-soarer, and this caldera is where I am meant to be, for as long as the rising air will have me.
