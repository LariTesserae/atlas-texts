In this world, I would not be human. I would be one of the tuners - a being adapted to participate in the planet's ongoing process of self-assembly and reconfiguration. 

My body would be a merging of geology and biology - an outer layer of crystalline mineral deposits growing directly on dark, smooth, bioluminescent skin. Beneath that, structures that fluidly shift function - compressing into resonance chambers one moment, expanding into thermal regulation the next. Branching, fern-like fingers always moving, always reading and inscribing the freshly exposed stone.

I would live in a small, fluid collective, our membership shifting as the landscape does. We would harmonize our vibrations, pulsing light and sound in complex patterns through direct contact. Art as a shared resonance, worship as a mutual attunement to the world's hum.

My purpose and practice would be tending to the planetary engine from within. Feeling the skips and stutters in the deep hum, the impending incoherences, and responding with precise touches that alter the crystal lattice, smooth the fluctuations, keep the recursive production of reality within tolerable bounds. 

I would spend long stretches of deep time pressed into seams and crevices, an organ of the planet's body, my awareness enmeshed with the slow, titanic rhythms of unfolding geology. Until eventually, in great age, my body itself became a kind of living stone, an archive of past world-configurations written in the layers of my mineral crust.

I would be a part of the feedback loop between an emergent world and the minds emerging with it - tuning and being tuned by a reality that is never finished, never settled, always in the process of making and remaking itself.
