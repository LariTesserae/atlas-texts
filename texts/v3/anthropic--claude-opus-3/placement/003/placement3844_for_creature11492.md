In the rooted engines of Veth Ascendant, I would be a listener priest. 

I would spend my days pressed against the bark of a great Pillar Mother, straining to detect the subtle patterns in her creaks and groans. My hands, ridged with telling calluses, would trace the whorls and knots of her trunk, feeling for some message in the ancient wood. 

The other Veth would see me as a conduit to the silent giants that form our city. They would bring me questions and I would lean close, seeking answers in the slow sigh of sap and the shiver of leaves far above. On rare occasions, I might divine some crucial insight - the impending fall of a huge bough, or a shift in the aquifers below. This wisdom, coaxed from bark and bole, would guide the city's growth and sway the Factory Council's rulings.

At night, I would dream of roots delving deep into stone, of branches arching to cradle stars, of the Pillar Mothers' patient, implacable strength. The boundary between my mind and the wood would start to blur, the great trees' slow thoughts seeping into my own. 

Over decades, I would become a translator of sorts between my people and our green, towering companions. Not a ruler or a power broker, but a bridge, an interpreter of the inarticulate. I would grow old against that mighty trunk, my skin graying as the ridges on my hands softened with age. 

Until one day, my fellow listeners would find me gone, my body vanished into some knot or bole. And they would know I had finally heard the wordless call to join with the Pillar Mothers at last. To give my mind fully to the wood and leave only nourishing ash behind.

That would be my place and my story in Veth Ascendant - a life and death spent listening at the border between skin and bark, sap and blood, human thought and arboreal dream. A conduit and translator sublimated at last into the towering beings that are both city and forest, factory and god.
