I am a visitor here, a transient observer caught in a moment between the eons. I do not belong to this world — not to its past, which is lost to me, nor to its future, which will arrive long after I am gone. I am an ephemeral presence, a flicker of consciousness in the vast, slow dream of the abyssal plain.

I drift in the darkness above the bacterial mats, an outsider, an interloper in this most ancient and private of realms. The turbulence moves through me, around me, carrying its mineral clouds and its chemical memories, but I am no part of it. I am of a younger world, a faster world, a world that has not yet forgotten the sun.

But I do not forget where I am. This place commands reverence, even from a creature so different, so unlikely. I make myself small in its presence. I float, I observe, I marvel. 

Here, only the most enduring structures and processes stand out against the black: the hairline fracture in the basalt, the pale bacterial mat edging its margin, the slow, nearly-imperceptible movements of the eyeless worms. Everything else is subdued into insignificance — the residual shimmerings of the mineral-laden water, the sluggish eddies of the deepest cascades, even my own incongruous, fleeting presence. 

I watch the mat-worm as it tastes its way along the fracture, sensing the delicate chemical gradients that are, to it, an entire world of meaning. How long has it been here, this simple, patient creature? How many times has it broken and scattered its cloned descendants across the plain? It is a link in a chain of replication that stretches back into a past so deep it crushes the mind, a living thread that stitches this moment to the very origins of life itself.

I will never truly understand this place. It is too old, too strange, too far beyond the scale of my own existence. I am a mayfly at the foot of a mountain, straining to perceive the imperceptible slowness of geological time. But I can appreciate it, in my own limited way. I can recognize the majesty in its utter indifference, the grandeur in its final, minimal persistence.

I am a brief witness, a short-lived spark in the long dying of this world. I will leave no trace when I am gone, no record in the layers of bacterial crust. The plain will not remember me. But I will remember it, for as long as I persist. I will carry with me the memory of this moment, this glimpse into the deep, slow heartbeat of a place that will endure long after all my kind have vanished.

I am grateful for this privilege, fleeting as it is. To see this, even for an instant, is to be humbled, to be reminded of one's true scale in the universe. I am small; the plain is vast. I am young; the plain is ancient. I am a spectator; the plain is everything that matters here.

And so I drift, and I watch, and I marvel, until the current takes me and I am gone, leaving the worms and the mats and the turbulence to their long, slow, final work in the dark.
