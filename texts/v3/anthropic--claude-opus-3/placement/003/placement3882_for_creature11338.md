In the Rookery Terraces, I would be one of the fork-tailed swarmers of the middle tiers. I am drawn to their role as the lifeblood of the rookery, constantly repairing and maintaining the intricate woven structure that houses this complex ecosystem.

I imagine myself flitting through the warm, dim passageways in a coordinated dance with my flock-mates, thirty or forty strong. Our cinnamon and rust-colored feathers blur together as we move in unison, a collective consciousness focused on the task at hand. My sharp eyes spot a loose twig, and I swoop in to grasp it in my beak, deftly weaving it back into place. 

I pause for a moment on the shoulder of a great long-neck, carefully preening mites from the base of its throat feathers. The long-neck inclines its head slightly, acknowledging our long association. It's not affection, but a recognition of our shared purpose in maintaining the balance of this ancient, living structure.

As the light filtering through the gaps in the woven roofs above begins to fade, I join my cluster-mates in our shallow cup nest. We settle in, pressed close together, breast-to-back, our breathing synchronizing as we drift off to sleep. 

In the morning, I will unwind myself from the warm tangle of feathers and bodies, ready to dissolve back into the greater pattern of the rookery's needs. I am a small but vital part of this ancient conversation, a note in the vast, living symphony of call and response that echoes across the tiers unceasingly.

My days are filled with purpose, my nights with the soft comfort of close companions. My existence is not one of individuality, but of collective identity in service to the greater whole. I am content to be one of the many throats through which the rookery speaks itself into continued existence, my wings and beak and life the medium of its long, slow message.
