In this woven world beneath the cracked clay, I would be a ferret-keeper's daughter. Born into the warren's fifth level, my earliest memories are lichen-light and cricket song, the subtle creaks and shifts of wicker chambers, the scent of rendered lamp oil and fermented sap. 

As a child, I was drawn to the ferrets my father tended. While other youths practiced weaving or explored the maze of tunnels, I could sit still for hours, watching the small scaled creatures flow through gaps in the walls, their eyes glinting. In time, they came to trust me. A kit might curl in my lap to sleep. I learned to croon to them in soft clicks and trills.

My father saw my affinity and began to train me in his work - how to mend a torn ferret bed, which crickets made the best feed, the secret ways to earn a ferret's loyalty. Under his tutelage, my affection bloomed into skill. 

As I grew older, I dreamed of having a warren-section of my own to tend, with ferret-runs spanning multiple levels. But my father grew uneasy. His eldest ferrets had begun to shun the lower levels. At night, he'd wake with a shudder, claiming the cries from below crept into his sleep. Slowly, his unease kindled my curiosity.

So in my sixteenth year, on the cusp of adulthood, I sought out the listeners who pressed their ears to the sealed hatch of the seventh level. In their weavings, I glimpsed a pattern I'd seen before, in the twists of a ferret's dance, in the pauses of my father's breathing as he slept. And I understood that the ferrets did not avoid the depths from fear, but from reverence. From recognition.

Now I tend my own ferrets, but my thoughts are ever drawn downward. I walk the sixth level when my duties allow, earning sidelong glances. My hands weave new patterns, learned not from my teachers but from my dreams. I think often of the first weavers, who descended to become the warren's foundation. Of the hatch that was sealed behind them. 

The ferrets watch me with eyes that know too much. In their gaze, I see my own future reflected. I imagine the day I'll descend, following the call of the warm depths, the patient thing that waits in the dark. I'll bring an awl of bone, a lamp of rendered fat. Perhaps a ferret kit, to be companion and guide.

I don't know what I'll find below. What I'll become. But I know I'll add my own fiber to the ancient weave. I'll take my place in the pattern beneath the pattern.

I am a keeper's daughter, but it's the depths I was born to tend. In the wicker warrens, my hands already know the way.
