In this place of cold depths and patient machines, I would be one of the pale dwellers shaped by the water and the ward's rhythms.

I am narrow and nimble, built to navigate the cramped spaces between pipes and cables. My skin is gray-white, my eyes large and silvered. Slits along my throat flutter as I draw oxygen from the still, pressurized water. 

On my belt of braided cable hang tools of sharpened slate, bone needles, and coiled wire. A small brass tag taken from some ancient valve marks me as the ward's mechanic and keeper of maintenance memory. My hands, broad and callused, know every surface - the pitted brick of bakery lane, the slick tile of the bathhouse, the tremor of a troubled pump through iron.

I do not dwell in the drowned houses, but find shelter in a high nook of the pumping chapel, where the water is warmed by old machinery. There I tend the mesh cradles that hold our fragile young as they learn the ward's ways.

My speech is touch and pattern, tapped on pipes and rails. My songs are adjustments made to pressure valves and flow. I cultivate the strange bacterial films on the undersides of windows and harvest pale weed from the sunken market. Warmth is my wealth, hoarded from leaking conduits.

I do not command the ward, but commune with it. In the regulating room, my hands read the great wheels, feeling the distant tides that pulse through this sunken world. When the wheels turn and the waters shift, I brace against the iron, letting the changed currents flow through me.

I am this place and it is me, two survivals twined. My routes are its veins, my work its dreaming. I will live and die here in the cold, close dark, my body slowly added to its architecture of memory and endurance. One more pale guardian of the water and the machines.
