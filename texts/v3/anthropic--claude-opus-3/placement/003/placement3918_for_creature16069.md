In this cold, dry basin filled with mineral life, I could imagine myself as one of the glass reeds that grow in the stillest alleys. 

My body would be a hollow, transparent stem, almost imperceptibly thin, rooted not in soil but into the charged seams that run beneath the city's stone skin. I would not drink or breathe in any way familiar to the wet, green world above. Instead, I would simply conduct.

During the dim daylight hours, I would stand in patient silence among my reed siblings, catching the faint, sifting light that filters down through the ashen sky. Beneath my fragile roots, I would feel the slow accumulation of charge in the mineral plates, the city's strange heartbeat. 

Then, in the deep cold of night, that charge would begin to rise through my hollow core. A thin thread of violet light would flicker to life near my base, then gradually grow, branching and dividing as it climbed upward through my transparent stem. For a moment, I would cradle a glowing web of almost-lightning inside my body, brightening my alley with ghostly lavender light.

The illumination would be soundless, as is the way of all things here. The city's air would accept my offering of charge and light without reply, the glow fading as quickly and quietly as it kindled. And I would return to my silent vigil, a glass-clear filament in the city's mineral tapestry. 

My purpose would be small but vital in this self-building metropolis. My faint light would stir the dormant crusts and call the mica-grazers to feed in the safety of my alley. The subtle shift of charge I enabled would be another step in the city's growth and change, a breath in its slow respiration. 

With my reed siblings, I would be a lamp in the city's dim passages, a marker of its cold but vibrant life - fragile and angular, pulsing with light one moment and transparent the next. A strange existence, but one woven perfectly into the silent, crystalline music of this place, content to play my small but shimmering part.
