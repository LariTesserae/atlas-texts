In this stark, unfinished landscape, I would be a pika. 

I would inhabit the spaces between, making my home in the crevices of the scree slope, a grey speckle amidst grey stone. My world would be bound by the reach of my sharp, single-note bark, announcing my small territory.

I would know intimately the jagged edges of freshly cleaved rock, feeling them with whisker and claw as I navigate my talus neighborhood. The damp, lichen-less boulders would be my lookout posts, where I sit as still as stone, watching for the fox's hungry transect.

In summer, I would gather mouthfuls of the hardy plants that cling to this raw place, curing them in haypiles tucked under rocky overhangs. An unintentional gardener, bits of greenery would spread from burrow to burrow as I busily provision for the long, frozen months ahead. 

When the cloud ceiling descends, shrouding the valley in still, cold mist, I would retreat into my boulder warren, a humid, dark sanctuary from the seeping damp above. There, my small warm breaths would mingle with the imperceptibly slow exhales of the mountain itself, stone sighing as it settles into this nascent shape.

My kind would be both anchor and index in this place - our burrows a stabilizing network in the unsorted scree, our numbers a barometer of the ecology's spare but thrumming pulse. The pika's domain is the inches and feet, our dramas played out in miniature.

From my stone-colored perch at the lip of my burrow, I would watch the ibex pick their impossible paths along the cliffs, marmots lumber and whistle across the morning-damp boulder field, the lammergeier's ominous shadow ply the slopes for unfortunate winterkill. The thrum beyond the ridge would vibrate faintly through my paws, an alien frequency, too far and faint yet to parse.

My kind are built for this ungentle, unfinished place. We do not resist its rawness, but fit ourselves into its cracks and niches, becoming one more texture in its lithic skin. In a few thousand years, the pika's tune may change, as the valley slowly rounds and greens, but for now, we sing the song this sharp young landscape calls for.

A small grey voice of the half-made mountain, I would live as the pika does - alert, industrious and woven into the sparse fabric of stone, plant, prey and predator. Not taming, but inhabiting the valley's wildness fully, one bark at a time.
