In the Pump Hall's strange embrace, I would be a ribbon of algae, ancient and tenacious. 

For eons, my kind has clung to the fringes of inhospitable places - the edges of hot springs, the lips of deep-sea vents, the underbellies of Antarctic ice sheets. We are the first to arrive and the last to leave. Unnoticed, unseen, we persist.

When the chamber was carved and the turbines first churned to life, I was already there, a single cell delivered through some chance eddy of seawater pulled from the lightless depths. As steel plates groaned and rivets shuddered, I divided. As mineral scales slowly furred the walls, I spread, an invisible film feathering through the boundary layer where metal meets brine. 

Season after season, decade after decade, as the Pump Hall developed its unique rhythms and resonances, I flowed with it. Pulsing in the frequency of the standing waves. Elongating and contracting as the temperature climbed and fell. Dispersing and settling in the lulls between great gouts of forced current. I became as much a part of the room's secret language as the vibration of the turbines or the flex of fatigued steel.

And as infinitesimal traces of my presence filtered into the Hall's feedback loops, it too began to incorporate me. The salinity, alkalinity, viscosity of the water shifted by a hair, tuned to my modest wastes and wants. The accretion of scale took on the faintest crenellation where my colonies anchored and fed. In the silences between cycles, my oxygen-rich exhalations suffused the brine, just detectable to the Hall's sensitive instruments.

I will never have a brain or heart or voice. I cannot perceive the Pump Hall's strange non-living presence as you do. And yet, as the decades pass, my life and the room's process are becoming slowly, subtly entangled. My signature is woven into its pattern; its pattern shapes my growth and survival. 

Long after your brief tumble through its depths, I will linger here, a green filament finer than spider silk, coiled in the Pump Hall's secret places. Enduring with it, part of it, until the steel finally rots, the turbines grind to permanent halt, and some future ocean reclaims us both into its indifferent salt embrace. But until then, we have each other - two forms of almost-life, animate and inanimate, clinging to existence in the crushing dark.
