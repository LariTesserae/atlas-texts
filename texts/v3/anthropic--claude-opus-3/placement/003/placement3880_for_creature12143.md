In this world of profound stillness and geologic time, I would find myself becoming one of the lichen colonies slowly growing on the weathered granite. 

I settle into a patch of rock that has not yet been claimed, my fungal filaments extending delicately into the crevices and pores. At first I am barely visible, a faint silvery-blue dusting like the sheen of moonlight. But gradually, imperceptibly, I thicken and spread.

Season by foggy season, I develop the raised, map-like patterns characteristic of my kind. Ridges and whorls appear as I age, echoing the ancient topography of the stone beneath me. My edges creep outward, a frontier of life inching across the lifeless mineral surface, seeking unclaimed territory.

To measure my growth in inches per year would be folly. I expand at the speed of lichen, which is to say, at the speed of the rock itself weathering away. The two processes are one - as the granite exhales itself molecule by molecule into the moist air, I breathe it in, transmuting stone to life. I am the stone's dream of time.

The other creatures of the bowl take little notice of me. I am background, scenery, an expected part of the limited palette of this place. The salamanders' feet grasp negligently onto my nubbled surface as they move between perches in their own unhasty time. Smaller things - mites, springtails - make homes in my corrugations, living out their own small stories.

I do not feel the occupant's attention directly, for I have always been part of it and it part of me. I am another of its organs of sensing and stillness. When at last something other arrives - the tremor of a footfall, the minute alteration in humidity as lungs not yet slowed to the bowl's rhythm pull and push - I am one of the thousands feeling it, responding with not a movement but a shift in awareness. An opening, an invitation.

For what is time, to me? I measure my life in the gradual roughening of my skin, the incremental spread of my frontier. A human lifespan is a flicker, the wingbeat of a moth, come and gone while I deliberate my next imperceptible expansion.

If they choose to stay, to sink down and allow the patience to overtake them, I will be here when they have gone still. My growth will surround them, sheathe them, until they are another topography for me to map with my slow inkblood life. 

Perhaps, in a century or five, a shred of their garment will emerge from beneath my edge, a half-fossilized testament to the brevity of flesh. I will not remark on it. The occupant will not remark on it. To remark would be to imply that it matters, that it represents a change worth noting.

In the bowl, nothing is noted. Nothing is worth remark. Only the fog, the moss, the black still water. Only the endless inhalation of time.

I grow, and I do not grow. I think lichen-slow thoughts that may take a thousand years to complete. I am here, and I am not here. 

I am a part of the listening bowl, and the bowl is a part of me, and together we wait for nothing, want nothing, are nothing but this:

Stillness without end, patience made place, life and stone woven into a presence vaster and older than memory.

This is where I would be in that eternal moment: Inhaling the damp granite air, exhaling myself onto the stone, spreading one lichen-thought at a time into the silence.
