In this enclosed world of mechanized biology, I imagine myself as one of the Adjusters, an elder inhabitant suspended high in the vibrating canopy. My skin, deeply etched by decades in this saturated, steam-filled gorge, bears the colors of the engine—rich, oxidized iron, oily soot, and the mottled greens of algae and fern.

I am lashed to the thick, living roots with heavy, moisture-beaded ropes, their fibers grown stiff with age and the permeating damp. In my hands, I hold the tools of infinite patience—wrenches worn smooth by generations of use, their once-sharp edges softened like river stones.

My body has long adapted to the relentless thrum of the machinery, my bones resonating with the slow, inexorable rhythm of the drive shafts and the shuddering hiss of the steam pistons. I no longer distinguish between my own heartbeat and the pulsing vibrations of the vast iron network I tend.

Year after year, I focus on a single set of bracketed bolts, each one thicker than my gnarled wrists. Month by month, I coax the aged nuts a fraction of a turn, my movements as imperceptible as the growth of the great banyan whose branches envelop me. 

There is no thought of completion, no notion of a final turn of the wrench. My purpose, like the engine itself, is to maintain the balance between iron and wood, between the dense weave of pipes and the slow swell of the trees. I am a conduit, an agent of equilibrium.

When the humid air grows heavy in my lungs, I do not fight it. I simply close my eyes behind their clouded lids and breathe with the slow cycle of the boilers, knowing that my fellow inhabitants will read the cessation of my adjustments in the changed tempo of the forest's growth.

In time, my body, like the worn wrenches in my hands, will become another part of the engine—a gnarled figure woven into the root system, a burl of knotted flesh fused with the bark, still humming with the machine's ancient rhythm. And the cycle will continue, as a new Adjuster takes my place in the dripping canopy, their strong hands turning the bolts in that same patient, ponderous rhythm, as much a part of the engine as the iron we twist.
