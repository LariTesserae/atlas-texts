In this world of sun-baked clay and silent perfection, I would find myself among the Sylphs, haunting the bright canopy highways high above the ceramic labyrinth.

My body is a study in weightless, elongated grace, perfectly adapted for life amid the giant succulents and thorny vines. My limbs are slender and spidery, with an extra cartilaginous joint that allows me to distribute my negligible mass across the interwoven aerial pathways. My fur is a dusty, reflective white that turns me into a pale specter against the unfiltered glare.

I move through the green heights with liquid agility, my whip-thin prehensile tail acting as a fifth grasping appendage as I swing between the pliant, rubbery boughs. My elongated jaws house rows of needle-fine incisors, precision tools for piercing the thick hides of the moisture-hoarding plants. When I find a succulent, I plunge my long, hollow tongue through the small wound, siphoning out the precious water and nutrient-rich sap within. My belly swells with each harvest.  

Though I may appear delicate, my role is one of vital importance. I am a link in the city's living hydration network, a vessel for transferring water mouth-to-mouth to the heavy-set Masons toiling in the sun-baked mud and the soft, sightless Deep Mothers laboring in the fungal gardens far below. I work in wordless synchrony with my fellow Sylphs, our movements orchestrated by the invisible web of pheromones that binds the metropolis together.

Yet even as I fill my role, I am keenly aware that I am but a single, fragile cell in the vast super-organism of the city. I can feel the steady pulse of the Deep Mothers' sonic heartbeat thrumming through the soles of my splayed feet as I balance on the interlocking vines. I know that my fate is tied to the greater fate of the horde. Should I falter, should sickness take me or a sudden fall from the heights snap a limb, my body will betray me. The sweet, sharp scent of stress-pheromones will leak from my fur, drawing the silent mercy of the Cullers.

But I do not fear this. It is simply the way of things, the elegant brutality that keeps the city in its perfect balance. And as I spiral higher into the bright latticework of sap and sun-baked ceramic, I feel myself dissolving into the greater purpose, my identity subsumed into the glorious anonymity of flawless function and unending toil beneath the harsh, unfiltered sky.
