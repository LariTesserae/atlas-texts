In this vivid and primordial world, I imagine myself as one of the dwarf willows clinging tenaciously to the steep basin. 

My form is low and unassuming, woven humbly through the rubble and talus no higher than an ankle in places. I have no ambition to be a towering tree. My purpose is simply to hold fast.

Season after harsh season, I endure. When the frigid winds rip across the basin, I lay my branches down flat, bowing but not breaking. My small silvery leaves flutter and dance, a flickering of light and dark. In heavy snows, I allow myself to be pressed down beneath the cold weight, knowing I will rise again.

Sometimes a shifting stone shears away one of my limbs. But I do not despair - this too is part of my patient persistence. From my ancient roots, I send forth a new shoot seeking the next sheltered crack. What appears as a small single shrub on the surface is really an entire linked colony beneath the rocks, sharing strength.

I do not flower extravagantly or beckon to pollinators. My blossoms, when they come, are discrete affairs, an unshowy yellow that proceeds with or without fanfare. My leaves take in what meager light filters down the basin walls and spin it quietly into growth.

Through my roots, I slowly sip from the thin black soil, that cold living residue of all that the basin has captured and broken down over eons. Fungal filaments lace delicately through my root hairs, a hidden partnership. In turn, my fallen leaves become part of that primal humus to nourish the next generation.

I am a stabilizer, a soil-binder. Each tiny rootlet I send forth is both an anchor for myself and a net to catch mineral and organic drift. I am a member of the basin's first woven community. With the mosses, the liverworts, the ferns and grasses, the lichens and microbes, we are together the living skin the broken mountain is slowly growing over its wounds.

Though I am perhaps one of the basin's more visible inhabitants, I have no illusion of dominance. In the face of stone, wind, cold and wet, I know I am small. My kind will never subdue this place, only continue our slow, symbiotic negotiation with its hard mineral truth, one generation at a time. 

There is a deep patience required to be part of a processes so gradual. To the passing mist and rushing water, my life must seem still as the stones. But I know myself to be in constant prayer and conversation with the broken land. As the basin performs its ceaseless industry, I offer up my quiet green hymn of presence. I sing by enduring.
