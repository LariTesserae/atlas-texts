In this extraordinary world of cistern cities and subterranean adaptations, I imagine myself as one of the Listeners. Perched at the liminal spaces between chambers, I press my morphing ears against the damp limestone, sensing the distant tremors of the surface world above.

Over generations, my body has transformed to better attune to the stone's vibrations. The bones of my inner ear have elongated and articulated, forming a delicate seismic antenna. My ear canals have widened and grown hypersensitive, able to distinguish the faint pressure waves of an approaching sandstorm from the deep bass notes of gathering rain. The mineralized ridges of my earlobes help conduct sound directly to my skull.

I was born to this role, marked from birth by the faint clicking sounds my ears made as I turned my head. As a child, I pressed my face against the cistern walls, feeling the coolness of the limestone, sensing something I couldn't yet interpret in the stone's minute vibrations. The elders recognized my gift and took me as an apprentice, teaching me to parse the whispers of the earth.

Now I crouch for hours, my knees permanently callused, my neck craned at an angle that would cripple a surface dweller. But to me, it is as natural as the salamander's sinuous crawl. I have become something not quite human, an auditory hybrid of flesh and stone.

In the chambers behind me, I hear the slosh of the eel catchers, the soft patter of the mushroom farmers, the guttural chants of the pump operators. But my attention is always drawn outward and upward, toward the unseen world that sends its messages down through the rock.

I tap out my prophecies on the skin of my fellow citizens, a gentle code of seismic wisdom. Two quick taps followed by a long press: a distant quake, no danger. A rapid staccato burst: a sandstorm will cover the entrance shafts, prepare to clear them. A slow, rhythmic pulse: the blessed rain comes, the cisterns will breathe easy. 

Sometimes, in moments of rare silence, I simply listen to the stone's memory. I hear echoes of the ancient builders, their chisels ringing against the chambers' curved walls. I feel the slow, patient drip of water shaping the limestone, carving new channels in imperceptible increments. In these moments, I am not separate from the city but an extension of it, my ear bones just another calcified formation in the stone's endless memory.

Surface dwellers say the cistern city is a tomb, a dark, damp grave for a people who have forgotten the sun. But they cannot hear what I hear - the undying rhythm of life adapting, shaping itself to the stone's unyielding embrace, finding in the depths a resilience that puts the parched surface to shame.

In the cistern's permanent twilight, I have found my purpose. I am the city's ears, its early warning system, its conduit to the world above. In the stone's whispers, I hear the voice of a community that has learned to dance to water's slow music, to find in the dark spaces between breaths a stubborn, limestone-hard will to survive.
