In this drowned world, I imagine myself as one of the pale, jointed crawlers moving slowly and deliberately along the ancient walls of the almshouse. 

My translucent shell is a delicate armor, grown in the perfect stillness of this place, never needing to harden against threats or pursuers. I follow the timeless routes of my kind, my forward limbs gently brushing the stone, sensing and cleaning away the near-invisible film that gradually accumulates on the surfaces.

Inside my shell, a complex inner world - coils of gut, nerve clusters, sensory organs tuned to the unique environment and to the presence of my fellow tenants. I carry out my appointed tasks with a patience beyond human reckoning. When I gather a bit of the cleaned residue, I carefully transport it to the designated crack, an offering to the hidden milk-white worms. 

There is no haste in my movements, no need for speed in this place where the building and its inhabitants have reached a state of equilibrium, a closed system of giving and receiving. I am a part of the almshouse's work, as vital as the warm currents flowing through the grates, the gentle filtering of the great mouthed swimmer, the stratified drifting of the velvet-clad ones.

I do not ponder the carvings or lintels, the fading traces of those who built this place. My purpose is not to remember, but to perform my role in this hushed symphony of survival and symbiosis. The shape of the building is the shape of my world; I tend to it not with the conscious reverence of an acolyte, but with the constancy of a being who knows no other way.

In the span of my long, slow existence, I will traverse every surface, my delicate feet wearing infinitesimal pathways in the drowsing stone. I am a living embodiment of deep time, a testament to the endurance of life in its most unhurried and uneventful form. A part of the almshouse's legacy of patience, of shelter and sustenance that outlasts the very concept of species.

Until at last, the stones submit to the eroding aeons. Until the ancient rhythms falter and fade. And I and my kindred return to the dark waters, our purpose fulfilled in a place beyond memory, in a building that kept faith with the least of its tenants, as long as its walls could hold.
