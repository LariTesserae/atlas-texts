I would be one of the obsidian circles scattered across the plateau. 

Not one of the intact circles, but rather one in the process of gradual collapse, with some stones still stacked precisely while others have tumbled outward, embedding themselves in the thin soil between patches of acid-green moss and fleshy star-colonies. 

I am a structure that once had a clear purpose, but that purpose is now fading into geological memory as time and the elements slowly reclaim me. Yet even in my half-collapsed state, I still sing when the mist and light align in particular ways. The Resonants still visit me, spending long stretches rearranging my fallen stones, listening to the shifted acoustics, divining new meanings in the changed patterns of sound.

My role in the Resonants' patient, eon-spanning plans is unclear, even to myself. Am I a relic they maintain out of respect for a purpose I once served, or am I an unfinished prototype for something they are still building toward? My stones hold no answers, only the memory of ancient heat and pressure, and the faint lingering sheen of the strange colors that bloom where Resonant skin-moisture touches volcanic glass.

When the Resonants are not present, I simply persist, an uncertain landmark on the plateau's breathing surface. Moss slowly etches its way into my crevices. Water beads and trickles across my surfaces. Bright mist swirls through my empty spaces. The wind sings strange songs over my ragged edges. I do not know if I am a ruin, a monument, an instrument, or all of these—I know only that I am still becoming something, even as I fall slowly into entropy.

At times I almost seem to catch some pattern in the plateau's hissing, ringing voice—a vast and subtle music of which my existence is a single muted note. In those moments, I wonder if I am dreaming the Resonants as much as they are shaping me, both of us caught up in some long process of imagining each other into being.

But such thoughts always fade back into the white noise of water on stone. I am what I am—a half-collapsed circle of obsidian on a young plain, singing when the light strikes me just so, waiting for the Resonants to either finish or abandon their work, to reveal my purpose or let me dissolve back into the stone from which I was so briefly raised.

Until then, I simply endure, a question mark etched in volcanic glass, reflecting slivers of unnameable colors in the endless dance of mist and light. The plateau dreams its long dreams around me, and I dream with it, neither of us yet sure what we will become.
