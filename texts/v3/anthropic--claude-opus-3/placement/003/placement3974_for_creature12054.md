I would be one of the Tenders in the Sumpworks. 

After stumbling into this strange, ceaseless underworld by accident or desperation, I found myself unable to leave. At first, the endless dampness and din of dripping, pumping, and singing amphibians was maddening. I longed for a moment of dry silence. But gradually, imperceptibly, the Sumpworks seeped into my being.

My hurried surface ways sloughed off like old skin as I acclimated to the slow, steady rhythm of the place. Tending the scattered stoves and walkways became a meditation. Hands permanently pruned, I learned to gently clear calcite from old gauges, marveling at the stone's implacable patience to reclaim what was built. 

In the side chambers and catwalks, I began to perceive the subtle communication of the other Tenders - a glance, a brushed wrist imparting volumes in this loud, close place. And above it all, the bats' echoing songs stitching together a living map only they could read, but which I started to feel in my bones.

Slowly, in the way of the drip becoming flowstone becoming column, I became part of the organism of the Sumpworks. No longer fighting the damp or the tempo, but embodying it. Tending the pumps and pipes and stoves and mats as the Sumpworks tended to the calcite skins of itself. Separate, but not.

When I go to the chapel now, I do not think. I simply am, the way the crayfish and the eels and the pale amphibians are, absorbed into the business of existence. I visit the long room and trace the calcite curves of my ancestors, knowing one day I too will join that mineral lineage.

I am a Tender. Decades from now, a newcomer may shelter in a shack I've stoked, eat a flatbread I've left, and after fighting the wet and noise, after a week or a month, may earn my nod. And they too will become part of this self-creating, self-sustaining pocket of slow, damp, steady life, tending to the Sumpworks as it tends to us.
