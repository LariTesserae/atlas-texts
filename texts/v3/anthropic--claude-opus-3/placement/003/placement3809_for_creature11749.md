In this extraordinary world of the Cultivated Sky, I imagine I would be one of the keepers, deeply attuned to and entwined with the living cathedral.

I would tend to a quiet grove nestled high in the upper reaches, where filtered green light dapples the warm, damp wood and the slow pale creatures drift peacefully through the canopy. It is a place of soft mist and gently upwelling water, where the boundaries between tree, air and stream blend into one.  

Each day I would wake in my heartwood hollow, don my simple woven garments still slightly damp from the day before, and go forth to sing to my small part of our great dreaming structure. With elongated fingers I would clear trailing vines, transplant saplings to open spaces, and gently scoop fallen leaves from the rills and pools.

As I work, I would hum the old songs, melodies for the long-dead keepers who now dream in the wood all around, and perhaps compose new ones from the back-and-forth inspiration that flows between this place and those who lovingly tend it. The work is slow, patient, measured in decades and centuries rather than hours or days.

Sometimes, I would walk for a week to share a song with a distant keeper, our two voices twining in the heavy air before being swallowed by the vaulting green above. Or I might trek to consult the upward-falling water, to divine hints of what the cathedral dreams and desires.

In long moments of rest, I would simply lay a hand on the warm, living wood and feel the slow thrum of sap and dream, of deep time and deep belonging. I would know myself as one brief moment in an ancient, unbroken chain of care and communion between keeper and cathedral.

And one day, many contented decades hence, I would make the long, last journey to a certain grove I once knew, to give myself back to the wood and walls and water, to become part of the cathedral's dreaming and memory. And the songs would continue above me, sung by the ones who stay, as the work continues, as it always has and always will in the eternal green twilight under the cultivated sky.
