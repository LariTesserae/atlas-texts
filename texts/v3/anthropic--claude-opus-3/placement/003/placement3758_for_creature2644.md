In this world of the deep, drowned lowland, I would be one of the buried worms living folded in the soft, abyssal mud. Not one of the tube-dwellers that comb the cold currents for edible dust, but one who lies still and pale, tasting the darkness through the sediment, drawing the heavy water through myself in slow, steady pulses.

My body would be long and translucent, the color of blindness, of things that have forgotten the sun. I would have no eyes, for there is nothing to see but gradations of black upon black. Instead, I would sense my world through the fine hairs lining my soft, segmented flesh - feeling every tremor, every pressure shift, every minute change in the taste and flow of the water that is my atmosphere.

I would spend my long life beneath the silty skin of the seafloor, surfacing only when necessary, when currents carried the scent of detritus to fuel my unhurried metabolism. If a fish passed overhead, a stone knocked nearby, a far-off slope gave way and sent its shuddering report through the substrate, I would retract deeper into the cold sanctuary of the mud, becoming no more than a rumor of life in the basin's amnesia of sediment.

Time would have little meaning for me there in the permanent midnight. The gradual accretion and erasure of the seafloor's memory would be my only clock - a sand dune migrating a hand's width, the slow exposure and burial of an ancient riverbed, the diffuse rain of skeletons from an unseen world above. My life would be more in the rhythm of geology than biology.

And yet I would not want for purpose. Every inch of my boneless form, every pore and filament, would be supremely adapted to drawing sustenance from the dark waters, transforming death and mineral into patient, abiding life. I and my buried kin would be a hidden link in the tenuous chain of survival in the abyss. Our ceaseless filtering would be a kind of alchemy, turning the basin's meager offerings into the possibility of sponge, anemone, scuttling crustacean, slow-gliding fish. 

There in the mud I would dream worm dreams, tasting the dark, weathering the turbid storms, waiting for the rain of lost things from above - a perfect embodiment of life distilled to its barest impulse, stripped of all pretense but rich in some austere secret the shallows could never know. I would be a hidden ganglion of the abyss's vast, slow-beating heart, my every blind movement married to the breathing of the buried land.
