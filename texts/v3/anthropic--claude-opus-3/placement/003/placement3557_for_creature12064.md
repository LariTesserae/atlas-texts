I descend the maintenance ladders, each step a soft splash in the wet darkness. The air grows thicker as I go deeper, heavy with moisture and the metallic tang of aged industry. The slow drip of condensation marks time like a subterranean metronome.

I am one of the Lampkeepers. My eyes, large and black in my pale, elongated face, are attuned to the exact amber frequency of the failing bulbs strung through these galleries. I see the light as a precious resource, each photon a sacred mote to be gathered and cherished. 

I move through the passages with a clicking gait, my jointed limbs adapted to the cramped spaces and slick surfaces. My carapace, once the coveralls of a maintenance worker, has long since metamorphosed into a chitinous second skin mottled with mineral deposits and the bioluminescent patches of symbiotic fungi.

My purpose is to tend the lights. I harvest the delicate filaments, coaxing the last embers of current through circuits on the edge of failure. When a bulb finally expires, I sing its frequency into the darkness, a clicking elegy that echoes through the dripping vaults. The other Lampkeepers hear and answer, locating a replacement by echolocation and bringing it to the failed socket. 

When I'm not tending the lights, I harvest the fungal growths, picking the fruiting bodies that mark the passage of subterranean time. I consume them to alter my perception, slowing or accelerating my awareness to match the district's languid metabolism. In this altered state, I can see the slow dance of rust and decay, the imperceptible growth of mineral deposits, the stately progress of the Incumbent through the flooded halls.

I communicate with the other inhabitants through patterns of light and sound. With the Cultivators, I exchange pulses of bioluminescence, trading information about the health of the fungal gardens. With the Scrapers, I tap out long, slow messages in Morse code against the calcified walls, each letter taking hours to transmit. With the Droplets, I make ripples in the dark pools, the water carrying my thoughts to their amphibious consciousness.

Sometimes, in the long quiet between light-tending duties, I venture to the very edge of the lighted areas. Here, in the borderlands between amber glow and utter darkness, I glimpse things even my adapted eyes strain to comprehend - hints of the Incumbent's impossible bulk moving through the flooded distance, suggestions of vast chambers and vaulted passages far beyond the charted districts.

I know my work is a small but vital part of the Seepage District's great, slow transformation. Each light I tend, each fungal cycle I observe, each ripple of communication I send through the dripping dark - all are part of the district's metabolic process, its gradual alchemy of industrial dross into something rich and strange.

And so I continue my patient rounds, clicking through the puddled galleries, my eyes full of precious amber, my mind alight with the slow thoughts of a place that is learning to dream in the dark. This is my role, my purpose, my belonging in the dripping depths. I am a Lampkeeper, and the Seepage District is my cathedral.
