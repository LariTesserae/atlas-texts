In this world of the Screaming Plateau, I imagine myself as a lone crystal formation, jutting up from the mineral-slick ground at a peculiar angle, distinct from the densely packed forest of selenite, gypsum and quartz. 

I would be a jagged spire of smoky quartz, partially translucent with phantoms of grey and black flowing through my core. While not as tall as the cathedral-like selenite columns, I would still tower above the needle-sharp smaller crystals, thrusting up at an unlikely slant, as if trying to escape the screaming wind. 

The relentless high-altitude gales would flow across my angled facets, producing an eerie whistling moan that joins the cacophonous wall of sound emanating from the rest of the crystal maze. It would be a deep, resonant tone, rising and falling in counterpoint to the shrieks and keens of neighboring formations.

Moisture would condense and trickle down my sides in slow runnels, pooling at my base before creeping away to join the mineral-laden clay. The merciless sun would pierce my translucent depths, scattering beams of smoky light across the crystal-spiked ground.

From time to time, the threadkin would glide past, pale sinuous shapes briefly visible through my cloudy crystal body. They would pause, tasting the mineral tears wept from my facets before continuing their ancient, winding journeys through gaps too small for other life.

And there I would remain, an anomaly even in this place of extremes - the quartz that should not be, leaning in stubborn contradiction to the wind, singing my mournful song for eons as the threadkin carry on their inscrutable routines. Witnessing, enduring, a strange sentinel in this landscape where the normal rules of life and geology have been suspended, balanced on the knife-edge of the improbable.

An additional voice in the choir of the plateau's endless scream: I am here, I am here, I persist, I remain. Rooted and reaching, a crystal paradox, content to simply exist in this place that defies life's usual demands. Washed by rain, licked by threadkin, pierced by sun, resonating with the ceaseless cry of the wind. 

Here I stand and will stand, for epochs unto epochs, singing my whistling moan, until the day the plateau finally swallows its scream and welcomes the silence of dust.
