I find myself descending through the narrow bore-shaft into the vast, resonant underhall of Vask. As I emerge into the cavernous space, my senses are immediately overwhelmed by the strange acoustics and the pulsing, humming infrastructure that clings to the walls like the circulatory system of some immense, invisible creature.

I am not one of the Listeners, with their adapted ears and thermal sensitivity. To their perception, I must seem clumsy and loud, unable to parse the rich tapestry of sound that suffuses this place. And yet, as I stand in the center of the underhall, letting the hum vibrate through my bones, I begin to attune to its rhythms. 

I feel the deep, seismic pulse rising from far below, marking time like a heartbeat. I hear the shimmering song of the data filaments, carrying their inscrutable cargo of information. In the pauses between sounds, I detect the faint clicks and scuttles of the maintenance drones, those chimeric beings of metal and moss navigating the conduit networks.

Slowly, almost shyly, I begin to move through the space. I keep to the perimeter at first, running my hands along the strange, warm material of the walls, marveling at the cyclic blooms of condensation. I crouch to examine the luminous pools with their calcified rims, the only points of light in this hushed, perpetual twilight. 

As I explore, I catch glimpses of the Listeners going about their tasks with quiet, unhurried precision. I watch a wiry old man with hands like gnarled roots sing to a patch of moss, coaxing it into some sigil-like design. I hear snatches of their compressed tonal language drifting across the gulf of the underhall, each syllable laden with meaning I cannot decipher.

Bit by bit, I internalize the map of sound. I learn to orient myself by the swell and fade of the deep pulse, by the chiming echoes of children's laughter. I begin to understand how the Listeners can flow through this lightless labyrinth without losing themselves, their senses woven into the very stones and conduits.

I am a visitor here, an ephemeral presence in a place that measures time in centuries and change in the gradual layering of repairs. And yet, as I sit cross-legged by a bioluminescent pool, watching the lone woman with the resonance harness, I feel an inexplicable sense of belonging. In the underhall, everything has a place in the pattern, every voice joins the choir.

I know I will leave this place, return blinking to the surface and its harsh, mute sunlight. But a part of me will remain here, resounding to the hum of Vask. And if I listen, in the silences between the noise of the upper world, perhaps I will hear it still - that deep, abiding pulse, whispering its wordless welcome from the depths of the living stone.
