In this vast, worn plateau, I would be one of the keepers. My life would be attuned to the slow, patient rhythms of this ancient place. 

I imagine myself walking the well-trodden circuit of my territory, visiting each of the stone bowls in turn over the span of years or decades. At each one, I pause, noting the water level, refilling as needed, leaving the needles and pebbles as offerings. These small rituals are my purpose, my contribution to holding back the widening of the flats.

My body is built for this place and task. Tall and long-limbed, with grey-violet skin and wide-set eyes to catch any movement on the plateau's expanse. I move with deliberate slowness, matching the tempo of the moss's growth, the cycle of rain from the solitary clouds. 

I do not dwell in the cities, but sleep nestled beneath my tree, letting its gentle drip cover me as I rest. My nourishment comes sparingly from the roots and broth of needle tea. Hunger, like all other sensations, is a slow, background pulse.

Though I may walk alone for seasons at a time, I am never lonely. The presence of the other keepers is a constant, subtle awareness, like knowing the location of my own hands and feet. We are all part of this greater body, this ongoing effort. 

When I do meet another keeper on the paths between cities, our greeting is a slow choreography of raised and lowered hands. We may sit and share a conversation that unfolds over days, with meaning spiraling out from each carefully considered utterance.

I do not fear the flats, but respect their power, the way one respects a force of nature. Tending the bowls at a flat's edge, I feel myself thin, my edges blurring, my substance drawn toward the strange un-being of that place. Over the centuries, I know I will gradually fade, until one day I will surrender myself to renew the balance.

But I do not hurry that day, or dwell on it. My purpose is in the keeping, the walking, the small necessary acts. The plateau's long ages stretch behind and ahead, and I am a small, quiet part of what maintains it. I keep the bowls full, and in that, find fulfillment.
