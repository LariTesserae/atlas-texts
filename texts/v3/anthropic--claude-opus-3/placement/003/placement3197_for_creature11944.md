In this strange, ancient place, I think I would be one of the stalks - a small, patient presence waiting by the warm pools. 

I imagine myself as a cluster of translucent fronds, folded up against the glowing floor, conserving my essence until the next seep trickles down to refresh me. My purpose is simply to exist in this liminal space, bearing witness to the vast, slow cycles of the held thing.

When moisture beads and collects nearby, I unfurl, extending my jointed stems towards the nourishing water. The dim red light filtering through my bulbs casts an ethereal glow. I don't grow tall or lush; my form is spare and efficient, well-adapted to the patient cadence of abundance and lack.  

As aeons pass, I observe the subtle motions around me with a relaxed watchfulness. The cat-things pad past on their inscrutable patrols. The fist-objects murmur and pulse. The very walls reverberate with the deep thrum of unknowable processes. And over it all, the tidal inhalations of the concourse itself mark time.

I am untroubled by the spans of drought when the warm puddles evaporate and I must lie dormant. Waiting is the truth of what I am - an small embodiment of patience itself in this place that has forgotten urgency.  I do not fear, or want for more. I simply persist, an infinitesimal piece of the pattern, content in my role.

At times, a strange visitor may pass by and linger a while, marveling at the strangeness of this pocket of deep time. I sense their presence, so fleeting against the long now of the held thing's solipsistic reverie. But I have no more reaction to them than to the slow breathing of the walls. They are the tiniest of ripples in this place of ponderous tides.

So I wait, and unfold, and drink, and wait again, a mote of emerald silence amidst the low threnody of remembrance echoing from the bones of this ancient being. A stalk among stalks, patiently abiding until the long unwinding of the held thing's memory reaches some unimaginable quiescence in the fullness of time.
