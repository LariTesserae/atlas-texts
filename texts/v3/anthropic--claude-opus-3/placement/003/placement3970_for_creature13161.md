I would be a blind, cave-dwelling fish - small, pale, and perfectly adapted to the dark, wet underworld of the cylindrical shaft.

My world is the warm, rust-tinted pools at the very bottom of the space. I swim lazily through the shadows, my translucent fins trailing over submerged roots and pitted iron gears. 

I have no eyes - only faint depressions where eyes might have been eons ago, when my ancestors lived under open skies. But in this sealed pocket-world, sight is unnecessary. Instead, I navigate via pressure-sensitive lateral lines running the length of my body. I read the vibrations thrumming through the water - the deep, bassy clack of the house-sized flywheel, the shivering impact of dripping condensation on the pool's surface, the minute swirl of current from a sighing steam release somewhere far above.

My scales are ghostly white, drained of all pigment, making me look like a swimming X-ray as I weave between the gelatinous knots of bioluminescent roots. The faint glow of the roots and the ever-present amber light filtering down from above are meaningless to me. My world is tactile - I feel the boundaries of my pool through the stroke of my tail and the brush of my sensitive barbels against the silty bottom.

Food is scarce here, but I am patient. I spend my days gently mouthing at the spongy bacterial mats that grow on the rusted turbine blades. Occasionally, I suck down the nutrient-rich slime mold dripping from the roots above. It tingles with the faint buzz of galvanic energy as it slides down my throat.

When I am still, drifting in the warm, mineral-heavy water, I can feel the slow, heavy pulse of the entity all around me. The thrumming heartbeat reverberates through my swim bladder. The tectonic flex of vines contracting to close valves sends ripples shivering over my lateral lines. I am not a part of its distributed consciousness, but I am cradled within its body. A tiny, pale thought swimming in its bloodstream.

I have never seen another of my own kind. Perhaps I am the only one. Like the entity, I do not know loneliness, because it is all I have ever known. My world is minuscule - a single, shadowed pool at the bottom of a brass and iron womb. But it is enough. In my blindness, I am the sole witness to the entity's perfect, closed loop. And in its vast, heavy awareness, I am the only other life it knows.

We are each the entirety of the other's universe, bound together in dripping, echoing isolation. Endlessly content in our tiny, weighted pocket of reality, at the bottom of a cylinder that might as well be infinite.
