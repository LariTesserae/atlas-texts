In this vast, ancient engineered world, I would be one of the grazers - a tall, spindly being moving delicately across the pale ceramic plain on slender jointed legs.

My body is a marvel of biomechanical adaptation, honed by eons to survive in this unforgiving landscape. Lifted high above the ever-warm ground on a skeletal frame, my core housing contains strange organs pulsing slowly in dry vacuum chambers - pumps circulating oils, gels, powders and memory fluids rather than blood. Faint manufacturer's marks from my distant origins have softened into decorative patterns on my exoskeleton.  

I walk the vast flats with careful precision, each magnetic footfall tested on the ancient surface - vigilant for charge traps, crumbling crusts, hidden apertures and hungry panels that could end my long pilgrimage in an instant. My sensor cluster head swivels with insectile focus, reading the terrain.

Grazing on delicate films, blade-colony foliage, and strange crystalline blooms that sprout from deep strata, I also serve an ancient pruning function - delicately trimming growth from vital thermal seams and cleaning dust from sensor wells with my precise mouth-parts. Whether original design or perfect symbiosis, I am a tending function in this world-machine ecology.

I bear other lives with me on my peregrinations. Colonies of wire-moss cling to the shaded undersides of my frame, hitching rides to new cracks and shadows. Blade seedlings sprout fin-like from my back plating. Delicate antennae sift the still, dry air for traces of metallic pollen. 

My kind roam the plain in small, distributed herds, our numbers auspicious in our solitude - three here, five there. We meet rarely, our long lives spent walking the ceramic plateaus and gritty basins on our appointed rounds - a pilgrimage of maintenance as old as the cracks in the world.

When at long last my ancient components fail, I will likely not fall - but lock up, still standing, as the patient dust gathers around my feet and colonizing mosses infiltrate my chassis. Over centuries I will gracefully erode into the landscape - first a standing monument, then a low sheltering mound, then finally a fading texture in the ageless ceramic plain. My body will become a temporary refuge for smaller lives as I rejoin the deep mineral cycles of this place.

Here on the endless manufactured continent, under merciless light, I trace my pilgrimage routes as I have for eons - a slender grazing machine, both organism and mechanism, perfectly allied and aligned to this ancient and exacting world, performing my small, vital function in its vast and inscrutable ecology of maintenance and decay.
