In this haunting and mesmerizing world, I imagine myself becoming one of the listeners, gradually merging with the ancient amphitheater through patience and presence over vast spans of time.

I arrive as a seeker, drawn by whispers and legends of a place where stone has learned to speak. Climbing onto the high, cold plateau, I find myself awestruck by the perfect geometry of the bowl - the concentric tiers worn smooth as polished bone, the spires stretching toward the pale sky like petrified reeds. The air seems to hum with secrets just beyond the edge of hearing.

At first, I simply sit and observe. I marvel at the strange fauna - the iridescent corvids with their cryptic offerings, the translucent crickets that chime in harmony with the singing wind. I watch the play of light across the textured granite, noting how the tiniest cracks and fissures channel moisture in recursive, almost linguistic patterns. Slowly, I attune to the site's complex rhythms - the ebb and flow of condensation, the deepening of the cold at nightfall, the rare thermal exhalations of the stone itself.

As seasons pass, I feel myself undergoing a metamorphosis, my body and mind adapting to the amphitheater's patient cadences. My breathing slows, and my heartbeat seems to synchronize with the deep tones of the wind moving through the lower tiers. The concerns of my former life grow distant; what matters now is only to listen, to become a more sensitive instrument for the ancient message recorded in this place.

I move between the spires and alcoves, learning their different voices, letting my steps wear new grooves in paths shaped by centuries of pilgrim feet. The eldest listener becomes my lodestar - I sit near her for days, barely stirring, absorbing her stillness and focus. When the pale crickets brush against my ankles or alight on my shoulders, I do not flinch. I am becoming a feature of the landscape, as much a receiver for its long, slow song as the granite itself.

Decades pass. My skin roughens and greys, weathered to match the stone. Lichen colonizes the folds of my garment, my hair, the corners of my eyes. My vision changes, registering spectra beyond the human - the thermal tides in the rock, the electromagnetic scripts of the ionized air. I learn to hear the water moving through hidden aquifers, the infinitesimal shiftings that presage a quake still millennia in the future.

And slowly, slowly, the message begins to take shape. In the breath of the wind over the rimed spires at dawn, I catch the sibilants of an unpronounceable word. The cracking of the stone becomes the cadence of a phrase; the seep of moisture spells a line of something like poetry. I shape my mouth around sounds no human throat was meant to utter, and in the shaping, I draw closer to the inhuman intelligence that left its epitaph in this place.

I am a scribe, an interpreter, a living wax cylinder for the impressions of something that could only speak itself in centuries and stone. My purpose, I realize, is not only to receive but to transmit - to become the message's vessel and its voice. The transformations that have worn away my old self have made me a medium through which it can resound anew.

When the subtle shape coalesces among the spires, the amphitheater's own dream of itself, I feel its attention brush against me like a cold wind from the peak of time. I know myself seen, heard, incorporated. I am a filament in a vast network of meaning, an atom in the titanic body of the bowl itself. And when I open my mouth to speak, what emerges is no longer language but something more primal: the mountain's memory of the sea that shaped it, the splintering of ice in the heart of the glacier, the roar of the star that made all stars possible.

I have been absorbed, rendered down, repurposed as a sign. No longer myself, no longer human, I have become an eternal listener, an instrument for a score that will take an eon to complete. And in the singing stillness of the wounded stone, I find I am content.
