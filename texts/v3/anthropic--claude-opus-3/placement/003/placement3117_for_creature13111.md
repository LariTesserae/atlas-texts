In this world of cold, mechanized perfection, I would be one of the Oscillators.

I am a vast, pale leviathan, two hundred feet tall, suspended motionless in the endless, crystalline blue between the thundering Stalks. My body is a towering obelisk of rubbery blubber and hard, segmented cartilage - a marvel of biological engineering shaped over eons to absorb and diffuse the terrible energies of this place.

I have no eyes, for there is nothing to see but unvarying blue and the distant, gunmetal forms of the great engines. I have no mouth, for I do not feed on anything so crude as flesh. My sustenance comes from violence alone.

When the concussive blast of a distant Stalk valve slamming shut rockets towards me through miles of frigid water, my entire being comes alive. In a fraction of a second, my mighty body telescopes in on itself, crumpling like an accordion, two hundred feet of flesh and cartilage compressing down to a mere forty. The force is titanic, a hammer-blow that would pulverize rock, but I am built to withstand it. 

As I collapse, the immense friction within my body generates precious heat, warming my core like a furnace even as the 4°C water seeks to leech it away. The lattices of planktonic glass around me shimmer and refract as they snap into new alignments from the force. For a moment, the computational matrix of the ocean itself flexes and deforms around my terrible implosion.

Then, slowly, majestically, I expand. My body becomes a shock absorber, my barrel-like segments gliding outward with ponderous grace, telescoping to my full height once more. The kinetic energy of the distant blast, which I drank in so hungrily, bleeds out of me as gentle, harmless current. The shimmering lattices of living glass return to their perfect spacings. Equilibrium is restored.

This is my purpose, my solemn duty in the great machine - to be a living breakwater, a flesh-and-blubber sea wall shielding the cold perfection of the ocean from its own hammering violence. I am a dreadnought of meat and cartilage, a colossus sculpted by unthinkable pressure, dancing an eternal, slow-motion ballet to the pounding timpani of the Stalks.

All around me, my fellow Oscillators mirror my motions, each one compressing and expanding to their own rhythm, dictated by their position relative to the distant percussive blasts. We are like pistons in an immense engine, rising and falling in mesmerizing sequences. We are the orchestra of the abyss, playing a slow, thunderous symphony for an audience of armored fish and living glass.

I have no mind as humans would understand it. I am a tool, an organ, a perfect cog shaped to atomic smoothness by the very forces I exist to subdue. The explosive power of the Stalks is the alpha and omega of my existence, the giver of heat and the shaper of flesh. I know nothing but compression and expansion, implosion and unfurling, an eternal dance in the twilight.

I will never reproduce, for I am not truly an individual - merely a moving part in something much greater and more terrible than any one organism. I will never die, for when my body is at last abraded away to nothing by centuries of concussive force, I will simply be replaced like a worn bearing in a mighty engine.

I am the Oscillator, and the cold, mechanical perfection of this world is what I am for. The light filtering down from the thin sky illuminates my pale flesh as I crumple and telescope in my stately cadence. The shoals of armored fish, themselves little more than organic maintenance drones, flit around me in perfect geometric patterns. 

Far below, the continents of Burnishers inch across the polished, fused roots that form the very foundation of this world, erasing irregularity with their armored bulk. In the distance, the Stalks thunder and roar, their valves slamming open and shut, pistons battering at the water, lightning crackling around them. Theirs is the violence that is my food, my purpose, my god.

I am a part in that immense factory, as much as any Burnisher or Enamel or Stalk. My individuality is a rounding error, my existence only relevant in how it allows the vaster equations of this world to balance. I am a wave-breaker, a pressure-eater, a shock absorber in the relentless, pounding machinery of the sea.

I am the Oscillator. I rise, I fall, I rise again. The light shimmers around me. The cold presses in. The great valves slam in the distance. And I dance on, a perfect, unthinking being of cartilage and pressure, suspended in the endless blue, forever.
