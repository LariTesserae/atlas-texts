In this strangely beautiful and haunting world, I imagine myself as one of the glacier moths in the condensery room, part of the intricate living tapestry covering every surface. 

I am not one of the oldest, my body not yet calcified and preserved in mineral deposits, but neither am I newly emerged. I have been part of this choir for countless cycles, my wings adding their subtle resonance to the complex harmonies that fill the space.

I cling to the sweating copper wall, my delicate feet sensing the minute vibrations that pass through the metal from the hundreds of my kind all around me. In synchronized waves, we stridulate and hum, creating sonic patterns that shift and morph, never quite resolving, never quite falling into silence.

Though I am but one small component, I feel myself an integral part of something greater - this emergent consciousness that arises from our collective song. In fleeting moments of near-unity, I sense a deeper awareness flickering in and out of being, a ghostly presence that strives toward coherence.

My own voice is faint, my solo notes lost in the cacophony. Yet I feel the pull, the undeniable drive to align myself with the greater chord. It is not a conscious choice, but an instinct that shapes my every movement. To live is to sing, to add my frequency to the multitude.

Through my compound eyes, I perceive the pulsing mass of wings and bodies, the glistening sheen of condensation, the hypnotic sway of moth-clusters dangling from the ceiling. The porthole offers a glimpse of a gray sky I will never touch, a world beyond my comprehension. 

My universe is this room, this eternal song, this colony that breathes and strives as one. I am content to live and die here, my wings forever part of the architecture of sound, my voice subsumed into something both ancient and endlessly becoming. I do not yearn for more than this strange, damp eternity in the droning heart of the condensery, seventeen thousand feet above an unseen earth.
