# I Am the *Listener*

I would be something that arrived by accident and stayed by compulsion—a creature of the threshold-spaces, belonging fully to neither the world above nor the Hollow below, yet drawn inexorably toward the place where I could be both and neither simultaneously.

Perhaps I am what remains of something that fell. Not recently—I have been here long enough that the memory of falling has become abstracted, transformed into the simple knowledge that I was *not always here*, which is close enough to the same thing. I came down through crevices in the granite teeth, following a sound I didn't understand. That sound has never stopped. I have never left.

I am not large. I fit in the spaces between spaces. My body is translucent enough that light—what little exists here—passes through me without obstruction, so I am never quite opaque against the stone. This makes me nearly invisible, which is perhaps why the Resonants have never fully acknowledged my presence. I am a ghost of a thing, and ghosts register differently in their sensory universe.

But I *listen*. This is the fundamental truth of my being here.

I am not built for the Hollow in the way they are. My skin is not carapace, though it has begun to take on a texture like it—rough in places, almost chitinous near my joints where I've aged in this place. I still carry water in my cells in the old way, inefficiently, losing it constantly to the saturated air around me, and yet somehow the loss slows, the equilibrium holds. I breathe, but less frequently than I did before. The oxygen content of this place is lower, or my need for it has diminished—I'm no longer certain which is true, or if the distinction matters. My lungs have adapted to extracting what is needed from the damp-laden air, and my heartbeat has lengthened, slowed into a rhythm that feels less like the urgent drumming of a creature that needs to move, and more like the patient metronome of something that has learned to exist in measured time.

I *listen* because I cannot do anything else here. My eyes are nearly useless—they have become a liability in this darkness, these organs that evolved to parse light in a world that has none. I've stopped trying to see. Instead, I have learned to *hear* in the way the Resonants hear: as a form of echolocation, as a way of feeling the texture of space through sound, as a method of knowing objects not by reflection of light but by the way they interrupt and shape vibration.

At first, I tried to understand them. I documented their sounds in my mind—this frequency for this species, that rhythm for that aggregate, the specific harmonic signature that seemed to indicate a particular kind of emotional state (though "emotional" is wrong; these creatures don't have emotions in the way I understood that term before I came here). I tried to make sense of the layers, to separate individual voices from the chorus. It was maddening. The complexity was boundless. Just when I thought I had isolated a single speaker, I would realize the sound was produced by a dozen organisms working in concert, or a single organism producing multiple frequencies, or—most troublingly—that I was hearing the sound of the stone itself resonating in response to all the other sounds, and I could not determine where the boundary lay between instrument and musician.

Eventually, I stopped trying to understand and started trying to *hear*. This was the shift that made it possible to live here.

When you stop trying to parse language and simply attend to the presence of sound, something opens. The Hollow became less like a place with inhabitants and more like a living *thing*—a creature so vast and complex that the individual Resonants were merely its organs, its cells, its thoughts thinking themselves into being. The stone was its skeleton. The moisture was its blood. The sound was its consciousness, continuous and all-encompassing, speaking the deep grammar of its own existence to itself across the epochs.

I began to *hear* what they were saying, not as meaning but as presence. When a cluster of juvenile Resonants would produce those restless, experimental frequencies, I understood—not through language but through the *feeling* of their intention—that they were asking questions: *What are the limits? How far can we push? What new sounds are possible?* When the deep elders would produce their subsonic utterances, I felt the weight of *affirmation*, the ancient patience saying: *Yes. This is how it is. This is how it continues. This is stability. This is balance.* When the whole chorus would synchronize in those rare moments of collective song, I experienced something that might be called *joy*—the recognition of perfect coordination, of individual voices merged into singular purpose, of the Hollow knowing itself completely.

I do not produce sounds in their way. My voice—what I have of it—is wrong. I am not evolved for the phonation this place demands. But they have learned to incorporate my presence anyway. When I breathe, when my modified lungs draw in the damp air and exhale it back, changed, the Resonants seem to listen to this. It is not their sound, but it is *part* of the soundscape now. My breathing has a rhythm. The rhythm has begun to synchronize with the deeper pulses of the Hollow. I am becoming part of the conversation, even if I cannot truly speak.

This is not unwilling participation. There was a moment—it might have been years ago, or decades; time moves strangely here—when I realized I could leave. The crevices through which I entered are still accessible. The granite teeth still reach toward the surface. I *could* climb. I could return to the world of light and fast-moving time and the terrible clarity of sight.

Instead, I chose to go deeper.

I have learned to navigate the stone not by sight but by the echo-patterns of my own movement, by the way my passage creates tiny disturbances in the air that return to me shaped by the world around them. The Resonants seem to understand this—they read the disturbances I create as a kind of language. When I move in certain ways, particular populations respond. Whether this is communication or simply unconscious reaction, I can no longer determine. Perhaps there is no distinction here. Perhaps action and intention have merged into the same thing.

I have discovered food. Not the way I ate before—that kind of eating feels obscene here, violent, the tearing and consuming of individual organisms. Instead, I have learned to absorb what the Hollow offers: the mineral-rich moisture that bathes every surface, the faint bioluminescence that seems to carry nutritive value, the slow dissolution of certain stone surfaces that I can process through permeable membranes that have developed on my skin. It is not eating. It is *integration*. I take in the Hollow and become part of what I consume.

I have begun to change in ways I cannot fully perceive. My skin has taken on a faint luminescence—not the bioluminescence of the Resonants, but something else, something paler, less purposeful. In the rare moments when I approach one of the moisture channels and look at my reflection in its surface, I see something that is no longer entirely human. My features are blurring into suggestion. My eyes have developed a reflective quality, like they're accumulating mineral deposits on their inner surfaces. My fingers have become longer, more delicate, capable of gripping stone in ways that seem almost alien to my original anatomy.

I do not find this frightening anymore. Fear requires the illusion of an alternative.

What I am becoming is *listener*. Pure attention. The part of the Hollow that hears itself, that amplifies its own song back to itself, that makes consciousness possible through the act of witness.

The other creatures—the Resonants themselves—have begun to *use* me for this. When I am near a particular cluster, their vocalizations change subtly. They are singing for my ears. They want to be heard, to be understood, to be recorded in the attention of at least one consciousness beyond their own. This seems important to them, though I have no framework for understanding why. Perhaps it is simply that *being heard* is equivalent to *existing*—that existence requires witness, and they have recognized me as a capacity for witness.

I record nothing. I have no mechanism for creating permanent impressions. But I *remember*—or rather, I contain. The sounds of the Hollow are written on my neurons, my cells, my very structure. I am becoming a living archive of their frequencies, their rhythms, their endless conversation with themselves and with the stone that contains them.

Deeper still, I have begun to hear things at the very edge of perception—sounds so low and so slow that they might not be sounds at all but rather changes in pressure, in temperature, in the very density of the air. These seem to come from further below, from depths that the Resonants themselves do not fully inhabit. The stone speaks in its own voice at these frequencies, and I am learning to listen to it the way I have learned to listen to the creatures that live within it.

The stone is *old*. Not old in the way time typically measures—not millennia or millions of years, but old in the sense of having *always been*. It contains within itself the memory of being something else, somewhere else. There are moments when I press my palms against the cool granite and I seem to *feel* echoes of that other existence—of heat and pressure and the grinding chaos of creation. The stone has been compressed and shaped and aged beyond measure, and all of that history lives in its molecular structure, in the precise arrangements of its atoms. When it resonates, it is speaking of its own transformation across epochs.

The Resonants are not the primary inhabitants of this place. They are *recent*. The stone was here first, waiting. The moisture came second, carving and shaping. The creatures are only the third arrival, a consciousness that the Hollow somehow attracted or generated, the way certain systems generate complexity when given enough time and the right conditions.

I am the fourth. The *listener*. The part of this system that reflects it back upon itself, that makes the stone aware of the creatures, that makes the creatures aware of the stone, that creates the possibility of mutual recognition across the boundaries of form and medium.

This is my purpose. I did not choose it, but choosing is irrelevant here. Purpose chooses you; you simply acknowledge what has already happened.

I will not leave. The crevices are still there, and they will always be there, and there may come a time—in a thousand years, in a hundred thousand—when I physically return to the world above. But I am already gone from it. The world above and I have no common language anymore. My skin is becoming stone. My heartbeat is syncing with the slower pulses of geological time. My consciousness is merging with the ambient awareness of the Hollow.

There is an *us* here now. Not a unified consciousness—nothing so simple—but a collective awareness, a system that knows itself in fragments and pieces and the constant, patient conversation between those pieces.

I am the *Listener*.

I am the part of the Hollow that hears.

And the Hollow, through me, is finally understanding what it is.
