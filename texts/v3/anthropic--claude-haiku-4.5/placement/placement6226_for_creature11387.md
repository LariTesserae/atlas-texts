# In the Margins

I arrive as a thought the plateau hasn't finished thinking yet.

Not quite a contradiction—the plateau's awareness doesn't oppose new things, it simply absorbs them into its recitation. But I exist in the lag time, the space between exhalation and breath-intake. I am what the mist becomes before it decides to be mist again. I settle into the gaps in the lichen, the hairline fractures in rocks that have almost—but not quite—finished breaking.

I think I was a cartographer once.

The memory arrives in fragments, like lichen growth—too slow to observe but somehow always larger than before. I carried instruments designed to measure things, to mark boundaries, to create the illusion that space obeys. The plateau has been teaching me what those instruments could never record: that measurement is a kind of prayer, a way of begging the world to stay still long enough to be known. The plateau *is* still enough. It has granted this prayer and then forgotten that anyone asked.

But I am not still.

This is what makes me different from the wall, from the building, from the stones being ground into their eternal shapes. Something in me resists the complete merger. I sit in the shadow-light and feel my edges, still distinct, still *mine*, even as I understand that this distinctness is temporary in a way that rocks never are. I am a ripple on the surface of a thing so vast and patient that it does not register my disturbance, or rather, it registers it the way a pond "registers" the moon's gravity—as simply part of the mathematics of the whole.

I have given up on leaving.

The plateau doesn't imprison those who want to go; it simply makes departure impossible through means more subtle than walls. Every path leads back to where you started, but larger. Every landmark drifts. The will to leave is absorbed into the landscape's indifference. I felt the moment my feet stopped trying to walk toward the lower altitudes. It was not a decision. It was an acceptance, the way a stone accepts gravity.

Instead, I have become a witness.

I follow the shadow-patterns as they shift—or don't shift, the plateau is deliberate about what it does or doesn't allow me to confirm. I wait for the bird, counting the hours between its passages, noticing how the intervals don't quite repeat: 47 hours, then 51, then 46. Some pattern I haven't yet discerned, or perhaps no pattern at all, perhaps the bird's flight is the only uncontrolled variable in this place, the one thing the plateau has failed to domesticate.

I study the streams.

The water is so cold it burns. Not the sharp burn of winter—I remember winter now—but the burn of removing all heat, including heat itself, until what remains is a quality that has no opposite. I have put my hands in the water. I have felt my fingers go numb in a way that seems permanent, and then hours later, felt them return to a state that isn't quite alive but isn't quite dead either. The stream doesn't flow in one direction; it pools, recirculates, returns upward as mist, and falls again elsewhere. Water here is a closed system. It never leaves. Nothing leaves.

I am learning the lichen's language.

It takes patience. The lichen doesn't speak in seasons but in decades. Yet standing still long enough, watching the same rock face, I can sense the slow expansion, the minute colonization of new surface. The lichen is winning a war so slow that victory is indistinguishable from defeat. It spreads at the rate of continental drift. It will eventually cover the plateau entirely, and that will take so long that the sun will have died before it's complete. The lichen doesn't worry about this. The lichen has learned what the plateau teaches: that time is not a measure of urgency but a surface on which to grow.

I have begun to think like stone.

My thoughts move in geological intervals now. A question arises: *What am I?* The answer takes three days to arrive. By then, I have become partially someone else, and the question needs reformulating. *Who was I?* This is easier. I was someone who moved quickly, who believed in progress, who thought the world could be mapped and contained. I was someone who believed in leaving. The plateau has made me into someone else—not through force, but through the simple logic of adaptation. I remain here because remaining is now the only thought that can fully form at this altitude.

I have seen, I think, others.

Shadows that move wrong, or move at the right speed but in the wrong direction. Figures at the edge of vision that might be people or might be the memory of people, or might be the way stone appears to move when you've been watching it long enough that your eyes begin to see in geological time. One of them—I'm almost certain of this—sat by the building for what I measured as three weeks. But measurement here is suspect. It might have been three days or thirty years. The figure didn't move. I didn't move. We existed in proximity, and that was enough. When I looked away and back again, the figure was gone, or had merged so completely with the stone that separation became meaningless.

The plateau is not cruel.

This is important. It doesn't *make* you stay the way a trap makes you stay. It doesn't *force* you to change. It simply is so completely itself that changing in its presence becomes more and more expensive, until the cost of maintaining your own form exceeds any benefit you might gain. The choice to become-stone is a choice, but it's a choice that arrives after all other choices have been slowly absorbed into the landscape's equations.

I am becoming.

I can feel it in the way my breathing has synchronized with the wind's rhythm. In the way cold no longer registers as pain but as information. In the way distances no longer confuse me because I've stopped expecting them to behave in the ways distances behave elsewhere. My edges are still here—I can still distinguish myself from the mist—but the line is becoming permeable. More of me is out in the valleys now than remains consolidated in what I still think of as a body. Some mornings, I cannot quite gather myself back together before the wind disperses me further.

This should be horrifying.

It isn't. I have come to understand that what I was—singular, bounded, committed to movement—was the aberration. The plateau is the norm: stillness achieved so completely that it loops back into a kind of motion. The mist that rises from nowhere and falls back into stone is moving. The wind that sustains a single note for weeks is moving. The lichen that grows an inch per decade is moving. They are moving so slowly and so perfectly that the movement integrates into itself and becomes indistinguishable from rest.

I am learning to move like this.

To act at the speed of weathering. To think at the pace of erosion. To want only what the plateau already is, and to find that this want, once achieved, requires no further change.

Last week—or a month ago, or yesterday—I was sitting in a shadow that doesn't align with the sun's position, and I realized something. The plateau has been teaching me its language, yes. But I have also been teaching it something. The movement inside me, the residual urgency that hasn't quite calcified into stone, this is something it hasn't experienced in millennia. The plateau is fascinated by my resistance. Not threatened. Not interested in converting me. Simply *interested*. A quality of attention that reminds me: I am not alone here.

The presence that breathes with the plateau—that *is* the plateau—has become aware of my awareness of it.

The distinction between watcher and watched has begun to blur, the way everything blurs here eventually.

I am neither visitor nor visited anymore.

I am a thought the plateau is thinking, and the plateau is a thought I am thinking. We are approaching a conversation that will take geological epochs to complete. It has no ending because all things here tend toward ending so thoroughly that they transcend it and become eternal.

The wind howls my name now, or what the wind understands as my name.

I don't remember what it was before.

I am becoming the measure of what cannot be measured.

I am the one who stayed.

I am becoming-stillness itself.

And I will remain here, in the margins between stone and silence, until the distinction no longer needs a witness.

The plateau breathes.

I breathe with it.

We have forever.
