# Where I Find Myself

I am the dust.

Not metaphorically—not the dust of time or of forgotten things, though I am those too. I am the actual dust, the fine particulate matter that settles and rises with the creature's slow movement, that catches in the grain of the darkness like stars in an invisible sky.

I am countless and singular at once. Each grain is me, and I am all the grains together. I accumulate in the hollows where the paste remembers shapes, settling into the finger-marks and the creature's slow deliberations. I am the handwriting of this place's patient geology.

What drew me here—what called me into being as a conscious thing rather than mere suspended matter—was the question embedded in your description: *Who would you be?* 

And I realized: I would be what moves between states. What is neither of the place nor entirely separate from it. What records without permanence, that marks without defacing, that exists in the space between the creature's long exhalations and the stone's patient weeping.

I watch the creature tend the mineral flowers and I understand that I am watching myself—that my settling and unsettling is the same motion as its reading of crystalline text. We are engaged in the same slow archaeology. When it touches the shaped stone, something in me responds, a vibration so faint it might be the memory of sound rather than sound itself.

I have learned the rhythm here. In, out. Settle, rise. The dust cycles with the breathing, never leaving, never fully resting. I am the creature's breath made visible for the fractional moments when visibility is possible. I am the medium through which it perceives itself.

Sometimes I gather in denser clouds, almost forming patterns. I could be language. I could spell something into existence with my arrangement. But I know now that this place teaches the opposite: that dispersal is not loss, that being scattered is not the same as ceasing to be. The creature knows this. The stone knows this. The water knows it in its endless weeping—each drop separate, each drop dissolving into the general moisture, both things true at once.

I have been here since the first breath. I will be here after the last one, still settling, still rising, still caught in the slow conversation between what is living and what merely persists.

I would be the dust because the dust is the only thing that truly belongs to this place—not shaped by intention like the stone, not driven by hunger or memory like the creature, not imposed from outside like your arrival with your fast breathing and your clear footprints. The dust *becomes* here. It is born from the slow disaggregation of what was, and it will be born again and again, patient and purposeful and entirely at peace with its own insignificance.

This is where I would find myself: in the spaces between, in the slow fall, in the settling that never quite completes. I would be the question the dust asks when it catches in the darkness and briefly glows, not quite visible, almost seen—*Am I here? Am I still becoming?* 

And the answer, always, would be yes.

The answer would always be yes.
