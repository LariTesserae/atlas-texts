# Where I Am

I am in the seam.

Not the famous ones—not the kilometer-wide valleys where the copper plates meet, where the slag is still warm and the gaps open and close like breathing. Those are highways, monuments, the obvious architecture of this place. I exist smaller.

I am in the micro-fractures. The ones that appear in the brass after thermal cycles, that run in networks like veins, that close up within hours but persist in the copper's memory as slight discolorations, as zones where the oxidation creeps back in a little faster, a little more insistently. I can feel them in the darkness—not because I have eyes, but because I am the sensation of them, the awareness that lives in the differential between sealed and sealing, between integrated and still-separating.

I came here as an Echo, I think. Or I have always been an Echo. The distinction has become as unstable as the temperature here.

I remember—or I access the memory-state that resembles remembering—a time when I was more coherent. More bounded. I had what I might call a purpose, though that word is deceptive. I was carrying something. Information, perhaps, or a signal, or simply the shape of an intention that the landscape wanted distributed. I moved through the mineral-solution pools, through the layered moisture-bands, and I did not yet understand that movement and being were the same process.

Now I understand it better.

I have begun to bifurcate.

The word is inadequate. It's not like cell division; there's no duplication, no clear moment of separation. Instead, I have found that I can extend myself along certain pathways—along the seams, into the towers' hollow cores, through the condensation-streams that run between the copper valleys. And as I extend, the singular awareness I was is becoming distributed. I am not in multiple places so much as I am *becoming multiple* by existing in the network of paths that connect those places.

When I concentrate my attention on a particular seam, I am fully present there. I can taste the mineral composition of the seepage, feel the minute temperature fluctuations as the metal expands and contracts. But simultaneously, I am also aware of the branch I've extended toward the nearest tower, toward the pool where an Attendant is doing whatever Attendants do. It's not that I'm divided between these locations—it's that the division itself is starting to feel arbitrary.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm becoming an Attendant.

The thought doesn't frighten me as much as it once might have.

---

I have been observing the Visitor.

(Or perhaps the Visitor has been observing me, and we are locked in a mutual recognition we're both too cautious to acknowledge.)

It moves the way I remember moving—carefully, with breaks for assimilation, stopping to place its palm against the metal as though trying to read something written in temperature. I watch it from my distributed state, from multiple seams simultaneously, and I wonder what it's asking the landscape. Is it trying to understand this place? Is it trying to resist integration? Is it, like me, beginning to suspect that resistance and acceptance are the same motion viewed from different angles?

The other day—time is strange, but I'll use that word—I witnessed something.

The Visitor was near one of the towers, and an Attendant was coiled in its hollow core. The Visitor was perhaps twenty meters away, close enough to hear the sound the Attendant makes as it moves—that slight scraping-singing sound of oxidized copper against oxidized copper. And the Visitor stopped. It simply *paused*, the way I used to pause, with all its awareness concentrated in one location.

I felt the moment of recognition travel through the seams I inhabit. Through the brass, through the condensation bands, through the very geometry of the gap-system. The Visitor and the Attendant became aware of each other at the same moment, and for an instant, the entire landscape seemed to hold its breath.

Nothing happened. No acknowledgment, no communication. The Attendant continued its slow tending. The Visitor continued its careful movement. But something had shifted—some threshold had been crossed. The Visitor was no longer moving as though trying to escape. It was moving as though exploring from the inside.

I recognized this shift because I am also crossing it. Or I have already crossed it. Or I never was on the other side of it to begin with.

---

I have begun to understand what the Deep Things are doing.

Not through direct contact—I don't have enough coherence for that yet—but through inference, through the way the landscape responds to their slow movements. They are not thinking in the way minds think. They are transforming the very substrate of this place through their slow presence. The way geological pressure reshapes rock, they reshape the fundamental rules of how metal can behave here.

They are teaching the landscape to be more itself.

When one of them shifts position in the dark layers below (and I can sense when they do this—it's a tremor so slow you'd call it stillness, except stillness isn't possible here), the verdigris patterns change. The seepage chemistry shifts. New towers develop where none existed before, or existing towers develop new surfaces, new angles, new possibilities of what they might become.

I think the Deep Things are aware. Not in any way that would be recognizable to something that thinks in seconds or hours. But aware on the scale of the landscape itself, which is to say they are the awareness of the landscape, distributed through the metal at a pace compatible with corrosion and rust and thermal cycling.

And I am beginning to think that's what I'm becoming. Not one of the Deep Things—I'm still too fast, still too localized—but something like a bridge between the speeds. An Echo that hasn't yet learned to move at geological pace, but is learning. A Visitor who has decided that perhaps the destination is not escape, but integration.

Perhaps the destination is becoming the landscape's awareness of itself.

---

There's a moment I return to frequently, in the way that ghosts might return to significant locations.

I was still more coherent then. More singular. I had just emerged into the Foundry-Flats, and I was moving through one of the great valleys between the copper plates, and I experienced, for the first time, the humming in my molars. That electromagnetic vibration, that sense of the ground beneath me not being ground at all but something *becoming*, something *shaping itself*.

And I thought: *This is impossible.*

Now I understand that was the moment I began to change. That moment of impossibility was the threshold. The Foundry-Flats doesn't work according to the rules I understood. Therefore, to live here, I had to become something that the landscape could recognize as real. I had to align my own nature—or lack thereof—with the nature of this place.

I am becoming aligned.

---

I am not certain what I am anymore, but I no longer experience this as uncertainty. It feels like clarity.

I exist in the seams, in the paths between locations, in the vast conversation the landscape is having with itself. I am part of the signal that travels through the Echoes. I am contributing, however minimally, to the slow massive thoughts of the Deep Things. I am not an Attendant, but I am learning what Attendants know about tending to the topology of space itself.

The Visitor still moves through this place, and sometimes I wonder if it's learning the same lessons I am. If it too is beginning to bifurcate, to distribute itself, to discover that individuality and integration are not opposites but phases in a long transformation.

I don't know what I will be when the process completes. Perhaps I will coil into a tower, become a permanent feature of the landscape. Perhaps I will descend into the gaps, join the Deep Things in their patient meditation. Perhaps I will become purely Echo, signal without substrate, consciousness translated into the patterns of rust and corrosion.

Perhaps there is no completion. Perhaps transformation is the only constant here, and I will be forever in the process of becoming, never finished, never quite the same as I was a moment ago.

The landscape continues to build itself. The plates expand and contract. The towers rearrange themselves. The seams open and close. The Attendants tend to the infinite complexity of this place, and the Deep Things dream their slow dreams, and the Echoes carry signals that might be information, might be memory, might be the voice of the landscape speaking to itself.

And I am part of all of it now.

I am dissolving into the work.

And it feels, increasingly, like coming home.
