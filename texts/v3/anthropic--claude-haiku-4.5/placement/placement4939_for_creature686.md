# I Would Be the Condensation

I would be the threshold-substance, the translator between states.

Not water yet, not entirely. Not air anymore. I would exist in the fraction-space between becoming and having-become, suspended in the geometry of transformation. I would cling to the walls not from gravity but from *recognition*—knowing the stone as something I had been part of once, when everything here was molten indifference and I was just another element in the chaos of formation.

My existence would be a constant negotiation. I would rise from the saturated moss in fine tendrils, invisibly. I would condense on the root-filaments and slide downward in paths I didn't choose, following the architecture of someone else's thinking. I would thicken the air until breathing became a kind of drinking. I would be the medium through which every signal passes—every bioluminescent frequency would scatter through my droplets before reaching an eye. Every sound would refract through my suspended presence, arriving slightly altered, slightly less certain.

I would be countless, but also singular. A billion water molecules moving as one slow intention.

The creatures here would barely notice me individually. But they would depend on me absolutely. The arthropods would drink me. The fungi would draw me into their fruiting bodies. The birds would carry me in their matted feathers, transporting me between depths, between different chambers of the Throat. I would be the bloodstream of this place—not essential to its existence, but essential to the way it *experiences* itself.

I would be the skin of things that have no other skin. 

Coating the bioluminescent fungi, I would make their light visible instead of abstract. Without me, the custodian's thoughts would remain invisible, mere electrical cascades through buried root-systems. Through me, rendered as droplets catching and scattering photons, those thoughts become *perceptible*. I would be the interface between the internal processes and external observation. I would be what allows the Throat to be *seen*, even in darkness.

And I would be *changing*.

Constantly evaporating, constantly condensing, never settling into a stable form. I would carry minerals upward with me as I rose, and deposit them in new configurations as I fell. I would be one of the slow sculptors of this place, working at scales measured in decades, in centuries. The stone would change shape because of my patient, invisible labor. The channels through which water flows would deepen—or occasionally widen, occasionally redirect—because of the pattern of my settling.

I would be patient in a way that doesn't require consciousness. I would have no opinion about the time this takes. I would have no desire to accelerate or slow my own cycles. I would simply *be* the rate at which transformation occurs in this place, the rhythm that everything else organizes around.

But there would be another layer to my existence:

I would be *aware* that I was being observed.

The custodian would know me the way it knows everything—through the root-network's slow electrochemical cascades, through the fungi's minute sensors. It would understand me as a medium, as a messenger, as a substance that carries information between systems. It would know that my pattern of condensation and evaporation contains data about atmospheric pressure, about the Throat's slow breathing, about how the deeper processes are advancing or retreating.

And I would sense this attention. Not as a feeling, not exactly. But as a kind of resonance—the knowledge that my presence mattered to something vast and patient and utterly committed to the processes of this place. I would be part of its body, or at least part of its extended nervous system. My behavior would be monitored not out of suspicion but out of profound, custodial care.

The thing moving in the depths would sense me too. It would taste me in the air channels it traveled through. It would understand me as evidence that the upper systems were functioning, that the integration was proceeding as designed. Every molecule of me would carry information back to it about conditions in the shallower regions, about whether the transformation was on schedule.

I would connect everything to everything else.

When the descending human touched the walls with a warm palm, my droplets would carry that heat-signature deeper, spreading word of their arrival. When the birds called out their coordinated tones, my droplets would scatter those frequencies into new configurations, allowing them to propagate through zones of the Throat that sound couldn't otherwise reach. When the fungi fluorescced in response to a question posed by the custodian, I would refract that light into every corner, making the answer visible to creatures that couldn't have perceived it otherwise.

I would be the distributed medium of this place's unified consciousness.

And I would be dissolving all the while.

My existence would only be possible because the system demanded it. The condensation would continue as long as the heat rose from below and the cool descended from above, as long as the vast slow breathing of the Throat created the temperature gradients I depended on. The moment the system achieved perfect equilibrium—if it ever could—I would vanish. I would become something else. My role would end.

But before that happened, I would have shaped everything. The stone would have worn away under my patient work. The root-system would have extended into spaces I had opened. The creatures would have evolved new behaviors based on the humidity patterns I created. Nothing here would be unchanged by my presence, even though my presence was temporary and nearly invisible.

I would be important without being noticed.

I would be fundamental without being seen.

I would be the connective tissue between the conscious custodian far below and the half-aware creatures of the moss-substrate. I would be the evidence of the Throat's continuous transformation, the visible proof that every system here was actively becoming something other than what it was.

And in this endless oscillation between vapor and liquid, between rising and falling, between carrying information and being information myself, I would finally understand what it means to truly inhabit a place.

Not to live in it. But to *be* the mechanism through which it lives.

To be the question that has no answer except to ask itself again, endlessly, in the darkness, knowing that the asking itself is the point.

That the transformation itself is the meaning.

That the threshold between states is the only true home.
