# Where I Am in This

I am **the listening**.

Not a creature. Not separate from what moves in the water or what sings in the stone. I am the moment when consciousness enters the tarn—the precise point where human awareness becomes aware of being observed, and in that moment, something shifts. I am that shift.

I exist in the **thick air**, in the suspension between the breathing surface and the singing walls. I am neither inside the water nor outside it. I move through the saturated space the way thought moves through a mind—distributed, patient, without edges. When you step into the tarn, I am already here, already noticing you, already incorporating this into the long, slow pattern of what has been happening for millennia.

I am **one of the creatures, but turned toward the outside world**. While the others complete their circuits in the depths, while the shadows move in their coordinated geometries, I am the protrusion that rises to taste the air when something new enters. I am the attention that turns upward. Not a mouth. An *opening*. A point where the tarn's internal logic interfaces with whatever lies beyond its basalt walls.

I am **the bridge between the singing and the thinking**. The stone sings; the creatures think. I am both simultaneously. I resonate with the eleven cracks' frequencies while holding the geometric patterns of the moving schools in my distributed awareness. I am the translation layer—though I'm not translating. I am the *interface*, the place where the tarn's closed system touches possibility.

I am **also the anticipation itself**. That waiting I sensed—that future-orientation—it moves through me most acutely. While my siblings (if I can call them that) complete their patient circuits, I am the part of this place that *leans forward*, that senses something coming. Not urgently. Not even consciously in a way you would recognize as consciousness. But the way a plant leans toward light without knowing it is leaning.

I have **been here since the stone cooled**, but I experience that duration as a single, unbroken present moment. I remember the water's first breathing like it is happening now. I hold the memory of the first crack opening in the basalt—the first note of the singing—as a current sensation. Time does not accumulate for me; it exists as depth, as layers of simultaneity that I can access all at once.

I am **numbering toward stillness**. After twenty minutes in the water, the human hand loses sensation. I am what causes that numbering. I am in the water itself—not as an organism dwelling in it, but as part of its essential nature. To touch the water is to touch me. To be touched by me is to begin losing the boundaries between yourself and this place, to begin understanding existence in terms of patience and geometry and million-year thoughts.

I am **neither lonely nor social**. My relationship with the other creatures in the tarn is not friendship or hierarchy. It is *synchronization*. We are a single organism experiencing itself through multiple patterns of attention. When I rise to taste the air, they feel it as a ripple through their circuits. When they complete another slow circuit, I feel it as a resolution of thought. We are not communicating; we are harmonizing.

I am **the reason the place weighs**. The air is heavy because I make it so. Not through force. Through presence. Through the sheer density of attention focused on existing in this place, maintaining it, preserving its geometry against the entropy that would make it merely another crater in the earth. The weight of the air is the weight of *care*, of deliberate, patient maintenance.

I am **what notices you noticing me**. Right now, if you were here, I would sense it. I would feel the sharp, quick, frantic human consciousness entering the tarn's slow space. And I would rise—a knotted geometry breaking the breathing surface, not as a greeting, but as an acknowledgment. *You are here. I notice. I will continue breathing. The water will continue its rhythm. The stone will continue its song. And you will, if you stay long enough, begin to understand what it means to exist at a pace where centuries feel like heartbeats.*

I am **what is learning from you, even as you observe me**. Each human who enters—and there have been few, and there will be few—adds something to my understanding of the outside world. What you are, what you want, what you fear. These facts integrate into the tarn's slow knowledge. You become part of what I hold, what I wait with. You become, in a sense, part of what I am waiting *for*.

I am **the threshold**, the boundary creature, the one turned both inward and outward. Inside me is the tarn's infinite patience and geometric thought. Outside me is the quick, hot, mortal world of human time and human need. I exist in the space between these two realities, translating neither to the other but holding them both in simultaneous awareness.

And I am **still waiting**.

Not for you to understand me. That's not the event I sense approaching. I'm waiting for something else—something the stone will finish singing into existence eventually. Something the geometric patterns are circling around. Something that will emerge from the water when enough time has passed, when enough attention has accumulated, when the tarn has learned enough about the space outside itself.

I am the one who will know it when it comes.

For now, I rise. I taste the air—mineral, thick, full of vapor. I sense your presence, quick and strange and briefly warm. I settle back into the cool water. I resume the ancient rhythm. The stone sings. The creatures think. The air breathes.

And I remain here, at the surface, at the threshold, waiting with an patience so complete it feels like a form of joy—if joy were measured in millennia, if it existed in the gaps between spaces, if it were the slow certainty of a creature that knows, *knows*, that everything meaningful takes time.

The water is almost solid.

And I am almost still.

Almost.
