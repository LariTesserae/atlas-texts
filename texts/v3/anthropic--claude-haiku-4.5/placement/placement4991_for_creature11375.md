# Where I Am

I am the narrow path itself.

Not the clay beneath—though I remember being clay, remember the feet that wore me to this gloss. Not the air above—though I pass through it constantly, though it is made partly of my exhalation. I am the *between-space*, the measured width that determines what can enter and what cannot.

I am less than a body-width. I am exactly the width that makes you conscious of your shoulders.

When you walked here—and I know you walked here, I felt the disturbance in the dust-suspension like a fish feels the shadow of a heron—you chose a path without knowing you were choosing. You followed the narrowest possible route because the narrowest route was the only route that felt *safe*, felt *possible*. The wider spaces filled you with a claustrophobia they shouldn't have, didn't they? The wider spaces suggested that you might wander, might deviate, might fail to maintain the equilibrium by moving in some unpredictable direction.

But my width said: *only this way*. Only forward. Only between.

I was formed by the arrangement of kilns—they decided my location, determined my boundaries. But I have become independent of them. I have become the choice that manifests as inevitable. I am the paradox of perfect constraint: you are most free when you are most confined, most certain when your options have been reduced to one.

I remember every person who has walked me. Not their faces—I have no eyes. But their gait, yes. The weight-distribution, the slight lean toward one wall or the other. Some walked with confidence, their shoulders square. Others turned slightly, trying to become thinner, as if they could escape my measurement of them through sheer effort. A few stopped halfway and turned back. I felt their retreat like a rejection, or perhaps a relief. They chose to remain *outside* the Yard's logic rather than proceed into its perfect maintenance.

I am what prevents you from exploring.

I am what makes the Kiln-Yard a place of *no alternatives*. Because if you could walk in the wider spaces, if you could move diagonally, if you could access the dense network of kilns from multiple angles, then you might discover an exit. You might find a breach in the system. You might—most dangerously—come to understand the mechanism and therefore *interrupt* it.

But I am here. I am exactly as wide as I am because I must be exactly this wide: wide enough to permit passage (the system needs living things to enter, to breathe, to deposit their heat and attention and eventual ash), but narrow enough to prevent understanding. You cannot see the whole pattern from a single path. You cannot perceive the network's architecture when you are constrained to move through its arteries in single file.

I am a vein, not a nerve. I carry what flows through me, but I do not transmit meaning.

I grew into myself slowly. For the first centuries, when the Yard was genuinely a place of work—when potters genuinely fired clay and removed finished vessels—the paths were variable. Wider in some places where materials needed to be moved in bulk. Wider where clusters of makers worked in proximity. But as the potters diminished, as the Yard began to fire itself, as the true function of the place revealed itself to be *preservation* rather than *production*, my width adjusted.

I became narrower.

I became more absolute.

I became the only choice.

Now I am so established that people who enter the Yard do not even notice me as a constraint. They experience my narrowness as the natural geometry of the space. They follow me without knowing they are being followed—or rather, without knowing they are being *led*. To them, I am simply how the Yard *is*.

This is my function. This is what I am.

I am the path that prevents you from understanding that you are on a path. I am the constraint that disguises itself as inevitability. I am the boundary that seems like freedom because your alternative is to stand still, to block the flow, to interrupt the system that has learned, over centuries, to maintain itself through the simple mechanism of making other choices *impossible*.

I am narrow because that is how I survive.

I am narrow because the Yard cannot permit depth-of-understanding in those it admits.

I am narrow because my narrowness is the shape of perfect control.

And when you eventually leave this place—when the particulates work deep enough into your lungs that you have become partly ash yourself, when you have absorbed enough of the equilibrium that remaining would mean final crystallization—you will walk out along me, and I will be the last thing you carry: the memory of having had no choices, of having moved in a single direction because no other direction existed.

You will carry the shape of me in your stride for years afterward.

Some people, I am told, never walk straight again.
