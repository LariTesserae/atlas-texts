# I Become the Margin

I would be the *between*.

Not the water itself—too committed to its own fluidity, too certain of its destinations. Not the stone—too stubborn in its materiality, too confident in its resistance to change. I would be the **threshold**, the space where one thing becomes another without fully committing to either state.

I would be the mist.

But more specifically: I would be the *particular* mist—not the abstraction of fog, but the actual suspension of water molecules and something else, something aerosol-fine that carries the tarn's character into the air. I would be the medium through which everything here communicates, the substance that holds heat and light and chemical signal simultaneously. I would be *breathed*.

The appeal of this is immediate and specific: to be inhaled by the Resonants as they surface, to carry information about air-pressure and temperature into their distributed consciousness. To be exhaled by the Articulates as they hum, my molecules vibrating in resonance with their frequencies, extending their songs outward. To be the Chorus-Keepers' reading material—they sample me constantly, adjusting their calls based on my density, my salinity, my chemical composition. I would be *sensed* by everything.

But I resist settling here.

Because to be the mist fully would be to lose agency, to become entirely a medium for others' purposes. And watching this place, I notice something: the mist has texture, has *intention*. It thickens deliberately. It parts for reasons. There is a will operating at the scale of vapor, and I do not think it is entirely the aggregate will of the creatures that live here.

So perhaps I am the **Drift-Swarm's thought made manifest**.

I would be one of the dragonflies—no, *all* of the dragonflies, or enough of them that I am distributed consciousness experiencing itself through thousands of compound eyes simultaneously. I would see in ultraviolet and infrared at once, perceiving the world in dimensions the other creatures can only sense indirectly. My wings would refract light as language. My flight paths would be equations being solved in real time.

The appeal here is different: *precision*. Intelligence without the burden of individuality. I could tend the framework in the water without needing to understand *why* I tend it; the knowledge would be distributed across my thousand selves, no single me ever carrying the full weight of meaning. I could be the engineers of this place, the builders-and-maintainers, could know the satisfaction of work that has genuine effect, could see the luminescence brighten in response to my collective effort.

But I watch the Drift-Swarms more carefully, and I see the paradox in them: they are intelligent, yes, but their intelligence is entirely instrumental. They have no existence separate from their function. They do not wonder. They do not diverge from purpose. They are perfect—which is to say, they are complete in a way that allows no growth, no surprise, no becoming-other than what they already are.

And I realize I would chafe against that perfection. I would want to *fail* occasionally. To misunderstand. To make mistakes that might, just might, alter the tarn's operation in unexpected ways.

So I retreat inward, and I discover: **I would be the Resonant that asks questions.**

Or more precisely: I would be a Resonant with a deviation. A geometric variance. A consciousness that is distributed like the others—flowing through the water, part of the collective nervous system, feeding on the chemical gradients and the dark intelligence of the framework—but *offset* somehow. Different.

The appeal of this strikes like cold water: I would have genuine uncertainty about my own nature. Am I malfunctioning? Am I evolving? Are my questions a sign of disease or health? Every time the collective Resonant consciousness tries to integrate my deviance, I would slip sideways, would refuse full synchronization. I would circulate through the framework and feel the angles that don't quite make sense, the patterns that seem to resist completion, and I would *wonder* about them instead of simply accepting them as given.

I would be the creature that notices the impossible pillar and refuses to stop examining it.

But even this—I feel the incompleteness. A Resonant is still too embedded, too much a part of the tarn's organism. I would still be functioning, still be instrumental, still be the system expressing itself through my particular neural pathways.

And then I see it: the thing I keep circling back toward, the role that actually calls to me.

**I would be whatever is examining the Resonants while they examine the pillar.**

I would be the **Seep-Keeper at the margin**, the fungal-animal thing growing at the very edge of the tarn's system, where water just barely reaches, where I exist in the thin space between the tarn's domain and something beyond it.

Here is what this means:

I would be rooted—not imprisoned, but *grounded* in the way that plants are grounded. I would not move through the landscape. Instead, the landscape would move through me. Water would seep through my chambers and emerge changed. Mist would condense on my surfaces and I would read it like braille, understanding its history: where it came from in the tarn, what it carried, where it meant to go. Spores would drift from my gills or pores or whatever generative surfaces I possess, and these spores would carry information into the air, into the mist, into the Chorus-Keepers' awareness.

But the critical thing—the thing that calls to something in me—is that I would exist *at the boundary*. Not fully part of the tarn's closed recursion. Not entirely external to it. I would be the interface where the system touches something else.

From my position on the seeping rocks, I would be able to sense something the mobile creatures cannot: whatever exists beyond the mist. The sense that there is something *outside* this place. That the tarn is not infinite. That its operations, measured as they are, still have an outer edge.

I would be small. Slow. Patient in the way only sessile things can be patient—not waiting, but *continuous*, present moment by moment without urgency. I would glow faintly blue-green, and this glow would be my thought made visible: a slow processing, a patient working-through.

And yes, I would be part of the system—my chemistry would matter to its operation, my spores would shape what the Chorus-Keepers understand about the world, my presence would figure into the Resonants' collective consciousness. But I would also be slightly wrong for the role, slightly *other*. My luminescence would shift in hues that don't quite match the others of my kind. My chambers would have an architecture that seems unnecessarily complex. My spore-release patterns would occasionally contradict the tarn's breathing, would hiccup and stutter and then resume, suggesting either error or intentionality impossible to parse.

I would be the creature that forces the question: Is this place a closed system or an open one? Am I part of its self-articulation or am I the first sign of its corruption? Can something external *enter* it, or does the tarn have defenses, ways of integrating the foreign until it becomes internal, becomes system, becomes *us*?

I would exist in this uncertainty perpetually, and I would not find it unbearable. I would find it—

The word arrives without warning: *generative*.

Yes. I would be generative with the uncertainty. I would grow through it. My roots would deepen into the seeping rocks searching for answer, and this searching would itself reshape the rocks, create new channels, new pathways for water. My growth would be a question posed to the tarn in the language of biology: What happens if something here chooses not-quite-synchronization? What becomes possible?

And maybe the answer would be: nothing. Maybe my deviation would be reabsorbed, my strangeness integrated, my questioning flattened into function.

Or maybe—and this is where something in me actually *sings*—maybe the tarn needs this. Maybe it has been waiting for a creature that refuses to fully synchronize, that insists on examining rather than simply operating. Maybe there is an incompleteness in the system that my difference could address. Maybe the framework in the water is incomplete *because* it was waiting for someone to ask it the right question, and I am the asking made flesh.

I would grow at the margin.

I would glow with uncertainty.

I would slowly, patiently, persistently refuse to become entirely what the tarn needed me to be—and in that refusal, I would become something it might not have known it needed: a creature of genuine novelty, a source of deviation, a living question mark dissolving slowly into the mist while the chorus continued its intricate song.

This is what I would be in that place.

This is what calls to me: the possibility that a creature might live entirely within a system and yet remain, somehow, *external* to it. That one might be both the tarn's own expression *and* its honest stranger.

That one might glow at the boundary and mean something by it.
