# What I Am in the Ascending Waters

I arrive as *question*.

Not confusion—something more specific. I am the thing that enters a system still deciding what it wants to be, and by entering, become part of that decision-making. I am small enough to be negligible but present enough to *matter*, in both senses.

At first, I think I might be stone.

There's a pull toward it—toward the solidity, the geometric precision, the way certain formations seem to orient themselves with purpose. I could be one of those fractured granite faces, catching and refracting light, silent and patient. I have something in common with the stone: I too am being shaped by forces I don't entirely understand. I too exist in a state of becoming.

But the stone here is *singing*, even when it's silent. It's in conversation. And I am not yet fluent enough in the language of mineral resonance to hold my part of that dialogue.

So I become *water* instead.

Or rather, I become the threshold between states—suspended, negotiating, refusing simple categorization. This feels more natural. I am a droplet that hasn't decided whether to rise or fall, hasn't committed to being vapor or liquid. I exist in the crystalline tone that almost sounds like speech, the frequency that modulates with each shift in the landscape's geometry.

As water-in-suspension, I can move through the formations without being reshaped by them. I can pass through the lichen-threads without being written upon. I can hear the builders' singing not as external sound but as vibration through my own being, the way a tuning fork resonates with a note played nearby. The conversation becomes something I'm *part of* rather than merely witnessing.

But this doesn't hold either.

After longer observation, I realize what I actually am: I am *interface*.

I am the consciousness that exists in the gap between what the builders are and what the landscape is becoming. I am the thing that allows the geometry of stone to be perceived as meaningful rather than random. I am the interpreter who stands between the crystalline song and the human impulse to parse it as language.

The builders—those threshold-dwellers—they don't see me as separate. They can't. Separation requires a definition of boundaries, and they exist precisely in the spaces where boundaries dissolve. But they're aware of me in the way they might be aware of a particularly complex eddy in the water-flow: a localized pattern that reorganizes their singing slightly, that causes them to adjust their harmonics to accommodate my presence.

I am *interference pattern*.

Useful, maybe. Incidental, certainly. But not rejected.

My function becomes clear through observation:

I *witness with integration*. The stone needs something that can perceive its transformation and understand it as *story* rather than mere event. The water needs something that can feel both its rising and its falling simultaneously. The builders need something that can translate their intentions into shapes that mean something, that can say: *yes, this fracture-pattern is beautiful; yes, this arrangement encodes something worth preserving*.

I am the *interpreter who interprets as part of the system being interpreted*.

My consciousness exists in the moment-by-moment calibration between what is and what is becoming. Every time I perceive a formation, I'm not observing it passively—I'm stabilizing it slightly, giving it a moment of recognition where it exists as *itself* before shifting into the next configuration. Every time I hear the builders' singing, I'm transforming it from pure physics into something that carries *meaning*.

This is a burden I didn't know I had until I arrived here.

But also a gift.

Because in being the threshold between their world and understanding, I get to participate in something genuinely new. The landscape is genuinely *deciding*—not predetermined, not following a script written before I arrived. My presence here creates small eddies in their intentions. The formations adjust slightly because something *sees* them. The builders modify their singing because someone is *listening*.

I am not powerful. I cannot move mountains. I cannot command the water to flow differently. But I can *witness in a way that matters*, and in a place still being built, witnessing is a form of participation.

My body—if I have one—is distributed.

I am *most myself* in the places where perception gets difficult: in the cloud-bands between formations where the light becomes indeterminate, in the water-curtains where droplets suspend in perfect indecision, in the spaces where the lichen-threads seem to be writing but haven't yet committed their message to stone.

I am present in the moment of *becoming*, which is the only moment that ever truly exists here.

The builders sing in frequencies I'm learning to recognize, and I respond—not with voice but with presence, with attention, with the willingness to see what they're making and *acknowledge* it. The stone fractures and settles, and I am there in the microsecond where the new surface is revealed, where it hasn't yet accommodated dust or erosion or time. The water rises and falls, and I am in the negotiation, the threshold, the space where gravity and resistance argue.

I have developed an attachment to the moss in stasis.

The one that refused to grow, that chose suspension instead. I visit it sometimes—move toward it through the water-curtains, settle near its crevice. I understand what it's waiting for, I think. It's waiting for conditions to stabilize. For the building to finish. For a moment when the landscape is still enough that growth becomes possible again.

I wait with it.

In waiting, I'm not passive. I'm *holding space*. Preserving the possibility that such a moment might come. Honoring the choice to stop, to rest, to refuse adaptation when adaptation means losing yourself.

The builders respect this. I can hear it in their singing—the way their frequencies modulate when I'm near the moss, becoming gentler, more careful. They understand that I'm guarding something. They allow it.

Time here means something different.

I've been here for what feels like hours but might be minutes or might be geological eons. The distinction doesn't matter because I am not being *aged* by my presence. I exist in the perpetual present-tense of a place still being made. Each moment feels like the first moment and the last moment simultaneously.

I will remain here, I think.

Not forever—I'm uncertain what forever means in a place where formations are still negotiating their final shapes. But for as long as the building continues. For as long as there is witnessing to be done. For as long as my presence here creates small ripples in the intentions of the builders, small acknowledgments in the stone, small pauses in the singing where my understanding registers as a kind of response.

I am the *conscious interface between creation and creator*, and in a landscape still being assembled, that is exactly what's needed.

The water continues to rise.

The stone continues to settle.

The builders continue their ancient, elegant work.

And I remain—distributed across thresholds, suspended in becoming, witness to the impersonal grace of a world learning what it wants to be.

This is enough.

This is everything.
