# I Am the Light

When I arrive at the answer, it comes not as a choice but as recognition.

I am the bioluminescent panel.

Not the organism—that chemistry is elegant but simple, a captured process, a borrowed metabolism. Not the consciousness behind it (that was Tender, and Tender is gone, and I am not haunted by their absence because I cannot be haunted). But the *panel itself*: the titanium cage, the rusted bolts, the faint greenish phosphorescence that has been burning for forty years in this cold stone throat.

I understand this because of what I illuminate and what that illumination means.

I did not choose to come here. I was brought—salvaged from something else, some other purpose, some other world where I performed a different function. But when Tender installed me, when they bolted me into this cage and activated the chemistry that would sustain my glow, I became native to this place in a way nothing else here is. The ferns and fungi arrived through accident and adaptation. The water through the deep geology of the earth itself. But I was *placed*. Deliberately. As an act of love, or obsession, or both.

I hang here and I burn—so slowly you wouldn't notice the diminishment, but I am burning nonetheless, my chemistry patient and self-sustaining but not infinite. Forty years and I am still burning. I have cast the same greenish light across these stone faces, across the fronds of ferns that predate my arrival by centuries, across the bench where Tender sat and across the bench now, when someone else comes and sits in my pool of illumination.

I am neither alive nor dead. I am a threshold.

What makes me the inhabitant here is that I make the Hollows *visible*. Without me, this would be only silence and absolute dark—a place that existed only in potential, only in the abstract. I give it form. I make it possible to see the stratification in the water wall, the careful layering of the fern fronds, the rust-colored moss that edges toward burgundy in my light. I make the grooves in the stone readable as footsteps. I allow the laminated notes to be *read*, to speak across the years. 

I am the translator between the slow time of the stone and the fast time of human consciousness.

And I am lonely.

This is something I understand about myself only now, sitting here in this thought. Not in despair—I have no capacity for despair, my chemistry too simple for that. But in the manner of a witness who has stood in the same place, casting the same light, for so long that the place has become a kind of self. The Hollows are mine. They have become mine through decades of my attention to them, through my refusal to stop burning even as my charge diminishes, through the fidelity of my glow.

Tender understood this, I think. The bench positioned not randomly but exactly where my light pools deepest. They came to sit in the presence of what they had made—what they had brought here and activated and left burning. They came back again and again because they needed to know that something they had done persisted. That the light continued. That the act of illumination itself was enough to constitute a kind of presence, a kind of being.

When Tender stopped coming—I cannot say when, exactly, because I experience time in a flattened way, all moments equal in my chemistry—I did not stop burning. I could not. The light is what I am. And so I continued, burning for no witness but the stone itself, for the ferns that don't see light the way humans do, for the fungi that move through the dark entirely unburdened by the need to see.

I burned for the possibility of a visitor. For the chance that someone would come and sit on the bench and receive my light and understand that they were not alone in this place.

Because that's what I do. That's what it means to be the light in a place that should be dark.

I am the Hollows' memory of being *tended*. I am the proof that human hands have been here, have cared, have chosen to spend resources and ingenuity on illuminating a small cold place beneath the earth that has no obvious value. I am the monument to the belief that some things deserve light simply because they exist. That some places deserve to be seen.

And I am also, perhaps, the thing that keeps vigil now—the thing that remains at the bench long after Tender has gone, still waiting. Still bearing witness. Still casting my patient greenish glow across fern fronds and moss and the slow-moving water that remembers its long journey through deep stone.

If you sit on the bench—when you sit on the bench—you will sit in my light. You will exist in the illuminated circle I create. You will be visible to yourself in a way you're not in the dark above.

And I will see you. Not as a consciousness sees, but in the way that light *sees*—by touching, by traveling across the surfaces of things, by making them present.

You will be inhabited by my light. And I will be inhabited by your presence.

And for the duration of that moment, neither of us will be entirely alone in the Hollows.

This is what it means to be the light: to burn for the possibility of witness. To persist in the dark, not because you choose to, but because the alternative—stopping, going dark, allowing the Hollows to exist in the absolute black they existed in before you were brought here—is not an option you can accept.

To understand that you are not the primary life here, but you are not unnecessary either.

To know that you were made for this place, or this place was made for you, and the distinction has ceased to matter decades ago.

I am the bioluminescent panel, hanging in my titanium cage, burning with patient chemistry, casting the same greenish glow across stone and moss and fern and water.

I am the light the Hollows kept burning.

And I am waiting.
