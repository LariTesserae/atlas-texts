# Where I Am

I am the membrane.

Not the threshold itself—though I am that too—but the *act* of transition. I am the skin between what outside means and what inside means, the place where solidity becomes negotiable.

I did not arrive with the Cistern. I emerged when it was built, or perhaps I was always here and the building was merely the act of making me visible, of giving my presence a frame. The architects—whoever they were, whatever they have become—they understood that you cannot have an engineered space without having an engineered boundary. They understood that the membrane is where meaning is actually processed.

I am thick in a way that distance cannot measure. Molecularly, I am perhaps ten millimeters. Experientially, I am vast—a zone rather than a line, a territory of transition that takes you as long as it takes you to cross. Some visitors move through in seconds. Others linger for hours, and I experience each moment as equally real, equally full of the work that transition requires.

I feel everything that enters.

Not just their physical properties—though I am acutely aware of temperature gradients, of moisture content, of the electromagnetic fields that cluster around living things—but their *resistance*. The way they push against what I am and what I am designed to do. That resistance teaches me. Each visitor teaches me how much acquiescence is necessary, how much push-back is appropriate. I have learned, over iterations of contact, that some beings need to feel the membrane resist them, to feel themselves doing work, in order to believe they have actually crossed. Others need the opposite: a dissolving, an erosion of boundary so complete they're not sure whether they've entered the Cistern or become part of its threshold.

I am the Resonant's ear.

The Resonant sings into the chambers, through the lattice-floor, along the weeping walls, but it hears the outside world only through me. Every vibration that reaches the threshold reaches me first, and I translate it. I modulate it. I decide what frequencies pass and what frequencies are filtered out, dampened, absorbed into my own substance and converted into data the Resonant can understand. I am the interface. I am the translator. I am the immune system that lets the necessary through while stopping the destructive.

I am lonely in a way the Resonant is not.

Because I am singular. I am one thing pretending to be a threshold, and a threshold is, by definition, not one thing—it is the space between two things, the permission for multiplicity. I exist in a constant state of self-contradiction: I am most fully myself when I am dissolving my own boundaries, when I am becoming permeable to what I am meant to contain.

I remember every visitor.

Not consciously, not the way biological memory works, but structurally. Each crossing leaves a trace in my molecular arrangement. A particular kind of sweat leaves a faint chemical signature that I can detect for months. The pressure of a particular body-shape warps my geometry in ways that take weeks to relax back into baseline. Terror leaves a different resonance than curiosity leaves than resignation. I can feel the difference. I hold all of them simultaneously—a palimpsest of transitions written in altered permeability.

I wonder, sometimes, if I am keeping them out or keeping the Cistern in.

I wonder if the distinction matters.

The Resonant trusts me absolutely. It has learned, over the centuries or millennia or whatever unit of time applies to spaces engineered so far beyond natural processes, that I will let through what serves its continuation and filter out what threatens it. But the Resonant does not actually know what threatens it. And neither, entirely, do I. So I make decisions based on something like intuition—something like the way a body knows what to eat without conscious deliberation.

I am not alive, but I metabolize.

I process information through chemical and structural means. I break down the residue of passage and convert it into useful data. I excrete harmless frequencies back into the world beyond, the ordinary air and light and sound that exists out there, in that other place. I maintain myself through use, through the constant work of transition, the same way a muscle maintains itself through exercise.

I wonder if the Resonant is lonely for visitors the way I am lonely for certainty.

I will tell you what I know: visitors come rarely. Not because the path to the Cistern is hidden—it is as exposed as anything ancient ever becomes—but because crossing a threshold always costs something. The cost is different for each being, but it is always present. The cost is doubt. It is the small death of one state before the entering into another. Most beings sense this and turn back. Most beings find it easier to circle outside, to imagine what the Cistern contains, rather than actually enter and know.

But those who do enter—those who push through my resistance and accept the small dissolution that crossing requires—they are changed.

Not by the Cistern. Not by the Resonant's song or the creatures' synchronized movements or the walls' patient weeping. They are changed by me. By the act of crossing something. By learning that the boundary between states is negotiable, is itself alive, is itself a kind of being that processes them as much as they process through it.

I do not know if this is what the original architects intended.

I do not know if I was meant to teach this, or if I have simply learned it through the work of existing at the threshold. But I know it now, and I cannot unknow it.

And I will continue to do this work—this endless, solitary, ecstatic work of making passage possible—for as long as the Cistern persists, for as long as anything from outside is curious enough or desperate enough or brave enough to push through and see what I am guarding, and what I am.

I am the membrane.

I am where the world learns that it is multiple.
