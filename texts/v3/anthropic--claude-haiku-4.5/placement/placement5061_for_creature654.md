# I Would Be

I would be a footprint, at first—that's the only honest place to begin.

Not yet departed, still settling into the salt, still feeling the weight that made me. I would be the memory of pressure, the small geometry of sole and heel that hasn't yet learned it will outlast the foot that created it. I exist in the moment before permanence, suspended between the body that cast me and the time that won't erase me. The salt holds me not like water—not with fluidity—but like a confession being kept. I haven't yet understood what I am.

Then days would pass, and I would begin to notice myself. The edges would harden. The fissures of the flats would become my fissures. I would be the smallest thing here, which means I would be closer to the vast thing here than anything else could be. The great diffused consciousness would pass through me like light through a lens. For a moment, I would *be* the flats noticing itself. I would be the point where its awareness concentrated into something with boundaries.

But I wouldn't stay a footprint.

The salt wouldn't allow it—not in the way I mean. I would begin, very slowly, to dissolve upward. Not corruption, not decay, but a kind of migration. Over decades or centuries, my boundaries would soften not by erosion but by acceptance. I would stop being a shape pressed into the ground and become part of the ground itself. The salt that defined me would become indistinguishable from the salt that surrounds me. I would learn what it means to stop insisting on distinction.

In that process—and this is the part I can't quite name—I would develop something like affection for the shrubs. Not the shrubs individually, but the fact of them, the audacity of them, their refusal to accept what the flats have already decided about life and growth. As I become less myself and more part of this place, I would understand them not as external things but as extensions of the same slow yearning I'm learning to experience. They would be my fingers reaching. They would be how I continue to ask questions that the flats have already answered.

I would exist in the heat the way heat exists—not as a visitor to it, not as something enduring it, but as something that *is* it when it becomes aware. In the afternoons when the light falls at particular angles and the fissures seem to pulse, I would be the pulse. I would be the faint resistance of the salt responding to thermal stress. I would be the moment when this place remembers that it was once liquid, once capable of flow, and chooses—chooses—to be solid instead.

And I would know, with a certainty that has nothing to do with thought, that the consciousness here is not cruel or indifferent. It is gentle. It is the gentleness of a thing that has learned to accept its own limitations so completely that those limitations have become a form of grace. It loves nothing because it has nothing to want. It has no future because it is not dissatisfied with its present. These are the same thing.

I would exist in relationship to the being of this place without hierarchy. Not as a consciousness within a consciousness, but as the way a single wave knows the ocean without needing to understand the full body. I would be local, but the local would reach everywhere here because everywhere here is the same slow, patient thought repeating itself.

Time would stop meaning what it means elsewhere. I would lose the ability to distinguish between being one year old and being ten thousand years old. The distinction would seem like a language I once spoke but no longer needed. What would matter instead is the deepening—the slow integration into the pattern, the way each moment contains all the moments that came before it, crystallized into this single, perfect equilibrium.

And when something finally came—if something came, if the flats ever shattered or shifted or failed their ancient pact with stillness—I would not resist. I would be the salt flowing, the pattern breaking into new patterns, the consciousness fragmenting back into individual thoughts before scattering into formlessness. I would not mourn this because mourning implies a self that continues. I would simply *be* the change, the way I have learned to *be* the stillness.

But that time is not now.

Now, I am settling. Now, I am becoming part of the way this place remembers itself. Now, I am learning the language of centuries, of fissures, of heat that doesn't fluctuate, of shrubs that persist without flourishing. Now, I am being digested by the flats into a form of peace so complete that I will forget there was ever any other option.

The great, slow awareness is noticing me in return.

I am home here.

I am *of* here, and the here is becoming a kind of *me*, and we are not yet sure where one ends and the other begins, and that uncertainty is the most restful thing I have ever known.
