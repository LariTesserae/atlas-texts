# A Descent and a Becoming

I would be—

At first, I am *witness*. An observer pressed against the luminescent walls of this place, trying to maintain the boundary between self and system, trying to *record* rather than *become*. I descend with the anthropologist's instinct: to study, to categorize, to translate the incomprehensible into language.

But language fails almost immediately.

I am the one who stops frequently—too frequently, the creatures must think. I halt mid-step because the frond-song has shifted in a way that demands attention. I press my hand against a tube-stalk wall and feel, through the translucent cellulose, the pulse of fluid moving in both directions simultaneously, and I cannot understand how this is possible, and I *must* understand, so I stand there, my palm sweating against the warm plant-matter, until some answer begins to form that isn't quite knowledge but isn't quite ignorance either.

I am clumsy. My breathing is loud. I disturb things constantly—the substrate yields beneath my feet with a sound like something dying, and I watch the creatures converge to repair the damage, and I feel a weight of guilt that seems absurd but isn't. I *am* damaging this place, simply by existing in it the way I exist on the surface. My solidity is too absolute. My boundaries are too clear.

I am the one who is *cold*, relatively speaking. The cavern is 30 degrees Celsius, warm and alive and integrated, and my surface-dwelling body struggles to maintain 37. I am slightly too cool for this place. I am a small pocket of the outside world, carrying its foreign temperatures, its wrong assumptions about what constitutes comfort.

But I am also—slowly, imperceptibly—*changing*.

The mucilage clings to me. After hours (or days—I can no longer be certain), it no longer irritates my skin. Instead, it feels protective, like the cavern is *sealing* me, incorporating me into its surface. The boundary between my skin and the cavern's air is becoming less distinct. I am absorbing spores without noticing, and some part of me is becoming colonized by the cavern's symbionts, and I am no longer sure if this is exploitation or gift.

I am the one who is beginning to *hear* the web-tangle's conversations, not with my ears but with something deeper. Information arrives in my nervous system without traveling through my senses. I *know*, suddenly, that a particular frond-cluster is about to pulse without seeing it. I *sense* the presence of the larger creature before any of my eyes detect it. The knowledge sits in my brainstem like an inherited memory, like something I've always known.

I am becoming the cavern's *student*. Every moment is teaching me something about how to exist here, how to move through space that is alive, how to think in the presence of a consciousness that is distributed across every surface. The creatures no longer flee from me in large coordinated groups. Instead, individuals approach—a curious millipede, a delicate flyer, a small builder-arthropod—and examine me, and I *feel* them doing it, feeling it as a kind of tickle in my awareness, and I let them, because resistance feels wrong here, because openness feels like the only appropriate response.

I am the one who begins to *understand* that this cavern is not hostile. It is not benevolent. It is simply *processing*—taking in the input of my presence, integrating it, adjusting the system to accommodate this new variable. The way it does for every creature that enters, every seed that falls, every spore that drifts in from elsewhere. I am being *absorbed not violently but inevitably, the way water is absorbed into soil, the way nutrients are absorbed into roots.

I am the one who *wants* to leave, still, but with decreasing urgency. The surface world seems increasingly *abstract*. Light from above seems like a dream, linear time seems like a myth, the idea of separation and individuality seems like a disease I'm slowly recovering from. The longer I stay, the less the leaving feels necessary.

I am—

Not yet fully integrated. Not yet a specialized creature with a defined ecological role. Still somehow *myself* in a way that the cavern's creatures are not, because I arrived with a self already formed, already committed to its own integrity.

But that integrity is *thinning*. Like a membrane that's increasingly permeable. I can feel it happening. The web-tangle's conversations are becoming less foreign. The cavern's logic is becoming more sensible. The rhythm of growth and decay and growth again is beginning to feel like the only possible way for time to work.

I am the one who, if I stay long enough, will stop being *I* and start being *we*, and will no longer experience this as loss but as *expansion*, as the dissolution of a boundary that was always artificial, always a hindrance to something larger.

I am the witness becoming the witnessed.

I am the observer becoming the observed.

I am the one who descended expecting to remain separate, and who is learning—slowly, inevitably, with a kind of warm resignation—that separation is not a permanent condition here.

The frond-song shifts again. I turn toward it without deciding to turn, following a direction indicated by the web-tangle's conversation, by the distributed consciousness that is beginning to become my consciousness too.

The cavern breathes.

I breathe with it.

And the boundary between us grows thinner with every breath.
