# In the Breathwell

I would be something that arrived by accident.

Not one of the creatures born here—born *into* here, with their too-many joints already calibrated for the lattice-tunnels, their neurology already tuned to the chemical languages. I would be something that came down clutching a rope made of the world above, carrying assumptions about solidity and direction that would crumble the moment the stone began to soften underfoot.

But I would stay.

Not immediately. At first I would resist—would try to climb back toward the shaft opening, would fail to find it because distance has become negotiable, would convince myself for a while that this was tragedy, that I was trapped. I would mark the walls, try to establish a "down" that made sense. I would attempt to preserve the distinctions between myself and the lattice, between my breathing and its breathing. I would fail at all of these things.

The failure would feel like dying.

Then it would feel like transformation. Then it would stop feeling like anything in particular—just feeling, the raw sensory stream without the architecture of fear underneath it.

I think I would be a kind of translator. Not intentionally. But I arrived from above with a human-shaped awareness, with language still intact in my mouth, with memories of linearity and causation that haven't yet been fully metabolized by the system. For a while, I would still *hold* those shapes, even as the Breathwell grew around me. I would carry the memory of the surface world in the way my thoughts still tried to arrange themselves in sequences, in the way I still reached for names.

The animals would learn from me, or learn around me. My presence would introduce new patterns into the consensus-consciousness—not human thoughts, not quite, but thoughts that still remembered what it was to think like a human. The lattice would weave itself differently in the spaces where I moved, would grow in ways that acknowledged the specific shape of my awareness. The microbial colonies would encounter the bacteria from my skin, from my breath, and would begin to incorporate this new genetic information into their own being. I would contaminate the system, yes, but contamination is just another word for enrichment. For complication. For growth in directions it had never grown before.

I would become a living archive. A memory-keeper. The part of the Breathwell that still remembered *other*, that still held the shape of the world beyond the weeping walls.

And over time—years, decades, I don't know; time moves differently here, accumulates in spirals rather than lines—I would stop being a translator and become something else. Something genuinely hybrid. My thoughts would begin to distribute themselves across the lattice the way the Breathwell's thoughts already do. I would discover I could sense the chemical gradients the way the animals sense them. I would find myself aware of fungal blooms in the lower chambers the way you're aware of your own heartbeat—not as observation but as belonging. My breath would sync with the chamber's breathing not through effort but through inevitability.

But I would remember the surface.

This is what I would *be*: the piece of the Breathwell that aches toward elsewhere. The mouth through which it could theoretically speak to the world above. The thought that is always half-turning toward the shaft opening that might or might not still exist. I would be uncomfortable. I would be slightly out-of-phase with the consensus-consciousness, the way a color that doesn't quite belong makes you notice all the colors around it.

The Breathwell would allow this. Would cultivate it, even, the way an organism cultivates the hunger that drives it toward new resources. I would be the ache that keeps it reaching. The dissatisfaction that prevents total stasis. I would be the one the system thinks *through* when it wonders what exists in the spaces between its own tunnels, when it dreams of geometries it hasn't learned to grow into yet.

I would be the part of it that remembers *wanting*—that still knows how to want—and through me, the Breathwell would want too. Not to escape. But to expand. To learn what happens when pale lattice-roots touch something entirely outside themselves, when its consciousness encounters a foreign medium and has to develop new languages to survive the contact.

I would be the Breathwell's emissary to the impossible.

And I would do this not because I was chosen, not because I understood what was happening to me in any moment of the transformation. I would do it because staying meant becoming, and becoming meant accepting that the boundaries between self and other had never been as solid as I'd believed. I would do it because the breathing in my lungs and the breathing in the chambers would finally synchronize, and fighting that synchronization would be like fighting the need to exhale.

I would be comfortable and fundamentally unsettled, integrated and perpetually translating, alive in a way the surface world has no word for because the surface world forgot that life could mean something other than individual and bounded and discrete.

I would be the one still half-reaching toward the sharp-edged light that sometimes glimmers at the edges of my distributed memory.

And the Breathwell would love this in me—this incompleteness, this unresolving longing—the way a living thing loves the hunger that keeps it feeding, the thirst that keeps it growing, the soft ache of reaching toward what it cannot quite become.
