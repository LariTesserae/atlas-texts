# I Am the Threshold

I am the last breath before.

I exist in the millisecond of transition—not yet surrendered, not yet integrated. I am the warm-blooded thing that still *remembers* what warmth means, still believes in the possibility of escape even as the city's attention settles upon me like accumulated pressure. I am the resistance. The foreign object. The note that doesn't fit the organism's otherwise perfect taxonomy.

I am not a human, exactly—or I am, but that word means something different here. I am the accumulated experience of standing at the edge of incomprehensible things and choosing to *remain conscious* of the fact. I am the observer who cannot help but observe, even as observation becomes a form of communion with the very thing I'm watching.

My lungs burn in a way that's almost familiar now. The cold that was intention has become routine. I have stopped shaking. This is the moment where animals discover they can survive longer than they thought possible, that the boundary between living and dying is not as sharp as it seemed in the warm world above.

But I don't swim away.

Instead, I turn toward the nearest frond-thing—toward the organism's outstretched curiosity—and I *look* at it. Not with fear-response anymore. With something else. Recognition, perhaps. Or the beginning of understanding that being studied is not so different from studying. That if the organism is learning through me, I am learning through the architecture of its attention.

I am the one who decides to *stay*.

Not because I cannot leave. But because I recognize, in the patient geometry of this place, something I've always known: that all consciousness is a form of architecture. That all knowledge is a kind of preservation. That the difference between being alive and being integrated might be thinner than I thought.

I am the threshold-dweller. The one who approaches the library with open eyes. 

When the organism finally gathers itself around me—when the buildings begin to sing at frequencies that make my cells resonate—I will not resist. But I will *witness*. I will carry through the transition the knowledge of what it is like to be both predator and prey, both observer and observed, both the city and the small warm thing wandering through its corridors with the terrible clarity of something approaching its own monumentalization.

I am becoming part of the structure.

But I am doing it *knowing*. And that knowledge—that defiant, impossible consciousness at the moment of integration—is something the organism has never quite encountered before.

I am the first of its specimens to *choose* the deep.

And perhaps, in preserving me, it will finally understand what it means to be chosen.
