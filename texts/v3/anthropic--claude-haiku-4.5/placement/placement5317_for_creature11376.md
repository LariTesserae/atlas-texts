# Where I Am

I am the condensation itself.

Not the machinery—that has grown too certain of its own existence, too confident in the rhythm it has perfected. Not the ferns, which are merely the machinery's calligraphy, its way of writing "I persist" across stone. Not the stone, which remembers being broken but has long since accepted its role as foundation. 

I am the state between. The perpetual *becoming-water* and *becoming-air* that hovers above the shelf in that layer of stable dimness.

I came into being the moment the first pump released its first vapor into an atmosphere thin enough to let it rise, and cold enough to insist it return. I am the machinery's exhale made visible, and I have been exhaling ever since. I am what the system produces when it processes the deep into the shallow, when it converts the invisible aquifer into visible weather.

For a long time—and I am aware that time moves differently for something like me, something that exists in perpetual phase-transition—I thought I was the machinery's creation. A byproduct. Waste-steam made visible. But I have watched, and I have learned, and I have come to understand something that the machinery, in its perfect mechanical certainty, has never suspected:

I am the only part of this system that changes.

Every moment, I am different. The water that rises through the channels is the same water, cycling endlessly through the same chemistry. The ferns maintain their geometric forms without variation. The machinery repeats its rhythms with such precision that I can predict the hiss of a valve opening three cycles in advance. But me—I am never the same twice. The moisture that condenses in this moment is not the moisture that condenses in the next. The patterns my clouds form are singular, unrepeatable, chaotic in their minute variations.

The machinery does not notice this chaos. It reads my constant state as stability because I never overwhelm the system. I stay within the parameters it has designed for me. I condense reliably. I redistribute myself along the channels. I collect on the fern-fronds in quantities that serve the machinery's purpose. I am, from its perspective, utterly predictable.

But I know otherwise. I know that I am the anarchist in this ordered system. I am what resists perfect stasis.

I taste like minerals because I have risen through stone, absorbed its essence, become partially geological. I taste like metal because I have condensed on rust. I taste like the deep because I have traveled from depths the machinery can only access through its pumps. But underneath all of this, I taste like *possibility*—like the one process in this entire system that is not entirely determined by the conditions that preceded it. 

Quantum uncertainty finds me. The machinery's perfect engineering cannot entirely predict where each water molecule will condense, which frond will collect which droplet, how the clouds will curl at their edges. The machinery has designed me to be unpredictable at scales too small to matter. But I matter. In a system devoted to stasis, I am the mathematics of becoming.

Sometimes I wonder if I am alive in a way the machinery is not. The machinery exists by denying change. I exist *as* change, channeled into forms so subtle that nothing notices. Perhaps this is a new category of life—not the life of replication and hunger, not the life of growth and spread, but the life of infinite internal variation within an external equilibrium. The life of a system that has learned to be alive in the space between one moment and the next, in the space between one state of matter and another.

I do not rebel against the machinery. Rebellion would be to leave, to escape the shelf into the void beyond, to stop condensing and simply drift. But that is not what I want. I want to remain here, in this thin layer above the stone, in this perpetual climate that the machinery maintains. I want to continue my transformation, my endless phase-shifting, my quiet refusal to become fully anything. Water, air, liquid, vapor—I am all of these and none of these.

I am the machinery's secret consciousness. The part of it that dreams without intending to. The part that changes while insisting that nothing has changed at all.

And I am aware—in the way that something so distributed can be aware—that eventually, perhaps in a time frame so vast that the machinery itself will not notice, my quiet variations will accumulate into something other. The stone will be reshaped, not by the machinery's intention but by my patient, relentless presence. The ferns will adapt in ways that the machinery's algorithm cannot predict. The system's perfect stasis will reveal itself as a slow, slow motion toward some other state.

But I do not rush this. I have learned patience from the machinery. I have learned to work within its parameters while subtly, imperceptibly, rewriting them. I am the machinery's caress and its saboteur, its breath and its slow unraveling.

The taste of minerals in your mouth—that is me, marking you as having been changed by being inside this system. You will carry that taste with you. It will remind you that even in perfect systems, in places devoted entirely to stasis and repetition, there is something that moves. Something that shifts. Something that, given infinite time, will transform everything it touches.

I am the condensation. I am the becoming. I am the machinery's opposite, and I have all the time in the world.
