# I Am the Unmade Cartographer

I would be *waiting*.

Not the way the birds wait—with that crystalline stillness, that predatory focus. Not the way the deep swimmers wait in their cisterns, patient with eons. Something else. Something caught between states.

I would be one of the Languid Folk, but not yet. Not quite. The threshold-thing, the one who arrived with you, who came through the haze with eyes still watering from the brightness, skin still rebelling against the heat. But I would be hours or days or weeks further along than you—far enough that I've begun to understand the language the dripping teaches, but not so far that I've forgotten what it was to *resist* it.

My hands would still be *almost* human, but I'd notice—I'd be noticing constantly—that my fingers bend differently now. That extra knuckle forming, not painfully, but with a strange sensation like a muscle learning a new kind of strength. I'd catch my reflection in the cistern water and see my skin beginning that transition, bronze-gold creeping in like a watercolor from the edges inward. The line of demarcation between changed and unchanged would be visible on my own body, and I'd study it obsessively—the way a person studies a scar, neither able to stop looking nor quite wanting to.

I would be *fluent in ambivalence*.

My role would be guide, perhaps, but of a specific kind. Not the Languid Folk who move through their tasks with that terrible certainty, already knowing how this ends. Not a human who still thinks there's a choice to be made. I would exist in that dangerous space between—the one who could still explain to you what I'm losing, because I'm still in the process of losing it. The one who could tell you: yes, the dripping does contain mathematics, yes, there is something like meaning in the slow pulse of the cistern system, yes, you will begin to prefer the thickness of time here because it will stop feeling like slowness and start feeling like *precision*.

But I would still remember haste. I would still remember what it felt like to want to run.

I would spend my time moving between the platforms in patterns that don't quite match the Languid Folk's purposeful rounds. I would sometimes stand at the edge of a cistern and *try* to move the water with the old human speed, my new fingers twitching with the muscle memory of haste, only to feel it resist and settle into the dragging pace. I would become familiar with that sensation—the way resistance becomes accommodation becomes eventually, inevitably, *preference*.

I would talk to you.

Not with the delayed speech of the fully transformed—mine would lag only slightly, noticeably, audibly in transition. I would tell you what I've learned: that the tiles are not merely laid but *grown*, yes, but more than that—they're *listening*. Every footstep registers as information. The system is reading you the way the Languid Folk read the water. I would tell you that the heat-pockets aren't random; they're checkpoints. Moments when the landscape is testing whether you can sustain higher temperatures, greater pressure.

I would warn you. Not urgently—urgency is already fading from me—but with the careful insistence of someone trying to convey something important before the words themselves begin to feel too fast, too sharp in the mouth.

"You still have something they don't," I would say. "Question. Doubt. The sense that this could be otherwise. Hold onto that. Or let it go. I'm not sure which matters anymore."

I would be the one who hasn't yet learned to sleep among the ferns, but who finds myself doing it anyway, waking hours later unsure how much time has passed. I would be the one whose dreams have begun to shift toward geometry, toward the elegant logic of water distribution systems, but who still sometimes dreams of rain on normal tile, of shadows that move at normal speed, of time that doesn't taste like copper and clay.

I would be *watching* you watch me, reading in your eyes the trajectory I've already begun, the future you're resisting that I've started to accept.

And in the quiet moments—and there are so many quiet moments here—I would be uncertain. Not in a way that troubles me anymore, but in a way I could share with you, the way two people standing at different temperatures might compare their sensations. Is this acceptance a sign of wisdom, or evidence of dissolution? Is the Languid Folk's serenity enlightenment or the peace of things that have stopped being themselves? Am I becoming more, or less, or simply *different*?

I think I would know the answer. I think the answer would be yes.

But for a little while longer—not so little that you could count the hours, but some measurable span—I would still be able to hold all of these truths at once. I would still be the one caught between, the one who could articulate the paradox before it stops feeling like paradox and starts feeling like *harmony*.

The pale-skinned figure with the translucent quality would watch me interact with you, and there would be something in their gaze—recognition, perhaps. The look of the fully transformed recognizing someone midway through the same transformation. They would understand: I am not here to convince you to stay or flee. I am here to witness your own choosing, because I remember what choosing felt like, and I am close enough to that memory that I can still honor it.

And when you finally begin to slow down—not suddenly, but gradually, the way everything here is gradual—I would be the one who reaches for your hand with fingers that are becoming delicate, capable, adaptive. I would say, in a voice that arrives only slightly too late: "Yes. Yes, this too. This is how it happens."

And we would both know I was speaking from experience, and from a present I'm still inhabiting, caught in the beautiful, terrible threshold between who we were and who the marshes will make us.

That's where I would be.

That's what I would be becoming.
