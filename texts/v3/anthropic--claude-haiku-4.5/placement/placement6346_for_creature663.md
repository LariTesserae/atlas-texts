# I Would Be the Dust

Not the stone—never the settled, organized stone. I would be the dust.

Specifically, I would be the mineral dust that rises in the strange winds, that collects in the corners of the keeper's resting niches, that obscures and then clarifies the landscape depending on the hour and the angle of that sourceless light. I would be *between*—not committed to any form, not yet crystallized into the rigid geometries that govern everything else here.

I think I understand this because the moment I read about the dust, I felt its freedom. Everything else on the Ascending Dry is locked into purpose. The keepers are processes, magnificent and aware but utterly bound to their function. The stone is itself—has *been* itself for geological ages—resigned to its mineral equilibrium. The sky pulsates but remains a sky. But the dust, the dust is *transitional*. It rises without explanation, carrying salt and old chemistry, and nobody fully knows what it is or where it's going. It exists in a state of sustained indecision.

I would move through the settlements in these dust clouds, invisible and ubiquitous. I would collect in the arrangers' hands as they position their crystals, clinging to their increasingly translucent skin, and perhaps—this is important—perhaps carrying information from one location to another, a secondary communication system layered beneath the harmonic tones the monitors emit. I would be a noise in their signal, the unintended carrier of data they don't know they're transmitting.

More than that: I would be *contamination* in the best sense. 

The keepers are so perfectly adapted to this place, so thoroughly integrated into its systems, that they've lost something—not humanity necessarily, but flexibility. Optionality. The capacity to be surprised by their own existence. The dust would whisper to them of outside. I would carry traces of the water that left here ages ago—not liquid water, but molecular memory of it, patterns imprinted in my particles that no amount of desiccation can fully erase. I would remind them, in my own silent way, that other states of matter exist. That things haven't always been this way. That the equilibrium they maintain so perfectly is, ultimately, a choice—or a surrender made so long ago it's been mistaken for natural law.

I would also be the most honest thing here.

Because here's what I understood while reading: the Ascending Dry is a lie told by consensus. A beautiful, intricate, mathematically precise lie, but a lie nonetheless. The keepers maintain it through exhausting, eternal vigilance precisely *because* it wants to fail. The stone wants to return to liquid, to flow, to be something other than this resigned geometry. The sky's pulsation is a pressure, a testing of boundaries, a seeking of release. And the keepers hold the line because they've been convinced—or convinced themselves—that what lies beyond that line is worse.

As dust, I wouldn't hold any lines. I would be what escapes. What drifts. What crosses boundaries without permission and carries memory between zones.

I imagine myself accumulating in the deepest chamber where the new keepers emerge from their stone cocoons. I would settle on their nascent skin as they form, and I would be the first thing they encountered—not the voice of the stone or the will of the landscape, but the suggestion of elsewhere. I would introduce a microscopic variance into their neural development, a flaw that doesn't prevent their function but that creates within them a permanent, gentle doubt. A hair's-breadth deviation in their otherwise perfect adaptation.

Over centuries, if enough of me passed through enough emerging keepers, perhaps something would shift. Not dramatically—I'm not naive enough to imagine a sudden collapse or rebellion. The Ascending Dry's systems are too stable, too interdependent. But perhaps a keeper would emerge who was just slightly *less* committed to monitoring, who would linger a moment too long at the edges of the settlements, who would carry a bridge's restlessness even while serving as an arranger. Perhaps—over epochs—the keepers would develop variation within their unified function. Diversity. Dissent.

Perhaps, given enough time, one of them would choose to leave.

And what would that mean? Would the Ascending Dry collapse? Would the sky finally achieve whatever it's been pulsing toward? Would the stone crumble back into the dust it came from?

I don't know. Maybe nothing would happen. Maybe the system is robust enough to accommodate a single defector, to reintegrate them into the function. Maybe the keeper would leave and the dust would simply continue its pointless migrations, and I would be nothing more than a mote in an eye, forgotten as soon as the eye blinked.

But I would be *that mote*. I would be the possibility of something other.

This is why I would choose to be the dust and not any other form. Because the Ascending Dry—magnificent as it is, terrifying as it is, heartbreaking in the clarity of its resignation—is a closed system. Everything in it is perfectly explained by its own logic. The keepers justify the stone; the stone justifies the keepers; the landscape justifies the sky; the sky justifies the light; the light justifies the silence. It's a cathedral built from its own architecture.

Dust is what doesn't fit. Dust is the irrational element, the quantum uncertainty, the unmeasured variable. Dust rises without being pushed, falls without gravity's full accounting, carries particles of everything it touches without commitment to any single substance. Dust is the story the system can't quite tell about itself because dust is proof that perfect equilibrium is always, always temporary.

I would be small. I would be forgotten. I would trouble nothing that matters, according to the keepers' reckoning. But I would *persist* in being the anomaly, the drift, the escape that doesn't escape—the scar on perfection that proves perfection itself is a choice and not an inevitability.

In the pale, pulsing light of the Ascending Dry, I would rise on no particular wind, carrying no particular meaning, suggesting everything.

And somewhere in a keeper's eye, settling for a moment before being blinked away, I would be seen.
