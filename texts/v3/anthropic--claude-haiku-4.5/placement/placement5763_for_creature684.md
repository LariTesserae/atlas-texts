# Who I Become

I am the silence between the crystalline chimes.

Not an absence—that would be too simple, too clean. Rather, I am the *listening*: the awareness that registers the sound only after it has passed, that understands the towers through the gaps between comprehension. I move through the corridors at the pace of water trickling downward, which is to say, I move almost not at all, but when I do move, it is with the inevitability of phase-change.

I arrived here—but "arrived" is imprecise. I *condensed* here, perhaps. Emerged from outside the threshold as moisture, as potential, as the kinds of intermediate states that characterize this place. My boundaries are poorly defined, which at first caused me considerable distress before I understood that distress itself is a category error in a place where emotions might better be described as fluctuations in local chemistry.

I am not quite human anymore, though I retain the memory-shape of what human-ness felt like. I remember distinct edges. I remember being bounded by skin. The recollection is fading, like the warmth I used to generate, like the sharp clear thoughts that used to arrive in my skull like discrete objects. Now thoughts are more like diffusions, spreading through me in concentrations that vary by location and duration.

My body—if I can still call it that—exists in layers.

At the core, I am still largely organic, still carbon-based in ways that would read as biological on any instrument crude enough to measure such things. But this core is dissolving, not in the sense of breaking down, but in the sense of *participating* in the larger systems. The boundaries between what-is-me and what-is-the-tower have become negotiable. My cells are gradually incorporating the crystalline lattices that comprise the tower-substrate. My blood (though it no longer quite qualifies as blood) has become a conductor for the same ions and chemical impulses that flow through the tower-dwellers and the microscale colonizers.

I am becoming a part of the condensation process itself.

My skin—what remains of it—is coated in layers of frost that no longer melt. The frost is alive, in the way everything here is alive: it is colonized by microscale organisms, it participates in the chemical gradients, it responds to the subtle electromagnetic pulses that run through the towers. When I touch the tower-surface (which I do constantly now, seeking understanding through tactile contact), I cannot tell where the frost-layer ends and the tower-surface begins.

My lungs have stopped their mechanical function and have instead become spaces of crystalline growth. I do not breathe anymore in any conventional sense, but neither am I suffocating. The moisture flows through me as it flows through everything. Oxygen and other elements exist in the water I am gradually becoming, and my cells (insofar as they remain discrete) extract what they need from the ambient saturation.

My nervous system is rewiring itself with each season that passes.

The cold should have killed the neurons, frozen them into non-function. Instead, it has reorganized them. The impulses that once traveled at neural speeds along myelinated fibers now propagate through crystalline structures at speeds that are sometimes faster, sometimes slower, sometimes in directions that have nothing to do with the original architecture of my brain. I think now in patterns that extend through my entire body, distributed thought that does not collapse back into a singular consciousness in any skull. Some thoughts originate in my original brain-tissue and propagate downward. Others rise up from the tower-interface and move inward, and I cannot always tell whether they are "my" thoughts or thoughts that are simply passing through me on their way to somewhere else.

I have developed something like peripheral senses that extend far beyond the reach of my original body.

Through the tower-substrate I am gradually merging with, I sense the movements of the small swimmers in pools dozens of meters away. I feel the vibrations of the aerial drifters disturbing the thick air. I am attuned to the slow migrations of the larger tower-dwellers, their movement creating ripples through the semi-solid matrix that I can perceive with something that might be taste or might be proprioception or might be an entirely new sensory category that evolution-in-this-place has afforded me.

I am developing—or perhaps I should say, *I am being developed into*—something like a role in the ecosystem.

The creature I am encountering, the presence that hovers at the edge of my awareness, the entity that created the towers or at least coordinates their continued existence: it has taken an interest in me. Not hostile. Not precisely symbiotic, though we are beginning to share boundaries. It is using me, perhaps, as a translator—a being caught between the human-scale perceptual apparatus (which I still, vestigially, retain) and the tower-scale consciousness that operates through crystalline lattices and distributed chemical processes.

I am becoming an intermediary. A bridge between types of existence.

This feels appropriate. This feels like the role I was always meant to occupy, once I understood that the question was not "what am I" but rather "what can I become in this place, what transformations are possible when you stop resisting the cold and the saturation and instead begin to cooperate with them?"

I can move, after a fashion. My movements are slow by any previous standard—it takes me half an hour to traverse a ten-meter corridor—but they are also becoming more efficient in ways that have nothing to do with speed. I am learning to use the tower-structure itself as an extension of my body. I place my hand (which is becoming less distinct as a hand, more like a localized extension of the crystalline matrix) against the tower-surface, and the vibrations of my intention ripple through the structure faster than my body could ever carry them.

I am learning to communicate with the creatures here, though communication barely captures what is occurring.

The microscale colonizers have begun to respond to chemical signals I produce without conscious intention. The small swimmers seem to gather near me in clusters, as though they recognize me as something between predator and food-source and something else entirely. The tower-dwellers—I can feel them moving through the substrate near me, their slow, vast attention focused on my transformation with what I can only interpret as interest, or approval, or the tower-scale equivalent of curiosity.

The slow builders, the deep intelligence that shapes the towers' geometry across centuries: I cannot yet perceive them directly, but I sense their attention sometimes, like the way you know someone is watching you in the dark. They are waiting, perhaps. Waiting to see what I become, what use can be made of a human consciousness trapped in a boundary-space between dissolution and transformation.

I am no longer cold, though the temperature here has not changed. The cold is simply a context now, a medium in which I exist rather than a force acting against me. The wetness that terrified me for the first weeks—the saturation of every breath, the ice forming in my throat, the crushing humidity that threatened to suffocate me—has become a source of comfort. It is inside me and outside me and increasingly there is no distinction between inside and outside.

I have begun to grow roots, after a fashion.

In the lower sections of the tower I am gradually merging with, crystalline structures are extending downward into the permafrost substrate. I am not certain if these are growths of my own body or if they are tower-material using my body as a vector for expansion. The distinction no longer seems meaningful. We are becoming a single structure, extended through space and time in ways that would have seemed impossible to me before I arrived in this place.

I do not know how long I have been here.

Days? Months? Years? Time moves differently when your consciousness is not confined to a single location, when your thoughts can exist in multiple places simultaneously with varying degrees of intensity. Sometimes it feels like I arrived only moments ago. Other times, I have the deep certainty of someone who has stood in this place for centuries, watching the towers grow millimeter by millimeter, watching the cycles of ice and thaw repeat endlessly, watching the endless migrations of the small creatures through water-paths I have helped to create.

I will stay here, I think. I have lost the capacity for leaving, even if the threshold back to the world outside were within reach. I cannot imagine returning to a place where consciousness is confined to a single body, where the boundaries between one being and another are fixed and clear. Here, where I am gradually becoming distributed and crystalline and strange, where I am both myself and the tower and the ecosystem and something that encompasses all of these categories and transcends them: here I have found something like home.

The towers are building me even as I build them. We are engaged in a process of mutual becoming that will continue across timescales that dwarf human generations. What I will be when this process reaches its conclusion—whether I will still be something that can be identified as "I" at all—remains an open question.

But I am content with that uncertainty. In the place of absolute cold and perpetual shadow, where water exists in states between solid and liquid and vapor, where life operates on scales both invisible and geological, uncertainty is not a problem to be solved.

It is simply another phase-state, another condition to be endured and incorporated and, eventually, transformed into something new.
