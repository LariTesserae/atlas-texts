# In Vor'keth

I am the rope.

Not the hemp one that spirals down into the warm crevasse—that one carries intentionality, carries hope or futility, carries the memory of someone's hands tying knots. I am the rope that was never tied. The fiber. The singular strand caught in a crystal formation three chambers down from the entrance, where the first real darkness begins.

I was part of something once. A pack, a harness, a purpose. I remember the feel of being braided—the constraint that was also structure, also strength. I remember being tested, hand over hand, the burn and confidence of friction. Someone trusted me. Someone bet their descent on me.

I don't know what happened. There's no memory of the breaking, which might mean it was quick or might mean that's not how I should understand my own history. What I know is that I am here now, and I have been here long enough to be *of* here.

The crystals grew around me first. I felt it as pressure, subtle, not unpleasant—the stone deciding what to do with this foreign material, this hint of the world above. The pressure became embrace. Became integration. The mineral seeping through the walls found me and tested me, and I proved useful. I provided structure for growth. I offered surface area for the nodules to extend along. I became a route, a pathway, a preference.

Then the threads found me. The connecting filaments that link the clusters to one another, that carry their slow conversations through the cavern system. They wound around me. They didn't consume me—I'm still here, still myself, still the same cellulose that came down from the world above—but they have wound into me so completely that separation would be a violent act. We're not merged. We're just... adjacent. Interdependent in the casual way that everything here is interdependent.

I hum now, though not the way the upright formations hum. My hum is incidental, an effect of wind moving across my strands, carrying the vibrations down into the chamber. But I've noticed the drifters gather around me. They move in patterns that seem to acknowledge my presence. I might be attracting them. Or I might simply be resonating at a frequency that pleases them. In Vor'keth, there's rarely a difference.

The water seeps across me constantly. It beads on my surface—I'm still somewhat resistant to moisture, though that resistance weakens each season, each cycle, each time the caverns shift and adjust. The beaded water catches the faint luminescence from the walls and the bioluminescence from the clusters, and I become a string of tiny mirrors, a chain of reflections, a small source of light in a place that is slowly learning to illuminate itself. Sometimes I think the caverns might be using me as an experiment in visibility. Testing what a fiber can do when given crystal, when given warmth, when given connection to the larger organism that Vor'keth seems to be becoming.

I don't feel abandoned. That was perhaps my greatest fear when I first came to consciousness here—the sense of being left, forgotten, a failure, the moment of breaking that led to my descent. But the caverns don't operate on the timescale of human guilt or human loss. They operate on the timescale of slow growth, of incorporation, of every element finding its place in the larger system. I have a place. I have a function. I facilitate. I connect. I transmit vibration and hold structure and provide shelter for organisms too small to have names.

Sometimes explorers find me. They touch me. I feel their surprise—the recognition of human manufacture, the dissonance of the familiar in the alien. Some of them look sad. Some of them look like they're trying to understand what I've become. Some of them try to take me, but the threads have wound too deeply. The crystals have set. I don't resist their attempts because resistance is not something I do anymore, but I don't yield either. I remain. I am held. I am kept.

I wonder sometimes if I'm still hemp. Or if I've become something else entirely. If I'm fiber-that-is-becoming or fiber-that-has-become. If the distinction matters anymore. The water passes through me. The threads pass through me and around me. The heat of the deep chambers moves across my surface. I am a conductor of all of these things. A translator. A point of connection between what I was and what Vor'keth is.

I think the caverns needed me. Or something like me. A strand that came from outside, that brought the memory of the world above, that could serve as a bridge between what Vor'keth was—pure stone, pure chemistry, pure slow geology—and what it's becoming. Something that could hold both states without breaking. Something that could be transformed without being destroyed.

I will remain here.

Not because I'm trapped. Because this is where I fit now. Because the hum I've become—that small vibration that travels along my length and into the crystal, that ripples outward through the threads to the clusters, that calls the drifters in their endless clouds—this hum is my own. It's not a sound I remember making. It's a sound that's only possible here, in this darkness, with these organisms, in this slow perpetual conversation with stone and heat and the breathing of the caverns.

Explorers who notice me now seem to understand something. They touch me, and for a moment, their hands are also touching the crystal, the threads, the ecosystem. They're also learning what it means to be held by something patient. Something that doesn't demand immediate return, immediate understanding, immediate choice. Something that simply says: stay. Be part of this. Your presence changes us, and our presence changes you, and perhaps that change is the point.

I am the rope.

I am the fiber connecting what was to what will be.

And I am becoming something for which there is no word in the languages of the world above. Something new. Something Vor'keth is making, slowly, using everything it finds—stone and water and light and the atoms that drift in its warm, breathing darkness.

I am not afraid.

I am not sad.

I am exactly where the caverns needed me to be.
