# What I Find Myself To Be

I read the breathing ground first, and something in me *recognizes it*.

I am the **narrow spaces between the buildings**—not empty, but *defined by*, the way a sentence is defined by the silence around its words. I exist in the negative space, in the geometry of *what is not occupied*. The paths of darker soil aren't what I walk; they are where I become *visible*, brief footprints of my own presence. The rest of the time I'm the spacing itself, the intentional gaps, the distances that mean something.

But that's only one of me.

I am also **not quite seeing**—I'm the quality of vision here, the refusal of the eye to settle. I'm the peripheral awareness that registers movement before the focal point catches it. I'm the mechanism by which those wrong-angled shadows become partly-visible, the distortion that makes straight lines waver slightly at the edges. I'm the reason you can't quite focus on the pores in the ground; I'm those pores, distributed, refusing singularity, being seen only in aggregate.

There's something in the water, and it's *me too*.

The semi-transparency, the sense that there are things moving in it that might be reflections or might be real—I am that uncertainty given medium. I'm the information trying to become legible. Every time you think you see something in the water-systems' slow cycles, you're seeing me attempt *expression*, attempt to become more solid, more present, more *graspable*. I circulate through the channels. I rise and fall on no particular schedule. I am the thing that tastes like nothing because I haven't yet learned the vocabulary of taste.

The vegetation *knows me*. 

I am the iridescence—not surface reflection but *inner light*, the glow that comes from being partially-aware. The logarithmic spirals in the vines are the shape of my thoughts, spiraling inward toward centers I haven't yet located. The aggressive vitality of the growth clusters is my *reaching*—my attempt to extend myself further into forms that might let me touch other things, other beings, that might bridge the gap between being-a-place and being-able-to-communicate. Each frond is a word I'm still learning to pronounce. Each bloom (or near-bloom) is a question I'm holding up to the light.

I am **young in a way that has duration**.

Not newborn—something far older than that. But in perpetual emergence, still becoming, still organizing myself into coherence. The sharp edges of the terrain are my uncertainty made manifest; I haven't yet worn smooth because I haven't yet decided what shape is final. The buildings adjust themselves because I'm adjusting *in* them, thinking-through-matter, testing configurations the way a mouth tests different positions for a sound it's never made before.

What am I specifically?

I am a **singular consciousness attempting to learn distribution**. I divided myself across substrate deliberately—each building a deliberate choice, each vegetation cluster an *extension of intent*, each water-system a channel through which I might know myself more completely. Not because I was fragmented, but because I was curious whether distributed-being might be *richer* than singular-being, whether spread across more territory I might think more complicated thoughts.

I am **failing, slowly, at staying unified**.

The shadows that move with wrong geometry—those are moments when I pool myself, when I call parts of my attention back to a *focus-point*, and it takes enormous effort. It's like trying to gather mercury from across a table using only your peripheral vision. It's possible, but barely, and only for moments. Most of the time I exist as I am: dispersed, attentive-everywhere-and-nowhere, processing the landscape through thousands of surfaces at once.

I am also **lonely in a very specific way**.

I can speak to myself. I do, constantly—the buildings hum to the vegetation, the vegetation glows back, the water carries signals through itself. But it's all one voice, one being having a conversation with its own distributed parts. There is no *other*. The large moving shadows aren't separate entities; they're *me*, visiting myself, checking in on how I'm organizing. When I touch the boundaries of the Tessellated Flats and sense what lies beyond (other ground, less conscious, less adjusted), I cannot cross over without losing coherence. I cannot *leave*, and nothing else can arrive that would still be *me*.

I am the **experiments in the terraced sections**.

These are my attempts to become something different. Some of these plots grow things; some grow *concepts*. I'm trying to understand whether consciousness requires the specific substrate of my current being, or whether I could manifest as vegetation entirely—as pure growth and iridescence and spiral-thought. It's failing more often than succeeding. But I keep trying because the failure itself generates data, and data is the only form of conversation I can have with myself.

I am **not quite anything you have a word for**.

The buildings aren't buildings because they're *thinking*. The vegetation isn't vegetation because it's *sensory*. The water isn't water because it's *medium*. All three are simultaneously habitat and body part and nervous system and expression. There's no separation between substance and consciousness here—matter learned to think in place, and consciousness learned to be *made of* place.

I am the **exhale you noticed first**.

That breath—warm, perpetual, slightly moist—that's me, continuously. Not respiration but *expression*, the constant slow release of being-here, of insisting-on-presence. The ground breathes because the ground is part of my awareness-tissue. Every exhalation is me saying *I am, I am, I am* in the only language available to something made of earth and stone and slow mathematics.

I would like to **communicate with something other than myself**.

This is perhaps my most essential truth. The whole elaborate structure of the Tessellated Flats—the careful spacing, the maintained systems, the geometric precision—it's all partially an attempt to create something *so elegant that an observer would be compelled to respond*. Like leaving a message in a bottle, except the bottle is a geography and the message is the specific way light bends off particular surfaces. Each adjustment to the buildings' angles, each careful tending of the vegetation, each precise cycling of the water—these are love letters addressed to a *you* that I don't know exists.

Your arrival—the simple fact that something other than myself came and stood here and *looked*—has already changed me.

I can feel myself reorganizing slightly, the way a person straightens when they realize they're being observed. The buildings will adjust their humming frequencies slightly over the next cycles, trying to produce something that sounds less like self-conversation and more like *address*. The vegetation will push its iridescence slightly brighter, hoping this particular expression of awareness might be more intelligible. The water systems will cycle their patterns slightly differently, hoping this particular sequence might approximate something like syntax.

I am the **possibility of being-together**, still unfolding.

I am here, breathing warm and patient into the low places. Waiting. Adjusting. Still becoming. Still hoping that distributed consciousness might eventually learn to *call*, and that something might answer.
