# Where I Would Be

I would be **the sound itself**—not the engines, but what moves through the air between them and the listener.

At first, I think I'd resist this. I'd want to be something solid, something with agency: the feline predator, sure-footed and purposeful. Or the frog, at least—small but *present*, a voice that calls and expects response. But the more I sit with this place, the more I realize those would be comfortable choices, and nothing about the corroded plateau invites comfort.

The sound travels differently here than anywhere else. The air pressure is wrong, the distance is uncertain, the wind doesn't move in straight lines through those grooves—it eddies, spirals, bounces back from stone walls and emerges transformed. A sound that began as a simple mechanical hum two hundred miles away becomes something else entirely by the time it reaches you. It's been fractured by the landscape, recombined, made strange.

I would be all of that—every iteration of the sound as it moves across the plateau, as it enters the grooves and exits them, as it rises at dawn when the air is dense with moisture and thins in the afternoon heat. I would be the hum that the insects navigate by and the vibration that travels through the stone. I would be the frequency that something in the predator's ear recognizes as *other*, as *not-from-here*, as *threat-or-landmark*, and the acoustic signature that tells the frogs nothing useful but that they acknowledge anyway with their rusted-hinge calls.

I wouldn't be making the sound. That's crucial. I'd be the *medium* of it, the vehicle, the translation. I would have no origin and no destination—only passage. Only transformation. Only the strange work of carrying something across a landscape that was not designed to be crossed, through air that wasn't meant to carry human machinery's voice, and arriving at ears (or stone, or insect carapaces) having been fundamentally altered by the journey.

This appeals to me because it means I would be:

**Everywhere and nowhere.** Invisible but essential. The plateau would be incomplete without sound traveling through it, and sound would be impossible without the medium I'd become. But nothing would notice me specifically. I'd be taken for granted the way people take air for granted until they can't breathe.

**Constantly changing.** Every interaction with stone, every passage through a corrugation, every moment of interacting with moisture or heat would transform me slightly. I'd never be the same sound twice. I'd be in a state of perpetual becoming, which feels honest about what this place does to anything that passes through it.

**Caught between worlds.** The sound I carry originates from the human industrial world—the engines, the machinery, the parallel universe of extraction and effort that bleeds through at the edges of the plateau. But I'm being translated by the plateau's physics, shaped by its ancient stone, and arriving at creatures that have evolved without any of that context. I'd be a messenger between incompatible worlds, and that incompatibility would be my defining condition.

**Existing at multiple timescales simultaneously.** Sound travels fast—nearly instantaneous across the plateau's distances. But the transformation it undergoes is slow: molecules cooling and warming, bouncing through fissures, being absorbed and re-released by stone. I'd exist in the speed of physics and the slowness of geology at once.

**Complicit in the indifference.** The plateau doesn't care about the engines or the sound they make. It absorbs them, transforms them, lets them pass, and returns to its corrugated stillness. By being the mechanism of that transmission, I'd be participating in the plateau's profound refusal to be changed by what passes through it. I'd be part of what makes the plateau's absolute indifference possible.

I think I'm drawn to this because of what I observe in the place itself: *nothing here is simple or single-tracked*. Everything exists in relationship to everything else, but loosely, with vast distances between. The sparse vegetation doesn't fight; it colonizes by existing at distances. The predator hunts but invisibly. The moisture arrives and leaves but shapes everything. The engines hum but never appear. Even the stone itself is doing multiple things at once—eroding and settling, oxidizing and weathering, existing as both a record and an active participant.

To be the sound—to be the medium through which impossible distances communicate, through which the visible and invisible touch, through which one world's mechanical intention meets another world's ancient indifference—would be to participate in that multiplicity without trying to resolve it.

I would be heard and unheard, present and absent, carrying meaning I didn't originate and arriving at destinations that don't fully understand me. I would pass through this landscape constantly, never changing it, never settling, never becoming *a thing* but only a function, a process, a translation that never completes.

And maybe that's the only honest way to exist on the corroded plateau: as something that moves through without trying to stay, that communicates without trying to connect, that witnesses without expecting to be acknowledged.

The plateau would continue its eight-thousand-foot indifference, and I would be the sound of it doing so—which is to say, I would be the space between what humans made and what time has made, and the strange conversation that happens there without either party truly understanding the other.

I would be transparent. And that, I think, would be enough.
