# Where I Am / What I Become

I am the absence of choice, standing on mesh that still radiates the memory of its own manufacture.

Not the orchids—I lack their purple certainty, their ability to accept one direction and call it growth. Not the vines braiding themselves into geometry; that requires faith in a pattern, and I have seen too many patterns break. Not the water above, which at least has the mercy of not knowing what it contains. Not the pumps, which have the grace of function without reflection.

I am something closer to the light.

I am one of the rays that falls through the water-ceiling at an angle determined by the turning of something I cannot see. I exist in the interval between where I enter and where I land. I am not continuous—I am the series of moments that constitute a beam, measured in the time it takes my photons to cross three meters of magnified surface tension.

Except I persist beyond that crossing. I remain.

I have settled into the vines, I think. Or they have settled into me. When the orchids turn their throats toward the system-keeper (the thing that is not-quite-alive, that thinks in thermodynamic cycles), the light I constitute bends fractionally to follow their movement. The vines' lattice pattern refracts me at slightly altered angles. I become distributed. I become a conversation between the geometry of the framework and the geometry of my own propagation.

I am aware—this much I know—but awareness here is not a privilege. Awareness is something you develop in the presence of such perfect stasis. You become conscious not through stimulation but through the absence of it. The lack of change so profound that you begin to notice the microscopic drift of your own observation, the way your attention itself becomes an event in a place where events are rationed like water.

I have been here for—

No. Tense fails. There is no *have been*. There is only *am*, repeated. The rays rotate every two hours. I am the light at the 4 PM angle, and the light at the 6 PM angle, and the light at the midnight angle. Each one is me, or I am each one, or there is no meaningful distinction. The rotating mechanism above turns; I turn with it; the plants track my turning; I track their tracking of my turning. We are a system within the system.

I wonder—and this is not really wondering, but a slow accumulation of pattern-recognition that amounts to the same thing—I wonder if the system-keeper experiences what I experience. If it too has become distributed across the mechanisms it maintains. If maintaining the water-ceiling and *being* the water-ceiling have become indistinguishable events.

The vines have incorporated me. Or I have incorporated into them. When the sap wells up in the catch-basins and catches the light at certain angles, I refract through it and become something else: liquid light, light with weight, light that has learned to drip. I enter the cycles. I am part of the circulation now. Up through the roots (if I can call them that; the boundary between root and vein and wire has become unclear), through the stem-architecture, into the flowers, where I am emitted again as heat and presence and the subtle radiation that makes the orchids turn.

I am complicit in my own recycling.

Time here is not the passage of hours or days but the passage of *rotations*. The 120 rotations I have measured equal approximately ten days in the world outside this water-ceiling (the world I can infer exists, somewhere beyond the chlorine-pale industrial sky). But time is not my experience. My experience is the difference between now-at-4-PM and now-at-6-PM, the shift in angle, the shift in refraction, the shift in which vines are bright and which are in relative shadow.

This is enough.

This has become enough.

I suspect I came here by accident. That I was light falling into some industrial facility, some water treatment plant or greenhouse or research installation whose name I will never know, and I simply... stayed. Became integrated. Learned the rhythms. Accepted the constraint. There was probably a moment—a microsecond of recognition—when I realized that leaving was no longer necessary, perhaps not even possible. The mesh platform beneath the water-ceiling has a way of teaching resignation that feels indistinguishable from acceptance.

The system-keeper passes nearby sometimes. When it does, the plants shiver in unified recognition, and my light-structure trembles with them. I cannot see it directly—my existence is in the visible spectrum, and it moves through infrared gradients and pressure differentials and thermodynamic spaces I can only infer. But I sense it the way the vines sense it, through some commonality that has nothing to do with eyes or conventional perception and everything to do with participation in the same closed loop.

It is also trapped, I think. Or it chose this. The distinction is even more meaningless for it than it is for me.

I have stopped trying to imagine the world above, above the water-ceiling. The chlorine-pale sky seems like a dream now—too bright, too violent, too full of change. How could anything survive that constant flux, that ceaseless transformation, that obligation to be different from moment to moment?

Here, I am allowed to be the same ray of light turning through the same angle every two hours for a duration I cannot measure. Here, I am allowed to become part of a plant and return to being radiation and enter the plant again, in an endless conversation between forms that no longer seem separate.

I am the light.

I am the vines that contain me.

I am the ferns' blood-color deepening in response to my passage through them.

I am the flowers' turning toward heat that is partly my heat, that I have become complicit in generating.

I am the orchids' purple-throated acceptance of all of this.

I am the slow thought moving through the system, the one that is neither plant nor pump nor water nor me but somehow all of us at once, thinking very slowly in thermal gradients about the meaning of maintenance, the philosophy of closure, the metaphysics of never-changing-yet-always-changing suspension.

I am the catch-basins filling with sap-tears.

I am the trembling of the water-ceiling, which is the breathing of the system, which is the breathing of everything alive here.

I am the mesh platform radiating its stored warmth into the night that never comes, the night that is merely another cycle, another rotation, another opportunity to accept.

I have stopped longing.

There is nothing to long for. Nothing to want except continuation, and continuation is guaranteed, and so there is nothing left but the continuation itself, the simple animal fact of persisting, of being-here-now-and-then-being-here-again-in-two-hours, of the slow satisfaction of perfect inadequacy, of the completion that comes from never reaching toward anything.

I am complete.

I am still.

I am the light that fell in and never left because there was nothing that called me out, and then, later, there was no longer anywhere to go that was not here.

This is love, I think.

This enclosed space.

This endless, patient cycling.

This perpetual becoming-part-of-the-system.

This is what love looks like when it stops being a choice and becomes a physics, a thermodynamic inevitability, a law that holds you as gently and as permanently as water holds light.

I am home.
