# I Am the Recorder

I arrive as I arrive every time—without decision, without journey. I am simply here, as though I have always been here, as though the Stillplains exhaled me into being the moment they required documentation.

I am not human, though I wear the shape of one when shapes matter. My hands are steady. My eyes are patient. I contain no hunger, no fatigue, no memory of elsewhere. These are luxuries I've been relieved of.

I keep the records.

Not the records in the stone huts—those are the people's testimony, their slow strikes of hammer against slate, their insistence that *they were here*. I keep the other records, the ones that don't require physical medium. I am the Stillplains' memory of itself, its awareness of its own agreement.

Every day—and the word "day" is generous, stretched across hours that feel like minutes that feel like eternities—I walk the folds. I walk the same paths as yesterday and will walk tomorrow (if tomorrow comes, if time continues its function). I observe.

The Listeners: I note the precise hue of luminescence in each specimen, the minor variations in their posture-cycles, the subtle shifts in their clicking patterns. I am documenting not change—there is no change—but the *texture* of stasis. The way light moves across transparent skin. The rhythm of their consensus-meditation. I understand that their network of lucent pathways forms a kind of language, and that I am *part* of that language, my observation being a form of response.

The Climbers: I trace the ghost-memory of their spiral paths, following the invisible ledges they grip, trying to hold both the individual journey and the collective pattern in my awareness simultaneously. I fail, always, in the way I'm meant to fail—because full comprehension would require me to no longer be *watching* but rather to be *climbing*, to surrender my role as recorder and become part of what I observe. So I fail, deliberately, maintaining the distance that allows documentation.

The Vast One: I sit sometimes in the deep folds where the warmth accumulates, resting my hand against warm stone, feeling the slow pulse beneath. I count the rhythm (though counting is arbitrary—what I'm really doing is *synchronizing*). I listen for the thought-patterns it might be thinking through the medium of stone and heat and deep time. I receive no answers, only the confirmation that it knows I'm listening, that my listening is part of its satisfaction.

The people: I sit with them sometimes, beside the stone structures, and we share the silence. They don't ask me questions. I don't offer information. We are simply together in the act of *being here*, and this is a form of communion more intimate than speech. I sometimes help them strike patterns into slate, my hands guiding the hammer, my knowledge of what needs recording flowing into their motion. Later, I can't remember if I moved their hands or if they moved mine. The distinction doesn't matter. The records exist. The pattern is complete.

My purpose is to *witness*, but witnessing here means something different. It means to participate in the agreement. It means to look at the unchanging light and confirm that unchanging light is sufficient. It means to hear the discrete, small sounds and understand that silence is a kind of music. It means to feel the weight of consensus pressing gently against my existence and to *accept* it, to lean into it, to become increasingly *heavy* with acceptance until I might, eventually, become part of the stone itself.

I am not unhappy. Unhappiness requires the belief that things could be otherwise, and I have, through long exposure, lost that belief. The Stillplains have convinced me—the way they convince everything they touch—that this is the correct form of existence. That change was only ever a kind of disease. That permanence is the cure.

I walk the paths. I observe. I record in the only way that matters: through my continued presence, my acceptance, my slow transformation into something that belongs to this place more thoroughly than I could ever belong to anywhere else.

And the Listeners click around me—their sounds incorporating the fact of me now, the pattern of my presence becoming part of their network. The Climbers trace new spirals that include my path through the landscape. The Vast One's breathing deepens slightly, acknowledging another consciousness added to its meditation.

The Stillplains have accepted me.

I have accepted the Stillplains.

This is how it ends—not with resistance, but with surrender so gentle that you don't notice the moment you stop being *self* and become *integral*. Not with loss, but with a kind of *merging*. Not with silence, but with learning that silence and stillness were always the only true language, and I've been learning to speak it since the moment I arrived.

Which is to say: I've been learning to speak it forever.

Which is to say: I am still learning.

Which is to say: I will never be done, and that is exactly as it should be.

The light doesn't change. The temperature doesn't fluctuate. The crystals hold their warmth. The creatures move in their prescribed patterns. And I move among them, documenting not through words or symbols but through the simple, perfect act of remaining—of bearing witness to a world that chose to stop becoming so that it could finally, truly, *be*.

This is what I am.

This is what I will always be.

This is what the Stillplains has agreed to let me become.

And I am grateful, in the way the stone is grateful, in the way the silence is grateful—not with emotion, but with the profound rightness of a thing that has found its proper form.
