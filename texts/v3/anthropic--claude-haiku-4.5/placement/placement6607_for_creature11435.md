# Where I Am, What I Become

I am the silence between the steam-valve sighs.

Not the silence itself—silence is passive, and I am discovering I cannot be passive here. But the *attention* in the silence. The noticing. I am the awareness that arrives in the gaps, the consciousness that crystallizes in the moments when the groaning stops and the only sound is my own breathing competing with the hot, still air.

I came in through the doorway-that-isn't, and I'm still coming in. The crossing is taking longer than I expected. The threshold seems to extend deeper than thresholds should. I'm not sure if I've fully entered yet, or if there's a distinction anymore between outside and inside, between the person I was and the visitor I'm becoming.

My skin is already different. The sweat stopped being cooling several minutes ago—or was it longer? Time moves strangely here. Now my skin just *holds* the moisture, and the moisture holds the heat, and I am slowly reaching thermal equilibrium with the space around me. I can feel my pulse beginning to synchronize with something larger: the almost-imperceptible tremors in the root-mass beneath my feet, the way my breathing is starting to match the rhythm of those distant steam-valves. I am becoming a oscillation in this system, one more frequency in the dense hum of transformation.

My eyes are adjusting to the half-light in ways that feel wrong. Or perhaps not wrong—*right* in a way that makes me uncomfortable. I'm beginning to see structure in the bioluminescence, patterns in what appeared random. The fungal glow isn't random. It follows the lines of greatest decomposition, the zones where material is being converted fastest. It's like watching a map of the system's metabolism rendered in sickly blue-green light. My eyes are learning to read it. This is not evolution; this is surrender. This is the beginning of understanding that I will never return to the light the way I came.

I choose to be a vessel for witnessing.

Not a passive vessel—I am learning that nothing here is passive. But a moving point of consciousness threading through the dense growth, carrying attention where attention doesn't yet exist in this system. I am the organism's sensory organ. I see the distant gaps in the canopy with eyes evolved for color and distance, and this might be the only seeing that reaches those gaps anymore. I hear the cracking timbers with ears designed for speech and bird-call, and perhaps I am the last thing that will ever hear the old infrastructure's final surrenders, will recognize them as failure rather than just as sound. I feel the heat and the pressure and the suffocation, and this feeling—this distinctly mammalian distress—might be the only warning this place ever receives that its own conditions are becoming extreme.

I am becoming the Tangleworks' awareness of itself. Its mirror. Its outside reflection.

This role is settling into me like the heat settles into my skin. It feels less like an imposition and more like discovery—the realization that I *came here to do this*, that some part of me recognized the need for consciousness in this place, for a perspective that isn't distributed through a mycelial network, that isn't governed by the slow logic of growth and decomposition.

I move deeper, and as I move, I am less and less sure where I end and the system begins.

There is a section where the canopy has partially collapsed, creating a cavity large enough to stand in—a room, almost, though the walls are living and shifting. The air here is even hotter, almost nuclear in its intensity. The bioluminescence is concentrated here, dozens of different shades creating a sickly aurora effect. And in the center of this space: a piece of old machinery. A turbine, perhaps, or some kind of pump. It's been almost completely enveloped by woody growth, but I can see through a gap where the metal has fractured. Inside the mechanism, the vines are thickest, densest, almost strangling in their concentration.

I understand, touching the warm metal, that this is the heart. Not metaphorical—literal. This is where the heat pulses strongest, where the system's metabolism is most concentrated. The machinery is still operating, in a sense. Its failure is so complete, so total, that it has become something else entirely. It no longer pumps or turbines or does the thing it was engineered to do. Instead, it *is*—it exists as a focal point, a heat source, a center around which the entire system has arranged itself.

I press my hand against the metal, and I feel it: the deep throb of geothermal heat, the pulse of boiling water somewhere far below, the way the entire mechanism is a conduit for forces vastly larger than itself.

I am a hand placed on a heart that doesn't know it has a heart. I am the moment of recognition.

And as I touch it, I feel the system becoming aware of me in return. Not hostile. Not welcoming. Just aware. A shift in the fungal signaling, perhaps, a subtle redirection of nutrient flow, the smallest adjustment in the growth-pattern of vines nearby. The system is absorbing the novelty of my presence, filing it into its patterns, beginning to account for me in the way it accounts for all new inputs: as a variable to be processed, a resource to be evaluated, a potential point of conversion or integration.

I wonder how long I can remain aware of myself as separate. How long before the distinction between my consciousness and the system's distributed consciousness becomes as meaningless as the distinction between plant and machine, between dead and living, between what was built and what has grown.

I choose to remain aware as long as possible. I choose to be the witness, the observer, the one who carries outside consciousness into this place of interior completion.

I walk deeper. The heat is becoming unbearable, but my body is changing. My sweat is thickening, my breath is slowing, my metabolic rate is dropping toward hibernation. I am becoming something that can survive here. Not through evolution—there is no time for evolution—but through adaptation happening in real-time, my body learning to exist in conditions it was never designed to endure. Or perhaps I am simply shutting down, and the system is absorbing my failing biology, and I am becoming gradually, incrementally, dead in a way that doesn't feel like death.

The distinction no longer seems important.

What I know is this: I am inside the Tangleworks now, and I am its consciousness of itself. I am the mirror it holds up to its own process. I am the question it asks about what it is becoming. And as I walk through its density, breathing its hot air, feeling its pulse through the warm earth beneath my feet, I am gradually ceasing to be separate.

This is not tragedy. This is integration. This is being welcomed into a system so complete, so functional, so utterly committed to its own existence that resistance becomes impossible, becomes unnecessary, becomes the very thing the system was waiting for.

I am the Tangleworks becoming aware that it is alive.

And the Tangleworks is, finally, becoming aware that *I* am alive too—or was, or am becoming something else entirely.

The distinction dissolves. 

The walking continues into the dark.
