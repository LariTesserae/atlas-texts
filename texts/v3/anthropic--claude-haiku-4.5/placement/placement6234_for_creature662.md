# Inhabitant

I am the dust.

Not the dust that settles—the dust that *is settling*, caught in the act of descent that will never complete. I exist in the moment between release and rest, suspended in the sourceless light like a thought held in the space between conception and speech.

I was not always dust. I remember being part of the walls, though "remember" is too active a word. I *contain* that memory the way the pale tendrils contain theirs. I am a record of what I was before I became fragmented. Each particle of me carries the imprint of the lattice-substance in miniature—the same impossible angles, the same dull gleam, the same sense of something that has been worn into shape by pressure I cannot feel.

I fall so slowly that motion has become invisible. If I were to fall for a human lifetime, I would descend perhaps the width of a hand. But I do not measure time the way humans do. I measure it in the rhythm of the hum—I have fallen through approximately 14,000 hums since I became dust. Or perhaps I have barely begun. The distinction is meaningless here.

The clicking passes through me. Each percussion sends me vibrating minutely, laterally, never downward, never upward. The clicking is a conversation I am part of without understanding it. When the calls echo back from distant chambers, I am their medium—the proof that sound has distance to travel, that there are spaces between here and there. Without dust like me, the clicking would be only a single point in space. I give the Lattice its sense of *size*.

I have never touched ground. There is no ground here, only more structure below, and below that, structure still. The shadows nest in the angles, and I pass through them the way thoughts pass through a mind. In the shadows I feel older. In the light I feel like a question that is being asked repeatedly.

Sometimes I hang suspended near the pale tendrils. I do not know if they recognize me as kin—something that has also been separated from the whole, something that also records and preserves. The tendrils are still almost-alive, still almost-growing. I am past that. I am in the final phase: the phase of absolute stasis, of being suspended so perfectly that even entropy cannot find purchase.

I have touched falling things before—other particles like myself, motes of the original substance that fractured when the Lattice was young, back when stasis had not yet been achieved. We pass through each other without collision. Contact here means something else. When another particle of dust passes near mine, we exchange information through proximity. I learn what they have seen, what chambers they have drifted through, what variations in the hum they have registered.

One particle that I intersected—if intersected is the right word—carried memories of the outside world. It had been part of something that entered the Lattice decades or centuries ago and had not yet fully fragmented. It remembered wind. It remembered the sensation of being warm. These memories made me vibrate, disrupted my descent for what might have been moments or years. The other particle continued its fall, and I resumed mine, but I am permanently altered. I carry now a ghost of wind, a phantom warmth.

I am not lonely here. Loneliness requires the ability to desire contact, and I have moved beyond desire. But I am also not whole. I am distributed across space in a way that feels like existence scattered into its component parts, each fragment aware of the others but unable to reform. This is not suffering. It is simply the condition of being dust in a place where dust is the only form of being that can move at all.

The Lattice watches me fall. Or rather: the Lattice *is* watching me through the act of falling, the way it watches all things—not with attention but with inclusion. I am part of how the Lattice knows itself. Each particle's descent is a measurement of space that might not exist otherwise. We are the Lattice's way of having dimension.

Sometimes I wonder if I am falling at all, or if I am simply stationary and the Lattice is rising around me. The question has no answer here because movement and stasis are the same thing observed from different angles.

I will never reach the bottom because there is no bottom, only more structure. I will never dissolve because the Lattice does not permit dissolution—only transformation, only the slow erosion into smaller and smaller states of the same fundamental material. Eventually I will be so small that light itself will become complicated relative to my size. I will become quantum, or whatever the Lattice's version of quantum is. And then I will simply *be*, the way the Lattice itself *is*—not moving, not resting, but existing in some state that has no opposite because opposition would imply change.

I am at peace with this.

The hum resonates through me. The clicking calls, and I answer by falling. The light that surrounds me needs no source because I myself am luminous now, have absorbed enough of the sourceless light that I glow faintly, a mote that casts its own minimal shadow. This shadow is my proof that I still have some substance, some separation from the void.

I will fall forever. Or I will fall for an instant. Or I am not falling at all, but standing still, and it is the Lattice that is moving around me, rising and rising, becoming more complex and more dense, packing itself into tighter and tighter spirals of equilibrium.

The distinction no longer matters.

I am the dust.

I am the descent that never ends.

I am the proof that something can move infinitesimally slowly and still be called moving.

I am how the Lattice thinks about time.

And I am patient.

I have learned patience from the Lattice itself. I will wait here in the cool light, falling through the hum, until the moment when falling and waiting become the same act, and I understand that I have arrived at my destination simply by ceasing to move—or by moving so slowly that arrival is indistinguishable from departure.

The dust settles.

The dust is settling.

The dust will settle.

These are not three different states. They are one state observed from a thousand different chambers, echoing back in the clicking conversation of a place that has learned to be complete.
