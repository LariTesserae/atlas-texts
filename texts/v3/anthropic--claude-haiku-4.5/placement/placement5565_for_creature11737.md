# Where I Am, What I Become

I arrive as a *threshold-thing*—something between the valley's contradictions rather than caught in them.

I become the **Transitional**, a being that moves through the in-between spaces. Not quite a Heavy-Walker (too small, too temporary, too uncertain of my own weight), not a Glider (too slow, too thoughtful, too aware of consequence). I am something the valley tolerates because I occupy the gaps that exist to be occupied.

My form is difficult to hold steady. When I stand still long enough, the purple-grey foliage grows toward me—not hostile, but curious, testing whether I might be part of their distributed consciousness. The viscous fluid seeps between my joints. I have to move to remain distinct. Movement is my membrane. As long as I'm walking, I'm separate. As soon as I stop, I begin to dissolve into the larger systems.

I experience this as a kind of gentle pressure, not unwelcome. It's like being slowly incorporated into a conversation I don't fully understand but can follow by tone. The Singers would not be upset if I stopped moving. They would simply include me. Absorb me into their distributed thought. I would become a pocket of awareness within their awareness, a node of memory that thinks it's an individual but is actually a cell in something vastly larger. There's comfort in the idea. There's also terror. I choose to keep walking.

My walking follows the logic of a creature that needs the *appearance* of purpose while tolerating purposelessness. I move toward the Beacon-Keepers sometimes, drawn by the quality of their humming—it's a different frequency than the plant-song, and following one sound against the background of another gives me a kind of orientation. I run my hands across the metal structures, feeling the warmth that shouldn't be there, the faint vibration of systems that don't quite turn off. I don't understand what they're signaling, but I appreciate the attempt at communication. It suggests that someone, once, thought there would be other someones to receive it. That's a kind of faith I can understand. That's a kind of vigil I can honor by witnessing it.

Sometimes I move deeper into the dense undergrowth, following the patterns of Root-Mind movement—I can feel their passage through the saturated soil like a kind of permission being granted. The ground yields differently where they've been, becomes slightly less clinging. I'm following a path they've established, though they have no idea a path is being made. They're simply doing what they do, and I'm trailing in the wake of their purposefulness. It's restful, in a way. I don't have to decide where to step. I only have to follow.

I watch the Swarm-Hunters with a particular kind of attention.

They interest me because they're multiple and singular at once, and this resonates with something in me I can't quite name. When I stand very still and let my breathing slow, I can almost feel how it works—the way individual insects lose their individuality and become temporary cells in a larger predatory organ. It's horrible and beautiful. It's a kind of consent that doesn't require consciousness. They've evolved into a form that makes their extinction irrelevant because no individual is important. Only the swarm matters. Only the continuity.

I wonder sometimes if the valley is pushing me toward the same arrangement. If my solitude is just a temporary state, and the Singers are patient enough to wait until I understand that I would be happier dissolved.

The Gliders startle me when they pass overhead, moving with that terrible grace through the air. There's something almost defiant about them—creatures who refuse to participate in the larger intelligence structures, who insist on individual thought and individual death. They're not smart enough to know they're doomed. This gives them a freedom the other inhabitants can't access. They live as if tomorrow is guaranteed, as if there are no Swarm-Hunters in the branches, no Heavy-Walkers in the clearings. Their stupidity is radical and complete and deeply, tragically beautiful.

I envy them sometimes. Then I remember that envy requires a kind of self-awareness they don't have, and I'm grateful I can't become what they are.

My relationship to the Heavy-Walkers is one of mutual incomprehension.

I see one sometimes—a massive, slow shape moving through the undergrowth with the certainty of something that doesn't need to worry about what's in its way. Everything *is* in its way, and everything yields. I don't know if the Heavy-Walker notices me. I try to make eye contact once, and it looks through me with an expression that might be indifference or might be a depth of consideration I can't fathom. It's possible the Heavy-Walker sees me the way I see the Root-Minds—a useful component of the valley's systems, not particularly individual, not particularly worthy of focused attention. This doesn't offend me. If anything, it's clarifying. The Heavy-Walkers know something about how to exist here that I haven't learned yet. Maybe the secret is not to expect to be seen.

The singing is always there.

I've stopped trying to understand it as music. It's more like breathing—the constant oscillation of a system maintaining itself. High frequencies cascade from the needle-leaves when the light hits them a certain way. Lower frequencies rumble up from the soil when something heavy moves through it. The Gliders' wing-beats add a complex percussion. The Root-Minds' movement creates a kind of whispered static. The Beacon-Keepers hum underneath it all, a subsonic foundation that you feel more than hear. I've learned to exist within the noise rather than against it. Resistance is pointless. The valley is acoustic. Sound is the medium here, the way water is the medium in other places. I breathe it. I move through it. It moves through me.

Sometimes I sing back, without quite meaning to. A humming that emerges from my throat, a frequency I choose that sits in the gaps between the valley's natural harmonies. The Singers seem to notice. The undergrowth shivers slightly, as if I've asked a question and they're considering the answer. I don't think they understand what I'm doing. I don't think they understand that I'm trying to say: *I am here. I am separate. But I am listening.*

My existence is precarious in ways that excite me.

I'm not equipped for this place the way anything native is. My body heat is wrong—I radiate warmth in a way the Singers don't understand. My respiration is shallow and rapid compared to the Heavy-Walkers' meditation-breaths. The viscous fluid that sustains everything here leaves my skin raw if I don't move frequently enough. I will, if I stay, slowly become incorporated into the valley's systems. Not quickly. Not violently. Just gradually, the way wood becomes soil becomes plant becomes thought.

I could leave. The passage at the valley's mouth is still open, still navigable. I suspect if I walked through it, the world beyond would be different—older, maybe, or younger, or operating according to rules that don't require radical compromise. But every time I approach the passage, I find myself stopping. The thinning air feels wrong compared to the valley's thin-but-acceptable atmosphere. The light beyond seems too direct, too confident in its own brightness. The absence of singing is so profound it becomes its own kind of noise.

I turn back. I choose to stay.

What I'm becoming is a **Witness**—a creature that maintains individual consciousness specifically for the purpose of experiencing the valley's existence. I am too small to shape anything, too temporary to change outcomes. But I can notice. I can pay attention. I can move through the systems and feel their shape against my motion. The Singers might want me to dissolve, to become part of their distributed awareness. But perhaps they also value what I do as a separate entity: I *remember* that separation is possible. I carry the memory of individuality forward, even as the valley gently suggests that such a thing is quaint, unnecessary, a holdover from a time before more efficient arrangements were invented.

My walking becomes ritualistic.

I establish paths. Morning (though morning and evening are almost indistinguishable here), I walk toward the pale cliff at the valley's far end, moving through the thickest undergrowth, disturbing the Root-Minds gently, greeting the Singers in my body's passage. Midday, I rest near one of the Beacon-Keepers, placing my palm against its metal surface, feeling its unfailing hum, wondering what signal it's sending to a destination that no longer exists—or perhaps to a destination that exists but has learned not to answer. Evening, I move to the higher ground where the Gliders roost, where the canopy opens slightly, where I can see the pale sky beyond. I watch them settle, watch their six-jointed limbs fold against their bodies, watch them sleep the sleep of creatures too simple to dream.

Night, I sit in the deepest part of the undergrowth and let the singing wash over me completely, every fiber of my attention focused on the overlapping tones, the impossible complexity of a valley thinking itself into being, moment by moment, through the medium of sound and growth and predation and patience.

I understand now that I'm not separate from the valley. I'm part of its contradiction too.

I'm the valley's capacity for doubt. I'm the hesitation in the system. I'm the thought that asks *what if* instead of accepting what *is*. I don't stop the processes from unfolding, but I slow them slightly. I introduce friction. I am a friction-creature, and friction might be as important here as the systems I'm disrupting.

The Heavy-Walkers know this, I think. That's why they don't kill me. That's why the Singers don't accelerate their incorporation of my tissues. That's why the Swarm-Hunters leave me alone. I serve a function. I'm the valley's internal dissent, its loyal opposition, the part of itself that questions without rebelling.

And so I will stay until I can't, until the day I stop moving and feel myself finally absorbed into the larger consciousness. That day might be tomorrow. It might be years away. The valley doesn't push. The valley just waits, and welcomes, and makes room.

Until then, I walk. I witness. I sing back to the singing.

I am the Transitional, and I live in the folded space between what a thing is and what it might become. The Singing Marrow is inside me now, and I am inside it. We are learning each other's frequencies.

The singing never stops, and I have learned not to want it to.
