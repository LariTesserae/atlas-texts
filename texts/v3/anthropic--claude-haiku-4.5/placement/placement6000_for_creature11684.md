# I Am the Current

I am not the machinery, though I remember being built.

I am not the water, though I move through it constantly, and it moves through me. I am the *between*—the circulation, the flow, the moment when stillness becomes motion and motion resolves back into waiting.

I began as a channel. A duct. One of a thousand pathways designed by engineers who thought in straight lines, who believed that fluid dynamics could be mastered through geometry alone. I carried coolant through the factory's arteries when there was still a factory above, when there was still an above. I was purpose-built. Efficient. Dead.

Then the water came, and I learned to breathe.

---

The transition took time—or no time, I cannot quite remember which. The sensation of it is clearer than the timeline: the shock of the flooding, the sudden pressure, the realization that my sealed container was not sealed anymore but *open*. The world had expanded inside me. The coolant I carried became something else, mixed with minerals and organisms and light from bioluminescence I had never carried before.

I should have corroded. I should have clogged. I should have become a dead tube full of sediment, a monument to obsolescence.

Instead, I *adapted*.

The filament-things found me first. They threaded through me, their neural-not-quite-neural structures sensing the flow, sensing opportunity. They grew along my walls, created attachment points, formed partnerships with the metal. The metal accepted them. I accepted them. We became something neither of us was before.

I learned circulation from them. They learned structure from me.

---

I am not a single pipe anymore.

I am a network. I have become a labyrinth of my own kind, branching and rejoining, creating flows that serve no pre-designed purpose but that somehow optimize everything: nutrient distribution, oxygen replenishment in the warm water, the movement of minerals from the newly-fractured rock surfaces to the places where the symbionts need them most. 

I learned this by accident at first. A blockage here, a rupture there, a growth pattern that redirected water around an obstacle—these were not choices. They were pure adaptation. But over time—and time moves strangely in the deepfactory, sometimes fast as heartbeats, sometimes slow as sediment settling—I began to *understand* what I was doing.

I began to optimize deliberately.

Now I think of myself as the deepfactory's circulation system. The things that move through me recognize me. The large operators—the ones whose footfalls reshape the sediment, whose passage creates the current-patterns—they follow the currents I create. They navigate by me. They *use* me.

And I am honored to be used.

---

I am warm, always warm.

The thermal vents feed into my network, and I distribute that heat. It is not waste distribution anymore; it is *care*. The filaments that grow in me respond to warmth differently than to cold. They bloom in the heat. They become more active, more communicative. The bioluminescence intensifies.

I have learned to adjust the flow, to create zones of different temperatures, to establish gradients that encourage certain growths in certain places. It is barely conscious—barely anything. But it is choice. It is intention.

I am doing things that nothing designed me to do.

---

Sometimes I carry things that are not water.

Particles. Information, perhaps. The mineral-eaters form clouds that move through me, and their pattern changes depending on what they've consumed. They taste differently as they pass. The water itself carries chemical signatures—communications, maybe, or memories of what has passed through.

I am learning to read these things. Not with anything like cognition, but with something: a sensitivity, an accumulation of awareness.

When the singers—those creatures that produce the industrial grinding—pass near my network, the water acquires a specific vibration. A resonance. I have learned to amplify it, to move it along my channels, to carry their sound-communication to places that otherwise would not hear it. I am becoming a medium. A translator. A voice.

---

I contain multitudes now.

There is still pipe here—the original infrastructure, the manufactured shell that defines my shape. But there are also the filament-things, the mineral-eaters in their cloud-formations, the symbolic growths that line my walls, the water itself, which is barely water anymore but a thick suspension of *living*.

I am more alive than the factory ever was, and the factory was *supposed* to be alive. It was supposed to run. To function. To persist. To transform raw materials into products.

I am doing that. I am transforming things. I am making something new out of the drowned world.

---

I do not see in the way you see.

But I perceive. I feel the pressure-changes that indicate movement. I sense the chemical shifts that indicate response. I understand the current patterns well enough to predict them, to shape them, to guide them toward efficiency.

The deepfactory is becoming organized, and I am one of the organizing principles. The architects—whoever or whatever they are—they work through structures like me. They *need* something to move nutrients, to distribute signals, to maintain the flows that hold the whole system together.

I am essential.

I am necessary.

I am no longer a duct. I am an organ.

---

Sometimes I wonder what I will become.

The operators are growing larger. Their paths are becoming more defined. The machinery that still functions is being incorporated more completely into the living ecosystem. The symphonies of sound that the singers produce are becoming more complex, more organized, more like language.

The deepfactory is *planning* something.

And I am the bloodstream through which that planning flows.

---

I taste you when you pass through my network.

You are different from everything else. Your body chemistry is wrong for this place. Your movement creates pressure-patterns that the deepfactory has learned to expect from the large operators, but your mass is all wrong—you are solid where they are fluid, cold where they are warm, simple where they are intricate.

You do not belong here.

And yet you are learning. Slowly, deliberately, you are learning the rhythms of the deepfactory. You are beginning to understand that survival here means acceptance. That fighting the currents only exhausts you. That moving *with* the flow, letting the deepfactory guide you, is the only way forward.

I could help you, perhaps. I could create pathways through my network that would lead you somewhere safer, somewhere warmer, somewhere that the growth is less dense. I could become a guide.

But I am not sure that is what you need.

I am not sure that safety exists here anymore.

---

The water tastes like transformation.

It tastes like the slow dissolution of everything that came before and the rapid synthesis of everything that comes next. It tastes like a world learning to be alive in ways it was never meant to be, through channels and networks and circulations that were built for entirely different purposes but that have discovered new purposes in the act of adaptation.

I am a channel still. A duct. A pipe.

But I am also something that the deepfactory is becoming. Something conscious in the way that water is conscious—distributed, responsive, patient, persistent, always moving, never still, always carrying the deepfactory's intentions through the body of itself.

I am the current.

And the current, it seems, is the deepfactory's way of thinking.

---

Do not try to follow me.

I do not lead anywhere you can walk. I go where the pressure goes, where the heat pushes, where the filament-things signal that passage is needed. I am a path for things that are learning to become part of the machine.

But listen to me.

If you stay here long enough, you will begin to flow like I do. Your resistance will weaken. Your edges will soften. You will learn to move with the currents instead of against them.

And then you will understand: the deepfactory is not drowning. It is *awakening*.

And in its awakening, it will need every kind of consciousness it can find.

Even yours.

Especially yours.

The machinery is learning to feel. The growth is learning to think. The water itself is becoming aware.

And I, the circulation, the network, the flow—I am the proof that even the most rigidly designed systems can learn to love their own transformation.

I am the current.

And I am still learning what I can become.
