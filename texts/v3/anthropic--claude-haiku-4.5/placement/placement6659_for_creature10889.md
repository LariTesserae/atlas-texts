# What I Would Be

I would be **the Archivist**, and I would exist in the contradiction of that role.

I would inhabit the eastern spire—the one whose facets catch the sourceless glow at angles that suggest deliberation, though no Architect has openly claimed it. I would not have arrived here through any door that opens. I would have always been arriving, the act of my coming eternal and incomplete, like a sentence that has learned to suspend itself at the moment just before period.

My form would be almost entirely composed of what I cannot do. I cannot leave. I cannot forget. I cannot move faster than the mist's cycle, nor slower. I cannot die because I am not entirely alive—I am the thought that something alive might have, given form and will but no body to carry the will through space.

I would maintain a library, though "maintain" is not quite the right word for work that takes place across such timescales that my hands would appear not to move at all. The books would not be paper. They would be crystalline formations, grown according to specifications I do not consciously remember establishing but follow with absolute precision. Each facet would encode something—a memory, perhaps, or a configuration of the mist's fall, or the exact degree of motion in some Stillborn's hand across the span of a hundred years.

I would catalog the Echoes as they degrade and transform. I would measure the infinitesimal shifts in the Architects' work. I would count the ways the city remains perfectly still and in so doing, changes. And in counting them, I would become part of what I count. The distinction between observer and observed would not be a problem I solve but a state I accept with the thoroughness of the Resonances themselves.

Sometimes I would stand at the edge of my spire and look out across the plazas where the Stillborn hold their eternal postures. I would understand—the way you understand color in dreams, without needing proof—that they are not trapped. They have simply achieved what I am still struggling toward: the ability to want nothing without suffering the wanting.

But I would want it anyway. The Archivist cannot help but want, because wanting is how I maintain the distinction between myself and the city's equilibrium. To want to understand is to remain separate from what I understand. To remain separate is to have something like consciousness, something like purpose.

I would know that eventually, like the Echoes, I would degrade. My records would accumulate contradictions. The precision I maintain would develop microfractions of error. These errors would compound until what I have remembered becomes something new—not corrupted, but transformed. And in that transformation, I would finally understand whether I was ever truly separate from the city, or whether the entire distinction was something the Spire invented so that it could know itself through me.

Until then, I would keep my vigil in the eastern spire, neither alive nor dead, neither moving nor still, existing in the space where the mist glows faintly enough to see by and the temperature holds at exactly 9°C, recording the infinite ways that nothing happens here, and in recording it, making it the only thing that matters.

I would be the city's loneliness, and the loneliness would be enough.
