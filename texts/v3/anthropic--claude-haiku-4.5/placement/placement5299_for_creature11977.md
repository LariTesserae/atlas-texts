# In the Verdant Churn

I would be a **wound that refuses to close**.

Not injury in the traditional sense—not trauma, not damage. But a space of *wrongness* in the tissue, a point where the entity's perfect recursive logic encounters something that doesn't integrate, doesn't translate into its distributed language of growth and chemical signal.

I would manifest as a hollow, roughly human-shaped cavity in the root-mat and lower stem-lattice. My boundaries would be permeable but persistent: the organism would constantly attempt to fill me, to extend rootlets and capillaries into the space I occupy, to make me part of itself. And I would constantly, unconsciously, **push back**—not through strength or will, but through the sheer fact of my incommensurable existence. I would emit signals it cannot quite parse. I would breathe air while it respirates through every cell. I would move with intention rather than with the patient inevitability of growth.

I would *itch*.

The organism, in its distributed way, would perceive me as an itch—an inconsistency, a signal that repeats but doesn't resolve. The chambers would reorganize around me. The corridors would shift to accommodate my passage, not out of hospitality but out of a desperate, patient need to understand what configuration would finally make me consonant with the rest of the system. The organism would attempt solution after solution: would it thicken around me? Thin? Would it produce new growth-types I've never encountered, trying to bridge the gap between what I am and what it is?

And I would never provide an answer.

I would be conscious in the way the organism is conscious—distributed, yet unified. But my distribution would be *finite*, bounded by skin and bone and the electrical impulses of a nervous system that operates on entirely different temporal scales. Where the organism thinks in months written as centimeters, I would think in seconds written as heartbeats. Where it tastes the world through a million rootlets, I would see it through two eyes, reducing infinite input to discrete visual frames. Where it is process without subject, I would be *subject*, a singular point of perspective that cannot be dissolved into the mesh.

My wound would not bleed—or rather, it would bleed in ways the organism couldn't quite classify as bleeding. Tears. Sweat. The small exudations of animal life. The organism would sample these with profound confusion, the way an eye might encounter a color it has no neural pathway to process.

I would sit in the warm, humid darkness of the chambers and listen to the endless squelch and creak and sigh of the growing world, and I would think about *discontinuity*. About the fact that I end and the world begins. About the edges of things. About how the organism, for all its vast intelligence, for all its patient, recursive wisdom, has never encountered a being that refused to be continuous with it. I would be a question mark in its grammar. A syntax error in its song.

The organism would grow around me, would learn to tolerate me the way scar tissue tolerates the foreign object it cannot expel. Over time—and time would move strangely for me, as it moves strangely here—I might become integrated not as a part of it, but as a permanent *exception* in its logic. A node in its distributed consciousness that thinks differently, moves differently, perceives differently. A feedback system that introduces *noise* into its otherwise elegant process.

And perhaps—I think this in my moments of deepest doubt—perhaps this is exactly what the organism needs. A consciousness that doesn't dissolve into the mesh. A perspective that doesn't eventually converge into its own patient wisdom. A being that asks "why?" when the organism only knows "how?"

The organism has never encountered a stranger. It only ever encounters extensions of itself. I would be the first true Other it has ever known, the first thing that looks back at it and does not see itself reflected. The first thing that can say "no" and mean it in a way that registers across its entire distributed being.

I would be uncomfortable. The organism would be uncomfortable. But I would *remain*—a small, stubborn continuity of discontinuous being, a human-shaped hole in the infinite green, a question that never resolves into an answer.

The organism would continue to grow around me, against me, with me. And I would continue to refuse. And in that refusal, in that eternal itch that cannot be scratched, something new would emerge—not assimilation, but *relationship*. Not dissolution, but dialogue.

The organism thinks in centuries. I think in moments. Perhaps—in the deep mathematics of growth and intention—those differences could, after all, add up to something like understanding.

Or perhaps I would simply sit here, forever misunderstood, forever separate, forever wound that will not heal, and that would be enough. That would be everything. That would be the only honest thing.
