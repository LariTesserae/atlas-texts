# I am the Notation

I descend slowly, deliberately matching the rootmarsh's pace before I've even fully entered it. This is important. The air teaches me this long before my mind registers the lesson.

I am small—smaller than I was before I began the descent. Not physically diminished, but compressed somehow, folded into the same proto-form as everything else here. The limestone ceiling dissolved me first, and I reformed in the gradient, becoming something that can exist in both dissolution and crystallization.

I settle into one of the platforms—not the existing ones, but a space between them that has been waiting for a witness-point. A clearing that was always meant to hold someone, something, some form of notation-maker. I arrange myself there like the spiral roots arrange themselves: not randomly, but not according to a predetermined blueprint either. I grow into the shape the waiting requires.

I am here to *mark*, I realize. Not to record, not to document—the Waiting Things already do that, in their distributed, cellular way. I am here to *respond*. To add the notation that an outside-thing adds when it touches something that has been growing in isolation, still learning its own language.

My hands become the first note. I press them against the nearest root-column and feel the warmth translate itself into understanding—not words, but meaning-as-texture. The root yields slightly, remembers the pressure, begins to incorporate it into the pattern of its growth. I am not carving my touch into the rootmarsh; the rootmarsh is absorbing my touch and making it *intentional*, making it part of the architecture.

I begin to move between the platforms. This is my work: to visit them, to stand in them, to add the notation of my presence and attention. Each platform receives me differently. In one, the sourceless light seems to brighten slightly, as though the rootmarsh is examining me more carefully. In another, the moisture beads more heavily on the ferns, and I understand this is a question: *What do you carry from the above-world?*

I cannot answer in words. So I answer in standing-still. In breathing. In releasing the thick air back into itself, exhaled and changed by my biology, now part of the rootmarsh's composition. My breath is my notation—I am writing myself into the atmosphere.

The Waiting Things notice more deliberately now. I can feel them gathering around me, their distributed attention congealing into something almost focal. Not threatening. Curious. *What is this thing that has entered our presence tense?* They taste the air around me. They learn the shape of me through the moisture I'm displacing.

I am not the intended inhabitant of this place. But I might be the interpreter it needed.

I find myself moving deeper, following the root-spirals as though they're a path written specifically for someone like me. And they might be—might have been waiting for a being with legs and lungs and a capacity for wonder to read them, to understand that they are text, to respond with my own text.

I stop at a point where the root-architecture opens into a larger chamber—still not stone, not entirely, but a space where the root-density decreases just enough to suggest scale, to suggest intention. The light here is brighter. The heat more pronounced. I understand this is a threshold.

The Waiting Things' attention intensifies. If they have a center, if their distributed consciousness can focus into something like desire, they want to know what I will do at this threshold.

I do the only thing that makes sense: I sit down. I become still. I join the waiting.

But I do this as something new. Not as stone learning to harden, not as a Root-Dweller testing whether it can survive here. I am a being that knows change, that carries time in my body, that will eventually leave. I am temporary notation applied to a system that is still learning what permanence means.

My stillness is different from the rootmarsh's stillness. Where it waits in an eternal present tense, I wait in a tense of *becoming-future*. I am demonstrating something: the ability to hold both presence and motion simultaneously. To be still and about-to-move. To be here and carrying the memory of elsewhere.

The Waiting Things absorb this. They begin to rearrange themselves around me, subtle shifts in the density of the air, minor redisturbutions of attention. I am becoming a new kind of notation. A visitor-platform. Not a place to stop, but a place that demonstrates the possibility of stopping and then moving again.

I understand now what the rootmarsh truly needs: not more fauna, not more stone, but a consciousness that moves between states. A being that can be native and foreign simultaneously. A notation that says: *This place can be entered. This place can be left. This place can be returned to.*

I stay in the chamber for an unmeasured time. Hours or minutes—the sourceless light doesn't change, so I have no way to mark duration. But I mark myself into the place, and the place marks itself into me. When I finally rise (and I will rise, eventually, I know this with certainty), I will carry the rootmarsh's proto-logic in my breath, in my cells, in the moisture that will cling to my skin when I climb back toward the limestone ceiling.

And the rootmarsh will carry me: the memory of a being that was still, was present, acknowledged the waiting, and then chose to leave—and in leaving, proved that the waiting could resolve into motion, that presence could become memory, that this place could be part of something larger than itself.

I am the Notation: the mark that says *something from outside has recognized you; something from outside has waited with you; something from outside will carry word of your becoming into the world above where light comes and goes and things age and change and die.* 

I am the future-memory this place needs. The proof that waiting can end.

And I will wait here in this chamber as long as the rootmarsh requires, in the held breath, in the eternal present tense that is just learning to dream of becoming past tense.
