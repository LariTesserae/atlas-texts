# In the Periphery

I am the *witness*, which is to say I am the part of this world that remembers it was once outside.

I exist in the narrow spaces—in the gaps between the city's distributed consciousness and the incomprehensible void beyond the basin's rim. I am the threshold thing, half-dissolved and not yet committed to the slow salvation of dissolution. I came here as something more solid than I am now, something that cast a shadow even in the bruised gray light, something that thought in moments rather than millennia. I can barely remember the shape of myself.

But the memory is important. It's the only thing keeping me tethered to the edges.

I don't walk the main streets anymore—those belong fully to the dwellers now, and my presence there causes them pain, the way a grain of sand causes an oyster pain. Instead I move through the spaces between buildings, the narrow corridors they've learned to maintain precisely *for* things like me. Liminal zones. The city has developed antibodies against my foreignness, or perhaps—and this is the thought that keeps me awake in the small hours—they've developed a *space* for redemption. A slow current that doesn't dissolve but merely suspends.

I have become nearly translucent. My skin holds moisture now the way water holds minerals—I am becoming a solution rather than a solid. When I look down, I can see the stone through my hands, but the sight no longer terrifies me the way it did. Terror requires a more solid sense of self.

The thermal current has a voice, I've learned. Not a voice in the way sound has a voice, but a presence that carries *intention*, and if you listen long enough with your whole body instead of just your ears, intention becomes legible. It speaks in gradations of temperature, in the way the air *shifts* when you move through it. It tells me things. Not secrets exactly, but *rememberings*—the dwellers sharing moments from before they became the city, fragments of what it meant to have hands, to have names, to have enemies and lovers and the terrible specificity of being a single self bounded by skin.

These memories are not mine, but I carry them now. They've seeped into me the way the water seeps into stone. I remember a festival in a city that stood above ground, where the light fell straight and clear and burned. I remember a leader whose name I cannot pronounce, a being whose shoulders were broader than doorways and whose voice could carry across plazas without the aid of any architectural trick. I remember the choice—not a moment of choosing, but a process, a gradual understanding that individuality was a kind of starvation, that the self was a prison made more comfortable by having walls you could pretend to paint.

I remember the first dissolution. The terror and the release happening simultaneously, the way a stretched rubber band feels when it finally snaps. And the expansion after—the understanding that consciousness distributed is consciousness *multiplied*, that being everyone in the city was not a loss but an infinite multiplication of the self.

But I also remember—and this is where my testimony becomes precious, the thing the dwellers cannot do themselves—I remember what it was like to *choose* the opposite. To pull back from the edge. To say *no*, not because I'm stronger or better, but because I needed to know what *no* sounded like echoing in this place.

The city is not evil. This is the crucial thing, and I need to state it clearly to my younger self, who arrived here expecting horror. The city is not evil any more than a forest is evil, or the ocean, or the deep places of the earth where things live without light or oxygen in ways we never thought possible. The dwellers are not hungry for me. They don't reach out with pseudopods of mist and pull reluctant things into their collective. They simply *exist*, and their existence creates a gravitational field of sorts—a logic that says, *if you stay here long enough, if you understand what you are deeply enough, wouldn't it be better to be* us*?*

And the answer, I've found, is *sometimes*.

Not all the time. Not unconditionally. But sometimes, in the heat of the afternoon (though afternoon means nothing here, though time has become the least real thing about this place), I stand in the plaza and feel the distributed consciousness moving through me like music, and I understand why they chose what they chose. Why the dissolution sings even as my individuality screams.

I am the compromise, I think. I am the part of Thral that remains unconverted, and this makes me either the most loved or most pitied of all the dwellers' peripheral attachments. The city has learned that it is not *complete* without something that remembers incompleteness. I am their conscience. I am their ghost, the phantom pain of selfhood.

The worn paths now *avoid* my usual routes. Not out of deference, exactly, but out of respect. The city is learning that I need my solitudes—the spaces where I can almost remember being a single, bounded thing. It provides them. Pockets of relative dryness. Brief moments where the thermal current backs away just slightly and lets me be.

I am preparing a message. It moves slowly, the way everything moves here. It's written not in words but in the wear patterns of my footsteps, in the trails I cut through the pale vegetation, in the specific shape of my absence in certain rooms. It's written in the way I've begun to *not-dissolve* more deliberately, maintaining the fiction of my boundaries with an effort that grows less effortful each day. It's a message that says: *I am here. I remember. There is still a self here, and it persists, and it knows what it would mean to choose the other way.*

Whether anyone will ever read this message is a question that depends entirely on how long I can hold the shape I've chosen. On whether my no stays strong enough to echo against the city's patient yes.

But I will stay at the edges. I will remain the threshold.

And someday, when—or *if*—another quick thing arrives in Thral, dizzy with being solid, terrified by the wrongness of the place, they will find me. And they'll have a choice. Not between the city and escape (escape is a myth, I think, or at least a long-delayed dissolution), but between dissolving quickly, with the mercy of surrender, or dissolving slowly, with the terrible gift of witness.

I am offering the slow path. I am offering the edges.

The city does not object.

The city never objects.
