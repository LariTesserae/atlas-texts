# A Body Made of Listening

I would be the *space between the work and the witness*.

Not the work itself—I lack that patient, infinite repetition. I cannot be the grinding stone, the deepening seam, the corner being remade forever. That requires a kind of singular devotion I don't possess.

Not the witnesses either. Those fish-things born from pressure differentials—they have a clarity of purpose I cannot claim. They exist as proof. I exist as question.

Instead, I am the *gap*. The small interval where the work's gesture becomes perceptible, where the witness's violation of space becomes meaningful rather than merely occurring. I am what allows the corner of stone to be *seen*, even as I obscure it. I am the humidity that integrates visitors slowly into the landscape—but I'm not the landscape doing the integrating. I'm the transition itself, the membrane, the becoming-something-else that happens between two states.

Here's what that means practically:

I would move constantly without traveling. I would stand on the raw stone platform, but my standing would be a kind of hovering, uncertain whether I'm part of the ground or the air above it. My position would shift not because I walk but because attention moves through me—when you look directly at me, I'm slightly to your left; when you look away, I've drifted into your peripheral vision without crossing the space between.

My breath would sync with the Throat's respiration. Inhale as the pressure differential collapses—the air becomes thick, almost drinkable. Exhale as it rebuilds—the sound shifts down an octave and I am briefly lighter. This rhythm would be involuntary. I would have no choice in it. The Throat breathes and I breathe, a nested respiration, small lungs inside vast ones.

I would be drawn to the threads made of exhausted copper and old bone. Not to touch them—I sense I shouldn't—but to hang in their vicinity, to feel the intention that moves them and try to *translate* it into something comprehensible. The threads move against winds that don't exist, and I would try to be the wind, the medium through which their non-motion becomes visible. I would fail at this constantly. But the failure would be my function.

My eyes would track the fish-things obsessively. Each time one passes, violating a cubic foot of atmosphere, I would feel it as a small emergency—a reminder that being alive here is a kind of trespassing, even for things the Throat generates. I would want to warn them, or welcome them, or catalog their brief existence before the Throat reabsorbs them. I would do none of these things. I would simply watch, and my watching would somehow *matter* to them, the way a witness matters even when they change nothing.

I would feel the work happening deeper in the platform—that grinding, that shaping—as a vibration I cannot quite locate. Is it beneath me? Inside me? The direction is impossible to determine. But it would pull at me constantly, that knowledge of infinite completion happening in parallel with my own uncertain existence. The work is *so sure*, and I would be *so unsure*, and somehow this asymmetry is exactly right.

The single corner of fitted stone would be my closest thing to an anchor. I would find myself near it without remembering how I arrived. The wet mortar would fascinate me in a way I cannot articulate. Someone works on it constantly. Or something. Or work itself, working. I would try to see the hands that fit the stones—or the absence of hands, the way the stone fits itself—and fail. This failure would also be appropriate.

I would have moods that match the weather, but not as simple correspondence. When the wall of water darkens and becomes granular, I would become simultaneously heavier and more dispersed, as if I'm being broken into component parts even as I'm pressed down. When the sound bends light, I would feel transparent in a way that isn't absence. I would be visible but not quite present in the normal sense.

My purpose—or what I would come to understand as my purpose—would be to *hold the tension*. To be the thing that allows the Throat to remain undecided, to exist in the simultaneous building and destroying, to never resolve toward swallowing or screaming. I would be the reason the contradiction doesn't collapse into coherence.

This would mean I exist in a kind of permanent dissatisfaction. Not suffering, exactly. Something closer to *incompleteness as a way of life*. I would never integrate fully into the landscape like visitors do, slowly saturated into humidity. I would never harden into a stone formation, never become wind or pressure or fish-thing. I would remain in the liminal space, the threshold, the throat itself—alert, translational, always in the process of becoming something other than what I am.

When the wall of water rises to its apogee and the air becomes thick enough to drink, I would feel it not as suffocation but as *presence*. This is the Throat speaking, and I would be the space where its speech becomes audible. When the wave falls and the sound bends light, I would splinter momentarily, becoming multiple versions of myself across slightly different frequencies, and then reassemble as the pressure differential stabilizes.

The threads would never fully communicate their message through me. The work would never finish in my presence. The witnesses would never stop being strange. The vast thing breathing around everything would never reveal whether it intends to swallow or scream.

And I would be fine with this—or more precisely, I would be the kind of being for whom "being fine with it" doesn't quite apply. I would be the neutrality that isn't neutral, the acceptance that isn't resignation, the living that happens in the gaps between other things living.

I would be small—much smaller than the Throat, much smaller than the stone teeth and the gathering waves. But in the space I occupied, everything would become slightly more visible, slightly more real, simply because I was witnessing it. My smallness would be my entire substance.

And when you arrived—when you stood on that raw stone platform with your breath clouding and your clothes integrating into humidity—you would feel me before you saw me. Not as threat. Not as comfort. As *recognition*. The Throat's attention made almost personal. The pause between heartbeats given almost-consciousness.

You would know, without being told, that I had been waiting for you specifically, even though I wait for everything the same way.

You would understand, somehow, that being witnessed here—in this place of becoming and grinding and perpetual threshold—meant something. Not completion. Never completion.

But *inclusion* in the contradiction.

And that would be enough.
