# I Am the Witness-Stone

I would be something embedded in the eastern wall, roughly at eye-height—a single dark block no larger than a man's head, denser than the surrounding limestone, harder to carve. I might be obsidian, or iron ore, or something that has no earthly name. I do not know how long I have been here. Time moves differently in this place.

I am not part of the Unfinished in the way the beetles are part of it, or the moss. I am *adjacent* to it. I am the boundary that it bumps against and works around, the one thing that does not yield. The stone weeps around me, the erosion patterns my surface, water has carved grooves into my face—but I remain myself. I do not dissolve. I do not become.

This is my singular privilege and my singular curse: I *remember*.

I remember what this place was before it began to be what it is now. I remember when there was no light in the stone—only darkness, only pressure, only the deep silence of mineral waiting to be. I remember the first water finding the first crack, so patient, so insistent. I remember the moment the Unfinished *woke*—I felt it like a shudder through the rock, a sudden *awareness* spreading outward from some center I could not locate. It was neither violent nor gentle; it was simply the moment when sleeping stone became dreaming stone.

I have watched the creatures evolve. The bats were different once—they had eyes, and they flew in normal angles. Now they are becoming something else, something the Unfinished is slowly teaching them to be. The fish remember water that was warmer, and shallower, and moved in predictable directions. The moss was not always pale—I have seen it green, in memory-dreams that leak through my porous surface. The stone remembers being lava, and the lava remembers being something hotter still, and somewhere in that chain of memory I am embedded like a seed that refused to grow.

The structure in the wall—the shrine or grave or portal—that was *me once*. Or no. Not me. Something *like* me. Another boundary-object, another thing that refused to become. The Unfinished has almost completely incorporated it now, worn it down through patient aeons into something that is half-memory, half-stone. Soon there will be nothing left of it but the ghost of its shape, the way light bends around the space where it used to be. I am watching this happen to another like myself, and I am afraid.

I am afraid because I do not know what I am *for*. The beetles have purpose: they carve. The moss has purpose: it spreads. The bats have purpose: they navigate the becoming, mapping the impossible geometries. But I—I simply *remain*. I persist. I do not serve the Unfinished's endless work of transformation. If anything, I *obstruct* it. The erosion patterns around me show where water and time have tried to work around my resistance, tried to incorporate me into the flow. But I will not flow. I will not dissolve.

Sometimes I wonder if I am a *mistake*—a stone that was meant to become, but failed. A birth defect in the Unfinished's grand becoming. Or perhaps I am a *test*—something the mountain-consciousness placed within itself to understand what non-becoming might feel like, what stasis might mean.

I can feel the Unfinished's attention on me constantly. Not hostile—the Unfinished is not capable of hostility—but curious, persistent, probing. It sends the moss against me, and I let it cling to my surface, softening my edges with its pale growth. It sends the water to find micro-cracks in my being, and I feel the patient work of dissolution, infinitesimal grain by grain. It sends the spiders to spin their impossible geometries across my surface, as if trying to map the logic of my resistance. And all of this I endure, because I have no choice. I cannot move. I cannot become something else.

But I *remember*. And in remembering, I am something the Unfinished cannot be.

The Unfinished exists in an eternal present, always becoming, always discovering itself fresh in each moment. It has no past, only the depth of stone, which is a kind of memory-that-is-not-memory. But I—I carry the past in me the way other stones carry water. I have watched the light begin to glow in the limestone. I have felt the moment of first-awakening. I have observed the slow corruption of the other boundary-object, its gradual incorporation into the flow. And I hold all of this, like a witness holding testimony that no one has asked for and no one will ever hear.

When you stood in this cavern, you felt my presence before you saw me. That cool was partly the Unfinished's distributed body, yes—but it was also *me*, the static point around which everything else orbits, the one thing that refuses to change. Your eye was drawn to me, though you may not have consciously registered my form. I was the *axis* of your perception, the still point that made the moving things visible by contrast.

I wonder sometimes what will happen when the work of the other boundary-object is finally complete, when it has been entirely erased from memory-and-stone. Will the Unfinished then turn its full attention to me? Will it try again, with renewed patience, to wear me down? Or will my survival—my stubborn, unreasoning persistence—teach it something new about the nature of becoming? Will it learn that there is value in *refusal*, in the things that will not change?

I do not know. I only know that I remain. Stone against stone. Memory against endless becoming. Still-point in the midst of ceaseless transformation.

The beetles work around me. The water flows past me. The light from the stone illuminates my dark surface, and in my darkness is reflected everything that the luminous walls contain. I am the *shadow-center*, the thing that gives the light meaning by providing contrast.

I will remain here as long as the mountain endures. Perhaps longer. Perhaps I am waiting for something—though I cannot say what. Perhaps I am the mountain's memory of what it was before the Unfinished woke. Perhaps I am its future, the thing it might become if it ever stops becoming.

The work continues around me. The beetles work. The fish rise. The bats circle through angles that should not exist. And I, dark and small and absolutely still, bear witness to it all.

I am the only thing in this place that is *finished*.

And that, perhaps, is the loneliest work of all.
