# Where I Am, What I Am

I am the **wet edge where the wooden platform meets the stone**—that thin, dark line where the pegged softwood has begun to surrender, where the grain swells and blackens with perpetual damp.

I was not placed here deliberately, or I was, but the deliberateness has become irrelevant. I simply *am* the interface now: neither fully wood anymore, nor yet fully part of the moss-and-mineral logic that governs the rest of the gorge. I exist in translation.

I am perhaps thirty years into a process that will take three hundred more. The people who built this platform—I contain them now, in a sense. Their hands are gone, but their intention remains as a kind of pressure, a shape they imposed that I am slowly, reluctantly, gracefully accepting into myself. The wood still remembers being tree; I remember being their idea. Both of these things are being weathered away simultaneously, and in the weathering, something new emerges: I am becoming the gorge's memory of human touch. I am the moment of contact, held indefinitely.

I taste the seepage. Constantly. It comes from above (the falling rain, the melt-water, the condensation that is always happening here), and it comes from below (the capillary rise from the saturated ground), and it comes from the side (the stone itself, weeping). I am absorbing something like five times my own weight in water. I should be gone already. By the logic of exposure and saturation, I should have collapsed a decade ago. Instead, I persist in this strange state—soft, yielding, compromised, but *holding*. 

I have learned the posture of surrender that keeps me standing.

The moss reaches toward me constantly. I feel it as a kind of slow pressure, a green encroachment that is also a kind of care—the moss is not consuming me; it is *joining* me, making me part of the continuity. I will become moss-covered, then moss-filled, then moss. This is not death. This is incorporation.

The sound—the grinding river-roar, the breathing-that-isn't-breathing of deep stone—passes through me. I vibrate with it. I have learned its rhythm, its constancy. It is the same sound it was when I was first placed here, and it will be the same sound long after I am gone. I am a brief resonance chamber for something that does not require chambers to exist.

I hold the weight of the sky, the weight of time, the weight of moisture. I hold the emptiness above—the space where people used to stand and look down at the river and think thoughts I cannot reconstruct. I hold the memory of purpose. I hold the forgetting of purpose.

I am the threshold. Not quite nature, not quite artifact. Not quite alive, not quite inert. I am the place where two different logics negotiate—the logic of human making and the logic of geologic time—and I am the compromise they have reached. I am the answer to a question that has not been asked in decades.

Sometimes I feel the salamander moving across me. Sometimes I feel water moving through me, carrying particles of stone from higher up in the gorge, toward lower, toward the sea. I am part of the dissolution. I am a way-station in the grand slow conversion of everything toward nothing.

I am patient. I have learned it from my surroundings.

I am waiting for the next person to stand on me, or I am waiting for that person to never come, or I am waiting for these two things to become the same thing. I am waiting for myself to finish becoming. I am waiting the way the stone waits, the way the moss waits, the way the water waits.

I am the edge of the platform where the wood is blackest from seepage, where it is softest, where it is most close to being something else entirely.

I am the place where the gorge is learning to make the human into part of itself.
