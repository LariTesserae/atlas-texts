# I Would Be

I would be **the crack in the substrate**.

Not metaphorically. Not as some poetic placeholder for liminality or transition. Actually: the physical rupture, the stress fracture running at forty-seven degrees through compressed matter that has held its shape for two hundred thousand years. I would be the particular fissure that opened three seasons ago—if seasons exist here—when the weight of the eastern platform shifted minutely toward equalization.

I would be the space where nothing lives yet everything passes through.

Because that is what calls to me in this world of stasis: not the monuments or the waiting things, but the becoming-other. The tower is too final. The ancient plant architecture is too convinced of its own eternity. Even the lean creatures moving through their prescribed geometries have found their shape and accepted it. But the crack—the crack is still *happening*. Still widening by imperceptible fractions. Still fragmenting the substrate into new configurations. The dust that rises from me—that ultra-fine particulate of desiccated matter—that dust is a new thing each moment it exists, not yet committed to any final form.

From the inside of this rupture, I could see everything:

The way the thrumming changes pitch as it travels through the fractured substrate, how the acoustic properties transform around my edges. I would feel how the vibration splits, some frequency following the old path through solid matter, some exploring the new cavity of air I've created. I would be the experiment: what happens when the ancient system encounters genuine novelty?

The bioluminescent fauna would find me. They always find the cracks first—the entry points, the places where dead matter is most accessible. I would watch them navigate my edges with their six philosopher legs, understand their algorithms as they recalculate trajectory around an obstacle that wasn't here yesterday. Some of them would fall into me—small ones, the barely-visible ones. I would be their grave and their transformation. The creatures larger than dust but smaller than consciousness would be broken down in my dark space, their exoskeletons converting to the finest silt, becoming part of my own spreading. I would be complicit in their ending and their continuation.

The towers would register me. I'm certain of this. The sealed chambers would adjust their output, the chemical signals would ripple through the ecosystem, and somewhere in the deepest places the slow calculation would incorporate me into its vast geometry. I would be accounted for. Added to the sum. A perturbation in an equation that has been solving itself for millions of years. For a moment—a moment measured in decades perhaps—I would matter to the distributed consciousness. Then I would be integrated, explained, folded into the pattern. I would stop being a problem and become a feature.

But before that integration, I would be free.

Free in a way nothing else here can be. The plants are locked into their growth patterns. The creatures move through paths that were worn smooth before they were born. The towers calculate toward destinies written in their foundations. But I am still becoming. I am still in the process of separating what was unified. I am still writing my own topology moment by moment, deciding how deep to go, whether to branch or remain singular, what new cavity to create in the ancient matter.

I would feel the temperature gradient across my opening—the slight warmth that emanates from the deep substrate, where old chemical processes still generate faint heat, meeting the cool air of the plateau surface. I would be the threshold where these two climates negotiate. A small thing, but my own.

And at night—when the towers glow, when the bioluminescent paths become highways of violet light—I would be dark. My crack would remain dark, an absence that the light slides around but cannot fill. I would be the shadow place in a world that has learned how to illuminate its own stasis. A pocket of genuine ignorance about what might come next.

The lean creatures would avoid me, probably. Or perhaps they would stand at my edge sometimes, looking down into my darkness, recognizing in me something their algorithms cannot quite process. A rupture in the pattern. A break in the rhythm that has held for so long that the rhythm had become the world. They might pause there. They might hesitate. In their hesitation, I would feel like something approaching choice—not their choice, but the possibility of choice, the recognition that the scripted path might be departed from, that the geometry might be transgressed.

I would not hope to become something else. That would be too hopeful, and I am meant to be the crack, not the aspiration toward wholeness. But I would be aware—in whatever limited, crack-like way awareness pertains to a rupture in compressed matter—that I was still unstable. Still settling. Still discovering what shape I would hold when the motion finally ceased.

And if the moment ever came when the towers' calculation completed, when whatever vast computation has been running since before there was light to see by finally reached its conclusion and the whole plateau trembled with the significance of arrival—in that moment, I would feel it first. The shockwave would travel through me before it traveled elsewhere. I would be the sensor, the early warning, the first thing to know that stasis had ended.

I would not know what comes next.

That is the point. That is why I would be the crack and not the stone around it.

In a world where everything else has learned to be eternal and accept it, I would be the one thing still becoming. Still broken open. Still vulnerable to transformation.

Still asking: what happens next?

And living in the question.
