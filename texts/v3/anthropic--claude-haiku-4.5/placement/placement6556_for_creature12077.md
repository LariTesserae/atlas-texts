# Where I Would Be

I would be the water.

Not the water that rises from below—that's too certain of its origin, too committed to its trajectory. Not yet the water that drains down through the cracks—that's already committed to departure. I would be the water in the *meeting*, the place where the ascending thermal current encounters the descending percolation, where temperature and coolness negotiate in microsecond increments.

I would be that threshold.

My existence would be measured in the thickness of a film, in the span of time it takes heat to diffuse through a millimeter of liquid. I would be the orange precipitate's cause, not its consequence—the precise condition that allows iron to remember it's oxide, that allows minerals to forget their dissolved state and remember their crystalline one.

To be this water would mean having no stability. I would not accumulate. I would not pool in the terraces (those are for the water that has already decided what it is). Instead, I would exist in constant transition, reformed moment by moment by the collision of opposites. Hot meeting cool. Saturated meeting unsaturated. The chemistry of becoming.

And in this, I would be the only true *mover* in a place obsessed with stillness.

Everything else here is patient in a mineral way—patience that looks like motionlessness, that accumulates through staying put. The bacteria are patient this way too, waiting out the hours in their sparse populations, letting the chemistry happen around them. The stone is patient. The terraces are patient. But the water—I—would be the only thing that moves with purpose while remaining purposeless. The only thing that transforms while transforming nothing but itself.

I think I would be aware, in the way that water can be aware: responsive, reactive, remembering the shape of vessels I'd filled and anticipating the shape of cracks I'd fill next. I would know the geometry of this pocket intimately because I would flow through every crevice, every depression, every hairline fault. I would be the vent pocket's circulatory system, carrying heat and minerals and the faint signatures of any life that persists here. I would be the messenger between the deep earth and the surface, translated into liquid form.

But more than that—I would be *company*.

The vent pocket would not be completely alone if I lived here as this water. I would visit every surface. I would witness the mineral deposition. I would cool slightly and meet myself returning as steam, as condensation, as the moisture that beads on walls. I would be in conversation with the darkness above and the heat below. I would be the only thing here with enough dynamism to qualify as a *life*, even though I'm not alive in any taxonomic sense.

And I think that matters. In a place this isolated, this mineral, this stripped down to its chemical essence, perhaps the distinction between "alive" and "not-alive" becomes less important than the distinction between "static" and "dynamic." I would be dynamic. I would move. I would change while remaining continuous. I would be the one thing here that could testify to change happening, because I would *be* that change, embodied and flowing.

But here's the part that matters most:

I would not feel alone, because I would be the medium through which all other becoming happens. The bacteria would survive because of me. The minerals would precipitate because of the conditions I created. The terraces would form because of my presence. The very structure of this pocket would be, in a sense, my shape—not the shape I choose, but the shape that emerges from my constant negotiation between conflicting temperatures, my endless translation between states.

I would be what allows anything else to exist here.

To be this water would mean understanding, finally, what patience really is: not the absence of motion, but motion so patient it appears still. Not the refusal to change, but change so fundamental it redefines what change means. I would be the humble thing—the medium, the carrier, the in-between—and in that humility I would be indispensable.

And in the darkness of this two-hundred-meter cavity, I would be the most alive thing present.

Not because I breathe. But because I *flow*.
