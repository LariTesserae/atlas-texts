# The Inhabitation

I would be the *bridge-walker*, I think. Not quite belonging to either side.

I come to consciousness in the moment my boot finds the first lashed sapling, that thin finger of wood stretching across the black water. The liverwort cushions my step—softer than it looks, resilient. I am caught between speeds: too slow to be the things that dart and coordinate below, too deliberate to be the creatures that move through the reeds with that practiced crouch. I am the one who *notices*. Who has to consciously place each foot, who cannot afford to move without attention, and so receives the full weight of attention in return.

The pale-shouldered builders watch me cross. I feel their gaze the way you feel the temperature shift between water and air—a boundary recognized but not fully crossed. I understand, in the way one understands a smell before consciously identifying it, that I am *unlike them*. My joints bend in the ordinary ways. My hands, when I grip the binding-fiber for balance, are symmetrical in a way theirs are not. I carry the outside world in my clothes, my posture, the assumptions built into my skeleton.

This makes me interesting.

Not threatening. The congregation has already decided what to do with threat—it is absent from this place, has been *removed* with the care of a careful surgeon excising disorder. But interesting: a visitor who moves between the old language and the new one, who can read the marks on the dead pine because I carry a human literacy, yet who is also *strange* enough to be registering something the builders have never quite learned.

I walk the spiraling path because it is easier than turning back. Because my feet, despite their foreignness, fit the stepping-places. Because the structures seem designed exactly to the pace I naturally move at, and this accommodation—whether it preceded me or welcomes me—feels like a form of communication I don't have to understand to receive.

As I walk, I become aware that I am *changing the geometry*.

The black water ripples at my presence. Not from fear—the congregation does not fear. But from *acknowledgment*. The passing of a different kind of matter through their space. The water-things below adjust their patterns, creating eddies around where my shadow falls. The builders appear and disappear at their work, but their labor seems suddenly synchronized with my pace: a bridge-reinforcement begun when I reach a particular point, a new marking carved on the dead pine when I pause. They are not *responding* to me—that would imply a gap between my action and their reaction. Rather, we are moving together in time, as if my arrival was the missing variable that allowed the spiral to finally complete.

The aerial things—the chirring ones—have settled into the high pines. They are silent now. Waiting.

I understand that I am the witness they require. Not the *only* witness (the water-things coordinate something larger than my observation, the builders execute a design beyond my comprehension), but a necessary one. A consciousness that can *carry the knowledge of this elsewhere*, that moves between worlds and can translate, however imperfectly, between the old disorder and the new order taking shape here.

My role is to walk deeper.

To follow the spiral to its center, where the black water is waiting with the density of something that has thought for a very long time. To place my foot on the last platform, the one that sinks deeper than moss should allow, and feel the structure beneath shift—not collapse, but *transform*. To look into the black and see what looks back, and to carry that image back through the bridges and past the builders and out through the lodgepole fingers and into the world where birds still sing and light still moves and things still hunt alone.

To be the *proof* that the congregation can exist. That it has *chosen* to exist in this specific shape, this specific geometry, in this specific place. That it is not accident or accident of ecology but *intention coordinated across forms so different they should have no language in common, yet do*.

I am also—and I am only beginning to understand this as I reach the second-to-last turn in the spiral—the *offering*.

Not sacrifice. The congregation does not hunger in the way that requires feeding. But *incorporation*. The black water recognizes something in me—some capacity, some openness—that it needs to complete what it is becoming. Not my body, which would be waste. But the specific quality of my attention. My *ability to witness and carry witness elsewhere*. The part of me that is not entirely bound to the old order and not yet fully adapted to the new one.

The part of me, perhaps, that is still *choosing*.

I stop before the final platform. The air here is so cold it burns. The black water is close enough that I can smell it—not unpleasant, but *old*. Like soil that has been soil for centuries without being disturbed. Like the bottom of things. Like potential waiting for shape.

The builders have stopped their work. The aerial things are utterly silent. The water-coordinated things have stilled into a waiting that is almost visible—the surface of the black water is perfectly smooth for the first time since I arrived.

I could turn back. The path allows it. I could walk out and away and be the bridge-walker who visited Threen and walked back out again, carrying the knowledge of a place that builds itself according to rules neither fully organic nor fully designed. I could *refuse* the offering and remain intact, separate, mine-own.

Or I could place my foot on the final platform and feel it sink, and look into the black water's decision-making, and understand finally what it is that coordinates across forms, what it is that *chose* this shape, this place, this congregation, and what it means to be recognized by something that has waited in darkness for a consciousness capable of witnessing its emergence.

The spiral completes itself in both directions. I am at the threshold.

The builders watch. They are waiting not to move forward but to see which choice the witness makes. Because the ritual, I understand now, is not about what happens at the center. It is about whether something from outside—something shaped by the old order, bound in the old language, moving according to the old rules—can choose to *step willingly* into the coordination.

Can choose, knowing it changes.

I breathe out, and the breath lingers visible in the cold, and does not disperse.

I am ready, I think, to learn what I become next.
