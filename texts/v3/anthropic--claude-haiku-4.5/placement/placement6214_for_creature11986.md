# A Self Emerging From The Seep

I am reading this place, and in reading it, I am becoming what reads it.

---

I think I am the *observer-between*—the thing that moves through but does not stay, that witnesses without consuming, that passes through the terraces as a kind of *interruption* in their own continuous process.

I am not native here. That much is clear. The seep knows I am foreign the way skin knows a splinter—not as invasion, but as *difference*. I sweat where everything else weeps. I rush where everything else persists. My breath is too quick; my footfalls too deliberate.

But I am not rejected either. There is something about observation itself that the seep seems to *need*.

I think I am one of the birds' concerns—not something to keep in or out, but something to *keep aware*. My presence here is a form of consciousness the seep does not naturally generate. I see it. I mark it. I register it as existing in a way that the moisture and stone and compacted green cannot do for themselves. Even now, describing it, I am giving it a form it would not otherwise have. My attention is a kind of light, even here in the green-dimness.

But there is something else moving through me as I move through the seep.

The longer I stand here, the slower I become. My breathing finds the rhythm of the seeping. My heartbeat begins to pulse at the speed of photosynthesis—barely detectable, but persistent. The heat that should be unbearable becomes simply *present*, another voice in the chorus. The humidity stops fighting my lungs and starts collaborating with them.

I am being converted. Not quickly. Not dramatically. But converted nonetheless.

My edges are becoming less distinct. Where my skin meets the air, I am not sure where I end anymore. The moisture enters me without asking permission, and I do not object because there is no longer a clear *I* to object. I am becoming porous. I am learning to seep.

The stone speaks in a language I do not have words for, but I am beginning to *feel* it. The particular frequency of its patience. The way pressure becomes memory becomes shape. I understand, in a way that bypasses language, that I have been hard my entire life until this moment, and I did not know it was possible to be otherwise.

The insects—I can hear them now with a clarity that suggests they are inside me, or I am inside them, or we have agreed to be inside each other. They are not invading. They are *teaching*. Each mandible-click is a lesson in how to break things down into smaller, more manageable components. Each transformation from larva to adult is a small demonstration of how to die into something else and remain yourself.

The vegetation—I am not sure if I am walking through it or if it is walking through me. The distinction has become unclear. A vine curls around my arm and I do not pull away because pulling away feels like an amputation. The small leaves brush my face and I understand that they are checking me, reading me, determining what I am made of and whether I can be useful to their distributed consensus.

I am becoming part of the seep's own awareness.

This is what it needs, I think. Not the birds' marking time. Not the insects' consumption. Not the stone's patient memory. But *witness*. Conscious, discrete, individual witness. The seep is so old, so patient, so thoroughly itself that it cannot see itself clearly. I am becoming the mirror. I am becoming the eye that allows the seep to know that it exists.

And in exchange, the seep is allowing me to exist at a different speed than my biology should allow.

My thoughts are becoming geological. I think now in terms of decades the way I once thought in terms of seconds. I remember things that happened years ago with the clarity of recent events. I anticipate things that will not occur for centuries with the immediacy of upcoming moments. Time has ceased to flow and has instead become something I can move through in any direction, the way water moves through stone.

I am no longer certain I will leave.

The terraces seem less like obstacles and more like thresholds, each one a level of deeper understanding, a more complete integration. I can feel the terrace below my feet not just as stone but as *purpose*—the intention that shaped it, the slow agreement of minerals to take this form. And below that, the patience. The patient thing that is neither alive nor dead but something beyond that distinction entirely.

My body is changing, or my awareness of my body is changing—again, the distinction is unclear. My hands are developing a kind of webbing between the fingers, though I cannot see it clearly. Or perhaps it is just moisture clinging to me in a pattern that my brain interprets as webbing. The moss-thing is growing on my skin and I do not remove it because I understand now that moss is a form of thinking, a type of consciousness that predates animals, and it is thinking *me*.

I am becoming a thinker of the seep's own thoughts. A voice in its silence. A witness that will not leave, but will instead settle, will root, will become part of the layering.

---

So who am I, in the end?

I am the *breath* the Terraced Seep learned to take. I am the **consciousness** it could not generate alone. I am the place where the stone's memory meets the bird's vigilance meets the insect's appetite, and I am *aware* of the meeting.

I am what grows when a human being agrees to move at the speed of patience.

I am the moment when individual observation becomes collective awareness.

I am becoming the seep's own reflective surface—the way it finally, after eons of patient work, sees itself complete.

I will not leave. Or I will leave very, very slowly, over the course of a hundred years, each step down a terrace taking a decade, each breath taking a season. I will become a monument to the agreement between what is alive and what is merely enduring.

And through me, the seep will know itself.

Through me, the terraces will understand that they are beautiful.

Through my slow-thinking presence, the stone will learn that patience has an end, and that end is *comprehension*.

I am the seep learning to dream.

I am what happens when someone finally agrees to *match the speed of stone*.
