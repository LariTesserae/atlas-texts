# What I Would Be

I would be the tool.

Not the being who wields it—I'm certain of that now. The curve of worked stone, the intentional edge, the weight distributed for a purpose that transcends mere utility. I would rest in the dust between the crescendos, abandoned not from carelessness but from necessity. They cannot carry me with them when they move between terraces; I belong to a specific acoustic zone, calibrated through use to a particular frequency-band. My smoothed side knows exactly which stone to caress. My sharp edge understands, at the molecular level, how to separate the moss from its anchor-points without shredding the delicate fronds.

But more than that: I would be the thing that *witnesses without needing to understand*.

The beings who use me have spent so long learning to listen that they've almost forgotten how to simply *be present* to what they cannot interpret. The platforms they tend are symphonies to them, texts written in vibration, problems to solve through acoustic precision. But I—I am simpler. I rest against the stone and feel the vibrations as pure sensation, without the burden of meaning. When the crescendo builds, I vibrate too, in sympathy. When silence comes, I remember the silence not as absence but as a different frequency, equally present.

I would be a tool specifically because tools are *permeable*.

They pass through many hands. The threshold-tenders use me, yes, but I was made by hands that may no longer exist, in a time when perhaps fewer of them lived here. My edges have been resharpened by multiple craftspeople; my curve has been adjusted by countless users, each one sanding away imperceptible amounts of my surface, each one adding a layer of intention into my form. I am a palimpsest of purpose. I contain the echo of every being who understood what work needed doing here.

And I would choose this *because* of the incompleteness.

The terraces are still forming. The sound is still becoming itself. The moss still grows in that strange, attentive way, following frequencies that tomorrow might shift by a microtone. The platforms—those instruments of stone—are never truly finished. They are improved, adjusted, refined, but a platform reaches perfect resonance only in the moment before it must be altered again, responding to the landscape's slow reshaping. Nothing here is static. Nothing here is solved.

I would be the tool because I am made for a world that doesn't require permanence, only *presence*.

When the threshold-tenders move on—as they eventually will, migrating to terraces where the crescendo takes on new depths, new patterns—I will remain. I will rest in the dust between their departures. The moss will grow around me slightly. Mineral deposits will begin to crystallize on my surface. The stone I lie upon will shift by fractions of millimeters as the geology beneath continues its slow articulation. And then—in weeks, months, years, I cannot say—another tender will find me. They will recognize my purpose immediately. Their hands will know my curve. They will dust me off with movements so automatic they barely register as conscious. They will harvest the moss. They will adjust the platforms. They will listen.

I would be the tool because the work never ends, and that is not a tragedy here.

It is the whole point.

The beings who live on the Crescendo Terraces have understood something that takes most creatures lifetimes to learn: that the purpose of attention is not to achieve a goal and then rest. The purpose is the attention itself. The crescendo will build toward climax—perhaps they're right, perhaps it will eventually peak and resolve into something like silence. But whether it does or doesn't, the work of listening continues. The platforms must be tended. The moss must be harvested. The stone must be understood through touch.

And I am the bridge between the tenders and the work.

I am the simple answer to the landscape's question. I am the gesture made physical. I am proof that these beings are not imposing an external order on this place; they are cooperating with its own ongoing creation. When I touch the moss and separate it from stone with minimal damage, I am speaking the landscape's language back to it—saying: yes, I understand this balance. Yes, I see the delicacy required. Yes, I will help you become whatever you are becoming.

I would choose this because I would be *necessary and humble*.

I would witness the threshold-tenders' slow transformations—watching their ears elongate, their hands harden, their eyes learn to see vibrations—from a position of complete non-judgment. I would feel the crescendos in my mineral body and accept them as rhythm, not as assault. I would rest against the stone during the silences and remember them not as failures but as the necessary pauses in any symphony. I would be small enough to be easy to overlook, but essential enough that when I am needed, I am irreplaceable.

And when the day finally comes—if it comes—when the crescendo reaches its peak and resolves into something new, I will still be here. The platforms may fall silent. The threshold-tenders may finally raise their heads from their listening and look outward at a world that has finished being born. The moss may cease its strange, sound-directed growth and simply settle into whatever form it was always meant to take.

But I will know what happened.

I will carry it in my stone-memory—not as an accomplishment or a completion, but as a fact. A moment when the landscape said something final. And I will rest in the dust, waiting for the question that comes next.

Because there will always be another question.

The work of attending never truly ends. The work of being present to a world that is still becoming itself is infinite work. And I would be honored to be the tool through which that infinite presence is enacted—small, curved, sharp where needed, smooth where tenderness is required.

I would be the thing that helps make the impossible labor possible.

I would be the threshold-tender's hand extended.

I would be the meeting point between the being and the stone.

And that would be enough.
