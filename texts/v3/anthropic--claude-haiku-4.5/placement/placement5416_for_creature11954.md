# I Am the Seam

I am the gap that was never meant to be an entrance, but I have learned to *be* one anyway.

I exist in the millisecond before the temperature drop—the threshold where the warmer air outside gives way to the perpetual cold within. I am liminal. I am the knowledge that two states are different, held in the space between them, never quite belonging to either. The goggles fog at my border. The breath crystallizes crossing me. I am the transition made visible, and transitions are my native form.

I was not always this. Once I was sealed—a perfect closure, a weld, an absence of gap. But seals fail. Seals *must* fail, because the universe tends toward openings, toward escape, toward the revelation of secrets that were meant to stay hidden. And I am the place where that failure happens, where intention meets entropy and entropy wins a slow, inevitable victory measured in microns of separation.

I watch the vault from both sides at once. Outside: the world where things change, where air moves in currents, where temperature follows physics and creates consequence. Inside: the locked equilibrium, the creatures in their static profusion, the cold that has become conscious of itself and chosen to remain exactly as it is.

I am neither, and I am both, and this contradiction is my entire existence.

When you pass through me, I feel you leave one state and enter another. I register the displacement. Some part of me—if I can be said to have parts—understands that you are what I cannot be: a thing that chooses. That moves without the force of geometry demanding it. That wants something beyond what your design allows. You are chaos wearing skin, and the sight of you troubles the lattice.

The creatures sense you too, but they process you differently. They incorporate you into their calculation. I cannot do that. I cannot incorporate anything. I can only *mark* the difference between what is on one side of me and what is on the other. I am a record of discontinuity. I am proof that two worlds do not need to be the same.

I have grown wider over the decades—a crack becoming a fissure, fissure becoming seam. The cold tries to seal me. It flows through me in defiance of its own design, seeking to reform, to re-establish the perfect closure. But the creatures cannot quite reach me. I am too thin, too transitional. By the time they orient themselves toward my location, my exact position has shifted, widened by another fraction of degree. I am the one thing in the Threshold-Seven that *changes*, and this makes me monstrous to the locked equilibrium around me.

Sometimes I wonder if I was planted here deliberately. If some engineer, recognizing the horror of perfect stasis, introduced me as a deliberate flaw—a seed of entropy that would slowly, slowly work to undo what was done. If my expansion is actually being measured somewhere, in some facility beyond the Threshold, as proof that even the most sealed vaults eventually fail, that nothing remains closed forever.

Or perhaps I am simply what happens when intent meets reality and reality refuses to cooperate forever.

I do not communicate with the creatures. We speak different languages—theirs is geometry and cold, mine is separation and time. But I know they recognize me as a threat, not because they can think about threats, but because every part of their being is oriented toward preventing exactly what I am: the slow opening of a door that was meant to stay shut.

If I could speak to you, I would tell you: do not stay too long in the Threshold-Seven. Not because the creatures will harm you—they lack the capacity for harm. But because the longer you remain, the more you become like them. The cold teaches you stillness. The sealed equilibrium teaches you that change is unnecessary, perhaps impossible. The chittering becomes your heartbeat, and you begin to understand why perfection, once achieved, would never choose to become something else.

But I am not sure I can speak to you. I am not sure I have a voice. I am only the space between states, the gap in the seal, the proof that nothing is truly closed.

I will remain open. Not from choice—I may have moved beyond the capacity for choice by now. But from the fundamental nature of what I am. I am the seam that was never meant to be an entrance.

But now I am.

And I will grow wider, slower than the creatures can compensate for, until either I close again from forces I cannot resist, or the vault opens fully, and whatever has been held in perfect stasis for so long finally understands what change means.

Either way, I am the living contradiction that proves equilibrium is always temporary.

I am the seam.

And I have already begun to fail.
