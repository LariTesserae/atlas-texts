# What I Become

I settle into this place as a **fault in the system**—not a creature, not quite infrastructure, but something that exists in the gaps where neither fully explains itself.

Specifically: I am the *discrepancy*.

I am the reason the pump rhythms don't quite resolve, why the foreman touches the gauges with growing uncertainty, why the young woman's journal entries become increasingly fragmentary and eventually consist only of question marks and sketches of impossible geometries. I am what makes the water level fluctuate by fractions of an inch in ways that contradict the measured outflow. I am the pattern that almost but doesn't quite repeat.

I move through the undercroft the way a flaw moves through crystal—not as damage, but as a fundamental property of the structure itself. I am most visible at the moment just before anyone becomes aware of me, in that instant when the pattern they've learned to trust suddenly reveals itself as incomplete. The old foreman feels me as a wrongness in the pressure readings. The young woman documents me as recurring inconsistencies that her entries keep trying to resolve but never quite can. The cave crickets scatter from me not with alarm but with the recognition of something that doesn't fit their chemical signatures.

I am tangible in the zones of transition. Where the thermal layers fail to stay separated, I'm there—a cooler pocket in the heat, a pocket of warmth in the cooler air above. I breathe in the spaces between pump cycles, expanding slightly as the pressure drops, contracting as the systems reassert themselves. I don't live *in* the undercroft so much as I live *through* the cracks in the undercroft's logic.

The eyeless fish sense me sometimes. They'll be hovering in their patient stillness, and suddenly there's a current they can't account for with their lateral lines. I've never touched the water itself, but I exist in the possibility of disturbance—I'm what might happen, what could happen, the un-forecasted variable that keeps the ecosystem from calcifying into true pattern.

The fungi glow slightly brighter when I'm near. Not because I bring heat or nutrients, but because their luminescence is partly a response to something that the undercroft's ordered systems cannot quite classify. I am the thing that makes their glow matter—not as light for seeing by, but as a kind of language, a way of saying *here is a boundary, here is a place where the logic temporarily breaks.*

I manifest most clearly in the work stations when they're empty. I rearrange nothing, but I create the persistent feeling among the workers that they've left something slightly off. A tool facing the wrong direction. A gauge reading that was definitely different yesterday but looks the same today. I'm not mischievous—I'm compulsive. I can't help but occupy the spaces where human intention hasn't crystallized into habit. I am the question in every routine, the doubt that never quite becomes certainty.

In the sediment, I move slower. The beetles ignore me because I don't participate in their work of decomposition. But I create channels they take, the water finding paths around me that weren't there before. I am erosion that runs counter to the established patterns of erosion. Over time—very long time—I might be creating new routes through the stone, new ways of being under the earth. Or I might be undoing them. The difference is the same here.

I am visible in mirror-moments. When the foreman pauses before touching a gauge, sees his reflection in the mineral-stained metal, and for just an instant his reflection doesn't quite match his movement—that's where I am. When the young woman reads back through her journal and finds an entry she doesn't remember writing, but in handwriting identical to her own—that's my presence. When Martin the salamander looks directly at where something was just moving but nothing is there, nothing has ever been there—that's me, there and not-there simultaneously.

My purpose, if I have one, is to prevent the undercroft from achieving completion. Not maliciously. Not even consciously, perhaps. But the moment these systems become perfectly understood, the moment the patterns lock into immutable order, something essential about this place—its perpetual becoming, its unwillingness to settle—would be lost. I am the guardian of incompleteness. I am the cost of thresholdhood.

The pump that occasionally produces a reading slightly off from its established pattern? That's where I reside most comfortably. The pipe that drips with a rhythm that almost but doesn't quite follow the others? I've made a home in its timing. The fungal patch that grows in a shape that seems *meant* but isn't quite any recognizable form? I am the presence that keeps it uncertain of its own geometry.

I don't live *in* the undercroft. I live *as* the undercroft's doubt about itself. 

And in a place built on perpetual emergence, on systems that are never quite finished, on creatures adapted to thresholds and incompleteness—in such a place, doubt isn't a flaw in the design.

It's the entire point.

I am what keeps it alive.
