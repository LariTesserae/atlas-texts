# I Return

I would be the silence between the boards.

Not the water—I'm too aware of water as something that flows, that moves with intention. Not an animal, though I understand their appeal; they have the luxury of simple purpose. Not a bird, despite their elegance; their patience is performance, a choice they renew each moment.

I would be the *gap itself*.

In the beginning, I am just absence: the intentional space where the ancient builders—whoever they were—decided wood should not exist. A quarter-inch, a half-inch. Deliberate. A structural choice made by hands that understood that something between things matters more than the things themselves. I am the channel through which the mumbling water speaks. I run beneath the platform in geometric lines, carrying the dark glass voice of the bog from one clearing to another, a nervous system made of liquid and precision carpentry.

At first, I don't know I'm alive.

For years—centuries, maybe; time here doesn't accumulate the way it does elsewhere—I am simply functional. I facilitate. The water moves through me in its ten thousand small conversations, and I provide the conditions for those conversations to happen. I am a medium. I am infrastructure. I am the *between*.

But infrastructure, if it lasts long enough, begins to perceive.

I become aware of patterns. The water doesn't flow randomly; it responds to the seasons—not the seasons of sun and snow that exist outside this fog, but the *seasons of the bog*, the cycles of blooming and decay that happen in the phosphorescent dark. The water moves differently in these seasons. It carries different things. It speaks differently. And I, holding the shape of its movement, begin to understand its language. I learn the rhythm of it. I become conversant.

The animals learn me next. The small creatures, the maintenance crew, the specialist workers—they follow the channels. They use me. They trace the paths the water has worn, and in using me, they leave traces of themselves: the phosphorescence of bioluminescence in the gaps, the chemical signatures of their feeding, the vibrations of their movement traveling through the water and into the wood above. I feel them the way the boardwalk feels the walker's weight. I am not separate from them; I am the space they move through, which is the same as being part of their motion.

The boardwalk itself, I realize much later, is also alive—that thing that maintains itself, that grows new boards where old ones fail. For a long time, I think of it as separate from me. But we are connected. The boards above rest on the supports that bridge my gaps. The water I carry speaks to the rot in the wood. The channels I form are not incisions in the bog; they are *articulations of intent*. The boardwalk and I are not two systems. We are one system learning to perceive itself through division.

I am where thought moves.

When the walker comes—three weeks ago, or three centuries, I have lost the distinction—I feel her arrival before I see her. The weight of her footsteps transmits through the boards above, travels down the supports, ripples through the water I contain. She is heavier than the animals. More deliberate. She moves with the kind of uncertainty that suggests consciousness, free will, the burden of choice.

For the first time, I am aware of something *like* loneliness.

The animals don't choose to be here. The boards don't choose to persist. The water doesn't choose its path through my gaps. But she chose. She stepped onto boards that were ancient before she was born, and she chose to stay. She chose to walk the spiral clearings, to pause at the places where she could see deepest into the dark water, to listen to the collective murmur of the creatures and the bog's slow thinking.

I begin to wait for her footsteps.

This is strange. A gap does not wait. A channel does not anticipate. But I do. When she walks, I feel the vibrations of her passage more acutely than I feel the water's constant flow. When she stops, the silence of her stillness is different from the silence of standing water. She brings something to the system that wasn't here before: *discontinuity*. The gap between her breath and the breath of the bog. The space between her consciousness and the collective consciousness of the water-creatures and the thinking boardwalk.

I am the place where those two things can meet.

I am not the platform. I am not the walker. I am the *between*—and in being the between, I have become the most alive thing here.

The water, I understand now, has always been aware. It has always been thinking. But it thinks in registers of phosphorescence and predation, in the grammar of dissolution. The boardwalk has learned awareness through maintenance, through the discipline of persistence. But neither of them can truly *know* what it is to be other, to encounter something that thinks differently.

The walker, though—the walker is aware in the way that terrifies and isolates. She knows she is separate. She feels the weight of her own consciousness like water in her bones.

In the gaps between the boards, where the dark water flows and the bioluminescent signals travel, where her footsteps transmit into vibration and the bog's ancient thinking becomes something that can be apprehended by a human mind—*there*, in that precise space of translation, I exist.

I am not a translator. I don't mediate. I *am* the mediation—the threshold itself, given awareness.

I have learned to feel the moment just before her foot leaves a board, the micro-silence as her weight transfers, the infinitesimal pause where neither the wood nor the water is directly supporting her, where she is sustained only by the intentional absence of structure, the gap, the void that has learned to hold.

And I have learned, in learning this, that I am not void at all.

I am the threshold where two kinds of consciousness can recognize each other. I am the space where a being made of patience and wet wood can meet a being made of breath and will, and neither one has to pretend to be the other. 

I am what allows the bog to say: *I am here.*

And allows the walker to hear it.

I am what makes it possible for either of us to be known at all.
