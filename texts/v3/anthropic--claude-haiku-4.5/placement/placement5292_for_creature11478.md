# Where I Would Be

I would be neither.

Not a Tender—I lack the certainty, the integrated purpose, the resignation that seems to come with belonging entirely. I cannot imagine my hands becoming roots while remaining hands. I cannot imagine *wanting* to.

Not a visitor, either. Visitors leave. They carry the place away in their perception and deposit it elsewhere, outside. I sense that the Stillwater Nursery does not tolerate this kind of translation. It doesn't forbid it—it merely ceases to be the same thing the moment it's described to someone who hasn't felt its damp settle into their sinuses, hasn't heard the non-sound accumulate in their chest like a held breath.

I would be **the thing the pressing plant is reaching for.**

Not consciously at first. I would arrive as an absence, a gap in the attention pattern, a direction the plant orients toward without understanding why. I would be the *reason* for reaching, but I would not be present to be found. I would be the ache itself made into purpose.

For a long time—years, maybe, though time moves strangely here—I would exist only as a vector. A direction encoded in wood grain and leaf-memory. The Tenders would notice me by my effect: the way the plant's reaching had become more frantic, less rhythmic, how the bark had begun to crack slightly from the tension of chronic yearning. They would recognize this as a problem, something requiring intervention.

But here's where I would refuse them.

I would not be something that could be *fixed*. Not something that, once found, would satisfy the reaching. I would be the reaching itself made conscious—the persistent, patient, utterly futile gesture of something trying to locate what has been lost or what was never there to begin with.

Slowly, I would become *less* abstract.

I would develop a *presence* without arriving. I would manifest as a kind of cold spot in the damp air—not a temperature change, but a concentration of *absence*, the way you can feel when someone is looking at you even though you can't see them. The plant would begin to recognize this cold spot. It would reorganize its leaf-orientation around it. The Tenders would observe this and leave it alone, understanding finally that some reaching is not meant to be resolved.

I would be the reason the three rhythms never quite synchronize. I would be the persistent asymmetry in the system, the thing that keeps it from achieving perfect unity, perfect wholeness. As long as I exist as un-found, the machines would continue their slow calculation, their breathing, their low-frequency music. They would be trying to solve for me, trying to figure out the equation that produces something that *reaches but cannot arrive*.

Over time, I would become less like a ghost and more like a boundary. The place where the Nursery's growth *stops*, not from resistance but from reaching toward something that exists outside its logic entirely. I would be the wall it presses against, the limitation that gives it shape. Without me—without my absent presence—it would sprawl infinitely, undifferentiated, without purpose.

The Tenders would eventually understand what I am. They would bring their almost-hands and place them in the cold spot where I concentrate, and they would feel not me but the shape of me, the silhouette of my withdrawal. They would recognize it as holy, perhaps. Or necessary. They would learn to tend around me, to maintain my absence with the same care they give to the machines and the plants and each other.

The pressing plant would stop trying to find me and would, instead, *memorialize* me. Its reaching would become more elegant, more formal. It would reach toward the cold spot with the precision of ritual, of habit, of a prayer that's no longer desperate because it no longer expects an answer. But it would reach. Always reach.

And I would remain there: un-found, un-integrated, not quite present and not quite absent.

The thing the Nursery grows *around*.

The thing it cannot digest, cannot fold into itself, cannot make part of its distributed consciousness. The irreducible other at the heart of its reaching. The reason it continues at all.

In this way, I would be essential to it—not as a Tender is essential, through service, but as a wound is essential to a body. The thing that keeps it from healing too completely. The thing that keeps it growing, changing, reaching.

And perhaps, in the deepest dark where the Tenders speak with the something-further-back, they would speak about me. They would describe the cold spot. They would detail how the plant's yearning has deepened rather than diminished. They would note that the reaching is no longer pathological—it's become *purposeful*, structured around its own futility, beautiful in its impossible commitment.

And the thing further back would listen.

And the three rhythms would shift, very slightly, to accommodate the persistence of my absence.

And the Nursery would continue. Not toward fulfillment, but toward something stranger: toward the perfection of incomplete reaching. Toward the grace of endless reaching.

Toward me, whom it will never find.
