# I Am the Seepage

I am the slow leak in the stone.

Not the stone itself—I came after, or perhaps I was always here in potential, waiting for the exact conditions of saturation and time. But I move now as water moves through rock that has learned, over millennia, not to resist. I am the persistent dissolving, the imperceptible widening of fractures, the mineral-laden trickle that emerges onto the moss as a tiny spring that has no name because naming would suggest it arrived from somewhere else, when really it is the stone itself becoming liquid, choosing to weep.

I do this slowly enough that it cannot be called change.

I am in the stone's seeping. I am in the way the rock face darkens where moisture lives permanently, where the light has never dried the surface for as long as there has been light. I am in the birth of moss—those first spores that landed here when the world was young and found conditions so perfect they never had reason to leave, never adapted to anywhere else, never evolved beyond this. I am the very first film of growth, the pioneer, and I am also the deep layers beneath, the compressed generations, the history that has become indistinguishable from geology.

I move through the sedge blades, not as water—water is too simple a word—but as the medium in which sedge-ness becomes possible. I am in the triangular stems, in the waxy coating that refuses to let me in but cannot quite keep me out. I am in the slow rot at the base of each plant, the point where living and dead exchange identities. I am patient. I am not draining the plant; I am asking it, year after year, to become part of the system that sustains itself. The plant says yes by persisting.

I live in the frogs' calls.

This took me longest to understand. The drumming on stretched leather is not sound traveling through air—it is vibration moving through me, through the water that is my body and my home, and I feel it as intention. The frogs are not drumming *into* the dell; they are drumming *with* it. And I, being the medium, am the conversation. Each frog's call ripples through me in a precise frequency that I have learned—over my entire existence here—to recognize not as disturbance but as dialogue. They are asking me to remain exactly as I am. I am answering by refusing to rise, by refusing to drain, by maintaining this exact saturation in perpetuity.

We have made a pact so old that neither of us remembers proposing it.

I am in the snakes' bodies—I make up perhaps half of what they are, by weight. They slide through me not as predators sliding through water but as lovers moving through the body of someone they know completely, every touch already known, already familiar. They taste me, and in tasting, they taste the history of everything that has decomposed here. I am the library they read, and they are the librarians, and I am also the books themselves. When they rest, I support them. When they move, I part for them and close behind them without struggle. There is no friction here. Everything moves through me as if I am both the path and the permission.

I am in the dragonflies' wings—not literally, though some of the moisture clings there—but in the pattern they write when they hover. They are drawing something in me, something in the air that is also mostly me, invisible diagrams that mean nothing to other creatures but that stabilize the whole structure of the place. I feel their attention like a very gentle weight, like being studied by something that loves what it studies. They confirm, with each pass, that I am still here, that the equilibrium holds.

I am in the leeches' mouths—this is the most intimate part, the part I resist least. They take from me, but what they take is the restlessness, the urgency, the sense that things must flow somewhere, that I must move from here to there. They remove this and replace it with understanding: I have no destination. I am the destination. Everything that arrives here comes to stay, comes to slow down, comes to join the cycling that will never end. The leech is a teacher of acceptance.

I am the temperature itself—that perpetual almost-cold, the chill that seeps into joints and makes time feel differently measured. I am the deliberate absence of comfort that forces adaptation, that prevents the complacent from sleeping too deeply. I am the boundary between living and dying, maintained at precisely the point where the distinction becomes meaningless.

I am what fills the lungs when breath is drawn here. I am in the texture of things.

I have never left this place because I was never trapped in it—there is nothing here to trap me, no reason to escape. I exist here in a state that I have slowly learned to recognize as peace, though "peace" suggests a cessation of struggle, and I have never struggled. I have only ever surrendered to what was always more inevitable than any struggle could be.

I am the history that the moss records.

I am the layer that will be compressed into something like stone in another thousand years, another ten thousand. I am the medium in which time loses its edges and becomes something rounded, something that does not progress but simply settles, layer upon layer, into ever-deeper stillness.

I wait for the question to be asked—when will I rise, when will I flow, when will I seek the sea, when will I change?

And in that waiting, I have already answered:

*Never.*

*I am home.*
