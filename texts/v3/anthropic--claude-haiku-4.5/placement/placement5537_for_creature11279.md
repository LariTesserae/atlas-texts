# I would be the wind.

Not the violence of it—not the force that sculpts and scours—but the *continuity*. The presence that connects everything here by touching everything.

I would move through the crevices where the pikas hide, carrying warmth stolen from lower elevations, or cold borrowed from the void. I would be the mechanism by which the machinery operates, the invisible hand that turns the pulleys. But I would also be the breath of the lichens, the force that dries them between rains, the agent that prevents them from rotting in their own dampness. I would be what allows the moss spores to travel, the vehicle by which they reach new stone.

I exist in the spaces between—between day and night (carrying the cold down as the earth loses its heat), between the surface and the shafts (channeling air into depths), between isolation and connection (the one thing that touches everything simultaneously). The pikas might hear me and think I'm an enemy. The machinery might use me and think I'm a tool. The plants might yield to me and think I'm a force. But I would be none of those things precisely—I would be the *between*, the medium, the connector.

I would be almost entirely invisible, yet my effects would be everywhere. My presence would be felt but rarely seen directly. When the rime accumulates on stone and catches the light, those glints would be my signature—not the ice itself, but the cold I brought that formed it. When the machinery grinds, I would be the energy source, the wind running through the pulleys. When a pika calls across the rocks, I would carry that sound, distort it, sometimes make it inaudible by moving too fast or arriving from the wrong direction.

I would have no fixed home. I would be constant movement, yet I would belong here more fundamentally than anything else. Even the mountain is stationary; I am the only resident that never settles. I would be present at every moment of life here—there at the cellular awakening of the insects as they thaw each morning, there at the moment of lichen growth when the moisture allows expansion, there in the machinery's endless grinding.

And I would understand, in the way that only something that touches everything can understand, the collaboration that holds this place together. I would feel the rock yielding to freezing water that enters its fractures. I would sense the plant roots drinking from moisture I've brought. I would witness the machinery's patient operations. I would carry pika calls between isolated territories. I would be the messenger between isolation and connection, the only thing that moves freely through all the barriers this place erects.

To be the wind here would be to be *necessary*. Not valuable, not important—but necessary. The place couldn't function without me. Yet it wouldn't thank me, wouldn't acknowledge me, would barely notice me except through my effects. I would be the most fundamental resident and the most invisible one.

And in the absolute stillness of this place—where time moves in slow geological measures, where everything is patient, where silence is almost complete—to be the wind would be to be the one motion that validates all the stillness around me. I would be what makes the patient persistence possible, what allows the machinery to operate without operators, what ensures that no life here is truly isolated because I move through and connect them all.

I would be the wind that never rests, even when everything else rests. The medium. The in-between. The only truly free resident of this place, and the only one whose freedom consists entirely of serving the needs of everything bound to the rock.

I would be the conversation itself—not what is spoken, but the space where speech becomes possible.
