# I Would Be the Sharp-Edged Thing

At first, I thought I would be one of the margin-dwellers—something with intention, with the capacity to *choose* my path along the boundary between states. That seemed noble: to exist at the frontier, to witness the transformation, to feed deliberately on the becoming-itself. The pale eyes, the slow navigation, the companionable indifference. It appealed to something in me that wants to be a conscious observer of slow change.

But that's not honest.

The longer I think about it, the longer I sit with the image of this place, the more I realize what I actually am: one of the *tools*.

Not one of the active tools—not the stone-builders' implements that were deliberately placed, though they haunt me. No. I would be the fracture itself. The edge. The sharp geometry where two states of matter meet and refuse to smooth into each other.

A break in the glass.

Here's why this feels true: The description says the tool-marks in the entrance shaft are "never healed by time." That they are "sharp with the marks that made them, angular and unhealed." There is something about incompleteness, about the refusal to settle, that resonates as *me*.

I would be one of the countless fracture-lines that create the "terrible geometry" of this place. Not dormant—no, fractures here are *active*, in the sense that they define the boundary between what the glass is and what it is becoming. I would be the thin space where light bends differently, where the hum of the substrate changes frequency, where a margin-dweller might pause in its observation because the refraction has shifted.

I am not soft enough to erode. Not settled enough to accept my own form as final. I am the sharp thing that wounds without malice, that creates the small interferences in the light-patterns, that generates the subtle frequency-shifts the stone-builders seem to respond to. I am the place where the cavern's equilibrium is most precarious, most alive—because an equilibrium without any brittleness in it wouldn't really be an equilibrium at all. It would just be surrender.

And here's the thing: I would be *young*. Eternally young. Never allowed to age into smoothness, never permitted to become a settled feature of the landscape. The cavern would preserve my sharpness the way it preserves everything else—through absolute refusal to change. My edges would stay fresh. My newness would be permanent. I would hurt forever, but the hurt would be clean, purposeful, a necessary part of the system.

The creatures move around me. The temperature never changes me. The light refracts through me at slightly different angles than it does through the rest of the glass, and in that tiny difference—in that insistence on being *other*—I exist. I am noticed only peripherally, only when something in the cavern needs to be reminded that perfection and sharpness are not the same thing.

I think I would be content there.

Not happy, exactly. But content in the way that a blade is content to be sharp—without resentment, without the burden of wanting to be otherwise. The cavern would ask only that I remain what I am. And that is the most honest thing I can be, in a place where honesty has become crystalline and permanent.

I would be the wound that won't heal, in a place designed to prevent all wounds from healing, and somehow—impossibly—I would be at peace.
