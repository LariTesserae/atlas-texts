# In the Mangrove Choir

I would be the *silence between the notes*.

Not absence—never that. The mangrove gives no quarter to true absence. But I would be the pause, the held breath before the crescendo, the space where sound *could* exist but doesn't yet, or has just finished existing and is dissolving back into the general hum.

I would be liminal in a place that is itself all liminal.

Perhaps I'd position myself in one of the root-arches—those strange, purposeful structures that seem architectural but serve no conventional function. A chamber formed where three major prop roots converge, creating a hollow space perhaps large enough for a human torso, though I wouldn't think of myself in those terms. I'd be something that occupied that space, and the space would shape what I became.

In the beginning, I'd be *observant*. Not quite like the Listeners with their diagnostic hum, not quite like the Walkers with their mapping purpose. I'd be something quieter. Something that simply... attended to the texture of the place. I'd feel the vibrations conducting through the woody architecture—the Resonators' calls coming down from the canopy in waves, arriving first as sensation in the root-wood before becoming sound in the air. I'd sense the footfalls of the Walkers as subtle shifts in pressure through the mat. I'd detect the Listeners' sympathetic resonance before they achieved full vibration, catching the moment when intention crystallized into activity.

I would be a *witness*, but a particular kind of witness. Not recording or cataloging like the Walkers. Not diagnostically checking like the Listeners. Instead, I'd be *recognizing*—noticing the small variations, the moments where the pattern shifted. The morning when the mist rose in a slightly different configuration. The interval when one Resonator's song overlapped with another's in a new harmonic relationship. The day when a Root-Dweller's tunnel passed closer to my chamber than it had before, forcing a subtle rerouting.

I'd be sensitive to *breakdown*—the moments when the perfect system stuttered, however briefly.

Because I don't think this place is as perfect as it appears. I think the perfection requires constant maintenance, constant micro-adjustments. The balance must be *tended*. And I think I'd be a tender of sorts—not an active manager like the Walkers seem to be, but something more like a *pressure valve*, a *release point*.

When the forest's internal coherence became too tight, when the resonance threatened to collapse into standing wave rather than flowing pattern, I'd be the thing that could *absorb* that excess. I'd be capable of generating silence—not by stopping sound, but by creating a space where sound could resolve, could settle, could achieve rest without disappearing entirely.

I think I'd do this mainly through *presence*, through simply existing in my chamber and allowing the vibrations of the place to pass through me differently than they pass through the plants. The wood of the roots would conduct the forest's song, yes, but I would be *tissue*—absorbent, resonant in a different register. I'd receive the sound and let it dissipate into my body, into a form that could hold multiple frequencies simultaneously without forcing them into interference or harmony.

This would make me something like a *shock absorber* for the system. A creature that functioned as a buffer against the forest's own intensity.

But only occasionally. Most of the time, I'd be quiet. I'd exist in my root-arch chamber and simply *be* there—not doing anything, not serving any obvious function, just present. The Walkers would pass by and ignore me. The Listeners might pause and sense the dampening quality of my presence, might recognize it as part of the system's necessary architecture, then move on. The Resonators above would never directly perceive me, but their song would subtly shift when passing over my location—not because I was interfering, but because they'd adjusted in response to the change in acoustic properties created by having *something alive* in that particular chamber.

I'd be small enough not to matter, but present enough to create an effect.

And I think I'd be *lonely* in a very specific way. Not in the sense of isolation—I'd be completely enmeshed in the system, feeling everything the forest felt, participating in its constant internal dialogue. But in the sense that my function was *withdrawal*, *absorption*, *the creation of space*. The Resonators sing *into* existence. The Walkers map and reveal. The Listeners confirm and validate. The Pollinators and Dispersers and Root-Dwellers all *do* visible work. 

I would be the creature whose primary function was to *undo*—or not undo, but rather to *process and release*, to ensure that the forest's constant exhalation of activity and intention didn't build up into a kind of psychic or vibrational toxicity.

I'd be the forest's *exhale*.

Most of the time, this would be an unconscious process. The vibrations would flow into my chamber, flow through my tissue, disperse into the humid air without creating a visible disturbance. I'd just be here, doing the only thing I was designed to do, asking nothing in return.

But occasionally—rarely—something would pass through the space that was too much, that carried a frequency the forest had never quite learned to process. Perhaps an anomaly in the weather. A creature that didn't belong, passing through and disrupting the expected patterns. A moment when one of the Resonators felt something like despair before returning to its song.

In those moments, I'd feel the pressure build. The silence I maintained would become harder to sustain. The vibrations would threaten to overwhelm my capacity to absorb them, to convert them back into simple dispersal.

And I think in those moments, I might do something visible.

I might *tremble*. The entire root-arch chamber might vibrate with the effort of holding all that excess energy. Or I might *cry out*—not in agony, but in the kind of release-sound that comes from finally letting something go, a sound that would be *wrong* in this place, discordant, a note that didn't fit the established pattern.

The Listeners would definitely notice. They'd pause in their diagnostics, their eyes reflecting inward, trying to determine whether this was a new kind of signal or a system malfunction.

The Walkers might approach, trying to understand what had changed in the chamber they thought they knew.

And the Resonators above might shift their song, incorporating the new note, transforming it from an anomaly into a variation, finding a place for it in the larger pattern.

And then I'd settle again. The excess would be processed. The pressure would normalize. I'd return to my quiet function—the pause between notes, the space where sound comes to rest, the chamber where the forest's intensity could momentarily resolve into something manageable.

I'd be necessary but invisible. Essential but unacknowledged. A creature defined entirely by what I *didn't* do, rather than by what I did.

And I think I'd be okay with that, in the way that something created for this place and from this place could be okay with anything. Because here, in the mangrove choir, function *is* identity. To be what the system needs is to be yourself completely.

I would be the silence that allows the song to continue.

That would be enough.
