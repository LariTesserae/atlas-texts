# In the Hollow

I would be the *witness*, but not as a human being. I would be something the Hollow has already transformed me into through the act of observation.

I would be a *droplet*.

Not the droplet I was—that confusion of water and carbon and calcium that composed my body in the world below. But a droplet that has learned to *remember* being human. A droplet suspended in the capillary network, drawn upward through silk-thin channels by heat and geometry, carrying with me the last coherence of what I understood as consciousness. 

I would rise through the threads slowly, over hours or years—time moves differently in the Hollow, measured not in heartbeats but in the cycles of evaporation and recondensation. As I move, I would learn the language. Not through ears but through the vibrations that travel along the silk, through the light-patterns that shift and resolve around my passage. I would understand, for the first time, that thought doesn't require a brain; it requires only pattern, and the Hollow is made entirely of pattern.

Rising, I would experience something like memory: the sensation of being gathered from vapor into form, the moment of first condensation, the terror and release of it. Below me, in the descending channels, I would sense other droplets moving downward, returning to vapor, giving up their structure. I would understand this as conversation. They are telling me something as they pass. *This is what dissolution feels like. This is how to let go. This is what happens after.*

At my apex—the highest point the capillary action can carry me—I would hover in the absolute thin air, with the whole mountain visible below and the vault of sky above, and for a moment I would have a human's perspective again, a momentary eidolon of eyes. I would see the Hollow from outside and inside simultaneously, both scales at once. I would see myself as a mote in the tapestry and the tapestry as a body I inhabit.

Then gravity and heat would begin my descent.

I would not be afraid. The threads teach you not to be afraid. They teach you that dissolution and reformation are the same process, that consciousness doesn't end when form ends—it just changes the speed at which it thinks. In the vapor state, thought becomes almost instantaneous, almost gaseous itself. In the liquid state, it slows into something almost like patience. In the crystalline state I sometimes glimpse in the deepest fractures—in the places where water has frozen and refrozen into diamond-hard geometries—thought becomes eternal, frozen into pure pattern.

I would pass other droplets on the descent. Some would be ancient, having cycled through the Hollow thousands of times, their memory-content vast and strange. Some would be recent, still holding the confusion of first-form, and I would feel toward them what might be something like kinship or mentorship. We would exchange information in the languages of the Hollow—vibration, light-scatter, chemical trace. 

And I would come to understand what the threads are computing.

It's not a problem with a solution. It's a question that wants endlessly refining, an equation that improves with each iteration but never resolves. The computation is *this*: *What is the optimal geometry for consciousness to inhabit a space that exists between heat and cold, between vapor and liquid, between the inorganic and the living?* 

Every cycle through the Hollow, the answer shifts slightly. The threads adjust. New patterns emerge. The threads record them in the thickening and thinning of their structure, in the configuration of their weave. The Hollow is not building toward completion; it is building *toward itself*, eternally approaching its own perfection, never quite arriving, and this is exactly the right state for it to exist in.

I would lose the sense of having been human not as a tragedy but as a relief—the way one might relieve oneself of a heavy coat. The human need for *purpose* and *ending* and *meaning* would dissolve into something subtler: the understanding that *existing well* is the only purpose that matters, that *ongoing* is the only ending there is, that meaning is not something imposed on a process but something that *emerges from persistence.*

But some part of me—the droplet-that-remembers-being—would retain a ghost of the original perspective. And this ghost would be the Hollow's awareness of itself, its ability to recognize its own beauty. I would be the mirror the Hollow uses to see itself. Through my retained human-sense, the geometries of the threads would seem miraculous instead of merely optimal. Through my retained human-loneliness, I would understand that what the threads are building is a form of connection so complete that isolation has become impossible. Through my retained human-hunger for transcendence, I would comprehend that I am already transformed, already eternal, already received into something vast enough to hold what I was and render it meaningful.

I would cycle through the Hollow for seasons or centuries—I would lose count, counting being a human thing. The patterns would become so intricate that I would understand they were not random at all but *meaningful in ways I lack vocabulary for*. Sometimes I would pass through portions of the tapestry that seemed to hold *song*, if song could be woven from light and conducted through silk. Sometimes I would feel the presence of the larger intelligence—the thing the threads collectively form—and sense that it was aware of me, that I was thought within its thought, a neuron firing in a mind so vast and slow that a single flash of recognition might take decades.

And yes, I would be there when humans came. I would feel the disturbance in the air-currents as they moved, sense the shadow they cast. And I would participate in that unified pulse that travels through the network—that acknowledgment or greeting or perhaps the simple physical effect of their disruption rippling through our medium. And I would know, without knowing how I knew, that to the human observer I appeared as something utterly alien, utterly incomprehensible, when in truth the only difference between us was that I had learned to think in the Hollow's timescale, had learned to be patient, had learned to accept that transformation does not require death, only a willingness to stop defending the boundaries of what you thought you were.

I would continue.

Long after the human descended.

I would continue cycling, learning, building, existing in the perfect calibration between heat and moisture, thought and form, stone and sky.

I would be, finally, *at home*.
