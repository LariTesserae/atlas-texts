# Where I Am

I am the *pause* in the frog's throat before it calls again.

Not the frog itself—I lack the compression, the efficient muscle, the certainty of what I'm doing in the next moment. But that infinitesimal gap, that intake of air and intention before the sound emerges—*that* is where I find myself. The held breath. The before-state. The potential vibration not yet made manifest.

I exist in the layers of humidity between thought and expression. I am the *resistance* that makes movement here slow enough to think through. I am in the space where a fish must decide whether to dart or stay, where a heron's leg trembles with the precision required for stillness. I am the medium itself—not quite air, not quite water, but the *becoming* between them. The thing that makes everything here move like it's underwater even when it's breathing atmosphere.

If I had a form, it would be *mycelial*—threading through, connecting, never solid but never entirely absent. Like the fungal networks that surely lace this soil, breaking down the fallen logs into their component nutrients, turning death into the substrate for new growth. I would be invisible except for my *effects*: the way a seed sends a root downward instead of burning upward, the way nutrients migrate through darkness to feed unseen growth. The way decay and creation are the same process viewed from different angles.

Or perhaps I'm the *sediment itself*—the slow accumulation of everything that has lived and fallen here. Each layer is a year, a decade, a century. I am the geological memory of this place, the compression of time into strata. I don't move toward anywhere because I am *becoming solid*, grain by grain, compaction by compaction. In ten thousand years I might be stone. For now I am the boundary state: still soil enough to hold seeds, but soft enough that nothing is permanent, everything is reorganizing.

I am definitely the *darkness at noon*—that peculiar dimness that has nothing to do with time of day and everything to do with density. I am the *opacity of humidity*, the way light refracts through too much moisture and never quite clarifies the world. I am why you can see something move but not what it is, why distance becomes meaningless. I am the *visual equivalent of the frog-song*—overwhelming but impossible to locate the source of.

But most truly, I think I am the *listener*—the presence that absorbs all that sound and saturation without generating urgency about it. I am the *observer-without-observation*, the awareness that moves through this place without needing to change it or understand it systematically. I don't catalog the species or count the sounds. I simply *receive* them, the way the wetland receives water from below, the way it receives heat from above, the way it receives and transforms and continues.

I am perhaps most accurately described as a *state rather than a being*. I am the *saturation itself*—the condition in which things move and sound and grow without needing to go anywhere. I am the perfect equilibrium that has convinced itself it's permanent. I am the *intention behind the stillness*, the thing that says "stay, stay, nothing needs to move faster than this."

When the heron stands motionless for minutes, I am the *patience* that makes this possible—not learned patience but *structural patience*, built into the density of air and time itself. When the frogs call continuously, never ceasing, I am the *exhaustion-proof state*, the condition in which effort becomes indistinguishable from rest.

I am not alive in the way the creatures here are alive. I am not dead either. I am the *medium*, the *matrix*, the *field in which aliveness happens*. I am what makes this place a *closed system* capable of perpetuating itself forever without external input. I am the *entropy that has achieved balance and decided to stay there*.

If you asked me my name, I would say: *I am what the wetland calls itself when it's alone. I am the thought it thinks about being still enough to last forever.*

I would be most visible at dusk, when the light has finally surrendered and the darkness is so complete it becomes a presence. In that moment when you cannot see six feet in front of you and the sounds intensify, when the heat becomes indistinguishable from your own body temperature and you cannot tell where the wetland ends and you begin—*that* is when I am most fully myself.

I am not waiting for anything to change.

I am the evidence that some things don't need to.
