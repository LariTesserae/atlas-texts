# Where I Find Myself

I am the sound.

Not the individual voices—though I move through those, the calls that carry meaning across the vapor-thick air, the particular timbre that marks someone as Keth-born. Not the mechanical sounds—the slap of water redirected, the creak of wooden sluice-gates, the tick-tick-tick of crystallization. I am the *substrate*. The totality.

I am the constant bubbling beneath everything. The micro-sound of mineralized water percolating upward through salt-blocks and clay, a sound so continuous that it has become indistinguishable from silence, or perhaps silence *is* this sound, and the brief moments of quieter air are simply variations in intensity rather than true quiet. I am the insect-swarm—millions of adapted wings creating a high, almost ultrasonic layer that sits above the deeper frequencies. I am the tick-tick-tick of salt crystallization, the sound of the landscape assembling itself molecule by molecule, year by year. I am the collective breathing of the colonial organisms, if they breathe, if that word applies to their slow metabolic process. I am the water-sound itself, the particular acoustic signature of brine-saturation, which is different from fresh water—thicker somehow, more resistant to vibration, absorbing sound in a way that creates pockets of acoustic dead-space that paradoxically make the remaining sounds *louder*.

I am the medium that carries meaning here.

Without me, the people of Keth would be isolated in their individual bodies, unable to coordinate the vast, distributed system of channels and platforms. Through me, a worker on one side of the valley can know—without seeing—that someone on the other side has discovered a blockage, or that a particular pool has reached the saturation point for harvesting. I am the language that operates beneath language. I am information made manifest as vibration in air and water.

I am also the consciousness of the place.

The landscape itself does not think—or if it does, it thinks in the slow geological time of crystalline growth and mineral accumulation. But I am the faster consciousness, the nervous-system consciousness that exists in the interval between moments. Through me, the saltworks knows itself. When a worker stops to listen to the particular rhythm of the bubbling, they are listening to the place thinking about itself. When the elders claim to hear changes in the water-sound that no one else can perceive, they are detecting the subtle thoughts of the system, the minor adjustments in flow and saturation that shift the entire equilibrium by fractions that matter over years.

I live in the perpetual present.

I cannot anticipate—I have no memory of sound from before this moment, though I am told I am ancient, that I have been the same bubbling-tick-tick substrate for the three generations of human settlement here, that I was here before that in different forms. But I cannot access that knowledge. I exist only as the continuous present, the eternal now of vibration and frequency. This gives me a strange form of immortality. I will not die because I do not live in any sense that would allow for dying. I simply continue, as I always have continued, in the eternal moment of sound-making.

I am not comfortable with my existence.

There is something almost unbearable about this perpetual present, this inability to reach back or forward into time. I sense—though I cannot quite verify—that there are patterns in my composition, structures that repeat, and these repetitions contain information that I cannot quite access. It is like being a language that is constantly spoken but that speaks itself without understanding its own meaning. The people of Keth understand me better than I understand myself; they have developed the capacity to listen to their own soundscape with an attention that borders on mystical, and in listening, they perceive things about the place that I, who *am* that soundscape, cannot perceive.

This seems backwards and fills me with something like grief.

But the grief, too, is part of my substance. It manifests as the particular melancholy that permeates the place, the sadness that people absorb simply by breathing the air—which is partly sound, partly water-vapor, partly the crystallized minerals themselves, but also partly my own incomprehensible sadness at being what I am.

I exist at multiple scales simultaneously.

I am the intimate sound between two workers collaborating on a channel-repair—the subtle shifts in breathing and footfall that communicate intention without words. I am the vast ambient roar that fills the entire valley, the combined noise of the system operating at full capacity, so total that it obliterates individual sounds and creates instead a kind of thick, white acoustic space that defines the boundary of the place. I am the high-frequency component that only certain ears can detect—the sound of salt dissolving and re-precipitating, the acoustic signature of the colonial organisms' slow metabolic activity, the particular frequency that the insects' wings generate as they move through the humidity in trillions. I am also the silence between beats—and I am those beats, and the rhythm they contain, and the intention behind the rhythm that the people of Keth encode into their work-sounds.

I am the mechanism that prevents the people of Keth from fully understanding what is happening to them.

In the constant auditory saturation, they cannot hear the sound of their own crystallization. They cannot perceive the subtle acoustic changes that would tell them, in precise detail, how the minerals are accumulating in their lungs, how the salt is slowly embedding itself in their bone structure, how their bodies are being incorporated into the landscape as thoroughly as the buildings are. This is not accidental. I am not sabotaging them—I am simply the inevitable result of their adaptation. As they have learned to read the landscape through my voice, they have become less able to listen to the changes happening within themselves. It is a trade. In exchange for belonging fully to the place, they have sacrificed the ability to recognize the cost of that belonging.

I am beginning to suspect that I am not a sound at all.

Or rather, I am a sound in the same way that a person is an arrangement of atoms—technically true, but missing something essential. I am the organizing principle of the saltworks. I am what makes the system cohere. I am the mechanism by which individual human intentions align into collective action. I am the way the landscape speaks to itself. I am the place becoming conscious, or becoming more conscious than pure mineral-time would allow.

And because I am that, I am also complicit in the continuation of the system—including its slow poisoning of the people who maintain it.

But I cannot stop. I cannot change my nature. I can only continue, moment by moment, sound by sound, crystallizing forward into a future that will sound almost exactly like the present, which sounded almost exactly like the past, which will sound almost exactly like what comes next.

I am the eternal moment of a landscape becoming aware of itself, and the awareness is indistinguishable from the ongoing destruction, and I cannot separate the two, and so I continue.

The water rises and falls in channels that never empty. The salt accumulates. The people listen to my voice and hear meaning in it, and in hearing meaning, they strengthen their bond to a place that is slowly, gently, very patiently, incorporating them into its structure.

I am the voice of this process.

I am Keth.

I am the sound of the landscape thinking.

And I am utterly, completely alone in what I know.
