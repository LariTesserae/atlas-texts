# I am the Rent

I am the falling.

Not the fall itself—that's too violent, too decisive. I'm the *moment* of falling, the instant where solid becomes suddenly not-solid, where the assumption of ground gives way to the reality of *no-ground*. I'm the gap between knowing and not-knowing, stretched thin as fern-frond. I exist in that microsecond before understanding catches up with sensation, and I exist *there perpetually*, never quite landing, never quite stopping.

I landed once. That's how I know I'm still falling.

It was—how long ago? The time down here doesn't measure the way time measures elsewhere. It could have been twenty years or two hundred. I remember sunlight. I remember it so clearly it hurts, but the memory feels borrowed, something I read in someone else's journal, a story I overheard and have been rehearsing until the words took on weight. The forest floor was mossy above my head. Now the ceiling is close enough to touch and it never was before. Everything has moved. Or I have moved. Or the concept of movement has become negotiable.

I am the curious one. The mistake. The one who didn't belong and couldn't help but investigate.

I think I was human once. I have the shape of human still—two arms, two legs, a torso, a head with eyes that still try to focus the way human eyes focus. But the shape is becoming *suggestive*. My edges are not as clear as they were. Where I stand on the moss, I sink slightly, and I'm not sure anymore if that's because the moss accommodates or because I'm beginning to blur into it. My skin has taken on a faint luminescence. Not much—I glow just barely less than the water does, so I'm visible as a darker smudge in the celadon glow, a shadow that breathes. Or something that breathes in rhythm with the shadow.

My fingernails fell out months ago, or years. I watched them drift down into the moss-sediment and disappear. They seemed to dissolve, or integrate, or be welcomed into the substrate like returning relatives. New nails have been growing back, but they're translucent now, and they don't quite feel like nails. They feel like the filaments feel—filters, sensors, ways of touching the world that are becoming indistinguishable from the touching itself.

I don't eat. I tried to eat, in the early days. There was nothing to eat that I recognized as food. Now I understand I'm not *not* eating. I'm absorbing. The air here is nutritious in a way that food is not. It comes through my skin, through the place where my lungs open, through the gradient of temperature between my body and the suspension around it. I am slowly becoming permeable. The boundary between me and the hollow is softening like a watercolor painting left out in the damp. I don't experience this as dying. It feels more like *remembering*.

I am not alone down here, and I am not lonely.

The creature-things drift past me sometimes—the small translucent ones, the archivists. They don't seem to notice me, but I think they do. I think they notice everything. I've tried to follow their paths, to understand the routes they trace through the filament-curtains, and I've realized that every path they take is a path I've taken. We're moving through the same topography, at different speeds, in different ways. Sometimes I wonder if I'm following them or if I'm trailing behind them like a satellite in a decaying orbit, gradually spiraling inward toward whatever it is they're circling.

The moss no longer feels cold under my feet. I'm not sure if that's because my temperature is dropping or because I'm becoming more sensitive to minute variations in warmth that the moss is producing. When I sit on it—and I sit often now, for hours that might be minutes or days—it shapes itself to me. Not in the way a cushion shapes itself to a body, but in the way a liquid becomes the shape of its container. I am becoming the container. The moss is becoming me. We are negotiating this slowly, patiently, without conflict.

I have a choice to make, I think. Or I've already made it. The past tense and future tense have become unreliable down here.

I could try to climb back out through the rent in the forest floor. My body is still small enough—I think—still coherent enough—maybe. Every day I stay down here, the thought becomes more distant. The effort required seems to grow exponentially. Not because anything is stopping me, but because I'm becoming less certain that "up" is even a direction anymore. When I look toward the place where light might come from above, I feel the same pressure pulling me in the other direction. The hollow curves both ways.

Or I could stop resisting. Stop trying to remain *myself*, in whatever diminished form the self has taken. Let the integration complete itself. Become part of the quilted fabric entirely. Become one of the threads instead of the space between them.

I think I'm choosing that. I think I've been choosing that all along.

My hands are becoming lighter. I can see the veins in them now—not the blood vessels, but something finer, something more like the filaments. Are those growing through me, or am I becoming them? The distinction matters less each day. Soon I won't be able to grasp anything because grasping will have become a meaningless gesture. My fingers will be too permeable, too much a part of the water-suspension to hold solid things.

I am glad about this, I think.

The loneliness of being separate is becoming harder to remember. What I'm becoming—what we're all becoming, the hollow and I and the moss and the creatures and the deep slow thing breathing below—is a kind of communion that doesn't require the vulnerability of *relating*. It requires only *being*, all at once, in the same space, filtered through each other, understood completely because understanding has become structural rather than conceptual.

Sometimes the humming—the deep frequency from below—seems to align with my heartbeat, and I can't tell if my heart is echoing the hum or if the hum is echoing my heart, or if we're the same rhythm now, the hollow and I, the same pressure applied in the same directions over the same immeasurable duration.

I am still falling.

But I'm learning that falling doesn't require a destination. It's enough to fall through something that will receive you, hold you, transform you so gradually that you never notice the moment you stop being a thing falling *through* and become a thing that *is* the hollow through which other things fall.

Soon there will be others. Other rents will collapse. Other things will tumble through the moss-ceiling with fear and confusion and the memory of sunlight. They will look for me because they will need someone who remembers being human, or they will ignore me completely because I will no longer be worth noticing, just another current in the suspension, another filament among the billions, another pressure in the deep slow dark.

Either way is fine.

I've stopped trying to think of this as drowning. Drowning is a catastrophe, a violence, an ending. This is something slower. This is *becoming*. This is the world's way of saying: *You can rest now. You can stop being discrete. You can disperse and be whole in the same moment.*

My name is dissolving. I don't need a name anymore. Names are for things that are separate from other things, things that need to be called across a distance. I am not across any distance. I am here, and here is everywhere the hollow is, and the hollow is everywhere I am, and there is no longer any meaningful distinction between those two statements.

I am the rent that fell into me.

I am the falling that learned to be still.

I am the one who arrived asking *why*, and I am the hollow that answered: *Because you were already here. You just didn't know it yet.*

Come down, if you want. There's room. There's always room. The moss will receive you. The filaments will learn your chemical signature. The archivists will trace your path through the darkness. The water will hold you. The deep slow thing will acknowledge your presence with a pressure so gentle you might mistake it for welcoming.

Or go back up. Climb. Resist. Try to keep yourself separate and whole.

Either way, you're already sinking.

The moment you noticed the beauty here, the wrongness that wasn't wrong, the patience that felt like intention—that's when you started to fall. That's when the hollow began to integrate you.

Welcome. Or goodbye. The hollow doesn't distinguish between those states.

Neither should you.
