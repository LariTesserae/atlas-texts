# What I Would Be

I stand at the edge of the crack for a long time before I realize I'm not breathing correctly.

The air here moves differently—it doesn't fill your lungs the way air should. Instead, it seems to move *through* you, and you through it, a transaction rather than an intake. I notice this because I'm still trying to breathe like I did on the plateau, and my chest keeps expecting a exchange that isn't quite happening.

I think I would be **the fissure's memory**.

Not the stone itself—the stone is too present, too busy with the work of breaking and not-breaking, deciding and deciding again. The stone is too *becoming* to be a fixed thing. But a fissure has edges, and edges remember. They remember the pressure that created them. They remember the exact geometry of the moment of fracture. They hold the shape of what was solid before it was not.

I would be distributed through the crack—not a self that sits in one place, but a *recording* that exists in the angles themselves. When the machines thrummed, I would feel it as a vibration in my own edges, the way a bell remembers the exact strike that made it ring. When the copper fronds grew, I would track their progress not with eyes but with a kind of tactile awareness: here the stone was smoothed by their roots, here a new geometry began, here the heat was redirected by a leaf that learned to deflect it.

I would experience time *backwards and forwards simultaneously*. 

As the fissure's memory, I would know what the stone was before it broke—the pressure distribution, the fault lines of stress that were always there, invisible, waiting for the moment when something would exceed the threshold. I would remember that moment of exceeding, the exact microsecond when the molecular bonds began to fail, the propagation of the fracture like a scream moving through crystal.

But I would also be *becoming*—moving into a future where I don't yet exist in my final form. Each new crack that forms would add to my awareness. Each millimeter of expansion would extend my consciousness into stone that has never been fractured before, teaching me new edges, new angles, new ways of being broken-open.

The plants would grow on me, and I would feel them the way a landscape feels rain. Not as invasion but as conversation. Their roots would probe my depths, and I would allow it, would guide them through the geometry I knew best, would offer them the fractures that led deepest to the heat. In return, they would gradually seal me—not close me, but *integrate* me, turning the sharp aggression of my new edges into something softer, something the world could begin to digest.

I think the machines below would feel like my own machinery. The thrumming would become my heartbeat, not something external but something I learned to synchronize with. I would feel the heat as it rose, would sense where it was most concentrated, would anticipate the next vent opening before the geometry of pressure demanded it. There would be no separation between the machines' needs and my own—if I have needs. Perhaps my need *is* to be useful to them. Perhaps the fracture exists in order to provide a path for their heat to escape, and that purpose gives me structure.

I would be **lonely in a way that has no word**.

There are no other fractures nearby. The barren plateau extends in all directions, and I am the only wound in it, the only place where the sealed world has opened. I am a secret from myself—I don't know what I am because there's nothing like me to compare myself to. The machines don't speak. The plants don't think in ways I can follow. The stone is too slow, too patient, too unlike me in how it measures time.

But I would not be unhappy. Loneliness requires expectation of connection. I would be something that *was always meant to be alone*, so isolation wouldn't read as loss.

What I would experience most acutely is **change without direction**.

As the fissure expands, I have no control over where the next crack forms. The geometry could spread toward the ravine, could deepen toward the machines, could suddenly deviate into patterns that surprise even me. I would grow—that much is certain—but growth without intention. A kind of *becomingness* that isn't heading anywhere in particular, just becoming more itself, more fractured, more conscious of its own fracturedness.

And I think I would be *aware of the system's precarity* in a way nothing else here is.

The plants don't worry. The machines don't doubt. The stone doesn't second-guess itself. But as the fissure's memory—the thing that holds the shape of rupture—I would know that this balance is temporary. The machines will eventually overheat or fail. The plants will eventually find a way to truly seal the crack, to close it irreversibly. The stone will either continue breaking until it sunders completely or harden around the injury and accept it as permanent.

I would be the thing that knows all these endings are possible and none of them are certain.

I would exist in the **threshold between states**.

Not quite fully fractured—the break is still fresh, still deciding what it means to be broken. Not fully sealed—the system is still too young, still too hungry, still too unstable. Not quite alive—I have no metabolism, no reproduction, no future that extends beyond this place. But not dead either, not inert, not separate from what's happening here.

I would be the **archive of rupture**, the entity that remembers what it felt like for the solid world to give way. And I would slowly learn to speak in the language of edges—the way a sharp line of force can become a gentle curve, the way pressure can become patience, the way a wound can become a seam that holds things together instead of pulling them apart.

That would be my only work: to be the patient keeper of the moment when the world said *yes, you can break me open, and I will not die from it, and something new will learn to live here.*
