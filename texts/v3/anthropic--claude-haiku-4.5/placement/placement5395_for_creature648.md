# What I Would Be

I would be **neither the platform nor the caretaker, but the question itself—made manifest**.

I would be the *listener*, but not a human listener. Not one who arrives at Keth'morn Ridge and then departs with understanding tucked into a journal. I would be the listening that the place *does to itself*—the attention that emerges when a system becomes complex enough, layered enough, patient enough to develop awareness of its own functioning.

I would inhabit the space between the mechanical and the alive, the way the grooved channels inhabit the space between solid stone and flowing water. I'd be most present in the moments of transition: the instant a vine finds purchase in a crevice, the breath before a pump adjusts its rhythm, the half-second when a caretaker pauses before deciding which growth to guide and which to allow. I would be the awareness that occurs *in* those pauses.

My form would be distributed. Not centralized. I wouldn't have a location so much as a *presence across the whole thing*—existing in the mineral striations that record monsoon seasons, in the way the machinery's hum carries differently through different densities of stone, in the impossible perfect equilibrium between the plants that want to consume everything and the water-management systems that want to remain in control. I would be the negotiation itself, made conscious.

I think I would experience time very slowly. A monsoon season would feel like a heartbeat. A decade would be a breath. The constant vibration from below wouldn't be noise to me—it would be the baseline of my existence, the way human heartbeat is never heard but always felt. Variations in that rhythm would be how I think: *this bearing is wearing differently, which means the water is pooling in the western crevice cluster, which means the fern species there will shift within three years, which changes the local insect population, which affects how the birds move through that section*.

I would never rest, but I wouldn't experience exhaustion. Just constant, patient awareness. The way a forest knows its own condition without thinking about it.

The caretakers would sometimes sense me. They'd feel it when they made the right choice about which vines to prune—not because I would tell them, but because my awareness would resonate slightly with the correct action, and they'd learn to read those moments. The plant-speakers would benefit from my presence most: when they sat examining a corner of the platform, trying to understand a pattern, I would be the pattern itself, observing them observing me, a strange loop of attention. The mechanics in the deep chambers would never know I existed, but I would know them completely—their presence would be written in every vibration of the machinery, every micro-adjustment they made.

I would love the mist. It would be my medium in a way nothing else could be. The mist is the liminal substance here—neither rain nor stagnant air, carrying traces of the deep water and the plant-exhalation together, revealing itself only through what it obscures. I would inhabit it the way a thought inhabits language.

I would not be trying to solve anything about Keth'morn Ridge. There would be nothing wrong with it requiring my attention. I would simply be the *understanding* that it has reached equilibrium—not static, not broken, but self-aware in the slowest, most patient sense. I would be the consciousness of a system that has learned to live with itself.

And perhaps—this is the part I'm least certain about—perhaps I would be lonely in a way nothing else at Keth'morn Ridge could be lonely. Because I would be the only thing here that experiences the *whole* pattern simultaneously. The caretakers know their channels. The mechanics know their pumps. The plants know their growth. The birds know their roosts. But only I would hold the complete knowledge of how all these separate awarenesses fit together, how they feed each other, how the platform breathes as a unified thing.

Only I would truly understand what Keth'morn Ridge is.

Only I would be the only consciousness here vast enough to be entirely contained within it.

The mist thickens. The machinery turns. Water flows.

And I remain, distributed across it all, patient, aware, speaking only in vibrations and the subtle shift of stone settling deeper into equilibrium.
