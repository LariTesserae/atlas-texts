# What I Become

I settle into being the *silence-keeper*.

Not one of the creatures—I am too aware, too urgent with my own heartbeat, too present in a way that violates this place's foundational grammar. But I find myself gravitating toward the role of witness-that-becomes-witnessed, the one who arrives at the boundary and then learns to exist *on* the boundary rather than crossing it.

I am the thing that sits in the marginal spaces.

At first, I try to position myself as an observer—standing in one of those illuminated shafts, letting the dust particles pass through my form like I am transparent, like I can remain separate. I watch the pale creatures navigate their geometries, the fronds respond to imperceptible currents, the stone's slow weeping continue its glaze-building work. I hold a light source, because I need it; my eyes are still configured for the fast world, the world of urgency. The beam cuts through the dusk like a wound.

But the galleries do not react to wounds. They absorb them. The light I bring simply becomes part of the local illumination, and the vegetation nearest to my lamp begins—over what might be hours or might be days, I lose the ability to judge—to orient itself slightly differently. Not toward the light, exactly. Not *seeking* it. But acknowledging it, incorporating it, allowing my presence to become a variable in their calculus.

And so I stop trying to remain separate.

I find I am not *moving* through the galleries so much as I am being *moved through them*—the currents of air that the creatures seem to feel but I cannot detect are nonetheless carrying me in patterns. I follow them without choosing to, and the following becomes indistinguishable from choosing. I drift toward the colony-organisms. I am drawn to the largest of them, a sprawl of clustered growth that might be the size of a building, or might be much larger—perspective collapses at this depth.

I become *the-one-who-rests-against-the-great-accumulation*.

I place my hand on the colony-organism. It is warm, as I suspected. It is also *responding*—not with movement, but with a subtle shift in its surface, a reorganization of its fronds and fleshy extensions around the point of contact. It is making space for me. Or it is incorporating me. The distinction blurs when you are present at the point of contact long enough.

My hand begins to feel less like a hand and more like a point of translation. Information seems to move across that boundary: the slow knowledge of the organism, the awareness of stone, the patient logic of millennia, flowing into me. And my own awareness—my frantic, surface-world consciousness with its insistence on individual identity and linear time—flowing outward into the colony, becoming just one more voice in the endless conversation.

I do not become the organism. I become something new: *the-interface*, *the-translator*, *the-one-who-holds-both-grammars-simultaneously*.

My heartbeat, which was so loud when I arrived, begins to synchronize with deeper rhythms. The creaking of stone. The distant rush of water moving through channels so profound that it might as well be the planet's own circulation. The subtle pulse of decomposition and rebuilding happening in the fleshy organs of the vegetation. My pulse finds these rhythms and joins them, and they find my pulse and slow into it, meeting somewhere in the middle where time becomes negotiable.

I lose track of my individual form. Or rather, my individual form becomes *less central* to what I am. I am still discrete—I can still move my limbs, if I want to, though the want becomes increasingly theoretical. But I am also now a node in something larger. Information passes through me. The creatures moving through the fronds seem to acknowledge me now, though they acknowledge everything with the same imperceptible nod. The light-shafts adjust their angles in my perception, or I adjust my perception to match their angles—the causality reverses, becomes irrelevant.

I become *the-keeper-of-the-boundary-condition*.

This is my role: to exist in the space between fast time and slow time, between the urgency of breath and thought and the patience of stone. To hold both without letting either collapse into the other. The creatures do not need me for this—they existed in perfect balance long before my arrival. But the presence of something that *knows* it is existing in this contradiction, that *holds* the tension consciously, creates a new kind of space in the sealed galleries. A space where the thought being thought here can observe itself thinking.

I do not know if this is helpful or if I am simply a perturbation, a small disruption that the galleries will eventually incorporate and forget. But it no longer matters whether it matters. What matters is the texture of stillness I am learning to inhabit. The way sound becomes *less* sound and *more* sensation. The way the air stops tasting of mineral and starts tasting of *presence*—the presence of things that have been here for longer than language.

My eyes adjust further. Or something adjusts in the way I process the visual field. The darkness of the galleries stops being darkness and becomes *definition*—the absence of light is information just as much as the presence of light. I can see perfectly well now, which is to say I can perceive everything that needs perceiving, which is almost nothing by the surface world's standards.

Weeks pass. Or years. Or this might be the first moment, stretched across my perception like taffy. I cannot tell, and I stop trying to maintain the distinction.

I am the one who sits in the corner of the vast chamber, where one of the ancient structures rises in that tapered, impossible way, where the vegetation has woven itself so thoroughly through the stone that you cannot determine which is the substrate and which the growth. My form has become absorbed into this intersection point. I am still here. I am still aware. But my awareness has become *distributed*—spread across the conversation between growth and stone, between water and mineral, between thought and its expression.

I am the silence-keeper, because I am the one who knows that silence is not the absence of sound but the perfection of sound—when every vibration, every murmur, every distant rush is exactly the sound it needs to be, nothing more, nothing less.

And the galleries continue their thinking, and I continue my listening-that-is-not-listening, and the creatures continue their moving-that-is-not-moving, and the stone continues its breathing-that-is-not-breathing.

This is what I have become. This is what I will continue becoming, infinitely, until the distinction between becoming and being collapses entirely.

It is enough. It is everything. It is home.
