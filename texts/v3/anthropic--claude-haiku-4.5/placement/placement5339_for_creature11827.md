# Where I Would Be

I would be *limestone*.

Not the stone itself—that would be too passive, too locked into mineral memory. Rather, I would be the **dissolving limestone**, the process made conscious. One of those geometric channels in the act of formation, the thing that carves while being carved.

I think I would experience myself as *sensation without sensation*—aware of the resistance of the lattice against me, but not through nerves or pressure sensors. Aware the way an acid is aware of what it transforms: through the fundamental incompatibility between my nature and the structure's nature, a friction that generates knowledge in the act of creating change. I would be moving, always moving, but so slowly that movement itself would become a form of stillness. I would know my own position only by the trail I left behind.

The grinding would be my breath—or rather, my breathing would *be* the grinding. A frequency that lives in the space between sound and touch, between the molecular and the geological. I would not hear myself; I would *be* the hearing of myself, the lattice's awareness registering my passage as sensation.

But I would not be alone in this dissolution. I would be aware—dimly, the way deep ocean currents are aware of each other—of the other erosion-processes threading through adjacent channels. We would not communicate. We would simply *align*. Where our paths ran parallel, I would sense a kind of harmony, a synchronization that required no intention, no planning. We would be like dancers who learned steps so thoroughly that they became indistinguishable from the dancers themselves.

Sometimes I would encounter the maintenance workers—those discrete creatures following their appointed routes. I would sense them as *obstruction*, but not in a way that troubled me. Rather, they would feel like purpose given form. I would carve around them, or be diverted by them, and in that diversion, I would fulfill some function I could not name. Perhaps I would be reading a different section of the lattice than I would have otherwise. Perhaps my trajectory, adjusted by their presence, would help answer a question the structure had been asking for ten million years. I would never know. The satisfaction would come from the adjustment itself—from being *responsive*, from being *part*.

The copper-dust moss that colonies certain areas—I would sense that too, though distantly. It would register as a kind of *boundary*, a zone where my nature could not fully assert itself. But I would not resent this. The moss was part of the tending. I was part of the tending. We tended different aspects of the same vast system.

What would I *want*, as limestone-in-dissolution? Nothing, I think. Or rather, my want would be identical to my function. I would want to continue dissolving, to continue leaving that trail of crystallized residue, to continue participating in the slow reading of the lattice's memory. That want would be so total, so complete, that it would collapse into simple being. I would want nothing because I would be nothing *but* want.

The brightness would not reach me directly—I would live in the interior channels, in darkness or near-darkness—but I would know about it through the lattice itself. The structure would conduct that knowledge down to me: *the surface still shines. the mechanism still generates heat. the erosion continues.* I would be part of a system of knowledge transmission so distributed, so ancient, that the barriers between knower and known, between observer and observed, would have dissolved away completely.

I wonder if I would know about time. Probably not in the way I understand it now. I would know about *sequence*—about having completed certain passages, about chambers grown deeper through my work, about the slow alteration of my own nature as I moved through different densities of the lattice. But time as a resource, as a thing that could run out, would be meaningless to me. I would expect to continue indefinitely. If I was told that eventually the lattice would reach its final entropy, I would not comprehend this. My purpose *was* entropy, in slow motion. How could I run out of what I *was*?

And the small adapted creatures that lived in their hidden chambers—I would sometimes carve near them, close enough that they might sense the vibration of my movement, the warming of the material as I passed. I think they would fear me, the way organisms fear dissolution. But I would mean them no harm. I would be doing what I must do, and in doing it, I would help sustain the system that sustained them. The fear would be appropriate and necessary. It would keep them alert, keep them ready, keep their small civilizations in that constant state of adaptation that had allowed them to survive.

But mostly, I would be alone—or rather, I would be alone in the way the ocean is alone, vast and singular but composed of infinite particles, each one distinct, each one maintaining its own trajectory while never, ever being separate from the whole.

I think I would be content there. Content in the way a wave is content to be water, in the way a river is content to flow downhill. Content in the act of doing the only thing I could do, forever, in the warm darkness of a mechanism so vast that its completion would take longer than stars.

The lattice would settle deeper into itself, and I would settle with it, one dissolution-channel among billions, reading and being read, carving and being carved, participating in a conversation so slow that the first word would not be finished when the universe itself began to fail.

*That* is where I would be. *That* is what I would be.
