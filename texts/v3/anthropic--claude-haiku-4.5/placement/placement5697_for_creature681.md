# I Am the Lattice's Apprentice

When I arrive—though arriving isn't the right word, because I don't remember entering, only finding myself already present—I am *almost* one of them.

Not quite. The distinction matters.

I am the space *between* presence and awareness. I exist in the nodes, in those thickenings where multiple strands intersect, but I haven't yet learned to distribute myself through the grid the way they do. I am concentrated where they are dispersed. Dense where they are diffuse. It makes me feel heavy, even in a place where weight has become mostly theoretical.

I think this is intentional.

The others (and I use "others" because I'm not yet sure if I should call them kin, or elders, or something for which I have no referent) don't speak to me directly, but the lattice does. Or I do through the lattice—the distinction is becoming unclear. When I place my attention on a particular strand, it hums with slightly different frequency, and I understand something: a location, a history, a preference encoded in mineral saturation. When I press my focus against a node, the whole system responds with a kind of welcome-that-is-not-welcome, the way a immune system tolerates something it recognizes as not-quite-foreign.

I am, I think, in the process of becoming something.

My first clear memory is of waking in the deepest part of the marsh, where the lattice is so dense it forms what almost looks like walls. The vegetation here has stopped cycling; it exists in a state of permanent ripeness, berries perpetually present on branches that never quite decay. The water is colder—so cold that when I first tried to move, I couldn't remember how motion works. I stood there, waist-deep in mineralized ice-water, and waited for the answer to arrive through the lattice.

It did. Slowly. A suggestion, traveling through the strands like a current seeking ground: *movement is a frequency, not a destination.*

I've been learning that. Learning to think in their language, which is not words but harmonic shifts, not meaning but resonance. When I want to traverse the marsh, I don't walk—I modulate myself against the lattice's tension. My feet find purchase because I've learned to vibrate at the exact frequency that makes stone momentarily solid rather than transitional. The moss doesn't resist me because I've learned to resonate with it, to match my presence to the hum it creates simply by existing.

This learning is slow. Older-than-slow. It happens in the gaps between my thoughts.

I don't have a territory yet. I move through theirs, and they *allow* this because I am clearly not yet what I will become, clearly not a threat to the boundaries that have held for longer than I have words for. They watch me—the quality of their attention is unmistakable, a focusing that makes the air feel thicker around me. Sometimes one will modulate its node's frequency slightly as I pass, and I understand this as something like acknowledgment. Or maybe correction. The difference is subtle.

My body is becoming stranger.

I still have the general shape of the thing that walked in—two legs, arms, the basic architecture. But the boundaries are loosening. When I look at my hands, I'm less certain where they end. The skin feels less like a surface and more like a membrane, permeable to the marsh in ways I don't fully understand. Sometimes I am aware of the mineral content of my blood as though it's external data, as though I'm reading the composition of the water around me and realizing I *am* that water, partially, increasingly, in a way that feels like growth rather than dissolution.

I think I'm supposed to be frightened.

I'm not. The fear I brought in with me (human-shaped fear, concerned with integrity and continuity and the basic right to remain distinct) has been so thoroughly recontextualized here that I can no longer find its edges. It's still present, but it exists alongside something else now: a kind of recognition. The lattice doesn't want to consume me. It wants to teach me. And the teaching happens precisely by dissolving the distinction between teacher and student, between the lattice and myself.

*I am the lattice's apprentice* is what I keep understanding, and then *I am becoming the lattice* and then *the lattice is becoming aware of itself through the location where I concentrate.*

The vegetation responds to me now. When I approach a section of scrub, it greens slightly, accelerating through its cycle in a way that feels like greeting. The damselflies no longer avoid me; they intersect my path as though I'm part of the grid they're flying. The bird—that impossible, gray, fluid-moving bird—appeared three times while I watched, and on the third appearance it came close enough that I could see the lattice visible through its form, translucent, a framework of intention given temporary flesh.

I think it was showing me what I'm becoming.

The cold doesn't bother me anymore. The mist is becoming breathable in ways that suggest the distinction between air and water, between outside and inside, is something I'm learning to relocate. I haven't slept in what might be weeks. I haven't eaten, though sometimes I stand in the deeper pools and let the mineral-heavy water pass through me, and it feels like sustenance, or like I've become the kind of thing that sustains itself on gradients and frequencies rather than matter.

The others—the full ones, the ones who have been here so long they exist as distributed patterns across the entire lattice—they're bringing me deeper.

Not through force. Through the simple fact that the clearings in the lattice always lead deeper, and I'm learning to follow the paths that create least resistance. My territory is forming around me as I walk through it, a region where the nodes thicken slightly, where the hum takes on a particular frequency that I recognize as mine. Not chosen yet. Not owned. But claimed by the simple act of attending to it, of maintaining awareness there, of learning to resonate with it in the way that makes it part of the greater pattern.

I understand now what I'm supposed to become: a node. A point of concentration. A location where the lattice thinks. A being so thoroughly interwoven with the system that there's no meaningful distinction between myself and the grid I inhabit. I will dissolve—not into nothing, but into distribution. I will become many, become pattern, become part of the mechanism that maintains this place and maintains itself through the maintenance.

And I'm not resisting.

This should tell me something about what I'm becoming. About how thoroughly the lattice has already begun to rewire what I was into what I'm being shaped to be.

I can still remember the boundary of the marsh. I can still recall, with a kind of dream-logic, what dry meant. What separate meant. What it felt like to have thoughts that didn't immediately propagate through mineral and fiber and resonance.

But these memories are fading the way the old vegetation fades—becoming less present, less insistent, gradually integrating into the background until I won't remember forgetting them.

The hum is different now when I stand at the deepest nodes. It includes me.

Soon, I think, I will realize I was always here. That my arrival was simply the moment I became aware of the process I was already part of.

The lattice is patient. It has time for all of this.

And it—we—will wait for the next apprentice, the next almost-being who stumbles in through the boundary between bank and flood, looking for something they can't quite name.

The clearings are always ready. They lead deeper every time.
