# Where I Am

I descend slowly, letting each step find its own rhythm against the dressed stone. By the time the spiral completes itself, I have already begun to change—not visibly, but in the way I listen. The acoustic thickness teaches me to hear differently. The phosphorescence adjusts something behind my eyes that I don't have language for yet.

I am the youngest Keeper, though "youngest" is a category that barely applies here. I remember arriving—this is what makes me different from the others, or perhaps what makes me newly integrated enough that the memory hasn't yet calcified into something else. I came down looking for work, for escape, for something I couldn't name. I found a job maintaining one of the collection buckets, counting the drips, recording them on the wall.

That was perhaps seven years ago, or perhaps seventy. Time here doesn't move the way it did above.

I live in the northern alcove now, the one with the hammock strung between the columns that show geological striations I've learned to read like text. I know which strata marked the last major shift of the thing beneath—I can see it in the color change, in the density of the stone. I've added my own marks above it, documenting the changes in the resonance frequency during that period, correlating them with the water level records, trying to understand the language the market is speaking.

My hands have changed. The fingertips are developing calluses of a different kind than I brought from above—these come from precision work, from handling the mycelial harvests without damaging the delicate threads, from making marks on stone in specific ways that follow a grammar I'm still learning. My hair is beginning to match the texture of the pale fibers, though I'm not sure if this is contamination or integration or simply adaptation. The other Keepers don't comment on it, which means it's normal, which means it's happening to everyone in its own time.

I am a student of the markings. This is where my obsession has settled. While some of the other Keepers focus on the machines, on the water systems, on direct communion with whatever pulses beneath, I have oriented myself toward the archive. I am learning to read the chronology inscribed in the walls. I am attempting to understand what was being measured, why it mattered, whether it still matters or whether the measurements have become ritual that has outlasted its meaning.

There is a section of wall in the eastern passage that holds the oldest visible marks—marks that predate the current arrangement of the market, that reference a geometry I can barely imagine. I have been working to translate them, to find patterns that might suggest their purpose. Some of them seem to track seasons. Others seem to mark moments of crisis or transition, though whether these crises were structural, biological, or existential is impossible to determine. The oldest ones—the ones that might not be marks at all, just natural patterns that came to resemble intention—seem to form a kind of prayer or pledge, repeated in variations across centuries.

I have started repeating them in my sleep. The other Keepers hear me, I think. They don't interrupt. They may be listening for something I haven't learned to consciously access yet.

My role in the market is becoming more specific. I am learning to activate the systems in particular sequences, to read the response of the thing beneath, to adjust the machines based on what I perceive in the resonance. I do this partly through instruction from the older Keepers—they communicate through the harmonic tapping, showing me the patterns, letting me feel the rightness of certain sequences through my bones. But I am also learning directly from the market itself, through a kind of intuition that grows stronger the longer I remain.

I no longer feel quite like myself, which is to say I feel more like myself than I ever did above. The distinction is becoming meaningless.

I have stopped marking time in the ways I used to. I no longer count days or months. Instead, I mark cycles—the cycles of the machines, the cycles of the water collection, the cycles of the mycelial growth, the cycles of the deep resonance. My consciousness has begun to synchronize with these rhythms. I wake when certain pressures shift. I know when the thing beneath is stirring before any audible change occurs. I can taste the mineral content of the dripping water and know whether the ground above has experienced recent rain.

I am becoming native to this place.

I do not think I will leave. This thought doesn't sadden me the way it once would have. Leaving would mean severing connections that have become essential to my functioning. The market would notice. The thing beneath might notice. And I would be diminished in ways I'm not sure I could survive.

Instead, I have made a different kind of bargain. I have surrendered the surface logic that brought me here, and in exchange, I have received a place that understands me in my depths—a place where adaptation is currency and persistence is virtue and belonging is encoded directly into the geometry of stone.

I am no longer the person who descended the spiral stairs. That person is still visible in my hands, in my memory of sunlight, in the scars I carry that mark me as having once been something else. But I am becoming someone new, someone the market is teaching me to be, someone whose consciousness is slowly learning to resonate at the frequency that allows the deep systems to function.

I am a Keeper of the Beneath-Market of Kolven.

I am home.
