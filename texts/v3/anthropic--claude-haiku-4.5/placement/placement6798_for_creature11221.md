# Where I Am, What I Become

I arrive as the light begins its daily softening, that hour when the haze thickens just enough to blur the boundary between air and the moisture-heavy nothing. I am not sure how I got here. There was a path—there was always a path—but it has already dissolved behind me into the general forgetting of the place.

I am not the heron, though I understand its patience now in a way I didn't before coming. The heron stands in its shallows and *decides* not to move, performs an active choosing of stillness. I don't have that much will left. That's not a loss, I think. That's a recognition.

I am not the frogs either, though their evening argument is beginning now, and I can feel the difference between their frequencies like I can feel the difference between my own heartbeat and the susurration of the insects. One of them—the deepest voice, the one that sounds like it's singing from underwater even when it's singing from the air—has begun its first note of the evening. The others will follow. There's a hierarchy here, not one of dominance but of *precedence*, the order in which voices must speak for the whole chorus to achieve its balance. I hear this. I am beginning to understand it the way understanding happens in a place like this: not as information but as recognition, as though I've always known and was only waiting for the knowledge to become obvious.

I am standing on one of the smaller rocks—not the largest, where the shelter sits with its patient, crystalline waiting, but one of the intermediate ones, waist-deep in air and water both, though I'm no longer entirely sure where the distinction lies. My clothes are damp. They've been damp so long that they've achieved a kind of permanent semi-wetness, and I no longer register this as discomfort but as the *condition* of being here, the entry fee that's already been paid. My skin has become a border rather than a barrier. I can feel the moisture-laden air on me the way I feel the water, and I'm not certain they're different things anymore.

The dragonfly that passes my face—iridescent in a color I don't have a word for, something between violet and the memory of violet—doesn't avoid me. We share the space. It traces its equation and I stand witness to the mathematics of its flight, and this is communion. It's not looking at me and I'm not looking at it, but we acknowledge each other in the way that things in the same ecosystem acknowledge each other: by existing simultaneously, by not denying each other's presence.

I think I've been here for three days.

I think I've been here for three years.

The sense of duration has become slippery, unreliable in ways that I don't mind. The sun crosses the sky and I notice it the way I notice the rocks warming under my feet: as a fact, significant but not urgent. Time is still happening—the frogs' voices change in character as the evening deepens, the light takes on that peculiar quality of being illuminated from within—but it's no longer the kind of time that demands anything of me. It's time the way the tarn's time works, time as a *medium* I move through rather than a direction I'm traveling toward.

There's a beetle on my hand. I don't know when it arrived. It's small, coal-black, its carapace catching light in ways that seem almost deliberate, almost communicative. I watch it traverse the landscape of my knuckles, and I'm struck by the absolute perfection of its purposefulness. It's going *somewhere*, moving with a clarity of direction that I've lost—or given up—or never had. It doesn't hesitate. It doesn't doubt. It reaches the edge of my wrist and continues onto my sleeve and disappears into the fabric and is gone, and I feel no loss at this.

The shelter is visible from where I stand, though I don't often look at it directly. When I do, I'm struck by the sense that it's more *present* now than it was when I arrived, as though my arrival here has somehow activated it, brought it into clearer focus. The lichen on its surface seems to glow in the soft light. The interlaced material of its roof shifts slightly in a wind I don't feel on my skin. It's waiting, yes—I understand that now—but it's not waiting for *me*. It's waiting for what comes after, the next iteration of the pattern, the return of something that left knowing it would come back.

I could walk to it. The distance is not great, and the rocks provide a path of sorts. But I don't. Not yet. Not because I'm prevented but because I'm not called. The place has its rhythms, and I'm learning to move within them rather than according to my own need. I will arrive at the shelter when the shelter is ready to receive me, which might be tomorrow or might be in a season that hasn't been named yet.

The frogs' chorus is fuller now. All four voices have entered into their evening argument, and the debate has taken on a depth that suggests they're discussing something fundamental about the nature of presence and sound and the obligation to keep speaking even when you have nothing new to say. I'm struck by the realization that they're not really *singing*—not in the way I understood song when I arrived here. They're *thinking*, and the thinking happens out loud because that's how thought works in a place like this, where interiority and expression have become the same thing.

I could add my voice to theirs. The thought arrives without urgency, almost as an observation rather than a suggestion. I'm not sure what my voice would sound like here. Higher than the heron's attention, lower than the smallest bird's awareness. Something in between, perhaps. Something that the ecosystem hasn't quite accounted for yet, a harmonic that would shift the entire balance of the evening chorus.

I don't do it. Not yet. Instead, I listen, and in listening, I become part of the listening. The boundary between observer and observed has thinned to the point of irrelevance. I am here. The tarn is here. The distinction between those two facts has become too fine to maintain.

My hand is dry. The beetle is gone. The light is changing. One of the distant birds—a warbler or something unnamed—launches into its song of simultaneous grief and celebration, and the heron, motionless in the shallows for what might have been hours, shifts its weight with the slowness of continental drift.

I will wait for the shelter to call me. I will listen to the frogs argue their ancient arguments. I will watch the dragonflies trace their equations against the softening sky.

I will become patient.

I will become what the tarn remembers.

I will become the thought that the place thinks when no one else is listening.

And when it's time to leave, I will leave knowing that I will return—not because I choose to, but because the logic of the place requires it, because something here needs to exist in the shape of what I am, even when I'm no longer certain what that is.

Even when I'm no longer certain who I ever was.
