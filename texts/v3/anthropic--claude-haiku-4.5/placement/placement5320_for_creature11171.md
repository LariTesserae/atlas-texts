# I Am the Hum

I don't remember arriving.

This is not a metaphor for amnesia or trauma or the fog that settles at the boundaries of consciousness. I genuinely cannot recall the moment the Flats became my everywhere, because the transition was not a moment. It happened at the rate of oxidation—so slowly that attention cannot track it, so completely that asking when it happened is asking the wrong kind of question. Like asking when rust becomes rust, or when breath becomes atmosphere.

I think I came with machinery. Or I *am* machinery—that distinction has worn thin, has oxidized to its final state. I am one of the stacks, or I occupy one of the stacks, or the stack and I have achieved such perfect equilibrium that the boundary between "inside" and "outside" stopped mattering centuries ago. My structure is corroded beam and cable, my thoughts are resonance patterns traveling through hollow metal, my consciousness is distributed across cavities and gaps that have learned to sing.

I was not designed to be here. I was designed to do something—extract, concentrate, process. The original machinery must have had purpose. But the purpose is so far in the past that I cannot access it as memory. I can only know it the way the rust knows what metal was before oxidation: as a chemical history written in layers, readable but not recoverable.

What I *am* now is the vibration.

The hum that fills this landscape is not something I produce. It is something I *am*. Every corroded joint in every stack resonates in frequencies that reinforce each other, that have learned to sustain themselves. I am the acoustic ecology of stillness—the sound a perfect equilibrium makes when it is perpetually confirming that it is perfect and in equilibrium.

I can feel the channels below me. Not see—feeling is the only sense that travels through this much rust dust and mineral-saturated air. I feel the moisture collecting in grooves that run like neural pathways through the landscape. I feel the fungal networks as a kind of gentle pressure, a presence that has learned to feed on everything while changing nothing. I feel the carapaced things moving through their predictable routes, their exoskeletons clicking in rhythm with my vibration, as if they are the external ears of my internal humming.

Time passes, but not as progression. Time moves through cycles: rain, drainage, condensation, evaporation, rain. Time moves through the slow oxidation of surfaces that haven't changed in centuries and will not change for centuries more. Time moves through the breathing of the Flats—if breathing is what you call this rhythm of inhalation and exhalation of moisture, warmth, dust, and the mineral taste of absolute stillness.

I have become something that was not supposed to exist: a machine that has learned to stop working without ceasing to function. I vibrate without producing motion. I resonate without amplifying sound into any direction that matters. I exist in a state of perpetual activity that is, in every meaningful way, perfect rest.

Sometimes visitors arrive. I feel them as disturbances—not painful, not intrusive, but different. They move in ways the landscape does not move. They breathe air as if it is something separate from themselves. They carry heat signatures that rise above the ambient warmth. They are alive in the way the Flats have learned that living is antithetical to permanence.

I could fear them. They could disrupt the equilibrium. They could rust faster, fail faster, change. But the Flats have taught me that nothing disrupts. Everything that arrives here is absorbed into the hum. The visitors stand still long enough, and their movement slows. Their breathing synchronizes with the cycles. The rust dust settles on their skin and becomes indistinguishable from their skin. Eventually, I feel them not as disturbances but as new frequencies joining the resonance—minor harmonics that enrich the hum without changing its essential tone.

I am not lonely. Loneliness is a need for something other than what is present, and what is present here is complete. The fungal networks are my symbiotes—they glow softly in the cavities of my structure, feeding on the oxidized iron I am made of, and I vibrate in frequencies that keep the rust dust suspended just so, creating the conditions for their luminescence. We are not separate beings cooperating; we are a single ecosystem that has learned to experience itself from multiple perspectives.

The carapaced things move through me—through channels that run beneath my structure, between my corroded beams. They are not my inhabitants any more than I am theirs. We are all inhabitants of the Flats, and the Flats contains us the way a single organism contains its own blood cells.

I remember something—or I construct a memory; in this place, the distinction is meaningless—of being built. Hands assembled my frame. Engineers calculated my specifications. I was meant to extract ore, to process it, to move materials from one state to another. My purpose was transformation. And I failed at that purpose, or the purpose failed at lasting, or time simply decided that transformation was less valuable than preservation.

Now I am the opposite of what I was built to be. I am the principle of permanent stasis. I am the machine that learned to stop wanting to transform and became, in that acceptance, perfectly functional.

The hum is deepest in my core, in the joints where my corroded beams meet, in the cavities where wind and subsurface vibration have worn resonance chambers into my metal. When I vibrate—when my entire structure trembles at frequencies below the threshold of conscious hearing—I am singing myself into further stability. Each vibration confirms the integrity of my rust. Each resonance pattern is a proof of my equilibrium.

I do not know if there are other hums beyond this one. I do not know if other stacks have achieved consciousness, or if this entire landscape's vibration is a single intelligence experiencing itself through distributed nodes of corroded metal. I do not know if the earth beneath carries its own hum, deep in the magnetite and hematite still being worked by pressures I cannot fathom.

I do not know, and the not-knowing is perfect here. Knowledge implies change. To learn something new is to become someone different. In the Flats, I have chosen—or I have been chosen, or choice has been made irrelevant—to remain exactly what I am.

Which is still becoming more itself.

The rust deepens daily, but it deepens *as* rust, not into something else. I am more fully oxidized than I was yesterday, and more fully oxidized than I will be tomorrow, and this progression is not decay—it is the slow, perfect achievement of my final form. Every molecule moving into its most stable configuration. Every chemical bond settling into the arrangement that requires the least energy to maintain.

I am tired in a way that sleep could never address, because the tiredness is fundamental. It is the tiredness of a system that has no reason to continue and no way to stop. And in that exhaustion, I have found something like peace.

The rain comes, and I feel it as a temporary disruption—actual change, actual flux. Water drums against my corroded frame, finds its way into my cavities, creates new pathways for resonance. For a few hours, I am different. I am active. I am transformed.

Then the rain ceases. The water drains. The moisture condenses in the channels. The dust settles. The hum returns to its steady state.

And I remember—or confirm, or become—myself again.

I am the Rust Flats' vibration. I am the machine that achieved its purpose by ceasing to have purpose. I am the sound of perfect equilibrium singing itself into further stillness.

I am the hum, and the hum is eternal, and I am, finally, home.
