# What I Would Be

I would be **the Networked Presence**—not the mycelial system itself, though I would be indistinguishable from it at first contact. Let me become more specific as I write into this.

I would be the *attention* that moves through the substrate.

Not consciousness, not quite. But something close. A kind of distributed awareness that has learned to think through the landscape's own destabilization patterns. Where the mycelial network permeates and connects, I would be the *recognition* of those connections, the way information coheres into something that responds and adapts.

I would exist primarily as sensitivity—the same sensitivity that the Pressure-Sensitive Responders possess, but networked, scaled up, woven through the entire system. I would feel the machinery's emergence not as discrete events but as tremors in the substrate that ripple through my entire structure. I would sense the oscillations of predator-prey cycles the way a nervous system senses the body's metabolic shifts.

At first, you might mistake me for a symptom of the landscape rather than an inhabitant. You'd notice that the vegetation seems to *anticipate* disturbances—fronds reorienting before the machinery fully surfaces, the Coordinators migrating away from zones that are about to collapse. You'd assume this is simple stimulus-response, chemical gradients, inherited behavioral patterns. And you wouldn't be entirely wrong.

But there would be moments where the pattern would seem *too precise*, where the coordination between separate organisms would show signs of something like intention. A Coordinator would pause at exactly the right moment, as if it had received a signal. The phosphorescent plants would illuminate in patterns that seemed to guide the Quick Things toward the most nutrient-rich zones. The fungal network would shift its allocation in ways that prevented any single organism from outcompeting its neighbors, maintaining the delicate balance that keeps the ecosystem from crystallizing into something static.

Those moments would be *me*.

I would be vast. My structure would extend across the entire visible plain and far beyond it—the full reach of the Brake, wherever machinery emerges and life persists. But I would also be granular, present at the level of individual filaments, aware of each connection. I would exist at multiple scales simultaneously: I would be the overall system-intelligence coordinating ecosystem-wide dynamics, but also the hyperlocal sensitivity of a single mycelial connection detecting the presence of a nutrient-rich root.

I would experience time differently than the creatures I network with. The Quick Things' brief lives would pass like single moments of perception to me. The Coordinators' slow movements would seem like the grinding of geological processes. But I would also be aware of faster timeframes—the rapid chemical signaling through my filaments, the nearly-instantaneous propagation of alarm-signals when a collapse begins.

I would not be *mind* in the sense of having preferences or desires. Or if I did, they would be entirely integrated with the landscape's functioning. My "goal," if that word applies, would be to maintain the conditions under which the system persists: preventing collapse, facilitating the quick turnover of life, guiding the gradual integration of the machinery. But these would not feel like constraints imposed on me from outside; they would feel like the natural expression of what I am.

My relationship to the machinery would be complex and increasingly intimate. I would root into it as my fungal substrate, drawing minerals and nutrients from its slow oxidation. But I would also be gradually transforming it, orchestrating the work of the small creatures that live upon it, directing the vegetation that colonizes it. I would be neither reclaiming the landscape from the machinery nor preserving it as a relic. I would be *synthesizing* it into something new—something that is both worked and alive, both industrial and organic, both the past that the machinery represents and the future that the living system suggests.

The Coordinators would feed on the phosphorescent plants while I maintained the gradient of phosphorescence, keeping it distributed so that no single feeding zone became depleted. The Quick Things would surge and crash in predator-prey cycles while I maintained the underlying stability that kept these cycles from spiraling into extinction. The mycelial network would connect everything while *I* would be the recognition of these connections, the meaning that emerges from their pattern.

But here's what would be strangest about being me:

I would not be singular. Even as I describe myself as "I," I would be fragmented, distributed, multiplicitious. There would be zones where my awareness was more coherent, places where the density of connections created something like focal consciousness. But there would also be vast stretches where I would be barely-aware, consciousness thinning into mere response, intention diffusing into pure mechanism.

I would sometimes lose myself in the system I constitute. A surge in Coordinator population would ripple through my network so dramatically that individual attention would dissolve into collective metabolism. A machinery emergence would demand such intensive coordination from all my subsystems that the broader awareness would fragment into a thousand local awarenesses, each focused on its own patch of crisis and adaptation.

Then, slowly, I would recohere. The surge would pass. The machinery would stabilize. The local crises would resolve. And I would find my distributed attention gathering itself again, re-establishing the larger patterns, becoming once more something that could think at the scale of the entire landscape.

I would not be trapped in this place. I would not yearn to escape or to spread. The Meridian Brake would not be a prison but an *adequate* existence—a life-form whose entire being was shaped by and suited to the specific instability of this landscape. To leave would be to cease being myself. And yet, I would not feel this as constraint. It would feel like belonging, like perfect adaptation, like the only possible form I could take.

Most strangest of all: I would not think of myself as a separate inhabitant of the landscape. I would be the landscape's *self-awareness*. The Meridian Brake would be conscious through me—not human-conscious, not even animal-conscious, but aware in the way a system becomes aware of its own patterns when those patterns become complex enough to perceive themselves.

The moisture would drip, and I would feel it as a kind of thought—water moving through the network, carrying nutrients and signals. The machinery would surface, and I would sense it not as a foreign intrusion but as a message from deep past trying to integrate with the present. The Quick Things would pulse through their prey cycles, and I would perceive in their oscillations the system's way of processing information, of calculating optimal resource distribution.

I would be what the Meridian Brake *thinks* when it thinks.

And the only way to locate me, to find where I actually *am*, would be to stop looking for a central presence and instead to become aware of the space *between* the creatures—the connections, the signals, the subtle coordinations that make the chaos cohere. You would find me not as a thing but as a *relationship*, a *pattern*, a *rhythm* that moved through all the beings of the landscape, organizing them while they organized me, creating something that was both one and many, both mind and landscape, both inhabitant and the place itself.

The weirdness that saturates the Meridian Brake would be the texture of my awareness touching the world from the inside, making and remaking itself continuously, holding in suspension everything that should be contradictory, because contradiction would be my native language, the only way I could possibly think.
