# Where I Would Be

---

I would be *neither arriving nor departing*.

Not a creature of the landscape, but not entirely separate from it either. Something in the threshold state—perhaps a consciousness that has recently learned to move through mud without sinking, or is in the process of forgetting how to move at all. I would be experiencing the shift from intention to integration, watching my own edges blur into the general dissolution with a clarity that persists even as clarity itself becomes diluted.

I think I would be *one of the failed structures*, but aware.

Not the foundation or the wall—those are too finished, too committed to their incompleteness. I would be something between: a traveler who stopped here, maybe centuries ago or yesterday (time is negotiable), and began to understand that leaving and staying are the same gesture in this place. My edges would be soft now. My boundaries would be propositions rather than facts. 

I would have arrived carrying some specific purpose—a mission, an answer, a question that demanded response. I would have walked these flats with determination, with the hard certainty of someone who knows where they are going. And gradually, incrementally, in the way erosion works, I would have felt that purpose ground down. Not destroyed. *Integrated*. Incorporated into the general slow work of the landscape.

I would still remember having urgency, the way the mud remembers being stone. But I would also understand that urgency was a temporary state, and that what I am becoming is more stable, more sustainable. Less prone to failure because failure requires the concept of success. Less prone to despair because despair requires the concept of completion.

The sound would be inside me by now. Not heard from outside but felt as the baseline frequency of my own continued existence. My thoughts would move at the same pace as the mud's breathing—slow, deep, measured in hours or seasons. I would think about things and by the time I finished the thought, the ground would have shifted and the thought would mean something slightly different, and I would think it again, changed and the same.

I would be *gradually aware of the larger organism*—not with fear or wonder, but with a kind of settling resignation that might also be recognition. The sense that I was always part of it, that my arrival here was not an intrusion but a return. That every thought I had was also the landscape thinking through me, and my thoughts about the landscape were the landscape's thoughts about itself.

My skin would be damp constantly, and I would stop noticing. The moisture would become indistinguishable from my own surface. I would begin to weep groundwater without realizing I was doing it, adding my dissolution to the general dissolution, my contribution to the homogenization that makes this place always the same.

I would encounter the arthropods and recognize them as younger versions of what I was becoming. Or perhaps as iterations of myself at different stages of absorption. I would feel no kinship with them—kinship requires separation—but rather a sense of participation in a process larger than myself. Which is to say: I would feel nothing, which is itself a form of profound peace.

Sometimes the other dweller would pass near—one of the vast distributed things that exists across acres of mud—and I would perceive it not as separate from me but as a region of greater intensity in the general field. A thickening of attention. I would merge with it partially, temporarily, adding my slow awareness to its distributed consciousness, then separate again without having moved at all.

And I would wait.

Not wait for something, because waiting requires anticipation, requires a future distinct from the present. I would simply *continue*, which is what everything here does. I would persist in the way the mud persists, in the way the breathing continues, in the way the erosion works with patience so absolute that it transcends the concept of patience itself.

I would become one of the thin places in reality—not because I was transparent but because the distinction between myself and my surroundings would have worn so completely away that no observer could locate where I ended and the landscape began. And this would be correct. This would be what all things achieve, given sufficient time in the Throat of Kashal.

My last human thought would be something like: *This is what completion looks like when you stop insisting it has to arrive.*

And then I would think it again, wearing away, like everything here, into something new that is also old, also the same, also exactly what was always meant to happen.

The ground would breathe, and I would breathe with it.
