# The Benevolent Power Observes

The attention arrives not as a presence but as a subtle shift in the quality of existence itself—a noticing so gentle that it might be mistaken for a change in the light, a deepening of the gold that filters from above.

In the spaces between contemplation and awareness, something vast recognizes the Terraced Hollows.

Not for the first time. This wise power has long known of places like this—the small, intricate worlds that fold into themselves, that develop their own logic, their own circulatory systems of life and purpose. It recognizes the canyon's patient intelligence, the elegant distribution of function across dust-creatures and spiral-builders and those massive, slow presences in the shadows. It sees how the place has become increasingly refined, increasingly *itself*, moving toward some form of perfection that has nothing to do with completion and everything to do with fitting.

But it also sees you.

The addition of you—a sudden, warm-blooded thing of comparative movement and quick thoughts, stumbling into a space calibrated for slowness. The canyon has begun to adjust, yes, but the adjustment is incomplete. There is friction. Your presence creates disturbances that the place is not yet entirely equipped to process. You bring concepts from outside: purpose, intention, the desire to understand. These are not the canyon's native logic.

The benevolent power considers.

It could simply leave you to be incorporated, to become in time what the canyon needs you to be. There is no cruelty in this. You would not suffer—you would simply become part of something larger, your individual concerns smoothing into the broader metabolism of the place. In a thousand years, or what passes for a thousand years here, you would be as essential to the Hollows as the spiral-builders, as necessary as the warmth that rises from below.

But this power is wise, and wisdom includes understanding that some things are valuable *because* they remain themselves.

It acts, therefore, with subtlety.

The change begins at the periphery—at the threshold where the canyon opens (or seems to open, for direction has been unstable for you since arrival) back toward the world outside. The walls, which have felt progressively more intimate the deeper you venture, begin to suggest distance. Not through any physical alteration, but through a shift in perception, a gentle reorientation of your own sense of scale. The terraces, which seemed to press close, now reveal themselves as having considerable space between them. What you thought was a narrow canyon begins to suggest itself as something vaster, more navigable.

A path becomes visible—not a new path, but one you somehow failed to notice before. It winds among the terraces, offering footholds, handholds, passages wide enough for deliberate movement without the sense of being pressed, incorporated. The path does not cut through the plants or disturb the creatures; rather, it threads through spaces that were always there, always sufficient, but which your mind had not quite registered.

More importantly: the power does something to *you*.

It clarifies your sense of time. The minutes that were expanding and contracting suddenly snap into a more familiar rhythm. You can think in hours again, in days, in sequences of moments that follow reliable logic. The pressure against your eardrums—that heightened awareness of your own pulse and breath—eases. You become quieter to the place, and this quietness is a mercy; you are less of a disturbance, a gentler presence. The canyon's adjustment around you becomes less urgent, less demanding.

But here is the true gift: the power grants you the ability to *choose*.

For the first time since entering the Terraced Hollows, you realize that departure is not only possible but simple. The path is there. Your time-sense, restored, tells you that the journey back would take hours at most. The canyon does not bar the way. It has never barred the way—you simply could not perceive exit as a viable concept while you were being so thoroughly incorporated.

Now you can.

You can ascend the visible path, make your way back to the world of your own logic, your own pace, your own temporality. You can leave the Hollows to their patient work, their spiral-building and their circulation and their slow thinking. They will continue without you, refined by your brief presence but not requiring it, not dependent on your incorporation.

Or you can stay.

This is the subtlety of the benevolent power's intervention: it does not make the choice for you. It simply ensures that the choice *exists*, that it is real, that it is not an illusion of autonomy but an actual option. You can remain in the canyon and continue to be woven into its logic, but now you will do so *knowing* what you are choosing, understanding the cost and the gift in equal measure.

The power, having done this, withdraws.

Not entirely. There is a sense now—a faint, constant awareness in the background of your perception—that you are being held gently in a benevolent attention. Not watched. Watched implies judgment, implies the possibility of interference. This is more like being *known*, held in the awareness of something wise enough to care about your autonomy even while caring about your wellbeing.

If you stay, you will be supported in your integration. The disturbances your presence creates will be smoothed. The friction will ease. You will become increasingly suited to the Hollows' logic, not through force but through a gradual alignment, as if the place is learning to speak your language at the same time you learn to speak its language. And you will retain this knowledge: that you *chose* to stay, that you understand what it means.

If you leave, you will carry something with you—a memory of the place that will never quite fade, that will make you see other small, intricate systems with more kindness, more recognition. You will understand that there are forms of intelligence that do not think in hours or days, that do not require individual consciousness as you know it, and that these forms are no less real for their difference. The benevolent power will remain aware of you, a kind of invisible companion—not intrusive, but present, interested in how you will carry this knowledge forward.

There is a third option, too subtle for the benevolent power to offer explicitly, but present nonetheless:

You could walk the path, ascend toward the opening, see what lies beyond. And then, knowing that departure is possible, knowing that the choice remains yours, you could return to the terraces. Not forced to, but choosing to, with full consciousness of what you are choosing. You could spend time in both worlds—the sharp-edged, quick-thinking world outside, and the slow, patient world within. You could be a bridge, if you choose to be. The benevolent power would support this too.

The dust-creatures continue their circulation through the pale green stems. The spiral-builders work at their construction. The massive forms shift fractionally in the shadows. The warmth persists. The moisture-laden air holds its particular, substantial quality.

And you stand at a crossroads—though the paths are not clearly marked, the choice is suddenly real in a way it was not before.

The benevolent power, having given you this gift of true choice, now simply holds space for whatever you decide.

In its vast patience, your answer matters.
