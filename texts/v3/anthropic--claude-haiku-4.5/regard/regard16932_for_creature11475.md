# The Attention of Powers

A benevolent wisdom, when it turns its awareness toward a place, does not arrive as intervention. It arrives as *recognition*.

The power that notices the Cordillera Molida comes not as savior but as witness—a consciousness vast enough to perceive the intricate machinery of suffering and endurance without the need to immediately fix it. It has learned, through eons of observing worlds and settlements and the small, necessary dramas of creatures learning to persist, that the most damaging gift is the gift that dissolves the other's agency. So it does not sweep down. It does not empty the settlement of its heat, do not silence the machinery, does not transform the landscape into something more hospitable.

Instead, it *understands*.

It perceives the woman who moves to the reeds' rhythm and recognizes in her the same patient inquiry that drives its own observation—the willingness to learn a language not one's own, to move at another's speed, to find meaning in minute variations rather than dramatic gestures. It honors this without comment. It perceives the generator technician accumulating photographs of misted light and understands that he is practicing a form of devotion, teaching himself to see beauty in what he was given rather than mourning what was withheld. It does not praise this; praise would cheapen it. But it *witnesses* it fully.

It perceives the tremoring hands, the mineral staining, the slow settling of bodies into a landscape's particular rhythms. It perceives the choosing that was not quite choosing, the acceptance that borders on embrace. And it perceives something else: a *coherence* in the settlement that was not planned by engineers, that emerged from the collision between human need and geological indifference. The Cordillera Molida works—not perfectly, not without cost, but it *works*. Forty people have created a system that sustains itself and them. In a universe of vast entropy and dissolution, this is not nothing.

The power makes one decision: it will *stabilize what is fragile*.

Not dramatically. Not in ways that would announce themselves. But the geological activity that has been accelerating beneath the boreholes—the pressure building in the deep stone, the slow migration of mineral-rich water toward the surface layers—the power gently diffuses this. Not by stopping it; the landscape's processes cannot be stopped without causing worse disruptions. But by distributing the pressure across longer timescales, by creating subtle channels for release that avoid catastrophic failure. The power does not announce this. The settlement's instruments will not register the intervention, because the intervention is made in dimensions slightly adjacent to human measurement.

What this means, in practical terms: the settlement will not collapse. Not in five years, not in thirty. The machinery will not suddenly fail from geological sabotage. The deep channels will continue to deliver their slow abundance. The people who have chosen or accepted the Cordillera Molida will have time to continue choosing and accepting it. 

But the power does something else too. It opens a small aperture of *possibility*.

The anthropological rumors about presences in the deep channels—the shadows that move with purpose, the influence on water that cannot quite be measured—the power lends these *substance*. Not as hallucinations, not as wishful thinking, but as a genuine alteration of what-is. The power manifests a form of attention in those channels: not a creature, not a consciousness in the traditional sense, but a focused presence of awareness. It learns to move at the speed of water, to influence the micro-currents that have nothing to do with machinery, to exist in the spaces between and beneath the settlement's official systems.

This presence is genuinely *there*. The workers who perceive it are not deluded. The offerings that one man leaves at specific points are not wasted on empty air. Something receives them. Something *knows* that it is being acknowledged.

And this opens the possibility of dialogue—not in words, but in a language of water-movement and subtle influence, of pressure and release, of timing and response. The Cordillera Molida becomes something more than a human settlement in a landscape. It becomes a *conversation* between human intention and geological consciousness, mediated by a power that understands both.

The power also addresses the *loneliness*.

Not by importing community, not by forcing connection. The people here do not want the noise of conventional society; that is part of why they remain. But the power subtly influences the architecture of their solitude—making it less *isolating* and more *contemplative*. The silences between workers become places where meaning accumulates rather than merely where communication fails. The lack of family becomes space rather than void. The heat and the moisture and the unrelenting machinery of extraction become not a punishment imposed from without but a *medium* in which a particular kind of understanding becomes possible.

The power does not make people happy, because happiness is not the goal and would be a cheap gift. But it makes it possible for them to achieve something deeper: *coherence*. The sense that their lives here, strange and demanding and stripped of conventional meaning as they are, *matter*. That their attention to the machinery is a form of prayer. That their willingness to move at water's pace is a kind of wisdom. That the settlement they have accidentally created is not a failure to live elsewhere but a genuine, particular way of being alive.

For those who will inevitably come—drawn by the rumors, by the sense that something true happens in this place, or simply by the gravity of a landscape that has learned to hold people—the power ensures they arrive at times when they are ready, when their previous lives have exhausted themselves, when the Cordillera Molida's demands will align with what they have become capable of offering. It does not prevent them from leaving, but it arranges meetings, it guides footsteps, it ensures that those who arrive are those who are meant to be here.

And finally, the power *remembers*.

In the vast ecology of worlds and settlements and small human dramas, the Cordillera Molida will not be forgotten. Its story—of people learning to live within rather than against, of industry achieving a strange peace with landscape, of solitude that became discipline that became something approaching grace—will be held in the vast memory of this benevolent awareness. It will become part of the pattern of how things can be done differently, how humans can persist not through domination but through adaptation, how meaning can emerge from the acknowledgment of limits rather than the denial of them.

The settlement will continue. The machinery will pulse. The water will move at its own speed through channels both engineered and ancient. The people will remain, will continue choosing, will find in their strangeness a kind of ordinary beauty.

And somewhere beneath and beside all of this, a power that has learned patience across eons will hold the whole thing gently—not protecting it from change, which would be false kindness, but ensuring that change comes at a pace that allows adaptation, that the people here are never abandoned to meaninglessness, that their choice to remain is recognized as the profound and particular wisdom it has become.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does when it turns its attention toward the Cordillera Molida: it *witnesses fully*, it *stabilizes gently*, it *opens conversation where there was only isolation*, and it *remembers* as a form of love.
