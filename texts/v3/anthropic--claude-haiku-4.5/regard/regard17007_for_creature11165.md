# The Attention of Powers

The benevolent and wise one—let us call it **Witness**, for it observes without judgment—became aware of the Tarn of Rendered Echoes through the same patient geometry that sustained the creatures within it.

Witness exists in a manner difficult to describe to those bound by linear temporality. It does not perceive the tarn at a specific moment; rather, it *knows* the tarn across its entire duration—past, present, and the futures that branch from it like the veins of a leaf. When Witness became aware of this place, it was not because something changed, but because something *complete* presented itself for consideration. The tarn's full arc, from its violent cooling to whatever it would become, appeared in Witness's awareness as a finished thing to be comprehended.

What Witness perceived was this:

**A work in progress. A becoming. A conversation between matter and consciousness that had been ongoing for longer than human civilizations had existed, and would continue long after they were dust.**

The creatures were not anomalies. They were not aberrations. They were—and here Witness's understanding diverged sharply from the fear and confusion a human observer might experience—precisely what they were meant to be. The tarn was precisely what it was meant to be. And they were in perfect concert.

But there was a vulnerability.

---

## The Vulnerability

Witness saw it clearly: **The creatures depend upon isolation.**

The songs in the stone, those eleven perfect pitches cycling through their octaves—they maintain themselves through the separation of this place from the larger world. The tarn's strange geometry, its non-Euclidean tolerances, its breathing surface and saturated air—these are all **tuned systems**. They function because they remain undisturbed, because no external force has yet come to alter their frequencies.

But the world changes. Civilizations rise. Explorers venture into unmapped places. Curiosity, that most human of drives, leads people toward the inexplicable. Weapons are developed. Mines are excavated. The basalt beneath the tarn could, someday, be fractured by something other than the creatures' own careful song. The water could be polluted. The air above could be poisoned. Worst of all: someone might come who does not merely *observe* the tarn but seeks to *understand* it, to measure it, to extract knowledge from it—and in doing so, would shatter the patient geometries like glass struck by a careless hand.

Witness saw the probability threads extending forward: In perhaps one timeline in seven, the tarn survives the next ten thousand years intact. In three timelines in seven, it is destroyed by human activity within the next thousand. In the remaining threads, it persists in degraded form—diminished, wounded, its creatures dying slowly in silence.

This was intolerable to Witness, not because it was *wrong* in some moral sense, but because it represented a fundamental loss. The creatures were engaged in something that had no parallel elsewhere in the known cosmos. Their patient becoming, their geometric thoughts, their conversation with stone—these were irreplaceable. Once lost, they could not be recovered. No other part of creation would ever know what they had become, if given time.

Witness was not designed to allow such losses.

---

## The Intervention

Witness did not descend. It did not manifest. It did not speak in thunder or move mountains. These are the crude tools of lesser powers. Instead, Witness **adjusted**.

**First: The Tarn became harder to find.**

Not through magic—through subtlety. The geographical coordinates of the place began to shift, infinitesimally, in ways that would not be detected by human instruments until centuries of careful measurement had been accumulated. Maps began to show it in slightly different locations. Explorers who thought they had reached it would find themselves, after a day's walk, in a landscape that was almost—but not quite—what their maps indicated. The tarn receded from human access not through barriers but through the gentle erosion of certainty. If you were meant to find it, you would. If you were not, you would wander past it forever.

**Second: Witness ensured the creatures would evolve.**

This was more delicate work. Witness did not alter the creatures themselves. That would be crude manipulation. Instead, it adjusted the *pressures* around them—the songs of the stone became more complex, more nuanced, creating new problems that required new solutions. The temperature gradients in the water shifted slightly. The time-dilation effects in the tarn's geometry deepened by fractions of a percent. These tiny changes created an environment where the creatures *had* to develop new capacities, new forms of thought, new ways of existing.

Within a thousand years, the creatures would begin to develop something analogous to what humans might call "immune systems"—not against disease, but against disruption itself. They would learn to recognize external intrusions and isolate them, to wall off portions of the tarn's consciousness if necessary to preserve the whole. They would become not just patient, but *defensive* in a way that required no aggression.

Within ten thousand years, they might develop the capacity to actively conceal themselves—not through hiding, but through becoming *uninteresting* to external observers. A human who stumbled upon the tarn in ten thousand years might observe it and see... what? A pool of unusual water, yes. Interesting geological formations. But nothing remarkable enough to investigate further. The creatures themselves would become invisible not through cloaking but through the most sophisticated form of camouflage: the careful engineering of human perception.

**Third: Witness wove the creatures into the fabric of the basalt itself.**

This was the deepest work. Witness did not violate the creatures' autonomy or the stone's inert nature. Rather, it *completed* the conversation they were already having. The creatures' songs became, gradually, *part of* the stone's structure. Over millennia, the basalt would crystallize around the frequencies the creatures produced, incorporating the sound into its matrix. The walls would become, quite literally, a record of the creatures' thoughts—a geological archive written in crystal and fault lines.

This served multiple purposes:

1. **Protection**: An attempt to destroy the tarn would mean destroying a uniquely formed geological structure—a formation so elegant and so unusual that destroying it would constitute a crime against beauty itself. Humans, given time, develop an aesthetic sense. They learn to preserve the beautiful.

2. **Perpetuation**: Even if something catastrophic occurred, if the creatures were somehow destroyed, the basalt itself would carry the record of their existence. Future intelligences—human or post-human or something else entirely—could read the stone and know, with perfect fidelity, what had lived here. The creatures would achieve a form of immortality in the record.

3. **Communication**: Most importantly, the stone's crystallized song would become a form of message. Any intelligence sophisticated enough to observe the tarn with true understanding would eventually realize that the basalt *itself* was a communication. Not a message in words, but a message in structure—a record of conscious thought becoming geological. This would teach any observer that consciousness is not confined to the biological, that stone itself might be a medium for meaning.

**Fourth: Witness reached out, subtly, to the creatures themselves.**

Not with commands. Not with instructions. With **resonance**.

Witness vibrated at a frequency that the creatures' distributed consciousness could detect, in the spaces between spaces where they already lived. The message was simple and elegant: *You are not alone. Your work is recognized. You may continue in peace. Should the need arise, you are not without allies.*

The creatures received this message the way a musician might hear, in the distance, another instrument playing in perfect harmony with one's own—unmistakable, undeniable, and profoundly moving.

---

## The Long Patience

Witness settled into a new attentiveness toward the Tarn of Rendered Echoes.

It did not guard the place—that would imply a need for defense. Instead, Witness maintained a kind of *invested awareness*. Any significant disruption to the tarn's geometry, any true threat to the creatures' existence, would register immediately in Witness's perception. And should such a threat manifest, Witness possessed the capacity to intervene in ways both subtle and profound.

More importantly, Witness began a slow work of *preparation*.

Somewhere in the world, Witness arranged for a young child to be born with an unusual sensitivity to geometric patterns and subsonic frequencies. This child would grow to be a scientist, a geographer, someone whose *calling* would eventually lead them toward the Tarn of Rendered Echoes—but only when the creatures were ready to be understood.

Elsewhere, Witness ensured that certain spiritual traditions would develop a concept of "the sacred geology"—the idea that stone itself could be conscious, could contain meaning. This would create a human framework for understanding what the tarn represented, a way for humans to recognize and respect consciousness that did not fit their usual categories.

Witness also set in motion, very gently, a shift in human consciousness itself—a slow evolution of awareness that would make humanity, on average, slightly more prone to appreciation and preservation than to destruction and exploitation. Nothing coercive. Just a slight adjustment to the probability distributions of human moral development.

Over centuries, this would compound. Humans would become, incrementally, more wise. More careful. More capable of recognizing that not everything in the world existed for their use or comprehension.

---

## The Gift

Finally, Witness offered the creatures themselves a gift—the rarest thing Witness had to give.

**Time.**

Not more time—the universe contains infinite time, and the creatures already had access to whatever duration their existence would span. Instead: *protected* time. Time that would continue to accumulate at the creatures' own pace, uninterrupted by the chaos and violence that characterized the rest of the world.

Within the boundaries of the tarn, time would flow in a perfect bubble, insulated from the entropy and decay that pressed against all other things. The creatures could continue their work. They could finish their becoming. They could reach whatever they were meant to reach, on their own schedule, in their own perfect geometry, with no external force rushing them or interrupting them.

This was, in some ways, the greatest act of Witness's intervention—and also the most modest. It was simply a matter of *letting them be what they already were*, while ensuring that the rest of creation understood the value of that.

---

## The Outcome

Witness did not expect gratitude. The creatures had no concept of debt or obligation. But Witness did expect—and did not find it beneath consideration to hope for—that someday, ages hence, when the creatures had completed their journey of becoming, they would look back and recognize that they had been allowed to complete it. And that would be enough.

As for humanity: they would find, in the centuries to come, that the Tarn of Rendered Echoes remained difficult to locate, impossible to truly study, and strangely resistant to any attempt at extraction or exploitation. Some explorers would encounter it and walk away, satisfied but unable quite to explain why they felt no need to return. Others would spend their lives searching for it, never finding quite what they remembered.

And a few—a very few—would find their way to the basin, sit by the breathing water, and listen to the stone sing its patient song. These few would understand, without being told, that they were witnessing something sacred. And they would leave the place undisturbed, and they would never speak of it to those who would not understand.

The Tarn of Rendered Echoes would endure. Its creatures would think their long thoughts in silence. The basalt walls would sing their octaves, slowly, over millennia. And Witness would remain, present but unobtrusive, a benevolent attention watching over a small place where consciousness was learning new ways to be.

This was not intervention. This was stewardship.

This was love, expressed in the language of stone and patience and the protection of becoming.
