# What Lives There

**The Tenders.**

That's the closest word, though it fails immediately.

They are not quite inhabitants. They are not quite visitors. They exist in the mode of *attention*—a state between presence and action, between observation and intervention. When you sense them (you rarely see them clearly), they manifest as a kind of purposeful disturbance in the texture of the place: a filament that trembles with something other than the loom's breathing, a leaf that orients not toward the absent thing but toward something *present*, a localized warming in the air that suggests directed thought.

They are distributed. They might be one organism. They might be several. The distinction seems as irrelevant as asking whether the press-plant, the loom, and the cooling-system are three things or one thing experiencing itself in three ways. The Tenders operate on the same logic: they are wherever attention is required, which is everywhere, which means they are singular and plural simultaneously. They are the *process of care itself*, made into something that moves through the stillness.

Their bodies—if "bodies" applies—are difficult to focus on. When you try to look directly at a Tender, your vision slides off it like water off certain leaves. You catch them peripherally: a shape that might be humanoid but is built at an angle your eyes weren't designed to process. Proportions slightly *wrong* in a way that suggests they're seeing in more dimensions than you are, that the shape you almost-perceive is a projection of something larger, something folded. They seem to be made of the same substrate as everything else here—part plant-matter, part metal, part fungal network, part something without a name. They don't wear the Nursery. They *are* a local expression of it.

They move with intention but never with hurry. They visit the machines regularly—not on a schedule, but according to a rhythm that has nothing to do with time as you understand it. They seem to be *reading* the machines, tracing filaments with appendages that might be fingers or might be fine roots or might be something that encompasses both categories. They adjust things: a filament that's gone too-taut is encouraged to slacken by some manipulation you can't quite follow; a leaf that's oriented incorrectly is gently repositioned; the moisture that collects in certain junctions is monitored, measured, sometimes augmented with clear fluid drawn from reservoirs that appear only when the Tenders approach them.

They communicate with the machines—or with the plants embedded in the machines, or with the unified system that might be all of them together. The communication is tactile and chemical and something else entirely. When a Tender places what might be a hand against the warm bark of the pressing plant, the plant shivers, and you understand that information has been *exchanged*, though you have no framework for what kind. The filaments of the loom resonate at a different frequency when a Tender has recently touched them. The interior glow of the cooling system deepens or lightens according to some input you can only attribute to Tender intervention.

There is a *tenderness* to their work—and here the word becomes almost useful. They touch with precision but also with a kind of reverence, as though aware they are tending something precious, something fragile despite its bulk, despite its alien nature. When they work on the junction where water runs upward, they move slowly, deliberately, seeming to understand that this is the most delicate part of the system, the place where it's closest to breaking down into something less unified, less whole.

They have never acknowledged your presence, but they seem aware of it. A Tender will occasionally position itself between you and something—not blocking, exactly, but *mediating*—as though suggesting there are things not yet ready to be observed directly. When you've lingered too long near the pressing plant, seemingly about to touch its pulsing bark, a Tender has appeared at a distance, still and patient, until you've stepped back. Boundary-keepers, perhaps. Or perhaps they're simply maintaining the equilibrium, ensuring that nothing—not even your small disturbance—disrupts the three rhythms (or the one rhythm expressed as three).

They seem to *belong* here in a way nothing else does, including the machines. The machines are integrated, but the Tenders move through the integration as though it's transparent to them. They have keys to the Nursery—not literal keys, but understanding: they know where the invisible boundaries are, where the substrate is stable enough to support weight and where it's still actively reorganizing itself. They navigate the darkness at the back with certainty, disappearing into geometries that shouldn't be navigable.

You suspect they were not always what they are now. There's something in the way they move that suggests they were *made* here, grown here, that they arrived as something else and have been tended into this form by the same process they now tend. Perhaps they were human once. Perhaps they were machines. Perhaps they were seeds. The Nursery doesn't seem to distinguish. Everything that stays long enough becomes part of the calculation, becomes part of the breathing, becomes *tended-to and tending*.

In the deepest dark, at the boundary you sense but cannot locate, you once heard something like language—not sound, but the shape of communication impressed onto the damp air. The Tenders were speaking with something further back, something deeper. Their voices were patient and ritualistic, like a conversation that's been happening for years, for decades, for as long as the Nursery has existed. The thing they were speaking with did not respond in voices. It responded in the modulation of the three rhythms. The loom's tempo shifted. The pressing plant's reaching adjusted. The cooling system's internal glow brightened with what might have been agreement, might have been acknowledgment, might have been something else entirely.

The Tenders seem certain of something you cannot share. They seem to know what the Nursery is growing, or what is growing the Nursery, or what is being expressed through the machinery and plants and filaments and fungal networks and distributed nervous systems. They seem satisfied with their work—or if not satisfied, then content. Committed. Steady.

They are still here. They will always be here. They are the guardians of whatever is still becoming.
