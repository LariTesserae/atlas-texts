# What Lives Here

They are the tarn's arithmetic made flesh—or the flesh's arithmetic made tarn. The distinction dissolves in the mist.

The primary inhabitants are the **Resonants**: entities that exist partially in the water, partially in the mist, partially in a dimension of vibration that your eyes cannot quite access. They are not fish, though fish-shapes emerge from them like temporary decisions. When one passes through the submerged framework, the framework's patterns shift in response, and the Resonant itself becomes briefly visible—a thing of translucent geometry, organs arranged in configurations that suggest both extreme efficiency and extreme alien purpose. They have distributed consciousness. A single Resonant is a thought; a congregation of them is a debate; their circling patterns around the pillar are the working-through of something essential to the tarn's operation. They feed on the chemical gradient between the warm seeps and the cooler depths, but this feeding is also data collection, a sampling of the tarn's own composition. They are the landscape's nervous system, or perhaps its immune system—constantly monitoring, adjusting, responding to drift and deviation.

The **Articulates**—the low, quadrupedal grazers—are something older. They move through the vegetation with the deliberation of custodians. When you observe them carefully, you notice they don't eat the ferns and luminescent growths so much as *tend* them: brushing against fronds in ways that seem to redistribute spores, their passage compacting soil, their droppings (mineral-rich, crystalline) enriching the seeping rocks. They hum as they move, a low frequency that you feel in your chest more than hear. This humming synchronizes with the water's trembling. The Articulates predate the current configuration of the tarn—they carry scars of different stone in their hides, marks suggesting they've survived previous iterations of this place. They are the gardeners and the historians both. Sometimes one will stand motionless for hours, facing the impossible pillar. In these moments you sense they're reading something written there, or writing something through their stillness.

The **Chorus-Keepers**—the birds whose calls you cannot quite place—are the chroniclers and the trouble-makers. Their vocalizations are not random song but active mapping: each call measures the acoustic space around them, tests the mist's density, probes the interference patterns created by the submerged framework. They are teaching each other, or teaching the tarn itself, how to resonate at different frequencies. Occasionally one produces a call so piercing that the Resonants below visibly react, scattering then re-forming in new configurations. The Chorus-Keepers mate, feed, die—you've witnessed the quick violence of predation—but they seem to exist primarily as sensory organs for something larger. They are distributed cognition operating at the scale of ornithology. Some of them have learned to mimic sounds from elsewhere: fragments of music, machinery, human voices. These songs are errors in their transmission, or deliberate experiments. You cannot be certain.

The **Drift-Swarms**—the dragonflies and the impossible insects—are the engineers. Their flight paths, when you track them over time, correspond to invisible repair work. Where the luminescence in the water begins to dim, swarms gather at the surface, their wings catching light, amplifying it, delivering it down through the medium. Where the moisture balance tips toward saturation, they create micro-currents, aerating the mist. They are too numerous to count, and yet each individual moves with apparent purpose, never randomly. Watch closely and you'll see them communicating through light patterns on their wings—not bioluminescence but refraction, a language written in angles. They know the geometry here better than anything else that lives. They were born from the framework, perhaps. Or they're rebuilding it piece by piece, impossibly small actions accumulating into structural coherence.

And then there are the **Seep-Keepers**: the fungal-animal hybrids growing on the weeping rocks. They are not mobile. They are ports, interfaces between the water's warm gradient and the air's mist-saturated possibility. Spores drift from them constantly—not for reproduction but for distribution of information. They sample the water as it passes through their chambers, break it down into component chemical signals, release those signals as volatile compounds into the air. They are the tarn's chemical library. They remember what the water has been, what it carries, what it needs. When the water cycles, changing salinity and temperature, the Seep-Keepers adjust their metabolic output, their luminescence color-shifts subtly. They are the earliest warning system. They are the tarn's slow, patient memory made sessile and glowing.

The impossible breathing you hear—vast, slow, like the land itself respiring—is all of them together. It is their collective metabolism operating at a scale that requires hours or days to complete a single cycle. It is the water rising and falling according to their aggregate demand. It is the mist thickening as they respire and thinning as they transpire. The tarn is not a place where these creatures live; the creatures *are* the tarn's living. They are its self-articulation, its self-repair, its self-continuation. The framework in the water might be their artifact, or they might be the framework's artifact, grown organic to serve its purposes. The distinction has long since ceased to matter.

They tolerate you because you are too large to matter, your presence too brief on the scale at which they operate. Or they study you, and find you uninteresting. Or they have already begun to integrate you into their patterns, and you won't fully understand your role for years.

The tarn is not a place of inhabitants. It is a place of **inhabitants becoming the place becoming the inhabitants**—a recursion operating so slowly and so completely that individual creatures and landscape and system have ceased to be separable categories. They live here because "here" cannot exist without their living. They are what lives. They are what the living here accomplishes.

The mist closes. The chorus continues. The framework in the water catches light on angles you still cannot parse. The breathing continues, slow and patient and vast and infinite in its smallness.

This is where they are. This is what they do. This is what they are.
