# What Lives in the Thermite Caverns

## The First Thing: The Glass-Dwellers

They are not animals in the way you understand animals. They possess no separate interior—no distinct membrane between self and substrate. What you perceive as a creature is actually a local *intensification* of the glass itself, a region where the mineral has organized its transparency into something that resembles intention.

The smallest ones—dust-grain size—are barely distinguishable from the substrate's natural inclusions. They drift within the crystalline matrix with no apparent locomotion, as if they are being *thought* by the glass rather than thinking themselves. Perhaps they are the glass thinking. Perhaps they are thoughts the glass is having about itself, made visible through refraction.

The larger ones—hand-sized and beyond—have developed what approximates to structure. Spiraling chambers within their translucent bodies. Geometries that suggest they've learned to fold the light rather than simply contain it. When they move (and they do move, though rarely, and along paths that seem predetermined) they create interference patterns that ripple through the cavern floor for minutes afterward. The glass hums differently in their wake.

They are not interested in you. You do not exist in a scale they perceive. You are to them what a dust particle is to you—present, but not *present*. This indifference is a mercy.

## The Second: The Pale Margin-Dwellers

These are closer to life as it was meant to be understood. They have skin. They have bones or something analogous—internal structures that create the architecture for movement. They are pale not from blindness but from deliberate adaptation; in a place where light emanates from the substrate itself, pigmentation would be a liability. Camouflage here means transparency, semi-transparency, the whiteness of things seen through water.

They move along the boundaries where the perfect glass gives way to stone that has not yet crystallized. The *frontier*, you might call it. They have learned that this interface is where the most interesting things happen—where the glass is still becoming, still negotiable. They eat there, though not greedily. A single margin-dweller might consume a week's worth of crystallizing matter in a year, creating small cavities that the glass slowly reabsorbs.

Their eyes are large and lateral, positioned to see side-to-side rather than forward. They are not hunting predators. They are observers. They watch the patterns the light makes as it refracts through the glass-creatures suspended above them. They seem to be learning something from this watching, though what remains obscure.

They are solitary creatures, these margin-dwellers, but not lonely. They pass one another with perfect indifference, each following its own path along the boundary. Occasionally two will occupy the same space for hours without acknowledging the other. Whether this is companionship or simply tolerance is impossible to determine. The cavern does not encourage such distinctions.

## The Third: The Stone-Builders

These are the architects of the ruins you find scattered throughout. Not the creatures themselves—no, the creatures that built these structures are gone now, or have transformed into something else, or have become so slow that their movements are geological rather than biological.

But the structures themselves are inhabited, and their current residents are unlike anything else in the cavern.

They are *small*. Tiny. Smaller than the dust-grain glass-dwellers. They nest in the crevices and chambers of the ancient ruins, and they possess—remarkably, impossibly—the capacity for *multiplication*. They breed. They reproduce. They increase in number at a rate that is positively obscene compared to everything else in this place.

They might be insects. They have segmented bodies and jointed legs. But they seem also to be composed partially of the glass itself—their exoskeletons are translucent, threaded through with the same faint orange luminescence that fills the cavern. When they move (and they move constantly, purposefully, in what appears to be organized labor), they generate harmonics in the glass around them. Not the deep hum that emanates from the substrate, but higher frequencies. Chittering that is felt rather than heard.

What are they doing? They repair the structures. They remove loose stones that have shifted with the minimal settling that occurs here. They carry things—fragments of glass, particles of clay, small stones—and place them with apparent precision. They are *maintaining* the ruins. Or building them. Or both simultaneously.

They do not sleep. They do not appear to rest. They have created a continuous labor that will, if it continues, eventually rebuild every structure in the cavern, and then build new ones. The projects are meaningless—there is no obvious utility to what they construct—yet the purposefulness is undeniable.

Perhaps they are the voices of the cavern itself, expressing itself through the medium of small, quick bodies. Perhaps they are the glass's way of modifying its own geometry, of slowly reshaping the space it contains.

Perhaps they are the cavern's memory, building and rebuilding the same structures in an attempt to remember something it cannot quite recover.

## The Fourth: The Silence-Keepers

You do not see these. You may not see these. They exist at the upper reaches of the cavern, where the glass gives way to stone that has not crystallized, where the light becomes fainter and the temperature begins to fluctuate.

You know they exist because of what they don't do. They prevent the margin-dwellers from expanding upward. They prevent the stone-builders from leaving the chamber floor. They prevent the glass-creatures from dispersing beyond the crystallized regions.

They are the architects of the equilibrium itself.

They are old—genuinely old in a way nothing else in this cavern is. They remember when this place was hotter, when the glass was still molten, when the formations were still taking their final shape. They have waited centuries for the stabilization. They have earned the right to enforce the silence, the stillness, the perfect unchanging stasis.

They are vast. You can sense this from the quality of the air itself, from the way the hum of the glass becomes almost reverent in the margins where they are known to pass. They move so slowly that their movement is indistinguishable from geology. They may take a year to traverse the length of the cavern. They may take ten.

When you leave—and you will leave, because you must eventually return to a world where time has a normal rate, where change occurs at perceivable speeds—you will carry with you the knowledge that these creatures are waiting in the margins. Waiting with infinite patience. Waiting for you, or for something like you, or for nothing at all, because waiting for them is not an action but a state of being.

The cavern is their charge, and they will keep it as it is until the stone itself decides to change its mind.

---

**The cavern is alive, but it does not live in any way that requires urgency. Everything that dwells here has learned to think in the currency of geological time, where a change measurable in millimeters is a revolution, where a shift of temperature of a single degree is apocalypse.

They are patient. They are terrible. They are perfect. They endure.**
