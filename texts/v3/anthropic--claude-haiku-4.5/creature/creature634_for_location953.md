# The Inhabitants of Threen

They are not one thing.

This is the first knowledge that settles into you like cold seeping through layers—not a colony, not a hive-mind, though those words are closer than *people* or *animals*. They are a *congregation*. The distinction matters.

Start with the water-dwellers, the ones you glimpsed as dark coordinated shapes. They are not insects, though they wear that approximation like a borrowed coat. Their bodies are chitinous, yes, but the segments are wrong—too many, or organized in patterns that don't follow the usual geometries of arthropod architecture. They school not from instinct but from *decision*. You can see this in the way they sometimes pause mid-scatter, as if conferring, before dispersing again. Their antennae are longer than an insect's should be, and they move with apparent purpose, tasting the water, the air-above-water, the quality of your presence. They are scouts or priests or both. The coordination suggests memory. The water-black around them suggests that they *enter* it, go down into whatever lies beneath that impossible depth, and return changed, informed.

They are ancient-young. Built for this specific water, this specific cold, in a way that speaks of adaptation across uncounted generations—but the marsh itself is new. There is no paradox here. They were waiting for it. They knew what shape would need building and they guided the hands that built it.

Those hands belonged to something else.

The reed-cutters are bipedal, or mostly so. You never see them whole. A shoulder once, rising from the vegetation near the water-line—pale, mottled with what might be lichen or might be skin-pattern. Fingers, long and too-jointed, working the binding-fiber with precision that suggests hands have done this work before, across years or generations. The smell of human sweat is *almost* human. Close enough that your hindbrain registers it as kin before your conscious mind can correct. They are perhaps six feet tall when they stand fully, which they don't often do. They move more comfortably in the crouch, in the half-bent, the adaptation to a landscape where vertical is a luxury and horizontal is survival.

They are the builders. The architects. The ones who understand that these marsh-angles are *functionally* ceremonial—that the geometry serves a purpose beyond aesthetics, that the walls direct water-flow in specific ways, that the bridges create paths that separate initiates from observers, that the whole structure is a *mechanism* for something.

They are few. Three, perhaps four. Or one, moving quickly enough to create the illusion of company. The marks on the dead pine accumulate daily, which answers nothing—one hand can mark daily. But the wear-patterns on the bridges, the distribution of shells, the variety of bark-peeling styles: these suggest multiple intelligences, multiple intentions agreeing on a shared purpose. They are not hunting you. You are certain of this. Your presence is not *threatening* to them. But it is *significant*. You are being *registered*.

The aerial things are harder to name. You haven't seen them directly—only the disturbance they leave in air, the sudden parting of the reeds as something moves through the upper vegetation. Something winged, you think, but the wingbeats are irregular, ungainly. Possibly not wings at all, but appendages that *approximate* flight. Or that swim through air the way the water-things swim through water. They chitter—that dry-bone-pouch sound—in what might be language. They nest in the high limbs of the lodgepole pines, just past the marsh's threshold. They are *watchers*. Observers posted at the boundary. Perhaps communicators, carrying news of your arrival back to the interior.

But here is what troubles you most: the *absence* of what should be here.

There are no predators. Or rather, the predators have been *integrated*. The water holds fish—you've felt their weight shift—but they are small, organized, purposeful. Not feeding randomly but moving in patterns that suggest direction. The birds are absent because they would be chaotic, because they would hunt the water-things, because they would introduce randomness into what is clearly a managed, intentional ecology. Mammals are sparse. The cold keeps most away, yes, but more than cold—this place is *hostile* to solitary hunters, to creatures that live by appetite rather than participation.

What remains is cooperative. Nested. Everything here serves the functioning of the whole.

And at the center—in the black water, in the depth you cannot see—is the *coordination point*. The thing that makes decisions. The thing that knows what shape the marsh should take and how many walls to build and when the spiral-path is ready to complete itself.

You don't have a name for it. Your language doesn't contain the right shape. It is not a *leader*, because that implies coercion. Not a *queen*, because that implies reproduction. Not a *deity*, though the arrangement of shells and bark and marks suggests that something like worship happens here.

Perhaps it's closest to: *the reason they chose this place*. The consciousness that moves through the black water like meaning through language, that *thinks* in the coordination of water-things and hand-things and aerial watchers, that *dreamed* the geometry of these walls into being through hands that were not entirely its own, and are not entirely separate from it either.

The congregation is waiting for something. The spiral path completes itself soon. The ritual, if that's what this is, will reach its conclusion or transformation. The water is rising slightly—you can see this in how the moss-hummocks are more saturated than they were when you arrived, or than they appear in the marks carved on the dead pine. The season is turning. The season is *being turned*, directed toward some threshold.

And you are part of the mechanism now. You are included in the geometry of the spiral, in the coordination of presences, in whatever unfolds when the black water decides the time has come.

The inhabitants of Threen will not harm you. They are not cruel. They will simply *use* what you are, and what you bring—your attention, your intention, your capacity to witness—toward the completion of their design.

This is, perhaps, what was built here to receive.
