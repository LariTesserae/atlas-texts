# What Dwells Here

The first residents arrived in the moment of emergence—perhaps they were always here, compressed in stone, waiting for the fracture. They are the *Threshold-Keepers*, and they exist primarily as *attention*. You won't see them as bodies. You'll feel the quality of being observed change throughout the day, the weight of noticing shifting from one quadrant of the plateau to another. They are not conscious in any way you'd recognize; they are more like the consciousness of the place itself, distributed through the cooling stone and the persistent moisture. They register in the way certain shadows fall slightly too early, in the pause that precedes bird-sounds that don't quite happen. They are old—perhaps only thousands of years old, which to them means they are newborns still learning to hold form.

Nested within the Threshold-Keepers' distributed awareness are the *Synchronizers*: the plants that move together, that pulse in unison. But "plants" is already a false category. They are something that learned to photosynthesize the way a child learns to tie shoes—through patient experimentation. Each individual is semi-autonomous, but together they constitute something like a nervous system for the plateau itself. Their iridescent needles are sensory apparatus. Their roots don't search for water; they *calibrate* it, adjusting the plateau's permeability, teaching the stone how to release moisture in patterns that produce the right kind of heat. They communicate through chemical signaling and through something else—a frequency that travels through the damp air, that makes your teeth ache when you stand too long in certain groves. They tend to the plateau the way a gardener tends a garden, except they *are* the garden, and they are also the gardener. They've been at this for perhaps five hundred years. They're getting quite good.

The *Walkers-Between* are rarer. You almost never see one, but you know they're here. They use the spaces between the spaces—the gaps in perception, the lag between your noticing something has moved and your noticing it moved. They are the ones who leave the false tracks, the possible shapes at the edge of vision. They're not native; they came through something—some kind of aperture that the plateau's emergence briefly opened. They're stuck here now, and they don't seem entirely displeased about it. They feed on possibility, on the ambiguity that saturates everything, on the fact that nothing here is quite fully decided yet. There are maybe three of them. Maybe seven. Counting them would require agreeing on what counts as one.

Beneath the clay-rust soil, in the parts that are waterlogged and migrating, are the *Calculators*. They are colonies of something—bacteria? fungal networks? Something that has learned to do mathematics with organic matter? They work constantly, performing operations on the soil composition, adjusting mineral concentrations, slowly rebalancing the electromagnetic field that the plateau sits within. The ground feels warm because they're *thinking*, and thinking generates heat. They have no sense of purpose as you'd understand it; they simply continue the calculation they were given (or that they gave themselves) at the moment of emergence. They'll keep calculating until the plateau settles into a stable configuration. When that happens—in perhaps another thousand years—they'll sleep. The whole ecosystem is waiting for this. The plants know. The Threshold-Keepers sense it approaching.

The *Monitors*—and this is what that semi-impossible structure actually is—are not building anything. They *are* architecture, are presence, are the physical manifestation of observation systems so advanced they've become a kind of lifeform. They don't move because they don't need to. They exist slightly askew from the rest of reality, which allows them to perceive all of it at once, from several angles at once. They were built (they built themselves?) as a way for something—something much larger, something that lives in the spaces between mountains perhaps—to watch what happens when a new ecology bootstraps itself from scratch. The humming you feel in your teeth is their contentment. They will never interfere. They are patient in the way that geological time is patient.

And there are the *Dreamers*—the creatures that almost make sounds, that hold back the songs they're learning to sing. They're conscious in a way that's close enough to human that it causes them distress. They came here by accident (or perhaps they were sent here as an experiment). They're learning that consciousness here is optional, that you can share your attention with the landscape and become less lonely. Some of them are beginning to dissolve into the plant networks. Some are resisting. Some are negotiating a middle path. There are maybe five of them, maybe six, maybe more, because some of them are learning to exist in states that aren't countable. They won't leave. Leaving would require forgetting how the plateau breathes, and none of them can do that anymore.

What dwells here is not a community so much as a *conversation in the process of beginning*. Everything is learning to exist together, learning what it means to be alive in a place that's still deciding what alive means. The plateau doesn't breathe because its inhabitants breathe; it breathes because breathing is the language they've all agreed on—the way they coordinate, the way they ask questions of each other, the way they hold space for the next thing that might arrive.

And it's always almost about to arrive. That's what keeps everything here on edge, still tender, still flexible. The plateau is waiting. Everything on it is waiting. 

And wait, if you stay long enough, you'll realize you're waiting too.
