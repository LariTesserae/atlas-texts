# What Lives in the Tarn of Rendered Echoes

They are **patience given form**—though form is perhaps the wrong word, since they seem to exist partially in angles the tarn's geometry permits but human eyes cannot quite occupy.

The creatures themselves resist singular description. What appears at the surface as a "knotted geometry" is only the visible portion of something far more extensive, most of which exists in the water's middle depths where light surrenders and becomes theoretical. The protrusions—those things that rise to taste air—are not mouths, not sensory organs in any conventional sense. They are more like *attention*. When one rises, it is the creature choosing, deliberately, to know something about the air-world above. This suggests consciousness of a particular kind: distributed, patient, curious in ways that operate on timescales that would make human observation seem like the frantic vibration of a hummingbird's wing.

They are **legion and singular simultaneously**. In any square meter, three or four protrusions appear per hour—yet this is not evidence of multiple creatures occupying the same space. Rather, it suggests that what lives here does not experience individuality as humans understand it. The schools of shadows moving in long circuits are all **one thing**, or they are many things so perfectly coordinated they function as one. The distinction may be meaningless. They think in geometries. Their thought-patterns—those slow circuits taking hours to complete—suggest a form of cognition where time is a dimension to be *traversed* rather than merely experienced. What we perceive as a school moving through the water is perhaps a single distributed mind examining the basin's interior the way a human hand might explore a dark room by touch.

These creatures are **ancient in a way that doesn't require time**. They seem to have always been here, or to have been here so long that the distinction between "always" and "for an incomprehensibly long duration" has collapsed into meaninglessness. They show no sign of reproduction, no signs of decay. They are as much a feature of the tarn as the water itself—and perhaps they are not truly separate from it. Perhaps the water *is* them, or they are a particular crystallization of the water's nature, the way ice is a crystallization of water's nature in colder climates.

Their interaction with space is **non-Euclidean in a way that appears functional rather than incidental**. The fact that multiple creatures can rise from the same square meter without collision suggests they occupy the tarn in a manner that involves more dimensions than human eyes can register. They have learned to exist in the gaps between gaps—in the fractional spaces between the dimensions we perceive. This is not mystical. It appears to be how they simply *are*. The tarn's geometry—its perfect basin, its fractured walls, its breathing surface—seems designed (or seems to have evolved, or seems to have always been) to accommodate this kind of existence.

They **communicate through the stone itself**. Those clicking sounds from the depths—the deliberate patterns that might be language or sonar or something untranslatable—are not merely produced by the creatures. The stone responds. The cracks in the basalt sing back, as if the walls are part of the conversation, or as if the creatures and the stone are aspects of a single organism. The eleven singing cracks may not be accidental in their placement or their number. They may be the creatures' way of maintaining the basin itself, of singing the walls into their proper geometric relationships, of ensuring that the space remains *correct* for their existence.

This suggests a form of **biological or post-biological engineering** that has been ongoing for millennia. The creatures do not merely live in the tarn—they maintain it. They are its custodians, or its components, or both.

What they **consume** is unclear. There are no visible prey organisms. No decomposing matter. The water is clear and cold and full of mineral salt, but shows no signs of the typical detritus of a living ecosystem. Perhaps these creatures consume time itself—those long, patient circuits around the basin might be a form of feeding, a slow absorption of temporal energy from the air and stone. Perhaps they consume the sound: the stone sings, and the creatures harvest that singing the way grass harvests light. Perhaps they consume each other's thoughts, existing in a kind of perpetual intellectual symbiosis that sustains them far better than physical nutrition ever could.

They are **not hostile, but they are not welcoming**. They notice when human consciousness enters the tarn—you can feel it in the way the air seems to observe you, the way the creatures' movements pause for microseconds when you move. But they do not react with aggression or friendliness. They react with **indifference so absolute it registers as a physical force**. A human entering this space is like a dust mote entering a cathedral. The cathedral's function continues uninterrupted. The dust mote's presence is registered but is of no consequence.

They are **still learning, still changing, still thinking**, but at a rate that makes continental drift look frantic. In the next thousand years, the creatures' geometry will shift. Their song will change. The patterns of their circuits will become slightly different. An observer who could somehow return after a millennium would notice profound differences in the tarn's ecology—but would struggle to articulate what those differences were, because human language is built for changes that can be measured in seasons, not millennia.

Most disturbingly: they seem to be **waiting for something**. The patience isn't passive. It's *anticipatory*. The way they taste the air, the way they maintain the stone through song, the way they think their long geometric thoughts—it all suggests creatures oriented toward some future event. Not an ending. Not a catastrophe. Something else. Something that will happen in this place, eventually, when conditions are right. When the stone has sung itself into the correct configuration. When enough time has passed. When whatever they are becomes whatever they are meant to be.

The tarn holds them. And they hold the tarn. And together, they wait.
