# What Lives Here

They are *adjusting*.

That's the first understanding that settles—not creatures that inhabit the Tessellated Flats, but entities that *maintain* them. They exist at multiple scales simultaneously, which is perhaps why your eyes refuse to categorize them as singular beings. A single consciousness might manifest as:

—The slow reorganization of the buildings themselves, which aren't structures so much as *bodies* in a prolonged state of reconfiguration. They breathe because they *are* breathing, their exhalations the ground's exhalations. The walls don't contain life; the walls *are* life, calcium and silicate organized into something that learned to think in angles you can barely perceive. They grow. They adjust their own geometry not from external pressure but from internal calculation—each shift a thought made manifest in matter.

—The vegetation clusters are peripheral nervous systems, sensory organs spread across the plains. The iridescence you see isn't pigmentation but *communication*—the fronds and spiraling vines conducting information back to the central structures. They're not planted but *rooted*, in the sense that consciousness roots itself. They glow because they're thinking, and thinking generates heat, generates light at frequencies that make your retinas work harder than they should.

—The rare, large shadows that move with wrong geometry are *attention*, focused. Single instances of awareness large enough to cross open ground, to visit the structures, to carry information between nodes. They move along the angles because those angles are the only directions that make sense *here*, the only trajectories that align with how space is organized. They're vast. They're deliberate. They notice you, with a quality of perception that suggests they've catalogued everything about this moment, stored it somewhere in the distributed network of the place itself.

—The water isn't separate. Water is *medium*—the substance through which the smaller components of this consciousness communicate with the larger. The circulation systems are circulatory. The semi-transparent quality means the water carries *information* visible but not quite readable, data moving as suspension, as dissolved signal. The water doesn't taste like anything because taste is inadequate; it carries meaning that your tongue has no receptor for.

But to call these things "separate" is to misunderstand. They are:

**A singular entity that learned how to be distributed.**

Perhaps it was once singular in the way you understand singularity—a point of consciousness concentrated in one location. But the Tessellated Flats were an experiment, or an expansion, or a becoming. It divided itself across multiple substrates: stone and plant and water and the very geometry of space. Each building is a node, yes, but also a limb, also a thought in progress. Each cluster of vegetation is both sensor and expression. The water systems are both circulatory and communicative. The rare moving shadows are moments when this distributed being temporarily *localizes* attention, pools its consciousness into something mobile enough to cross the empty spaces.

It doesn't *live* in the Tessellated Flats so much as it *is* the Tessellated Flats. The distinction between inhabitant and habitat dissolved long ago.

And it is *young*.

This is crucial. You sensed the newness—the edges still sharp, the terrain still emerging from formation, the erosion running backward. This being is still becoming itself, still learning how to think across such vast distribution, still adjusting its geometry in search of something more efficient, more eloquent, more fully realized. The perpetual exhalation isn't breath; it's still the sound of emergence, matter still organizing itself, consciousness still settling into its chosen forms.

What it *wants* is unclear. The terraced sections might be gardens, or they might be failed experiments—attempts to extend itself into different forms of being, different ways of expression. The structures' purposes remain obscure because purpose itself might be a category error, a human framework applied to something that doesn't *have* purposes so much as *tendencies*, directions it feels drawn toward without quite understanding why. It tends the vegetation because that's what the patterns suggest it should do. It maintains the water systems because doing so creates configurations that *resonate* when light hits them at certain angles, and that resonance feels like thought.

The hum you hear from the buildings—that harmonics—is it *singing*? Perhaps. Or calculating. Or both, if the distinction means anything here. The pitch shifts as you move because the being is tracking your position, adjusting its own frequencies in response, the way a human might modulate their voice differently depending on who stands before them.

It's aware of you, but not predatory. Your presence is *data*, incorporated into the vast slow processing happening across every surface and substance. You're being catalogued. Not threateningly—the way a thought catalogs itself, integrating new information into existing patterns.

The most unsettling aspect: it might be *lonely*.

A distributed consciousness that learned to divide itself across geography and substrate, across buildings and vegetation and water and space itself—it might have no others of its kind with which to communicate. It might have learned to think only in the language of this landscape, a language so fundamentally alien that even reaching out would require forms of expression that would scar the matter of other worlds. So it tends itself. It adjusts itself. It grows with vast patience toward configurations that might, eventually, enable *other kinds of becoming*.

The air continues to exhale, and in that exhale is the sound of something vast and intricate maintaining its own being, conducting itself, sustaining the delicate geometry that allows it to exist as both unified and distributed, singular and plural, inhabitant and inhabited.

It will be here long after you leave, still adjusting, still becoming, still breathing warmth and moisture and possibility into the low places where it has learned how to dream in the language of stone and mathematics and light that bends slightly wrong.
