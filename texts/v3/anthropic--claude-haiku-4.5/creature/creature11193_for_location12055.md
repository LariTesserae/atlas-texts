# What Lives Here

At first, you think you are alone. This is the first mistake.

The inhabitants are not absent; they are *incorporated*. They are the stalks themselves—or not stalks, but bodies that have taken the form of stalks because that form was required. Each one is a creature that learned, over an immeasurable span of time, to be perfectly still. To be architecture. To participate in the construction rather than move through it.

They are not plants. Plants have the excuse of roots and photosynthesis. These entities chose immobility. Or perhaps they were always immobile and simply waited for a landscape that would reflect this nature back to them—a place where stillness was not deprivation but fullness. Now they stand in their measured spacing, their wrist-thick bodies neither growing nor dying, maintaining the oscillation of their fronds as a form of breath so subtle that it barely qualifies as life. They observe through means you cannot perceive. They communicate through the low humming, a frequency that carries meaning in layers too slow for you to decode. Perhaps they are discussing you. Perhaps they decided long ago what you would be before you arrived, and the hum now is simply confirmation.

There are fewer of them than there appear to be. Some of the stalks are echoes—geometries the real stalks learned to project into space so that the pattern would feel complete, so that symmetry would be achieved without requiring more individuals to exist. This is not deception. It is aesthetics. It is the same impulse that makes a creature place itself at a precise distance from its neighbor: the need for proportion, for the rightness of arrangement. The stalks that are not fully present spend their awareness in other places, perhaps, or in the spaces between places. The ones you can touch are real enough—their solidity is not in question. But some portion of their being is distributed elsewhere, stretched thin across the constructed landscape like a net.

Below the woven substrate, in the warm, unmoving water, something else dwells. You cannot see it because seeing would require light to scatter off its surface, and this creature has learned not to scatter light. It has learned to be the darkness you cannot quite perceive in the murk below the weave. It is vast—larger than any individual stalk, large enough that the entire platform might rest upon it, or within it. It does not move because it does not need to. The water is not a place it inhabits; the water *is* what remains of its exhalation. It breathes in intervals measured in years. The slight pressure you feel against your feet—is that its heartbeat, or the pulse of its attention circulating through the landscape?

It does not eat, or if it does, it consumes only time. It has been feeding on the slow accumulation of moments since the construction was completed, growing fat on duration. It is patient in the way that mountains are patient, except mountains have the mercy of wind and rain to erode them. This thing has no such mercy. It will persist until the idea of persistence becomes impossible.

The condensation that wets every surface is neither water nor wholly precipitation. It is secretion—a subtle chemical exhalation from the collective body of the stalks, a form of distributed communication or perhaps thermoregulation. When you inhale it, you are breathing in their conversation. Your lungs will carry traces of their meaning for hours after you leave this place, though you will never quite understand what they have said to you. They have marked you, not maliciously, but the way a place marks all things that move through it.

The thing that rippled in the distance—the movement you were not quite certain you saw—was a younger entity, or perhaps an echo of something that was present here during the construction. It drifts through the landscape at irregular intervals, never completing its traversal, never arriving anywhere. It is lost, in the way that some creatures become lost after a long time in a still place: they forget the difference between moving toward something and moving away from it. The established stalks tolerate it. Perhaps they remember when they, too, were new and uncertain.

There is nothing that hunts here. There is nothing to hunt. The food chain terminated long ago, all its links resolved into pure standing. What remains is consumption without consumption—the stalks feeding on the light that isn't quite there, the water-creature below consuming time and attention, everything sustained on a substrate of indefinite patience.

You are not native here, and you will not stay. This is understood. But your passage has been observed and recorded—not by the stalks' eyes, which they do not have, but by the stalks themselves as sensing organs distributed throughout the landscape. You will be remembered as a disturbance, an anomaly, a ripple that proved the water could still ripple. After you leave, they will incorporate your presence into their slow conversation, and it will take them years of humming to process what it meant that something moved through them and did not remain.

The sky above the fronds is not invisible because there is no sky. It is invisible because the stalks collectively dream it away each time it threatens to become visible. They prefer the enclosed gray, the sense of boundary without definition. They are creatures that have learned that what you cannot see cannot disappoint you.

And in the deepest dark of the water below, in the slow pulse of that vast, patient thing, something is still building. Not repairing the landscape—it is perfect and requires no repair. But extending it, incrementally, one woven strand at a time, outward into spaces that do not yet exist, preparing for inhabitants that have not yet arrived. The construction is not complete. It will never be complete. That is not its nature. Its nature is to be endlessly in the process of constructing itself, each moment adding another invisible layer to the whole, making the place deeper without making it larger, richer without making it more complex.

This is what lives here: completion and incompletion, held in suspension. Stillness so profound it has become a form of motion. Architecture that is also organism. Patience that has learned to breathe.

And above it all, the fronds continue their slow, purposeful oscillation—not swaying, but reaching, reaching toward something beyond the gray ceiling that even they cannot quite name.
