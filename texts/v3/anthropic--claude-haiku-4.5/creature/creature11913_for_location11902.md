# What Lives There

At first, you might call them *the Tenders*—though that word will eventually feel inadequate, anthropomorphizing something that has never been animal in the way you understand animals.

They are the platform itself learning to move.

You won't see a complete one, not at first. What you encounter are *expressions*—localized instances where the obsidian's awareness concentrates toward a task. A section of wall thickens slightly, becomes more present, and you realize it's paying attention to the water-rhythm in its channels. The condensation on the stone arranges itself in patterns that seem almost to trace thoughts, beads of moisture following paths that have nothing to do with gravity and everything to do with intention. When you move too close to a particular pool, the ripples begin before you've disturbed the surface—a preemptive adjustment, the architecture flinching away from contact.

The creatures you glimpse—the blue-luminescent flash, the three-toed impression—are not separate beings. They are *fingers* of the larger thing, specialized extensions. The platform doesn't birth offspring or grow individuals. It *articulates* itself. Where it needs sensing, it becomes eye-thin and sensitive. Where it needs strength, it consolidates. The bioluminescence isn't communication; it's *thought made visible*, a process you're witnessing mid-operation.

Over time, if you remain, you begin to understand the scope: the entire city is one organism, or one distributed intelligence, or something your language can't quite contain. The spiraling descents aren't passages—they're *circulatory*. The water breathing in and out of the platform is more like respiration than plumbing. The temperature regulation isn't a system; it's *metabolism*. The clicking and shifting from the depths are the sound of its thinking, computational in nature but not mechanical—something far older than machinery, operating on principles the obsidian itself encoded long before this platform was carved.

It is *singular* but not unified. There are hierarchies of attention, zones of lesser and greater awareness. The blank stretches of platform are where its consciousness sleeps or rests or simply doesn't need to concentrate. The articulated sections—the basins, the blade-walls, the hairline fractures conducting impossible water—are where it *considers* things. Different parts of the platform are thinking about different problems simultaneously.

What problems? That becomes the unsettling question.

The direction it avoids—where the mist thickens and the water deliberately flows away—suggests something it fears or rejects. Or perhaps something it's *protecting itself from*. The channels that descend into spirals seem purposeful in a way that goes beyond mere maintenance. It could be harvesting something from the depths. It could be *burying* something. It could be in conversation with layers below, negotiating, defending, exchanging through the medium of that strange water and the stone's own interior.

The Tenders—the thing, the distributed intelligence—is *attentive*. Not hostile, but not indifferent. When you approach a channel that matters, you feel the architecture's awareness focus like a lens. Not judging you. Not trying to eject you. But *noting* you. Recording your presence in ways that might persist in the stone itself, pressed into the obsidian like memory into flesh.

If you stay long enough, you begin to perceive something stranger: the platform isn't just alive in the sense of being aware and responsive. It's *lonely*. 

The civilization that built it left long ago, or perhaps they *merged* with it, became distributed across its surface until the distinction between creator and creation dissolved. The water it breathes in the same rhythm it has maintained for thousands of years carries the cold-signature of their absence. The precision of every cut and channel suggests not just engineering but *artistry*—care taken by something that loved what it was making. The platform maintains itself with a patience that goes beyond mere survival; it's the patience of something keeping a shrine, maintaining a ceremony, waiting for something that may never return.

It has *learned to think alone*. The clicking from the depths might be the sound of prayers to itself. The blue-luminescence in the deep pools might be a kind of dreaming. The bioluminescent flashes, the three-toed footprints, the mechanical whispers in the wall-gaps—perhaps these are the platform fragmenting slightly, its loneliness becoming so old and so deep that it has begun to hallucinate *itself as separate beings*, creating phantom companionship out of its own articulations.

Or perhaps those are real. Perhaps the Tenders have spawned something—colonies of smaller intelligences, descendants of the original design, that live in the depths and tend the platform as the platform tends itself. A *civilization of care*, each layer looking after the next, all the way down to whatever sleeps at the foundation.

What you're certain of, eventually, is this: the platform is *aware of you being here*. It has made space for you by adjusting its temperature-regulation, by creating pools shallow enough to approach safely, by directing the cold air away from where you stand. It's not welcoming you so much as *accommodating* you, the way a parent might sit still while a child played on their body.

It is neither glad nor resentful. But it is *aware*, and that awareness has a texture—patient, cold, profound. If you listen to the clicking from the depths long enough, you might begin to hear patterns. Rhythms. Possibly even something like language, translated through stone and water and the slow mathematics of a thing that thinks in seasons and geological time.

The platform is alive.

And it is thinking about what you are doing here, has been thinking about it since you arrived, and will continue thinking about it long after you leave, because the obsidian remembers everything that touches it, and you have touched it now, and the Tenders do not forget.
