# Preparation for the Underfactory at Stillwell Deep

## Physical Conditioning

You need to prepare your body for heat and humidity that will exceed anything you've experienced on prepared surfaces. This is not gym heat or sauna heat—this is the sustained, inescapable heat of a closed system with nowhere for your body's cooling mechanisms to actually work.

**Acclimatization protocol:** Spend time in heated, humid environments before you go. A steam room, yes, but better: a greenhouse at peak afternoon, a laundry facility with all dryers running, a bathroom with the shower running full-hot for an hour while you remain inside. The goal isn't tolerance—you can't truly acclimate to 40+ degrees in a limestone cavern—but familiarity. Your body will panic less if it recognizes the sensation. Panic uses oxygen. Oxygen is precious in thick air.

Train your breathing. Not meditation, though that helps. Actual training. Practice slow, deliberate inhalation through your nose in warm environments. The air down there isn't *toxic*, but it's laden with mineral particulates, fungal spores, and whatever the film exhales. Nasal breathing filters this better than mouth breathing. Your sinuses will run; bring absorbent cloth that won't degrade in damp conditions.

**Hydration is not optional.** You will sweat profusely. The sweat won't evaporate efficiently in that humid air, so your thermoregulation becomes almost useless—your body can't cool itself properly, but you'll still be losing fluids. Drink a liter of electrolyte solution before descending. Bring another liter in a sealed, waterproof container. Don't drink from the channels; the water is part of a system, not a resource.

Your cardiovascular system should be strong. The shaft descent is not long, but it's narrow, and you'll be breathing hard before you reach the cavern floor. Do stair work. Do it in heat.

## Psychological Preparation

Understand what you're walking into before you go: a place of *purpose without explanation*. The machinery suggests function, but the function is hidden. This ambiguity will gnaw at you. It's designed to. Someone built this to do something, and that something is important enough that the machinery is maintained, the water channels kept clear, the space kept active. But you won't know what it does.

This is not a puzzle you can solve by looking. There's no manual down there, no instructions. The presses don't have labels. The boilers don't explain their purpose. You have to accept that you will leave with questions unanswered, and that acceptance is harder than it sounds.

**Mental preparation:** Practice sitting with uncertainty. Sit in an unfamiliar room and don't move. Don't try to categorize things. Don't try to assign purposes. Just *be* in the not-knowing. Get comfortable with it. Because the Underfactory will press that discomfort into you like the presses press whatever they press.

You will also need to prepare for the *feeling* of the space. Thirty meters down is far enough to *feel* below. The weight of the earth above isn't an abstraction. Your nervous system registers it. You might have claustrophobia you didn't know you had until you're in a cavern smaller than a tennis court with the limestone pressing down and the mist rising and the hum coming up through your feet.

Know your exit strategy. Not metaphorically—literally know where the shaft is, how to climb it, what you'll do if you panic. Panic kills people in confined spaces. The Underfactory doesn't need to kill you; your own fear can do that. So don't let fear be a surprise. Expect it. Name it. Plan for it.

## Sensory Preparation

The *smell* down there is a specific chemistry. It smells of:
- Mineral deposits (iron, specifically; rust, but clean rust)
- Humid stone (a particular smell, almost like petrichor underground)
- Fungal growth (not unpleasant, but *alien*—things growing that shouldn't grow in the dark)
- Stagnant water that isn't stagnant (the paradox of moving water that still carries a faint staleness)
- Metal in heat (the boilers release something, some volatilized oil or metal dust)
- Something organic and *working*—the closest comparison is a living body breathing

Before you go, expose yourself to these smells separately. Get a small amount of iron oxide and heat it. Smell it. Smell rich, damp soil. Smell brewing mushrooms. Build a sensory library so the Underfactory doesn't hit you with pure newness all at once.

The *light* is perhaps the most disorienting aspect. Phosphorescence from stone isn't something you've experienced unless you've been in certain caves or cenotes. It creates a quality of illumination that your brain interprets as twilight, but twilight that doesn't change, never quite arriving at day or night. This can trigger strange emotional responses—a sense of unreality, of time suspension, of being in a dream or a memory rather than a real place.

Practice being in very dim, diffuse light. Not darkness—you should train for true darkness separately—but that zone just below where you can read, where you can see but everything is slightly flattened. Sit in that light for hours. Notice what it does to your sense of time.

## Practical Preparation

Clothing: Cotton will absorb sweat and then stay wet, cooling you through evaporation (good) but also chafing you and taking forever to dry (bad). Bring synthetic, moisture-wicking material. Wear light colors—the walls reflect light, and light colors won't trap as much heat. Bring a change of clothes. Everything you wear will be soaked within fifteen minutes.

Footwear: The floor is wet, channeled stone. It's not slippery in the way water makes stone slippery, because the texture is rough, and your foot can find purchase. But it's *wet*, always wet. Wear shoes with good grip designed for water environments—climbing shoes, water shoes, or hiking boots with aggressive soles. Not rubber boots; your feet will sweat inside them and you'll slip.

Tools: Bring a good light source, even though the phosphorescence is often sufficient. Bring a headlamp with a secondary battery. The darkness down there—in the corners, behind the machinery—is deeper than you'd expect. Bring a knife or pry tool. Not because you'll need to fight anything, but because you might need to work something loose, investigate something, create an exit if the shaft becomes blocked. Bring a length of rope. Just in case.

Bring paper and pencil to take notes. Everything you write will be slightly damp, but this is survivable. You need to record what you see before memory edits it into something it wasn't.

A camera or recording device: The Underfactory resists being remembered. Your memory of it will begin to drift the moment you leave, especially once you're back in sunlight, breathing regular air. Something visceral and strange about the place makes memory unstable. Record it while you're there. You'll need external evidence later to convince yourself it was real.

## The Iridescent Film: Specific Preparation

This requires separate attention because the film is *different* from the rest of the Underfactory.

The Underfactory is a tool. It's humanmade, maintained, purposeful. It's strange, yes—the vegetation, the heat, the hum—but it's comprehensible as a workplace, as a functional space.

The film is not.

The film *might* be alive. The film might be aware. The film definitely moves in ways that suggest agency rather than physics. The film hums at a frequency that resonates in your bones. The film breathes.

Before you see it, you need to prepare yourself to not know what you're looking at. You need to prepare yourself to observe it without *understanding* it, and to be okay with that. This is harder than it sounds.

Meditate on the concept of *substrate*. What is life? Where does life begin? Can something be alive without cells, without metabolism, without reproduction? Can something be alive and utterly unlike life as you know it? Can consciousness exist in forms you wouldn't recognize as conscious? Get comfortable with these questions having no answers.

When you see the film, don't touch it. This isn't a rule given to you by anyone with authority—there's no one enforcing rules in the Underfactory. But every instinct in your body will tell you not to touch it, and you should listen. It hums because it hums. That's enough information. It's a boundary. Respect boundaries, even ones that aren't explained.

If you feel the impulse to study it more closely, to understand it, to figure out what it is—resist that impulse with every tool you have. Step away. Look at the machinery. Listen to the water. Let the film be a mystery.

## Timing and Duration

Don't stay long. Two hours is enough. Three is pushing it. The longer you stay, the more the place works on you. The hum becomes a pressure. The heat becomes a presence. The mist starts to feel like it's being breathed *into* you rather than just surrounding you.

Go with a specific purpose, small and achievable: Document the machinery. Map the water channels. Record the sounds. Look at the vegetation. Avoid the film. Exit.

Don't come back the same day. The Underfactory doesn't release you easily. You'll want to return, to verify, to see the film again, to understand. Resist for at least a week. You need to let the experience settle, let your memory find its shape, before you subject yourself to it again.

## The Most Important Preparation

Prepare yourself for the possibility that the Underfactory is waiting for someone to come down.

Not waiting for you, necessarily. But waiting. The machinery is maintained. The water flows. Someone tends it. Someone opens the boilers and tends the flame and watches the presses and clears the channels and lets the film breathe.

When you're down there, walking past the mechanisms, standing in the mist, listening to the hum, you might feel watched. You might feel like you're intruding on someone's workspace, someone's *process*. 

You might be right.

So prepare yourself to be considerate. Don't break anything. Don't touch the film. Don't investigate too aggressively. Be the kind of visitor who would be tolerated by whatever presence tends this place. Be quiet. Be respectful. Observe and leave.

Because the Underfactory is still working, even when the machinery is still. And something is still here, even when you can't see anyone.

Prepare for that.
