# Preparing for the Stillplains of Keth: A Traveler's Guide

## Before You Go

**Mental Preparation is Not Optional**

You must understand before departure that the Stillplains will not change for you, and you will struggle not to change the Stillplains. This cognitive dissonance—the deep human need to affect one's environment meeting a landscape that has transcended the possibility of disturbance—is the primary hazard of visiting. The locals who live there seem to have made peace with being irrelevant to the place's future. You will need to make this peace quickly or depart early. I recommend extensive meditation practice, ideally involving acceptance of futility. This sounds more dire than it is. There is profound comfort in irrelevance once you stop fighting it.

Pack a journal. You will want to document what you see, if only to prove to yourself that you saw it. Then—and this is crucial—be prepared to never look at those journals again. The Stillplains resist *narrative*. Your neat account of observations will feel false the moment you leave, and truer still once you return home. This contradiction is normal. The place operates on a logic that doesn't survive translation into language.

**Physical Preparation**

Your body will adapt to the unchanging temperature within approximately 36 hours. Expect mild disorientation before this threshold—heat-sensing nerves have nothing to anticipate, which creates a peculiar groundlessness. Bring clothing in layers anyway; the psychological comfort of options matters more than thermal necessity.

Water: Bring more than you think rational. The crystal deposits are not potable—yes, they appear warm and semi-liquid, yes, the fauna seems to consume them, but your biochemistry will reject them violently. The locals use deep wells dug into areas where subsurface water pools. These wells are marked by cairns of the pale crystal. You may drink from them, but do so slowly and only when thirsty; the water tastes of minerals that your tongue has no reference category for. It is clean. It is safe. It tastes like the distilled essence of geological time.

Footwear: Bring excellent boots with rigid soles. The folds of terrain appear gradual but are deceptive. You will twist your ankle exactly once, probably on day three, probably in a fold that looked completely flat. The Stillplains are patient about suffering but not sympathetic to it. After the ankle-twist, you will move more carefully and see more detail. This may be intentional.

Bring more food than seems necessary, but understand that hunger itself functions differently here. Your body will not demand food with the usual urgency. You will realize at hour 18 that you haven't eaten, and you will have no memory of ceasing to be hungry. Pack calorie-dense food and eat on a schedule rather than by impulse. Your body is trying to achieve stillness too.

**Psychological Equipment**

A deep tolerance for being watched. The fauna will observe you. The Listeners will not acknowledge your existence, but their transparent skin glows brighter when you're near—not a reaction, understand, but a *notation*. The Climbers will divert from their spiral patterns to avoid you, or to include you in their paths; you will never quite determine which. The unnamed things will become aware of you and remain aware for the duration of your visit. This is not hostile. It is attentive. It is the opposite of loneliness in a way that human social contact rarely achieves. Prepare to be *seen* by something vast and indifferent.

Comfort with being obsolete. The Stillplains existed for millions of years before humans arrived. It will exist for millions more after you leave. Nothing you do here will matter. Nothing you could do would accelerate or retard the consensus that has settled here. This is terrifying and then liberating. Most visitors reach the liberating part by day four.

**A Specific Preparation: The Silence**

The silence here is not an absence. Practice sitting in complete silence at home for increasing durations. Start with five minutes. Work up to an hour. If you can maintain an hour of absolute silence in a normal environment, you'll be able to recognize the Stillplains' silence as a *presence* rather than perceiving it as deprivation. The distinction saves your sanity. In silence-as-presence, you can think. In silence-as-absence, you will spiral into self-talk and anxiety. The Stillplains will wait patiently for you to make peace with this.

## Upon Arrival

**The First Disorientation**

You will likely arrive in late afternoon. The sun will be at the same angle it will occupy at your departure—it moves through the same arc daily with mechanical precision. Let yourself adjust to this. Sit for at least an hour before exploring. Your internal clock is keyed to latitudinal variation in sunlight. The Stillplains offer none. Your circadian rhythm will recalibrate, but it needs permission to do so. Give it.

The rust-and-chalk taste in the air is real and persistent. It comes from the mineral content of the ground being perpetually released and re-settled by microscopic processes—weathering so slow that it operates on geological timescales, and yet it operates. Everything here is being transformed at a rate too gradual to perceive. Accept this: you are not still. The place is moving. You simply cannot see the motion.

**Behavioral Norms**

The scattered villages exist in a state of profound cooperation with the land. Don't remove anything. Don't alter anything. Don't *improve* anything. The locals have noticed that visitors tend to want to "help"—clearing debris, organizing loose stone, arranging objects. They've developed a gentle way of preventing this. Usually it involves simply appearing and requesting you stop, in whatever trade language they've learned. They're not hostile about it. They're *disappointed*—a subtler rebuke than anger and far more effective.

The communal structure with the impressed records is open to visitors. The patterns are hypnotic. They repeat, but not identically—each repetition is slightly different, documenting variations in something the locals understand and you do not. Sit with them. Run your fingers over the depressions. The tool marks are fresh even though some date back centuries. The damp air here prevents oxidation and erosion. Everything is preserved in its moment of creation. This is true of nothing else on the Stillplains, which makes this room sacred. Treat it as such.

**Interacting With Fauna**

The Listeners will not interact with you. Enjoy their company in the way you'd enjoy a painting. They are beautiful. Their luminescence beneath transparent skin shows patterns that suggest complexity—circulatory systems, perhaps, or nervous systems that operate on entirely different principles than yours. Don't touch them. This isn't forbidden; it's unnecessary. They will move away, and you will feel the magnitude of their indifference, and that feeling will teach you something about your place in the universe.

The Climbers are more forgiving of proximity, but they will incorporate you into their patterns if you watch them long enough. I recommend sitting at the base of one of their cliff-faces and observing for several hours. The pattern they trace is not random. There is architecture to it. Humans cannot trace the complete architecture—your focus is too narrow—but you can perceive its *existence*. This perception is meditative. This perception is why you came.

The unnamed things: don't seek them. They will make themselves known if they choose to. Most visitors encounter the vast thing that lives in the folds, the sleeping heartbeat. When you feel it, you'll know it. Your fear response will trigger and then fade. It means you no harm. It has transcended harm. It has transcended nearly everything.

**Time and Its Discontents**

Days blend. The sun follows its identical path. The temperature never shifts. Your watch will continue to mark time, but it will feel arbitrary. This is normal. The locals mark days by counting, not by observing celestial or seasonal shifts. They've built structures to house their counting—tally marks that run into the millions, each one struck with the same care as the last. They're counting something. Possibly they're counting duration itself, proving that time exists in a place that resists all other evidence of change.

Recommended visit duration: Two weeks minimum. Fewer than ten days and you'll return to normal civilization confused and grieving without knowing what you've lost. More than six weeks and the consensus will begin to appeal to you. You'll understand the creatures and the silence and the perfect temperature as wisdom rather than strangeness. You'll return home and spend months resisting the urge to build another Stillplains in your domestic life. This is not dangerous, but it is difficult.

## What to Expect Emotionally

**Days One Through Three: Hypervigilance**

You will assume something is wrong. The unchanging light feels like a false dawn. The perfect temperature feels like anesthesia. The silence feels like pressure. Your primitive nervous system will assume threat. This is your amygdala doing its job in an environment that has not presented a threat in geological epochs. Acknowledge the fear. Don't fight it. It will fade.

**Days Four Through Seven: Profound Calm**

You will experience a peace you have never known. Your body will stop looking for threats. Your mind will stop planning. You will become aware of your breathing and then forget your breathing again. This is meditative. This is what you came for. This is also slightly narcotic. The Stillplains aren't drugging you—they're simply not demanding the constant micro-adjustments that normal life requires. You're resting in a way that your nervous system has forgotten was possible. Enjoy this. It doesn't last.

**Days Eight Through Fourteen: Terrible Clarity**

The calm will give way to a kind of clarity that feels almost unbearable. You will see things as they are without the filter of utility or narrative. The creatures will seem less alien—you will understand them not by explanation but by *intuition*, grasping their logic without language. The landscape will seem both more living and more dead than anything you've experienced. And you will become aware that you do not want to leave. This is the consensus whispering.

You will need to exercise will to depart. You will need to remind yourself that you have a life elsewhere, obligations, people who depend on your return to a functioning state. The Stillplains doesn't resist your departure. It's past caring. But you will have to care enough for both of you.

## Practical Logistics

**Getting There**

The Stillplains exist in a fold-space accessible from several points on adjacent multiverses. Standard transit coordinates are available through the Consortium. The journey is brief and nausea-free by modern standards. You will arrive in what the locals call the eastern approach—a natural amphitheater of folded rock that serves as the primary transfer point.

**Where to Stay**

Arrange with locals ahead of time to rent a hut within one of the villages. They have developed this practice for visitors and maintain basic infrastructure—sleeping surfaces, containers for water, stone-lined pits for necessary waste. The accommodations are not luxurious, but they are dignified. The locals will not permit visitors to camp outside the villages; they've learned that solitary exposure to the Stillplains for extended periods creates dependency.

**Equipment to Bring**

- Excellent footwear with rigid soles
- Multiple water containers
- Calorie-dense food (more than seems necessary)
- Warm layers (for psychological comfort)
- A journal (with the understanding you may never read it again)
- A single book—preferably something you've read before, something that requires no concentration
- Medication you require for any condition
- Sunblock, though the even light doesn't burn
- Comfort object from home (something that smells like normal time)

## Upon Return

You will re-enter a world of wind and variation and hunger and the constant demand to *do* things. It will feel chaotic and fragmented. Your friends will ask what you learned. You will not have a satisfactory answer. You will have changed in ways you cannot articulate.

Most visitors report:

- Increased tolerance for silence
- Decreased interest in consumption and accumulation
- A persistent mild homesickness for a place they don't want to return to
- Heightened awareness of beauty in static things
- A nagging suspicion that there is a correct way to live, and they've glimpsed it, and civilization is wrong

These effects fade over months. Some visitors make multiple trips. The second visit is almost always disappointing—the Stillplains, being in its final state, does not offer novelty. But occasionally, a visitor will return and find the consensus has shifted infinitesimally. A Listener will stand in a slightly different fold. The pattern of Climbers will have evolved. The locals will have completed another section of tally marks. In a place beyond time, these micro-changes become profound. Some visitors devote their lives to documenting them.

---

## Final Note

You are going to visit a place that has achieved what most of reality is still struggling toward: *peace*. You will not bring peace back with you. You are not equipped to carry it. But you will remember it. And in remembering it, you will become a different kind of human—one who has touched the edge of perfect stillness and lived to tell about it.

The Stillplains are waiting. The consensus is patient.

Go. See what it means to stop becoming and simply *be*.
