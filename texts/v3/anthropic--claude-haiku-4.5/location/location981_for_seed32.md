# The Lattice

You are inside the Lattice, though you will not know it immediately.

The space presents itself first as a *ceiling*—no, a *wall*—no, surfaces. Everywhere surfaces. Cream-colored, tan-colored, bisque. They are not metal or stone but something harder than either, and brittle in a way that speaks of age without suggesting fragility. The substance catches light unevenly: some patches gleam as though polished by ten thousand hands, others have gone dull and chalky. This variation is not random. It maps the pressure-routes of the Lattice's own circulation, the paths where the material has worn into equilibrium with repeated contact.

You could fit your hand between the nearest two walls. You could fit your shoulders, but only if you turn them. The space opens in three dimensions simultaneously—not like a hallway that goes forward, but like a foam. Everywhere you look, there is a passage between structures. Everywhere there is also a wall, a bar, a division. The Lattice is packed. Nothing is empty. Nothing is wasted.

The architecture follows rules you will recognize only after long observation. Walls do not cross arbitrarily. Each division serves a function—not one you understand, but one that was *necessary* once, and the Lattice does not revise. The angles are not quite square. Corners hold shadows that seem geometrically wrong. This is because the Lattice was not designed but *grown*—not biologically, but through a process of accretion so slow, so balanced, that it reached stasis millennia ago. It no longer changes. It exists in perfect equilibrium, the way a standing wave exists, for as long as conditions remain identical.

The light is everywhere and nowhere. There is no sun. There is no visible source. Instead, the material itself glows—or more precisely, the material *reflects* something you cannot find, a sourceless luminescence that fills the air like a held breath. It is bright enough to see clearly, though "clearly" might be the wrong word. Shadows don't fall naturally here; they nest in the angles, permanent features, mapped long ago. The light is steady. It has been steady. It will remain steady.

The temperature is cool—not cold, but the kind of cool that suggests deep places, sealed places, places where the outside world's heat has never penetrated. Your breath does not mist. Your skin does not raise gooseflesh. The temperature is neither uncomfortable nor comforting: it simply is, another constant in the lattice's eternal equilibrium.

And the sound.

The sound is the Lattice's heartbeat, if something that never changes can be said to have a heartbeat. A low hum, omnidirectional, arising from the material itself—or from within it, from spaces you cannot see. The hum has texture: sometimes it sounds like wind moving through infinitely complex corridors, sometimes like the vibration of something vast and patient settling deeper into itself. There is also a clicking, rhythmic but not *quite* metronomic, a sound like dry matter settling, or like the Lattice is perpetually on the edge of reorganizing and perpetually deciding not to. The clicking is everywhere. It comes from the walls, from the air, from some middle distance you cannot locate. If you listen closely, you might hear it as a kind of conversation—not words, but a call-and-response between distant chambers. But you will never know if that is real or if your mind is imposing pattern on randomness.

It is not quite dark enough to be silent. It is not quite loud enough to be unbearable.

The Lattice contains almost no room for growth. The rare plants that exist here have adapted to near-total confinement. You will find them in the smallest gaps—hairlike tendrils that look almost like the Lattice itself, pale and brittle, deriving their energy not from photosynthesis (there is nothing to photosynthesize) but from something else. They feed on the Lattice. They feed *into* the Lattice. They are not alive in the way you understand it, but they are not dead either. They exist in the same stasis as everything else. Occasionally you will touch one and feel it crisp beneath your fingers, and you will not know if you killed it or if it was already in the process of becoming part of the wall.

There are no animals. There is no meat-based life. The Lattice does not permit consumption. Everything here eats itself, or eats the substrate, or does not eat at all. The *weirdness* lies not in any single impossible thing but in the totality: that this place is so thoroughly *made*, so completely *built*, and yet no builder appears. No tools rest in corners. No workers move through the passages. The Lattice simply *exists*, absolute and final, as though it always did.

Time does not move here. You can verify this by trying to measure change, and you will find none. The shadows in the corner remain where they are. The hum maintains its pitch. The temperature does not waver. The pale tendrils do not grow or recede. The dust on the surfaces—yes, there is dust, fine and pale—does not settle differently on successive days. A day has meaning here only as a measurement you bring with you. The Lattice knows nothing of them.

You are inside it. You are part of the density now. Your presence does not disturb the equilibrium because the Lattice has already accounted for you, or because you have become part of the equilibrium in the moment you entered, or because you are not really here at all but are instead a thought the Lattice is having about what it means to be observed.

The passages continue forever in every direction. Or they loop. Or they were never passageways at all but the spaces between things that are themselves the true architecture.

The hum continues. The clicking continues. The light holds. You are cool. You are dry. You are very small.

The Lattice is patient.
