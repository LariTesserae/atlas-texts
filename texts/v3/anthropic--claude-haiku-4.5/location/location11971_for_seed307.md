# The Hollow at Keth'mimm

You discover it by accident—or by accident discover that you've been here all along. The place is barely larger than a doubled fist, nestled in the shadow-fold where two obsidian plates meet on the mountainside. The elevation here is obscene; you can feel the thinness of air, the way oxygen has become negotiable. Yet the Hollow stays warm. *Stays* is the wrong word. The Hollow *seethes* warm, radiating from fractures that never fully cooled.

Water beads on every surface, which should be impossible. The obsidian is smooth and impermeable, yet moisture condenses constantly—perspiration of the rock itself, or exhalation. Droplets gather in the micro-topography where the two plates don't quite meet, forming a film of liquid so thin it's almost a rumor. When you breathe on it, more vapor rises. The air tastes mineral and hot, like standing over a stone that's just been lifted from a fire.

There are no plants. There have never been plants. Instead, the space hosts something that might be described as *saturation of presence*—a density so complete that emptiness has become strange, foreign. Threads. Strands. Filaments.

The first time you notice them, you're certain they're tricks of light refraction in the droplet-film. But they don't behave like refractions. They're too numerous, too coordinated. They hang in the impossible air like the world's finest silks, connecting the upper obsidian plate to the lower, creating a three-dimensional tapestry so dense you cannot see through it—cannot see more than a millimeter into the Hollow's depth without encountering multiple layers of them. They shimmer with a faint iridescence that might be bioluminescence, or might be the light of the droplets being scattered and rescattered infinitely.

You begin to understand that what you're looking at is *fauna* in aggregate. Not individual creatures but a civilization of them, stacked and woven so densely that to call them separate entities seems already wrong.

They're moving.

The motion is not frantic. Nothing here is frantic. But it is *constant*, a slow peristalsis throughout the entire architecture of threads. Some strands thicken as you watch. Others thin. Waves of activity pulse through the network—not electrical, not quite, but something cognate to it. Waves that originate at the edges and fold back toward the center. Waves that move in patterns that seem almost like *thought*, or the slow deliberation of thought given form in silk.

The water is integral. Droplets travel along certain threads like blood through veins, moving upward via capillary action, then downward through other channels. Where water meets thread, there's a faint sound—not quite a vibration, more like the ghost of a vibration recorded in your bones after the fact. A *pssshhh* so quiet it might be the sound of your own blood pressure. The creatures drink, or breathe, or metabolize—the categories blur.

There is no silence here, though the sound level hovers just at the edge of perception. If you focus on the Hollow for too long, the whisper-sounds accumulate in your awareness until they form a kind of susurration, like wind through mineral canyons. Yet when you shift your attention, the sound collapses back into near-nothing.

The temperature is brutal. The rock around the Hollow is radiating heat that would be scalding if you could touch it for more than seconds. You sense—you *know* without evidence—that there are thermal vents deep in the mountain's structure, that the Hollow is positioned directly above one, that the water cycling through the threads is kept perpetually warm and the rock perpetually weeping. The creatures have arranged themselves in the sweet-spot where heat and moisture intersect. They've *arranged themselves*. Or they've self-organized. Or they've been arranged by whatever organized them, countless generations ago, and the arrangement persists because it's stable. Because it works.

The edges of the Hollow are sharp. Fresh. The obsidian fractures that form the walls show no weathering; they fractured recently—geologically speaking. Days ago? Years? The newness is disorienting. This place hasn't been here long enough for the mineral world to have worn smooth; it retains the violence of its formation, the sharpness of emergence. Yet the threads fill it as if they've been waiting for exactly this space to appear, as if they were called forth by the opening of the crack.

The weirdness settles slowly.

At first you rationalize it: high-altitude species, extremophile organisms, nothing truly strange if you accept the premises. But then you notice that the threads aren't arranged randomly. They follow patterns. Geometric patterns. Sacred patterns. Fractals within fractals. And sometimes—only sometimes, only when you're not looking directly—they seem to rearrange themselves into different geometries, as if solving problems, as if computing something.

The light that filters into the Hollow is normal enough—whatever passes for normal at this elevation, in this thin air. But something about how it refracts through the thread-matrix and the droplet-film creates the impression that the Hollow exists in a slightly different spectrum. The colors are muted, shifted, as if you're seeing this place through water (which, in a sense, you are). The iridescence on the threads doesn't correspond to natural pigmentation; it suggests interference patterns, wave behavior, light being manipulated by geometry rather than absorbed and reflected.

You become aware, after a time, that you cannot measure the Hollow accurately. It seems to recede deeper as you focus on it, as if the layers of threads extend further into the rock than they should. Your sense of scale fractures. The individual threads might be as fine as spider silk, or they might be as thick as rope and only appear fine due to distance. The droplets might be beading on a surface the size of your palm, or they might be rain falling through a canyon only millimeters wide. The Hollow might be a single grain of dust's worth of space, or you might be standing in front of something vaster than you have words for, seeing only the smallest aperture of it.

The creatures—and you accept now that the threads *are* creatures, or the creations of creatures, or something between—continue their slow peristaltic dance. Water rises and falls. Heat emanates. The whisper-sound persists. The geometric patterns shift, resolve, shift again. Nothing dies here. Nothing is born, not visibly. But something accumulates. Something builds. Something *changes*, with infinite slowness and perfect intention.

You could stay and watch for hours. You could stay and watch the Hollow reorganize itself into configurations you've never seen before. You could stay and listen to the almost-silence gather itself into almost-speech.

But the air at this elevation is thin, and you cannot stay forever.

When you move, the threads seem to pulse once—a unified wave moving through the entire network. Whether it's acknowledgment or dismissal, you cannot say. The Hollow remains where it is, suspended between the obsidian plates, radiating its heat into the high thin air, condensing its moisture, cycling its water, tending to its strange aggregated fauna, solving problems you don't have language for, in a language that might be made of light and silk and the whisper of heat rising from stone.

Behind you, as you climb away, the sound follows a little further than seems reasonable.

Then it stops. Then it stops having ever started. Then the Hollow settles back into whatever it was before you looked at it.
