## The Lives of the Unoccupied Town

Nothing lives in the rooms. Nothing sleeps in the stacked chambers, crosses the bridges to reach home, or keeps anything in the courts. The visible town is not a shelter with a hidden population. It is a set of surfaces on which a very sparse population becomes intermittently visible.

Most of that population never becomes visible at all.

### The foundation

The foundation is not a creature concealed beneath the town. There is no buried bulk to uncover, no heart beneath the central court, no final chamber in which the whole animal might be found curled around itself. Its body is a condition of certain fractures: extremely thin mineral lattices, organized and active where the plain permits them to spread, absent where it does not.

To call it one organism is convenient, but the architecture cannot confirm the convenience. Lobes may remain connected far below the basin or separate without any visible event above. Several cold seams might belong to a single advancing front; an apparently continuous district might record fronts that never met. The foundation has no outline that corresponds to the town’s outline.

Nor is all the grey shell its living body. Much of the visible town is material deposited around tissue that has already withdrawn. A chamber is not a cell still inhabited by the colony. It is more nearly the shape left where feeding has ceased. A wall is the margin of that feeding; a stair records repeated obstruction; a conduit is a passage once occupied by a process rather than by anything meant to travel through it.

The foundation’s present location is therefore better detected by cold than by form. Where it is active, fresh laminae whiten and the adjacent air loses a little warmth. Elsewhere, structures that appear equally intact may contain no living tissue at all. The colony does not maintain them for future use. It narrows them, seals them, sheds them, or leaves them standing while its attention—if that word does not imply too much—moves elsewhere.

It does not build in order to occupy. It deposits in order to continue, and occupation of space is the residue.

### The bronze fans and the crusts beneath them

The fans are the first inhabitants that can simply be pointed to, though even they change category under observation.

Folded in shallow dimness, a fan looks applied to the wall: a dark ornament, a small mineral bracket, perhaps an unusually regular flaw in the joint. Opened in deep dimness, it becomes an account of the local weather. Its branches turn toward currents too slight to move air. Fine divisions in its filaments correspond to divisions in the dust stream, so that the fan seems less to stand in the courtyard than to explain how the courtyard is flowing.

It lives where flow separates, not where dust merely accumulates. A deep drift would be less useful to it than a thin stream repeatedly passing the same edge. Charge moves through its bronze-black branches in slow patterns. Scarce elements are retained; new material is added without swelling, sap, or any visible act of growth. The temporary coppery scales along its edges are the brightest part of its life and the part most likely to be taken by something else.

A fan does not wilt when it fails. Its organization simply becomes harder to maintain. Branches cease to open. New scales no longer appear. The foundation may close over part of it, leaving the remainder fixed in a wall that is still growing. Eventually its substance flakes or is taken back into the general economy of mineral and charge.

The soot-coloured crust sometimes found beneath a fan occupies its lee more than its shade. It persists where the fan has altered the dust stream and left a narrower, poorer possibility of feeding. It is easy to overlook even when exposed, and easier still to mistake for a stain. But there are no stains here in the ordinary sense. The crust is one of the few living surfaces that can cover something without becoming part of the wall.

### The scalebacks

A scaleback at rest has almost no outline. Its flat, segmented body follows the lamination of a wall so closely that stillness and absence become difficult to distinguish. A court may contain one without containing any visible shape that does not seem to belong to the architecture.

Its movement is not merely slow; it is rationed. It may remain fixed through several light cycles, then advance smoothly and stop again. Nothing in its posture suggests urgency between movements. A crossing is often known only by comparing positions: the inscription was beside a doorway, and later it is near the opposite curb. The court itself supplies no track.

Scalebacks feed on the charged films left by settling dust and on the fans’ new coppery growth. Their feeding leaves almost no evidence. A film becomes thinner; a few temporary scales are absent. There are no scraps beneath them and no damaged fan-heads marking a favourite place. They do not gather in herds around the fans, and nothing about their spacing suggests companionship or avoidance. They are simply rare enough that each encounter can seem like the discovery of a unique animal.

Their bodies are small concentrations of material already concentrated by the fans. In a town where almost everything is reused, this makes them valuable without making them numerous.

### The hunter

The hunter is easier to mistake for new construction than for an animal.

Extended in a floor channel, it has the pallor of a fresh seam. Its narrow body follows the available joint so exactly that the channel appears to have been repaired. It can remain there while dust weather fills and empties the court above it. Nothing hangs over its resting place; there is no burrow entrance, no accumulation of remains.

It is usually spoken of in the singular, though the town offers no reliable census. There may be only one. There may be several whose lives almost never overlap in visible space.

Most of a hunt occurs before contact, in stillness and position. There is no pursuit across an open court, no sudden acceleration to distinguish hunter from scaleback. When the pale body finally lifts from a seam, it does so almost imperceptibly. It folds around a scaleback and draws the animal slowly out of its plates. Even nearby, the capture has no audible struggle. It appears less like violence than like a gradual change in which parts of the wall are organized and which are not.

Afterwards, a small fan of empty segments remains. The hunter returns to narrowness. Dust passes over the plates, and eventually the foundation takes them. Nothing marks the place as dangerous.

### The ecology that occupies nothing

These lives do not form a secret society within the town. The foundation does not cultivate the fans. The fans do not shelter the scalebacks except incidentally. The hunter is not a guardian, and the central court is not a sanctuary. There is no shared use of the architecture and no common response to its city-like form.

Their effective geographies are different. For the foundation, the town is a pattern of fractures, resistance, cold fronts, and spaces already exhausted. For a fan, it is a few dust streams and the places where they divide. For a scaleback, it is a discontinuous set of surfaces bearing edible films or temporary scales. For the hunter, it is the connected narrowness of channels and joints. The settlement visible from the highest roof is none of these towns exactly.

What connects them is the passage of matter. Dust brings in what the plain and weather supply. The fans retain scarce elements; the scalebacks concentrate what the fans have gathered; the hunter concentrates what the scalebacks carry. The foundation incorporates the residue and whatever organized life fails to retain. Death is not followed by decay but by the loss of organized charge, then flaking, dispersal, and reuse. An entire ecology can pass through a street without leaving a stain.

This is why the animals are so rare. Their scarcity is not necessarily the remnant of a richer population. It is the visible expression of how little surplus exists above the foundation’s own continuing growth.

In shallow dimness, almost all of them can be overlooked. The fans are folded darkly against their joints. A scaleback is part of a wall. The hunter is a pale line in a channel. Active seams are colder than the air, but nothing moves at the growing edge quickly enough to betray itself.

In deep dimness the town becomes busy without becoming fast. Fresh layers whiten. Dust turns through the conduits and divides at the curbs. The fans open and develop their brief copper edges. Somewhere a scaleback is no longer where it was. The hunter rises a little from the pavement, or only changes the tension along its length. Around the central court, columns of dust stand at the rim while the single bronze fan opens and the centre remains untouched. Nothing emerges from the black joint to explain the settlement. There is no sovereign, source, or hidden occupant waiting there.

By the next shallow dimness, the evidence is mostly negative: a court swept clean, a film gone, a fan folded, a stair carrying one new tread, a doorway slightly less open than before.

Nothing lives in the rooms. The rooms are among the things that living leaves behind.
