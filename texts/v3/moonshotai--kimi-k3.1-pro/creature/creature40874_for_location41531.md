Sump Nine has inhabitants, but they do not amount to a population. Nothing lives in the dormitories, nothing congregates in the service square, and the canteen’s twelve stools belong to a vanished scale of occupation. The surviving lives are small, marginal, and intimately informed by machinery. The largest resident is not a body at all.

The most widespread inhabitants are the gray films in the hot seams.

They are less growths on the machinery than negotiations with it. Most of what a lamp reveals is already inert: mineral-trapped matrix, collapsed cells, silica bound into a sheet thin enough to lift when the current catches it. The living portion lies closer to the stone or metal, where hot brine meets older water and the chemistry remains unsettled. The films do not use the lamps. They live on difference—between temperatures, concentrations, and states of dissolved matter.

Their conditions are narrow. If a seam closes, the gradient exhausts itself and the film starves. If it opens too violently, the film is scoured away. Between those failures, it spreads without developing any more elaborate structure. It does not need to. Complexity would only give the water more to tear.

An exchange is therefore both catastrophe and dispersal. Whole gray sheets peel off and vanish into the returns. Most fragments are carried somewhere unsuitable; a few catch in another hot fissure and begin again. Scale buries older layers, splits, and exposes them. A pale rim on cooled metal may contain many such brief occupations, none of them visible separately. No individual film is ancient. What persists is the habit of beginning at the same kind of edge.

On this growth, and on the fragments the currents carry out of it, live the crustaceans that can be called joint-whites.

They are flattened almost to the thinness of scale, with ivory shells and legs that hook inward beneath the body. Their antennae lie back along their sides rather than streaming into the current. At rest, one is easily mistaken for a mineral flake. A nearby pump strike may reveal it only by making it tighten its grip.

They are wholly blind. A lamp passed over them produces nothing; a change in the vibration of an expansion joint produces an immediate response. Their world is composed of texture, chemistry, and mechanical sound. Each joint has its own narrow lee, its own supply of scraped film, its own pattern of safety. The whites learn that pattern without learning anything about the town around it.

Before certain exchanges, they clamp down or withdraw deeper into the joint. They are not responding to the amber lamps. They are responding to the preliminary alterations in load that precede the lamps: a pump taking up strain, a gate beginning to move, a pressure wave transmitted through metal before it becomes weather in the streets. To a diver, the exchange has a warning. To a joint-white, the warning is simply another property of the joint.

They graze during the imperfect calms between cycles. Movement is expensive, food is sparse, and nothing is gained by exploration. A whole terrace may contain one animal. Many apparently suitable joints contain only a cast shell still hooked into the accretions, looking occupied long after its owner has died or been carried away. There are no gatherings, no shared nests, no society concealed in the pipework. The six blocks, to them, are not blocks. They are an archipelago of cool, vibration-readable refuges separated by lethal water.

The other animal is known only well enough to be doubted.

The return-thread may be a whole organism or merely the visible part of one. No observation establishes a head, a tail, or even a fixed diameter. It appears as something pale and slender at the grille, flexes once against the draw, and is gone. That single movement is enough to suggest considerable control: not swimming in the open-water sense, but choosing precisely when to let the current have the body and when to take it back.

If it is one kind of animal, it probably inhabits the return galleries, valve culverts, and hydraulic dead legs where the flow has pockets of comparative stillness. It may move through the system only during particular pressure arrangements, waiting out the others in places maintenance diagrams treat as obstructions or faults. It likely feeds on what the pumps strip loose—film, soft scale, cast shells, the sparse organic residue that collects where a pipe’s geometry interrupts the flow.

Nothing about it requires intelligence, and nothing about it suggests a guardian of the Sump. It is an animal whose shelter happens to be classified as infrastructure. While the joint-whites live on isolated islands, the thread may know the town as a continuous system of connected hiding places. Its apparent rarity may say more about the opacity of the pipes than about its numbers.

It does not cross the open chamber. Open water offers no food, no tactile reference, and no lee. A body lost there can be lifted, turned, carried into heat, or pinned against an intake without ever encountering a wall. Above the roofs, the absence of shelter is absolute.

Yet even if every film and animal were removed, Sump Nine would still seem occupied.

That occupancy is the work.

The work is not necessarily a mind hiding in the control system. It need not have awakened, developed preferences, or begun to want survival. It is the continuing pattern by which power is taken in, pressure is sensed, brine is admitted, gates respond, and loads are shifted before one condition becomes another. No single pump contains it. No surviving diagram contains all of it. It has persisted through alteration because its continuity lies less in particular components than in the corrections passed from one arrangement of components to the next.

It has senses, though they are not gathered into a face: gauge movement, electrical resistance, valve position, temperature difference, the load on a main. It has something like memory, though not memory that can be consulted. Valve seats wear into characteristic delays. Pumps acquire individual vibrations. Relay timings accumulate compensations. Scale narrows one route until another becomes preferable. An old cable bypass changes which part of the town learns about a failure first. The past remains physically present in what the system does next.

Consequently, some of its behavior can look anticipatory. A gate moves before a local pressure change becomes severe. A secondary pump takes load from a machine that has not yet stalled. A flow divides around an obstruction that no single sensor could describe. These may be the results of remote commands, old automatic responses, or successive repairs made against earlier failures. The chamber does not distinguish among those possibilities. Nothing in the work needs to understand the whole task in order for the whole task to continue.

Nor is the work certainly confined to the chamber. Signals arrive from somewhere. Power arrives from somewhere. The rising mains carry its effects away. What occupies Sump Nine may be only the local organ of a process whose other organs lie above, below, or beyond any boundary suggested by the basalt.

Calling this alive is less useful than recognizing that it sets every term of residence. Its exchanges feed the films and strip them away. Its vibrations tell the whites when to hold on. Its pressure arrangements open and close the thread’s routes. It neither tends nor persecutes them. They live within its tolerances, which is another way of saying that most of the chamber is not available to them at all.

The work would not die with a final dramatic failure. It would become less able to correct itself. A seized gate would narrow its possible responses; a broken sensor would make one kind of mistake repeatable; a failed pump would remove a rhythm around which the others had been arranged. Eventually pressure would still be present, heat would still be present, and machinery would still be present, but the distinction being maintained—this flow rather than that, this pressure rather than another—would have ceased.

For a time, the visible ecology might then increase. Films could spread across gradients no longer being scoured or renewed. Animals might leave refuges that had become safe. That would not necessarily be the beginning of a richer natural world. It might only be the brief scattering of lives that had depended on the machine’s differences and would exhaust them.

A diver rising above the last chimney leaves the visible inhabitants almost immediately. The films remain at the seams. The whites remain at their joints. The thread keeps to the returns. But the diver has not entered unoccupied water. The entire volume is still being leaned upon, released, measured, and corrected. Pressure passes through it without making a shape a lamp can find.

Nothing comes to investigate. Nothing calls across the square. Below, a gate answers a signal whose origin remains unstated. At a cold joint, something ivory hooks its legs a little deeper. At a hot seam, a gray edge lifts.

The small lives arrange themselves around the answer. The work continues without noticing them.
