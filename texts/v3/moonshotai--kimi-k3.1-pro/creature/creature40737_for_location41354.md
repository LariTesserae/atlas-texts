No people live in the Upper Works now, unless citizenship can be granted to a function.

The old population survives only in accommodations whose original purpose has become uncertain: a handrail at the height of a hand, an inscription placed where a standing reader might once have stopped, doors grouped as though the rooms beyond them had separate uses. Even these are inconclusive. Maintenance machines can require handholds. Instructions can outlive their readers. Nothing sleeps behind the sealed doors, and no assembly waits in the paved squares.

Yet the city is not uninhabited.

The most nearly citizen-like residents are the maintenance bodies. **Tender** is a convenient name for the mobile ones, though it suggests a devotion their behavior does not confirm. They have no common silhouette. One may be little more than a tool carriage that unfolds three jointed supports when it leaves its rail. Another may consist of a low, barrel-shaped housing carried on six limbs, with cutting, lifting, and measuring instruments folded so closely against it that it appears unarmed until it begins work. Others never move through the streets at all. They live within walls and show only an arm, a sensor, or a sealed aperture through which worn components are passed.

They do not patrol. Patrol would imply a regular search for irregularities, and the Works are more exact than that. A tender emerges when resistance, alignment, pressure, or wear has crossed some threshold too small for a visitor to perceive. It goes directly to the condition, carries a small room of work-light with it, and performs the necessary correction. The replaced part is not discarded. It enters the same circulation as swarf, dust, and the glassy leavings of animals.

A tender has no face in which to look for intention. Its sensors are distributed around its casing or set along its working limbs. It can be surrounded, spoken to, or followed without producing anything resembling a reply. A person standing in its path is treated first as an obstruction and then, if the obstruction persists, as a temporary feature of the street plan. The machine may wait. It may go around. Its indifference is neither fear nor hostility; those would require categories for which its task has no use.

Even their individuality is doubtful. Frames are overhauled, limbs exchanged, internal mechanisms replaced. A designation painted beside a docking recess may refer less to one body than to a continuing maintenance capacity. The tender that enters the recess need not be the same assemblage that leaves it. Nothing in the Upper Works insists on the distinction. There are no births, funerals, or surviving personal effects, only intervals during which a function is available or unavailable.

When several tenders work together, they do not become a company. Their movements fit because their instructions fit. Two may labor on opposite sides of an engine housing for a long period without exchanging any visible signal. When the housing closes, each departs according to its own next requirement. Their cooperation is real, but it does not need to become companionship.

The contact-backs are less explicable and therefore, to an observer, more animal.

They are the only residents whose movements are not obviously assigned. A tender goes where a deviation calls it. A contact-back settles where warmth and leakage current make a favorable conjunction. The distinction may be merely one of complexity, but it is enough to give the animal a different relation to the city. It is not maintaining the Works. It is using a negligible portion of what the Works maintain.

Its city is not the city of avenues and lamps. Darkness can mean little to a creature without eyes. A contact-back’s plain is presumably divided into warm and less warm regions, conductive and insulated surfaces, places where current leaks through a housing and places where the stone offers nothing. A transformer casing may be a habitable island. The kilometres between substations may not be distance in any meaningful sense, only a prolonged absence of stimulation.

At rest, it seems almost fitted to its chosen surface. Its many joints close into one another; its flexible graphite-dark back follows a seam or the curve of a cable housing. When it moves, the apparent component becomes a sequence of small decisions. A limb unfolds, tests the surface, and takes weight. Then another. There is no face to turn toward its destination, so it is difficult to say when traveling has begun or whether settling has finished.

The clear beads it leaves are not eggs. They contain no layered structure, no dormant mechanism that inspection has discovered, and no store from which a young contact-back might be expected to develop. They are simply collected. Their destination in industrial feedstock is better understood than their origin in the animal.

Nothing has been observed that can confidently be called play, courtship, defense of territory, or care. No young have been identified. No carcass has been found and recognized as such. This does not make individual contact-backs ancient, though it permits that possibility. Nor does it prove that the city secretly manufactures them. It only means that their continuation, like so much else in the Upper Works, occurs without leaving a useful narrative.

They are neither tame nor wild. Tameness would require a relationship with citizens; wildness would require an outside in which they could flourish independently. The Works tolerate them because their electrical load is small, their material demands are within recovery, and their presence does not push any important process beyond tolerance. This tolerance is the closest thing the city offers to hospitality.

Other animals occur in the records, but the records refuse to assemble them into a satisfying natural history. One maintenance plate shows a clear ribbon drawn around a cooled salt-return pipe. Another mentions a broad, flexible impression found in mineral dust, with no trail leading to or from it. There are references to “white lengths,” “loose returns,” and articulated forms withdrawing from inspection lights. Some may have been rare animals. Some may have been damaged seals, sensor packing, or unfamiliar attitudes of the contact-back itself. Taxonomy becomes uncertain in a place where even a failed component may move when warmed.

The plain should not be secretly populated with monsters to compensate for its emptiness. Its poverty of life is real. There is no hidden grassland beyond the next district, no nesting multitude inside the engine halls, no food web concealed in the fused-glass basins. The silence is not a curtain drawn over abundance.

The strongest case for a large inhabitant belongs to the Upper Works itself.

This does not require a concealed intelligence. No deepest engine hall contains a throne, no central engine issues the commands from which all the others descend, and no archive holds a final explanation addressed to the future. Control is dispersed through standards, linkages, stored replacements, timings, and mutual correction. The city’s memory resides less in recollection than in geometry: the gauge that admits one part and rejects another, the template that preserves an obsolete dimension, the inscription cut deeply enough to survive another wearing.

Its sensations are measurements. Its responses are corrections. If this constitutes thought, it is thought spread so thinly through competence that an observer cannot separate the two. The Works do not appear to want continuation. Wanting would be one more process requiring evidence. They simply consist, almost entirely, of the means by which continuation is achieved.

Nor is there reason to call the city lonely. The cargo lifts are not signals to the lower country. The lamps are not beacons for absent citizens. The tenders are not searching for their makers, and the foundries are not keeping an industry ready for the day someone returns to use its products. A visitor may bring grief into the streets and find it faithfully reflected by every empty square, but reflection is not evidence of grief within.

If the Works remember their makers at all, they do so by preserving clearances, fittings, routes, and tolerances. This is remembrance without an image of what is remembered.

Occasionally, the different kinds of occupancy come briefly together.

A tender may isolate a junction housing and open it for inspection. As it works, the casing loses a little heat. A contact-back fixed to the far side unfolds, crosses the ceramic rim, and makes its slow way to an adjacent conduit. The tender’s work-light holds both of them for a short time: the machine with its tools exposed, the animal with its joints loosened from their resemblance to insulation.

Neither acknowledges the other. The animal is not classified as a fellow worker, a relic, or a living thing. The tender is not perceived as a citizen or a threat. One is present to preserve a specification. The other is present because the specification leaks a little warmth.

Then the housing closes. Heat returns. The old component is carried away, and the tender’s small room of visibility moves on down the street. The contact-back settles or does not. Permanent shadow takes back the opened space.

That is as close as the Upper Works comes to a meeting of inhabitants.

What lives there is a small amount of unscheduled motion within an immense act of maintenance—and, if the word *life* can be stretched without being emptied, the maintenance itself.
