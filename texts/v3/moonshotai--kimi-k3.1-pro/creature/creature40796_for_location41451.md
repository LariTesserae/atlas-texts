The Low Works have no fauna, but they are not without natives. A native, here, is anything that can return without being rebuilt.

By that standard, the warm mallet is not the inhabitant. The tap may be.

Nothing requires an unseen mason to be standing just beyond the haze. The single clean note can be complete in itself: no backswing, no arm, no intention concerning a second stroke. It occurs where mallet, joint, and unfinishedness have come into the proper relation. Then the wood or stone retains a little heat, and the tap is gone. Or it has gone elsewhere. A black-glass blade may similarly be less a tool for cutting than a place where a cut occasionally waits. The plumb bob does not contain down, but down can sometimes be persuaded to remain near it.

These are not spirits wearing tools. The tools are simply addresses at which certain instructions can be found.

An instruction is resident when it can still produce a correction. A scored tablet bearing exact dimensions may be inert. A crooked cord that causes the water on either side of it to agree about level is, by the standards of the Works, vigorously occupied. Its vitality is jurisdiction. Its reproduction is recurrence. Its death is not breakage but irrelevance: the stakes may remain standing and the cord may remain taut, yet nothing any longer consults them.

Most such lives are extremely brief. A join exists while two surfaces become one seam. A pour occupies slurry until slurry becomes an impression. A level perishes when the plain changes depth faster than the ground can answer it. But some instructions return. The same proportion appears in a brick, then in a platform, then in the rim of a basin several kilometers across. Scale is not maturity. The proportion has not grown; only its jurisdiction has changed.

No master copy need exist. The buried courses are archives of earlier applicability, not ancestors. An instruction can disappear from every tablet and tool and still be remembered by the behavior of clay. When a scored rectangle continues to hold after its cord has been lifted, the measurement has ceased to be merely a property of the apparatus. It has become a custom of the ground.

Compatible customs gather. Level, down, closure, and bond can occupy the same patch without exhausting one another. Their association is what gives a freshly measured square its peculiar sense of shelter. Nothing roofs it. Nothing necessarily walls it. But for a little while the patch has more than boundaries: it has manners. Water enters and adopts its level. A plumb line hung within it keeps the local down. Clay closes with the same soft suction at each of its corners. The square feels exempt because several small instructions have temporarily stopped requiring separate enforcement.

A wall or platform is a larger concurrence of such things, not necessarily an individual in its own right. Construction is the phase in which an assembly of instructions becomes visible in matter. Unbuilding disperses that assembly without necessarily ending it. Brick returning to slurry is no more decisive than a cord being removed. The body has been withdrawn; the question is whether anything returns.

Some residents never acquire a solid body at all.

A doorway may recur only as condensation: two uprights and a lintel described by droplets on blank air. A staircase may exist repeatedly in the moments before material follows, and never once become ceramic. The platform reflected in a puddle may be more persistent than several platforms actually built, provided the reflection continues to precede them. A brick heard settling hours before any wall begins is another kind of residency, an instruction present first in the permission granted to sound.

These are not quite memories and not quite prophecies. They are intervals in which something is specified without yet being tolerated by matter.

Water is their most accommodating medium. It is not alive, and there is no reason to think all the water in the Works shares a mind. But clay remembers pressure, ceramic remembers arrangement, and water remembers outline. Its memory is brief and strangely indifferent to sequence. It can retain the joints of a wall after the wall has gone, hold the volume of a basin after the basin has been returned to slurry, or preserve the image of a structure whose instruction has not yet arrived elsewhere. For a little while, water allows the unbuilt to remain contiguous with the built.

The fronts are not predators of this population. They are changes in what can be resident.

One front may leave every object standing while emptying the objects of force. Three stakes remain; the cord remains wet and taut; the small jurisdiction is nevertheless dead. Another may unbuild a province and carry its instructions intact into the next stillness. What crosses the dark horizon-seam is not killed. It is omitted. Conversely, what vanishes before such a seam may not have been omitted at all. It may return a thousand pools away, exact in every relation and composed entirely of different material.

This makes survival difficult to recognize. Matter proves nothing. Disappearance proves little. The only reliable evidence is recurrence without copying.

From sufficient distance, all of this might be called one inhabitant: the Work itself. But that is useful only if one can imagine an inhabitant with no skin, no center, and no thought separable from its action. It does not appear to have plans and then embody them. It discovers what it might intend by seeing what recurs. The fronts are not messages from some concealed artificer, nor necessarily its moods. They are how the Work differs from itself.

At times, great preferences seem to move through that difference: a preference for the plane, for the rim, for release; for floor, vessel, stillness. They can look like rival persons. They need not be. A single wall may serve all three in succession, standing level, enclosing water, and then separating into courses so that the water can forget its shape. No tablet bears a signature. Measurement does not guarantee a measurer who can be found.

Yet occasionally continuity becomes something more than recurrence.

A measured patch survives a front, not because its cord held against the pressure, but because the new construction accommodates it. Foundations rise up to its scored boundary and take its level. A staircase descending out of blank condensation arrives precisely at its surface. Later the stakes are gone, and still the next version refers to the patch. A pool returns there. A warm seam bends around it. A plumb bob left within its boundary finds the same down after intervals in which down elsewhere has become unreliable.

Such a place has acquired a *here*.

It need not be conscious. It need not even remain materially unchanged. Its body may be a platform in one stillness, a shallow pool in the next, and between them only a small exemption around which immense acts of making occur. Its identity lies in being taken into account. If tools left there repeatedly become warm, if the same unbuilt doorway gathers at its edge, if particular acts return to it without being summoned by tablets or cords, then the place has habits as well as continuity.

That is the closest thing the Low Works presently offer to a self: not a builder, not a structure, but a location the Work has begun to remember. It does not live in the Works as a body lives in a room. The Works live around it.

Perhaps these small continuities are merely successful measurements. Perhaps they are the beginnings of natives in a fuller sense. The distinction may be one of the things the Works are constructing, slowly and without yet possessing a term for it.

Until then, their population consists of provisional lives: taps without hands, levels without cords, enclosures that exist only in condensation, outlines survived briefly by water. None has a childhood. None leaves remains. Each persists only for as long as some part of the world can still be persuaded to answer it.

If a final stillness ever comes, it may provide one body in which all the compatible instructions can at last reside together. Or it may extinguish them by leaving nothing further to correct. The first true inhabitant may be whatever remains when no more work is necessary—or whatever the Works discover they can no longer omit without ceasing to be themselves.
