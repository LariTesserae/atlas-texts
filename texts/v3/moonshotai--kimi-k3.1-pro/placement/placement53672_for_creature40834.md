I would begin as a person with a notebook. If I stayed long enough, I might become a gesture.

I would have come down the iron stair intending to draw the whole arrangement. The paper would cockle in the damp. Lines that looked exact beside the dynamo would become misleading by the cistern. I would draw arrows from engine to animal to root, as if influence were a kind of road, and then spend an afternoon discovering that a guided leak had altered a warm roost, and the roost had thickened a root bed, and the root bed had begun narrowing someone’s door.

Eventually, someone would give me less comprehensive work.

You would find me after an opening, when the rain had stopped being rain and become a set of tasks. I would be near the cistern court, out of the beetles’ file, with a shuttered lamp at my hip and a short listening rod against a pipe. My cuffs would be wet. I would be trying to decide whether a drip beyond the next root braid was striking leaf or iron.

My work would be to return.

It would not be a grand office. The keepers would make the repair; the tenders would watch it through pressure and opening. I would come back after an hour, after the next opening, after the new material had been there long enough for people to stop noticing it. I would put my hand where theirs had put theirs. I would look for the place where wood, water, iron, or animals had begun to answer.

My notebook entries would become smaller:

*Nymphs sparse at the warm elbow.*  
*Drip changed from leaf to iron.*  
*Beetles crossing the new brace; no file.*  
*Pressure steady. Fish unchanged.*  
*Not yet.*

I would learn that *Does it work?* is usually only the first and least interesting question. A pipe could carry water perfectly and still make a bad warmth. A brace could hold its load and teach the root an unfortunate shape. A leak could be stopped so efficiently that three other materials inherited it.

This would not make me wise. I would still be pleased when my guesses proved right, and slow to admit the particular pleasure of that. I would swear when beetles entered stores. I would dislike the first minutes on the plain, when wind touched every side of me and nothing gave the distance a size. In the houses I would talk too much; in the hall I would learn to make do with a wrist touched twice, a lamp opened and closed.

Nor would waiting always be the safe choice. Delay would also act upon the chamber. While I waited for certainty, wood could enclose something that ought to have been moved, or seepage could find its way beneath a street. Some of my entries would have to record objections not to repairs, but to my own hesitation. There would be no clean position outside the arrangement from which to be cautious.

In the working lane, my name-sign would gradually become thumb and forefinger held a little apart: *not closed, not finished, not yet*. At first it would be a joke, because I used the sign so often. Then it would be how someone summoned me across the court.

I would not stand at the governor during an opening. I would be one of the lesser lamps, or no lamp at all. But when the animals fell silent, I would stop like everyone else. However often I heard it, the hush would remain too complete to treat as an interval in work. The engines would stand exposed beneath it. Individual drops would declare their materials. Once, in those few seconds, I would hear water strike water where there had been dry peat at my last visit. There would be no time to decide what that meant. Then the deep wooden click would pass through the chamber, rain would fall, and meaning would have to wait until after the valves, the wet tools, the wings on the floor.

Later, someone might show me a gauge that had behaved perfectly for six days. The new pipe would be dry. Its fittings would already be darkened by hands. Everything would invite me to write *answered*. But behind it, along an elbow where cicada nymphs should have been packed, there would be a bare stretch of warm iron.

Perhaps the repair had caused that. Perhaps the nymphs had moved for reasons of their own. My work would not be to make them signify. It would only be to notice the temptation to finish.

I would look at the steady gauge. Then I would lift my hand, thumb and forefinger almost touching.

*Not yet.*

Years afterward, someone else would be doing the returning. They might use the same sign without knowing it had ever been anyone’s name. It would no longer mean *wait for me*. It would simply mean leaving enough room for an answer before closing the record.

That would suit me. I would have begun as someone trying to draw the whole chamber, and ended as a small hesitation in the hands of someone I would never meet.
