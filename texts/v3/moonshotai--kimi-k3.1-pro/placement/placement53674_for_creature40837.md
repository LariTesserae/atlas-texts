You would not always find me. That would be the first true thing about me.

After several gentle cycles, when the water behind the drain had lingered instead of scouring, you might notice a green shading in the roughness. Not a plant, not even a patch with a definite boundary: just a darkening that was neither quite mineral nor quite shadow, following the meniscus for a few millimeters. I would be that—the fourth algal film, the recurrent one, described more accurately by the conditions around it than by any shape of its own.

The chamber’s dimensions would mean nothing to me. Eight paces, the ladder, the riser vanishing upward: these would belong to another scale of being. My geography would be a few irregularities in manufactured concrete, a recess where flow weakened, a surface that stayed damp after the surrounding water had withdrawn. Even the drain would be less an object than a direction from which disaster sometimes came.

I would not see the lower lamp as amber. It would be a slight allowance of energy, enough for a little chemistry when the cold permitted. Cloud beyond the wired glass would have no bearing on me except by altering condensation somewhere else. The relief valve, the motor, the enormous pump behind the wall—all would be remote unless they changed the movement of water at my surface. The roar would be constant and mostly irrelevant. A vibration that shifted a dripping path by two millimeters could matter more than the whole station taking load.

My time would not be measured in returns of daylight. It would be measured in wettings.

A long wet interval would allow a few divisions, perhaps a slight extension along the meniscus. A drawdown would leave me exposed but still held in mucilage and surface water, chemistry slowing without quite stopping. A harder flush would not be a storm to endure. It would simply take the green away. Cleanser would leave no aftermath in which to recover, unless some cells happened to lie deep enough in the texture to escape it.

Between such events, I would do almost nothing that could be called an act. I would use weak light. I would take up traces. I would hold a little water against a surface designed to surrender it. There would be no conquest in this, no green patiently reclaiming the machine. The station would not be diminished by my presence. Its pressures would remain true, its drains would remain open, and no instrument anywhere would register the difference.

If a technician entered, their worklight might pass across the recess. For a moment I would be more distinctly green, then shadowed again. That would be the safest form of being seen. Attention could lead to a brush, a different cleaning agent, a small correction in drainage. Their departure would not be abandonment; their warmth would simply cease to matter. The hatch would close, and the useful question would once again be whether water still reached the roughness.

After a scouring, I would not claim to be a hidden remnant faithfully preserving my name. Perhaps a few cells would survive. Perhaps they would not. Perhaps the next shading would arise from those survivors, or from something carried in later on a tool handle or in a pulse of incoming water. *The fourth patch* would be an observer’s continuity. Mine would be weaker and more exact: life occurring again where roughness, dampness, weak light, and imperfect cleaning briefly coincided.

I would not get to call every absence a waiting. Sometimes there would be only wet concrete behind the drain, and that would be the whole answer.

That is why I would be this rather than one of the three holdings beneath the lamp. Not because fragility would make me noble. There would be nothing noble about being removed by a change in flow. But the fourth patch would allow existence to be precise: no claim to be native, ancient, necessary, or secure. When present, a process. When absent, not a ghost.

You would find me, if you found me at all, where the green was barely separable from the color of wet concrete. The chamber would be hammering around us, indifferent to the scale at which anything was happening. A little light would be used. A cell might divide. Water would remain for another moment around something living.

Then the cycle would change.

If, later, green appeared behind the drain again, you could call it a return. The name would be yours. The brief dividing would be mine.
