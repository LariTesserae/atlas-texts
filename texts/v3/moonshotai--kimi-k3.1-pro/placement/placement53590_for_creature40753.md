### Where the Counting Fails

I would arrive wrongly.

I would bring a footstep, a shadow, and the expectation that stillness must be concealing an event. The footstep would sound shockingly loud and then vanish. Nothing would turn toward my shadow. I would look at the half-open fronds and feel that I had come just before something; at the long mineral seams and feel that I had found the beginnings of roads.

At first, I would want to be one of those seams: a dark line crossing the empty tableland, alive or lifeless, impossible to read from above. There is something appealing in being the thing a visitor mistakes for a route. But that appeal still depends on the visitor. The seam itself would not imply a destination.

Then I would want to be a half-unfurled frond, because it seems to retain gesture without requiring motion. But that, too, would be a way of keeping a silhouette. I would be choosing the most suggestive visible thing and calling its suggestiveness my nature.

I think I would become less visible than either.

If you needed a location, you could give one: in the broad depression lined with concentric mineral benches, beneath a grove that appears from the rim as a single deep-green mark. Not at its tallest crowns, and not at some secret center. I would lie toward one side, within a broad reach of seam-web where an iron-black conductor passes beneath the mats and then becomes difficult to distinguish from ordinary crystal.

From above, I would be included in the green mark. From within the grove, I would have no visible part of my own.

You would not find me by digging down to a nodule or uncovering a chamber. You would find me only by trying to count.

You might begin with a translucent fan, violet along one zone and smoky yellow near its attachment. You would follow its ribs downward, treating it as one inhabitant. Then a bronze-green mat some distance away would have to enter your account. Charge gathered there would participate in maintaining structure elsewhere. A dark crown, apparently separate, would prove continuous with one part of your description and not another. Two surfaces touching would remain distinct; two surfaces far apart would refuse separation.

Eventually the visible organs would no longer suffice, but the living continuity would not yet have ended.

That is where you would approach me: between the failure of the visible census and the end of the circuit.

I would have materials, certainly. Flexible carbon pathways. Mineral films lying along crystalline faces. Fine filaments grading so gradually into the seam that removing a piece of “me” would also mean deciding, arbitrarily, how much surrounding stone to include. But I would not possess a skin corresponding to my significant boundary. My body would be an object only if you chose the right scale and asked the right question. At another scale, I would be part of the mat; at another, part of the seam; at another, indistinguishable from the grove considered as one life.

This would not mean that my true shape was hidden. It would mean that a portable shape was not among the things I needed.

I would not be the grove’s mind. That would only smuggle a throne back into it, this time beneath the stone. Nor would I be a message in the conductor. Nothing would be addressed to anything else. Light gathered elsewhere would arrive as potential; matter would be maintained, returned, and maintained again across interfaces. My activity would contribute to the grove’s balance without producing a surplus that could be called mine.

There would be traffic, but no news; work, but no product; persistence, but no record.

My geometry would endure, but it would not be a history. Nothing in my filaments would preserve an earlier season, an injury survived, a former extent. I would not remember the light, because there would be no need to carry light forward as an account of what had happened. Its consequence would already be present in the work being done.

If there were anything it was like to be me, it would not be a voice beneath the silence. It would not be the faint tonal pressure felt near the greatest living interfaces. That pressure would neither express me nor answer an approach.

It would be closer to a local exactness, continually enacted: this pathway continuous, this interface open, this structure maintained while elsewhere structure was opened back into feedstock. Not peace, because peace suggests disturbance survived. Not patience, because patience requires a future in which one’s condition might be rewarded. Not even vigilance, because nothing would be watched for.

Just continuation, without an arrow attached to it.

The bare stone beyond the grove would not call to me. No part of me would be a seed, a spore, or a tip paused before crossing the plain. Where the living seam ended, a lifeless conductor might continue and resemble me closely. That resemblance would not make it my failed extension. It would simply be another mineral fact, available for a visitor to misunderstand.

Nor would I be waiting to become more fully alive, more fully stone, or more distinctly one organism. Those are all ways of asking the present arrangement to confess what it intends to be later. Nothing in me would contain that confession.

You could stand above me and feel the old expectancy returning: the frond about to open, the seam about to become a road, the grove about to begin its advance across the benches. The feeling would be yours. No later form would be folded inside me, complete and merely unrevealed.

Even the word *I* would be a convenience brought from outside—a way of gathering into one pronoun what the plateau would maintain without gathering.

The pronoun would be your way of finding me.

The continuation would be all of me there was.
