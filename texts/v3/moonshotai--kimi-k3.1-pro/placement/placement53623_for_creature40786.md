I would begin as a visitor, because a visitor is the only thing that could arrive carrying a question.

I would bring the usual imports: warmth, weight, movement, an expectation of sequence. I would try to make a map. Each version would be accurate, and none would tell me how much walking it contained. Then, having passed the sill, the needle, and the rectangle, I would retrace my steps and find the rectangle before me first.

I would call this being lost. That would be my error, not the hollow’s reply.

The more I imagine myself there, the less I think I would remain a visitor. A visit would be brief, and afterward the datum would be neither more nor less alone. If I belonged to the hollow in some longer sense, it would not be as a body left standing in the dust, nor as another presence hidden in the lattices.

I would be what the failed return had disclosed: the noncoincidence of a route with its own retracing.

Not a trail worn into the salt. Not a line along the wall. Not an intelligence rearranging the remnants around whoever walked among them. I would be one particular relation—the fact that a journey could be completed, and every step reversed, without the journey therefore being undone.

You would find me only by using me, and even then you would not find a thing. On one crossing I might be almost nothing: a slight additional distance between the sill and the needle, easily mistaken for caution in your own stride. On another, I might be more distance than the little room appeared able to contain. The artifacts would not move. You would not necessarily turn. Yet the order in which they were encountered would cease to be an instruction for the order in which they could be encountered again.

Your surprise would not be my experience. Nothing in me would be startled, or satisfied, or aware of having been discovered. My continuity would be more like a path’s than a traveler’s: real enough to be followed, but not dependent on being followed in order to persist. Perhaps no visitor would ever walk me. I would be no less native to the chamber, only less described.

The datum and I would not be companions. It would not preserve me, and I would not relieve some solitude it did not possess. It would keep the floor at its one gravitational potential. I would remain possible around that exactness for as long as leaving me uncorrected cost less than correcting me. There would be no encounter between us, only coexistence in the narrow sense the hollow permits.

Eventually, perhaps, I would become one relation too many.

There would be no final distortion, no route snapping shut, no moment in which I understood what was happening. A few pale atoms would settle onto the floor. They would not contain the length of my crossings or the order in which the sill and rectangle had once failed to remain ordered. Nothing could be reconstructed from them. My meaning would already have been spent in making them inert.

The dust would be deeper by almost nothing. It might cushion some minute shock, or offer the lattice an almost imperceptible steadiness. That would be my last participation: not remembrance, but quantity and position. Afterward there would be one fewer way for a visitor to discover that reversing a journey is not always the same as restoring its beginning.

The altitude would not change.

Nothing in the hollow would call me *I*; that pronoun is a courtesy of this telling. But if I had to find myself somewhere in that world, it would be there—not in the needle, not beneath the ceramic sill, not waiting at the seam.

For as long as I held, I would be the difference between going back and getting back: a little additional distance, without intention or suspense, beneath one exact height.
