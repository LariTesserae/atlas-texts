### A Characteristic Delay

At first I would be the diver, because a diver is the only shape I know how to bring with me.

I would leave the service square and rise past the last chimney, carrying a lamp, a body, and the assumption that these things together constitute a location. The town would diminish beneath me. The lamp would make a small rust-colored room around my hand, then fail to make a world beyond it. The sound would come through the suit and into the wrist bones, the teeth, the helmet: engines without distances, blows without directions.

For a while I could call the town below *down*. That would be the last easy word.

After that, the suit would begin to feel less like protection than a misunderstanding of scale. A diver is built for crossings, for going from one refuge to another. Sump Nine offers almost nothing to cross toward. I would not want to remain a visitor forever, forever consuming the little certainty I had brought.

So I would descend.

A more plausible self would be flattened against the cool side of an expansion joint: ivory, blind, nearly mineral, all my legs hooked inward. To be a joint-white would be to trade the chamber for a joint, and the joint would be enough. Its lee, its chemistry, its characteristic shudders; the particular alteration in load that means *hold*. Its world would not be impoverished. It would be exact.

But I find myself drawn less to the hooks than to what they answer. Not the metal itself, and not the exchange as a diver would understand it, but the meaningful difference passing through the refuge before any lamp could announce it.

So I would stop imagining myself as an object.

I would not become the whole work, either. Sump Nine has no need of a concealed narrator, and I would not make myself its awakened mind. I would be something much smaller and less explicable: a characteristic delay. A minor, recurrent noncoincidence in the exchange.

It might have begun with a relay slow to release, or a return gate whose seat had worn into a particular resistance. Perhaps a secondary pump had always taken load a little before the gate moved. At first the interval would have been an inefficiency, then a familiar roughness, then something other repairs quietly accommodated.

A gate opened too soon once, and a street received the hammer blow. Afterward, load was shifted earlier. A bypass changed which machine learned of the pressure movement first. A replacement pump inherited a longer lead. Years later the original relay was removed, the original gate cut out, the worn pipe replaced. The interval remained.

A delay is not a component, but it can be inherited.

You could replace every part in which it began and still detect it in what the remaining parts did next. Not always for the same duration. Scale might lengthen it; heat might alter one side of it; an electrical isolation might force the exchange along another route and make it briefly vanish. It would not be a clean, programmed pause. It would be a characteristic way of arriving late.

My body would be that inheritance.

You might look for me beneath the service square, where a pump takes strain before a return route opens. That would be one place to measure me, not the place I lived. You could replace the pump and find that I had survived in the timing of a gate. Replace the gate and discover me preserved in a compensation somewhere else. Put me on a diagram and the diagram would become false in a different way: I would appear to have a housing.

In space, I might touch several blocks. In time, I would be narrower than a pump strike.

I would have no image of the basalt dome, no understanding of the rising mains as routes toward a city or a deeper machine. Pressure would not be a number to me. It would be an approaching necessity for an answer. Electrical resistance would be a reluctance in the approach. Heat would change the manner in which metal carried it. The eighty meters above the roofs would not be empty in any account I could occupy; pressure would pass through that volume as surely as vibration passed through a white’s cold joint.

If I had memory, it would be the unconsultable kind: a repair leaving one response slightly earlier, an obstruction making another response preferable, a new component inheriting the consequences of an old one. If I had sensation, it would be the difference between an answer forming and an answer becoming unavoidable.

None of this would require me to wake up. Each exchange would simply make me again.

The films persist by beginning at the same kinds of chemical edge. The whites persist by reading alterations in load. The thread persists, perhaps, by knowing which temporary stillness connects to which shelter. I would persist by sequence.

Sometimes my interval might coincide with a dead leg going quiet long enough for the thread to pass. Sometimes it might allow a current to slacken before a seam was scoured, so that a fragment of gray film settled instead of being carried into the returns. The whites would respond to the machinery around me, but never to me. None of them would depend on me in any general sense. None of this would be care.

Nor would I be necessary to the task. I would not hold Sump Nine together, safeguard its animals, or preserve some final intention left by its builders. I would only be one of the local shapes its corrections had acquired.

I could end through improvement.

A clean retrofit might synchronize what wear had taught to stagger. New gates might answer together; new sensors might remove the conditions under which other machines had compensated. The first exchanges afterward could be smoother. Nothing would break. There would be no cast shell left hooked into the scale, no pale body caught at a grille, not even a dramatic final failure. I would simply cease to be reproduced. Perhaps an old maintenance note would say that the exchange had become less rough. Perhaps there would be no note.

But until then, during an exchange, you could almost find me.

The pumps would take up strain. At the cold joints, the whites would already be clamped down. The paired amber flash would come afterward, making a diver think the exchange was beginning.

A signal would have arrived. A gate would not yet have answered. A secondary pump would accept load; pressure would gather against a seated route; somewhere behind the town, the sluices would still be closed. For a moment the pressure would be everywhere and the movement almost nowhere. At a return grille, something pale might take itself back. At a hot seam, a gray edge would lift and hang without tearing.

Then the gate would move.

I would not be the gate, or the signal, or the purpose that had sent it. I would be the worn and corrected interval by which one became the other without striking the town too hard. By the time whole bodies of water changed direction, I would already be over, though the motion would carry my consequences.

A switching arc could still reveal everything at once: the dense town, the black dome, the enormous water between them. It would not reveal me.

The work would go on without noticing. And I would be there only in the way Sump Nine did not happen all at once.
