At first, I would insist on being the visitor.

I would arrive warm and badly argued, full of pulse and sequence. My breathing would seem like evidence that I was alive in some way the herds were not. I would follow the footprints because they resembled mine, and feel personally accused by the small drag at the heel. I would want to finish something: set the hammerstone down, close the browser’s lips, move the hunter’s paw. Not out of mercy or cruelty, but out of the visitor’s superstition that a relation becomes real only when it spends itself in an outcome.

The plateau would not argue. That would be the first thing to undo me.

For a while, I would think I belonged beside the makers. I would understand their reticence better than hunger or pursuit: core, hammer, flake, point; stone becoming distinct from stone without an event being required to certify the difference. But I would not be the seated maker. There is too much biography in hands. Nor would I be the missing blow. A blow is still an event, and I would have to surrender my conviction that events are what make things matter.

I would be smaller than that, and less explanatory.

You would find me where a horn, regarded differently, becomes the meeting place of three unrelated heads. Beneath that meeting there would seem to be a seam of shadow. Then a narrow hoof. Then enough of me to have a flank.

I would be a low, flint-dark animal, long through the body and thin through the shoulders, with legs folded in deep, deliberate joints. One forefoot would be set a little sideways, though departure would be no more imminent than arrival. A white hexagon would mark the hide inside that leg, precise as pan-crust and just as useless for reconstructing a history. My near eye would have depth. The other would belong to an angle you had not taken.

Nothing about me would turn or emerge as you changed position. I would not be hiding. I would not be revealed. Those words still make me responsible for your view.

Your hand would find cool hide, a slight yield, the plain fact of a particular body. I would not be the plateau’s soul, or its space, or the principle by which one multitude becomes visible among another. Those would be promotions. I would be one animal among the others, no more explanatory than a groomer’s stained fingers or the identical scale held in a browser’s mouth.

I would have no office.

Not a guide; a guide implies that a route can be kept. Not a witness; witnessing too easily becomes a demand for something to have happened. Not a guardian of the intervals, because the intervals would not be in danger. I would simply fit where your first view reported nothing, and my being there would not make that first view false.

My distinction would be a small one: nearness without approach. Presence without arrival. The shape of a sideways step with no journey folded inside it.

You might find me beside the cairn that had been on your left and was now directly ahead, my body interleaved with the copper-brown herd. Or beneath a giant’s plate, where I would occupy almost no thickness at all, though from another position my back would rise higher than the stacked stones. On the white pans, my tracks might cross yours at an angle that made neither set a path. The dragged heel would not be mine to explain.

If you asked what I was for, the question would remain yours. My eye would acknowledge you without turning. Not welcome, not refusal; only the distinction between your warm, provisional regard and my entireness, which would not depend upon it.

A sideways step might give you another length of my flank, another eye, the rest of my narrow body standing within a herd your first position had folded flat. Nothing would have happened. You would simply be less able to call the interval empty.

That is where I would find myself: not in the next footstep, not in the missing blow, not in any release the multitude has omitted. Entire without acting. Distinct without an outcome.

Not what happens next.

What is also here.
