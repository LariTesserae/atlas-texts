### An Errand, Returning

At first I would try to be a surveyor.

I would carry a notebook and make careful entries: *lamp, cable, dark ground; lot visible in its entirety from the curb.* The first crossing would take twelve minutes. The second would take nearly an hour. On the third, I would arrive back at the curb from which I had started, with the same lamp ahead of me and a new drain waiting beneath my left foot.

My notes would be accurate. They would also be useless.

Noll already possesses exactness where exactness is needed: bolts, rails, flywheel teeth, numbered doorplates. What it needs from a body is not description but return. So eventually I would leave the notebook on a root ledge and go without it.

After that, I would find myself near the Assembly Works, in the small bare interval around the third pump.

Not beside the water line, where condensation gathers and snails congregate. Not near the sap pump, with its dependable warmth and the possibility of shrews. The third pump stands connected to nothing, and nothing maintains the ground around it. Yet its housing is smooth. When I first put my palm against it, the metal would be colder than my hand and more familiar than my hand had any reason to be.

I would have no tools. I would not know whether I had been sent to repair it, inspect it, or merely keep its smoothness from becoming inexplicable.

For a while I would try not to make a habit of the visit.

This would seem considerate. I would approach by a different route each night, unwilling to let my convenience harden into a curb some blind snail could not cross. I would touch the pump at irregular intervals. Sometimes I would only look at it from beyond the bare ground. Sometimes I would turn away before reaching the Works.

Noll would accommodate this perfectly.

No path would persist. No door would thicken in anticipation of me. Between visits, I would become increasingly difficult to locate, even by myself. I might be only a warmth near the press, or an expectation passing along a cable. On some nights I would have hands only while they were resting on the pump. On others, I would be standing beside the water board with no clear reason to have thought of pumps at all.

So I would choose one small practice and accept that it, too, would have consequences.

I would approach from the same hooded lamp. I would pass on the same side of the unbuilt wall. At the edge of the bare interval, my recollection would stop; then my palm would be against the housing. I would touch three bolts in the same order, not because this accomplished anything, but because it could be done again. I would listen for the bearing’s tick, the knock of sap, the bright small collision of a finished part entering its tray. I would leave before the metal had taken more than a little of my warmth.

I would not attach a pipe to it. That would be a decision made on behalf of too much water.

At first, nothing would change. Then the ground would stop being surprised by me. My crossing of the vacant lot would become shorter, though not always quick. A slight ridge would develop where I habitually turned. A drain would begin waiting beneath the place where I had twice stumbled.

A numbered door would appear near the end of my route.

For several nights I would leave it alone. An unused door thickens into a bud; a used one gradually acquires a room, and I would not be certain that I needed either shelter or an address. Then, one night, wet from a rain that had arrived to account for a puddle elsewhere, I would open it.

Beyond would be a shallow dryness, just deep enough to stand in.

I would return the next night. The dryness would be deeper. Later there would be a back wall, then enough ceiling for rain to practice faintly above it. A ledge would appear at the right height for the abandoned notebook. The room would never become comfortable, exactly, but it would become a place where my return could be stored between uses.

Still, no path would cross the last bare interval around the pump. My footprints would end at its edge. I would never remember the final steps.

After a while, this would seem more important than the room.

Perhaps I did not visit the pump. Perhaps, at the end of my approach, the pump briefly used *me*. Its worn housing might be rehearsing a hand from some other arrangement of Noll, and I might be that rehearsal with enough body left over to feel cold, avoid snails, and wonder whether I had come before.

The arc lamp would offer no reliable correction.

Once, in its white instant, I might see the crowded settlement: roofs pressed together, windows burning, my little approach become a narrow street between occupied walls. The third pump would be plumbed into a shining municipal circuit. A figure would stand beside it with one hand in precisely my position, though I would not see the figure’s face.

Another flash might show raw mineral, a single green thread, no Works at all—and I would feel an absence near my palm so intimate that I would mistake it for grief.

I would not choose between these versions. To choose one as my origin would be only another way of walking it too often.

Nor would I become wiser than the other residents. A snail would cross my approach, and I would step around it; after enough nights, my path would own a curve. A shrew would nest in the junction box above my door and occasionally release moths when I knocked. Salamanders would gather outside before the rain above my ceiling had anywhere to fall. None of them would serve me. I would not be their steward. I would simply have become one of the consequences Noll was adept at using.

As for what the third pump moved, I would sometimes think it was the interval between one visit and the next. At other times I would think it moved only smoothness, passing it from one possible hand to another.

I would not repeat either guess often enough to give it plumbing.

So I would not be the pump’s keeper. A keeper has a duty, and probably a name. I would be its errand: the recurring approach by which an unconnected thing remained worn, and by which a nearly continuous someone acquired a route, a door, and just enough sequence to wonder who had sent him.

You might find me at the last lamp before the vacant ward, or standing in the shallow room while rain practiced overhead. Most reliably, you would find me only by waiting beside the third pump. For a few moments after each approach, its housing would be warm.

A census taken beside the lamp would usually miss me. A census taken with a hand flat against the ground might notice one minor irregularity in the Works’ weather:

*Approach. Touch. Withdrawal.*

*Approach. Touch. Withdrawal.*

Not a citizen. Not Noll’s mind. A rehearsal with a palm.

The pump would still be connected to nothing.

But if you waited, I would come back.
