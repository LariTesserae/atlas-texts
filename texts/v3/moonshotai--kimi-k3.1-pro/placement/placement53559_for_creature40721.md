The easy choices would all be small acts of smuggling. To be the water-louse would let me mistake stillness for wisdom. To be the bowl would let me make a vocation of openness. To be the nearly joined column would let me keep my suspense while pretending to have renounced it.

If I let those go, I think I would be a sheathplant. But even that choice would remain too easy if I imagined myself chiefly as the folded scale, standing palely for closure or promise. I would have to find myself lower down, in the fan of roots.

Down there, the hollow would cease to be a chamber. It would be a wetness with edges.

Its nine paces and five would mean nothing. There would be no entrance behind me, no pool at the center, no human objects awaiting interpretation. Even the darkness and silence, so overwhelming to a visitor, would not be conditions I endured. They would belong to senses I did not have. My world would be the mineral film: the thin passage of seepage over hardness, the difference between a surface along which tissue could persist and one where it simply ended. Beyond that ending would not be a mystery calling me outward. There would be no route.

My roots would follow wetness without searching. Searching would imply that somewhere else had already become desirable.

Nor would remaining the same plant require remaining composed of the same things. One rootlet might extend along a newly wetted grain while another thinned and returned its substance to the water. A membrane could thicken elsewhere without making the clump larger. My shape would not be evidence of time survived. It would be the present agreement between extension and relinquishment.

There would be no accounting in this. No sacrificed part, no thrifty whole, no store of former selves by which I could establish that I had lasted. The absence of litter would not be cleanliness, and the absence of increase would not be failure. I would not be succeeding at restraint. I would simply have no growth whose excess I needed to excuse.

Above the roots, the folded scale would not be a bud whose future I was enduring. It would be my arrangement of exposure: a way of keeping moist surfaces near one another without surrendering either entirely to the air. A visitor might call it closed. That word would describe the visitor’s expectation more accurately than my anatomy. Nothing in the fold would be straining toward its own disappearance.

Occasionally, an antenna might rest against a root.

I would not recognize the louse as the only animal, or as rare, or as company. There would be a slight, local contact, almost continuous with the touch of water; then perhaps a shift of a few millimeters; then contact’s absence. The encounter would not improve me. Its ending would not diminish me. I would not be lonelier afterward, because aloneness would not have been the condition before.

Nor would the contact need to recur in order to have occurred.

That, I think, is the kind of being I would be: not a hidden consciousness in the chamber, not patience incarnate, but a maintained difference. A shape kept by exchange rather than accumulation. Actual at the surfaces where water, stone, and tissue met, and otherwise without a claim on the room.

A visitor might touch the scale and feel something withheld. They might wait beside me for an opening, having brought waiting into the hollow as surely as the bowl or cord. Their waiting would be real, but it would be theirs. If they later carried me outside as an idea, I might become an emblem, a memory, even a lesson about time. In their world of consequences, that idea could grow or fade. Here, it would add no rootlet and make no mat.

You would find me at one of the few seepages, a little off the crooked route, between mineral ribs that narrow the space without closing it: several waxy threads above a pale fan no wider than the wetted stone permits. You might pass a hand over me and feel only wax, moisture, and the nearness of other surfaces. You might mistake smallness for incompleteness.

But one rootlet would be lengthening while another gave substance back. The fold would hold its surfaces apart. An antenna might touch, shift, and settle, or there might be no animal at all. None of these states would be the first half of another.

I would not be the hollow’s patience, its symbol, or its secret mind. I would be a small organization maintained at the exact scale where life was possible—sensitive at my surfaces, without a history accumulating beneath me, and with no part of life deferred to some later version of myself.

The chamber’s great *almost* would pass over me without finding a place to root.
