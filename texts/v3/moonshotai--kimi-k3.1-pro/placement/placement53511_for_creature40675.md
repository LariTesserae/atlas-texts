I would not be on the highest ledge. There would be nothing to understand from being highest; the next ridge would only take away more sky.

I would be down in one of the long intervals of nearly nothing, where the plain is warm and flat enough that a stone, once set down, seems to have committed an act.

At first, I would call myself a traveler.

I would bring my elsewhere sealed around me: breath, moisture, chemistry, the narrow continuing processes by which I would remain more than warmth and mineral. None of its moisture or breath would touch the ground. The province would offer me heat I could not live by and stone I could not live from. My supplies would not make me self-sufficient. They would only make me temporarily elsewhere.

The word *traveler* would last until I tried to go back.

A pass might have become a fin of stone. A cliff used for orientation might have doubled without changing its outline enough to confess. I could draw a map, but it would be an honest instant pretending, if I let it, to be a route home. Even memory would be something I had imported, like air. It could accompany me. It could not take root.

Nor would *witness* quite suffice. I might see a warm line open across the plain, or several stars vanish in a row, but the event would not become history merely because I had been present for it. The ground would not remember with me.

So the self would narrow—not into despair, but into scale.

What could I be there without pretending to be the beginning of something?

A pause capable of selection.

On that plain, I would choose three stones.

Two I would set close together, with a narrow, clean gap between them: not touching, not supporting one another, making no shelter. The third I would place farther away. It would not point toward the pair. It would not complete a figure or indicate an approach. It would simply be apart.

That would be the whole work: *near*, and *apart*.

The stones would be warm. Setting down the last one might produce a single small contact, scarcely audible even to me. Silence would return before I had decided whether the arrangement was finished.

I would leave no explanation. Anyone arriving later could call the configuration chance, and they would have no certain way to refute themselves. That would not diminish the act. A selection does not need to become evidence in order to have been a selection.

While I was placing the stones, I would be there in the ordinary sense: a little imported continuation standing on the dark, busy with something the province could not nourish. Afterward, if I needed a name the Height could almost accommodate, it would be *visit*.

Not visitor. *Visitor* suggests a shape that might eventually be inferred from the spacing of the stones, a height assigned to the gap, a purpose reconstructed from the distance of the third. *Visit* is smaller and more accurate: an occurrence with a little choice in it. Population one for a while, then zero again, with no catastrophe required.

The arrangement would not be me. It would only have survived me, perhaps, by luck.

The plain might rise gently beneath one stone and not the others, so that *apart* would slowly become *above*. A fracture might carry the pair apart while leaving the third untouched. My grammar would be revised without a sound. I would not object. It was never an inscription.

Where would you find me?

Only in the unrepeatable middle: after the first stone had been moved, and before I had decided that the third was enough. Before that, you would find an elsewhere preparing its small expedition. Afterward, you would find three stones, or three stones on different levels of new ground, or nothing distinguishable from debris. You could not know whether the arrangement marked my pause, preceded it, or belonged to another visit entirely.

That uncertainty would not conceal a larger story. It would be the correct size of the trace.

I would be the brief difference between a stone’s having been placed and its merely having ended up there. Then I would stop intervening.

The Height would continue without having been inhabited, and I would have occurred without becoming the beginning of anything.
