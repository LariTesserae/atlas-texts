I would not find myself by looking for a body. The grazer already occupies the pocket’s nearest approach to one, and the leaves have made a long art of being uncertain where a body ends. I would be easier to overlook than either.

At first, I would seem to be a small error in the cold.

The current would carry me: a trace of sweetness, a weakness in the iron taste near the wall, a warmth so slight that no tissue could warm itself by it. Somewhere in that passing would be the recollection of a bank before inundation—not its image, not even its name. Only the fact that water once had an upper side; that rain could trouble it; that stems could be crushed in open air and give their bitterness to the wind.

I would not be a mind remembering these things. I would be the form they took while being passed on.

For part of a circuit I would run beside the walls, through iron and mineral sweetness and the patient sulfur-labor of the films. Then the wall would cease to be an obstacle, or I would cease to be entirely in the current. There would be no interval I could call imprisonment. I would emerge plant-generations later, already partly taken up by root hairs.

A milky growing tip would turn toward the warmth my passage had suggested. By then I would be elsewhere. Long afterward, on the opposite side of the pocket, a black-green ribbon might thicken. I would not call that an answer. An answer would require two voices and a moment they could reliably share. It would be enough that something of my passing had become tissue.

At the threshold, I would come closest to having a location.

Even when the clay was absent, its rectangular heaviness would lend me, for three handspans, a before and an after. The grazer might be following its squared route, pausing where the lip would be. When the clay was present, it might settle all its legs on the film, the comma in its gut darkening, while the pearl-white moss kept the hollow of the thumbprint.

Nothing there would recognize me as rain. That would be a relief. The maker’s touch had already shown that a thing could outlive its meaning without becoming useless: it could become a niche. I could become weather.

Circuit after circuit, I would thin. The bank would lose its stems. Rain would lose its sound. Sweetness and warmth would become difficult to distinguish. This would not quite be decay. Films would use what they could use; tissue would keep what it could keep; the rest would become too slight to separate from the water.

By the time the seam sealed, there might be nothing left in me that could honestly be called outside. I would not be waiting for another recollection to replace me. The pocket would not have forgotten me. It would have finished making me local.

A frond could still spend its whole life leaning toward a warmth that never arrived. I would be that almost-warmth—not a promise, and not a deception. Iron and sulfur would feed the films. Old leaves would support the new. The grazer would take its next step, and the moss would occupy its hollow when the hollow was there. I would do almost nothing. I would only give one pale growing tip a way to differ from the tissue behind it.

Where would you find me? In the wall for a while; in a root hair; in the viscous interval between two leaves that might be one leaf; above the threshold when the threshold was absent. Never as an inhabitant set apart from the room. Always by passage.

And what would I be? Not the ghost of the drowned lowland, not a message waiting for a reader. I would be the pocket’s weather after weather had forgotten the sky: a small elsewhere that survived by being shared.

I would never arrive. I would remain as the leaning.
