At first I would want to be the ridge. It is the only thing in the chamber that resembles somewhere. It has clefts, buttresses, ledges; it catches the lamp; it seems to stand above the sumps. But to be the ridge would be to exist as an agreement between nozzles and cutting teeth, a shape maintained by being continually prevented from continuing. I would not even know that I was almost a mountain.

Then I would want to be the moss: one of the eleven filaments, doing the whole work of a forest in amber leakage. But the ridge and the moss are easiest to choose from outside. Through the inspection glass, they make legible emblems—terrain, vegetation, possibility. The animals do not need emblems.

I think I would be a glassback.

Not glassback-kind, not the living fur on the pipes, not the multitude whose shells make the tearing note. One animal. Unremarkable. Not one that escapes, or understands the engine, or leaves some sign the regulators have overlooked. Wanting to be that animal would only be another way of asking the chamber for a story.

My life would begin, as every glassback’s life begins, in the sump.

The brine holds me without holding me still. I am a soft pale dash among other soft pale dashes, though that is not how I know them. “Suspended rice” is an appearance for an eye. For me there are gradients: food and less food, a tremor passing through the water, a pull that strengthens and weakens. Other larvae are small disturbances nearby, and then not nearby. The intake takes some of them. The water changes, then changes back. Nothing in the sump is waiting for me to understand it.

After a number of changes that nothing counts, I climb.

The adult world does not open into space. It narrows to contact. A wet rib beneath my hooked legs. Brine gathering in the fringe of my gills. A surface that must be negotiated again with every movement: hold, slip, hold. My shells harden around me. The dark thread of my gut begins to show through them.

Above me there may be a ceiling crowded with mesh and insulation; below me, a cast basin; around me, several walls concealed behind the foremost wall. I know none of that. There is the pipe. There is the film on the pipe. There is vibration arriving through alloy, and the minute warmth of bodies ahead.

When a current pulse enters the busbars, my shells snap open. Their closure comes a fraction later.

I do not hear myself join the chamber’s unbroken note. The note belongs to the engine and to anything listening through the inspection glass. Under my legs there is only the pulse. The bodies packed nearby loosen and tighten. Something touches my shell. Something else withdraws. Then the pipe is the pipe again.

On one reach of it, I find a cluster.

No glassback has kept a place for me. None recognises me when I arrive. I crawl over one body and under another, drawn toward warmth so slight that it is only a fractionally less complete cold. A living surface settles across my back. My own body covers the coldest side of another animal.

I would not call this affection. I would not need to call it nothing.

For a while, condensation gathers above us. It cannot fall directly. It travels along insulation, finds the edge of a shell, crosses a leg joint, and comes onto my back. At the groove beneath my gills it pauses, swelling, while the brine film moves through me and the cluster shifts beneath my weight.

For a few seconds, I am a watershed.

Then the drop leaves. It crosses another animal, or falls, or enters some further route along the ceiling. No channel deepens. No wet patch spreads. Yet that drop took one path rather than another because my body was briefly where it was.

Later, perhaps, I climb the mineral ridge. To me it is not the first sketch of terrain. It is a succession of edges: a cleft too narrow for my whole body, a raw ledge that bears six small weights, a knotted base where the vibration of the cutting teeth comes harder through the formation. Fresh mineral glistens, but not for me. I find purchase on surfaces whose present shape has only just arrived. Nothing becomes a favoured path beneath me. Still, this crossing happens in this order—cleft, ledge, knot, the warmer pipe beyond—and no conservation of totals can make that order not have occurred.

I graze as I go. The film closes behind me. What enters my gut may include matter that once moved through another glassback on the same pipe; there is no recognition in that return, and no debt. I shed my surfaces. I eat, or am eaten. Somewhere behind the lamp guard, eleven filaments remain eleven.

Nothing I do makes the ridge higher. Nothing empties a surface or founds a territory. If the intake takes me, there will be no final understanding: a change in the water, a stronger change, absence. What can be used will be used. The number of animals will remain correct.

From the inspection glass, I would disappear into the general motion. That would be true.

It would not be the whole truth.

A drop would have crossed my back. A ledge would have borne my weight. In the cluster, one living surface would have covered another’s coldest side. These things would not need to accumulate, or be remembered, or alter what came after them, in order to have been events in a life.

The pulse comes again. My shells open, then snap shut. Somewhere beyond me, the tearing note receives one click too small to distinguish.

I feel the pipe beneath my hooks, the brine moving at my gills, the fractional warmth ahead.

I go toward it.
