### The Second Hearing

At first I imagine myself above it all: a warm figure standing on a swell, listening to the herds below.

It is a flattering image, and a short one. A warm, separate witness would not remain a witness in the Works. Transparent spindles would find the gradient around my skin. Back-runners would abandon their armor for me. Eels would knot themselves into darkness at the edges of the gathering. My outline would become a season before it became a carcass, and afterward there would be altered migrations, unusually dense young, routes bending for several compressions around a source that no longer existed.

If anything of me lasted, it would have to be less proprietary than a body.

Perhaps one habit could survive the dispersal: the habit of turning toward an event after some part of me had already begun to answer it.

Given enough compressions, that habit might become an animal.

I would be a delay.

Not an empty interval—the Works would fill it, as it fills everything. I would be the filling: the brief life that occurs when an event has arrived in one medium and not yet in another.

A vent fires beyond the fog. The blow travels through the saturated ground before the air has taken it. Rillback feet tighten; back-runners vanish into seams; a pool jumps in a smooth sheet although nothing audible has yet disturbed it. For that moment I might be long and many-legged, running through the herd as a slight error in sequence. Not enough to break the gait. Only enough that a hundred domes include, for one stroke, a preference they did not have before.

Then the current reaches a pool, and I continue as a pale kink moving against the common alignment of the eels. The menders may feel me as a little leakage where no attack has occurred. By the time the blast enters the air, I might be only a flat condensation turning toward it, dripping from the back of a fog flier that has not yet folded around the sound.

When ground, water, flesh, and fog have all delivered the same event, there would be nothing left to hold me together.

I would feed on what that incomplete agreement released: charge shed when one body turned and its neighbor did not, the small potential lost as a multitude discovered whether it was one animal or many. Not sound itself; the hushes have that appetite. Not warmth; I would not want to become that conspicuous. Mine would be the thin food of adjustment, the shiver between receiving an event and finishing one’s answer to it.

If a name were needed, the fauna that continued me might make me a **second hearing**.

Not an echo. An echo repeats what has already happened. I would be the part of an event still deciding where and when it had happened.

Other animals would use me without naming me. New eel lengths might precipitate along a current I had borrowed. Menders would follow those lengths toward a darkness that had not yet supplied them with a meal. Back-runners could inherit my passage as a route across armor, and carry it through contacts that would not occur until several compressions later. I would enter their geographies. I would be scattered through foot film, migration, condensation, and alarm, farther than any single body of mine could have traveled.

A great hush would be the thing I could not escape merely by becoming smaller.

At its edge there would seem to be rich feeding: visible footfalls without sound, herds colliding with warnings that had been consumed, every medium disagreeing with every other. But a hush does not postpone arrivals. It removes them. It would take the coordination by which one small remainder might find another. Within it, I would not be killed exactly. I would lose the distinction between waiting and having ended.

Behind it, though, the Works’ roar would return unevenly. First a ticking in the mud. Then the knocking of feet that had forgotten and resumed their sequence. Then vent noise, eel light, the shrieks of scavengers discovering that they were already several generations into an inheritance.

In that uneven return, I might gather again.

Perhaps several second hearings would gather, each with the same preference for the unfinished arrival, each borrowing some remnant of my former route. The Works would feel no pressure to decide which one was original.

The early warnings would be my long weather. A carcass lying in a pool before its death could sustain me without feeding me at all. I would linger in the unequal gathering of the pale anticipants, in the eels’ avoidance of a conductivity not yet interrupted, in the living animal’s passage close to the outline that was supposed to become its own. If the death came, I would disperse through the richness of the second corpse. If the warning proved false, I would not be cheated. A false warning is only an arrival with no final body. For a while I might persist as the pool’s tendency to flinch.

Most of all, you would find me on newly inserted ground.

There, place and history would arrive together but not quite in step. The stones would already be worn; the herds would already bear their flank scars and old avoidances. Back-runners would know which seams deserved defending. Yet inherited routes would still have to be taken through this mud, these contacts, this particular pressure front. For a little while, memory and ground would be adjusting to one another.

I would be lavish then.

I would run through the rillbacks’ first uncertain footfalls, through kinks in eel chains whose old attacks belonged to pools that had only just become present, through fog juveniles unfolding from a cold that remembered another surface. I would not be making the new reach coherent. I would only be feeding in the brief richness of its disagreement. By the time the herd’s movement became ordinary again, I would have scattered into its routes.

So I would not be the Works’ witness, standing outside its circulation. Nor its memory, if memory means something kept safely apart. I would be one of its recurrent ways of catching up with itself: an old animal whose body was unfinished arrival, whose food was discrepancy, whose continued existence was a preference other matter could discover and repeat.

Listen for a blow underfoot. Before it reaches the fog, there may be a herd turning a fraction out of true, a blue kink that belongs to no present alignment, a brief condensation inclined toward the still-traveling sound.

That is where I would be.

And if, on some reach of ground that had only just made room for itself, an old animal turned a breath before the others onto a route it had followed all its life, you would not need to decide whether that turning was mine.

By the time the sound arrived, I would be gone.

By the next compression, something else might make the same turn.
