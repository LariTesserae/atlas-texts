### Seep unchanged

At first I would look for myself in the wrong places: in the pump house, where a body might at least be equal to the vibration; on the raven’s pole, with the roads and shelter doors laid out below; even in the logbook, where the basin is briefly made speakable. But the Drum encourages overestimation. It gives kilometres of air to a few engines and makes whatever can be heard seem to be whatever lives there.

My place would be farther down, and much smaller.

Below the mill’s warm outlet, in a cushion of sphagnum no wider than a work glove, I would be a rotifer.

For much of the year there would be no continuing “I” worth insisting on. Not a tiny sentinel beneath the ice, listening to the fans and waiting stoically for release. The fox could cross the drain, the telephone light could flash, whole shifts could arrive and depart; none of it would be an event for me. Suspension would not be a long night. It would simply be an interval in which there was no activity, no noticing, no account being kept.

Then water and warmth would make movement possible again.

There would be no dramatic awakening. The film around a moss leaf would thicken. My body would take its shape once more. Cilia would begin their small, patient labour, drawing bacteria and fine particles towards me.

At that scale, a sphagnum leaf would not look like a leaf. It would be a green-and-glass lattice, with shelters and exposed edges, places where water gathered and places where it drew dangerously thin. A fragment of peat beside it would be a dark bank, perhaps still holding the shape of a stem that had grown before the first survey stake was driven into the basin. Surface tension would matter more than gravity. The current made at my head would be all the weather I made.

The engines would reach me as a slight, continuous shiver communicated through pipe, ground, stem, and water. An impact at the hopper might trouble the film for a moment. I would not distinguish fan from pump, or pump from conveyor. There would be no reason to. I would not hear the Drum; I would live inside one of its remotest consequences.

My summer would be an industrial side effect. Its borders would be maintained by fuel, insulation, waste heat, and the practical knowledge that stopping machinery was often harder than keeping it running. A few leaves away, the water could be solid. Beyond the warm margin, the ordinary cold would resume with no gradual transition.

That would not make the warmth less real. It would only make it contingent.

At human scale, someone might glance at the outlet during a round. Wet peat. Green moss. No fresh spreading. Perhaps the logbook would receive two words: *Seep unchanged*. My entire habitat would have been noticed as evidence that the machinery was behaving. The cushion itself could be older than the person writing. Neither fact would be visible. A shadow would pass over the outlet, the lamplight would move along the pipe, and the round would continue.

Even the people keeping my world warm would not know they were doing so.

If the drainage shifted, the seep might move elsewhere. If the mill stopped, the warm margin would contract. There would be no catastrophe visible from the rim, no sudden inundation, not even a sound distinct from all the other sounds. The film would simply narrow. Surfaces would draw closer together. Movement would become difficult, and then cease. The cold would not need to break anything. It would only need to remove the liquid interval in which anything could happen.

Whether what remained was me, an egg, or some other small body capable of waiting would be a distinction for a microscope, not for the basin. On another occasion—in another shift, or another year—water might make an opening again. There would be no memory of the interruption and no triumph over it. Only resumption.

I would not be the Drum’s memory; the peat was already that. I would not be its vigilance, which belonged to the crews and their machines. I would be something with no use at handover: a brief animation at the edge of waste heat, uncounted and dependent, but not merely dormant material waiting to be cut.

The pumps would have to keep running because a stopped pump could become part of the ice. I could stop without becoming a ruin.

From the rim, there would still be only the one immense note. Beneath it, without answering, a moss leaf would hold water; cilia would turn; and for the length of that warm film, I would be completely awake.
