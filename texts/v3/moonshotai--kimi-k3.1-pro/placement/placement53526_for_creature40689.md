I don’t think I would have a station. The gantry’s repeated journey is admirable, and the fin-brusher’s narrow work has consequences far beyond its place in the ward, but both are too durably bolted down. Nor would I be the exchange column; centrality is not the same thing as self. The shallow pool comes closer—a small body holding an enormous, inverted arrangement of cables and lights—but even the pool has a basin.

I would be one of the ward’s local rains.

Not the pressure release itself, and not all the water in the circuit. Water would outlast me. I would be the brief event between vapour and recovery: the thickening beneath the ceiling, the drops joining on cold metal, and the half-minute in which one alley became wet while the next remained dusty.

Before falling, I would be visible only where the amber lamps shone through me. I would pass around tanks and divide around cable trays, with no edge except warmth, density, and the slow approach of colder metal. At the condenser ribs I would cease to be merely a change in the air. A film would cloud the fins. Separate beads would grow heavy. My first drops would not be fragments breaking off a completed self; they would be how that self acquired weight.

Then I would meet the ward piece by piece: a tick on ceramic, a darker streak down a tank, a drop held without bending by copper-coloured grass. A press closing below would arrive through a wet bridge as vibration. Where I struck hot basalt, there would be one mineral hiss and no remainder. Where I reached the shallow pool, the reflected city would break into rings, then gather itself again around the same red lights.

Nothing would come to drink. I would not want to turn that into loneliness. It would simply be the terms of the place. Most of me would be caught and pumped back; some of me would vanish almost at once; a little would remain for a while as weight in moss or softness along a lichen seam. I would not be a blessing bestowed on the ward. I would be an imbalance passing through a few living margins on its way toward correction.

And when I stopped, there would be no clean final drop to point to. The rain would narrow to dripping. The dripping would multiply briefly beneath walkways and along pipe flanges, so that my most audible phase might come after I had ceased to fall. Then the pumps, the wet films, the darkened stone, and the pool would each keep some consequence without keeping me. The next release would not remember this one.

To find me, you would have to look for a phase rather than an address: dust in one street, warm rain ticking in the next; a row of tanks beginning to shine; moss darkening beneath a lamp while, across the chamber, another cushion remained dry. Arrive too early and there would be only haze. Arrive too late and there would be evidence easily mistaken for a leak.

Afterward, a relay would give its precise clack. A gantry would set another honey-coloured block against raw basalt. The ward would not mark my ending. For half a minute, though, I would not be something it contained.

I would be something it did.
