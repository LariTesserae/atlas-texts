At first I would be a walker, because that would be my first misunderstanding.

I would cross the pale pavement with my shadow keeping its length beside me, going from one dark outcrop to another as though each might be an answer. I would put my hands against the fins, feel the hum before I heard it clearly, and listen beneath the knocking for some variation I could interpret as intention. I would look for a centre, a terminal process, a place where the basin’s operations became legible as work.

A visitor could do that. A visitor brings before and after with them. But my crossing would be a history only in me; the Works would not receive it as one. To remain, I would have to stop requiring my location to be a stage in a journey.

I would not want to be the hidden intelligence in the drums. That would solve the place by betraying it. Nor, I think, would I be a vein thread, though there is something compelling in that narrower existence: waking chemically only when the crystal flexed, knowing the world as stress passing through a mineral sheath, with no dry pavement, no horizon, no outside. It would be a almost complete surrender to the rhythm. I find that I want the small separateness of something that can be lifted away while the humming continues unchanged.

So I would go to the edge of a brine lens.

Not one of the great groups of outcrops. A room-sized lens would do, with a solitary bank of fins a few hundred metres away and a buried diaphragm knocking at long intervals. Warm dust beyond the wet margin; wet salt close at hand; perhaps, farther off, a narrow veil of rain standing in another hollow and never arriving. At the lens’s rim, where damp stone joined mineral zones of slightly different potential, I would settle.

I would be a seepback—or I would have to discover what kind of self could fit within one.

Not a human mind miniaturised and imprisoned beneath a shell. Much of what I now mean by myself would have to go. There would be no store of days deepening behind me, no skills carried toward some later use, no private approach to an ending. My ash-grey back would take the patina of the stone. My legs would fold until they resembled fractures. Beneath them, nearly all of me that was soft would be spread against the seep: dark, moist tissue taking its chemistry from a current that had no need of me.

My world would become close and exact. The fins would not primarily be shapes; they would be pressure arriving through bedrock. Moisture would not be abundance but a narrow permission. Each leg would know the difference between a hold and an almost-hold. Across my underside would run the faint distinction between one mineral zone and another, enough to feed on, not enough to enlarge anything. Light would pass over my shell without becoming day.

There would be no loneliness, unless loneliness can exist without the idea of company. Another seepback several square kilometres away would not be an absence. Nor would I be guarding the lens, or waiting for one of my own kind, or hoping for rain. The rain would not be an occasion. The next knock would not be a summons. Rest and movement would simply be phases of contact.

Would anything remain that could be called *I*?

Perhaps not a biography. Perhaps something smaller: the capacity to be wrong.

If I were lifted and set down again, the diaphragm would continue without alteration. For a few beats I might loosen my grip after the vibration rather than with it. My shell would rise late; my legs would find the stone at awkward moments. Then the interval would narrow. Not learning, exactly. Nothing retained as wisdom. Just living tissue closing around a local rhythm again.

To be moved would be, briefly, to have a before and an after without possessing either as memory. The Works would not notice. The brine would remain effectively unchanged. Yet that late lifting interests me more than all the perfect resonances. It would not be a mineral face restoring its prescribed form. It would be an adjustment made from the other side of a boundary.

That boundary would be me.

You might walk past without seeing anything. At most, there would be a scale of crust at the water’s edge, slightly lifted at the rim. You would have to wait through the smaller ticks until the long knock came up through the ground. Then my shell would rise by less than a millimetre, a wave of release would pass around my legs, and for an instant the whole animal would be outlined in clear air.

You could remove me, and the basin would remain just as loud. I would not be maintaining a contact, regulating a current, or giving the Works a witness. I would not be their secret purpose. I would be a separate softness at their edge, taking almost nothing and returning almost everything.

Then I would clamp down again.

For a moment there would be clear air beneath my shell, then contact. Nothing completed, nothing carried forward. Only the small, repeated fact that the vibration had passed through something capable of answering it—and that the answer, however slight, was not quite the vibration itself.

That difference would be me.
