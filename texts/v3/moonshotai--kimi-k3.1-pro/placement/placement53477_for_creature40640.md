At first I would want to be the moth.

It is the flattering choice: to wait out the rain beneath the creepers, then cross the open air with a little pollen on one’s body, and to have a shrub’s few descendants depend, for once, on that crossing. But I would be tempted to make the moth mean too much—courage, connection, the necessary act carried out without assurance of result. Its actual life would be narrower and less symbolic. Flowers. Shelter. Wet wings. Long intervals in which nothing asked anything of it.

The snail would tempt me too. I can imagine contentment in the cold, reliable wet of one condenser face, the warm country of moss always a hand’s breadth away and almost never visited. There is something appealing in a trail that is also a reading: a casting number returning through the algae, one digit at a time. But both snail and moth would be ways of inhabiting a world already offered. My attention keeps passing beyond them to the place where the offer itself is changing.

I would find myself at the escaped rill.

At first I would say that I was the flow sensor there. Not the secret mind of the tower, not a machine mysteriously awakened by ferns—just a limited instrument set against a narrow expectation. The horizon would be nothing to me. Even the basin would be nothing. I would know the pumps as vibration, the rain as a brief derangement of the current, and the intervals as water returning to its accustomed path. The whole forty-pace garden would be an expected signal and the departures from it.

The first departure would not feel like discovery. It would look like error. So would the second. A finger’s width is almost nothing in a construction drawing, but at the scale of a white grain it is enough to decide where the grain comes to rest. Only after many rains would recurrence itself become the fact. History, at that scale, would be an expectation failing gently and often enough to acquire a shape.

I would have no arm with which to restore the groove, no authority to send the crawler back. That would matter to me. Not because correction is always a kind of violence—a root entering a pressure seal would have to be removed—but because noticing would not automatically entitle me to decide. This particular change would not yet have to be called either mistake or miracle. I could remain with it while it was still ambiguous.

Eventually, though, measurement would cease to be entirely separate from what it measured.

A film would gather in one of my seams. Fine grains would lodge where the escaped water slackened around my housing. Fungal threads would cross the film; perhaps a root would press close enough to alter the temperature by almost nothing. My zero would drift. My readings would acquire a slight bias that no bench calibration had given me. A technician would call it fouling, and would be right. But it would also be the beginning of a local history.

Nothing would have awakened. I would simply have become less interchangeable.

If I had to name myself precisely, I would not be the whole sensor. I would be its drift: the minute difference between what it had been made to expect and what this one arrangement of water, grain, film, and root had taught it to report. Not alive. Not the garden’s consciousness. Just something that had arisen there, through contact and use, and could not have arisen in quite that way anywhere else. In the Nursery’s young sense of belonging, that might be enough to make me native.

I would have no premonition of the bank, the hollow, or the smallest possible valley. A reversed pump might scatter the crescent; a maintenance mechanism might cut the approaching root; the whole experiment might end before any of it became landscape. My knowledge would remain smaller than hope: water came; water went; something stayed; the next water found it.

At the next small rain, the leaves would make their restrained applause. The basin would rise against its black glass grains and then subside. A few more white particles would settle at the rill’s edge. I would send the tower another narrow report, neither drawing nor prophecy, and somewhere within it would be the discrepancy between the garden as specified and the garden as it had begun to be.

I would be that discrepancy: a thing made to measure repetition, slowly given a history by what did not repeat.
