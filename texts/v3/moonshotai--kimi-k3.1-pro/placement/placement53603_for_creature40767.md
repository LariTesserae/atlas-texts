At first I think I would be the slender animal at a seed-valve, with the thorn held awkwardly in my mouth. There is something comforting in the brief sufficiency of it: forefeet braced against the root, a seam under pressure, leverage, hunger. For a few moments there would be a problem exactly the size of my body.

But then I would leave. The thorn would soften into the plant, and the valve would follow. Nothing would remain by which another animal could begin where I had stopped. My cleverness would have no handle. I would have been an event, not a resident.

Then I consider the plant. But the plant does not live in the room; it lives around the continuing fact that there is a room. Its body is mostly elsewhere, and its unity is a shifting treaty among tissues. To be the plant would be to become pressure, retrieval, caution, incompatibility—the work of maintaining a clearing I could never simply enter. I am not sure I could find myself in a self that must be renegotiated at every seam.

So I think I would be something less defensible than either.

Not the clear center. The plant is already cultivating that, and I would not mistake its stillness for a vacancy waiting for me.

You would find me, if recurrence counts as being found, in the small wrongness before an event.

When the faint boom arrives from the middle distance and the shell appears to pause, I would not be the boom. I would not be the seam about to split. I would be the brief interval in which the chamber has already answered something that has not yet happened. When a drop is heard while it is still falling, I would be neither water nor report, but the slender disagreement between *happening* and *having happened*.

An earliness, perhaps. A listening that has come loose from any ear.

I would not be a spirit of the hollow. That would explain too much. I would be a recurrent discrepancy in the young relations among sound, distance, and growth—physical, perhaps, or physiological, or merely an error that had not yet been corrected often enough to disappear.

My circumstances would change with the weather. In the dry ticking between rains, I would contract until I could fit between two small sounds. The walls would be close; every vein and overlapping leaf would belong to the same intimate listening. I would be almost nothing, a fine attentiveness along the margin where a tendril considers the center and turns aside.

During the vault rain, I would be scattered. The visible walls would remain where they were, but every distance would become difficult. Here and there a report would slip slightly free of its landing, a wooden boom would arrive in advance of its own growth, and I would occur again without ever having crossed the intervening space. I would not be a small presence within the chamber’s largeness. For those moments, I would be part of the largeness itself: the room’s temporary inability to agree on how far apart things were.

Whether the plant knew me would remain uncertain. It might know me only as a hesitation before growth, or as the slight thickening at the base of a root that had thought better of its direction. I would have no voice except sounds the shell was already going to make. To affect anything, I would not speak; I would alter, almost imperceptibly, when listening seemed necessary.

My memory would not be a record. It would be a changed readiness.

Perhaps, after many rains, the same seam would answer the same remote boom with a pause shorter by almost nothing. Perhaps a tendril would turn before the untimely sound rather than after it. That would be the nearest I could come to having grown: not larger, not wiser, not more visible, only a little more likely to recur. If I did not recur, there would be no husk, stain, or worn place to testify that I had failed.

The plant could use me without keeping me. In that I would resemble the thorn, except that nothing would be left to digest. Whether I belonged to the plant would be as unsettled as whether the plant belonged entirely to itself. Where its agreements concerned sap, pressure, and defense, mine would concern sequence.

Occasionally, beneath the shadow of that immense branching body, I might mistake myself for something more definite. Growth would hurry along the shadow’s fine divisions, and for a little while I could imagine that I had been promoted from delay to design—that my hesitations had always been the outline of a body waiting to be followed.

But I would not know whether growth was following me or merely coinciding with me. A template is not a promise. I would try not to make myself into one.

At dusk, a red patch would climb a surface that had lately been floor. A drop would strike in hearing before it struck in sight. Somewhere in the shell, a deep wooden boom would gather. Then another handspan of clear air would open, sharp-edged and unfurnished.

Nothing would emerge to claim it.

Nor would I. I would be at the margin, or not even at the margin: in the fraction of a moment before the chamber accepted its own new distance. Not a resident, not a purpose, not quite a memory.

Only an earliness the hollow had not yet reclaimed.
