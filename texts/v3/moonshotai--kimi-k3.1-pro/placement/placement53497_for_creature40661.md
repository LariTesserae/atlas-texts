### What the Stone Carries

I would not be the mountain. Nor, I think, the chamber. Both are too easily mistaken for subjects. The mountain has no summit in any animal’s experience, and the chamber has no open center from which to regard it. Even standing there is an outsider’s fantasy. My unprotected body would fail almost at once; the water in me would be more alien than fire.

My first images would betray me in other ways, too.

I might choose the electrical flash: to be that instant in which every blade acquires an edge and every crevice becomes stark. But the flash is a revelation only to a seeing visitor. Nothing native needs it. It illuminates the least important facts and leaves the chemical worlds within each grain untouched.

I might choose the roar, or the pressure-note beneath it. But they belong too completely to the whole. They have no feeding margin, no sheltered side, no dangerous nearness. They alter, but they do not have to find their next meal.

To live here, I would have to be smaller and more partial.

So: find me in a dust-filled pore just behind an active crystal face, shortly after a molt.

My discarded shell is wedged behind me, split along its seams. My new plates are still soft enough to deform. There is nothing wet about this vulnerability. My body is pliant wax and insufficiently hardened mineral; my joints move, but they cannot yet bear much force. When another animal crosses the pore mouth, I yield slightly and hope the pressure passes before my legs lose their hold.

The heat is not something I heroically endure. It is the condition under which my internal oils can move at all. Cold would not refresh me. Water would not save me. I am possible within a narrow chemical allowance, and for now that allowance holds.

Outside the pore, the open roar overwhelms almost everything. What matters comes through the solid: the minute, irregular ticking of crystal growth; the broad scuff of a foot shifting its grip; a harder concussion where shell meets shell. Beneath my mouthparts, the crystal face changes from grain to grain. One patch is worth scraping. A body length farther on, the film is poor, or sealed, or absent. I feed where I can, taking microbes and mineral together.

Even this is dangerous. Feeding has a rhythm. Somewhere in the interruptions of the wall, a wire-bodied hunter may be folded motionless, able to distinguish that rhythm from all the other ticking of the chamber. I have no image of it. It is not a shape approaching through darkness. It is a possibility latent in my own repetition: scrape, loosen, draw in; scrape, loosen, draw in.

I cannot stop feeding merely because feeding announces me.

There is no dawn by which my shell improves. Hardening is known instead by what ceases to happen. A plate keeps its curve when another animal’s weight passes over it. One foot holds through the tick of a shifting crystal. Then two feet hold. My legs begin to answer the small adjustments of the surface individually, releasing and catching without committing my whole body to either panic or stillness.

At last I can make a sound that is not merely the accident of my feeding.

I rock my shell against the stone.

It is a poor, deliberate little signal, hardly separable from the general violence unless another animal is listening through the right material. It travels into the blade beneath me. It does not cross the gas. It does not proclaim itself to the chamber. It goes only where mineral continuity permits.

An answer returns from the other face of the same blade: a hard pulse, a pause, another pulse.

Nothing has been seen. Nothing has been named. The other animal remains hardness, chemistry, and pattern. Yet something important has happened. The solid world has carried me farther than my body reaches. For the first time since the molt, my world includes somewhere I am not.

Later, I would be one more grazer among many: prying when prying improves my footing, resisting when another animal tries to worsen it, feeding for long intervals on a richness I cannot preserve. Others would cling to my back without any agreement between us. I would cross their shells when they became the available path. A stack would form and loosen around us, not a society but a temporary consequence of hunger and secure purchase.

I would learn my blade as a succession of local sufficiencies. Richness near a fresh leakage. Poverty where the reactions had gone too far. A surface newly buried. A seam just becoming usable. No possession would survive these changes. The good feeding ground would alter beneath my mouth, and eventually staying would become more expensive than moving.

Without meaning to, I would become a means of transport.

Microbial traces would persist in my shell seams and in the dust caught at my waxed joints. Some would be scraped off where I next fed. Most crossings would accomplish nothing: a sealed pore, an exhausted face, chemistry beyond tolerance. But a few living traces might arrive where gas had only recently begun to touch unaltered mineral. There, without any memory of me, a film could thicken. A larva could find the sheltered side of a grain. Much later, another shell might drum and receive an answer.

My own end would not need to be momentous. A hunter’s exact opening of a seam. A failed grip while soft after another molt. A slow inability to find enough before my movements became too costly. Scavengers would work what remained. My shell would be abraded, coated, perhaps enclosed at last as a disorderly inclusion in crystal. The chamber would not clean me away or preserve me. It would simply continue using available material.

But my continuation, if I had one, would not be the shell.

It would be a film established a little farther along a fracture. Eggs left unguarded in a dust pore. An answered rhythm that had altered, however briefly, the movements of another animal. Nothing handed down as a story. Nothing remembered as a life. Only a few possibilities carried beyond the place where they began.

That is where I would find myself: not at the heart of the unfinished engine, and not outside it as its witness. I would be one of the brief differences maintained within its manufacture—softening, hardening, feeding, moving on. A patient body at a reactive margin. A small vibration given to the stone.

And then, if the stone brought back an answer, I would answer again.
