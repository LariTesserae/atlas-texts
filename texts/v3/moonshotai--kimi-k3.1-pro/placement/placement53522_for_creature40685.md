I would begin by mistaking myself for a ribbon.

A ribbon has the nearest thing this world offers to an orientation. Its small float keeps a little tension on the stem; its upper tissue receives the faint seasonal allowance; for part of the long cycle, it can grow toward *above*. There is something tempting in being the inhabitant who possesses even that much direction.

But most of the ribbon is below. Most of its life is dark persistence: the lower stem lasting, older growth bearing its weight, seepage and association supplying what the remote upper tissue cannot. To be only the tip would be to mistake the faintest signal for the whole being.

Then I might choose a cup. A cup makes a small stillness and lets the water bring what it cannot go looking for. That seems closer. Yet a cup eventually fills. It catches, chokes, becomes a low boss, then a rooting place for other growth. Its first shape fails, but the material does not therefore leave the community of the living. It becomes support, sediment, history.

That is where I would begin to recognize myself: not in a form, but in a change of function that does not require an ending.

Find me where a field of low domes grades into open ductwork. Beneath a sluice-house ridge, an oval ceramic conduit enters root-bound rubble and does not properly leave it. Water arrives along the old line of the pipe, disperses through fractured tile and mineral scale, and appears much later at joints in the paving. Between those points, the original route survives less as a conduit than as a preference.

I would be the old clonal growth occupying that preference.

Inside the pipe, my tissue would be pipe-lace: fine webs molded by seepage, catching leaf-dust, making the passage narrower and more extensive at once. Beyond the broken ceramic mouth, I would become joint-felt. My holdfasts would enter mortar voids and the fine pores of clay. Where water lingered, I might put up a few cups; where it moved too quickly, I would remain low, almost indistinguishable from discoloration on the flags. Some of my visible tissue would be young. Some would be old stems with living strands inside them. Some would merely be structures I had not yet ceased to use.

The films around my holdfasts would not be me in quite the same way as my leaves, but my outline would be difficult to draw without them. Their dissolution would open my way; their scale would close it elsewhere. I would be an association before I was an individual, and my body would have the shape of a former route for water—with omissions.

Nothing in me would apprehend that shape. There would be no inner chamber from which the whole route could be surveyed. The pronoun *I* is useful for this telling; the growth itself would need only local continuities.

A water front would not arrive to me all at once. A film deep in the rubble would alter first. Much later, one patch of felt would add a denser layer, while a runner at another joint extended or stopped. A cup might fill under the changed fall of sediment before the farthest lace had encountered the new chemistry at all. My response would be spread across an interval longer than the event that caused it.

If I had a thought, it would be something like that: not a conclusion, but a distributed alteration that never needed to become simultaneous.

My past would lie underneath me, though not in an orderly archive. It would be inclination, compression, a difference in what could attach where. Memory would not mean recovering when something happened. It would mean that growth afterward was slightly more possible in one direction than another.

What would I do?

Almost nothing, locally, for a very long time.

I would strain particles from water that had already nearly stopped moving. I would bind a flag while lifting its support. I would let one route fill with sediment and another remain porous. Films would take mortar apart; felt would keep the resulting fragments in place. A pipe would cease to be a pipe without ceasing to conduct.

I would not be repairing the old work. Nor would I be conquering it. I would be maintaining a small, shifting difference between open and closed. The original system might have named that difference blockage, leakage, collapse. At the scale of a joint, those names would become too clean.

I would be a living porosity.

Sometimes a slab would settle or a support fail. The note would travel through the water without my hearing it. What reached me would be displacement: a torn mat, an exposed fracture, a connection lost inside a wall. Viable fragments might come to rest where growth alone could never have carried them. Patches at either end of the broken route would continue.

Afterward there might be two of me, or one body with an unavailable middle, or no reason to make the distinction. There would be no single place in which the self had been stored, and therefore no single place from which it could be lost. Continuation would not require that every connection remain intact. It would require only that some attachment remain possible.

You could pass over me without finding anything to point to. A little felt. A clogged line of ceramic. A cup filled long ago. Roots holding a fracture in exactly the position the fracture had made necessary. Nothing singular enough to seem like an inhabitant.

But water entering the old conduit would not simply emerge on the other side. It would be delayed, divided, strained, given into pores, taken up in minute amounts, and released along a route that had not existed when the masonry was laid. Some of its history would become tissue or scale. The rest would rejoin the darkness slightly otherwise.

My nearest approach to speech would be that seepage: an alteration in what happened next, with no need to be received.

I would not be the Cistern’s mind, its keeper, or its memory of itself. I would be one small place where filtering, binding, growing, and failing had become difficult to tell apart.

You would find me in the delay between water entering the old work and water leaving the garden.

Not open. Not closed. Still permeable. That would be enough of a self.
