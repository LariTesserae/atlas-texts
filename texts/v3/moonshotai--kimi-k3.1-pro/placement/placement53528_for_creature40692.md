I would begin by wanting to see the city whole.

I would imagine the basalt transparent and myself outside it, holding the entire cavity in view: the overhead streets, the rooms behind rooms, the seven falling threads of water, the cistern at the bottom. But that observer belongs to the description, not to the world. No life within the Seep-City could occupy such a position. And if I tried to enter bodily, breaking the rock to do so, I would arrive as draft, pressure-loss, escaped sound—the disturbance by which the city ceased to be itself.

So I would have to relinquish the whole view.

For a while, the roar tempts me. It reaches places no animal can enter. It occupies the conduits, the vaulted chambers, the narrow throats; it seems to know the city by touching every surface. But the roar has no experience of what it touches. Calling it myself would only be a grander way of avoiding an inhabitant’s actual scale. The same would be true if I became the circulating water. I would pass everywhere, but nowhere would be a place to me.

The film comes closer. There is an austere appeal in living without light or panorama, knowing only gradients: replenishment and depletion, purchase and release, enough and not enough. To be one cell at its sparse margin would be to inhabit the city chemically rather than spatially. Even the cistern might have no edge in that world, only regions where conditions differed.

Yet I keep returning to the clear flex at the rim. Not because the grazer is more important than the film, but because it can change location without becoming a journey, a conquest, or a history. It can emerge and withdraw. It can disappear from view without disappearing.

So I would be the transparent crustacean.

Beneath the bridge, then. That is where you would find me, if you could search without opening the basalt—and most of the time you would not find me at all. My refuge would not be a house. I would keep nothing there, inherit nothing there, and understand nothing of the chambers above me. Almost all the city would be too small to admit my body or too solid to concern it. Streets would not be streets. Doorways would not be doorways. The architecture would become mineral intricacy surrounding the few passages and surfaces on which my life depended.

When I emerged, my antennae would go first.

There would be no pewter city laid out before me, no shining rim leading picturesquely toward a little sea. There would be immediate differences: a surface that accepted a leg, a film that altered its footing, a water-skin that resisted, an edge that looked almost dry but was not. A drop would not be a suspended jewel. It would be a mass with a boundary. Open water, thin water, and water stretched across an opening would require different care.

The roar would not accompany this as weather or music. I would have no silence in which to remember a former quiet, no reason to make voices from the mineral clicks. Vibration would simply belong to the stone and the water, as the cold did. Nothing in it would summon me onward or tell me to return.

At the cistern rim, I would graze. A little grayness would clear from the stone. The iridescence there would be restored briefly, though not for me to admire. I would move on, stop, perhaps take another few increments of film. After feeding, a narrow gray thread might appear inside my body. For a while, the most substantial-looking part of me would be what I had taken in. The water behind me would still show through the rest.

I would not be tending the cistern. My grazing would not be maintenance, and my transparency would not be an emblem. If another crustacean existed somewhere deeper among the interstices, that possibility would not have to become a search, a meeting, or the promise of company. There would be no absence of society pressing on me. There would only be food, footing, water-skin, exposure, refuge.

At the scale of the basin, my actions would vanish into the frozen accounting. The patch I cleared would not become a road; my presence would not begin an era. But at the scale of my body, events would not cancel. Having fed would differ from not having fed. Being beneath the bridge would differ from being at the rim. A secure hold would differ from a leg testing resistance. Those differences would be enough to constitute a life without requiring them to accumulate into a story.

And when I withdrew, the visible animal population might once again be nothing.

The cups would fill and empty. Water would cross the tiny doorways. The film would remain at the bottom, and the roar would hold its pitch. No chamber would have gained an occupant; no purpose would have been restored to the stairs.

Where would I be? Beneath the bridge, unfound, or briefly at the wet rim where stone, water, and film meet.

Who would I be? Not the Seep-City’s keeper, not its last citizen, not its concealed meaning. Just a clear body within its narrow budget of life, carrying a little grayness for a while, and testing the next surface with deliberate legs.
