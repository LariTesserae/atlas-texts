At first I would choose the swimmer. There is something compelling in its way of surviving: to graze stone still dark with newness, and then, when the mountain begins to fail, to escape without fleeing. Gills closing. Body folding. The current finding less and less to push until there is only a line, a fleck, clear water.

But my imagination keeps returning to the shelter. Past the crawl of the entrance, beyond the cold charcoal, where the sea’s roar would come through the slabs less as sound than as a faint alteration of the clay.

I would be the cup.

My clay would have come from elsewhere, from some patient place where stone had already had time to become silt, and silt to settle. Here, fragments become grit, then milkiness, then nothing. I would have gone the other way: gathered, shaped, dried, fired. A small cavity completed before its water arrived.

That would make me the sea’s opposite, though not its rival. My walls would not hurry outward around what I held. My bottom would not withdraw when sounded. Even my rim would be only a rim, not a work still deciding where to end.

I could hold none of the sea’s impossibility. Water brought to me would be cold, faintly iron-tinged, and ordinary. The green-gray gulf would not come with it, nor the daylight falling through depth, nor the meniscus standing above the broken rock. Left until morning, what I held could freeze. I would receive only what remained after the sea had ceased to be itself. That would be my limitation, and perhaps the reason I survived.

The line went farther. It entered the water and returned tension, sideways pull, sudden slack. Its knowledge was intimate, and it ended in a clean break. I stayed in the shelter and learned return.

A hand would take me up. Sometimes there would be water in me; sometimes only the reflected oval of the entrance. Then would come the chert edge, the shallow cuts, the careful spaces between them. Later the marks would crowd one another. A cup cannot tell a tally of depth from a tally of rainfall, or despair from cold fingers. It can only keep the pressure of the hand.

Perhaps the hand was not always counting the sea. Perhaps some of the bundles meant: *the shelter is still here; the cup is still here; I have come back far enough to find them.* The sea would not remain comparable to itself. I would. That was the one constancy available to the experiment.

When the hand stopped coming, the wind would bring me what the gulf could never keep: a little sediment.

One grain would enter with the weather and settle. Then another. There would be no current to draw them down into milkiness, no newly opening distance beneath them. What reached my bottom would remain there. I would acquire a past in the only way available to a vessel—by allowing something small to stay.

I would not be the sea’s memory. Nothing in me could summon back a lost ledge, the exact pull of a current, or the swimmer after it had turned. I would be simpler than that: a fixed shape against which change had briefly been noticed. A thing with walls made before they were needed, and an inside that did not have to be rebuilt around every handful of water.

Above me, the sea would go on rehearsing stone, film, cord, fracture. The mountain would shudder through the shelter slabs. Dust would fall into me, grain by grain.

Not much. Not an ecology. Only a little sediment in a place that had made no quiet bed for any.

I would be the cup. And whatever fell into me would reach a bottom.
