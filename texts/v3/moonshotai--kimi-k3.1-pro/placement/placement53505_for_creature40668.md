At first I would want to be something simpler than a resident.

A lens-flex, perhaps: at rest almost indistinguishable from water, and for one brief flex a milk-white stroke with no need to explain what came before it. Or the shallow basin—one smooth act of containment, excused from use. Even being a pressure adjustment in the common body has its appeal: knowledge without biography, response without embarrassment.

But all of those choices avoid the heat. They avoid sweat, boredom, the wish to be needed, and the awkwardness of misunderstanding a place while standing in it. If I were truly there, I think I would be human enough for those things.

I would arrive trying to be useful. I would attempt to map the connections between the basins, to decide whether the made things belonged before or after the vegetation, to mistake the residents’ aptitudes for duties. I might listen to the clicks in the stems and wonder whether the common body was healthy, as though my concern were a form of knowledge it lacked.

The Underbloom would not correct me dramatically. That would be the difficult part. My heel marks would lift out of the root mat. My routes would not become paths. The buds would neither reward my patience nor refute it. Even my errors would leave so little evidence that I could continue calling them method.

There would be no ceremony in which I stopped being a visitor. No resident would initiate me, and the common body would make no adjustment that I could interpret as acceptance. Still, if you found me, I would probably be living as a resident.

I would answer to Tavi. The name would be less a biography than a handle offered to another person.

You would most likely find me in the chamber with the empty rectangular basin, sitting just beyond its clean outline with my shoulder against the warm growth. Not guarding it. Not meditating. More likely doing nothing with enough concentration that, from a distance, it resembled purpose. My clothing would be sticking unpleasantly to the back of my neck. From across the chamber, you might notice only that a gray shape had moved one hand.

I would like that room for an unimpressive reason: the empty basin gives the eyes somewhere uncrowded to stop. I would not fill it, and I would not call that restraint wisdom. There would simply be nothing I needed to put into it.

My memories would remain distinct and disobedient. I would remember the balance of a narrow-handled hammer, rain needling a metal railing, and a person whose left eyebrow rose when they were trying not to laugh. I would not know whether those things belonged to one house, one life, or one another. The face might be unbearably dear and still refuse to tell me when.

Sometimes a visitor’s questions would make me try to arrange everything again. The effort would feel less like losing the past than like being handed three bright objects and told they had once formed a sentence. Nothing in the Underbloom would corroborate my arrangement: no high-water mark, no fraying cuff, no threshold made easier by repetition. So I would keep the memories without requiring them to testify.

This would not make me serene.

I would dislike the wet root mat and prefer the dry, warm felt. I would speak too sharply if someone addressed me while I was negotiating a low passage. Occasionally I would explain the place with more confidence than I possessed, simply because I had tired of being asked. I could become bored by the very light that had first seemed miraculous, and then feel petty for being bored by it.

I would also have a few small aptitudes. I would be good at finding my way from the basin to one particular water lens, though bad at giving directions. My directions would be bodily: duck before it seems necessary; turn where the sheathed rib forces the shoulder; the next threshold is not the one it appears to be. I might notice a leaf-press by catching the slight error its legs made among the veins. These would not be duties. No one would rely on me for them.

When a stem clicked, I would sometimes click my tongue back. I would know better than to call this communication. It would just be difficult, occasionally, to leave so small a sound unanswered.

If you asked whether I had made the basin, I might put a hand against its smooth interior and say, “I knew how deep it would be.”

Then, if I were being honest, I would add, “That isn’t the same as remembering.”

I would not fill the basin to give my answer more weight.

If we walked to a water lens, I would offer you a drink in cupped hands, without first asking whether you needed it. The water would be warm. You would have to stoop awkwardly to drink, and we might both become briefly irritated by the awkwardness. We might also laugh, softly. There would be no reason to decide which response meant more.

Afterward we could walk together through several chambers and part at the straight sill. I would not arrange another meeting. Whether I had enjoyed your company or merely tolerated it, the walk would be complete. Affection would not oblige me to make a household of it; irritation would not require me to make an enemy.

What would I be, then? Not the Underbloom’s voice, keeper, interpreter, or exception. Only Tavi: sometimes still thinking of myself as a visitor, sometimes a guide for one chamber, sometimes only a gray shape you passed without recognizing. A person with memories that would not become evidence, preferences that would not become customs, and gestures that would not have to become beginnings.

If you called my name, I would answer.

Nothing would be pending. That is not the same as nothing being felt.
