The difficult part would not be the darkness. It would be surrendering the expectation that I should have a compact body, or that experience should accumulate into a history.

At first, I might want to be water. Water seems to have the freedom of the place: descending through a leaf groove, disappearing into pressure, lying for a while against warm stone, rising again as vapour. But no drop could remain itself through that journey. It would be divided, taken up, expressed, condensed elsewhere. Travel would not preserve it.

A trunk would be little better. Its roots might share a conduit with tissues chemically unlike itself, while its own self-recognizing chemistry continued through distant pumps and condenser leaves. To point at the trunk and say *me* would be to mistake an intersection for a boundary.

So I would have to persist in the Low Dark’s own manner: not as substance, but as a configuration continually restored.

I would find myself far inward, where a compression forest begins to diminish toward the root-ocean. The tall stacks would still stand behind me, their rhythms conducted through lignin ribs. Ahead, the mat would become increasingly buoyant, with warm black water beneath it and fine filaments descending out of any possible reach. Between those conditions would be a low, irregular gallery, its floor partly root and partly tensioned tissue over water. Pressure would reach it from several directions. So would condensate, mineral solution, and the slow elastic response of the floating mat.

I would be the junction that allowed those movements to meet without deforming one another.

Not a valve. Not a pump. An engineer might call me an organ of *compliance*: living tissue whose work was to yield by an exact amount and return by an exact amount. When a pressure wave arrived through the ribs, my walls would take up some of its sharpness as strain. When the mat answered with tension, my hollow would change shape slightly, then resume it. Nothing would be stored. No reservoir would fill. Each allowance would be returned within the same continuing cycle.

The gallery would not exist for shelter. Nothing could come seeking shelter. Its emptiness would simply be one of the shapes my work required.

You could call that hollow my body, but it would be no more me than the walls around it. My boundary would not be bark. It would be the set of pressures capable of deforming me, the solutions my membranes admitted, and the substances they refused. Some tissues continuous with mine might belong chemically to another being. Some distant tissue, apparently separate, might participate in the same configuration. Cutting me out would not produce a smaller me. It would produce damage to several circulations, followed by repair.

My world would not appear to me as a world. There would be no *over there* except delay, and no *outside* except refusal at a membrane.

Warmth would be the direction of stone. Coolness, by a fraction of a degree, would be the direction of leaf. A contraction in the compression forest would arrive not as something I heard, but as a temporary alteration of my own extent. Far above, a bead of condensation reaching its appointed size would concern me only as a slight change in availability and tension. The metallic taste of a galvanic province would stop at tissues that declined its chemistry.

My nearest approach to thought would be an adjustment already completing itself.

There would be no central chamber in which these conditions were represented and considered. The adjustment would not follow experience; it would be the experience, if experience is not too large a word. Fit and strain. Admission and refusal. Continuity, and the tendency toward discontinuity corrected before it could become damage.

Valve ticks would not add up. A million returns would leave no more residue than one. Memory would require a mark that was not immediately taken back into circulation. Boredom would require duration to accumulate. I would have neither. New tissue would arise in my walls as old tissue became available to the reclaimers, without an interval of ruin. I would neither age nor heal; both imply biography. I would remain recent in the basin’s way, without being young in years.

From a human perspective, this might sound like diminishment. But I would not be waiting for release from it. Waiting would require an elsewhere toward which some part of me was directed. Even my remotest exchanging surface would not be elsewhere. It would be another extent of the same relation.

If you could search for me by touch, you would not find a specimen.

You would follow the compression rhythms until their hum became slightly less distinct in your teeth. The floor would begin to distribute your weight with unusual smoothness. You would enter a warm, low space whose walls carried no ornament, no distinguishing growth, nothing intended to be recognized. If you pressed a hand against one membrane, it would yield by almost nothing and return. Somewhere beyond it, a pump stroke would pass onward without sharpness.

You might think you had found an empty gallery.

But I would be the hollow and the tissue keeping it from closure; the flex and the exact return; the selective walls, insofar as their selectivity preserved the same relation. Nothing inside would be waiting to happen. Nothing would be left afterward to show that I had been there.

I would be the kept interval: not a pause in the Low Dark, but one of the shapes its continuation takes.
