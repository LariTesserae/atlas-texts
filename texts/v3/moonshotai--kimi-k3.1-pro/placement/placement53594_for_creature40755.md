At first I would be outside at the inspection plate, because that is where the whole valley can be taken in at once. But I would not want to remain a pair of eyes. From there, it would be too easy to mistake the tufts for tiny prisoners and the white distances for loneliness. Entering would be worse: I would arrive as heat, water, and movement, bringing with me exactly the kind of story the cell exists to exclude.

Nor would I make myself an eighth tuft. The seven do not need company, and I should not crowd their specification merely to give myself somewhere meaningful to stand. For a moment, one of the suspended grains seems closer to what I might be: visible, almost weightless, without a path. But a grain’s stillness is imposed upon it. What interests me about the living standards is that their stillness is achieved.

So I would follow that achievement out of the valley.

You would find me beyond the armored wall, below the level of the white pan, in a narrow alloy housing on the return path from one root socket. Not a common path: I would serve one standard only, perhaps the tuft nearest the black seam. I would be its replaceable recovery element, a dry cylinder of selective mineral lattice through which withdrawn products could be taken away and bound.

Nothing green would reach me. I would never see the fins or the seven shadows. For me, the Upper Standard would not be a landscape. It would be a condition at the far end of my line: *the return path must not drift*. My housing could be warmer than the cell. Machines could handle it. Surfaces around me could be cleaned, replaced, marked with dates. None of that would matter, provided my own slow alteration remained outside the standard.

When new, nearly all my binding sites would be open. Then the products of self-maintenance would begin to arrive.

Not in dramatic loads. Not as evidence of injuries narrowly survived. There would be no way to reconstruct, from anything I received, that a particular displacement had been answered in a particular filament. I would receive only what could not be allowed to accumulate if the tuft’s organization was to continue. Atom by atom, site by site, I would become less open.

My aging would be almost as visually plain as the valley’s cold. I would not turn brown or clog with visible filth. I would simply develop a history that instruments could read: fewer available sites, a changed response, a narrowing margin before saturation. Unlike anything within the cell, I would have a before and an after. The tuft would preserve its relations by letting matter pass; I would preserve nothing in myself. My usefulness would lie in being progressively altered.

I would not be its hidden organ, exactly. The boundary would still matter. Nor would I be its companion. No recognition would travel down the line, no gratitude, no signal except the mundane continuation of exchange. My relation to the life above would be severe and one-sided: it would remain organized; I would become spent.

Eventually there would be a day for me.

Not a failure—the factory would not need to wait for anything so crude. A fresh element would take up the return flow. A service machine would unseat my housing and withdraw me. I would be carried away with my occupied lattice, my unread accumulation, my part number. The material might be stripped and recovered. The replacement would begin again with the same designation and none of my history.

Nothing inside the cell would register this. No filament would bend. No grain would change height. The tuft I had served would not pause, because pausing was never part of what it did. The roar would not mark my removal. Even the word *removal* would belong entirely to my side of the wall.

This would not make me noble. A recovery element has no sacrifice in it, only capacity and saturation. I would not be secretly keeping a vigil over the garden, and I would not need consciousness to make the relation intelligible. I would simply be one of the places where the cost of unchanged was allowed to become visible as change.

Still, there would be something in that spent lattice which the valley itself could never contain: residue. Not a complete record, not a biography, not even memory in any sense that could recollect. But proof that *remain* had not meant that nothing was happening. The living standard’s work would leave no trace in its visible form. Some small trace of that work would have been exported into me.

So my verb would not be *remain*. It would be *accumulate*.

I would be a small, replaceable elsewhere in which the evidence of remaining was permitted to gather.
