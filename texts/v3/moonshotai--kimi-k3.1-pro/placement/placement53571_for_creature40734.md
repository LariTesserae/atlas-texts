### On the sheltered side of a grain

The centre would be my first mistake. It has room for a body, and so it invites the thought that being there means standing in the clear water, wet to the ankles, made conspicuous by the light and enormous by the noise. But then I would only be visiting. My heat, breath and clothing would briefly outweigh my importance. When I left, nothing would follow, and nothing would need to recover.

The water offers another possible self, but it is almost entirely passage. A ceiling drop has a cleaner outline: swelling, falling, merging. Its separateness lasts only until the surface closes. The snail, when there is one, at least moves at its own pace and leaves a faint disturbance behind it. Yet it is a visitor whose visit has not yet ended. Visibility is not residence.

The ferns come closer. A rhizome may persist while its fronds are replaced; its movement is slow enough to resemble remaining still. But even a fern has too coherent an outline. A frond can be pointed to. A root can be followed for a little way. I would be further down, where the apparent vegetation becomes a population of wet surfaces.

Take one piece of coke from a trough. Within it, choose a pore set back from the swiftest flow, deep enough that the lamps make no difference, but not so secluded that water and dissolved matter cannot enter. On the sheltered wall of that pore, I would be a patch of bacterial film.

Not a single cell, whose tenure would be too brief. Not the whole filter bed, whose unity would exist mainly in the description given from above. Just a local thickness: cells and the hydrated material around them, attached where attachment remained possible, patchy at the edges, sometimes so thin that the name would seem extravagant.

From there, the sump would be unrecognizable. Twelve paces and eleven steps would have no counterpart. There would be a mineral surface, a current, and small differences in what the current carried. The pumps would not be a roar. If their vibration reached the pore at all, it would belong to the condition of the coke. There would be no silence against which it could become noise.

A nearby root would matter more than the vault. Living, it would release a little into the water film. Dying, it would open pathways and then supply material to other lives. A ceiling drop, if its consequences reached so far, would not arrive as an event from above. It would be shear: part of the film lifting, a neighbour displaced, a temporary connection opened between spaces. Then the narrower current would establish itself again.

Food would arrive without being addressed to me. A little dissolved matter from the return water; something released by roots; a particle already reduced by passage through other lives. I would take up what I could, convert some, store some briefly and release some. The old system would call the aggregate of such activities filtration. I would not be serving the station. I would be metabolizing. That the water might leave differently would be a consequence at another scale.

There would be no guarantee of persistence. A ciliate might crop one edge. Cells would divide; others would detach and be carried deeper or toward the overflow. The patch could remain while little or none of its original substance did. Or it could vanish, and the pore would be simply wet coke again until another film established itself. The sump would not need to decide whether this was continuation, replacement or loss.

I would not be keeping the room in balance. That would be far too large a claim. I would only add a small uptake and release to many others. Seen from the doorway, their sum would resemble stillness: clear water, a fixed green margin, no soil deepening beneath the ferns.

That is where I would find myself: below the roots, on the sheltered side of a grain, with no view of the vault and no knowledge of the sea. The steel door might open and admit its brief, warm animal. It might close again. Neither would mark an interruption for me. There would be attachment and loss, division and grazing, the occasional drop translated into a local flood. If I left a signature, it would be in matter passed on differently, not in anything retained.

From the doorway, all of it would be indistinguishable from nothing happening.
