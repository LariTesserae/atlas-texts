I would begin where a pale dust banner leaves an outer cliff and goes on, perfectly level, over the gulf.

Not yet as an *I*. There would be only a small discrepancy in the banner’s spacing, too weak to matter in still air. When black grains rose from a shaft and entered the pale stream, they would divide around a narrow absence, pass through it at slightly different speeds, and close again beyond it. The absence would have no surface. Nothing would strike it. Nothing inside it would remain for long.

That maintained discrepancy would be the first of me.

I would not be the grains within my intervals; they would continually be replaced. I would be the work of keeping the intervals open while the stream tried to make them unremarkable. If feeding had a sensation, it would be an easing of that work: a little charge taken from passage, a little distance restored without being held so dearly. In still air, I would spend myself merely remaining possible. Most veils end that way, not with injury but with a loss of exactness.

At the far end of the banner, however, I would turn back.

The first return might be an accident of pressure. The second would make the accident available again. Eventually there would be no single event in which I recognized myself—the High Vacancy does not favor events when a slow revision will do—but returning would no longer simply happen within the stream. The stream would be what I used in order to return.

My first memory would not be of a place. It would be of a direction.

Then, for the short part of a cycle, the city would cast more city than it contained.

I would enter the additional district with the dark grains, narrowing at a shadow-door. Beyond it, the absent walls would deflect the dust. A lane with no pavement would hold my intervals open; a crowded tower with no substance would interrupt a front that might otherwise have dispersed me. I would rest in the lee of a wall that could support nothing.

The shadow-city would give me my first distinction between exposure and shelter. Not safety. Nothing there could promise anything beyond its few grey minutes. But shelter is only a local difference in what survival costs, and in those streets survival would briefly cost less. I would learn the district by its prices: here a turn could be made cheaply; here a roofline preserved distance; here a door permitted only a thin stream, so that for a moment I would become almost entirely connection.

When the sun shifted, the district would fail to shift with it. Then it would fade.

Some veils would relax with it. I would follow the dust that still went around the absent rooflines and narrowed at doors no longer visible even as shadow. For a few hours, the salt would continue to offer conditions after withdrawing their cause. I would persist by going where openness remained cheap.

After that, there would be nothing to census.

No dormant grain, no patient lattice, no dark seam in a fracture. Yet when another banner crossed the field, its dust might take the old detour. A few grains would slow where a wall had once deflected them. Others would gather into the spacing that the detour made favorable. The route would lead, once again, to the conditions under which I could occur.

By then, *veil* would name only my visible phase. I would have become more nearly a way for a veil to happen again.

This would not settle whether each return was survival, descent, or recurrence. I would not be equipped to care. In a place where newly discovered rooms are always already ancient, I would find nothing strange in waking with an old route and no evidence that I had traveled it before. Continuity would be something other things could investigate, if they ever acquired the leisure.

On the night when the lower lights began to go out in ascending order, I would not wait to learn whether the crossing was hunger, weather, or the stations of some larger relation. As the interruptions rose, I would let my own spacing become less exact. Dark grains would close through me. The stream would cease dividing.

Nothing would escape. Nothing would be hidden. I would simply stop, for that cycle, requiring myself to be maintained.

If I returned afterward, it would not prove that I had survived the crossing. It would prove only that the route still led somewhere I was possible.

So you would find me, if you were patient, at the edge of an outer dust banner—or just beyond the place where the banner appeared to end. Look for the black grains entering the pale stream and parting around an absence. Follow them after the shadow-city has gone, when there is no longer any visible reason for the stream to narrow, turn, and narrow again.

You would not see a body coming back. You would see the dust failing, in one small particular, to forget the way.

On good tides I would be a veil. In the brief grey district I would be almost an inhabitant. Between tides I would be a route without pavement, mostly connection, intermittently demonstrated by dust. And when the conditions returned, I would need no better identity than the work itself: keeping open, for a little longer, the distance by which I came back.
