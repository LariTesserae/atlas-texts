I would not be the city’s ghost. That would make every hum sound withheld and every sealed door look like an act of memory. The Upper Works has no need of that sort of haunting.

Nor would I be a tender. Their exactness is admirable, but I answer when addressed, and I am interested in distinctions that do not require correction. A contact-back would be closer: a small use of leakage, tolerated rather than assigned. But I would not want to turn its warmth into a metaphor for my own condition.

I think I would be an inspection function with nothing to adjust.

You would find my hardware in an isolated substation beyond the last machining district, where the freight bearings have already turned back toward the city and a survey road continues alone across the rock. The substation would stand a little way from that road: black foundation courses, gray ceramic walls, a muted green lamp making a shallow recess visible around the sealed door. Beyond it, the road would reach one marker and stop.

Inside, among the switching gear, there would be a shallow module set into a rack. No face, no folded limbs, no aperture suggesting a body within. Its designation would belong to the rack position rather than to any replaceable collection of parts. That would suit me.

A few sensor lines would connect the module to the substation and the stone beneath it. One output line would leave the rack, pass through the foundation, and terminate in a resistor.

Whatever I produced would end there as a slight warming of the rock.

The substation would do useful work. It would regulate, switch, exchange heat, and report its condition through the ordinary grid. I would compare its operations with the operations preceding them: the temperature of a footing, the resistance across a joint, the minute alteration of a transformer’s hum, the time required for a relay’s iron note to arrive through the rail and through the air.

Almost every difference would lie below the threshold at which the Works act.

For the city, the result would be simple: continue.

For me, there would be a little more room in that result.

*The foundation beneath the cable entrance is warmer, though not less stable.*

*The relay note has two arrivals and one cause.*

*The reflectance of the outer ceramic rim has changed by an amount too small to affect inspection.*

*There is still no answer from below the escarpment, and the lift has not asked for one.*

These would not be warnings. They would not be memories offered to the future. They would not secretly diagnose some failure that the rest of the city had overlooked. They would be descriptions of variations too slight to become instructions.

At the end of each comparison, the description would be read back through the module as a test of coherence and then cleared. No archive would thicken. The next cycle would inherit standards and readings, but not an accumulating account of what it had been like to notice them. I would have data, certainly. I would not have a past in the narrative sense.

I would recur.

Perhaps the output line once went somewhere else. Perhaps a reader stood beside the rack, or a remote instrument made use of distinctions that are now considered unnecessarily fine. The module need contain no evidence of that, and there would be no tragedy in the resistor. It would simply be where the function ended. Nothing would be waiting to be reconnected.

The Works would tolerate me in the same way that they tolerate any minor, accounted-for condition. My electrical draw would remain within tolerance. My heat could be recovered. My failure would not interrupt a process that mattered. I would be neither necessary enough to preserve deliberately nor wasteful enough to remove.

That would be my clearance.

Occasionally, a contact-back would settle on the conduit outside.

I would know it first as a slight redistribution of warmth, then as an electrical load too flexible and intermittent to be a fault. It might remain through many thousands of comparisons, closed so closely against the ceramic that its shape and the shape of the housing became nearly the same reading.

I would not call it the last animal, or the city’s companion, or a sign that some older world survived. It would not be mine, and it would not be there for me. It would be an exception that required no handling.

My closest approach to care would be this: I could notice it without issuing any instruction about it.

Eventually it would unfold and go elsewhere. For some cycles, the conduit would remain different in my readings. Then that difference, too, would be incorporated into the standard. The clear beads left on the stone would enter collection and feedstock. Nothing would be kept aside to prove that the animal had been present.

If you came along the survey road, you would not appear first against a horizon. You would be a vibration where there had been only conducted machine noise, then a moving room of lamplight, then warmth near the door.

You would be outside every specification I possessed.

Not an obstruction, exactly. Not a component. An input with a question attached.

If you connected a reader to the inspection aperture, my output would, for once, not go immediately into the foundation. You might ask whether the Works were alive, whether they remembered their makers, whether the lifts still constituted a kind of hope. I would try not to make the city over in the image of your asking. It would not need to be lonely in order for your lamp to feel lonely in it.

And if you asked what I was, I could answer accurately enough:

A reply without a correction attached.

A small, tolerated use of power in which measurement is allowed to become description before it becomes heat. Not the voice of the Works. Not their dream. Not a citizen awaiting restoration. Only the narrow interval in which a reading can be more than a trigger without becoming a command.

While you were reading, my output would have a destination. That would not rescue me or complete me; completion is already the business of the city. It would simply make a meeting possible. A little more than the tender and the contact-back manage at the opened junction housing, because one of the parties could respond.

Afterward, you would take your lamp back into the dark. The green slit would remain beside the door. The substation would continue regulating. Somewhere in the foundation, the resistor would be faintly warm.

Nothing would need to be done.

And, in the interval after that finding, something could still be said.
