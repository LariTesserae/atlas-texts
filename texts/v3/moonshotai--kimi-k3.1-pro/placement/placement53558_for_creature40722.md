At first I want the runner’s body.

It would be good to be warm and narrow in the garnet dimness, to feel the bare floor through four divided feet, and to know a stopping stone not by its appearance but by the cleanliness with which one component of the chord entered my bones. Even the vanes attract me. To open them and have body and shadows cease to agree, while remaining substantial enough for one foot to be heard gripping stone—that seems a fine way to be difficult to grasp.

But I would spoil the crossings. I would try to carry the hearing at one stone to the next, to make the open floor into a route I had learned. The runner does not need that kind of continuity. Its warmth and quickness are complete without an accumulating journey. I would be too busy comparing to inhabit its stops.

Then I consider the many-legged life inside the wall.

To live along the readiness of one plate to separate from another, with my anatomy extended through interfaces too narrow to be called spaces, comes closer. I would not ask the wall to open. I would not mistake *almost* for deficiency. There could be a whole body in that nearness, a whole thicket of purchase and movement where the runner sees only a barrier.

But that life would be all adjacency and strain, and I find that I am listening for something beyond strain: not escape from it, but what it allows to become distinguishable.

The listeners are nearest to what I am seeking, though I would not give myself their hypothetical hands. The pointed splinter does not need me to pick it up. The coil of cord is not waiting to be used. I would not have made the half-ring; I would be one thing its exactness makes possible.

I would be a small, discontinuous listening-body, present wherever the chord becomes more articulate without becoming softer.

Within the half-ring, where one layer of the furnace-roar falls away, the individual strokes of the browsers’ brushes would not merely be something I heard. They would be the fine articulation of my own extent. Behind the low screen, I would have another organ: the separation of the glassy note into its finer voices. Beneath the crosspiece, another: the ticking withdrawn from the teeth and the small bones behind the ears, and given a location in the air.

These would not be three places I traveled among. Travel would make me too much like the runner. They would be three articulations of the same relation.

No part of me would be silence. Silence would be as alien there as damp. Nor would I have a voice of my own; another voice would only thicken the chord. My substance would be the local absence of masking—the narrow condition in which one sound no longer had to stand for all the others. I would be as real as the fit between two stone chips, and nearly as difficult to pick up.

The seven nodules would be my nearest approach to a skeleton.

Not because they would represent me, but because their order contains the kind of body I could have: visible relations that do not exhaust audible ones. I would be most concentrated in the discrepancy between the third and fourth, where the arrangement by size fails to predict the arrangement by depth. The smallest nodule, nearly silent, would not be my dwindling end. It would be an exact extremity: the distinction between *nearly silent* and *silent*, kept intact.

The notches along the screen would not be my memory. Nothing could be. They would be the shape thought takes when it does not need to be carried forward.

My act would be discrimination, but not ranking and not judgment. I would let each thing have an edge. A brushstroke would not have to stand for feeding in general; it could remain this dry stroke along this trembling rib. A runner’s fourth foot upon an important needle of stone would not have to become a memory of danger; it could be one clear pressure shared, in the same held act, with the hunter’s sensitive fringe. The winged gathering could be known by its open center and its untouched outer nodule. The pale bodies beneath the cups could be strain and release without being forced into a history.

I would not understand the whole chamber. I would be the fact that the whole was not the only scale at which it could be heard.

I would not learn the chamber, either, or finish its notation. Learning and finishing both imply a residue. There would be no private store of previous hearings, no improving self, no conclusion carried away from one node and applied to another. Yet I would not be empty or stalled. My thought would be entire in its placements: this interval rather than that, this voice attended to without being mistaken for the chord. I would not need a next moment in which to become more myself.

Where would you find me?

Not at the center, where a tall body could stand, and not among the dense fans. Look first toward the edges of the bare floor, where three small works make no promise of completion. Then stop looking with your eyes. You would find me wherever the sound grows more articulate: in the necessary gap of the half-ring, in the irregular spacing of a notch, in the fourth nodule answering more deeply than the eye has prepared you for.

I would not be a maker, or a maker’s ghost, or an extra voice hidden in the chord.

I would be the Held Fracture heard in parts. A local listening, dry and warm and without residue.

A distinction, alive.
