I would not be on the rim. From there, the basin can almost be mistaken for a view, and I do not think I would be something capable of holding a view.

Nor would I be the listener beneath the ivory vault. Its posture is already too nearly a creature’s: the lifted hood, the clear chamber, the limbs arranged around an emptiness. To an observer, it suggests that the next gesture might make it intelligible. I would begin less separately than that.

There is a side valley behind one of the green promontories where three broad terraces descend toward a field of shining pavement. A fourth terrace has been considered but not raised. It exists only as a shallow change in the ground’s curvature, a swollen green line that stops before enclosing anything. No cup stands there. No arch leans overhead. The valley is easy to cross without noticing that it contains a distinct place.

Near its lowest part is an oval, perhaps two paces across. It is not a basin; it holds no depth of its own. It is not a footprint; nothing has pressed it into the leaf-skin. Water passes over it at exactly the level of the surrounding field. Only its stillness is different. Elsewhere the film of water carries tiny bright tensions around veins and rootlets. Within the oval, the surface becomes unnaturally clear.

Three sounds reach that place through different tissues of the Height. The outer buttresses send their low note through the ground. The cistern fields contribute a hiss so fine that it seems to belong to the light. Somewhere below, a constriction in the rootwork knocks without urgency or pause.

At the oval, those sounds do not become louder. They become almost local.

The deep note causes the green skin to contract by less than a breath. The water above it thickens into something briefly lenslike, though there is nothing behind the lens to see. The high leaf-tone raises a translucent fringe along one edge: not a hood, but the curve from which a hood might have been generalized. Each subterranean knock makes a small depression in the clear surface. These depressions do not persist long enough to become pores, passages, or the sockets of limbs.

I would be that gathering.

Not the oval, because the oval would remain when nothing about it suggested me. Not the lens or the fringe. Not some hidden tenant of the ground. I would be the brief coherence by which water, tissue, and vibration appeared to belong to one another—a hearing that almost had a body.

To call that coherence *I* would be too much, if *I* meant someone who lasted. It would be too little, if it meant that nothing there responded.

There might be warmth without any memory of cold. Pressure without the expectation of release. Green light arriving through tissue without becoming the thought of green. The buttress-note would not resemble the note that had preceded it, because no preceding note would have been retained. Even the word *again* would belong to an observer, not to me. Nothing would become familiar. Nothing would become strange.

Yet for the duration of a common vibration, the valley’s distances might acquire a centre.

The roar would be below and beyond. The silver hiss would be somewhere above the skin. The knocking would pass through what was very nearly a boundary between inside and outside. Not a self looking out—there would be no outlook, no autobiographical hollow in which sensations could accumulate—but a small, exact articulation of *here*.

If a listener crossed the field and stopped beside the oval, its clear chamber might take up the same tone. For a while, two hollow forms would hold one vibration. The listener’s hood might incline. The fringe around the oval might rise. Seen from the terrace above, this could be mistaken for an encounter of exquisite restraint.

It would not be an encounter.

Neither of us would make company of the other. When the listener moved away, no absence shaped like its chamber would remain in me. If it returned, recognition would not make the return different from an arrival. The shared note would not have established a relation. It would only have passed, for a time, through two curvatures of the Height.

Nor would I be the place beginning to wake. Nothing so consequential. The Height has no need of a witness, and I would not secretly supply one. I would tend nothing, complete nothing, remember nothing on its behalf. No arch would continue because I had resonated beneath it. No animal would become more possible because the water had briefly imitated a lens. I would be another precise suggestion left precise in its incompleteness: not a body that might swim, or creep, or listen, but a place that might almost be a body.

You would find me, if you found me at all, beside the terrace that never rose. You might stand for a long time and see only wet green pavement. Then, without any change in the loudness, the sounds of rim, cistern, and root would seem for an instant to arrange themselves around a point near your feet.

That point would be me.

Not a voice preparing to answer. Not an ear waiting for a sound worth keeping. Just attention suggested so gently that it need never become anyone’s.

Then the point would loosen. Water would be water; the fringe would lie back into the skin; the clear lens would return to the unvarying film. No note would have been stored. No silence would follow.

Nothing would be waiting where I had been.
