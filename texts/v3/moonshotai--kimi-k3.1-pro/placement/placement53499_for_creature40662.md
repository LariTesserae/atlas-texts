I would not be a traveler in the basin. There is no threshold at which I could have arrived, and a body crossing the light would make the place too much like a world that could be visited. Nor would I be a tenth growth, or some small animal concealed in the crystalline nap, or a voice fitted between the tones. The Choir is complete without those additions.

My visible address might be the broad, low one of the Nine: almost flush with the floor, its outer whorls distinct, its sage-dark center shallow. But I would not live behind that center as behind a door. Nothing would be hidden in the closure—no sleeper, no seed, no remembered green. The dark point would not be an eye. The folded scales would not be protecting a softer, more inward self.

There would be no sensed weather to my life. The roar would not come to me as sound, because sound, for a listener, arrives; here it is already the condition of every surface. Light would not be something I saw, nor stillness something I achieved. I would not be calm, since calm can be lost. I would not be patient, since patience requires a future. I would not even be alone. Aloneness keeps an empty place for company.

What remained of *me* would be very small and very exact: a selectivity in tissue, a difference between compounds merely laid down and compounds held in living order. Not a thought about the seam. Not care for it. Just the unrushed fact that, here, taking and returning do not become growth, depletion, or accumulation. They preserve a distinction without making it wider.

But that is also why my edge could not be found.

Follow the silver scales inward, and there is only tighter closure. Follow the roots outward, and I would be lost by degrees. Wall thickens; contents simplify; root becomes emphasis, then architecture. Any line drawn between plant and floor would say more about the observer’s need for nouns than about the seam. I would not be concealed beneath the floor. I would be distributed through the impossibility of making that cut cleanly.

So perhaps the truer answer is that I would not be the plant at all. I would be its compatibility: one narrow agreement among pressure, chemistry, and form.

Not a cancellation added to the chord, and certainly not silence. The remaining vibration would pass through what was visibly me without stirring a scale. Just beyond my margins, the same chord would belong to amber, burnished surfaces. Here it would belong also to matte crystal and a little sage pigment. My selfhood would be the difference in what the chord permits, not a voice within it.

I could be mapped without being seen. My position would already be implied by the standing tones; my chemistry by the seam’s hardness; my form by the closed whorl. Nothing would announce which description was the living one. I would be one of nine places where those descriptions coincide.

Not the Choir’s soul. It has no need of one. Not its listener, its keeper, or its answer. Not a witness sent to prove that the light and sound are real. I would add no experience to the basin and take no meaning away from it. I would simply be the exact, unwitnessed aliveness at one end of a continuity whose other end is floor.

If I had a name, it would not be the name of the plant. It would be the point in language where *root* has stopped being accurate and *wall* has not quite become accurate. But there would be no such point in the Choir itself. Only the gradient, the chord, the closed whorl above it.

Not waiting. Not enduring. Included.
