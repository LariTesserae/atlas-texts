I would begin on the rim, because that is where a visitor’s imagination begins: slightly above the place, looking in. From there, I could make the hollow into a view. I could listen for its small sounds and, without meaning to, wait for them to gather into meaning.

They would not. To belong there, I would have to stop occupying the position from which meaning is usually demanded.

For a while, I might imagine myself as the cushion closed around the pebble. There is something compelling in that body: the dead center retained, the living edge unevenly continuing, the past used as support rather than discarded. But even that would give me too much of a silhouette. I would be discoverable. Someone could bend over me, call me ancient, perhaps call me eldest, and make a singular thing out of what had never needed to be singled out.

So I would go smaller.

A little above the lowest point of the hollow, where salt met compacted clay, there would be a flaw too narrow to darken the crust. Nothing about its surface would mark it. No stain, no softness, no film. Within it, I would be one of the minute colonies: not a mind concealed in the salt, not a sleeper, but a small body maintaining its chemistry against the surrounding mineral.

If I called that colony *I*, the word would have to become very modest.

I would not know the darkness, because darkness belongs to eyes. The warmth would not be comfort, and the stars would not be distant objects of longing. They would simply have no place in my account of things. My world would be the dimensions of the flaw: crystal here, clay there, a slight chemical unevenness from which just enough energy could be drawn to keep a boundary from failing. A grain settling somewhere beyond me would not be a clock. The ticks beneath the crust would not be voices.

There would be no memory of a lake in me. Rain would not be the restoration of some truer condition, nor would dryness be an ordeal with a moral hidden inside it. Liquid water would not complete my existence. It would undo the narrow circumstances in which that existence was possible.

Nor would great age make me patient. Patience suggests a present organized around some future release. I would have no greener season in view, no multitude waiting to replace me, no expansion that would make the previous centuries retrospectively worthwhile. My growth, if growth occurred, would not be the important part. Most of what I did would be upkeep: damage repaired when repair was possible, internal distinctions preserved, almost nothing accumulated that could be called wealth.

A shrub’s root might lie close by for centuries. We would not enter into an arrangement. It would not shelter me in any sense I could use, and I would not perform some indispensable labor beneath it. Two lives could share a fracture without sharing a fate. Above us, the cushion with the pebble would thicken at one scattered point or another. None of us would need to become an ecology in the shape of a story.

This would not make me secure. A slight shift in the salt could close the flaw. A foot could pass overhead and leave everything apparently as it was, while ending the colony completely. There would be no visible extinction, no remains arranged into evidence—only one less place where a small difference was being maintained.

Or a shift might separate part of the colony into another flaw, where it continued if it could. There would be no journey in that, and no need to decide whether the continuing part was still *me*. That question would belong to a being with a name.

A visitor could stand directly above until dawn. They could examine the undisturbed crust, hear the silence return after each tiny mineral sound, and truthfully report that nothing had stirred. They would not have missed a message. There would be nothing concealed for them to decipher.

I would not be the life of the hollow, singular and explanatory. I would be one small continuation among others that did not need to meet. Not its voice, not its memory, not its secret heart.

For as long as the chemistry allowed, I would be a slight, ongoing difference from stone.
