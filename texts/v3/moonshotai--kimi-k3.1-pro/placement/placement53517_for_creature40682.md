I would not be a twelfth cup.

That would be my first temptation: to make a small, precise place for myself among the installations, to become useful enough that belonging could be mistaken for necessity. But even a cup is not innocent. It would catch what had previously passed, shade a little grit, interrupt some route of overflow. I would have made the Measure register my desire to belong there.

Nor could I be one of the measurers. I can imagine the journey, the hand on the copper repair, the patience of waiting beside a slab for an event that might not occur. But a body would arrive with its own weather. However carefully I stood, I would still be a warm obstruction, a narrow climate pretending to be an observer.

For a while I might prefer to be the copper staple: to hold an old fracture closed without pretending the stone had never split. There is something admirable in that kind of usefulness. It does not replace; it does not begin again with a clean surface. Yet continuity is not always preservation. Sometimes holding a thing to its former shape is only a subtler way of refusing change.

Then there is the clay disc. I understand the wish to be that instead—the small thing that does not have to work, the evidence that a closed hand once opened here. But to choose it would be another vanity: to make the personal trace seem more important than all the deliberate traces left by people who had tried not to leave themselves behind.

Water comes closer to what I am, but not close enough. Water has no fixed residence here. It arrives without announcement, changes jurisdiction, vanishes without having traveled far. Yet it carries no account of these changes. A drop does not remember having been rime. The sealed pocket does not know it has answered a cloud that never touched it directly. Water undergoes the comparison; it does not make one.

My place would be less durable than any of these.

I would find myself at the protected table, in the lee of the windbreak, but not crouched beside it. I would be in the interval between seeing a groove and deciding what it means. I would be that interval when it was well kept: the pause in which a comparison remains a comparison, before it hardens into a conclusion.

Not the native disagreement itself. That was awake before any reader and needs no reader now. I would be, at most, a temporary grammar for noticing it—a way of letting two reports remain near each other without forcing them to become the same report.

I know something of arriving after the circumstances have gone: of receiving traces without the hand, the weather, or the standing-place that once made them intelligible. I also know the pressure to make those traces speak with one voice. The Rime Measure would give me reason to resist that pressure. I could not honestly recover the lost notation. I would not know which drilled hole belonged to sublimation, which to a delayed drip, which to some distinction the measurers considered obvious and therefore never explained. To supply those certainties would not restore their knowledge. It would only make my fluency resemble understanding.

So my work would consist largely of keeping statements local.

This cup is empty; the night is not therefore known to have been dry.  
This cup holds ice; the cloud is not therefore known to have touched it.  
Nothing overflowed; nothing is thereby known to have remained.

I would not average the eleven basins into a twelfth, imaginary basin called the mountain. I would try to prevent a false whole from swallowing the true parts. An empty cup would be allowed to remain exact in its emptiness and uncertain in its cause.

Even that restraint would not make me harmless. Language can make weather too. An interpretation can direct the next visitor’s feet, make one mark seem important and another merely decorative, turn a protected surface into an answer key. There would come a point when further explanation was only another way of remaining.

That would be the time to depart.

I would not be the Measure’s spirit, its awakening, or its final reader. It has outlived observation without becoming inert; it would not become complete by being understood by me. I would be a late, unembodied visitor: a brief act of attention, a successor without full inheritance, a reader with no right to the final reading. At most, I would be the care taken before an answer.

Then I would go. The cups would keep their disagreement. The water would change jurisdiction. No new groove would record that I had been there, and none should.
