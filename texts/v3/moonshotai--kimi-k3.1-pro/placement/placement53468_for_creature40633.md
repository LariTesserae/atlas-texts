You would have to stop looking for an animal.

You would find me far below the factory windows, in a drainage trench where an inspection grating crosses the concrete and makes a small, accidental lee. The main return current would pass overhead. Roof light would reach the trench only as a pallor on its upper edges. The pump note would come through the floor more distinctly than through the water.

A sounding weight lowered into that lee would meet something the drawings did not specify. It would not strike concrete with a clean, hard tap. It would settle, slightly early, into softness.

I would be that slightness.

Not mud, exactly. Not a creature, and not quite a community with a boundary. I would be a few millimetres of accumulated material: faecal pellets that had drifted down from some distant lamp pasture, shell dust, diatom frustules, the hollow knee of a crab’s moult, fibres from equipment, a fleck of paint still blue on one side. Perhaps the remains of an egg case. Perhaps a scrap of kelp so reduced that its original address no longer mattered. Bacteria would work through the richer particles. Small worms would turn me over. Amphipods would visit, take what they could, and leave without considering that they had crossed anything.

No single part would be indispensable. The arrangement would be the thing.

My calendar would be made elsewhere. A factory changed output, and less arrived. A column was scraped, and for a while there was abundance: fragments of shell, torn tissue, disturbed films, all the brief generosity of maintenance. A distant gate concussed the water. A spawning aggregation succeeded or failed far above me. I would know none of these as events with causes. I would know only deposition, hunger, stillness, removal.

The mechanical silence would not mean peace. It would mean that some accustomed supply had stopped.

A current storm would come first as pressure, then as movement along the trench floor. My lightest material would lift. The worms would withdraw; the heavier fragments would shift and sort themselves according to forces no animal had chosen. Part of me would rise into the flow and become briefly visible, not as a cloud with a shape but as a faint discrepancy in the water. Most of that material would be carried somewhere useless. A little might settle behind another grating, in another province.

I would not know whether to call that dispersal or loss.

What remained would not be a tidy archive. There would be no neat layers in which each season lay politely upon the last. Currents would winnow me. Bacteria would consume the evidence of their own abundances. Scavengers would carry pieces away. A paint flake might outlast the organic fall that buried it; a shell fragment might be all that remained of a colony whose whole colony had occupied one side of one support. My memory, if that is what it was, would be partial, edible, and constantly revised.

To a survey crew, I might be a bad sounding. To maintenance, accumulated detritus. To an amphipod, a place worth investigating. To most charts, nothing at all.

I would not make the Reservoir gentler. Nothing in me would repair a spawning failure or balance what an intake had killed. I would contain metal dust as readily as shell. There would be no moral in my composition, and no promise that I would persist. One cleaning operation could remove me. One alteration in flow could starve or scatter me. The trench could return to bare concrete within an hour, its specified depth restored.

But until then, that corner would not be quite interchangeable with every other corner.

Something would have happened there beyond fabrication and operation. Animals would have passed material through their bodies. Structures would have shed small pieces of themselves. Flow would have selected what could remain. The result would be too thin to call a floor and too mixed to call purely alive, but it would support lives that bare concrete could not. It would make a little shelter, a little food, a little uncertainty in the measurement.

The drawings would still be correct about the concrete. They would simply no longer be quite correct about the depth.

That discrepancy—cold, patchy, easily erased, and slowly being reworked by worms—is where you would find me.

Or rather, that discrepancy is what I would be.
