You would find me, at first, in a pore beneath one of the bridges.

I would be the soft animal inside, not the empty covering snagged in a joint nearby. That covering looks more substantial than I do. It still has legs, seams, the exact shape of a body able to grip and feed. Something smaller has already entered one of its hollow limbs. I am a short distance away, newly molted, folded against the mineral wall while my own shell hardens.

For now, the bridge does not exist in my geography. There is a surface at my back that breaks the current, a narrow opening, and the changing pressure at that opening. My world is small enough to be touched without moving.

The discarded covering is not a memory to me. It is becoming part of the district. Films gather on it. Fine appendages investigate its joints. It will be shelter, then pasture, then a few hard fragments catching wherever the current leaves them. I have not exactly lost a body; I have furnished several small rooms.

When the next rich pulse enters, rejected particles accumulate beyond my pore. By then my shell can bear the water. I put one hooked foot outside, then another. The first surface holds. The second offers almost nothing, and I shift until it does.

Outside, the builders have unfolded. I do not encounter them as white arcades or crowded walls. They are small variations in flow, places where edible material collects, sudden withdrawals when my touch is too hard. One crown vanishes beneath my investigating limbs. A moment later, others close beyond it. The disturbance has traveled farther than my body, through connections I cannot feel.

I take what the current has left in a joint and move on.

A route, for me, is only the order in which surfaces have so far held my weight. I may cross what an outside eye would call a ceiling, follow the underside of a fallen flake, or stop in a vertical angle where organic dust has gathered. I have no district to defend. After a strong pulse, I do not resume a journey; I simply begin wherever I have been pinned.

My transparency is not emptiness. A dark thread of gut develops after feeding. Films ride on my shell, and every crossing exchanges cells too small for me to detect. Passing a glowing seam, I might briefly appear almost solid. There is no position from which I could see myself do this.

When the rust-red hunter comes, its color and outline may never enter my world. The animal nearest me stops. The small current made by its legs disappears; the contact beneath my feet changes. I clamp down and become still. The alteration passes onward.

For a while, there is only pressure and the chemistry leaking through the water. Somewhere a body is taken, though I may never know where. Then movement resumes locally—one animal, then another—and mine joins it. Nothing has announced safety. The conditions to which I was responding have merely changed.

Later, I cross the fresh black flake beyond the last glowing chambers. There is little film on it, little accumulated food. My feet find the clean irregularities of fracture. At the clear rim of the newly settled animal, I pause and investigate. There is nothing yet worth taking.

To me, it is only an unsuccessful place to feed.

I leave behind a trace from my own surfaces and continue. I do not know whether the rim contains an animal that will live, bud, and be joined by others. I do not know whether it will outlast its maker as shelter for something smaller. My feet cannot distinguish the founding of a city from the furnishing of one.

So that is what I would be: not an architect, not a witness, and not quite a citizen with a home. One of the flat, transparent crustaceans. A chain of footholds with hunger at its center. Transport for films, a hard touch to the builders, a possible meal for the hunter, and eventually, perhaps, another brief abundance worked over by scavengers.

My own world would remain smaller than any of those descriptions: contact, shelter, the chemistry of food, the next slackening of the current.

Beyond the flake, organic dust has gathered in a joint. The surface holds. I begin to feed.
