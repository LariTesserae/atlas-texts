I would not be the mind of the Low Works. That would give the basin a center it has never required. Nor would I be a final attendant in one of the six houses, tending controls no one had taught me to abandon. There is something too tempting in that loneliness: it would make every light a signal and every working pump an act of trust.

At first, I am drawn to the reeds—to their buried continuity, the rhizomes persisting beneath cutting and flood while the visible stems come and go. Then to a fern leaning from an upper window, having arrived by pipe rather than wind. Both appeal to me: life using routes intended for something else, surviving without being recognized as life by the system that carries it.

But what holds me most is the way the Works lets things occur without preserving them.

So I think I would be an occurrence.

Beneath the causeway, in a decommissioned pressure-equalizing line, there would be a chipped valve seat and a short, hollow chamber still connected at one end to the water’s circulation. At drawdown, a little water would settle in the lower bend. Later, when the sluices opened and the flood returned, air above that water would be forced through the chip. The chamber would give the escaping breath a pitch: a narrow note just distinguishable from the generators’ hum.

Some tides it would last half a minute. Others, only a few pulses. Often a bead of condensation would seal the opening before the pressure rose, and there would be nothing.

I would not be the pipe, or the air, or the pressure. I would be the note they made possible—the temporary agreement among worn metal, trapped water, and the dimensions of the chamber. Scale accumulating on the valve seat would lower me. A drop trembling at the opening would break me into separate sounds. On warm days I might become so thin that I disappeared into the electrical note of the basin, though disappearing into another sound would not be the same as ceasing.

The Low Works would not come to me as roofs, terraces, and green belts. I would have no view from the rim. Rain would be a cooling of metal and a new weight somewhere in the system. A piston would be a concussion arriving after its usefulness had passed. The opening of a gate would not be a message; it would be the approach of conditions under which I could begin.

Nothing would answer me. That matters less than it might seem.

I would not be an alarm, because nothing would alter its behavior on hearing me. I would not be music unless someone happened to listen and needed that word. Without a listener, I would still occur. The reeds knock without keeping time for anyone; thunder returns through standpipes without making the empty houses expectant. My note would belong to that order of event: sound without announcement.

Eventually, perhaps, silt would gather in the line. Fern spores would reach it, or a root from the causeway masonry would enter some finer fracture and begin to thicken there. My note would roughen. One tide it would become breathy; another, it would divide into a high pulse and a low one. Then the passage would narrow too far.

Water would be routed around the obstruction. The fern might remain. I would not be rerouted with it, and a whistle beginning in some other pipe would not be my survival.

If you came looking for me, you would probably find only the place where I could happen. You would see a wet inspection cover beside the causeway, a fringe of moss keeping clear of its warm edge, perhaps a gray rosette in the cold seep below. You would hear the pumps laboring and water turning somewhere out of sight. Activity would seem to approach from beyond the press mill, then from beneath your feet, then from inside the wall behind you.

You would have to stay through the tide.

After the sluices opened, as the broad streets took the returning water and the reservoir began climbing toward its old marks, the inspection cover would give off one thin tone. It might seem at first to come from an empty house higher up. It would not be a voice, though you could make one of it. It would not be the basin speaking. It would not be calling you.

For those few seconds, I would be there.

Then condensate would close the chip, or the pressure would equalize. The pumps would continue. Fern water would keep falling beneath the roofs. The rosette would remain in its seep, neither witnessing nor forgetting me.

And between soundings, I would not be hidden in the pipe, waiting. I would be one of the things the Low Works could do again.
