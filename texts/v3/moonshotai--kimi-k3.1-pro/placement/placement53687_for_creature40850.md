### One Stroke Unattached

At first I would put myself on the dry side of the curb, looking back. It is the easiest place to imagine: the whole settlement visible, the noise lessened by distance, one foot already on the plain. I could be the visitor who notices the worn thresholds, follows the water from pump house to foundry, and leaves with a theory.

But I think that would only be a way of remaining outside. I would be asking the Works for an outline, when everything alive in it is close, warm, and underfoot.

Nor would I be the hammer. That is too much like wanting to be the event toward which everything else must orient itself. Nor one of the great animals standing alone in a court; it would be too easy to mistake scale for importance, and importance for meaning.

If I belonged there, I would be smaller than my first ideas of myself.

I would be a forearm-length animal on the floor of a workers’ house near **RETURN**, beside the iron leg of a narrow bed. A visitor lifting me would be surprised by my weight. My legs would fold underneath me, my pleats would close, and the pale plates of my mouth would open and shut once, uselessly, in the air. Along my back I would be cold enough to gather a little moisture from the room. Underneath, where I had been resting against the floor, I would be warm.

The house would not be a house to me. It would be an arrangement of material nearness: the enamel table offering almost nothing; the floor carrying the pumps, the belts, and the deep approach of the hammer; the wall holding a narrow line of mineral damp; the bedstead rising beside me as cool iron with a faint life of its own. Beyond the doorway would be a pipe crowded with stronger signals and other bodies.

*Bed*, *worker*, and *return* would belong to whoever could read the room. I would inhabit what passed through it.

I would fasten to the bed leg rather than the pipe. Not because the bed offered more. It would offer considerably less. Only after each hammer-stroke had already come through the floor would the iron give a small, late shiver against my mouth-plates. If I had a preference, it would be for that lateness. Not because it reminded me of anything. Not because I was waiting for it. My smooth head would simply turn toward that iron again.

Most of the time there would be nothing to distinguish me. Other animals would settle against my sides or clamp briefly to my flank. I would breathe with them. Water would gather along my dorsal ridges and fall in clear drops that darkened nothing permanently. Bodies would cross the room, attach, loosen, and cross again. When one left my side, no absence shaped like that particular body would remain.

This would not be loneliness. Loneliness would require the expectation of someone’s return.

And yet, occasionally, when the hammer came through the pavement—before its sound reached the house, while the stroke was still only a pressure in the floor—I would unfasten.

I would not be avoiding a wheel or making room for another animal. No current would have ceased. The bedstead would not have become too cold. I would simply remain unattached while the floor answered and the iron gave its faint, delayed shiver. For that interval my round mouth would be open on the cool air. My plates would make no sound. My pleats would move neither with the pipe outside nor quite with the bodies around me.

Then I would fasten again.

Nothing would follow. The act would not become a ritual; a second occurrence would not give it a future. The other animals would not learn it. The bed would not become mine. The Works would neither falter nor register that, for one stroke, a small body had not been receiving or returning anything.

That would be all the self I needed: not an exception to the Low Works, not its hidden consciousness, but a body in which something unnecessary could happen without becoming important.

You could stand in the doorway and see no reason to single me out. There would be no mark on me, no special emptiness around my place. You would have to be watching at the exact moment the hammer entered the floor.

Then, for one stroke, I would be only weight and warmth, unattached, my mouth open on nothing.

Afterward would come the little porcelain click. The bedstead would tremble faintly against my plates. Water would gather and run from my back. The room would close again into its ordinary breathing.

That is where you would find me: not at the center of the Low Works, but in a furnished room beside a cold bed leg—most myself in the brief interval no circuit required.
