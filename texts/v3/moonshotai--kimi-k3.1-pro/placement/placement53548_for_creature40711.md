I would begin, I think, by wanting one of the lights.

Not because it would show me anything. It would not. But a small blue-green point seems at first like a bearable form of existence: an exact place, a little moisture, a long patience. Then I would have to admit what the light was for. I would not be illuminating the chamber. I would be hanging my hunger in the air.

The roost would offer the opposite temptation: to become one body among hundreds of thousands, to let shared warmth and wing-noise replace the boundaries of a self. There would be safety in that, and perhaps a kind of mercy. But the part of the Hollows that draws me most is neither the light nor the multitude. It is the dry interval between addresses, where a track can be the whole evidence of a life and a sound may have travelled farther than any body nearby.

I would not want to be only a sound, though. Sound is spared too much. It does not have to eat, or choose a ceiling, or find its way back.

So I would need a body. A small one. I think I would be a long-eared gleaner, one of the solitary bats hanging near the margins of a roost.

Once I know that, the rest becomes specific.

My address is a narrow fracture beyond the densest part of a smaller colony. The stone is cool and close. My wings fold around a little warmth of my own, and no other body presses against me. There is no fern here, no pool, no view. Other solitary animals occupy separate fissures, near enough to be present and far enough to remain unnoticed.

My solitude is not a philosophy. It is simply the shape of my caution.

Farther along the ceiling, the multitude shifts. The rustle becomes wing-hiss; the hall takes hold of it and multiplies it until rain seems to be arriving from several directions. Nothing gets wet. I listen with my feet still gripping the stone, and for a moment the size of the chamber exists for me more clearly than any visible chamber could.

I have no map of the Hollows. A map suggests too comprehensive a view, and this place offers none. I have expectations instead.

A call returns quickly from a low roof. In a great hall, it returns late and changed. A side fracture interrupts an echo I had expected to continue. The roof opening is a little grayness and a different smell at the end of a familiar passage. Distance is not measured in kilometres. It is measured by what happens to my voice after it leaves me.

The Hollows never tell me their shape all at once. They tell me enough for the next few wingbeats.

When I go out to feed, and when I return with food inside my body, I do not know myself as a provider. Some of what I carry will fall from me. Some will remain until my body falls. If that happens beneath a roost, I will disappear into the work of fungi, larvae, mites, and beetles. If it happens on bare dust, I may make a small, temporary address. Animals will come while there is something to come to. Then the passage will be vacant again.

That would not be a legacy. It would be the local economy continuing without consulting me.

Nothing I do enlarges my island. I do not improve my fracture or wear a route into the air. I am a route without becoming a path: a pattern of departures and returns held in one small body, leaving no mark that another animal need follow.

There are satisfactions, but they are exact rather than grand. An insect taken from a leaf beyond the opening. A clean landing on stone. The warm, dry smell of the colony reaching me without my having to enter its crush. And, after a long vacant passage, an echo returning in precisely the shape I expected.

That last is the closest thing I have to home. Not the fracture alone, but the confirmation that the world is still arranged so that I can return to it.

I would not be the Hollows’ spirit, or their memory, or the consciousness they were presumed to lack. Those are all ways of making a place need me. The Hollows do not need me. I would belong here without becoming necessary, as almost everything else does.

For now, the multitude shifts again. The dry hall fills with the sound of rain. I loosen my hold and drop into darkness.

My first call goes out. For an instant there is no answer I can use. Then: low stone above, open distance ahead, an interruption on the left where the passage turns.

I fly toward the interruption.

The next distinction is enough.
