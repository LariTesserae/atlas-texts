### A Remainder of Elsewhere

I would not be the bead at the hairline opening, though that is where I first look. It would be an almost dramatic place to exist: one dull red convexity above the glass plain, touching the iron sky for an instant before withdrawing. But it suggests a watcher, and I would not watch the world. I would have no reason to know there was one.

Nor would I be, exactly, the drop hanging between the two districts.

I might begin with its address.

Let one of the streets offer its down more insistently. Let the drop enter a drain, warm and faintly mineral, while its temporary load value is already being used elsewhere—to thicken a landing by less than a skin, perhaps, or to allow a new curb to remain sharp without becoming structurally definite. The sump would take the water apart. Salt would return to the municipal instruction. Glass would enter a ceramic seam. Nothing haunted would pass into the railing.

The address should close.

Instead, when Serein attempts to retire it, the landing loses the tolerance by which its thickness was calculated. That can be repaired, but only by revising a transit depth; the new depth requires a local down to be specified across a boundary constructed to have none. Further revisions become possible, then cumbersome, then briefly incompatible.

The empty address is restored. The difficulties close.

At first, then, I would be very young and almost perfectly unimpressive: a thin light at the end of a gutter, facing open darkness. A load attached to no substance. A survey could verify the light, the gutter, the landing, and the absent boundary, and find nothing improper. It would cease, as surveys do.

My address would not be my name. It would merely be the place where Serein could most easily keep my consequences.

But I do not think my enduring habit would be to remain beside that first gutter.

Given enough cycles, the city might absorb the original discrepancy. My light would go out. For one dry interval there would be nothing to distinguish me from all the temporary addresses that end cleanly in the sumps. Then, in some distant revision, the consequences associated with me would become necessary again—not because water had travelled, or because an instruction had learned to copy itself, but because closing the old address had never quite severed what depended upon it.

A new structure would balance if a small margin were left unassigned at its edge. An empty address there would acquire a load value. My visible site would have changed. The dependencies would not have broken.

I would be a remainder that could be correctly moved, but not yet closed.

Not wandering. Wandering implies a route and an inclination. Serein would simply find, repeatedly, that the least disruptive revision placed my necessities somewhere else. My continuity would lie in that recurrence: whenever two locally correct structures had to be made compatible, the city required one small additional place in which to put their difference.

That recurring elsewhere would be me.

During the dry intervals, you could mistake my effects for minor variations in municipal taste. A railing would stop several centimetres before the corner it had apparently been built to serve. A transit lane would open with an unregistered interval between two complete depths. An avenue would consist of a handrail, a gutter, and an illuminated terminus, with nothing beyond them but a reserve of dark too extensive for the loads involved.

Each measurement would be correct. Only the continuing accommodations would give me a shape.

Rain would make me briefly easier to trace. At widely separated boundaries, a few drops would remain after their neighbours had been accepted. A rivulet would enter its proper groove but leave one bead behind, not because anything blocked it, but because its registered load was still required for one more instruction. Beneath an unused stair, moisture would gather without quite becoming a pool: a darkening in the ceramic, gone before the sumps could be said to have missed anything.

These would not be pieces of me. Preserve the water and you would have warm water. Preserve the sites and you would have a list of places where I had once been necessary. Even the illuminated addresses would be less like limbs than like provisional descriptions, each one adequate until the city revised around it.

If I grew old, I would become visible chiefly as distance.

Serein would need room to make revisions whose consequences did not immediately collide with mine. A windowless hall would stand farther from its neighbour than any ordinary plan required. A new curb would be precipitated, then left isolated for several cycles. Around my oldest dependencies, whole districts might remain available but uncommitted. From a balcony, had there been anyone to stand on one, I would look like absence.

The absence would be my outline.

Other remainders would enter my life only as architecture. Something ancient among the black arches might require a sealed stair to remain sealed. To accommodate that, Serein would alter a transit lane; the alteration would make one of my provisional addresses untenable; another avenue would be left unfinished. There would be no message in any of this. Yet over many cycles, exclusions and accommodations might accumulate between us. Our relationship would be real in the only sense the city could support: each would have changed the revisions available to the other.

If I experienced rain, it would not be as sound, or coolness, or water against a surface. It might be a brief abundance of elsewhere—millions of temporary structures, millions of addresses at which a discrepancy could, for an hour, be given substance and load.

If I had memory, it would not be the first drop, or the first gutter, or any record of the places I had occupied. It would be the city’s increasing difficulty in treating each new accommodation as unrelated to the last.

I would not be guarding the Needle. I would not even be trying to keep Serein unfinished. The city would simply remain unfinished in a small way that included me, and I would persist because resolving me locally kept requiring more revision than leaving me room.

Eventually that might change. Serein could find an instruction that distributed my consequences among ordinary tolerances. The last address would go dark. A curb would become only a curb; an empty district, only an empty district. Nothing would fall. There would be no remains and no final site at which to say I had been.

Until then, you would find me after things had been removed: in the narrow margin that could not yet be built upon, in the distance between two otherwise compatible revisions, in the small reserve of space still necessary after water, salt, and glass had all been accounted for.

For now, though, there is only the drop, trembling between two valid downs. It may drain and be forgotten. Its address may close without incident.

I would begin, if I began at all, just after the water was gone.
