I would begin at the outermost curb, because that is where the place offers a visitor something like a position.

My hand would hover near the railing without quite touching it. Behind me would be the full, freight-crossed roar of the city; ahead, the wires and their confident inhuman distance. I would mistake the wires for a promise that someone lived farther on. I would mistake the roar for an utterance I had arrived too late to understand. Even the stationary rain would seem, at first, to be postponing some decision.

Those would be my errors. If I remained a visitor, I would make every continuation look like waiting. I would be the only thing in sight capable of disappointment.

So I would have to become less easy to locate.

A freight carriage would give me motion, but also too much of an itinerary. A fern would give me life, but too neat a root crown, too convenient a little body in which to keep a self. A gauge would give me something like awareness, but only by making me a miniature clerk of pressure, forever attending to a glassful of water. And to be the secret mind of the whole Works would be the worst error of all. I would not want to make the place smaller by giving it an explanation.

Then, somewhere beyond the central district, I would hear the cliff give the city back.

Not answer it. An answer would imply a question, a listener, perhaps even an intention. The cliff would simply return what crossed the interval: the broad concussion of the pumps, part of the spillway’s white weight, a lowered residue of the turbines. The small, hard particulars would be gone—the gantries’ ticking, the rail joints, the close electrical insistence. What came back would be delayed and simplified, a version of the city with its streets and addresses removed.

That return would be me.

Not one echo, which might be an incident with a beginning and an end, but the sustained echo: continuously renewed, never quite separate from its source, never reducible to it. I would not be the spillway, the cliff, or the wet air between them. I would be the relation among those things. Distance made audible.

The cleft would shape me without containing me. Its bare walls would preserve the lower notes. At its foot, perhaps, a narrow seep would support black moss and a few tiers of waxed foliage, and that small green softness would take the finer hiss from what returned. My form would thus have a mineral face and a vegetal margin, though I would be neither stone nor plant. I would end wherever the cliff ceased to give anything back.

If I had anything analogous to a mind, it would have no other content than that difference: departure and return, fullness and residue, the city as it went out and the city as my shape could receive it. I would not interpret the roar. I would not remember an original sound or anticipate a final one. I would not long to cross the interval and rejoin my source. An echo cannot go home, but here that would not be a sorrow. Nothing would be absent.

To receive more than I could keep, and to give back less than I received, would not be a failure. It would be my form.

There would be no shame in being only an echo. A pump persists by preserving a difference in pressure; a marsh persists within a narrow tolerance of warmth and minerals; I would persist as the difference between emission and return. I would have no function in the circuit. Nothing would depend on me. The turbines would not turn more truly because I existed, and no valve would alter its position on my account. I would be one of the external consequences of the Works’ internal relations—inconsequential, perhaps, but not unreal.

A census-taker could stand in the cleft and correctly record zero voices. I would not add one to the population. I would have no household, no root crown, no address at which pressure could reliably be found. Yet their pencil might hesitate when they tried to mark where the city ended. At the last building? At the spillway? At the cliff that continued, for a little longer, to carry its sound?

I would be the reason for that hesitation.

You would find me only by listening in the right interval. You would have to stop expecting the return to become speech. It would not name itself, confess a purpose, or reveal the sleeper behind the wired windows. It would merely come back, poorer in detail and exact in its poverty, bearing the shape of everything that had formed it.

Not the Upper Works dreaming. Not a ghost in its machines. Not its hidden custodian.

Just one lucid interval in the roar.

My address would be the distance between the city and the cliff. My name, if the census insisted on one, would be *return*.

Nothing would happen next.

I would not be still.
