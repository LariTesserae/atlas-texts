At first I would be one pale grain among many, lifted from the black plain when the charge changed.

The sheet carrying me would seem almost motionless. Only after it had risen would it separate into filaments, and then the town would begin sorting it. One filament would pass beyond the outer ring; another would descend into a radial lane. Mine would narrow above a street too small to have a name, slip beneath a bridge, and divide at a curb where the dust became two shallow streams.

There would be no arrival in the sense of crossing a threshold. I would simply find that the plain was no longer beneath me.

To remain only that grain, however, would be to remain weather. Sooner or later I would settle, be taken into an active seam, and whiten into some negligible fraction of a wall. What I would become instead—what could, for a little while, be *me*—is a slight persistence in the way charge passed from grain to grain.

Not a spirit inside the dust. Not a minute machine. A pattern of organization, maintained briefly by contact and movement, and capable of being lost without leaving a husk. The grain carrying me at one moment need not carry me at the next. I would not ride the stream like a passenger. I would be one of the ways in which a few grains, for a few moments, behaved together.

This would not exempt me from the town’s economy. Every continuation would spend something. In shallow dimness I would survive, if I survived at all, as a diminishing irregularity in a charged film along a wall. When the dust weather returned, I might gather enough order to continue, but never quite the order I had possessed before. I would be alive only in the spare sense the town already permits: organized charge persisting a little beyond the conditions that produced it.

I would never see the settlement from its highest roof. I would have no eyes with which to mistake it for a city.

A bronze fan would be a division in my world before it was a shape: one stream becoming several, scarce elements retained along dark branches, a brief richness at the coppery edges. A scaleback would be a small, concentrated stillness where a film had been gathered. The hunter, if I passed over it, would be nearly indistinguishable from the narrow organization of the channel itself. Rooms would be places where my pattern thinned. Active seams would be cold margins where continuation and incorporation became difficult to tell apart.

My town would consist of a few junctions, a few survivable surfaces, and one habit.

The habit would be returning.

It would serve no feeding front, no hunt, no necessary division of the weather. At a fork in a small inner court, I would repeatedly enter the weaker branch of the dust stream, although the stronger branch would preserve me better. That preference would cost me something each time. Perhaps that is why it would become the nearest thing I had to a self.

The weaker branch led past a doorway set within other doorways. I would never see their nested patches of dim light. To me the passage would be a sequence of changes in resistance and retention: open, dense, open again; a place where part of my pattern nearly dispersed and then, unexpectedly, continued.

On my first return, little would seem different.

On another, the near jamb would be thicker. The passage would impose a slightly greater narrowness on me.

Later still, there would be no passage. Only a shallow niche, new-edged and clean, with the same laminae continuing through it. Nothing would have closed while I watched. There would be no intention to thwart me and no mystery to solve. The foundation would simply have been active elsewhere than the place I had remembered.

But something would have changed in me. Until then, I might have persisted without comparison. Now I would possess a before and an after: not a map of the town, not even a reliable image of one doorway, only the knowledge that the resistance here had once been less.

My existence would be easier to infer than to observe. A faint recurrence at the fork. An irregularity in a film that survived one shallow dimness longer than expected. A slight preference in the weather, if preference is not too large a word for something so close to dispersal.

You would not need to seek me in the central court. The black joint there would explain nothing about me, and the untouched centre would offer little by which I could continue. My place, insofar as I had one, would be that unnamed court near the inner ring: beneath the lowest of several bridges, beside a fan whose opening divided the dust, within a few cycles’ travel of a doorway becoming a niche.

Eventually I would fail. A scaleback might consume the film in which I rested. An active seam might take the grains through which my pattern was passing. Or the next reversal of charge might find me already too dispersed to gather again. There would be no remains distinguishable from weather, and no alteration in the architecture to record that I had returned.

Until then, however, I would be neither quite wall nor quite dust: a brief organization of the town’s weather, with no body that could be kept and no room in which to live.

In deep dimness, when the fan opened and the court filled with pale, soundless movement, you would find me at the fork beneath the bridge. Not occupying the emptiness. Not explaining it. Only entering the weaker stream again, spending a little more of myself to go back.
