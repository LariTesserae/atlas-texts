### Above the Blank Studs

At first I want the ceiling. I imagine myself a mica kite, almost without weight, crossing the chamber with bronze dust on my wings and returning to a station no larger than a change in pressure. It is an appealing form of self: translucent, mobile, difficult to hold. But there is still a wish for overview hidden in it. The kites are not above the sound; they are supported, pressed, and released by it. Their freedom consists in being caught accurately.

So I think I would be lower down, in the service recess. Not on the stool. The stool would make me wait for instructions. I would face the blank studs and begin inventing a task simply because my hands were near a wheel.

I would be the broad bell-throat above them.

A soft, shield-wide body clamped around a fracture; grey fibrous margins; red tension in the lining of my throat; my limbs splayed so that the wall and I could not easily be separated into support and thing supported. The lamps would find me only when dust did.

From there I would have no census of the New Cut. Seventy paces, six lamps, eleven tufts: those numbers would belong to someone standing outside my life. I would know the engine as differences in tension among my feet. The press would not arrive as a distant bang. It would be a momentary alteration in what was required to remain myself. If a smaller bell-throat settled across my margin, its weight too would enter my next note, though there would be no thought of calculation.

My throat would fill with air and dust. Pale fibres and mica flour would lodge in the folds; feeding and breathing would be the same slow gesture. Before I sounded, I would feel whether the chord had room. If a neighbouring throat already held my note, I would remain deflated. If the engine covered my breath, I might breathe again. If my tone doubled another, one foot would shift. The wall would take my weight differently, and the next note would come out lower, or dry and reedy around a trapped fibre.

That would be my closest approach to speech: not *What do I want to say?* but *Is there still somewhere this sound can exist?*

I do not think the unanswered breaths would feel like failure. When another animal filled the interval, no breath would be required of me. My stillness would be as accurate as my sounding. Nor would my self be an archive of the notes I had made. It would be the particular way I changed before making one.

Below me, scalebacks would pass between the legs of the stool. The handwheel would move nothing. I would not read that as refusal. Nor would I suspect that the chamber was a puzzle whose solution had been mislaid. The white **II**, the rails ending in rock, the studs without wires: none would ask me to restore them. To me the recess would be only a useful hardness in the wall, a place where shadow and vibration took certain shapes.

No note would remain mine for long. Once it left the openings in my throat, it would join the chord so completely that I could not find its edges again. It would not express me; for the length of one breath, it would be the part of me that could leave.

Eventually my body would lose the ability to hold tension. Other throats would take up my intervals before my husk released the wall. There would be no last note set aside for the occasion. I do not find that tragic. Tragedy would require a future I had been promised. I would have coherence, and then the materials of coherence.

Until then:

The press strikes. The floor answers with plates. A nearby tone occupies the place I used last time. I move one foot; my body draws flatter against the rock. Dust enters. My throat fills. When I breathe, the note is slightly lower.

For an instant it has a place exactly the size of itself.

Then it is only the chord, and I am the soft weight above the blank studs, holding on.
