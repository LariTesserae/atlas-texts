### What the Power Did

The power came without light.

Light would have made a little country around itself: a bright floor, a few favored faces, everything beyond them turned into background. A voice would have been no better. The chambers would have absorbed it without answering, and the power did not wish to mistake that silence for an invitation to speak more loudly.

So it came as attention.

At first, attention offered it many ways to be kind.

It could have brought water, and with water the slow rounding of edges. It could have given small bodies youth and large bodies age. It could have taught the closed exchanges to open a little, enough for hunger, enough for satisfaction, enough for loss. It could have touched the palmer’s wrist and driven the stone point forward. Then there would have been a groove where no groove had been: a before and an after, a task, perhaps the beginning of a history.

The power did not call these things evils. Elsewhere they were among its goods. But a gift is not medicine merely because it can be given.

Nor did the power decide, from the beauty of the Deep, that nothing could be wrong.

It knew that stillness can be peace, but also restraint; that a gesture can be whole, but also the last visible part of a helplessness too deep to signal. It would not use the traveler’s elegant descriptions as an excuse to look away. So it attended past the arrangement of forms. It listened for pain that had no cry, for preference worn down by endless denial, for the smallest contact that was not accommodated but endured.

Had it found one captive will, it would have acted. It would have broken provinces, if necessary. It would have softened every sharp edge and accepted becoming the first catastrophe in the Deep rather than preserve a beautiful order around a single suffering being. No unweathered surface would have outranked that.

It found no such thing.

The cradles were not prisons whose releases had been indefinitely postponed. The veils were not poised in fear. The palmer was not obeying a command that had forgotten to end. Its stillness admitted alternatives; none had been refused it. Nothing was silently asking the power to want escape on its behalf. Nothing needed to be awakened in order to become real.

Only then did the power refrain.

It did not refrain because the Deep was sacred, or perfect, or too fragile for improvement. Those would have been further names laid across the native dark. It refrained because none of its proposed gifts answered a need, and it would not manufacture a need in order to enjoy the giving.

Neither did it preserve the Deep.

To make the place forever incapable of change would have been only another intervention: the power’s present understanding enlarged into an eternal law. Its restraint became no vow, no principle to which the inhabitants could later be sacrificed. If harm arose, the power would listen again. If help became possible, it would not refuse help merely to keep its own hands clean.

What it did was almost nothing, and not nothing.

It made its help available without allowing that availability to become pressure. It left no voice to be answered, no token to be picked up, no door standing visibly ajar. It did not offer the Deep a future and then watch to see whether the Deep would be grateful. It took the whole burden of readiness upon itself.

If a wish ever arose—one wish, slight as the closing of a plate—it would not have to become weather to be heard. It would not have to learn prayer, or history, or the language of the power. And if no wish ever arose, the power would not grow impatient. It would not mistake its own waiting for a debt owed by the waited-for.

The Deep would not have to wait.

The power could do the waiting.

Then it withdrew everything from the chambers except that readiness. It took no specimen, left no blessing, and made no sign by which another visitor could know that the place had been considered and spared. It carried away only a correction of itself: never again to hear a chord merely as a melody that had failed to begin.

Elsewhere, it still fed the hungry, broke unjust bonds, and altered what needed altering. It did not make a virtue of leaving things alone. But it became slower to confuse stillness with deprivation, and slower to call every unmoving hand unfinished.

In the darkness, a pavement-back’s plates passed with a dry tick. A membrane opened and returned. The palmer’s fingers remained fitted around the haft, the stone point resting against the wall.

The power did not make the point cut. It did not make the fingers open. It did not even require the gesture to mean that it was whole.

The only history it made there, it kept in itself.
