### One More Margin

The power did not arrive as light. The Cistern already had more light than gentleness would have known what to do with, and the green mats would only have stored the addition.

It came as attention, and for many cycles it did nothing but learn the difference between a catastrophe and a breath.

It watched the stone brighten and the basin empty. It saw the rosettes fold before the worst spray, the young grazers withdraw at a warning too slight for it to have noticed unaided, and the animal in the bowl close its tissues around the water it would not surrender. It watched the return scatter possibilities through the chamber, nearly all of them to be lost.

At first the bowl seemed the stillest thing in the room. Then the power understood that its stillness was an activity: closing, holding, opening, filtering; a continuous act performed too slowly to look like one. The animal was not merely enduring the Cistern. Its whole life was one of the Cistern’s slower consequences.

The power considered the easy mercies.

It could have silenced the ring, made the water rise gently, and given the chamber still pools and dependable shores. It could have carried the bowl away to some fertile darkness where nothing ever struck the shelf. It could have filled the spray with nourishment until the bare walls softened beneath growth.

It did none of these things. The ring’s purposes might extend beyond anything visible in the chamber, and its maintenance was not made wicked by being violent. The mats lived on its light. The bowl animal fed from its returns. Even the warning that saved the grazers was part of the machine’s indifference. To make the room gentle would not have preserved it. It would have replaced it with something easier for the power to love.

Nor did the power listen to the bowl and invent a plea from its silence. It did not call the animal lonely, or bored, or heroic. It did not give it a voice so that it could become sufficiently legible to deserve assistance.

But neither did it mistake endurance for perfection. That almost every young disc failed did not make failure sacred. The great age of the adult did not prove that nothing could be improved. Reverence, the power thought, could become only another name for refusing to help.

So it looked for an act small enough to leave the Cistern itself, and real enough to be more than admiration.

Above the principal scour, in a part of the high wall crossed by no luminous vein and required by no returning channel, a shallow concavity formed in the stone. It was not large enough to be called a bowl. Its floor was uneven; its lip turned away from the direct jets. During a return it would catch a little spray. During a lull it would hold a few drops against the rock. A transparent disc might flatten itself there and be overlooked by the water.

It was not safety. Nothing in the chamber could offer that without becoming another chamber.

The power made only one. A hundred such refuges would not have meant a hundred more lives; the spray carried no more food merely because there were more places for it to fall. Abundance imposed from outside might only have taught more mouths to wait for what could not sustain them.

Then the power waited for something that already happened from time to time.

At the next return, violent spray lifted a disc over the bowl’s rim. It turned once in the whiteness, almost indistinguishable from a drop that had momentarily kept its shape. Its path would have taken it down into the basin and from there, with the next drainage, through the walls.

The power turned aside a thread of water.

No more than that. The disc settled in the new hollow. The power did not make it attach, or feed, or grow. It did not improve its body or promise it an adulthood. It had not created a life. It had only prevented one accident from being immediately final.

When the chamber quieted, water settled to the old dark line. The last ropes broke into drops. Beneath the causeway, a transparent back separated itself cautiously from the stone. A rosette opened by less than the width of a thread. In the first bowl, the adult resumed feeding so slowly that only the water seemed to move.

The new hollow appeared empty.

The next surge might still tear its inhabitant free. It might remain there for centuries and never become more than a milky line in a film of water. The first adult would continue releasing possibilities into a room that nearly always refused them.

But now the room did not refuse quite absolutely.

The power had not stilled the storm or taught the machine to care. Against all the Cistern’s ways of losing things, it had left one more way for something to remain.
