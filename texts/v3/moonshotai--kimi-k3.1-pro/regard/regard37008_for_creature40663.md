### Until Dawn

The power came without changing the weather.

For a long time, that was all it did.

It saw the large mercies available to it. It could make the meres permanent. It could deepen the dust in the courts until soil gathered there, and summon grass, shrubs, insects, and birds until the old mineral shores disappeared. It could wake every dormant seed and cyst, repair every channel, keep every seep open. It could give the black cistern newt an unaging body and the city an eternity of intact grades.

It could even bring back keepers, or create new ones, so that no court would ever again be without an heir.

But it stayed past dry noon.

It watched a promising shower fail to wake one court while another greened. It saw a film on submerged stone dry into something like a mineral stain and then, weeks later, resume. It saw seeds refuse rain after rain, not because they were dead, but because refusal was part of their lives. It saw a shrub endure where a richer refuge would have drowned its roots, and pool animals hatch only when the water had proved, by staying, that the pool was worth hatching into.

A census taken on one day would have moved it to rescue. A census taken on another would have moved it to congratulate itself. It remained for both.

So it did not make the High Catchment bloom. Abundance would have been a kind of replacement. It did not fill the silence with voices, or call the newt lonely. That loneliness was its own sorrow, looking for somewhere to settle. It did not restore the builders merely to soothe the appearance of abandonment, nor appoint new inhabitants to spend their lives maintaining work they had not chosen.

It did not even preserve every cistern. A city kept correct by perpetual intervention would slowly become another thing: not stone keeping the grades, but power keeping the stone.

Yet, having refused the grand gestures, it did not immediately decide that nothing should be done. It did not want its restraint to become another form of self-regard. Small lives were not made painless by being parts of large processes. A dry barrier was only weather to the plateau; to the creature stopped by it, it could be the whole extent of the world.

In one covered reservoir lived a black cistern newt.

It was not the last of its kind. It was not essential to the future of the Catchment. The power did not require it to be either. It was simply there: a cold, particular life, waiting in the manner of its kind, with no prayer and no knowledge that anything was listening.

On a night of mild rain, the newt left its reservoir and followed a shaded seep. Beyond it lay wet joints, a narrow runnel, and then a stretch of stone already beginning to pale as the shower slackened. Beyond that were other cisterns. Some would be unsuitable. Some would be empty. One might hold food and refuge. The newt knew none of this, and the power did not put knowledge into it.

The rain would ordinarily have stopped before the way remained connected.

The power did not summon a storm. It did not carry the animal, command it, or choose a destination for it. It only kept that one passing shower above that one roof a little longer.

The additional rain was slight. No mere rose because of it. No channel changed its grade. But one seam stayed dark, and one thin thread of seepage continued to shine along the foot of a wall.

The newt could have turned back. For a while it did nothing visible. Then it went on.

By dawn it had reached another cistern. The power had arranged nothing there for it. There was cold water, a submerged fissure, and no promise. Whether it would remain, return, breed, or die on some later journey had not been decided. The gift was not safety. It was only that a few paces of drying stone had not been allowed to settle everything that night.

For the High Catchment itself, the power did nothing further.

It let exposed paving dry. It let one snail’s refuge seal while another ledge formed elsewhere. It left courts to silt and collapse to make its low banks of rubble. It did not promise that the meres would always be meres, or that the grades would always be grades. Its love did not require the place to remain recognizable to the one who had first loved it.

By morning, the newt’s road had vanished. There was no mark of the power’s presence, no shrine, no fresh repair, nothing a later traveler could distinguish from luck.

It had left the city’s future unwritten, and one door ajar until dawn.

Farther away, another cloud was crossing the plateau on its own.
