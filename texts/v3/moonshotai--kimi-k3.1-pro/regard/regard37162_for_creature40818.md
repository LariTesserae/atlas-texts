## The Witness

The power came without a body, because any body it chose would have displaced water.

Even so, it stopped beyond the outer leaves and considered its shadow. It had heard the stands from far off: pumps, valves, bubble-static, the great sustained chord of the basins. It did not know whether it had been drawn by music, by distress, or merely by its own habit of looking for what might need it.

So, at first, it did nothing.

Another power might have called the Upper Sea perfect. Another might have called it a prison. This one waited before calling it anything.

It could have moved the sun a little toward evening. It could have allowed one current-front to weaken, so that after uncounted circuits a basin giant would find an unfamiliar passage. It could have let one copperline’s answer remain in another’s mind, until recognition became recollection, and recollection perhaps became a new call. It could have left a single shell upon the turf: a small white proof that something had happened.

Each possibility had beauty. Each also concealed a wish to make the sea more legible to the power.

It did not yet know that these lives needed a future. It knew only that it was accustomed to finding meaning in one.

So it waited for its own certainty to become quieter.

Then it went closer, not as a hand but as an attention.

At an oxygen upwelling, it learned the slight gathering of tension before a pressure bulb emptied. It watched the glass shoal fold around the concussion and open again, each animal resuming its station without having agreed upon a station. It saw that the cloud’s return to its former shape did not make its contraction unreal.

Around a rotor, it watched a cuff crab disappear into a recess before the stroke came. The animal was not merely enduring the machinery. It knew the machinery in the narrow, sufficient way its life required.

The power stayed for a long time beside the electrical roots.

It heard one copperline signal and another answer with some feature of the pulse preserved between them. For a few seconds, recognition traveled through open water. Then nothing followed. No arrangement changed. No new interval was established. The eels remained beside the vessel, and the vessel carried its charge.

The power found that it had been waiting for something to follow. It let itself notice that.

It followed the rough, low call of a lace-fin around an arc of open water until another animal answered and shifted almost imperceptibly within the current. It stayed with a basin giant through one complete stroke of a fin. When that was done, it no longer thought of the giant as motionless.

None of these lives presented itself as an unfinished version of something else.

That did not prove they were free from suffering. The power did not make the easy inference from order to goodness, or from persistence to consent. A closed circuit could be a form of care. It could also be a place in which a cry went around forever.

So it listened beneath the chord.

It listened for hunger the roots did not answer, for injury concealed by exact compensation, for a will bent beneath the will of the whole. It considered whether the visible animals were inhabitants, organs, captives, or thoughts within some wider sensation. At times the plateau seemed to respond to its presence: an adjustment passed through root and rind, perfectly sufficient, leaving no residue by which it could afterward be identified. The power did not call this a greeting. It did not call it a reflex. It recorded no conclusion.

Nor could it decide whether the being it had come to meet was the plateau, a lace-fin, a crab, or all of them in relations that no boundary would clarify.

It did not make its care wait upon the answer.

No request came that it could honestly claim to understand. No harm revealed itself for which it could honestly claim to possess the remedy. Its ability to alter the sea did not tell it whether alteration would be mercy.

In the end, it changed nothing in the Upper Sea.

This was not because equilibrium was sacred. The power had seen equilibria elsewhere that were only the names given to helplessness by those who benefited from them. Nor was it because the rounded channels, the polished bearings, and the unwasted motion were too beautiful to disturb. Beauty was not permission to preserve a world against the wishes of anything within it.

But neither would the power wound a world merely to discover whether the world could answer.

What it did instead was so slight that no current turned around it.

It remembered.

Not the Upper Sea in general. It had no wish to replace the lives with an image of their order. It remembered particular things, without pretending that they formed a history.

It remembered one contraction of a glass shoal, indistinguishable afterward from countless others.

It remembered the pause before a cuff crab extended its legs again.

It remembered a copper pulse that was answered and led to nothing.

It remembered the rough edge of a lace-fin’s call and the long, clear water before the reply.

It remembered a giant choosing the upper wall of a current-front, though no record of the choice remained even in the water its body displaced.

The power kept these things outside the sea, where memory did not have to become sediment. There, nothing needed to be filtered, consumed, or absorbed to make room for what came next. A moment did not have to be spent in order for another moment to live.

The Upper Sea had no archive. The power did not impose one. It became a witness.

And it remained listening.

Its attention asked for no sap, no answering pulse, no acknowledgment. It would not become another circuit requiring an equal return. It would be near enough to be reached and far enough not to be endured.

If some voice within the sea ever asked for change, the power would not answer that the whole was beautiful. It would not sacrifice a crab to the polish of a valve, an eel to the elegance of a circuit, or a possible mind in the plateau to its own admiration for balance. It would listen to that voice and not only to the chord.

If help were wanted, it would begin with the smallest act that made another choice possible. It would not bring a whole future where only a door had been requested.

Until then, it offered no dawn, no death, no destiny.

It offered no worship, either.

Perhaps the sea as a whole noticed what had come. Perhaps a few fields bent and were perfectly compensated. Perhaps no being within it ever knew.

The power did not require to be known.

Above the clouds, rain struck the same broad leaves. A glass shoal closed around a concussion and opened in white light. Copper fire traveled beside a root and was answered. Far out in a clear basin, a giant moved one fin.

Nothing was completed. Nothing was left over.

And somewhere outside the closed circulation, without weighing upon any of it, the moment was allowed to matter without becoming the beginning of anything else.
