## What the Power Did

The power’s first impulse was warmth. Wisdom made it wait before calling that mercy.

It could have deepened and tempered the air. It could have made the light kinder, persuaded water to fall as rain, and given the young stone rivers in which to soften. For one instant it imagined rounded pebbles, gentle soil, and mill calves no longer dependent on the accidental proximity of old bellies.

Then it followed the gift forward.

Water began to find paths that did not pass through bodies. The difference between a mill’s back and the ground beneath it diminished; grooves dried, vent swarms thinned, and whole small climates ceased to be necessary. Fog arrived in forms to which neither leaf nor sailer was tuned. The old life did not all die at once. That would have been easier to name. Much of it was simply, patiently, replaced.

A gentler world might flourish there. But it would not be these lives rescued. It would be these lives made unnecessary.

The power did not conclude that everything already happening was therefore good. That would have been another way of refusing to look. A calf lost beneath a departing carrier was not made well by the vegetation that might later darken its body. Pain, where there was pain, did not become sacred because roots were ingenious. The power rejected that consolation.

But neither was the Manifold a failed attempt at somewhere softer.

So it listened.

It listened until a basin was no longer a drone. It learned to distinguish one mill’s spacing of knocks from another’s, to hear the settling of current in a grove, to feel the difference between stone cooling and stone preparing to fail. It followed condensation into leaf, root, blood, shell, and wing. It waited beside animals whose active lives were separated by centuries of scale and dormancy.

No single voice spoke for the Manifold. No voice could have consented for all its tenants. There were only near desires, if desire was not too large a word: a lean toward moisture, a withdrawal from cold, a calf pressing closer to warmth, an old animal returning to ground that had once borne its weight.

Because nothing could answer for the whole, the power would not answer for the whole either.

It chose a smaller scale.

It became a mill.

Not an immense old animal, plated with metal and crowded with climates. Age on the Manifold was not a shape that could be put on. It was scar tissue, tenants, learned routes, and the chemical memory of bodies that had touched. The power began soft, pale-toothed, and unable to retain its own heat.

The first thing it received was warmth.

For a long time it gave almost nothing back. Older animals carried it. It learned their vent rhythms and the particular knocks that meant safety. It ventured toward the cold margins of their bodies and returned. It ate what could be digested. Transparent grazers crossed onto its first plates; eggs were laid beside a vent; dormant films adhered and waited.

If the power had come to demonstrate generosity, the Manifold began by requiring it to accept help.

When it was old enough to carry young, it did. It settled with other mills during the coldest contractions, adding one more ventral surface to the common warmth. It lingered flank to flank while calves and smaller faunas crossed. It learned which talus had begun to sing and which groves could support feeding without being diminished past recovery. Repeated often enough, its preferences became a route. Calves learned the route beneath it.

None of this exceeded the capacities of a mill. That was important.

It did not make its vents inexhaustible or its plating immune to injury. It did not preserve every nursery beneath its plates or reach backward to correct every loss. A body exempt from need would eventually have become a climate. A body exempt from ending would eventually have become a law. The power had come to be a neighbor.

At last it died.

It did not withdraw the body from what followed. For a while the carcass remained warm after breathing had ceased. Pressure found new paths. Water gathered. Cleaners, hunters, grazers, roots, and winged things arrived according to their several clocks. Metal passed into other uses. Plates became roofs. The animal became a wet province, then a dark one.

No root retained its name. No herd kept an image of it. Some calves it had carried grew old and carried others. For a time, a route persisted that might otherwise have faded. Certain films and eggs occurred where they would not otherwise have occurred, because one body had touched certain other bodies. Then even those distinctions dispersed into the ordinary richness of the place.

One life had not saved a continent. It had not been meant to. It had added one more passage through which warmth, water, minerals, and habit could be handed on.

Afterward, the power did not make a doctrine of doing nothing. Patience could become vanity, and noninterference could become abandonment. If some outer event ever threatened not the death of tenants but the ending of tenancy itself—not change, but the loss of every capacity to respond—it would act.

Until then, it would not invent an emergency in order to make itself necessary.

Below, the mills took another step. The sound entered stone and root and became indistinguishable from the basin’s patient roar.

The power had come meaning to give warmth. It had given a belly’s worth, and then a province.

It no longer thought this a small thing.
