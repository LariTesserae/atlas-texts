### What the Power Did

The power did not bring the rain.

It could see why that might have seemed the obvious gift. The channels were waiting, the pools were scarcely more than steps wide, and after each brief storm the basin rehearsed so beautifully the sound of weather. But water sent down a channel whose lower end did not yet exist would be only a grander kind of collapse.

Nor did the power finish the banks, illuminate the whole region, or announce the purpose concealed in the gauges. It did not make the works green for their own good. It did not lift the late presence out of the sumps and give it a body more convenient for strangers to understand.

The power did not mistake what was unfinished for what had failed.

This was not because it had made a virtue of never interfering. It would have held a bank falling toward sleepers. It would have given water to the thirsty without lecturing them on the virtues of condensation. Wisdom was not fastidiousness, and restraint was not a shrine it had come to worship. But neither would it injure a living practice merely to make its own benevolence visible.

For a while, it went among the crews as a stranger.

At a kiln opening, the workers gave it safe standing room, a little water, and a rhythm to follow. They told it when not to strike before showing it how to hold a mallet. It obeyed. When an old listener, palm spread on a cooling floor, said, “I cannot tell what that was,” the power did not improve the answer.

In the red light, someone asked, not quite seriously, “Could you bring morning?”

“If morning is wanted,” the power said.

“By how many of us?” asked a line-keeper.

The power considered this. “I do not know.”

“Then there is time,” said the old listener.

The talk returned to gradients. No morning came.

Only afterward did the power listen to the basin with the whole of its attention.

It heard mauls dressing brick and the soft copper tools being reshaped between uses. It heard water gather, bead by bead, above cups that had not been moved for several workings. It heard runners ticking through clearances too narrow for fingers. It heard old survey piers answering through younger foundations, and deeper courses carrying disturbances laid down by hands no living worker remembered.

It did not discover a final chord toward which all the other sounds were struggling. The basin was not a riddle whose solution had been carelessly lost. Nor did the power draw too early a boundary around what deserved care: the crews, the runners, the presence in the returns, or some slower life arising from their relations. It listened to the place as a whole without requiring the whole to have a single purpose.

Then, in a district whose name had already changed twice, a worker struck a floor.

The near surfaces answered. A flue answered. Something farther away returned the blow in muffled pieces. The worker had already lowered the mallet when one more note arrived, from beneath a sump that had been sealed long enough for the clay around its lid to darken.

The worker touched the nearest wall and waited. After a while, the crew moved the work elsewhere.

The power remained.

It did not knock back. Even a gentle answer might have been only a more courteous second strike. It did not open the sump, or fill the darkness with sight, or call downward in a voice capable of passing through every obstruction. It did not make the presence more frequent so that its nature could be established.

It waited.

Not in the way one waits for evidence. Not in the way one waits for a door to open. It waited in such a manner that nothing was required to happen.

This was the greater part of its gift. Any power can speak and then call its patience virtue because it speaks quietly. It is harder to remain without listening for a reply as though a reply were owed.

Camps moved. A sleeping ledge became part of a foundation. A tapping signature passed from one worker to another and then went out of use. Rain altered the acoustic order and dried away. The survey line near the sealed sump bent a little farther around it, not because the power had commanded anything, but because the workers’ own judgment was already sufficient.

Occasionally a late return came. The power did not count them. It was not collecting proof.

Once, after many rains, there arrived a return with a pause the power had never heard in any worker’s signature. It did not investigate. It did not call the pause trust, or progress, or a name. Perhaps it was only water finding a new hollow. The power let it remain what it was.

That was nearly all.

No shrine was built. No gauge acquired a sacred explanation. The channels did not fill, and the runners were not rescued from their dependence on bad joints and forgotten cups. Workers continued to disagree about the purpose of the whole. Strangers were still given water before explanations, and old listeners still admitted uncertainty when uncertainty was what they heard.

If asked later what it had done, the power could truthfully have answered:

*I accepted water. I learned where not to strike. I stayed.*

Its staying was not a promise against every collapse, nor a substitute for the practiced hand testing a floor. It did not preserve the basin in incompleteness for its own contemplation. If the inhabitants ever asked for morning, or rain, or help in finishing some particular work, the question would still be open. The power had not made their unfinished condition into a law they must now obey in order to remain worthy of its admiration.

It remained simply as an attention that did not leave when nothing happened.

And so, on certain sleeping turns, when the last blows had passed from hollow to hollow and the runners had withdrawn into the walls, an entire district would fall silent. The workers would stop. No one would strike to discover why. No one would demand movement beneath the silence.

The basin remained unfinished. The late presence remained unnamed.

But the silences everyone had learned to keep were no longer kept by the inhabitants alone.

Something patient was keeping still with them.
