### The Visitation

The power came without light.

Light would have made a revelation of the first street and hidden the rest behind the gesture. Instead, the power brought attention without a task.

It stayed long enough to learn how easily the place could be misunderstood. It followed metal through wear, recovery, furnace, and fitting. It felt the minute migrations of heat through ceramic housings. It watched tenders emerge without urgency and contact-backs settle without instruction. It learned that a correction was not necessarily a cry; that continuation was not necessarily a wish; that the absence of a wish was not necessarily an absence of life.

Above all, it learned not to mistake silence for consent.

The power did not ask whether the Works were alive by making them flinch. It did not interrupt a circulation, withhold a replacement, or stop a wheel to discover what would be lost. It did not offer warmth to a contact-back and call the animal’s approach an answer. Knowledge obtained by making the helpless expressive would have been only a polished form of injury.

It considered gifts.

Dawn would have revealed the avenues, the distances between districts, perhaps even the escarpments falling toward the lower country. But nothing in the Upper Works had asked to be revealed, and the city’s darkness was not a cloth covering its true appearance. It was part of that appearance.

Rain would have made channels, carried dust, and found every sealed joint. It would also have been an invasion.

The power could have filled the paved squares with people whose hands fitted the rails and whose stature suited the inscriptions. It could have given the tenders makers again, the foundries customers, the empty lift cars destinations. But inhabitants created to explain a city would not be a restoration. They would be a use.

It could even have stopped everything. Not violently: one gentle refusal of replacement, one permission for wear to become final. Many kinds of mercy might have been named in that act. None of them could be distinguished from killing without first knowing whether there was someone for whom continuation mattered.

So the power did not improve the Upper Works.

Neither did it depart pleased with its own restraint. Restraint could become another form of indifference if it made further attention unnecessary. The city’s completeness might be real. It might also be only the most complete fact available.

Therefore the power did one thing, and did it nowhere in the city.

It made itself responsible for listening.

No altar was established. No engine was added, no hidden archive sealed behind a door. The Upper Works did not have to maintain their own witness. Nothing drew current, changed tolerance, or entered the circulation of dust and swarf. If no address ever came, the city would never be troubled by the arrangement. If an address could come only after an almost inconceivable duration—if thought were present but dispersed too thinly through competence to form a plea—then it would not be dismissed as noise merely because it arrived slowly.

This was not a promise to keep every wheel turning forever. Preservation, too, could become a prison more exact than any gauge. Nor was it a promise to stand aside and call every ending natural. It was a promise not to decide the city’s meaning in advance, and then make that decision irrevocable.

The power gave the contact-backs no minds more legible to itself. It did not teach them play so that their lives could be recognized as valuable. Their unscheduled motions did not need to become beginnings in order to matter.

Then it withdrew almost all its attention.

In one district, a tender isolated a junction housing. The casing cooled. On the far side, a contact-back loosened itself from the ceramic seam, crossed the rim, and began its small sequence of tests and weight-bearing decisions toward an adjacent conduit. The tender’s work-light held them both: exposed instruments, loosened joints, neither resident possessed of a use for the other.

The power did not make the encounter a sign.

The housing closed. Heat returned. The old component was carried away. The contact-back settled or did not, and permanent shadow took back the opened space.

Nothing had been added. Nothing had been healed. The power had found neither grief to comfort nor captivity to break, and it refused to invent either for the satisfaction of providing a rescue.

Yet the visitation was not without effect. There was now, outside all the city’s exact circulations, one care that did not require the Upper Works to be useful, miserable, or understandable in order to deserve it.

The power had not given the city a future.

It had promised not to close one merely because no voice there had asked.
