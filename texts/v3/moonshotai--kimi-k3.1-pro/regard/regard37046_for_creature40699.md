The power would not begin by giving the Level a future.

That would be the first temptation, and they would recognize it as a temptation. To bring green because their own idea of life included leaves; to admit rust, erosion, and seasonal storms because a world without them seemed unfinished; to teach the sleepers what the pumps were for; to loosen one equilibrium somewhere and watch, with the satisfaction of a spectator, as consequence became history. Any of these might make the Level more legible to a visitor. None would necessarily be kind.

Nor would the power worship the equilibrium. Quiet would not persuade them that nothing hurt. The absence of requests would not be mistaken for consent. A needle starving beneath a deck would not need to understand pressure halls in order to be trapped, and a lid cooking against a housing would not need to conceive of another world before its pain mattered.

So the power would remain for a while without improving anything.

They would let the rind divide around their shadow and close again. They would watch an old lid breathe, its scars puckering at the margins. They would see a sail compensate for a wing injured long ago, and another lie beside its wet, flightless young on an open roof. They would stand in a court where a sleeper investigated a dead companion, and they would not decide in advance whether to call the investigation grief. They would follow carrion chemistry down into the sumps and see how readily the world converted an ended life into the continuation of other lives.

None of this would indict the Level. Neither would the Level excuse it.

At last the power would choose a modest, difficult work: they would put a little give into the world’s dealings with individual lives.

Not a destiny. Not a general ascent. Slack.

A needle that had entered a gallery while smaller would find, on trying to return, that one cable tray could flex without breaking and one deck plate could lift without emptying a pressure hall. It would emerge into the warm wet with no understanding that anything had been forgiven.

A housing whose heat would have killed every lid attached to it would shed that excess before their flesh failed. The animals would still have to release their hold, still have to make the slow walk toward cooler metal. They would merely be given time in which to do it.

When renderers gathered around an animal that was immobilized but not dead, the power would lend their chemical judgment one further distinction. Not innocence, not reverence for life—only enough uncertainty to make them wait. Sometimes the animal would wake and scatter them. Sometimes it would die before they folded away. The power would not pretend that both outcomes were the same because the collection sump received them equally well.

A failed feed outlet would not be allowed to become a famine simply because famine would eventually restore the usual pressure of bodies against the court. But neither would the power pour in thoughtless abundance, leaving later animals to pay for it through crowding and competition. Where mercy required food, they would provide food. Where it required room, they would make room. Where it saved a life, they would accept responsibility for the conditions of that life rather than quietly transferring its cost to another district.

They would not heal animals back to specification.

An old sleeper would not be made young, its eye restored merely so that its body better resembled the others. A scarred lid would not have its scars smoothed away. A sail too torn to remain in the gyre might be given a sheltered roof, water, and food within reach, but it would not be repaired into usefulness and then returned to the sky as though flight were the only permissible remainder of its life.

The power would be care without restoration to type.

Nor would they try to make all appetite harmless. They would not turn eels into creatures that did not eat, needles into hunters that did not hunt, or sleepers into animals incapable of injuring one another with their weight. Some harms could be relieved without merely moving them; some could not. The power would do what could be done, and they would not call the remainder beautiful in order to spare themselves discomfort.

They would also remember.

Not as compensation. Memory would not make pain good, and no private eternity kept by the power would be used to excuse neglect in the living world. They would remember because otherwise it would be too easy, looking down from a sufficient height, to confuse replacement with renewal.

The next lid on a housing would not be the lid that had pressed margin to margin there and lost a strip of flesh. The next young sleeper in the center of a court would not be the one that had been injured at an outlet. The braid would remain a braid, but the animal swallowed within it would not, in the power’s attention, become nothing merely because the water closed without a mark.

The power would keep noLedger of usefulness. They would not rank lives by how nearly they approached affection, recognition, or anything else a visitor might find moving. A sleeper’s vigil beside a dead companion would not need to be proved grief before it deserved gentleness. Confusion would be enough. Preference would be enough. The brief completeness of an animal doing what it did would be enough.

And if any preference began to persist, the power would not erase it for the sake of the pattern.

If an animal sought repeatedly the same edge of a court, the same cooler surface, the same companion; if a route became a habit; if a habit somehow outlasted the body that had formed it—the power would not mistake this for a malfunction. They would not cultivate it, either. They would not stand over the animals hoping to be rewarded with culture, descent, or the first stirrings of an age.

Nothing would be required to become a direction in order to be worth preserving.

Eventually, from a raised service stair, there would be little to show that the power had ever attended. The pump houses would still repeat into gray distance. The same white weather would stand over the same exchanger province. Warm drops would fall into pans. Rind would thicken along the curbs, sails would turn above the digesters, and sleepers would breathe flank to flank in the roofed courts.

Here and there, an animal that would otherwise have died would still be alive. Another would have died with less pain. An old body would occupy a place where nothing was gained by hurrying it toward the sump. None of these events would accumulate into a new Level.

The output of the Level would remain the Level. But it would no longer be the only measure of what mattered within it.

Nothing was waiting. Nothing had been given a reason to wait.

The power had not given the Level a history. They had refused to make history the price of mattering.
