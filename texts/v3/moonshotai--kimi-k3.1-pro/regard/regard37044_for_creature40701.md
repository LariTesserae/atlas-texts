## What the Power Did

The power noticed the swimmer first.

That was natural. In so much stillness, a pale fin turning above the canopy could gather attention out of all proportion to its size. For an instant, the swimmer seemed to be *the* inhabitant, and everything else merely its vast green dwelling.

The power let that impression pass.

It could have given the swimmer companions. It could have made the open passes populous, filled the long silences with other bodies, and ensured that no animal crossed a saddle without the possibility of meeting another of its kind. To an outsider, this might have seemed the obvious kindness.

But the power did not know that the swimmer was lonely. It would not turn its own pang at seeing a single pale body into a command over the water. It gave the animal no companion, no voice, no larger purpose. It did not make it the last of anything, or the first. It did not even follow when the swimmer passed out of sight.

Preservation was the next possibility.

The power could have closed the deep fractures before they opened. It could have returned every escaping droplet, regulated the warmth below, and held the silver upper plain forever at its present level. The Upper Flood need never have become a memory enclosed in dry stone.

Yet that first act would have required others. A forest preserved too long would still settle; a gap held open would still alter what grew beneath it; water prevented from leaving would eventually need to be prevented from changing in other ways. To rescue the interval from its future, the power would have had to become increasingly responsible for its geology, its weather, and finally its succession. The flood would have remained visible, perhaps even beautiful, but less and less of it would have been allowed to happen without permission.

So the power did not preserve it.

Neither did it decide that everything already occurring must therefore be good. Stillness was not proof of peace, nor slowness proof that loss did not matter. Had some outside hand come to tear open the range for its own use, or to stock the flood with hungry multitudes merely to make it more eventful, the power would have resisted. Accepting a world’s own ending did not require accepting its theft.

But no such hand came.

The power remained long enough to stop waiting for something to happen.

A film spread a little farther into a fresh fracture. Beneath an old leaf, a cleaned crescent widened, then began narrowing at its oldest edge. Somewhere below the canopy, two mineral-sheathed remnants touched and made a sound too small to have a direction. A veil settled by almost nothing. Above it, the swimmer returned along another line of open water, carrying a dust so sparse that the power could not have said whether any of it would survive.

None of these events was a sign. Together they did not become a message. They were simply the life of the place, and the power allowed them to remain so.

In the end, its only work was accuracy.

It did not remember the Upper Flood as green sunlight alone. It remembered the unlit basins, the long uninhabited reaches of angular stone, and the forests without grazers, where old leaves thickened beneath uninterrupted films. It remembered that most reproductive dust traveled almost nowhere, and that rarity could be important without being tragic. It remembered the swimmer without making the swimmer stand for everything else.

When the distant drainage finally came, the power did not arrive with an ark. It did not teach the turf a dry-land form or gather a representative garden into some safer water. It did not call the ending good merely because new mountains would emerge, nor use the beauty of future impressions in stone to excuse what would be lost.

It did not hold back the water. It did not hasten the water. It did not look away.

No second flood was made in memory, where nothing could fail and no interval could close. Memory was not used as a substitute world. It was only a refusal to let the Upper Flood be reduced to its ending.

Nothing there recorded the visitation. A root entered a hairline fracture. Dust settled onto a ledge. High above the canopy, a pale fin turned once and was gone.

Nothing had been made eternal.

Nothing had been treated as negligible.
