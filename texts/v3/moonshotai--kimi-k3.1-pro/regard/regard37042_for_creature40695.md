It would not begin by improving anything.

It would not silence the roar. That might seem the purest mercy to a visitor whose skull had become a resonating chamber, but the inhabitants had not merely endured the vibration; they had formed their senses within it. A mite’s step was a slight failure of that immense continuity. A hunter’s meal, a runner’s warning, a sleeper’s safety depended on detecting such failures. To make the country quiet in the ordinary sense might bring peace to the visitor and disorientation to everything that lived there.

Nor would it pour water into the bright basins. A pool no larger than a cup was not a failed lake. The film that imprisoned one springtail also sustained others; the damp joint was a geography, not a deficiency. To make the hidden water visible would be to drown some worlds, wash out others, and demand that the survivors become grateful for a catastrophe.

It would not leave a shaped pebble beside a rockrunner. It would not teach the runners to carry, store, or inherit their tools. Their intelligence was not a stalled version of some other intelligence, waiting for permission to begin. The dropped pebble was not evidence that a mind had failed to become interesting.

For a long while, then, the power’s action would look like attention.

But attention can become another form of vanity. It can admire a balance so completely that it ceases to notice what the balance costs, and to whom.

The power would not make that mistake.

A country could remain in equilibrium while a young runner exhausted itself searching for its mother. The number of soarers could remain sufficient while one came down on cold stone and opened its wings again and again upon air that would not take them. A cup-sized pool could persist through generations while, within it, one small animal struggled against the surface that held it. Nothing in the totals answered these events. The equilibrium belonged to the country; the pain belonged to the animal.

So the power would not make a religion of leaving things untouched.

Its help would be small because its understanding was large, not because its benevolence was small.

A soarer downed but uninjured might feel the stone recede beneath it. It would be carried no farther than the warm face where rising air could receive its weight. Then the power would let go. The bird would not see a hand, hear a name, or understand that anything had been given. It would feel the current gather under its wings. It would simply live.

Such mercies would not be installed as weather. No creature would be taught to expect them. Help would not become a door through which the power entered to rule, nor would good fortune be made into evidence of virtue. There would be no chosen runners, no sacred clefts, no requirement that any animal interpret its rescue correctly.

And when a life was ending beyond any small repair, the power would not turn away merely because it had decided not to replace the whole country.

It would not tell a dying runner that its substance would be useful. That might be true, and it would be no answer to the runner. It would not call fear beautiful because scavengers would inherit the body. Nor would it describe agony as the necessary price of a balance that the dying animal had never agreed to maintain.

Pain that could still guide a body toward release belonged to the animal’s own struggle; the power would not steal urgency from life. But pain that could do nothing except continue would be taken away. The animal would not need to understand, or become grateful, or die improved. Its body would still return by the old paths. Nothing would be taken from the scavengers except an agony that had never nourished them.

Only after all this would the power do the thing no inhabitant could use and no visitor could detect.

It would remember.

Not merely the species, the food web, or the enduring pattern. It would remember the particular circuits by which one runner had crossed apparently identical stone; the pressure of a foot before drinking; the exact pebble once chosen, used, and dropped; the dark flash by which one mother had gathered her young. It would remember the first flight of a soarer that had never returned to the cavity of its birth, and the small, complete geographies of animals whose world had been the curvature of a few drops.

It would not put these memories back into the country as instruction. It would build no archive among the clefts. It would not use the dead to improve their descendants or turn their difficulties into lessons. Memory would not be offered as proof that everything was therefore all right. It would simply be the refusal to confuse a life that left no artifact with a life that had contained nothing worth keeping.

On an open dome, a runner would still stop with one foot pressed to the ground. Above it, a soarer would adjust a wing. Beneath it, mites would alter their paths, a hunter would wait with its forelegs in the water film, and white animals would turn through the dimensions of a pool.

The light would not change. Nothing would be built. No name would be cut into stone.

The country would keep its one note.

The power would keep the irregularities.
