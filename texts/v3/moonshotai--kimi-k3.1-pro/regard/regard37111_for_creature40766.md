### What the Power Did

The power came without light.

It did not descend in fire, or speak through the storm, or send a searching current through the roots. In the Vey Crown, even a question asked in current might be heard by something as a heartbeat.

So, for a long while, it only listened.

In one basin it found an old matback resting beside a colony of opened fans. The animal’s coat had acquired a garden: wine-black spores lodged among its filaments, a few pale grains moving near its feet, a thread of red cushion-root where there should have been no root at all. Its mouth hung almost above the new inner leaves.

The matback touched its lips to stone.

Nothing lifted in its whiskers. It did not feed.

The power’s first impulse was pity. It did not mistake that impulse for wisdom.

It could have altered the potential in the mat. A small thing, it thought: one meal for one old animal. But the change would travel. Valves would answer it. Storage nodules would alter their balance. Clear grains might wake into a richness that vanished before they could use it. Somewhere beneath the roots, a hunter would read a new irregularity in the circuit.

And the matback was not dying. It was waiting.

The power waited with it.

When the suitable pulse came, the animal fed. It cropped heavily and left the deep nodules untouched. Then it moved on, slowly enough that the power had to learn a new way of seeing movement.

Elsewhere, waiting was not so easy to distinguish from loss.

The power saw sails fold against the stone and never open again. It saw clear grains dry beside a lens that failed before they reached it. It found an old hunter reduced to such pallid thinness that its waiting seemed scarcely distinguishable from the absence of life. It watched reclamation begin around injuries whose owners were still trying to escape them.

It did not learn to call these things good.

Nor did it decide that, because charge and tissue continued afterward, nothing had truly been lost. A sail was not made replaceable by the spores it had carried. A matback was not consoled by the future richness of its mineral stain. The power let itself grieve without immediately turning grief into a plan.

Still, it considered plans.

It could have brought water. But water in the Crown was never only water. It was ice opening stone, brine altering current, a lens waking one crowded little world while another remained dry. A lasting abundance would not simply have relieved the country’s thirst. It would have rewritten the intervals by which its lives were shaped.

It could have brought warmth. Then the buried ice, the persistent brines, the balance of fracture and cement, even the old endurance of the mountains, would have entered another history.

It could have killed the hunters. It could have made every animal proof against hunger. It could have joined the oldest mats into something able to remember every injury at once and called that awakening.

Each gift, followed honestly, became less like help and more like replacement.

The power could have made another world. It began to understand that it could not necessarily make that world and still truthfully say it had helped this one.

So it searched for someone to ask.

There was no one who could answer for the Crown. A root network knew its fractures and abandoned paths. A matback knew the long suitability of its grazing route. A sail knew gradients, weight, and the approach of the conditions under which it must fold. A hunter knew occupied connections from empty ones. None of these knowledges was a vote. None was a request to be ruled, rescued, or completed.

To create a voice capable of speaking for all of them would itself have been an intervention greater than rain.

The absence of a request was not permission. It was a reason to wait longer.

The power remained until its first clean divisions began to fail. Stillness was not always dormancy. Dormancy was not always diminishment. Silence could be warning, abundance, hunting, or merely the failure of thin air to carry sound. A patch could share charge and withhold sap. An animal could be pasture, passenger, predator, and ground. Even care, the power discovered, could become a form of not looking closely enough.

In time, the old matback it had first watched died.

Its body became a feeding ground, then a region of vigorous growth, then an arrangement of roots and nodules whose meaning the power could only partly read. The power mourned it. It did not pretend that the country had done so.

Then, having stayed long enough to know that its presence was not necessary, the power left.

It took no specimen. It carried away no spore, no charged nodule, no seed with which to establish a more manageable Crown elsewhere. It left no mind behind to tend the whole and no law telling the country what it ought to become. Its only intervention was to prevent its benevolence from becoming an event.

But the power itself was changed.

Afterward, it was slower to call stillness death, silence loneliness, or difficulty evidence that a place had been abandoned. It was slower to confuse suffering with permission to remake a world. It was also slower to excuse suffering merely because something else grew from it.

It did not learn never to act. It learned to distinguish an emergency from its own discomfort at witnessing a life unlike its idea of a good one. When a voice could answer, it listened before offering choices. When no voice could answer, it did not mistake its own power for consent.

Perhaps this seems too little.

The power had arrived capable of performing a miracle. Instead, it surrendered the pleasure of being the one who had performed it.

In the Vey Crown, pure water entered a fracture. Much later, charged brine followed. Leaves tightened against drought. Somewhere a sail unfolded, gathered weight, and was forced down. Somewhere else a matback touched its lips to bare stone, and the frost on its whiskers lifted outward.

Nothing thanked the power. Nothing had been asked to.

The country ran.
