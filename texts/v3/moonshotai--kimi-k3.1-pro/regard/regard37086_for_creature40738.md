### The Open Hand

A benevolent and wise power would not begin by saving the water.

Holding the lens in place would be the easiest mercy, and the most deceptive. From the valleys, the silver drop would remain exactly where it had always been. Inside, staple rows could still die, clinkers could strip a vein, and one loop after another could develop a hesitation from which it never recovered. The power would have preserved the sign of the Kettle while doing nothing for its lives. It might even come to prefer that sign, because it was the part its own strength could keep unchanged.

So the power would wait.

It would enter without making a body. A body would cast a shadow, and clinkers would close beneath it; it would displace a tier, alter a charge, leave some small mouth either fuller or emptier. It would come as attention, and for a long time attention would be all it allowed itself.

At first it would hear one continuous roar. Then valves, mineral teeth, the bass passage of contractions, the missing beat in an injured association. It would listen through a reversal before deciding what sorrow looked like. When the boundary slackened, the old desire to hold it would return. But bells would release their brood into the quiet; gametes would cross former separations; a wheel fragment would drift where the ordinary field would have turned it back. When the current returned, some associations would not restart. The power would not call the silence good because new life had used it, or evil because something had ended. It would simply know more than before.

If it had come for an audience with the Kettle, there would be none. No inhabitant could answer for the whole. A staple knew the fracture beneath its flesh. An eel knew what arrived through its neighbor. A veilback knew rich water from poor. Their lives touched without becoming a single petition.

The power could make the sulfides inexhaustible. It could close every wound, still every needleback, provide food without appetite and room without death. But beyond some point, preservation would become authorship. The animals would retain their precision, while more and more of that precision expressed the power’s decisions. The Kettle would have gained a keeper at the cost of ceasing to be the thing that had drawn its attention.

Even giving the whole a mind might be no kindness. The absence of central awareness was not evidence of a central awareness waiting to be repaired. A single consciousness stretched across every grazing tooth and failing loop would not necessarily be the Kettle awakened. It might only be a new being, unable to look away.

None of this would make injury beautiful. The power would refuse that comfort too. It would not say that a torn zooid had been repaid by the scavengers it fed, or that a larva’s death became good because a comb-bell continued. Nor would refusal leave it innocent. Doing nothing is also an act for the strong.

It would therefore make no law of noninterference. A law would be another way of stopping attention. If some small mercy became clear, it would not refuse merely to keep its hands clean. If an outsider came to split the spire for its veins or carry off a wheel as a specimen, it would prevent the harm. What it would not do was treat its own grief as proof that the entire place had gone wrong.

Its chief act would be to remain.

It would stay until admiration became more accurate. It would learn the repetitions of the spire as biographies: this wheel lopsided around one ancient sector; this chain faltering where neighbors had drawn a gap closed; these clinker plates shaped by a neighbor years dead; this transparent staple fixed across fresh black stone, beginning without any knowledge of the ocean its body would help hold.

It would not love the Kettle because it was balanced. Sometimes it was not. Nor because it was peaceful, innocent, or eternal. It was crowded with appetite, and it would end.

The power would remember without trying to keep. Memory would not be used to embalm anything.

At last, after a span of time that had no meaning to the tiers, the final contraction would pass through the outer chains. There would be no answering pulse. The power would wait through the length of an ordinary silence, and then longer. No isolated valve would join another. No bass note would gather beneath the ticking. The remaining wheels would tilt; the tiers would loosen; the old geography would not return.

Eventually there would be nothing left that a little more time could restore.

Still, the temptation would come back, purer than before. The water could be held now without altering any living circuit. The silver lens could remain above the snow forever: clear, motionless, safely beyond further loss.

But a circuit can look intact after much of its life has gone out of it. The power would not build a monument on that principle.

It would let the Kettle finish becoming rain.

Only then would it make a hand for itself. Not to catch the water, but to feel that it was still cold. Drops would strike the open palm, run together, and fall. The power would mourn, because much had been lost. It would not use its power to make that mourning unnecessary.

From the valleys, there would soon be one less bright thing in the sky.

The power would not remember the Kettle as one thing.
