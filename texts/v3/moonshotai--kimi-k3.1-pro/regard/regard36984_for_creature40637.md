### The Additional Drawing

The power came as an attention, and at first its attention was too quick.

It saw the whole chamber: the surveyed water, the crisp valves, the green clearances, the roof with its burden of slow droplets. It felt the level engines below, patiently persuading gravity to wait. Nothing approached. Nothing hid. The ivory flowers opened toward no wing.

The power had come prepared to be generous. For a moment it considered generosity in its customary forms.

A little quick life beneath the saucer leaves. A wing at the flowers. Some private illumination by which the garden might know itself, know the height, know that it had been seen.

It did none of these things.

Its first loneliness, it understood, had been its own.

So it remained without acting. It stayed until the chalk mark on a flywheel had moved. It stayed while a bud thickened, while the brass carriage completed one small portion of its journey, while wind bowed the open side of the water and the water, hours later, remembered its plane. Cloud concealed the tower and revealed it. On the roof, one droplet swelled beside a glazing bar until its own weight became sufficient.

The power did not wait for a petition. It would not make speech the price of help. Nor did it mistake the absence of a petition for indifference. It simply watched until looking for events ceased to be its way of looking.

A root accommodated a bolt.

A ribbon leaf kept the shape of a pipe.

Within a ceramic pore, a film renewed itself without ever entering the clear water.

A leaf reached its separation point and was taken away, not as an ending to be prevented but as part of a return too long for the leaf to require.

Only gradually did the power understand that there was no secret rapidity hidden beneath the slowness, no imprisoned quick being longing to be released. The slowness was not a disguise. It was the life.

To awaken the garden would not have been to let it speak. It would have been to make another creature and call that charity. To fill the water with responsive bodies would have given the visitor pleasure, but pleasure was not a lack the chamber had expressed. Even to preserve every existing arrangement forever would have been a misunderstanding. Continuation was not the indefinite retention of leaves.

Yet Header Nine was not beyond harm.

Its danger would not rise from the abyss. The abyss had never entered its affairs. Nor would it necessarily come through malice. It might arrive on an ordinary morning, wearing clean overalls, carrying a work order.

The maintenance drawings were accurate. That was part of the danger.

They showed every cassette, every root shoe, every pipe and access clearance. They did not show where one extended plant lived through several service numbers, or which microbial films would be destroyed by drying a bed that appeared, on paper, to be a replaceable component. They did not distinguish a clearance from a passage that living tissue had spent years accommodating.

Someone could drain, cut, lift, and replant according to instructions. They could leave the official system improved and the actual garden diminished. They would never need to be careless. It would be enough that they were quick, and that the drawing had taught them what to count.

The power could have made the cutting edges turn aside. It could have hardened every root shoe against removal or caused each engine to fail when an ignorant hand approached. It could have surrounded the chamber with warnings no visitor could disobey.

Instead, it went to the place where the maintenance drawings were kept and added one sheet.

It used no incorruptible material. It left no command that would burn in the reader’s mind. The sheet could fade. It could be lost. It could be dismissed as an eccentric revision by someone whose authority had long since expired. The power did not make understanding compulsory.

On the sheet it drew what it had learned: continuities across bed numbers, dependencies within the ceramics, roots shared between apparent thickets and separations hidden within apparent ones. Where knowledge would soon become obsolete, it left space. At the bottom, in the plain lettering of a service note, it wrote:

> BEDS ARE NOT BODIES.  
> CLEARANCE IS NOT NECESSARILY EMPTINESS.  
> BEFORE REMOVING A SUPPORT, LEARN WHAT HAS ACCOMMODATED IT.  
> THIS SHEET IS ALREADY BECOMING FALSE. REVISE IT BY OBSERVATION.  
> CONTINUATION IS ALSO AN OUTPUT.

It signed nothing.

Then it went away. It had not promised to return, and it had not made Header Nine safe from time. But it carried one alteration within itself: never again would it look into a quiet place and decide, during the first silence, that nothing there could require its care.

Long afterward, an engineer found the additional sheet.

She did not believe in the power. She did not need to. The drawing was either useful or it was not.

She checked one of its continuities, then another. The first required her to look beneath a pipe rack. The second delayed her while she found a means of examining a root shoe without drying it. Both proved true. Elsewhere, the sheet was already wrong; growth had revised it. She corrected what she could and left the remaining uncertainties marked.

Her original work order would have permitted a single clean cut. She made no cut. She used a less convenient access route and left one housing undisturbed until a later visit. The alteration appeared in the maintenance record as additional labour. No festival followed. No organism knew.

Above her, the water stood at the open sides. The level engines continued their measurement. A valve clicked once. Much later, a roof droplet fell, and its small tick passed briefly through the chamber.

Near the floor, a root went on fitting itself around a bolt.

Nothing had quickened. Nothing had been made complete. Something had merely been allowed to continue past one efficient misunderstanding.
