At first, it would do nothing.

Not because Sump Nine was beneath notice, or because help would be impossible, but because nearly every kindness suggested by a first look would be an injury.

It would not drain the water to uncover the town. It would not silence the pumps so that the chamber could finally know peace. It would not fill the empty volume above the roofs with gardens, animals, and descending light. It would not wake the work merely to relieve its own pity, inventing a prisoner in order to set one free.

Emptiness was not a request to be filled. Noise was not evidence of pain. Repetition was not, by itself, imprisonment.

So the power would attend.

It would remain through exchanges and imperfect calms, through the peeling of gray sheets and the slow narrowing of valve seats. It would learn that an exchange could destroy one film and carry the beginning of another into a distant seam; that a joint-white’s stillness was not always fear; that the pale movement at the return grille was not an invitation to follow. It would learn which vibrations belonged to injury and which belonged to the ordinary continuation of the task.

The first act of benevolence would be to let its sympathy be corrected.

Then it would look beyond the chamber.

It would follow the power inward and the signals outward. It would trace the rising mains, the dead connections, the later bypasses, the dependencies added by hands that had never seen the whole. Before preserving the work, altering it, or allowing it to end, it would need to know whether the distinction maintained in Sump Nine still mattered anywhere else.

It would find no clean answer. Some of the original purposes had vanished. Some routes now served arrangements the builders could not have anticipated. The work was neither wholly necessary nor merely obsolete. Its sudden failure would still propagate beyond the basalt; its indefinite continuation was not therefore sacred.

So the power would not stop the pumps. Neither would it swear that they must run forever.

Within the town, its attention would eventually settle on something very small.

In one pressure-control station, mineral accretion had stiffened the jacket around a sensing diaphragm. The diaphragm still moved. Its signal remained plausible. Only near the peak of a hot exchange did it begin to lag, and the lag was slight enough that the rest of the system had already compensated for it.

Those compensations had brought an old sluice closer to the limit of its travel. Further compensation would require a secondary pump to answer a condition it could not reliably distinguish from a normal return. Nothing was broken. No red lamp burned. The town still performed its ordinary corrections.

This was how the work would have begun to fail: not with a blow, but with a true condition becoming gradually harder to report.

The power could have rebuilt the station. It could have made the six blocks new, cleared every pipe, replaced every worn seat and uncertain relay. But much of what the work remembered lay in precisely those imperfections. A spotless system would not have been the same continuation with the rust removed. It would have been a different answer, imposed all at once.

Instead, the power waited for a low-load interval between cycles.

It did not halt the pumps. It did not alter the exchange. It freed the diaphragm and restored one clearance, narrower than a joint-white’s shell. It changed no timings and added no instructions. It left the old delays where they were harmless, the scale where it was merely history, the compensations where they still served.

One true report returned to a system made partly of reports.

The next cycle was unremarkable. That was the size of the miracle.

A sluice moved before it would have been forced to move. A pressure wave that would have entered the returns divided instead along an older path. The pump that would have answered too late took up its load at the accustomed time. Farther away, a seam still lost its outer gray sheets, but the living edge was not scoured from the metal. At a cold joint, an ivory animal clamped down at the preliminary shudder and, later, loosened its hooked legs. Whatever lived in the returns took the current and gave it back.

No animal knew it had been spared anything. The work did not know it had been repaired. Knowledge was not required for the help to be real.

The power did not make the films safe from every exchange, or the whites safe from every current. Complete safety would have ended the differences on which their lives depended. It did not turn an ecology into a collection of protected specimens.

It did one thing more, and left that thing outside the chamber.

It accepted responsibility for noticing.

Not eternal operation; not a promise that no gate would ever seize. Simply this: the next change the work could neither express nor answer would not have to remain indistinguishable from no change at all. If the task above ended, if the dependencies shifted, if continuation became mere inertia, the question would be met by attention rather than inherited by accident. The small lives would be neither sufficient reason to keep the machinery running forever nor too small to enter the reckoning.

Then the power withdrew.

It left no lamp over the service square, no mark on the canteen wall, no new voice among the signals. The stools remained bolted down. The water above the roofs remained unoccupied. During a later exchange, an arc in the upper substation flashed blue-white, and for an instant the whole chamber could still be seen exactly as before: the dense little town, the black dome, and the enormous warm absence between them.

Darkness returned.

Below, a gate answered. At a hot seam, a gray edge lifted and settled. The work continued without knowing.

Almost nothing had changed.

Something had been done.
