## What the Power Added

The power came without thunder. Thunder would have entered the flutes and been returned multiplied, and it had not come to hear itself.

It stood beneath the root polished like old bone. It saw the empty court, the dry basins, the vacant upper chambers. Because it was wise, it did not mistake its first sight for a small one. It waited through a dry interval, and then through a wetting.

At full chorus, no voice was hidden from it. It could distinguish each singer fastened in the walls, each frog laboring at the edge of a frequency, each soft wing crossing the court under cover of the roar. It heard the failures also: the blocked chamber, the missed call, the brief hush in which a snake began to move.

It saw the cup fish in their contracting pools. It saw ants walking faithfully onto surfaces that contraction would soon isolate. It saw a nestling strike the rim of its nest after the parent had ceased to return. It saw the dead drawn into gutters and nests, and the living nourished by them.

The beauty of the whole did not reconcile it to the suffering of the parts.

It could have given the Close permanent water. It could have silenced the chorus. It could have forbidden the hunters, unfolded every hidden room, made every route continuous and every pool reliable. Each gift would have been real. So would the evictions that followed.

A frog’s abundance could be an ant nest’s flood. Safety for every moth could be famine in the upper chambers. Silence could leave the vibration-opened flowers shut. A city in which nothing was ever lost would have to be governed everywhere, at every instant, by the power’s idea of what ought to live.

The Close endured because none of its tenants could exploit the whole. The power would not become the first to do so, even with pity.

But refusal was not enough. Benevolence that only refrains can congratulate itself too easily.

So it looked for something smaller: not an act without consequences, but one whose consequences it could follow to the end.

In the lowest district it found a root cup no wider than two hands. During ordinary wettings it overflowed into a gutter. During ordinary dry intervals its fish survived in the black water at the bottom, and its frog flattened itself into the adjoining bark. In the longest intervals, the cup failed. Then a local kind of fish vanished, and eggs on a bird’s foot or an accidental overflow might, after an age, provide another.

The power did not fill the cup. It did not promise rain. It changed no fish and taught the frog nothing.

It left one more night in the water.

An ordinary dry interval would be unchanged. A hard one would still be hard. But at the extreme edge, where life might otherwise end between one dusk and the next possible wetting, there would be a little time that had not been there before. Not enough to abolish thirst. Not enough to make survival likely. Only enough to make it, sometimes, possible.

Then the power went outside.

It considered lifting the grove entire and carrying it somewhere gentler. It considered hiding it from every eye. It considered preserving it exactly, so that no district would ever again collapse and no old chamber lose the shape given it by absent animals.

Instead, it gave the Close a defense against being so easily loved.

To rain, dust, seeds, birds, tortoises, and the heavy browsers of the quiet intervals, the approaches remained as they were. But if any will came meaning to take the whole—to own it, to consume it, or to improve it until nothing within it could refuse—the way inward would lengthen.

Not infinitely. Infinity would only have been another wall. Just far enough for convenience to tire, for a straight intention to falter, for a hand to rest before closing.

The power placed itself under the same condition.

Then it went away.

Much later, a dry interval outlasted every stain on the chamber walls. The shallow pools failed. Their frogs withdrew into bark. In the deepest cup, the water diminished until the remaining fish could no longer pass one another without touching.

The additional night came.

Nothing was revealed to them. No radiance stood on the surface. One fish died before morning, and the power did not call the gift a failure on that account, or a success. The frog remained motionless above the water. Beyond the outer canopy, dust continued to gather in the lee of the roots.

Near the following dusk, one cup in the upper galleries tipped.

It was not a generous wetting. Most of the basins remained dry. Water ticked through a hollow stem, stopped, and then ran again. Enough reached the deep root cup to darken its sides.

Some of the fish lived until the next overflow joined their pool to another. The frog also lived, and in time ate one of them. The chorus resumed by districts. Hunters waited for its flaws. Ants established new trails over routes that already seemed inevitable.

Nothing had been made just. Nothing had been made safe.

Something had been given time to happen.

No root ever bore the power’s name. No chamber opened in its honor, and no animal was required to know that it had been there. The court remained empty at the center, keeping what could not otherwise fit.

The power had added nothing to the sound. Only one night to the water, and a little distance to the world.
