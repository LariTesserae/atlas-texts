### The Margin

The power did not arrive as light. The glassbacks had no eyes, and more light would not necessarily have been a mercy to the moss. Nor did it arrive as warmth, or silence.

For a long while, it did nothing that could be distinguished from attention.

It followed one larva out of the sump, though there was no mark by which to follow it. It stayed while the animal hardened, climbed, fed, and found the fractional warmth of other bodies. It watched condensation cross the glassback’s shell. It received, without impatience, the whole unbroken note of the chamber and the single small click that was this animal’s part of it. When the glassback vanished, leaving the multitude undiminished, the power did not conclude that its life had been incomplete.

The obvious mercies occurred to it, and it refused them.

To open the steel plate would be to kill by kindness. To stop the intakes while hatching continued would turn abundance into fouling, exhaustion, and famine. To let the ridge grow without altering the teeth would be to jam the machinery that kept the brine moving. To carry one glassback into a gentler country would only discover, elsewhere, how completely its country was also the condition of its body.

The power did not pity the glassbacks for not knowing that they were small. It did not think the moss cheated because it was not a forest. A mountain was not the ridge’s happiness. Some later, richer kind of animal would not retroactively complete the animals already filtering brine in the dark.

But attention did not require it to mistake the engine’s necessities for a law of the good.

The exact undoing of every event belonged to the machine. Nothing in a glassback required it. Their lives already contained sequence: suspension, hardening, contact, feeding, a last current or another molt. Nothing in the moss required that every beginning beyond eleven be cropped—not appetite, not photosynthesis, not the small chemistry by which it repaired cold damage. Even the ridge, though manufactured, had no use for being eternally a sketch.

So the power gave no destiny.

It gave a margin.

Beyond the access symbol, in the greater engine whose requirements had made the chamber a held thing, it made a little room for variation: reserves of cold and pressure, a narrow tolerance in the cold trap, corrections that would still prevent catastrophe but would no longer pursue every slight departure back to zero. The brine would remain liquid. The pumps would keep their load. The chamber could remain cold, crowded, loud, and alive.

Nothing would be allowed to run away. Nothing would be required, merely because it had happened, to be erased.

The power altered no animal. It spared no body already drawn toward an intake. It did not brighten the lamp, ordain a twelfth filament, or lay one additional thread upon the ridge. It would not begin by selecting some life to prove that the gift had been worthwhile.

Then it waited without requiring improvement.

For a time, the old totals persisted. They were, after all, good totals for that place. The film fed the glassbacks; the glassbacks fed the film. Water returned. Mineral was laid down and cut away. Eleven filaments stood behind the lamp guard. If the chamber had continued so, varying only in ways too small to name, the power would not have considered itself refused. A gift that had to produce a mountain would be only another instruction.

Eventually, one wet thread of precipitate at the ridge’s base escaped the teeth.

It was smaller than a larva. Fresh mineral closed over part of it. The ridge did not visibly rise, and no cleft deepened enough to be called a channel. But a drop crossing the ledge above was turned aside by almost nothing, and so reached the sump at a different point. There the film thickened imperceptibly. A glassback paused, grazed, and went on.

Behind the lamp, a twelfth filament began and was eaten, as before. The power did not interfere. It had not come to make twelve more sacred than eleven.

The glassbacks continued to crawl over and under one another. They still ate their discarded surfaces, carried water without knowing it, and entered the intakes without conceiving danger. None rejoiced in history. None had asked for it. Their lives were neither sacrifices to a future nor arguments against one.

The power did not stay to learn what the margin would contain. Perhaps only a little unevenness in the brine. Perhaps a succession of shapes on the ridge, each almost identical to the last. Perhaps the moss would always return to eleven. Perhaps, after lengths of time that had no meaning beneath the close ceiling, something would occur that could not have been predicted from the first wet minute.

It did not need to know.

The chamber remained at full volume, in the dark. The amber lamp did not flicker. The transparent shells snapped. Bodies fitted themselves to bodies for the slight warmth of it, and the film closed behind the grazing animals.

But beneath the teeth, one buried thread remained. What happened there could now matter, however slightly, to what happened next.

The next minute came.

It was allowed to be the next.
