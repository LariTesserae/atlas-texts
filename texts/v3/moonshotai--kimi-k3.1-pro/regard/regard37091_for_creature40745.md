## The Last Approach

They would begin by refraining.

A power that mistook wisdom for exactness could kill the High Loom while repairing it. Make every distance singular, and an old column tree might have to choose which court received its shadow. Make every address present at once, and the households that live in the city only on certain approaches might be answered by a blank wall. Restore the ancient rivers, and the whole patient economy of condensation, cisterns, runoff rights, and lengthened bridges would become an obstacle to someone else’s idea of what the country had been.

So they would not put back the peaks. They would not give the root-mat one mind, as though unity were necessary before it could deserve care. They would not provide the grey browsers with herds merely to make the basins look less empty, or fill the silence with birds because silence made them uncomfortable. They would not settle the argument about the governor houses by making every governor infallible.

They would leave no monument. Anything exposed would only give someone else the work of pruning it.

Instead, they would learn the place at the speed of maintenance.

They would clear a gutter before deciding whether the intermittent court it served was real. They would copy an approach book and carry the copy to a separate building. They would wait while a tester tried the resin door last. They would eat seed meal without praising it, listen to complaints about missing tools, and sit with a road keeper whose bridge no longer joined its two visible ends.

They would not tell that road keeper his grief was a failure to understand erosion.

What they would learn is that change and loss are not synonyms. The High Loom did not need saving from looseness. Its inhabitants sometimes needed saving from having only one connection left.

The mat already lived by alternatives. Its roots occupied possibilities without demanding that those possibilities be reconciled. Wealth, among the people, often purchased a smaller version of the same security: a second pipe, a duplicate foundation, another road home. Poverty was having one door. An unlisted household could be poor in approaches even when its cistern was full.

The power would act there.

First, they would take the unallocated maintenance shift.

Where a gutter served a court absent from the current books, and no council could agree who owed the labor, they would clear it. Where an empty structure still braced a roadbed or marked a necessary approach, they would not allow its emptiness to become an excuse for letting it fail. Where a crew had asked for a press guard and been told to wait for freight, they would help fit one. A crushed hand would not be too small a matter for infinity.

They would not take cistern rights, freight priority, or authority in council in exchange. Help would not become a new form of ownership.

Then, for the cases in which maintenance could not help quickly enough, they would establish one last possibility.

It would not be a new kind of door. A new door would soon acquire a district, a custodian, a lock, and eventually another yard. It would be a relation, slight enough to escape most instruments and stubborn enough to outlast erosion: when someone had exhausted every approach and asked for refuge, their knocking would not go unanswered forever.

Sometimes the door before them would open. Sometimes the knock would be heard in another district, beneath another weather, by a household with water, floor space, and a willingness to receive a stranger. No one would be compelled to answer. If the first household could not, the knocking would find another. If no one could, the power would answer themselves.

It would not necessarily be a way home.

It would be a way not to be abandoned while home was still lost.

The last approach would carry no freight, troops, warrants, or debt collectors. It could not be allotted with wages or included in a water account. It would not prove that a traveler was honest, pleasant, or even especially unfortunate in an interesting way. It would require no belief in the power; belief is a poor requirement for emergency infrastructure. Cold, thirst, exhaustion, and the statement *I cannot get back* would be sufficient.

The gift would be reception, not restoration.

The inhabitants would, quite properly, try to write it down. Door testers would refuse to certify it as an ordinary route. Road authorities would ask whether it altered their obligations and be told, unhelpfully but correctly, that it did not clear a single drain. Governor keepers would disagree about whether it worked more dependably under load. Some districts would omit it from their books on principle. It would work there too.

On other approach slates, beneath the cistern arch, the third insulator, the wet upper yard, and the resin door, a final line would eventually be added:

*IF NO APPROACH REMAINS, KNOCK. SAY WHAT YOU NEED. YOU NEED NOT PROVE YOUR ADDRESS.*

Children would still learn their home sequences first. Neighbors would still clear one another’s gutters. The great processes would remain unchanged. Rain would fall in one court and not the next. Roots would enter food bins. Mileposts would continue to be correct in inconvenient ways. Households would keep spare keys for doors that no longer agreed with them, and some of those doors would never agree again.

One evening, long after the power had ceased to be anywhere visible, a press worker would come off shift with wet shoulders and try to go home.

Cistern arch. Left at the third insulator. The upper yard would be wet, so she would take the stair. She would try the resin door last.

It would open onto cable storage.

She would try the reverse sequence, then an older approach through the covered market. She would find the market, but not the street beyond it. She would be frightened, and the gift would not require her to be serene about that. Only when nothing else remained would she knock.

Across the plateau, in a kitchen where someone was complaining about seed meal, there would be a knock at the outer door. A stranger would be standing outside in rain that was not falling on that district.

“I can’t get back,” the stranger would say.

“Come in,” would be the answer. “We’ll see about your address after you’ve eaten.”

Her house would not thereby be restored. Her street might be reachable by morning, or not that week. In the morning she would help clear her hosts’ gutter, not as payment but because it needed clearing, while they copied her description of home into two books and sent for someone who knew the western approaches.

Outside, an empty arcade would light itself. A pale moth would cling to a warm vent. The mat would tighten as the air cooled, and somewhere a governor would miss its beat.

Nothing had been made permanent. Nothing had been made whole.

The door had been answered.
