The power did not arrive as light.

Light would have made a garden. It did not arrive as warmth, or as a current, or as a voice speaking through the pipes. For a long time it permitted itself only attention.

A hurried benevolence might have drained the Cistern Ward. It might have opened the sealed doors, scoured the conduits, uncovered the lamps, and set the flywheels turning at their old speed. It might have called this rescue. But the place rescued would have been a place that no longer existed, and much of what lived now would have been treated as the evidence of its loss.

So the power waited.

It remained through a long pressure cycle, and then through another. It learned the weak firmness near an intake and the almost imperceptible inclination of fronds answering it. It followed a crab’s journey along a cable tray without assuming that the crab was lost. It watched a maintenance arm clear a grille and, in the same motion, crush a sheltered seam of eggs. It saw that a cleared passage could be a mercy, an eviction, or both.

It looked for the being of the ward.

There was no sleeper in the main pump hall, no mind imprisoned behind the status lamps, no weary intelligence waiting for someone to ask whether it wished the turbines to stop. If the ward possessed a self, it was not one that could be summoned by standing in the right room and speaking loudly enough. It lived, if it lived at all, in households and responses: warmth and thread, lamp and leaf, pressure and piston, the long reciprocal obscurity of an intake and the mat closing over it.

The power found no wish it could honestly attribute to the whole. It refused to invent one merely so that it could have the pleasure of granting it.

Nor did it decide that, because the ward had no single voice, nothing within it mattered. Slowness was not insensibility. Scarcity was not insignificance. Equilibrium was beautiful, but it was not innocence, and the power did not call every injury good simply because the ward had survived injuries before.

It did not clean the lamps. Their green sleeves were not dirt.

It did not empty the storage cages. To the snails, a cage was not failed production but geography.

It did not multiply the eels, give the crabs destinations, or teach the snails to remember their paths. It did not awaken the maintenance mechanisms to the long consequences of their obedience. A mind given as an improvement may also be a solitude, a grief, or an obligation; the power would not bestow one merely to make the ward more comprehensible to itself.

It did not make the residents harmless. To do that, it would have had to unmake nearly all of them.

And it did not grant the ward eternity. Nothing there had asked for eternity. Continuation was the ward’s present business, not necessarily its final desire.

For a while, it seemed that attention would be the power’s only act.

Then, beyond the outer retaining wall, deep in the limestone cup, it found a strain accumulating along a fault. The failure lay far in the future. Nothing in the ward anticipated it. When it came, however, it would not come as a sagging roof or a narrowing conduit. It would come in a single convulsion: masonry, growth, cages, warm seams, and pump courts crushed together before pressure could change, before roots could bind, before even the slowest maintenance instruction could become irrelevant.

This was not one of the ward’s ordinary injuries. It was an injury faster than any possible answer.

The power did not seal the fault. It did not make the bedrock unbreakable or pledge that no roof would ever fall. Instead, it eased the deep strain so gradually that even its intervention took on the ward’s timescale. A little stress passed into the surrounding stone. A possible fracture became a possible succession of fractures. What might have been one blow became, if it came at all, a long difficulty: a new seep, a shifting seam, a court growing warmer, a passage narrowing over generations.

Not safety. Time in which to respond, if response was possible.

No gauge trembled. No lamp brightened. In the foundry, the press descended and lifted a pale tablet from the silt. Beneath a heat exchanger, the pallid eel either moved or did not. A valve clicked once.

The power considered leaving a sign and did not. There was nowhere to put one that was not already a joint, a road, a feeding surface, or a shelter. It left no guardian machine; the ward was already crowded with instructions that had outlived their reasons. It left no command to continue. Continuation had been commanded enough.

Then it withdrew, having altered almost nothing and having refused to call that refusal indifference.

Afterward, the Cistern Ward remained warm, crowded, dim, and unfinished. Roots still opened the mortar that held them. Gardens still fouled the circulation that fed them. Tablets still returned to grit. Nothing had been healed of being itself.

But somewhere in the limestone, a crack that would have opened in an instant now had centuries in which to become something else.

The ward had not been given forever. It had been spared, once, being hurried.
