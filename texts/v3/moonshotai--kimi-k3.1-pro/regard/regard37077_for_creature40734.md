The power would not begin by asking what the sump lacked. That question, so natural to a visitor, would already contain a danger.

It would begin by staying long enough to see the place without comparing it to a garden, a ruin, or a prison. It would follow a dead frond into the films among the coke, and the released matter back toward a root or outward over the weir. It would see that the clear pool was not an absence to be filled, the bare vault not an unfinished surface, the narrow green margin not a failure of courage. It would hear the pumps without supposing that anyone was enduring them.

It would not require the wet margin to speak before deciding how to treat it.

Then it would see how easily help could become replacement.

Soil might make the troughs look more generously inhabited, but it would alter the filters and the terms on which their present residents lived. Fish, frogs and insects might animate the open water, until they starved, or consumed something, or made the pool’s clarity impossible. The pennywort could be carried beyond its wet boundary and kept alive there, but only by turning a record of many small attempts into a display maintained by force. The lamps could be given a dawn and dusk; the room could be taught seasons. Nothing in it had been asking for either.

Even silence would be a gift largely for the giver.

To awaken the margin would be a greater intrusion still. It would not be the liberation of the being already present. It would be the creation of another being, with senses to gather and time to remember, and then the calling of that creation a kindness. There might then be someone to find the centre empty. The power would not make loneliness merely so that it could be comforted.

Nor would it try to prevent every loss. It would not restore each brown frond, return each washed-out spore, or set the snail back upon the weir whenever it vanished. Too much of the sump’s continuance depended on things not being kept. A little tissue had to disappear. Surplus had to leave. Even the luxuriance of the ferns rested upon removal.

This would not make passivity a law. If poison were entering with the return water, if a bed were being desiccated by some new fault, the power might act. But its act would be small and conservative: not an improvement in the picture, only the restoration of a condition by which life there already persisted. It would clear an obstruction, perhaps, or divert an injury. Then it would stop.

Here, however, there is no such emergency. A wise power would not invent one out of its own pity.

Neither would it bind the pumps to turn forever. Permanence would be another kind of possession. The sump’s balance is exact, but it is not a covenant, and preserving it eternally might one day require the needs of everything beyond the foundation to be subordinated to a hidden room. The power would not confuse benevolence with embalming. It need not hasten the sump’s ending in order to accept that an ending could come.

So, in the room as it is, it would do nothing.

Not the nothing of indifference. Indifference can also leave a place unchanged. The difference would be in the completeness of the seeing, and in the refusal to turn seeing into ownership. The power would not put a sign upon the steel door, appoint the next visitor a guardian, or make the sump into a lesson for people above. It would not require the place to justify the attention it had received.

The only change, if there were one, would be in the power itself: it would have lost the illusion that love must leave evidence.

Then it would turn its attention elsewhere.

Behind it, a drop would swell at the usual low point in the vault and fall without an audible announcement. Somewhere in a trough, a film would break and close. A root would enter a space another root had left. The clear water would slip over the rounded lip at precisely its former height. The pumps would neither change their note nor become meaningful.

Nothing would have been rescued. Nothing would have been improved. Nothing would have been asked to become worthy of concern.

Inside the room, the power’s mercy would be indistinguishable from the room continuing as it was.

That would not trouble the power. It had not come to be distinguishable.
