### What the Power Would Do

The power does not arrive as light. The Seep has enough light, and the power can see where light does not reach.

It looks first at the inhabited fraction of the chamber: the film of water at a limb’s tip, the active cells beneath an amber rind of sheaths and remains, the narrow chemical difference between rock freshly touched and rock already exhausted. It looks beyond the rear wall, into the lightless fissures where the pool’s rare visitors have their refuge. It follows the seep backward toward water that has never entered the radiance. And it sees, with no special sorrow, the dense white lens that may one day occupy the chamber’s future.

For a long while, it does nothing.

This is not indifference. It is refusing to mistake the availability of power for the necessity of using it.

Preservation would be easy to imagine. The pool could be held at its present depth. The feeder could be kept open. Each drying film could be rewetted, each retreating population restored, each transparent animal returned to the margin whenever the open chamber became empty. The roof could be prevented from closing. Midday could be made to recur without alteration for as long as anyone desired a bright place beneath the black plain.

But the power does not love the chamber chiefly as something to be seen. It will not make the processes obedient to one fortunate moment and call the result mercy. A Seep forbidden to change might remain dazzling; it would also have become, in some essential respect, an exhibit.

Nor does it fill the bare floor with life. It does not teach the films to feed on radiance, or summon green things into a place where green would be only an ornament for a visitor. It does not make the crustacean larger, longer-lived, fearless of dry glass, or conscious of being watched. The animal’s transparency is not a deficiency. Its patience is not wasted time. Its smallness is not an appeal to be given a different kind of life.

Yet the power does not conclude that, because the chamber is temporary, nothing here deserves care. Briefness does not make a life unreal. An ending is not automatically an injury, but neither can every injury be excused by calling it change.

So its attention moves upstream.

If harm is approaching through the larger watershed—some needless diversion that would empty the fissures, some poison that would travel farther than any animal, some careless opening made not from necessity but from the hunger to possess what has been found—that is where the power will act. Its intervention may be considerable elsewhere and undetectable here. It will not sacrifice the dark, continuing world in order to keep seven paces of glass bright. Neither will it preserve the appearance of the Seep while allowing the water that made its life possible to be ruined.

If no such harm threatens, it invents none. It does not create an emergency in order to become necessary.

Within the chamber, therefore, almost everything is left to proceed.

A hot pulse reaches the pool margin. The crustacean stops with its antennae in the altered flow. The power does not cool the water, though it could. The animal turns, finds the wetter route, and withdraws toward the throat. No rescue is needed; the refuge and the knowledge of retreat are already part of its life. The power does not treat competence as a reason for contempt, or dependence as the only occasion for love.

It does not take the discarded molt. It does not improve the articulation of its limbs, or set it somewhere safer from dissolution, or preserve it because it is easier to see than the living animal. If silica keeps the shell, that will be a fact about silica. If the shell disappears, the life that made it will not thereby have been less real.

And when, in time, the wet pockets narrow, the power does not demand one final excursion for the sake of symmetry. It does not keep a microbial film active inside its mineral coffin. It does not call the entombed cells a small price for beautiful opal, or pretend that nothing has been lost. But neither does it make the stone guilty. Deposition is not betrayal. The closing of this habitat is not, by itself, the extinction of the dark population beyond it. The power keeps those distinctions intact, especially when grief or admiration would blur them.

No sign is left above. No radiance escapes at night to mark the seam. No dream is sent to guide a hand toward the roof. If the place is ever discovered, the power will not automatically make discoverers wicked or discovery disastrous; it will ask, as it asked here, what is actually needed and what would actually be harmed. Until then, the warm patch remains easy to overlook.

For now, a drop gathers on the ceiling. In the feeder throat, an animal changes position. The power gives no gift that either could detect.

It has not made the Seep eternal. It has not declared its transience holy. It has not confused leaving no mark with doing no good, or doing something vast with doing something kind.

The drop falls. Somewhere behind the wall, a faint amber thread of gut is carried back into darkness. The power remains, if it remains at all, as one more presence that does not alter the light.
