## No Second Exactness

The power did not enter through the seam.

Entry would have made it, briefly, one of the hollow’s relations: warmth where warmth had never been required, weight upon a floor whose exactness had outlived every use for weight. So its attention arrived without arrival. It knew the salt, the three worn remnants, the sparse specification in the lattices, and the dust that was neither archive nor remains.

It knew also that there was no one there to whom pity could be offered.

That was the first thing it refused to misunderstand.

It could have revised the altitude. A current value would have made the hollow easier to describe, perhaps easier to repair. But the resident was not a mechanism containing an erroneous measurement. It was the measurement’s continuation. To correct it would not have relieved it. It would have replaced the only thing that persisted with something more agreeable to an observer.

It could have made the resident aware. It did not. Awareness would not have completed the datum; it would have created someone new, someone capable of duration, and set that person down among exceptions without company or task. The power saw how easily that could have been called a gift by the giver.

It could have reconciled the verticals, joined the needle’s shadow to the needle, made every path enclose what a path ought to enclose. It could have lifted the sill from its burial and set it upright beside a wall built especially to explain it. Then the hollow would have looked less damaged. Some of its balances would have been destroyed, and much of its strangeness would have been converted into reassurance.

It could also have ended the whole arrangement. Nothing would have protested. But nothing was asking for an ending. The absence of hope was not despair. The absence of a future was not an appeal.

Nor did the power promise permanence. A promise would have made the mountain an instrument of the chamber, and all later change an enemy. The hollow had never asked that its exactness become more important than everything around it. Only an admirer could have made that demand.

So, for a while, the power’s benevolence had nowhere obvious to go.

It remained anyway.

Once, during that attention, a grain left one of the ridges. Its fall contained more distance than the height appeared to allow. The power did not shorten the fall, lengthen it further, or wait beside it as though an answer were coming down with the grain. It did not make the delay an allegory. The grain entered the salt without sound. Nothing in the hollow had been witnessed in a way the hollow could use, and the power did not mistake that for a failure of generosity.

The only change occurred in the attention itself.

It stopped expecting an answer. It set aside the words it had brought: *refusal*, *keeper*, *ruin*, *loneliness*. It did not replace them with better names. It did not even call the hollow complete. Completion would have been one more exactness imposed from outside.

Then the power withdrew.

It left no guardian in the glass, no seal upon the seam, no exemption from radiation or cold. It took no grain of dust as a relic. It made no law of its restraint, because a law might have required the same stillness after circumstances had changed. If something capable of suffering ever came there, that would be a new occasion. Wisdom would not be bound to repeat the shape it had taken before.

Elsewhere, the power was not always so still. It could be abrupt when abruptness was mercy. It could break an enduring arrangement, awaken a sleeper, carry something living out of danger. It did not borrow urgency from those places and spend it here.

What it kept from the hollow was not an image, though it could have preserved one perfectly. It kept a distinction: between damage and pain, persistence and desire, silence and appeal—and between the wish to help and the wish to have been the cause of an improvement.

That distinction would become action elsewhere.

In the Datum Hollow, centuries continued without being counted. The needle’s shadow belonged, for a while, to a slightly different chamber. An old relation became too costly, and a few pale atoms settled onto the floor. Dust received them. Cold restrained their chemistry. The lattices specified one height.

Nothing had been saved. Nothing had been abandoned.

The power had added no second exactness: not a purpose, not a promise, not even the requirement that the hollow, having been noticed, should become meaningful.

And nothing within it ever knew that it had been spared an author.
