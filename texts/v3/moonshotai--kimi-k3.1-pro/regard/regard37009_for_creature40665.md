### The Accurate Mercy

The power came to the edge of the High Vacancy and did not arrive.

They could have stood in the central court without leaving footprints. They could have crossed the avenues without shortening them, entered the oldest rooms without becoming the breath later returned by their walls. But even attention has edges, and the power did not yet know what their presence might alter: a difference of charge across a wall, the spacing of three grains, the destination of a door.

So they remained outside the first court, and watched.

Their earliest impulses were kind.

They thought of giving the sun a little warmth. They thought of moisture sealed in the deepest shafts, not enough for rain—nothing so crude—but enough to soften the perfect dryness. They thought of repairing the worn distances, so that an ancient avenue would again require the journey its perspectives promised. They thought of giving the veils surfaces, the dispersed animals continuity, and the crack-animals some safer tissue than a body borrowed from a fracture.

They thought of providing the crossing with an end.

Each kindness was postponed.

A warmer sun would revise the pressure tides. Moisture would not merely add a chemistry; it would make the existing chemistries unnecessary. Restored distance might tear apart the old court-animals whose lives had become minute in traversal and enormous in relation. A veil given a surface would no longer be a veil. To preserve every lattice would be to cover the walls. To keep every dispersed animal from failing would be to fill the courts with the accumulated obligations of mercy.

As for the crossing, the power could discover no evidence that it wished to arrive.

The fact that a thing can be imagined completed did not, they decided, prove that it was living in an state of incompleteness.

They watched a lattice relax into dust.

It would have been a small matter to preserve the electrical difference between the two faces of its wall. The power could have done it without warming the stone, without touching a grain. The stepped outline would have remained exact. New activity might have continued at its edges.

They did not do it.

The angles wandered. The interior became unremarkable first, then the margin. There was no struggle and no residue. On another wall, under a later tide, a similar lattice arose. The power could not determine whether it was a descendant, a recurrence, or some third thing for which their categories were too anxious.

They did not conclude that the first lattice’s life had been unimportant. They concluded only that its ending had not necessarily been an appeal addressed to them.

In one of the great courts, after a regional settling, three grains remained around a tilted slab and no longer answered one another. Dust passed through the court without being altered. The power felt grief, or recognized in themselves something that would have become grief if allowed to continue.

Still they did not restore the relation.

It would have been easy to call the pattern back and difficult to know whether they had thereby saved an animal, repeated an old arrangement, or made a new being wear the shape of their regret.

During the next crossing, they nearly intervened.

Beforehand, the veils turned back from the gulfs. The crack-animals withdrew into the deepest parts of their fractures, and lattices spread over stairs ordinarily grazed bare. Then the lower lights began to vanish, one narrow interruption above another.

The power could have illuminated the depth. They could have made every interval visible at once, or placed themselves across the ascending sequence to learn whether it would divide around them.

They did none of these things.

Several times during the crossing, they thought the sequence had turned toward them. Each time, on longer attention, it became apparent that this was their own wish to be answered.

Afterward, some stairs remained empty. Others slowly regained their fine, motionless fracturing. The power did not call this balance. Balance was too often the name an observer gave to a relation they had decided not to disturb. They called it insufficient knowledge, and declined to make a law from it.

Nor did they mistake silence for consent.

They knew that a being might be unable to ask, that an appeal might resemble weather, that suffering need not possess a voice available to another kind of mind. So they stayed. Their restraint was not an excuse for convenience. It cost them the pleasure of rescue, which was not nothing, and the assurance of their own goodness, which was more.

At last they understood that the High Vacancy was not a wounded version of some other place.

Its courts were not settlements from which the settlement had been taken. Its doors were not evidence of absent hands. Its thin lives were not failed approximations of thicker ones. Even the empty spaces between an animal’s grains were not deficiencies in its body. They were where its body became possible.

Nothing there was waiting to be made numerous, continuous, warm, or visible.

Nothing there needed to be forgiven for ending.

So the power made no change within the highlands.

They left no spring at the bottom of a shaft. They established no eternal charge across any wall. They did not appoint a guardian, fix the avenues, forbid the settlings, or preserve the shadow-city beyond its hour. They did not even leave a blessing. A blessing would have been one more relation imposed upon a place already exquisitely dependent upon which relations were allowed to remain unstated.

But they did not simply turn away.

Beyond the highlands, where other great wills might one day look and see altitude without use, stone without owners, and courts large enough for their own intentions, the power left their witness.

It was not a barrier. It compelled no agreement and prevented no violence. It merely made the place difficult to perceive first as vacancy.

A will inclined toward building would have to see, before its plans, a populated stair that resembled bare stone. A will inclined toward generosity would have to pass its attention through a court and perceive the distances being maintained there. A will seeking somewhere to begin again would encounter the veils entering shadow-doors, the dust dividing around relations without surfaces, and the possibility that an empty room might already have been inhabited for longer than the intention to improve it.

After that, another power might still choose destruction. The witness was wise enough not to confuse information with goodness. But no one could easily destroy the High Vacancy in the innocent belief that there was nothing there.

No one could call its erasure an act performed upon unoccupied space.

The power also kept open the possibility of being wrong. If some later tide brought evidence of pain where they had found only ending; if the crossing made an appeal rather than merely a sequence; if some relation in the highlands turned outward and asked, by means no observer had yet imagined, then attention could return. Help was not forever refused. It had simply not been allowed to precede understanding.

Then the power withdrew by degrees, so gently that no black grains rose from the shafts and no pale banners altered their level extension over the cliffs.

At the dimmest part of the cycle, the additional city lay briefly across the saltfield. Veils passed through its narrow lanes. In the solid wards, lattices continued their angular feeding. The crack-animals became faults. In a court whose far corners had nearly touched, three dark grains maintained distances no eye could have distinguished from chance.

For a short while afterward, one enclosed room below the western terraces returned a breath drawn with unnecessary care.

No breath had been drawn there. It was the faint remainder of the power’s final impulse to speak—to offer a name, perhaps, or to assure the place and its inhabitant that they had been seen.

They had almost said, *You are not alone.*

They had not said it. The comfort would have required the wound.

The room returned the breath once, very softly. Then the sound was gone. Nothing came to investigate it. Nothing visible looked up.

Within the High Vacancy, nothing had been saved, nothing improved, and nothing made everlasting. Its emptiness remained intact, and with it the lives that emptiness made possible.

Beyond it, mercy had become a little more accurate.
