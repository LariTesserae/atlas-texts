## Slack Water

The power did not arrive as light. The Reservoir already had light, apportioned by district and power setting. It came as attention, and for a long time attention was all.

It listened first: to the low labour of the pumps, to valve concussions crossing the floor, to steel ticking as an outflow cooled. During a shutdown it heard the machinery fall away and the patient, irregular knocking of spawning fish continue. It heard sounds no crew had separated from the general note: barnacles closing, snails crossing painted steel, a crab hooking its legs more firmly into a joint.

It did not think the Reservoir counterfeit because it had been made. Nor did it mistake vacancy for peace, or management for care. There was no earlier sea to restore. The walls were not the tragedy. The tragedy, where there was one, was that a living thing could have its whole world altered by someone who would never have occasion to know that it was there.

Neither did the power mistake people for villains. It saw crews sleeping through concussions and waking at silence; divers whose knowledge extended a few millimetres into the growth on steel; someone standing at a window with a cup going cold while, beyond the glass, a squid used the light to hunt. Most were solving local problems with tools that made distant consequences difficult to feel.

Then it attended to one being.

In a lit expansion joint, far from any station whose name it might have borne, lived a joint crab with a brush scar on its carapace and one crookedly regenerated leg. It knew where the flow slackened, which lip of concrete broke a cleaning blade’s path, and what vibration meant food drifting down from above. It did not know the Reservoir. It knew less than a metre of it.

The power gave that metre the same completeness of attention it had given the gyres.

The crab was not a keystone species, a last survivor, or a creature with a hidden destiny. It was simply alive. The power did not need it to be important.

It did not make the crab human, deathless, or grateful. It did not carry it to the outside sea and call the journey rescue. Nor did it make the Reservoir over into a coast, a reef, or a garden. There would have been no purity in adding a second, more pleasing design.

What it gave the Reservoir was slack: not stillness, not safety, but a small interval between decision and consequence, and a little more knowledge on both sides of the walls.

It made the machinery more legible to the animals, and the animals more legible to the people who worked the machinery.

From then on, a gate always ticked before it boomed. A lamp field faded before it failed. A discharge plume shivered before it lifted. Before the pressure front of a current storm, a faint strain-note ran through the structure—not everywhere, and not always in time, but consistently enough to be learned. Intakes due to surge gave a brief, regular alteration in flow at their outer screens.

The operations did not stop. Intakes still killed. Plumes still abandoned eggs. A storm could strip a seam bare. The signals were not promises of escape: eggs cannot leave a cable tray, and a sump animal cannot outswim a pressure change. But where shelter existed, it could be reached more often. Where there was a choice of spawning site, some choices could be better. Behaviour could accumulate around the signals as shell accumulated on steel.

Nothing in the animals was rewritten. What they learned would be theirs.

For the people, the power used a less exalted language: annotations.

A fouling estimate could be opened beyond tonnage, to the bands of growth, egg strands and shelters it described. A report on material removed from an intake screen could be opened to what the material had been before it reached the screen. A spawning forecast appeared beside a proposed load transfer when the two were actually connected. Maintenance maps began to distinguish growth threatening a seal from growth merely inconvenient to inspect.

None of the information was miraculous. Patient human work could have gathered all of it. No command appeared, and no one was compelled to read. The power did not make ignorance impossible. It made ignorance less convenient.

Some people closed the additional fields and worked as before. Some used them to harvest more efficiently. Others changed a ramp rate, delayed cleaning by a shift, or held a warm plume through one spawning aggregation when another basin could take the load. Their motives were mixed: fewer blockages, better forecasts, less wear, a diver’s stubbornness, a supervisor’s sleeplessness. The power did not require pure motives.

Only where an action would have ended a whole community’s capacity to recover did it intervene more plainly. Where the work could wait, it waited. A nonessential cleaning cycle refused to start. A transfer paused at its warning stage until another route was found. These interruptions were rare, exasperating, and never accompanied by explanation.

The power did not prevent death. It prevented a few endings after which nothing could have made more of itself.

The crab in the expansion joint benefited without knowing it. A maintenance supervisor, reading that the growth posed no danger to the seal, left a narrow lee-side strip as a control. The decision saved time on the work order and eighteen centimetres of habitat. The supervisor never saw the crab and would have been embarrassed to be told they had done anything merciful.

Later, an eelpout took the crab during a slack-water crossing. The power did not call that good, and it did not treat the eelpout’s hunger as an error. Fragments of the crab’s carapace entered the organic dust in a drainage trench.

The reprieve had still been real. A life need not be permanent for more of it to matter.

Years afterwards, there was no miracle to see from midwater. The Reservoir remained cold, loud and mostly vacant. Freight crossings still took days. Current storms still arrived early or stalled where none had been expected. People scraped columns; animals starved; some careful practices were abandoned when output rose. It did not become harmony.

But at certain gates, animals began to gather or withdraw before the operators saw a load change. Cleaning maps retained narrow bands of old growth, some for engineering reasons and some because station crews had become stubborn. Warm plumes acquired spawning seasons in control-room forecasts. A few biological provinces persisted that would otherwise have been reset again and again. In low-flow trenches, sounding weights more often failed to strike cleanly through to concrete.

The Reservoir had become a little harder to erase without knowing, and a little easier to survive without being understood.

Then the power stopped intervening. It did not stay to turn every consequence towards the outcome it preferred. That would have made it the last and most thorough engineer, and life would once again have been something done by commission.

It left the pumps working and the roof sealed. It left the water cold.

At night, a station window made its small field of light. Krill gathered unevenly beyond the glass. A squid waited farther out. Somewhere a gate ticked. Along the downstream joints, crabs withdrew, and a school turned before the pressure boom arrived.

For a little while, nothing happened.

It was not nothing.
