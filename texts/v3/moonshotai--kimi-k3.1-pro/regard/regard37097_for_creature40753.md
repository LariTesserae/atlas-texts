## What the Power Did

The power came without a body. At first, that was the whole of its courtesy.

It cast no shadow on the fans. It displaced none of the thin gas, warmed no seam, bent no mineral film beneath an arriving foot. It did not announce itself. An announcement would only have made the plateau responsible for its arrival.

It had been drawn by the appearance of incompletion. To one accustomed to helping, the place seemed full of invitations: a basin to fill, a seam to extend, a frond to open, an empty plain to make less empty. But the power was wise enough to recognize how seductive an invitation could be when it was really only the shape of one’s own abilities.

So, before giving anything, it searched for the wound.

It followed charge through the photoelectric films and down into the dry interfaces. It watched gas become tissue and tissue become feedstock again. It examined the margins where living continuity failed and bare crystal began. It looked for exhaustion, starvation, injury, a chemistry maintaining itself at ruinous cost. It looked for some organ strained by persistence, some form held together against its own conditions.

There was none.

Nothing was enduring its shape. Nothing was spending the last of itself to remain alive. No seam-web strained at its boundary. No crown was imprisoned in its canopy. The half-unfurled frond was not frustrated. The shallow basin was not thirsty. The silence was not the silence of something holding its breath.

Still, the power considered what it might give.

It could have brought water. Elsewhere, it had done so with joy: rain on cracked ground, springs returning to buried channels, green growth softening a field of stones. Here, water would not have answered a need. It would have created an entirely new order of needs. It would have carried matter away from interfaces that had never required transport, opened pathways through the untouched mineral surfaces, and made the groves’ precise economies crude or impossible. The first loss on the plateau might have been caused by the gift intended to enrich it.

It could have filled the canopy with wings. But wings were not ornaments. Animals would have required surplus, and surplus would have altered the balance that made the groves possible. There would have been feeding, seeking, injury, remains. None of these were evils in themselves. The power had no hatred of hunger, movement, or death. It simply saw that it would be introducing them so that the plateau might become easier for a visitor to recognize as alive.

It could have let the groves spread. It could have made the mineral benches green, raised summits from the suggested ridges, elaborated the fractures into valleys. It could have given the place succession, catastrophe, recovery: a history.

Then there would have been a story to tell about it.

The power lingered over that temptation, because it loved stories. It loved beginnings that led somewhere, lives that could be followed, gifts whose consequences unfolded beyond the moment of giving. Only gradually did it see that this, too, was a preference it had brought with it. A story was not necessarily a good imposed upon a bad stillness. Sometimes it was only a form of change cherished by the one watching.

Last, and most tenderly, it considered giving someone the ability to witness the place.

Not rain. Not animals. Not a future across the plains. Just an inward eye: a being who could know the violet zones in a fan, feel the mild warmth, understand that the grove persisted, and perhaps respond to the power’s attention.

This seemed, for a moment, the purest possible gift.

But there was no loneliness asking to be relieved. To make company necessary, the power might first have to create the experience of absence. To be welcomed, it might first have to make someone capable of feeling alone before its arrival. To receive gratitude, it might first have to create a being who could need it.

It would not manufacture a debt and call the relationship communion.

Perhaps, the power thought, there was already some inwardness in the living geometries, something no examination of charge and matter could disclose. It did not know. Wisdom did not require it to pretend that it knew. But uncertainty was not permission to force an entrance. Nor was silence a locked door behind which the power was entitled to imagine a prisoner.

It did not take silence as consent.

Neither did it turn silence into an excuse for rescue.

What remained was not indifference. Indifference would have looked away as soon as it found no use for its strength. The power remained until its attention no longer demanded an outcome. It saw the grove without asking what the grove might become. It saw the permanent suggestion of the land without requiring the land either to fulfill the suggestion or to be mourned for never doing so.

It stopped calling the place unfinished, even in the privacy of its thought.

Then it acted upon the only thing in the encounter that was wholly its own to change.

It gave up the wish to be necessary.

It took no sample. It left no marker, no protective signature, no delicate addition that only another power would recognize. It did not name the grove after itself or establish a shrine to restraint. Nor did it command that the plateau must forever remain exactly as the power had found it. Preservation, too, could become authorship. If change ever arose without its interference, the power would not call the place spoiled merely because it no longer matched a cherished memory.

Its only vow concerned its own hand: it would not return with an improvement.

Elsewhere, this did not make it passive. It still healed injuries, opened prisons, and brought rain where rain was longed for. It still gave aid gladly when aid could be received without first manufacturing the need for it. But it became less quick to confuse movement with freedom, plurality with abundance, response with relationship, or an unfolding future with the only legitimate form of a life.

It had not learned to do less. It had learned to tell the difference between love and authorship.

On the plateau, nothing commemorated the visit. The conductive seams carried their small currents. Tissue was reclaimed and renewed. A pale fan kept its color; a nearly black-green surface did not become a sign of age. The half-unfurled frond remained exactly what it was.

The grove had not been blessed, if blessing meant an addition. It had not been abandoned, if abandonment meant the withdrawal of something needed. It had been accurately seen, and the seeing had cost it nothing.

The power remembered. The plateau did not need to.

The power had brought waiting with it. When it left, it took the waiting away.
