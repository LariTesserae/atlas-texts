### What the Power Did

For one full cycle, and then another, the power did nothing.

It had not come in answer to a prayer. Nothing beneath the paving could make one. The cells in their films did not need to be told that they were significant, and the rush did not suffer from being hidden. Even its endurance was not a wish in any human sense. It was a root entering a pore, a leaf returning slowly toward upright, a little stored substance spent and not yet replaced.

If attention was to become help, it would first have to become accurate.

So the power remained attentive. It heard the change of pressure before the siphon and the knocking after the siphon broke. It saw where a root tip had flattened itself against clay, and where the outer edge of an algal patch had been lifted away. It followed one cell from roof dust to open water to drain, and another into the still interior of a pellet. It did not confuse the fate of the first with failure, or that of the second with a promise.

It saw how easily benevolence could become interference.

To silence the siphon would be to alter the chamber’s whole weather. To feed the water richly would cloud it and favour lives other than those already there. To keep the rush from every torn root and yellowing leaf would be to love a specimen rather than a plant. And to preserve the cistern exactly as it was—white, sharp-edged, nearly empty—would require a continuous exclusion of the very life that had drawn the power’s attention.

The builder’s fingerprint could not be kept forever without making war on everything that might one day cover it.

The power refused that kind of preservation. It did not love the fingerprint more than the films, or the clear water more than the stain that might eventually darken the drain. Nor did it hurry those changes. The chamber did not need to be made more interesting. It did not need fish, or a voice, or a secret history. Its smallness was not a deficiency waiting to be corrected.

But wisdom was not the same as abstention.

The lives below were already attempting to remain. The power could assist one such attempt without deciding what had to follow.

It waited for something the chamber would produce without help.

In time, the rush lost an old leaf.

There was nothing extraordinary in the death. The leaf had served through several fillings and drawdowns; its tissues had thinned; its attachment had weakened. When it came free, the water carried it away from the brightest wall. It turned once beside the basket and began the slow passage that would, sooner or later, bring it within the influence of the outlet.

The power did not restore it. It did not make the rush whole again, or suspend the inlet, or weaken the next siphon. It gave the leaf only a slight turn in the water.

That was all.

No light marked the act. The level continued rising by imperceptible degrees. The leaf drifted behind the basket and came to rest against the clay, where a small irregularity interrupted the current. It was the sort of thing that might have happened without assistance. There would be no sign by which the chamber could be claimed, and nothing in it would know to give thanks.

When the siphon next took, the leaf lifted.

For a moment it flattened against the basket. One end slipped into the open water and was drawn toward the outlet; the other caught among the pellets. The whole chamber roared. The rush bent, the filaments near the inlet streamed outward, and water was drawn down by a handspan. Then the flow weakened. Air returned through the roof slot. The siphon broke with its deep gulp and smaller knocks.

The leaf remained.

Nothing was thereby guaranteed. A later storm might still flush it away. The basket might shift. Decay might loosen it before enough sediment had gathered to hold it. The power did not close those possibilities. It had not promised the chamber an outcome.

For several cycles, however, the leaf stayed where it was.

It softened. A few mineral grains settled against it. A film crossed the narrow boundary between dead tissue and clay. Some of the leaf’s substance was carried off in particles too small to see; some entered the growth of cells sheltered in the basket’s pores. A little remained as dark matter among the grains. The open water stayed clear. The three patches of algae continued their nearly balanced work of spreading and being cut back. No visible abundance announced that anything important had occurred.

One root was still torn when it ventured too far through a perforation. A filament still broke near the inlet. Most of what drifted freely through the chamber still left by the drain. The power did not return to correct these things. It had not mistaken its small act for a duty to prevent every loss.

Above, the paving warmed and cooled. Below, sunlight continued to break into moving meshes on the white plaster. Mineral deposits slowly blunted the tile edges. The rush extended another pale root. Behind the basket, where there had formerly been only clear water and clean clay, decay had been given time to begin.

The power had not made the chamber safe, or fruitful, or everlasting. It had only kept the drain, once, from taking everything.

Then the water began to rise again.
