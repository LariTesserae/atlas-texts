### The Size of the Gift

The power did not arrive as light. The Hollow had its own arrangements with light and would, in any case, have received the visitation late.

Nor did it arrive as water. It came as attention, and for a long while it did nothing but learn what its attention was for.

It stayed through a rain. This meant staying until long after the plain was dry: through the darkening of the seam, through the brief lens in the basin, through the moths’ descent and the beetles’ separate disappearances, and later still, while the scale-leafs were opening over water no longer visible. Only then did it begin to understand how easily a gift could arrive at the wrong time and call the resulting damage generosity.

It considered giving the basin permanent water. It considered softening the seedcases. It considered teaching the slatebacks to keep a good pebble, then to keep several, then to make a place for them. It could see how readily a path might appear in the dust, and how satisfying that path would be to the one who had caused it.

It did none of these things.

The Hollow was not a failed version of a wetter place, and the slatebacks were not unfinished makers. The power did not hold their scarcity sacred. It simply refused to treat their lives as material for its own idea of progress.

Nor did it mistake the coherence of the whole for the comfort of every life within it. Waiting could cost something. Hunger was not made pleasant by being economical. A spider’s meal was still an ending for something else. The power did not call these things beautiful in order to excuse itself from seeing them.

But there was no single wound called the Hollow that it could heal.

It made no doctrine of restraint. Had the roof been falling, it would have held the roof. Had some catastrophe entered that admitted no answer from within, it would have turned the catastrophe aside. But there was no catastrophe, and it would not invent one to justify the scale of its abilities.

Then, because it had learned to look at the scale on which life there was actually occurring, it noticed something small.

A splinter of calcite had worked beneath one scale at the base of a slateback’s foretoe. The flesh had closed around it. The injury was not mortal and scarcely visible. The animal had learned to grip without putting pressure on that place, and its adjustment was so complete that it looked like the natural shape of the foot.

The splinter did not prevent the lizard from hunting or sleeping or opening seeds. It merely made certain movements hurt. It would probably continue to do so for much of the animal’s life, without teaching it anything or improving its chances. The power could find no reason to preserve that.

It waited until the slateback slept. Then it removed the splinter and closed the tiny place where it had been.

Nothing brightened. The roof did not release its light early. The lizard did not dream of a voice, wake with a new thought, or feel itself chosen. It was left with its ordinary body, its inaccurate blows, its preference for certain sleeping pockets, and its own habits, which would change or not.

The act was made the size of the hurt, not the size of the power.

That was all it changed within the chamber.

It left no water behind, no mark on the wall, no promise that the Hollow would last forever. It did not confuse a continuing good with a good that must never be allowed to alter. It remembered the place, but kept the remembrance in a form that could not become a map. It did not speak of the Hollow as something it had found. Finding, it knew, was often the first form of taking.

Then it went.

Some days later, the slateback found a seed beneath a scale-leaf. It turned the seed against the floor, braced it, and missed with the first blow. The seed jumped; the animal recovered it. The second blow opened the case. The lizard ate the kernel and dropped the pebble.

There was no improvement in its character, no new ambition, no impulse to search for the stone again. It put its weight on the mended forefoot and continued along its range.

The foot did not hurt.

There was no way for the animal to know that this was an event. For the lizard, the event had been the seed.

Late that evening, when the plain above had turned blue, the chamber still held a white afternoon. A bead swelled at the eastern seam. Somewhere along the wall, a pebble clicked once. The bead fell without an echo.

Nothing in the Hollow had been made greater. One small life had been made less costly to live.

The power did not return to admire its work.
