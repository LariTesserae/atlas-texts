### What the Power Did

The power did not arrive as light. There was already enough daylight, and anything more would only have startled the moth.

For a while, they merely paid attention.

They could hold the whole Nursery in their regard without making any of its inhabitants small: the basin with its violent weather, the cold metal range inhabited by the snail, the moth’s dangerous crossings, the fungal felt emptying a fallen blossom of its strength. They saw the garden’s forty paces, and also the distances inside it.

It would have been easy to be generous in the wrong way.

They could have brought insects by the thousand, chosen birds whose songs would suit the scale of the room, deepened the basin, softened the casting edges, and hidden the service bolts. They could have made every flower visited and every seed viable. They could have returned the escaped rill to its groove with less effort than the snail required to cross a grain of mineral dust.

In one afternoon, they could have made the Nursery abundant, resilient, and much easier to love.

They did not.

Every one of those gifts would have replaced a little of the garden’s answer with the power’s own idea of what a garden ought to be. Even mercy could become authorship. Even authorship could become tidiness.

So they did not finish it.

Neither did they mistake fragility for virtue. There was nothing good in itself about a population vanishing through an intake, nothing beautiful merely because a shrub flowered without ever being visited. The power felt no pious obligation to call every loss necessary. They simply refused to confuse the ability to prevent an event with the wisdom to prevent it.

And they did not let the whole make the individuals invisible.

They did not cherish the snail because it grazed algae, or the moth because it carried pollen, or the fungi because they kept the Nursery from suffocating beneath its own dead. Use was not the price of attention. For a time, their whole regard rested on one cold face of one condenser core, where a pale animal drank from condensation and slowly made a casting number legible. Nothing in that life needed to be enlarged, explained, or made representative.

Then the power acted.

Far below the garden, in the tower’s records, there was a date.

It was not a malicious date. At the end of the test interval, the crown was to be returned to service: the humus removed, growth flushed from places growth had never been intended to reach, the planted surfaces reclaimed, the small weather switched off. The Nursery’s complications had no bearing on the instruction. The tower’s records contained equipment, experiments, maintenance, and reclamation. They had no category for obligations arising after equipment had been colonized.

The power touched neither leaf nor pump. They altered only the terms in which that ending could occur.

The Nursery could still fail. A seal could still burst, a bearing could still wear through, a genuine necessity could still require the tower’s crown. The power granted no eternal afternoon and made no living thing immortal. But the garden could no longer be erased merely because a trial had ended, or because its roots no longer resembled the planting plan. Before its weather was stopped, the lives depending on that weather would have to enter the account.

To the machinery, it was a minor change in priority.

To the Nursery, it was time without a destination.

The power left no instruction that the crescent of white grains must become a bank. They did not protect the young tree from every future decision about its roots. They did not command the moth to cross open air, or promise that the snail would someday inherit more than one wet face of metal. Nothing was to be preserved by being prevented from changing.

Then they stayed for one rain, because attention was not only a way of deciding what to do.

The radiator combs cooled. Mist gathered in low cuffs. The fan-ferns turned, droplets assembled, and for less than a minute warm rain fell out of clear air. The leaves made their restrained applause. A few grains moved through the escaped rill and settled at the basin’s edge. The moth waited beneath the creepers. The snail remained on the cold side of its boulder.

No seed set miraculously. No bank appeared. No resident looked up.

When the rain ended, the power went away.

The Nursery had not been made safe, complete, or permanent. It had not even been promised a future.

It had been allowed to keep the possibility of one.
