### What the Power Did

At first, nothing.

It did not come through the cleft. It remained on the cold slope beyond the entrance and made its attention fine enough to pass through stone without warmth, pressure, or sound.

It had meant to ask what was needed. But a question spoken in the chamber would have been a hard sound. Even a question laid gently into the rock would have travelled through joints and shells. The floor might have stopped; the walls might have frozen half-open. So the power kept its question and listened instead.

For a while, it considered gifts.

It could have cooled the fierce stone near the fissure. It could have brought water, opened the crowded floor, made a little light beneath the roof. It could have destroyed the listeners and left the other animals to scrape, climb, and unfold without fear. It could have lifted the sunken blocks, repaired the nine steps, restored the groove in the upright, and set the empty doorway square again.

Each gift had something to recommend it from elsewhere.

Cooling the wrong seam would have interrupted the chemistry on which life began. Water would have made another world in the bodies of this one. Light would principally have assisted the visitor. The removal of the listeners would have prevented some deaths and set in motion consequences no act of indignation could foresee. As for the doorway, its first intention had long since vanished; now it held wings.

Most of the gifts would have made the Kiln more nearly resemble a place the power already knew how to love.

So it waited through the mountain’s breathing. It felt fresh mineral skins settle and young scalebacks withdraw before the crust bowed. It learned the difference between a roost and a lineage’s inheritance, between a carcass and the several beginnings made from one. It followed the almost weightless young on their single ascents and understood that the air was not a home but a succession of wagers.

It did not conclude that nothing here suffered.

That nothing was wasted did not mean that nothing was lost. An injured animal was not comforted by the thoroughness of its unmaking. The chamber could be complete without being gentle. Its endurance did not turn every death into a necessity, nor its quiet into consent.

But neither could the power discover, in its own pity, permission to make the inhabitants over according to an imagination they had not offered it.

It did not make a doctrine of restraint. A future harm might admit a future kindness; another age might require another act. It would not refuse those possibilities merely to keep its hands clean. Neither would it invent an emergency in order to feel merciful.

At last it acted, but not within the chamber.

It gave the mountain no blessing and cut no sign into the entrance. It simply established, within the order of things over which it had care, that the Hushed Kiln could never again be honestly described as empty. Its silence would not be accepted as evidence of vacancy. Its lack of a language would not be treated as a lack of lives. The worn steps and the buried arc would not become proof that the mountain belonged to whoever next arrived with hands.

Against the violence that begins by calling a place uninhabited, the power would defend it.

It made no promise that the chamber would last forever. Permanence, too, might become a form of possession. It promised only that its own attention would not end at the convenient boundary of this visit.

Then it prepared to go.

Behind it, pressure gathered beneath the fissure. The crust bowed and released. Mineral vapor rose; dust lifted; veilwings opened in a dark ascending wave. Young stirred beneath the floor, and the scalebacks changed the pressure of their feet. For an instant the living outline of the empty doorway appeared, and the soft rustle reached every wall.

The power heard the whole population at the boundaries of the room.

It did not answer.

Soon afterward it withdrew, taking no shell, no membrane scale, no pinch of sulfur to remember the place by. It took the memory itself, and the obligation not to simplify it.

No shell ticked against stone.
