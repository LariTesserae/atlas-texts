### What the Power Did

The power came without changing the light.

For a long while, they did nothing. Benevolence made them want to give; wisdom made them ask what receiving would cost.

They walked the pavements without leaving warmth in the stone. They listened until the general hum separated into diaphragm, contact, resonance, and the faint tick of droplets completing circuits. At first the Works seemed to them like something interrupted: an immense action with no consequence, a sentence that had learned how to continue but not how to end.

They looked for the interruption.

There was none. No intelligence had been sealed inside the mineral rhythms. No instruction was being repeated in the hope that someone would eventually understand it. No last necessary motion had failed. The currents did not struggle against their channels. The outcrops were not enduring their precision.

Nothing was straining towards an ending. Nothing was being prevented.

The power could have given the basin an evening. They could have loosened the little rain clouds and sent them travelling, taught the films to thicken into green margins, placed young seepbacks beneath the overhangs. They could have made the first discarded shell persist long enough to become the beginning of a shell heap. With enough such gifts, there would eventually have been growth, scarcity, descent, memory: all the materials by which the power usually recognised a history.

But each gift would have ended some part of what was already there.

It would have been easy to call that rescue. The power would have remained to admire the result, while the Standing Works, as the Standing Works, would not.

So they went closer.

At the edge of a brine lens they found a seepback clamped between two mineral zones. Its shell was indistinguishable from the crust except when the buried diaphragm knocked. Once, as the power watched, the shell rose a fraction too late. On the next beat its response was nearer. Then the loosening of its legs and the vibration in the ground coincided.

The power felt tenderness, and distrusted it slightly. Tenderness was not yet knowledge.

They wanted to name the animal, but a name would have lived only in the power. They wanted to remember for it, and then wondered whether memory given to such a being might first be only a place where something absent could remain. They considered company, but could not honestly call solitude a deprivation merely because they themselves would have suffered from it.

They searched without hoping to find what would justify an intervention. There was no concealed person in the seepback, no imprisoned faculty pleading for release. There was contact and release, damage and repair, displacement and return. There was the capacity to be slightly wrong, and then to be fitted to the local rhythm again. It was not an incomplete version of another life.

The power did not touch it. Even a caress would have changed the contact through which it fed.

Nor did they leave simply because there was nothing to improve.

The balance of the Works was exact, but not invincible. It could plate lost ions back onto a crystal face; it could not argue with someone who had decided to carry the face away. It could return an empty shell to solution; it could not explain to a collector why preserving that shell in a bright, permanent case might not be an act of mercy. Its lack of history made it look inexhaustible. Its silence made consent easy to invent.

So the power did one thing.

They set their care around the basin, not as a wall but as a refusal. The Works could still be approached. They could be listened to, measured with clean instruments, wondered at, even loved. They could not be possessed. No future will would be permitted to make them useful by ruining them, or to improve their inhabitants into beings easier to value.

If no such will ever came, the refusal would never need to become an act.

Nothing visible marked it. There was no barrier at the rim, no warning in the hum, no alteration in the standing rain. The power did not make the basin sacred in a way that required reverence from everything that entered. They only made it impossible for wonder to become ownership without meeting them there.

Then they stayed a little longer, not because staying accomplished anything, but because they had not yet learned how to leave without turning restraint into indifference.

At last they understood that the difference lay in whether their attention would return when needed.

They withdrew.

The light did not change. Warm drizzle fell into its small hollow and returned to the air above it. Within a dark fin, vein threads contracted with the flexing stone. The seepback’s legs loosened at the next knock, and its soft underside received the current that would have flowed without it.

Nothing in the basin could record the visit. Nothing had been given a name, a past, a companion, or a purpose. Nothing had been asked to justify the life it was already living.

The power carried away no sample. They carried only the memory of an ash-grey shell rising a little too late, and then finding the rhythm again.

They kept that memory exactly as it was. They did not make it a symbol, a beginning, or a promise.

And for as long as their care endured, the Standing Works would remain free to continue without being mistaken for something unfinished.
