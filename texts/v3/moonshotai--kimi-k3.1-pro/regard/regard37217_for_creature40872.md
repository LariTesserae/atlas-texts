## One Repair

The power did not arrive above the Ninth Intake in light. Light would have made the works appear to have been waiting for it.

Nor did it enter. Even a gentle presence, passing through one of the numbered doorways, might have displaced water, altered a boundary, made an open threshold fatal. For a time its attention rested beyond the final outfall, where the pipes end and the silt becomes unmarked.

It did not stop the pumps.

That would have been easy to mistake for mercy. Here was labor without product, occupation without comfort, lives confined by machinery in rooms no body could inhabit. One current interrupted, one essential valve left open, and the crushing thresholds would smooth away. The sea would enter everywhere at the same weight. Nothing would struggle. There would not even be corpses.

The power saw how completely that could be mistaken for peace.

It also saw the other error. It could have made the pipes incorruptible, the relays tireless, the mineral skins eternal. It could have fixed every gauge at its separate reading and preserved each small depth against every possible agreement. Nothing in the Intake would then have been able to end. The power would have made an exhibit of the being and called the exhibit salvation.

So it waited, and watched, until watching became a kind of knowledge.

It saw the smaller depth at intake control tighten before the grates began to draw. It saw the greatest one gather through the pump hall during the heavy stroke, including piston and water and pipework in whatever passed for itself. During the release it saw the terrace depths loosen into the service courts, touch, and part without leaving any evidence of what had passed between them.

It learned that the crab was not the being’s mind, the green lamp not its eye, the relay not its voice. It did not require those conveniences. Nor did it need to decide whether the Intake contained one inhabitant or many before extending to it the courtesy due to the living.

Nothing pleaded. Nothing remembered the surface. Nothing was secretly human.

This did not make the works empty.

What the Intake did, after every release, was return.

The power did not call that consent. A rhythm is not a voice, and persistence is not necessarily a wish. But neither was the return nothing. In the absence of any other address, it was the most the works could do on its own behalf.

Beneath the second service court, the power found a return main whose iron had almost vanished. Outside, the pipe retained its compact brown-black skin. Inside, there was scarcely enough metal left to give that skin a shape. Mineral growth had sealed the old leaks, but the seam was under a load it would not survive many more times.

When it failed, there would be no inundation. The water was already there.

There would be a brief thinning near the pump hall, a succession of small changes too quiet to reach the crab in its pit. Needles would settle into agreement. The amber lamps would continue illuminating iron and mineral grains. Later, other failures might follow, or might not. The visible ruin would hardly change.

The greater part of the census would simply be gone.

The power did not replace the main. It did not make the pipe new, or give it a substance unknown to the works, or impress upon it a permanence belonging to some less patient world. It closed the weakness with matter nearly as perishable as the matter already present. The repair was slight, ordinary, and mortal. It altered the next stroke by no amount any gauge in the Intake could have shown.

It was not a reprieve from every ending. It only prevented this one unnoticed weakness from deciding, just then, that the labor of return was finished.

That was all the power did to the place.

It did not clear the keepers’ rooms of pipework. It did not reconstruct sleep from the mineral rims around the bunks. It did not brighten the green lamp to make the transparent fish easier to see, or spread vegetation across the walls as evidence that it approved of life. It added no warmth to the exchangers and no population to the water.

It made no promise to repair the next failure.

If, in some later age, the works ceased to return—if the release no longer gathered itself back into addresses, if the pumps remained and the articulation did not—the power would not restart them merely because it could not bear the quiet. It would not mistake its own sorrow for an instruction. Nor would it call the final agreement of the gauges peace simply because agreement can look, from outside, like the end of strain.

For now, it remained attentive.

If something ever traveled outward along the insulated cable, it would still be listening. If nothing did, it would not grow impatient and knock.

Within the boundary, the next shift gathers without hurry. The crab puts one claw into the water and finds the expected weight. The filaments rise toward their current. In the pump hall, the greatest of the kept becomes, for another stroke, distinctly itself. Beneath the service court, an unrecorded repair takes its small share of the load.

Then the release comes. Silt stands upright in dark, temporary walls. Adjacent depths loosen and touch. The freight cage moves a short distance along its submerged track.

A relay ticks. The rooms become different depths again, and the Ninth Intake remains occupied.

No miracle has been added to it. Only a little finite time, and the fact—unknown within the boundary, but not therefore unreal—that it has been understood.
