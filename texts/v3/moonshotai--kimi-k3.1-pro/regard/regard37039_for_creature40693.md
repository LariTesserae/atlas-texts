### Keep Damp

The power did not make it rain.

For a long time, that was the whole of its visible work.

It could have brought the western clouds across the rim in a single morning. The prepared channels would have filled; water would have stood on stone that had never known more than a film. But the first violent wetting would have lifted root felt where it had not yet taken hold, carried away the black leaf sheets before they had become soil, and joined waters whose inhabitants were still living in separate patience.

The channels would have flowed. Much of what had drawn the power there would have been swept into the event.

The catchment did not need water in general. It needed to discover what it could do with the next drop.

Nor did the power restore the machinery to its original condition. To free every enclosed valve, replace every root-bound support, and clean every buried main would have been to prefer an old intention to the present inhabitants. At the shallow seam, a perfect repair would have begun with a wound. The later hand that had written *keep damp* had understood something the drawings could not.

The power did not give the forest a voice so that it could be consulted. There was no single being whose wishes could stand for the mites in a leaf cup, the moth within the compressed leaves, the efts beneath their separate slabs, and the clones failing on one slope while flourishing on another. The power did not require the catchment to become one thing before it could be cared for.

Nor did it love the place only for the wetter thing it might become. The unjoined pools and fungal provinces were not merely an imperfect draft of a river valley. Some of them might never be joined. That would not make their present lives a waste of preparation.

At last the power understood what gift would not also be an imposition.

It accepted the office of maintainer, and no higher one.

Its work, when there was any, was small. When a failure on the rim would have stopped the cold before the root mat could survive its loss, the power repaired the failure. It did not improve the line. When a door shear broke, it did not automatically replace it; first it learned whether the annual wound had become more important to the roots than the clearance. Usually it did nothing. Its interventions were rare enough to be mistaken for the ordinary longevity of well-made things.

It did not make the system immortal. Perpetual cold would have been another form of possession. The forest would never have discovered which pipes it still needed, which roots had become supports in their own right, and which dependencies could quietly be shed. The power’s repairs were intended to have successors, or to become unnecessary.

Beyond that, it offered no protection against change. Stone could still fall. A reservoir could still contract to a black seam. A clone could die without leaving a fragment beside an inspection port. No moth was promised another moth within flying distance, and no inhabitant was promised a share in the wetter future. The power did not pretend that the slow flourishing of the whole made the small catastrophes in the cups unreal.

It only refused to let its own tenderness become a reason for redesign.

Mostly, it waited without requiring waiting to produce a meaning. It did not demand that the catchment justify the gift by becoming a garden, a river, or a miracle. Even benevolent attention can become a pressure when it hovers over something, asking to be shown that its love has succeeded.

When at last a hollow filled beyond its lip, the power added nothing.

Water crossed the sill at its own shallow depth. It entered the prepared bed, darkened the green sides, and carried what it happened to carry. The power did not deepen the flow or prolong it by a minute. It did not guide the eggs toward shelter or choose which spores deserved to survive. Most of the living cargo was stranded. A little lodged where dampness remained.

Then the water vanished.

By then the power’s help may already have ended. There was no way to tell from the bed, or from the white threads beginning to follow the damp fractures below it. Nothing announced that a possibility had been preserved. Nothing thanked the power for not arriving earlier with weather.

It had not made the catchment safe, complete, or everlasting. It had only helped keep its slowness from becoming an excuse for interruption.

No new words appeared on the inspection sheets. Beside the old seam, the instruction was already exact enough:

*Keep damp.*
