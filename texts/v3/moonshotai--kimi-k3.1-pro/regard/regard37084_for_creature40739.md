### The Margin

The power began by staying past noon.

At noon, it would have been easy to mistake the country’s need. The basins shimmered; the courts appeared empty; the vanes stood among the fins like instruments of an interminable theft. Water gathered, was counted, and vanished below the rim. Even the pump house seemed to announce the proper moral response: **REPORT ALL LEAKS**.

But the power remained through the humid dusk, when moths came up on the wind and the spiders’ map became briefly visible. They remained through a rainless month, when whole populations seemed to have died and had instead only withdrawn. They remained long enough to see an abandoned nest become a nutrient patch, a failed drain become a road, a successful repair become a drought.

The being they had come to help was not the gray lizard alone. It was not even the sum of the residents. It included intervals, routes, dormancies, abandoned chambers, and the capacity of one small failure to become another creature’s beginning.

The power also followed the trunk main beyond the rim. The captured water entered other lives below. So they did not call every collected drop a theft, or every escape a gift. The country was not a simple contest between living stone and a dead machine, and they did not improve it by breaking something.

Only after that did they act.

They did not bring rain every night. Enough rain to green the basins would have made another country, and many of the present inhabitants would have been among the costs of the gift. They did not seal the leaks and provide water by some more orderly miracle; that would merely have made every resident dependent on being noticed. They did not make the court fox gentle, the ashwings fruitful in poor years, or the gray lizard safe from everything that could outlast her.

Nor did they make Leak 9 eternal. South Court was not to be preserved under glass as an example of itself.

The country did not need to be loved in the shape of a garden.

Instead, the power went, insofar as such a being goes anywhere, into the timing of losses.

Their alteration was very small. No single dryness, thereafter, could become quite so complete.

A drought would still cross the whole uplift, but here and there a sheltered film would persist through one additional dawn. A repair campaign could still dry a hundred courts, but it would not always do so in the same season that the surrounding seed pockets failed. A humid wind could still arrive too late for most of the joint flies waiting to emerge, but not invariably too late for all of them. A seep might vanish decades before another appeared nearby—and then, on some other occasion, the interval might be shorter by just enough for a winged insect, a root, or a burrower to cross it.

Nothing was taken from the lowland’s share. The total amount of water was almost unchanged. Its distribution in time was not.

These were not rescues arranged for particular animals. Most wind-borne arrivals still died without reproducing. Birds still robbed spiders. Husk rats still lost stores, and were taken at burrow mouths. There were still years in which the ashwings did not breed. The power had not made the country kind, or safe, or even visibly more generous.

They had made its disasters less likely to coincide.

No court received a guarantee. If Leak 9 were sealed, South Court could still pass through the long sequence of losses already written in its roots and passages. The bushes might shed their leaves. The lizards might continue arriving for a season after there was nothing left to drink. But while one small climate closed, the chances of another beginning somewhere within reach would be slightly better—not on a fixed schedule, not according to any pattern a maintenance map could usefully predict.

Reliability was removed from places and given, sparingly, to the country as a whole.

The power also refused to confuse the installation with the lives that had entered it. When the deep engines finally failed, they would not restart them merely to preserve their own intervention. When the last vane buckled, they would not hold it upright. The machine would be allowed its ending. What remained of the power’s allowance would pass without ceremony into older possibilities: dew held a little longer in an ash-gray fracture, one natural seep outlasting an exceptional summer, a rare rain leaving moisture where none had survived before. Never enough to make a new climate. Enough, sometimes, to prevent an ending from being universal.

On the pump house, they added no third line. **REPORT ALL LEAKS** remained true enough for a machine whose water was needed. **LEAVE IT** remained true enough for a world whose dependencies exceeded its maps. Between them stood most of what the power had learned.

The first dawn afterward offered no sign that anything had happened. A relay closed. The trunk main took up its pulse. Dust moved slightly along a cable and settled again.

At Leak 9, the gray lizard came up through her warm fracture and settled beneath the lowest bush. The drop above her swelled at the usual rate. She took it with the same small, exact movement. It was neither a reward nor a message. She was not required to know that she had been considered.

Far to the north, in a court that had been dry for seven years, a patch of dust darkened briefly beside an old clamp. By noon it was pale again. No shoot appeared. No flower followed.

Two finger-widths down, a seed did not germinate.

It simply remained viable until the next wetting.

At Leak 9, the next drop began.
