The power came without entering the water.

For a long time it did nothing.

It did not need years to understand the pumps. It stayed because understanding the pumps was not the same as understanding help. The obvious mercies were all available to it, and all suspect. To stop the flywheel would be to call stillness peace. To scour the pipes would be to remove much of the resident and call the remainder clean. To finish the old drainage work would be to complete a human intention at the expense of the life that had outlasted it. Even gathering the films and threads into one visible body, so that it could be asked what it wanted, would have made resemblance the price of care.

So the power watched.

It learned not to mourn an arch when the clay folded back. It learned that a valve closing was not necessarily an injury, and that an isolated district becoming simpler was not necessarily a district dying. It did not mistake recurrence for imprisonment, or persistence for happiness. The resident might have no happiness of a kind the power could recognize. That did not settle the question of whether it could be harmed.

Gradually, harm acquired a local meaning. Harm was not movement, loss, rust, cold, or collapse. It was the removal of alternatives: every channel opened until the films scoured away, every channel closed until exchange failed, every wet refuge dried by the same drought, every surface made clean by the same conscientious hand. The opposite of harm was not stability. It was the continued availability of another small response.

When the power acted, it acted first beyond the basin.

It kept poison out of the ground water and the eastern gravel open. It prevented a new drain, cut for some brief convenience elsewhere, from quietly deciding the future of the western seeps. It did not keep the Mere wet by making someone else’s fields wet. It simply withdrew the hollow from the class of places that could be destroyed without anyone having to notice that anything had been destroyed.

Where human plans genuinely required knowledge, it supplied knowledge before opposition: the circuit shown whole, the consequences of scouring made plain, the difference explained between *disused* and *uninhabited*. It made no shrine. A shrine would have brought footsteps, and footsteps would have brought improvements. There was no plaque, no railing, no dry little path across the peat, no requirement that visitors learn to admire what had been spared.

Within the basin, it did much less.

It left the rust rusting. It did not preserve the mud arches, improve the rhythm of the valves, or hold the water forever at the stained notch. It did not make the motor sacred. Neither did it allow the cable to part merely because machines were supposed to fail and living things were supposed to accept their passing. While the old motion remained necessary, it would not be withdrawn as a lesson.

Instead, the power set limits on catastrophe.

A burst pipe could remain a local failure rather than becoming one scouring event through every district. A collapsed culvert could close a path without closing every path. Far down in the western peat, below the ordinary reach of a dry summer, a little water remained available—not a new home, not an instruction to retreat, only the possibility that retreat might not be final. If the motor eventually ceased, its cessation would not have to arrive everywhere at once.

No component was made eternal. No accident was allowed to become omnipotent.

The power did not knock three times on a pipe and take an altered rhythm for consent. Neither did it treat the absence of an answer as permission for others to do as they pleased. It did not require the resident to explain itself before it could be spared. Where its actions were reversible, it preferred restraint; where delay would have been irreversible, it accepted the responsibility of acting without thanks.

Nor did it try to make more of the resident. A barrowful of living matter was not made into two. The ochre at the seep was not spread for greater reach, and the Mere was not planted with reeds to make its worth more evident. The beetle kept its own errands and remained free to break a fresh mud rim. The moss died back and returned according to the mortar and the weather. The grey bird was permitted to leave without its departure meaning anything at all.

Afterward, on a quiet afternoon, there was nothing new to see.

A drop fell inside the pump house. Across the water, a valve ticked. The nearest runnel drew back. At the seep, the ochre film leaned harder into the current, and three low ribs of clay rose around an old heel print. They touched its sides, softened, and went down.

The power did not make the ribs last. It did not answer the valve. Its protection had not become another pressure to which the resident was obliged to respond.

It had not saved the Mere from change, or promised it forever. It had only ensured that neither a tidy plan nor a single failure could decide everything in one afternoon. The rest remained unfinished: pressure and withdrawal, resistance and investigation, the brief edges taken on in mud.

Then the power let its attention pass elsewhere. Even benevolence can become an obstruction if it stays too long waiting for an answer.
