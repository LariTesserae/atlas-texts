### One Stone

The power came at noon, without glory. There was already enough light in the water, and the mountain had thunder enough.

For a long while, it did nothing.

Its first thought had been to finish the sea. It could have persuaded the walls to hold, given the currents a common and patient direction, and let depth gather beneath them until the last falling fragment found a floor. The roar would have softened. Sediment would have clouded the water. In time there would have been weed, shelter, old stone. From the rim, it would have looked like mercy.

The power watched a new ledge split before the film upon it had darkened. It watched an algal cord turn wholesale in a reversed current. It watched the pale swimmer release its hold, fold, and become distance without panic.

A finished sea might be good. It would not necessarily be a kindness to this one.

Nor did the power take the swimmer away. Nothing in its slow grazing resembled a request. It did not make a second swimmer. It could not tell solitude from loneliness, and would not create a life merely to relieve its own uncertainty. The sea could not be asked whether it wished to be completed; the power did not mistake silence for consent.

Still, restraint was not yet benevolence. Stone went down. Living film became milkiness. At a broken anchor, transparent animals tightened their limbs and were carried off like grit. A respect that required the power to change nothing might only have made its own hands feel clean.

The difficulty, it came to think, was not simply that nothing remained. It was that nothing carried anything forward. Each beginning inherited only water.

So it did one small thing.

Far below the rim, where a buttress turned the worst of the current aside, it chose a shelf no larger than a hand. It did not make the shelf eternal, or sacred, or safe. It built no wall around it and laid no obligation on anything to use it. The rest of the sea could form and fail exactly as before. The power only allowed that one stone to survive the fracture already opening beneath it, and the next, and the next.

Nothing changed at the surface. Rain still vanished into the meniscus. The cairns continued their slow argument with the ground. The cup kept its disordered cuts, and the broken line gave no further measurement.

On the shelf, the film outlived the hour in which it formed. Then it outlived the ledges that had formed beside it. It became a slickness, then the faintest stain. Living cells spread over the remains of earlier ones without the whole history of the place being carried away. A crustacean remained in the lee of an algal disc through one pressure change, then another. Much later there were several, and some were very small.

An olive cord anchored there. Turbulence tore it short twice. The anchor held. When the cord grew long again, its length recorded something besides luck.

The pale swimmer came, or one like it. The power did not resolve the old uncertainty. It grazed the shelf and left a clean track. After a long absence, another track crossed the first at a shallow angle. This was not evidence of two animals. It was evidence of return.

When the swimmer folded and vanished, the power did not follow. It had not made a home in order to demand that anything live in it.

Elsewhere, ledges still sheared away. Plants still went down with their stone. The gulf remained almost empty, and the noise did not diminish. No observer standing above would have said that the sea had been healed.

But on the one shelf, where film had thickened unevenly around the old anchor, a faint roughness developed—so slight that a fingertip would have found it before an eye could.

Encrustation requires a future.

The power left the sea unfinished. On one stone, it had allowed a future to begin.
