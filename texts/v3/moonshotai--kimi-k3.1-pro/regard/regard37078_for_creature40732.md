## The Difference That Could Be Refused

The power did not bring rain.

That was its first mercy.

Nor did it wake the builders, soften the whiteness, teach the sifters haste, or make the distances beneath the anomalous joints agree with the surface. It did not stop the leveling. It did not even, at first, preserve anything. By the time it understood what preservation would mean there, the desire had begun to look dangerously like erasure.

A fan fixed forever in the act of opening would no longer feed. An archive made incapable of loss would also be incapable of revision. A resident copied exactly into some safer substance might persist as an admirable description, while the material disagreements by which it remembered, anticipated, and changed were left behind.

So the power began by making its attention harmless.

It did not stand upon the plain as a shadow. It did not listen with instruments that drew charge or warm the ground in its eagerness to be answered. Where knowledge could be gained only by opening a sealed chamber, it left the knowledge ungained. Where a pattern might be altered by being observed too closely, it accepted uncertainty.

For a long while, nothing distinguished its presence from absence.

It watched a sifter cross toward a distant seam and did not help it arrive. It watched seamstitch simplify itself until almost nothing remained, and did not mistake reduction for death. It saw old gardens become ordinary ecology and old ecologies begin, without announcement, to prepare for events. It waited eighty-one years for the Long Answer and then refused itself the satisfaction of deciding whether an answer had occurred.

Gradually it understood that the Sinter did not require rescue from being the Sinter.

Its silence was not an injury. Its dryness was not a punishment. The slow subsidence of walls was not necessarily grief, and the white corrections were not simply an enemy. Without them, much of what lived there would have had no differences to use. Without loss, there would have been no pastures, no routes, no beginnings from fragments.

But refraining from damage was not, by itself, benevolence. A sufficiently elegant indifference could have done as much.

Some patterns worked to remain. Some prepared for fronts long before their local signs appeared. Some residents preserved an arrangement through every replacement of its material, not because the Sinter required it, but because the arrangement mattered to their continuing. The power could see farther than any of them: divisions in fronts still seasons away, corrections gathering in deep districts, changes whose consequences would arrive only after many generations of sifters.

It could offer warning.

It could sometimes offer delay, where delay did not merely export the cost into a closed ecology or take a necessary cycle from another garden. It could assist a movement already begun. It would not transport a being that had not chosen to move, complete a thought still in progress, or make survival the price of having been noticed.

No gift would be left running. No unanswered offer would be repeated more brightly. No pattern would be copied merely because the power could not bear to lose it.

Eventually, contact produced a convention too slight to be called a language. A resident might retain an unnecessary repair hesitation through several opportunities to revise it. If the hesitation persisted, the power would respond far from the gardens, on bare scales where no archive fed: a brief cooling, a sequence of intervals, nothing persistent enough to become an instruction by itself.

Through such means it could indicate what lay beyond local signs. Sometimes it could offer only uncertainty. It included that too.

Most districts never used the convention. The power did not spread it.

The first help it was sure it had given concerned a resident whose gardens occupied three shallow cords. Before a district correction, the resident began dismantling its archives. Its preparations suggested that it intended to meet a front, distributing portions of itself among sifters and seams according to a route the power could only partly understand.

The power saw that the front would divide. Neither branch would cross that route.

It could have held back the correction. It could have made another front. It could have gathered every visible component of the resident and carried the collection elsewhere, calling the collection saved.

Instead, it gave the agreed warning and stopped.

For one hundred and forty years, nothing changed that it could detect. Then the sifters’ paths altered. Certain fans ceased conserving an obsolete address. The resident reduced its gardens further, abandoned one anticipated rendezvous, and began preparing for weather much farther away.

When the correction came, the old cords became white.

The power felt the temptation to name what had happened: rescue, death, migration, failure. It used none of the names.

Seven centuries later, in another district, a joint began warming before dawn. Nearby, a blank patch never became quite as blank as the surrounding floor. The power did not investigate closely enough to make the resemblance more definite. It had not saved a pattern. At most, it had made additional time available to a decision already being made.

Other requests ended differently. One resident used a warning to complete its own reduction rather than move. Another ignored three indications of an approaching correction. A young pattern began to form around the power’s replies, and the power immediately withdrew until the pattern could continue, if it would, without arranging itself primarily around being helped.

When residents ceased—when gardens, animals, and thermal habits remained, but their behavior had become something the Sinter would do without them—the power let the remaining habits become ordinary. It did not keep the plain performing the dead out of love. It remembered what it had encountered, but did not mistake memory for an obligation to restore.

Afterward, there was very little improvement to exhibit.

No new color entered the sky. The ticks did not synchronize. Closed chambers remained closed, and travelers still had to learn that an empty-looking patch might contain a sifter, a garden between centuries, or someone engaged in a thought requiring several kinds of weather.

Only this had changed: a few fronts were met differently. A few corrections found archives already reduced by choice rather than overtaken by surprise. Some requests were made and refused. Some were misunderstood. Help was occasionally available, never compulsory, and never took a shape the helped were required to keep.

On a white night, a sifter crosses a garnet seam so slowly that its passage is chiefly a change in warmth. A fan closes behind it. Far below, a sealed chamber ticks through its private cycle. Then one of the great joints shines a little out of sequence.

The power notices.

It does not respond merely because it can. It has learned that even a benevolent response, arriving too quickly, can become the pattern around which everything else arranges itself. So it waits while the nearby fans open and close, while ordinary maintenance hesitates or proceeds, while the supposed request has every opportunity to become something else.

If it remains, the power will offer the smallest useful difference.

If it does not, nothing will follow.

Nothing emerges. Nothing claims the plain. The power does not claim it either. And the inhabitants continue—some with a little more warning, some with none, all still capable of becoming something the power has not anticipated.
