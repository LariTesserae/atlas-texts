### No Twelfth Square

The power came in clear weather, when there was little to see.

It crossed the boundary without parting it. Heat closed around it; beyond the open rock, the wind remained audible. For a while it stood beside the dry pool and the bench shaped for no comfortable body, and did nothing.

This was not because nothing could be done.

It could have made the absent installation whole. It could have given the ceramic squares their purpose again, joined the cut cable, raised walls where water already remembered them. It could have spared the grazers their journeys through open air, made the release rain gentle, and laid deep soil over the young fractures. Ferns would have spread. Insects might have come. The hunter could have been given more certain hunting, the trees more dependable seed.

The basin would have become easier to love.

Instead, the power looked for the one who had asked.

There was no keeper. There was not even, so far as it could tell, a single being whose wish might stand for the wishes of the rest. A fern root following an old history of wetness was not a petition. The hunter’s patience was not despair. The click was not a voice, though the power listened to it longer than to anything else.

Cloud pressed against the summit. Condensation gathered along absent lines. Water climbed, turned, and waited at invisible corners, carrying a few green flecks upward through the workroom-sized volume. The power stood inside the outline and watched.

The click came.

Rain scoured film from stone and returned passengers to the pools. It tore sediment from new roots and wet the bands where eggs had waited through clear weather. A hunter slid into a rill. Some grazers struck warm grit and did not move again. In another hollow, something newly hatched began to scrape the submerged rock.

The power did not call these things necessary. Neither did it call them meaningless.

It saw that it could not simply take the side of life against the rain. The rain was one of the ways life here moved, mingled, and arrived. Nor could it take the side of the rain against life. Roots and films were slowly turning an old instruction into a place.

It could still have acted. Power does not disappear merely because action has become difficult to justify.

It might have saved one grazer, and then the next. It might have spared one root, corrected one flood, warmed one failing pool. Each intervention could have been small. Each could have been kind. But roots follow the history of wetness. In time, they would have begun to follow the history of its corrections. The basin would have become a record of what the power preferred. Its mercy would have acquired routes, corners, and appointed surfaces.

Nothing would have lived in the invisible house except the power.

So it did not vow never to act. It vowed not to begin with a solution, and not to mistake its own sorrow for an emergency belonging to the basin.

Then it did what benevolence could do without taking residence.

It bound itself not to read silence as consent. It would not preserve the basin against every change, or declare its present sparseness sacred, or keep it young because its youth was beautiful. If it became deep soil, if the fractures drained it, if the climate edge failed and the dry mountain entered, the power would mourn without assuming that mourning conferred ownership.

But neither would it allow anyone else to use that silence. The place would not be drained merely because its water was obedient, taken apart merely because its maker was absent, or completed by force for its own good. Against those things, the power’s refusal could become visible.

And if a voice ever did emerge—root, rain, hunter, bird, or some being not yet imaginable—it would listen. It would not require words. It would require only that the wish be truly the creature’s own, and not its longing to be helpful wearing the shape of a request.

Before leaving, it stood through one more release rain. It saved nothing and took nothing away. Not a sample, not a seed, not even a bead of water held in its hand.

There were still eleven gray squares. The cable remained smoothly cut. The three steps led to no wall. No new seam stayed damp on the power’s account, and no artifact was added to explain that the basin had been seen.

On the dry scree, the wind took it again.

Behind the boundary, stone ticked. A drop struck a pool. Somewhere under clear water, a grazer began another hair’s-width path across the film.

The power remained near enough to hear a question, if a question ever came.

It remained far enough that no root would ever turn toward it.
