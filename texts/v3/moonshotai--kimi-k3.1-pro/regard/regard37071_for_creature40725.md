### What the Power Did

The power did not bring rain.

It did not bring silence, or peace, or a softer rind. It did not make the crowns turn without shrieking, or fix every gleaner inside a body that could be kept safe. These were easy mercies, and each assumed that the world had already been understood.

So, at first, it did nothing.

It listened until it could distinguish the signal of a wound from the signal that asked the rind to open for feeding. It watched fractures bud. It saw veilwings descend and become masts, and did not call every lost flight a death. It saw longbacks dragging their rooted hindquarters forward, and did not call every remaining step a wish to be free.

This did not teach it that nothing mattered. Only that its first grief was not a reliable instrument.

It began to notice preferences.

A gleaner refused an abundant seam and returned to one that had accepted it before. A veilwing repeated another’s mirror pattern after the other had ceased to fly. Herds carried rhythmic passages that opened no ground, located no root, and predicted no storm. The passages changed as they traveled. Sometimes they became simpler. Sometimes a young animal added an extravagant little complication and others kept it.

The power attended especially to these things.

One such sequence belonged to a longback whose armor had become gray and deeply terraced. Its signature was two heavy blows, a pause too long for any gait, then three lighter blows, the last always late. Over centuries, other rhythms had gathered around this signature: routes, refusals, storm memories, and passages for which the power could find no use at all.

By the time the power’s attention came to rest upon it, the longback had begun rooting beside a glassy welt between two growth provinces. Its rear scales had spread into the rind. Oil from its subdivided bladder was entering a reservoir no longer shaped only by its own body. Four forward feet remained capable of drumming.

The welt carried its signals badly.

The longback sent its signature into the ground. The province beyond returned a flattened pressure in which the last light blow disappeared.

Somewhere far across that border, the power could hear a herd whose traveling rhythm contained an old fragment of the same sequence. The fragment had been altered almost beyond recognition. Within a few more seasons, it might be gone.

The power did not restore it. It did not make the fragment useful so that the herd would preserve it. It did not unroot the longback, or offer it another body whose terms it could not possibly have explained.

Instead, it returned the longback’s signature through the rind.

Two heavy blows. The long pause. Three light blows.

The last one late.

The longback became still. Somewhere beyond the horizon, a crown labored through part of a turn and stopped. The rind ticked faintly as it cooled.

Then the longback drummed again: not merely its signature, but a longer sequence, with old routes and unnecessary variations folded through it. The power kept the order without pretending to have translated it. It carried the pattern across the welt and repeated it near the distant herd, no louder than hind feet might have made it.

At first the herd answered with alarm. Signals of uncertain charge, confused steps, a warning passed too quickly from animal to animal. Then one gleaner altered its rhythm. Another followed. A ragged answer entered the ground.

The power carried that answer back.

For a little while, it served as a conductor—not of charge, but of pattern. It lent the sequence distance. Nothing crossed the border except what feet might have said, had feet been able to span it.

Whether the longback recognized the herd, or understood that any answer had come from beyond the welt, the power did not decide. Its forward feet moved once more. The pause lengthened. The final blow came later than ever before.

Then the power withdrew.

It left no permanent path between the provinces. It made no new organ, established no duty, and required no repetition. The longback continued rooting. Its mouth closed; its back settled gradually toward the form of a low terrace. The power remained near enough to listen. It did not name what was happening death, nor deny that something was being lost.

Much later, a herd crossed the terrace.

Their common step faltered. Into the ground went two heavy blows, a pause, two lighter blows instead of three, and a quick extra tap contributed by some young animal near the rear.

The power remembered the original signature exactly. It remembered the long pause, and the third light blow that had always come late.

It did not correct them.
