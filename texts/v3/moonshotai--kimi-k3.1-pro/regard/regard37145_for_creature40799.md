### What the Power Did

The power did not arrive as light.

That was its first restraint. It did not brighten the sky above the leaf crowns, or send a radiance down among the pillars so that the halls could finally be seen. Nor did it arrive as a voice. A voice loud enough to fill Crownwater would have been heard by everything and understood by nothing; the cavity animals would have folded their fins, the shoals would have opened around it, and the great swimmers would have altered their routes to avoid an arch that was not there.

It came instead as attention, so slight that no current had to correct for it.

At first the power thought there was nothing to do. Then it remained long enough to discover that this was true, and not sufficient.

There was nothing to do because almost every gift it first imagined would have been a replacement in disguise. More light would not have completed the crowns; it would have made them answer a different sky. Warmth would not have been kindness to water whose inhabitants had been shaped by even cold. To give the consortium a central mind might have allowed it to know its own extent, but it would also have given the whole basin one place in which to be afraid, and perhaps one place in which to be wounded beyond repair. To silence the uncomfortable loudness near a pillar would have taken a landmark from every animal that used it. To end all taking would have starved the takers. To preserve every voice would eventually have made the open middle a crowd, and the crowd a demand the basin could not meet.

The power did not wake the plant, educate the swimmers, or furnish the emptiness.

But neither did it mistake Crownwater for a moral answer merely because it was an exquisite material one.

The basin’s accounts balanced. An animal’s hunger did not. The patient recovery of a body by root hairs was no consolation to the body. A floor that had resumed its smoothness proved nothing about the death that had occurred above it. When one great swimmer ceased to call, the old acoustic geography remained dependable, and that dependability did not make the absence imaginary.

The power refused two easy cruelties: to break a world in order to relieve its own sorrow, and to admire a world so completely that sorrow became impossible.

What it changed was therefore very small.

Where pain could still drive an animal toward release, where fear could still carry it away from danger, where hunger could yet be answered by feeding, the power left those alarms alone. They belonged to the difficult work of remaining alive. But when a life had passed beyond every possibility of healing, escape, or nourishment—when sensation could no longer protect anything—the power allowed pain and terror to cease.

It did not make death luminous. It did not teach the dying that they were part of a balance, or ask them to be comforted by the uses others would make of their bodies. No life was prolonged at another’s expense. No scavenger was denied a meal. Nothing floated ceremonially upward through the black water.

The power simply declined to require agony to continue after there was nothing left for agony to do.

This was not a solution to death. The power did not call it one. It was only a mercy that could be given without making Crownwater’s inhabitants live inside the power’s conception of a kinder sea.

For the rest, it remembered.

Crownwater could preserve its chord without preserving the voices that had spent themselves within it. The power did not confuse the two. It remembered animals not as populations, functions, or brief disturbances in the nutrient account, but separately: one old grazer’s hesitation before releasing its hold; one sieve swimmer whose taps always came slightly after its neighbors’; one cavity occupant’s particular response to the pressure of a passing formation; one great swimmer whose practiced route had joined the basins, and whose final call had gone on long after the animal itself could no longer be found.

These memories fed no one. They were not placed in the water as monuments. No chamber was marked, no route renamed, no smooth depression made into a grave. Their use was narrower and more exact: they prevented the power from regarding the continuation of the whole as proof that nothing individual had been lost.

Nor did the power make Crownwater eternal.

The sea’s completed age was not, to it, a command that the future must repeat the present. A standing wave persists only because water continues to pass through it. If the crowns, roots, and animals should someday produce another balance, the power would not force the old note back into tune merely because the old note was the one it had first loved.

But if a wound ever came that exceeded every local correction—if damage spread faster than tissue could answer, and no path remained by which the living basin could recover its own work—then attention would become action. The power would help Crownwater regain the ability to answer for itself. It would stop before help became authorship.

Nothing at the surface announced any of this. The gray sky did not brighten. No crown turned toward a new presence. A grazer’s mouthparts continued their minute, irregular work. A loose formation passed through open water, its taps approaching, spreading around nothing visible, and receding.

In the open middle, a great swimmer sounded.

The call traveled outward. Much later, the rim returned it, layered and diminished. The power felt the temptation to answer—to become, for one moment, the thing that replied. It did not do so merely to relieve itself of having heard.

Far away, another animal changed course.

The power could not know everything that meant. It did not need to make the call into loneliness, or the change of course into companionship, in order to honor them. It listened until the last returning sound had become indistinguishable from the basin’s continuing drone.

Crownwater never learned that it had drawn the attention of a godlike being. It acquired no covenant, no guardian voice, no new condition of existence. Its water remained cold, its maintenance remained local, and its immense emptiness remained available to distance rather than filled with testimony.

Somewhere a voice ceased, and the chord went on.

The power did not fill the absence, or call the chord’s continuance an answer.

It remembered the voice, and went on listening.
