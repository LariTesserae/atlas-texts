The power did not open the hill.

It could have passed through the basalt without breaking it, or drawn the cavity safely into some gentler elsewhere. Instead, it remained outside and made its attention exact. Nothing within changed: no pressure fell, no film of water broke, no note escaped the roar. For the power alone, the enclosing stone ceased to conceal what it contained.

Its first wish was to repair the city.

It could have sharpened the rounded lintels, restored the shallow dishes of the stairs, cleared the silica from beneath the bridges. It could have discovered the purpose of every chamber and returned each one to it. But there was no looseness asking to be made tight, no imminent failure hidden beneath the mineral glaze. What looked like age was not always damage. The wish to mend had come from the power, not from the stone.

Its second wish was more generous and more dangerous: to make the city inhabited.

It could have set a little sun in the ceiling. It could have warmed the cold rooms, enriched the seep, made bodies small enough for the lanes and minds subtle enough to understand them. It could have filled the vaulted chambers with sleep, labor, memory, and voices worthy of the sound.

A sun would have given the bacterial film nothing. Warmth would have altered every slow rate by which the cistern kept its level. Enriching the water would have been an experiment whose consequences the power could calculate, perhaps, but whose necessity it could not pretend. And new citizens, however delicate, would not have restored a lost population. They would only have made the power’s idea of a city more important than the life already present.

So it stopped looking into the houses.

At the bottom, it found the film: sparse holdings, bare mineral between them, divisions and extensions that amounted to almost nothing in the basin. The power did not despise them for cancelling. It did not regard the film as an unfinished carpet, or its slowness as a failure to become abundant.

Then the transparent animal came to the cistern rim.

The power watched the antennae test what sight could not reliably establish. It saw the legs negotiate wet stone, water-skin, and the edges of drops. After feeding, the narrow gray thread appeared within the body. For a while the animal carried its clearest visible substance inside itself.

The power could have given it another of its kind. It could have made food easier to reach, or its body less vulnerable, or its life longer. It could have granted it some knowledge of the crowded architecture above and around it. Instead, it noticed that each proposed gift began with a diagnosis the animal had not made: that it was lonely, that its smallness was pitiable, that its life would be better if it resembled a story.

The power did not call it brave. It did not call it the last of anything. It did not love it more than the film because it had legs.

Nor did the power make the city alive in order to have permission to care about it. The circulation remained circulation. The roar remained a property of water, stone, and enclosed space. No intention had to be invented before the place could be regarded with tenderness.

At last the power considered preserving everything.

It could have held the seven seeps at their present rates forever. It could have fixed the level of the cistern, suspended erosion and deposition, prevented the film from starving and the grazer from ever meeting a danger it could not survive. It could have made the frozen accounting into an eternal promise.

But the equilibrium was a fact, not a request. To sanctify it would have been to love the city’s appearance more than whatever life might later become possible there. The power would not break the balance merely to give the place a history; neither would it make the balance unbreakable merely to spare itself the grief of change.

Restraint was not its principle. It was its conclusion.

This was not a vow never to help. If suffering came that could be eased without simply replacing one life with another more pleasing to the helper, wisdom could begin again. But no imagined future emergency was allowed to justify a present intrusion.

What the power did was very little.

It kept its discovery from becoming a direction others might follow. It left no mark on the slope, no tempting account of wonder, no map of the fracture system, no protective seal whose existence would announce that something precious lay beneath. Its attention did not become a door. The city did not become its sanctuary, its specimen, or its possession.

Then it withdrew.

There was no less light within, because it had brought none. The roar did not change pitch. A cup filled; a drop swelled beneath an arch; the cistern received water without rising. Beneath the bridge, the clear animal either moved or remained still.

The power did not stay to give the scene an ending.
