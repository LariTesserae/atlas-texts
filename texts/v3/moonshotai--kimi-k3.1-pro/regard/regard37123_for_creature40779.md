The power came to the basin without radiance, and at first did nothing at all.

This was not yet wisdom. It might only have been hesitation.

It stood beyond the mineral rim, where the crust was warm and the roar arrived carrying more city than the eye could find. It did not ask what the growth was for, or why so much organization had been produced without anyone to use it. Its first question was narrower:

*Is anything here suffering?*

The sound alone could not answer that. To an unwise kindness, the basin might have seemed a single creature undergoing continuous catastrophe. Fibers split. Columns boomed. Whole wards sank into their roots. At the growing edges, destruction could be heard before there was anything standing to destroy. It would have been easy to mistake the noise for an outcry and silence it.

Instead, the power attended.

Its attention moved with water rather than with streets. It followed a drop from a leaf gutter into a sealed chamber, through a living column, and out beyond districts the drop had never visibly crossed. It waited beside a graft while two growths became continuous, and beside another while a contraction made several bodies where there had lately been one. It watched a wall blanch and collapse, and saw how much of its substance continued in the pavement. It watched a severed portion of avenue persist without any knowledge that an avenue had been lost.

There was strain, thirst, injury, competition, and failure. There was no concealed testimony beneath these events. No central self endured the city as a succession of wounds. No population whispered in the capillary clefts. There was no one to rescue from the architecture, and no one for whom the architecture was a failed home.

The being and the place could not be separately addressed. To speak to the whole, the power would first have had to decide which living connections constituted the whole. Any answer would have been temporary plumbing elevated into a command.

At the meristems, it was especially careful.

Here its first judgment had been most nearly wrong. The brief towers, the uncompleted arches, the avenues conducting water after their vessels had vanished: these affected the basin, and their vanishing was not nothing. But none of them waited in terror on the far side of fixation. None possessed an interior history that was being cut short. Their disappearance was real without being a massacre.

To preserve every one of them would not have been mercy. It would have prevented any growth from becoming accountable to a world shared with other growth. The basin would have been granted an infinity of beginnings and denied every consequence.

Only then did the power consider what it might give.

It could give quiet. But quiet achieved by fixing the present city would only complete lignification everywhere. The roar would cease; so would revision. Resorption would become destruction rather than the withdrawal of commitment. The basin would remain alive in the narrow sense—conducting, storing, photosynthesizing—but its life would henceforth be required to maintain a shape chosen by a visitor. It would be a monument that happened to metabolize.

It could give water without limit. That would remove much failure, including the failure of small green precincts beyond the rim. It would also remove one of the conditions by which districts became distinct. Storage, withdrawal, papery afternoons, the opening of gutters, the sudden centrality of a fragment that had managed to send back a living connection: all would be changed by a gift that appeared to alter only abundance. The basin might become greater in bulk and simpler in every other respect.

It could give the growth a mind.

This was the gift the power considered longest, because it most resembled company. But a single awareness joined to a body whose boundaries changed by graft and rupture would experience severance as amputation and union as invasion, unless the power also remade the life to suit the awareness it had bestowed. Even resorption might become bereavement. The basin would finally be able to know that possible cities had vanished. It would not necessarily be able to stop grieving them.

There would then be someone to thank the giver. The power distrusted that advantage.

So it did none of these things.

Nor did it make a virtue of leaving merely because leaving was easy. Indifference could also wear the form of restraint. It stayed through a dry interval and through rain. It remained ready to act if it found pain where there had seemed to be only process, or a captive persistence where there had seemed to be only temporary form. Its refusal to interfere was not a doctrine that nothing could ever need help. It was a conclusion about this life, at this time, reached without demanding that the life become more legible first.

When the rain came, it struck the dry leaf-scales all at once. The basin unfolded to receive it. Courts deepened, towers leaned aside, and the roar rose until it seemed less a sound than a pressure against the ordinary sky. Pale growth appeared everywhere. For an instant, incompatible cities cast their shadows across one another.

The power could have held that instant.

It could have chosen the most beautiful of the possible skylines and spared it revision. It could have made the others harmless without letting them cease. It could have left, somewhere in the deepest reservoir, an image of everything the basin had almost been.

Instead, it let the rain stop.

One skyline remained.

The power did not call it the right one. It did not mourn the others as dead. It did not fix the survivor as a reward for surviving. Already the edges of that city were softening into roots.

Then the power went away.

It left no blessing in the tissue, because anything fixed there would have become a constraint on later growth. It left no central reservoir, no guardian root, no seed containing a better plan. It set no boundary against dangers that had not come; even protection, established without need, could become the beginning of possession. It took no cutting with which to remember the place. If it remembered the basin afterward, it kept no image of one perfect city. That would have been only another way of choosing among the shadows.

Nothing in the basin would ever have to grow around the fact that it had been loved.

Behind the departing power, a deep report travelled through the ground. A moment later the sound reached the mineral rim from a configuration that no longer existed.

The power did not turn back to correct it.

The roar was not pain, and it was not praise. It was growth, and the power did not require it to be anything else.
