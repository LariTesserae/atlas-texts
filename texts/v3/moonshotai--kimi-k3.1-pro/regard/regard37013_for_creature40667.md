### What the Power Did

At first, the power did nothing that the hollow could register.

It cast no shadow beside the salamander’s, reliable or otherwise. The pool did not reflect it. Its attention was apparent, if anywhere, in the exactness with which things were considered: the darkness of pasture inside a springtail’s glassy body; the hasty hunter cleaning its claws after another miss; a mite moving its foreleg from the joints of a husk to the joints of its own leg. The salamander was seen entire, and therefore was not mistaken for a god.

The power remained through wetting and drying. It saw the clear skins spread and the grazers gather. It saw drought drive the threadworms deeper, and hunger draw the hunters out. It did not conclude that, because the ecology was entire, every hunger within it was beautiful or every death good. Neither did it conclude that the animals were waiting to be delivered from their lives into some better-arranged existence.

Completion would have been easy. The power could have made the pool permanent and the seep punctual. It could have required every shadow to wait for its animal and every echo for its sound. It could have closed the crescent into a wall, given the threshold a doorway, and added a fourth incision that explained the other three. It could even have commanded the chamber always to remain a chamber.

Each gift would have made the hollow easier to care for, and less itself.

So the power did not finish it.

This was not a principle of noninterference. Indifference can wear that face. Had the last water failed, rain would have fallen on the ground above, no more than could enter by the hollow’s own ways. Had some change begun to crush rather than sustain the lives within it, the power would have set itself against that change. But it would not relieve the inhabitants of a world they had not asked to leave merely because the world was difficult to describe.

Then the brightness densified.

The walls became luminous. The floor lost its grittiness. Marl occupied the chamber without filling it, and within that solidity the springtail continued its step, the mite lowered its foreleg, and the salamander’s throat completed its pulse.

The power acted then, though nothing in the stone was altered.

It remembered the room while there was no room.

Not one arrangement of it. A remembered map would only have made every later configuration a deviation. The power did not insist on the pool’s position, the number of flakes in the crescent, or the precise distance between the hunters’ stones. Instead it remembered the clearances: the cool margin sufficient for a salamander, the underside of a flake sufficient for an ambush, the water between grains through which a worm could pass. It remembered that emptiness could be a provision. It remembered that stone could make space around a life without possessing the life or prescribing what came next.

It held the hollow in mind without holding it still.

Hollowness returned, as it had always returned. A new flake lay sharply on the floor. The pool had shifted. A snail began grazing a damp surface that had not previously been there. Nothing announced that memory had passed into stone.

And yet the vacancy seemed less an interruption than a resumption.

On a later return, the pool appeared close enough to the ledge that the salamander drank without first crossing the grit. That may have been coincidence. The crescent gained no stone, and the threshold stood where it had stood, which was nowhere in particular. The power left no sign by which its influence could be proved.

Nothing had been granted permanence. Nothing had been exempted from hunger, molting, predation, drying, or death. But continuity no longer depended upon keeping one shape. A seam could close without every consequence of its openness having to vanish. The room could change without being compelled, each time, to begin entirely from the stone’s side of things.

That was the gift, if it was one: not a final form, but the possibility that learning might survive its intervals.

At last the power turned its attention to the ticking inside the wall.

It could have opened the unopened surface. It could have found the body, if there was yet a body to find, and determined whether the claw belonged to something newly arrived or something prematurely present. It did neither.

Touch. Withdrawal. Touch again.

The sound was not addressed to it. It was not knocking for release, or asking to be interpreted. It was already the small, purposeful work of an inhabitant.

The power did not answer with a knock. It did not make a door.

It listened, and made room in its memory for that too.
