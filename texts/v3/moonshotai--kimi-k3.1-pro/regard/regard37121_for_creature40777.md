### What the Power Did

The power thought first of warmth.

It was a generous thought, and a dangerous one. Warmth would have quickened decay as well as growth, opened liquid pathways where life had organized itself around films and lenses, and perhaps spent in a brief abundance what the garden had learned to spend over millennia. Light would have served chiefly the power’s desire to see. An entrance would have admitted more than air. Even an increase in available energy, given without limit, would have been a flood in an economy built upon almost none.

So the power did not enter. It attended without breath, sample, pressure, or lamp.

It remained long enough for its first pity to become unnecessary.

The pocket was cold, but cold was not a wound inflicted upon it. The darkness concealed nothing from its inhabitants. The silence was not loneliness. The folded fern leaf was not waiting to be encouraged, and the brine bead was not a suspense requiring release. Nothing below was enduring the power’s presence with the hope that presence would soon become assistance.

The power looked, nevertheless, for someone to whom help might be offered.

There was no one who could answer for the whole. A fern could not stand for the moss beneath it, nor the moss for the films interrupting and resuming their work inside its dead shoots. The root-curtains did not share one fortune merely because they shared a wall. Even to regard the garden as a single patient would have been a convenience for the power, not a fact about the garden.

It found no suffering it could honestly interpret as a plea, and no preference it could honestly call consent. This did not make the life beneath the frost less worthy of care. It made care less easy to perform.

The power considered restoration. It could have completed the ceramic ring, cleared the old gutter, repaired every interface, and coaxed the surviving lineages back toward the forms recorded in whatever intention had first assembled them. But the broken ring was not asking to become a circle. The descendants were not failed copies of their ancestors. To restore the archive would have been to decide that the makers’ purpose outweighed everything that had happened since.

It considered permanence. It could have held the roof against every future stress and prevented the loss of each frond, root, and cushion. Yet which garden was it to preserve: the one sparse in activity, or the one briefly thickened by richer brine? The garden before a creeper fragmented, or the several living portions afterward? To choose one arrangement and defend it against all succession would have been to make a specimen of a process. Even the dead had uses that could not be indefinitely postponed in the name of saving the living.

Had the power found the pocket at the edge of some irreversible erasure, it might have acted. It made no doctrine of refusal. It simply refused to invent an emergency so that its goodness could become visible.

At present, there was no such necessity. The roof had finished collapsing. The ordered material was repairing enough of itself. Scarcity was not a new disaster but an old condition within which continuation had become possible.

Therefore the power did nothing to the pocket.

It took no spore to carry elsewhere as insurance against loss. It made no duplicate garden, since the wish to leave a duplicate would have been its own. It left no marker above, no account that could become a destination, no blessing requiring a detectable change in chemistry. It did not make the place a shrine, a lesson, or a promise to the surface.

Nor did it appoint itself eternal guardian. Watchfulness, prolonged without necessity, can become another form of claim.

It stayed only a little longer.

In time the brine bead fell. The power did not cause it to fall, and did not prevent it. The drop entered the moss without a splash. A film resumed work that had been left unfinished; another remained salt-bound and inactive. A few cells gained an opportunity. Others did not. The folded fern leaf did not open in response. No whole was completed, and no whole needed to be.

Then the power withdrew.

It carried away no specimen. If it carried anything, it was a correction to its understanding of help: that attention need not end in touch, and that refraining from alteration need not be indifference. Sometimes the greatest available mercy was to keep one’s care from becoming another condition to which life must adapt.

Above, the weather continued to be delayed and attenuated by stone. Below, the garden resumed being unwitnessed, though it had never required witness.

Nothing in the pocket knew that it had been loved.

Nothing in the pocket was asked to.
