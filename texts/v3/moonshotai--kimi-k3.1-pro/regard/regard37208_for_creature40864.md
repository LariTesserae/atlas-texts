### No Event

The power could have hushed the roar with less effort than a visitor would need to lift a hand. It could have cooled the air, opened darkness beneath the tiles, made the copper bright, and given every Seated relation a name by which it might be called.

None of this would have taxed it. That was why it waited.

To give the Seated memory would have been to make absence possible. A hand once lifted could then remain as something no longer present; a pressure could become a loss. To give them expectation would have made delay possible, and with delay the first form of disappointment. To give them sight would have made the summit small, the parapet an edge, and the depth below something that might concern a body. To give them speech would have required the possibility of speaking without being answered.

Even silence was not the simple mercy it first appeared. The roar was not an affliction laid over their lives. It was among the textures by which those lives were precise. To remove it for their sake would have been to decide, without possibility of correction, which parts of them were merely conditions and which parts could be taken away.

The power could also have gathered them into one consciousness and called the gathering communion. It did not. Their plurality was not an unfinished unity. To comprehend them in that way would have been to end a multitude by explaining it.

Almost every miracle available to the power would have made the Seated more available to the one who had come to help.

So it did not touch the Crown.

It did not lay even a weightless attention into the clay, or leave a pulse in copper to discover whether novelty might be precious to a life without a next. Novelty was precious to the power. That was not sufficient reason.

Nor did it make the opposite mistake. It did not suppose that, because no Seated life could dread dismantling, dismantling would matter less. It did not lift the smallest bar to see whether anything would be missed.

Nothing would have been missed. There would have been no survivor in whom an absence could register, no remaining relation troubled by an empty place, no memory from which the ended life could be recalled.

But nothing being missed was not the same as nothing being lost.

The power made no test of its understanding. It did not need to turn wisdom into evidence.

For a while it considered remaining as a witness. Then it saw that witness, too, could become a vanity. The Seated would not be less alone for being watched, because aloneness was not their condition. They would not be honored by its attention, because honor would have required a place in which being regarded could differ from not being regarded. Its continuing presence would console only the one capable of feeling that presence had been offered.

It did not call the Crown holy. It did not call the Seated perfect. It did not make their completeness into a reproach against mortal restlessness, or their lack of memory into a parable about peace. They were not its teachers. Nothing in them had been arranged to improve a visitor.

When it withdrew, it did one thing elsewhere.

It left no route by which its discovery could easily become another’s errand. No map remembered the height. No revelation preserved the warmth. No account of the roar was sent among curious beings as an invitation to test their endurance against it. The power did not praise the Seated in a song that might outlive its own caution.

It built no wall around the Crown. A wall would have belonged to the architecture of approaches: gates, guardians, permissions, the slow conversion of regard into custody. Instead, it made its own approach unrepeatable. Whatever closure was necessary occurred at the visitor’s end of the way.

It did not know that another visitor would have come. It did not know that any visitor would have done harm. Wisdom did not require it to invent a danger in order to justify restraint. It was enough to see how readily knowledge becomes possession, possession becomes access, and access lays a hand upon what knowledge had once been content to leave untouched.

The power did not call this saving the Seated. Nothing had been saved from a named peril. It did not call preservation their destiny; they had no destiny to fulfill. It simply refused to let its attention become the first event in a history that would belong entirely to others and end, perhaps, with someone patiently taking the summit apart.

Something changed in the power.

Afterward, when it wished to mend what was still, it hesitated long enough to ask whether stillness was a wound. When it wished to awaken what was silent, it asked whether silence had been imposed. When it encountered a life that could not receive its gifts, it no longer concluded too quickly that the life must therefore be beyond good and harm.

It did not call this change their gift. The Seated had given nothing and were not for giving. The change belonged to the power alone, as did the time in which it could occur.

On the Crown there was no coming, no going, no afterward. No vessel bore a new pressure. No copper tongue carried a foreign recurrence. The amber light did not mark a visitation, and the roar did not become the memory of one. The Seated remained precisely contacted and utterly unaddressed.

They had not been rescued, blessed, understood on their own behalf, or used.

Then the power closed the way.

Not around them.

Behind itself.
