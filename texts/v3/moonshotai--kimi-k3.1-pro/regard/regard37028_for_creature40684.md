### A Late Answer

The power came as an attention willing not to conclude.

There was no clearing in the cloud ceiling, no sudden warmth over the meres. It did not stand upon a stone rib and survey the basin, because from the beginning it understood that surveying was not an innocent act here. It did not ask how many pools there were, which shoreline was the true one, or where the Low Chorus ended. Instead, it listened through old stone, deep peat, root mats, and rain.

It listened until it could distinguish the collapse of a bank from the report of an earlier collapse arriving late; until it could tell a reach returning a sound from an animal answering. It did not call the discarded states false, or the state that remained real merely because it had remained. It made no census of visible animals. Most of what it came to love could have been washed from a stone on the tip of a finger.

A less wise benevolence might have wanted the reeds to stand still in their reflections. It might have given every pool a lasting depth, every channel one course, every creature a body whose arrival did not have to be waited for. The power did none of this. It saw how much life depended on what such kindness would remove: water yielded between rains, sediment left by arrangements that no longer obtained, mosses crowded with several inheritances, sedges risking shallow roots on brief ground. Even the lowbacks’ routes lay partly beneath the visible valley, in sequences of old vibration that a fixed watershed would have erased.

To make the basin safe by making it singular would have been to preserve its appearance and discontinue its life.

So, for a long time, the power did nothing that could be distinguished from weather.

Beneath one of the nearer ribs rested an old lowback. Its back held dark films, a little moss, and the pale marl of pools that had not collected for years. It had persisted through so many local arrangements that the rain now tended to revise the water around it rather than the animal. It could remain motionless through whole seasons. It could feed on new shoots after a hard rain, then bury itself again while the fresh deposits thinned or ceased to have been laid down.

After the hardest rains, it called.

The valley answered generously. The note returned from olive clay, from behind black reed islands, from meres that seemed nearer than the animal’s own forefeet. Sometimes a reply arrived with the weight of another body beneath it and then resolved into a bank slumping in some distant reach. The lowback remained still through all of this. Eventually it would settle its belly against the old ground and wait for the bass note to come up through the stone.

The power did not name this loneliness. The animal was not a person, and waiting was not necessarily sorrow. But neither did the power decide that, because the lowback could wait almost without spending anything, its waiting cost nothing.

It would have been easy to answer in the lowback’s voice. The power did not. A counterfeit reply would have been a small cruelty in the shape of mercy. It also did not make another animal, or preserve some discarded state in which a companion might be found already standing in the reeds. It did not draw a living lowback from its own range merely to put an end to the calling.

It waited until there was something it could do without becoming the valley’s keeper.

At last, during a rain so heavy that the nearest pool carried a second surface just above the first, an answer came.

It was genuine. It was very late. It did not fit the old lowback’s call exactly, and the animal that had made it was already turning away. The sound had crossed more distance than the lowback could have judged. Much of it had been spent on rain. A quiet hollow had taken part of it into vagueness; another part had travelled along a channel that no longer existed and entered a pool which had ceased to collect. What remained would not have crossed the last reach of peat.

The power acted then.

It did not make the answer louder. It did not repeat it, improve it, or bring the distant animal nearer. It held no mere still and silenced no rain. For only as long as the sound required, it kept one vibration from being divided among arrangements of the ground that had no need of it. A buried channel remained continuous beneath young mud. The note passed from that channel into deep peat, from peat into the old rib, and from the rib into the lowback’s feet.

The rest of the basin continued disagreeing about its shorelines.

The answer reached the animal once: rough, weighty, and late. Water shifted slightly against its hide. For a long while it did nothing. Then, after the rain had begun to thin, it called again.

There was no immediate reply. The power offered none.

The old lowback rose. It did not walk toward the apparent direction of the sound, which would have led it across young mud and into a mere lying faintly across its own slope. It turned along the stone rib, following a pressure sequence older than the present channels. At the first junction it stopped, not because the power guided it, but because something in the ground had changed and needed to be remembered again.

Far beyond the mist, another weight entered the reeds.

The power did not stay to make a meeting out of these movements. It did not ensure courtship, eggs, or another generation. It left no sentinel on the ribs and no promise in the water that every true call would henceforth be answered. Before its care could become a climate, it withdrew the pressure of its attention.

Rain clicked in the fern cups. A colourless swimmer held itself against an old rhizome until its reflection arrived. Moss took up the water left by a state that had already passed out of the valley. Somewhere, a stream ran for several minutes across firm peat and then began, patiently, to cut its channel back.

The Low Chorus had not been healed, completed, or made one. It had been heard.

And one true answer, arriving almost too late, had not been lost.
