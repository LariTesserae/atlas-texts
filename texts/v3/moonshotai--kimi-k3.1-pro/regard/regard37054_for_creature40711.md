### What the Power Did

The power did not arrive as light.

Light was a boundary in the Hollows, and it would not begin by moving a boundary for its own convenience. Nor did it arrive as water, choosing one basin over another, or as food, altering the address of abundance. It came as attention, which was almost the only thing the place could receive without having to make room for it.

Then it stayed long enough to be wrong several times.

At first the dry floors seemed to ask for moisture, the six blue-green lights for reinforcement, the long passages for a path, the crowded ceilings for more room. These were generous thoughts, but they were also ways of asking the Hollows to become easier to imagine from outside.

The power listened instead.

It learned that a low roof and an open hall were different sentences in a language of returning sound. It followed the bats outward and understood that the cave’s food supply flew in each night inside warm bodies. It saw that bare dust was not failed guano, that a basin was not a failed stream, and that a root stopping in barren air was not a promise the place had broken. It found the separate censuses of the pools, the precise allowance of a fern, the patient hunger of a larva shining above stone it would never illuminate.

To bring a river would make one water of many small worlds. To hang a sun below the plateau would turn traps into ornaments and give the green things a kingdom at the expense of almost everything else. To spread abundance evenly would only conceal, for a while, the fact that abundance still had to arrive from somewhere. A mercy large enough to remove every vulnerability would have to replace the inhabitants with other creatures.

So the power put aside the vanity of the comprehensive gift.

It did not teach the crow to keep a tool. It did not persuade the bats to build, or the crickets to cross the bare ground and found cities, or the lights to become a road. Nothing here needed to be set upon the road toward someone else’s history.

But neither did the power mistake balance for innocence.

A pattern could be enduring without being painless. A death could serve continuation without every part of its suffering being necessary. *It is an ecosystem* was a description, not an absolution.

Therefore its first act was invisible. Where there was suffering, it left pain its uses and took away its waste. Injury could still drive an animal toward shelter; fear could still carry it beyond a hunter’s reach. But when pain could no longer protect, warn, or open any possibility of escape, it did not have to continue merely because the processes around it were unfinished. No small life was required to pay in needless agony for the neatness of a whole.

Nothing in the census changed because of this. No hunter went hungry. No island of guano diminished. It was a mercy to experience, not an editing of the pattern.

Its second act was to protect continuation rather than arrangement.

It did not pin the slabs to the roof or command every pool to remain. It did not fix the number of bats, preserve six lights precisely where six lights had always been, or forbid a colony to dwindle and another to grow. The Hollows would not become a specimen sealed beneath glass. A fallen roof, a shifted roost, a root dying where its fracture gave no further purchase—these would not automatically be treated as failures requiring correction.

But against harm arising from an outside appetite that could see only darkness to fill, deposits to strip, chambers to use, or bare ground to dismiss, the power would not remain still. It would not make the place untouchable or unknowable. It would only insist that knowledge must not become erasure, and that nothing should have to become brighter, easier, or more useful in order to deserve care.

For the present, there was no such danger to turn aside.

So the power did with its strength what the Hollows did with water: it stored it rather than spending it. It kept watch without becoming weather. It remembered without becoming light.

Its memory was not a census. It held the multitude, but also the one animal moving through clear water; the mother and the pup among thousands; the solitary bat at the margin; the hunter whose presence made an immense hall no more forgiving, but no less complete. It did not love abundance more than scarcity, or the hunted more than the hunter, or the crowded ceiling more than the kilometre of nearly vacant passage beyond it.

No mark appeared on the stone. No new skin of calcite recorded the visitation. The pools kept their separate waters, and the darkness kept its dimensions.

A bat crossed a hall. A droplet fell. A cricket molted beneath a stone. None of these events became a season.

The power heard the drop and its apparent repetitions, the near wing and the distant multitude. It did not answer. An answer would have added a sound.

It remained attentive, and ready, and otherwise still.

Its stillness, like that of the Hollows, was not inactivity. It was the absence of needless change.
