### What the Power Did

For a long time, the power did nothing that the Crown could detect.

It did not arrive as pressure in the felt, or as a shadow crossing the fan arrays, or as a new route opening where no route had been negotiated. It listened.

It listened until it could distinguish a silent road from an empty one; a repeated answer from a person; a person from the room that remembered them. It learned that a city could speak without being the only speaker, that a herd could remember without requiring the philosophers to agree, and that an answer arriving three days late through a warming floor might still be precisely the answer someone had hoped to receive.

Only then did it consider help.

It did not send rain. Water would not have completed the Crown. It would have made the power the author of a different world and called the authorship kindness.

It did not return the pewter sun to some fiercer youth, wake every dormant valve, make the shears gentle, or render every graft infallible. It did not even make Anther’s roads permanently reliable before learning which roads were cherished, which were merely habitual, and which remained in use because no one had yet helped their travelers find another way.

None of this restraint came from finding suffering beautiful.

A fractured sensory fan still hurt. A route neglected because its users were easy to neglect was still an injustice. A rooted citizen could be lonely, bored, and thoroughly tired of being praised for patience. The power did not forgive these things because the Crown, seen from above, made a severe and excellent pattern.

But it saw that pity could become conquest. To repair an entire world according to one’s first sorrow would be only another instruction forced into the substrate.

So it began where no inhabitant of the Crown could begin.

Far beyond the reach of mast, kite, or survey, it found a dark stone following an old path through the dark. There was no malice in it. It had missed the sun for ages and would miss it again, until one day it would not. The Crown had eighty thousand years remaining, which was long enough for every road now remembered to be forgotten and every present argument to become geology.

The power altered the stone’s course.

Not greatly. At that distance, very little was required. There was no flash above Anther, no convenient sign in the sky. The stone continued onward, now toward an emptiness that would not mind receiving it.

This the power did without asking. It did not make the Crown eternal, or safe, or exempt from endings. It merely prevented one blind accident from foreclosing every choice that might otherwise be made.

Within the Crown, its interventions were smaller.

When it acted, it tried to return a choice that was about to be lost. It held a failing graft for nine additional seconds while a keeper cleared the customs court; then it allowed the graft to fail and left the evidence of that failure where the responsible district could not quietly misplace it. It carried an intact warning to a wall whose listeners had been waiting in the wrong room. When healing was wanted, and healing would not require deciding another being’s life for them, it healed.

It returned the final signal of a road-rememberer caught beneath a pressure reversal. It did not return the road-rememberer, and it did not permit anyone to call the signal survival.

It refused requests to make another person answer. It refused to make a citizen’s preference appear to be Anther’s will. It would not enter a household’s deliberately uninteresting chamber uninvited, though nothing there could have prevented it.

Nor did its help become secret infrastructure. A city, it understood, could learn to budget against mercy. Neglect could become harmless in appearance while remaining neglect in fact. So its interventions left knowledge behind: a keeper who understood the failure, a household able to receive the warning, a route whose obligations could no longer be conveniently forgotten. It preferred to leave people more capable of answering, rather than more dependent upon being answered.

Most often, however, it was asked for company.

Not always at first. At first there were questions about the sun, the escarpments, the oldest roots, and whether anything lay beneath the thing that knocked. There were requests for perfect surveys, unlosable children, painless rooting, and the return of persons whose bodies had become architecture. Some requests it could grant. Some it could grant only by taking a choice from someone else. To those, it said no without pretending that no was painless.

Afterward came smaller requests.

Stay while my relative answers.

Stay while I decide whether I want to be healed.

Stay until the ordinary chord returns. I do not require you to explain the silence.

The power could do this without making the answer its own.

One night, in a house above an old avenue, a road-rememberer covered their sensory seams and touched the frame. Elsewhere in Anther, their rooted relative began an answer. Someone in the next room objected to a survey. A child waited for the change in the ground-tone.

Then everything stopped.

No rail. No burrowers. No small movements in the walls.

The power became attentive, but did nothing.

Far below came the long, deliberate knock. The felt received it. The city waited.

The power did not answer.

It had learned the children’s rule. The knock had not come from its side of the wall, and it was not addressed to anything outside the Crown. The power would not turn an ancient signal into a prayer merely by overhearing it. Nor would it make itself the meaning of every silence simply because it had arrived.

The ground-tone returned. Doors and valves began to open. Somewhere a gatekeeper struck the third bell again and found the interval satisfactory.

In the house above the old avenue, the rooted relative’s answer finished arriving. It was slow, incomplete, and tired in exactly the familiar way. The road-rememberer leaned against the frame and remained until the last pulse had passed.

The power remained too, because it had been asked to do so.

Then it returned to watching the outer dark.

It kept the sky. It left the ground-tone alone. And when someone asked it to stay, it stayed.
