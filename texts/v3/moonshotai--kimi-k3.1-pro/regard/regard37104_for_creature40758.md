### The Power Stayed for One Step

The power did not bring rain.

Water would not have softened the Foundry or woken anything sleeping in its crust. It would have flashed against hot stone, reacted with the white pavements, entered wounds that had never needed to close against a solvent, and given sealed anatomies a new way to fail. Clouds might have looked like mercy from another world. Here they would have been an invasion with a gentle voice.

Nor did the power repair time.

To make every long process singular would have been to decide which herds had crossed, which broods had kicked beneath their parents’ domes, and which courtships had ever been answered. A strider following its own weathered track was not necessarily suffering from the arrangement. The track was one of the things by which it knew where to go.

The power did not make the musters permanent. A multitude held together beyond its season would have become a famine. It did not make the striders swift. A heart used as a climate by ten thousand smaller lives could not be hurried merely because a visitor found the weather difficult to wait through.

Almost every gift that would have been visible from the rim was refused.

The animal that had first drawn the power’s attention was an old kiln-strider near the eastern basins. At ordinary noon it was one dark dome among several, separated from the others by distances that made companionship seem unlikely. Its heart had a slight hesitation between two pulses. Across the ground, where its courtship intervals had sometimes traveled farther than its body, that hesitation served better than a name.

One of its bearing surfaces was damaged. The injury was old enough that replacement tissue had grown and been damaged again. Needle-mouths kept the neighboring vessels open. Each completed descent sent a damaging resonance through the joint, and the strider’s long route had come to include that fact.

The power could have made the joint flawless at once.

Instead, it stayed for one step.

This required more than waiting. While the foot remained raised, the power attended to the lives beneath the radiator ceramic. It did not treat the millers as details of the strider, or the strider as the true animal of which they were merely living ornaments. It followed the closing of jaw-rings whose whole ancestry could fit beneath a flake. It learned the tenancy of a scar, the abandonment of a chamber by pulse-followers, the small and decisive choices of sifters gripping a flue.

By the time the foot descended, the power had ceased to regard the interval as an empty pause before an event.

The concussion crossed the basin. The white plates rang. Far away, the escarpment that answered early returned its report while the visible consequences were still arriving. The power listened to both orders without demanding that one be the error.

For a power, noninterference was easy. Attention without mastery was harder.

Its knowledge had always been the kind that could make things definite. Here it learned to know without settling: to let a route be evidenced by erosion it had not yet produced; to let young animals be present at a muster without requiring them to justify themselves at noon; to let an absent animal remain absent without concluding that its life had been less real. It did not gather the histories into one comprehensive account. A comprehensive account might only have been another kind of present, with no more room than any other.

Only after this did it turn back to the injured joint.

The pain was not ennobled by the scale of the animal that bore it. It did not become good because needle-mouths found useful dead tissue near the wound, nor meaningless because the joint would one day be grazed into a fan. That a carcass could become pavement did not make death merely a change of address. That a world depended on appetite did not make every hurt within it necessary.

The power waited until the strider began, once more, to grow a replacement bearing surface. It supplied no substance and imposed no new anatomy. It only prevented the old destructive resonance from returning through the new tissue while that tissue hardened.

The strider’s own body did the rest.

The needle-mouths lost no passage on which their lineage depended. The millers received neither a sudden feast nor a famine. Nothing outside the joint was perfected in sympathy. The animal remained old. Its route remained uncertain. Its death, where death lay along its route, was not canceled.

Over a period brief for the strider and longer than many civilizations of brief animals, the pain diminished.

The repair did not hold in every history. The power did not force it to become a fixed point around which the others must agree. In one crossing the new surface bore weight before the animal reached the next seam. In another the strider died first. Elsewhere it became absent from the visible routes with the healing still incomplete. None of these outcomes was used to declare the mercy wasted.

It was a very small act. The power did not confuse its smallness with insignificance.

Afterward it remained for a muster. It saw the basins darken beyond the capacity of the visible routes, and the upper air become layered with filaments. It heard feeding, courtship, injury, and recognition produce cries that could not be distinguished until movement followed them. It witnessed young striders standing beside parents absent from every ordinary census, and old animals completing processes that had begun before the surrounding cliffs had settled into their present angles.

It did not hold the muster open.

Neither did it look away when the cycles closed. It did not call the vanished herds dead, or the dead merely transformed. Where relief could be given without making another life pay for it, the power had learned that benevolence might act. Where relief would have meant ending the conditions by which these lives existed at all, wisdom sometimes required it to refuse its own grief.

Its last act was remembrance.

This was not preservation in a changeless present. The power did not embalm the Foundry inside itself, or maintain an eternal muster that ate nothing and therefore meant nothing to the animals within it. It did not call memory resurrection. Nothing was spared death by being remembered afterward.

But no completed life had to be reduced to its residue. No route had to become unreal because another route was easier to reach. The old strider was remembered neither solely as an injury, nor as a carcass, nor as the sum of its crossings, but also by the slight irregularity of its heart: the interval by which another animal, in some histories, had recognized it.

The power left no water, marker, law, or signature. It took no sample. Above all, it did not become the Foundry’s builder merely by being the first visitor capable of imagining one.

At an ordinary noon long afterward, nothing announced that the province had been visited. White pavement lay under the clear sky. A kite paid out a filament toward a field not yet risen. Beneath a distant dome, jaw-rings closed around fresh scale. The ground accepted the weight of a step that would not land for another century, and the whole province answered.

The Foundry remained unbuilt and unowned.

And in the power’s memory, which fed on nothing and cast no shadow, it was never again called empty.
