### What the Power Left Undone

The power began by not arriving.

Even a gentle arrival would have made weather. A shadow broad enough to cross the cloud ceiling would have cooled whole provinces. A foot placed at the rim would have become a fracture, a reservoir, a diversion in the path of rain. A voice sufficient to reach through the roaring would have altered the pressure of the air.

So the power remained beyond the upland and attended without instruments, without pulses of inquiry, without sending anything ahead to be answered.

For a long time, it listened.

It did not mistake the roar for a cry. Nor did it decide that, because nothing was calling, nothing could possibly need help. It followed water downward through leaf and vault and pale receiving tissue. It watched injuries become changes in conductivity, failed spores become moisture again, and living roots pass out of flexibility into stone. It perceived the coherence moving through the growth and did not immediately go looking for its center.

There was no center to find.

The power considered what it might give.

It could have made rivers.

It could have cut the first channels with such delicacy that no living connection was unnecessarily severed. It could have led the sheeted water into valleys, given the waterfalls beds to approach, and laid rounded stones in reaches where presently there was only raw mineral and spray. The country would have become more legible. Later eyes might have called it more beautiful.

But the rivers were not waiting underground like prisoners. They were possibilities, and only some among many. To make them now would be to admire one future so much that all the others were reduced to delay.

The power could have given the upland a voice.

The coherence was already there: in the fronts of opening stomata, in the lifting hairs, in reservoirs altering their thresholds together, in weather answering growth that had occurred days before. A sufficiently great power could have drawn that coherence inward. It could have made a place where responses gathered as intention, where memory became something that could be addressed, where the continuity might at last say *I*.

Then the power could have asked what the upland wanted.

It recognized the seductiveness of this gift. It would have made communication possible. It might also have made gratitude possible.

The power distrusted the gift accordingly.

There was no evidence that becoming answerable would benefit the upland. A question can be an environment. Asked with enough force, it can reorganize everything that attempts to survive it. The power would not create a need for speech merely because speech was the form in which it preferred to receive consent.

It could have preserved the place.

It could have fixed the advancing rim, held every living connection against storm and fracture, prevented the failure of spores, and kept the plateau forever at the pitch of intricacy that had first drawn its attention. Nothing visible would have needed to change. The green scales would still have received the dim light. The pale tissues would still have hung in their wet chambers. The root tips would still have assayed stone.

Yet the power saw how easily preservation could become a way of killing a process while retaining its appearance. To protect the upland from every loss would be to protect it from the means by which it remembered. To keep it young forever would be only another way of refusing its future.

So it did not make the upland eternal. Neither did it make it safe from time.

It took no cutting. A single filament would have been enough to occupy less patient scholars for ages, and its absence would have caused no measurable harm. The power took nothing. It left no charge in the lifted hairs, no warmth in a water film, no miraculous growth by which its visit might later be inferred. It did not even still the rain long enough to obtain a clearer view.

This was not the stillness of indifference.

Indifference does not have to learn where its shadow falls.

When the power at last withdrew, it had opened no channel, awakened no mind, planted no companion life, and established no law over the plateau. It had not fenced the rim against whatever might eventually arrive. If rivers came later, they would not be a corruption of what it had admired. If separate lives arose and remained separate, if animals one day clouded the water or made paths through growth, if the upland became something in which the power could no longer recognize the form it had loved, none of that would justify restoring the past.

It had not fallen in love with a specimen.

Nor did it make an eternal vow never to interfere. Such vows can become another way of ceasing to listen. If some later harm required a hand, wisdom would have to consider the harm then, not consult the elegance of a decision made now. The power simply refused to invent a present need in order to exercise its strength.

At the rim, a minute scale held back its first drop. A root cable descended toward it over the wet mineral face. Surfaces met; vessels joined. The pioneer’s brief boundedness became difficult to locate, and its life continued without having been witnessed as a loss.

No thunder paused. No province brightened. The stone roots went on ordering silicate behind their advancing membranes. Water was admitted, stored, released, and handed downward. Somewhere in the interior, a basin emptied into a lower reservoir, and the concussion traveled through the packed green vaults without becoming a message.

The power had not saved the upland. It had not been in danger merely because it was strange.

It had not granted the upland freedom. Freedom was not its possession to grant.

It had simply declined to make the place bear the consequences of being admired.

The only change was in the power. It went away less eager to bless, and therefore more capable of benevolence.

Behind it, without rivers, the water continued on its way.
