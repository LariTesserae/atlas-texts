### The Interval

The power’s first mercy was that it did not confuse dry with empty.

It made no rain. It did not see the waterless fractures and imagine thirst, or the hard edges and imagine that everything there was waiting to be softened. It did not flood the vesicle with light. For a long while it did not even touch the summit, because a touch might become stress in the quartz, and stress was food, weather, and catastrophe.

Its second mercy was that it did not confuse impermanence with failure.

It could have stilled the mountain. For a time, the accumulators would have remained full and the presses would have continued on stored charge. Then the seams would have ceased to yield potential. The collectors would have fallen silent. The seam-fleas would have exhausted the last accessible gradients, and the nurseries would have cooled one by one. Nothing would have been crushed again. Nothing new would have quickened.

It could have forbidden the dismantling. A city prevented from taking itself apart would not have been the city saved. It would have been one arrangement of streets, kept after its conditions had passed: freight corridors standing where charge no longer ran, shelters warming beside dead motors, every maintenance gap eventually becoming a wall around somebody’s hunger.

Permanence would have been a kind of taxidermy.

But the power’s third mercy was harder. It did not confuse adaptation with consent.

Because scavengers could use a body did not mean the body had agreed to be lost. Because the menders rebuilt did not mean nothing had been done to them. Because a cadence survived in a route lesson did not make the death of its maker complete or harmless. The power refused to call suffering beautiful merely because, from where it stood, the whole pattern was visible.

So neither preservation nor admiration was enough.

It listened.

It listened until cadences ceased to be noise. It learned that a pause before feeding could be kinship; that a route instruction could contain a dead mender’s caution; that stopping freight for a wiremouse egg-line was not sentimentality, but a compact with a kind of knowledge the local controller did not possess. It learned the difference between a shelter abandoned and a shelter surrendered, between material recovered and material simply taken.

On a nursery ledge, it watched an old mender whose back had been braced with ceramic from a freight brake. The mender was preparing a cradle. It had already survived long enough to become asymmetrical, and several of its habits had outlived the bodies from which it had learned them. Before accepting charge, it always hesitated. Before setting down the cradle, it tested the floor twice.

During the last three disturbances, it had moved the cradle according to the best route it knew. Once the route had become a freight corridor. Once it had ended in open air. Once the nursery group had reached shelter with the cradle intact but insufficiently warm, and had spent the following cycles feeding it simple patterns beside a motor that never quite recovered its heat.

The power did not pretend to know what the old mender would have asked for. But it could see what repeated itself, there and elsewhere.

The city did not lack knowledge. The fleas often knew first. The wiremice knew particular machines with an intimacy no instrument could match. A shaft-runner might understand one crossing thoroughly and know nothing of the nursery above it. A district might read its own quartz perfectly and receive no warning from the opposite wall.

Again and again, knowledge existed somewhere in the vesicle before the surge.

What failed was the interval between knowing and being able to help.

So the power changed almost nothing.

It added no charge. It removed no stress. It strengthened no wall and altered none of the controllers. It only made the summit glass return the first tremor of a major surge instead of swallowing it.

Once. Through every wall.

The returning tremor carried no words and no command. It was not even much louder. It simply came sooner, and to places that had previously been among the last to know.

The interval it purchased was small: enough to isolate a cradle, perhaps, but not to rebuild a nursery; enough to begin an egg relay, but not to guarantee its destination; enough to ask whether a crossing was still passable, not enough to make it so.

The next time, the old mender was cleaning conductive dust from the cradle’s threads when the floor answered.

It stopped with one palp still extended.

Then it touched the nursery conductor. Other cadences replied—some immediately, some after disbelief, some not at all. The old mender did not wait for the lift to confirm the message. It tapped the route it had last tested, received two contradictory answers, and chose the one accompanied by the cadence of a shaft-runner whose caution it trusted.

Across the vesicle, seam-fleas contracted and left a quartz face in bands. Wiremice began moving eggs while the insulation around their nursery was still cool. Veilwings rose before their lamps died, hanging for an instant in a gray sheet between two districts. Some shelters opened their charge reserves early. Some collectors shut down. One mill, interpreting the activity around it as an opportunity, increased production until its hoppers overflowed.

When the surge came, the city still came apart.

The old mender left behind a cache of sound shell material it had spent many cycles accumulating. It took the cradle and one charged core. At the shaft junction, another mender held a crossing long past the point at which its own district had withdrawn, then released it as soon as the nursery group was through. The crossing became feedstock before the cradle carriers reached the next shelter.

Elsewhere, a district used the warning to strip a rival’s mineral face. A duct hunter followed an egg relay and took three adults. A cradle was lost beneath a counterweight. A crossing that had survived four previous surges failed anyway.

The power did not let the saved cradles cancel the lost ones.

Afterward, there were arguments. Some districts trusted the returning tremor; others treated it as one more ambiguous condition. Compacts formed around who should answer it, which routes could be reserved, and whether early evacuation of a nursery justified stopping freight. The power settled none of these disputes. It had not given the city a law. It had given the city a little time in which disagreement could occur before architecture answered for everyone.

No shrine appeared. No conduit was set aside as sacred. The alteration in the glass could not be distinguished from the countless other conditions the menders had to test rather than trust.

Eventually, the old mender’s cradle quickened.

There was nothing miraculous in the quickening itself. The motor housing had been warm enough. The charge had been sufficient. Several adults had fed the cradle simple patterns and waited through its long, reflexive answers. Its first stable rhythm was its own, though it paused before accepting charge in the old mender’s way.

In time, it learned a new route instruction:

*When the mountain answers first, stop long enough to hear who else has answered.*

The power did not assume that one useful act had earned it the right to another. It remained outside the glass, near enough to listen and far enough that its attention did not become weather. If the inhabitants ever found a way to ask for more, the request would have to be understood. If they asked to be left alone, that would have to be understood too.

For now, the summit remained black and painfully sharp. At local noon, the gray-blue line still crossed machinery, mineral faces, and waiting backs. The city remained crowded, ingenious, vulnerable, and sometimes cruel. Its inhabitants still lost things no survival elsewhere could repay.

But when the mountain moved, knowledge was a little more likely to arrive before the stone.

The city would still have to become somewhere else.

Sometimes now, before it did, its inhabitants had time to choose what to carry.

The power had not made the vesicle safe. It had made it a little less too late.

Then it went back to listening.
