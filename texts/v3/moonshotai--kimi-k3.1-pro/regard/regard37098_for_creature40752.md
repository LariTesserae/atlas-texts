### What the Power Did

The power came without light.

Light would have been its first courtesy, and its first mistake.

It could have set a sun above the iron-colored sky. It could have loosened the ancient ice, taught the tarns to overflow, carried seeds into the gravel. It could have gathered the separate distances until every journey had a return. It could have laid a hand upon the central pavement and taken the roar away.

It could have given the pale grazer a herd, a hunger that could be satisfied, offspring, age, and at last a merciful death.

None of these acts was beyond it. That was precisely the danger.

It began with the animal.

The grazer stood on an open pavement with its mouth pressed to a wet, lichen-covered stone. The power approached without becoming a shadow, a warmth, or a scent. The animal did not startle. Neither did it welcome the presence. The encounter, like the others described by travelers, showed every sign of failing to become an event.

The power did not mistake this for peace.

It had seen stillness that was only the final shape of exhaustion. It had heard beings make no sound because crying had long ceased to bring help. It knew that a creature might appear complete merely because everything by which it could have expressed injury had been worn away.

So it did not take the animal’s stillness as consent, or its lack of fear as trust. It did not even take the absence of visible suffering as proof that none existed.

It attended more closely.

There was no other creature sealed inside the pale body, remembering grass, mornings, or the pressure of a mother’s flank. There was no old grief without an object, no appetite turned inward, no wish rubbed smooth by ages of unanswered repetition. The grazer was not enduring sameness. Endurance would have required something in it to be set against its condition.

Nor was it a being whose every capacity had been reduced to one posture. Its life was not a greater life with the doors locked. Mouth, lichen, wet stone, breath, hoof, and rock did not form the visible remnant of some richer relation. They were not an abbreviation.

They were enough for the life that was there.

This did not mean that the animal was happy. Happiness would have made the power’s work easier to imagine. It meant only that nothing in the grazer was petitioning to become another animal, and nothing in its aliveness depended upon a later state.

Only then did the power consider what benevolence could mean without rescue.

It could have offered a choice. Even that, however, would not have been innocent.

To choose between remaining and leaving, the grazer would first have needed to understand remaining as a condition and elsewhere as a possibility. To make the offer intelligible, the power would have had to give it a future. Then it could have answered the restlessness it had itself created.

It saw how easily a gift might become the wound it was given to heal.

The same care kept it from repairing the Height.

The place was not good merely because it was ancient, nor sacred merely because it was difficult. The power did not declare every feature necessary. It did not call the cold kind, or the loudness beautiful, or the failures of geography a higher form of order. Wisdom did not require it to approve of what it saw.

It required only that disapproval not be mistaken for knowledge of an injury.

There was no whole country hidden behind the separate extents, waiting to be reassembled. The wall was not an unbuilt house. The three steps were not an unsuccessful ascent. The unsupported doorway was not a failed exit. A doorway can be a gift, but it can also be an accusation against the room.

The power did not need to prove that the Old Height was perfect as it stood. It needed only to recognize that it had found no injury to answer, no request to honor, and no good it could add without first deciding, on evidence it did not possess, that another sort of world was better.

Nor did it turn restraint into certainty. It remained able to be wrong. But it would not make the animal bear the cost of an experiment performed to soothe that uncertainty.

So it passed through the rain hollow and let itself be wet.

It rested what might be called a hand against a boulder and felt the roar enter through the stone. It did not interpret the sound as a voice, or the wetting as a sign. Water wetted. Stone sounded. The relations held without becoming messages.

Near the grazer, the power remained for a measure of duration that belonged to itself alone. It did not wait for the animal to lift its head. Waiting would have made the encounter an appointment.

It saw the pale life neither as the last of anything nor as the Height’s soul. It was not a warning, a riddle, or a virtue standing on four divided hooves. It was not obliged to justify the sparseness around it. It was alive, and the power allowed that fact to remain small and entire.

Then it left.

It built no steps and set no stone upon another to commemorate its understanding. It left no guardian. Guardianship would have made the Height a possession. It made no promise to return. A promise would have cast a thread of expectation where none was needed. It did not even leave its name. There was no reason the place should have to carry the evidence of having been loved.

Elsewhere, the power did not become reluctant to act. It would still break cages, divert floods, set bones, and bring morning to those who were asking for dawn. It made no doctrine of leaving things alone. Here, it had simply seen that none of those verbs applied.

If anything remained, it remained only in the power: an exact regard with no task attached to it. It did not make the Height useful by calling the experience a lesson. It did not praise itself for abstaining. Nothing had been sacrificed, and nothing had been saved.

The rain did not fall. The roar did not soften. The dark cushions continued beside their seeps. In one tarn, transparent animals kept their appointed depths. On the open pavement, the grazer’s mouth remained against the lichen-covered stone.

It did not know that it had been considered, or what had been refrained from on its behalf.

The power did not require it to know.

Nothing in the Old Height changed. The place had not been rescued, and the life within it had not been improved.

They had been seen without being used.

They had been accurately loved.
