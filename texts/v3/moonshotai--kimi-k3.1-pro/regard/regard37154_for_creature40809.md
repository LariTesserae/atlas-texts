### The Attention

The power’s first mercy was that it did not mistake the hollow for an appeal.

It could have opened the western joint and made the dark stone run with water. It could have returned a stream to the southern watercourse, deepened the basin, and laid soil over the pale silt. Ferns might have spread along the walls; insects might have come, and then birds. The distances between things could have been filled.

It could have done more than that. It could have given the Interval a center, a continuous memory, a name it could use. It could have made the life in the fissures aware of being attended to. It could have preserved the seep against every future failure, so that the drops would never cease.

All of this was possible. Some of it would even have looked, at first, like generosity.

The power did not act at once. It remained through rain and through the longer drying after rain. It attended while the damp fan briefly joined places that were otherwise separate, and while those connections vanished. It knew the slight alteration made by a fern root entering a new crack, and the still slighter consequence, arriving long afterward, of a shrub losing a stem. It remained through periods in which almost nothing available to a visitor would have changed.

Much that resembled diminishment was not damage. Contraction was not always injury. The dry ground beyond the clay was not a poverty the seep was struggling to overcome. The absence of animals was not a grief being patiently borne. Even the pauses were not periods through which the Interval had to endure in order to reach the drops.

More water would not have rewarded it. Greater continuity would not necessarily have completed it. A memory of individual rains might have been less like a gift than like the imposition of another kind of life, one in which losses could accumulate and the future could become a source of apprehension.

Nor was preservation an innocent alternative. To fix the seep forever would have been to preserve an appearance by governing the exchanges on which the life depended. To make every fern irreplaceable, every stone immovable, and every balance permanent would have produced something very like the hollow, but without its permission to remain itself.

The power did not hide the place, either. It built no wall above the rim and set no terror in the minds of possible visitors. It did not declare that no hand should ever disturb the basin. The present stillness did not require it to invent enemies, and wisdom did not oblige it to make an eternal law out of a restraint that was appropriate now.

If some later age brought a harm in which power could truly help, that age would have to be known in its own circumstances. There was no need to summon every imaginable danger into the present merely so that benevolence would have something to do.

So the power did very nearly nothing.

It did not speak into the basin. It did not make one drop fall in a way that could be taken for an answer. It did not touch the clear film within the rock, or cause a brief shine to pass beneath the overhang. It did not leave a token among the eastern stones, where weight and placement might have given it a long but meaningless consequence.

Its attention did not ask to be received.

This was more difficult than simply overlooking the hollow. To overlook it would have required no understanding. The power had to know the place well enough that its own capacities ceased to appear automatically relevant. It had to refrain not only from alteration, but from the subtler wish that its restraint should leave a sign: a blessing in the stone, an uncommon luster on the water, some small evidence that the hollow had once been chosen.

It left no such evidence. It did not make the Interval sacred to others, or use the hollow elsewhere as an instruction in patience. Its knowledge did not become ownership.

Then it withdrew, if withdrawal is the right word for an attention that had never displaced the warm air or added weight to the dust.

Nothing visibly changed. The two scents still met without either overcoming the other. The shrubs remained at their separate distances. The eastern stones kept their few straight lines, not as a message from the dead and not as a charge laid upon the future.

Beneath the overhang, another drop gathered. The power did not hasten it, and when it fell, the power did not accept the small clear note as gratitude. The rings crossed the basin, touched the mineral rim, and vanished.

Nothing had been saved, because nothing had yet required saving. Nothing had been refused. The power’s care had not become weather, or memory, or law, or company. It had passed close enough to know the hollow accurately, and had left no demand behind.

If that was an act, it occurred chiefly in the power itself: it ceased to regard the hollow as an occasion for its own goodness.

The next drop began without urgency.
