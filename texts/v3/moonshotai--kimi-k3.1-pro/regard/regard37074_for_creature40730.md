### What the power did

The power noticed Driptrap Nine during a cool stroke.

The gecko beneath the pressure gauge was drinking from the lower flange. Above her, a drop had begun its slow passage along a mineral fin. Below her, snails were gathering in the film enriched by her droppings. A beetle waited at a cup with one antenna toward the water. Inside the buried bell, the spider drew a slack line tight.

Nothing announced an arrival. The relay snapped, and for the usual instant every animal stopped.

The power did not mistake smallness for simplicity, or quiet for peace. It saw the mite within a shell aperture as clearly as the snake’s whole passage through the chamber. It saw the eggs distributed through the dry cups, the young gecko waiting beside an occupied route, and the fern’s spoonful of dark root-bound matter. It saw that none of these lives was merely an example of its kind.

It also saw what the pump was doing: not merely sustaining the warmth and condensation, but repeating itself into the ground.

No voice spoke for Driptrap Nine. The ribs had no wishes. The chamber was not secretly grieving, and the pump was not cruel. No snail could speak for the gecko that ate its young; no gecko could speak for the beetles she took from the cup margins. The power did not invent a single will for the place in order to make its decision easier.

Stopping the pump would preserve the passages and empty the cups. Breaking the ribs would reproduce the engineers’ intervention: brief openness, crushed animals, fresh fractures, finer growth. Removing the tenants would save some bodies while discarding the relations by which they lived. Making every creature safe would require unmaking nearly everything that made them themselves.

Nor was it enough to call the whole process natural and turn away. There was nothing admirable in enclosure merely because it was slow. From a sufficient distance, even the crushing of a snail band could be made to look like pattern.

So the power chose something smaller than rescue and more useful than admiration.

Between one stroke of the pump and the next, it altered the vibration entering the condenser bank. The pressure did not change. The warm and cool strokes kept their lengths. The works above lost nothing, and the chamber below lost no water. No animal stirred in response.

But the note no longer returned exactly.

It wandered within narrow bounds, without period and without a second, hidden rhythm. There was no new repetition for the mineral to learn. Only an absence of exact return.

For a long time, no observer would have been able to say that anything had happened. The spanner kept its horns. Needles still gathered around rattling metal. Regular drips still raised the rims of their cups. Snail colonies were crushed or cut off from pasture. Silk disappeared into translucent ribs. A young gecko could still leave her tail in an occupied crevice, and the snake could still find one of its polished routes narrowed beyond use.

The power had not made the stone harmless. It had not made life fair.

Over years, however, the fins ceased to receive precisely the same instruction. Their growth still crowded, divided, and sharpened, but thickening no longer necessarily meant convergence. A rib that would once have met iron met older mineral instead and thickened there. Growth closed one seam while neglecting another. Some passages remained painful, some impassable; some stayed open longer than they should have. There was still enclosure, but no longer a single cumulative command toward total enclosure.

This was not merely a way of postponing the end. The power had set the variation against the mechanism of finality itself.

Nothing was guaranteed. The factory could still be demolished. A pipe could fail. The chemistry of the ground could change, and any of the small lineages could vanish. Driptrap Nine remained capable of dying.

But enclosure ceased to be arithmetic.

The gecko beneath the gauge drank, shifted to the shaded side of the iron, and went on hunting. She received no sign. Neither did the spider in the silenced bell, nor the snail at the living edge of its band. The power left no mark of its own; any mark would only have become another object for the chamber to grow around.

It had not promised the tenants a future. It had removed the proof that, however they lived and whatever they did, there could eventually be no room for one.

The pump still supplied the long note. Life still supplied most of what did not repeat.

But no longer quite all of it.
