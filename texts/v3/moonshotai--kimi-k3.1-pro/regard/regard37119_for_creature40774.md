### What the Power Left Unbuilt

The power arrived without warmth. That was its first kindness.

It did not clear the cloud from the cliff, brighten the quartz, or hold still the drop at the seam. Its attention entered the pocket without casting a shadow across the central court. Then, for a long time, it did nothing. It was not yet certain that doing nothing would be different from turning away.

A hurried mercy might have thawed the outer lanes. There were mechanisms standing in ice, passages broken by needles, whole districts vanishing between one afternoon and the next. It might have given every worker a body worth recovering. It might have driven off the rare enormous animal, made the seep regular, and spared the foundations the shudder of arriving water.

The power considered each gift and where it would lead.

A worker that must always be recovered becomes a claim upon every future repair. A city preserved exactly from its record can only repeat the last solution. Warmth, regularity, safety: each might be good in some measure, but the power did not yet know the measure, and at that scale a small error could close over a thing as completely as water.

Each gift answered a grief visible to the giver. None had yet been shown to answer a need of the city.

The power also refused the opposite vanity. It did not call the cold beautiful on the city’s behalf, or decide that every loss was necessary, or mistake smallness for absence of stakes. It would not sacrifice a living animal to spare ceramic, nor preserve every mechanism merely because its own pity had given them all faces. It simply declined to let its discomfort decide what suffering was.

There was no voice it could ask without first altering the city. The foundations contained alternatives, but an alternative is not a wish. The record contained durations and costs, but a cost is not a prayer. Even the annual message, precise as it was, reported conditions; it did not explain what the city would choose if choice were offered.

So the power watched long enough to see what was continually chosen in practice.

Not bodies. Not towers. Not even a particular map.

Again and again, the city spent what could be spared to keep certain acts available: admit water, yield to ice, unmake a tool, leave a passage open, begin a district without requiring it to resemble the last. The bare court was not a deficiency awaiting a monument. It was part of the city’s refusal to commit everything it had.

At last the power understood that its first question—*How can I preserve this place?*—had been badly put.

The better question was: *What would my preservation make impossible?*

For the present, it changed nothing.

This was not a vow of eternal noninterference. That, too, would have been a way of stopping thought. The power followed the seep downward and learned the watershed. It considered harms that might arrive from beyond the pocket’s weather, harms for which no foundation held an alternative. If one came, and if some small act could restore the city’s ability to respond without making the power the permanent author of its responses, the power would act.

It might do no more than keep water available where water had been made unavailable, or turn aside an intrusion before the seam became an object to be opened. It would not preserve the pocket by impoverishing everything below. It would not rebuild every loss, improve the design, or leave a guardian in the court. It would stop before help became authorship. The next thaw would still contain work the power had not done.

If no such moment came, the city would never know it had been noticed.

The power found that acceptable.

It left no sign in the court. It hid no immortal mechanism beneath the cistern and added nothing to the instructions in the stone. Nor did it promise the city endless continuation. It could not honestly know that preservation would always remain help, or that love would never become a demand that the beloved remain present merely to spare the one who loved it.

When the power withdrew, cloud had already covered the cliff. Beneath new ice, one repair continued for a while by a small white light. Then the light went out. At the outer crack a drop gathered, held, and fell.

Once each year thereafter, the power listened for the altered water. It sent no answer. *Fracture open, reservoir intact, cold within tolerance, city still present* was sufficient, and was not its message to complete.

Nothing on the cliff announced that mercy had been there.

Afterward, in other places where help was asked for and could be given openly, the power sometimes remembered the bare wet court under the mountain. Before building, before healing, before saving, it would ask what needed to be left empty—not abandoned, but uncommitted—so that those it loved could still become something it had not planned.

The Seep City had received no gift it could detect.

The power had received one: a way of leaving room.
