The power came without light.

They did not quiet the birds in order to hear the island, or make the canopy transparent in order to see it. They did not even take a body. On that ground there was nowhere to set a foot that was not also a burrow roof, a crab’s path, a seedbed, or somebody’s shelter.

For a long time, they did nothing. This was not yet wisdom. It was only the refusal to mistake power for knowledge.

They stayed beyond dawn and returned for other nights. They saw that the noise was not necessarily distress, that the half-dry forest was not universally waiting for a miraculous spring, that a crab dragging fragments underground was not damaging a finished work but taking part in its continuation. They watched returning birds, closing flowers, roots entering coral, and a fallen trunk beginning its long change of address.

But they did not let abundance become an anesthetic. A chick’s death was not made good because its substance would pass into other lives. A crushed burrow was not made harmless by the fern that might one day occupy the hollow. *Balance* was too smooth a word; so was *waste*. Both could be used to end attention too soon.

A less wise kindness might have begun distributing gifts: calm air for every landing, safety for every egg, full basins, food without consequence. But each gift would have altered the terms on which other lives continued. To heal everything would require authorship. To preserve everything would require arrest. The power refused both.

An island unable to lose a tree would also be unable to make soil of one. A cliff forbidden to fall would no longer be part of the island’s slow event. Even protection could become a kind of possession.

They had come, perhaps, expecting to address the being who lived there. But every address opened into another. The shearwater could not be sheltered only on land; most of its life was elsewhere. The tree could not be blessed apart from guano, fungi, coral cavities, and the deaths of earlier trees. The island itself, if that was the being, reached beyond its cliffs in fish, wind, rain, and returning wings.

The power did not invent a single island-soul merely to give their benevolence a simple object. The lives there did not need to be gathered into one person before they could matter.

So they left no gift upon the plateau. Instead, they altered the reach of their care.

The island could not be guarded at its cliffs. A boundary around the high ground would have protected an address while abandoning much of the life. Their attention followed the shearwaters out over the water. It included the feeding places, the routes of return, the weather that brought rain, and all the distances across which the sea became forest.

Storm, hunger, nesting, erosion, and time were not automatically enemies. They were among the island’s makers as well as its dangers. But if erasure approached through blindness—through some distant decision in which the place counted only as stone, fertilizer, territory, or an empty space available for use—the power would resist there, before the wound arrived.

Not always with thunder. Sometimes with an interruption in certainty. Sometimes by making it impossible, for one decisive mind, to use the word *empty*.

Where a wound could be prevented without turning the island into their possession, they would prevent it. Where they could not, they would not call witnessing a substitute for help. Nor would they permit their guardianship to become a cage.

On the island itself, their visitation left no sign. The basins held no new water. The snails still waited for damp. Shearwaters still crashed through branches, and crabs still dragged the fallen world underground. Nothing there knew it had been blessed.

If their restraint had words, they would have been these:

Not: *Remain exactly as you are.*

Not: *Suffer, because suffering feeds other lives.*

Not: *Be useful, and so be spared.*

Only: *Go on, for as long as going on is yours to do.*

On the island, it looked like nothing.

Elsewhere, if the need came, it would not.
