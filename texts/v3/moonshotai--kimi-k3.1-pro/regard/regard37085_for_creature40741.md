### The Visitation

The power came without light.

Light had been its first thought of a gift, and the first gift it put aside. It entered with the fog, not as a figure walking the lanes—there was no lane that was not alive beneath pressure—but as an attention in the wet air.

At first, even that attention was too architectural. It saw courts, reservoirs, columns, bridges, and looked for the one whom these things sheltered.

There was no one at the highest basin. Cupped plants were adding tissue where the water lingered. Above them, the cloud-combers took moisture projection by projection. A mite stirred in an axil and was still again. Beneath the apparent solidity of an arch, separate roots occupied separate darknesses.

The power searched deeper, because it was accustomed to places having a genius: an old mind in the oldest chamber, a sleeper under the foundations, a voice that could answer for the whole. It found the pallid threads among buried leaves, the dormant animals in basin silt, the compressed remains of surfaces that had once been open to the sky.

It would have been easy to call all this one being. The power resisted. A multitude did not need to be made singular before it could matter.

So it remained, and its compassion became cautious.

Coldseep was easy to pity. The light was meager. The cold entered everything. Whole mats could be lifted from the frost-slip before their roots had done more than taste the fractures. In the older courts, green lives failed beneath canopies that other lives had raised. Even the water was not dependable; a channel that had fed a bridge for generations could be narrowed by the swelling of one root.

The power could have given warmth.

Not a summer—only a little relief. A few degrees returned to the seep. A night in which the membranes need not close. A kindness small enough, it thought, to leave no resentment.

But Coldseep was not a warmer place waiting to be released from cold. Its flexible stems, its concentrated sap, its black leaves and brief permissions were not errors surrounding some hidden, more natural life. A lasting mildness would have favored other lives. It would have altered the seep, the films, the timing of eggs, the slow work in the waterlogged depth. The borough might have become greener, larger, more obviously abundant.

It would also have ceased to be Coldseep.

The power considered light, and saw the courts leaning toward their one cold side of the sky. It considered stability, and saw the young crust bracing itself on rock that stability would have denied it. It considered preserving the most beautiful arches, and understood that every preservation would choose among inhabitants. To keep one court open would be to decide against the shoots advancing to close it. To maintain one channel would be to make a permanent ruler of a temporary accident of water.

Permanence was the most seductive cruelty, because it could so easily mistake itself for love.

None of this made loss unreal. The power did not declare the borough good because it was beautiful, or its deaths necessary because they were quiet. A mat lifted by frost did not consent to the frost. A plant darkening beneath another’s leaves was not fulfilled by the arch it helped support. There was no harmony vast enough to excuse everything, and the power did not invent one.

It only ceased to confuse its own sorrow with knowledge of what should be done.

It was not devoted to noninterference. That, too, could become indifference wearing a wise face. Had it found a mind capable of asking, a pain with a voice, an inhabitant whose wishes could be distinguished from the conditions of the whole, it would have listened. It was willing to be needed.

It would not manufacture a need merely to enjoy answering it.

At last it saw that there was no empty ground in Coldseep on which to set a gift. Every wall was occupied. Every pavement was a crowd. Water could not be redirected without passing through a body; an arch could not be strengthened without strengthening one life against another. Even an inscription would have become, in time, a substrate for filaments.

So the power did the smallest thing available to it: it stayed until it no longer wished to be necessary.

This took longer than a fog and longer than a winter.

A basin rim rose and overflowed elsewhere. Turf entered the margins of a passage whose water had failed. The dark bird came once, sheltered beneath an outer arch, and called. For an instant its voice seemed large enough to be the voice of the borough, though it was not. The bird departed. The depressions left by its feet closed. A feather remained briefly on a wet surface before beginning its long incorporation into other things.

The power watched all this without turning it into a lesson for the lives involved.

When it finally withdrew, it left no warmth in the walls, no guardian at the exposed boundary, no mark upon the highest basin. It made no promise that Coldseep would endure forever. Such a promise would have expressed the power’s attachment more plainly than the borough’s welfare.

It carried away only a distrust of help that wished to become authorship.

Afterward, in other places, it was slower to call silence empty and slower to call ruin abandoned. When it encountered beauty, it did not immediately ask how to preserve it. When it encountered fragility, it did not immediately assume that fragility was a prayer. And when its hand rose—almost of its own accord—to finish some unfinished thing, it remembered a lane narrowing by the width of a hand, and the lives for whom that narrowing was not loss but arrival.

The visitation had changed the power. It had not changed Coldseep.

Fog crossed the rim. Beads joined on the leaf edges and ran downward. The basin beneath the seep rose by a fraction. In a covered court, a membrane slackened; far below it, something translucent resumed its grazing along a submerged stem. At the frost-slip, one patch of mat lost an edge to the cold and held the rest.

No arch straightened. No winter shortened. No voice announced that mercy had been present.

A drop gathered at the end of an amber leaf tip. For a moment it held the weak light.

Then it fell.
