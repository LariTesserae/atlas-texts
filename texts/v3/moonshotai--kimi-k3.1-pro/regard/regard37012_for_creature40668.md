It would not begin by improving anything.

It would be capable, perhaps, of opening every bud with a thought, sending water through the three basins, completing the mineral arches, and giving the white sky a sun whose passage would teach the shadows to move. It could make the steps continue downward and the roofless room acquire a roof. It could awaken the common body until it had a voice, then ask that voice whether it wished to have been awakened.

It would do none of these things.

A gift that first makes the recipient resemble the giver’s idea of them is not always a gift. The power would understand that the Underbloom’s stillness is not, by itself, evidence of injury. A folded leaf is not necessarily imprisoned. A sufficient room is not necessarily a failed house. Even perfection would be the wrong name for the place, because perfection suggests an end toward which it has been tending.

So the power would remain long enough to learn the difference between a wound and a form.

It would not require the common body to become a mind in order to deserve consideration. It would attend to the distributed adjustments of pressure and moisture, the closing of damage without surplus, the absence of any struggle being forcibly suppressed. Finding no plea hidden in the vessels and no unfinished process aching for release, it would leave the body entire. It would neither worship it nor diagnose it. It would not call its unity bliss, its warmth contentment, or its silence consent. It would simply refuse to make those things worse through interpretation.

But it would not stop there.

Reverence for the place could become another way of overlooking the residents. Their small numbers would not make their wishes negligible, and their lack of households, plans, and institutions would not prove that none of them wanted anything. The power would not decide, from the beauty of the Underbloom, that no one could wish to leave it. Nor would it decide, from anyone’s boredom or irritation, that everyone required rescue.

It would therefore make itself guest-sized.

No radiance would fill the central basin. No command would pass through the root mat. The power would enter narrowly enough to duck where others ducked, and would accept warm water in cupped hands without miraculously cooling it. It would learn the residents’ names where names were offered. It would speak softly near the water, not because the water was holy, but because that was the courtesy.

Then it would ask, privately and without gathering everyone into a council:

*Is there anything you want?*

*Is there anything you want changed?*

*Do you want to remain here? Do you want to be able to go? Do you want to be able to ask me again?*

It would not treat reluctance as a riddle to solve, or an answer given once as an eternal vow. An answer should not become irrevocable merely because the valley cannot record its revision.

The help would be exact. To the resident who wanted nothing, it would give nothing and would not secretly leave a blessing to be discovered later. To one who wanted company, it would offer company without making company into devotion. To one who wanted assistance ordering a particular memory, it would help with that memory, not undertake to repair the whole person. To one who wished to depart, it would provide departure without wounding the valley or condemning those who stayed. Departure need not mean exile; remaining need not mean agreeing to remain forever.

It would not give everyone a door because one person wanted to leave. Nor would it seal the door because the others were content.

And it would remember.

This would be the one gift it could keep entirely within itself. It would remember what it had been freely shown: the names offered, the preferences spoken, the order of its own visits, who had walked beside it and who had desired it gone. It would cut no archive into the white stone and plant no recording organ in the common body. It would not turn the residents into a story for others. It would simply ensure that their gestures need not become negligible merely because water, cloth, root, and cliff could preserve no evidence of them.

If welcomed back, it would know that this was another meeting. It would not demand that anyone else prove the first.

Then, unless asked to stay, it would go.

No inscription would mark the visit. Repeated footsteps would still leave no path. The water lenses would remain level, the buds folded, the clean-edged steps sufficient at the waterline. A leaf-press would adjust one limb. A lens-flex would bend into a brief pale stroke and vanish again into clarity. The common body would continue without having been improved, completed, or made to explain itself.

The place would be unchanged.

Its inhabitants would not be unheard.

The power would not make the residents remember it.

It would remember them.
