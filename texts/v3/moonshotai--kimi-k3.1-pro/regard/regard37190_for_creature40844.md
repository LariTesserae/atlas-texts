### The Answer

The power came without thunder. The Bellows had enough of that.

Nothing announced its attention. The carriers did not settle, the suspended rods did not stir, and no seed case mistook the arrival for a summons. It stood, if standing is the word, on the southern rim at midday, while the ordinary sunlight showed the districts below in leaf-black, crimson, and wax green.

For a long time, it only listened.

At first the sound was what it seemed to every newcomer: one continuous excess. Then it began to separate. Wing-drone from stalk-boom. Growth from subsidence. The location-knock of a huskback from the knocking that preceded a rush. A bellower’s call from the answering labor of warm stone.

The power learned that a note could be courtship to one body and catastrophe to another. It learned the inhabited quiet of the hushfelts, where tiny feeding ticks continued, and the held quiet in which even those stopped. It learned that the Bellows had no single voice with which to consent to being helped.

It saw, too, that the place was not a paradise. Chordlings remained sealed when a stand changed pitch too soon. Kites were grown over where they hung. People lost their hearing, their paths, and sometimes their lives. Distance did not make these things beautiful.

But neither did they make the Bellows a failed version of some quieter world.

Rain would have been an invasion. Silence would have confused refuge with disaster and left countless delicate signals unheard. Still ground would have closed the fresh fractures to which old bellowers went to root. Even a permanent human road, offered as a kindness, would soon have become either a wall or an unusually straight obstruction inside somebody’s body.

So the power made no gift to the whole.

It did not confuse this with doing nothing.

Below the eastern rim, a working party was cutting cane under a living arch. Their listener was old and tired. Years below the rim had taken most of her ordinary hearing, but her attention was still good; she had one hand on a stem and her eyes on the rods. The power felt the fault tightening beneath them.

It did not close the fault. It did not make the arch incorruptible or the party safe from every future hour. It touched the shortest rod, just once.

The rod leaned and struck its neighbor twice.

The listener looked up. She touched the cutter’s shoulder, then the wool-carrier’s. Tools were gathered. One basket was left. They withdrew in the quick, undramatic order of people who had rehearsed misfortune without expecting to be exempt from it.

The arch came down behind them. The gallery filled slowly with dry fragments and then with wings.

No one thanked the power. There was no evident reason to think anyone required thanking. The listener examined the rod afterward, tightened its suspension, and appeared dissatisfied with its new sensitivity. The power was content with that.

On its last night below the rim, it heard a young bellower.

The creature was standing at the edge of a hollow-stem district, cropping corky growth between attempts. Its chamber was still too new to hold a note for long. The first call broke, resumed, and went out through the stalks: a rough, hollow vibration with more body than music in it.

No answer came.

The power looked closely. It did not wish to be kind to an imaginary creature.

The bellower was not injured. It was not trapped, and its behavior offered no sufficient warrant for calling it desolate. After a while it lowered its head and resumed feeding. It would probably call again the next night. If no one ever answered, the Bellows would continue without noticeable difficulty.

Still, the absence of tragedy did not make the call meaningless.

So the power answered once.

Not through the open air. Not in the voice of an adult bellower, with a summons or a promise the creature would have had no way to distinguish from truth. It made instead a small returning vibration within the bellower’s own ribbed chamber, shaped to the note that had just faded there.

The bellower stopped with a stem half-cropped.

No seed case opened. No carrier unfolded its wings. No root turned aside. Nothing was fed, warned, gathered, or begun. For once, a vibration in the Bellows did not become growth, shelter, flight, or another body’s work.

It only went back to the one that had called, and ended there.

If it carried a meaning, the meaning was no more elaborate than this: *received.*

After a moment, the bellower finished cutting the stem. Later it called again. The power did not answer that call. An answer, unlike a law, does not have to be repeated forever.

By morning there was nothing to show where the power had been. It had left no temple to be incorporated into a trunk, no relic whose use would have to be guessed, no map exempt from date and hour. A handful of harvesters were alive who would otherwise have died. One young voice had received an acknowledgment that asked nothing of it.

From the rim, the Bellows appeared unchanged. By evening, of course, that was no longer true; by evening nothing there ever was.

After dark, the bass calls deepened. The young bellower tried again. Its note entered the crowded air and did all the ordinary, prodigious work of sound.

The power listened until it faded.

Then it let the Bellows have the night.
