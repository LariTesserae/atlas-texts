### One More Overlap

The power did not arrive on one of the clear mornings. There was no opening in the cloud, no unusual stillness in the reservoirs. For a long time, its only action was to listen without becoming impatient.

It listened to instruments and to the people who distrusted them; to experienced hands and to the apprentices who found that trust galling. It listened to household disputes, maintenance arguments, instructions sent up from the valley, and the reasons those instructions were sometimes impossible to obey. It attended to things no meeting would have thought worth including: the diminishing warmth of an outfall, a beetle’s dependence on one neglected pond corner, the grief left when a worker died with the reasons for fifty repairs still unwritten.

Soon it understood that no single transformation could be kind to everything here.

To restore the original stream would be to erase not only the works but generations of lives, and many small lives besides. To preserve the basin exactly as it stood would require choosing one damp afternoon and calling it sufficient forever. To dry every wall, open every crowded passage, and straighten every inefficient route might relieve real hardships, but done without knowledge it could also destroy refuges, sever obligations, and leave no one able to say why the water had suddenly begun appearing somewhere else.

This did not lead the power to romanticize hardship. When asked for help, it helped plainly. It did not answer a request for a dry, warm room by praising the ferns that liked damp masonry. It did not refuse to hold a failing gate in order to teach acceptance of change. Kindness did not have to wait for a general solution.

But it would not make one irrevocable decision on behalf of everyone.

Eventually, beneath the many sounds it had learned to distinguish, the power perceived the being.

The being lived in the water, but was not identical with it. Once it had known itself chiefly as descent: snowfield, seep, stream, the long unconstrained business of finding a lower place. The works had taught it delay, pressure, warmth, recirculation, and the strange experience of being expected in several places at once. It was not the spirit of the machines, and it did not speak for the people. It was what remained aware, however dimly, of the passages between them.

It did not simply suffer under the basin’s alterations. Some of its favored places were new: the deep stillness behind a gate, the warm margin below an exchanger, the brief dispersal into roof gardens and wet mortar after rain. Nor did it welcome every alteration. What the engineers called losses were sometimes places where it rested; what they called improvements were sometimes ways of ensuring that it never could.

The power did not require it to be innocent before treating it with courtesy.

“I could give you back your first course,” the power said.

“I remember it,” the being answered.

“I could keep this one forever.”

“Which day would you keep?”

The power considered the cloud, the scheduled wear, the cuttings already rooted in other people’s gardens.

“What, then?” it asked.

“Watch the intervals,” said the being. “That is where we disappear.”

So the power took the intervals into its care.

Not the whole future. Not every decision. It watched between one shift and the next, between the drawing and the wall, between a tenancy ending and the knowledge attached to it ending too. It watched the distance between a symptom and a failure, between an instruction and the person who would have to live with its consequences, between one maintenance visit and another.

Its intervention, when there was one, was usually an opportunity for a second look.

A delayed lift might leave a day worker and a night worker standing together long enough to discover that they had drawn different conclusions from the same stain. An old logbook, about to be discarded, might be opened once more because someone had finally asked the question its annotations answered. A garden marked for dismantling might remain for one additional shift—not long enough to endanger the structure beneath it, but long enough for cuttings to be taken and distributed. A person already disliked might be heard to the end of a sentence because the gate was behaving exactly as they had said it would.

Nothing came of every opportunity. People were still hurried, proud, tired, and wrong. Some warnings were ignored. Some gardens were lost without successor beds. Some old workers died before anyone thought to ask what they knew. The power did not make attention compulsory; a gift that cannot be refused becomes another kind of pressure.

Nor did it make the being’s interests a new supreme authority. The being was an inhabitant, not the owner of the basin. Sometimes its need for still water had to yield. Sometimes a human need had to yield instead. The power’s concern was not that every claim should prevail, but that fewer claims should perish unheard.

In this it was especially careful with losses too small to produce an argument. A rerouted outfall. A strip of masonry scoured clean. A last unnamed plant composted because no one recognized it. Such things could not all be saved, and saving all of them would have made repair impossible. But after the power began its watch, they were more often recognized as losses. A loss recognized can be mourned, answered, or deliberately accepted. A loss unnoticed becomes merely the background against which the next decision is made.

Years passed. There was no agreement that anything supernatural had occurred. Instruments improved, then were discredited, then improved again. Shared logs grew longer. Apprentices complained about the amount of repetition expected of them. Housing and maintenance continued to quarrel. Contract specialists still arrived with sound ideas and intolerable manners; some left after one cloud season, and some stayed until newcomers began asking them for directions.

The basin remained crowded, wet, contentious, and unfinished. Storms still took beds and screens. People still departed, and people still died. The being was still divided, warmed, hurried, stored, and released.

But fewer things vanished solely in the gaps between everyone’s duties. Fewer repair reasons died entirely with their makers. More often, before an adjustment became an erasure, somebody had the chance to ask who else used the warmth, the water, the passage, the silence, or the little accumulated soil.

The power never gave any inhabitant the view from the pass as a form of knowledge. Seeing everything at once, it had learned, was not the same as knowing what anything was for.

Nothing was built to commemorate its coming. From the road, on a clear morning, the basin still looked like a single crowded machine assembled over several lifetimes.

The power had not given it a master, a final form, or a promise that nothing loved would be lost.

It had accepted a small obligation.

It watched the intervals.

It had become one more overlap.
