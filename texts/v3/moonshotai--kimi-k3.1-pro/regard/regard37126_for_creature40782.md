### No Addition

First, it would do nothing for a long time.

This would not be hesitation, and it would not be indifference. The Ninth Heat Ward was a place in which even kindness could become an abandoned crate. A wise power would not arrive as a body in the central court, or set down a lamp while it decided what ought to be improved. It would remain where the ward did not have to accommodate it, and listen.

It would listen until the city’s loads became distinguishable from the ward’s answers: the pressure before the gatehouse boom, the three dense chords, the rain loosened from the grooves, the long settling in which separate drops could be heard again. It would learn the cool margins where ashgrass could live and the bare metres beyond them. It would follow seepage to the root-fans and the films. It would notice the isopods only after a discharge, and the loaches mostly by the small occasions on which they were not still.

It would not make the mistake of calling the ward alive. Nor would it need to call the fish wise. Nothing there had to be enlarged into a symbol before it deserved care.

The first mercies would therefore be refusals. It would not hush the Choir. It would not make the basin rich with food. It would not spread the ashgrass over the warm floors. It would not leave a shelter, a monument, or one immortal inhabitant to explain the place to visitors. There was no emergency that such gifts would answer. Most of them would merely move the cost.

But it would find something fragile.

Not the coupling, though no one understood it well. Not the loaches, though no one could count them. The fragile thing was the city’s memory of restraint.

The ward could endure a lost foundry mark, an unresolved depth, a worn stair left worn. It could not endure becoming, in the minds of those above, merely empty. Its survival depended on people continuing to understand that zero was a census entry, not a permission.

So the power would act there. Its miracle would be administrative.

It would preserve the notes.

Not the bold figures or the clean schematic: the notes. The long qualifications attached to failed surveys. The record of what happened when a partition was tried. The apprentice’s observation that a sound had seemed to come from inside the sealed gate. The reminders, written in several generations of plain and increasingly old-fashioned language, that disturbance was also data, that *elsewhere* was not an answer, and that the cavity itself was part of the machine.

Through replacements of archives and revisions of practice, those notes would remain inconveniently attached to the plans. No one would be compelled to read them. One person would.

Years later, in a bright room far above, a proposal would arrive to make the ward quieter, cleaner, and temporarily useful. Absorbent lining. A flushed basin. Equipment in the courts, only for a season. It would be a sensible proposal by every measure in the room. It would even reduce the time maintainers had to spend below.

The person who had read the notes would ask one question:

“Where does the load go?”

Someone would answer, “Elsewhere.”

She would ask where.

The meeting would last longer than planned. In the end, no work order would be signed. There would be no vote to remember, no averted disaster that anyone could exhibit. Only a maintenance schedule renewed, a basin left unflushed, and three courts still useless and clear.

Before she retired, she would take her successor down. She would give the four instructions exactly as she had received them. At the gate she would add only:

“Wait until after a discharge before you decide that it’s empty.”

That would be all. The power would not sign the gatehouse or teach the loaches its name. It would leave no new depth in the deep basin and no patch of grass beyond its margin.

Below, the next discharge would come. The gatehouse would boom; the exchangers would answer one after another. Warm rain would fall briefly in the dark. Transparent grazers would appear at the basin rims. In the third basin, a pale fish would make one short movement and return to stillness.

Above, trains would brake and lifts would stop with their usual smoothness.

Nothing in the ward would have been improved. Nothing would have been added.

Something would have been spared.
