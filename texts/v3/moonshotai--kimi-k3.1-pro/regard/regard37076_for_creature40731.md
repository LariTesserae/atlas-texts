### The Necessary Neglect

The power did not open the seam.

Even an arrival without body or breath might have displaced enough air to become weather. The moths would have risen. The hunters would have climbed above the runnels. Rain might have come before its time. So the power remained outside the warm alloy and made its attention small enough to cause no pressure change.

Its first knowledge was of everything a kindly visitor might wish to correct.

The chamber was too dark, too wet, too loud. Its floor was not ground; its food was leakage; its inhabitants lived among obstruction, runoff, hunting, and wear. A merciful hand could have brought light, softened the roar, cleared the channels, multiplied the algae, and made clean water stand quietly in the basins.

The power considered each mercy long enough to see what else it would alter.

Silence would not be peace. Abundant light would not merely console the fern. A chamber cleared of every obstruction would also be cleared of shelter. To rescue the animals from the spire’s indifference would require replacing, one by one, the conditions by which they knew how to live.

So the power did not teach the water to fall softly. It did not widen the hunter’s little world, or give the snails an understanding of the walls beyond their trails, or show the moths an image of open sky. It did not appoint the fern the soul of the sump because it was green, nor the moths its people because they had wings. The hunters were not villains. The drains were not enemies. The spire was not a negligent god awaiting correction.

Neither did the power call every death good, or proclaim that nothing suffered because everything was recycled. Benevolence did not require that lie. It only recognized how easily pity could become design, and design become authorship, until the chamber had been preserved chiefly as an expression of its preserver.

It would not become a more thorough machine.

Nevertheless, it acted once.

Its act was not inside the sump, but at the boundary of future attention. No light was added, no current strengthened, no animal healed at another animal’s expense. The power simply made the cavity difficult to mistake for an opportunity.

If the alloy breached, the spire would still repair the injury. If a support thinned, fresh material would still swell into the load. Warmth, condensate, pressure, and drainage would continue according to the spire’s own changing life. But when some later inventory came looking for cavities to flush, surfaces to sterilize, mechanisms to restore, or wasted spaces to seal, the sump would not present itself as an easy act of improvement. Attention would pass onward. The warm seam would remain a warm seam.

The power did not make the chamber sacred. Sacred places acquire custodians. It left it unimportant in precisely the way that had allowed it to become inhabited.

Nor did it protect the sump’s present arrangement. The three basins might alter. The fern might vanish, or another spore might at last establish itself beyond the reach of grazing. A roost might be displaced by repair foam; a new roost might form along its edge. Some lineage of moth, snail, crustacean, or hunter might dwindle while another arrived through a service duct and found the conditions tolerable. The power had not fallen in love with an inventory.

It had not even promised the sump forever.

Forever would have been another way of holding it still. The chamber was made of slight alteration: edges rounding, trails clouding over, bodies entering silt, current finding a different path around an obstruction. To preserve it against all ending, the power would eventually have had to preserve it against change. Instead, it left the sump time.

Then it remained for one weather.

The roar deepened. Water narrowed into threads and withdrew. Fog gathered around the ceiling combs, and the moths pressed their dusty wings closer together. Above the runnels, the ivory hunters became still.

Pressure released.

Warm rain struck the basins. Shells, wings, larvae, sediment, and living animals moved together through the brief violence of runoff. The hunters struck. The crustaceans spread and fed. The fern bowed beneath the water, its old root mat holding.

The power did nothing.

The floor drank. Fog thinned around the amber lights. Stranded animals began their small, local journeys, or did not. A snail uncurling beside a drain tasted no difference between the world before the power’s visit and the world after it.

That was the nearest thing the visit had to a success.

Afterward, the moths unfolded. A hunter set its feet along the runnel. The fern lifted one wet frond, and overhead another drop began to gather around a fleck of buried light.

The power withdrew without leaving a mark on the alloy. There was no blessing for the sump to discover, no sign that its existence had been witnessed, no demand that it continue for the sake of having been loved.

From inside, its mercy was indistinguishable from the next thing happening.
