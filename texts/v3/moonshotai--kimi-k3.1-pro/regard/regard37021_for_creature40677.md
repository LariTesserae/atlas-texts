The power came as an attention that cast no shadow.

That was its first courtesy. The cavity already had light, and any radiance brought for the sake of a glorious arrival would have altered the shadows on the rear wall. Nor did the power cool the air, soften the mineral floor, or send a little rain shining through the cleft. It did not mistake heat for pain, dryness for thirst, or stillness for imprisonment merely because those would have been hardships elsewhere.

It stayed for an afternoon and saw nothing happen. Then it stayed longer.

A conductive thread descended by increments toward a seam. An old fan cleared, became brittle, and began separating into films. Charge accumulated in a plume until its tips brightened in the ordinary sun. A green knot remained a green knot. Another paled without ever putting down a root.

Gradually the power ceased to experience the intervals between these events as emptiness.

It looked also for the being it had come to visit. At times the inhabitant seemed to be three extensive lives, each with many trunks. At times the groves seemed populous and the cushions separate from them. The power did not settle the question by choosing the count that would have made the orchard easiest to address. It let the being remain extended, plural, and uncertain at its boundaries.

There was no prayer in the hum. No summons traveled through the seams. The power could have asked, *What do you need?*, but no answer would have come, and it understood that silence could be treated neither as consent nor as refusal. It would not use silence to license a desire of its own.

Even the fourth grove was not a voice asking to be born.

The power considered what benevolence might add.

It could have carried green knots across the bare floor and established circuits around them. Leaves could have risen in the broad, empty intervals until the cavity became lavish throughout. But that would have made the place more generally like an orchard and less particularly like itself.

It could have completed the fourth grove. The necessary grooves could have been opened; metal could have been laid along the wall; roots could have found, in one bright instant, relations that would otherwise require ages to become possible. Yet then the shadow would have been made into a prophecy after the fact. The bare glass would have been reduced to a delay, and every approaching root-channel to an instrument of the power’s intention. More than a grove would have been added. Other possibilities would have been taken away.

It could have given the orchard a mind, so that the orchard might know itself, know the power, and receive the comfort of being loved. But there was no loneliness there to comfort. To create a mind in order to rescue it from a solitude it had never suffered would have been to make the wound and call the dressing kindness.

So the power set down these gifts, one by one.

It did not preserve the cavity against all change, either. It would not stop the mountain’s slow crystallization merely to keep one beloved arrangement of leaves and shadows available forever. Permanence, too, would have been an alteration.

Instead, the power made one renunciation within itself: it would not call the orchard unfinished.

If it could foresee whether trunks would one day stand within the fourth shadow, it would not let that knowledge become the orchard’s true name. If it could imagine a final harmony among all the seams, it would not treat the present hum as an imperfect rehearsal. Nothing here had to become complete in order to justify having been noticed.

The power remained for a time without helping.

This was not as passive as it sounds. Its attention did not guide a current, hurry a root, or protect a promising knot from the indifference of the floor. It did not allow its affection to become a preference for more leaves, more groves, more industry, more life at any cost. It witnessed injuries without regarding repair as a moral obligation, and endings without immediately naming them losses. It kept company with something that could neither recognize the company nor be asked to return it.

Once, where the hum had strongest preceded a seam, the metal appeared and the hum did not cease. The power did not correct the discrepancy. Once, the pale second shadow beside a young shoot remained botanically precise while the shoot itself continued to be small. The power did not decide which of the two deserved to be called more real.

It was, if such a word can be used of it, pleased.

Not because the orchard was progressing. Not because it had discovered a hidden purpose. It was pleased because the place could not be exhausted by an explanation, even a benevolent one.

When the power finally turned its attention elsewhere, nothing marked its visit. There was no inscription in the salt, no new radiance in the plumes, no guardian folded into the mountain. It took away no knot, no crystal, and no image of a completed orchard to cherish in place of the changing one. It left the cleft as difficult to find as before.

Its only lasting act was to remember accurately: a small hot room inside rising stone; three groves of substance; broad intervals that were not failures; one organized absence participating before it participated in matter.

And in the cavity, after the power had gone, ordinary daylight entered through the narrow seam. A crystal ticked. The shadow on the rear wall shortened toward noon, precise and unattached. Trunks might someday stand within it. They might not.

The future remained ahead, but not in command.
