The power’s first impulse was to stop the grader.

It did not despise itself for the impulse. From outside, the pocket sounded like a small, interminable accident: metal striking metal, shells cracking, winged bodies battering the screens. Mercy easily attaches itself to the loudest suffering.

But the power listened longer.

It heard the heavy stroke become pressure, falling dust, stillness, and return. It followed current into mineral film, film into feeding, feeding into the vibrations by which a buried wireback found its prey. It watched wings become flakes, flakes become ground, and ground become cover for young hunters. To cut the current would be to end the film. To silence only the screens would be to impose a new climate. Even abundance would not be simple: the ledges were already crowded, and food without limit would not make crowding, obstruction, heat, or hunger disappear.

Every clean mercy it imagined was clean only at the scale at which it imagined it.

Nor could it ask the pocket what the pocket wanted. There was no one to answer at that scale. The grader could be given a voice, but that would not make it responsible for lives it had never intended to shelter. One buttonback could be changed until it understood the whole chamber, but then the power would have created a new kind of animal and used its answer as permission. The wirebacks, the fretters, the joint-mites, and the animals travelling invisibly in falling dust would still not have spoken.

So the power did not make the pocket conscious. It did not appoint a caretaker. It did not rescue the visible animals at the expense of those it would have to learn later how to see.

This was not because it found the arrangement perfect. It did not admire the crush, or call every death necessary, or decide that a short life could not contain harm. A ledge looking unchanged after all its animals had been replaced did not make those animals interchangeable. The elegance of the cycle did not anesthetize the power.

But neither would it let pity for the life before its attention become permission to disregard all the lives beyond it. It would not purchase a cleaner conscience and send the bill downward through the food web.

Within the pocket, therefore, it changed nothing. It took no specimen, left no new light, widened no seam, and made no animal immortal. It did not preserve the grader merely to keep the arrangement available for its admiration. Eternity, too, could be a form of interference.

At the boundary, however, it accepted a duty.

If some later intelligence crossed the plain and mistook its silence for emptiness; if a cable were to be cut because nothing visible appeared to depend on it; if another benevolent power arrived eager to open the roof, quiet the machinery, water the dust, or otherwise bestow an excellent and fatal gift—then this power would act. Not to freeze the pocket, not to make it sacred, but to give continuation a chance against erasure by ignorance. Its protection would be of the old terms, not of a display.

It left no marker on the surface. A marker might become a destination, and a destination a claim. It simply remembered, accurately and without possession, that the plain was not empty.

When it withdrew, no animal below knew that anything had been considered. The relay closed. The screens struck their frames. Wirebacks stopped in the ridges, and the white lamps vanished briefly behind wings. Dust descended with its minute passengers. Then the ledges filled from their edges, feeding taps resumed, and predators gathered where the ground had begun to sound again.

The power’s first impulse had been to stop the machine. Its mature mercy was to protect the pocket from first impulses, including its own.

The grader supplied the beat. The lives supplied the deviations. The power added no rhythm of its own.
