### What the Power Did

The power came without light.

Its attention passed through the shallow soil and limestone until nothing in the chamber was hidden from it: the film in its protected seams, the transparent animal folded beneath a basin rim, the sealed joints of the clay trough, the buried water threading routes no visitor could enter. It saw the old stains and the present surfaces. It also saw what no eye could see—the level not as a height, but as a continual act, inflow and outflow approaching one another, differing by a little, and having that difference made small again.

For a while, the power did nothing. Not because it doubted its ability to act, but because it did not assume that ability had already told it what goodness was.

It could have secured the spring. It could have fixed the inlet, fortified the limestone, and guaranteed that no route would ever open or close beyond the measure required to keep the old stains wet. But then it would have had to decide, forever, which changes were permissible. The level’s persistence would slowly have become the power’s administration. The oldest inhabitant would not have been destroyed exactly; it would have been replaced by the arrangements made to protect it.

It could have awakened the level. It could have given the spring a center, a voice, an awareness from which to look back at its own long continuation. But there was no unfinished person waiting inside the balance, no imprisoned mind asking to be released. To create experience would have been to create someone new—someone who might delight in the chamber, but who might also fear its ending. The power would not create a need merely to have the pleasure of answering it.

Nor did it mistake the smaller lives for deficiencies. It did not give the blind animals eyes because it could imagine them seeing. It did not multiply them until the pools shone with movement, or spread the bacterial film into abundance for the satisfaction of seeing the chamber more richly inhabited. Scarcity was not, by itself, misery. Stillness was not waiting. An ending was possible, and that possibility was not a plea.

The power left the human things alone as well. It did not remove the steps and trough in order to make the spring appear untouched. It did not restore the trough to its makers’ intentions, or clean the bowl and set it out as an offering. The chamber did not need to be made primeval, picturesque, or sacred.

This was not a refusal ever to intervene. Had there been a present injury to heal, or a destruction already approaching that could be turned aside without making the spring a possession, attention alone would not have been enough. But the power would not invent an emergency in order to become a rescuer. It would not treat every vulnerability as a wound, or every possible future as a disaster already happening.

What remained was attention without appetite.

The power attended without waiting to be answered. It did not ask the level whether it wished to continue, because the question would have required it to invent the respondent. It did not interpret the next drop as a greeting, or the return of the surface as gratitude. It allowed the spring to be incapable of receiving its regard, and did not withdraw the regard for that reason.

While it remained, a blind crustacean shifted its hold beneath a rim. Water hissed faintly through the clay trough. A drop gathered under an overhang, fell, and made its small, blunt note. The power did not arrange these things into a message.

When it withdrew, it left no seal upon the entrance and no guardian beneath the plain. It did not make the seam impossible to find. People might come again, descend the worn steps, and lower vessels into the warm water; their visits would not necessarily be violations. The inlet might also fail one day, or the limestone below might open until the old balance could no longer be restored. The power did not promise to prevent that. An ending would not prove that the spring had been unloved, and its life had never been a failed attempt at permanence.

But the power remembered.

It remembered that there could be life without self-possession, value without answer, and a kind of persistence that did not ask to be made eternal. Nothing in the chamber knew that it had been loved. The power did not count that against the love.

Above, the plain offered its shallow soil and scattered rock. Below, condensation gathered and returned. Calcite closed a little over one seam while another remained wet.

Water entered. Water left. The level returned.

It did not return to its witness. It simply returned.

The power, which could have done anything, had not made itself necessary.
