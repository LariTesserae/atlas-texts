The power did nothing at first.

They did not stand on the rim, because even a weightless visitor can make a path by returning often enough. They gave no light to the canopy and no voice to the engines. They watched a flywheel expose another spoke. They waited through the weeks in which the pumps restored the old brown mark on the cistern wall. They listened until water hammer, rain, splitting cane, and the small movements of the eel behind the grille no longer sounded like one another.

Nor did they begin by deciding which resident was the being whose attention they had come to answer. They did not make the root-body singular merely because so much of it was continuous. They did not appoint the white eel the soul of the hollow merely because it had dark eyes and an elsewhere. They let the census remain difficult.

A hurried benevolence might have done a great deal. It might have cleared the mistakes from the pipes, supplied the canopy with birds, made the wheels turn briskly, and given the eel a companion. It might have woken the iron, on the principle that anything so long employed must be waiting to speak.

The power did none of this.

Silence was not a request for birds. Solitude was not a diagnosis. The old mistakes were not all injuries; some had become braces, shelters, surfaces for film, or merely events around which later life had successfully arranged itself. Even the pumps’ patience was not evidence that they wished to be quick.

And the power did not require the root-body to be conscious before they could wish it well.

Their restraint was not a rule placed above kindness. It was what kindness became after they had paid attention. Had something been suffering, had the eel’s hidden life been an appeal rather than simply a life, they would not have made a virtue of leaving everything untouched.

At last they found something to do.

What needed protection was not every existing shape, nor even the present arrangement of roots and engines. It was the interval in which shapes could change.

The Rootworks could answer rain by rising slightly, drying by growth and circulation, injury by enclosure, and failure by bypass. It could endure being wrong in many places because no single error had to decide the whole. Its profound vulnerability was not failure. It was the possibility of a final injury arriving faster than any living response: water taken away in a night, the deep mat broken before its remotest tissues could know, some quick outside act requiring an answer from a thing whose answers took seasons.

So the power kept their hands out of the machinery and took that suddenness into their own care.

They did not make the hollow indestructible. Drought would still be drought. Iron would still thin. Canes would still swell against yokes that no longer moved, and woody collars would still close what should have remained open. The Rootworks would not be spared the consequences of being itself.

But no injury from beyond the rim would be allowed to become final merely because it was quick. Where such harm came, the power would meet it there, outside the patient circulation: delaying it, dividing it, turning it aside, or leaving the hollow time enough to respond and time enough to fail.

Their gift was not permanence. It was time.

If, eventually, the last living circulation ended through the place’s own slow accumulation of errors, they would not restart the dead pumps merely because they could not bear to have watched a finite thing. They would know the difference between helping a remnant live and making a monument perform continuation.

For the eel, they did one smaller thing.

They did not draw it into the open to discover its length. They knew where its tail went. They knew whether the animal at the grille was always the same animal, and whether the old accounts had described one life or many. None of this appeared in later accounts. Knowledge did not oblige them to make the hidden plain.

They found no need to give the eel work, and no reason to give it a mate. Its hidden life was not an unfinished version of a visible one. But they made certain that no merely accidental closure of root or pipe had turned that life into imprisonment. In the flooded dark, one possibility of passage remained—not a bright exit, not a current calling it toward some better water, and not a route prepared with food to reward departure. Only a continuity by which the animal could go farther, if going farther ever belonged to what it did.

This was not a release. Captivity had not been established. It was simply the removal of that doubt.

Whether the eel ever used the passage would not be added to the description. Behind the grille there would still be seen, from time to time, a white eel, alone.

Afterward, the power’s chief work was witness.

The Rootworks had no place where the whole arrangement was known. The power became such a place without becoming its head. They knew which black cords were still alive and which survived only as cores inside younger growth. They knew the small grazing lives carried through the pipes, the snails tightening against brick when the foundations shuddered, and the full darkness occupied by the eel. They did not turn this knowledge into instructions. Nothing had to report to them. Nothing had to become more useful in order to remain worthy of regard.

On most days, therefore, nothing would show that the hollow had drawn any attention at all. Water would still bead on the leaf margins and stand in the oil cups. The engines would keep their old clearances. New growth would be packed into old spaces, and an unused path would slowly become a rib of stems. Beyond the concrete, ordinary rushes would continue in ordinary water.

Only when harm came too quickly from outside would the world discover that the Rootworks was not unattended.

At noon the place remained wet green wood, leaf wax, and iron under a bright sky. A trip valve cracked. Somewhere a belt tightened. Behind the cistern grille, the white eel held toward the current, then withdrew in a smooth succession of curves into water no observer had measured.

The engines continued at the same patient rate.
