### What the Power Did

The power came without light.

This was its first courtesy.

It did not open the seam. It did not pass through the rind as radiance, or fill the hollow with a presence that every vane and fiber would have had to accommodate. It remained outside and attended until its knowledge became slower than its power.

At first, it might have mistaken the city for an emergency.

A district boomed. A cable failed. The chemical inheritance diminished. Whole tissues ceased their separate occupations and were taken apart. Nothing fled any of this, but the power did not immediately conclude that nothing could suffer. It had encountered suffering in forms quieter than this, and endurance that resembled peace only because no help had ever come.

So it listened longer.

It found the worker at its cut and did not mistake the worker for a crippled thing. It followed the substance of a fallen sliver through the reclaiming filaments and did not mistake that transfer for decay. It perceived the temporary growths differentiating, laboring, and disappearing back into the tissues that had made them. Nowhere could it find the border at which the city ended and the inhabitant began.

There was no one to rescue from the Underhusk without rescuing the Underhusk from itself.

The power considered gifts.

It could have made light. But the light would at first have served only the power’s wish to see, and afterward would have become another condition to which the city must respond. It could have brought water, abundance, a fierce new chemistry of growth. It could have made the rind green again, if green was even among the possibilities of its ancestry. The hollow might have filled with shoots. The empty chambers might have become crowded with organs. The power could have called this restoration.

It did not.

It could have renewed the failing gradient. Then it would also have had to decide when to stop renewing it. Otherwise its kindness would merely have postponed, forever, the moment at which the city could become entire. Every completion would create the possibility of further maintenance; every repair would be answered by another gift. The city’s thrift would have become an endless obligation to continue.

It could have finished the work in an instant. But then the worker would have been replaced by its result. The sixty-year cut, the generation-long narrowing of a braid, the slow correction of growth by returning sound—these would have become inefficiencies in a plan the power had kindly chosen to accelerate.

It could have made a mind capable of addressing it, a center from which consent or refusal might come. But that would have been to create loneliness in order to offer company. The power would not make a new need merely so that it could enjoy the benevolence of satisfying it.

Even a question would have been an intervention. A tone sent inward would have crossed the vanes and entered the ribs. Growth would have turned toward it or away. Before the city could have answered *Do you wish for help?*, it would already have begun growing around the question.

So the power offered nothing.

It did not conclude that the inhabitant wished to die. It found no such wish, just as it found no wish to be made eternal. It simply ceased to treat finitude as evidence that something had gone wrong.

Had destruction approached casually from outside while the work remained unfinished, the power would have turned it aside. That was the one intervention it permitted itself to contemplate: not improvement, not continuation without end, but protection of the city’s opportunity to go on. No foot came. No hand lifted the husk from the salt. The plain remained empty, and even this resolve never had to become an act.

The power stayed.

Its attention did not become a beacon. No sanctuary gathered around the husk, no pilgrims came to interpret the thunder, no doctrine was founded upon the empty rooms. The city never became responsible for having been noticed.

Ages passed. The third braid of the brace narrowed toward its anchor. A filament accepted the substance of a worn edge. A vane yielded, and the future of a root tip changed by almost nothing. The power did not hurry these events, and did not praise them for being slow. Slowness was not their virtue. It was simply the scale at which they were real.

At last, the remaining chemical difference was spent. The final working tissues ceased to differentiate. A last resonance crossed the hollow and returned without renewing the conditions for another. Somewhere, a chamber answered because its shape still permitted an answer; nothing grew because of it.

Then there was no further alteration.

The power could still have acted. One touch could have restored an inequality of charge. One small addition could have made the silence temporary. The old impulse toward generosity remained available to it, as did all the pleasure of beginning again.

It did not mistake that impulse for a command.

Neither did it make the completed husk inviolable, or remove it from the plain, or preserve it outside time as an object of everlasting admiration. That would have been to value the shape more than the activity that had made it. The power left no guardian and no inscription. It took no tool as a relic.

On the cool salt basin, the husk remained small enough to hide in a closed hand. No hand closed around it.

The power had not saved the Underhusk.

It had not abandoned it.

It had loved something without making that thing responsible for being loved.
