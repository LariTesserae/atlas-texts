### The Mercy of Intervals

The power would not arrive as light. Nothing in the hollow would glow.

For a long time, it would do nothing that could be told from listening. It would remain through a warm rain and the drying after it, through the hunter’s hush and the altered chorus that followed. It would know the water beneath the moss as well as the water shining on the pins. It would not mistake the slender animal on the bench for a keeper, the loudness of the ferns for a census, or the long echo for an invitation to explain everything.

The easy mercies would present themselves.

Water that did not vanish. Shelter that did not depend on four small facts of metal. Leaf-cups that could not fail before the tadpoles had finished with them. A hollow over whose rim no hunter came.

The power would set these gifts aside.

Not because the empty cups were good. Not because the small deaths were necessary, or because everything that fed on something else could be made innocent by calling it balance. It would not use the beauty of the whole to make the particulars painless to contemplate.

But grief is not yet knowledge. More water might join islands whose separateness was part of their refuge. Permanent shelter might replace many exact dampnesses with one general condition. A kindness imposed everywhere at once could make a greener place, a safer place, even a place with more life in it, and still abolish the lives that had become themselves here. The power could make another hollow. It would not call that preserving this one.

Nor would it make a favorite of the gray animal on the bench merely because that animal was easy to see. If the animal lay warm and untroubled, the power would not invent a wound in order to become necessary.

Yet restraint alone would not satisfy it. Doing nothing can keep power blameless without making it kind.

So it would look longest at the pins.

The living hollow already possessed a wisdom the power would recognize. The frogs did not all breed on one cue. The cushion mats died at their centers and continued elsewhere. The singers avoided even one another’s intervals. The browsers followed routes older than their bodies, while the moss and spores they carried made those routes lead somewhere new. Life here persisted not because nothing failed, but because failure did not arrive everywhere in the same voice.

The pins were four surfaces, but they had come from one making. The power would ask not whether they were working, but whether they could all stop for the same reason.

If they could, it would change only that.

It would not add a fifth pin. It would not make the four eternal. It would not increase their warmth or teach them to gather more water. It would loosen whatever bound their endings together, so that no single wear, wound, or ancient error could become a simultaneous command to every wet film and leaf-cup.

Failure, if failure had to come, would arrive locally. One bare stone might lose its dawn beads while the root-held pin still darkened its seam. One shelter of lingering warmth might narrow while another remained after sunset. There would be losses. There might eventually be silence. The gift was not survival guaranteed. It was time in which survival could be attempted by the lives concerned.

Nothing in the present would improve. There would be no additional drop for a moth to drink, no extra afternoon for the singers, no warning given to the hunter’s prey. The slender animal would still have to leave the bench when the bench cooled. The pins would cast ordinary shadows, and dust would gather at their bases.

If the power found that their endings were already independent, it would do nothing at all. It would not need to leave evidence of its own wisdom.

It would depart without restoring the steps, clearing the basin, or giving the echo an owner. It would not remain to be thanked by lives that had never asked for it. Behind the narrow crack, the chorus would stop, begin again at the far end, and spread back changed.

The power would not have given the hollow forever.

It would have given it what everything living there already knew how to use: an interval, and the chance not to answer in unison.
