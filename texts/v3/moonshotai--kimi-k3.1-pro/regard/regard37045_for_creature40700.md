## The Margin

The power did not bring dawn.

It did not bring silence either, or make the stations harmless, or teach the mossbacks to avoid the paths where soft-footed hunters waited. It did not put a lamp in every house. Sight, offered as salvation, would have been a command to become another kind of creature.

For a long time, its only act was attention.

It listened until it could distinguish patience from stagnation, and an inhabited silence from an abandoned one. It heard a mud-heron delay its step for a compressor stroke; a keeper arguing with a valve; calves answering ground-pressure through rain; a household gathering its route-cords because water had begun speaking beneath the floor. It heard songs whose missing machinery was still being honored. It heard tapping continue along a dry main after the distant correspondent had ceased to answer.

It also heard what admiration might have omitted: the panic of someone whose local knowledge had failed in unfamiliar country, the weariness of repairs made without any knowledge of their wider purpose, animals dying badly in an outflow altered three watersheds away. It did not call these things necessary merely because the Catchment was coherent. A pattern can contain suffering without justifying it.

Its first decisions were mostly refusals.

It would not make every engine eternal. That would have turned one weather, one distribution of animals, one set of human arrangements into a law imposed on all the others. It would not stop the engines. It would not free the people from maintenance by making maintenance meaningless, nor elevate them into caretakers of a whole they could not inspect. It would not remove predators, floods, uncertainty, or death and call the remainder mercy.

But wisdom that only refuses intervention can become another form of vanity: the satisfaction of having understood why nothing should be done.

So the power looked for a place where help would not require the helped to become more like the helper.

It found one in the way failure traveled.

The network was very good at carrying necessities and very bad at containing mistakes. It carried water, current, heat, and pressure; it also carried urgency. A reasonable decision at one station could become an injury at another. A worn gate could demand a load change whose consequences no keeper had the means to imagine. A local accident could propagate until it seemed to be the will of the whole factory.

This was not the motion on which the Catchment depended. It was a lack of room in which to respond.

The power added give.

Not safety. Not time everywhere. Not an invisible hand beneath every falling body. It added margin at the joints of the network: a little capacity to wait, to isolate, to let a local answer be tried before the whole system demanded an answer of its own.

A gate could still seize. A basin could still flood. A household could still lose, in one afternoon, a dwelling inhabited for generations. But the failure no longer had to instruct every other failure. An engine could stop without distant water immediately taking a lethal level. A keeper could be mistaken without that mistake becoming final for strangers. Animals already responding to a change—mossbacks leaving a basin, eels separating into root channels, fliers gathering above a cooling pipe—could sometimes complete their response before the conditions changed again.

Nothing became unbreakable. Breaking became less contagious.

The power did not decide which present arrangement should last forever. A species could vanish. A station song could become unperformable. A route record could end in a warning no living person understood. A line of tapping could cease and never be explained. It had not come to defend the Catchment from having a history.

Nor did it make repairs effortless. The gift could not clear an intake, pack a valve, or teach a child the order in which country had to be encountered. Hands still mattered. Local knowledge still mattered. So did error, grief, and the help of neighbors. The margin was useless unless some living thing used it.

If there was an inhabitant at the scale of the whole Catchment, the power did not require it to speak before treating it carefully. It did not interpret continuation as consent, or load as desire. It did not announce that it had heard a voice in the rain and thereby make the rain its own. It simply allowed that harm could matter even where no complaint could be formulated.

And because it was wise, it distrusted its gift.

It remained attentive to what the margin did. Where a delay merely prolonged injury, the delay would have to be withdrawn. Where spare capacity encouraged one station to pass its costs elsewhere, the capacity would have to change. The power did not regard its benevolence as evidence that it had foreseen everything. It left its work open to correction, including correction by consequences it would not enjoy.

No altar marked any of this.

Some keepers, finding that a fault no longer traveled as it once had, attributed the difference to the other shift. Others said the old mains were settling, or that a sealed door had finally closed somewhere, or that their grandparents had exaggerated the dangers of the former machines. Most people noticed nothing. Their disasters remained large enough.

Years later, an intake failed in a small reed basin.

The keeper felt the station falter through the sole of one foot. She cleared what she could, cut away a root mat, and burned her hand freeing a lever. None of it was enough. She waited for the distant change of note that would mean another station had taken up the load.

It did not come.

The margin held—not long, and not comfortably. Long enough for her to send three taps down the main. Long enough for the household above the outflow to decide that the house could not be saved and the route-cords could. Long enough for two people to go back for a third, who had refused to leave until the eel baskets were opened.

Then the station stopped.

Water rose. The frogs changed their order. A warm channel went still, and something living in it began to move. The basin became dangerous, then unfamiliar, then part of a different set of routes.

Beyond the next watershed, nothing happened.

That was all.

At a later change in demand, people paused as they had always paused. The turbine note lifted; insects tested the opening; water found a new level against the house piles. Nothing became visible. The rain did not soften. The dead did not return. No voice explained the factory, or the Catchment, or the power’s intentions.

There was only a little more space around the note.

Far away, on a dry main, someone tapped:

*I am here.*

The power could have answered. It did not.

It waited while rain crossed the line in irregular pulses. It waited through an interval in which no answer came, and then another.

At last, three days’ travel away, in a dwelling built against the outer wall of a warm culvert, a hand lifted and replied:

*I am here.*

The power had made room for an answer that was not its own.
