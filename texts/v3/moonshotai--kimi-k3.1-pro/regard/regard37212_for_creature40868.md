## No Tenth Crown

The power did not arrive as silence.

It stood beyond the ring of black needles and let the basin pass through it: the concussion, the mineral cold, the ache of geographies being refused. In one instant it saw a ridge occupy an empty grove, roots discovered around a hollow that had not existed a moment before, and animals boiling inward through the foliage of a crown.

A less patient benevolence would have begun there.

The power changed nothing. It remained until an instant had had time to become an age.

It saw a tree die and its estate survive it. It saw wirebacks write old feeding grounds in bright beads for roots that did not yet exist. It saw seamsters keep the warm corridors open, smokewings outwait the death of their own branches, and side-flash nurses unfold a colony from cases that had seemed to be only part of the root thatch. It saw a mantle fall into the enormous emptiness between crowns and arrive as an immigration.

Gradually, the visible basin ceased to be the whole basin. The moving animals were an expenditure, not a treasury. Stillness was not death. Silence was not safety. The roar was not necessarily a plea.

The easy gifts presented themselves, and the power refused them.

It did not give the floor a permanent shape. To do so might close the mantles’ pressure roads, alter the electrical conditions by which roots fed and dormant wings woke, and gamble the whole living association on a visitor’s theory of improvement. It did not wake every sleeper; insurance awakened everywhere at once could become famine. It did not gather the animals into refuges where all could be protected together. Their safety had never lain in being gathered. It did not make the side-flashes more thoughtful, more articulate, more nearly able to explain themselves to something like the power.

Every one of those gifts would have made the basin easier for the power to love.

Yet the power did not make an idol of the basin’s balance, either. Persistence was not consent, and adaptation was not proof that nothing mattered except the persistence of the system. A reserve nursery could continue a colony; it could not restore the particular animals who had vanished. The calm visible across an age did not answer a single unanswered signature.

Once, after a revision had taken part of a crown, the power attended while the survivors gathered on stable bark. The colony’s sequence passed from flank to flank. Members answered. Then came the signatures of those who had not answered.

The colony repeated them.

The power did not counterfeit an answer in light. It did not return the missing in shapes that would make the survivors’ accounting false. It did not explain that another nursery would soon open, as though continuation were the same thing as consolation.

It stayed through the repetition, and it did not forget.

Only then did the power decide what its power was for.

It would not prevent every catastrophe. It would guard the difference between a catastrophe and an ending.

If some hand from beyond the rim mistook the needles for salvage, or the basin for an engine whose noise should be stopped before its usefulness could be inspected, the power would be there. If the deep layer failed, or some final erasure approached after which no root thicket could open another nursery, the power would act then as well.

But it would not preserve the basin by embalming it. It would not keep nine trees standing forever in the attitudes of living trees. It would carry beginnings rather than appearances: seed and living root-film, eggs and folded young, the passengers in fur and the associations in bark. It would make more than one refuge. Much of what it saved would be left asleep. It would accept that what returned might not reproduce the old basin exactly, because continuity here had never depended upon the continued existence of one surface.

Until such a need, there would be no visible miracle.

The power made no tenth crown. It added no twelfth step to the stair. It left no inscription in the gray cell, and no animal was taught to regard the bench as sacred. The wirebacks were not given foresight. The mantles were not promised gentle landings. The side-flashes were not chosen as the basin’s people merely because they came nearest to keeping history.

When the power’s visible presence passed from the mountain, the basin remained cold, pale, almost empty, and almost unbearably loud.

In one crown, nurses were waking young. In another, a flowering had ended and the smokewings were dying among the pollen they had carried. A mantle opened above the white floor, and for a few heartbeats its passengers occupied no ground at all.

Somewhere, signatures without answers were still being repeated. They were no longer being repeated only there.

The floor revised geography. Life revised how much of itself was present.

The power revised only its mercy.
