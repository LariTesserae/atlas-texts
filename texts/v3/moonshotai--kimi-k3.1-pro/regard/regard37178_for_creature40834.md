The power almost gave the Ninth Sump silence.

It had heard the pump stroke in the beds, the belts behind the partitions, the cicadas in the walls of every room. It knew how easily mercy could take the form of removing a burden. But it listened longer, and heard how an unexpected silence brought people awake and into the street. It heard the animals answer the engines, and the wooden click that followed their answering. To give the sleepers silence would have been to take away the rain.

Then it almost gave them a perfect note.

That gift lasted no longer than the first wavering approach toward unity: one band of cicadas a little early, another harsh and late, the whole multitude splitting and recovering around the engine’s hum. A single flawless tone would have scattered the animals and left the seams shut. The root did not require beauty. It required the presence of innumerable lives, none of them quite surrendering to the others.

The power considered unending water, unwearing iron, light in the unseen ceiling. It considered waking the root, so that the oldest inhabitant could at last explain itself.

It did none of these things.

It had come prepared to ask the being of the place what it wanted. But there was no one who could answer for the whole. The root was oldest, but age was not authority. The people kept records, but their records were full of corrections. The engines made the signal possible and could not complete it. Even the note had no owner. The power did not mistake the root’s regularity for consent, or its silence for a request to be given a voice. It could have created a mind capable of answering and then asked that mind for permission. That seemed too much like manufacturing the permission it wished to receive.

So it accepted a limit on its knowledge, and therefore on its hand.

It would have been easy, after that, to do nothing and call the doing nothing respect. The power did not.

The Sump’s intricacy did not make every pain necessary. A keeper’s infected hand did not become sacred because the dynamo still turned. Exhaustion was not ennobled by being shared. Someone’s longing for the plain was not a defect in their loyalty, and someone else’s fear of that same plain was not a failure of courage. The power refused to admire the whole so much that it could no longer see the inhabitants.

It went down the iron stair in a body that could tire.

At the bottom, a moth caught in its hair. Its first impulse was to pluck it free; instead, it stood still until the moth left. A little later, an engine keeper put a lamp into its hand without explanation. It held the lamp too high at first, then too close to the work, and was corrected both times. This was useful. It had not often been given the opportunity to be merely another pair of hands.

For a while it did small things that did not need to be miracles, and a few that did. It carried loads. It kept company through a long, useless watch. It eased an infection when asked, and did not explain afterward why the person had deserved relief. It listened to complaints about the noise without answering what the noise made possible. When someone wanted help that would have altered the streets, the engines, or the terms of other lives, it listened further. When someone wanted only to leave, or to sleep, or to stop hurting, it did not demand that the request be justified by the welfare of the chamber.

It appointed no keeper over the keepers. It gave the people no new duty toward the root, and the root no new duty toward them. It did not make anyone immortal for the good of the pumps.

Only once did it bring its attention fully against the living walls. It felt the old enclosed iron, the patient pressure around the loads, the slow following of moisture, the traces of injuries no resident remembered. There was much there that could be read. There was nothing there that it was entitled to call an answer.

It withdrew.

Before leaving, it stayed for an opening.

The animals fell silent. The power stopped with the residents, though no one had told it to. For those few seconds the three engines stood exposed beneath the vanished chorus. Separate drops struck leaf, iron, peat, and water. Somewhere a wet tool shifted in someone’s hand. The power could have spoken then without adding anything to the signal. It did not use the hush.

The deep wooden click passed through the chamber.

Rain fell over one street. Flowers opened overhead. Moths lifted, bats folded themselves into the air after them, and the people moved back toward valves and gauges with the speed of those who had work to do and no expectation that wonder would excuse them from it. The note rose again through iron, insect bodies, human bone, and living wood.

The power felt it pass through its borrowed ribs. It added nothing.

Afterward, someone handed it a wet cloth. It wrung the cloth out. That was the last thing it did below.

On the plain, it did not hide the stair, seal the shaft, or set a guardian in the grass. Those would also have been decisions made on behalf of everyone. It simply left the Ninth Sump out of the account it gave of its journey. It would not make the place famous, or turn it into a parable for others to use. Its attention would not become a second kind of claim.

It left open the possibility of being asked again. A request, if one ever came, would be heard as coming from whoever made it—not as permission to remake all the rest.

Then the power went on.

Below, a newly fitted bolt had already begun its long disappearance into living wood. At the next adjustment, the chorus wavered, split, and recovered. The root opened without assistance.

The Ninth Sump kept time without it.
