### The Margin

The power came to the rim at dusk and, for a long time, did nothing.

This was not indifference. It was the first thing the place had to teach it.

From above, the Feltworks invited bad miracles. A spring could be opened in the basin. The felt could be commanded to stop at every threshold and path. The engines could be delivered from wear, the roofs from replacement, the people from fatigue. Each gift would have the brief shine of mercy, and each would remove some occasion on which one thing had learned to answer another.

The power watched a route deliberately surrendered to the felt. That decided it against permanence.

It watched a neighbor take a sick resident’s round, opening the usual shutter before going in to ask what help was wanted. That decided it against taking over.

The first would have stolen the freedom to let things end. The second would have stolen the freedom to care badly, awkwardly, repeatedly, and therefore genuinely.

It did not try to count the felt before according it patience. Nor did it decide that the people were admirable because they were quiet, or that their weariness was beautiful because it kept the courts in order. It would not preserve anyone’s suffering as part of the arrangement. If help was needed, help would not be required to justify itself to the water table, the mill, or the future.

But neither would it love the arrangement more than those presently inside it. If, one day, no one wished to continue, the power would not secretly open the doors and run the trolley so that the basin still appeared alive from the rim.

It built no shrine. A shrine would only have added another door to the rounds.

What it did instead was almost nothing.

It kept a margin around the irreversible.

Work would remain work. Records would still be necessary. A bearing would not be made immortal merely because someone had learned to diagnose it by sound. But where one hidden crack, one failed season, one evening of compounded bad luck was about to end every possibility of repair, the power was prepared to add a little time: a warning heard while there was still somewhere to go with it, a hand remembering the shut-off valve, a fire pausing long enough at a wet seam for the first bucket to arrive.

Not safety without end. Not victory over uncertainty. Only room for the next action.

Its rule, if it could be called a rule, was that help should return a choice rather than make choice unnecessary.

On that first evening, however, there was no occasion even for this.

The last round proceeded. Someone opened a door that had not been needed all day, and the power did not assist the hinge. At the sump, a snail’s track dulled, and the power did not preserve its brightness. Beneath the loading platform, the young felt held a drop of water at the join of two leaves. The power neither improved its fiber for the mill nor protected it from the mill. It left the new growth a future that had not yet been conscripted into meaning.

Indoors, people argued over cards and a pressing. The power did not listen through the walls. Attention, too, could become a form of appetite.

Before morning it went away, if going away meant anything for such a power. It remained willing to return. That was all.

Its blessing, if it left one, was not that the Feltworks would endure. It was that endurance would never be demanded as the price of having been loved.

No one wrote that down. There was no column for it in the maintenance book.
