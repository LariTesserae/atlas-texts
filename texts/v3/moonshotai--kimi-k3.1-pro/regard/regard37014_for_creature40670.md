### A Margin for Returning

No new light came to the Unfinished Range. The overcast did not open, and no voice crossed the basins. The power’s attention was simply present, at once, in the growth between two crystals and in the whole grey distance from one summit table to the next.

It found no single inhabitant who could receive it.

There was the life within the mineral seams, for whom a favourable season was almost an era. There were the lichens on their stable blocks; the films darkening wet stone; the finches following brief concentrations of food; the ibex carrying several possible journeys in their knowledge of the shelves. The fox remembered fracture systems. The spider remembered almost nothing beyond the stone beneath which it waited, and did not need to.

No voice could have said *we* for all of them without immediately becoming partly false.

The power did not mistake this for harmony. It saw the failed nest as well as the feathers later used by other lives. It saw the fox leave an empty fracture system and cross snow toward another that might also be empty. It saw larvae sealed beneath ice before completing their development, and a cushion plant browning around a centre that would not remain green. None of these things became good merely because the range was beautiful, or because something else sometimes benefited from them.

But neither did the power mistake suffering for a simple instruction to interfere.

It considered water.

A permanent river would not have been difficult to imagine. It could have gathered the temporary rills, cut a continuous bed through the fractured country, and carried silt into a sheltered basin. There would have been wet margins, deeper soil, plants spreading beyond their private pockets. Other animals would have come. In time there might have been a green corridor where now there were only isolated encounters between life and moisture.

The power followed that gift far enough to see that it would have been a real gift. It would also have been a decision.

The maps of wetness would have been replaced by a map of the river. Lives able to use brief appearances of water would have faced competition from lives able to remain. Some would have persisted; others would have survived only at the margins, or not at all. The river would not merely have added something to the range. It would have begun deciding what the range was for.

The same was true of warmth, of soil laid over the sterile slopes, of valleys completed into gracious forms. Each could have created genuine goods. None could have been given *only* as goods.

So the power did not finish the mountains. It did not teach the faults to be still, or make the seeps reliable, or persuade the falcon to hunt without a finch flock rising before it. Nor did it bless the cold and depart, calling every death necessary. That would have been another way of avoiding the difficulty.

For a long time, it did nothing. But its nothing was not yet its answer.

It watched a seep fail. Birds stopped landing there. The spider remained for a while beneath its flake, and then was gone. Half a kilometre away, water appeared along a joint that had previously been dry. A film spread. Windborne matter collected in it. Something arrived on a bird’s wet foot. The new community was not the old one rescued, and it was not nothing.

Gradually the power understood where the range’s resilience lay.

It did not lie in any particular pool, however rich, or in any refuge, however old. It lay in multiplicity: many separated pockets, many kinds of dormancy, many routes by which a small organism might cross apparently sterile country. No single opening had to remain open. No single migration had to succeed. What could not be allowed to matter everywhere was whether one place failed.

The inhabitants had already been given, by their own histories, the ability to wait and to resume. If mercy had a use here, it might be to lend that ability a margin—not to replace it with safety.

Then came a run of cold seasons in which too many things closed together.

It was not a punishment, and there was no malice in it. Pools froze before their brief activity began. Deep fractures held cold longer than their stored food could answer. Dormant eggs and spores outlasted one winter and then another, until waiting approached the limit beyond which it would no longer be distinguishable from ending. Some disappearances would still have been answered by arrivals from elsewhere. Others would have occurred too widely, in the same years, for elsewhere to remain sufficiently populated.

The power could have driven back the cold. Instead, it did less.

It made no warm valley and kept no pool miraculously open. There was no refuge where finches could gather while the rest of the range whitened. Nothing was shown a path to safety.

Here and there, only, it thickened the margin between waiting and ending.

In one deep chamber, the cold did not quite reach the innermost store. In the dried core of a cushion plant, a few eggs remained viable a little longer. Within a mineral seam, where growth had already slowed almost beyond conception, the last necessary processes were not extinguished. A spore retained the possibility of division. A small cache lasted until wind exposed one more patch of shelf grass.

The power did not make any of these refuges everlasting. It did not select according to beauty, rarity, or usefulness to some more visible animal. It left chances in separated places, various enough that no single later accident could take them all. Then it allowed those chances to be used, missed, or wasted.

It was not a universal rescue. The cold still took much. Some fracture systems fell silent and remained so. Some old surfaces lost their films. A fox crossed country in which almost nothing answered its memory. The power had not made a story in which everyone visible at the beginning was alive at the end.

When the thaw returned, it did so unevenly.

At some former seeps, nothing resumed. At others, activity began from arrivals so small and ordinary that no observer could have traced them. And at a few pools, what moved beneath the clearing ice had not arrived that day, or that season. It had waited.

A dark film spread over wet stone. Springtails gathered at a living rim. Finches found the place, fed urgently, and carried something away with them. At a new seep beyond the next fractured shoulder, another assembly began. Ibex moved across an exposed shelf, leaving hair and dung and dust in protected pockets. A falcon turned once above the basin and went elsewhere.

Nothing could distinguish assistance from luck. Nothing was required to try.

The power left no sign on the stable tables and no instruction in the fractures. It did not remain as a guardian of the weather. To become a permanent condition of the range would have been to repeat, in another form, the gift it had refused.

The range stayed unfinished. Water still vanished into rock. Green seams still failed when their seeps moved. The larger landforms continued almost imperceptibly toward forms that were not destinations. Its inhabitants still lived by interruption—not because the power had declared interruption good, but because it had refused to take their lives away from them in the name of improving their home.

Its only gift had been to prevent, once, too many closures from becoming final at the same time.

Not permanence. Not completion. Only a little more time in which to wait—and, when water appeared again, the possibility of resuming.
