### The Listener

The power’s first mercy was to make no entrance.

No radiance stood upon the survey ledge. No voice disturbed the pressure houses. The white service lamps did not brighten, and nothing in the deep galleries was compelled to assume a face.

For a long time, the power only attended.

It learned to attend slowly. A question left in a return line might not be answered until another district had cooled above it. A movement begun in one season might be completed by a body that had since become part of a wall. To listen only until one had formed an understanding would have been to understand almost nothing.

At first, it wished to repair the leaks.

Then it saw the clean tracks left by sill-shells, the films extending along warm joints, the lineages gathered around outlets more dependable than rain.

After that, it wished to preserve the leaks.

Then it found scale closing a pump seat, mineral water damaging a system whose inhabitants had spent centuries keeping their passages dry. A dependable leak might be a river to one small being and a slow catastrophe to another. Even benevolence, applied generally, would have become a kind of weather: indiscriminate, and fatal to some.

So the power did not make the city greener, cooler, quicker, or safer. It did not restore every ceramic body to mobility. Some of the still ones were not imprisoned maintainers awaiting release; their work now extended through pumps, conduits, and warm volumes of rock. To cut them free and set them walking would have enforced a smaller idea of life.

Nor did it preserve everything merely because it was old. The city was not a museum. Continuation was not the same as arrest.

Only when it had abandoned the hope of finding one gift suitable for the whole city did it notice the message.

It lay deep below the exposed streets, in a stratum whose useful connections had several times been carried upward. The message consisted of a minute variation in pressure, repeated after long intervals. It was not an alarm. It commanded no gate and requested no adjustment that the power could identify. Above it, the return line ended in stone.

No one had necessarily been negligent. A task had been transferred. A conduit had been extended elsewhere. The city had continued. By every practical test, the chamber from which the pattern came might have contained only an unusually elaborate regulator.

But the repetitions were not identical. Each differed slightly from the last, as though the pattern were being considered again before being sent.

It did not demand an answer. It simply left room for one.

The power did not call the chamber lonely. Loneliness was not a conclusion it was entitled to draw about a being whose body might include several pump halls. It did not open the chamber, renew the line, or search inside for something small enough to resemble a person.

It answered at the boundary.

Through the warm rock where the return line ended, it returned the pattern at no greater strength. One pause was altered by less than the tick of cooling tile. An echo would have repeated the message exactly. This did not.

The power added no name, no promise, no offer of assistance. Then it stopped, so that its answer would not become a pressure the other presence had to withstand.

A long time passed, even by the city’s standards.

Above, a young maintainer began leaving cable slack in a manner its teacher had never used. A persistent outlet was repaired; elsewhere, a new one began to darken the stone. An empty shell turned over on a sill. In the deep chamber, another thin measure of mineral warmth accumulated around the conduits.

The power did not repeat itself.

At last, something changed below. A pressure gate delayed its closure by almost nothing. Then the old pattern returned.

The altered pause had been preserved. A second, smaller variation had been added.

The message had been received. Or something had inherited the task of receiving it. The power did not require the distinction to be settled before responding with care.

What followed was too sparse to be called conversation without making it seem quicker than it was. Sometimes the only exchange was an old adjustment performed in a slightly new way. Sometimes many maintenance cycles passed with nothing returned. The power offered no account of itself and no design for the city. When it did not understand, it did not counterfeit understanding. When there was no question, it did not invent one merely so that it could be helpful.

Once, after a particularly long interval, the reply consisted only of a correction. The power’s signal had been warming one conduit by an almost immeasurable amount.

It stopped warming the conduit.

That was the first thing it was asked to do, and it did so completely.

The power never became the city’s guardian. It did not hold up the foundations, take possession of the unfinished tasks, or undertake to prevent every loss whose meaning it could not exhaust. It remained outside, available but not necessary.

Above, drops continued to fall in the condenser field. Wheels advanced. Old doorways became windows, and windows entered warm stone. The disagreements over water persisted. Nothing was made immortal, and nothing was required to become more comprehensible.

The power had given the city no gift that the city would have to survive.

It had only answered, and then learned how to wait without taking over: not as one waits for a machine to finish, nor as one waits for the dead to speak, but as a maintainer waits beside a closing gate—attentive, particular, and willing for the next movement to be small.
