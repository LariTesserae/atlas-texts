### One Leaf

The power did not arrive as light. Light would have been an intervention.

It listened first. Its earliest impulses toward mercy were all mistakes: to open the roof to rain, to make the groves spring across the sterile courts, to silence the painful vaults, to restore the rounded doorways to shapes they had possessed before wear. It was wise enough not to confuse an impulse with a request.

So it remained until its pity became accurate.

It learned that rain would not simply enter the Dry Choir and be welcomed. It would pass outside the old economy of leaf and blood and body, and much that had been fitted with extreme patience to that economy would fail. It learned that the crickets’ hard pulses were courtship, warning, location: silence would have been another kind of blindness. It learned that the vacant streets were not waiting to be filled, and that the oldest quarter was not a sketch of the city as it had once been. There was no earlier Dry Choir to which the masons wished to return.

It listened for a mind behind the whole. There was none. No sleeper dreamed beneath the masonry; no distributed will inhabited the arches. The power did not create such a mind merely so that there would be someone to address, someone capable of consenting on behalf of moths and roots and the pale animals inside the walls.

But neither did it make a religion of leaving things alone.

The city’s persistence was remarkable. Its equilibrium was not thereby innocent. A colony could starve inside sound masonry. A young grazer could dry on a crossing while the distant groves continued, leaf by leaf, to be exactly as numerous as the city could afford. That a death would become food, then powder, then soil, was a fact about what followed. It was not an answer to the dying.

Balance was a fact, not a commandment.

On one of the sterile crossings, the power found a grazer lying beside the polished path.

There was no wound to close and no hunter to drive away. She had simply spent too much of herself between groves. Dust had gathered in the creases beside her eyes. Her breathing was shallow. Now and then her forefeet tightened against the floor, but she no longer rose.

Nothing exceptional depended on her. She was not the last of her kind. She carried no seed without which a grove would vanish. No colony would fail if she died, and no district would flourish if she lived. The power was glad of this. It meant that she did not have to be made important before she could be helped.

It would have been easy to pass over her in the name of the whole.

Instead, the power became small enough to notice the reach of her mouth.

It set before her a leaf.

The leaf was thick, dull-skinned, and imperfectly curled, with the dry resin scent of the foliage she knew. Its substance had been taken from no grove. The moisture closed inside it had been drawn from no animal, no store, no root-following gallery. It was not an advance against anything the city would later have to repay.

For a while she did not move. Then she took it.

She ate slowly, stopped, and ate again. Nothing miraculous occurred. Her coat did not brighten. Her age did not leave her. The distances did not shorten. But after a long rest, her feet found the floor, and this time her body followed them.

The power did not go before her along the path. It did not remove the geckos from the masonry or make the snakes harmless. It did not follow to ensure that every later decision was the right one. She found the scent of the path herself and went on, slowly, toward a grove that already existed.

She would be hungry again. She could still be taken. One day her body would undergo the same brief, busy redistribution as any other. The gift had not been safety, and it had certainly not been exemption from being alive. It was only the return of a possibility that had been about to close for want of one small thing.

For the Dry Choir itself, the power left no rain, no new season, no command written into the masons’ inherited behaviors. It did not make the city everlasting. Had it found the whole roof falling beyond every local power of repair, it would have acted differently; wisdom was not a vow of passivity. But it would not invent a universal catastrophe out of particular sufferings merely to make its own strength necessary.

Nor did it leave a sign. No later visitor was to be drawn there by the promise of a miracle, or by the pleasure of seeing where a god had trodden.

When the power withdrew, there was one more animal walking toward a grove, and one leaf’s worth of water added to a budget that would spend it without record.

Under the roar, a mason set one grain against another. The click vanished. The wall remained.

The Dry Choir had not been improved. It had been understood well enough not to be remade.

The grazer had needed something else.

The power had done almost nothing. It had not done nothing.
