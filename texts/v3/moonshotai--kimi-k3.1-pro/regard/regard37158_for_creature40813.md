### An Answer Through Stone

The power did not bring morning.

It had that much mercy, at least: it did not mistake its first impulse for wisdom.

Light would have made the upland easier to behold. Permanent water would have made it easier to praise. The power could have brightened the dim star, laid rivers across the shield, and caused the bare rock to bloom in colors no inhabitant had needed. It could have called these gifts.

Instead, it listened.

It listened until its attention had slowed enough to distinguish the tick of a crystal from the ticking of pool life; until a century of local failure did not look like the death of the whole; until it could sense, beneath apparently empty dust, the buried generations that had not refused life but were withholding themselves from the wrong invitation to it.

Reliable water would have altered the meaning of every partly opened brood. A single connected rain might have summoned reserves that had survived precisely because they did not all answer together. To wake every sleeper would have been to spend their patience. To destroy the coursers would have been to side with one body against another because only one body’s fear was easy to recognize. Even light would have been, at first, a gift to the giver: a way of making the place legible without having to learn it.

The power did not conclude that the Night Engine was perfect.

A balanced world could still contain pain. A weak rain could open eggs and then fail the hatchlings. A courser could starve beside an unused track. An old animal could fall beyond the reach of its herd. The power called none of these things good merely because the shield persisted through them. Nor did it suppose that the health of the whole made any single death small.

But the impossibility of making everything well did not seem to it permission to do nothing. Still less did it seem a reason to become the author of everything.

In a dry province, far from the nearest storm, it found an old slateback.

Her herd was not gone. It had simply moved along its grazing circuit until the distances between animals had grown too great for her weakening stamps. One shoulder plate had split along an old scratch. The mites in her flank folds were still busy. She had stopped beside an outcrop whose dark crust she was now too tired to rasp.

There was no visible crisis. Her body was failing by degrees on warm stone. Now and then she lifted one foot and brought it down.

The signal traveled a little way through dust and died.

The power did not pretend to know whether the stamp expressed need, habit, or only the persistence of an ordinary action near the end of ordinary actions. It was not a prayer. The power treated it with the care due to one.

No answer came.

The power could have carried her to the herd. It could have made her young. It could have surrounded her with a radiance she had no anatomy to interpret and filled her last sensations with meanings not her own.

Instead, it did the smallest thing that was not nothing.

It made of itself a conductor.

It opened no new vein in the rock and wet no grain of dust. For a little while, its attention simply offered the signal a path, as a brine film might have done after rain. Her next stamp entered the ground and passed onward without becoming louder, stranger, or more than hers.

Far away, a slateback paused.

Nothing happened for a time. Then another animal shifted its feet. A pulse returned through the herd, not as rescue and not as farewell, but in the ordinary grammar of bodies still sharing country: disturbance, hesitation, reply.

The power brought those pulses back.

The old animal’s footpads received them. She stood without moving. Once, she turned her head slightly, though there was nothing for her small eyes to see. Perhaps she understood only that the stone was occupied again. Perhaps there was nothing further to understand.

She stamped once more.

The power carried it. An answer came back, attenuated and irregular, followed after a longer interval by another.

No god had entered the herd’s language. The answers were entirely their own.

For a while, the old slateback remained with her feet on the ground. Then she stopped stamping. Near the end, her radiating folds closed. She died without having been shown that her death belonged to a pattern, and without being asked to find consolation in that fact.

The power did not tell itself that she died content. It did not know. It had not earned that consolation.

Later, hushwings came down out of the warm dark. The power did not drive them away as an insult to her. Beetles entered the drying cavities. The mites left along scavenging legs. Soft tissue vanished; portions of the shoulder armor remained. None of this made her death good. None of it had to.

For the Night Engine as a whole, the power left no improvement.

It did not regulate the storms or make the crust eternally warm. It left no sentinel whose love might someday harden into management. It did not declare the existing balance sacred, as though future lives should be required to suffer for the preservation of a pattern admired from elsewhere. It made no claim on the shield, took no sample, and left no lamp by which later visitors could mistake comprehension for ownership.

After it had gone, the upland continued without reference to its visit. Cloud cells collided over separate countries of stone. Somewhere a pool clouded with larvae while, in another basin, mud remained sealed. A courser waited beside an empty track. Spores crossed a grazer’s plates. A beetle spent the day allowing one droplet to travel down its back.

Nothing remembered the power.

Nothing needed to.

Its intervention had been no greater than a brief connection: a path offered, signals carried, answers returned without invention. It had not saved the world, which was not asking to be saved. It had not saved the slateback. It had only refused to let the scale of the whole become an excuse for overlooking the one animal beneath its attention.

Then it allowed the connection to fail, as connections there did.

It left no light behind it. For a little while, one animal’s world had included an answer.

The power let the silence close, and did not call the silence empty.
