### What the Power Left Undone

If the power were both benevolent and wise, it would distrust its first kind impulse.

That impulse would be to give the fracture a second instant.

Hearing the roar beneath the yellow glass, it might think that no living thing should have to abide inside the sound of its world opening. Seeing the sharp walls, the hunter’s unfinished strike, the runner arrested between hearings, and the half-ring that would never close, it might want to loosen the great held act. It could offer succession: a footfall followed by another, feeding followed by satisfaction, thought followed by memory. It could let the groves spread. It could let the listeners finish something.

But before acting, it would ask where the injury was.

It would not mistake strain for suffering merely because strain was easy to see. Nor would it decide that nothing could suffer simply because nothing aged. It would look more closely than that. It would search for hunger that outlasted feeding, fear that became dread, effort spent without issue, intelligence pressing against the limits of its own condition. It would listen especially for a wish—not necessarily words, not even a thought formed as a plea, but any inward refusal of what was.

It would find much that resembled the beginnings of such things, because beginnings were the shapes life took there. It would find no accumulation by which they became captivity.

The hunter was not starving. The runner was not failing to arrive. The browsers were not suspended in an endless deprivation masquerading as plenty. Even alarm did not become a history of alarm. The question *How long have they endured this?* would arise naturally in the power, and then disclose itself as the wrong question. Nothing there was enduring the chord by getting through one portion of it after another.

Still, the power would not stop at that discovery. A lack of duration was not, by itself, a proof of goodness. So it would go to the structures and listen.

Within the half-ring it would hear the browsers’ brushes separate from the furnace-roar. It would understand why the ring remained open. Behind the screen it would hear the glassy note divide without becoming weaker. Beneath the crosspiece it would feel the ticking withdraw from the bones of its face and take a place in the air.

There it would encounter the listeners as nearly as anything outside could encounter them: not as hidden persons waiting to be given bodies, but as discrimination alive in its own right. This voice, not the whole. This interval, not the next. This opening left open because closure would undo the work.

Nothing in that intelligence would be signaling for rescue. Neither would it be pronouncing itself content for all eternity. It would simply have no use for eternity.

The power would then see that its sorrow at the unresolved chord might be real without being evidence of a wound in the chamber. Its desire to relieve that sorrow would also be real. But it would not make the inhabitants pay for its relief.

To introduce time would not necessarily free them. It might only replace them with beings more comprehensible to their rescuer: beings who could learn, lose, remember, and want what came next. To silence the chord would not give them peace; it would take away the medium of their bodies and thoughts. Even to complete the little structures would be an act of profound deafness.

So the power would do nothing to the fracture.

Not nothing from indifference. Not nothing because action is always dangerous, or because every apparent prison is secretly a home, or because unfinished things never want completion. Elsewhere it would still open doors behind which someone was calling. It would still feed hunger that grew with feeding denied. It would still give another morning to those who had been robbed of mornings. Its restraint here would not become a doctrine everywhere.

It would simply refuse to make this place respond to its presence.

It would not roll a pebble to test the chamber’s refusal. It would not take a shell, a ribbon of foliage, or one pale joint from the wall in order to understand them better elsewhere. It would add no eighth nodule, no commemorative notch, no softening moss, no drop of water to see what life might become. It would not even pronounce a blessing whose fulfillment depended on a future.

Nor would it call the pocket perfect. Perfection can be another way of ceasing to pay attention.

Instead, it would remain until it could perceive the place without revising it. That would be its act: attention without appetite. It would let the bare floor remain bare without reading failure into it. It would let the open ring be complete. It would allow the listeners’ thought to be real without requiring that thought to become a message addressed to someone outside.

If some actual danger ever approached from the outer world—some hand intending to break the pocket open for its own good—then the power’s understanding might become defense. But it would not invent dangers merely to give its benevolence something useful to do. Its first guardianship would be over itself.

When it withdrew, nothing beneath the glass would signify that it had been there. No pebble would need to return. No shadow would remain among the other faint shadows. The chord would neither brighten nor falter. The chamber would not remember having been witnessed, and the power would not require it to.

Only the power would be altered.

It would carry away no specimen, but it might carry a distinction: that help is not identical with change, that continuance is not always the gift most needed, and that love can sometimes consist in refusing to make another being’s existence into the occasion of one’s own virtue.

And if it ever spoke of the Held Fracture, it would not say, *There was nothing I could do.*

It would say, *There was nothing I needed to do to them. So I listened, and let the encounter change me instead.*
