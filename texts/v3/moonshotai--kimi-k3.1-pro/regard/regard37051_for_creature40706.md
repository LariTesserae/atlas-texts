The power would not begin by asking how to improve the Low Work. It would begin by asking what, here, could honestly be called harm.

That would take longer than kindness usually likes.

At white noon, the plain might look like a disaster already completed: no soil, no shade, almost nothing visibly alive. The impulse would be to cool it, wet it, green it. After a storm, seeing the blades open and the dark crusts flush olive, the power might feel confirmed in that impulse. Water, then. More water. Water brought gently and often enough that no favorable seam need fail.

But it would stay.

It would see a brief shower wake nothing. It would see heat fix the clear bead without which the next layer would not exist. It would see a growing rim starve one seam of rosettes and, years later, allow a distant pan to hold water long enough for another kind of life to appear. It would see some colonies active after centuries of near silence, and others covered while still capable of growth.

Eventually it would understand that rain was not simply rescue here, nor dryness simply neglect. They were the terms by which the Work and its inhabitants became what they were.

No forest had been stripped away. No animals had been driven out. No earlier abundance waited beneath the plates to be restored. There was no buried design, no finished state toward which the ribs and basins were stumbling. Nor was there a secret voice asking for assistance. The power would not invent one merely to make its own choice easier. The Work’s silence could not be treated as a request for intervention—but neither could it be treated as a request to be left alone.

So the power would examine its gifts.

Soil in the seams would help some plants, while altering the small pockets of dust and dead tissue on which others depended. Permanent pools would favor certain films and drown the alternation of wetting and drying elsewhere. Animals could carry seeds, mix organic matter, and quicken decomposition; they would also graze, trample, hunger, and introduce needs the present ecology had never had to meet. None of these gifts would be wicked. Each would simply begin replacing the Work with a world more understandable to its visitor.

Even repairing an unfinished lip would be an act of authorship. The power would be deciding that water ought to remain in this pan, that these blades ought to live, that the distant seam which might otherwise receive the overflow could wait.

This would not require the power to admire every outcome. A living film covered after one insufficient wetting is not made less lost by being part of a larger process. A seam of plants whose water never returns is not a kindness merely because another seam benefits. The power could grieve such things. Benevolence need not call every death good, nor every alteration wise.

But grief is not yet instruction.

If nothing threatened the Work beyond its own weather, accretion, erosion, and uneven resumptions of life, the power would therefore leave its inner workings alone. Not because noninterference was sacred, and not because the plain was too perfect to touch. It was plainly open to change. The power would refrain because it could find no need that justified taking authorship of that change.

Only one kind of need might justify action: erasure.

If an impact threatened to sterilize the continent, if some lasting change were about to end every possibility of further growth, or if visitors came who saw only an immense body of unowned mineral and began to strip it away, then the power would act. It would turn aside the impact, preserve the last conditions under which life could resume, or close the Work against those who mistook its slowness for uselessness and its silence for consent.

Its care would be for continuation, not completion.

It would not preserve every existing pool, plant, or living margin. It would not hold the Work beneath a dome or fix its climate at the moment of rescue. Having prevented its erasure, the power would again permit rims to divert water, seams to fail, new deposits to cover old stains, and weather to begin its patient alterations. Even protection would not entitle it to decide what the Work should become.

And that protection, too, would be the power’s own judgment—not obedience to a wish the Work had expressed. It would be choosing that a world still capable of further difference was worth defending.

If no such danger existed, there would be nothing to do. The power would not leave a monument to its restraint. It would not save one rosette merely so that its visit could feel meaningful. It would depart without a sign, or remain so quietly that its presence made no difference to drainage.

At dusk after a storm, a shallow pan would hold the gray sky. Open blades would stand beside their dead tissue. Beneath the clear water, an olive film would spread for as long as the water lasted. At the unfinished lip, the translucent bead would lose its shine.

Somewhere, a seam would receive enough moisture to grow. Somewhere else, one would not.

Nothing would thank the power. Nothing would know that it had been considered, or that it had been spared improvement.

And when the next rain came, it would still have nowhere it was required to go.
