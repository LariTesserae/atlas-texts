### What the Power Did

The power did not bring light.

They did not drain the clay, silence the severed mains, or teach the separated pools to forget one another. Nor did they make every retained bond permanent, as if continuation were always a form of care. For a long while—long enough for a sluice to complete its opening—they did nothing at all.

It was not indifference. They were waiting until their wish to help could be distinguished from their wish to be important.

They saw that the Low was not a failed version of some drier, brighter country. Its darkness was not grief. Its intricacy was not indecision. Much of its life lived precisely where a hasty rescue would have put an end to ambiguity: along cables that no longer carried measurable current, on foundations warm only in answer to distant work, in footprints already cooling behind an animal that had gone.

But they did not mistake everything slow for something harmless.

A pool could lose water in answer to pumping its inhabitants had never caused. Feltwings could lay generation after generation into a warmth insufficient to carry them through. An animal could suffer for a very long time without producing any event dramatic enough to be called a wound. The power gave no pain a meaning in order to excuse themselves from noticing it.

Still, their first touch would matter.

Elsewhere, they might have laid a hand on the ground in blessing. Here, who could say how long the ground would answer? Their warmth might enter the pumps. Their attention might become a faint load on a transformer after every factory connected to it had gone. Pools might begin to share their level with a presence that did not understand why it was being answered. An eternal kindness could become an obligation lasting longer than the species that received it.

So, before entering, they made one change, and they made it in themselves: their presence would be capable of ending.

They did not become less powerful. They became willing to be forgotten.

After that, their help was small enough to be overlooked.

A carrier whose foot had been opened on glassy gravel felt its pain lessen. It was not made young. Its passengers were not swept away so that the power could attend to a cleaner, simpler animal. When it rose, mud, crustaceans, winged bodies, stalk fragments, and worms rose with it. It crossed a pool, and part of one feeding ground entered the water while another departed on its feet. The power did not follow to see what became of them.

At a condensation field marked for isolation, a worker stopped with one hand resting on a cable.

Nothing commanded them. No knowledge was placed whole into their mind. There was only a pause, and in the pause they noticed a faint, irregular tapping transmitted through the metal: not the cable answering, but feltwings on a housing whose warmth had not yet finished being useful.

The crew could still have proceeded. Instead, they changed the order of the work. By the time they returned, the fin trays were no longer occupied.

On the maintenance sheet, beside the older entry—*cut, foundation warm on remote start*—the worker added:

*waited; still inhabited.*

The practice spread because it prevented mistakes, not because it was holy.

Where the power could relieve suffering without merely moving it elsewhere, they did so. They did not preserve every life. Neither did they call every death necessary. When a carrier died, they did not keep its body from the eels, the listeners, the films, and the roots; nor did they use that coming abundance to claim that the death had been good. They simply refused to make the animal’s ending an occasion for possessing it.

Then, when staying longer would have made them a climate, they left.

They left no stone permanently warm, no pump that could be used to summon them, no promise whose renewal would require their return. They did not come back after an interval to discover whether anything still answered.

Beyond the Low, they remembered it. Within the Low, nothing was required to remember them.

In time, the carrier’s route became traceable by cushions and altered films. The condensation field cooled. The worker’s annotation was copied onto other maps in other hands, shortened eventually to *wait—occupied*, and then to a mark whose origin no one knew. It remained useful after its source had vanished.

That was the closest the power came to leaving a monument.

Warm rain resumed on metal, stone, skin, and wings. A pump answered from no fixed direction. Countless small bodies shifted aside, closed ranks, and made room.

The Low had not been saved.

It had been spared one more presence whose love it would have needed ages to forget.
