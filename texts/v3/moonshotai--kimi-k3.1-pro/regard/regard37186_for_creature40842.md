### What the Power Did

The power came without light. Even the gray triangle at the entrance did not widen. No warmth crossed the pool. A drop fell, flashed once, and struck; behind the wall came the double knock and the wet intake of air.

For a long while, that was all.

The power did not stand outside looking in. It knew the hollow as its tenants knew it, without mistaking any of their knowledge for the whole: the narrow chemical opportunity of a bacterial cell; grit closing over a worm; pressure arriving through a nymph’s legs; the shrew’s circuit measured in hunger and heat loss. It also knew what none of them could—the loosening roof, the weakness in the slab, and the bright floor toward which the chamber was tending.

Its first impulse was to keep the place.

That impulse passed when the power understood it. To hold the roof together would be to choose the blue-dark room over the sunlit moss and sedge that would follow. To keep the pool cold and deep would be to require the seep, the fish, the veils, and the springtails’ narrow country to continue serving an arrangement the power had happened to find beautiful. The chamber would remain, but its youth would become a sentence. Its tenants’ brevity would become part of the furnishings.

The ownerless works would have acquired an owner.

So the power did not make the hollow permanent. Nor did it improve the pool with abundance, or teach the fish to school, or spare the shrew the necessity of hunting. It did not choose the shrew because the shrew was easiest to love, or the dipper because it could be heard, or the bacteria because they were the most continuous tenants. It did not even choose the hollow itself, as if rhythm and enclosure were enough to make one life out of many.

Care did not require a representative.

But neither did the power make a virtue of leaving everything alone. It saw that *natural* did not mean painless, and *brief* did not mean negligible. The shrew’s cold was not less real because its fur shone silver in the water. A larva’s months were not rendered nothing by the swiftness of the dipper’s beak. The power refused the easy wisdom that admires a pattern and then calls every cost within it necessary.

Its mercy, when it came, was therefore small enough to enter the lives without rebuilding the room.

Pain, where pain existed, was not required to continue after it could no longer help a body avoid harm. Terror was not prolonged merely so that a death could finish undisturbed. Where an escape could be opened without taking food or shelter from another tenant, the power opened it: ice failed to seal one narrow route to dry peat; a thread of floodwater lasted long enough to be found; a body at the limit of cold reached shelter before cold became injury. These were not rewards. Nothing had earned them by being useful to the works.

The power neither saved every life nor declared every death necessary. It did not make hunger imaginary, or fear foolish, or loss an illusion. It simply refused to demand additional suffering as evidence that it had respected the whole.

And it remembered.

Not as an inventory. Not as a specimen of each kind, fixed at its most representative moment. It remembered the partial worlds: the bacterium’s boundary between too much iron and too little oxygen; the worm’s burial and resumption; the fish’s patch of darkness beneath the slab; the midge’s reflection trembling under the surface before its wings had dried. Remembering was not offered instead of help. It was simply that no life became too small to have been noticed individually.

When the slab finally fell, the power did not catch it in order to preserve the room.

Stone struck water. The pool’s stillness broke. Some animals were carried out through the flood; others remained beneath shifted rock or on wet rubble. The shrew altered its circuit. In time, light reached places where only the bacterial veils had lived, and algae followed, and then moss. The hollow did not become nothing. It became other places, none of which owed the power a resemblance to what had been lost.

The power grieved, if its way of knowing loss can be called grief. It did not make the plain keep a ruin to accommodate that grief.

Elsewhere, frost had already opened another joint. Seep water found air. At first there was only bare fracture, cold flow, and a chemical difference too slight to stain the stone. The power was there before the first visible ochre, not to begin what water and iron could begin without it, and not to decide in advance how long the new chamber should last.

No path led to either place. No name was fastened to them. The river kept its several shallow channels, and the works their several clocks.

The power’s gift was not permanence. It was that no life had to become permanent, or useful, or representative of the whole, in order to merit care.
