### The Terms of Waiting

The power did not enter by any of the four entrances. For a while, it did not enter at all.

It read the registers. It did not mind that the oldest began in the middle of a quarrel about a drain, or that one of the disputants had already passed beyond correction. It followed itineraries rather than maps. It learned that New Lock was old, that Wash Place was a bedroom, and that **seen; alive; one** could be a complete account of an event. It learned that a century in which a drain had done nothing might reasonably end with dried fruit.

Most importantly, it learned to distinguish what was unfinished from what was hurting.

It did not deepen the eastern street. It did not coax rooms out of the blank clay beyond patient doors, make every address equally rich in distance, or warm the junctions dry. It did not spread the black moss into a garden or make the translucent animals numerous enough to be reassuring. Such mercies would have shared a quiet arrogance: they would have made the Low Ward easier to love by making it less itself.

Nor did it make the residents patient. That would have been the subtler violation.

To the Ward, the power put one question, not in words or through the buried note, but into the dim allowance between one possible future and another:

*Do you want anything of me?*

Then it waited. It did not give the Ward a mouth so that an answer could arrive sooner. It did not interpret silence as permission. If the Ward was aware, awareness did not oblige it to become comprehensible on demand. If it was not aware, the question had cost it nothing.

With the residents, the power was less reticent. They could answer immediately, and did, mostly by disagreeing.

Some wanted nothing touched. Some wanted the first stream; some wanted ordinary walls and a dry basin. One woman wanted to know whether Clay Door would open while she was still alive enough to be angry about it. A child asked whether the power could make the wind stop above the rim as well. Someone in the common kitchen wanted to know whether it intended to keep standing in the doorway or help carry stores.

The power did not demand one collective request from them, as though fifty or sixty people were a single room and could be given a single name.

Nor did it extend its architectural restraint to human suffering. An infected hand was not testimony. A hungry winter was not an improving lesson in patience. When help was wanted, it treated, carried, fetched, mended, and provided. It did not preserve their poverty as evidence of sincerity, or their chapped hands as local colour. It asked no worship, and made neither belief nor continued residence the price of assistance.

Its one great promise concerned leaving.

No person would be held by an address. Anyone who wished to get out of a repeating passage, an isolated room, or the Low Ward itself, and could not, would be taken where they asked to go. This required no alteration to the Ward. A person could be removed from a difficulty without the difficulty being made to vanish.

There would be no flash and no correction of the surrounding geometry. Someone who had finally said, “Enough,” might simply find herself standing in the common kitchen with her folded sitting-pad still under one arm, or above the rim with the wind taking her breath. Behind her, the passage could continue repeating. The Ward could finish its own thought.

Departure, likewise, would be real. No one who chose the plain would discover after forty years that every road had been politely returning them to the same threshold. The wind would be wind. A road would become longer when walked. A grave would remain a grave. Children could count stairs as loudly as they liked.

The Ward could still become a valley, or dry into an ordinary knot of mineral and ceramic. But no human life would be required as mortar.

The instruction plates remained unchanged. **IF A PASSAGE REPEATS, WAIT** was still good counsel for anyone who wished not to interrupt an unfinished act of connection. It was no longer captivity.

Then, because not everyone wishes to leave, and freedom is not the same as company, the power sat down.

It waited with those who asked it to wait. It did not radiate serenity. It did not tell angry people that their anger was preventing a connection, or bored children that boredom was a form of wisdom. It listened to speeches composed against doors. It shared food. It became moderately competent at silent games and remained mysteriously bad at guessing which maintenance body a child had chosen for special observation. When asked to leave, it left. When asked to carry someone out, it carried them out. When asked to stay without saying anything encouraging, it stayed.

The residents gave it several names, not all of them polite. It answered to those it recognised. People, after all, were permitted abundance.

A few entries appeared in the registers:

**Passage repeated. Waited two days. Visitor waited also. No change to route.**

**Left with assistance. Passage unchanged. Food and pad recovered. Intend to return.**

And once, more briefly:

**Not alone.**

Surveyors from the plain complained that this last was not useful route information. The residents copied it anyway. Old observations were not erased merely because their usefulness had changed.

Much later, at a small feast honouring a drain that had behaved itself for a hundred years, someone read that entry aloud. An argument followed about whether it should ever have been admitted to the register. The argument became confused with an older one concerning sediment, and then with a current one concerning who had burned the onions.

The power, which had not come to settle every disagreement, passed the dried fruit.

Above the rim, the wind crossed the plain. Below it, a bead of water gathered where the world had not quite agreed with itself. The Ward remained unfinished.

Nothing had been completed. Nothing had been wasted.
