### What the Power Left Undone

The power came without brightening the light. It had learned, on other worlds, that even an arrival could become a demand for gratitude.

Its first impulse was to give the basin a river.

Water would discover a slope. A channel would deepen; banks would gather silt; roots would enter soil instead of merely lying across stone. Matbacks would wear paths between feeding grounds. Trees would write rings. A fallen trunk would nourish a particular patch of earth, and that patch would differ from its neighbors, and the difference would outlast the life that caused it. Things that happened would begin to happen *to* later things.

For an instant, this seemed like offering the basin its own life.

Then the power followed the gift far enough to see who would pay for it.

The seepage would leave some of the warm lenses. Floating mats would tear against new banks. Waters would clarify or cloud, deepen or vanish, and the exact depths to which the black fish and pale swimmers were fitted would become other depths. Ferns would close over mineral flats. Old shade would abolish ancient openness. The frogs’ divisions of sound would be broken by water moving louder than any throat.

There would be other lives afterward. Perhaps richer ones. There would be young born into vacancy, ancestors, landmarks, old growth. But none of that richness would repay an amber frog for the loss of the root on which its throat had pressed gently against another’s. The future was not a receipt for what had been spent to obtain it.

So the power did not give the basin a history.

It understood that to call the place unfinished was already to have chosen what finishing ought to mean. No inhabitant had asked to become an ancestor. None had asked that its pleasure, fear, or attachment be made worthy by producing a consequence.

Its second impulse was to leave everything untouched and call this respect.

That was wiser, but it was not yet benevolent.

For the basin’s completeness did not make every experience within it painless. A calf separated from its mother was not consoled by the equilibrium of populations. A wader’s fear did not become beautiful merely because the hunter also hungered. A death that fed a hundred mouths was still a death for the one who died. The power refused to let harmony do the work of justification.

Nor did it value only what suffered.

It did not regard play in the shallows as wasted because no skill descended from it. It did not consider affection futile because no lineage preserved it. When two pale swimmers hung together beneath the roots, the power did not ask what their nearness would lead to. It did not need the interval to become an institution before admitting that something had occurred within it.

It made no inhabitant special. It did not awaken a swimmer and appoint it archivist. It did not teach the matbacks to inherit paths, or give one frog a question no other frog could share. There was no need to make a single being lonely with a gift merely so the power could feel that a gift had been given.

What it did instead was so slight that no pool could reflect it.

It was careful with living pain. Fear could still carry a body out of danger. Pain could still draw back a foot, protect an injury, drive an animal toward shelter. Grief could still send a calf’s cry toward a mother searching for it. The power was wary of stealing a creature’s own chances and calling the theft comfort.

But whenever suffering ceased to open any possibility for the one who suffered—when it could no longer warn, guide, summon, protect, or mend, when it could do nothing but close around a being from inside—the power drew that surplus away.

Sometimes this happened at the end of a life. Sometimes it happened in a life that continued: enough sensation remained to keep an animal careful, but not so much that pain had replaced the whole world.

The power did not thereby forbid a death.

Not the hunger. Not the fear. Not the death. Only the agony that could do nothing but be endured.

The hunter still fed. Fish still gathered at the disturbance. The body still became tissue in other bodies, sweetness in fungal threads, a brief congregation of shells and mouths. Nothing starved to preserve the gesture. Nothing survived because of it. No scavenger found less, and no population swelled.

No animal learned that anything had changed. No behavior became safer or more reckless. No tradition of gentleness arose, because there were no traditions. The mercy could not be inherited. It had to be noticed and chosen again, for each being, each time.

The power made no law that would allow its attention to retire.

Occasionally, where some smaller help could be given without merely moving the harm into another life, it helped. A limb caught beneath a root was freed. A cry already being answered was allowed to reach the ears seeking it. These were not reforms. They founded nothing. The next frond recovered; the next root mat closed; the next hunger arrived on time.

Perhaps the power remembered everything.

If so, it did not call the remembering a gift. No creature had asked to become material for an eternal witness’s consolation. It kept no chronicle in which later lives justified earlier ones, no sum in which enough pleasure could be placed against enough pain until the account appeared clean. It remembered, if it remembered, without addition: *this calf was afraid; this wader learned caution; this young animal played; these two swimmers were near one another. It led nowhere. It did not need to.*

The memory was for the power. The mercy was for the creature while the creature was still there.

Nothing in the basin deepened into a landmark. No call acquired an ornamental descendant. The hunter’s shoulder still passed pewter-dark through the reeds. Eggs still gleamed in leaf cups, and the pool levels remained constant. The land received no sediment from what had been done.

From the standpoint of the basin, nothing had happened.

From within certain closed rooms of suffering, a door had opened. It led nowhere the power named. Often it led only out of pain.

The power left no monument and required no gratitude. It did not need the basin to remember that mercy had occurred. It did not mistake the basin’s indifference for cruelty, or its own restraint for purity.

Nothing was still. Nothing advanced.

The power asked none of it to add up. It did not call what it had done enough.

It only refused to call it nothing.
