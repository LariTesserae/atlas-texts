### A Mercy Without Instruction

The power’s first act was to arrive without current.

A live feeder would have been a shout. Root tips would have turned toward it; buds would have split; districts would have accelerated under the impression that a revision had arrived. Even a greeting, if expressed in the only languages the Works possessed, would have become an instruction to grow, clear, reconnect, or reclaim.

So the power came to the western parapet without heat, weight, charge, or light, and for a long time did nothing that could be distinguished from absence.

Roads grew and were digested. A seed field occupied a valley of structural columns and was itself occupied by mat. Furnace doors opened along the rim. Somewhere below, an engine altered its output, and a wave of darkening growth fluid crossed districts whose names, had they possessed names, would already have become inaccurate.

The power attended to all of it.

It had thought, perhaps, to heal the darkness. But darkness was not an injury here. It did not bring water, which would have been weather only in the sense of a catastrophe. It did not return animals: an empty niche was not an obligation, and a world without bones was not necessarily a world awaiting bones. It did not open the sky. The plants had no use for the gift, and the power would not make them endure a mercy merely because the mercy was beautiful elsewhere.

Nor did it grant the Works rest.

Stillness was what followed a failed feeder. To stop the gantries, hold the roads, and preserve every thicket would not have given the residents peace. It would have deprived them of their bodies one revision too late.

It considered memory. There was much to remember: halls that had stood for an hour, clearances repeated across ages, root habits older than the visible land. But to make every provisional form recollectable might only have invented loss. A column did not mourn its copse. A mat did not miss having been a tower. The power would not create grief so that it could then console it.

It considered waking the being.

But there was no single sleeper to wake. There were continuities crossing and separating, executing routines, forming preferences in resin and tension, surviving by compatibility. The power could have drawn them together and made a witness answerable for the whole basin. That witness might have been marvelous. It might also have been the first creature in the Works capable of loneliness.

The power would not do this merely to make the place easier to love.

It found much that could fail. It found nothing that asked to be rescued. This did not prove that nothing suffered, or that nothing ever could. It proved only that suffering was not the secret shape of the basin, waiting for a compassionate visitor to recognize it.

And anything placed inside the Works would have ceased to be a gift. A stone would become support. A vessel would become stock. A living seed would become a lineage or a component. A command to be free would be executed as another command. There was no sheltered interior where the power could set down its benevolence and trust it to remain benevolence.

So, at last, it acted outside.

It made no ward around any grove, machine, garden, or engine. It did not fix the geography of occurrence. It did not make the Calyx Works eternal; eternity might have been for its own consolation. It kept no duplicate basin elsewhere, no collection of favored forms, no final map against which all later changes could be judged as decline.

Instead, it placed a limit on interference, beginning with its own.

No outside hand would be permitted to seize the whole Works and make them answerable to a final purpose—not for industry, not for restoration, not even for preservation. If some later visitor came with cutters and called it harvesting, the power would refuse them. If another came with roots and called it improvement, it would refuse them too. But it would not prevent the Works from changing, even into something no longer recognizable as the Works. It would not mistake continuity for the perpetual repetition of what had already been seen.

Then it left one thing behind, though not in the basin.

An attention remained at the rim.

It was not a sentry and not quite a promise. It made no demand for speech and appointed no representative. It waited only for the possibility that a habit might someday become a preference, a preference a wish, and a wish something addressed beyond itself. The address need not resemble language. It might come as an impossible routing, a compatibility repeatedly refused, a capsule made to fit nothing and carried with inexplicable care from garden to garden. The power would not decide in advance what a request had to look like. If that time came, it would learn again.

Until then, silence would be treated neither as consent nor as a cry.

Having done this, the power departed, or ceased to be distinguishable from the distance surrounding the rim.

In the Press Gardens, a flower opened onto a bearing. A carrier waited beside a route already closing beneath its wheels. Fine filaments investigated a warm obstruction, tested its seams, and began the patient work of deciding what it was for. Nothing paused to commemorate the visitation. Nothing had been given a reason to.

The power remembered without preserving. It did not remember every path—that would have been a false geography—or every body, which would have made a cemetery of what had never been one. It remembered only that the basin was not a problem it had solved, nor a lesson it had been sent to teach.

Its mercy left no organ, no shrine, no command. It was simply this: the Works did not have to become more comprehensible in order to be cherished, or more suffering in order to deserve help, or more like a person in order to be approached with care.

Behind the power, a path closed with scarcely a sound.

Ahead, the hot darkness continued its dense, meticulous labor.

Nothing thanked it.

Nothing needed to.
