### What the Power Did

The power came without radiance. There was already enough light.

Its first impulse was to save something.

In the court below, a blister had opened beneath the water. Pale tissue rose through it, folding upward into an arch. Across the court, a column leaned as its water was drawn away. The collapsed lattice near the pool had begun to sink, strand by strand, into the living floor.

The power could have held the arch together. It could have made the column upright and the pool permanently deep. It could have given every rib the strength to bear its load, every reservoir an unbreakable lining, every growing thing exactly the water it required.

Instead, it waited.

Not because waiting was always wise, and not because destruction became beautiful when sufficiently large. It waited because the impulse to heal was not yet knowledge of a wound.

The arch cast its shadow. Its crown split. Water fell through it in a single sheet, and the court disappeared into spray. When the air cleared, there were two shallow terraces where the flat had been. Pieces of the lattice lay in the former pool, already pale with new growth. The column stood farther away than before, though it had not moved.

Nothing had fled. Nothing had cried out.

Still, the power did not conclude that all was well. Silence could mean peace, but it could also mean that nothing possessed the means to call.

So it searched.

It looked for the inhabitant among the towers and found only organs, remnants, and possibilities. It looked beneath the green skin of the courts and found continuities that made the word *individual* uncertain. It followed living threads into crushed darkness. It listened in the sealed cisterns, where tannin-dark tissue contracted around the last of the water. It attended to the smallest cups in the fractures, each of which might have been a creature, a wound closing, or a temporary thickening of something immeasurably larger.

It searched for pain rather than rupture; for a frustrated will rather than a discontinued shape; for some awareness imprisoned within the growth and begging to be let out.

Had it found such a presence, it would have answered. Reverence for a landscape would have been no excuse for abandoning someone within it. Nor would the power have offered a sermon on the necessity of change to a being that longed for rest. If rest had been asked for, rest would have been considered. If an end to suffering had required intervention, the power would have intervened.

But it found no captive mind behind the hydraulic voices. No lament persisted beneath the knocking floors. The tearing of tissue was damage, but not every damage was an affliction imposed upon the whole. A shape ended; substance returned; pressure took another path. The being was not simply enduring the revisions.

In some manner that had no need of a single center, the being was the revisions.

This did not make every collapse good. It made the usual kindnesses dangerous.

To finish the highland would have been to choose one of its momentary forms and condemn all the others to remain possibilities. To make it safe would have been to prevent pressure from becoming architecture, and architecture from becoming substance again. To fill its courts with birds, insects, grazing animals, and voices would have comforted a visitor, not necessarily the inhabitant. Even to preserve the arch because its single shadow had been lovely would have been to mistake the power’s attachment for the highland’s need.

And to give it a voice merely so that it could be understood before being left alone would have been another way of asking it to become more like its visitor.

So the power did none of these things.

It remained through the whole event. It did not look away when the arch broke, nor did it preserve the shadow afterward. It remembered the court before and after without making either arrangement into the correct one. It let something transient be complete in its presence, without demanding that completeness become permanence.

That was the first thing it did.

The second, if it was needed, would happen beyond the highland.

The power would build no fence around the rim and alter no current of cloud. Wind-dust would still escape. Fragments would still root or fail. The region would remain free to exceed every map made of it.

But where other wills might one day approach with claims, instruments, or benevolent plans, the power would leave a refusal: the highland was not to be made available by calling it empty, or worthless by calling it unfinished, or consenting by calling it silent. No one was to take its tissue merely because no hand withdrew it, or impose a garden upon it and name the imposition rescue.

Any future help would have to begin as the power’s had begun: with the patience to discover what help would cost, and to whom.

Within the highland itself, the power left no mark.

The new terraces darkened. Condensation gathered beneath their lips. In a wet fracture, a piece of reservoir lining curled into a cup no larger than a scar. Farther off, water began to climb a fluted column, bead joining bead until the whole wall seemed to be thinking in reverse rain.

The highland never knew it had been visited. The power required no acknowledgment. It had not come to be worshipped, to complete a work, or to prove that nothing should ever be done.

It had come to help, if help was needed. It had stayed long enough to learn that restraint was not the same as indifference.

Far away, a green horizon lifted into ordinary daylight.

The power watched it rise.

It did not hold it up.
