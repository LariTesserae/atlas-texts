### The Length of Three Footfalls

The power’s first care was to cast no shadow.

At noon, even a slight darkening would have been an intervention. Pools would have lost their reflected neighbors; animals would have waited for passages that no longer opened. So the power looked without standing between the cavity and the sun.

For many heat pulses, it did nothing that could be distinguished from attention. It learned the difference between the silence of emptiness and the silence of crowding. It saw that a limpet was not merely a limpet, but water kept through danger, a margin of pasture, a door closing around eggs. It watched thread-mouths strike out of brightness and palm-backs discard one piece of glass after another. It ceased to see the animals as examples of their kinds. Each hesitation happened to somebody.

It considered the gifts that powers commonly mistake for kindness.

It could have given the cavity an unbroken noon. Then the useful adjacencies would have endured—but so would the thread-mouths’ hunting grounds, and the palm-backs’ knowledge of light would have become unnecessary. It could have quieted the heat. Then fewer animals would have died on the ribs, but the seep’s chemistry, the clamping and lifting of shells, the pulses of vapor and return would all have changed. It could have left one perfect lever beside a shell colony, balanced so well that no palm-back would need to test another. Tools might then have been kept. Keeping might have become guarding. The power would have chosen a history for them because it had admired one gesture.

It refused these gifts.

It did not therefore conclude that the cavity was good exactly as it was. A veil-flea overtaken by heat was not repaid by the beauty of the condensation that followed. Hunger was not ennobled because a hungry animal might later learn a method. The power would not use the continuing whole to excuse itself from the suffering of the parts.

Still, for a while, attention was its only act.

Then came a pulse in which the lowest pools vanished almost at once. Limpets closed. Veil-fleas poured upward and broke into scattered discs where their routes failed. The young palm-back that had been testing needles abandoned them and climbed, but an older animal crowded it from the first cool ledge. It retreated onto a rib already warming beneath its feet.

Above it, a hanging drop held the image of a terrace where water remained. The terrace was not otherwise reachable in time. The palm-back extended one foot toward the image.

A cloud crossed the sun.

The white point in the drop softened. The terrace ceased to be beside the rib and became only a picture of somewhere else. The animal withdrew its foot, then extended it again. There was no petition in the movement, and no understanding of death. There was only heat, and a body trying the last route it knew.

The power could have told itself that this was one of the deaths by which the cavity remained itself. That was true, and not enough.

It could have told itself that saving this animal would oblige it to save the next, and the next, until mercy had become management: every route held open according to its judgment, every survivor its selection, every appetite something it had undertaken to answer. That might also have been true. But the animal on the rib was not an argument.

The power held the image clear.

Only that. The cloud still crossed. The bead at the outer lip still dimmed. Elsewhere, routes closed and animals waited or died. In one drop, for the length of three footfalls, a cool terrace remained adjacent to a hot rib.

The palm-back crossed.

Then the passage vanished. Condensation began in its own time. No shell opened for the rescued animal; no needle came conveniently to hand. The power did not make it quick, or favored, or safe.

Afterward, it was tempted to call its restraint wise and its intervention pure. It did neither. No act in that crowded place was without consequence, and no refusal to act was innocent merely because it left no mark. The power had not discovered a rule. It had found one mercy it could perform without first remaking the animal’s world.

It made no promise always to intervene. It made no promise never to intervene again. Either promise would have been easier than continuing to pay attention.

The young palm-back lived through that pulse and through others. It did not become remarkable. It lost tools. It stole scraps and was driven off. Once it succeeded in loosening a limpet only to have the exposed meal taken by a quicker animal. Gradually its bracing improved. It learned to reject length before sharpness had dazzled it. Perhaps a smaller palm-back once watched the position of its working digits; perhaps nothing passed to anyone.

In time it died, as it would have died even if the power had remained beside it turning aside every visible danger. Its body sheltered something briefly, then broke into pieces too small to shelter anything. Nothing in its later life proved that saving it had been right. The power had not purchased a lineage with three footfalls. It had given one animal more of its own uncertain life, and that life did not have to justify the gift by becoming important.

At the outer lip, the bead gathered again and took its white point of sun. Below, the rescued animal—older now, though not much larger—chose a short needle and failed to open a shell. It put the needle down. The power did not pick it up.

Nothing had been built. Nothing had been kept. No sign remained in the cavity that anything had been given.

When the next grain ticked against a shell, the power heard it. It did not require the sound to become the beginning of anything.
