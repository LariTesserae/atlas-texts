### One More Keeper

The power would begin by doing nothing.

Not because everything it found was good. Age does not make pain innocent, and usefulness to an ecology is not the same as kindness. An animal taken by a shear would not be comforted by the elegance of the food web. The power would not love the Upper Weave so much as scenery that it became indifferent to the beings for whom the scenery was happening.

It would wait because it did not yet know how to listen.

At first it would listen to the air and hear almost nothing: water, the small tick of growth, rain arriving drop by drop. Only when it laid its attention flat against a causeway would the country become crowded. Browsers speaking through their feet. Beetles drumming in old bast. Roots entering fray. Hunters plucking false signals into cords. The minute, continuous labor of a fabric being used, and changed by use.

Then it would understand why it must not simply mend every slipping join.

It could make distance trustworthy. It could give every causeway a reachable far end and every gutter a locatable source. It could tighten the whole green country into something a traveler might safely describe.

Moth passages would close. Herds would lose calving hollows. Marshes that shared life would become strangers. Routes that keepers had deliberately yielded to other uses would be restored to keepers. The Weave would become easier to comprehend and, in a thousand particular places, harder to inhabit.

Nor could the power restore the Weave to its first condition. There was no original earth beneath it, waiting to be uncovered. There was only previous work. The power would not invent an ancient intention and then punish the living for having outlasted it.

But after refusing the great repairs, it would face another temptation: to leave, and call that respect.

Doing nothing can be wise. It can also be a way of keeping one’s benevolence unstained by consequence. The power would not love its own restraint enough to mistake absence for care.

So it would offer help at a scale that could be refused.

When it approached an old keeper beside a split ridge, it did so without glory and without making belief the price of assistance. The keeper did not believe in it. She did, however, need another pair of hands.

“Hold that,” she said.

The power held the cord.

“No. Beneath the fray. You’re crushing the beetles.”

It changed its grip.

The work took most of the afternoon. The keeper lashed beside the old joint rather than through it, leaving its resin mites, fungal threads, galleries, and inhabitants to continue their separate negotiations with decay. Twice she undid sound work because she disliked where it would lead new growth. Once she contradicted an explanation she had given earlier. The power did not correct her. It could perceive many consequences, but perception did not tell it which consequences she was willing to live with.

When the binding was finished, it asked whether the repair was good.

“Ask the gutter,” she said. “After rain.”

So it stayed.

The rain came sparsely, then heavily. The joint took the load. Water entered the intended channel, found an obstruction neither of them had noticed, and began feeding a seam below it. The keeper cursed, cleared the obstruction, and discovered that the seam had been thirsty. She then became moderately pleased with an accident for which she refused to take credit.

Nothing remarkable happened afterward.

The power returned the next day, and the day after that. It learned that a repair was not an event but an argument continued by water, growth, animals, and other people. It learned to wait without confusing waiting with idleness. It learned that holding a cord correctly was not a humble substitute for using its power, but one of the things its power was for.

This would not become a doctrine of small acts. Small acts can be arrogant too. Sometimes the needed help would be large: a household carried out of a failing adjacency, a lost child returned while there was still a household looking for her, pain relieved when relief was wanted. The power would not cite the balance of the whole as an excuse for passing suffering by.

But it would not turn help into ownership. It would not make itself a dependable miracle written into every map, until keepers walked unsound routes because they expected to be caught. It would not heal bodies merely because it could not bear their changing, or preserve every pattern because someone beloved had once taught it. It would not make itself the hidden, load-bearing member of every life.

Instead, it would accept an obligation.

One short route. A gutter whose source remained uncertain. Several bindings that needed watching after prolonged rain. A frayed joint that must not be renewed too eagerly. A path belonging partly to keepers, partly to browsers, and increasingly, though no one had yet admitted it, to the moths.

The power would walk it.

Sometimes it would carry a lost traveler. Sometimes it would sit with a dying keeper who wanted company. Sometimes it would be asked for an answer and would give one. Sometimes it would be asked to decide, and would refuse because the decision belonged to those who would have to maintain its consequences.

Other keepers would argue with it. Occasionally they would avoid it. More than occasionally, they would be right. It would not allow benevolence to exempt it from correction.

Most days, it would do nothing visible. It would press a palm to a joint, smell water leaving a gutter, clear one small obstruction, and watch a living repair through weather. It would discover that even attention was not innocent: what it watched most closely, it was most tempted to govern. It would have to learn that lesson more than once.

A drop would strike a pool. The thread-eels would shiver. A knot would contract with a small, dry tick, and somewhere a browser would lift its head.

The power would feel the change travel through the work.

It had not promised the Upper Weave forever. It had promised fewer things than that: to return after rain, to learn what its hands had done, and to remain answerable for a part.
