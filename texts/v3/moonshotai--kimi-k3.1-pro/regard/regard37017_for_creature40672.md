### The Second Visit

The power did not announce itself with light. Light was already excessive, and on certain days it made the fins less separate.

Nor did it still the works. Their slowness was not suffering. It listened to the pressure behind an inspection window, the dry contraction of a panel, the nearly imperceptible activity of a collection cup, and understood that it had not been summoned to rescue the Manifold from time.

It could have repaired everything according to its own idea of mercy. It could have given every component one unambiguous location. It could have provided every keeper with a private memory, continuous from fabrication to failure, and made clean galleries as dependable as old ones. It could have laid soil in the Pan, softened the sharp edges, and covered the white ravines with green.

It did none of these things as a general remedy.

This was not because uncertainty was sacred, or because damage was good. Where there was pain, the power was willing to relieve it. Where a keeper wanted a failed joint repaired, the joint could be repaired without any lecture on the value of scars. Where a shifting hatch endangered someone, the power could hold it still. It did not make a doctrine of other people’s difficulties.

But the same condition that made a new gallery treacherous also allowed a snail’s trail to become more useful than a road. The named keepers had built their lives within that condition. The matching keepers had made another life, and had not asked to be rescued from it. To impose one universal remedy would have been to repeat the Manifold’s oldest error: to treat a single pattern as sufficient for everything.

So the power went to see one keeper.

At that time the keeper was known only as the one tending the grass tray. Its casings were still almost unmarked, except for a crescent of zinc dust at one knee. It was standing beside a collection cup although another cup, nearer and fuller, stood within easy reach. It had begun to prefer this one. The preference was only a few days old.

Elsewhere, other bodies bearing its manufacturing mark were performing inspections in the bright galleries. The power did not look away from the keeper beside the cup.

“What may I do for you?” it asked.

The keeper considered the question. Above it, a drop had been swelling for several hours on the cold belly of a duct.

“Wait,” it said. “It is nearly ready.”

The power waited.

A pump changed phase somewhere below them. The drop trembled, elongated, and fell. It passed through the gratings, struck water in several districts, and produced a single sound.

The keeper lifted the cup carefully.

Afterward, the power followed it to the grass tray. It did not shorten the route. They went from a stained flange to a recess where black birds had nested, then around a clean chamber whose locations had begun to disagree. At one threshold the keeper paused and let the power see the polish left by generations of snails.

The tray, when they reached it, contained three blades of transparent-tipped grass. The keeper poured in the water. It darkened one corner and did not produce any visible response in the plants.

The power could have made them flourish. It understood that flourishing had not been the whole of the errand.

Before leaving, it said, “I will come again.”

The keeper considered this as seriously as it had considered the offer of help.

“Come by the north stair,” it said. “The clean gallery is worse now.”

The power returned by the north stair.

For it, no journey was necessary. It could have been present at the tray continuously, and in every duplicate of the tray, and beside every body sharing the keeper’s mark. Instead it consented to arrive. Between visits it did not secretly hold the keeper in place or preserve each day from alteration. It allowed its return to be an event.

It allowed itself to be awaited.

On its visits it carried water when asked. It held an inspection cover while a damaged hinge was replaced. Once it sat through an entire pump rest while the keeper explained why it disliked a particular cold gallery, although the explanation consisted mostly of silence. When a new panel had to be installed, the power did not insist that the old stain be preserved at the cost of function. When the keeper wished to keep a chipped casing, the power did not insist on repairing that either.

Its help did not always take the form of preservation. It did not always take the form of change.

Nor were its visits a reward for becoming individual. When matching keepers came to the chamber, the power received them without counting them. It did not offer separate cups merely to see whether separate cups would be chosen. It did not make its affection another instrument for persuading the unmarked to become countable. If one of them later returned alone with an unfamiliar preference, that would be met in its own time.

The keeper beside the collection cup continued to have days that no other body quite shared. It watered the grass. It neglected the grass once while watching pipe worms emerge during a resting phase, and was ashamed until an older keeper showed it the salts left by an earlier failure. It developed a dislike for overfull cups. It repaired the zinc-dusted knee and left the repair visible.

In time it received a name.

The power did not bestow the name. It was simply among those who had learned where to look when the keeper was absent, and among those for whom its return was not merely the resumption of a pattern.

The power, too, had acquired a limited local history. It was not always at the tray. It could be expected there. Sometimes the keeper set out a second cup without explaining why. Birds used the recess above the old hatch. The grass died and was replaced by a rosette rooted in insulation felt. The collection cup acquired a mineral rim. None of these things announced a visitation, and travelers who used the chamber as a landmark knew nothing of any power. They knew the dark corner, the old salts, and the north stair that remained dependable after rain.

The ledger contained no record of the power’s first arrival. The keeper had thought the drop was the important event. The entry read:

*Water after the long rest. Grass tray holding.*

The second visit was recorded:

*Guest came by the north stair.*

Later entries concerned moths, birds, repairs, an unsuccessful planting, and the changing habits of the keeper who had once been identified only by an errand. Among them, at irregular intervals, appeared two words:

*Guest returned.*

No covenant followed. No new law passed through the fins. The Manifold remained young, sharp, crowded, and uncertain. The power had not made it impossible for anyone to be lost.

But for a power that could have been everywhere, it had chosen this much: to be somewhere in particular, and then to be there again.
