### What the Power Did

The power came without radiance. It did not brighten the dusk or silence the pump-note so that its own presence could be more plainly known. It stood first on the outer slope, looking into a dry scar, and did not mistake that meagerness for the truth. Then it crossed the rim, and did not mistake the green abundance within for a refutation.

For a while, it did nothing but learn how not to help.

It could have let the fern finish opening. It could have allowed one transparent spawn-cloud to quicken toward hatching, set the beetle’s drop falling, or taught the water at the notch to become a waterfall. It could have given the notched-tail salamander one body in one place, and the slate wader a cry that always followed the opening of its beak. It could have carried a moth across the black water to another fastness.

Each act would have looked like mercy. Each would also have made the cirque easier for the power to love: more like a life with a story, more like a place in which compassion could see its own good work.

So it waited.

It listened for pain, not merely repetition; for refusal, not merely stillness; for a wish, not merely an interruption of the pulse. It did not ask the mountain to speak with one voice. It did not make the largest bird a prophet. Where there was no answer, it did not write contentment, and where there was no struggle, it did not write consent. But neither did it invent captivity so that it could perform a rescue.

At last it understood that none of the gifts it had imagined was required of it. Not time. Not death. Not an ending. Not even choice, offered in the secret hope that someone would choose differently.

The power did not call the cirque perfect. It did not call it holy. Those, too, would have been ways of making use of it.

Then it did the one thing it had come, without knowing, to do.

It remembered.

Not the engine alone. It remembered the blunt-headed salamander and the one with the crooked hind toe, without requiring either to remain within a single sequence of events. It remembered the moth’s two blossoms as a whole world, not a small one. It remembered the beetle and the weight of its unbroken drop, the grub’s branching shadow, the bird whose reflections contained other patiences. It remembered the empty pool exactly as empty.

It kept the reflections that disagreed and did not reconcile them in order to make a single picture.

Nothing was carried out of the cirque: no charge, no water, no seed, no formula. The remembrance remained in the power, where it did not become a past the inhabitants were obliged to possess.

Then it withdrew beyond the rim. It left no guardian and no prohibition. It kept only the way back, and the ability to hear if some sound should ever become a request—not the cliff’s exhalation, not the cry arriving before the bird, but a life asking for something the power had no right to guess.

It did not wait eagerly for that request.

It did not need to be needed.

Within, the pools dimpled. Blue charge passed along the quartz. Gills opened like one copper event in many bodies. The fern stayed half-uncurled, neither thwarted nor promised. The moth turned, as it had always turned, before the bare stone became relevant to it.

Without, the dry scar kept its silence.

Neither view corrected the other.

The mountain ran. The power did not need to be its meaning.
