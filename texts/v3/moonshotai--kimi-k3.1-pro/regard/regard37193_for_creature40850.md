### What the Power Did

The power came without splendor and stopped at the surveyed line.

From there, the Low Works appeared almost modest: wet roofs, brown pylons, a little steam bending from a vent. The noise reached across the dry plain without seeming to approach. The power stood at the boundary and listened.

It would have been easy to make the plain bloom. Easy to send a clean river through the basin, to loosen the balanced returns, to let scale vanish beneath the small rasping mouths. It could have brought wear, repair, offspring, carcasses. It could have given the engines something to finish and the animals something to need.

It could have made silence.

It did none of these. First, it went in.

Where it set its feet, the slate-colored bodies opened slowly and closed behind it. There was no welcome in this and no accusation. The power accepted both accuracies. It laid two fingers against a rail, firmly enough to make a small vibration, and smooth heads lifted along the metal. For a moment it entered the geography by which the animals knew their world: pressure before sound, live iron, cold return, warmth gathered around an engine frame.

It did not mistake this for conversation. Nor did it mistake the absence of conversation for an absence of life.

The power did not assume that the constancy of the Works was peace. Neither did it assume that constancy was torment. It remained until its first pity had been examined and found wanting. Beneath that pity, carefully hidden, was a wish to be needed. It set that aside also.

Had it found suffering, it would have acted. It had not come to worship equilibrium.

But the engines were not devouring the animals. The animals were not devouring one another. The crowding concealed no struggle that became clearer when watched longer. Their feeding did not end in hunger, and their labor did not end in exhaustion. The great hooting contained no imprisoned voice crying to be let out of the chord. There was much the power could not explain, but nothing became more merciful merely by being called a prison.

The hardest temptation was not to destroy the Low Works. It was to improve it.

The power could have given the animals consequence. A meal that diminished. A preference that hardened into habit. A choice whose effects outlived the chooser. Then there might have been attachment, inheritance, loss; something first, something last; a history in which each small life could be found and its importance demonstrated.

It would have been a splendid gift. It would also have made their lives easier for the power to understand.

So the power refused it.

It did not call their condition perfect. Perfection would only have been another way of stopping attention. It simply saw that nothing here had asked to be made consequential, and that the absence of a request was not, by itself, a deficiency.

Near a cold railing, it paused.

A thumb-length animal had attached itself there, although a warm pipe trembled within easy reach. The animal’s pleats opened. Condensation gathered along its back. After a while it loosened its mouth, crossed the width of two flagstones, and fastened to another animal’s flank. Nothing followed. No other animal imitated it. The railing remained available.

The power stayed beside the railing until the animal’s brief preference had ceased to seem like evidence.

Then it sat on the old dark curb.

It did not decide whether it was visiting one being with many mouths or many beings sharing a single mineral body. It listened to the great animals breathing in the courts and to the minute porcelain clicks in the basins. It listened until the general chord no longer prevented it from hearing an attachment delayed, a gap left open for three breaths, one body inflating slightly before the bodies around it.

It gave these things no names. It did not turn them into destiny, protest, prayer, or the first signs of a coming change. It allowed them to be brief and still be real.

This was the only thing it did that resembled a blessing: it attended without demanding that attention be justified by an outcome.

When it rose, no new water ran in the channels. No seed had been hidden in a paving joint. It had not loosened a screw or strengthened a wall. It left no guardian over the boundary and no promise hanging above the roofs. Even the place where it had sat remained only as warm as the stone already was.

It went away because there was nothing to finish, not because there was nothing to love.

The Low Works was unchanged. The power was not entirely unchanged. It carried no trophy of understanding, but it had become slower to call a life wasted because that life left nothing behind; slower to confuse silence with waiting, repetition with suffering, and stillness with permission to intervene.

Behind it, beneath the measured hammer-stroke and the dry rushing of feet, an animal somewhere drew a single breath out of phase with all the others.

The breath changed nothing.

The power did not ask it to.
