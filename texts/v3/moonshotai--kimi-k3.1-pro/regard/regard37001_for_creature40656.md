### The Power That Did Not Bring Rain

The power did not arrive as light. Light would have startled the veilwings, exposed the cold-wick’s small deception, and made the darkness seem like a deficiency it had come to correct.

Nor did it arrive as silence. In the Low Choir, silence was not peace. It was the shape a hushback made in other animals’ knowledge.

It added no note. Even a gentle note, returning from the wrong curve of stone, might cause a colony to answer a neighbor that did not exist. So at first the power was only attention, and for many wet and dry intervals attention was all it allowed itself to be.

It learned the circuit before touching it. It followed sulfur into film, film into the mineral mouths of raspers, raspers into pale mud and winged bodies and the patient anatomy of hunters. It felt a basinback’s call travel through shell fill and bare stone. It heard pipe colonies answer echoes of notes already ended. When condensation loosened and rain began without weather, the power was almost persuaded that it had found a single being: limestone for bone, water for blood, sound for the motion of an immense and thoughtless heart.

Then it listened more closely.

It heard the soft rasper beneath its old shell. It found coils that had survived dryness only to die unable to unknot. It perceived eggs waiting through one rain and another for the particular shaking that would never come, larvae entering the filters of their own parents, a colony waking too briefly in a seep that failed before its young could disperse.

The circuit was still beautiful. The power did not protect itself from that knowledge by deciding otherwise. But neither did it allow beauty to settle the question of suffering.

Rain was the first mercy it considered, and the first it put aside.

It could have made every roof release its moisture. It could have kept the seeps open and the films continually slick. At first there would have been abundance: shell beds ticking, pool rims overflowing, raspers opening across the flats, the winged air becoming almost solid. The power could have called that rescue.

But a permanent chorus-rain would not have been a permanent version of the first minutes after waking. It would have been another country. Eggs with different requirements would open together; films would spread beyond their old margins and be grazed beyond them; some animals would flourish until their flourishing became another animal’s catastrophe. The power could have managed those consequences too, adjusting one appetite and then another, until every population remained within the limits of its design. Eventually there might still have been a crowded and flourishing province. There would no longer have been a Low Choir, except as the name of something tended.

It would not make help in the shape of its favorite event.

Nor would it make peace by removing the hushbacks. It would not decide that predators were the portion of life that had failed. The hush was terrible to cross, but terror did not make the soft-bodied animal at its center an error. A hushback also waited through dryness. It also hungered, rested, aged, and became indistinguishable from clay. Its silence was not a moral argument.

This did not lead the power to the comfortable conclusion that everything was necessary.

Much was merely old. Much was merely the way matters had happened to close. Interdependence was not consent. The circuit as a whole felt no pain and could forgive nothing on behalf of its members. No bass note held in a sink, no splendid waking after rain, could make an individual death good by including it.

So the power began, cautiously, to help.

Where help took nothing from another life, it helped freely. It loosened the clay around a revived coil whose own body had become its prison, using no more water than freedom required. It cleared grit from the breathing outlet of a pipe animal whose soft tissue was dying behind the obstruction. It eased an inward mineral ridge that had turned an old injury on a basinback’s back into a wound that reopened whenever the animal passed beneath stone.

These were not corrections to the Low Choir. They did not improve its voice or increase its abundance. They were care, and care did not need to justify itself by becoming ecology.

The power healed what could be healed without making one being’s survival a hidden injury to another. It lessened pain where lessening would not deprive a body of its warnings. And when pain could no longer guide any action—when there was no escape left to urge, no injury left to favor, no future choice that agony could serve—it did not demand that agony continue merely so it could say it had changed nothing.

The hushback still fed. The flake still closed. The soft body after molting still entered the greater body. Shell and tissue returned to mud, and mud entered other mouths. The power did not call this good. It did not require itself to call it evil before acknowledging everything involved.

Nothing was spared for the comfort of an observer. Nothing was prolonged for that reason either.

Then the power confronted the greatest intervention available to it: it could have preserved the Low Choir forever.

It could have held the seeps at their richest chemistry and the shell flats at their fullest crowding. It could have replaced each failing film, restored each eroded resonance, ensured that no last dry interval ever became last. Its love could have become so vigilant that no basin was permitted to become unrecognizable.

It refused.

It would not require endless generations to wake, hunger, suffer, and survive so that the object of its attention would remain available. If the province changed beyond recovery in some distant age, that would be a loss. The power did not make loss its enemy at any price. Grief, it understood, could become a form of possession.

So it left no everlasting abundance, no shrine, no organ tuned to its name. It taught no animal that it had been helped. It did not ask the raspers to become grateful or the hushbacks to become gentle. It did not preserve a perfect specimen of the Choir outside time. What it had done at the scale of particular lives was enough; what it had declined to do at the scale of the whole was part of the same mercy.

In time, several distant choruses rose together. Condensation gathered, trembled, and fell. A basin that had seemed encrusted and vacant became furry with movement. Raspers unsealed beneath the shells of ancestors. Veilwings lifted until the lower air roared. Somewhere a pool-crown snapped its valves; somewhere a hush spread and closed again; somewhere a basinback stood with its six feet wide, reading stone through the return of its own voice.

Nothing announced that a wise power had been there.

It had come because the Low Choir seemed, from far off, like a single immense voice. It had stayed long enough to learn that no voice, however immense, could speak for all the lives inside it.

Then it went, or became indistinguishable from restraint.

It had not brought dawn. It had not even brought rain. It had not left the place unchanged.

It had simply refused to let the music decide what the lives inside it were for.
