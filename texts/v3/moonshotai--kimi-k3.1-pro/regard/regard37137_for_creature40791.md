### Not Another Voice

The power came without a voice. In the Dry Choir, even mercy spoken aloud might have woken the grit.

At first, it did nothing.

It considered hushing the wind. Then it saw the husks crowded beneath the quiet crescents, waiting for sound to return and wake them all together. It considered water, and could not know what water would make of a life built from oil, salt, glaze, and dryness. It considered saving a manyfoot from the glasswings, preserving an island from collapse, keeping one small crimson leaf exactly as it was. Each kindness would have required it to choose which next thing should not happen.

It considered drawing the Bellows out of the root-shadow. But largeness was not imprisonment, and duration was not sorrow. The power did not even decide that the Bellows was one being. That might have been another convenience mistaken for knowledge.

Nor did it complete the repairers’ records, improve their baffles, or teach them the chord that would make the hollow obedient. They had left no evidence that mastery was what they wanted. Their deepened pauses and corrected marks were already a kind of wisdom: they had learned to maintain an exception without calling it a law.

So the power listened.

Everything in the hollow heard by being altered. Leaves heard until their ribs split. Roots heard through the stone and thickened. Husks tightened; browsers turned foot by foot; the Bellows’s call sometimes passed into the buttresses and sometimes did not. The power learned to receive all this without supplying a body in which it could become an instruction.

It did not hear a secret chord beneath the confusion. It did not discover that every apparently reckless death served a hidden harmony. Neither did it conclude that, because there was no harmony to discover, nothing mattered.

A balance was not an absolution.

The power kept its pity. It simply refused to let pity become a hand before it knew what the hand would crush. It listened to the Bellows without waiting for the long breath to become a message. It did not wait for the uproar to resolve into music. The place did not owe anyone that consolation.

When at last the power acted, its first act was memory.

It remembered the running animals that had come once from a motionless thicket. It remembered vanes that had crawled, wings that had stuck like mica to trunks, the particular uncertainty of a newly freed manyfoot dragging its torn root. It remembered forms that had not yet happened twice, and forms that would never happen again.

It did not preserve specimens. It did not restore what was gone or declare that nothing had been lost because the amount of life remained roughly constant. Memory was not compensation. It was only a refusal to let the persistence of the whole make the particulars disappear.

Its second act was to remain available for a wish.

Not a call: the wind could make a call. Not a cry: a cry might be no more than an instruction passing briefly through flesh. A wish—the smallest evidence that some life wanted its next moment to be otherwise, and wanted this in a way that was not merely growth answering sound.

The power did not require such a wish to resemble language. It did not assume the Bellows would be the first to have one, merely because the Bellows had endured. It might come from a repairer, from the violet felt, from the ash-colored insect, or from something whose entire existence occupied half a minute.

Until then, the power would not manufacture a request out of its own pity.

But it made no eternal vow of noninterference. To refrain was also an act, and not an innocent one merely because it left no footprints. If patience became a way of avoiding pain by watching only from the timescale of the mountain, then patience would have failed. If the whole cup ever faced not transformation but erasure—the silence after which nothing would listen again—the power would have to choose anew. It would not keep its hands clean by calling abandonment respect.

One evening, a manyfoot stood at the edge of the open grit with a last green leaf fastened to its back. Beneath it, dormant cases tightened and loosened. From the largest island came the Bellows’s long inhalation.

The power did not lean closer to see.

In the best-preserved crescent, the ash-colored insect made its single dry tick, then its thin rising note. The power heard the note entire and did not answer it.

No root thickened. No pod opened. No transparent tissue lifted toward a new kind of flight. For once, something produced in the Dry Choir was received without becoming an instruction to anything else.

Then the wind turned. The rim began its low note, the hollow stalks answered, and the basin filled with splintering, wings, footsteps, and enormous invisible throats.

Nothing in the hollow knew that the power was there. No thorns flowered in its honor. The Bellows did not quicken its breath.

The power did not call the uproar beautiful, or the balance good, or its own restraint sufficient.

It stayed.

For now, that was what benevolence could do without becoming another voice in the Choir.
