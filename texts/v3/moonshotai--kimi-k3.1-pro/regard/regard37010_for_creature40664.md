At first, the power did nothing.

Not because the terraces were perfect, or because nothing there could ever be lost. It waited because it could not yet reliably distinguish a wound from a form of life.

It saw a wright slacken in a washing court and did not mistake that for the death of a citizen. It saw a fracture divide an interflow and did not hasten to reunite the portions, restoring an earlier wholeness they might already have outgrown. At the communal cistern, it learned that an agreement need not resemble a proclamation. It could be a working condition: a garden receiving more overflow, an unstable district relieved, an outlet permitted to close. A sealed inlet might be the whole of a refusal.

Every grand gift it contemplated began to resemble an act of authorship. Unfailing rain would strip the young ash. Permanently open routes would make correction unnecessary and neighbors less distinct. Preserving the wrights would preserve the wrong thing. Even keeping every roof seated forever would make a monument of what was still in use.

So the power waited until it could tell the difference between the interflows’ needs and its own desire to be useful.

When at last it acted, its first gift was the opportunity to refuse it.

After heavy rain, it cleared the same small settling basin on the middle step. It moved no roof stone, altered no watershed, and added no water. It merely loosened a little accumulated sediment, work that could have been accomplished by a few fine wright cords. Then it waited.

The first response was correction. Sediment was returned. An outlet narrowed, cancelling the slight change downstream. Once, an inlet remained sealed through a wet season.

The power did not treat these responses as obstacles to overcome. It made no brighter sign and attempted no more intimate contact. It repeated the small act only where it remained harmless, always leaving the interflows able to undo it. Had correction hardened into persistent exclusion, it would have stopped. Attention that cannot accept refusal is only another kind of pressure.

Instead, over many rains, correction gradually became accommodation.

Nothing was said. No figure appeared beside the cistern, and no name passed through the ash. The basin simply became part of an order that included the disturbance. Water approached it a little differently; a branch below answered with a slight delay. The power was known, insofar as it was known at all, as a recurrent coolness at one margin and a minor regularity where no regularity had been required.

Only then did it consider what else might be asked of it.

Nothing followed that it could honestly call a petition. It did not invent one. It did not assume that the interflows secretly wished to cross the open shelves, to keep their temporary bodies, to possess a panorama, or to make their decisions in a form more readily admired by visitors. Nor did it mistake the absence of such a petition for perfect knowledge of them. It knew only that it had found no injury it understood well enough to heal, and no desire it could satisfy without guessing at the cost.

It therefore left the achievement of the terraces to their inhabitants. The pools would still return to their dark marks through innumerable small corrections, not because an alien constancy had been imposed upon them. Roofs would still wait for building seasons. Congregations would still end without a text. Neighbors would remain capable of becoming strangers, and strangers of becoming neighbors again.

This was not a vow never to intervene. The power remained attentive, especially to harms that might arrive from beyond every route the interflows could affect—harms against which a slow answer would be no answer at all. It would not require a spoken plea before preventing the end of all response. But neither would it use the possibility of future danger as a reason to govern the present.

Its guardianship consisted largely in not becoming such a danger itself.

From the upper rim, nothing announced that the plateau had been noticed. The town remained a fine grey seam. The marshes separated into scattered patches, and the water nearly vanished. Cloud shadows still crossed the districts without distinguishing them from stone.

Only after rain, in one small part of the middle step, a settling basin cleared a little sooner than it used to. Then the outflow eased. A pool returned to its dark mark.

The power had come capable of changing everything. It stayed to do one thing that could be undone.
