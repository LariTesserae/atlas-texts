### The Second Round

The power did not enter by the stair.

It understood that even a benevolent arrival might trouble the rock, and that good intentions would not shorten the delay between a vibration and the water’s answer. So, at first, there was nothing to record. No lamp brightened. No gauge trembled. The shrimp kept their small stations above the screens.

For a long time, the power only attended.

It could have dried the chamber. It could have made the pumps tireless, fixed the canal at one level, or filled the gray light beneath the shaft with gardens. It did none of these things. Not because wear was sacred, or because the keepers needed their burdens in order to become worthy. The power had no wish to preserve hardship for the beauty of it. But neither did it mistake the ability to change everything for knowledge of what ought to be changed.

It watched a dropped chain become a weeping seam hours later. It watched a storm no resident below had seen arrive as silt, then as thickened felt, then as a gathering of shrimp. It saw doors raised by people who would never pass through them, and notes left by dead hands amended rather than obeyed. It saw that a screen scrubbed perfectly clean could be poorer than one imperfectly tended.

It also saw ordinary fatigue. A keeper rubbing her eyes over an inspection tag. A split boot. A card game resumed after an argument. Maintenance did not make anyone lofty, and the power found no reason why it should.

When at last it made itself known, it did so behind a padded door, over tea, in an ordinary voice.

“What would help?” it asked.

There were questions. There was suspicion. There was a practical discussion about whether assistance eventually expecting worship could properly be called assistance. The power said that it could leave if asked, and that no shrine, title, or permanent appointment was required.

The keepers considered this.

“For the moment,” one of them said, “you can take the second round.”

Another added, “If you clean Screen Four, leave the south margin.”

So the power took the second round.

It learned to put down a wrench with its whole hand, although it had no particular need of wrenches. It learned to check the darkness behind Motor 3 indirectly and to accept a withdrawn length of ivory skin as sufficient evidence of a salamander. It left the crab gap at curb 6 clear. It filled Oil Cup 2. It did not improve Fern 5.

Sometimes the requested help had little to do with the chamber: a heavy part carried up from stores, medicine fetched, a rotation covered during an illness, an hour of company for someone too tired to sleep and too tired to talk. The power did not regard these as lesser uses of attention.

Over time, it offered one thing no resident could easily provide. A human working life was shorter than the canal’s cycle, but the power could remain for the whole of a change. It volunteered to keep the long round.

This did not make it the author of a plan. It left dated observations, open to correction. It could say which refuge had dried during the previous rise, where an eel had found a passage before the plans showed one, which lamp bracket had proved reachable when the lower gallery became water. It did not turn memory into law. Conditions could depart from precedent. New keepers could notice something it had missed. Its notes were amended in other hands, which it considered proper.

Decades passed.

The canal rose through the lower galleries. The walls gradually ceased weeping, and certain warm places behind the housings became less suitable. Animals moved according to necessities no single map could hold. The intake landing approached the water. There was no final stand upon it, no command that the cycle stop. A door was moved. Cable loops were lifted. A lamp found the bracket prepared for it. There was inconvenience, extra work, and some private sadness for familiar routes lost beneath black water.

Then the landing became a canal edge.

The power had not rescued the Hushworks from time. It had helped a few answers arrive in time, and had remained to see consequences that would otherwise have outlasted their witnesses.

Eventually the keepers ceased to think of it either as a marvel or as indispensable. Both developments seemed to please it. When its help was wanted, it helped. When it was told that its help was not wanted, it accepted that too. When there was nothing useful to do, it did nothing, without calling neglect patience or making a doctrine of restraint.

On an ordinary round, long after its coming, the north-gallery ledger read:

*Oil cup 2, full. Screen 4, south margin left. Motor 3, salamander present. Crab gap at curb 6 clear. Fern 5, two fronds. Upper intake brackets checked.*

There was no separate account of the power’s work.

On the central bridge, a shutter opened and closed above the gallery. Hundreds of transparent bodies corrected their positions in the current. An eel disturbed a lamp’s reflection, and the reflection slowly closed.

A crab touched iron once.

The power finished the round. Later, it returned and looked at the wall.
