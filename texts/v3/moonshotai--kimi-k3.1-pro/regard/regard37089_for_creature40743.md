### Memory Without Rehearsal

The power did not arrive.

Arrival would have required a route, and in Noll a route was already a proposal. It did not cross the rim, test a numbered door, or stand beneath a lamp. Even pity, returning to the same place night after night, might have made a room around a wound.

It attended once.

In that attention it saw the Noll of deep valley walls, the Noll of roofs and blazing windows, the single green thread on raw mineral. It saw other arrangements too, including some that the arc lamp had never shown. None announced itself as the true one. The crowded city was not necessarily a promise. The present emptiness was not necessarily a failure.

The power could have given Noll a river. It could have taught water to arrive after rain and made every drain dependable. It could have filled the vacant wards, supplied rooms to the doors, and put warm, habitual people behind the immaculate letter slots. Such a city would have been easy to mistake for kindness.

But completion would have been authorship, and authorship was not the same thing as care.

Nor did the power decide that Noll must remain exactly as it was. To preserve it permanently in provisionality—to keep every lot negotiable, every future available, every roof merely possible—would have been another way of choosing for it. There was no special innocence in refusing to let anything end.

It did not make the municipality speak. Giving Noll a mind merely so that it could answer a question would have been an extraordinary violence. Its replies remained local: sap sealing a cut, roots rising beside repeated pressure, a stair maintained by the fidelity of moths. The power did not pretend that these were votes.

Neither did it conclude that, because Noll had no mind, nothing within it could matter except Noll.

It saw the shrew with the torn ear, and the later absence of her particular warmth from a cable. When another shrew used the route, the power did not call the route’s continuation a consolation. It saw a puddle contract before its frogs had spawned and did not call the failed generation necessary. Much in Noll became useful after it happened. That did not make everything that happened good.

Yet there was no clean place to begin a universal repair. Protect every pupa, and the warm nests would hunger. Spare every hunted thing, and the hunters would become the things in need of rescue. Forbid the browser’s cropping, and some walls would never branch. Empty the drains of predators, and other abundances would press against other lives. A sufficiently thorough tenderness could become a climate as indifferent as any other.

This did not excuse indifference. It only meant that benevolence could not be measured by the visibility of its alterations.

So the power made a limit in itself.

Its approval would add no weight to any future. Its delight would not make a flower more persistent. Its sorrow would not cause a wound to recur so that it could be healed. Its hope would not summon people into the rooms. Noll would not become more crowded because the power loved multitudes, or more empty because the power loved possibility. Nothing would be obliged to demonstrate that the power’s attention had been a blessing.

Then it did something Noll had no tissue for.

It remembered without rehearsing.

The shrew was not remembered as nesting fiber, predation, or pressure along a cable. The moth was not remembered as the maintenance of a flower or a stair. The browser was not remembered as an agricultural consequence. If it was one animal, it was not remembered as a deficient herd; if it was a route that had acquired a body, it was not therefore remembered as less alive.

Even the transparent young in an unsuccessful puddle were not kept merely as material for a later attempt.

This memory was not a census. It counted nothing toward a population. It was not a design, and nothing could be manufactured from it. It did not make what it held more likely to happen again. A life did not have to become a habit in order to remain real.

Noll itself was held in the same way. If the flywheel stopped one day—if the roots loosened, the doors thickened, and the streets failed to agree—the power would not feel compelled to restart the engine in order to prove that Noll had deserved its regard. Persistence was not the price of having mattered.

There was a cost to this, though Noll would never pay it. Among the futures the power relinquished was one in which it descended, completed the works, relieved the small sufferings one by one, and was recognized as good. It gave up the city in which its benevolence could have been seen from every window.

Afterward, nothing in Noll carried an address for the miracle.

The flywheel turned. A drop gathered on copper. Mites interrupted a lamp. Somewhere a snail grazed the edge of an old silver line, and somewhere a moth waited on a stair with the patience of an error that had not yet decided to become architecture. Around the third pump, the bare interval remained bare. Its housing stayed smooth.

The arc lamp flashed.

Perhaps it showed roofs. Perhaps raw stone. Perhaps, for one instant, only the small dark settlement as it already was: not a rehearsal awaiting judgment, not an impoverished version of something more populous, but alive in the unrepeatable present.

Then the darkness returned.

The power did not come back to discover what Noll became. It had not loved a becoming.

It did not make a street of its mercy.
