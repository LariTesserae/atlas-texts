### The Unspent Miracle

The power came without light. Even a little more light would have been an ecological decision.

It did not stand on the central bridge or cool a handrail with its touch. It did not bend over the largest pool and give the reflected city a face. For a while it only attended: to heat rising from the shaft, to power vanishing into cables, to a drop swelling beneath a cold return line and falling with no witness.

At first it looked for the single being to whom its help might be offered. Then it surrendered that question. The ward did not divide neatly into an inhabitant and everything around it. The machines had consequences without longing. The lichen, moss, and grass had persistence without petition. Water passed through both. A repair could end one green seam and begin another; a wall could shelter spores while closing the route by which others had arrived.

It stayed long enough to see a pressure release, a local rain, and the beginning of a hot draw. Long enough, too, to see a gasket sealed. Beneath it, the living stripe softened once more, then began its slow return toward brown.

The power considered what it could give.

It could cool the basalt. It could make the rain general. It could hold every lamp at its most generous angle and adjust every leak to weep just enough. It could bring soil, insects, birds, footsteps. It could wake the machines and be answered.

Each gift would have made the ward easier for it to love. Each would also have decided, in advance, which parts of the ward were allowed to matter.

The copper grass was beautiful to it. That was not a reason to make more grass. The silence was sorrowful to it. That was not a reason to make a voice. Even its tenderness toward a cushion of moss darkening after rain was not, by itself, an instruction.

This did not mean that only voices mattered, or that every ending was good. It meant that help, to be help, had to answer something more precise than the power’s own discomfort.

So, for that visit, it changed nothing.

No pressure schedule was altered. No spore was guided to a kinder ledge. No drying patch was rescued at the cost of making its leak eternal. The gantry was neither blessed nor blamed for setting another honey-coloured block against the young rock. The power left no guardian, no commandment to continue, no promise that the unfinished passages would one day be completed.

Indifference would have been quicker: a glance, a classification—*barren, automatic, empty*—and departure. The power stayed until it could distinguish its own sadness from a need in the ward. Its care took the form of accuracy.

When it went away, there was no mark by which another visitor could tell that anything had happened.

Warm vapour rose through the narrow streets. The condenser ribs clouded. Rain ticked along one row of tanks while the next alley remained dusty. A tuft of copper grass held several bright drops without bending. Across the chamber, a seam of lichen stayed dry. A relay clacked. The pool trembled, and its upside-down lamps broke apart and returned.

The ward had not been declared complete, or corrected for being unfinished. It had not been saved. It had not been abandoned.

It had been loved without being revised.
