### What the Power Did

At first, the power thought of light.

Not a sun. Nothing so crude. Only a pale radiance in one of the high halls, enough to silver the wet edges of the leaves and reveal the vaults descending beyond sight.

Then it understood that this would be a gift to the visitor. Nothing in the Low Dark had an eye to open, a surface made for display, or a posture that awaited illumination. The basin would not be less dark to itself. Only the power would be less alone.

It let the thought go.

It considered other mercies. Memory, perhaps. Variation. A few animals moving gently through the galleries. The possibility of an unbalanced season, an unrepairable year, a consequence that might become a story. But each gift concealed its true size. To give memory would require leaving some consequences uncorrected. To give choice would require growing a chooser. To introduce animals would be to introduce hunger into a place where every exchange already had a return path. None of these things could be added without making the Low Dark into the first stage of something else.

So, for a long while, the power did nothing.

It remained through the complete passage of water along one of the continental loops. It learned the provinces without brightening them: by coolness, pressure, mineral taste, the faint potential in a root bundle. It did not expect speech and did not mistake the absence of speech for an answer.

Neither did it mistake silence for proof of peace. It searched carefully for anything being overridden—for tissue exhausted in the service of a remote command, for a province consuming itself so that another might remain unchanged, for damage repeatedly inflicted and repeatedly concealed by repair. It looked for an imprisoned awareness in the hum, a frustration in the recurring pressure waves, a desire surviving somewhere beneath the necessity of maintenance.

It found no command behind the pattern and no prisoner within it. Wear was met as it arose. Nothing had to fail in order for something else to continue. The equilibrium was not a peace imposed upon struggle. It was the shape activity took when nothing within it needed to be at war.

Whether this constituted one being or many, the power still could not determine. It stopped treating that uncertainty as important. A boundary did not have to be simple before it deserved care.

Nor did the power call the Low Dark happy. Happiness would have been another visitor’s word, another attempt to make the basin legible by comparing it with somewhere else. It was enough that nothing there appeared to be enduring its life. Continuation was not merely happening to the Low Dark. Every valve, conduit, root tip, and reclaimer cup was engaged in the intricate work by which continuation occurred.

That was not consent. The power did not pretend that it was. But it was sufficient to forbid casual improvement.

For a time, the power simply kept company with the basin. It asked neither to be perceived nor to be changed by the experience. It followed condensation from a leaf surface into the grooves, down through the pumps, across the warm fractures, and back toward the canopy. It attended to the making of a cable that would never be separate from the body using it, and to the unmaking of old tissue that left no carcass behind. Nothing waited for its attention. Nothing became more meaningful because its attention was there.

The power discovered that it could love this without needing the Low Dark to love anything in return.

At last it acted, but not within the basin.

Wherever its own authority reached, it caused the Low Dark to be regarded as inhabited.

Not owned. Not sacred. Not finished. Inhabited.

Its silence would not be entered as vacancy. Its inability to refuse would not be recorded as permission. The pumping flats would not become available merely because their output could be diverted, nor the root-ocean because its warm water could be drained, nor the galvanic provinces because their minerals were already so beautifully separated. No later visitor, however gentle, would be entitled to seed the galleries with more comprehensible life and call the result enrichment.

If danger came from outside, the power would turn it aside where it could.

But it placed no protection around the existing equilibrium itself. It would not preserve the Low Dark against the Low Dark. If some cloud boundary were ever to move and remain moved, if a province began to accumulate, if a self-recognizing chemistry refused an ancient junction or a conduit persisted along an unnecessary route, the power would not automatically return the basin to its former condition and call that healing.

It would come back first as it had come now: without light, without correction, without assuming that difference was damage.

Its protection would mean preventing use, not preventing change.

Then it withdrew.

It left no footprint compressed into the mat, no charge lingering in the conductive vessels, no seed, symbol, blessing, or monument. It did not even leave a record within the roots. The Low Dark would have no burden of having been visited.

Behind it, a bead of water reached the size at which its weight carried it into a groove. Pressure passed from one chamber to the next. A valve closed with a soft tick. Somewhere old tissue released a mineral ion; somewhere new tissue took one up.

The power felt the tender impulse to read all this as assent, and the tick as farewell.

It did not.

That was its final kindness.
