The first thing the power would do is nothing.

The second would be to keep doing nothing until they understood why.

From the stair, the chamber would seem to invite the wrong mercies. Seal the leaks. Correct the faults. Silence the knock. Bring in clean water, cool air, light. Make the engine sound and the garden safe.

But a sealed engine would give the blackfans no vapor. An efficient one would withdraw the leaked warmth and current from the pithcanes. Silence would leave some capsules closed around their spores forever. Stopping the machine would only lengthen the grove’s dying by however much water it had stored.

To rescue the vegetation from the engine would be to rescue it from the conditions that had become its body.

So the power would stay.

They would stay through one turning of the wheel, and then through enough turnings to see the ceiling release its uneven rain. They would learn why one root crown received drop after drop while the floor beside it powdered. They would follow water into a petiole and find it again, years later, behind a waxy valve. They would watch a quiet zone shift by less than a hand’s breadth and see, much later, a root knot binding dust where previously nothing could have held.

They would find no imprisoned person in the greenery, no keeper concealed in the booth, no plea encoded in the gauges. Nor would they need to find one. The absence of a voice would not make the place empty, and the absence of gratitude would not make care meaningless.

They would not wake the grove. There was no sleeper.

They would not give it a mind merely so that its welfare could become easier to consult, or a face so that its continuation could matter more readily. That would not be reverence for what lived there. It would be a preference for what resembled the visitor.

Neither would they make the chamber eternal. To hold the engine forever at one pitch, preserve every leak at its present flow, and fix each acoustic refuge in place would save only a census of plants. It would end the slow following, the losses and replacements, by which the grove had persisted through ages. Permanence would be another form of repair.

One kind of action might remain.

The power would set a watch, not a ward. Nothing below would be altered: no additional drop, no protective stillness, no charmed immunity from wear. But if the chamber were ever threatened by haste—by someone stripping its copper, sealing its leaks, flooding its heat, or improving it back into usefulness—their care would become delay. Time for knowledge to interfere with confidence. Time for the would-be rescuer to discover what a faulty seal fed before calling it a fault.

Against age, the power would set no barrier. Against an eventual ending, no sentence. A life did not have to be endless to deserve protection from a careless hand. They would defend the grove from time no more than they would defend it from thirst. They would defend it only from being mistaken for nothing.

In the maintenance booth, they would read the surviving **DO NOT** and leave the blistered space blank. The chamber did not need to be made into a commandment, or a parable, or a monument to the power’s restraint.

Then they would go.

Below them, the flywheel would advance without visible haste. A bead of water would enter a copper-veined channel. Somewhere a capsule would open, or remain closed, according to a note no one had written for its benefit. Nothing in the grove would know that it had been considered, and nothing would need to.

If the power left any blessing behind, it was only this:

*Go on in the manner of your going on. Not unchanged. Not forever. Not for me.*
