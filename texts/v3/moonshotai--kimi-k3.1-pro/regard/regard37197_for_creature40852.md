### If Stillness Came

The first mercy the power imagined was: *Be still.*

They did not speak it.

For an instant they perceived the landscape that command would make. Water finding one level and keeping it. Rain falling only downward. A tree spending its whole life with a single crown, its roots remaining roots. Mud beginning the long, uninterrupted labor of becoming soil. Stones, at last, having time to round.

Then they listened more deeply.

In that imagined quiet, seeds tuned to the three-note chord remained shut. Others waited in vain for a pressure reversal, an intake’s shredding, the brief failure of vibration that had always told their coats to open. Reed fabrics grew old without the tearing that had planted their descendants. Warm circulation slackened; tissue settled beyond the reach of the fans. Some thickets flourished for a while and exhausted themselves. Not everything died. Not everything dwindled. But the peace would not simply have been the Low Works relieved of its pain. It would have been another revision—more thorough than most, and imposed according to an outsider’s preference for still water.

A rescue, perhaps. Also a replacement.

The power looked for someone to address.

Had they found one consciousness dreading the next revision, one being able to remember an address and grieve its loss, they would have offered refuge immediately: offered it, not assigned it, and without demanding gratitude or departure in return. But there was no operator sealed inside the pump house, no suffering intelligence behind the engines, no forest mind dreaming beneath the casting flats. The Braid remembered processes, not wishes. If the power had come expecting a single inhabitant, they found instead an abundance of lives that did not add up to a person.

There was no plea.

There was no permission either.

The power refused both easy conclusions. They would not treat voicelessness as worthlessness. Neither would they turn adaptation into consent. A seed’s need for the chord did not make the chord kind. A reed’s aptitude for surviving its own destruction did not make destruction a gift. These were not arguments for the Works. They were simply the shapes life had found under its conditions.

So the power did not stop the engines. They did not preserve a favorite forest, awaken the Braid, or make the brief trees permanent. They looked instead for an act that would take no present life, silence no existing seed, and close no path already open.

What they found was almost nothing.

Into the Braid’s vast archive of dormancies they placed one small hereditary exception. A few unremarkable propagules gained the ability to remain viable through long quiet, and—if quiet endured beyond anything the Works had ever produced—to begin without an engine harmonic, a pressure wound, or a passage through contradictory waters. Their descendants would not be stronger. They would merely be capable of slowness: able to tolerate water that stayed where it was, to leave a little fallen matter long enough for soil to become conceivable, to grow without first being struck.

They might fail. They might leave no descendants at all. While the machines continued, they would be only seeds among seeds, with no dominion and no promised future. The power had not planted a paradise in embryo. They had added no destiny, only an alternative to necessity.

Then they did nothing more.

The engines stopped together, as they sometimes did.

For that fraction of a second, vibration-held coats loosened. Suspended filaments began to settle. The new possibility did not stir. It had not been made for a pause, but for an ending, should an ending ever arrive without being imposed.

The piston-stroke returned. Turbines climbed toward their shriek. Black crowns shed water; copper reeds divided beneath the surge; rosettes came apart in the shifting eyes of whirlpools. Somewhere a reflection showed a forest before its ground had been cast. Nothing thanked the power. Nothing had been asked to.

They had not saved the Low Works by turning it into something gentler. They had not blessed its violence merely because life had learned to make use of the blows. They had left the basin its noise, its brief forests, its terrible generosities—and had made the future very slightly less compulsory.

Behind them, the Works resumed manufacturing their youth.

Ahead, perhaps never to be opened, was one door that did not lead back to the machines.
