## What the Power Did Elsewhere

The power did nothing to the town.

That took longer than intervention.

It remained beyond the basin, where its presence could neither warm an active seam nor deflect a filament of dust. It attended through many cycles, until the differences between growth and weather, withdrawal and death, stillness and absence, were no longer merely things it had been told. It did not enter the central court. It did not put a question into the black joint.

A less wise benevolence would have begun by asking what was missing. It would have found the list almost inexhaustible: water, warmth, brightness, voices, furnishings, doors that could be opened, rooms with reasons for their shape. It might have brought rain and called the resulting catastrophe awakening. It might have populated the courts, planted the conduits, given the fans sap and the scalebacks blood. It might have made the foundation a single mind, imprisoned beneath its own city, and then graciously set that mind free.

Thus it could have filled the basin with mercy and left nothing it had intended to save.

The power asked a different question: *What here would my help make impossible?*

It found no citizens awaiting return. No architect had been interrupted. No message had been concealed in the narrowing doorways. The town’s resemblance to a city was not a promise, and its emptiness was not a bereavement. The foundation could not be assumed to desire company merely because it was alone, or speech merely because it had none. To wake it into a voice so that it could consent to being left unchanged would not have been consultation. It would have been the manufacture of a petitioner, followed by whatever answer the petitioner had been made capable of giving.

So the power brought no gift into the rooms.

Neither did it mistake restraint for reverence toward everything that happened there. The hunter’s feeding was not made innocent by silence. A fan’s failure was not proved painless because it left no stain. The power did not pretend to know what no cry revealed. It kept that ignorance open: not as permission to experiment upon the lives before it, and not as an excuse to call their every loss good.

It simply refused to make their uncertainty serve its own appetite for action.

Nor did it preserve the town.

It did not hold open a doorway that the foundation was closing. It did not fix the central rim in its most beautiful shape, renew every copper scale, or forbid an old district to shed its plates. It did not save each scaleback from the hunter, or the hunter from the possibility of an empty channel. To protect the architecture against the foundation would have been to preserve the map by preventing the journey. Even permanence would have been an occupation.

What the power did, it did elsewhere.

It made the town unavailable to ownership.

No arriving need could turn the laminae into quarried pages, the fans into ornaments, or the animals into specimens whose rarity justified taking them. No later benefactor would be permitted to flood the basin merely to discover whether greenness was possible. No keeper would be allowed to embalm one favoured cycle and call the result rescue. Knowledge that could depart without residue was not forbidden; but there would be no knowledge purchased by making the town incapable of continuing without its students.

There was no fence around the plain. A fence would have been the first portable purpose imposed upon the place. There was no warning cut into the outer walls, and no emblem set upon the highest roof. The town was not to be forced to explain, even to potential despoilers, why it deserved to remain unexplained.

In its own keeping, remote from every weather course, the power left only this instruction:

*Protect the conditions of continuance, not the present form. Do not confuse silence with consent, or emptiness with need. Do not answer a request that has not been made. Do not make a future request impossible.*

It promised no eternity. The fractures might one day cease to nourish the foundation. The fans might fail to open. The hunter might already have taken its last scaleback, or might outlast every visible fan in the settlement. If the colony divided, spread beyond the basin, became another kind of life, or ended without ever having been a self, the power would not regard its protection as betrayed. It had not decided what the town must always be in order to deserve what had been done for it.

And if, someday, something there became able to address what lay beyond the pewter sky—without address having first been forced upon it—the power would listen again.

Then it withdrew.

No seam cooled at its departure. No dust column bent. Nothing portable remained behind to mark the difference between having been noticed and never having been noticed at all.

In deep dimness, fresh laminae whitened. A bridge thickened from below. The single fan in the central court opened toward a division in the flowing dust. Somewhere a scaleback was no longer where it had been, and along a floor channel the pale hunter changed, almost imperceptibly, the tension of its length.

The power left no monument to its restraint. Even restraint, if sufficiently pleased with itself, can become a way of occupying a place.

The centre remained untouched.
