### What the Power Did

The power’s first impulse was to repair something. Its second was to wait.

It did not descend as warmth, because water would have climbed toward it. It did not arrive as an electrical presence, because the veinscale would have braided after it and written the visitation into a wall. It did not speak through the basalt, because somewhere a wader might lift its head and leave a spillway before the food came. For a long time, its presence was only attention, and even that was kept from becoming a pressure.

It remained through the building of heat, the descent of the cloud ceiling, and the release. Then it remained through other cycles, less regular than the first had suggested.

A single release would have misled it. It would have seen reservoirs stripped of their grazers, living film carried over the rim, clear bodies stranded on mineral shelves. A single hot interval would have misled it differently: the city might have seemed to be dying by evaporation, its inhabitants reduced to grey scars and transparent flakes cemented into cracks. Only repetition showed how much of what looked like loss was also transport, and how much apparent endurance was merely the durable remainder of something that had not survived.

At first the power looked for a being who could receive its help.

The wader seemed to offer the clearest possibility. Once, during a long condensation, the power let its attention rest beside one. Water had begun to follow the same narrow threads down the animal’s crazed skin. Its head was folded against its body. Nothing about it distinguished expectation from absence.

The power waited with it.

After many hours, one toe shifted on the stone. The wader raised its head a fraction and began walking toward a spillway where no water yet moved. It had not been waiting for company. It had not, so far as the power could honestly determine, been waiting for anything that could be called deliverance. Something in the deep sequence of the pumps had changed, and the animal had heard it.

The power did not make the wader speak. It did not place another pale reflection beside it to answer an imagined loneliness. Neither did it turn that restraint into a general rule and declare that nothing here could ever want assistance. It simply ceased to confuse the form of a life with a request to alter it.

The longer it remained, the less any single mercy resembled wisdom.

It could clear the veinscale from every valve and control cut, and starve the cistern lenses. It could protect the films from every automatic tool, and allow their shells to close the paths on which both machines and animals depended. It could make every overflow gentle, and leave the next wetting without the colonists that violent water had once carried between wards. It could return all water to the discipline of slope, and undo in an instant the old, exact separation of water that fell from water that climbed.

Even abundance would have been a preference. To fill every reservoir and keep it full would have chosen the wet interval over the dry one, the grazing lens over the folded survivor, the active film over the long viability of its scars.

Restoration was the greatest temptation, because at first it resembled respect.

The power could have reopened the mineral-sealed facades, freed the buried canals, polished the handles, and made the towers ring with new machinery. It could have put bodies on the stairs again. The avenues were wide enough for processions; the plazas could have held multitudes. With sufficient power, the city might have been made to explain itself as a settlement.

But the worn stairs were evidence, not instructions. The empty plazas were not requests for crowds. The builders’ geometry did not necessarily possess a greater claim than the paths water had chosen since, or the clearances preserved by grazing mouths, or the dry upper districts whose emptiness gave vapor room to cool.

Nor did the power preserve the present city as a specimen. It did not fix each colony at its violet edge, each cistern at its population, each wader forever beside its appointed spillway. That would only have been restoration with more delicate tools.

It made the engines no promise of eternity. Permanence would have been the largest intervention of all.

This did not mean that nothing suffered, or that every failure was good because it was old. The power refused that consolation also. It remained willing to answer a request, from any being, in any form it could genuinely understand. It simply would not invent an emergency in order to make its strength useful.

Its visible answer to the Condensary was therefore none.

Its lasting act was performed elsewhere.

Where the power’s memory carried authority, it caused the High Condensary to be remembered as inhabited.

Not as a ruin awaiting an heir. Not as vacant industrial country. Not as a reservoir system with incidental life in it, or a wilderness inconvenienced by pipes. Inhabited.

The word was made large enough to include veinscale in its dry dendrites as well as at its spreading edge; lenses beneath black water, in overflow, and folded into cracks; waders beyond the visible wards; the small lives in return pipes; and whatever else occupied habitats that could not be opened without changing the pressure that made them worth occupying. It did not require the question of the tenders to be settled first. Uncertainty was not to become an excuse for carelessness.

No one acting in the power’s name could thereafter call the plateau empty because no door opened, or available because no voice refused them. They could not justify gardens by the absence of leaves, inhabitants by the width of the avenues, or new engines by the patience of the old ones. Restoration would have to begin by learning what was being restored, and for whom. Silence could not be used as consent.

This was not a command that the city remain unchanged. Channels could still seal. Colonies could die back to their youngest branches. A release could carry away nearly every grazer in a reservoir. The engines could continue their selective maintenance, or fail in it. The power did not make the present arrangement sacred merely because it had witnessed it.

It protected only one distinction: that between a place difficult to perceive and a place without claims of its own.

Then it withdrew.

On a wall above a dry gutter, isolated hairlines darkened. Beneath a cistern’s surface, clear rims lifted in succession before any current arrived. At a spillway, a pale shape unfolded one leg, then became still again.

A single lamp came on and reflected in a street that had been dry a moment before. The power did not take it for acknowledgment.

Nothing came down the road.

Its only repair had been to the word *empty*.
