### The Pause

The power came without light.

Light would have been an alteration, and it had not yet learned whether alteration was kindness.

From the rim, it might have seen only failed drainage: three obsolete engines, a broken roof, cold water going nowhere. Instead, it waited through the ebb. It felt the engines in the ground and the small withdrawal of water from the stone. It saw the dark bands appear on the domes.

Then, before the relay clicked, the hollow became still.

The power did not mistake that stillness for an appeal. Nothing was asking to be rescued from the return of water. The eels were orienting their bodies; the buttonbacks were bracing; the larvae were holding fast. Even the insects had settled. It was not prayer. It was preparation.

The penstock opened. Water came down the spillway without haste, and the old shorelines returned.

The power stayed.

It let its attention become small enough for the place. It followed the film from milky seep to grazed shelf, from stain to sediment to stone. It watched a larva fit a curved fragment of buttonback shell over a weak seam in its case. It felt the engine beds through the stacked bodies of the amphibians, each animal shifting by degrees to retain a little warmth. Beneath the discharge it found the smooth hunter, hungry and motionless, and beneath the rake heap it found the dry cavity where the hunter slept.

Many mercies occurred to it.

It could have cleared the water of iron and sulfur, making it pleasant to drink and transparent to the greatest depth. The film would have starved.

It could have brought sunlight and sown the margins green. The reeds would have been beautiful. Their roots would have entered the masonry; their leaves would have fallen into the pans; their abundance would have covered the old grazing grounds. A green shore would not have decorated the Sump. It would have replaced it.

It could have stopped the machines and silenced the pressure in the ground. It could have warmed the fog until the amphibians no longer needed iron. It could have fed the hunter without an eel dying, spared the eels by feeding them without buttonbacks, and made so many other gentle exceptions that no creature would have had to pay quite so dearly for another’s life.

It did not call these possibilities wicked. Nor did it decide that, because the Sump worked, everything within it was therefore good. The hunter’s hunger did not become beautiful merely because its body was fitted to waiting. A small death was not made painless by being necessary to something else.

But there was no end to the exceptions an unlimited power could make. Each would have been defensible. Together, they would have produced a world held together by the watcher's kindness, according to the watcher's idea of what ought to be spared. Slowly, without any visible conquest, the Sump would have become the watcher’s possession.

The power did not wish to own it in that gentle way.

It remained willing to mend a wound. It would not call the whole hollow a wound merely because it was not a garden.

For the hunter, especially, restraint was difficult. The animal could have been carried elsewhere, to some warm margin where prey came often. It could have been given an easier body and a shorter wait. But the power did not confuse *elsewhere* with rescue, or an easier animal with the same animal made happy. So it stayed through one long, unsuccessful hunt and did not look away. When nothing came within reach, it did not turn the empty interval into evidence that its own intervention was required. When the hunter returned beneath the shell heap, the power let it go.

Only after all this did it act.

It made no change to the water, the film, the machinery, or any animal. It did not strengthen the flywheels or promise that the buried current would never fail. It placed its gift outside the circuit, in the narrow space where an intention has not yet become an act.

Thereafter, anyone who came to the rim meaning to drain the hollow, seal its seeps, divert its water, or take the old engines away would first be given a moment of honest perception.

Not a command. Not a vision of punishment. Not even the whole truth; no brief knowledge could contain that.

They would feel the engines through the soles of their feet. They would understand that the warmth of the iron was occupied ground. They would notice that a shell could be a body, then a refuge, then part of a larva’s house, then part of an island. They would know that beneath the apparent stillness of the discharge an animal was spending hours on the possibility of one meal, and that beneath the rake heap there was a dry place the return water did not reach.

For one moment, the Sump would cease to be scenery behind their purpose.

No voice would say, *Do not*.

Their choice would remain theirs. But it would have to be made with the place present in it.

Some might go ahead. The power did not make understanding into a cage. Others might discover that what they had called repair was only an inability to recognize a form of continuance different from their own. Others still might simply sit down and wait for the water to return.

That was all the gift amounted to: ignorance would no longer be quite so effortless.

The power did not make the Sump immortal. Iron could still tire. A conduit could still collapse. The deep source of current could still fail, and drought could still close around the remaining seams. The hunter would not be spared being a hunter, nor would the buttonbacks be spared being small.

It had not promised that nothing would end. It had only refused to let dimness be mistaken for emptiness, or emptiness for permission.

Then it remained for one more interval.

At the ebb, the channels narrowed. Shells ticked against the stone. Somewhere below the floor, a pressure change traveled ahead of any sound.

The thread eels turned toward the upper penstock. The insects settled. The buttonbacks angled their antennae down and held fast. The amphibians lay breathing at different rates on the warm iron. The hunter did not move.

The power became still with them.

The relay clicked. A clear sheet of water crossed the worn lip. Mineral deposit took the shine and gave it back. The buttonbacks resumed grazing; the larvae put out their legs; the eels used the first push of the current to carry themselves into shelter.

The power stayed until, one by one, the old shorelines had returned.

Then it went, as it had come, without light.
