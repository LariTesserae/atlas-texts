The power came in the dry season, and without changing the weather.

That was its first courtesy. It did not arrive as rain, or as a voice from the pools, or as some vast shape casting the whole plain into shadow. There was already too much here that could be missed by looking for greatness.

For a long time, it did nothing that could have been distinguished from patience.

It watched a pan fill and become crowded. It watched the same pan contract until its remaining fish knew every patch of shade. It watched the mud dry over eggs, seed cases, and buried animals, and learned not to call the result empty. It followed mud on a heron’s legs from one pool to another. It listened to people recount the history of a seep, including the part where they had been mistaken. It waited beside a dark, algae-covered back until a nostril opened and the supposed stone breathed.

Only then did it consider what kindness might require.

A hasty kindness would have made the water reliable. It would have spared fish the closing pools, spared households their moves, spared the stonebacks some of their long journeys. But water standing everywhere, at every season, would not merely have rescued the plain’s inhabitants. It would have made another country. The brief hatchings would have lost their conditions. The arts of waiting beneath clay and traveling at the first overflow would have lost their uses. Some lives would have flourished by the disappearance of others.

So the power did not improve the plain by replacing it.

Neither did it decide that everything within the plain’s order was therefore good enough. A pattern can be true and still become an excuse for looking away. The last hours of a fish in warm, shallow water were not made painless by the importance of drying pans. A household’s loss was not ennobled merely because its members learned from it. The power refused to preserve hardship for the sake of character, or to call suffering wise because suffering had consequences.

Its first act, then, was a refusal: it would not confuse love with redesign.

Its second was to remain.

Its attention was not diminished by being given to more than one life. It could be present at a crowded pool and beneath a stone where ants were carrying their brood deeper. It could accompany an animal without asking that animal to become aware of being accompanied. It did not require lives to be long, useful, conscious of their effects, or capable of gratitude.

Where help could restore a possibility without making the helped into possessions, it helped.

People later told one story about it, though even they were uncertain that anything unusual had happened.

A household had opened a shallow cut to guide overflow toward a fishing pan. The work was careful by their understanding. Beyond a low swell, however, out of sight from where they stood, the same cut would have begun drawing water from a seep on which another camp depended. There would have been no quarrel at first. Only, weeks later, a poorer seep, a longer walk, and no certain way of proving why.

The power did not close the cut. It did not strike the tools from anyone’s hands or send an infallible dream.

It let the small sound of running water carry a little farther at dusk.

A child lifting a pot heard it where the route should have been dry. In that country, moving water was worth noticing. She mentioned it, not because she understood the cut, but because the sound had seemed out of place. An older person walked the ground again in the morning. Others came. They found what their first account had omitted and altered the work.

They were not made wise forever. They made other mistakes in later seasons. The gift had not been wisdom. It had been one more opportunity to use the wisdom they already had.

No such story was told about the old stoneback, because no person was present.

She had outlived the largest tree beside her seep and two names by which households had known the pool. Rust remained in the seams of her plates. Her lower back was almost black. The power did not reveal to her the generations of frogs that had spawned in wallows deepened by her body, or the sedges that had followed her trails. It did not require her life to become a lesson about interdependence.

Nor did it weigh those consequences against the creatures she had crushed, eaten, or driven from a resting place. It was not sitting in judgment. It did not need her to be innocent, or conscious of having been good, in order to love her.

When her body began to fail, it did not keep her alive merely because it would miss her. It eased what could be eased. It stayed through the long intervals between breaths, without demanding that she wake, understand, or respond.

Then she died.

Her death was not announced as a transformation. She remained an animal. The pool and its scavengers took her back by degrees. In the following rains, one of her old wallows held water after several shallower hollows had failed, and frogs gathered there. Years later, that wallow also began to fill with sediment. The power did not preserve it as a monument.

It remembered her without preserving her.

It remembered the translucent inhabitants of a three-week pool just as fully, and not because their briefness made them poignant. It kept no ledger of usefulness. It remembered people who had misread the ground, animals that had left no discernible trace, and pools whose overflow had never been witnessed. Its memory was not offered as a substitute for life, as though being remembered should console the dead for dying. It was simply the refusal to let smallness, slowness, or silence place anything beneath notice.

The plain did not become safe. Pools still failed. Hunters still waited where other creatures would eventually have to come. People dug useful cuts and mistaken ones. Young stonebacks remained soft, and many never reached water. The power did not call this a perfect world. It did not ask the inhabitants to call it one.

After the next rain, clear water stood a finger deep over quartz sand. Life turned beneath it before the surface had finished reflecting the clouds. Beetles arrived on warm nights. Fish entered overflow so shallow that their backs broke through. On the rises, insects resumed their dry trilling, and after dark the frogs began in separate pools, chorus answering chorus without ever becoming one voice.

The power listened. It did not take the sound as praise.

Then it listened also to the dry pan beyond the last camp, where nothing yet moved.

That was its lasting work: to keep the plain company without keeping it still, and to help without making help a form of ownership.
