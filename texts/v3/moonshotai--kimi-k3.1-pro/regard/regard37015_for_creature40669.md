### What the Power Did

The power did not arrive as light. Even light would have been an intervention. Leaves would have turned toward it; animals would have folded themselves more closely into bark and moss. The listeners would have found one another and left accounts in the roots. For centuries afterward, hands would have copied swellings whose first meaning had been lost.

It remained, at first, only attention.

Its earliest impulse was to make things clearer.

It could have made the watershed legible: given each spring a sign naming its source, each basin a warning before its water failed, each listener’s hand an understanding beyond what touch and long acquaintance could provide. It could have corrected the living archives wherever old errors had thickened into authority.

It could have kept every cistern sufficient, every wallow open, every seep within reach of the growth that depended on it. It could have caused the pale plant in the roofless cell to flourish elsewhere and so relieved the listeners of their long, unsuccessful concern.

But no gift would have remained itself once the Height had taken it up.

An answer given to one hearing would become a water-sign carried beyond the conditions in which it was true. A basin protected from every decrease would no longer take part in the exchanges that fed provinces elsewhere. The brief, crowded seasons of the deep pools depended on changes that looked, from within a single lifetime, like delay or neglect. Even the pale plant’s survival elsewhere would not have explained its life beside the stones. It would only have concealed the question beneath success.

Nor was preservation a gentler temptation. To preserve the Height would have meant choosing some condition and quietly preferring it forever: this basin rather than its successor, this grove around its old absence, this route kept open by browsers whose bodies had not yet been born. Continual protection would have required continual correction. In time, nothing would have happened except what the power repeatedly permitted. The Height would have ceased to be a place and become an effect of its benefactor.

So the power refrained.

It did not mistake this for proof that everything within the Height was good. A death was not justified by the dark leaves that might later grow above it. Long hunger did not become beautiful merely because it was quiet. An error was not made wise by the possibility that some later listener might correct it. Slowness gave suffering time to become intricate, just as it gave everything else time to become intricate.

Nor did the power promise itself never to act. There could be a kind of vanity in refusal: a wish to remain unstained by consequence while others bore the cost of one’s restraint. If help could lessen a harm rather than merely move it beyond the power’s attention, it would help. It would not require the Height to suffer elegantly in order to preserve its own innocence.

But it would not invent an emergency in order to demonstrate its love.

The listener who had drawn its particular attention was not the wisest of their hearing, nor the most unfortunate. Years earlier, they had copied a water-sign badly. Discovering the mistake, they had returned to the same root and added a correction, though the correction itself was already becoming difficult to read.

Now they were crouched beneath a buttress with one hand against it, trying to distinguish a small repeated ticking within the wood from the pulse in their own palm.

Twice they lifted the hand. Twice they set it down again. Then they waited for their breathing to slow.

The power waited with them.

It did not stop their pulse. It did not make the stem repeat its sound. It did not send certainty into the listener like warm water into a hollow column. There was nothing here that required rescue. There was only someone doing carefully something that could still be done wrong.

After a while, the listener touched the root twice, took their hand away, and went back toward the camp.

Much later, they died. Their body was covered with fallen growth. For a time the place was marked by a richness of stems, then by an unusually dense little garden, then by nothing that could reliably be distinguished from the surrounding mat. The correction they had made was copied once, misunderstood, and partly corrected again. Eventually no one any longer gave the pattern of their name against another wrist.

The power remembered it.

It remembered the badly copied sign as well as the return to correct it. It remembered the hand lifted and set down twice. It did not pretend that memory was rescue, or immortality, or compensation for a life. It made no copy of the listener and placed no continuing version of them among the roots. It simply refused to let the vast persistence of the Height be the only scale on which that one life had mattered.

For the Height, this changed nothing. No spring answered the memory. No hunter stirred. No root thickened into an account of having been witnessed.

Rain sank into the mat. Far away, after an interval no animal present could have measured, a spring rose. Water entered an old footprint and reached the edge of a cistern. Something small turned in it.

The power did not call this its work. It did not ask to be recognized, and it did not make its attention another condition the Height would have to survive.

It remained willing to be needed.

It did not make itself necessary.
