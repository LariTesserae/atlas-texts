### What the Power Left

The power would not arrive in the quiet.

To do so would be to mistake the hollow for a place that became intelligible only when a human ear could bear it. It would attend first through the roar, and then at a smaller scale: until a water film had breadth, a grain of silt had a sheltered side, and the distance from one end of a snail’s grazing mark to the other was a journey.

Its first kindnesses would all have to be refused.

It would not silence the cleft. The roar was not a sorrow inhabiting the stone, and the current was not merely the roar’s cause. It carried moisture and removal, drought and delivery. To stop it for the sake of peace would be to impose a peace meaningful chiefly to a visitor.

It would not make the floor wet, the walls warm, or the green lining spread across the bare limestone. Beyond the exact boundaries of shelter and water, that new growth would find no corresponding gift of food. A larger pasture followed by a larger hunger would not be an improvement.

It would not multiply the snails because their shells were few, nor spare the springtails because the beetle was slow, nor feed the beetle without noticing what its feeding meant. It would not bring the wren down from the rim to enliven the room. Nothing there required enlivening.

The seam-snail that had drawn its particular notice was, at that time, sealed within its shell. The power would not wake it.

A hasty benevolence might see imprisonment: a small animal closed in darkness, waiting out conditions it could not command. But the seal was not a wound. It was one of the snail’s ways of remaining possible. To break it, to keep the animal continuously active and safe from interruption, would be to destroy the very fitness by which it occupied its seam.

So the power waited without making the waiting into an ordeal.

When moisture returned, the snail pushed aside the seal and resumed grazing. There was no discernible rejoicing. No sign marked the power’s patience. The animal passed over the dark film, interrupting it almost invisibly, and the film’s slow continuation remained part of the same event. Even here, benevolence could not simply take the side of the most easily imagined life.

Only then would the power ask whether anything was needed.

Had there been some fresh injury—the seep poisoned, the shelter newly crushed, some careless force breaking the terms on which the place depended—it might have acted. Its touch could have been exact: one obstruction removed, one course of water restored, one irreversible harm turned aside. Restraint would not have required a vow of passivity.

But there was no such emergency.

The hollow was sparse, not stricken. Its smallness was not a failed attempt at abundance. Nothing in it was asking to be delivered from every condition of its existence.

Nor would the power preserve it forever in its present form. That would have been a subtler interference. The rounded rim, the buried roof fall, the softening timber, and the worn steps all showed that the settled place was not exempt from change. To fix every cushion of moss and every cloudy shell against loss would be to make the hollow into an image of itself: safe, perhaps, and no longer quite alive.

So it did nothing to the stone.

It cleaned no silt from the trough, added no drop to the seep, left no warmth in the limestone. It did not even lay upon the place one of those invisible burdens called a blessing, under which everything must afterward signify the giver.

Its only act, if it could be called an act, was to remain until its attention no longer contained the wish to be thanked.

Then it withdrew.

A later visitor would have found nothing to report. The old snail shells would still be misleading. The beetle would still spend days beneath one stone. The human crescent would still interrupt the current by no more than a hand’s breadth, and moss would still occupy that margin without knowing it had been given.

At the next turning of the air, the roar would lessen. A drop would strike the trough. Water would click in a crack. The beetle might range a little farther while scents lingered on the wet wall.

Then the current would resume. The little sounds would vanish. Nothing hidden would emerge, and nothing would have been rescued from being itself.

The power had come prepared to give.

It went without taking even the satisfaction of having helped.
