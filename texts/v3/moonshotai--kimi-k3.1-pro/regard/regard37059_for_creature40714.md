The power came without light, and did not stand in the mouth.

Even its shadow, had it cast one, would have been an event. Instead it offered the cleft the first courtesy of wisdom: it paid attention long enough to be changed by what it found.

It did not see a community first and individual creatures afterward. It saw both at once. A larva in the last wet thread of a seam. The seam itself, narrowing toward dryness. A moth dying beneath other moths while its body still helped hold their warmth. A bat carrying food from a distant valley home in its blood. The amphibians beneath the grit, patient beyond any easy notion of hope. None of these was merely a part of something more important.

The power’s first impulse was to give water.

Then more warmth. Then more space. It imagined basins that did not fail, broods that did not vanish, hunters fed without hunting, small bodies spared the long pressures of cold and hunger. Each vision began as kindness. Each also contained a subtle temptation: in every one of them, the power could see its own generosity more clearly than it could see the cleft.

A less wise benevolence might have made a garden and called it a rescue.

The power did not. To secure every life against every other would not have been a gift the cleft could simply absorb. It would have been a new creation wearing the old one’s name. The crickets lived partly through what fell; the multitude on the ceiling depended on journeys into the outer cold; the amphibians’ lives were shaped around water that could not be commanded. Even comfort, added carelessly, might only have taught the whole assemblage to die in a more pleasant-looking chamber.

But the power did not make the opposite mistake either.

It did not call the cleft perfect. It did not use the word *balance* to make a drying larva fortunate, or say that nothing had been lost because something else would eat the body. The whole could be wonderful without every life within it being well. A death could nourish the floor and still be a death. No elegance of circulation was required to sanctify pain.

So the power left the mountain its breath. It left the seep free to abandon one wall for another, the basins to diminish, the films to whiten at their edges. It made no promise that the roost would remain forever, or that the three amphibian notes would always answer one another from the grit. Permanence, imposed from outside, would have been only a gentler kind of embalming.

Then it helped where help did not require possession.

One night a young bat lost its hold. It fell through the warm, crowded air and struck the floor too weakly stunned to climb. The crickets had not yet gathered, but they would have. The power returned the bat to the ceiling and settled it within the press of bodies.

It did not refuse this small act because the crickets might have wanted a meal. It did not make a philosophy out of walking past. The roost stirred, objected, shifted, and closed around the young animal. Below, the cleaners continued through the dust.

For those beyond such help, the power did not offer immortality in the forms that had already failed them. It did not restore an old moth to flight merely so that it could fall again. It did not keep a body inhabitable after every means of inhabiting it had gone. But when pain could no longer guide a creature toward shelter, food, warmth, or escape, the power let that pain cease. Fear remained while fear could still carry a body to safety. Hunger remained while hunger could still lead it toward life. Nothing was taken away merely because it was uncomfortable to witness.

Death still entered the chamber. Nothing that fed on death went hungry on that account.

The power also remembered, though it did not mistake remembrance for repayment. It remembered not only the cleft—the warm organ, the crowded wonder—but the unrepeatable passages within it. A particular lizard waiting with its chin against a particular groove. One amphibian’s hard note rising from wet grit and starting no answer. The brief life of a moth whose wings would presently become weather, then food, then dust. Their usefulness to others was true. It was not the whole truth.

When the power’s visible presence withdrew, if it had ever had one, there was nothing to mark the visit. No stone had been arranged. No channel had been cut. The three basins held only what the last rain had given them. The pebbles dropped by the birds remained pebbles.

One bat lived who might not have lived. Some final sufferings had not been prolonged for the sake of leaving nature untouched. Otherwise, the cleft remained exposed to its own precarious future.

A drop fell near the middle basin. For an instant some of the insects stopped. Then cricket rasp, moth-wing hiss, and the small disputes of the ceiling closed over the gap. At the rear, the moths rippled toward a warmth too slow for any one of them to notice. The mountain began drawing cold air inward, and the crowded life of the chamber withdrew by a few body lengths.

The power had not made the cleft safe.

Neither had it abandoned the cleft in order to admire its own restraint.

It had loved the place without requiring it to become an emblem of love, and had helped without needing the help to be visible.

And the slow breathing went on.
