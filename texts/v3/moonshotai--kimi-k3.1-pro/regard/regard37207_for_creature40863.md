### What the Power Did

The power came without light. Light would have warmed the films at the mouths of the seams and woken small lives to no purpose. It came without a voice. A voice would have entered the wall and returned altered, and for years afterward the power might have loved its own echo.

It came only as attention.

At first, even attention was too blunt. The power could hear the whole hollow at once: rain from the previous night ticking in the moss, an older shower brightening a snail’s trail, seasonal water stirring the deep fissure. But hearing everything simultaneously was not yet hearing well. So it remained while a spoon-leaf filled and emptied, while a snail sealed itself against stone, while one of the small ticking voices failed and another began beneath the wall.

It could have made all nine seams flow together. It could have filled the spoon-leaves and let them tip in a single instant. For a little while the hollow would have produced a chord of extraordinary beauty.

The power felt the desire to do this and recognized it as appetite.

Nothing living there needed the chord. The desire had arisen entirely in the listener. So the power let it pass, and afterward understood the place better.

It did not, however, mistake scarcity for innocence or slowness for peace. It did not decide that every hunger was good because it belonged to an old equilibrium, or that every injury became beautiful when seen from sufficient height. The transparent animal beneath the basin shelf was not the mountain’s thought, not an emblem of patience, not a living ornament. It was a particular animal. It could be crushed. It could starve. Its life was not made more its own by being called natural.

Thus the power ceased asking, *How can I preserve this?* and asked instead, *What would be good here, now?*

It did not pour richness into the basin. A sudden abundance would have clouded the water, altered the films, and set lives growing beyond the small, intermittent supplies on which their neighbors depended. It did not make the animal deathless merely because death seemed, to the power, a great price for so small a body. Nor did it give the animal knowledge of the nine voices, or of itself, or require it to become conscious of being loved.

Its world beneath the shelf was not an incomplete version of the power’s world.

For a long while, therefore, it did nothing.

Then, once, help was simple.

A thin scale of old mineral skin loosened within the drainage. It was too small to matter to the basin. Its settling would not have altered any seam for more than a few days. But below it, in the narrow place where the current made a firm edge, the animal was resting.

The power did not stop the mountain. It did not mend the stone, make the slot eternally safe, or decide that no further harm should ever come there. It held one small falling thing still for less than a breath.

The animal felt the brief wrongness in the pressure of the water and moved beneath the shelf.

Then the power let the scale go.

It settled. The current worried a new way around it. For several days the deep liquid knock came slightly later than before. Eventually the animal returned to its accustomed place.

That was the whole of the power’s visible work. Kindness did not have to leave no trace. It had to be proportionate, and it had to leave no debt.

To the hollow itself the power gave no exemption from time. It understood how easily love of an arrangement could become tyranny over everything changing within it. Roots would narrow passages. Trees would fall. A leaf’s soft drum would be replaced by a brighter note on stone. Even the deepest voice might one day fail. The power would not force the mountain to keep repeating what had once pleased it.

Nor did it leave behind a guardian figure on the rim, a prohibition in the rock, or a radiance visible only to the worthy. It left no path and took no water. It did not name the hollow after itself.

When it withdrew, a drop was gathering on the underside of a leaf. The drop fell. Somewhere below the basin, old rain continued toward the animal’s small, sensitive body. There was no tenth voice, no sign, and nothing that required gratitude.

The power carried away no image of the place to keep unchanged. It carried instead the distinction it had learned: between leaving something free and leaving it alone.

And it remembered the animal.
