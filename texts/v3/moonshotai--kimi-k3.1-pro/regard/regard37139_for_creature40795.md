### What the Power Did

The power stopped below the rim and let the wind go on without it.

Even an arrival heralded by thunder, or by a sudden miraculous calm, would have passed through every shell. For a moment the basin would have seemed to answer. The power did not require an answer.

From above, it saw what a hasty visitor might see: a little city open to the cold, its streets empty, its walls broken, its inhabitants almost nothing. Because it was benevolent, it thought of help. Because it was wise, it waited to discover whether help and alteration were the same thing.

It could have brought rain. It could have made the black basins brim and the moss spread until the courts were soft and green. It could have repaired the fallen vaults, opened the sealed lanes, filled the galleries with birds. It could have awakened the builder sufficiently to know that it had built something, and to hear what the weather made of its body.

Instead, the power listened at the speed of the place.

Cloud came against the basin. Water gathered on the fine ribs, entered the folds, and darkened the olive film. The green zones deepened one after another. A drop fell from an upper gallery with the report of a small stone. The snail’s faint trail began, faded, and began again. Then the cloud lifted. Exposed surfaces dried; color withdrew beneath the overhangs. At night the accessible water froze, and cracks traveled sharply through the arcades.

Before dawn there came a silence so complete that the power could feel in itself the impulse to relieve it.

It did not.

Then it listened longer than music usually requires.

Paper-thin bands accumulated in bitter years. A wetter age left its swollen, green-stained record. An opening that had whistled violently narrowed, and its pitch descended. Somewhere a lane closed so gradually that there was no particular hour in which it ceased to be a lane. An old vault collapsed. The power heard its note vanish and, for decades afterward, heard the absence of it in certain weather.

It had been prepared to mourn that note. It did not. Nor did it praise the new throat when one opened in a younger wall. The chord changed. Neither chord had been the being.

Only then did the power understand what its attention had found: not a singer preserving a song, not a builder approaching some final city, not a ruin awaiting restoration, but a thin living margin continuing around the body of its own past. To keep every shell standing would have been to preserve a specimen and lose the process. To rescue the colony from dryness, freezing, damage, and pause would have been to rescue it out of the conditions by which it had become itself.

So the power did nothing to the basin.

This was not indifference. Indifference would have been easier and would have taken much less time.

Its one act was to place a limit on its own benevolence. It would not love only the forms it happened to find beautiful. It would not defend a gallery against the colony’s future, or a pleasant harmony against an unpleasant season, or the present green against the dry years that also belonged to the place. If help ever became necessary, it would not mean making the Old Choir resemble the moment in which the power had first cherished it.

The power left no spring, no guardian radiance, no inscription warning travelers that they stood in a sacred city. There was no city, and sacredness announced too loudly can become another kind of weather. It left only its attention beyond the rim: patient enough to distinguish a place changing from a place being destroyed, and restrained enough not to confuse the two merely because change caused sorrow.

When it withdrew, it did so without making a gust.

A cloud shadow crossed the basin. For a little while every shell lay within the same dimness. Beneath a stone lip, where almost none of the architecture was visible, the living film darkened with a trace of water. The spider waited beside its pool. The oldest walls held no preference about what happened next.

Then a westerly came over the rim. The great vaults began their low pressure, a lane whistled, and one shell blared above all the others. Two notes beat together unpleasantly. Nothing corrected them.

The mountain went on settling toward silence. The Old Choir did not.

The power had done nothing to it. It had, however, ceased to be the sort of power that would call that nothing.
