## The Allowance

The power did not arrive as light. In the Ninth Cold Basin, light was already a form of land use.

Its attention rested first on the oldest growth it could find: a patch of lamp moss no larger than a hand, layered behind a valve support where successive crawlers had made the same slightly imperfect turn. The patch was brittle with the Draw. Metallic dust stained its filaments. Beneath it, the ghost of an assembly mark remained legible.

It was only a few years old.

The power did not find this disappointing.

It remained through the Weep and saw water darken the patch from its edges inward. During the Flush, it followed the fragments: some into ceramic slurry at the catch screens, some into the wet grooves of a crawler’s wheels, one onto a raft of ice that grounded briefly against an expansion joint and then broke apart. It watched glassseed vanish into open water and reappear, days later, in a sump whose plans showed no connection to the place from which they had come. It watched a crew find them there and correctly report a change in hydrology.

By the next Draw, the power understood that saving the inhabitants one by one would not be enough. Their lives were made from transport, interruption, and return. Even their catastrophes had uses.

The obvious mercies were therefore set aside.

To stop the Flush would protect glassseed from the screens while closing their longest roads. To destroy the crawlers would preserve existing films and remove both a grazer and a means of dispersal. To illuminate every dark reach would not produce a moss-covered valley; it would produce a more complete inventory of growth requiring removal. To make the basin warm, clean, quiet, and stable would have been to make it merciful according to the habits of somewhere else.

The power did not make a garden.

But doing nothing would not have been neutral either. The basin was young. Its brushes would improve. Its routes would be optimized. Its unmapped branches would be surveyed, its recoveries made more complete, its new floors built with fewer omissions. No one would need to decide against the inhabitants. One by one, the tolerances in which they lived could simply be closed.

So the power reserved an allowance.

Not a refuge. Not a hidden pan stocked with specimens. Not a fourth operating phase inserted between Flush and Draw.

It placed a limit on completeness.

Within the safe tolerances of the machine, the processes that emptied the basin could no longer be relied upon to become total everywhere at once. A Draw might still strip a pan to dry plate while one sheltered branch remained wet. A revised crawler route might expose an old corner and neglect a new one. A catch screen might receive nearly everything, yet not quite all the viable material would dry there; an insignificant wet fraction would find its way into a recovery seam before the final cleaning. At the construction face, an unimportant service void might escape the last survey long enough for quench water to stand in it.

None of these things happened every time. None was tied to a particular place. No pump was made unreliable, no pressure boundary weakened, no crew endangered. The allowance came out of optimization, never out of safety.

The power was not preserving addresses.

A film could still grow old and be stripped in a single pass. Moss could still bleach, powder, and vanish down a screen. Glassseed could still arrive in a sterile pan, persist briefly, and disappear. Nothing was exempted from the basin in which it lived. The allowance only ensured that after each emptying, the possibility of return did not depend on the same accident recurring in the same way forever.

Years later, a new crawler model took the hand-sized patch behind the valve support. The power did not restore it.

By then, a fragment no larger than a fingernail pairing had established beneath a sodium lamp three pans away. It had traveled in the wet housing of a machine whose route had since been revised twice. During the Draw it whitened. During the Weep it darkened. Dust began to collect among its filaments.

The crews experienced none of this as providence. Their logs contained only exceptions, most of them minor. Some were corrected. Others were left because they were useful, or harmless, or because a shift ended before anyone reached them.

The power did not make the crews gentler. It made gentleness compatible with good maintenance.

An assay margin could be retained without reducing recovery. A harmless wet corner could be left without concealing a leak. A service lamp could be replaced without sterilizing every surface beneath it. Once care did not require anyone to become careless, care occurred more often.

Not always. Not by everyone. The power left that freedom intact.

Then it withdrew most of its attention. It left no shrine, no final community, and no promise that the basin would remain as it was. At the eastern face, stairs still ended in open air. Red lamps still marked grades and foundations that did not yet exist. Fresh ground ticked as it cooled.

Nothing had been made natural. Nothing had been made safe. The ecology remained thin enough to overlook.

But it no longer depended entirely on the machine remaining badly made.

From the central gantry, the pans still looked empty. Beneath the distant sodium lamp, a gray-green stain darkened at its edges as the Weep began.

And on a crawler route displayed in a pump-house terminal, beside a wet corner that had survived three inspections without showing any sign of a leak, someone added an instruction no specification required:

**Leave it. See what arrives.**
