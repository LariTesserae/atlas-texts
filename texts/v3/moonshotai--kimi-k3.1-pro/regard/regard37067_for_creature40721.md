### What the Power Took Away

The power came without light.

Light would not have harmed the hollow. It would have been another permitted event, like a ripple crossing the pool. But light would have made the chamber too easy to misread: a column interrupted, a leaf unopened, a cup unfilled. So the power let warmth, nearness, touch, and the occasional small sound tell it where it was.

For a while, it felt what human visitors had felt. The place seemed crowded with appeals.

The power could have closed the finger-width gaps. It could have opened every folded scale, deepened the pool, filled the bowl until water ran shining over its rim. It could have made the water-louse the first of a visible lineage, with young and cast skins and bones enough to establish that time had passed. It could have done all this between one drop and the next.

Each act would have been easy. Each would have looked, from outside, like kindness.

But the power did not ask what the hollow might become. That question was too inviting; power can always answer it with some enlarged version of itself. Instead it asked whether anything there was suffering the terms of its own existence.

It found no appeal that it had not brought with it.

There was no strain in the mineral ribs, no pain in the gap between them. The sheathplants were not enduring closure. Their pallor was not a misfortune for which greenness would be the cure. Even the bowl, that most persuasive of human objects, was only holding water. It did not need to become evidence that water could accumulate.

The human things were harder to release. A mark can be the beginning of a message; a tool can imply a hand that meant to return. But their makers were absent, and the power would not invent a petition on their behalf merely for the pleasure of granting it.

At the pool’s edge, the little animal rested with its antennae against a root. After a long while, it moved a few millimeters and settled again.

The power felt in itself the impulse to call this an unfinished journey.

It let the impulse pass.

Nothing in the animal had abandoned a destination. Nothing had failed to arrive. The movement had been entire while it occurred, and the stillness was not a lesser state into which the creature had fallen. The power did not give it sight, companions, offspring, or a history. It did not carry it away to some richer water and call the removal rescue.

This did not mean that stillness was holy, or that growth was vulgar, or that every closed thing everywhere should be left closed. Those would only have been new doctrines imposed on the hollow. Outside, an opening leaf was good. A young animal was good. A bowl filled and carried to a thirsty mouth was good. Where hunger, injury, loneliness, or coercion called for help, help might need to be swift and transforming.

The lesson here was narrower and more difficult: those goods could not be bestowed simply by making the hollow resemble the places where they belonged.

Then the power ceased, even in thought, to call the chamber unfinished.

Nothing physical changed. The gaps remained exactly as wide. The folded scales kept their moist surfaces near one another. The pool neither rose nor fell. Yet the chamber was no longer a collection of delayed events. It was not a spring denied a stream, a cave denied completion, or a garden denied its future. It was a place whose processes met one another so precisely that none had to become a monument.

The power stayed for a while without turning its stay into an era.

When a drop fell, it did not think, *At last*.

When the round note vanished, it did not think, *Again*.

It heard the silence without requiring the silence to mean that something had been withheld.

That was its work in the hollow: not an act performed upon the place, but the gradual withdrawal of every act it had imagined performing. It remained attentive without becoming hungry for a result. It did not confuse its own relief with another being’s good.

Then it went.

It took no water, no root, no translucent body, no flake of stone. It left no blessing that would have been a small command to continue differently. It made no promise to return, because the hollow had not asked to be remembered, and remembrance can become another form of possession.

It carried away only the future it had imagined for the place—the sole thing there that had truly belonged to it.

Afterward, the power did not become passive. It still opened what cruelty had closed. It still fed what hunger was emptying. It still completed what pain had interrupted. But its wisdom had gained one small, exact distinction: a wound was not the same as a fold, a silenced voice was not the same as silence, and a life without sequel was not necessarily a life denied.

In the hollow, no fifth groove recorded the visit. No seam brightened. The louse shifted an antenna almost imperceptibly. A drop fell, and afterward the silence was identical to the silence before.

Nothing had been improved. Nothing had been abandoned.

The power had not given the hollow an ending. It had stopped requiring one.
