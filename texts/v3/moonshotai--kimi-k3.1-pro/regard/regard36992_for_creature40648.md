### What the Power Did

The power’s first mercy was to look without deciding beforehand what mercy should look like.

It did not mistake stillness for consent. It searched the cool flanks, the open eyes, the colonies beneath the armor, and the attention of the makers for pain, coercion, or any wish held helplessly in place. It listened for an appeal too quiet to resemble sound.

Had it found even one life imprisoned in its final shape, it would have acted without regret. It would have accepted weather, damage, even loss, if loss were the price of release. It would have let rain scar the white pans and time enter through the scars.

But nothing here was being endured.

The raised hoof was not cramped. The hunter’s teeth were not an agony of appetite. The browser was not starving with food at its lips. The maker was not aching to strike. Suspension belonged to the visitor’s grammar, not to theirs.

So the power did not make the wind rise.

It did not lead rivers up the ancient slopes, though water was beautiful to it and it knew ten thousand generous ways for water to arrive. It did not spare the prey, because no death was approaching. It did not permit the hunter to strike, because mercy toward one life could not justify making an ending of another. It did not bring the hammerstone down upon the obsidian core.

The point was already entire.

Nor did the power pronounce the plateau perfect and turn away. Perfection, too, can become a way of not looking.

It attended without counting. It did not gather the interleaved herds into a single visibility and call that flattening understanding. It did not make the multitude one being merely because one being would have been easier to love. The scar on a flank did not become evidence for a history; the worn hoof did not become a clue. They remained particular. The power let them remain so.

It knew the hunter neither as a threat removed nor as violence deferred. It knew the prey neither as a victim nor as a creature miraculously forgiven its vulnerability. They were two lives in a relation that no longer needed to prove itself by an event. The same courtesy extended to the scale and the shrub, the maker and the stone, the almost weightless life beneath a lifted plate.

This attention added nothing to the plateau. The power did not mistake that for failure. It did not require its love to be received as a gift, still less to be answered. It did not need the multitude to need it.

For the breathing visitor, however, its kindness took another form.

A visitor still required sequence. A throat could dry. Legs could shake. The wish to go home could remain real even when home would not stay reliably behind. The power did not treat these things as spiritual failures. It did not require the traveler to admire the multitude correctly, or to become still enough to deserve stillness.

If water was needed, there was water. If the traveler wished to leave, leaving was not made into another riddle. If they wished to remain, their warmth and hunger were not treated as trespass. No new cairn appeared upon the pan; no reassuring inscription was cut into the rim. These mercies belonged to the traveler’s road, not to the completed country.

Elsewhere, the power remained willing to be less subtle.

It put bread into hungry hands. It stopped descending blades. It opened doors before those knocking had finished being afraid. It sat beside the dying without telling them that stillness would make them entire. It never used the plateau as an argument against grief, or as an excuse to admire what should have been repaired.

On the high country, no mark remained.

The mineral plates rang only beneath the feet that were already part of their ground. The fixed shadows held. The maker’s fingers lay around the finished point, and the unnecessary blow connected nothing. Somewhere among the interwoven horns, an eye acknowledged what it acknowledged.

Nothing had been improved.

Nothing had been abandoned.
