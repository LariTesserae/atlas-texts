### The Returning

The power did not heal the High Drowning.

That was its first mercy.

It did not lift the water from the mountains, give the ribbon trees a sky, or return the rain to the other side of the air. It did not straighten distance. A tree whose crown leaned over a basin no open-water path joined to its roots was not asking to be made simpler. It did not gather the broad beings into assemblies, preserve their signs against wear, or make their deaths occur at identifiable moments.

Much that was slow was not suffering. Much that was unfinished was not loss.

Nor did the power mistake its attention for knowledge of what was owed. It remained until attention ceased to be a search for something to fix.

The being it had noticed was then living against an old wall. On one return it cleared a shallow groove above the silt. On another it left the groove alone and turned a root across the stone below it. Later it abandoned both and rested beside a hollow that offered no food. Still later it came back and undid part of what it had done before.

An impatient kindness might have completed the groove, protected the root, or given the being a voice with which to explain itself. The power did none of these things. There was no emergency concealed in the returns, and it would not invent one merely so that help could have an object.

Neither did it turn away on the grounds that nothing was wrong. A world need not be broken before company can be offered. But the offer had to be capable of refusal.

So, when at last the power acted, it made something that could be ignored.

Beside the being’s work, not within it, it pressed a shallow trace into wet clay. It gave the trace no permanence and no signature. Rain could soften it. A root could cross it. A reader could wear it away in the act of reading. The power did not make it beautiful, did not ensure that the being would find it, and did nothing to prevent its being found by another.

Then it let the place alone.

The being returned. It touched the old groove, investigated the root, and left the new trace untouched. The power did not renew it. Rain took a little of its edge. To keep offering what had not been accepted would only have been a slower form of insistence.

Much later, on another return, the being touched the trace.

The touch made it shallower.

Nothing else happened. The being remained against the wall for a while, then went away. The power did not follow, did not place an answering warmth beneath its garden, and did not repeat the sign along the being’s route. It waited.

When the being next came back, it cleared moss from a stone nearby.

Perhaps that was an answer. Perhaps it was only the next thing the being wished to do. The power did not require certainty before continuing, but neither did it pretend uncertainty was knowledge. On its own next return, it altered only the trace it had made. It left the being’s work free.

That was how it entered their society: not as an ever-present witness, but as a continuity of alterations that could be continued, contradicted, misunderstood, or allowed to end.

No language resulted. There were long intervals in which nothing happened, and these were not failures of communication. Sometimes the being changed the power’s mark almost beyond recognition. Sometimes the power found no alteration it could make without presuming too much, and so made none. Once it renewed a trace too faithfully, restoring what rain and touch had changed; the being ceased visiting that curve of stone. The power did not restore the curve as well.

It learned that preserving a sign could prevent it from saying anything further. That continuing another’s work might be agreement or trespass. That an answer fitted too exactly to another’s intentions left no room for the other to have changed its mind.

A wisdom that could not be corrected by so small a being would have been only force with better manners.

The power never announced what it was. It was not worshipped. It may never have been distinguished from another broad being, from the memory of one, or from a habit the stone had acquired. It did not correct this. It had not come to be identified.

When the being died, the power did not make the event cleaner.

The garden flourished after the body beneath it had ceased to respond. Roots remained in the outer tissues. For a time the low shape still held its margin against the stone. Then it subsided, gradually, into unusually rich silt. Seedlings prospered where it had been.

The power did not preserve the body, separate the garden from it, or select an instant at which grief would have been appropriate. Neither did it complete the abandoned works in the name of fidelity. Some were buried. One was altered by roots. The groove remained open for a while, collecting pale grains, and then ceased to be a groove without ever having become anything else.

Much later, another broad being came to the wall.

It may have been a lobe that had separated before the death, carrying an older garden and some habits of return. It may have been a stranger drawn by the clearing. The power did not settle the question. Identity here had always been connection, then resemblance, then return; to insist upon one continuity could break another.

The newcomer touched the faint scar where the last trace had been. Its reading nearly erased it. Then it went away.

The power did nothing.

On a later return, the newcomer turned a root beside the scar and pressed a small chemical trace of its own into the clay. Only then did the power answer, once, with an alteration slight enough to be overlooked and shallow enough to be undone.

Not because it had identified the dead in the living. Not because every continuity must be maintained. Only because an answer had again become possible without becoming necessary.

That was all it changed.

Rain still descended through the forests in silver columns. Shells filled slowly with silt. Distant crowns leaned over basins to which no dependable path led. Nothing had been rescued from time. No observer could have pointed to the marks beside the wall and proved that the world had been blessed.

The power had taken care that this should be so.

It had not come to make the being eternal, intelligible, or complete. It had come near enough to be corrected, and had left every answer free to be the last.
