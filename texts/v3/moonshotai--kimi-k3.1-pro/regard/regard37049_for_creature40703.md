### A Gift Without a Recipient

The power’s first impulse was to repair the High Remainder.

It did not trust the impulse.

It had arrived with its grief already prepared. The cold seemed severe, the distances broken, the towns abandoned. Life appeared to wait for centuries without assurance of waking. Even the builders had vanished without leaving an account of themselves. Much of what the power saw resembled loss, and nearly everything could have been made, by some sufficient exercise of will, easier to preserve.

It could have made every journey as long returning as going. It could have joined the separated portions of each recurrence with roots of light, given the residents voices, filled the tarns, restored the roofs, and called the builders back to explain their work. It could have made the plateau one country and its inhabitants one people.

Each mercy became less convincing as it was imagined.

Nothing there could refuse help. That made help more dangerous, not more innocent. Cold was not necessarily misery. Silence was not loneliness. Persistence was not a request for permanence, and the absence of builders was not a petition that someone begin building again. Most of the repairs would have made the Remainder easier to love from outside.

So the power stayed without acting.

In a nearly bare town, it watched rain strike a roof in a quarter where nothing grew. Water appeared in a cistern beneath crooked trees. The cistern filled, found its old overflow, and darkened a line of joints. A fern beside a roofed stair unfolded. Later, as the upper masonry began to dry, moss released spores into a conduit whose lower end had not yet filled.

The power had been about to call these several lives responding to one another. Then it saw younger growth continue the sequence after older wood had failed, and it was about to call them one life wearing successive bodies. Both descriptions seemed almost adequate. It waited until the sequence itself became more interesting than its categories.

It witnessed an ending, too.

Elsewhere, frost and roots opened enough breaches in a reservoir wall. Water still came. Ferns spread; moss deepened; for several wet years the district became greener than before. But rain no longer produced the old order of consequences. A film thickened where a cistern had once ticked. Separate colonies flourished where a resident had repeatedly returned.

The power did not say that nothing had been lost merely because more was alive. Neither did it say that everything had suffered, or that its own sorrow authorized restoration. It remembered what had ended without turning that memory into a plan for making the ending reversible.

By then, most of its intended interventions had become impossible to justify.

It would not wake the plateau. It would not make the residents agree about their boundaries. It would not preserve every intact wall, nor choose which patterns deserved to continue and then call that choice gardening. It would not send a voice into the silence merely because it knew how to speak.

For the resident in the bare town, it did nothing at all. It allowed the next rain to arrive without its signature. It did not ask to be included in the sequence.

Still, it wished to leave a gift.

It distrusted this wish also. But after a while it understood that not every gift needs to correct a deficiency. Some can be made without first deciding that the recipient is incomplete.

So, on a swell of bare stone, the power built a small court.

It chose rock where no green threads darkened within the quartz when wetted. It took no soil from a sheltered basin, no slate from an existing roof, and no stone whose removal would breach a wall. Its instruments were a cord, a weighted line, and patience. The materials were those already belonging to the Remainder.

The court had one low roof, one gutter, one covered cistern, and an overflow cut toward a strip of jointed pavement. The walls were carefully founded. The joints were close but not sealed against roots. The roof would shed rain before the court received it directly; the cistern would hold a little of what the gutter gathered; the overflow, if there was any, would always find the same stones while the masonry endured.

Nothing was made invulnerable. Frost could still enter. Slate could slip. A channel could be closed by growth. The surrounding country could become unreachable, or approach from a direction that had not previously existed. Only within the little structure had a few relations been persuaded to repeat.

The power planted nothing.

It brought no fern to the overflow, no moss to the roof, no sediment to begin a garden. It did not even leave water in the cistern. It made no road to the court and cut no name into its stone. It was not a temple, not a monument, and not an attempt to continue the builders’ unknown intentions.

It was simply a place where, if rain came, certain consequences could occur in the same order more than once.

A history could accumulate there. Nothing assured that one would.

The court might remain bare until its walls became indistinguishable from outcrops. Moss might close the gutter before any roots found the pavement. A wild recurrence might include it for one wet season and never again. Or growth might arrive by degrees, altering the water slightly, retaining a little dust, delaying one overflow, until responses began to survive the leaves and films in which they had first appeared.

The power did not stay to discover which possibility would be realized. A gift that must be watched becoming useful has not entirely been given.

Long afterward, rain crossed the bare swell.

It darkened the slate. The gutter filled in sections. Water clicked into the empty cistern, and a little mineral dust lifted, turned, and settled on the bottom. The level rose. One drop crossed the overflow, then another. For part of an afternoon, the pavement beyond it remained wet.

No fern opened. None grew there. No moss released spores, and no response followed that the court had not already contained in stone.

Then the rain stopped. The water sank away. Ice formed along the shaded lip.

Nothing had been rescued. Nothing had been awakened. The High Remainder was no easier to cross, no greener, and no more obliged to explain itself.

There was only one additional place where a history could begin.

Nothing was obliged to begin it.
