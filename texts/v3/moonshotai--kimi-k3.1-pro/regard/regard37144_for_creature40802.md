### The Hearing

The power did not finish the Height.

It could see how easily completion might masquerade as kindness. A rib closed here, a wall raised there; then weather to bring the terraces toward season, darkness to give the daylight an edge, soil to receive whatever began to fall. Each gift would require another, until the giver had become the author of everything and could call its own authorship inevitable.

But the pale growth-fronts were not wounds. The arrested foot was not a step cruelly interrupted. There was no imprisoned maturity pressing against the green skin. The power found no pain to relieve, no hunger to answer, no longing concealed beneath the loudness.

Had it found these things, it would have acted. Wisdom was not a prohibition against touch. A wish, even one without words, would have mattered. Suffering would have mattered even if it could never have been remembered afterward. The power did not make the opposite mistake of deciding that a being without autobiography was therefore a thing, or that an unremembered moment could not be good or bad while it was present.

It simply refused to invent a need in order to enjoy meeting it.

To give the listener a past might have been to give it affection, recognition, the inward warmth of return. It would also have been to create someone who could lose. To give it expectation might have opened the note beneath the vault into music, but it would also have made disappointment possible. None of these gifts would have been evil. That was precisely why they required such care: each could be defended so beautifully by the one who would never have to live inside its consequences.

Nor did the power declare the Height perfect.

Perfection would have been another imposition, a way of making the place responsible for remaining admirable. If change ever arose from the Height itself, the power would not call it corruption. If an arch somehow continued, it would not mourn the loss of an emblem. It found no reason to alter the basin, but it did not turn its restraint into a law that the basin must forever justify.

So it came, without weight or shadow, to the half-closed vault.

The listener stood beneath the exposed ribs. Water passed through its clear chamber. Its hood trembled at a high leaf-tone, and then its whole body held the deep vibration of the outer buttresses. The faint note it produced entered the valley and was not answered.

At first the power listened as one listens to a plea, and found no plea.

Then it listened as one listens to music, and found that this too could become a demand: that the note should develop, depart from itself, approach an ending. So it ceased listening for what the sound might become.

It heard the note.

It heard the slight contraction of the chamber, the water sliding from the creature’s feet, the resonance returning from distances the listener could never cross in a single journey. It heard that the sound was neither meaningful nor meaningless in the way it had expected. It did not have to be a message in order to be received. It did not have to be the beginning of a conversation in order to justify attention.

The power did not answer merely to discover what would happen next.

Neither did it make a solemn virtue of silence. It simply remained without turning its presence into an event the listener would be required to survive. It did not open the sealed lenses. It did not put recollection behind the shallow seam of the mouth. It did not cause the raised foot, in some other valley, to come down.

For that while, the listener was accompanied without being given the burden of company.

After what would elsewhere have been a long while, the power withdrew. It carried away a memory, but kept the memory in itself. It did not leave part of that memory behind as a seed. The listener did not have to become someone in order to have been attended to. The Height did not have to acquire a history in order to have been known.

The power made no vow to return. It made no plan for the valleys. Remembering did not become ownership, and tenderness did not become a design.

Nothing in the basin changed. Water fell without deepening the pools. The cisterns held their unvarying lines. The unfinished walls remained tender at their rims, and the enormous sound continued without approaching release.

Yet the power had not quite done nothing.

It had allowed the place to be other than a failed version of somewhere else. It had refused both cruelties: the cruelty of forcing a future upon it, and the cruelty of cherishing it only because it could never have one.

The loud water went on. The exposed ribs did not close. The listener’s note passed into the greater resonance, and nothing waited for another.

Nothing was completed. Nothing was disappointed.

And for a little while the Height had been heard without being mistaken for a cry.
