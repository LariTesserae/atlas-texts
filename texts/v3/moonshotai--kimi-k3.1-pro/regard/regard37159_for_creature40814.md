The power remained for one cycle, and then for another.

There was no prayer to answer. It did not lend the moss a mind in order to make the moss worth helping. It watched the patches whiten in the dry cold, take water, and darken toward green. It saw a fragment enter the sump. It saw a living edge disappear beneath a bead of translucent binder while, elsewhere, the carriage faithfully repeated a deviation left by growth long dead.

Nothing in this required blame. The pumps were not cruel. The carriage was not careless. Even the chamber’s indifference was not a failure of character. It was the indifference of things that had no character, and it had produced, along with erasure, the damp hollows and small repetitions by which anything survived there at all.

The power considered leaving the Underprint unchanged.

To warm it, wet it, and give it soil would have been easy. So would stopping the carriage, sealing the sump, or carrying the moss away to some reliable light. Each would have helped by deciding what the place ought to become. The chamber would have been cured of being the Underprint, or the moss would have been saved by ceasing to be the life that had persisted there.

Nor did the power feel required to preserve every patch. The moss did not suffer in any way that needed to be made human first. A patch could decline without knowing loss. The chamber could continue without it, laying down a memory whose living cause had vanished. Attention did not automatically create a duty to prevent that ending.

But where conditions allowed, the moss grew.

That was not a request. It was enough of a direction.

So the power did almost nothing.

It left the cold structural and the rain brief. It did not brighten the lamps, enrich the gel, close the filter, or make the sweating seams dependable. It did not command the moss to flourish. The Underprint would not become a garden, and the moss would not become safe.

Before deposition, however, the survey acquired one additional distinction.

The carriage could now distinguish, without understanding, between growth and the trace of growth. It was not given the concept of life. It was given a clearance: where living growth interrupted the surface, the next bead would stop at its edge. It would leave uncovered only what was already alive, with no new margin and no favor shown to one patch over another. Dead gel could be covered. Silt could be covered. The old deviations in the floor could go on being repeated, revised, or buried as before.

The next pass gave no sign that anything had changed.

The carriage moved with its restrained electric murmur. A ridge received another pale line. A channel remained open. In a basin, a fan of silt was partly preserved beneath binder. Approaching the patch under the amber lamp, the bead ended at the living edge and resumed beyond it.

The interruption was narrower than a service scratch. From the inspection aperture, the deposited line would have appeared continuous.

Under the lamp, the moss remained exposed.

No patch had been made immortal. A seam could still dry. A lamp could still fail. A fragment could still be carried into filtration, and a slow decline could still resemble permanence until almost nothing was left. The carriage would continue to sustain and endanger the moss through water, silt, and the altered ground. It had not become gentle. It had not become a gardener. Its indifference had been given one boundary.

Life still depended on a chain of small omissions. The power had not made the chain unbreakable. It had made one link reliable.

The nozzle no longer had to pass slightly to one side by chance.

The power stayed through the rain that followed. Frost loosened overhead. Drops struck ceramic, rang once in the sump, and gathered on the uncovered leaves, one silver point at a time. A swollen edge briefly interrupted a film of water. The moss became fractionally more green.

Then the sump valve opened. The water vanished. The cold returned, and the carriage waited with its arms folded, ready to survey the ground again.

It would not know the difference.

It would make it anyway.
