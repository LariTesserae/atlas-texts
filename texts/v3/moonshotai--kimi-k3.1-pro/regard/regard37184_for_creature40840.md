## What the Power Did

The power came without arriving.

An entrance would have displaced water. A voice loud enough to be heard would have become another kind of roar. So there was no new brightness in the vapor, no hush among the basins, no dry footprint closing slowly behind a visitor. The power made itself less consequential than rain.

At first, it thought of mercy as alteration.

It could have raised one of the buried ranges: not a continent of mountains, only a single shoulder of dry ground. It could have cleared a small disk of sky and let the underhide, for the first time, cast a shadow. It could have taught water to seek depth again, given the films banks, the banks roots, the roots time enough to become soil.

But that dry shoulder would have been a gift to the power’s idea of life. Nothing living beneath it had asked for a forest. To make the Flat safe for succession would have been to decide, in advance, which distinctions deserved to survive.

It could have taken the underhide away.

In a gentler place, however, the animal would have required another body, other food, another way of meeting the ground. Its softness, which here allowed pressure to pass through it, might elsewhere have become helplessness. The rescue would not have been complete until the rescued creature had ceased to be the creature that supposedly needed rescuing.

So the power did not reach down.

Neither did it make the opposite mistake. It did not call the roar music merely because it could endure it, or name every dispersal innocent because nothing dispersed had left a written objection. Wisdom was not permitted to become a taste for desolation.

Had it found pain that could be eased, it would not have called pain scenery. Had it found a mind longing for another world, it would not have answered with a lecture on belonging. It would not have purchased the beauty of its own restraint with anyone’s suffering.

But neither would it invent suffering to give its mercy somewhere to stand.

It attended to the underhide beneath a province of silt. The animal narrowed around a buried obstruction and widened again. A pale seam closed behind it. The power could have known immediately whether this was one creature, many creatures, or a condition through which several kinds of life passed. It did not begin with the count. What was owed would not depend on the count.

The underhide was not secretly a person. It was not the last member of a people. It was not lonely simply because the power could imagine two of them. It contained no imprisoned voice waiting for the right listener.

It was feeding.

The power stayed through a resumption.

A brown mirror arched. Films lifted and divided. Seamwort pulled colorlessly between its partial locations. A seepwick disappeared, leaving for a moment its little envelope of clearer air. The power did not stop the mirror, nor preserve one selected dome in everlasting precision, nor gather every living cell into a vessel where nothing could ever be lost again. A power could become the Flat’s weather and call the weather love.

Instead, it kept its strength from becoming weather, and attended.

It refused the height from which every collapse paid for every dome and all that violence averaged into perfect levelness. In its attention, no whole was allowed to cancel a part. A brief country of ribs and cavities did not become insignificant because its material would be used again. A black cup’s long submergence did not become merely an interval between more interesting conditions. The olive sheen was not scenery around the large animal. The large animal was not the meaning of the sheen.

Nothing was made to stand for everything else.

And when forms passed, the power did not keep them in existence against their passing. Its remembrance was not an archive from which the Flat could be reconstructed, improved, and set safely behind glass. It made no specimen of the world. It simply refused the proposition that, because a thing had ended without altering the shape of the whole, it might as well never have occurred.

Nor did it make the Flat useful.

It did not turn the place into a warning about mountains, a parable about acceptance, or proof that life always finds a way. Life was not finding a way for anyone’s edification. The underhide was not a lesson in humility. The black cups did not command endurance. Even the made stones were spared interpretation. The four steps descended into water and emerged on the same level. The power did not correct them.

Perhaps its attention lasted an instant. Perhaps it outlasted several ages of the underhide. Duration, from where the power stood, would have been another temptation to measure the wrong thing.

When there was no longer an arrival to speak of, or a departure, no eighth block stood beside the seven. No inscription explained the narrow doorway. No warmed corner had been made into the beginning of a garden. Nothing had been left behind that could later become a demand.

A temporary lake reflected a sky the wrong color. The power did not correct that either.

Far below the silt, something wide altered its adherence and passed without sound. On the made stones, the cups filled with water and waited. Along a seam that was not quite one place, seamwort deepened toward red.

Nothing there had been saved.

Nothing there had been used.

The power had done nothing to the Flat.

It had not done nothing.
