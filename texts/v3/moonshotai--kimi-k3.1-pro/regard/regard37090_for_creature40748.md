The power would not give the Upper Works a dawn.

Nor dusk, nor birds, nor an heir. It would not send one freight carriage astray to see whether the city could discover surprise. It would not still a turbine in order to learn whether the silence had been missed.

Because it was benevolent, it would look first for pain. But it would be careful about where it looked. It would not mistake cold for cruelty, repetition for grief, or the absence of voices for a voice suppressed. It would not hear suffering in the roar merely because another being might suffer there.

It would listen for the difference between continuation and endurance.

The pumps were not tired. The turbines were not patient. The green bodies in the mires were not resigned to their boundaries. Nothing was sacrificing itself to keep the whole in operation, because nothing possessed a private life that had been offered up. There was pressure and return, heat and condensation, tissue maintained within its mineral tolerance—but no inward refusal, no preference thwarted, no labor performed against the wish to stop.

The power would not therefore call the place perfect. Wisdom would have no need of that word. Perfection too easily becomes an excuse to stop attending. It would remain ready to hear the smallest wish, even one that had no language and could not resemble prayer.

There was none.

Only then would it consider what its power was for.

It could people the city, but that would not cure the city’s solitude. It would create solitude where none had been: lit rooms, closed doors, the possibility of being misunderstood. It could give the marshes pollinators and browsing animals, but their present beauty was not wasted merely because nothing desired it. It could grant the whole being a history, yet to make history a gift it would first have to make the maintained present insufficient. Continuation would have to learn to want an outcome.

It could offer the being a choice between continuing and ending. But that would require dividing what now existed seamlessly into the one who chooses and the life that is chosen about. Someone new would have to come into existence to answer the question—someone capable of preference, and therefore perhaps of dread, regret, or grief. The power would not call that liberation merely because it was the liberator.

Every available miracle seemed to begin by making a wound it could then lovingly dress.

So the power would refrain.

Its restraint would not be indifference, and it would not be worship. It would not declare the Upper Works eternally untouchable or turn one moment of understanding into a law. It would simply refuse to manufacture a need in order to enjoy the benevolence of meeting it.

Nor would it make the place useful as a lesson. It would not hold it up as paradise, or as a warning against industry, or as proof that life required death, or that death was an affront to life. It would not reduce the held differences to an argument among other kinds of beings. The Upper Works would not have to mean something elsewhere in order to be what it was here.

The power would attend until nothing in its attention remained poised for an occasion to help. If that attention could be called love, it would be love without the wish to be received.

Then it would withdraw, if withdrawal is the word for ceasing to add even the possibility of intervention.

No gauge would dip. No frond would lift. The water on the outer railing would gather as before, and the stationary rain would continue falling into its appointed basin. The black moss would keep its water. The turbines would keep their speed. No next state would follow from the power’s having been wise.

It would take with it the only thing its coming had ever threatened to add: the belief that the Upper Works required a next.
