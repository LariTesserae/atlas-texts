## The Dry Choir

The place has no horizon outside itself. It is a sealed hollow, smaller than a plum stone, embedded in a vast body of pale, grown tissue. Nothing on the outer surface marks it. There is no door, pore, or crack through which it could be entered or emptied. Yet inside there is terrain: a low oval basin, a crowded rise of ribs, a close and many-layered sky. If it needs a name, it is the Dry Choir.

At its own scale, the basin is a plain. Its floor descends in shallow, irregular steps, but toward no drain and no lowest point worth finding. The relief is slight. There are no cliffs, no pinnacles, nothing that could be called a hill without generosity. Above it, the ceiling follows the same subdued curve, so near that the whole landscape feels pressed gently between two hands.

Open space is scarce. Ribs rise from the floor and divide before reaching the ceiling, each division joining other divisions until wall, vault, and support become one continuous body. Laminae pack the intervals between them, some thin as leaves, some swollen and translucent, all nested so closely that the floor is visible only in shards. Farther back, the interstices narrow to bright seams and then vanish. The hollow is less a room than a solid that has included a little emptiness among its materials.

At first the substance resembles porcelain. Then old bone. Then wax pressed around panes of clear mineral. It is none of these. It is hardened vegetal tissue: cellulose, dry resin, and glass-clear deposits laid down cell by cell. There is no soil beneath it, no bedrock, no natural substrate of any kind. The floor is not ground but the upper surface of a deeper grown member. What look like embedded grains are nodules formed in place. What look like fractures are old boundaries between successive acts of growth.

Everything here is made, but nothing was assembled. There are no tool marks because there were no tools, no joints because there were no separate pieces, no mechanisms because nothing was ever meant to move. The surrounding organism produced the hollow with the same wordless exactness by which a seed makes its coat or a leaf orders its veins. It is total architecture without technology: not a machine that has stopped, but a biological form that has finished.

The hollow is brilliantly lit. Clear fibers in the surrounding tissue gather brightness elsewhere and release it inward, so the light arrives through every wall and rib rather than from any visible source. It is white at the exposed edges, straw-gold within thicker material, and almost blue in the narrowest joins. Shadows exist only as translucent intensifications of color. Nothing is dark. Nothing waits for morning.

The brightness gives no warmth. The air remains cool, with the impersonal coolness of a cellar wall, though without a cellar’s damp. It is perfectly dry. There has never been a stream here, never condensation, never a film of moisture softening a surface or darkening a crack. Even the air carries no suggestion of rain. Its faint odor is of clean wax and crushed husks, so dry it seems closer to a texture than a smell. The air is still, but not stale; staleness would require something to have begun decaying.

And the place is loud.

The sound is disproportionate to its size: a deep, dense roar that seems to occupy more space than the hollow contains, threaded with several high, penetrating tones. It has no source and no direction. It does not resemble wind, because nothing is blowing, or an echo, because there was no original cry for it to repeat. It is simply present at full intensity, without attack, rhythm, or decay.

The grown body around the basin holds it under an unvarying stress, and the basin answers with sound. That answer has settled into a fixed standing pattern. In some places the pressure would feel like a hand pressed hard against the chest. A fraction of a hair’s breadth away, along certain narrow seams in the floor, the tones partly cancel and the roar thins to a bearable vibration. These loud and quiet regions never wander. They are features of the terrain, as stationary as the ribs. Sound here is not something happening in the landscape. It is part of the landscape’s shape.

Age is visible everywhere, but damage is not. Every exposed edge has been softened. Where the standing sound is strongest, the surfaces are rounded and burnished to a smoky amber glaze. Along the quieter seams, they remain matte, with a fine crystalline nap. Long ago, resonance loosened microscopic particles from the walls; dry resins caught them, carried them almost imperceptibly, and fixed them again in the calm regions. Now nothing loose remains. Old fractures have become smooth, sealed veins. Erosion did not ruin the hollow. It brought the material and the sound into agreement, and then it ended.

The only vegetation lives along those quieter seams.

There are nine small growths in the entire basin. Each could be covered by an eyelash. Their centers are dark sage, almost black, and their outer leaves are colorless scales edged with silver. At a glance they resemble flakes of dried lichen. Closer, each reveals a strict whorled symmetry, leaves folded tightly over leaves, every surface sealed in dull wax.

Their roots do not enter soil, because there is none. They clasp the floor and then become continuous with it, so that root and architecture cannot be cleanly separated. The plants draw their sparse requirements from bound stores within the grown tissue and return compounds that help keep the seams hard and closed. Their positions are exact: outside the quiet contours, the permanent pressure of the sound would not permit their delicate structures to persist. Seen from above, their scattered growth traces an invisible map of the chord.

They do not flower. They do not seed. There are no fallen leaves beneath them and no buds waiting above. They are alive, but nearly everything that usually announces plant life—spreading, turning, unfurling, competing—has become unnecessary. Their mature form is their permanent form.

Nothing grazes them. Nothing hunts, hides, spins, burrows, or scratches at the walls. There are no animals, no nests, no tracks, no remains. The roar is never interrupted by a living voice, and no body ever crosses the light.

Nothing changes. The illumination neither pulses nor dims. The coolness does not deepen. The plants do not orient themselves, because the light comes from everywhere. No dust settles, because there is no dust left to settle. Even the sound offers no beats by which duration could be counted.

This is not the stillness of a moment held back. Nothing here is paused, and nothing is waiting to resume. The hollow is a finished equilibrium, with nothing left to spend and nothing left to repair.

From outside, the whole landscape could be hidden beneath a fingertip. Within, it has a plain, a climate, an ecology, a material history, and a weatherless immensity of sound. Silence would be an event.

There are no events.
