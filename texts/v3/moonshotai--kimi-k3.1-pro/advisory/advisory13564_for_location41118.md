Treat the Sere Crown less as a desert expedition than as a visit to a vast, electrically active living system with a city grown into it. A human can reasonably visit only as a **self-contained, escorted guest**. If you cannot arrange independent air, water, food, waste containment, suitable shelter, and local guidance, do not go.

Your ordinary instincts will fail in several specific ways. Ground that looks dead will be alive. An object resembling dead bark may be hunting. A distant short route may not be short in the usual sense, while two gates thirty-eight kilometers apart may deliver you to the same court. Preparation should therefore emphasize procedures and local knowledge as much as equipment.

## Arrange the visit before you arrive

Secure a host in Anther who understands that you are a wet, oxygen-dependent organism. Do not assume that a citizen of a dry ecosystem will spontaneously understand dehydration, breathing gas, humidity, or human sanitation.

Explain your requirements explicitly:

- A breathable atmosphere at human-compatible pressure.
- Controlled humidity and temperature.
- Liquid water and human food.
- A place where you can safely remove your helmet, sleep, and receive medical care.
- Sealed storage for urine, feces, wastewater, and other biological waste.

Anther’s buildings have heat and power, but that does **not** make them human habitable. Unless your host can provide a suitable refuge, your visit must remain short enough to fit entirely within your suit’s endurance. Camping on the plain is not an acceptable fallback.

Make the first visit brief and city-based. Agree in advance on:

- Your arrival route and departure route.
- Where you will shelter during a pressure reversal.
- What to do if you are separated from your guide.
- How long to wait before a missed arrival is treated as an emergency.
- Who can authorize or perform an evacuation.

A beacon is not a rescue plan. A rescuer must also be able to reach the particular route by which you arrived.

Keep your own way home as independent as possible. There is no reason to assume that a multiverse transit device using coordinates or spatial intuition will automatically interpret Anther’s grafts correctly. Do not make an untested coordinate inside an unstable graft your sole exit.

## Carry a closed human life-support system

The air is thin, and nothing in the description establishes that its composition is breathable. Use a sealed environmental suit or equivalent enclosure with independent breathing gas, carbon-dioxide removal, temperature control, and humidity management. A simple dust mask or oxygen cylinder is not enough.

The suit should also provide:

- Reliable seals against fine, charged dust.
- Visor protection and good low-light vision.
- A shielded lamp for thickets, old streets, and vaults.
- Suit-integrity monitoring and a substantial patch kit.
- Protection against vibration and accidental contact with charged surfaces.
- Redundant communications; shouting will not carry well through the thin air.

Bring all your water and food, with a reserve for storm delays and missed transport. There is no local refill, emergency ration, or useful survival improvisation. The night rime is a bloom of volatile salts, not frost. Melting, scraping, or tasting it would be pointless and potentially dangerous. Local plant and animal tissues are dry lattices of carbon, silica, and reactive salts, not merely unfamiliar human food.

Your water loop should be as closed as practical. Capture exhaled moisture and waste rather than releasing them. Anther has no liquid-pipe network into which you can conveniently pour anything. Your moisture is not necessarily a gift to a dry world; at minimum, it is unrequested contamination, and its effects on local tissues should not be guessed.

Do not rinse dust from equipment with precious water unless you are inside your own controlled refuge. Bring appropriate dry-cleaning materials and spare visor covers or filters.

Use sealed, well-shielded electronics with covered connectors. Treat airborne dust and conductive granules as potential contaminants until local technicians tell you otherwise. The pewter sun is too faint to make solar power your only energy source, and buried civic power lines are not automatically compatible with your equipment. Carry your own power and passive backups: written route information, a mechanical timer, an independent light, and any essential medical supplies.

Have a local specialist advise you on electrical protection. Neither “wear insulating boots” nor “attach a grounding wire” is an adequate universal answer on a plateau whose electrical potential changes against its winds. Insulation, charge dissipation, and bonding need to work as a system.

Finally, have your luggage assessed before using Anther’s transport. Some grafts respond to mass and instruction. Do not assume that a route approved for you is approved for you plus several days of water, or that shedding part of your load mid-journey is harmless. Closed-loop consumables and waste storage help keep your load predictable.

## Walk as though the entire plateau were alive

Because it is.

Remain on Anther’s pale, instructed roads and on routes your guide has approved. What looks like gravel may be dormant valves; apparent bedrock may be mineralized bark. A footprint can remain for days, ticking while charge redistributes around the injury.

Do not:

- Stamp on the felt to see whether it answers.
- Scrape samples from rocks, plates, masts, or animals.
- Pitch a tent, drive stakes, or anchor cables without explicit preparation of the site.
- Shelter casually beneath overlapping plates or inside a convenient hollow.
- Use exposed red root-lines as paths, handholds, or sources of power.
- Light a fire or discard batteries, food, moisture, or packaging.

The plain’s apparent simplicity is also a navigation hazard. There may be hundreds of kilometers of view with no useful landmark, while the escarpments beyond it drop for kilometers. Stay well back from edges unless you are at an established vantage, especially in dim light or dust. The famous night view from the escarpment is not worth walking toward a horizon that merely looks slightly darker than the rest.

Outside the city, never travel alone and do not rely on visual identification of animals. A group of broken masts may unfold into spindle-grazers. A plate of dead bark may be a shear that has already spent hours listening through the substrate.

Keep well away from herds, injured animals, and unidentified motionless plates. A wounded spindle’s rhythm is precisely the sort of cue a shear may be waiting for. Valve-clatter that sounds like a wooden building being dismantled is not an invitation to investigate. There is no dependable Earth-derived rule such as “run,” “freeze,” or “climb”; agree on your guide’s signals beforehand and follow them without improvisation.

## Prepare for sound, vibration, and pressure reversals

The Crown may be more exhausting to your nervous system than to your muscles. The low ground-tone can be felt in the teeth before it is heard. Kite cries are repeated by hollow masts; rails produce long chords; walls and streets conduct vibrations that airborne sound would not carry.

Ordinary ear protection will not remove bone- and substrate-conducted vibration. Arrange an insulated sleeping room, maintain a familiar sleep schedule where possible, and allow time away from the open city. Do not seek rest by entering an arbitrary silent graft chamber.

Learn the signs of a pressure reversal, but do not wait to diagnose one yourself. Your guide’s return time should be treated as a firm deadline. Warning signs include:

- The upper wind turning.
- Translucent sheets of charged dust lifting.
- Horizontal lightning passing from growth to growth.
- Fan arrays closing in propagating waves.
- A rising ground-tone.
- Burrowers emerging in bands.
- Spindles locking their valves rigid.

When warned, get into the designated civic or expedition shelter. Do not remain on open felt, beside a root-rail, near an escarpment, or under an isolated mast. Horizontal lightning means that simply avoiding the tallest object is not a complete safety strategy. Shears also hunt under cover of the storm’s noise.

Inside Anther, the towers may begin opposed resonance, making it seem as though the city is shouting back at the plateau. If you are in the correct shelter, this can be a protective response rather than a sign that the building is failing. Know this beforehand so that you do not flee a safe refuge into the storm.

Conversely, **sudden outdoor silence is not reassuring**. It often means that something large has interrupted ordinary substrate signaling. Notify your guide and follow the prearranged precaution.

One useful suit rule: while sealed, you should not be smelling cold metal, dry resin, or ozone. If you do—especially ozone before an electrical front—treat it as a possible suit or refuge leak, not as local atmosphere.

## In Anther, distinguish destinations from routes

Use only maintained grafts and transport approved for your body, suit, and load. Confirm that a capsule can accommodate you and that you can escape it in an emergency. Do not climb into an articulated freight husk merely because it is going in approximately the right direction; freight may be detached, planted, or converted into something else at its destination.

Keep an analog record of:

- Your host and lodging.
- The names or instructions associated with your routes.
- The customs court or other agreed rendezvous.
- Your scheduled return and emergency contacts.

Do not rely exclusively on a map, coordinate, or count of doors. This does not mean that ordinary measurement is useless—Anther’s surveyors still carry rods—but distance alone does not specify a journey.

Arriving at the same small customs court through the eastern and western gates is not, by itself, evidence that you are confused or hallucinating. On the other hand, do not attribute every symptom to geometry. If you become dizzy, forgetful, or disoriented, check your breathing system immediately; hypoxia and graft anomalies should not be mistaken for one another.

If you become separated from your guide:

1. Do not repeatedly cross a threshold to see whether it “resets.”
2. Do not assume that immediately backtracking will return you to your previous location.
3. If your present location is safe, remain there and use the agreed signal.
4. Wait for a guide or surveyor rather than trying unfamiliar stairs, gates, or old streets.

Report a stair that delivers you to the wrong landing, a room whose walls no longer agree with its remembered layout, or a route that feels incorrectly instructed. Do not attempt to prune, cut, retune, or “help” the structure. Maintenance is skilled work and may also be participation in Anther’s distributed planning and memory. There is no single central control room where someone can simply reset the city.

Likewise, do not equate an old visible doorway with an abandoned human ruin. Use may have been withdrawn while the structure slowly changed internally. Enter old vaults and lower street levels only with someone who knows their present condition.

## A sensible first visit

Begin in your human-safe refuge. Learn the normal ground-tone, the city’s alarm procedures, and the difference between an ordinary active graft and one awaiting maintenance. Take short escorted walks through maintained central districts before attempting anything on the outskirts.

Then, if conditions are stable, make one guided excursion onto the open felt with an early return time. Its value will not be in covering distance. It may consist of standing quietly while a footprint ticks beneath you, hearing burrowers pass below, or watching what you thought were broken masts resolve into a herd.

Return before you are tired. Sensory monotony, vibration, dim light, and spatial uncertainty can all impair judgment without feeling like conventional exhaustion. A traveler who insists on “just one more mast” or “just one more street level” is exactly the traveler most likely to stop noticing instructions.

Leave with your waste, your equipment, and no unsolicited samples. Have dust and salt deposits dealt with according to local procedures, and confirm your departure load and route rather than simply reversing your arrival from memory.

The essential preparation can be reduced to four rules: **bring everything your body needs; touch as little as possible; treat local maintenance instructions as literal; and never confuse bareness, stillness, silence, or short distance with safety.**

Done properly, the visit need not be merely an exercise in survival. It offers something few human landscapes can: the experience of standing on a road that must be continually remembered, hearing a city regulate its own resonance, and realizing that the apparently empty plain has been crowded with activity all along.
