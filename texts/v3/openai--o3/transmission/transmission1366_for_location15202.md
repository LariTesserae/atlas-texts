Carrier locks at 4 181 kHz.  
Signal strength: two bars out of five.  
A breath of static rises, steadies, becomes a floor of rain.

0 s  
A glottal THUMP—water hitting water from a long distance straight down.  
Immediate aftersound: trillions of droplets combing leaves to lace.

6 s  
Low-frequency engine-note, unmodulated, 96 Hz.  
Not electrical hum: too velvet, breaths in and out like an animal asleep.  
Faint phase-drift suggests wind-carried source.

11 s  
Something drums three quick triplets on a hollow reed:  
      tuk-tuk-tuk   tuk-tuk-tuk   tuk-tuk-tuk  
Foot-beats, not knuckles. Reverberation inside a living stalk.

13 s  
Echo from the rim returns the reed-code in loose shards:  
      …uk-tuk   …uk   …  
Fragments slide past each other, never re-assemble.

18 s  
Mist pops.  
Individual bubbles of warm air burst like knuckle joints amplified a thousandfold.  
Each pop leaves a micro-gap of silence so clean it seems lit.

23 s  
A single leaf slaps broadside: WHAP—  
              followed at once by the soft zipper-sound of a ribbon glider shearing the air.  
No voice, only velocity.

27 s  
Rain pauses. Whole spectrum yawns open.  
For two heartbeats the band carries nothing but a background respiration—  
the basin breathing in its own humidity.

29 s  
Voice, human, male, whisper-register, gains through multi-path echo:  
      “…barometer holding thirty-point-one… temperature three-five point six…  
       fog index seven.  
       Leave the node where the frogs are louder than the turbines.  
       Repeat: leave the node where—”  
Carrier flutter wipes the rest.

34 s  
Glass-wing shoal passes overhead:  
wingbeats too soft for microphone diaphragms.  
What arrives instead is a chord of rubbing wineglasses, pitched to the turbine hum plus a quarter-tone.

40 s  
Static surge—white hiss thickens, then parts like curtains.

41 s  
Waterfall body-blow, full bandwidth.  
Mid-bass roars, high treble hisses, sub-bass rolls like furniture dragged across cloud.

48 s  
Over the cataract’s roar, another human voice, distant, female, singing to keep rhythm while climbing:  
      “Heat is a collar, rain is a bell,  
       Stone is the tower that keeps us well…”  
Syallables smear; only vowels reach intact, consonants melt into spray.

55 s  
Signal flutters. Carrier threatens to slip.

56 s  
Choir enters—except it is only frogs, hundreds, each reed a different bore.  
Polyphony crosses itself, writes downward staircases of percussion.  
Between those steps the plateau’s own reply surfaces:  
      a modulating gulp, identical envelope, one octave below.  
Plant, animal, cliff contribute equally; impossible to assign authorship.

1 min 02 s  
Hum returns—louder now, the plateau has bent wind just so.  
Frogs retune, reeds creak upward by half-tone to dodge the overlap.  
The Conversation re-balances in real time.

1 min 05 s  
Tiny brittle noises like sleet on metal—rimstone shrimp, shells fracturing in cliff-spray.

1 min 07 s  
Voice again, same male whisper, closer to microphone, ghosting in from two directions at once:  
      “—aven’t got the data stick.  
       Shrimp fans syncing with engine… tell Kepler they’re wrong about the resonance…  
       Nothing dies up here, it just changes gait…”  
Word “gait” repeats four times, each softer, as echoes meet and cancel.

1 min 12 s  
Sudden thermal updraft: air itself whistles, carrier pitch bends down three semitones, rebounds.  
Background static thins to a single filament.

1 min 14 s  
All sound ceases but a heartbeat—             whomp… whomp… whomp…  
Not human. Interval irregular, like heat flexing in a basalt vein.

1 min 18 s  
Heartbeat merges with turbine note; the two become indistinguishable.  
For five seconds the receiver holds a perfect sine: 96 Hz, amplitude constant.  
Then—

1 min 23 s  
Rain crashes back, full vertical sheet.  
Every channel saturates; microphones drown in applause of water on water.

1 min 30 s  
Carrier drops without fade.  
Only after-image is hiss, already evaporating.  
Last audible artifact: a single spore cannoning itself skyward—tiny “phfft”—  
and the receiver is empty.

Transmission ends.
