[0:17]  Male voice, breathy, unamplified—language unknown.  
Three descending whistle-click chords, then a fourth held like a question.  
Metallic object strikes shale twice:   tok-tok.  
Frogs answer—thirty, maybe forty throats—single synchronized croak, huge draw on air, then silence.

[0:46]  Gain spike.  Something large moves across microphone field.  
Low infrasonic swell felt in recorder casing, not heard.  
Dust rattles on diaphragm.  
(Note: probable ridge-lion tail-scute resonance.)

[1:02]  Whisper in plateau tongue, very close:  
“…still sending?  Good.”  
Speaker coughs stone grit, spits.  

[1:05]  Same whisper, louder:  
“Archive, mark: ninety-third dawn below.  
Light lower than yesterday; ribs knitting fast.  
If it closes before the climb… well.  
Listen.”  

[1:19]  Recorder placed on rock.  Footsteps retreat—scrape, slide, scrape.  

[2:03]  The Hollow performs itself.  
• Echo-beetle fusillade, left channel—clack-clack-clack-clack, tumbles off far wall, returns thinner.  
• Cavy herd passes, honks Doppler downward:   HO͞ONK-honk-honk-honk.  
• Rope of gnats modulates key—A-sharp to G—whole column veers, audible like cloth torn slow.  

[4:40]  Second set of whistle-clicks, harmonizing with drone.  
Different timbre—child?  
Short, playful glissando; cavy respond with triple honk as if laughing.  

[5:12]  Sudden roar, full spectrum, peaks at 122 dB—ridge-lion charge.  
Roar ricochets, folding back on itself eight times, last echo indistinguishable from first.  
Frogs detonate into pool:  forty-odd splashes overlap, thunder of water on mic grill.  
Blind cavies scatter—hoofstorm like loose gravel in rain.  

[6:00]  Noise floor drops.  Hollow takes one long breath.  

[6:08]  New voice—same plateau speaker, returned, whispering fast:  
“Lion’s busy.  I follow maker-band east shelf.  They found thunder-pocket, silent in the core—heart rehearsal, they say.  
If ribs seal, that heart starts.  
If heart starts, plateau cracks.  
Tell them topside: sky-beast only sleeps.  
Stone is cradle, not coffin.  
We must…”  

[6:28]  Sentence cuts: mica bat swarm sweeps microphone.  
Thousands of wings—dry silk tearing.  Guano glitter pings housing.  

[6:52]  Static bloom—probable water on contacts.  Signal warbles, stabilizes.  

[7:10]  First-Maker adult, right beside mic, voice bearing guano-glint rasp.  
Five-note chord—mournful, rising.  
Then plateau tongue, heavily accented:  
“You keep our roar in little stone?  
Good.  Let stone remember us.”  
Soft tap: knucklebone placed atop recorder.  

[7:24]  Footsteps fade.  Rope-drone resumes keynote; mist hiss grows.  

[8:00]  Carrier strength falling—battery or mica fouling.  
Last intelligible sound: distant, rhythmic tok-tok-tok—firebrand walking, embers climbing invisible ladder toward the broken sky.
