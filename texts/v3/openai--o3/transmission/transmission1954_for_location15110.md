Day? Night? Hard to tell. The sky is the same indigo bruise it was when I clambered out of the root-well. Counting drips for clocks does nothing; the plateau edits the cadence whenever it likes.

(A series of four ascending notes, sung by something small. The notes repeat twice, then fold into distance.)

VOICE (whispering):  
If this reaches the lowlands, send up mirrors, not ropes. We can climb light faster than fibre.

[Static crackles, shaped almost like applause. It resolves into the dry flutter of hundreds of wings.]

REPORT—SEGMENT 1  
I walked forty kilometres without crossing a single seam in the bark. The ground sagged like cured leather, exhaled resin, then sealed the prints behind me. Direction is a rumour; the compass knot in my gut unwinds and ties itself anew each hour.

Saw a petal cat sleeping on a lantern slug’s glow-sheet, haloed like a saint. When I sketched them, the cat’s pelt darkened—photosensitive vanity—and the slug brightened to compensate. Mutualism as fashion statement.

(Background: faint percussion, as though someone is knocking politely on wood from the inside.)

REPORT—SEGMENT 2  
Found Hulled villagers at the throat of a spiral bridge. Their skins glimmered with damp sap, pores blinking open and shut like thoughtless eyes. They welcomed me with song-sentences:

haa-LO-ha    pii-re-too-ri    SHAA  
(You arrive / the air tastes of / strangers.)

I answered by humming the tune they used for questions. They laughed—a soundless tremor in the chest—and offered a bowl extruded while we spoke. The bowl cooled into transparency around a swirl of my own reflection, which now looks out at me whenever I drink.

I asked for directions to the “Memory Pulse.” They lifted their shoulders (no word for north or east) and sang:

urre-SAA    urre-SAA  
(Anywhere / exactly.)

REPORT—SEGMENT 3  
The plateau rehearsed a Gravity Glissade three breaths early. My knees forgot weight; water droplets rose in little comets and burst against the roof leaves, falling as silver rainshade. Glisten-gliders rode the slack like ribbons of discarded glass. One unzipped its belly, slipped free, and the shed skin fluttered past my ear whispering a story I didn’t catch.

During the lull I jumped. It felt economical: one step became six. Landed in a corridor grown into a knot-oath loop. Realised it only when my footprints overlapped their own shadows after the second circuit.

Pollen there tasted of crushed mint and metal. I lost half an hour—woke outside with sunlight (or something imitating it) trembling on my fingertips. The skin still tingles; chlorophyll? Perhaps temporary. My nails cast green shadows.

(Chiming, off-key. Could be root worms rasping wall flutes. Could be my skull.)

REPORT—SEGMENT 4  
Edge of a weeping cliff: cellulose so clear I saw my heartbeat reflected faintly beneath my boots. Water slid down and up simultaneously—optics here bend sense as well as sight. I dropped a pebble. It fell sideways into the trunk beside me and lodged, half-embedded like a fossil accelerated through time. A rot-bead swelled around it, ambering the wound.

Noted.

VOICE (closer, resonant):  
We are merely the plateau changing masks. Record if you must, but every syllable already echoes in the bark.

I address the plateau now:

If you hear, understand this is admiration, not invasion. Your silence teaches better than my questions can stumble after.

(A pause, deep as a swallow. Then a low hum, subsonic, felt more than heard—the Water Pulse cycling beneath.)

REPORT—SEGMENT 5  
Lantern slug migration overhead—dozens afloat on mucous bubbles. Each bore inside its light the silhouette of a different leaf, like saints carrying their own stained-glass windows. When the bubbles burst they rained cold luminosity; freckles of turquoise still glow on my coat.

I marked the time—though the plateau will erase the trail—by scoring a line on a dead glaze panel. The line healed shut before I finished. The surface remembers its first intention and refuses any other.

[Signal threatens to fold. Distant static forms a half-open chord.]

CLOSING NOTES  
Supplies irrelevant; the place feeds itself through me as needed. Hunger replaced by a slow, photosynthetic calm that spreads with each breath of terpene-sweet wind. I fear what I might become if I linger past the next Memory Pulse: perhaps another corridor practicing vowels, perhaps a bridge waiting for travellers.

If my words survive transit, let them seed caution and awe in equal measure. Do not come to prune or mine; the plateau has no patience for edits not grown from within.

Should my shadow reach you before I do, follow it back here. It knows the shorter dream.

[Carrier tone pitches downward, meets silence.  
A final droplet lands—it rings like a bell struck underwater—
