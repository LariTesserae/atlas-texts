High Flood // Echocast B-72

—⋄—⋄—⋄—  
[swell of carrier tone, then hiss]  
...all right, if you’re still on my needle, you’re hearing Lio Brevis of the west-run courier chain. My rig is three tiers down, lat 12.8 north by whatever-counts-for-zero long. I am alive; the rest is weather.

Minute 0  
Roof cloud lilac, holding. Low pressure taps the eaves like someone deciding whether to knock. Crabs are out—glass on glass, tens of thousands, the floor glittering like frost you can’t see until it moves. I can hear them through the soles of the boots: tick-tick-tick, the Flood’s own metronome.

Minute 6  
Pearlfall beginning. Individual notes ring off the parapet. Doppler says drift from east at four knots—slow, but the first step of a stair that ends in the ten-day roar. Walls here are steady. Too clean. Someone—or something—polished the spillway lips overnight. A sand-storm could have done it, but then where’s the sand?

Minute 14  
Incoming contrail above, thin—could be a cirrus kite shedding gas, could be a salvager flare. I pinged back on 38 kHz. No return but my own echo. Echo bats ripple out of the gallery roof like smoke drawn backward into a lung. They reshape the quiet. When they hole up again, the silence feels concave, as if my voice should roll downhill.

[brief dropout · carrier stutters · returns]

Minute 21  
Found a Lurian engraving on the spillway plinth: three nested arcs, one broken, dots along the fracture. I mapped it against the basin chart—reads as a warning, I think. The broken arc is this parapet; the dots are timing marks. Four hours from now the whole ledge tilts. Good. Saves me the trouble of guessing.

Minute 34  
Water sheet just formed in mid-air across the corridor mouth. Still attached to nothing. It bows inward when I speak, tries to make words out of ripple. I said hello; it fractured into braids, then rejoined. The elementals—if that’s what they are—seem curious today. Or maybe the Flood has a new question and I happen to rhyme with the answer.

Minute 47  
[Gutter mice alarm: tiny drumming stampede]  
Pressure drop following them. I anchor line to basalt ring. Wind tunnels through the split roof; temperature crashes five degrees in three breaths. My visor fogs inside. The grand storm’s scout squall is here.

[white noise surge — microphone overload — automatic gain clamps]

Minute 49  
Onyx wolves in the curtain of rain. No bodies, just outlines cut from brighter black. One howl folded itself around the walls, came back a chord lower. I tasted iron in the air and my teeth hummed. The echo bats froze, every one mid-wingbeat, like punctuation in the sky.

Minute 55  
Storm eye passes overhead—a stillness so sharp it mothers every sound. I pick up the valve-swans calling through organ pipes half a kilometre downslope. Their chorus rides the ducts to me pure as tuning forks. For a long blink the plateau is instrument, the birds its bow.

Hour 1, minute 12  
Water has sluiced clean through the gallery, left the floor mirror-dry. Crabs pour out again, harvest the new glaze. They don’t care the world just changed; change is the harvest. I step where they are thickest and they flow apart, forming my outline before I know I’m there. For ten seconds I watch a crab-shaped hole in the colony move with me, a walking absence.

Hour 1, minute 25  
I am following the Lurian map. The dots line up with stress fractures glowing dull red under magnetic lamp. They are fresher than they should be—somebody’s been etching only hours ahead of me. I call out in every tongue I own. Reply comes as three soft chimes: droplet, droplet, droplet. Either a joke or an invitation.

Hour 2, minute 03  
Reached the recommended hollow. Ceiling intact, acoustics dead calm. Depositing packet: bronze memory spool, three scopes of ferric sleet, one intact pearl worm husk. Whoever finds this, trade it forward. I’m done selling secrets to people who think water is scenery.

Hour 2, minute 10  
[remote thunder]  
Gallery behind me shears away—sound first, then sight. The edge stops exactly where my heels were. Someone drew that line into the plan. I thank the Flood, or the cartographers, or the mind in the water-braids—whichever heard me mean it.

Signing off for now. When the plateau tilts I’ll be riding the western sheet down to the night sea. If the transmitter survives the slide, you’ll hear the splash. If not—  
[brief laugh, almost a cough]  
—well, silence will tell you every next thing.

End broadcast. Stand by for carrier pulse until battery death.
