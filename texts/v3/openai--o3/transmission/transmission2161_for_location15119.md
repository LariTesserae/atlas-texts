[sibilant bed of low static, like sand sliding across stone]

[tighter focus—static thins to a faint breath]  

—single drop, yes—  
[plick]  
—purity holds—  
[plick]  
—next bead—  
[plick]  

Voice 1 (female, tone barely above wind):  
“The measure is two hundred even. Write it.”

Voice 2 (male, older):  
“Written. Cistern Rim-South remains sweet.”  

[a cedar baton taps twice on schist: tok–tok. Silence re-settles.]

[raven croak enters, scratches across the signal, then is gone]  

Voice 3 (youth, whispering excitement):  
“Shadow-Reader says the noon line drifts a hair west. First I’ve ever seen.”  

Voice 1:  
“Stone remembers longer than eyes. Give it another forty seasons, little cliff.”  

[laughter, small, contained; the microphone diaphragm trembles with it]

[soft swirl of fabric; linen nets being unpinned from roof spars]

Voice 2:  
“Fog’s leaving. Fold quick, before sun dries the weave stiff.”

[thirty-one heartbeats of wordless work: cord squeaks, cloth flutters, droplets patter into bronze pails]  

[wind lifts—gentle—singing one clear flute-note through a pine ladder’s hollow rung]  

Child, distant, chanting the First Beading rhyme:  
“Silver to brow, silver to stone,  
Fog drinks the sky, we drink the fog—”  

[chant cuts as the child notices being overheard; a raven clicks its beak like a closing lock]

Voice 4 (very old, hard to gender):  
“Quiet-Guard approaches.”  

[cedar baton again: tok . . . tok tok; the settlement’s polite knock against the air]

Quiet-Guard (Voice 4, louder now, authority carried on softness):  
“Out-valley trader climbing the mule path. Two packs, one bell. No wheel.”  

[raven chorus confirms with overlapping rasps, then hushes]

Voice 2:  
“Stone-Joiners, cover the seam. Outsider eyes slip where light catches.”

[slow scrape—stone block nudged to conceal a hairline child-groove]  

[static thickens; perhaps the receiver is shifted by a hand; next words come staccato, intermittently lost]

Trader (breathless, projecting, unused to ridge etiquette):  
“—grain—rye and—oats—climbed since—dawn—”  

Quiet-Guard response, calm but firm:  
“Let the stone speak instead.”  

[long pause; even static dims, as if holding breath]

Trader, now imitating the hush:  
“I… bring rye and oats.”  

Voice 1:  
“Firecourt Six will greet. Water first, measure after.”  

[splash—cup dipped, offered, accepted]  
[the trader drinks; swallow audible, a private thunder in this place]  

Trader, reverent whisper:  
“It’s like tasting cloud.”  

Voice 3 (the youth, curiosity slipping out):  
“It is tasting cloud.”  

[rain-bird (juniper sparrow?) trills once, surprised to find itself so high, then wings away]

[Minutes pass. The relay holds the plateau’s steadiness: drips, footfalls muffled by felt-soled shoes, a chisel’s single test-tap far off—ping—then restraint.]

Voice 2, reciting the standing oath for visitors:  
“Hear Cyclion’s four.  
Water belongs to none; stone must breathe; sound walks farther than sight; nothing changes quick.  
If these rest easy on your shoulders, stay as long as patience fills you.”  

Trader, exhale of awe:  
“I will try to slow.”

Voice 1, almost inaudible, as though to self:  
“Stone will know.”  

[plaza bell—hollow bull-horn—three low drones that melt into one another]  

Narrative interference—perhaps the fog itself—rolls across the microphone: a cool hiss, dense yet gentle, swallowing edges of every sound until only the heart of each remains.

Child’s voice, returning, a secret offered to whoever listens:  
“If you meet the memory goat, don’t follow. Just thank it for holding the cliff.”  

[the receiver quivers; a second, stranger signal flickers underneath—non-verbal, like moonlight translated into code—then fades]

Voice 2, concluding trade ledger:  
“Stone-coils, twelve; shadow slates, five; juniper jars, two. Fair carried.”  

Trader, in newly learned quietude:  
“Fair received.”  

[footsteps depart toward guest Firecourt; canvas pack creaks; raven escorts overhead with three wingbeats]

The fog sighs its retreat. Netwrights snap lines taut for drying. Shadows contract toward the plaza’s standing stone until the noon shaft glows straight and unwavering.

For a breathless stretch, Cyclion holds absolute stillness. The relay hears only the inner workings of air: the tiny tick as a droplet, formed but undecided, finally releases and strikes pooled water—plick—

—and in that single note the ridge, the people, the plateau, and the listening ear feel arranged, aligned, complete.

[carrier tone elongates, thins, vanishes]  
[end transmission]
