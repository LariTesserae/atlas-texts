a chorus of six voices, age indeterminate, begins signing Lith; the broadcast mic cannot see, but the hands toss light that pings along crystal seams, so each gesture returns as glints—Morse made of moonflashes:

WE OFFER SALT-MEMORY  
WE NEED BLUE THREAD

THE CEILING LISTENS

Silence, thicker now, like water about to freeze.

Far overhead a single droplet falls the height of a cathedral. When it lands inside a singer pot the mic picks up the note: F sharp, dry, sustained ten full seconds while cavities harmonize. The Oruli hold their breath; the broadcast holds it too; even the recording needle seems to balance on tiptoe.

The note fades. Conversation restarts three paces softer.

near-flame, go—  
touch the hush line  
warn the beetles we borrow their spiral tonight

Footsteps: six-leg cadence—nyx-hares herded by felt switches. Their translucent fur refracts torchlight across the mic like ripples in shallow water. One pauses, ears erect; its heart stutters an ultrasound plume that the receiver half translates as nervous bells. Roof-slip premonition logged.

A brass instrument—surface-made, iron mouth circled by copper coil—clears its throat with a mechanical cough. Surface trader testing valves. The Oruli nearest wince, but instead of protesting they wrap the bell in woven silence cloth. The next note emerges like a breath into snow, velvet and weightless. Approval murmured:

better  
much better  
keep it sleeping

Background registers a clacking swish: mirror beetles arriving, hauling a sled of fresh-ground shard-paste. Their footfalls pattern—three fast, two slow—tapping code into bedrock: VENT SHARE? VENT SHARE?

Reply through slab-drum: one padded strike, deep, affirmative. Paste trade accepted. In return the city will glow mural-colors come thaw.

Static burst—receiver flooded by broad-band resonance. The Deep Listeners have surfaced? No—just a human sigh close to mic, but here a sigh is thunder. Gain throttled back.

Someone speaks, rare full volume, ceremonial:

Trem, we cross your marrow with clean intent.  
Carry the hush we leave behind.  
Carry it where storms forget us.  
Let no stone tire of our weight this night.

The words drop, each more hushed than the last, until only exhaled consonants remain, like moth wings brushing slate.

Then: music.

Not flute, not drum—a braided sound, half air-manta bone flute, half breath blown across salt plates. It begins as single thread, splits, weaves itself through the chamber so the mic receives different strands with micro-delay—a living interference pattern. The selenite picks it up; walls light in syncopated shimmer. Children race the light cascades, laughing silent, mouth-open, sound swallowed before born. Their footfalls flicker instead of thud.

The flood troughs, momentarily dormant, answer with resonant hum—low, tidal. Is that water far off? No, only fog rolling down-slope, its edge combed by beetle shafts into long silver curtains. The humidity coats equipment; readings blur; but the carrier wave persists, wrapped in mineral grace.

Third act: negotiation.

A surface scholar—voice trembly, still not mastered the whisper—barters a sealed jar of thunder-rain.

I offer stormspeech—  
light caught inside water—  
listen…

He uncorks. The jar exhales a crack of distant lightning—compressed roar, reduced to a throat-click at this altitude. Even so, Oruli flinch; beetles freeze; the hush recoils like struck skin. For three long heartbeats no one moves, not even the recorder’s second hand. Then an elder, charcoal-elbowed, glides forward:

the jar is brave  
we accept its echo  
in return—take memory    

Salt plate passed across. Under torch, previous generations flicker through its translucent layers: silhouettes of roof-slips, schematics of wind, births annotated by singer pot pitch. The scholar gasps too loud; quickly covers mouth; nods.

Closing rites.

Hands to wall—hundreds now—palms splayed, a forest of fingertips tasting tremor. In that contact the transmission blooms: tactile data converts into sub-aural choir. Our headphones vibrate with words unspoken, hearts of limestone beating in macro-slow meter:

rest now  
stone is gentle  
rest now  
wind has eyes below  
rest—

Signal stutters. A swirl of warm draft lifts grit; beetle vents recompute paths; dust devil births in center square, children cheer. The mic picks up only their foot-scuff, all laughter eaten by air density.

Final image before carrier drops: an air-manta slips from the ceiling, wounded? No—shedding age. Wings refract torch-sourced pearl into prism fan. It glides through the spiral dust, sowing color like seeds that can never sprout. When it beats once, twice, our meters spike: each wingstroke is a silence so perfect it registers as sound.

The manta ascends; the hush reseals; crystal veins fade to dusky blue.
