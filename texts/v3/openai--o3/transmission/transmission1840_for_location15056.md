01:08–01:10

01:11  
Child’s footfalls (bare, quick) cross left to right, then a gasp:  
“Look—eel eggs for the moth-paste, fair?”  
A second child: “Fair—and hush, Mum’s counting the breath.”

01:12–01:59  
Nothing but heart-like ticking of condensation. Receiver gain climbs, trying to bite something out of quiet. The hall’s lungs fill with their own absence.

02:00 NULL HOUR ENDS  
Thousands of clatterbats erupt. Bandwidth saturates—white-grey storm of card-on-slate wingbeats. From somewhere overhead a loft door rattles open; wicker squeal, rope groan.

Over the swarm a measured chant, braided of many whispers:

“Tiles breathe, ribs bend—  
We hear, we mend.”

03:37  
Roof membrane rasps as the in-breath begins; reed lids cup downward. Wind climbs the shaft; parapet whistles low D, sustained.

03:45  
Percussive code inside the whistle: toe-toe-heel / heel-toe / rest / heel-heel. Transliteration margin notes: “Parapet secure / sump calm / Echo willing.”

03:58  
Initiate’s ceremony (mic auto-attenuates):

A teenage throat, trembling: “I-am-Cord-of-Third-Loft, let my note be stone.”  
She leans over the shaft. Sings a held A below middle C. Voice thins with depth, falls away.

Lag… nine, ten, eleven, twelve— 

RETURNING CALL, same pitch, harmonics braided, as though sung through gills. Dust avalanches off hidden rafters; receiver overloads, recovers.

Elders (whispered exhale, relief): “Bond strong. She may climb with knives now.”

04:27  
Slugs surface. Mic bathes in soft chemoluminescent hum; parquet of wet flesh sliding. One elder counts: “Twenty-seven, twenty-eight… full circle by thirty-three. Good glazing tonight.”

05:10  
Barometric swing reverses—out-breath. Roof swells upward; gutters gurgle. Crabs pick up 5-beat hydraulic rhythm under it all: tak-tak-tak-TAK-tak / tak-tak-tak-TAK-tak. A stone releases somewhere with the pop of a sucked thumb.

05:22  
Urgent under-stone message: heel-ball-ball / heel / heel-toe-heel (“Flood path found, relieve south runnel”). Two adolescents sprint with reed wedges; laughter echoes like marbles dropped down a well.

06:00  
Outside rain begins—first on reed lids (hollow patter), then forced through seams in glittering needles. Roof becomes a drum head. Someone adjusts the reed seams wider; distant shout from a mud-beard trader caught mid-floor: “Doors! Where are—” but the Rope-Setters have already vanished into mist.

06:04  
Thunderclap. Echo does not answer—it laughs, a descending chord of three notes, subsonic tail that vibrates the carrier itself. Slugs freeze; bats hush; crabs lock claws mid-air.

06:05–06:13  
Silence taller than the walls.

The laugh drains away; rain softens; life resumes.

06:14  
Whisper, almost at mic: “Hall is pleased. Keep her pleased.” Fabric rustle. A tablet of wet clay thumps into mortar—someone recently passed, name pressed in while the clay still remembers heat of hands.

06:40  
Signal frays—rain static, sump wind climbs, frequency drifts.

06:41  
Final intelligible transmission: Chorus of children, skipping-chant tempo:

“Stone drinks water,  
Water feeds wind,  
Wind keeps the roofskin tight.  
Stumble the rhythm,  
The hall goes blind—  
No Echo, no day, no night.”

[carrier fades, replaced by long, watery breath; receiver falls back to hiss, then nothing]
