Stillness-Beacon / Plateau Band 73.8 kHz

[carrier locks; the noise thins to glass]  

A single bronze note rolls outward, low as a turning planet, then stops.  
Silence remains, perfectly upright.

Voice begins—Ket language, syllables paired like mirror wings:

    “Ea-mon  ea-mon  
     the stone remembers,  
     we remember the remembering.”  

A second voice, same tongue, same pitch, picks up the line halfway through, speaks it backward to the start, and leaves the last word hanging.

                ·

Soft percussion—three taps of obsidian on bone.  
Between the taps you hear water condensing, droplet by droplet, a whole river forming in negative.

Whisper, almost inside the receiver:

    “Cloud-edge clear.  
     Runnels aligned.  
     No moss advance today.”  

Static again, but it does not hiss; it breathes once every eight seconds, like bellows made of frost.

                ·

A chorus now, children by the timbre, reciting without hurry:

    “First ridge, narrow sleep.  
     Second ridge, open lung.  
     Third ridge, keep the goats from climbing memory…”  

The chant unspools for seven ridges, seven verses, each folding back on itself until all you hear is the first line again, fossil-clean.

Bronze: GONG.  
The mic diaphragm rattles.  
All voices cease at the instant of impact; the echo continues alone, shedding overtones the way a cliff-goat sheds resin flakes—slow, precise, inevitable.

                ·

A woman speaks after the echo cools. Her words step so lightly that gain levels spike to catch them:

    “Ledger of motion, midday tally—
       one tortoise eastbound,
       two finger-fish returned to pool four,
       no birds.
     Plateau remains exact.”

Paper turns. The sound is huge, as if the page were the size of the sky.

    “To the lowland listener:
       Your clocks are unsuited.
       Lay them flat, let them forget.
       We will keep the hour for you.”  

She exhales; the air whistles through her ribs like wind in a reed bed.

                ·

Frequency drift—beat tones slide across one another, resolve into a field recording: boots on scree, then the impossible hush of ghast moss.  
Nothing, nothing, nothing—then a single copper ping, clear as a dropped pin on marble and utterly uncorroded.

Male voice, distant, speaking Ket but the meaning comes through as if your own delayed echo were translating:

    “Still Circle counts twelve; absence of two noted.
     Memory holds their chairs in place.
     Decision deferred until arrival of their shadows.”

Wind skims the plateau rim, a ribbon tearing against glass. It cuts to a lower channel where wingbeats might be expected; instead, emptiness sails by, featherless.

                ·

Another bronze stroke, softer, like the gong is dreaming of itself.  
In its wake: overlapping murmurs, each an earlier recording of the same moment, replayed at fractional volume until the layers become a standing tone, neither past nor present.

A child giggles. The sound is swallowed before it can echo.

                ·

Click—carrier wavers; a mantric fragment loops:

    “Stone upholds water upholds cloud upholds stone upholds…”

The phrase tightens, syllables drop until only breath remains, a binary of presence / absence that the receiver dutifully renders as:

    1 0 1 0 1 0 1 0 …

It lasts one hundred twenty-eight bits—exact power of two—then stops clean.

                ·

Faint footfall—goat or human? You hear resin unglue with a microscopic kiss.  
Somebody hums a descending minor third; the pitch hangs in the air long enough to grow roots.

Voice, genderless, directly into your skull:

    “If you are listening from below the clouds,
     remember us exactly as we are now.
     Do not allow the edges to round.
     Hold the reflection after it has left the pool.
     Keep still, and the mountain will go on rising.”

A switch thumps; the transmission begins to fold in on itself, all frequencies narrowing to one hair-fine line of silver. Inside that line the last thing audible is the river’s absent rush—water memorized so thoroughly it no longer needs to flow.
