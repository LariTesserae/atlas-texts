ROOT-9 / SPIRAL BROADCAST  

[open carrier hiss]  
[two quick pops as the transmitter syncs]  
[the yard’s signature tone: a descending triad tapped on boiler-skin with a drift-uncle’s brass mallet]

Voice—female, mid-20s, half-sung over turbine hum:  
“Morning pulse, fourth swell after grey-dawn.  
Sap gauge at Lung-West reads nine fingers and climbing—  
sweet enough to skate a squeegee, not so fast we drown the worms.  
Copy that, Copy that.”

[flutelike squeal of a steam bleed; background clank of link-chain]  

Chorus of children reciting, breathless, in unison as they jog:  
“Eighty-three valves shut, one fluttering at the elbow of the dorsal vein—  
chalked it blue, chalked it blue,  
report to Gardeners two.”

Static wavers; a low percussion takes over (hammer on anvil, slow 5/4).  

Gravel voice—foundry foreman:  
“Heat-bay three reports blossom-smoke, color of new coin.  
Good omen.  
Casting window opens in—”  
[distant gong struck once]  
“—there’s your bell, lads. Quench libation ready?  
Mind the rim, don’t boil the bats.”

[roar of furnace door, then a tidal hiss as molten iron meets swamp water;  
the reverb rings through metal catwalks like struck glass]  

A pause—only the soft mammalian creak of the banyan-reef shifting.  
Mic diaphragm crackles as humidity spikes.  

Old voice, whispering a lullaby meter for pumps, almost a prayer:  
“Four slow, two quick, hold the breath,  
let the triple-head drink and rest.  
If her heartbeat slips to stone,  
chuck a pebble, coax her home.”  

[Pump answers: CHUNK-shhh…CHUNK-shhh…then settles into the hummed rhythm]  

Rustle of paper, then the apprentice announcer again, words smudged by echo:  
“Posting today’s drift: catwalk Delta will lose three finger-widths by next low-tide.  
Owners of lockers 12 through 19, relocate or trust the water.  
Root Court in session sunset-side if disputes arise.  
Bring your own tape measure; the reef abides no guesswork.”  

Sudden bright chord—forty voices inside a hollow banyan seed-pod.  
They’re rehearsing, the resonance enormous, every vowel wobbling like a bell:  
“Green light, rust light, melt the hours thin;  
Here the wall is breathing,  
Here the roof wears skin.”  

Mid-song, a thudding splash—pod begins to sink.  
Cheering, laughter, the choir keeps singing down into the water until only bubbles reach the mic.

Brief dead air.

Receiver picks up a quieter channel: ethanol crew.  
A match is struck; flame crackles.  

Voice—cautious:  
“Blue taper steady. Batch thirteen is safe for draw.  
Captain Farrow’s barge waiting starboard, engines sleeping, good manners noted.  
Three drums gratis; log it under reciprocity.”  

[Ledger stamp THUNK]  

Wind through cattails outside the peat rim, a single sustained chord; it rises, merges with the yard’s sodium-lamp buzz, then fades.

Final segment—Pulse Board meeting, already in motion.  
Seven chairs, one empty. Mic is close to the sap pot; we hear viscous plops.  

Elected voice:  
“Motion to extend scholar stay to a month—”  
(loud POP from the pot)  
“—noted. Reef speaks, we table it. Next.”  

Another voice, younger:  
“Proposal: rename boiler Number Six after late Aunt Salla, on account of her bones lighting the dormitory to this day.”  
Silence from the sap.  
“Carried by consent.”  
A tap of gavel on living wood.  

[transmitter gain rises; background noise swells—pumps, bats, chain, distant laughter—until it sounds like surf]

Same female voice that opened: softer, almost inside your ear.  
“For any drift-uncles tuning from dry upland hospitals,  
know the reef still hums your cadence.  
We keep the pulse.  
Come home when the clay cools.”

Carrier hiss lengthens, thins, snaps to silence.
