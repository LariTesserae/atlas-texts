Voice, hushed but close:  
“— the vent cleared itself at third bell. Mark the seam dry till next mist-rise.”  

Second voice, younger, breathier:  
“Tier-mother, listen— the fog folds twice, not three. Tide’s late.”  

A pause; droplets tick against bamboo. Rope groans.  
Farther off, a slate rib pings—high, crystalline—then quiets.

Tier-mother again, murmuring toward the drum:  
“Relay upslope: weight-shift, one span east. Say it twice.”  

[tap-tap / tap-tap]  
A ripple of identical taps scatters into distance, roof to roof, like stones skipped over water skin.

Static thickens, resolves into a chorus of almost-voices—many throats in library hush—reciting:

“Cradle hinge, soft.  
North gutter, slick.  
Second stilt under Bitter-Ridge, breathing.  
Salt-lip sound, clean.  
Mirror-pool at cistern mouth—shows cloud underside, no tear.”

The litany ends with three heart-slow drumbeats. Silence floods in, heavy as dusk.

Now a tremor—low, sub-audible—rolls up the bowl. The receiver’s gain spikes; we hear the Hollow itself exhale.

Child’s whisper, exhilarated:  
“The throat-well belched! Hear it? Hear it? The ebb pulls hard—”

Older laugh, instantly shushed. Someone folds the child’s ears for quiet.

Mist begins to fall—soft hiss like pages turned under water. Through it a woman sings almost under breath, words syllabled to fit between drips:

“Stone still soft,  
water still thin,  
lean across, let the day begin.  
Slate still keen,  
hands still sure,  
clear the gutter, keep the Many pure.”

Each line lands on a metronome of creaks: plank, rope, plank, rope.

Signal flutters—green luminescence hums in the carrier band. Glass clink: mirrors catching frog-light, flashing code. Translated fragments overlay the song:

…ALL CLEAR FOR HIGH WALK…  
…BRING REED FOR SPARE RAIL…  
…COIL LINES; RAIN PROMISED BY THIRD DRIP…

Sudden crack—slate fracture—sharp as a bone flute snapped in anger. Every other sound cuts mid-breath.

One heartbeat. Two. Then a single drum stroke: weight-shift. Another: weight-shift. A rolling palm: all tier above evacuate.

Footsteps—many, rapid but measured—cross suspended boards. Infant cradles swing, ropes squeal, water sloshes beneath.

Old man, calm amid the churn, addresses no one and everyone:  
“Stillness tasted our roof. We lean to keep breathing. Remember the Rake.”

He spits a pebble into his palm—smooth toadstone, mute in air. Sucks it back between teeth. A hiss like distant surf: pressure tests the shale.

Static strengthens; words smear into rainfall. Through the wash, three clear clangs of iron studs striking together—authority sealing a verdict.

Voice of the gutter-ward, ringing yet soft:  
“Cut a new lip under the cracked seam. I name six hands for the work—pay is tomorrow’s first light. Mark me!”  

The Many answer with one communal breath, half sigh, half affirmation.

Rain arrives—large, hollow notes on tile and skin. The transmission gutters, water splashing across the pickup. For a moment only the storm speaks: dense, silver, unpunctuated.

As it eases, children’s mirrors blink along the highest rails, spelling a last message before the carrier fades:

“Sky in the pool. Needles by dawn. All ears open.”
