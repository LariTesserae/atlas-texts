“Check, check. If this is looping back on the mast antennas, somebody ping the choir pods and tell them to shut up for a minute. I’m running solo tonight; got assigned Midnight Drift on account of the storm jockeys punching a new hole in the wave map.  

Anyway. This is Mara Keel, Shift Diver grade four, tags M-K-1492. Position: eleven-hundred metres mid-column, twenty-three klicks sun-spiral from central mast. Lamp-cycle is three minutes from full dark; water temperature holding at 6.8 °C; currents negligible but building.  

I’ve cut suit thrusters. Letting the chamber breathe me along.”

[A second tone joins, five semitones up, harmonics flickering like distant sheet metal]

“Sight log:  
Above—last lamp-ring winking out. The housings go black one by one, like somebody snuffing candles down a cathedral aisle. Still see after-glow on the anodized fins. Looks like dusk on repeat.  

Below—latticework is a starscape. Welding crews have string-lit their gantry so every fresh bead throws sparks straight up at me. From here it’s meteor shower in reverse.  

Mid-water is empty. Feels like the chamber forgot to spawn anything tonight.”

[a wet crackle; suit hydrophone catches baleen skimmer call, ​5 sec, 29 Hz; structural resonance answers in far trusses]

“Heard that? Old bassoon ghosting somewhere east. They always sing once the lamps quit—maybe they think night is something they should announce.  

Chamber kids swear the skimmers run a count, that they know if a lamp fails to relight come morning. A living inventory system. I kinda buy it.”

[voices layered faintly—choir pod trio humming cancellation chords around a distant condenser horn; beats interference into shimmering alias]

“Status: drifting south by west, 0.4 knots. I’ve got filter eels schooling around my calves—four dozen at least, laid out like chrome ribbons. They’re tapping code on my ballast tanks. Probably begging for cover from carnifex. I slap the tank twice: Not prey. They spiral off. Good luck, little ground wire angels.  

Personal note: suit HUD just flashed a ‘spectral unknown’ on channel 8. Probably graphite ink from that runaway octopus clouding the sensor. If I see the thief tonight I’ll try for a photo; R&D still owes me rations for the last clear shot.”

[Sudden broad-spectrum roar: intake pumps spool to 95 %. Hydrostatic shove lifts the whole soundbed; microphone peaks; auto-gain ducks]

“Whoa. Intake surge early. Storm schedule said plus seven minutes. Somebody up-mast is greedy for freshwater. All right, swim fins out. Trim. Riding the shoulder of it—yeah, there it is—a standing wave you can’t see but you can feel in your teeth.  

I’m sliding, falling upward; up has opinions tonight.”

[Rapid pings—maintenance sonar from a pod of scrap crabs cutting salvage below. Metallic clatter, irregular 3/4 rhythm]

“Crabs underfoot again, tearing last month’s busted stanchion. They’ve grown symbols on their backs—white chevrons, looks like. Territory tags? Or just fashion?  

Kelp curtain at two hundred metres ahead—pale in night-glow spores. Fronds braided; shepherd handiwork. They left mourning knots—someone must’ve lost a crewmate this shift. I’ll add a tag.”

[Soft beeps as suit printer extrudes a phosphor ribbon; water-shear flutter]

“Ribbon away. Watching it fall, slow as thought.  

Lamp night’s halfway over. Biolume mites are waking—cloud of cold green fireflies, each the size of a grain of sand. They shape invisible thermals: updraft spirals painting corkscrews around my arms. If I hold still, I look like a constellation pretending to be a woman.”

[Silence, except micro-rain tick of plankton on facemask]

“Things I’m thinking while the world murmurs:  

1. The chamber’s only ten years old. I’m twenty-six. I remember real sky from when I was six and a half, but the memory warps—gets smoother, less detailed—every lampdown I spend here.  

2. The filter eels copy the pump’s carrier wave to hide. What happens when they learn to fake our voice traffic? Will they sing weather reports?  

3. Scrap crabs might already have a king. How long before the king needs priests?”

[electric thrash, off to starboard; carnifex strike—pressure boom passes through body cavity]

“Carnifex just took a skate, maybe fifteen metres away. No visual—I only saw the wing flash then vanish. Chamber giveth, chamber eateth.  

HUD lit again; unknown echo big as a service pod, but slow. Could be baleen, could be something else. Moving closer.”

[Water pressure tick climbs; microphone begins picking up faint turbines overhead—freight blimp passes, ballast vents hissing]

“I see it: shape like a lantern custodian but ten times scale. Shell patched in stainless sheets, rivet rows sloppy—hand-built. Eye ports glowing blue from inside, maybe borrowed LEDs.  

It’s carrying scrap—armfuls of truss shavings woven into a ring. No propulsion jets; it walks the lattice, but tonight it’s free-swimming. Mass must be tonnes. Yet buoyant.  

It turns—spots my suit lights—raises the ring between us. For a second it looks like—hell, like a crown.  

Signal from it: single ping, 41 Hz, sustained four seconds. Choir pods in the dark answer with a chord I’ve never heard—a minor thing slantwise to the usual safety song.  

I think the crabs built themselves a monarch and now they’re parading it through free water where the suns can’t see.  

Closing log, transmitting raw. Somebody topside better save this file before lamp-up burns the story away.”

[Tone falls off, carrier hiss, then nothing but manganese rain on distant hulls]

—END
