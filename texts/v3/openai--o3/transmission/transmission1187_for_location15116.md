[The carrier wave steadies. A hollow drone like wind funneling through bone. Under it, a wetted drum-roll—raindrops on stretched willow—travels left to right, returns, repeats.]

—whooomph—

[One deep pulse, almost subsonic. Loose crockery rattles somewhere near the microphone. Several heartbeats of silence follow.]

Child’s voice, close: “Ready. I’ll mark the fall.”

Adult, farther off, laughing: “Mark nothing; the river isn’t born yet.”

A chorus of children counting in thirds, not seconds:

“Vent… vent… vent…”

[They fall quiet. Over their hush, the rim-organ begins: nine notes stepped like a descending stair, each held long enough to braid with the next. The air thrums; the receiver clips, distorts, then regains fidelity.]

Whisper, female, almost lost in the resonances: “North mouth has soured—too flat. We’ll tease it wider tomorrow.”

A twig pops, is bent, re-bent. Fingertips run along living bark—grit, click, grit—testing graft seams.

Old voice, grainy: “Stone says dawn is near; feel it tighten? Hear the tick?”

[Mic is placed against rock. An arrhythmic ticking, faintly metallic, like cooling glass.]

Static sheet, clears.

Sung phrase, solo tenor, syllables lengthened by echo:

“Open the breath, keep the breath open—”

Others answer two octaves lower:

“We only borrow the hush.”

[Drain-drums swell, water on wood becoming applause.]

Child (maybe the same): “There! Look! Sparks in the vent!”

Adult: “Forge mites turning the glass again. Let them dance; they mend the cracks we cannot reach.”

—whooomph—

[Second furnace exhale. The receiver’s meters spike, then settle.]

Murmured inventory, recited quickly:

“Thirty-four gut bladders full, four skins torn, need stitching; six basalt blades dulled, swap at dusk; willow lattice 5-east rooting strong; future creek advanced a finger-width—”

Crackle, hiss.

Grandmother Te’in, soft but commanding, so near her breath fogs the mic: “Kneel now. The depth note comes.”

Silence. Then the organ’s basal register—a pressure more than a tone—bows every other sound away. Even the static hushes.

Afterlong pause, someone rises, joints creak like ice.

Same elder, amused: “All this mountain asks is that we listen. Harder task than shaping wood, you’ll find.”

Faint giggle; the giggle becomes distant footsteps squelching through moss.

Wind curls, drops.

A final fragment—two stones struck together in a bright chime—followed by a whispered farewell:

“Receiver below, carry this upwards: we are still here, keeping the young earth company.”

Signal thins. Rain’s fine hiss takes the channel until only sky-noise remains.
