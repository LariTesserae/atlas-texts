Veinbeat Relay / 077.  
(The slate receiver names itself in a rasping tick:  
“Basin-Hammer-Shadow / channel is open.”)

—BEGIN TRANSMISSION—

[silent carrier … then a pulse like two stones struck under water]

. . .   drip /    drip /    drip  
{three slow drum-beats bloom in the low end, reverberant, the timbre of stretched eel hide}

Voice (breathy, close to the pickup):  
“Datum check. If you hear me beyond the roofs, count the angles with me.  
One: the span from my thumb-scar to the copper stanchion.  
Two: the span from stanchion to lantern gutter.  
Three: the span from gutter to the sky we made of planks.  
Measure holds. Nothing has drifted but my lungs. Proceed.”

[tap-tap-tap of chisel on wedge, four times, then a grunt—wood swelling in stone and a muted crack]

Choir of far hammers replies, maybe forty in number, syncopated:

tchk-tchk-TUM       tchk-tchk-TUM  
tchk  tchk  tchk      (pause)      BROKEN!  

A child’s voice, half-sung, threads through:  
“Surface still breathing, surface still young,  
Hit it again till the edges are gone.”

She laughs, coughs; copper dust rasps.

[The carrier overloads—snow of static—settles back into a steady drip and the creak of suspended bridges.]

Second Voice (old, vowel-thin):  
“Angle-Tender Pliq reporting to anyone who tends farther angles.  
Shadow length on marker twelve has slipped one bead past tolerable.  
I have chalked the quarrel: six wedges, three score taps, rebuild shorter.  
Send me twelve quick hands and one brave waller.  
Send me the hymn of falling stone.”

[A rhythmic code on rawhide drum answers: three slow / one crack / rolling cascade. Translation banner scrolls in the noise: WATER RELEASING—CLEAR LOWER LANES.]

Third Voice, overlapping, hurried, shoes slapping slate:  
“Surge-warders pop the dam in ten, nine—by the Doctrine, let the Basin drink!”

Rumble like thunder unfurls down a thousand gutters: SHHHHHRAAAAAAL.  
Receiver diaphragm jitters; low frequencies smear.

Shouts, distant:  
“Lift your torches—up, up! Let the moths feed on the flame!”  
“Headcount your hammers!”  
A single cheer as the flood roars past.

[Swell subsides; droplets tick again. Somewhere a tower wind-vane twins on its axis, squealing.]

Soft Voice, almost prayerful, so quiet you must lean in:  
“Listen: after the surge, the stone sighs.  
Can you hear it?  
That is the Basin forgiving us for carving too slow.”

A pause. Only breathing.

Then: clack—clack—clack—(bone upon abacus beads).

Same Voice, steadier:  
“Correction: the forgiveness is brief.  
Corner of Slip-Way Seven is off true by half a heart-pulse.  
We will break it, square it, name the shard.”

[flutter of soot-moths across microphone, wings like paper scratching silk]

A chorus of teenagers chants in labor cadence:  

“Name the shard, scar the name,  
Wall the scar, start again.”

Deep echoing BOOM—something major has fallen. The choir dissolves into hoots.

Shifting static. The channel glitches; for six seconds only pulses like a heartbeat remain.

Heartbeat becomes drumbeat, drumbeat blooms into song:  

torches hiss / chisels kiss /  
water runs / surface lives /  
stone dreams / we wake it  

Male voice, baritone, official:  
“Notice from the Drum-Callers.  
Lightning scent in the up-draft.  
All crews near roof-gaps: brace copper nets, ground your mauls.  
If the sky breaks through, ride the spark, don’t fight it.”

Sudden high crackle—distant lightning indeed.  
Delay, then a crash like a mountain sneezing.

Voices in jubilation, half terror:  
“It chose the rookery spire! Perfect cut—clean down the mistrued seam!”  
“Map it! Map the fracture! The sky has done a day’s work for free!”

[the transmission flares with harmonics, then steadies]

Wind, rare and damp, curls through beams; it hums a five-note minor mode.

A new storyteller enters, breathless elder waller entombed halfway in plaster (you can hear his speech echoing off confined chamber walls):

“If you hear me, know this:  
I feel the stone leaning back.  
My ribs keep time with its cooling.  
When my breath fails I will become another hush in this corridor.  
Carry my tool to a child.  
Let no surface fall to Drift.”

His words fade beneath the gradual build of hammers returning—faster now, as though racing some unseen partner.

Chisel-squeal modulates into a rhythm your speaker cannot resolve; it becomes almost music:

UP–cut / DOWN–beat / side–shear / TURN  
(repeat, accelerate)

All other noise mutes; a lone young woman addresses the receiver, voice inches away, conspiratorial:

“Outsider, if your ear is real, remember a secret:  
The perfect surface is the one we never finish.  
Leave one edge breathing and the Basin lives forever.  
Send no silence.  
Send only echo, so we may carve against it.”

She taps the microphone three times—hammer, palm, hammer.

[carrier signal wavers; static braids with the drip]

Final composite message rolls out in overlapping voices, children to elders, a fugue of intent:

“Stone to bone,  
water to will,  
shadow to hammer,  
surface to self.  
Keep the Quick-worked quick—  
keep the Drift at bay—  
strike, strike, strike…”

—END TRANSMISSION—
