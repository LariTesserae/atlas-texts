Malletcast – Rivulet Band 19211

[carrier catches—rush of falling water, hiss of mineral air, then a throb like a slow heart struck from stone]

TUNK …                 TUNK …                 tunk-tunk-TUNK  
(three-stroke beacon: “channel holds”)

[echo climbs the terraces, comes back iron-flat: the line is clear]

CLACK | CLACK-clack | CLACK-clack-clack  
(low code, public tempo)

>   “Listen, kin.  Upper shelves speak."

A second voice answers from farther off, its mallet smaller, higher:

tak-tak-TAK-tak   …    tak-tak-TAK-tak  
(confirmation—“we hear, we hear”)

Between phrases the water answers, forever the same vowel of ssshhh.  
Somewhere a gut-rope groans; a basket rises toward haze.

First message—news roll:

TUNK---TUNK---TUNK TUNK  
(short-long-short-short = birth)  
The strike rings upward; infants are announced from the middle ledge.  
In the carrier’s overtones you can taste the cistern steam—the only warm nook in all that stone.

Second message—warning:

CLACK-CLACK    CLACK (silence) CLACK  
(gap inside the measure means obstruction)

>   “Drift-body lodged in Slot-Seven.  
>    Two spans down-current from Mothers’ Bay.  
>    Water snarls—remove before salt takes the face.”

The words are rhythm; the rhythm is law.  
You feel the city pivot: ropes unlash, chisels lift, feet splash through ankle flood.

Static surge—green motes erupt as the hourly glow-gnats spill from ceiling cracks.  
Their brightness overloads the receiver; a wash of photic buzz drowns code for nine, ten heartbeats—  
then gutters out.

Third message—rarity:

TUNG-tung-TUNG---TUNG  
(pattern reserved for “sky gift”)

A hush wider than the nave falls; even the stream seems to duck its head.  
When the reply comes it is voice, not mallet—barrel-chested, chest-deep:

    “…moth-dust sighted.  
      A widow beats the quartz ribs,   wings   shredding.  
      Catch what gleams.  
      Eleven winters of lamplight ride on that powder.”

The sentence arrives as a single infrasonic exhalation; its echo quivers in the spiraled ears of every Shelf-Born and inside the coils of the listening gear alike.

Now ambience only:  
• flutter—parchment tearing high above: the Lamp-Widow in death orbit.  
• rain of scales, like sugar on slate.  
• childrens’ palate-clicks counting the green fade of gnats: “…seven… eight… nine…”

A tremor—sub-audible, purely mineral.  
The quartz vault begins its once-or-twice-a-day dream-hum, a note too long to be pitched, too low to be denied.  
Water wrinkles concentric rings; each mallet lowers in reverence.

For fifty heartbeats nothing human moves.  
The receiver drinks raw cavern sound: hiss, heartbeat, hum, the ancient patience of dripping stone.

When life resumes it does so in half-voices, meant for near ears but caught in the gain:

    “Lift the winch-head two finger-widths—floor’s risen again.”  
    “Oil the rope before the dry bite snaps gut.”  
    “Mind the newling; its ears haven’t found the walls yet.”  
    “Was the corpse old?”  
    “Old enough. He chose it.”  
    “Then unjam him gentle; the river judges what we cannot.”

A last code volley, closing tag:

CLACK-clack-CLACK---CLACK  
(“finished until next low bell”)

Silence does not follow—silence never lives here—  
but the organized sound subsides, yielding to the endless rehearsal of water on stone.

Carrier weakens; the hum gutters; gain falls;  
only the far-off whisper of the Rivertongue remains, sluicing past unseen with its russet veins,  
curling around the freed body, towing it to whatever dream lies past the ledges’ last reflection.

[signal grain by grain dissolves into wet static, then ends]
