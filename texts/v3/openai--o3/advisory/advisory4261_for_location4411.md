Field Guide for the Multiverse Traveler  
Destination: The Flood Kiln (active sub-tidal curing chamber)

────────────────────────────────────────
1. Executive Summary
────────────────────────────────────────
The Flood Kiln is an occupied, underwater construction site—half foundry, half cathedral—kept permanently flooded with sun-warmed seawater. The space is small (≈ 25 m × 6 m × 5 m) but hydraulically violent during operating cycles. Expect dazzling light, 38–42 °C water, continuous low-frequency thunder, and zero macro-fauna. Your greatest risks are: impact trauma from the feeder jet, acoustic overpressure, eye/skin irritation from lime-rich brine, and angering the diver-builders by contaminating their sterile workspace.

3. Timing Your Arrival
────────────────────────────────────────
The Kiln runs on a tidal schedule:

• Surge Window (≈ 4 h, twice daily)  
  – Paddle-wheel shutters are open.  
  – Water velocity at dais: 7–10 m s⁻¹ (fatal to unassisted swimmers).  
• Still Window (≈ 50 min, straddling slack tide)  
  – Shutters close. Flow drops to < 0.3 m s⁻¹.  
  – Builders enter to inspect joints, replace hoops.  
TRAVELERS MUST ARRIVE AND DEPART DURING A STILL WINDOW.

────────────────────────────────────────
4. Approach & Entry
────────────────────────────────────────
Surface Staging Point: A limestone cenote 600 m upslope (camp of the builders).

Descent Route: A 1.2 m-wide utility shaft cut in the roof of the Kiln, fitted with rope ladders and handholds every 0.5 m. The shaft doubles as the daylight “sun tunnel.” When shutters are open this shaft is a roaring waterfall—do not descend then.

Depth: 9–12 m below mean sea level (modest free-dive depth; decompression not an issue).

────────────────────────────────────────
5. Required Equipment
────────────────────────────────────────
1. Breathing
   • Lightweight, closed-circuit “bottle-breather” rated 60 min.  
     (Compressed-air SCUBA works, but off-gassing bubbles violate Rule #4—no extraneous life/bubbles.)

2. Hydrolight & Vision
   • Mask with amber or rose polarization; glare inside Kiln can temporarily bleach retinal pigments.  
   • Backup chem-light in case sun tunnel clouds.

3. Hearing & Head
   • Vented hydroplugs (–15 dB) or bone-conduction helmet; internal noise runs 130–140 dB in surge window, 95–100 dB in still window.

4. Skin & Thermal
   • 1–2 mm neutrally buoyant dive skins to protect from lime burn; water feels like a hot bath but the alkalinity (pH ≈ 8.7) stings after 15 min.  
   • Rinse suit in freshwater post-visit.

5. Mobility & Safety
   • Soft-soled, lead-tipped reef boots (neutrally buoyant) to grip slick travertine.  
   • Wrist-mounted micro-grapple or mag-clip to anchor catwalks if shutters reopen unexpectedly.

6. Sanitation & Biosecurity
   • Mandatory peroxide dunk of all gear at builder camp. Even a fragment of barnacle is taboo; remember Rule #4.

────────────────────────────────────────
6. Etiquette & Law
────────────────────────────────────────
• Do not speak to the builders while they are on a breath. They count strokes mentally; breaking that rhythm risks drowning.  
• Do not touch algae threads—they are deliberate light baffles.  
• Never introduce tools made of bright steel (seen as “unfinished, raw”). Bronze, iron or stone only.  
• Photography is tolerated but disable flash; white-strobe interrupts the workers’ hand-signal code.

Expect spot inspections. The senior mason (“Hoop-Mother”) carries a dulled harpoon and enforces compliance without debate.

────────────────────────────────────────
7. Navigation Inside the Chamber
────────────────────────────────────────
Zones (starting at entry shaft):

A. Oculatory Well  
   • Column of descending brine, kaleidoscopic light. Wait here until eyes adjust.

B. Timber Catwalk (Cat’s-Eye)  
   • Pegged planks 2 cm from wall; dead spots in current. Primary observation perch.  

C. Central Dais (No-Step)  
   • Concave stone cone; slippery, subject to sudden jets. Admire from ≥ 2 m.

D. Twin Flume Throats  
   • 70 cm slots drawing water out. Suction mild in still window but fatal in surge. Keep limbs clear.

────────────────────────────────────────
8. Sensory & Experiential Highlights
────────────────────────────────────────
• Noon Cords: At local zenith, refracted sunlines appear solid; many travelers experience minor “occultation vertigo” as shadows fry into brightness.  
• Tone Ladder: Strike the iron hoops gently; each sings a perfect sub-aquatic hexachord. Bring a padded mallet if you are musically inclined.  
• Lime-Snow: During shutter closure, watch micro-flakes settle onto the catwalk like inverse snowfall—ghostly but harmless if not inhaled.

────────────────────────────────────────
9. Hazards & First-Aid
────────────────────────────────────────
Impact/Entrapment: If shutters reopen early, cling behind the lee of a pillar. Water accelerates to lethal speeds in < 12 s.

Acoustic Barotrauma: If you feel chest flutter, exhale slowly and retreat. Permanent tinnitus possible above 140 dB.

Chemical Burn: Rinse eyes with boric saline; neutralize skin with weak vinegar wipe.

Biofilm Rash: Itches like fiberglass; treat with mild steroid cream topside.

────────────────────────────────────────
10. Emergency Egress
────────────────────────────────────────
1. If Still Window unexpectedly ends:  
   – Anchor to iron hoop using mag-clip.  
   – Tuck inside acoustic shadow of pillar.  
   – Wait 4 h for next slack tide (rebreather required!).

2. If Breather Fails:  
   – Ascend shaft hugging interior wall; natural convection provides slow up-current you can ride without bubbles.

3. Medical: Builder camp has herbal poultices and boiled-salt compresses, but bring your own broad-spectrum antibiotics.

────────────────────────────────────────
11. Souvenirs & Leave-No-Trace
────────────────────────────────────────
Permitted: Sketches, hydro-phone recordings, samples of dried lime-snow (≤ 10 g) sealed in watertight ampoules.

Forbidden: Travertine chips, algae, iron hoop shavings, any artifact still in use.

Absolute Rule: Depart with everything you carried in—plus any stray microplastics you find.

────────────────────────────────────────
