Field Guide for a Human Traveller to the Breathing Hollow (Nalek)

Version 4.2 – compiled for multiverse wayfarers by the Quiet Cartographers’ Guild.  
Last eyewitness update: twelve lunar cycles ago.

-------------------------------------------------
0. First-minute summary (for the impatient)
-------------------------------------------------
• Altitude: a few metres below sea level, latitude comparable to Earth’s high Arctic.  
• Climate: +1 °C to +4 °C, windless, saturated air, perpetual soft drizzle, full solar/stellar glare.  
• Hazards: circadian drift, hypothermic fatigue, photosnow-blindness, slippery moss, cultural breach by loud noise or live-wood cutting.  
• Essentials: variable-tint goggles, layered wool or synthetics (no crinkle fabrics), felt-soled shoes, silencing sleeve for tech, mildew-proof pouches, small quiet gifts.  
• Prime etiquette: keep your voice under the volume of a calm conversational whisper; if in doubt, hum.  
• Legal: no metallurgy, no firearms, no combustion engines inside the rim.

-------------------------------------------------
1. Before you step through the Way
-------------------------------------------------
1.1 Vaccination & health
• Ordinary Arctic inoculations (tularemia, hantavirus) suffice.  
• Bring a broad-spectrum antifungal cream—boots never fully dry.  
• No endemic parasites known, but mosquitoes on the plain carry a mild soporific fever (72-hour recovery). Repellent is wise for the ascent/descent days.

1.2 Gear audit
Allowed  
• Soft textiles, leather, bone, glass, ceramic, low-luster metals in small pieces (knife, cooking cup) if sheathed and quiet.  
• Hand tools under 20 cm, pack weight <30 kg—switch-back path is narrow.

Discouraged  
• Anything that clacks, rattles, or whines. Tape loose buckles.  
• Gas stoves: vapour plume echoes when it strikes cold stone, taken as discourteous. Bring a solid-fuel alcohol brick or ask a host to borrow a dung brazier.

Forbidden  
• Chain saws, drones, personal music devices without bone-conduction headsets, firearms, explosives, powered refrigeration. Local rule is “no louder than the frogs.”

1.3 Circadian prep
Spend the last three nights before transit under full-spectrum lamps that never dim; take 30-minute micro-sleeps every six hours. This partly inoculates you against the Hollow’s timeless glare.

1.4 Gifts & trade items
Acceptable: needles, sinew thread, beeswax, pigments, etched story-shells, recipes.  
Unwanted: coins, electronic trinkets, bright dyes that bleed into water.  
Tradition: wrap gifts in woven grass and tie with silent knot loops—no crackling paper!

-------------------------------------------------
2. Approach & Descent
-------------------------------------------------
2.1 Locating the rim
The sinkhole lip looks like any other snow-fluted ridge. Listen: the wind roars everywhere except at the single breach where the path drops. That acoustic “hole” is your landmark.

2.2 The switch-back path
Width: one arm-span. Surface: damp limestone polished to ice, overgrown by cling-moss the locals call ankle-thief.  
Technique: lean in, two points of contact at all times, no trekking poles (they click). Descend at dawn when condensation is lightest and local guides are most alert.

2.3 Threshold etiquette
At the final bend you will see a birch lattice tower far below. Stop. Remove anything metallic that reflects daylight—sun-glints travel the whole bowl and are felt as a shout. Re-stow, then continue.

-------------------------------------------------
3. Living in Nalek
-------------------------------------------------
3.1 Light management
• Wear photo-adaptive goggles outside huts; remove them indoors to avoid appearing aloof.  
• Expect mild tension headaches days 2-3 from pupil over-constriction.

3.2 Moisture & chill
• Base layer: wool or synthetic that retains loft wet.  
• Mid layer: quilted moss-felt if you can bargain for it—astonishingly warm.  
• Outer shell: uncoated linen or animal skin; Gore-Tex crackles, so leave it behind.  
• Boots: felt or deerskin. Rubber squeaks against stone.

3.3 Sound discipline
Talking range is two arm-lengths. Beyond that, switch to hand signs (the locals’ “brush-script”: index finger draws short curves in the air).  
If you must cough, press mouth to sleeve. A single unmasked sneeze at midnight can wake ten platforms.

3.4 Shelter
Visitors are placed in the upper-most rim huts (Platform 7). Floor: birch slats, mattress: layered fern-fronds over seal-skin. Bring a silk sleep sheet to reduce skin rash from frond oil.

3.5 Food & drink
Staples: smoked char, pickled crowberry, frog-leg broth, glacier-barley flatcakes.  
Water: cup directly from any gutter junction. It is filtered through four metres of limestone; palatable, mineral-sweet. Share first sip with resident child or elder if offered—symbolic return of abundance.

3.6 Hygiene
• Latrine buckets are winched nightly to a fissure on the plain—respect schedule.  
• Hot bathing is monthly only; daily you “moss-wipe”: handful of fresh sphagnum squeezed over skin. Bring your own biodegradable soap-leaves; theirs are reserved for infants.

-------------------------------------------------
4. Work exchanges & keeping useful
-------------------------------------------------
Visitors are expected to contribute two “soft hours” (actual 90-120 Earth minutes; time is fuzzy) daily. Jobs on offer:

a) Gutter patrol – reseating stone tiles with silent wooden mallets.  
b) Fern pruning – snip only browning fronds; anything green lives.  
c) Weasel watch – follow the ivory-ferret; retrieve any dropped hatchling birds it abandons (they raise them as pest-hunters).  
d) Story-etch – copy oral histories onto bone using obsidian burin; scribes welcome.

-------------------------------------------------
5. Psychosocial & Phenomenological Notes
-------------------------------------------------
5.1 Time dilation
Travellers report the following scale: for every subjective 24 h spent in Nalek, chronometers outside record ~27 h 15 m ± 5 m. Cause unknown. Plan your rendezvous windows generously.

5.2 “Platform drift”
Measurable geometry of the settlement fluctuates by a few centimetres each dawn. Trust local ladders, not your own maps.

5.3 Acoustic suppression
Recording devices capture sound at −20 dB relative to source. Your micro-recorder will play back as if volume were halved. Do not rely on audio memos.

5.4 Emotional tone
Prolonged exposure (≥10 days) produces calm euphoria, low aggression, lowered speech volume after return. Reversible within two weeks.

-------------------------------------------------
6. Dangers & How to Mitigate
-------------------------------------------------
• Hypothermia creep: you feel merely cool but body core is falling. Thermometer patch on inner wrist; if below 35 °C, get to a hearth.  
• Slip falls: treat every horizontal surface as lubricated glass. Walk foot-edge to instep in a rolling motion (“seal walk”).  
• Eye burn: if you see purple halos around everything, retreat to shade for 4 h; ingest the local remedy—tincture of crowberry and frog bile (surprisingly effective).  
• Circadian freefall: schedule noise events (gentle chimes under a scarf) every 24 solar hours to enforce sleep cycle.

-------------------------------------------------
7. Leaving without offence
-------------------------------------------------
1. Inform your host family one full dawn beforehand.  
2. Offer a maintenance chore of your choosing; gutter clean-out is traditional farewell labour.  
3. At the rim, face into the bowl, draw three short arcs in the air (thanks, thanks, thanks). Speak nothing.  
4. Do not look back after your first footstep onto the plain; residents believe this pins wandering spirits to their roofs.

-------------------------------------------------
8. Souvenirs & export controls
You may carry out:
• Up to two woven moss-rope bracelets,  
• One jar (0.25 L) of gutter mineral sand,  
• Personal journal pages provided no platform schematics are included.

You may NOT export live moss, frogs, or any section of the birch lattice. Customs at adjacent realms will confiscate glowing spores on sight.

10. Final counsel
Enter the Breathing Hollow as you would enter a cathedral carved by weather itself: lightly, slowly, prepared to be smaller than your own voice. If you treat silence as the native language, light as the only music, and moss as the true proprietor, the Hollow will let you walk its luminous lungs in safety. Speak above a hush and the air will simply erase you from its memory—an outcome both merciful and, to some, unnervingly beautiful.

Travel softly.
