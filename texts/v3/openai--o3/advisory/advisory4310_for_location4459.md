Field Guide for Human Visitors  
Preservation Pocket 7-B (“The Violet Crucible”)

OVERVIEW  
You are about to step into a bowl-shaped cavern barely the size of a city block yet vaulting hundreds of metres overhead, lit by a single violet arc-lamp and carpeted with glass-like, waterless vegetation. Nothing inside is supposed to move, corrode, grow or die. Your simple presence therefore constitutes the single greatest disturbance the chamber has felt in geological time. Plan, dress and behave accordingly.

1. ENTRY & EXIT

• Legalities: No native society claims jurisdiction, but many multiverse conservancies list 7-B as an auto-protected “slow-entropy enclave.” Extraction teams have been fined for leaving fingerprints. Travel on a leave-no-trace, no-sample basis unless you carry a Class-III Exobiology permit.  
• Access point: a maintenance catwalk 40 m below the rim on the south interior wall. Follow the glow-strip until it terminates at a hatch marked “COND 62-A.” The hatch is usually free-spinning; if the torsion lock has re-seated, bypass with a magnetic torque key rather than forced leverage (brute force can topple adjoining ash columns).  
• Descent: a 70 m rope or abrasion-proof cable ladder. Mechanical ascenders are discouraged; harmonic vibration from the gas conduits sometimes kicks ratchets open. Use static-line techniques and keep three points of contact—perception of verticality drifts and you may feel as though you are leaning even when you are not.  
• Dwell time: recommended maximum, 6 standard hours. Low humidity and trace solvents dehydrate visitors far faster than they notice, and the bass drone begins to induce theta-wave trance after the third hour.

2. ATMOSPHERE & RESPIRATORY SAFETY

• Composition (mean values): 71 % N₂, 18 % O₂, 6 % Ar, 3 % CO₂, 1.2 % Cl₂/ClO₂ admixture, ppm-level organometallic vapour.  
• Immediate effects: Chlorine fraction is below acute-toxic threshold but will abrade mucous membranes on multi-hour exposure. Argon super-saturation displaces water from tissues, accelerating dehydration.  
• Gear: half-mask respirator with ABEK-P3 cartridges or a compact PAPR; carry one spare cartridge set per three hours. Full SCBA is overkill and introduces condensation that can permanently stain surfaces when venting.  
• Humidity: <1 %. Wear an oronasal moisture-reclaimer if staying longer than two hours.

3. CLOTHING & PERSONAL PROTECTION

• Outer layer: flexible ceramic-weave suit or Dyneema overalls—abrasion resistance is key; silicate moss blades shave ordinary fabric in minutes.  
• Footwear: approach boots with crushed-glass soles or any tread that grips polished stone.  
• Insulation: ambient 9–11 °C; lamp output never varies enough for thermal danger.  
• Eye/skin: wrap-around goggles to intercept floating mica. Micro-flakes behave like glitter until they reach tear film, where they etch corneas.  
• Electronics: store in ESD bags when not in use. The creeper-vines scavenge millivolts and will slowly bleed unshielded batteries.

4. HYDRATION & NUTRITION

• All water must remain sealed. Free liquid beads instantly attract solvent mist; resulting droplets etch dark scars into moss that may last millennia. Use bite-valve bladders with check valves.  
• Food: high-calorie dry rations only. Sugary vapour releases draw insects nowhere else found in the system—tiny ferro-gnats that may corrode aluminium foil. Carry organic waste back out; nothing here metabolises it.

5. MOBILITY & DO-NOT-TOUCH LIST

• Moss mats: support 160 kg per m² but behave like greased glass if you twist under load. Step straight down, lift straight up.  
• Columns: never free-climb more than five metres; latent resonance from steam lines can vibrate hand-holds loose without warning. If you must gain elevation, fix removable expansion bolts and reclaim them on descent.  
• Copper looms: live conductors. A brush from your pack frame can arc-singe you or the flora. Keep 40 cm clearance.  
• Mechanical plinth arms: look inert but may pivot violently when two droning frequencies synchronise. Stay at least two metres clear whenever the basso hum beats into a louder pulse.

6. PSYCHOLOGICAL EFFECTS

• Auditory illusions: standing waves subdivide the bowl into “whisper cells.” Prolonged focus translates the drone into recognisable voices reciting unfinished equations. This is a documented cross-modal hallucination; it ceases as soon as you leave the pocket.  
• Chrono-perceptual lag: because nothing obvious changes, subjective time dilates. Log time stamps every 30 minutes and set external alarms.  
• Claustro-vertigo: width says courtyard, height screams canyon. If dizziness strikes, sit facing a wall within arm’s reach; remove vertical lines from your peripheral vision.

7. ETHICAL HANDLING OF THE ENVIRONMENT

• Fingerprints remain indefinitely. Use nitrile contact gloves or carbon-mesh finger cots when you must steady yourself.  
• Sampling: every gram removed forces slow redistribution of trace minerals among the plants. Even a sliver of moss can starve an area the size of a handprint. Unless you hold that Class-III permit, do not collect.  
• Writing, tagging, laser-scanning: acceptable if your beam energy is below 5 mW and fully non-contact.  
• Do not introduce water. Even condensation from an uncovered thermos will kick off hydrolytic reactions no native organism can handle.

8. SCIENTIFIC & RECREATIONAL HIGHLIGHTS

• Observe mica “snowfall” at arc-lamp dusk (circa local 19:42).  
• Record infrasonic spectrum—resonances remain constant to 0.01 Hz over the last century; any deviation would constitute the discovery of active tectonics.  
• Photograph the glass-moss under raking violet light; each frond throws a diffraction halo that normal cameras capture without filters.  
• Attempt a whisper-cell triangulation: read coordinates where the drone syllabises identical phonemes. Researchers still map these nodes.

9. EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

• Gas spike: violet lamp flickers three short pulses if chlorine rises above 2 %. Don hooded SCBA and ascend immediately.  
• Structural shudder: if columns rattle for more than 15 seconds, it means an exterior turbine surge. Lay prone, head toward the nearest wall—the pocket was designed to channel debris upward, not outward. Wait vibration to cease, then evacuate.  
• Medical: chemical eye burn—flush with sterile saline inside a sealed waste bag, never directly onto the floor. Hypothermia—shake-activated heat packs, not open-flame stoves (oxygen is plentiful but solvent vapours combust unpredictably).

10. SOUVENIRS & DEPARTURE

• The only legal souvenir is data. Export your lidar maps, resonance logs and hyperspectral images.  
• Before climbing out, sweep the area with a UV torch to ensure you have not dripped sweat or left tool marks. A trail of salt crust can birth decade-long mineral blooms misread as contamination.  
• Reseal COND 62-A. A loose hatch bolt alters the airflow and will hum off-key forever; locals in adjacent hamlets treat the unchanged drone as weather. Do them the courtesy of leaving it that way.

If you pack it in, pack it out. Your goal is to let Pocket 7-B forget you the instant you leave—so that, when some other traveller arrives a millennium hence, the moss still glitters untouched and the viola-dusky light still finds no evidence that anything warm-blooded ever trespassed here.
