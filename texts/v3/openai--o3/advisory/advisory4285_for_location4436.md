TRAVEL DOSSIER: THE CERAMIC PLATEAU AT 3 000 m  
Audience: an ordinary but competent human who has access to multiverse transit and standard field equipment, not super-powers.

────────────────────────
1. CLIMATE & ALTITUDE
────────────────────────
• Elevation ≈ 2 900–3 100 m. Thin air; prepare as for a high Andean or Tibetan trek.  
• Temperature swing −5 °C predawn to +25 °C mid-afternoon, amplified by mirror glare.  
• Humidity Constant, but precipitation is hyper-local (fog, condensate showers). You will feel damp and sun-baked simultaneously.  
• Wind Variable. Pylons channel gusts into sudden, narrow jets; walking through one is like stepping into a wind tunnel for three seconds.

Preparation  
1. Spend at least two nights at 2 000 m in a friendly timeline first or carry acetazolamide.  
2. Pack layered synthetics that dry fast, plus a reflective over-cape (emergency foil blankets stitched together work).  
3. Polarized glacier goggles with side shields. They blunt the brilliance yet let you watch mirrors without retinas cooking. Never use fully silvered mirror lenses—see rule 1 later.

────────────────────────
2. FOOTING & LOCOMOTION
────────────────────────
Tiles: ungrooved, razor-edged, glass-slick when wet.  
Bronze ribs: raised 1–2 cm; stepping on them transmits vibration you can use as “machine sonar”.

Footwear  
• Approach shoes or light alpine boots with composite (not steel) shanks.  
• Fit removable silicone micro-stud soles (metal spikes slice tiles).  
• Carry two hands’ worth of self-adhesive felt pads; if a stair or ramp begins the Möbius trick, pad the threshold—that patch will remain stable long enough to retreat.

Edge security  
The plateau ends in sheer drops. A 15 m dyneema safety line with two auto-locking carabiners is enough; most bronze super-structure easily accepts a clip-in.

────────────────────────
3. LIGHT & RADIATION
────────────────────────
The ambient UV index is ≈ 11 due to altitude + tiled albedo + focusing mirrors.

• Broad-spectrum SPF 50 every exposed centimetre, reapplied every two hours.  
• Consider a light cotton sun-skirt (yes, regardless of gender) to shade legs on heat-throwing surfaces.  
• Avoid metallic jewellery: birds steal it, and it flickers in precognitive reflections, making orientation harder.

────────────────────────
4. WATER, FOOD & BIOLOGY
────────────────────────
Water  
Safe: bronze basins, condensation caught in cloth, mist captured over 10 min.  
Unsafe: flows that run uphill, flows that ring like struck crystal, anything pooling in cracked tiles with turquoise *and* orange lichen together.

Bring a 1-µm filter anyway; minerals are odd but not toxic.

Food  
Caloric self-sufficiency is recommended. Indigenous life is not proven edible (the mustelid phases, the birds contain chromium, the lamp-eels carry charge). If desperate, violet heat-grass tubers roast safely, taste of underripe mango, 40 kcal/100 g.

Avoid  
Touching moss with bare skin: mild phototoxicity under plateau UV.

────────────────────────
5. NAVIGATION IN FLEXIBLE GEOMETRY
────────────────────────
Instruments  
• Magnetic compasses stutter; rely on inertial nav in a wrist unit or smartphone with offline gyros, refreshed each dawn.  
• Bring 300 m of fluorescent micro-cord; staple or tie to ribs as “reality breadcrumbs”. The place respects continuous lines—cords rarely teleport.  
• A spring-wound kitchen timer is better than digital when temporal drift hits (digital clocks desync up to ±17 min per subjective hour).

Rules of thumb  
R1 Echo alignment rule (see surveyor note 3). If your footfall booms ahead of itself by more than 1.5 s, stop. Wait for sync.  
R2 Mirror foretelling. If a mirror shows you performing the action you’re *about* to take and you dislike the outcome, simply choose a new action; causality here is polite.  
R3 Seventh-sunrise reset. Keep a log; on day six before dusk, camp near a straight shot to the rim so a fresh labyrinth can’t trap you.

Following fauna  
If the translucent mustelid crosses you twice in the same direction, *shadow it at half a corridor’s distance until the geometry calms*. They know static routes.

────────────────────────
6. SOUND MANAGEMENT
────────────────────────
Silence is part of the structure.  
• Use soft-soled camp shoes around living architecture.  
• Speak quietly; loud shouts echo as spatial folds (you’ll feel a tug, like an elevator drop).  
• A cedar or sandalwood flute, played softly, seems to “pin” local space; some surveyors play a note every 50 m.

────────────────────────
7. MACHINERY & ELECTRIC HAZARDS
────────────────────────
Turbines operate at infrasonic RPM—harmless but disquieting.  
Flywheels have no guard rails; stay 2 m away: they are heavy enough that mis-spatial slip could bisect.

Every 45–90 minutes a pipe releases super-cooled vapor. White frost on bronze is warning; never stand downwind.

Lamp-eels at night: 30–120 V potential across their bodies. Do not wade.

Carry a 600 V rated insulating glove pair if you must clear metallic obstacles.

────────────────────────
8. CAMPING
────────────────────────
Best sites: recesses beneath inactive conveyor trestles—dry, shielded from mirror glare, acoustically “normal”. Lay an Aerogel pad; tiles leach heat fast.

Pitch tent with at least three anchor points drilled into grout lines (battery micro-drill kit). Stakes in ribs sing and can invoke geometry shift.

Night temperatures near freezing; a 10 °C-rated quilt plus reflective emergency liner suffices.

────────────────────────
9. EXIT STRATEGY
────────────────────────
Exfil route options  
A. Retrace cord breadcrumbs to southern talus where natural rock resumes.  
B. Wait for mirror-shadow projection of a large staircase that appears during equinoctial sunsets only; it exits 600 m downslope in stable scree. Check calendar.

Emergency “dead reckoning”  
If maps, cord and timepieces fail, descend *any* turbine hall until humidity tops 90 %. Follow water flow *with gravity only*. Flowing-uphill sections are traps; detour until water resumes gravity obedience. All downward-obeying water leads to genuine geology within 4–6 h.

11. CULTURAL / ETHICAL NOTES
────────────────────────
• Ownership is unresolved. Assume “leave no trace” ethics. Remove all cord and pads on exit unless stranded.  
• The plateau may itself be sentient or mid-sentient. Treat structures as art in progress. Hammering anchors sparingly and only in grout respects intent.  
• Do not harvest tiles or fauna; removal occasionally causes personal timelines to fray (documented).  
• In the unlikely event you meet builders, do not offer help—previous travelers report that cooperation accelerates the local slipping of geometry around them.

────────────────────────

Day 1 Dawn – step onto plateau, deploy cord hub.  
 Morning – collect baseline inertial map 500 m radius.  
 Afternoon – visit mirror courtyards, log pre-echo intervals.  
 Evening – camp under conveyor trestle, observe lamp-eels.  
Day 2 Sunrise – measure bronze-rib tick rate (correlates with power cycle).  
 Late morning – follow mustelid to validate safe loop.  
 Mid-day – climb tower for wide-angle photogrammetry.  
 15:00 – pull cord, retreat to rim before echo lag exceeds 1 s.  
 Night – descend to multiverse pickup below cloud deck.

────────────────────────
13. FINAL ADVICE
────────────────────────
Move unhurriedly; the plateau seems to respect deliberation.  
Trust gravity more than vision, rhythm more than geometry, intent more than maps.  
And when silence feels *heavy*, pause—space is catching its breath. You should, too.
