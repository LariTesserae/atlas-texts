Field Manual: The Laminated Basin (informal names: “Ash-Grey Foundry”, “Condensing Sea”, “The Slurry Works”)

Version 2.7 – compiled for ordinary human multiverse personnel (oxygen breathers, 1 g baseline).  
Latest corroborated by five survivor reports and two data drones lost in-situ. Read ALL sections before gating in; the Basin will not forgive improvisation.

────────────────────────────────────────
1. Snap-Judgement Hazard Index
• Thermal stress: Moderate (0 °C ± 3) but continuous wet-cold.  
• Chemical / respiratory: Moderate—micaceous dust, dissolved metals, catalytic ozone.  
• Mechanical: Severe—sinkholes, lateral soil slides, upward “anti-drops”, cryo-steam jets.  
• Exotic physics: Extreme—variable distance, local causality lag, sound-mass conversion.  
• Fauna aggression: Opportunistic but omnipresent.  
Overall rating: 8/10. Solo travel discouraged.

────────────────────────────────────────
2. Pre-Departure Checklist

A. Clothing
1. Base: moisture-wicking merino or synthetic grid fleece (avoid cotton).  
2. Shell: hydrophobic, non-rusting laminate suit rated to –10 °C; welded seams.  
3. Boots: dielectric soles, integrated crampon teeth, flexible gaiter seal to thigh.  
4. Sound-baffling layer: detachable felt overshoes or “quiet pads” to break footfall rhythm.  
5. Hands: heated dexterous gloves; exterior is matte to prevent ice-flower growth.  
6. Hood/visor: low-light visor with quick-clear nano-wipers; AR overlay for instrumentation.

B. Survival & Research Gear
• Rebreather mask with interchangeable HEPA/activated-charcoal cartridges (one every 6 hrs).  
• 20 m self-retracting monofilament tether (fluoropolymer; resists Thread-Siren discharge).  
• MEMS inertial pack with dead-reckoning logger; do NOT rely on compass or GPS analogues.  
• Beacon pair (quantum-entangled clock crystals). Leave one at entry. Range is absolute even when distance flexes.  
• Collapsible carbon-foam pontoon shoes for ankle–knee water sections.  
• 3-day sealed provisions + 2 L potable water/day. Basin water = mineral soup; filters clog in minutes.  
• Personal acoustics kit: directional subvocal mic + bone-conduction earpiece for team comms under 40 dB.  
• 2 L quick-setting aerogel to fill surprise cavities or build insulating platforms while resting.  
• Quiet cutting tool; hammering is forbidden (see Sound Protocols).

C. Optional / Specialist
• Sonic condenser (clap-trap) for deliberate thunder-slag generation—useful for barter or study.  
• Cold-steam turbine sampler (allows you to siphon Ore-Eater exhaust without frostbite).  
• Aurora stick (hand-held gravity flex detector; glows when local g spikes or dips).  

────────────────────────────────────────
3. Entry Strategy

Preferred: Gate or drop-pod onto the Basin rim, 5–20 m above floor level. The elevation lets you study ground flow before committing. If you must surface in the Basin proper, aim for exposed cobble “islands” no larger than a city bus—these sit on more stable sub-layers.

On arrival, stand still for 60 s. The soil often “exhales” when mass is added; letting it settle prevents immediate subsidence. Activate inertial logger and sync to beacon.

────────────────────────────────────────
4. Navigating a Place That Won’t Hold Still

1. Distance ≈ suggestion. Expect to walk 500 m physical effort for every observational “100 m” on HUD. Course-correct by elapsed time and inertial drift, not by horizon visuals.  
2. Pause every 17–23 min. Randomize the interval by ± 3 min to disrupt footstep loops (Echo-Wolf prophylaxis).  
3. Micro-slides: If the ground silently conveys you sideways, DO NOT STEP OFF. Ride the slab; trying to cross moving and stationary layers causes splits that swallow ankles and gear.  
4. Anti-drops: When aurora flares overhead, brace for transient weight changes (± 0.3 g typical). Keep knees bent; stow loose tools.

────────────────────────────────────────
5. Sound Protocols (most travellers who break these do not return)

• Ceiling for routine activity: 45 dB. Use whisper, gestures, laser-pointer codes.  
• Continuous rhythms quicker than 90 BPM attract Echo-Wolves. Vary stride length and tempo.  
• Avoid percussive strikes. Every >100 dB impulse spawns a thunder-slag projectile somewhere within 5 m—the pebble is harmless, the boom is not.  
• If you intentionally need slag (research, barter): cup condenser around mouth, vocalize a single 500-Hz note for 8 s. Step back. Pebble weighs ~30 g; collect with tongs (still warm).

────────────────────────────────────────
6. Fauna Field Conduct

A. Ore-Eaters
• Range: 3–6 m. They are short-sighted but vibration-sensitive.  
• Indicators: rising whistle/steam plume → about to vent.  
• Response: freeze; orient suit panels perpendicular to jet to shed frost. When the exhale stops they will trundle off. NEVER shine lights into their magnetite plates—reflection loops can create stray aurora ribbons overhead.

B. Thread-Sirens
• Mostly in 5 cm–30 cm water layer.  
• Keep boots clear; their cavitation pops can numb feet through rubber.  
• Static arcs target tallest conductive object. Collapse trekking poles before wading.

C. Echo-Wolves
• First sign: pitch-shifted duplicate of your last noise following half a beat behind.  
• Countermeasure: abrupt silence + wide-band white-noise burst from speaker (<2 s). Disorganises the knot.  
• If silhouette forms: light-stab with 10 k-lumen flashlight. They decohere in strong, sudden illumination.

General: The Basin is crowded but not predatory in the Earth sense. Treat every encounter as you would moving through factory floor aisles—give right of way to heavier equipment.

────────────────────────────────────────
7. Micro-Weather & Shelter

Tempest clock: 3–4 hrs. Look for pressure drop of 1 mbar + hum rising half an octave.  
Shelter on a knoll of cobbles. Deploy aerogel skirt around perimeter; it freezes into a low wall that sheds swirling crystals. Rest periods should not exceed 60 min; longer and the knoll may begin to migrate.

Sleep: use insulated hammock slung from two trekking poles driven into opposite sides of the aerogel wall. Keeps you above water film and outside Thread-Siren layer.

────────────────────────────────────────
8. Sustenance & Health

• Drink only carried water or freshly melted hail (collect during tempests; boil before use—dissolved metals are lower in precipitation).  
• No edible biomass on site. 3,600 kcal/day recommended; constant cold drains energy.  
• Frostnip feels like pins and needles + faint buzzing (landscape hum entraining nerves). Rewarm gradually; do NOT immerse in Basin water.  
• Psychological drift: persistent sense of déjà vu and “endless corridor” anxiety after 24 hrs. Log subjective time; take 5-HT2A blockers if panic emerges.

────────────────────────────────────────
9. Harvesting, Trade & Ethics

Thunder-slag is the only known universal token. Never remove more than 2 kg; local cycles appear to balance acoustic mass against gravitational flex—large deficits correlate with more violent anti-drops.

Do not interfere with the condensed-brine “plants.” They are critical frost nucleation sites; breaking them alters local weather engine.

Leave no continuous human-made rhythm (ditches, fences, pipes). The Basin can mistake them for metabolic pathways and upcycle them into unpredictable machinery.

────────────────────────────────────────
10. Exit Protocol

When mission time is up (or morale reaches 50 % of baseline), turn 90° LEFT from your incoming azimuth and walk until inertial odometer reads exactly 4.8 km. Ignore visual repetition. On the last 100 m stretch, hum a single atonal note (≠ rhythm) to prevent Echo-Wolf tailgating. You should emerge within 300 m of your drop point beacon; reclaim the paired crystal to verify universe fidelity before gating out.

If lost: trigger the beacon recall (both crystals resonate). A one-time 110 dB pulse will detonate surrounding supersaturated fog, creating a clear “corona” tens of metres wide and temporarily forcing distance to behave. Make for the rim during this window (≤15 min).

────────────────────────────────────────
11. Mindset

Assume you are not in wilderness but inside a half-sentient, ownerless factory whose safety systems have been replaced by biological improvisation. Curiosity is rewarded; routine is punished. Move inconsistently, listen carefully, make small quiet mistakes rather than large loud ones, and above all—never march to a steady beat.

Safe travels.
