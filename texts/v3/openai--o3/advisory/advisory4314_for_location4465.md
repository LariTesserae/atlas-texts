Field Briefing for the Basin & the Works (Condensa)

ISSUED TO Any baseline-human traveller with ordinary physiology and 21st-century Earth experience  
VALIDITY Three local years (one “tower overhaul” cycle). Conditions change slowly.  
OBJECTIVE Visit, observe, or reside short-term in the oval basalt basin where the condensation works stand.

────────────────────────────────────────
1. CLIMATE & GROUND CONDITIONS
────────────────────────────────────────
• Temperature 5 – 16 °C most days. Chillier at dawn, never outright freezing except on freak solstice nights.  
• Humidity 90 %+ year-round; mist is constant. Assume everything you wear or carry will become damp.  
• Precipitation Brief afternoon showers; drizzle at night; no standing water deeper than mid-calf unless you create it yourself.  
• Wind Calm inside the rim. Outside the lip the delta can gust suddenly—secure hats and loose tarps on the climb down.  
• Solar Radiation Sun is filtered through haze; UV index is low but not nil. Photosensitive travellers still need protection.

Ground
• Basalt is razor-edged where fresh; shoe soles and pet paws shred quickly.  
• Moss and biofilm make all smooth surfaces slick, yet pockets of soil can swallow a boot.  
• Magnetite seams are patchy; anything that relies on a steady magnetic reference (phone compass, drones, magnetometers, MRI implants) will misbehave.

────────────────────────────────────────
2. MEDICAL & BIOSAFETY
────────────────────────────────────────
Vaccinations/Prophylaxis
• Damp-lung fungi: take itraconazole or the local cedar-oil inhalant if you’ll stay longer than two weeks.  
• Aerborne gnat fever (mild, dengue-like): DEET analogue “Ferrox-33” sold dockside works; standard Earth repellent does half the job.  
• Tetanus up-to-date—basalt cuts fast, rust laces many handrails.

First-Aid Kit Add-Ons
• Butterfly closures for knife-shard cuts.  
• Desiccant packs for electronics AND medicinal powders; mold blooms inside pill bottles within days.  
• Broad-spectrum anti-fungal foot powder. Socks rot first.

Hydration
• Ironically, potable water is strictly rationed. Carry a personal flask and register it at the cistern office; you’ll be allotted 1 L per daylight segment (≈4 h).  
• Portable filters work, but bio-fluorescent moss releases oxalates that clog membranes. Bring extra pre-filters.

Diet & Allergens
• Local staples: alder-shaded mushrooms, river-eel jerky, condensed-milk bread. Check shellfish allergy—delta craylets flavor most broths.  
• Vegan travellers manage: moss-fed goat cheese is avoidable, fungi and reeds are abundant.

────────────────────────────────────────
3. GEAR CHECKLIST
────────────────────────────────────────
Clothing
• Boots: high-ankle, cut-proof rand, deep lug, aggressive heel brake. Break them in before arrival.  
• Base layer: merino or synthetic that keeps warmth when wet. Cotton mildews by sundown.  
• Outer layer: waxed canvas or eVent jacket—must both breathe and shed drizzle.  
• Head/Hands: thin wool cap; leather utility gloves (railings hide sharp barnacle-like mineral growths).  
• Optional: light shawl—locals prize shawls; they double as trade items.

Equipment
• Navigation: laminated 1:5 000 basin sketch (buy at River Confluence Inn) + good eyesight. Optical rangefinders OK. Forget compasses.  
• Lighting: headlamp with rubber gasketing (fluorescent moss won’t help you read gauges).  
• Electronics: vacuum-sealed pouches, corrosion-resistant ports, gyro rather than magnetometer orientation apps.  
• Walking stick or telescoping staff—doubles as depth probe for peat troughs.  
• Micro-spikes for descending the wet basalt rim path.  
• Small roll of sacrificial cloth tape: locals patch boardwalk gaps with anything handy; you’ll earn goodwill doing so.  
• Ear gear: not full plugs—you want to hear gongs and locomotives—but musician-grade filters soften the 24-h drone.

Gifts/Trade
• Silicone O-rings, marine grease, or stainless M8 bolts: priceless in a town at war with rust.  
• Seed packets of wind-tolerant flowers (calendula, nasturtium): fit the “incorporate, don’t erase” ethic.  
• Stories told slowly. Speed narrations are considered rude.

────────────────────────────────────────
4. APPROACH & ENTRY
────────────────────────────────────────
Routes
A. River Skiff Easiest. Hire a three-river pilot at Estuary Fork. Two-day meander through reeds, dock at the South Pier hoist.  
B. Ridge Trail Half-day trek from Updelta rail spur. Final 60 m descend ropes over basalt lip—picturesque, treacherous in fog.  
C. Air Drop Not recommended; thermals are unpredictable and locals dislike rotor wash over moss beds.

Customs Check
There is none in the bureaucratic sense, only the Water Clerk at the South Pier. She’ll:
• Count containers you bring.  
• Weigh your baggage (downward elevator capacity is limited).  
• Offer a three-sentence welcome: “We build upward, we drain downward, we hurry never.” Echo the last line back—polite formality.

────────────────────────────────────────
5. ORIENTATION INSIDE THE BASIN
────────────────────────────────────────
Landmarks (clockwise from South Pier)
1. Tower Three—the warm one; look for bats spiraling out at dusk.  
2. The Gong Arch—brass hammers strike every 15 min; time your footbridge crossings so you’re not standing underneath during the peal.  
3. Alder Gallery—mini-forest irrigation pipes; pleasant midday rest spot.  
4. Black Wave Ridge—highest basalt crest; weak magnetism here if you really must try a compass.  
5. Furnace Row—five coal-steam forges; ambient noise can mask a loco until it’s two metres away.

Sound Corridors
• Whisper on Furnace Row boardwalk = audible clear at Tower Three landing.  
• Conversely, shouting from the South Pier dies inside 5 m; hand signals standard.  
Learn the major corridors on day one to avoid accidental eavesdropping or the reverse problem—calling for help unheard.

────────────────────────────────────────
6. DAILY RHYTHM
────────────────────────────────────────
0400 Mist thickens; boardwalks are slickest. Crews scrape moss from critical rails.  
0600 Sun disc shows; bat sheets return; first gong pattern (three strikes) initiates valve cycle.  
0900 Locos begin supply runs. Step off tracks—no horns until they clear Furnace Row.  
1200 Brief rain shower; tower louvers half-close; sounds dull. Ideal nap window.  
1500 Children released from workshop-school; wave offerings of moss terrariums. Accept graciously.  
1800 Evening pewter light; herons duel; second major condensation haul—cisterns overflow into alder roots.  
2300 Machine noise tapers; frogs crescendo; lone foghorn bleats mysteriously. Locals treat this as curfew bell.

────────────────────────────────────────
7. CULTURAL ETIQUETTE
────────────────────────────────────────
• Pacing Walk as if balancing a bowl of water. Running suggests emergency.  
• Speech Lower register, measured cadence; let sentences breathe between phrases.  
• “Equilibrium” Ethic Take nothing unneeded. If you harvest a reed for a whistle, weave its leftover sheath into catwalk caulking.  
• Water Rights Do not pour, spill, or toss water casually. Even rinsing a cup is done over a planter box.  
• Tools on Loan Return shinier than received. A wiped rag counts as interest.  
• Noise Offerings At dusk, many visitors gift a single deliberate sound (flute note, poem, spoon on rail) into the basin. Choose carefully—echoes travel strange and long.

────────────────────────────────────────
8. HAZARDS & EMERGENCY PROTOCOL
────────────────────────────────────────
Cuts & Falls
• Protocol “Blue-Black”: yell once, stay still. Three residents will materialise with a treated alder plank stretcher; they know hidden shortcuts.

Magnetic Disorientation
• Symptoms: spinning phone compass, light nausea, stumbling. Sit on wooden surface, eyes closed, three deep breaths; nausea passes. Locals carry chalk to mark safe heading arrows on rails—use them.

Flash Flood Micro-bursts (rare)
• Short downpour can fill peat troughs knee-deep in 90 s. Climb the nearest iron pier ladder. Every pier tops above the documented high-water mark.

Wildlife
• Dragonflies bite only shiny jewelry; tuck it.  
• Otters steal lunch sacks; suspend food in net bags.  
• Bats carry few pathogens but guano is caustic—avoid footing areas under Tower Three after storms.

Fire
• Basalt is non-flammable; wood boardwalk is. Water cannons mounted on every second tower power-spray distilled water. Obey the triple rapid gong if it sounds—follow arrow plates to refuge platforms.

────────────────────────────────────────
9. DEPARTURE
────────────────────────────────────────
• Re-dry gear on Alderside kiln racks 24 h before exit; mold sniffers at upstream customs fine spoiled baggage.  
• Leave one useful object behind (nails, poem, measured drawing). Locals log gifts in a “slow ledger”—names fade ink-wise over decades but deeds persist in gossip.  
• Stamp your passbook at the Water Clerk’s desk; the imprint is copper sulfate—turns verdigris green as it ages, a souvenir that keeps damp memory alive.

────────────────────────────────────────
10. SUMMARY: MINDSET
────────────────────────────────────────
Arrive curious, move unhurried, speak softly, trade fairly, and remember the basin’s prime directive: whatever enters should leave a roughly equal weight—if not of matter, then of story, care, or repair. Pack for wet chill, sharp rock, and long echoes. Give the place time; it will give you perspective distilled one droplet at a time.
