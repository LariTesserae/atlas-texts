Field Manual for Multiverse Travelers
Destination: The Hollow Cathedral of Heat and Rust  
(Working designation: Laterite-2/Basaltsink)

0. Snapshot
• Environment: Enclosed lava chamber, 20 m high, perpetually > 38 °C, near-total darkness broken by derelict sodium- and arc-lamps.  
• Surface: vitrified basalt glass, unforgivingly slick and razor-edged where fractured.  
• Biota: exclusively plant-like chemotrophs that metabolise iron and sulphides. No animals, no active microbes known to infect humans—yet.  
• Hazards: heat stress, laceration, ferrous corrosion of equipment, falling debris, electrically “live” surprises, unknown plant chemistry, psychological isolation.  
• Objective rewards: unique bio-ferro catalysts, pristine columnar basalt samples, archive-grade photographs of post-industrial geomorphology.

1. Legal & Dimensional Status
The chamber sits outside any recognised sovereign claim in its parent continuum; no indigenous sapients, no extant regulatory signage. Standard Multiverse Ethical Charter (Fifth Revision) therefore applies:  
• Take nothing alive or irreplaceable.  
• Leave no synthetic biocontaminants.  
• Record provenance for all samples ≥ 10 g.  
• Extract yourself if interference appears to accelerate ecological change by > 0.5 % (visual estimate).

2. Reaching the Chamber
A. Known Access Points  
   1. The ajar submarine hatch at ground level—most stable.  
   2. Vertical utility shaft 40 m west (requires single-rope technique; top exit opens into an abandoned smelter).  
B. Transit Windows  
   • Avoid entry during geothermal surge days (air ≥ 42 °C). Temperature graph from the plant spore bursts yields a sinusoid of ~11-day periodicity; arrive on Day 3–7 for tolerable 38–40 °C.

3. Personal Kit
Mandatory  
• Footwear: vulcanised rubber approach shoes with kevlar or ceramic toe caps; no metal eyelets (plants will “taste” them and etch through in < 3 h).  
• Lower body: rip-stop aramid trousers with built-in knee pads—glass shards are omnipresent.  
• Upper body: moisture-wicking base layer, breathable ceramic-weave shirt. Bring a reflective emergency poncho; it doubles as a heat mirror if temperature spikes.  
• Hands: cut-proof gloves, silicone grip.  
• Head/face:  
  – Climbing helmet with detachable mesh visor (falling rock, hot droplets).  
  – Half-mask respirator, P100 and acid-gas rated. Spore load is low but their chelating vapours irritate alveoli after ~2 h.  
• Vision: full-spectrum LED headlamp (4000–4500 K), CRI ≥ 90. Plants barely react to short visits of white light. Avoid high UV.  
• Hydration: 3 L minimum, in composite or glass containers. Stainless will pit visibly in minutes; aluminium disintegrates.  
• Cooling: phase-change neck band or gel pack; expect zero evaporative relief (RH ~95 %).  
• First Aid: superficial lacerations and heat collapse dominate incident logs. Add chelation ointment (calcium EDTA) for iron-rust burns.  
• Comms: short-wave radio or UWB mesh nodes; rock attenuates EM heavily—leave a repeater at entrance.

Optional/Scientific  
• Portable FTIR or XRF wand—iron/sulphur ratios map plant metabolism.  
• Non-ferrous corer for petrified tissue sampling.  
• Acoustic geophone: to listen for the sub-basalt turbine pulse (academic interest).

4. Behavioural Protocol
• Walk, don’t scramble. Glass-floor slip accidents have a 68 % correlation with decompression sickness upon later return due to unnoticed knee cuts inoculated by oxide particles.  
• Zero ferrous litter: buttons, coins, paperclips—leave them topside. Losing a multitool to a vine’s corrosive trumpet is inconvenient (and spectacular).  
• Lighting etiquette: lamps < 300 lumens, five-second bursts. Continuous flood causes spore rise after 20 min.  
• Touch nothing warm. The living vines peak at ~45 °C; accidental grip can blister.  
• Sound discipline: the ecosystem cues on vibration. Heel-stomping has triggered mass spore releases in two documented cases. Soft-sole steps, conversational volume < 50 dB.

6. Time Management
Recommended single exposure: ≤ 4 h.  
If you must stay longer, establish a bivy on the catwalk, deploy reflective tarp above and insulating mat below. Sleep cycles are disorienting; run a 90-min timer to avoid drifting into > 2-h REM where mild hypercapnia risk rises (poor air exchange).

7. Scientific & Commercial Yield
a. Bio-ferro Catalysts: root mucilage accelerates low-temperature rusting by 300×. Potential green-steel applications.  
b. Self-fossilising Tissue: cross-sections display nano-capillaries now mineral-clogged; promising template for ceramic foam manufacture.  
c. Columnar Basalt: perfect hexagonal prisms, 20 cm across—valuable for decorative architecture if export is licensed.  
Ethics note: remove only fallen fragments; never lever fresh columns.

8. Exit & Decontamination
• Soft brush wipe-down in vestibule; hose-style wash is impossible (no standing water).  
• Inspect gear for oxidising slime. Neutralise with sodium bicarbonate slurry.  
• Strip respirator filters and seal in mylar for hazardous waste disposal—spent cartridges off-gas iron chelates for ~18 h.  
• Log spore count on your person with handheld particle counter; shower within 2 h.

9. Psychological Aftercare
Travelers report:  
• Temporary anhedonia (“nothing tops that silence”).  
• Rumination on geologic timescales.  
Countermeasure: re-expose to wind, birdsong, or crowds within 24 h; keeps sensory baselines intact.

10. Returning
Expect modest changes over years. GPS-relative markers drift less than 5 mm a year (chamber is geologically stable). However, lamp infrastructure continues to sag; bring outriggers or your own light scaffolds on repeat visits.

Concluding Advice
The Hollow Cathedral rewards patience and restraint. Enter as a guest, move as though the floor were a museum exhibit still being installed, and remember: down here, heat is the sun and iron is the air. Treat both with the awe you’d give a star and an ocean, and you’ll leave unscarred—save perhaps for a permanent recalibration of what “alive” can mean.
