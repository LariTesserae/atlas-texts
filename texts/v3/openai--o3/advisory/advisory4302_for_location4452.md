Travel Briefing – Living Root-Cathedral Nursery  
(Continuum index: “Pink-white lung”, “Fluorescent driproom”, “Phase-pulse grotto”)

OVERVIEW  
You will be entering the first embryonic chamber of a planet-scale botanical super-organism that is still annexing the surrounding cave system. Temperatures hover just above 0 °C, humidity is 100 %, gravity is 0.93 g and the space is electrically, magnetically and acoustically alive. Nothing in the hall is inert; even the “floor” continuously knits and re-knits itself. Your prime tasks are therefore: 1) avoid killing or panicking the room, 2) avoid hypothermia, 3) keep your navigation and sensor gear from being confused by the endogenous field.

1. VACCINATIONS & MEDICAL PREP  
• No pathogens recognizable as bacteria or virus have been cultured, but the root-mass releases micro-RNA flakes that can temporarily suppress mammalian immune signalling. Receive a broad-spectrum interferon booster 24 h pre-jump.  
• A mild vasoconstrictor patch (ear-back placement) helps you tolerate the cold without steaming excessively—vital, because your surface heat signature is a disturbance signal to the plant.  
• Bring a magnetically shielded auto-injector of calcium gluconate: a defensive crab species can discharge cold-shock peptides that lock skeletal muscles for minutes. One 10 ml dose breaks the paralysis.

2. PERSONAL KIT CHECKLIST  
Cold-Wet Garment System  
• Base: hydrophobic silk/graphene weave (permits transpiration but sheds condensate).  
• Mid: aerogel-baffled vest with body-heat recapture micro-peltier, power draw <3 W.  
• Shell: non-abrasive silicone rainskin, colour soft violet or teal (matches native reflectance curve and reads as “not predator”). Hard fabrics will scrape membranes—avoid.  
Boots  
• Split-toe, soft-soled, siped for traction on living root tissue. Integrated psi-gum soles damp vibration so the plant’s mechano-sensors do not read each of your steps as micro-quakes.  
Respiratory  
• The air holds ample O₂ but pressure is ~90 kPa. Most travellers acclimate after five minutes, however carry a re-breather mouthpiece that can add two per-cent O₂ and scrub excess CO₂ during exertion surges.  
Lighting  
• White lamps blind both you and the room; instead use a helmet band of 410 nm “pulse-sync” LEDs keyed to every twelfth native photonic beat. They ride the plant’s own signalling window and appear invisible to fauna.  
Navigation  
• Compass, gyro and accelerometer all drift inside the internal magnetic field. Fit your wrist slate with a fibre-optic dead-reckoning loop and drop millimetre-scale optically tagged breadcrumbs (dissolve harmlessly in 36 h).  
Comms  
• Conventional radios fuzz. Ultrasound burst modem (17–22 kHz) penetrates tissue without trigging fauna-calls.  
Sampling tools (optional)  
• Only monomolecular ribbon scalpels; removals must not exceed 0.1 g per m² surface or growth repair kicks into defensive hyper-mode.  
Waste protocol  
• Urine is acceptable (plant will strip it); feces are not—bag and export. Salts jam the pool-eels’ electro-digestion.

3. APPROACH & ENTRY  
• Enter only during the six-hour “active surge”. A tide chart is appended; sync to it or the hall will be dormant, lightless and acoustically dead, raising collision risk.  
• Announce yourself by exhaling sharply three times on the threshold. CO₂ spikes are recognized by the mites, which then pause pruning so you do not crush them inadvertently.  
• Walk heel-to-toe, weight evenly distributed. A footfall of >0.35 kN can rupture sap closer than two metres.

4. CONDUCT INSIDE  
Touch etiquette  
• One gloved fingertip per surface is fine; palms transfer too much heat.  
• Absolutely never sever a translucent tube—the sap stream is the hall’s circulatory bus. A break creates a phase-field “hiccup” that paralyses every creature (including you if you have ferrous implants).  
Acoustic etiquette  
• Speech volume no greater than a murmur. Phonemes in the 1.6–1.8 kHz band overlap the crabs’ launch signal and will trigger spore detonations.  
• To test echoes or distances, clap once and wait a full minute: the returning root-groan supplies richer data than sonar.  
Illumination etiquette  
• Your pulse-sync LEDs must remain at ≤3 lux average. If the room flashes turquoise you have accidentally made yourself a routing node; dim immediately and stay still until brightness subsides.

5. HAZARDS & FIRST AID  
Slip/fall  
• The mattress floor rebounds slowly; a tumble is unlikely to injure you, but can bruise the tissue. Spray a pea-sized dollop of chitosan sealant onto any dent you make; it tells the plant “all clear”.  
Hypothermia  
• Symptoms creep in after 45 min. The room’s reverse-metabolism leeches heat faster than water alone would. Cycle your peltier vest to active once pulse illumination drops for the third time—this is the coldest part of the surge.  
Phase-field shock  
• If you damage a conduit and everything (including fauna) freezes, do NOT move. Wait until drips restart (30–90 s) before seeking exit. Sudden motion during a field dropout triggers a mechanical stiffening that can trap limbs.  
Faunal encounters  
• Ribbon eels may breach pools to investigate lights. Remain still; they will taste the air with infrared tongue-flicks then withdraw.  
• Crabs are harmless unless stepped on; if one lands on you, return it to the nearest wall—it cannot re-orient on the floor.

6. EXIT & DECONTAMINATION  
• Withdraw along your breadcrumb line, collecting tags on the way.  
• At least 40 m from the chamber, strike boots together: mites sense magnetic orientation and will disembark to return home.  
• In transporter foyer, run a five-minute IR bake at 42 °C to destroy surface spores, then a 15-second ultrasonic bath; do NOT use bleach—it denatures their quantum-coherent proteins, causing long-term pollution of the cave system.  
• Final step: breathe onto a cold mirror. If condensate fluoresces blue, residual signalling droplets remain on mucosa; rinse mouth and sinuses with isotonic saline until the glow fades.

7. ETHICAL & SCIENTIFIC NOTES  
• The nursery hall is effectively a foetal organ of a continental megaflora. Disturbance scales; a single broken root here can translate to kilometre-scale load redistribution elsewhere.  
• Because the organism already performs high-dimensional computation, every physical perturbation you cause is stored as data. Visit only if your presence justifies adding permanent “noise” to its memory.  
• All samples, images and telemetry are classified C-Green; release requires review by a xenobotanist and a digital-ethics officer.

Everything else is optional luxury.

If you respect the timings, whisper your way forward and treat every surface as both skin and circuit board, the root-cathedral will accept you as weather rather than threat. Enjoy the slow turquoise heartbeat of one of the oldest intelligences in the multiverse—just remember that each footstep is entered into its archive forever.
