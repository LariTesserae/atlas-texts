PRE-DEPARTURE BRIEF  
Destination name (local): The Cathedral-Mesa, or simply “The Bowl.”  
Depth: 38–42 m below mean sea level, sitting atop a drowned volcanic table-mountain that rises thousands of metres from abyssal floor.  
Climate: Temperate open-ocean water, 9–14 °C year-round.  
Atmosphere: None—entire site is submerged; breathable air exists only inside lithodendron cavities.  
Native society: Amphibious air-breathers (human baseline) called Rim-kin. Population ≈ 400.  
Core taboos: 1) Prolonged silence, 2) cutting live lithodendron without consent of the Root Elders, 3) introducing free-floating metal.  

1. Physiological preparation  
• Diving qualification: Be competent at 40 m depth on a closed-circuit rebreather (CCR) or at minimum sport/advanced nitrox certification. Ambient pressure is ~5 atm; unacclimated travelers risk nitrogen narcosis after 20–30 min on air. If CCR is unavailable, use trimix 21/35 or leaner.  
• Decompression: Even though you can surface directly in an emergency, decompression stops at 12 m and 6 m (3 min each) are advised after any stay >15 min to avoid DCS. A spare 40% nitrox stage slung under the left arm streamlines you through the lithodendron aisles.  
• Thermoregulation: The water feels “pleasantly cool” only if you never drop core temperature. Pack a 5–7 mm semidry or a compressed-neoprene drysuit with low-profile rings (metal rings must be taped in dull eelgrass-green). Gloves are mandatory; you will need bare fingertips only inside air chambers.  
• Audiological health: Continuous sub-bass resonance reaches 115 dB underwater. Bring vented ear-plugs or digital bone-conduction dampers that shave 15 dB without muting. A week’s exposure otherwise causes fatigue and mild arrhythmia.

2. Gear checklist  
Hard items to leave behind:   
 • Bright metal (copper and iron leach tastes that repel plankton grazers and offend locals).  
 • Combustion engines (exhaust bubbles disturb plankton pillars).  
  
Recommended kit:  
 1. Rebreather + two 2-L O₂ diluent cylinders (all housings anodised matte green).  
 2. Compact ceramic multi-tool; flake-edge style mimics native obsidian.  
 3. Acoustic slate (ultrasonic stylus + piezo panel) for taking notes that aren’t ruined by silt.  
 4. A “drum gift”: pocket-sized resonant object—wood envelope filled with seed-pebbles—to break ritual silence on arrival.  
 5. 500 g packet of dried citrified fruit: vitamin C is scarce underwater and trades at 5 pearls per slice.  
 6. UV-safe lantern (violet 405 nm) for reading kelp-paper scrolls: normal white LEDs bleach ink.  
 7. Biolum safe-tube—transparent sleeve that keeps captured lantern-corals alive if you earn permission to carry light of your own.  
 8. Surface marker buoy for rendezvous with topside extraction skiff; choose a muted algae-green bag to avoid manta-leaf harassment.  
 9. “Silt etiquette” fins: frog-kick-optimised, shorter than standard scuba fins to keep dust down.  
 10. Hydrophobic notebook & grease pencil for personal records inside air pockets (ink fumes sicken fireflies).  

3. Arrival, anchoring & first contact  
A. Descent corridor  
Approach from leeward (south-east) so surge drifts you toward the horseshoe rim rather than slamming you into it. The lithodendron police themselves; any sudden fin-beats that clang buttress walls are interpreted as aggression by resident chain-eels.  
B. Announce yourself sonically before you are in sight. Three even palm-thumps on your own chest plate then a pause tells Rim-kin “visitor, peaceful, single.” They will answer with two trunk thuds from inside the cathedral.  
C. As you fin through the nearest “window” between trunks, keep both hands visible, palms out. Do not touch dangling root-cords—they are sensory organs.  

4. Moving and breathing inside  
• Entry slits to air pockets are vertical. Fin gently upward, break the surface NOSE FIRST (so your exhaust valve is last to clear, preventing sudden whoosh that over-pressurises chamber membranes).  
• Remove mouthpiece immediately; Rim-kin converse while half-floating, lips kissing the water. Loud lungs signify greed—exhale in short puffs and hold neutral buoyancy via breath.  
• In air: switch to a light fleece layer under drysuit or unzip neck seal for comfort, but keep torso damp—rapid drying is a sign of arrogance (“desert-skin”). Fireflies dim if temperature rises above 23 °C.  
• Lighting etiquette: Torch beams on the walls blind the insects; bounce your UV lamp off your palm.

5. Communication primer  
The language, Risso, is mostly clicks plus air-cough consonants. You can be understood with:  
 • t’k-t’k (double front click) = greeting.  
 • hrr’ch (short throat cough) = thanks/okay.  
 • thoooom (cup palms on water to boom) = request for council.  
Carry a tuned bamboo clapper (locals will loan you one) to relay longer messages through the vascular pipes. Speech too soft may travel for kilometres; so always identify yourself with the signature double-click at start and end.

6. Social expectations  
• No silence festivals: Each night before plankton “daybreak” everyone drums roots to keep sap flowing. You are expected to take at least one shift; follow the clave pattern you hear—improv is frowned upon.  
• Root title law: You gain guest status from the trunk that first touches you. Never argue across a graft line; relocate instead.  
• Trade: Marble pearls, lantern-corals, and manta-leaf vellum. You can legally purchase pearls (about walnut size) at 8 dried-fruit coins apiece. Export of living lithodendron cuttings is capital taboo.  
• Courtesies: Move slowly; never force currents with flapping gestures. Accept offered eelgrass tea even if warm brine repels you—sip, do not gulp (slurping equals storm mockery).

7. Hazards & countermeasures  
Physical:  
 • Surge cycles every 6.2 h; use natural pauses (≈5 min) to cross open plazas.  
 • Chain-eels bite shiny objects; stow mirrors and polished lenses.  
 • Lithodendron sap coagulates on exposure to air; if you cut yourself, rinse immediately with seawater or a hard scab will glue your suit shut.  
Biological:  
 • Balloon-jelly hides among root cords; stings cause 4-h euphoria then migraine. Ocean antihistamine tablets neutralise venom.  
 • Infrasound fatigue: After 3–4 days, heartbeat may entrain to the 0.7 Hz swell bass line. Do a daily 10-min topside float to reset vagal tone.  
Psychological:  
 • Low-contrast lighting and continual resonance induce “Cathedral spell” (mild dissociation, desire to never surface). Keep a wrist chrono that vibrates hourly—glance at it, name today’s date aloud.

8. Sample 48-h itinerary  
Day 0 (evening tide): Stage cylinders on extraction skiff, descend during slack water, make contact, stow kit in visitor alcove of trunk #17.  
Day 1: Dawn plankton clouds = local “day.” Observe manta-leaf grazing. Noon: join root-drumming class. Late tide: swap to nitrox, collect chain-eel skin for souvenir (allowed). Sleep in lenticular hammock strung under buttress arch; rocking matches your REM cycle.  
Day 2: Guided cliff-rim tour, watch stonefin carp produce pearls. Conclude with gift exchange and decompression ascent timed for solar zenith to admire caustic-light pillars.  

9. Exit & decompression  
Depart on ebb so long-period swells sweep you seaward, away from fragile rim. Inflate surface marker 6 m depth and complete deco. On deck, rinse suit in fresh water; bikinite-scale plankton die on contact and smell rancid if left to dry. Record ear pressure anomalies—5% of travelers retain a low humming for weeks (harmless).

10. Long-term involvement  
Visitors who stay a full lunar cycle may request initiation as “Root Listener.” This grants a private alveole (air pocket room) and duty nights monitoring sap viscosity. Plan months ahead; it requires fasting from spoken vowels and surrender of all metal tools for duration.  

Remember: The Bowl endures because everything there grew patient. Pack light, move slow, keep the drumming alive, and the sea will let you listen to its ribs without ever once mistaking you for prey or noise.
