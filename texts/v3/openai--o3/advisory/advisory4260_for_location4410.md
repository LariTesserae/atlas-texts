Field Guide for the Human Multiverse Traveler  
Destination: The Permanently-Stormed Abyss (Hadopelagic Plain No. 7-β)

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
1. Pre-Departure Medical & Psychological Conditioning
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
• Barophysiology screening: Verify cardiovascular health up to 1,300 atm. A reinforced thoracic stent (“rib-cage girdle”) is strongly advised; it prevents micro-compression fracture during rapid suit pressurization.  
• Vestibular recalibration: Three weeks in a zero-visual, gyro-pod maze trains the inner ear to navigate by proprioception and micro-current feel alone.  
• Darkness acclimation: Practice tasks inside a black-tank flooded with chilled brine (−1 °C). Goal: maintain executive function for 90 minutes without photons or audible speech.  
• Hypothermic mindfulness: Learn “peripheral silencing,” a breathing pattern that shifts warm blood away from extremities to core while preventing panic. The deep cold here is more soporific than painful; travelers untrained in detecting creeping lethargy simply fall asleep and never wake.

2. Essential Equipment
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
A. The Pressure Exoskin  
   • Monolithic weave of non-porous graphene/kevlar sprayed onto an under-mesh; no seams, no fasteners—any cavity becomes catastrophe.  
   • Integrated osmotic gills strip dissolved O₂; carry a two-hour high-pressure bail-out tank in case of local anoxia.  
   • Micro-hydraulic muscles amplify swimming kicks; walking is impossible on the mobile seafloor.  
   • Active-heat ceramic nodes (powered by slow Pu-238 pellet, 200 W) keep core at 37 °C; waste heat radiates invisibly in infra-dark frequencies predators do not sense.

B. Navigation Kit  
   • Triple-redundant inertial unit with drift-correct algos keyed to deep-earth tidal tug; magnetic compasses fail—iron-rich silt scrambles them.  
   • 40 m electrum tether reel. One end attaches to you, the other to a tungsten grave-spike you hammer into basalt. Retrieve and leapfrog every 300 m; forget breadcrumbs—currents erase them.  
   • Sonar whisper-ping: 9 Hz pulses, amplitude capped at 30 dB re: 1 µPa to minimize ecological disturbance. High-power sonar provokes the large ridge-finned shadow.

C. Illumination Discipline  
   • Primary lamp: dimmable, 420 nm narrow-band (“dark-violet”) that many abyssal eyes cannot detect.  
   • Work light: white, 5-second duty cycle only. Rule-of-thumb: if you must read an instrument, do it once, then kill the beam. Remember Rule 3, “Light is an event.”  
   • Chem-tags: single-use adhesive pellets that emit sub-luminal phosphorescence visible only inside 2 m—good for marking equipment caches.

D. Tool & Sample Pack  
   • Scoop-vac set to gentle suction; anything vigorous builds a silt cloud that blinds you for hours.  
   • Corer tubes pre-pressurized to trench ambient; pressure loss on ascent will aerosolize your sample if you cheap out here.  
   • Sterile nutrient ampoules for optional bacterial barter (see section 6).

3. Transit & Entry Protocol
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
• Descent vessel: one-person drop-pod, external shape a solid teardrop of sintered tungsten carbide. Internal space is filled by you already inside the Exoskin, immobilized in viscoelastic gel. The pod carries no air pocket; it is flooded. Pressure equilibrium avoids catastrophic implosion per Local Rule 4.  
• Descent timeline: 4 hours from surface to 9,600 m, then another 40 min to plain depth (≈11,200 m). You emerge through an iris port; pod stays as stationary evac beacon.  
• Do NOT try to swim up—ever. Exoskin hydraulics cannot overcome 11 km of water column. Evac requires recall of the pod’s grapnel winch or rendezvous with scheduled retrieval sub (window every 40 hours, ±3).

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
4. Locomotion & Orientation Once In-Situ
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
• Stay buoyancy-neutral. Add or vent iridium micro-shot in your ballast sleeve; silt storms will bury anything resting too long.  
• Read the water: Lateral shear feels like opposing hands on either side of your body. When it strengthens, expect vortex knots within minutes—turn broadside and ride the flow until it eases; swimming against it wastes glycogen.  
• Elevation cues: Slight warmth and sulfur scent => nearby vent; plankton glow spikes => predator lane; constant chill, low particulate => mid-plain “quiet cell,” safest for overnight stationing.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
5. Environmental Hazards & Counter-Measures
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
1. Hyper-density Bruising  
   • Even slow collisions hit like punches; keep 1 m standoff from rock. The Exoskin’s gel-bladder collar inflates on sudden deceleration.  

2. Sand-Blinding  
   • If visibility <20 cm, kneel-float facing current, cover lamp, wait. Moving raises a personal dust-storm. Average wait time 17 min; worst recorded 2 h. Thermogenesis protocol will keep you conscious.

3. The Ridge-Finned Shadow  
   • Avoid continuous noise >60 dB or broad-spectrum light >10 s. If encountered, cut all emissions, curl into minimum silhouette, remain still. Its hunting is curiosity-triggered; lack of stimulus bores it.

4. Hypothermic Drift  
   • Heads-up display flashes amber when core <35 °C; initiate isometric muscle contractions, cycle heater to 120%. Sleep deprivation worsens risk; use 20-minute micro-naps anchored to tether.

5. Psychological Saturation  
   • Many travelers report “crowding of fundamentals” hallucination—sense that darkness, weight and cold are entities leaning on you. Pre-trip training mitigates this, but carry a single earbud player of familiar sounds to re-assert personal narrative (volume low).

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
6. Interactions With Local Life
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
• Microbial mats: Harmless if untouched; slicing them releases sulfurous haze obscuring sonar. If sampling, inject nutrient ampoule first to encourage localized thickening, then skim peripheral layers.  
• Isopods: Mildly inquisitive. If one folds into the silt beneath you, remain motionless—it interprets fin flicks as predator sonar and may nip Exoskin joints.  
• Blind eel: Will follow exhaled CO₂ trail. Provide a finger-length plankton biscuit (sealed in mesh) if you enjoy companionship; it loses interest after feeding.  
• NEVER tag a large shadow with a beacon. Past offenders have vanished, tether severed by unknown means.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
7. Field Tasks Worth the Journey
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
• Collect oxide-impregnated basalt shards for ultracapacitor research; the pressure-forged lattice stores charge densities unattainable at surface conditions.  
• Map internal tides via triangulated whisper-ping—useful in other universes where deep-current energy farms are planned.  
• Document bioluminescent gene sequences; their low-ATP thresholds could revolutionize dark-habitat lighting.  
Note: Intellectual-property treaties recognize “first extractor” rights but require you to transport samples still under ambient pressure until patent registry.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
8. Camps & “Sleep” Strategy
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
• Establish a tri-anchor tent: three tungsten rods in 10 m triangle, linked by spectral-blend netting. Net flexes with current, preventing shear tears.  
• Tent interior is not hollow—remember Rule 4. It is filled with water just like outside, merely screened from sediment so you can see instruments.  
• Sleep harness suspends you mid-tent. Set Exoskin to external alert mode: any pressure delta >0.2 atm or acoustic spike >20 dB auto-stimulates adrenaline injector.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
9. Extraction & Decompression
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
• Return to pod or meet sub. Both use iso-baric cabins: you will ascend still under full trench pressure. Only after docking topside will a four-day staged decompression begin.  
• Beware High-Pressure Nervous Syndrome rebound; anti-shiver meds are provided but disrupt REM sleep—budget extra recuperation days before onward multiverse jump.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
10. Quick-Reference Survival Rules
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
1. Seal everything; forgive no hollowness.  
2. Light briefly, knowledge forever.  
3. Sound small, hear large.  
4. When in doubt, wait—the abyss moves more than you do.  
5. Respect boredom: It is the apex predator’s camouflage.

Pack well, think slowly, and remember: in a realm already saturated with the elemental, your task is not to conquer or enliven it, but to pass through without leaving an unnecessary pattern in the forever-churned silt.
