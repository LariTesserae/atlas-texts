Field Guide for the First-Time Visitor to the Brickborne Warrens
================================================================

0. Quick-look checklist (pack this before you step through the gate)

• Breathable layers of natural fabric (linen, bamboo, muslin) that you can roll up to the knee and elbow.  
• Narrow-profile pack; anything wider than your shoulders will jam in throttles.  
• Two pairs of thin-soled sandals or tabi boots—rubber crepe is acceptable, hard soles are not.  
• Leather water bladders (never metal canteens) and ceramic cup.  
• A copper or glass knife; leave all ferrous metal behind.  
• Light: three beeswax candles in clay holders or a mica-faced lantern (no LEDs; spectrum and glare offend the mirror wardens).  
• Ear-vented mask or scarf to break the constant drip noise when you sleep.  
• Basic antifungal ointment and a broad-spectrum antibiotic; the warren’s native molds are benign to locals but not to outsiders.  
• A pouch of small items for trade: obsidian flakes, natural dyes, or seed pearls (the Brickborne prize iridescence).

1. Ingress & first orientation

Surface entrances are disguised as abandoned wellheads or termite chimneys; arrange a rendez-vous with a Brickborne escort or you will wander for days in lightless feeder tunnels. On descent:

• Count your steps downward (the main throat averages 420).  
• At the third acoustic “flute note” you are within their sovereign boundary—remove footwear, kneel, press both palms to the floor for one full breath; this signals peaceful intent.  
• The air will warm and lift a coppery tang. This is normal; it is evaporated trace salts, not blood.

2. Environmental adaptation

Temperature / humidity: 26-28 °C and 80-90 % RH year-round. Sweat wicks slowly; change into dry cloth before sleeping or risk trench rot.  
Light rhythm: Daylight trickles in shafts from roughly 06:00 to 17:00 local sun. Bring your own soft flame for night hours; avoid open torches unless offered communal pitch bundles.  
Airflow: Drafts reverse at dusk—post a ribbon on your pack; if it flutters toward you, you are walking deeper.

3. Locomotion & terrain hazards

Footing: Smoothed brick can be glassy when wet. Walk flat-footed, toes slightly outward.  
Vertical shafts: acacia ladder rungs are resin-coated and sticky; grip lightly or the sap will tear skin.  
Runnels & cistern lips: Treat as sacred plumbing—never block or divert.  
Sound traps: The Brickborne exploit echoes for navigation; loud foreign noise can mask these cues and cause collisions. Speak in soft consonants, avoid whistling.

4. Health & bio-safety

Pathogens: Low viral load, but endemic fungi (Genus Umbra-myces) cause “Chanter’s Throat,” a hoarse hum in visitors. Gargle lightly salted water nightly.  
Diet transition: Eel broth is high in copper; too much leads to gastrointestinal upset. Dilute first servings with boiled surface water.  
Insects: Glimmerflits are harmless but swarm to sweat salts—wear a light citronella smear.  
Allergy alert: Pipe toad resin, used everywhere as caulk, can inflame human skin. Do not sit on freshly sealed benches.

5. Equipment notes

Metals: Iron, steel, and nickel are taboo; the Brickborne believe they rot the memory of walls. Expect confiscation or quiet disappearance if you ignore this. Copper and bronze earn smiles.  
Electronics: The mica mirrors scatter EM interference—expect compass deviation and rapid battery drain. Go analog.  
Mapping: Paper mildews; carve notch code on reed tally sticks or memorize turns by drip tempo (see section 8).

6. Social protocol

Greeting: Place a fired brick (pocket-sized, you must have made it yourself) at the host’s feet, then tap it twice. They keep it and read the fingerprints like a calling card.  
Conversation: Their spoken language (Old Low Clay) is tonal and drip-paced. Easiest phrase to learn: “Tak-tak kora” = “Your wall, my shelter.”  
Gift economy: Water access is the prime currency. Offering a full goatskin grants a night’s lodging.  
Sacred priorities:  
• Water flows to eel fry first. Never suggest otherwise.  
• Children (“First Softs”) outrank elders during build rites. If a child interrupts you, yield.  
• No brick leaves the complex. Do not pocket shards.

7. Daily cycle planning

Dawn (Light-catch) – best time to move between districts; crowds thin, mirrors at maximum reflectance.  
Heat-peak (Condense Hour) – stay put, preferably near a vent alcove; slips and sprains soar now.  
Dusk Surge – bat avalanche; keep mouth closed and crouch to shoulder height.  
Night Forge – smoke accumulates; camp at mid-level terraces, never the lowest sump.

8. Navigation by acoustics (advanced but life-saving)

Each main corridor is tuned to a two-note motif corresponding to compass quarters:  
North = A-E, East = G-D, South = F-C, West = B-F♯ (local pitch). Hum softly; the returning echo tells you heading by which note swells. Practice before you’re alone.

9. Interacting with wildlife

Dust swifts: flutter overhead; do not reach up—they will rake your knuckles.  
Pipe toads: Step gently; their vibration sense mistakes boots for predators and they may release caustic resin.  
Copper eels: A delicacy; if invited to catch one, stun with low-frequency foot taps rather than grabbing—jolts can fracture their spines, an insult equal to wasting bread.  
Beetle tides: If a corpse pile starts to hiss, beetles are boiling out—retreat uphill, sweep none onto your clothing.

10. Potential anomalies & how to react

Autonomous repair: If bricks around you begin to “weep” mortar, remain still; movement interrupts the process and is said to anger the memory.  
Chrono-slip pockets: Rare chambers reportedly steal minutes—your watch will lose time. Pair up and cross thresholds together, recount shared memories afterward to anchor yourselves.  
Stack-effect gusts: Sudden reversals can extinguish flame; carry two tinder ropes at different lengths for redundancy.

11. Exfiltration

Signal your intent to leave by tapping your original travel brick three times at a junction post. An adolescent guide will appear (they draw lots daily for this honor).  
Ascending alone courts disorientation; vertical shafts twist acoustically and you will climb endless loops.  
On the surface, rinse soles with loose soil—symbolic “return of amended earth.” Failing to do so convinces locals you intend to colonize ground above and may provoke pursuit.

12. Post-visit decompression

Expect tinnitus for 24 hours, a lingering clay scent in hair for a week, and dream imagery of dripping bricks for several nights. Hydrate aggressively; elevated copper excretion colours urine blue-green—harmless within limits.

Final counsel
Do not treat the Brickborne Warrens as a quaint primitive hideout. It is an ecological super-organism that happens to be made of song, clay and muscle instead of DNA. Enter as a guest brick among billions: light, temporary, willing to fit the pattern—and you will depart whole. Try to impose your own geometry, and the place will outlast your resistance exactly the way geology outlasts sandcastles.
