Field File: Visitor-Prep Dossier R-777 “Roof-Spread Plateau”

compiled for ordinary, soft-physics, oxygen-breathing humans who intend a short exploratory stay (≤ 21 local day–night cycles) within the Plateau’s unstable frame

————————————————————————
0.   FIRST, ASK YOUR BODY IF IT WANTS THIS TRIP
————————————————————————
Altitude 5 000 m, partial-pressure O₂ ≈ 55 % of sea-level.  
Spend at least 10 terrestrial days in a hypoxic training camp or with a mask set to 12 % O₂.  Train slow walking while carrying 12 kg.  If you suffer migraine with aura, chronic inner-ear issues, or panic triggered by heartbeat audio, postpone: on the Roof-Spread you will hear your pulse louder than voices.

————————————————————————
1.   PACKING LIST (METAL-FREE OR METAL-MINIMAL)
————————————————————————
Metals lose structural integrity (iron goes rubbery, copper sublimates).  Build your kit as though you were entering an MRI scanner that hates metallurgy.

Allowed materials: basalt-fiber composite, high-density polyethylene (HDPE), ultrahigh-molecular-weight polyethylene cordage, ceramic, hardwood, bone, leather, glass, silica aerogel, graphite, carbon fiber.

1.1  Clothing & Personal Gear
• Three layers torso/legs:   
  – inner: merino or bamboo (quiet, moisture friendly)  
  – mid: aerogel-baffled quilted jacket (no mylar foil!)  
  – shell: silent eVent or similar microporous laminate.   
No Velcro; hook noise triggers local shimmer.  
• Footwear: rubber or leather boots with basalt-fiber cleats.  
• Polarized safety goggles; spore brilliance at new-moon can flash-burn retinas.  
• Two ceramic water flasks (1 L each) + chamois “dew cowl” for wringing fog.  
• Respirator mask P2 rating or higher; not for toxins (none detected) but for kilogram-per-day spore load.  
• Bone-framed trekking poles; tips sheathed in basalt epoxy.  
• 50 m 4 mm UHMWPE line dyed matte umber—doubles as lifeline across gravity dimples.  
• Chalk cylinder (soft calcium sulphate, squeak-free) for marking surfaces every 30 steps.  Carry two colours; one will vanish occasionally.  
• Headlamp: polymer body, sapphire lens, lithium cells encased in resin (lithium ≠ metal in macroscopic sense here).  Bring three spares; remember—no clicky switches; choose touch-sensor pads.

1.2  Shelter & Sleep
• Inflatable hemispheric tent, double-wall, tri-lobed footprint: small silhouette resists distance slippage.  Internal frame: carbon fiber hoops.  
• Stakes of ground-glazed ceramic; tie-outs of cord.  
• Bedroll stuffed with aerogel flake; groundsheet woven reed mat (quiet).  
• Ear-skin: silicone plugs with slow-leak channels; moderates heartbeat roar without complete silence (total silence induces derealisation in many travellers).

1.3  Food & Cooking
• Dehydrated plant-based rations; animal fats here resist rancidity but taste like pencil eraser after plateau exposure.  
• Alcohol gel stoves in borosilicate cups; flames burn blue, low hiss—acceptable.  Metallic stoves will puddle.  
• Calcium chloride desiccant packets for waste; compost nothing.

1.4  Instruments
• Time: sapphire-bearing ceramic watch, tritium dial.  Electronic quartz fails unpredictably when time pleats.  
• Navigation: bubble compass in glass filled with fluorocarbon fluid (mass-independent) + star wheel for thin-air constellations.  Laser rangefinder useless; photons cheat the distance rule as eagerly as you do.  
• Notebook of stone-powder paper; graphite stylus.  Data sticks & cameras technically work but their solder joints soften.  Disposable polymer-body film camera if records necessary.

2.   ENTRY SEQUENCE (“THE FIVE SILENCES”)
————————————————————————
Silence 1: Hardware—pack stripped of jingle, speak only when essential.  
Silence 2: Breath—slow 6/min rate reduces drum-head chest acoustics.  
Silence 3: Footfall—use rolling gait; keep sole contact continuous.  
Silence 4: Thought—expect intrusive “Was that ruin closer a second ago?” Note, don’t argue.  
Silence 5: Presence—accept visibility/ invisibility reciprocity with locals; do not chase figures you glimpse.  They decide the aperture of encounter.

3.   DAILY PROTOCOL
————————————————————————
Dawn (0400–0600 plateau time)  
• Scarcity tide: fog condenses sideways, thin rills form.  Fill both flasks immediately; water becomes alkaline (pH 9) by mid-morning.  
• Check tether knots; expansion overnight lengthens cords ~3 %.  Retie.

Midday (1100–1400)  
• UV higher by ≈40 % (thin air); apply mineral (titanium-free!) sunblock.  
• Gravity eddies strongest.  Toss a pebble ahead; if arc kinks mid-flight, rope up through suspect patch.  
• Avoid standing within 10 m of spongewood—the sap they exude midday bleaches fabric instantly and it never regains colour (the locals read bleached patches as territorial insult).

Dusk (1700–1900)  
• Spores gust; masks on.  If new moon ±1 day: full-cover goggles.  
• Hum a monotone ≤30 dB; plants respond by orienting fronds away—makes camp setup easier.

Night (2000–0400)  
• Statistical distance drift peaks.  Remain inside tent.  Place chalk cairn outside zipper; if cairn is missing when you wake, you have jumped location—backtrack via chalk blazes.  
• Do not remain motionless outside more than 7 minutes; encroaching moss permanently stains skin green-black (harmless medically, distressing cosmetically).

4.   INTERACTING WITH THE INVISIBLES (RESIDENT HUMANS?)
————————————————————————
They manifest when you are: a) alone, b) oxygen-deprived enough for mild tinnitus, c) carrying nothing obviously metallic.

Ethics:
• Speak in whisper or subvocal humming.  
• Offer objects only of plant origin (woven grass cord, dried fruit).  Stone gifts are considered waste; bone is funerary.  
• Questions about “time” understood as weather chat.  Ask instead “When is the moss calm?”  Answers arrive as gesture toward horizon or spiral scraped in silt.  
• Do not request to see their dead.  Nothing ever “dies,” and the query is obscene.

————————————————————————
5.   HAZARDS & RESPONSES
————————————————————————
Altitude edema: descent impossible (all directions eventually up).  Administer acetazolamide, hyperventilate deliberately.  
Sound Bloom (loud noise ⇒ matter duplicates with shimmer): Freeze until echoes cease.  Duplicate objects evaporate in ≈40 sec; do not touch—skin left inside copy when it vanishes.  
Time Snag (object or self pauses): Companion counts continuous prime numbers; the cognitive tether often restarts flow.  If alone, a soft whistle (≤20 dB) can jar local chronology.  
Gravity Pool: Sprawl belly-down, crawl to perimeter; center is only 0.7 g but exit rim spikes to 1.4 g—knees give out if you try to sprint.  
Acid Sap Burn: Rinse with plateau water immediately; pH naturally buffers to neutral within minutes.

————————————————————————
6.   EXIT STRATEGY
————————————————————————
Choose a clear morning after spores have settled.  Walk NORTH-EAST; cloud river always falls south-west so you move against the plateau’s breath.  Keep sun on right cheek until air thickens and distant ground flattens (horizon stops curving).  Total travel perceived 4-12 km, stopwatch time irrelevant.  Mark final chalk X; on next blink, you should be inside mundane stratus deck of lower world.  Do NOT turn to look back—rearward observation sometimes reinstates plateau physics around viewer for several seconds, leading to mid-air re-insertions.

————————————————————————
7.   WHAT NOT TO BRING
————————————————————————
• Firearms or anything that metal-splinters under pressure.  
• Loudspeakers, drums, whistling kettles.  
• GPS (satellites cannot guarantee distance rule compliance).  
• Pets; plateau reclassifies them as “mobile vegetation” after prolonged stillness—they seed hairlike runners impossible to remove without surgery.  
• Burial expectations.  If you die, companions must cremate immediately with gel fuel; otherwise you will turn to stone and your story belongs to geology.

————————————————————————
8.   MENTAL HYGIENE
————————————————————————
Expect derealisation, déjà vu, and the sense that your memories are re-filming themselves from a second camera angle.  Keep a log each hour: “My name is …, I just…,” etc.  Reading these entries aloud in a whisper proves surprisingly effective at anchoring.  If two consecutive entries appear identical despite your handwriting, evacuate on next good weather window; plateau has begun editing you.

————————————————————————
9.   SOUVENIRS
————————————————————————
The only artifact that remains stable off-plateau is dried spongewood pith (<2 cm length).  Anything larger resumes softening until it puddles sap.  Photographs wash out, recordings damp to static, stone blades lose edge.  Best memento: the interior hush you learn to carry, a portable respect for fragile physics everywhere else.

Safe quiet travels.
