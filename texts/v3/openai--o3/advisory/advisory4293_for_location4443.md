1. MINDSET: WHY COME HERE?
--------------------------------------------------
You do not go to the Plateau for comfort. You go because its age dwarfs biology, because a million-year machine still ticks beneath your boots, and because the place erases every human boast—voice, footprint, even time perception. Expect reverence. Expect vertigo that begins in the soul, not the stomach.

--------------------------------------------------
3. ENVIRONMENTAL THREATS
--------------------------------------------------
• Asphyxiation: 8–14 mbar is Mars-class. No bare-skin breaks longer than ten seconds; lungs will boil, not just freeze.  
• Abrasion/Etching: Chloride-silicate dust at 300+ m/s is sandblasting at the nano scale. Leave unshielded polymer and it becomes a mirror in days.  
• Radiation: UV-C flux is 30× Terran sea level at noon; plateau altitude leaves almost no sky shielding. Add cosmic and tower-related gravitic lensing that bends particle paths—dosimeters swing unpredictably.  
• Static/Discharge: Ionized dust snakes carry tens of megavolts but almost no current; they will flash-etch gear housings and wipe magnetic media.  
• Gravity Shear: Step inside an active pylon’s 150 m halo and g varies by up to 7 %. Human vestibular systems misjudge stride; ankle injuries common, time-dilation hallucinations rarer but real.  
• Thermal Quakes & Ground Breath: Daily crustal ripple amplitude ≈12 cm; walking surfaces “heave” for 90 s. Lose footing and you might tumble into a slot canyon exactly when the 300 m/s dawn wind arrives.

6. BEHAVIORAL PROTOCOLS
--------------------------------------------------
• Silence Is Normal: Do not rely on hearing for partner location. Use line-of-sight hand lights or IR beacons.  
• Respect the Xeroliths: They are the only rooted life here. Crushing a square meter could wipe out a century of growth. Camp at least 20 m away.  
• Tower Standoff: Minimum 30 m from any intact pylon. Gravitic gradient stronger than it looks; analog watches desync, cerebrovascular events reported in unshielded brains.  
• Leave No Surface Behind: Anything you set down will be polished anonymous by next century, confusing future archaeologists—and maybe yourself on a return hop. Engrave data tags 3 mm deep if you must leave equipment.  
• Sample Ethic: One shard, one dust vial, one xerolith disk per expedition is the informal scientific treaty. The plateau has no ranger, but reputation travels faster than light in the multiverse scene.

7. HUMAN FACTORS
--------------------------------------------------

Combine that with near-silence and g-lensing blur and many experience “Cartesian Dread”—a dissociative calm that tempts reckless wandering. Pre-agree on tethered exploration radius or continuous tracker ping.

Physiology:  
• You will hyperventilate even in suit because pressure is low; CO₂ scrubbers work harder—watch green bars.  
• Expect mild taste of metal (argon bleed in makeup air) and zonal headaches near pylons.  
• Sleep cycles drift because dawn wind sounds like distant surf in suit acoustics; bring auditory cues.

The Plateau is older than animal bones, older than multicellular ambition. Everything about it tells you that creation can be patient, meticulous, and nearly silent.
