1. EXECUTIVE SNAPSHOT
You will be stepping onto a high, wind-raked stone table roughly the area of a continent. Temperature: 5-16 °C, rarely below freezing in summer, but wind chill is severe. Air pressure ~75 kPa (equivalent to 2 800 m Earth altitude). Oxygen adequate but thin; hypertension sufferers take note. Weather swings from vivid sun to sudden cloudbursts; fog breeds out of the rock.

No metal, glass or synthetic polymer remains stable here; such items soften, pit, fracture or simply delaminate within hours. Plan a mostly organic or mineral load-out. Navigation is primarily acoustic; anything that disturbs the sonic hush will announce you, so loud technology is self-defeating. Something hunts in night fog—unseen but confirmed. Stay above the vapour deck after dusk.

2. ENVIRONMENTAL PROFILE

Atmosphere
• Cool, low-pressure, highly transparent; high UV levels despite cloud cover.  
• Acoustic damping: vegetation absent, stone non-porous; echoes travel until absorbed by distance.  

Geology
• Green-grey schist matrix with abundant quartz stringers. Rock is competent but sheets cleave off without warning.  
• Natural and architectural stone are the same substrate; boundaries are ambiguous.  

Hydrology
• Continuous seepage. Surface water potable after particulate settling; biologically sterile at source.  
• Canals and cisterns are engineered to move, store and evaporate water. Avoid obstructing flow; the city reacts.  

Biology
• Moss, sedge, dwarf conifers, micro-succulents.  
• Fauna scarce, shy, mostly nocturnal. Indicators of large predator: doubled-width claw scores at 30 cm spacing, spherical scat with bone dust.  

Cultural / Memetic Rules
1. Materials must be hand-shapeable or weather-shapeable to persist.  
2. Water and structure co-evolve—alter either and you rewrite local hydraulics.  
3. Echoes equal ownership; traditional mapping is done with tuned stone whistles.  
4. Night-fog belongs to the hunter.

3. PERSONAL KIT – WHAT TO TAKE, WHAT NOT TO TAKE

Permitted & Stable
• Leather, rawhide, felted wool, cotton duck, hemp.  
• Hardwood (oak, hickory, ironwood) holds up 2–3 weeks; treat with beeswax.  
• Stone, fired clay, bone, horn.  
• Copper or bronze in foil-thin quantities survives ~48 h—acceptable for emergency electronics only.

Vulnerable / Forbidden
• High-carbon steel, aluminum, modern alloys → exfoliate within a day.  
• Plastics and composites → craze then dissolve into waxy shreds, contaminating water courses. Carry at your own ethical risk.  
• Glass → crazes microscopically; shards enter airflow, damaging lungs. Leave it behind.  

Recommended Load-Out
Clothing:
• Base: merino/wool blend; retains warmth damp.  
• Outer: waxed-cotton anorak (vented), knee-length storm kilt or articulated trousers.  
• Hands: split-hide gloves for constant stone contact.  
• Feet: double-layer felt socks, leather mountain boots with hobnails replaced by flint studs.  

Navigation & Signalling:
• Three basalt or soapstone whistles cut to distinct pitches (240 Hz, 400 Hz, 650 Hz recommended).  
• Natural-fiber line (50 m) dyed with iron-free pigment for drop-line backtracking; fibres slowly erode without polluting canals.  
• Sunshadow gnomon disk carved from slate—works when sky is clear; no magnetics available.  

Shelter & Sleep:
• Treated canvas tarp + pegs cut on-site.  
• Thick felt ground mat (stone leaches heat).  
• Down or wool quilt inside waxed canvas bivy (synthetic sleeping bags delaminate).  

Tools:
• Obsidian or jadeite knives; bone-hafted if possible.  
• Stone micro-adze for shaping other stone (slow but reliable).  
• Flint striker & char-cloth; lichen-rich plateau lacks fuel, so fire intended only for signal or sterilization.  

Lighting:
• Tallow candles in shallow ceramic dishes. LED lamps corrode at battery terminals; if carried, seal in beeswax and ration to emergencies.  

Medical:
• Broad-spectrum antibiotics in beeswax-sealed vials (glassless packaging).  
• Linen bandages, arnica salve, pine-pitch adhesive.  
• Altitude/cold response tablets individually wax-paper wrapped.

Data & Recording:
• Wax-coated wood tablet + graphite stick; inks bleed in mist.  
• Memory by whistle patterns—learn the eight standard cartographic calls before departure.  
• Biological field press built from cedar slats and jute cord for plant samples.

4. CROSSING THE THRESHOLD: IMMEDIATE ORIENTATION

1. Stand still for two full minutes. Acclimate to thin air and near-silence.  
2. Take first auditory bearing: clap once, note direction and delay of echo. Longest delay indicates open plateau; shortest indicates carved corridors.  
3. Check stone directly beneath feet for hairline cracks; recent portal energy sometimes weakens strata. Move upslope 20 m if necessary.  
4. Tag entry point with a three-note whistle call (low-high-low). This becomes your “home” signature.

5. MOVING THROUGH OPEN PLATEAU

Pacing & Rest
• One kilometre per hour is average—rock terraces and micro-runnels demand constant ankle vigilance.  
• Use the Rule of Four Steps: every fourth step, shift eye focus to next ten metres to anticipate quartz slicks.  

Edge-Shear Hazard
• Shelf collapses (“soft detonations”) occur after freeze-thaw. Listen for muted thunks beneath surface; detour 30 m upslope.  

Weather
• Cloud skirts drop sudden rain curtains. A translucent front approaching from windward gives ~7 min warning. Don waxed hood and stow anything absorbent.

6. ENTERING THE CITY-WITHOUT-TOOLS

Threshold Signs
• First sighting of ruler-straight canal = outer infrastructure.  
• Dressed-stone block with interior angles smoother than exterior surfaces marks civic wall.

Route Strategy
• Stay within faint trickle-sound; canals are your breadcrumbs.  
• Every junction: leave one pebble stack on the side that faces outbound. Plateau winds seldom topple a properly balanced three-stone cairn.

Spatial Awareness
• The city grows denser downward. If claustrophobia rises, ascend until stairs widen enough for lateral arm stretch.  
• Ceilings may flex microscopically under temperature changes; tiny crackles are normal. Loud snap: retreat, that’s a falling slab warning.

Respect the Hydraulics
• Never dam a channel to wash gear—water routing equals structural integrity.  
• If you inadvertently block flow (pack, foot, tarp) uncover within 60 s or risk pressure shattering a wall panel.

7. LIVING WITH SILENCE, WATER AND ECHOES

Psychological Drift
• After 6–8 h many travelers experience “Sound Hunger”—urge to shout or sing. Controlled vocalization is fine, but continuous noise drains situational awareness.  
• Schedule 5 min each dawn and dusk for deliberate sound release, e.g., reciting a poem; log echo delays for map-making.

Echo Etiquette
• Encounter someone else’s whistle motif: repeat it once, then silence—the call-and-respect protocol. Persisting can be construed as territory challenge.  
• Metallic clanging is taboo; locals (if any) interpret it as ecological vandalism.

8. NIGHTFALL PROTOCOLS

Before Sunset
• Ascend at least one terrace above visible fog sea.  
• Choose alcove with single entrance narrower than arm-span; install stone door block (20–25 kg manageable).  
• Lay tallow candle where meltwater runoff cannot reach.

Predator Avoidance
• Maintain silence from full dark until star peak (approx. 01:00); even candle hiss travels.  
• When hunter presence suspected (air pressure drop, total bird silence, faint stone-on-stone scrape) perform the Anvil Maneuver: a) extinguish flame, b) crouch still, c) press flat stone plate against chest to dampen heartbeat echo.

9. FOOD, DRINK, HYGIENE

Water
• Gather from fresh overflows, not cistern basins (amphibians). Let settle 10 min then filter through felt sleeve. Boiling impractical; chemical tablets fine.  
• Daily need: 2.5 L at altitude.

Forage
• Moss can be steeped into weak tea—minor calories, vitamin A.  
• Silver sedge hearts edible raw, taste of cucumber-stone mix.  
• Star succulents contain mucilage; chew for moisture, expect mild laxative effect.

Imported Rations
• Hard cheese, pemmican, dense rye bread—all tolerate humidity and lack metal wrappers.  
• Strict carry-in/carry-out; residue impacts microfauna cycles.

Waste
• Designated “rigid void” method: defecate onto flat stone, fold moss over, slide into fissure already destined to exfoliate. Urine diluted with two parts seep water avoids salt crystal stain on heritage floors.

10. EXIT STRATEGY & DECONTAMINATION

Departure
• Return to whistle-marked portal point before 11:00 local time; afternoon mists distort distance judgment.  
• Two-note high-low call signals intention to leave—prevents search parties.

Gear Purge
• Brush every surface for lichen spores to prevent biocontamination of the next world.  
• Strip any stone souvenirs—removal disrupts balancing weight distribution. Photography impossible but etch sketches into slate for memory.

Health Check
• Inspect skin for quartz micro-splinters; UV exposure signs appear delayed.  
• Rehydrate with isotonic solution for altitude recovery.
