1. WHY GO
• To stand inside an industrial ecosystem at the instant of its birth.  
• To watch water behave like a living vein network.  
• To hear what perpetual invention sounds like.  
• To collect alloys and extremophile biofilm that do not yet exist in your home timeline.  
• To feel, for a few hours, what a planet might feel while it’s still being built.

2. PERSONAL READINESS CHECKLIST

Clothing
• Base layer: moisture-wicking thermals (temp hovers 1–4 °C).  
• Insulating mid-layer: fleece or aerogel hoodie (light; keeps range of motion).  
• Shell: arc-flash-rated, waterproof coverall with sealed seams.  
• Footwear: composite-toe ice-cleats or magnetic-sole work boots (grip on wet steel).  
• Gloves: two sets—1) thin nitrile for fine work, 2) cut-resistant kevlar for climbing.  
• Head: low-profile helmet with built-in ear defenders and mount point for lights.  
• Eye: splash goggles + amber lenses (improves contrast under sodium lamps).

Tools & Instruments
• Narrow-beam LED headlamp (270 lm minimum) plus chemical lightsticks for marking route.  
• Short safety line (10 m static) with auto-grabbing ascender; anchor to any grating.  
• Compact infrared thermometer (fast way to decide if a panel is scalding or frozen).  
• Handheld gas detector—O₂, CO, glycol-vapour. Pockets of deficiency form near trenches.  
• Folding grapnel or magnetic puck for retrieving dropped gear from pits.  
• “Soft tracker” beacons—inductive tags readable by your wrist unit to bread-crumb the way back.  
• Rugged audio dosimeter (sound levels spike to 110 dB).  
• First-aid kit emphasizing laceration closure and immersion hypothermia.  
• 1-L vacuum flask for hot drink; hydration bladder with in-line microfilter (condensate is potable once filtered).  
• Optional: collapsible packraft if you intend to drift the central trench—expert paddlers only.

Administrative
• Valided inter-facility visitor tag (orange triangle) if the Marsh falls under any extant authority in this branch. Counterfeit is easy; enforcement is rare but fines are lethal: robots may jettison trespassers down-process.  
• Signed waiver acknowledging undefined gravitational anomalies.

Mindset
• Treat every surface as either a knife or an ice-patch until proven otherwise.  
• Accept that the floor plan edits itself when new modules are slid in. Never expect yesterday’s map to be exact.  
• Keep a five-second mental buffer: the landscape moves, drones move, you move—predict collisions.

3. GETTING IN & FINDING YOUR BEARINGS

Approach
1. Descend the stairwell until turbine roar drowns conversation.  
2. Pause at the threshold; let your goggles defog and your hearing adjust.  
3. Step onto the gridded concrete. Feel it flex—this is normal; it rides on hydraulic screws that level new pours.

Orientation
• “Lamp-count rule”: distance ≈ (# of lit sodium globes × 23 m).  
• The entire Marsh drains toward the 20-m-wide effluent river called the Spillway. If lost, downhill leads there.  
• Walkways labelled with stencilled fractions (e.g., 3/7-B). Numerator = deck level from the ceiling downward; denominator = construction phase. Higher numerator = nearer surface.

Landmarks (stable so far)  
• Stack 135: four-story triple-piston compressor; glows cobalt under leak lights.  
• The “Kite Field”: dozens of crane cables hanging, hooks swaying like black kites in an orange sky.  
• Intake Cathedral: cathedral-sized arc filter that sings at low tide of air; good for navigation by sound.

4. DAILY TACTICS FOR STAYING ALIVE

Slips & Edges
• Keep at least one boot magnetized or cleated while photographing.  
• Never trust a seam—it may open for a cable pull at any moment.

Temperature Shocks
• Touch test with back of glove; delta can exceed 80 °C between adjacent panels.  
• Steam plumes are invisible in sodium back-light—use IR readout before walking through white fog.

Noise
• Insert earplugs but flip up defenders when moving so you can still detect drones.  
• Use hand signals with companions; vocal cords tear fast above 100 dB.

Water Hazards
• Runoff depth ranges 2–60 cm on flats; holes drop several meters. Probe before you step off a known path.  
• Spillway current ≈ 1 m/s—looks calm, sweeps you under decking in 3 s. Tie a recovery line if sampling.

Gravity/Acoustic Oddities
• If your footfalls sound slowed or lower-pitched, you are entering a dilation pocket. Backtrack; items dropped here sometimes “fall” sideways and vanish into service conduits.

Drones
• Beetle-forms are maintenance, not security, but will shove you aside to reach a task.  
• White-painted ones carry cutting lasers—avoid their queue lanes; they have right of way.

5. PHENOMENA WORTH EXPERIENCING—SAFELY

• Five-Minute Pulse: stand atop a surge tank when it drains; you’ll feel the whole plate sink 4 mm then rebound.  
• Condenser Rainbows: at shift change the lamps cycle spectrum; mist forms neon haloes—photogenic.  
• Biofilm Glow: shine 365-nm torch on the green streaks; they fluoresce indigo. Scrapings fetch a price in biotech markets.  
• The Silent Lap: roughly every 47 minutes most heavy machines idle simultaneously for 8–12 s. Record it; the near-silence is otherworldly.

6. INTERACTING WITH LOCALS

Human Crews (encounter rate: low)
• Hard-pressed, proud of the build, short on time. Offer spare coffee or batteries; you’ll gain temporary escort privileges.  
• Do NOT ask what the plant is ultimately for—nobody agrees, and the question reopens contract disputes.

Wildlife
• Water-rats: skittish; carry no known pathogens. Good barometer—if they stampede, a valve blow-off is coming.  
• The Fox (if real): folklore says stroking its back nulls your static charge for a day. Unverified.

Autonomous Systems
• Speak clearly at red terminals for directory assistance; voiceprint of “Visitor” downloads a crude but current floor plan.  
• If a drone flashes amber, it is about to weld. Duck behind a bulkhead.

7. EXTRACT & EVAC PLAN

Primary: Retrace lamp-count to the entry stairwell before shift-change (generators uptick, making ascent windy and loud).  
Alternate A: Ride an empty pallet elevator up two decks, then follow blue arrows to service lift 2A-G, which links to loading bay daylight.  
Alternate B (emergency): Jump a maintenance boat in the Spillway and steer downstream. Expect total darkness, steeper drops, and eventual discharge into the next subsystem (“Glass Delta”); only for seasoned spelunk-sailors.

Medical Evac
• Call code 777 on any wall intercom; a drone swarm will deliver a hypothermia capsule and tow you to a med-pod. You will later receive a debrief invoice.

8. SOUVENIRS, SAMPLES & DATA

Allowed (unregulated)  
• Lichen coins (≤25 g).  
• Audio recordings of pump symphonies (Creative Commons in most universes).  
• Rust flakes with nacreous sheen—use glass vials.

Restricted (tracked by exit scanners)  
• Prototype alloy coupons stamped PHASE-9.  
• Drone batteries—contain hyper-dense foam cell tech.  
• Technical drawings (optical watermarks trigger alarms).

Tip: Photograph info instead of pocketing; no scanner flags photons.

───────────────────────────────────────────────
9. AFTER-CARE

• Inspect every inch of skin for micro-cuts; iron oxide in wounds causes “Red Nail,” a mild but annoying galvanic rash.  
• Air out clothing for 48 h; sweet antifreeze odor persists and can migraines.  
• Back up recordings immediately—humidity corrodes unsealed drives.  
• Hydrate and warm gradually; rapid re-heating can trigger “chill dives” (vertigo) from prolonged sub-5 °C exposure.

───────────────────────────────────────────────
FINAL NOTE

The Foundry Marsh is a place still choosing what it will become. Enter as a respectful observer, leave no new scars on its unfledged skin, and it may allow you to witness the moment the newborn machine first believes it has always been alive.
