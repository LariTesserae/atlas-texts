Field Guide for the First Human Visitor to the Unfinished-Mountain Plateau

0. Premise and risk rating
Category: Extremely dry geothermical environment, non-terrestrial chemistry, localized chronal drag, hard-vac precision rule set.  
Fatality index (unprotected human): 30–90 sec via pulmonary desiccation or fall.  
With proper gear: Moderate. Comparable to a week inside an active steel mill perched on a skyscraper ledge.

If you are not comfortable wearing a suit rated for both foundry work and high-altitude climbing, do not go.

1. Transit planning

1.1 Time dilation
Inside-plateau clocks lose ~8 min per surface hour. Pack two watches: one sealed with you, one tethered outside the transition portal. Agree on a hard recall time with your pickup team and announce every milestone by radio; your “15 min look-around” is 23 min to them.

1.2 Legalities
The enclave is governed only by the plateau’s physical rules—no sworn wardens have been found—so standard Multiversal Survey Protocol (MSP) applies: take nothing that alters macro development, leave nothing that fails naturally within three local cycles.

2. Personal life-support kit

2.1 Suit
• Shell: aluminized aramid or basalt fiber cloth (≥ 650 °C continuous).  
• Underlayer: active humidification vest circulating 5 L of water + electrolyte broth.  
• Joints: double-gaskets; the dust is alkali and will sandblast O-rings in hours.  
• Boots: ceramic-scale soles, magnetic studs optional for traversing vitrified glass.  
• Gloves: cut-proof (level 5) with diamond-dust coating—remember: every fragment you touch is scalpel-sharp.

2.2 Respiratory loop
A rebreather rather than tanks; O₂ content is acceptable but moisture < 1 % and CO₂ spikes near vents. Add:  
• Moisture reclaim cartridge (lithium-chloride sieve).  
• CO₂ scrub (LiOH or soda-lime) rated 4 h minimum.  
• Trace-metal vapor filter (activated alumina).

2.3 Hydration & nutrition
You will not feel thirsty—your sweat never appears—but you are losing 0.7 L h⁻¹ through lungs and suit permeation. Program vest reminders. Bring dense, low-crumb nutrition bars vacuum-packed; organic crumbs breached to atmosphere vitrify and can plug suit vents.

3. Environmental Hazards and How to Dance with Them

3.1 Heat map
The plateau sits at 190–220 °C radiant, but hot spots flash to 400 °C when sub-vents open. Your visor’s infrared overlay must stay on. If glass underfoot shows rainbow irisation, step back: a vent is about to exhale.

3.2 Gas pulses
Cycles every 173 ± 4 s. Kneel, brace, and keep your center of gravity low during the tremor; the plateau edge is frictionless obsidian.

3.3 Razor geology
Anything that breaks keeps a machined edge forever. Carry self-adhesive ceramic patch strips: press one over any suit nick before it can unzipper wider.

3.4 The recursive doorway
DO NOT insert body parts. Robotic probe only. Mass loss is proportional to cross-sectional area and dwell time (≈ 6 mg cm⁻² s⁻¹). Retrieval tether must be sapphire fiber or it will ablate.

4. Navigation and Position Discipline

4.1 Edge protocol
Maintain a three-point stance within 2 m of a cliff line. Clip into a 9 mm aramid tether anchored to a piton you drill in non-perfect rock (veined zones only; perfect faces will reject the bit).

4.2 Landmarks
No erosion means every crack looks new. Tag your path with polarized micro-reflectors (MSP-approved biodegrades after 1 plateau year ≈ 1.4 surface yrs).

4.3 Plant fields
Stay at least one frond-length (≈ 1 m) away. Touching a plant destroys the perfection it feeds on; you could trigger a sudden growth burst that entangles you or steals the precision of your climbing hardware.

5. Science & Sampling (if that’s why you’re here)

Allowed take:
• ≤ 100 g of loose basalt shards (non-perfect).  
• 1 severed mica leaf (fallen only).  
• Gas and aerosol vials from two pulse cycles.

Collection jars must be ceramic or sapphire; metal softens in hot CO₂ plumes, and plastics occlude spectrometry.

6. Emergency scenarios

6.1 Suit breach (minor)
Patch within 20 s, then re-hydrate 250 mL. Inspect for crystalline growth—if any part of your patch shows geometric regularity, cut it away; it will continue to “perfect” until it bisects the suit.

6.2 Vent-induced tumble
If you go over the edge your tether reel will brake once; rappel to the nearest plant outcropping below and wait for drone lift. Do not climb glass unroped—static friction is nearly zero.

6.3 Heat stroke alarms
Core temp > 38.5 °C: retreat immediately. The local chrono-lag means longer exposure than you think.

7. Etiquette and Philosophical Notes

• The plateau is younger than erosion; you are its elder. Behave accordingly.  
• Replace any symmetry you break: if you sprinkle crumbs, sweep them off the precise planes.  
• Speak quietly; echoes are precious anomalies here.  
• Resist the urge to “collect perfection.” Removing flawless corners destabilizes adjacent rock and insults the ecosystem—it literally starves the flora.

8. Mission end & decontamination

Outside portal, undergo 30-min cool-down. Rinse gear with neutral pH water; alkali dust reacts exothermically when first wetted, so submerge slowly. Inspect for hitch-hiker mica spores—if present, bake gear at 350 °C for 1 h; they survive vacuum but not that heat without their mineral feed.

9. Souvenir you may carry home within code

A single folded mica “leaf” no larger than a credit card is permissible. Keep it in a shock-proof box; jolts may fracture it into molecular blades.

10. Final reminder

The place is in the middle of writing its own geology textbook. You are walking across rough drafts. Tread as though every line you cross might still be wet ink—because here, it is.
