Field Preparation Dossier  
FOR: Ordinary human traveller transiting the Multiversal Waystations  
DESTINATION CODENAME: The Silent High Plateau (SHP)  
REVISION: 7-β  

----------------------------------------------------------------  
1. First-Look Assessment  
• Appearance: Continent-sized bone-white mesa lashed flat by geological time.  
• Risk Class: Medium-High. No active predators, but physics is intermittently negotiable.  
• Stay Duration Recommended: 36 continuous plateau-hours maximum for first-timers; longer visits demand acclimation drills (see §11).  

----------------------------------------------------------------  
2. Core Environmental Parameters  
Atmosphere: ~58 kPa (≈0.57 Earth sea-level), O₂ partial pressure borderline hypoxic; elevated free iron aerosols.  
Temperature Envelope: −9 °C at dawn to +4 °C mid-afternoon, daily spread ≤2 °C.  
Hydrologic State: Anhydrous. No recoverable H₂O in soils; gaseous frost at dawn is non-potable.  
Light Regime: Solar arc normal, but shadow vectors remain fixed to objects. UV-B 1.7× Earth average.  
Acoustics: Absorption index ~0.96. Sound does not propagate usefully beyond 5 m.  

----------------------------------------------------------------  
3. Personal Life-Support Kit (PLSK)  
3.1. Respiration  
• Micro-compressor rebreather rated to 60 kPa external pressure OR bottled O₂ (≥6 L hour⁻¹).  
• Replaceable iron-chelation filters (hemosiderin build-up in lungs reported after 8 h unfiltered).  

3.2. Hydration & Nutrition  
• Carry full water requirement (4 L day⁻¹ minimum; higher loss via respirator dessication).  
• Electrolyte profiles skewed: pack magnesium, not sodium (salts present underfoot will contaminate).  
• Food must be odor-neutral; cooking smells are trapped inside helmets and induce nausea.  

3.3. Clothing  
• Layer 1: Wicking silk-graphene union suit.  
• Layer 2: Aerogel baffled jacket & trousers (insulation without bulk; wind is absent, convection minimal).  
• Outer: Photo-grey dust cloak; antistatic threads prevent attraction of silicate powder.  
• Visor: Category-4 UV & glare protection—shadows give no feedback about sun angle, so ocular strain is common.  

3.4. Footwear  
• Broad sole (<2 kPa point load) to avoid breaking subsurface burrow glassworks.  
• Integrated step-counter LEDs (see §6).  

----------------------------------------------------------------  
4. Instruments That Still Behave  
Bring:  
✓ Mechanical hourglass (30-minute cycle, borosilicate; magnetic grains stall).  
✓ Chalk or dust dye packets to mark travelled lines—colours remain fast even through causal skips.  
✓ Laser range-finder (distance is honest even when position is not).  
✓ Photonic compass (measures solar polarization instead of magnetic field).  
Leave behind:  
✗ Spring-driven watches (gearing deranges).  
✗ Magnetic compasses (spin).  
✗ MEMS gyros (logic loops).  

----------------------------------------------------------------  
5. Locomotion & Navigation Protocol  
Rule SHP-N-1: Count every step aloud inside your suit. Cognitive anchoring reduces “missing ground” episodes by 88 %.  
Rule SHP-N-2: If you lose the count, halt, back-track exactly 30 physical paces, then resume.  
Rule SHP-N-3: Never sprint; high exertion + low pO₂ + iron aerosols = pulmonary edema risk.  
Tether Option: Light nylon guide-line to partner at 12 m length. Lines do not shorten when space folds; slack accumulation warns of a skip event.  

----------------------------------------------------------------  
6. Communication in a Nearly Soundless Medium  
• Helmet-to-helmet bone-conduction pads; operate on 20 kHz ultrasonic carrier (inaudible to fauna).  
• Establish finger-tap code on each other’s shoulders if electronic link fails.  
• Do NOT shout: expiratory pressure can burst alveoli before sound transmits.  

----------------------------------------------------------------  
7. Cairn Etiquette & Spatial Integrity  
• View cairns as local spacetime calibration tripods. Photograph but do not touch.  
• If you accidentally dislodge a shale wafer:  
  1) Freeze.  
  2) Replace exactly; orientation matters (look for flake’s crystalline sheen alignment).  
  3) Wait 90 s. Distortions normally damp within that window.  
• Symptom of unresolved fold: horizon appears closer than arm’s length; peripheral blur; re-stack until corrected.  

----------------------------------------------------------------  
8. Biology: Look, Don’t Crush  
Fauna are sub-centimetre and silent; population density ≈ 3 × 10⁸ individuals km⁻².  
Avoid:  
• Stepping on raised “static sails” of tessellids (transparent, easily mistaken for mica shards).  
• Setting equipment in drain-bowls—the gastropods congregate there and calcify anything motionless.  
Bio-hygiene: Alcohol wipes only; organic scents cause swarm curiosity (harmless but obstructive).  

----------------------------------------------------------------  
9. Science & Souvenir Rules  
Permitted:  
• Surface dust, lithified slabs ≤1 kg, non-living plume-fern sheaths (drift-shed naturally).  
Forbidden:  
• Live capture—organisms petrify quickly off-site; ethical bans apply.  
• Removal of glowing nodules from cairns (liable to produce permanent coordinate drift in exporter).  

----------------------------------------------------------------  
10. Campcraft (if you insist on overnighting)  
Shelter: Low-arch reflective tent staked with broad plates (no loose pegs—soil lacks cohesion).  
Heat: Catalytic flameless pad warmers. Combustion flames asphyxiate at this pO₂.  
Sanitation: Closed-loop waste bags; bacteria cannot degrade organics here—pack EVERYTHING out.  
Lighting: Red-shift LEDs; white light creates prism scatter on plume ferns that lures tessellids into fabric seams.  

----------------------------------------------------------------  
11. Psychological Safeguards  
Expected symptoms after 6 plateau-hours: “silence pressure,” tachy-auditory hallucinations (imagined clicks, distant music).  
Countermeasures:  
• Pre-load playlist of sub-audible pink noise piped through suit (does not propagate externally).  
• Conversational partner or recorded voice log every 15 min to preserve temporal sense.  
• Daily post-visit grounding: eat strongly flavoured food, handle running water, engage with variable music tempo—recalibrates brain away from stasis fixation.  

----------------------------------------------------------------  
12. Exit & De-con Loop  
1) Brush suit with magnetized wand—removes iron whiskers before they seed in joints.  
2) Hyperbaric flush at 1.3 atm for 20 min to purge iron-laden micro-aerosols from lungs.  
3) Medical panel: ferritin blood test; chelation therapy if >300 ng mL⁻¹.  
4) Psychological checklist: Must accurately recount last 24 Earth hours. Failure indicates partial chronal flattening; remain under observation until episodic recall returns.  

----------------------------------------------------------------  
13. Red-Flag Events (Immediate Evacuation)  
• You hear sustained melody from substrate.  
• Personal shadow detaches >5 cm from feet.  
• Hourglass sand halts in mid-fall.  
• Cairn found toppled with nodules missing.  
• You feel air movement: wind does not exist here; something else is displacing volume.  

If any of the above occur, signal recall beacon, back-track along guide-line until you re-enter stable corridor (usually <500 m).

15. Final Advisory  
Treat the Silent High Plateau as a living archive, not a wilderness to conquer. It will not attack you, but it may quietly unwrite the path by which you reached it. Move slowly. Note the stillness.
