Guide for the Ordinary—if Determined—Multiverse Traveler  
Destination: Nadrel, “the Scaffold”

──────────────────
1. Paperwork & Permissions
──────────────────
• Dimensional Port-of-Entry: Your switch-back rail ticket doubles as a visitor’s visa. Keep the brass-etched segment; train guards punch it again when you depart.  
• Currency: Local trade works on stamped slugs of nickel-basalt (“bastles”). Bring small goods from home—needle bearings, chocolate, colored dyes—for barter; residents adore foreign novelties and you’ll earn better rates than at the Hoist-House exchange.  
• Language: Basaltine, a clipped creole of miners’ hand-signals and consonant-heavy speech. Learn four gestures:  
  1. Palm down, fingers waggling = “I’m balanced / not armed.”  
  2. Fist over heart = “My debt’s paid.”  
  3. Two fingers miming walking = “Safe angle today?” (gravity check)  
  4. Thumb hooked upward = “May I share your tether?”—polite when borrowing a safety line.

──────────────────
2. Physiology & Health
──────────────────
• Altitude: 2,800–3,100 m. Begin an acetazolamide course 24 h before arrival or budget two days on the lower terraces for acclimatization.  
• Air Quality: Mist laced with iron fines and turbine exhaust. A half-mask respirator rated P95 spares your lungs; locals chew moss-pellets that bind particulates—accept one if offered, but don’t swallow the core (it’s reusable).  
• Temperature: 4 °C pre-dawn, 18 °C mid-afternoon. Humidity steady at 90 %. Dress in three thin layers: wicking, insulating, waterproof. Outer shell must resist basalt glass edges (ripstop or dyneema).  
• Cuts & Spores: Basalt acts like obsidian. Pack butterfly closures and antifungal cream (the orange fungi will colonize un-cleaned wounds in 24 h).  
• Vaccinations: Standard tetanus + an extra for “cliff fever” (airborne lichen spores that trigger vertigo).

──────────────────
3. Kit Checklist
──────────────────
Essential • Lightweight climbing harness; most public walkways have eyebolt tie-ins every 3 m.  
• 8 m dynamic tether with auto-locking carabiner.  
• Magnetized boot-cleats (clip-on) for riding ferrous gantries.  
• Fold-flat oilskin umbrella—the rain falls in narrow shafts; an umbrella beats full rain gear.  
• Headlamp that tolerates perpetual dusk; warm-white LEDs preserve depth perception better than blue-tinted beams.  
• Pocket harmonizer (tiny chime). When gravity “drifts,” locals strike these; if tone warps you know the vector is bending and you should tether.  
• Ear-plugs molded for you at home; factory reverberations hit 100 dB during shift-change.  
• Notebook, not GPS. Space folds—mapping devices crash; paper never reboots.

Nice-to-Have • Condensation flask: wide-mouth bottle whose lid condenses vapor—free water everywhere.  
• Small rolls of copper wire; offer them to cloud-goat shepherds for fresh milk or guidance.  
• Binoculars with image-stabilization (thermal pockets warp sightlines).

──────────────────
4. Getting Around
──────────────────
• Hoists: Steam elevators on endless chains. Step quickly; the operator will not halt the cycle for one person.  
• Catwalks: Color code shows reliability. Green = angle fixed today. Amber = check posted drift vector. Red = locals avoid unless chased by lava (rare).  
• Suspension Bridges: Rockfall sirens will shut them without notice. If the klaxon howls, sprint—hesitation kills more tourists than actual debris.  
• Ride-Along Goats: Hoof-hooks fit ladder rungs; rent them if you trust your balance. Helmets mandatory (the goat’s, not yours).  
• Tram Cables: Scenic but exposed. Your ticket pays for a “sky-fisher” who rides with you; they hook any moth the size of a dinner plate that tries to drink from your axle grease mid-journey.

──────────────────
5. Food & Drink
──────────────────
• Meals: Fungus skewers (“ember caps”), cloud-goat cheese, and fern-tip noodles. Everything tastes faintly metallic; carry citrus lozenges to reset your palate.  
• Water: From roof gutters into cisterns—potable once boiled. The condensation pools up-slope look pristine; ignore them unless you like amphibious centipede larvae in your gut.  
• Etiquette: Never leave a plate completely clean; a smidge denotes you are satisfied. Empty plates say the host under-fed you and obligate a refill.

──────────────────
6. Culture & Conduct
──────────────────
• Timekeeping: Shifts, not hours. Four per “light cycle,” each with its own hammer-bell cadence. Schedule visits between hammers three and four; workers linger then, charitably talkative.  
• Conversation: Locals view the city as alive and co-authoring their designs. Speak of ledges “growing” or streets “choosing to intersect”—they’ll warm to you. Call the place “unfinished” and you’ll be politely corrected: “In rehearsal.”  
• Offer Assistance: Spot someone wrestling a gear-cog? Help. Favors circulate faster than money.  
• Photos: Fine outdoors. Inside workshops, ask first; a prototype may last only a day and secrecy protects patents.

──────────────────
7. Hazards & How to Stay Intact
──────────────────
Rockfall  
• Listen for the gunshot pop; you’ll have 5–30 s before the slab goes. Duck under an iron balcony (built for exactly this).  

Gravity Drift  
• Daily bulletin chalked near every hoist lists suspect walkways. If your dropped coin slides sideways, retreat and wait an hour; drift usually resets.  

Weather Shafts  
• Squalls hit like elevator shafts of rain. Your umbrella + tether combo is enough. If lightning arcs along the basalt ribs, unplug anything metal from your body.

Fauna  
• Vapor skimmers are curious; shiny objects attract them. Keep mirrors pocketed or risk losing them to playful snatches.  
• Chorus raptors may use your helmet as a reflecting perch; they drop bones to crack them—wear head protection.  
• Moths crave machine condensate; smear a line of citrus oil on your pack straps to deter them.

──────────────────
8. Lodging
──────────────────
• The Cantilever Inn: Rooms cantilever 40 m off-face; includes free tether lines and sunrise mist tea.  
• Torsion Hostel: Hammocks inside an old flywheel house. Vibrations lull you to sleep; earplugs necessary.  
• Cliff-Monk Guest Nooks: Monks of the Abeyant Edge rent prayer alcoves. Silence from dusk hammer onward. Cheap, safe, no conversation allowed.

──────────────────
9. Souvenirs Worth the Weight
──────────────────
• Chorus raptor echo-stones: slabs that sing back the birds’ chord when tapped.  
• Moss-loom scarves: water-repellent, smell of rain for years.  
• Pocket flywheel music boxes—crank once, spins fifteen minutes.

──────────────────
10. Departure
──────────────────
The rail only descends at dawn, exploiting the cold-dense air for brake efficiency. Arrive early; platforms occasionally migrate upslope overnight, and you may have to descend three provisional ladders to reach them.

Final Counsel
Stand still often. Let the half-light teach your eyes new mid-tones, let factory hammers set your tempo. Nadrel is an adolescent continent’s first draft—raw, irregular, exuberant. Travel with the same qualities and you’ll do fine.
