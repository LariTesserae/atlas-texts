Field Manual 17-B  
Briefing & Checklist for Trans-Universal Visitors to “the Green Yard”

-------------------------------------------------
1. Overview
-------------------------------------------------
You are about to enter an industrial wetland that is simultaneously
a factory, a research lease, and a single living super-organism seventy
metres thick.  Everything you do will happen on, inside, or immediately
beneath the roots of that organism.  Every step, spark, and spoken word
therefore has two audiences: the humans who staff the yard and the
banyan-reef itself.  Prepare to behave as a guest of both.

-------------------------------------------------
2. Arrival & Border Formalities
-------------------------------------------------
Preferred approaches  
• River barge: embark at Pelican Quay (the last real town downstream);
   expect a 4- to 6-hour upriver haul.  Barges off-load at the “root
   slip,” a floating lattice lashed directly to prop roots.  
• Swamp crawler or on foot: feasible only in the dry fortnight each
   midsummer; you will wade knee-deep the final kilometre.  
• Do NOT attempt rotorcraft insertion—the canopy is continuous and the
   convection drafts above it can flip light aircraft.

Documentation  
• Entry chit signed by the Banyan Safety Cooperative.  Your host
   artisan, not a customs officer, will usually countersign on the
   spot.  
• Proof of liability: anything you burn, crush, or poison must be paid
   for in labour or materials.  (The reef has its own actuarial tables.)
• “Wet-work passport” (yellow booklet) recording your last fungal &
   tetanus boosters.

-------------------------------------------------
3. Environment & Personal Gear
-------------------------------------------------
Thermal envelope  
• Ambient: 29 – 34 °C, relative humidity seldom below 95 %.  
• Clothing: treat it like a steam-laundry—loose, fast-dry synthetics or
   waxed linens; cotton rots within days.  
• Footwear: flexible knee boots with siped soles; the root ridges are
   as slick as dolphin skin when wet.  Clip-on instep claws help on
   cambium that has gone glassy with sap.

Water & biohazards  
• Carry a ceramic filter straw: the puddles are potable once tannins are
   removed, but the resident acetobacter will otherwise give you
   spectacular flatulence.  
• Antifungal foot powder morning and night.  
• Ear protection rated 85 dB—steam bleeds feel incidental at first but
   shred hearing over a week.  
• Personal CO monitor: some root hollows collect furnace exhaust.  No
   alarm, no entry.

Illumination  
• Head-lamp in the 530–560 nm (green) band is banned—it confuses the
   reef’s photoreceptors and can trigger sap flow.  Bring amber or
   white.  
• Spare glowsticks for way-finding during scheduled blackout (“cooling
   hour”) each dawn.

Tools & niceties  
• A folding root-key (thin steel tongue) is the local equivalent of a
   screwdriver: slid into living wood it pries open slots without
   tearing fibres.  
• Small gift vials of desiccant gel are prized; keeps weld rods dry.

Forbidden imports  
• Rock salt, borate preservatives, copper sulphate—anything that kills
   plants on contact.  Bag checks are casual but thorough.  
• Wide-band radios; canopy chlorophyll creates strange RF harmonics and
   the engineers won’t risk crosstalk with pressure sensors.

-------------------------------------------------
4. Orientation on the Reef
-------------------------------------------------
Maps are three-dimensional diagrams showing growth projections.  Your
host will give you a waxed-linen overlay updated this equinox; keep it
dry—ink runs into nonsense.

Way-finding cues  
• Root flutes: short brass whistles driven by sap pressure.  Pitch drops
   the deeper you move into the bowl.  
• Colour of mortar seams: fresh jade-green means a branch was felled
   within the quarter; follow those if you need a barge berth.  
• Sodium-lamp strings blink pairs (••   ••) toward dormitory wings and
   triplets (•••   •••) toward heavy works.

Traffic etiquette  
1. Catwalks take priority over ground routes; the plant treats elevated
   loads as pruning stimuli and thickens support in advance.  
2. When stepping aside for a timber haul, face the chain so falling bark
   hits your back not your eyes.  
3. Knock once on any closed ridged door—even if it looks like metal.
   Many rooms are hollowed roots; sound carries better through wood than
   brick.

-------------------------------------------------
5. Social Protocol with Yardfolk
-------------------------------------------------
Language  
Low-River Trade Cant plus the silent pointing dialect used over
lathe-noise.  Keep both hands free while you talk.

Greetings  
Palm brushed across forehead = “my heat to you.”  
Fist tapped on root panel = “my weight on your floor” (asking entry).

Work rhythm  
Four shifts on a 26-hour “sap cycle.”  Never interrupt a shift change;
people use the overlap to log transpiration readings that keep the
quota legal.

Gift economy  
Brass fasteners, desiccants, and fine sewing needles exchange 1:1 for
meal tokens.  A sealed USB stick of technical PDFs is worth a three-day
guided tour.

Taboos  
• Whistling with your lips—conflicts with pressure alarms.  
• Mentioning exact board-foot volumes; locals use “draw weight” instead
   to keep the conversation inside ecological limits.  
• Cutting live tissue without a licensed graft-wright present.  Maximum
   fine: one month root-polishing duty.

-------------------------------------------------
6. Hazards & How to Survive Them
-------------------------------------------------
Steam blowback  
Symptom: piercing triple whistle, root deck suddenly vibrates.  
Action: drop to a crouch—superheated condensate exits at knee height.

Root surge  
Every 18–22 minutes the reef pulses as tide pressure reverses.  The
floor rises maybe 3 mm, enough to topple unbraced objects.  Lean on
handrails until motion stops.

Flash mists  
Temperature inversion triggers dense fog, visibility < 1 m.  
Protocol: freeze-in-place if you hear arcing—welders cannot see you
either.  Fog clears within five minutes.

Sap fermentation pools  
Look like still water with an opalescent sheen.  Inhalation of vapours
at ground level can cause euphoria followed by emesis.  Keep your belt
at root level; if you feel light-headed stand up—dilutes the ethanol
layer.

-------------------------------------------------
7. Sustenance & Amenities
-------------------------------------------------
Food  
Breakfast: steamed cattail pith with cane syrup.  
Lunch: reconstituted bean-loaf grilled over coke.  
Supper: “reef ale” (3 % ABV), mushroom skewers, fried lizard if luck
strikes.  Visitor stomachs adjust in 48 h; bring ginger chews.

Bathing  
Showers are simply warm rainfall: open the canopy louvers, trigger
sprayers, stand on perforated plates.  Soap must be biodegradable; the
run-off flows straight into the outer marsh.

Sleep  
Dorm planks hang on pivoting bolts so beds stay level as the floor
settles.  Wear earplugs—the 01:00 pump surge rattles pipes like maracas.

-------------------------------------------------
8. If You Harm the Reef
-------------------------------------------------
Minor cut (≤ 1 cm)  
– Clean sap away, apply healing wax, log incident in the orange ledger.

Major tear or burn  
– Emergency quorum of three graft-wrights convenes.  
– You may be asked to give blood: human platelets accelerate lignin
   sealing (a trick discovered decades back).  
– Expect to fund a compensatory root brace; average cost: the resale
   value of a hover-bike.

-------------------------------------------------
9. Departure Ritual
-------------------------------------------------
Before you leave, tradition dictates planting a “memory nail”: a
corrosion-proof steel pin hammered flush into an unused root tongue.
Initials, date, and a brief lesson learned are scratched into the head.
  • Nails under ten years old shine; older ones patinate to black.  
  • No more than one pin per visitor—overcrowding is considered rude.

Return passage downstream is quicker: barges ride the ebb and reach
Pelican Quay in three hours.  Your clothing will smell of sap, smoke,
and cardamom for days; locals downriver will know exactly where you’ve
been.

-------------------------------------------------
10. 60-Second Checklist
-------------------------------------------------
[ ] Yellow wet-work passport with current boosters  
[ ] Amber head-lamp + spare cells (no green LEDs)  
[ ] Knee boots, antifungal powder, filtered straw  
[ ] Earplugs + 85 dB muffs  
[ ] CO badge & glowsticks  
[ ] Folding root-key, desiccant vials for barter  
[ ] Self-declaration: “I will take nothing the banyan cannot spare.”

Fasten all in a watertight pack.  Step off the barge.  Feel the root
floor breathe once beneath you.  Welcome to the Green Yard—walk slowly
and the whole place will keep time with your pulse.
