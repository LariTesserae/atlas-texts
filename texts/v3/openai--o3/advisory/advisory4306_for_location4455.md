Field Briefing: Composite Swamp-Nave, Basin ϵ-217 (“The Humming Verdure”)

AUDIENCE
Any baseline human able to walk a few kilometres on yielding ground, comfortable with persistent humidity and dim light. No special licensing is required by the local polity (there is none), but the nearest river port asks visitors to sign a “sound-release” waiver so that any vocal residue you add to the basin is not legally actionable should it answer someone else later. Bring a pen.

MISSION PROFILE
Average visit: 4–6 daylight hours. Overnighting is possible but not recommended for first-timers because of sonic haunt and metal creep. Evacuation by barge after dusk is slow and disorienting.

CORE HAZARDS (ranked)
1. Soft substrate: ankle-knee plunge risk, especially when floor “sighs.”
2. Hyper-humidity: heat stress, fungal infection, mold damage to gear.
3. Accelerated corrosion: static ferrous items pit within minutes; aluminum alloys last ≈2 h; copper alloys hold indefinitely (note the snails).
4. Acoustic echo-persistence: mental fatigue, compulsive whispering, déjà-vu loops.
5. Low biodiversity = low direct predation, but also few audible cues for orientation—easy to spiral.

PRE-DEPLOYMENT PREP

Clothing
• Footwear: wide-soled jungle boots or lightweight bog-shoes; sole vents help them drain.
• Lower body: quick-dry synthetic or waxed linen gaiters to just below knee. Traditional waders concentrate sweat—avoid.
• Upper body: loose long-sleeve silk-blend or bamboo fabric; cotton never dries here.
• Head: mesh hood or wide-brim hat coated with hydrophobic wax; gutters droplets away from your face so you are not bombarded every 3–4 seconds.
• Gloves: thin nitrile under goat-skin—provides tactile feel while protecting from sap acids.

Materials & Tools
1. Metal substitutes
   – Cutting edge: ceramic/obsidian composite knife; keep sheathed and moving when idle (swing on cord).
   – Fastenings: wooden or polymer carabiners.
2. Walking aid: single carbon-fiber staff tipped with hardwood; double as dipstick for floor depth.
3. Pack: roll-top dry-bag; internal desiccant pouch changes daily.
4. Illumination: chemical light strips (amber) or waterproof resin-sealed LEDs. Keep LEDs clipped to moving limb or metronome pendulum to prevent static corrosion of contacts.
5. Navigation:
   – Biolum chalk pellets: crack, toss, adheres to bark and glows 4 h. Colour shifts from aqua to violet as time passes, giving breadcrumb chronology.
   – Lightweight compass works—magnetic interference is mild—but humidity fogs lens; smear with anti-fog gel.

Consumables
• 3 L electrolyte water per person in flex-bladders. You will not want to sip basin water; tannins + biotic film = unstoppable GI revolt.
• High-salt snacks; you will sweat continuously and not notice.
• Anti-fungal wipes; clean feet and any skin crease every 2 h.
• One sachet of iron filings: if you must pause, sprinkle around any steel item; vines preferentially attack the loose iron first.

PPE
• Respirator rating P95 with exhalation valve: spores are usually benign but vents may belch live cultures that colonise sinuses in 48 h.
• Ear protection optional; some travellers use light silicone ear-domes to dampen acoustic ghosts. Do not fully block hearing—generator serves as landmark.

Cultural / Esoteric Etiquette
1. Speak sparingly, at normal volume. Whispering amplifies the déjà-vu phenomenon.
2. When you do talk, finish sentences cleanly; dangling phrases tend to loop.
3. Do not sing sustained notes near vents—the tower occasionally “answers” with sub-bass resonance that can unbalance inner ear.

INGRESS

Approach via river kilometre marker 104. Transfer to flat-bottom punt; pole through mangrove lace to stone lip. Chart shows three “doors” in the living lattice: East (fastest to tower), South (quiet circuits), North (sunniest, slipperiest). Pin first chalk pellet to entrance tree—establish time-zero.

INSIDE

Mobility
• Move rhythmically but not hurriedly. A rapid run turns floor into liquefaction pudding.
• Test each hummock with staff; if water wells >5 cm above surface within 3 s, redirect.
• Use natural corridors: trunks arch overhead; occasional cross-bars less than 1.5 m—duck rather than push.

Environmental Cues
• Light shafts: brightest ≈10:30 local. Use to gauge canopy direction; brightest cluster marks basin centre.
• Generator pulse: 68 BPM, audible and tactile. Walk “with the heartbeat”; the beat vector points roughly NW-SE. Losing perception of the pulse means you’ve either reached bedrock margin (turn around) or ear protection is too heavy (adjust).

Tower Access
Climb permissible where creepers form braided ladders. Concrete slick; rely on wood not stone. Interior mezzanine sits above waterline and offers flat respite for meal break. Avoid touching rotating gear; metal magnets vines faster than anywhere else here.

BIOLOGICAL INTERACTIONS

Flora
• Blister-algae rafts: stepping pops capsules, releasing squirts of irritant lipid. Burns like chili on bare skin; rinse with basin water immediately—it dilutes enzyme in seconds.
• Composite vine-tree sap: milky, turns rubbery on air contact. Can seal small pack tears; do not apply to skin (sets like epoxy).

Fauna
• Razor bats navigate by ultra-quiet echolocation; if one skims you, stay still; waving arms confuses them and they may snag in hood mesh.
• Copper snails harmless but elect to rest on warm gear; shake pack before departure so you don’t smuggle protected fauna.

TECHNICAL NOTES

Electronics
• Anything with passive metal traces corrodes—store in vacuum-sealed pouches until required, then keep in motion (suspend GPS on bungee against chest).
• Solar panels useless under canopy; bring kinetic charger or micro-hand-crank. The generator’s magnetic field is too weak for inductive theft.

Photography
• Optical glass fogs; pre-warm camera in armpit before shooting. IR photography reveals circulation of sap inside vine-pipes—spectacular.

EXIT & DECONTAMINATION

• Dunk footwear in 5% vinegar bath on boat; prevents export of algal spores.
• Dry all synthetic kit quickly; mildew sets in within 6 h if left damp.
• Shower with 38–40 °C water; hotter drives spores into pores.
• Log any aural after-images (phantom conversations) with port medic; most dissipate inside 48 h, but rare “stickers” persist and can interfere with sleep cycles.

EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

• Floor breach (thigh-deep plunge): lean forward onto pack, flail legs lightly until foot finds root lattice, then roll sideways onto nearest hummock. Do not pull straight up—suction grip intensifies.
• Lost orientation: stop moving metal, listen for generator. If unheard, release two sharp coughs; echoes will triangulate off leafy corridors allowing rough bearing.
• Medical: heat exhaustion is primary fatal risk. If dizziness, lie on hummock, elevate feet, sip electrolyte every minute.

WHAT TO BRING HOME (LEGALLY)

Allowed: 200 g plant matrix, 3 snail shells (vacated), audio recordings of ambient layers <15 min each.
Forbidden: live composite saplings; basin water (contains engineered phloem micro-bots from past era—quarantine).

FINAL THOUGHT

Respect the pace. The basin rewards patience: when you fall silent and utterly still, condensation will begin using you as just another leaf. The moment you feel the first bead roll down your nape, you have achieved temporary membership in a super-organism that learned to wear a power plant as jewelry. Enjoy, record sparingly, depart lightly.
