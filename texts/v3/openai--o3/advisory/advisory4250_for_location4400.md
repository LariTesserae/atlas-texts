Field Dossier 77-A:
How to Survive a Stay inside the Hollow-Trunk City  
(compiled for baseline human travelers with standard multiverse-wayfarer training)

0. Get the scale right
The entire “city” hangs inside a chamber only 6-7 m across and roughly 8 m tall. You will feel as though someone has shrunk an ecosystem to doll-house size and then crowded you in among its residents. Claustrophobia is a bigger killer here than the wildlife; rehearse in crawl-spaces before you go.

1. Vaccinations, chem-prep & personal biology
• Broad-spectrum antifungal course (Itraconazole or equivalent).  
• Tetanus booster—every surface is capable of slicing you.  
• Hepatitis-A/B; the pool water carries coprophagic amphipods.  
• Bring a week of prophylactic doxycycline; wound infections bloom fast in 5 °C humidity.  
• Trim nails short; loose corners snag on living wood and will be torn back.

2. Clothing & carried kit
Temperature is refrigerator-cold, humidity is 98 %. Think “winter caving” gear, but breathable.
• Base: thin merino or high-wick polyester long underwear—keeps you warm when soaked.  
• Shell: flexible, cut-proof splash suit (dyneema weave or chain-mail knit) with blunt edges so you don’t wound the tree.  
• Footwear: high rubber boots with instep-grip; depth never exceeds ankle but you will be standing in 2 °C water for hours.  
• Hands: double gloves—inner liner for warmth, outer nitrile-kevlar for knife-edges and chitin bites.  
• Headlamp is forbidden (see “Light Discipline”). Instead carry chem-sticks taped inside translucent bladder or low-heat photonic cloth cooled to tree-ambient.  
• Zero-heat rations: vacuum-dried blocks that rehydrate cold (spirulina, chia porridge, glucose gels).  
• Hip-bag of magnesium-peroxide tabs: releases oxygen to let fermentation sacks “cook” soup without flame—local families appreciate the gift.  
• Two collapsible carbon-fiber wedges (30 cm) for prying openings before they heal.  
• Three meters of smooth ceramic rod (in sections) to use as vaulting/bridge feeler; the wood does not try to digest ceramic.  
• Compact ultrasonic cutter powered by compressed nitrogen cartridges for silent shaping of living wood—it bypasses the “no fire, no sparks” covenant.

3. Light discipline—see without waking the city
The tree is sensitive to abrupt wavelengths hotter than its own bio-luminescence.  
Allowed: green <520 nm, diffuse, pulsed no faster than 3 Hz.  
Forbidden: white LEDs, incandescents, laser pointers.  
Best practice: knotted strip of algae-phosphor that you squeeze; it glows for 30 s— plenty for foot placement, fades before predators lock on.

4. Moving about
• Always test every root with the ceramic staff; a cambium shelf can swell to fill a gap in minutes and crush an ankle.  
• Keep knees bent; when (not if) the floor thickens your stance adjusts without toppling you into the moat.  
• Never stand beneath a fresh fissure—the lips can knit shut and trap hair, hood, or pack straps.  
• Memorize the “city hourglass”: footbridge, basket agora at midpoint. Use the faint up-draft through the smokeholes as nav beacons; warmer sapwood air is 1–2 °C higher.

5. Staying warm without fire
• Exothermic gel packs tucked in armpits and inner thighs. Change packs in the basket agora only; locals accept the tiny hiss as nonthreatening.  
• Keep moving. Extended stillness drops core temp faster than the gel can compensate.

6. Water & food
You can drink the moat if you must—filter first. Tannins give you stomach knots, so drop in basic resin chips (the inhabitants chew them) to neutralize pH.  
Boiling is impossible. Instead:  
• Cold-steep tea tablets (ginger works against nausea).  
• Fermentation bags (12 h) give you lukewarm gruel. Hang them from a slowly pulsing root; vibration speeds the culture.

7. Fauna protocols
Predators are small but fearless.

Fern-mantises  
• Active on nights 7-9 of the local 14-day rhythm. You’ll hear nothing until the snap. Wear the cut-proof hood; the only vulnerable zone they bite is nape of neck.

Moth-bats  
• Drawn to steady breaths. Use “stutter breathing” (out-out-in, pause) during flights overhead.

Millipedes  
• Non-aggressive but coated in cyanogenic slime. Don’t lean on walls; carry a vinegar pen to neutralize accidental smears on skin.

Deep Fauna (spark-woken)  
• Just don’t ignite anything. If you hear trunk-drumming, kneel, place both palms flat on the nearest root, and stay still. The inhabitants will chant a damping cadence; follow their tempo.

8. Social & cultural etiquette
Language = throat clicks, pouchy consonants, and percussive foot-taps on damp wood. You will not learn it in time, but memorise three universal signals:
• Two fingertip taps on your sternum = “I come harmless.”  
• Palm flat, push toward ground = “I will stay low, not loom.”  
• Slow spiral traced clockwise in air = request for architectural song.

Gifts: gut-cord, synthetic twine, or smooth glass beads (tree cannot digest them) are prized. Never present metal hooks—seen as invitation to wound the host tree.

Participating in a cadence: let your guide assign you a single beat; sync at half volume. Wrong rhythm can bulge walls unpredictably and collapse someone’s doorway.

9. Personal anchorage & exit plan
The bark wall heals; you might lose the portal you arrived through.  
• Insert one carbon wedge on arrival, marking height with glow-ink.  
• Every six hours, return and retighten; otherwise the wedge will be swallowed.  
• If shut completely: find a young fissure (still glossy), insert both wedges 30 cm apart, work the ultrasonic cutter horizontally, then lever out a 40 cm panel—large enough for the WayGate recall device to manifest.

10. When to go / when to leave
Safer windows: days 1-6 after a predator cycle— bark-lice bloom, things are distracted.  
Weather to avoid: Interior fog events (“white hush”). Visibility drops to 10 cm, sound deadens, and direction sense fails. If caught, tie off to any root with a ceramic hook and wait; fog clears in 40–90 min.

11. Psychological hygiene
The constant drip and whisper of chitin evokes misophonia in 62 % of visitors. Wear bone-conduction earphones playing low-volume white noise mixed with slow heartbeats—paradoxically calms the tree and you.

12. What you will smell like after 24 h
Resin, vinegar, and wet pennies. Accept it. The odor helps locals gauge your nearness; masking scents offends.

13. What to bring out
The people refuse payment. Instead, document an architectural song on your chest-mounted accelerometer; back home, transcribe the rhythm. Biobotanists pay handsomely for new cadences—each one grows timber stronger than steel when replayed through sonic shapers.

14. Last warnings
• Do not die here; the tree folds corpses into new footbridges within hours, complicating future rescue missions.  
• Keep every promise immediately. The inhabitants mark time by root-pulse, not by sun, and assume “later” means “never.”  
• Look up before you look down; most danger enters from above, not below.

Pack light, think slow, breathe clouds, and remember: the city is alive, but it heals. If you respect that fact, so will it respect you.
