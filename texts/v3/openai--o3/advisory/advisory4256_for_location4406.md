Field Briefing  
Destination code-name: The Charcoal Plateau (official cartographers give up before they finish the first contour line).  
Elevation: ±5 100 m above mean sea level in its parent reality.  
Thermal range: –4 °C at night to +3 °C midday, 98–100 % relative humidity.  
Barometric pressure: variable; expect the physiologic equivalent of 4 000 m in “normal” mountains because the local atmosphere is unusually heavy with micronized water.  
Known physical instabilities: magnetic declination near-total, minor time recursion along vascular plant surfaces, periodic reality redaction (“fold events”) proceeding from the horizon inward at ~20 km h⁻¹.  
Median storm cycle: 57 ± 9 minutes from vapor vent to hail collapse.  
Projected traveler dwell time without resupply: 48 h recommended maximum; 72 h absolute. After that the system begins to treat you as a recyclable component.

1. Pre-departure Conditioning
a. Altitude tolerance. Train to sleep at 3 500 m; you will still feel light-headed, but at least you will stay conscious when the plateau “pants” and steals half the oxygen near ground level.
b. Cold-immersion practice. Daily 2-minute stands in 5 °C water teach your peripheral vessels that they cannot quit; the ankle-deep pans are wet ice.
c. Sensory resilience drills. Play binaural beats at 40 Hz for an hour while solving math you hate. The plateau’s background A♭_0 (≈ 27.5 Hz) plus sleet percussion induces similar cognitive smear; you’re rehearsing not panicking.

2. Kit Load-out
Clothing
• Base: hydrophobic wool or synthetic that keeps insulating when soaked.  
• Mid: phase-change heat vest rated to convert 50 W of battery into radiant warmth; you will never be dry enough for conventional down.  
• Shell: silicone-impregnated ripstop with elastomer gaskets at wrist/ankle; think “drysuit you can hike in.”  
• Footwear: knee-high flexible waders with integrated micro-crampon dots (the basalt is polished glass until a sudden hailstorm and then it is polished glass hidden under marbles).  
Protection & Instruments
• Faraday mesh cape (0.1 mm copper-nickel) worn over pack—mitigates step-three lightning returns.  
• Direction gear: inertial nav unit plus stereophonic “sound compass.” The latter uses four microphones and an LED arrow that pivots toward the loudest sustained frequency so you can keep a heading while the magnetic needle chases drum solos in the wind.  
• Chronomorph recorder: cheap pocket metronome whose ticks flash green; when ticks start to echo, you are inside a time-thin eddy—walk out before the sixth feedback cycle or you join the drip loop.  
• Visual anchoring cards: fluorescent orange with a bold black ⟂ mark. When the horizon folds and spawns mirrors, throw one card to the ground. The “original” card will remain opaque; its copies fade. Follow opacity.  
• 30 m glow-core throw line for crevasse-like fissures that gape and re-close. Attach a carabiner to your harness whenever a fissure starts hissing (that is the basalt inhaling before another opening).  
Life Support
• Calories: 5 000 kcal d⁻¹ of high-fat ration paste. None of the plateau biota is reliable food; the green substrate is 80 % cellulose laced with metal ions that human kidneys treat as live scorpions.  
• Water: filter straw plus ceramic pre-filter for the plant’s mucilage. Water is everywhere but heavily laced with biologic polymers that gum conventional membranes.  
• Heat pouches in case your boiler-beetle curiosity ends with you stranded during aurora freeze-downs (can drop to –12 °C for minutes).  
Electronics
• Everything in IP68 cases with hydrophobic acoustic vents; humidity kills boards like acid.  
• Bring optical rather than radio beacons for rendezvous; the ionized fog chews RF into fizz.  
• Data storage on quartz-etched wafers. Magnetic media re-write when the plateau edits reality.

3. Route & Way-finding
Step length illusions roughly triple perceived distance. Plan progress in hours not kilometers. Mark every second outcrop with a strobe for the return; strobes clipped to plant walls drift time-slow, but not enough to vanish in 48 h.

If possible walk up-wind of the loudest mechanical groans; the compass and your inner ear align on that sound. A direct tailwind easily persuades you you’re walking forward while you are, in fact, pacing circles around a basin parapet.

4. Behavioral Protocol inside the Biologic Architecture
• Do not lean on the green walls. They wick heat; twenty seconds leaves a frostbite oval on your forearm.  
• When walls open overhead, halt. They sometimes drop metabolic condensate—super-cooled water that flashes to ice on impact hard enough to bruise ribs through a jacket.  
• Limit verbal communication to <60 dB (quiet conversation). Loud, percussive noise attracts folded predators.  
• Ungulates: admire. If they freeze-frame into static you have 10 s before they ripple out; be across any water before that or their wake unbalances surface tension and you go hip-deep.  
• Boiler beetles: they detonate if pocketed. People have tried.

5. Lightning Ceremonies
Strike pattern = tac-tac-tac / silence. The silent circle is an electromagnetic vacuum: compasses, heart pacers, even watch hands stop. Never camp in one. If caught, breathe slowly; the pressure drop makes hyperventilation seductive and lethal.

6. Psychological Hygiene
Expected hallucination set: delayed self-images, echoing footsteps, duplicated gear. Counter by:  
a. Writing a current timestamp and pulse reading on wrist every hour. If the next hour shows an identical line down to the second, you migrated into a recursive seam—move 50 m cross-slope.  
b. Talking about what you see even if traveling solo (record yourself). Auditory confirmation of self prevents the plateau from dubbing over your inner monologue.  

7. Emergency Scenarios
Fissure Cradle: if a fissure closes around a limb, inject 5 mL vasodilator (kit vial) to prevent crush-freeze necrosis; signal teammate to apply throw line to torso and haul at next opening cycle (≈ 11 s mean).

Fold Predator Engagement: a curtain of horizon rotates downward. Immediately shut eyes, count aloud to eight, then look. The fold requires line-of-sight to copy; eye closure breaks handshake. If duplication already occurred: keep eyes open, walk backward 20 paces. Your copied self will rewrite behind you; avoid standing in the shredding paper stream.

White-out Fog Bloom: drop to one knee, extend an arm. When the fog is metabolic it glows faintly green; wait. Within 90 s it will flow downhill, clearing air for ascent. Trying to outrun the bloom downhill exhausts you and wastes precious altitude.

8. Camp Craft (for the stubborn)
No fires. Combustion is nearly impossible in wet 02-thin air. Instead:
• stake a reflective bivy inside a triangle of basalt (rock still radiates geothermal warmth, ~2 °C).  
• Hang a condensation tarp under the plant vault to collect drip water while you rest; keep tarp edges inside pack after use—the plateau memorizes shapes, and a loose tarp can be “reused” by the system on you later.  
• Sleep in 90-minute blocks; REM disturbance from low-frequency vent chords peaks every two hours.

9. Extraction & Quarantine
Depart by the same sound bearing you used in. Directional drift doubles after 48 h exposure. On exit, spend a full minute under a UV-C arch; it kills residual plant spores that would treat Earth’s ecosystems as a buffet. Burn or magnetically wipe all digital storage; even inert survey data can carry fold-logic exploits.

10. Souvenirs
Take none. The place already keeps a copy of you. Bringing a counter-copy home risks both realities seeking synchronization—and they generally do that with lightning.
