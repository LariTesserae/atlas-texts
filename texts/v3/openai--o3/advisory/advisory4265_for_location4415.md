Field Brief: The Unresolved Summit (a.k.a. “The Cylindrical Sea,” “Glass-Crown Throat”)

OVERVIEW  
A circular lava-glass platform (Ø ≈ 35 m) is submerged inside an upright, ten-metre-tall oceanic column whose liquid and acoustic physics are unstable. A traveler remains physiologically viable in the medium but is assaulted by continuous, full-spectrum hydrophonic pressure. Gravity, temperature and matter-persistence fluctuate. Exfiltration temporarily deafens. No clear lethal vectors are reported, but plenty of maiming ones are. Treat the site as a Class-IV Reality Shear with Class-II Biological Compatibility.

1. PRE-DEPARTURE MEDICAL & PSYCHOLOGICAL

• Auditory conditioning: spend two weeks with broadband pink-noise generators at 95 dB for 2 h/day. Purpose: build partial tolerance and map individual tinnitus thresholds.  
• Pulmonary test (saline aerosol uptake). Anyone with chronic lung disease, severe asthma or low mucociliary clearance is disqualified; the mixed-phase “air-brine” equals breathing dense fog saturated at ~102 % RH NaCl.  
• Vertigo, motion-sickness and depersonalisation histories are red flags. The gravity lurches, shape-instability and auditory overload trigger dissociation in 40 % of first-timers.  
• Vaccinate for broad marine pathogens (vibrio, pseudomonas, leptospira) even though ordinary microbes may not persist; better safe.

2. EQUIPMENT LIST

Hearing/Communication  
• Dual-stage cortical bone-conduction plugs (commercially “Mako-Bones”) + passive gel slugs for external canal. These allow speech-range hearing yet damp everything above 100 dB by 40–60 dB.  
• Throat-mic with subsonic carrier (18 Hz) for team comms; spoken words ride under the roar and are picked up by matched haptics on the larynx.

Garments

• Bio-adaptive semi-dry suit, 5 mm, with integrated osmotic heat exchange pads. Must flex easily; bulky scuba neoprene is hazardous near low-g pockets.  
• Bleed-resistant gloves, open fingertips for tactile feedback; razor chips from edge-regrowth happen without warning.

Mobility / Safety  
• 12 m retractable dyneema tether anchored to central eye-bolt you will install on arrival (self-drilling magma-bit supplied). Snap gate must auto-release at >150 N to avoid being stretched into wall currents.  
• Pair of micro-crampons for glass; magnetic soles are useless—rock is non-ferrous.  
• 2 kg variable weight belt: jettisonable for sudden lunar-gravity zones, addable for strong down-swells.

Sensors  
• Wrist-mounted baro/graviometer (commercial “Inclinostat”) that pings when local g departs 0.7–1.3 g.  
• Elastic depth cell to verify water pressure remains benign (<1.5 ATM). If gauge spikes, retreat; the place sometimes “remembers” real abyssal pressure for seconds.

Illumination & Marking  
• Chemiluminescent ribbon (cool cyan) – non-electric light is least likely to agitate the lightning events.  
• Reversible fabric arrows with embedded piezo buzzers: stake two toward exit route; when the sonic field modulates they vibrate, making them findable by touch if you do go deaf inside.

Consumables  
• 1 L isotonic glucose-saline in bite-tube. The ambient liquid is drinkable but takes mental effort and causes mild hallucinations of taste (“drinking whalesong”).  
• 400 kcal quick gel packs—the roar raises basal metabolic rate by 30 %.

Prohibited  
• Loose metal items (rusts instantly into knife-edge oxide fans).  
• Conventional paper, maps or notebooks—they delaminate under acoustic cavitation. Bring wax-board slate instead.

3. ARRIVAL & ENTRY PROTOCOL

1. Approach outer wall slowly. Observe stormcell rhythm; enter during a lull (<0.5 s wall ripple).  
2. Exhale as you pierce the membrane. Your first inhalation inside will be half-brine. Trust the physiology override but keep respirations shallow for ten seconds.  
3. Immediately drop your anchor bolt at the geometric centre (still normal gravity). Clip tether.  
4. Allow 60 s for equilibrium; g often dips toward 0.8 g near rim right after a new water influx.

4. NAVIGATION & HAZARDS

Sound Shear  
• Even through plugs you feel infrasonic pulses in bone marrow; some travelers interpret these as language. Do not answer verbally—the column has mimetic feedback and may tailor current vortices to syllables you emit. Use hand signs.

Water Wall  
• Touch only with back of gloved hand. If drawn in, curl into fetal position; do NOT swim. Wall ejects most objects after 15–40 s once density mismatch builds.

Basalt Regrowth  
• Fresh razor petals grow 1–3 cm/min along cracks. Audible cue: a 6 kHz shimmering trill. When you hear it, freeze and lift feet; let growth pass unless you enjoy involuntary pedicures.

Thermal Bifurcation Vents  
• White steam & black chill radiate simultaneously. Stay ≥2 m away. Sudden contact can cause both frostburn and scald on same patch of skin.

Gravity Folds  
• If airborne more than 2 s, tighten into a ball and wait—not flailing cuts drag by 80 %. Belt-weights often pop you back down gently.

5. INTERACTING WITH THE FIVE ARTIFACTS (OPTIONAL)

1. Pipe-Cairn: blowing across apertures below 60 Hz modulates local gravity by ±0.05 g; handy for self-experiments, risky otherwise.  
2. Driftwood Chair: sitting longer than 90 s induces chronological stutter (logs’ fiber growth rings replay). Mark timepiece before use; recover by standing abruptly.  
3. Shell-Spiral: observe only. Reaching in arms multiplies petal-growth of floor 10×.  
4. Empty Bowl: pouring one droplet of personal blood creates a momentary “mute bubble” (radius 1 m) where roar ceases—excellent for regrouping, lasts 20 s.  
5. Ember Handprint: Do NOT touch bare. The print anchors the whole column’s time index; tampering has collapsed prior expeditions into non-retrieval.

6. DURATION & EXIT

Recommended dwell time: 30 min first visit, 60 min maximum without deep neurological training. Exceeding 90 min raises risk of “shape drift”: when you leave, your joints retain micro-oscillations that never fully stop (documented, uncomfortable, permanent).

Exit Steps  
• Reel in to centre eye-bolt, ensure respiration steady.  
• Slow heart to <100 bpm; tachy exit amplifies deafness period.  
• Cross membrane with eyes shut; luminous flash can stun retinas.  
• Post-exit: expect total silence for 1–3 h despite normal ambient sound. Use vibration-based communicator to coordinate recovery. Consume warm isotonic fluid, apply anti-inflammatory eardrops.

7. POST-VISIT AFTERCARE

• Audiogram at +6 h and +72 h. 5 % experience permanent 2–4 kHz notch loss.  
• Dream diary: roughly a third receive semantically rich nightmares which sometimes decode into actual seismic data from their home timeline. Submit logs to the Acoustic Geomancy Project.  
• Launder suit in 3 % acetic acid to dissolve lithic embryos (microscopic living stone buds) before they colonise plumbing.

8. ETHICS & IMPACT

The summit appears to create life-forms spontaneously; avoid souvenir collection—they disintegrate soon after, and removal may truncate local biogenesis cycles. No litter; even small detritus is sonically metabolised into unknown outcomes. The site may be a planetary proto-vocal organ—treat it like a larynx cousin, not a theme park.

9. CONTINGENCY: IF THE VOICE RESOLVES

Should the oceanic chord collapse into ordered speech (documented only once, 17 syllables), evacuate instantly. Pressure reportedly surged to 8 ATM for three seconds before resetting. Prearranged recall signal = team leader draws a clockwise circle on palm twice, then slashes vertically.

Remember: the summit is halted mid-sentence. Your presence is a punctuation mark; strive to be a comma, not a full stop.
