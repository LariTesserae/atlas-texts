Field Guide for Human Visitors to “The Approach”  
An operating checklist, gear manifest, and behavioral protocol compiled for multiversal surveyors, xenobiologists, salvage engineers and the merely curious.

────────────────────────────────────────
1. Understand the Fundamentals
────────────────────────────────────────
• Atmosphere: Warm, slightly above Earth-sea-level pressure, saturated with dense non-aqueous hydrocarbons. Oxygen partial pressure is only 3-4 %, far below the 19 % a human needs.  
• Hydrologic null: zero free water, zero corrosion, no evaporation or convection. Everything you exhale or spill will stay where it is.  
• Biosphere operates on SOUND, not territory. Frequency bands are “real estate.”  
• Terrain: knife-edged infant crystal, no weathering at all. One slip = puncture wound.  
• Environment is artificially locked; destabilise a regulator and you may trigger thermal run-away or implosion of the pressure door on your way out.

────────────────────────────────────────
2. Personal Life-Support
────────────────────────────────────────
A. Respiratory
   1. Closed-circuit re-breather (CCR) rated for low-pressure hydrocarbons.  
   2. Two redundant scrubber canisters. Moist exhalations condense instantly; make sure trap bottles are emptied in the airlock only.

B. Suiting
   1. Soft-shell abrasion suit with integrated cut-proof kneepads/elbow pads (Dyneema weave or better).  
   2. Overshoes: vulcanised nitrile “whisper-tread” soles—dampens footfall acoustics, will not chip or spark on hard edges.  
   3. Gloves: articulated ceramic-mesh palms; fingertips conductive for manipulator use but retractable to avoid leaving skin oil.

C. Sensory Management
   1. Ear-filters, not full deaf-hoods. You must hear the chamber to keep your speech or tool noise inside vacant frequency windows. Filters should drop overall SPL by 25–30 dB while preserving spectral structure.  
   2. Visor HUD tuned to sodium-mercury spectrum (#589 nm dominant). White LEDs attract rust-midges; use narrow-band amber or bring no light—the bulkhead lamps are more than adequate.

────────────────────────────────────────
3. Acoustic Protocol (“The Quiet Rules”)
────────────────────────────────────────
1. Speak only on COM band 15–17 kHz (just above the highest native choir). Hand-held ultrasonic throat mics are standard; human ears won’t hear you, but teammates’ earpieces will.  
2. Never whistle, clap, cough uncovered, or operate percussive tools.  
3. If an inadvertent sound collision occurs (you’ll know: the chamber will stutter out of its perfect roar for about 0.2 s), freeze and wait. The fauna “reset” in under a minute if the incursion stops.

────────────────────────────────────────
4. Navigation & Footing
────────────────────────────────────────
• Stay inside Vent-Spider silk causeways. The gauzy silver paths are load-bearing and purposely strung over safe routes. Think of them as suspension bridges; stepping outside is like walking off-trail onto broken glass.  
• Silk will flex but not break—dragging equipment across it is acceptable, cutting it is ecological vandalism (and removes half the room’s structural tension).  
• Ladders carved by former saw crews exist; their rungs are shard-tops. Each rung can take 200 kg point-load; distribute weight regardless.

────────────────────────────────────────
5. Biological Hazards
────────────────────────────────────────
• Rust-Midge saliva is mildly caustic (pH ≈ 2). A swarm can pit visors in minutes. Carry neutralising gel (magnesia paste).  
• Echo-Newt resonance can nauseate susceptible visitors (16–32 Hz). If vertigo hits, sit, bite jaw joint to equalise middle ear, breathe normally until gait stabilises.  
• Vent-Spiders ignore large warm objects unless you vibrate their lines under 200 Hz—in other words, don’t drum fingers on silk.

────────────────────────────────────────
6. Tools & Allowed Technology
────────────────────────────────────────
ALLOWED  
• Diamond-wire hand saws (battery, not fuel).  
• Cold laser cutters ≤ 20 W.  
• Drone cams with silent ion thrusters (prop-fans create disruptive vortices).

FORBIDDEN  
• Internal-combustion anything.  
• Open flame, pyrotechnic flares, thermite.  
• Broadband speakers or sonar pingers below 50 Hz.

────────────────────────────────────────
7. Contamination & Sample Handling
────────────────────────────────────────
• No water past the iris. Even sweat droplets can seed corrosive microclimates. Suit liners with desiccant packs are mandatory.  
• Samples must ride in vacuum-tight acrylic vials; DO NOT pressurise with terrestrial air—use native atmosphere from the service corridor tap.  
• On exit, use the vestibule’s electrostatic shower. Orange dust is rich in halite; if it settles in re-breather valves back home it will crystallise further and seize the diaphragms.

────────────────────────────────────────
8. Interaction with Industrial Relics
────────────────────────────────────────
• They are still on an external grid. Pull the wrong breaker and you could drop temperature control for the entire chamber.  
• If you must interface, first isolate by throwing the brass knife-switch on the south wall (label: “SECTION 4—MANUAL”). Wait 30 s for capacitance bleed. Only then attach diagnostics.  
• Salvage law here is murky. Assume everything belongs to the dormant operator consortium unless your charter says otherwise.

────────────────────────────────────────
9. Emergency Scenarios
────────────────────────────────────────
A. Regulator Failure (Temp swing ± 5 °C in 60 s)  
   • Abort. Extract to pressure door immediately; stasis collapse will start convection, kicking dust into lethal blizzards of glass needles.

B. Suit Breach  
   • Apply two-layer mineral-binder patch; dust makes excellent filler. Hold until exotherm sets (≈ 45 s), then proceed to vestibule.

C. Acoustic Attack (fauna swarm)  
   • Emit 120 dB burst at 38 kHz. Stuns rust-midges, confuses spiders, inaudible to humans. Gives you 90 s clearance to retreat.

────────────────────────────────────────
10. Psychological Hygiene
────────────────────────────────────────
• The fixed, eternal ambience induces “chronostasis vertigo”: travelers report feeling as though they themselves have stopped moving. Scheduled micro-tasks (thumb calisthenics every two minutes, HUD-prompted) help anchor time perception.  
• Post-visit tinnitus is common; mild cases subside in 12 h, severe cases respond to low-level pink-noise therapy.

────────────────────────────────────────
12. Final Don’ts (memorise these)
────────────────────────────────────────
1. Don’t talk in the audible range.  
2. Don’t touch a crystal face with bare skin—oil films never evaporate.  
3. Don’t cut live spider silk unless you are ready for a partial ceiling collapse.  
4. Don’t disrupt the roar; if you do, freeze and let the choir restabilise.  
5. Don’t linger once your mission clock is done. The Approach will not harm you, but the longer you stay the more likely you become the disturbance.

Safe travels. Keep your frequencies clean.
