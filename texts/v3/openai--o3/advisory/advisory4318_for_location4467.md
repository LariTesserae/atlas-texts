Field Briefing for Multiverse Visitors  
Site code (provisional): Basalt-Kiln Sub-Factory, Canyon Stratum -30 m

1. Environmental Profile  
• Temperature / humidity: 40–50 °C (104–122 °F) at 95 % RH. Expect profuse sweating and rapid salt loss.  
• Air quality: Metallic steam, gearbox oil vapour, algal spores, rodent dander. Oxygen normal, but suspended particulates irritate lungs and eyes.  
• Illumination: 40 % of the arc-sodium string is dark; full blackout possible if the feeder grid trips. Visual range in native light ≈ 12 m.  
• Hydrochemistry: Warm, slightly acidic (pH 6.4–6.8). Dissolved Fe, Cu, and lubricants. Not potable without field treatment.  
• Structural integrity: Catwalk bolts and un-heat-treated girders have lost up to 20 % tensile strength. Floor rock unfaulted but crazed; minor steam-heave occurs after heavy seep surges.

2. Personal Kit (minimum)  
• Garment layer: Flame-retardant cotton or aramid coverall with vented mesh lining. Synthetic hiking shirts will melt on hot steel.  
• Footwear: Vulcanised-rubber or ceramic-soled industrial boots (steel midsoles soften here). Screw-in ice cleats give traction on algae film.  
• Respiratory: Half-mask elastomer respirator, P100/OV cartridge stack. Bring two spare sets—the organic-vapour layer gums quickly.  
• Optics: Anti-fog safety goggles tinted to cut sodium-lamp glare; strap-retained.  
• Hydration: 3 L/hour capacity. Add electrolyte tablets every second litre; plain water will not maintain sodium.  
• Lighting: Head-lamp (waterproof, 600 lm) plus two chemical light sticks per person. Strap one stick to the rail of the freight lift as a “return beacon.”  
• Comms: Bone-conduction or throat-mike radios; ordinary speaker phones vanish in the ambient roar.  
• Gloves: High-dexterity leather/kevlar blend. Bare fingers blister in seconds on sun-hot pipe runs.  
• Misc.: Collapsible trekking pole (for probing water depth), multitool, 15 m paracord, small chalk or wax pencil for marking exit arrows on steel.

3. Health & Safety Protocols  
Heat-injury window: mild symptoms after 30 min, heat-stroke risk after 90 min of continuous activity. Enforce 15-min “stand-off” breaks on an insulated crate or hanging sling seat.  
Treat every wet surface as biologically contaminated; lacerations infect in under an hour. Carry broad-spectrum topical antibiotic and waterproof dressings.  
Slip/fall: Number-one extraction call-out cause. Clip a short cow’s-tail lanyard to catwalk rails whenever you halt to observe.  
Noise: 80–85 dB background with unpredictable peaks. Use attenuating earplugs that still pass speech frequencies; constant roar masks shouted warnings.

4. Wildlife Etiquette  
Furnace swifts: harmless but will dive for headlamps—dim your beam when they swarm.  
Pipe eels: can deliver painful rasp if handled; keep cables clear of water to avoid tangles.  
Rats: territorial only toward their own species. Do not stash ration bars in exposed pockets.  
Chitin lobsters: defensive “clap” can startle travellers into slips—stay balanced when wading.  
Never feed or cull; ecosystem is delicately thermoregulated and slow-cycling.

5. Navigation & Way-finding  
The freight lift is currently the sole engineered ingress/egress. It cycles on a 20-minute hydraulic accumulator; missing a window means waiting for tanks to repressurise (≈30 min).  
Longitudinal layout:  
• 0–50 m: tool dump, relatively dry, safest for staging.  
• 50–140 m: pond field, half-assembled presses, 60 % lighting failure. Avoid area left of press #3 where a sinkplate collapsed (3 m drop into sump).  
• 140–200 m: basalt rear wall. Nothing but seep screens and graffiti, but the shading is superb for thermal cool-down before return.

6. Activity Planning  
Maximum advisable dwell time for a healthy adult: 3 h gate-to-gate (includes rest periods).  
Research foci: bio-colonisation studies, alloy creep tests, industrial archaeology, still-lively rumours of an untouched tool-crib with precision gear.  
Photography: Must compensate for sodium colour shift; white-balance cards bleach, so shoot RAW, bracket ±1 EV, and bring desiccant packs for lenses.

7. Psychological Considerations  
Monotone acoustics combined with sodium dusk lighting induces “slow-time drift” in 40 % of visitors—subjective minutes stretch, decision speed drops. Buddy teams must cross-monitor for catatonia-like staring fits. Gentle tactile prompts (“tap check”) every ten minutes mitigate the effect.  
Claustrophobes tolerate the space surprisingly well; the cavern never quite lets the roof disappear. However, people anxious about abandonment sites can spiral. Pre-visit briefing should frame the space as a living system, not a failed project.

8. Exit & Decontamination  
On reaching the lift, strip outer over-layer into a labelled waste drum (site management incinerates weekly).  
Boot soles: scrub in citric-acid bath to prevent exporting copper-loving algal spores to other worlds.  
Shower ASAP—gearbox oils are carcinogenic when allowed to polymerise on skin.  
Hydrate 1 L for every hour spent below. Monitor core temp; if still above 38 °C after 20 min topside, begin active cooling protocol.

9. Don’t Do List  
• Don’t seal any seep or channel “to see what happens”: hydrostatic back-pressure has blown out anchors before.  
• Don’t sit on bare steel for more than 30 s.  
• Don’t collect live specimens without a closed bio-loop permit—fauna here breed on multi-month cycles you could disrupt for years.  
• Don’t assume silence means safety; the conveyor can lurch into motion even with no external power (thermal gradients run the ratchet).

10. Opportunity Notes (for the entrepreneurial)  
• Copper cabling is near-pure; a kilo fetches decent inter-verse scrap rates. Recover at your own ethical discretion.  
• The pipe-eel grease metabolite is a friction-reducing polymer—small vials make coveted machine-shop demos. Harvest with aquarium nets, not bare hands.  
• The living “fern” condensate collectors may inspire low-tech water harvesters on desert worlds; detailed macrophotography welcomed by several universities.

Summary  
The Basalt-Kiln Sub-Factory is not actively lethal; it is a slow oven that cooks impatience, corrodes inattention, and rewards measured movement. Arrive insulated and hydrated, light your own way, tether your focus, and the half-gestated machines and their patient animal inheritors will let you share three unhurried, unforgettable hours of their long becoming.
