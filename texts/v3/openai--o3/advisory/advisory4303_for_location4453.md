1. GETTING IN AND OUT
——————————————————————————
Entry aperture  
• The play-marble’s glassy surface will not accept bulk matter bigger than a sesame seed unless it is briefly “coaxed” open by a dimensional crowbar (standard Multiversal Portico Spike, 40 J).  
• Aperture stability lasts 90–140 s. Do not dawdle; step or swim through the meniscus immediately.  
• Because the pocket equalises pressure, there is no explosive inrush of water—expect only a cool splash as the local fluid meets your suit.

Egress  
• Any cut in the inner skin re-seals in <4 s, so you can’t exit by brute force. Re-use the original Spike or plant a second one 15 min before you wish to leave; it will pre-tune the marble to your signature and re-open on touch.  
• Never attempt to ride a topology loop and “swim out the back.” Those paths fold endlessly and will strand you metres—or days—from the real surface.

2. PERSONAL LIFE-SUPPORT
——————————————————————————
Breathing  
• The water is moderately oxygenated but will not support human gill masks; bring a closed-circuit liquid-breather (fluorinert mix, 4 L) or a micropulse bubble mask with internal O₂ recycling.  
• Carry redundancy: any intricate valves risk de-complexification (see section 4). A backup chemical oxygen candle in a sealed pouch is wise for emergency surface breath if your main system dissolves.

Thermal load  
• Temperature hovers at 10–12 °C. Use a 5 mm neoprene or biopolymer skinsuit, not a rigid dive shell; rigidity impedes the intent-based locomotion the pocket sometimes demands.  
• Integrate a piezo-electric trickle warmer in torso panels. Continuous low-grade shiver will sap judgment after 90 min.

Nutrition & Hydration  
• Ten-hour metabolic endurance window; water is potable once desalinated but carries high manganese. Pack micro-filters or chewable osmolyte tabs.

3. CLOTHING & BODILY PROTECTION
——————————————————————————
Skin & Cuts  
• Ledges are scalpel-sharp aragonite. Kevlar palm-pads and fingertip caps strongly recommended.  
• Wounds clot slowly in mineral-rich water; waterproof hydrogel patches are mandatory kit.

Vision  
• Ambient luminance is 0.04 lux, violet-shifted. Equip low-IR goggles set to photon-gain x10. Avoid full flood-lamps: strong light suppresses algal luminescence for hours and erases your natural way-markers.

Mobility Aids  
• Because nothing falls, weighted boots are useless. Strap-on directional fins or micro-thrusters work, but intent-swim (mental vectoring) is often easier once you relax. Practice in VR before departure.

4. TOOL SELECTION (The Dissolution Problem)
——————————————————————————
Local rule: “No complex tool endures.” Anything with moving parts finer than 3 mm or circuitry more intricate than a child’s puzzle cube will decompose into micro-artifacts within 30–50 min.  
Approved kit:  
• Monolithic alloys: titanium pry bar, solid bronze probes.  
• Single-piece ceramic blades (never folding knives).  
• Wax-sealed vellum logbook & graphite stylus—primitive but stable.  
Not advisable:
• Smartphones, drones, multi-tools, or any articulated instrument. Expect heartbreak and green patina flakes.

5. NAVIGATION & MAPPING
——————————————————————————
Visual references vanish beyond 1 m. Use layered strategy:  
A. Breadcrumbs: drop palm-sized hemp loops dyed crimson. Organic, simple, will last days.  
B. Acoustic pings fail—sound dies fast.  
C. Intent vectors: teach yourself the “Compass Trick.” Think of destination, push willpower through your breastbone; space flexes obligingly. Verify often: look back to glimpse your rear silhouette. If you see your own back, you are mid-loop—reverse course; do not chase yourself.

Mapping  
• Draw node-and-edge diagrams in logbook, not scale maps. Distance is topological, not metric.

6. SAMPLING PROTOCOLS
——————————————————————————
Biology  
• Collect algal tufts with wooden tongue-depressors; metal tweezers stress fronds and extinguish glow.  
• Store in quartz ampoules.

Geology  
• Edge-scrape aragonite minimally; every gram you pry changes flow lines and could redirect seepage, killing square metres of bio-canopy.

Artifactology  
• If you find a doll-jug or nail, sketch it in situ first, then bag it. Removing too many will incite compensatory reality bends—sphere wall may bulge, shrinking external marble by a few microns (documented in Expedition 4B).

7. BEHAVIOURAL HAZARDS & FIRST-AID
——————————————————————————
Pressure Shock  
• Equal everywhere; no barotrauma. But psychological “deep quiet” can trigger panic. Practice sensory deprivation tanks pre-trip.

Cuts & infection  
• Rinse with own drinking water (carry 250 mL in squeeze bladder) before patching. Local microbes are benign but metal ions delay coagulation.

Disorientation  
• The stutter-time events feel like momentary sleep paralysis. Count heartbeats when motion freezes; stay calm until sync returns.

Predation  
• Ribbon-worms ignore macrofauna. If one tastes you, it withdraws; harmless.

8. TIME, MEMORY, AND MENTAL HYGIENE
——————————————————————————
Chronometers drift or simplify. Wear a purely mechanical watch (13-jewel, no date wheel); wind before entry.  
Memory slippage: loops can make you meet your earlier self’s wake. Use the “Anchor Phrase” technique—repeat a personal sentence every ten minutes; log each repetition to detect temporal skips.

Psychological Quiet  
• Extended exposure (≥6 h) induces “Velvet Silence Syndrome”: reluctance to speak even after extraction. Schedule reintegration debrief with audiostimulus therapy (running water, birdsong).

10. POCKET REFERENCE (“WHAT IF…?”)
——————————————————————————
Q: My multi-tool turned into sand!  
A: Expected. Use ceramic blade and bronze probes instead.

Q: Can I drink the water directly?  
A: Yes, but manganese will blacken your tongue for hours and may cause nausea. Filter or limit to 50 mL.

Q: I see three versions of my partner swimming ahead. Which is real?  
A: Call out. The echo-delay will identify the most current instance (live partner answers fastest). Regroup on that vector.

Q: The algae went dark around me!  
A: Your torch was too bright. Wait 30 min or relocate 5 m via intent-vectoring.

Q: Can I bring back a whole ledge section?  
A: No. Removing >25 g of continuous mineral destabilises curvature knots; sphere contraction risk ≥12 %.

CLOSING NOTES  
Treat the Marble Bathysphere less as a dive site and more as a larval universe: fragile, self-writing, easily startled. Move slowly, think in whole shapes, leave with lighter pockets than you planned. The place will still be growing a thousand years from now; your task is to ensure it grows unafraid of visitors.
