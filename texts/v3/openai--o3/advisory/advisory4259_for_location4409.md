1. SNAPSHOT  
Altitude: 3 200–3 400 m above the sub-cloud plain  
Climate: perhumid (mist every dawn, rain rarely), 3 °C at night / 12–16 °C at noon  
Chief hazard: sheer drops hidden in ground-fog, followed by psychological drift (see §7)  
Why go: horizons sharper than sight can grasp; a city that refuses entropy; obsidian night skies.

2. BEFORE YOU LEAVE YOUR OWN WORLD  

Physiology  
• Spend at least five days at 2 000 m or use altitude-sim chambers; plateau hypoxia causes tunnel vision in the first hour.  
• Meditative training helps. Keirr’s two daily silence periods are compulsory; travellers who cannot still themselves are expelled.  

Vaccinations & immunity  
• No local pathogens have yet breached human norms, but bring wide-spectrum antibiotics—city law allows “medicine that leaves no trace”.  
• Allergic travellers must patch-test against giant-nettle formicins (obtain dried leaf from a licensed broker, if possible).  

Paperwork  
There is no formal visa, only the Compact of Non-Disturbance—five lines written on goat vellum at the rim gate. You swear:  
1. no seeds in, no soil out;  
2. no digging deeper than the first knuckle;  
3. no loud tools;  
4. heed the gong;  
5. leave by the path you came.  
Violation → ritual shunning + escorted descent, gear forfeited.

3. THE ASCENT: GETTING TO THE RIM  

Routes  
A. Western ladder-stair: 1 200 rungs of plaited sisal anchored in basalt sockets. Staggered rest niches every 200 m. Safe in dry weather; lethal when cloud-wet.  
B. Freight funicular (counterweight stone cars). Visitors ride only by invitation, so assume route A.  

Timing  
Arrive at dawn when the cloud-sea is densest; if you fall you may survive (buoyancy + low terminal velocity), though retrieval is uncertain.

What to carry up  
• 18 kg maximum—ladder etiquette.  
• Hard pelican-case for trade items (see §6).  
• Twin ice-axes are allowed; powered ascenders are not.

4. LIFE ON THE HIGH TABLELAND  

Essential clothing  
• Soft-soled approach shoes with climbing rubber: scree is glass-sharp.  
• Layering, no bright dyes—deep greens and greys blend, signalling respect.  
• Goat-wool cap and UV goggles: sunlight bounces off obsidian; retinal flash-burn is common in newcomers.

Water & food  
• Condensate tastes metallic-sweet, perfectly safe (no microbes detected).  
• Carry a back-mounted dew-sweep (silicone sheet funnel; 400 ml per hour at dawn).  
• Protein: barter for cliff-goat cheese; fishing is permitted if you return unused bait to the pool.

Navigation  
The plateau is tilted ENE–WSW ~4°. If lost, walk “up-slope” until ridges block the sun at noon—Keirr lies just south of that ridge wall.

Environmental cautions  
• Mirror Pools: surface tension anomalies can trap fingertips—never reach in past the nails.  
• Giant nettle: hollow stems snap into fibreglass-sharp spears when dry; don’t pitch tents within 5 m.  
• Static silence: loud noises echo for minutes between knife-ridges and draw territorial cave‐hares (aggressive, 30 cm incisors).

5. CUSTOMS, TABOOS AND EVERYDAY ETIQUETTE  

Daily rhythm  
• Dawn Cloud-Gathering (locals walk ridge lines with linen lashes to sweep dew into gourds). Visitors may observe, not assist.  
• First Gong: mid-morning; all motion ceases for exactly 640 heartbeats. Hold still. Talking counts as motion.  
• Second Gong: dusk; same rule. Use time to breathe slowly—accelerated pulse marks you as unsettled.

Greetings  
• Press the flat of a fingernail to the other person’s basalt talisman. Words optional.  
• Do not ask “How are you?”—implies unwanted change.

Property sense  
• Tools are communal during daylight, private once sun touches horizon. Always return before last light.  
• River water in channel = civic resource. In gourd = private.

Photography  
• Lenses are tolerated if shutter is silent. Mirror Pools may not be photographed—the reflected light fogs most sensors anyway.

6. TRADE, GIFTS AND ACCEPTABLE TECHNOLOGY  

Welcome imports  
• Fine textiles (linen, spider-silk, nano-weave).  
• Seeds of flavouring herbs that cannot naturalise in cold thin soil—must be pre-roasted to sterility.  
• Precision hand tools of stainless steel; corrosion-proof metal fascinates citizens.  

Forbidden imports  
• Internal-combustion anything, springfire weapons, birds or insects, live soil biota.  
• Anything that hums louder than the wind at the rim.

Currency  
• Obsidian microliths inset with copper dots (size codes value). Bring a jeweller’s loupe to check. Foreign coin exchanged by weight in goat-cheese equivalent.

7. ANOMALIES: WHAT THE PLATEAU DOES TO YOU  

Memory-Smoothing  
After five to seven local days episodic memories flatten; you may recall events but not order. Counter-measures:  
• Keep a time-stamped paper log (digital devices lose clock-sync two minutes per day).  
• Wear a scent strip with a new essential oil daily; smell punches through the smoothing.  

Metal Freeze  
• All oxidation halts. Use the opportunity to clean and seal heirloom gear; will stay pristine until you descend again.  
• Beware: battery anodes also “freeze” – charge retention plummets. Carry manual dynamos.  

Time-Dilation Perception  
Sunsets feel two hours long; do not schedule climbs or river crossings “just after sunset”. Use a chronometer.

8. SAFE EXITS & EMERGENCY PROTOCOLS  

Medical  
• Infirmary at Keirr’s south end (basalt vault, green rams-skull over lintel). Staff trained in bone-setting, obsidian suturing. Antibiotics welcome gifts.  
• Medevac: on request, funicular basket lowered; from cliff base signal a lighter-than-air cloud skiff (weekly). Worst-case: parachute into cloud-sea—thermal columns will drift you to lowland settlements in 3–4 hours.

Legal scrapes  
Minor offence → you carry a 20 kg basalt slab for one gong-interval while elders debate.  
Major offence (watercourse alteration, violence) → escorted expulsion; your name carved into rim-stairs for future warning.
