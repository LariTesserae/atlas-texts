FIELD GUIDE FOR HUMAN VISITORS  
Destination: The Steam Grotto, sub-badland geothermal chamber  
Difficulty: Moderate technical descent, sustained tropical-sauna climate, apex invertebrate predator  
Recommended stay: 2–4 hours (biological contamination and heat stress rise sharply after that)

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. BEFORE YOU LEAVE THE SURFACE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Vaccinations & Prophylaxis  
• Standard multiversal broad-spectrum antitoxin (covers mycotoxins, cheliceran venoms).  
• Fungal-spore allergy test; carry a single-use bronchodilator if you score positive.  
• Tetanus update—you will scrape yourself on clay sooner or later.

B. Conditioning  
• Two weeks of daily 30 min cardio in a steam room; train nasal breathing to keep mouth closed (minimises vapor intake and dehydration).  
• Practice “quiet step” walking (heel never fully loads) on soft mats; spiders key off pressure spikes.

C. Quarantine Kit  
• Pre-entry: 70 % alcohol spray, boot scrub pads.  
• Exit: double vacuum-seal bags for clothing, disposable biosuit if you plan to enter any other living cave system in the next 30 days.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. GEAR CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Essentials  
1. 9 mm static rope, 50 m, plus 20 m accessory cord for hand-lines (descent shaft kinks).  
2. Ventilated climbing helmet with detachable neck cape (blocks drips).  
3. Soft-soled amphibious shoes—neoprene uppers, felt or sticky rubber outsole.  
4. Two ultralow-lumen light sources:  
   • Headlamp that steps down to 2 lumens red.  
   • Photon-class cyan glow-stick (one-hour burn) for emergency signal that matches ambient fungus spectrum.  
5. Passive respirator, P2 or higher, with exhale muffler; swap filter after 4 h.  
6. 2 L water in collapsible bladder + electrolyte tabs (inside cave water is potable but laden with silicates; only for dire need).  
7. Light poncho of silk-Kevlar mesh: sheds condensation while resisting spider fang puncture.  
8. Compact tri-sensor monitor (temp, humidity, CO₂). Alarm set to 2 000 ppm.  
9. Vacuum tweezers & sample vials if you plan to collect—but only with permit from root-divers’ guild.

Nice-to-have  
• Infra-acoustic “thump box” (soft 5 Hz pulser) placed at camp to mask your pressure signature from heat-spiders.  
• Foldout kneeler for photography—keeps you off antibiotic moss that will bleach clothing.  
• Thermal chalk—lets you mark exits for humans; fades harmlessly in 48 h.

Leave at home  
Bright white strobes, drones with propellers (wind equals buffet equals spider alert), chemical heat packs (redundant).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DESCENT PROTOCOL
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Surface recon: slide a fever-probe into the crack. If temp spike >40 °C, fissure flood-vented recently—wait 3 hours.  
2. Anchor rope to basalt knob, not crumbly sandstone.  
3. Enter single file; keep a minimum 2 m spacing to dampen air-pressure oscillations.  
4. At first shelf, pause 5 min to let garments reach dew-point; this prevents constant rain of condensation off your body that would disturb drip-territories below.  
5. Transition to stealth lighting (≤2 lumens red) before you see first turquoise fungal smear.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. MOVING INSIDE THE GROTTO
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Walk: flat-footed glide on the ball, rhythmic and continuous. Sudden stops make micro-gusts → spider trigger.  
Hands: palm-up when brushing vines; many leaves store acidic sap along top ridges.  
Shelves: test with pole before loading—undercut clay occasionally “gives” like wet cake.  
Creek: step only in clear patches; turbidity hides amphipod nurseries that the local guides prize.  
Heat fractures: useful hand-warmers but stay <5 sec; copper-skinned heat-spiders also bask there.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WILDLIFE INTERACTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Fireflies  
• Desynchronize them by waving bright light and you will scramble the cave’s de-facto air-traffic control. Keep lamps dim.

Bats  
• Breathe upward (through nose) when passing roosts—warm exhalations rouse them and the resulting wingburst propagates pressure pulses.

Moss islands  
• Antibiotic plume smells like cedar & iodine. If odour sharpens suddenly, you have stepped too close to a defended drip line; withdraw or shoes will bleach within hours, skin in days.

Heat-Spiders (Thermopressura cuprica)  
Defence plan:  
1. Maintain steady pace; avoid coughing fits.  
2. If one presents (you’ll notice a sudden hush and a copper glint), create continuous slow airflow: swing a poncho gently or squeeze your drinking bladder to produce a hiss. The constant stream masks discrete pressure peaks, confusing its targeting algorithm.  
3. Worst case: drop an expendable heat source (chemical flare, glove heated on fracture) five steps away; the spider will pounce that instead.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
6. MICRO-CLIMATE WATCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Time your visit for local surface dawn. As upper crust cools, cave temp dips to ~32 °C, steam thins, spiders go sluggish.  
Fungal flash events occur about every 47 ± 3 minutes (geothermal harmonic). When light surges:  
• Freeze photography—best moment.  
• Notice spiders stop. You stop too; breaking pact is bad form with root-divers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
7. ETHICS & RULES OF THE ROOT-DIVERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Touch no pillar fragment—tiles fall into shards no one alive can restore.  
2. Collect nothing alive unless you carry twin tokens (one bronze, one bone) issued by the guild.  
3. Replace any drip you divert; if your bottle catches water, pour same volume back before leaving.  
4. Quiet exit: ascend at half the speed of descent to prevent chimney effect that drags surface dust inside.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
8. EMERGENCY SCENARIOS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Heat exhaustion  
• Symptoms: dizziness, “swimming” lights beyond fungal glow.  
• Treatment: sit on lowest shelf (coolest), sip 100 mL water per 5 min, fan self gently—NOT vigorously.  

Ceiling fall (stalactite rubble)  
• Helmet saves skull but pressure wave may invite spider investigation. Deploy thump-box to mask your panic breaths.

Flooding  
• Rare, slow. Follow upward-sloping shelves; watch for rust-brown moss fringe (mark of high-water line). Stay above it until seep recedes.

Lost rope  
• The chamber is topologically simple; keep right hand on wall, follow counter-clockwise until you re-encounter fracture line—shaft is always contiguous with hottest wall.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
9. POST-VISIT DECONTAMINATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Outer layer in seal bag for lab autoclave.  
2. Shower twice: first hot with alkaline soap (cuts grotto oil), then cool vinegar rinse to neutralise moss antibiotics.  
3. Hydrate 1 L per hour for next 4 h; watch for delayed spore cough (24–48 h window).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
10. SOUVENIR ALTERNATIVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Instead of ripping out plate fungi, bring back:  
• Time-lapse of waterbird mosaic “flapping.”  
• Audio of three-note firefly flash pattern (translate to MIDI; it’s oddly musical).  
• Your own pulse, slowed, steady—few places teach such patience.

Travel light, breathe like the cave, move as if you are already half-myth, and the Steam Grotto will accept your visit as a brief anomaly, not a wound.
