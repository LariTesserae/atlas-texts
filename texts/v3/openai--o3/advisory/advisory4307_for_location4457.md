Field Briefing  
Destination: the Subterranean Luminarium (colloquial name among way-farers)  
Approximate stay assumed: 24–72 surface hours.  
Profile of traveller: baseline human with modern multiverse kit but no body augments.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
1. Approach, Descent & Immediate Hazards
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
• Descent shaft: 200 carved limestone treads; each is shallow but slick with condensate. Friction drops to near-zero once you pass the twentieth step. Fit your boots with fine carbide studs or bring a removable crampon plate. A safety line clipped to the ceiling eyelets (spaced every seven meters) is strongly recommended if you are carrying more than 10 kg of gear.  
• Atmospheric shift begins after step 40. You will feel it first as a cool clamminess, then as a mild vacuum in the ears; equalise gently. Total O₂ partial pressure never falls into danger zones for healthy lungs, but the air is saturated at 97 % relative humidity: inhalations feel “thick,” exhalations feel weak.  
• Light transition: past step 120 you leave the last shaft-torch and must switch to your own illumination until you reach the chamber proper. LEDs on low, red or amber only; white light blooms harshly in the quartz and is considered aggressively exotic by residents.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
2. Personal Kit Checklist
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
a) Clothing  
• Inner: wicking silk or bamboo to keep a micro-climate; cotton never dries here.  
• Mid: thin neoprene or wool blend for 10 °C ambient.  
• Outer: ankle-sealed wader pants or calf-high waterproof gaiters; water is only ankle-deep in channels, but if you misstep into a cistern pit it becomes thigh-deep.  
• Hands: nitrile/latex liners under goat-skin gloves; horn tools splinter unpredictably.

b) Footwear  
• Flat, soft-rubber soles with micro-studs. Avoid hard heel strikes that ricochet sound.

c) Illumination  
• 2x coin-cell red micro-lamps for navigation.  
• 1x palm lantern on cool-white LED for emergency only (keep sheathed).  
• DO NOT BRING open flame unless invited to borrow a residents’ saucer; foreign combustion is taboo.

d) Respiratory  
• Dust mask is optional; sulfur note is noticeable but not toxic.  
• If you have reactive asthma, carry a micro-filter because mineraloid aerosols spike when the quartz “hums.”

e) Waterproof bag with:  
• High-energy dry rations (compressed algae wafers, honey lozenges). Anything fragrant draws the isopods.  
• 1 L squeeze flask—fill from a residents’ cistern only after clarifying request. The stream is potable if filtered for salt (carry a ceramic grail filter).  
• Compact ultrasonic distance measurer (see “Navigation”).

f) Trade Goods  
The culture operates on gift-obligation rather than fixed barter; arrival without an offering is rude. Favoured items:  
• Stainless or meteoric iron needles (<5 cm).  
• Fine braided cord (synthetic or silk).  
• Alcohol-free cleaning oil for antler tools.  
Present goods quietly with both hands, then step back to let the echo fade before speaking.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
3. Orientation & Movement Inside
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
Spatial challenge: the chamber is three-dimensional habitat. Footpaths shift with maintenance work; ladders may be repositioned daily. Use these methods:

Echo counting (local style)  
1. Cup hand around mouth, emit a single click.  
2. Count heartbeats until first return.  
3. Double the count for ceiling height; half for distance to nearest opposite ledge.  
Spend 30 minutes practising on the lower shelf before roaming upward. The dwellers will appreciate the effort.

Instrumental backup  
Your ultrasonic measurer set to 40 kHz will give distance but also infuriate the glow-gnats and interfere with the people’s acoustic sense. Use sparingly, under 3 s bursts.

Water lanes  
When unsure, wade in the central braid—never on dry shelves—because it is the universal “public thoroughfare.” Shelves are semi-private property; trespass implies theft sentimentally, even if accidental.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
4. Cultural Protocols
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
Language  
Audible consonants dominate; vowels are swallowed. A handshake between surface dwellers and locals is unknown. Communicate by:

• Initial echo: travellers announce presence with three slow clacks of stone on stone; residents respond from the nearest terrace.  
• Volume discipline: match the loudness you receive. Outsider booming is interpreted as mockery.

Taboos & Permissions  
1. Fire: only their low-wick saucers are tolerated. Keep batteries and heating elements concealed; warming pads are acceptable under garments.  
2. Corpses: do not inquire about burial rites. Any mention of “where the stream ends” is considered theft of destiny.  
3. Observation: feel free to watch tool work, but never touch a ledge-face—each carries generational identity.  
4. Quartz hum pauses: everyone stands still. Mimic them regardless of what you believe the cause is. When they resume, wait two breaths before you move, otherwise you ruin the communal rhythm.

Gifts/Return  
They will usually reciprocate with isopod-jerky or a thumb-sized vial of lamp-moth dust. Accept with visible gratitude, then stow carefully; dropping a gift is grave humiliation for both parties.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
5. Health & Physiology
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
Hydration  
High humidity masks dehydration. Drink 300 mL/hr while active.

Electrolytes  
Stream water is low-salt; however, perspiration does not evaporate, so sodium loss occurs invisibly. Add one electrolyte tab per litre.

Hypothermia  
Ambient 10 °C + wet skin = core temp threatens after 3 hrs idle. Keep moving or wear an active heating vest set <35 °C to avoid creating hot air pockets.

Auditory overload  
Continuous 70–75 dB waterfall drone. Carry musician’s ear-plugs (–15 dB flat attenuation) to stave off fatigue but still let you track echoes.

Sleep  
If staying overnight, ask for a “mid-ledge sleep frame,” a suspended cedar-reed cot above the water mist line. Use your own ultralight quilt rated 5 °C.

Pathogens  
No documented bacterial or viral agents harmful to surface humans; salt-rich environment suppresses most. Still, treat isopods & water as raw protein—cook (70 °C+) using a sealed alcohol micro-stove on an upper terrace with resident permission.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
6. Opportunities
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
• Anthropology: observe a society where acoustics equal social capital. Recordings are allowed but never play them back on site.  
• Mineralogy: quartz-salt chandeliers sometimes shed prismatic needles. With trade, you may legally purchase up to 200 g.  
• Mythic biology: lamp-moth dust is a unique bioluminescent reagent; transport in dark, cool vial.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
7. Exit Strategy
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
Plan to leave before your batteries dip below 50 %. Climb is strenuous in humid air. Take two 5-minute pause zones at steps 80 and 30 to avoid syncope. Clear your footwear of salt crust at the surface; it calcifies quickly in dry air and can wreck soles.

Should the stream rise (low-probability cataclysm)  
• Residents will direct you via bang-series codes to the “Fresh-cut Notch,” an emergency spiral that surfaces three kilometres downslope. Follow without question; the route is theirs, not yours.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
8. Quick-Reference “Never” List
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
Never strike a spark.  
Never whistle; it fouls their linguistic range.  
Never stand in a down-spout channel—reserved for water spirits in folklore.  
Never ask a person’s exact age; longevity is collective, not personal.  
Never attempt to photograph the quartz hum event—cameras malfunction from sub-harmonic vibration.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
9. 30-Second Survival Drill
––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
Fall off a shelf? Curl, roll into the water lane.  
Lamp fails? Two knuckle raps, pause, one rap—locals will throw down a cord.  
Feel dizzy? Sit on heel, back to wall, breathe through nose four counts in, six out—CO₂ purges better in humid air.  
Quartz hum intensifies? Freeze; monitor water. If rings grow to ankle-high waves, start ascent immediately— it precedes pressure shifts.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
10. Departure Thought
Stability, not stasis, sustains this hidden city. Your arrival is a tolerated ripple. Make your presence as light and brief as the glow-gnats’ green bloom—thirty heartbeats, then darkness, leaving only a faint memory of movement in a place where nothing wishes to move differently.
