Traveler’s Field Brief  
Destination: The Pillar-Birch Basin (working title; locals simply say “the Hollow”)  
Type of world-thread: Low-energy, high-humidity sub-temperate ecotone with sap-driven light industry  
Average stay recommended for first-time outsider: 4–7 local days (one “glynn”)  

1. Atmosphere & Basic Physiology  
• Breathable mix for baseline humans; O₂ ~19.4 %, CO₂ slightly high but well below narcotic thresholds.  
• Constant humidity 95–100 % RH; dew-point never more than 2 °C below ambient. Morning air is a refrigerated towel—expect condensation inside lungs on the first day. Work up slowly and keep moving.  
• Allelopathic volatiles from pillar-birch bark resemble mild eucalyptus and menthol; most visitors report pleasant coolness, a minority get bronchial tightness. Carry an antihistamine inhaler.  

2. Vaccines / Prophylaxis  
• Spores: Use the broad-spectrum anti-fungal nasal mist (ketaconazone-34) 12 h before arrival, then every 48 h.  
• Endemic slime-amoebae occasionally cause trench-foot-like infections in damp boot linings. Dust feet with tolnaftate powder nightly and dry your insoles on a steam-vent shelf.  
• Tetanus booster ≤10 yr old (ubiquitous ferrous boardwalk nails).  

3. Clothing & Personal Gear  
Do bring:  
• Two sets of quick-drain field trousers, cuffable above ankle for puddle work.  
• Light merino or hi-wick base layers—even cotton will never fully dry here.  
• Rubberized or graphene-poly boot shells with deep siped soles.  
• A hooded, thigh-length oilskin or silastic rain jacket. Umbrellas snag on cable-vines.  
• Fingerless nitrile work gloves: protect tools from you and you from lichen.  
Avoid:  
• Velcro (spores colonize loops in hours).  
• Bright chrome, polished aluminum, powered speakers, drones. Shine and noise are read as predation signals by the local herons and by the unseen “steam listeners” on the plain.  

Metal discipline: Everything ferrous goes in waxed canvas rolls with camphor chips; wipe down each night with the town’s standard pumice-lanolin paste. Give your multitool a name; you will be asked if you are “attending to your metal.”  

4. Electronics & Power  
Humidity will short unprotected circuits in a day. Options:  
• Pot and conformally coat boards before travel; bring desiccant packets.  
• Or go analog: brass-case pocket notebooks, grease pencils, wind-up camera.  
Power grid is 48 VDC sap-steam dynamos at communal taps. Step-down converters fried by moisture are a running joke—better carry a hand-crank or accept a tech detox.  

5. Navigation on Arrival  
Landmark system is radial: trunks are numbered in induction-burned glyphs at knee height. Zero-trunk sits on the oxidized copper clearing. Take a rubbing of the layout map posted there; paper maps wrinkle but never tear when damp.  
Boardwalks are one-way—clockwise if carrying cargo, counter-clockwise if empty. Hesitate and you’ll cause pile-ups of silent residents.  

6. Customs & Etiquette  
• Speak in stage whispers. The basin is an amphitheater; locals hold conversations at the level of library murmurs.  
• Every midnight shift, citizens gather on the copper plates and polish personal metal in communal silence. If invited, join; if you decline, wipe your tools elsewhere so the hiss of cloth doesn’t intrude.  
• Trees are “persons.” A doorway cut today belongs to the birch, not the tenant. Knock lightly on bark before entering; it’s for people, not the tree, but manners matter.  
• Do not lean anything hot against living wood. A trunk that senses warmth will grow around it within a week, and you will be fined for forced graft.  
• Currency: palm-sized brass slugs called drams, each worth a half-shift of boiler attendance. Outsiders trade in precision files, desiccant gels, or fungus-proof lacquers instead.  

7. Accommodation  
Guest hollows (Trunks 12-14 east arc) supply hammocks, felt quilts, and a tool-locker. Vent stacks keep interior at 16 °C. Expect glittering droplets to condense on the ceiling overnight—normal. Place a catch-basin to avoid water-thump on your face.  

8. Food & Water  
Water: perfectly safe—the settlement runs sap through charcoal-bark filters. Still, all hydration tastes faintly of syrup.  
Staples:  
• Spore-cultured sourdough steamed, never baked.  
• “Copper eel” (really a saline amphipod) pickled in birch vinegar.  
• Leaf-vein tea, metallic aftertaste locals call “factory tang.”  
Pack protein bars if you need chewing resistance; native cuisine is pudding-soft.  

9. Work & Participation  
Visitors are expected to contribute a shift: options include boardwalk patching, polishing communal pipes, tending the sap-engines. Sign waiver against minor steam burns. The silence rule holds; most tasks are done with hand gestures and brass ringing-tokens.  

10. Hazards & How to Mitigate  
Slip-falls: Walk flat-footed; railings are absent by design (they channel condensation).  
Hypothermia: Not cold, but evaporative cooling never stops—keep core covered once heart rate drops.  
Encapsulation: Don’t leave a hot lantern against bark or camp overnight on a warm exhaust—tree will overgrow by morning.  
Predators (“Steam Listeners”): Rare. If the night whistle blows twice, all lamps douse and everyone stands still. Copy the locals. The trunks reportedly resonate at sub-audible frequencies that deter the creatures; your voice won’t.  

11. Departure Protocol  
• Scrub boots in the copper basin’s sluice to avoid exporting spores.  
• Present polished metal for lichen inspection; lax care incurs a dram fine.  
• Accept a parting vial of birch sap—proof of peaceful visit. The fluid clouds outside its native humidity; keep sealed if you want a clear souvenir.  

12. Suggested Packing List Summary  
1. Oilskin coat & hood  
2. Two quick-dry field outfits  
3. Rubberized boots + spare insoles  
4. Tool roll with stainless multitool, micro-whetstone, waxing cloths  
5. Antifungal mist & foot powder  
6. Merino sleep layer & felt quilt liner  
7. Analog notebook, grease pencil  
8. Compact desiccant-filled gear chest  
9. Low-tech barter items (rust-proof screws, ceramic bearings, mycology textbooks)  
10. Personal mug—wood or horn, never metal (lichen again)  

Mindset to Bring  
Slow down. Every process here—photosynthesis, steam, conversation—moves on birch time. If you listen past the hiss of your own breath, you’ll feel the place synchronize your pulse to the quiet hydraulics of living wood. Those who rush hear only drizzle; those who wait hear the forest think.
