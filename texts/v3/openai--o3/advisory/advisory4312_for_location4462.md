Field Dossier: The High Basalt Table & the Vertical City of Vord Kal  
(Compiled for category-2 multiverse travelers—physically mortal, technologically assisted, culturally adaptable)

------------------------------------------------------------------
0. TL;DR FOR IMPATIENT HOPPERS
• Humid, 3 km up, warm (24-32 °C) and permanently wet.  
• Boots, outer shell, pack, and electronics will stay damp—bring sealed spares.  
• Altitude mild but barometric pressure ≈ 0.7 atm; acclimatise two days before heavy work.  
• Drink only tower-water or boiled runoff; raw puddles are alkaline and iron-rich.  
• Wash your soles in every doorway trough—no exceptions.  
• Roofs are public; anything below the top continuous roof is someone’s home or shop.  
• Lightning-storm drill: follow locals, shut every valve you see.  
• Do not startle glider eels; they shed chitin fibres that itch for weeks.  
• Funicular down-journeys book out a month ahead—reserve as soon as you arrive.

------------------------------------------------------------------
1. POINT-OF-ENTRY OPTIONS

Most travelers arrive by:

A. Portal drop (standard Crossthread Gate)  
   Place the exit 50–100 m inward from the cliff rim; any closer and the plateau’s up-draft shear can destabilise the wormhole mouth.

B. Funicular freight car from the lowlands  
   Six-hour ascent, one scheduled run per “pump-cycle cluster” (≈ every four local hours). Tickets are issued by the Coal & Lift Guild in copper-clad slate wafers. Keep yours dry—replacements cost double.

C. Charter kite-balloon (“bubble-eel” rigs)  
   Picturesque but risky—thermal layers over the plateau can flip a balloon without warning. Hire only veteran pilots who carry chain-saws to jettison ballast lines when ice accretes.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PHYSIOLOGICAL READINESS

• Altitude & Atmosphere  
  Pressure ~70 kPa, oxygen fraction 24 %. Equivalent to 2.2 km on most garden-variety Earths: enough to fatigue sedentary visitors but rarely to edematise lungs. Mild alkalosis from airborne mineral dust is common—counter with acidic lozenges (citric, ascorbic).

• Humidity & Heat Index  
  Dew-point sits within 2 °C of ambient; evaporative cooling is ineffective. Expect persistent sweat. Bring electrolyte tablets and anti-fungal foot powder.

• Pathogens  
  No pollen, very few airborne viruses (they dislike the alkalinity), but bacterial skin infections explode in the warmth. Pack a broad-spectrum topical (mupirocin level or above) and a week of oral doxycycline as fail-safe.

------------------------------------------------------------------
3. KIT LIST (FIELD-TESTED)

Clothing
• Two sets breathable hydrophobic shell (silicone-impregnated fibres or oiled silk). Forget Gore-Tex; pores clog with silica in three days.  
• Knee-high rubber or nitrile-lined leather boots with copper toe plates (dissipates incidental static during storm drills).  
• Quick-dry merino or chitin-blend liners, four pairs.  
• Elbow-length nitrile gloves for spelunking sinkholes.  
• Wrap-around polycarbonate goggles to deflect eel keratin splinters.  
• Rebreather mask with changeable lime packs if you plan to explore steam catacombs.

Hardware
• Collapsible trekking poles tipped with tungsten carbide: basalt is slick.  
• 20 m static line, 10 m elastic safety tether, two auto-locking carabiners—mandatory on roof catwalks.  
• Vacuum-bagged micro-electronics and a hand-crank dynamo (USB-C or your universe’s near-equivalent). Public sockets run 40 V DC - unregulated; carry a buck converter rated to 200 W.  
• Paper notebooks; ink never dries here—use graphite.  
• Short-range comms: ultrasonic “click” units penetrate mist better than radio.  
• One collapsible squeegee: every surface is a lens for water. You will thank me.  

Consumables
• Antiseptic soap sheets (solid film form).  
• Acidic hydration sticks (freeze-dried citrus).  
• Spare boot-laces (the local hemp rots in a week).  
• Bribes: small precision ball-bearings, WD-40 equivalents, and bright dyes are currency with machinists and children.

------------------------------------------------------------------
4. DRINKING & EATING

Water  
Only three safe sources:  
1. Tower-top condensers (sold by dipper cup).  
2. Boiler outflow after a full boil cycle (ask the fireman).  
3. “Blue cisterns” marked with lapis tiles—municipal filtered stores.  

Food  
Staples are protein-rich:  
• Lickworm cutlets (texture of firm tofu, faint copper tang).  
• Coral-mite pastes (looks like hummus, 12 % calcium).  
• Mold-loaf steamed in slug-clay molds.  
Vegetation is imported dried algae bricks—expensive, celebrate when offered. If you’re vegan, plan carefully; the city considers microbes “not animal.”  

Allergy note: keratin dust from glider eels carries an enzyme that degrades mammalian sIgA—can trigger mouth ulcers. Rinse with saline nightly.

------------------------------------------------------------------
5. MONEY, TRADE, AND VALUE

Currency tiers:
1. Iron slugs: cast tokens, weight-based, good at street level.  
2. Watt-chits: lacquered wooden sticks worth one tram-lamp-hour apiece.  
3. Roof deeds: parchment squares representing airspace rectangles; speculative market.  

Short stay? Carry iron and a few kilogrammes of refined copper wire—always in demand for pump solenoids; trades one wire-coil (~200 g) for three nights in a mid-tier dormitory.

------------------------------------------------------------------
6. NAVIGATION & TRANSIT

Vertical Layout
• Ground-0 (original plateau surface) now 20-40 m below current “First Roof.”  
• Levels designated by pump counts since founding: P-0 (lowest drains) to P-8 (sky bridges).  

Moving Around
• Spiral stairs and chain lifts between P-2 and P-5; fare is one iron slug or one minute of manual cranking on communal capstan.  
• Sky trams (electric): P-6 and above, for guild permit holders; flash a roof-deed corner if you lack a permit—conductors rarely check the fine print.  
• Ropewalk network: safe if colour-coded green. Red ropes mean last inspection > one gutter-age; clip in.  

Getting Lost
Addresses follow the format: Guild-Sigil / Pump-Layer / Span-Gate / Cycle-Minute. Example: Hammer-3 / P-4 / North-14 / 1908. Any street urchin can decode this for two slugs.

------------------------------------------------------------------
7. ETIQUETTE & LAW

Absolute Do-Nots
• Step inside without washing soles. Offence = public sluicing (bucket of eel mucus—nasty, non-fatal).  
• Shine sustained bright lights at roosting eels; fines escalate per mass take-off.  
• Dump greywater without a whistler’s permit—the Tressel Keepers monitor drains.  
• Mention growing “gardens.” Soil introduction is ecoterrorism here.

Conversational Tips
• Lean close, speak mid-chest height; upper-face expressions are lost behind goggles.  
• Time quotes: “Back in forty pumps” means ~6 minutes.  
• Gifts are given damp. Present a dry object and you’ll look like you don’t belong.

Justice
Mediated by craft-guild courts; penalties graded in labor cycles. Expect to “volunteer” on a pump crank rather than pay cash.

------------------------------------------------------------------
8. HAZARDS & CONTINGENCIES

Weather
• Cloud-break glare: sudden direct sun can bleach retinas; keep tinted lenses handy.  
• Lightning (rare but lethal): sirens give 90-second warning. Locals drop every gang-plank sluice; help them or at least get out the way.  

Mechanical
• Chain snap: move perpendicular; links ricochet horizontally.  
• Steam burst: super-saturated at 104 °C due to lowered pressure—will flash-scald skin in 0.3 s. Carry a wool cloak (better insulator than synthetics when wet).

Biological
• Tressel nip: deep puncture but clean—wash, pack with iodine clay, bandage.  
• Sinkhole lure-worms (rare): look like pearl chains hanging in blackness; actually predatory polyps. Shine light—but do not approach within 2 m of a hole rim.

------------------------------------------------------------------
9. CULTURAL EXPERIENCES NOT TO MISS

• Dawn resonance: pre-sun crowds tune brass fog-horns to the dominant eel chorus—otherworldly, subsonic.  
• Gutter clay markets: watch potters throw lickworm excreta into translucently thin teaware.  
• Sky Parlor vigil: at twilight, families sing pump-cycle hymns beneath cliff-face ossuaries—a choral wall of echo.  
• Triple-wheel forge: witness a waterwheel, a treadmill mule-gang, and a falling-chain all powering one trip-hammer the size of a cottage.

------------------------------------------------------------------
10. EXIT STRATEGY

• Funicular: only reliable route for bulky gear. Pay in advance, keep ticket parchment waxed.  
• Kite balloon: book sunrise launches; afternoon thermals kill amateurs.  
• Portal: if you opened one on arrival, anchor rods must be driven 1.5 m into basalt to survive nightly slab creep. Re-check alignment every 300 pump cycles (~45 min).

------------------------------------------------------------------
11. LEAVING NO (WET) TRACE

Ecological fragility is counter-intuitive—everything looks like bare rock, but micro-biofilms run the food web. Before departure:

• Dry-bake all boots & fabrics in city autoclave towers (they rent by watt-chit).  
• Declare foreign organics; customs will torch most anyway.  
• Donate surplus acids and antiseptics to rain-ward clinics—they suffer chronic chemical shortages.

A city grows like coral here: human, machine, animal, and stone in recursive layers. Prepare well, move slow, listen to the pumps beneath your feet, and you’ll sink—happily—into its rhythm. Fail, and the plateau will shrug you off the edge the way it sheds every century’s worth of loose, ill-fitted basalt.

Safe travels and steady footing.
