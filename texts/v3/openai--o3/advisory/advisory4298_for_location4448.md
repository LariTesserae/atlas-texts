Field File: Preparations for Veilspan   
Compiled for civil-grade multiverse travelers of baseline Terran physiology (oxygen breathers, 0.8–1 g musculoskeletal conditioning, no chromatic-vision anomalies).

————————————————————————————————  
1. Port of Entry and First 24 Hours  
————————————————————————————————  

1.1. Arrival vector  
• Veilspan maintains no formal spaceport or even clearings for atmospheric craft. Standard practice is to orbital-drop to Platform Tandem-17, a 70 m carbon-foam disk that drifts 400 m above the delta. Tandem-17 extrudes a ladder of hollow monofilament as soon as it senses ≤120 kg mass suspended beneath it. Heavier freight pods travel by rotor sling and are met by cargo drones.  
• The platform’s AI speaks Interstate Maritime Sign and whisper-band radio (ultra-high ultrasonic, inaudible to humans). Bring a bone-conduction earpiece that reaches 50 kHz or learn the sign set.  

1.2. Customs/Bioprotection  
• The delta’s ecology is still plastic; authorities are ruthless about biosafety. Wear a sealed clamshell over your boots until you clear decon.  
• Forbidden imports: vertebrate embryos, high-lumens flashlights (>30 lux at 5 m), combustion engines, any polymer that sheds microplastics, sustained-tone soundmakers (portable speakers, continuous sirens).  
• Mandatory inoculations: two-component anti-cyanobacterial serum (prevents skin colonization by the photophages that form those “silver nets” of moss); tetravalent anti-mycotoxin booster; topical chelator patch for nickel/basalt dust. Arrange these at least 72 h prior to transit; Veilspan med-pods assume you are already dosed.

2. Climate & Physiology  
————————————————————————————————  

2.1. Atmosphere  
Mean: 1.07 atm, 29 °C, 92 % relative humidity. CO₂ hovers around 700 ppm—safe, but you’ll tire faster. The air smells metallic (iron aerosols) with faint petrolic off-notes from volcanic outgassing. A charcoal nose-filter will remove the sulfur fringe.  

2.2. Light  
Peak daylight is 1 200 lux, but the spectrum is skewed toward 590–780 nm. Human circadian systems misinterpret this as late afternoon; jet-lag style lethargy is common. Pack a visor with cold-white micro-LEDs for 15 min dawn simulation each morning. Sunglasses are unnecessary; carry a crimson-canceling lens if you do color-critical work.  

2.3. Sound  
Ambient <25 dB. Your own breathing will seem loud. Practice “library gait”: heel-toe roll, loose shoulders. Travelers who clomp draw stares and, worse, trigger the territorial algorithms of fabrication drones (they interpret heavy footfalls as micro-seismic anomalies).

3. Equipment Checklist (pack weight ceiling: 14 kg)  
————————————————————————————————  

3.1. Clothing  
• Amphibious split-toe boots, Kevlar lacing, basalt-rated tread.  
• Quick-dry softshell with hydrophobic nanofleece lining; hooded, because drizzle is constant but rarely torrential.  
• Ultralight knee protection—basalt fins blade upwards without warning.  
• Low-rustle fabrics only (maximum 28 dB at walking speed—check manufacturer spec).  
• Optional: electrochromic poncho set to bruise-purple; it’s polite conspicuity on the mirror pools, and algae herders can spot you in case of a stumble.  

3.2. Tools  
• 30 m micro-filament emergency leash. Clip it to the graphene causeways; if the bridge retracts you’ll feel tension before you drop.  
• Induction hand-torch, ≤30 lux. Use short pulse mode; continuous beam annoys the algal collectors above and may shut down the segment you’re standing under.  
• Diamond-dust repair pen for patching foam terraces (gift-level etiquette; residents appreciate travelers who mend what they scuff).  
• Portable vapor-desal kit. All surface water is fresh, but nickel-heavy; run it through the kit.  
• Two meters of rootable code (seed-tape) containing something useful—bug-fixes for open hardware, fungus monitoring patches, poetry in executable form. Technology is a crop; offering new traits is the local equivalent of currency.  

3.3. Med / Survival  
• Broad-spectrum antifungal cream (Tinaxon B); apply nightly to feet and inside elbow flexures.  
• Liquid skin for basalt cuts.  
• Beta-blocker micro-tabs: unexpected infrasound from tectonic booms provokes adrenaline surges in many newcomers.  
• Personal locator: set to whisper-band (see 1.1). GPS analogs work poorly under the algae sheets.

4. Navigation & Infrastructure Etiquette  
————————————————————————————————  

4.1. Graphene Ribbons  
• Step lightly; the bridge senses load. Walking single-file is correct even if the span appears a meter wide—it narrows invisibly behind you, recycling carbon.  
• If you must stop, plant both feet parallel and wait three seconds. The span will thicken a perch pad under your weight. Sudden side-shifts can shear the ribbon.  
• Never run. Locals say noise binds consequence, but speed invites algorithmic pruning.  

4.2. Towers  
• Announce by low-band ping (−60 dB). The tower AI will sculpt a ramp.  
• Inside, glow patterns are data. Do not touch unless you can read the code; brushing a panel can reroute power pumps kilometers away.  
• Roof cisterns are community water. Filter before drinking; add 4 ml concentrate nutrients per liter to replace trace minerals that filtration removes.  

4.3. The Open Delta  
• Flats of glassy slag act like mirrors; polarization can hide pool edges. Shuffle-step and watch for star-necklace axolotls—they congregate where footing fails.  
• Afternoon steam columns mark active lava tongues. Keep 2 km distance; convection vortices can yank a causeway filament apart.

5. Social & Cultural Notes  
————————————————————————————————  

5.1. Speech  
• Volume: library whisper. Locals raise a palm, fingers splayed, if you drift louder than acceptable.  
• Gestures: “Invitation” is palm-up with fingers describing a slow spiral; “Decline” is wrist turning leaf-side down. Learn these two and you’ll avoid 80 % of social friction.  

5.2. Economy  
• Hard currency meaningless. Trade utility code, rare earth chelates, or “story objects” (tactile art, kinetic toys). Items should be quiet, dim, and either biodegradable or readily dissembled by tower recyclers.  

5.3. Gifts & Offenses  
Appropriate gifts: pruning a feral cable vine, reciting a new algorithm in exchange for a meal, mending someone’s walkway anchor.  
Major offenses: loud music, floodlights, hoarding carbon foam (it’s considered communal soil), leaving dead tech littering basalt.  

6. Hazards Recap (Mnemonic: D.E.L.T.A.)  
D – Damp cuts: Basalt shards + humidity = infected wounds. Clean immediately.  
E – Eroding bridges: Watch your leash line for slack.  
L – Light deprivation: Use dawn visor to stave off circadian creep.  
T – Tectonic booms: If the ground hums, squat low; bridge pylons can resonate and toss you.  
A – Algal overcast: Sudden density shifts can drop surface PAR to near zero—carry pulse lamp to find the next tower.

7. Suggested 5-Day Starter Itinerary  
————————————————————————————————  

Day 1: Descend from Tandem-17, acclimate in the Welcoming Groves. Help a vine-wrangler stitch a basalt fissure; trade code.  
Day 2: Dawn walk west to Tower Helio-Laminar; observe pipes that pump drizzle upward, volunteer for a condenser audit.  
Day 3: Steamwatch from Ridge 46B; maintain two-kilometer ring. Record atmospheric lensing patterns (in demand).  
Day 4: Night cross-delta trek under violet circuitry; with permission, ride messenger drone for 400 m—closest Veilspan has to public transit.  
Day 5: Participate in Quiet Festival: simultaneous city-wide 20-minute vow of silence while algae nets realign. Offer a story object, take home a carved foam tile that gently blinks your respiration pattern in violet.

8. Exit Protocol  
————————————————————————————————  

Notify the nearest tower six hours prior. It will print a graphene kite to winch you back to Tandem-17. Before lift-off, scrub boots with nickel chelator paste and return borrowed carbon tooling to the nearest communal rack. Say goodbye in sign: thumb to sternum, open palm rising—“I remove my noise from your air.”  

Pack out every atom you brought in that cannot be metabolized by vines, moss, or foam. Veilspan is young, and everything you leave behind shapes the adult it will grow into. Travel wisely.
