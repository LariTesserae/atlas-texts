Field Manual – Visit 1978-Sgx “The Echo Basin” (informal nick-name among multiversal roamers)  
Classification: Class-Fragile Biome / Pocket World  
Maximum recommended stay: 36 local hours

--------------------------------------------------
1. Environment at a Glance
• Scale: 60 m diameter, 30 m vaulted height. Everything you do affects a closed loop.  
• Illumination: Unshadowed white-gold, ≈100 000 lux (mid-Terrestrial desert noon is 1000 lux).  
• Temperature: 35–38 °C, humidity < 2 %.  
• Atmosphere: Breathable O₂/N₂ with persistent, copper-salt dust load (PM₂.₅ > 400 µg m⁻³).  
• Acoustics: Continuous 110–130 dB broad-spectrum roar, modulated peaks to 140 dB.  
• Ground: Brittle silica plates, razor edges, 2–6 cm voids underneath, prone to tilt.

--------------------------------------------------
2. Personal Kit (mandatory)

Eye / Skin  
• Shade visor or welding-grade goggles (DIN 11 minimum). Standard sunglasses will not stop retinal bleaching after 10 min.  
• Full-cover UV-reflective hooded suit (Tyvek-style is fine; white preferred so you are visible against the copper-green canopy). Exposed skin sunburns in under an hour and will pick up blue-green staining from airborne salts.

Respiration  
• P100/FFP3 respirator with copper/alkali sorbent pre-filter. Replace every 8 h; salty dust cakes fast.  
• Bring 4 L water per day; drink covertly (see Culture). Electrolyte composition should NOT contain copper—already abundant here.

Hearing & Communication  
• Dual-mode ear protection:  
  – 25 dB passive reduction plugs with central 2 mm vent (locals fear perfect silence; a total block is rude and disorienting).  
  – Bone-conduction comms or throat mic tied to a tight-beam radio/laser link for your own team.  
• Fluency in basic Basin Sign (appendix A) or an optical translator glove. Voice is impossible.

Mobility & Safety  
• Hardened hiking boots with 4 mm rock guard rand; kevlar gaiters deflect falling shards.  
• Lightweight arborist harness + 15 m micro-static line. Many pathways are aerial footbridges.  
• Cut-resistant gloves. Calcite tubes shear like glass.  
• Anti-static over-stripe (carbon fibre filament) to bleed charge picked up from dust fall.

Nutrition  
• Pack-in food. Local banner tissue is 40 % metallic salts—nephrotoxic to humans.  
• Zero open flame stoves (Rule 3). Use chem-warmers or cold-soak rations.

--------------------------------------------------
3. Arrival Protocol

1. Do not gate or blink inside 1 m of wall or canopy—micro-impacts have pre-stressed the salt-glass; trans-dimensional shock may fracture it.  
2. Touch down on the pale central plates, not in cultivated “root dark” zones.  
3. Wait, motionless, one full harmonic cycle (~9 s) so the nearest citizens notice you and sign acceptance. Moving immediately is read as predator behavior.

--------------------------------------------------
4. Cultural Etiquette

Silence Taboo: They equate mute soundscapes with death and ground-quakes. Keep at least a faint hiss or hum audible to bystanders (tap a metal cylinder, let air bleed from a valve, etc.). Your vented earplugs satisfy them.

Water Myth: Producing visible liquid startles everyone and may invite confiscation “for the ancestors.” Use opaque hydration bladders; drink inside a dwelling.

Fire Prohibition: Any sparked flame is sacrilege and rapidly melts banner filaments. Even a cigarette can collapse a footbridge. Carry only electrical or chemical heat sources.

Replant Duty: Visitors who snap a banner strand—accidentally or not—must insert two cuttings into a fissure. Keep a pair of 20 cm pre-cut calcite splints in your pack to settle any dispute swiftly.

Name Shards: Citizens will offer you a lenticular chip of ceiling crystal on first meeting. Accept with both hands, touch it to your forehead, return it. Refusing marks you “already dead.”

Conversation Windows: Social trade occurs during “cool verge,” 4–6 h after local noon when convection slows and sound drops to ~100 dB. Plan negotiations then.

Gifts & Trade:  
Allowed – optical polish cloths, sine-wound guitar strings (excellent cutting wire substitute), simple lenses, any stories carried in mem-stone or knotted cord.  
Dangerous – bulk metals (destabilize economy), magnets (scramble speaking tubes), or hydroponic seeds (seen as false children).

--------------------------------------------------
5. Hazards & First Aid

Glass Plate Shear: Treat cuts immediately—copper salts delay clotting. Irrigate with sealed sterile water, seal with hydrocolloid patch, then add external saline-free dressing.

Thermal Vertigo: Updrafts can hit 4 m s⁻¹. When crossing high bridges, clip your harness every 3 m.

Electrical Flash-Arc: Overhead discharges stay aloft, but metal pack frames can develop >1 kV static. Keep frames non-conductive or grounded with the carbon filament stripe.

Psychological Noise Fatigue: After 6–8 h the constant roar induces anger or catatonia. Rotate quiet breaks: retreat into a hive-dwelling (70–80 dB). Meditative low-frequency hum through your throat re-anchors proprioception (locals do this unconsciously).

--------------------------------------------------
6. Scientific & Ethical Notes

Biome Dependency Loop: The ion dust → calcite tubes → banner rasp → acoustic lift → ceiling discharge closes in < 20 min. Removing more than 3 kg of material or obstructing a vent shifts the loop enough to desiccate seedlings. Remain “mass-neutral”: what you bring in, you take out.

Photography: Great for spectrographic studies, but strobe lights mimic lightning and panic skimmers. Disable flash.

Sampling: Non-destructive only (e.g., tape-lift dust, micro-drill 1 mm core from fallen shards). Never core living stalks.

--------------------------------------------------
7. Exit Checklist

1. Visual sweep—no dropped batteries or plastic.  
2. Wipe gear with alcohol cloths so foreign organics do not seed the biome.  
3. De-stat yourself by touching an exposed copper vein on a felled banner.  
4. Announce departure in Basin Sign (“RETURN / AGAIN / DAY 8?” is polite optimism).  
5. Gate out from the same inert plate you arrived on.

--------------------------------------------------
8. Recommended Itinerary (36 h)

Hour 0–1  Acclimatize under root shade, basic sign greetings.  
Hour 1–6  Guided canopy walk; collect sonic and photometric data.  
Hour 6–10 Rest inside hive; share knot-stories; barter for banner fibre art.  
Hour 10–16 Observe “cool verge” marketplace; trade and interview elders.  
Hour 16–22 Ascend to upper bridges; watch lightning sheet across ceiling (safe perch: Beacon Ledge, SW quadrant).  
Hour 22–28 Nap / personal research in low-noise dwelling.  
Hour 28–34 Document dawn-height dust fall; sample skimmer swarm with electrostatic net.  
Hour 34–36 Debrief, pay replant tithe, exfiltrate before second full convection cycle peaks.

--------------------------------------------------
9. Final Advisory

The Echo Basin is less a destination than an interlocked, ringing physics experiment that happens to sustain a civilization the width of a football field. Your mere body mass is a perturbation. Travel lightly, leave whispers, not echoes.
