Field Guide & Pre-deployment Checklist  
Location designation: The Diluted Ocean / Basalt Production Plateau (catalogue code Φ-58K “Hollow-Sea Foundry”)

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Threat Assessment (top line)

Immediate lethality: moderate.  No crushing pressure, no toxins, no aggressive intelligence; nevertheless the environment is thermally hostile, intermittently airless, acoustically violent and patrolled by macro-predators as large as commuter trains.  
Long-term risk: high.  Damage from vacuum exposure, bone/labyrinth resonance, heat loss and psychological drift (“plateau fugue”) accumulates unnoticed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Atmosphere, Hydro-sphere & Gravity

• Medium: water at 0 – 4 °C whose bulk density is only 15–25 % of Standard Terran seawater because of kilometre-scale vacuum vacuoles.  
• Pressure: oscillates between 0 bar (pure vacuole) and 0.25 bar (water strata). A human lung will rupture in < 1 s if a vacuole opens across unprotected airways.  
• Oxygen: negligible.  Combustion is practically impossible, corrosion likewise.  
• Gravity: 0.42 g.  Enough to know which way is “down”, weak enough that swimming is slow and falls are harmless but disorienting.  
• Temperature gradient: ±1 °C across hundreds of metres; risk of hypothermia, no thermal up-currents to warn you.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Life-forms & Behavioural Rules

Micro-engines (“krill”): not dangerous; abrasive if inhaled or filtered into suit seams.  
Grazers: manta/medusa mats 5–20 m across.  Non-aggressive but blind; collision danger inside vacuoles.  
Long hunters (“cables”): apex predators.  Trigger radius c. 40 m, attack velocity 60 m s-¹ inside vacuole, 10 m s-¹ in water.  They sense pressure gradients, not light.  Metallic armour is ignored; acoustic profile is what matters.  
Machine complex: autonomous, indifferent, massive.  Compressor intakes produce suction up to 3 kN; outlet vents exhaust 400 °C slugs of hyper-compressed water every 10 s.  Maintenance drones will treat you as a movable obstruction; their manipulator torque can break ribs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Personal Equipment

Mandatory
1. Biphase Excursion Suit (BES)  
   • Outer shell rated to hard vacuum.  
   • Integrated variable-buoyancy bladders; you will alternate between swimming and free-floating.  
   • Active thermal layer (120 W minimum).  
   • Helmet valves lock automatically < 0.1 bar.  

2. Bone-conduction Audio Canceller  
   • Over-ear muffs are useless in this water.  Wear coil-band cancellers around skull base; they sample the plateau’s 0.9 Hz fundamental and generate counter-phase.  Without them most visitors vomit or black out within an hour.

3. Mobility  
   • Low-g flipper boots for water strata.  
   • Micro-thruster wrists (cold-gas) for manoeuvring inside vacuoles.  
   • Pitons & 20 m monofilament line to anchor on bare basalt during compressor surges.

4. Lights  
   • Hands-free LED stack (6000 K).  Sodium arcs fail unexpectedly when a moon-sized vacuole crosses a cable; darkness lasts 30–120 s.

5. Nav/Telemetry  
   • Triaxial sonar pinger—only functions in water pockets; supplement with optical range-finder in vacuum.  
   • DO NOT rely on magnetic compasses; the plateau is shot through with current loops from the machines.

6. First Aid  
   • Vacuum-rated dart injectors loaded with heat-shock proteins and vasoconstrictors (for hunter bite or sudden decompression).  
   • Reversible splints tuned for 0.4 g.

Recommended
• Grazer mimic panel: a metre-square sheet of translucent film that pumps out low-grade ionic turbulence; hunters perceive you as part of a passing grazer and ignore you 80 % of the time.  
• Sample pods pre-sterilised in argon if research is your goal.  ANY oxygenated object you bring will instantly pit micro-engines; ethical breaches carry inter-dimensional sanctions.  
• Personal chronometer with haptic alarms; plateau monotony distorts subjective time.

Forbidden
• Open flame or oxidisers (violate the “no corrosion” equilibrium).  
• Wideband sonar pulses above 140 dB—will enrage hunters and destabilise compressor resonance.  
• Electromagnetic weapons; arc flashes overload the lamp grid.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Movement Protocol

A. Descent  
   • Insert at 500 m above plateau via drop-capsule; retro-jets shut off 50 m out to avoid pressure shockwaves.  
   • Verify surrounding medium before exiting: if the capsule sits in a vacuole, flood entry chamber with 0.25 bar nitrogen mix first.  

B. Transit  
   • Adopt zig-zag rhythm: 200 m water, 100 m vacuole, staying within 30 m of solid basalt where possible.  
   • Pause every 8–9 s to let next compressor pulse pass; water slugs ejected from vents can push you 40 m off course.  
   • When darkness falls, anchor and wait.  Do not swim blind; vacuum pockets have knife-edged boundaries that can slice suit fabric.

C. Predator Evasion  
   • In water: go still, cut thrusters; hunters hunt motion.  
   • In vacuole: fire two quick puffs from wrist thruster opposite your intended line; the differential pressure confuses their strike algorithm.  
   • If capture is imminent: present mimic panel, vent 5 L suit ballast; the sudden mass-shift usually makes them abort.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
6. Staying Sane

• Soundscape journal: record one compressor cycle and play it back at 60 dB through helmet speakers to restore a normal sense of tempo.  
• Task discipline: schedule micro-objectives every 15 min.  The plateau encourages catatonia—visitors have stood motionless for 18 h before hypothermia woke them.  
• Visual landmarks: mark basalt corners with UV chalk; everything here stays unnervingly pristine, so a scuff of colour helps you believe time is passing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
7. Interaction With Machinery

Treat the complex as a living entity: it exhibits repair reflexes.  
• Approach intakes only during vacuum phase; suction peaks 0.7 s before slug formation.  
• If a maintenance drone vector-locks on you, freeze and broadcast service code “K-144 SAFEWALK”.  The drone will path around you.  Do NOT touch it.  
• Sampling from conveyor rails is tolerated; interfering with chevron-marked housings triggers acoustic alarms that will bring hunters (unclear why, but correlation is > 90 %).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
8. Emergency Extraction

• Primary: inflate 3 m helium balloon; in 0.42 g it will accelerate you upward at 0.8 m s-² until your beacon reaches pickup altitude (5 km).  
• Secondary: shelter inside an intake caisson—inner service corridors are atmosphere-maintained at 0.3 bar and 5 °C.  Signal home universe; wait.  The machines ignore quiet stowaways for at least 72 h.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
9. Departure & Decontamination

On leaving the water you will carry micro-engine spores.  Before you cycle through the portal:  
1. Expose suit to 0 °C vacuum for 20 min while pulsing 150 nm UV—shuts down ion grinders.  
2. Vent ballast water back into local sea; NEVER transport it.  
3. Confine all biological samples inside double vacuum jars with 0.01 ppm O₂; mark with red “mechanochemical” hazard tape.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
10. Souvenirs & Ethics

What you may remove: broken obsidian shards, inactive micro-engine shells, audio recordings, nonfunctional drone hull fragments ≤ 5 kg.  
What you must not: live fauna, compressor core fragments, sodium arc crystals (they are hive-minded photonic organs), or any object bearing black chevrons.  Violations attract intervention by the Inter-Continuum Preservation Accord; fines are levied in years of subjective memory.
