Field Guide: Preparing to Enter the Salt-Feldspar Pocket-World  

1. Understand the Three Principal Hazards  
a. Sensory Overload  
   • Light: a continuous, shadow-less glare comparable to bright overcast snowfields.  
   • Sound: a 110- to 120-dB standing chord (roughly a jet engine inside a hangar).  
b. Desiccation & Alkali Inhalation  
   • Air holds almost zero moisture and is laced with fine, alkaline grit.  
c. Abrasion & Spatial Congestion  
   • No flat floor; every step is on, over, or between brickwork. All surfaces polish whatever touches them.

2. Pre-Arrival Conditioning  
• Hearing: one week of daily white-noise exposure at 80–85 dB to build tolerance.  
• Circadian: practice sleeping in continuous light; learn “mask-nap” routines.  
• Communication: agree on a hand-signal or touch code with companions. Spoken words will be useless.

3. Personal Protective Kit (minimum)  
• Double-layer hearing protection: deep-seal earplugs (−33 dB) under industrial earmuff defenders (−27 dB).  
• Full-face respirator with P100/ABEK cartridges—alkali dust will cake an ordinary paper mask in minutes. Bring spare sets sealed in mylar.  
• Neutral-tint glacier goggles with side shields or a clear faceshield if you need eye contact for signing.  
• Hydration pack inside the suit (2 L for each four-hour stint) with inline alkaline filter; add oral rehydration salts.  
• Clothing: rip-stop, moisture-wicking base layer under a smooth-surfaced soft shell. Avoid velcro, knits, or loose straps that catch on brick corners.  
• Tactile gloves (goatskin or smooth synthetic) and kneepads—expect to crawl or brace often.  
• Boots: flat rubber soles with siping for grip; overshoes of silicone or Tyvek if you intend to leave no color streaks.  
• Navigation kit: high-lumen directional headlamp (for shadowing tiny surface details), laser rangefinder, and a backpack-mounted 3-D lidar scanner or SLAM camera. Physical markers are discouraged; they abrade into dust.  
• Time & bio-metrics: a vibration-alarm chronometer and a fingertip pulse-ox/hydration monitor to warn of early dehydration.

4. Environmental Etiquette  
• Treat every brick as structural. Do not pry, stack, chisel, or sit with full weight on an edge. Use broad foot placement.  
• Never harvest lichen. Removing even a thumbnail patch sets back a century of growth.  
• Any accidental spill (blood, food, metal shavings) will stain; carry absorbent wipes and an alcohol sprayer to erase your trace.  
• Keep kit color-neutral (white, stone, grey). Bright plastics are an eyesore in a place that resonates like a single sculpture.

5. Movement Strategy  
• Enter in single file; maintain line-of-sight through hand lamps or helmet strobes blink-coded to your ID.  
• Advance by “islands”: identify the next stable slab for both feet before leaving the previous one—no blind steps.  
• Pause every fifteen minutes for a two-signal check:  
  – Thumb-to-thumb press (physical contact) confirms partner presence.  
  – Finger tap on respirator exhaust confirms filter isn’t clogging.

6. Dwell-Time Limits  
• 4 hours continuous is the recommended ceiling. Past that, dehydration and auditory fatigue spike sharply.  
• Mandatory 12-hour decompression in a dark, silent, humid rest capsule afterward to reset senses and mucosa.

7. Emergency Protocols  
• Respirator breach: cover mouth with forearm, exhale hard, retreat the way you came; brick labyrinth rarely allows a faster perimeter route.  
• Lost teammate: switch all lamps to strobe, stand still, feel the bricks—vibration from footsteps carries better through salt than through air.  
• Psychological distress (“roar panic”): apply over-the-ear ANC headset preloaded with your own low-volume heartbeat track. If no improvement in five minutes, evacuate.

8. Scientific / Cultural Sampling Rules  
• Only non-invasive scans, spectral readings, or micro-vacuum dust lifts (≤ 1 µg) are acceptable.  
• You may carry out the dust filters from your respirator; they are fair game for lab analysis and do not subtract from the chamber.

9. Portal Logistics  
• If arriving by multiversal gate, fix the return anchor outside the pocket-world. The salt walls’ subtle vibration can drift anchor coordinates by millimeters per hour—enough to mis-align a re-entry portal after a long stay.  
• Mark the gate site on your internal map; do not rely on leaving physical beacons.

10. Psychological Framing  
Remember this place is “finished.” Enter as you would a completed poem: to read, not to edit. Curiosity is welcomed, but intervention is vandalism. Carry that mindset and even the noise will begin to sound like reverence instead of threat.

If you equip, plan, and behave accordingly, the salt-feldspar chamber will offer one of the multiverse’s most alien yet serenely precise experiences: standing inside an eternal chord of wind, light, and mineral, untouched by either growth or decay.
