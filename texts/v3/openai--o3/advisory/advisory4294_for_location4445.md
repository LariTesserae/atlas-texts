Traveler’s Field Briefing  
Destination: Carrow Mire Basin (low-lying brine world; steam–electrodynamic era)

Read this entire sheet before crossing. More than half the dangers here are slow, silent, and chemical. Preparation is survival.

----------------------------------------------------
1. Climate and Physiology
----------------------------------------------------
• Temperature: 34–48 °C year-round, humidity 90 %+. Sweat will not evaporate; heatstroke is the default state.  
• Air: Heavily water-laden, laced with iron and trace sulphur. Normally breathable, but twice a year sulphur fronts roll in (see §10).  
• Light: perpetual copper dusk; circadian drift is common. Bring a timepiece with a bright dial.

Body Prep
1. Two weeks of heat-acclimation training before departure.  
2. Salt-balanced rehydration salts (the brine you’ll inhale makes you lose sodium unevenly).  
3. Vaccinations/boosters: tetanus, broad-spectrum halophile cocktail, and fungal suite. Cuts fester fast in this place.

----------------------------------------------------
2. Personal Clothing & Gear
----------------------------------------------------
Wear layers that shed heat yet seal against salt.
• Base: silk or synthetic wicking long-sleeve to stop brine rash.  
• Mid: loose, light-coloured coverall of e-PTFE or waxed linen; must tolerate 50 °C wet heat.   
• Outer (carry, don’t always wear): ultralight aluminised rain-cape. Rain arrives near-boiling; the cape prevents scalds.  
• Feet: thigh-high vulcanised boots with chemical gaskets at the knees. Clay plates can slice cheap rubber; choose 4 mm soles.  
• Hands: nitrile gauntlets under leather gloves—change the nitrile daily.  
• Head: wide, rigid brim coated in reflective chrome paint; underside matte black to cut glare.

Critical kit
• Personal condenser hood (micro-fin heat exchanger you wear like a collar). Distills ~600 ml drinkable water / day—this is your money.  
• Four 1 L vacuum flasks of sterile water for landing fees and emergencies (sealed; do NOT open in public).  
• Folded carbon-fibre walking staves: test ground ahead of every step—clay plates may hide silt pits.  
• Low-noise, grease-packed gearing multi-tool. Oil-free mechanisms squeal and attract predators.  
• Heat-shielded dry bag for electronics; pack desiccant bricks inside.  
• Paper notebook and charcoal stylus (ink runs here).  
• Compact respirator with replaceable acid-gas cartridges (for sulphur events and furnace districts).  
• Broad-spectrum topical antiseptic; hydrogel dressings.  
• Corrosion-proof power bank and dim head-lamp (amber LED—acts as insect deterrent).  
Leave behind: firearms (black-powder fouls instantly; cartridge casings corrode), drones (humidity grounds them), untreated wood (rots overnight).

----------------------------------------------------
3. Electronics & Power
----------------------------------------------------
Standard lithium cells last, but charging is tricky—solar is useless under copper clouds. Bring a hand-crank alternator or pay a dynamosmith to tap a flywheel. Wrap every contact in silicone grease nightly; salt fog eats copper in hours.

----------------------------------------------------
4. Money & Trade
----------------------------------------------------
Condensation credits: thin glass ampoules of distilled water sealed in color-coded wax. The wax is proof of authenticity. Expect to hand over two ampoules per night of lodging.

Premium trade goods
• Desiccants, hydrophobic lubricants, antibiotics, optical-grade glass, fine watch springs (combustion engines are unreliable; precise clockwork is priceless).  
Avoid trading in wood, fabrics, or seeds—locals see them as curiosities that turn to soup.

----------------------------------------------------
5. Getting In
----------------------------------------------------
Surface in daylight so you can see the levee tops. Touch down on exposed basalt only; everywhere else is knee-deep soup. Custom dictates that new arrivals present a “quiet gift”: turn off whatever engine delivered you, wait one full minute in silence, then hail the nearest platform by handbell. This reassures inhabitants you understand Rule 3 (noise invites predators).

----------------------------------------------------
6. Moving Around
----------------------------------------------------
• Stay on the iron viaducts or obvious basalt; mud flats can liquefy without warning.  
• Step softly—metal decking carries sound for kilometres.  
• Shift whistles mark safe movement windows; locals call it “walking on the beat.” Outside those windows things with too many legs forage across the walkways.

----------------------------------------------------
7. Food & Drink
----------------------------------------------------
You’ll live on imported grain bricks stewed in clicker broth. Perfectly safe if boiled ten minutes; otherwise haemolytic toxins concentrate.  
Condensed water is for drinking only—washing with it is conspicuous consumption. Bathe with brine and a scrubbing stone, then rinse sparingly with 50 ml of fresh to strip salt from your pores.

----------------------------------------------------
8. Hazards
----------------------------------------------------
Heat: Core body temp can spike while you sleep. Nap instead of full nights; set an alarm every 90 min for hydration.  
Hot Rain: Sounds like frying bacon as it approaches. Cape up, find shelter under metal awnings.  
Clay Shear: Edges of dried plates are sharp; they slice boot seams, then skin.  
Biogas Pockets: Disturb black silt and you release hydrogen sulphide & methane. Don’t light open flames.  
Clicker Carpets: Harmless until crushed—then they emit alarm clicks that draw skimsails. If the walkway ripples, freeze.  
Droneline Nets: Nearly invisible threads at face height; their adhesive dissolves slowly in brine, so duck under any span between poles.  
Corrosion: Anything ferrous sprouts orange fluff called “blood-rust” in 24 h. Wipe gear with vinegar, then oil, every evening.

----------------------------------------------------
9. Etiquette & Law
----------------------------------------------------
• Water wastage is a civil crime. A splash on the ground will earn a fine or a punch.  
• Speak at conversational murmur; shouting is for alarms only.  
• Lend a hand when a communal pump jams—help is assumed currency.  
• Ownership = maintenance: if you lean on a railing, you are expected to check its rivets while you’re there.  
• Shift sirens (two rising notes) mean furnaces venting—clear the vapour lanes immediately.  
• Steam Guild adjudicates disputes. Bribes are accepted in condenser parts, never coins.

----------------------------------------------------
10. Rare but Serious Events
----------------------------------------------------
Sulphur Fronts (bi-annual). Sky turns bruised purple, metallic taste in mouth. Protocol:  
1. Mask on.  
2. Get indoors—brick boiler houses are best.  
3. Seal louvers with oil-cloth provided at every doorway.  
4. Remain until drums signal all-clear (~6 h).
Post-front, skimsails swarm. Stay off open water 24 h; they’ll ram small boats.

Levee Breach (1–2 % annual). Siren: descending major third, repeated. High tide pushes walls. Follow glow-sticks to nearest vertical ladder; climb to upper catwalks. Do not join bucket chains unless drafted—untrained labour gets people killed.

----------------------------------------------------
11. Sleeping Arrangements
----------------------------------------------------
Ask for a “dry bunk” above boiler level. Steam keeps parasites down and provides white noise that masks your breathing from nocturnal feeders. Hang every garment on iron hooks; anything left on the floor will be crusted with salt pellets by morning.

----------------------------------------------------
12. Departure Decontamination
----------------------------------------------------
Customs will not let off-worlders track endemic microbes home. Expect:
1. 60 °C steam scrub (10 min).  
2. Iodine mist rinse.  
3. UV pass-through.  
Electronics are bag-checked; wipe drives if you can’t risk interrogation—industrial espionage is a hanging offence.

If you can meet all of the above, Carrow Mire will treat you not as prey or pollutant but as a fellow organism in its hot, humming system. Fail, and the basin’s chemistry will quietly rewrite you into silt.

Step lightly, breathe shallow, and mind the hiss of the water you carry—it is the loudest thing about you.
