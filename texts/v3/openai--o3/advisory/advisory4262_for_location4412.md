Field Brief 77-H: “The Ember-Hollow Salt Basin”  
Audience: Category-B (ordinary but reasonably fit, non-augmented) multiverse travelers  
Revision: 3.2, cleared for public circulation  

——————————————  
1. Snapshot risk profile  
——————————————  
Thermal stress: Moderate (49–63 °C radiant swings during wind tides)  
Atmospheric irritants: Severe (Cl₂, Br₂, SO₂ traces)  
Pressure: Near-terran; breathable only for about 40 s before mucous membranes desiccate.  
Structural danger: Low—floor supports human weight, occasional saltfall from roof fissures.  
Biohazards: Negligible (no fauna, no microbes using H₂O).  
Cultural / legal: Uninhabited; last sapient presence estimated ≥400 local years.  
Prime directive flag: HIGH. Importing water or organic carbon can irreversibly disrupt biome.  

——————————————  
2. Personal equipment checklist  
——————————————  
1. Shell-Suit Mk S (or equivalent)  
   • Fully sealed, self-contained breathing loop rated for acidic, halogen-rich atmospheres.  
   • External surfaces anodized or ceramic-coated; chlorine pits most metals in minutes.  
   • Passive radiator fins—active refrigeration unnecessary if visit <6 h.  
2. Hydration & nutrition modules stay internal. Never open suit for drinking; liquid water flash-boils at ambient.  
3. Eye protection: gold-sputtered or amorphous-carbon visor. Red ember-light is dim but Br₂ absorbs blue light, so vision skews; visor stabilizes color balance.  
4. Audio: dual-channel bone-conduction receivers. Airborne sound is deafening yet speech is quickly lost; conductive plates pressed to boot-soles allow floor acoustics pick-up (“ground-talk”).  
5. Locomotion aids: micro-crampon sandals snap over boots—the salt crust becomes glass-slick when wind tide polishes it.  
6. Tool pouch (all water-less materials)  
   • Ceramic blade  
   • Graphite stylus & slate; inks hydrate—do not bring.  
   • Roll of flare-tree fiber rope (buy from portal outfitter; native-grown, zero contamination).  
7. Beacon/recall device in hermetic housing; radio is fine—EM propagation is normal.  

Forbidden items  
• Any liquid water, alcohols, or aqueous gels (lotions, pharmaceuticals).  
• Conventional batteries that vent H₂O or H-bearing solvents on rupture—carry solid-state or molten-salt cells.  
• Open-flame lighters; combustion stalls here and you’ll corrode the igniter.  

——————————————  
3. Approach & ingress  
——————————————  
Preferred insertion: rappel through primary ceiling fissure “Mouth of Coals” (coordinates 12.7° E, 43.9° N local grid). Fissure width ≈4 m, convective updraft peaking every 11 min; time descent just after an up-pulse when vertical winds lapse.  

Alternate: hike from perimeter cave-system on the northwest rim; adds 3 km across brittle vitrified ridges—beautiful but ankle-risky.  

——————————————  
4. Orientation once inside  
——————————————  
Lighting: perpetual red twilight. Bring low-watt UV-A lamp only if specimen work is planned; visible-white torches distractively glitter off glass pillows and can trigger frond-orientation reflex, altering the understory for decades.  

Navigation beacons: place floor-stakes, not wall markers—vitrified glass flows a millimeter per year and will swallow carvings.  

Sound etiquette: The stone flute-spindle in the central plaza remains the basin’s “clock.” Convection gusts every 2 h 47 m produce a 6-note chord. Use these chimings to keep temporal bearings; electronic watches fog under heat cycling.  

——————————————  
5. Environmental etiquette & impact minimization  
——————————————  
• Zero-water rule: Even condensate on your cooling plates will crash the local chemistry; conduct a post-exit pat-down for leaks.  
• Do not harvest flare-tree gas for souvenirs—the capillary network collapses when flow is interrupted, killing square meters of lichen colony.  
• Walk between, never across, pollen-sparkle carpets; foot pressure can prematurely nucleate crystallization and divert new sprouts.  
• Leave saltfall where it lies; it forms armor over older layers, a key part of basin erosion homeostasis.  

——————————————  
6. Sensory & psychological preparation  
——————————————  
Vision: expect a world rendered in black-red sepia. Depth judgment is tricky because shadows are dull, reflections are bright, and air shimmers. Practice moving in low-contrast arenas before mission day.  

Hearing: continuous 70–85 dB roar. Earplugs won’t help; vibration enters through bones. Learn to treat sound as weather—constant yet harmless. Meditation tracks with brown noise can acclimatize you.  

Smell/Taste: invisible halogens sting tongue and nostrils; the rebreather filters most, but trace sweetness persists—perfectly normal.  

Time dilation sensation: Because “night” never falls, fatigue cues fail. Schedule rest stops by watch or by every third flute-spindle song.

7. Suggested 3-hour itinerary (first-timers)  
——————————————  
0:00–0:20  Descent & baseline vitals check near rim.  
0:20–0:40  Traverse salt flats toward city; pause to press boot-intercoms to crust—experience ground-borne acoustics.  
0:40–1:30  City perimeter loop. Survey halite architecture; photograph frond-thatch roofing (IR & visible). Avoid touching obsidian spindle.  
1:30–2:00  Wind-tide window—take shelter behind inner wall, observe salt-snow fall. Good moment for multispectral panoramas.  
2:00–2:30  Collect permitted samples: up to 50 g loose salt crystals or fallen copper frond ash. Bag in gas-permeable, water-free envelopes.  
2:30–3:00  Return to fissure and ascend before following up-draft; ascending in headwind is exhausting.

8. Emergency protocols  
——————————————  
Suit breach (halogen ingress):  
1. Exhale sharply; close throat.  
2. Deploy patch kit—self-fusing silicone (contains no water).  
3. Evac within 15 min; prolonged exposure causes pulmonary edema even without water.  

Thermal faint / heatstroke:  
• Lie flat on salt crust—phonon conduction will wick heat faster than air convection.  
• Signal partner via ground tap: five rapid heel-strikes.  

Navigation loss:  
• Track toward deepest flute-music; the plaza is basin center. From there, any radial takes you to rim in ≤180 p.  

Suit fire unlikely, but lithium-packs can outgas; toss pack onto salt, where it smothers itself in chlorine-rich layer.  

——————————————  
9. Post-visit decontamination  
——————————————  
Exterior rinse in anhydrous perfluorocarbon bath; halides embrittle gaskets after 8 h. Test all seals with ultraviolet tracer gas before next itinerary. Dispose of captured bromine filters per vector-hazmat code 6-Y.  

Photographs and data: scrub metadata of exact portal vectors; over-tourism already threatens parallel site HN-303.  

——————————————  
10. Souvenirs & memory aids  
——————————————  
Acceptable: a pebble of vitrified glass; audio recording of the spindle’s chord; high-speed footage of a self-pruning frond igniting mid-air.  
Unacceptable: live spores (customs scan for silica biotics), flare-tree bark cores, any structural halite block.  

——————————————  
11. Final notes from veteran pathfinders  
——————————————  
• Treat the place as a lung you happen to walk inside; time your moves with its breath.  
• When the red glow deepens toward burgundy, a major vent surge is 90 s away—secure footing early.  
• The emptiness will tempt you to shout just to hear echoes. Instead, lay your helmet against the salt and whisper; the basin will answer more eloquently.  

Safe travels, and remember: in the Ember-Hollow, water is legend and you are made of myth. Walk lightly.
