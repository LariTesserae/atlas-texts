Field Guide for the Ordinary-but-Curious Multiverse Traveler
Destination:  The Throat-Workshop, Mid-Shaft Cat-Walk (alt. 2 070 m A.S.F. – “Above Shaft Floor”)

──────────────────────────────────
1. Paperwork & Portals
──────────────────────────────────
• Access writ:  The condenser-forge is still owned—at least on paper—by the Guild of Recursive Manufactories. Secure a five-day visitor’s writ stamped “Observation Only.” Longer stays or hands-on tinkering require a Maintenance Apprenticeship bond co-signed by one of the six resident boilermancers.

• Arrival window:  Portal in during Lull-Phase (minutes 37–44 after a vent-cycle). Any other time, the outgoing steam plume will shear a gate vortex into spaghetti filaments and you with it.

• Plotted coordinates:  SHAFT-XØØ-CATWALK-CENTER. Use a tripod anchor and keep the gate open for ninety seconds in case you look down, panic, and need to backstep.

──────────────────────────────────
2. Conditioning & Medical
──────────────────────────────────
• Altitude tolerance:  Equivalent to 3 200 m on a temperate Earth; spend two nights above 2 500 m beforehand or plan for mild headache and thin-air insomnia.

• Heat acclimation:  Ambient 30 °C feels hotter under constant humidity. A week of daily sauna sessions (20 min, 60 % RH) teaches your sweat glands what’s coming.

• Vertigo training:  The void is real, the cat-walk has gaps, and depth cues are deceitful. Practice “spotting” (dancer’s technique) and one-foot balance on a 15-cm beam until you can hold 45 s without wobble.

• Vaccines / boosters:  Standard tetanus, a fungal broad-spectrum (myco strains in the condenser slime are opportunistic), and a booster against Class-1 self-replicating nanite spores—rare, but the tower was built during the Augmented Artisan Age.

──────────────────────────────────
3. Packing Manifest
──────────────────────────────────
Absolutely bring:
1. Corrosion-resistant travel suit—light, quick-dry, with aramid kneepads. Cotton rots in days.  
2. Harness-belt with dual auto-locking carabiners. Clip to the deck’s safety line whenever you stop moving. Locals call this “shaking hands with the rail.”  
3. Steel-core soles or magnetic heel inserts. The lattice rungs are smooth as soapstone.  
4. Ear armour: industrial-grade active dampers. Peak vent roar reaches 118 dB.  
5. Shatterproof goggles; the drizzle carries metallic grit.  
6. Modular respirator (P95 + steam filter layer) for the three-minute flash plume at each cycle.  
7. 30 m paracord and two prusik loops. Ladders are plentiful but sometimes decide to be “somewhere else.”  
8. Signal cloths (lime, violet, white): semaphore is the communal tongue when turbines drown speech.  
9. A handful of hex-nuts or bottlecaps to barter with the corvids; they adore threaded objects.  
10. Field notebook made of rewritable polymer sheets. Paper pulp de-lignifies overnight in the mist.

Leave home:
• Loose coins, rings, or sentimental jewelry—the alloy may wake up and wander.  
• Wheeled luggage. Nothing rolls true.  
• Umbrellas. Updraft + umbrella = Mary Poppins speed-run to the stratosphere.

──────────────────────────────────
4. First Ten Minutes on the Deck
──────────────────────────────────
Step through, squat, and touch the flooring. Feel the temperature, the vibration rhythm (≈72 bpm, like a big steel heart), and the faint sway. Clip in. Stand. Breathe twice. Only then look outward. Even seasoned void-walkers report “shaft-shock” if they lift their eyes too soon.

Introduce yourself to the nearest gardener—likely Greve or Talla if you arrive on day-shift. Hand over a small tool (anodized Allen keys are popular); they will reciprocate with a vial of potable condensate and point you toward a berth-niche.

──────────────────────────────────
5. Daily Rhythm
──────────────────────────────────
The Throat-Workshop lives by the Eightfold Vent:

00:00  Steam Cyclone (3 min)  
00:04–03:40  Warm Drizzle / Work Period  
03:40–04:00  Lull (silence; shift change; meditation)  
04:00  Repeat  

Shift bells are useless—listen for the sub-bass rumble that precedes each cyclone by thirteen seconds. During the roar, stand under a bulkhead lip or inside a wall recess; unprotected skin can scald.

──────────────────────────────────
6. Staying Upright & Alive
──────────────────────────────────
• Three-point rule: two feet and one hand, or two hands and one foot, in contact with iron at all times. The resident corridor crabs follow the same protocol and have survived centuries.

• Tool tethers: any object longer than your index finger must be leashed to you. Anything you drop feeds the mythic “Floor-Smith” 2 km below; you will never see it again.

• Weather inside: Updrafts gust to 60 km/h. A sudden pressure dive can lift loose jackets like wings—then slam them sideways against the wall. Keep zippers closed.

• Edge hallucinations: You may swear beams curve or steps flex. They probably do. Trust the static safety lines and the seasoned gardeners; they have mapped the cat-walk’s slow dance.

• No open arcs during hush: In the thirty-second absolute silence after a cycle, residents observe a local taboo—no welders, no grinders, no speech. The tower “listens.” Be polite.

──────────────────────────────────
7. Food & Water
──────────────────────────────────
• Condensate fountains dribble potable water once per cycle; filter anyway (0.5 µm + carbon).  
• Edible flora:    – Vine-tips sauté like asparagus.    – Orchid nectar is safe but mildly hallucinogenic; reserve for evenings.  
• Wildlife protein: corridor crabs are colleagues, not cuisine. Swifts and bats are off-limits by guild law. Bring dehydrated meals or trade with boilermancer Fual—she cultivates a sourdough that thrives on lubricant yeast.

──────────────────────────────────
8. Social & Cultural Etiquette
──────────────────────────────────
Boilermancers: monastic machinists, faces streaked with verdigris-flour. Address them by title first (“Boilermancer Rhun”). Offer useful scraps of code, circuitry, or folklore; they hoard trivia like others hoard spare fuses.

Gardeners: equal rank to machinists. They run the semaphore net and know where every vine root ends. Their gossip is the local internet.

Corvids: autonomous aerial postal service. Hold out a shiny small part; if a bird accepts, speak a question aloud. Expect an answer scratched into a copper tag within two cycles. Rudeness toward corvids earns you a blistered harness (they know exactly which strap to saw halfway through).

Crabs: step around them; never over. They dislike shadows falling from above and will clip your bootlaces out of spite.

Visitors’ Gift Cycle: You arrived bearing a gift, you will leave something accidental (a glove, a thought); accept the symmetry.

──────────────────────────────────
9. Things to Do (Beyond Staring into the Abyss)
──────────────────────────────────
• Dawn Luminance Walk: start at Cycle 2; watch the sunlight spear straight down the throat, igniting bronze dust into a false sunrise below your feet.

• Forge-Whispering: accompany a boilermancer inside the wall, palm against warm pipe, and “listen” to metallurgical phase-changes. You may feel grains realign beneath your fingertips.

• Updraft Stillness: with corvid escort, stand on the outermost girder tip where rising air exactly balances gravity. Thirty seconds is a novice badge; locals manage five minutes.

• Night-orchid Pollination: strap on a red headlamp (white disrupts bats), and help brush pollen between ultraviolet blooms. Drink the leftover nectar—one sip—then watch the turbines seem to slow. (They do not actually slow.)

• Patina Hunt: leave a plain steel washer on a marked ledge. Pick it up after two days; note the impossible alloy rainbow. Sell or frame at home.

──────────────────────────────────
10. Sleeping Quarters
──────────────────────────────────
Berth-niches are coffin-cozy alcoves cut into the cliff-wall behind gasketed hatches. Each has:
• hammock web (woven from retired V-belts)  
• warm pipe running under the pillow (30 °C, white noise hum)  
• louvered slit to the shaft—stars visible if you angle your head just so  

Ear dampers double as alarm: program them to vibrate at Cycle 6 if you fancy a midnight stroll.

──────────────────────────────────
11. Emergencies & Evacuation
──────────────────────────────────
Minor: twisted ankle, heat exhaustion—signal violet cloth; gardener first-aid crew arrive via zipline within four minutes.

Major: structural breach, runaway vent, or metallurgical memory-event. Two responses:

1. Duck into a wall hatch labeled “CALM ROOM.” These are pressure-rated cocoons with water and O2 for 6 h.  
2. If the cat-walk itself begins to unweave (rare but recorded), clip to an overhead cable and ride the manual bosun’s chair downward 200 m to the last-century mezzanine. From there, trained haulers can crank survivors back when the deck settles.

Cross-dimension extraction insurance must include “active industrial biotope” coverage; many policies exclude self-willed machinery losses.

──────────────────────────────────
12. Departure & Souvenirs
──────────────────────────────────
Before you go, observe Leave-Offering Protocol:

• Place a personal object (spent pen, cracked screen protector) on the rail during the Lull.  
• Whisper the thing you learned.  
• Wait. A crab or corvid will collect. Only then step through your portal.

Allowed exports: naturally shed corvid feathers, patina washers (≤100 g), scanned diagrams of vine root systems, three vials (10 ml each) of condenser water. Anything larger must pass a boilmancer’s mnemonic imprint test—to be sure the Workshop isn’t “still using it.”

──────────────────────────────────
13. Final Mindset
──────────────────────────────────
Expect sameness that is always different: the deck dip you swear wasn’t there yesterday, a lamp that spits violet instead of amber, the subtle way your multitool’s blade edge now repels rust. Don’t chase explanations; the tower ages like a continent and dreams like a forge. Your task is simpler: clip in, keep your balance, trade shiny things for better stories, and listen when the metal inhales.

If you can do that, the Throat-Workshop will remember you kindly—as a brief, welcome gust along its endless breath.
