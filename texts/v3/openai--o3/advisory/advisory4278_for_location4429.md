Field Manual for the Salt-Glass Heights  
(working catalogue designation: “The Tall World,” ß–gamma drift sector 19, meta-azimuth 4.3)

Audience  
Solo or small-team travelers with ordinary human biochemistry, average high-altitude tolerance, and no innate reality-stabilising abilities.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Pre-Departure Conditioning
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Lungs & Blood  
• Target PO₂ at destination hovers between 8–10 kPa (⅓ of sea-level Earth).  
• Spend at least 14 days at 4 000 m or in a hypobaric chamber; supplement with intermittent normobaric hypoxia training.  
• Carry 2 L-min micro-compressor rebreather masks; full tanks are unnecessary but the scrubber cartridge must be rated for static-charged dust.

1.2 Vestibular System  
• Expect gravity to modulate from 0.4 g to 0.7 g in pulses lasting 3–7 minutes.  
• Practice balance drills in parabolic flights or variable-G centrifuge pods. Train muscle memory for both climbing (high-force) and bounding (low-force) gaits.

1.3 Psychological Immunisation  
• The city learns from attention. Meditation that soft-focuses vision (Soto-Zen, “open-monitoring” style) reduces unintended labyrinth formation.  
• Acclimate to spatial loops (non-Euclidean VR mazes recommended). Motion sickness patches optional but useful.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Core Kit
--------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Clothing  
• Three-layer shell: inner electrostatic liner, aerogel middle (for −40 °C windchill), abrasion-proof ceramofibre outer.  
• Neck and face wrap impregnated with hygroscopic gel; sip melted condensate through embedded straw to combat throat rasp.  
• Photochromic goggles, V-band tinted—light here is violet-heavy and dazzles on glass.

2.2 Locomotion Aids  
• Mixed-mode boots: retractable micro-crampons for brittle surfaces, plus gecko-polymer pads for smooth, wavering stone.  
• Two dynamic tethers (graphene ribbon with piezo reel) rated for 1 500 kg—clip yourself even on “level” terraces; the mountain sometimes decides a ledge is now a wall.

2.3 Tools & Instruments  
• Monomaterial blades shatter the salt-glass; avoid. Bring instead a low-heat sonic cutter if sampling is mandatory.  
• Tri-field gravimeter/chronometer combo: warns of approaching time/weight shifts (tone alarms at ±0.05 g or 3 % clock drift).  
• Sub-audible resonator (70–90 Hz). The massif hums around 82 Hz; ping at 83 Hz and read echoes to map hidden chambers without staring holes into existence.  
• UV-sterilising lamp on waist swivel—filament fauna recoil from 280–320 nm bands; use for clearing hand-holds.

2.4 Marking & Navigation  
• Paint, chalk, pitons: useless (they migrate).  
• Carry 50 m of micro-balloon line dyed black. Knot every metre; the line’s own mass keeps it tracking the ledge even when topology flexes.  
• Personal inertial nav puck stores vector history locally; do not rely on sat-fix (sky too ionised for constellations to agree).

2.5 Life Support  
• Water: humidity <2 %. At 0.4 g you sweat little but exhale a lot. Minimum 3 L/day in collapsible bladders.  
• Calories: high-fat, crumb-free blocks. Wind will steal powders and granules.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. On-Site Protocols
--------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 First 10 Minutes  
• Clip in twice. Taste the air—if it stings, the static load is high; dial suit liner to “bleed” mode to discharge.  
• Let your instruments settle. Initial readings often lie until the environment acknowledges you.

3.2 Moving Through the City  
• Bridges: step rhythm must stay irregular (1-1-2-3 pattern). Repeating cadence invites harmonic fractures.  
• Switchbacks: walk midpoint, not inner curve; edges flake.  
• Amphitheatres: descent screws with time. Enter only if you can spare an extra subjective hour per five terraces.

3.3 Interacting with Flora & Fauna  
• Filament nets: never grab—retraction whip can cut neoprene. Observe with side vision; direct gaze hollows the crack they grow from.  
• Gliders: if shadow passes overhead, crouch. Their sonar disturbs gravity pockets above 1.5 m; staying low reduces lift-off risk.

3.4 Handling Shifts  
• Boom Event (internal fracture):  
  a) Sit or clip to anchor.  
  b) Check gravimeter—if readout drops below 0.5 g, secure unslung gear (it will float off then spear downward later).  
  c) After 60 s, remap surroundings; do not trust memory.

3.5 Shadow Detachment  
• Occurs during peak violet pulses every ~11 hours local.  
• Your shadow will stretch; if it peels free, remain still. Movement strengthens separation.  
• Re-attachment: shine your UV lamp straight down; shadow seeks the shortest darkness. Missing shadow for >30 min → expect mild hypothermia and disorientation; abort mission.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Scientific & Ethical Sampling
--------------------------------------------------------------------
• Remove ≤50 g total material per expedition. Mountain remembers subtraction; cumulative loss raises resonance pitch and increases structural churn.  
• Store samples in Faraday pouches—the static dust tries to “re-home” minerals.  
• Photograph, don’t pocket, the honeycomb architecture. Remember: focused scrutiny digs metaphysical cavities.  

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Communications & Extraction
--------------------------------------------------------------------
• Acoustic speech carries <5 m. Use line-of-sight sign language or body-conduction throat mics paired to bone receivers.  
• Emergency beacon: low-frequency (30 Hz) seismic thumper. Extraction craft must hover 200 m away; closer thrusters set off plate avalanches.  
• Worst-case self-rescue: ride a gravity lull. When g ≤0.45 g, jump outward with 3 m/s horizontal; you’ll describe a slow arc to lower ledges (parachute not recommended—can become sail for unseen attractors).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
6. Departure Debrief & Decontamination
--------------------------------------------------------------------
• Brush suit with ion blower; the dust keeps minor charge for weeks and will corrode electronics on your home shard.  
• Psychological check for topological after-images: if doorframes at home seem to rotate or hallways shorten, undergo spatial-normalisation therapy.  
• Log any new rules you notice. The tally sits at five; field offices suspect discovery of a sixth alters return-vector targeting, so updates are crucial.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
Quick-Reference Don’ts
1. Don’t whistle; compressed sound can splinter salt-glass.  
2. Don’t shine coherent lasers—filament life treats tight beams as predators and mass-retracts, destabilising ledges.  
3. Don’t carry magnets; stray flux pins dust to lungs.  
4. Don’t look straight down for more than ten seconds. The drop will decide you are part of it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
If you respect the mountain’s hesitations, it will return the courtesy. Step lightly, think broadly, and remember that in this place even your certainty has weight—sometimes enough to crack stone, sometimes not enough to stop you drifting into the violet haze.
