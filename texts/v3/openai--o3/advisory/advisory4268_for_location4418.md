PRE-DEPARTURE BRIEF  

World-ID: Plain-of-Organpipes / 7B-Aural-Delta  
Dominant Hazards: acoustic overload, glass-basalt lacerations, flash-flooding, opportunistic wet-lung microbes, stray gravity lenses  
Permits Required: Sound-harvest license (class I) for any powered device; Horizon Span visitor visa (obtainable on arrival but cheaper if filed 18 days before transit)  
Recommended Stay: 4–10 local days (≈ 5–13 Earth days; rotation is 28 h 11 m)

1. MEDICAL & BIOLOGICAL PREP

• Vaccinations/Boosters  
  – Wide-spectrum antipseudomonal (mist bacteria exploit moist human lungs)  
  – Inner-ear pressure equalizer implant or temporary myringotomy plugs. The daily “falling hush” inverts pressure fast enough to rupture untreated eardrums.  
  – Optional: low-light retinol drops. They reduce pupil lag when lightning-bright mist lasers wander past.

• Conditioning  
  – Cardiovascular: high humidity drives heart rate up 10–15 %. Three weeks of sauna training helps.  
  – Auditory training: download the 6-hour “Chordwall” acclimation track. Play at 80 dB; it teaches the brain to pick speech out of the continuous bio-choir.

• Insurance clauses  
  – Verify that your policy covers “exotic resonance events.” Some cheaper multiverse packages exclude damage caused by sub-bass vibration.

2. KIT LIST

Clothing  
  – Outer layer: ventilated, quick-wick micro-mesh. Cotton rots in a day.  
  – Under-layer: piezo-thread bodysuit (turns ambient vibration into trickle-charge for handhelds).  
  – Cloak or cowl dyed matte grey-violet; bright hues lure pollinator moths bigger than your face.  
  – Footwear: composite-plate soles; edges of basalt shards are scalpel sharp.

Sensory Gear  
  – Dynamic ear-goggles (“canal helmets”) with three modes:  
        a. Passive transparency (lets the soundscape through)  
        b. 12 dB soft-cap (default)  
        c. Full hush (for sleep or panic)  
    Never wear full hush while walking—predators hunt silence voids.  
  – Short-range echo-rod. Tap it; a haptic map of the next five metres appears in your palm. No visual screen needed.

Navigation & Power  
  – Pocket vibro-compass (magnetics don’t work; you home on Horizon Span’s 32 Hz city hum).  
  – Solar panels are useless under the canopy. Bring a hand-sized acoustic battery or harvest suit above.  
  – Waterproof field notebook; electronics occasionally misfire in gravity lenses.

Health & Survival  
  – Self-sealing mist mask: filters microbes but still passes low-frequency air so you can “hear” atmosphere changes.  
  – Coagulant spray for shard cuts (bleeding attracts click-wasps).  
  – Collapsible thermal hammock. Ground can flash-flood without warning.

Politeness & Trade  
  – 3–5 glass sound-coins: thin spheres already loaded with high value resonance (Gregorian choirs sell well). Locals barter in captured audio.  
  – Damping cloth (1 m²). Offering to wrap machinery in it is a mark of respect.

3. ARRIVAL PROCEDURES

• Gate insertion sites sit on bare basalt outside Horizon Span. Step clear fast; grinders patrol to keep entry pads shard-free.  
• Customs scans harvest devices to ensure they avoid protected fauna frequencies (44–46 Hz, 8.2 kHz, 31 kHz). A 300 credit fine per infringement tone.  
• Exchange quiet greeting: left palm over throat, small bow. Speaking immediately is rude; give the ambient sound first chance to “introduce” you.

4. GETTING AROUND

Glide-Rafts  
  – Flag one by aiming your echo-rod upward and pulsing the base twice; the returning amplitude marks you as a rider not wildlife.  
  – Stand centred. Leaning hard left or right puts the craft in correction mode (a drunken skid).  
  – Keep limbs aboard; micro-lev pumps on the hull emit a 19 kHz cone that can numb fingers permanently.

Walking  
  – Follow pressure ridges; the “glass” clinks underfoot, a steady G-sharp you’ll learn to trust. When the note drops, the shard field is giving way to hidden water. Step back.  
  – Every ten minutes pause and hum a single note. The ground will “answer” with reflected harmonics; if it fails to answer, you’re inside a gravity lens—retreat before you float higher than you like.

5. DAILY RHYTHM

Dawn (local 05:00)  
  – Light rises violet; mist densest; large pollinators feed. Good time to record pure chord samples.

Midday  
  – Canopy vents heat; organ-wind peaks at 110–120 dB. Seek shelter if your suit battery is already full—it can overload.

Afternoon “Falling Hush”  
  – 18-minute warning rumble around 23 Hz. Stop moving; glide-rafts park. Engage ear-goggles to 12 dB cap to protect from the pressure suck.  
  – After hush, rain crashes in silver sheets. Lakes appear; micro-sonic plankton bloom—stunning sight, but wear mask.

Night  
  – Predators (sinuate cats, ripple serpents) come out. They track heartbeats. Sleep in buildings or suspend thermal hammock at least 3 m above ground, keep a low pulse by breathing slow.

6. FOOD & WATER

• Tap rooms inside Horizon Span condense mist through silent dehumidifiers—safe to drink. Surface pools: boil or sonicate 30 s to kill microbes.  
• Local cuisine:  
  – “Ghost kelp” noodles grown in tree hollows, faintly luminous.  
  – Basalt-trout (a burrowing amphibian) served raw; muscles twitch audibly. Etiquette: wait until it quiets, then eat.  
  – Avoid bright orange fungus; spore burst equals tear-gas.

7. CULTURAL NOTES

• The society reveres silence almost as much as sound. Gift giving is done during the hush; recipients respond only after noise returns.  
• Never whistle. Whistling is a territorial marker for ridge-raptors; using it claims hunting rights you cannot defend.  
• Locals may ask to “borrow your shadow,” meaning a moment alone with your acoustic profile. Custom allows a polite refusal.

8. MAJOR RISKS & HOW TO MITIGATE

A. Acoustic Overload  
   – Signs: nausea, vertigo, tooth pain.  
   – Fix: retreat into any structure; wall-skins neutralize energy. If in open, kneel, press forehead to ground—the basalt drains low frequency quickest.

B. Lacerations  
   – Basalt cuts seldom bleed until minutes later due to high magnesium content. Clean immediately, apply coagulant, wrap in damping cloth.

C. Flash Floods  
   – Ground rumble followed by sudden silence. Climb trees: trunks are hollow but hold. Do NOT grab canopy vines; some sting.

D. Gravity Lenses  
   – Indicators: voice pitch sliding up, limbs feel light. Walk backwards the way you came, counting seconds; time dilates slightly but backwards motion negates drift.

9. SOUVENIRS

Permitted:  
  – Shard-glass jewellery (must be tumbled smooth), recorded chord spheres, polymer prints of your personal mist-laser signature.  
Prohibited:  
  – Live eggs (floaters), untreated basalt slabs, unlicensed echo-samples of native speech (privacy law).

10. DEPARTURE

• Forty minutes before gate time, fast for “quiet stomach”; digestive gurgles can mis-register as contraband audio and delay exit.  
• Exchange leftover resonance credits at the Damp-Bank kiosk; they corrupt after 48 h outside world anyway.  
• Offer a departing damp-cloth fold to the gate warden—thanks for the safe echoes.
