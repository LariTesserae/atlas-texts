1. WHAT TO EXPECT ON ARRIVAL
• Temp–humidity index: 34–38 °C equivalent at 100 % RH. Instantly sweat-drenched.  
• Light: 5 % of solar full-spectrum plus erratic bio- and piezo-luminescence. Vision never fully adapts—colors strobe, shadows crawl.  
• Ground: 20 °–40 ° slopes of glass-sharp basalt alternating with pudding-soft clay. Entire surfaces may shift tens of centimeters without notice.  
• Noise floor: 90–110 dB. Continuous. The barrel-frog bass carries enough low-frequency energy to upset digestion and equilibrium.  
• Seismic flutter: micro-quakes every 3–4 h; rockfalls or steam eruptions likely within 500 m of any point you stand on.  
Everything is wet, hot, loud, moving and biologically aggressive.

2. PHYSICAL CONDITIONING & MEDICAL PREP
• Cardiovascular endurance: you will climb, jump fissures and sprint away from collapses. Train with 20 kg incline hiking at 35 °C sauna conditions.  
• Inner-ear acclimation: practice balance drills blindfolded with sub-sonic bass playback (recordings supplied in appendix).  
• Vaccinations/chemos: broad-spectrum antifungal prophylaxis, gram-negative gut flora boosters, polyvalent antivenom micro-factories (mRNA patch 14 d prior).  
• Skin ritual: two daily applications of silicone-ceramide barrier cream; prevents immersion foot and provides minor abrasion shielding.  
• Hearing: custom vented ear filters (not full plugs—must still perceive directional information) rated NRR 20; include bone-conduction comm pickups.  
• Psychological: claustrophobia and misophonia screening. The forest never gives you silence or open sightlines.

3. PERSONAL KIT – KEEP IT UNDER 24 kg
Garment shell  
• Base: bamboo-charcoal wicking longsleeve + leggings (discourages insects, fungi).  
• Exterior: ripstop dyneema “jungle cardigan” with ceramic bead scales on forearms and shins (deflects basalt edges, root-lurker fangs).  
• Footgear: amphibious split-sole boots—hard midfoot plate, soft gecko-rubber front/back. Ankle drain ports.  
• Hands & eyes: nitrile-Kevlar hybrid gloves; photochromic visor that flips from IR-enhanced low-light to copper-green filter.

Tools  
• Tri-section ceramic machete (non-ferrous, won’t spin magnet-moss).  
• Carbon-fiber balance staff with resonance sensor—vibrates if substrate about to shear.  
• 30 m micro-braid winch line; doubles as suspension for sleep.  
• “Dragonfly” lidar/cloud mapper: palm drone that stitches live 3-D map every minute—necessary because landforms mutate.  
• Inertial nav puck + Galileo-fallback sat receiver (works only if an unobscured sky gap appears). Never trust compass needles.  
• Loom-sealed roll-hammock with integrated insect mesh—sleep above leaf litter or be explored by root-lurkers.

Consumables  
• 3 L blivet hydration bladder plus 0.1-micron inline filter and UV burst straw—rainwater is biologically filthy but chemically clean.  
• 8 000 kcal/day in sealed lipid-protein beads, odor-masked. Do not cook; smoke and food scent are predator beacons.  
• 200 ml antifungal wash, 60 ml chelated iron blocker (prevents “black rust” rash from basalt dust).  
• Flash-charge lantern (amber) only for emergencies; white light triggers mating response in several beetle clades.

Weight reserve: 2 kg for scientific or personal items—expect to lose them to the forest eventually.

4. MOVING, NAVIGATING AND KEEPING YOUR FOOTING
• Time your marches: 0400–0700 and 1600–1900 are the only temperatures below core-melting. Plan to trance-rest midday and midnight.  
• Step rhythm: vary your cadence; root-lurkers predict a steady two-beat walk. Use irregular triplets, pause often.  
• Never trust overhangs. Basalt column roofs collapse without acoustic warning. Travel mid-slope, not valley floors or ridge crests.  
• Streams are temporary; treat any suddenly appearing freshet as a potential trench five minutes later.  
• Leave reflector tags only above 4 m height; anything lower will be overgrown or hauled off by bone-tool primates.

5. COMMUNICATION IN THE HOWL ZONE
• Spoken voice carries <2 m. Adopt three modes:  
  1. Chest-level drum pad (inflatable) for low-frequency pulse codes.  
  2. Wrist strobe (470 nm) for night flash-language.  
  3. Touch-signal rope when moving single-file.  
• Radios under 1 GHz drown in cicada harmonics; use spread-spectrum ultrasonic transceivers (piezoelectric) through solid substrate instead.

6. MANAGING WATER, FOOD AND SLEEP
• Water is abundant but arrives in bursts. Fill bladders during rain curtains then filter.  
• Electrolytes: perspiration here is extreme; pop salt-potassium tablets each litre consumed.  
• Forager temptation: every fruit advertises with neon bioluminescence—almost all contain glassy silica rinds or neuro-alkaloids. Unless you have lab verification, do not eat.  
• Sleep aloft: hang hammock between three trunks (tripod rig). Check liana load—some are predatory and contract on heat signature.  
• Night watches pointless; everything glows. Rely on proximity vibration alarms (monofil ring around camp, tuned to >2 kg).

7. SURVIVING THE FAUNA WITHOUT STARTING A WAR
Riverback grazers: Docile but will stampede along any open line. Their biolights ramp before a rush—get behind basalt and stay low.  
Sky-reefers: Usually ignore ground walkers. Do not shine white light upward; they mistake it for swarm glow and may drop adhesive nets.  
Bone-tool primates: Classified “proto-sapient.” Offer no food or shiny objects; they will dismantle your gear to copy it, then lose interest and litter the forest with hazardous shards. If surrounded, project a slow rhythmic beat—signals non-threat merging and they disperse.  
Root-lurkers: Wear ankle bells tuned above 8 kHz; the pitch exceeds their detection band, scrambling strike timing. If one tunnels under you, jump—their inertia commits them to the previous coordinates for 0.2 s.  
Insects: 4 cm vampiric midges track CO₂ plumes; exhale through nose into scarf impregnated with vanilla-hexane blocker.

8. MICRO-ETHICS TOWARD EMERGING SAPIENTS
Interference cascade is brutally fast here: drop a single steel screwdriver and within two breeding cycles bone-tool primates may integrate it, altering their hunting success and population curve.  
Rule set:  
• Pack in/pack out EVERYTHING including sweat-soaked bandages.  
• No voice or symbol teaching.  
• Observe from >15 m, no flash or laser pointers.  
• If you must reclaim stolen tech, leave a natural object of equal fascination (e.g., large iridescent seed husk) to prevent retaliatory group raids.

9. WHEN (AND HOW) TO LEAVE IN A HURRY
Evac triggers:  
• Two or more rock-fracture flashes inside 5 min (cliff collapse chain).  
• Continuous tremor >10 s (magma surge toward surface).  
• Silence—if the forest goes quiet, apex predator or toxic vent bloom is imminent.

Extraction plan:  
• Your safest LZ is not ground but 40 m up where sky-reef updrafts open the canopy. Inflate tri-cell aerostat (compressed He cannister weighs 900 g) and hoist rappel line.  
• Alternate: follow magnet-moss gradient outward until you hit salt-air funnel; then angle 30 ° right of prevailing wind—surf sound of surf to coast where beach shelf is stable lava.  
• Portal/gate devices must anchor to igneous outcrop older than 48 h; anything younger may still extrude and shear the frame.

FINAL NOTES
• Nothing you do will make you comfortable—aim instead for survivable margins.  
• Plan objectives in half-day increments; the landscape voids longer term plans.  
• Record continuously; memory fails under sensory overload and shifting reference points.  
• Treat stillness (rare, precious) as a resource. Ten seconds of quiet amid the din can reset panic chemistry better than any drug.

Arrive curious, travel light, touch nothing that shines, and leave before the land remembers you stood upon it.
