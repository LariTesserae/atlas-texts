Field Manual 7-Θ:
Preparations for a Multiversal Excursion to the High-Born Crater-Sea
(“The Warm, Dim Ocean in the Clouds”)

VERSION 2.3  |  Compiled by the Bureau of Remote Habitats  
For: Ordinary human traveler with standard Earth-norm biology, no extensive augmentations

———————————————————————————
1. Macro-Environment at a Glance
• Altitude: 6–7 km a.s.l. (thin but hyper-humid air; barometric pressure ≈ 60 kPa)  
• Climate: 34 – 37 °C water; 22 – 26 °C air; RH 100 % at all times  
• Illumination: 1 – 3 % of Earth noon lux; perpetual grey “pre-dawn”  
• Geology: Actively up-thrusting basalt; minor quakes daily, larger events monthly  
• Combustion: Impossible in open air; external oxidizer or chemical heat packs required  
• Electromagnetics: Weak, erratic readings near thermal vents; compasses unreliable  
• Biological unknowns: Low macroscopic diversity but abundant fungal/algal spores

———————————————————————————
2. Pre-Departure Conditioning

2.1 Cardiopulmonary
• Two weeks of high-altitude training (3 700 m+) recommended to mitigate hypoxia.  
• Practice diaphragmatic breathing and breath-hold (lake dwellings require frequent water entries).

2.2 Sensorial Acclimation
• Spend at least 48 h in a light-restricted, white-noise environment; the near-silence/lack of contrast in the crater can cause disorientation and panic in first-time visitors.

2.3 Skill Set
• Low-light navigation, wet-rock climbing, rudimentary free-diving, and single-paddle canoe handling.  
• Mnemonics and oral story-keeping: being able to memorize and accurately recite even short genealogies earns immediate social credit.

———————————————————————————
3. Equipment Manifest

(Everything must tolerate permanent condensation; no leather, no untreated paper.)

3.1 Garments
• Base layer: hydrophobic, quick-dry silk-protein or synthetic equivalent.  
• Over layer: 2 mm neutrally-buoyant wetsuit with attached cowl and ear vents (prevents middle-ear barotrauma when diving).  
• Footwear: gecko-rubber split-toe boots, magnetic inserts removed (sensitive basalt ridges can demagnetize them).

3.2 Light & Vision
• Active: head-mounted, low-output red/IR LEDs in IP-68 housing.  
• Passive: a pair of photo-multiplying goggles (light amplification ×1 000) for night paddling.  
• Etiquette note: never shine white beams at locals; it flattens the bioluminescent algae they use for orientation.

3.3 Information & Note-Taking
• Primary: subdermal or wrist-mounted solid-state audio recorder (rated for 100 % humidity).  
• Backup: laser-etched sapphire micro-slates—non-organic, immune to mildew, culturally acceptable as “eternal stone”.  
• DO NOT flaunt modern waterproof paper or plastic books; durable writing is effectively sorcery here and threatens the elders’ authority.

3.4 Heat & Cooking
• 12 × exothermal iron-salt meal pouches (fireless rations).  
• Micro-reactor kettles that mix calcium oxide + water for warm drinks.  
• Never light open flame; even a storm match will sputter poisonous ammonia-like fumes.

3.5 Navigation & Safety
• Inertial nav puck (gyro + accelerometer) with vibration feedback—works in zero-visibility.  
• 30 m glow-core climbing line; neutral buoyancy helps keep it from vanishing in gravity updrafts.  
• Clip-on mass tags (15 kg tungsten) to defeat “buoyancy corridors” when you need predictable weight.

3.6 Medical
• Altitude/AMS: acetazolamide 250 mg, PRN.  
• Mycology pack: topical econazole + systemic fluconazole (skin and lung spores are rampant).  
• Broad-spectrum antibiotics; water filter straw (proto-ocean carries novel bacteria).  
• Electroporator insect-sting kit (in case the unnamed cliff-cats bite—venom still under study).

3.7 Gifts & Trade
• Prism-cut pumice toys (children love to race them in vent updrafts).  
• Titanium sewing needles (immune to rust, prized by basket-weavers).  
• Four or five folk songs you can perform from memory—the society values live oral art over artifacts.

4. Transit and First 24 Hours

4.1 Entry vector
• Rift/portal must be plotted above the outer rim ledge; jumping straight into the lagoon risks scalding pockets from hidden vents.  
• Descend on static line to water level; remove ballast only after testing for buoyancy corridors.

4.2 Initial Physiological Checks
• Pulse oximeter: maintain SpO₂ > 88 %.  
• Hydration: 1 L electrolyte within first hour (sweat not obvious in 100 % RH).

4.3 Approach Protocol
• Announce presence with rhythmic paddle taps (their equivalent of a door knock).  
• Offer a short memorized greeting—never a written letter.  
• Allow the elders to assign you a mooring; do not pick an empty basalt niche on your own.

———————————————————————————
5. Cultural Operating Instructions

5.1 Rule of Voice
• Memory is law—contradicting an elder’s recitation is treasonous unless you can sing an accepted counter-ballad.  
• Meetings conclude with the phrase “Let it hang like stone,” signifying that spoken words now carry weight.

5.2 Property & Space
• The water column above each barge is considered private “roof”. Passing overhead without permission equals walking on someone’s house.

5.3 Technology Taboo
• Combustion, plastics that don’t rot, and long-life ink threaten their cosmology. Use discreetly or trade explanations in song form first.

———————————————————————————
6. Environmental Hazards & Mitigations

6.1 Gravity Updrafts
• Identified by shimmer in mist and 1–2 °C temperature rise.  
• Secure all loose tools; sudden buoyancy can launch a 30 kg pack 10 m upward.

6.2 Hydrothermal Vents
• Surface bubbles ≠ boiling—water can be 80 °C directly beneath. Keep at least one body length above visible effervescence.

6.3 Rock Falls
• Basalt terraces may “unzip” when nightly expansion reverses at dawn. Sleep tethered to your craft, not beneath rock shelves.

6.4 Fauna
• Glass-wing rays are harmless but startling when they breach.  
• Spiral eels: mildly electric shock on contact; wear 0.5 mm neoprene gloves when gathering vent minerals.  
• Cliff-cats: territorial; shine IR strobe downward (they perceive heat) and back away slowly.

———————————————————————————
7. Scientific & Personal Opportunities

• Petrology: direct sampling of fresh mantle basalts younger than 15 years.  
• Biology: observe adaptive radiation in real time—bring portable genome sequencer if waterproof.  
• Sociology: witness formation of a proto-literate oral society; rare chance to document mnemonic governance.

———————————————————————————
8. Exit Strategy

Give the elders three nights’ notice—departures are ritualized so your absence fits oral record.  
Hand over a final gift (popular: a complete story of your visit in verse).  
Ascend before local dawn when updrafts are weakest; secure ballast to maintain footing until clear of crater humidity.

———————————————————————————
Pack well, speak carefully, and remember: here, only the spoken word endures. What you say writes itself directly onto the living memory of the place—make every sentence worth the stone they believe it weighs.
