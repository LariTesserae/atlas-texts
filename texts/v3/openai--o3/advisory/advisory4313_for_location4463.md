TRAVEL BRIEF  
Destination code (Wayfarer’s Registry): W-310-∞ / “The Hollow Continuum”  
Transit category: Open-access but non-self-sustaining.  
Recommended stay for first-timers: 48–72 hours. Longer visits require disciplined heat management and psychological countermeasures.

1. CLIMATE & GENERAL ENVIRONMENT
• Temperature: 31 °C / 88 °F, locked.  
• Relative humidity: 99 – 100 %. Expect permanent clamminess; sweat cannot evaporate.  
• Illumination: full white “late-morning” light on a precise 26-hour brightness cycle; no color change, no night sky.  
• Precipitation: a fine, warm mist every 3–6 minutes. It beads on everything and seeps into every seam.  
• Wind: effectively nil; convection currents only.  
• Surface: endless floor of razor-sharp modular plates. Water is channelled in open gutters; hidden pumps recycle every drop.  
• Altitude variation: negligible—never more than a two-metre step between tile field and tallest machine skirt.

2. HUMAN-SPECIFIC HAZARDS
Heat stress: You will overheat faster than you expect because sweat does not cool you. Monitor core temperature; schedule purposeful “rest-immersion” in the wider sluices every hour.  
Lacerations: Plate rims are as sharp as fresh machining burrs. Trip once, slice deeply.  
Electrical & moisture damage: 100 % humidity will infiltrate all unsealed electronics within minutes.  
Orientation loss: With no landmarks, no sky features, and rapid erasure of footprints, travellers spiral in minutes.  
Psychological drift: Monotony plus a 26-hour day deranges circadian rhythm by day four; mild dissociation is common after day two.  
Automatic repair systems: Stand on a plate that gets scheduled for replacement and you may find yourself lifted, tilted, or glued by fresh fusion resin.

3. PRE-DEPARTURE GEAR LIST
A. Clothing  
• Base layer: hydrophobic micro-mesh (polypropylene or equivalent).  
• Outer layer (mandatory): thin, cut-resistant softshell with sealed seams; built-in vent zips for air-pumping cooling vest.  
• Footwear: knee-high puncture-resistant rubber/composite boots with tooth-less tread (reduces rim damage).  
• Gloves: ANSI-cut-level 4+ for falls and tile handling.  
• Wide-brim crushable hat or hood; you cannot count on sunburn, but constant drizzle in the eyes causes fatigue.

B. Life-support & Med  
• Active-cooling vest or phase-change ice packs (2 kg per 12 h) if you plan work, not just sightseeing.  
• Oral rehydration salts; you will sweat salt that the mist does not replace.  
• Broad-spectrum antibiotics/antifungals for skin infections—cuts fester fast in this wet heat.  
• 2000 IU vitamin-D capsules per day if staying longer than a week (no UV here).  
• Compact suture and sterile strip kit for plate-edge injuries.

C. Electronics & Instrumentation  
• All devices IP-68 MINIMUM, vacuum-greased gasket reinforcements; desiccant packs useless.  
• Strap-down bone-conduction comms—no open microphones; excess noise may violate local “silence preference.”  
• Inertial navigator (foot-mounted step counter + gyros). Do not rely on magnetic compasses; latent fields from under-floor conveyors drift.  
• Light beacons: low-spectral-shift LEDs (<570 nm); anything orange/red is suppressed by ambient refraction and can confuse local fauna.

D. Navigation & Marking  
• Twelve-metre micro-line reel with spring hooks. Clip free end to a machine skirt before exploring; reel yourself back.  
• Ultra-violet/infra-violet chalk is useless (plate replacement erases). Instead, bring a spool of surface-safe suction cups or tiny weighted magnets; retrieve them on exit.  
• Acoustic pinger (under 70 dB) every ten minutes to locate teammates; higher sustained volume risks attracting maintenance entities.

E. Shelter & Camp  
• Avoid tents—exceed height rules and trap water. Use clamshell bivy sack with collapsible 0.8 m frame, or sleep raised on a cooking grate laid over two gear boxes.  
• Pack a mylar groundsheet trimmed to square <1.5 m per side so edges will align with tile seams; larger sheets can divert water and trigger fauna agitation.

F. Food & Water  
• Water: harvest condensation; it is sterile and mildly sweet. Bring carbon filter only for taste.  
• Food: all indigenous fauna are metallic-tissue or silica-cellulose hybrids—non-toxic but nutritionally void. You must carry your calories. Choose vacuum-sealed high-fat bricks; they tolerate heat.  
• Cooking: unnecessary; eat cold to minimize odor and steam.

4. BEHAVIOURAL PROTOCOLS
Rule 0: Do not impede water flow. Kneel over a gutter and you’ll become a rock in the river—everything here reacts badly to that.  
Noise discipline: Short bursts under 70 dB are tolerated; continuous drone or music has historically led to visit-terminating “vanishings.”  
Touch etiquette: Press or tap—yes. Pound, gouge, carve—no. If you unintentionally chip a plate, leave the area; archivores will arrive to sterilize and retexture.  
Wildlife respect:  
• Glossworms: step over sluices to avoid crushing them; their dissolved trails can etch boot rubber.  
• Coil gulls: ignore; shining lasers makes them dive.  
• Archivores: black tripods, one-metre tall; if one changes course toward you, you are blocking a flaw it wants. Move.  
• Murmuration spawn: treat as rolling ball bearings—do not collect souvenirs; they disassemble and may slice textiles when they merge.

5. ROUTINE FOR FIRST 48 HOURS
Hour 0–3: Acclimate. Sit on a monolith skirt, run cooling vest, synchronize your watch to the 26-hour light waveform by matching peak lux.  
Hour 3–12: Short radial walks on tether, returning when reel hits 200 m. Note plate morphology changes; use them as mental compass points.  
Hour 13–25: Attempt one “sluice follow”—walk WITH the flow; you’ll reach a valve shed every ~5 km. Avoid standing directly over valves during hiss events; plates sometimes lift.  
Hour 25–30: Full rest, legs elevated (reduces edema). Silence period; ear-plugs help if pump bassline keeps you alert.  
Hour 30–48: Optional ascent of a mid-rise monolith (stairlike catwalks). Maximum allowed height is 200 m; do not climb cranes—those were deprecated mid-epoch. From above, plan return route by spotting the pearl-white observation rostrum that marks your ingress point.

6. LONG-STAY CONSIDERATIONS (>3 DAYS)
• Circadian drift: Move to a personal 26-h schedule; blue-light goggles at “dusk” help anchor melatonin.  
• Skin care: Twice-daily chlorhexidine wipe-down; fungus loves perpetual damp.  
• Equipment assimilation: Objects left motionless for >6 h begin to acquire ghost-imprint patterns. Retrieve and wipe gear nightly to avoid it being adopted by the substrate.  
• Data corruption: Quantum noise from sub-floor drives can hash unshielded SSDs. Keep backups in Faraday roll.

7. EMERGENCY EXFIL
If injured or overheated beyond self-help, fire the orange (not red!) flare; portal services monitor specific wavelength flashes. While waiting:  
• Lie prone across two tiles so you don’t obstruct a gutter.  
• Maintain <65 dB vocalization.  
• Do NOT build a signal fire; smoke alarms hidden in cloud layer trigger a local “rain downpour” that can drown unconscious humans in siphon pits.

8. ETHICAL AND LEGAL
This world is believed to be an ongoing automated artwork or industrial process. Harvesting fauna, removing floor plates, or dumping foreign microbes violates Interversal Heritage Charter §7. Offenders are extradited to the Custodial Consensus; sentences involve restitution labour in far less hospitable constructs.
