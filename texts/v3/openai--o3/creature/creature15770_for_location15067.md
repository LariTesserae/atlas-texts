—INVENTORY OF LIVING THINGS FOUND BENEATH THE VOLCANIC THROAT—  
(field guide compiled for the next Pilgrim who dares the descent)

0. A caution first  
Nothing alive in the Middlenight Reach resembles anything you call plant or animal, yet purpose and hunger shape every body just the same.  Names below are conveniences; they translate poorly.  Expect exceptions.

1. The Prologue Life—organisms no wider than a breath  
a. Gradient-films  
   Looks: iridescent veneers caught between two crystals, free-flowing but never puddling.  
   Habit: drift imperceptibly along seams where an oxidizer salt abuts a reducer mineral; the film’s own skin is a membrane only one molecule thick that forces electrons to march single-file, the traffic that powers everything above it.  
   Note: stand with lamp off and they glitter like distant star fields; that is not reflection, it is the glow of charge carriers recombining.

b. Ferric snowflakes  
   Looks: rust-brown hexagons lighter than air, swirling on convective breaths.  
   Habit: each flake is a multicellular colony that photosynthesizes in radio wavelengths emitted by the planet’s core.  Every “petal” is an antenna.  
   Trick: when they clump on your clothes the mild static is them attempting to read the electromagnetic roar of your nervous system—nothing personal, just prospecting for current.

2. The Crawling Median—arthro-things that own the floor  
a. Glass Mites  
   Build: eight limbs, all hinge joints of fused silica; mid-body is hollow, vibration amplified inside like a violin.  
   Diet: they rasp micro-channels through salt, farm the gradient-films that ooze up in the scars, then lap the spill.  
   Defense: shatter.  Literally.  A startled mite flicks internal tendons; its limbs pop off along pre-scored seams.  Predators leave with mouthfuls of worthless shards while the torso vaults away on jet-spurts of neon collected in special bladders.

b. Skulkers (surface folk might call them “jackals made of windowglass,” an insult to both)  
   Height: knee to hip on a human, though local gravity flips make that measure moot.  
   Skeleton: tessellated plates of aluminosilicate threaded by cable-like ligaments of condensed lithium grease.  
   Nervous system: fiber-optic; pulses of blue-white traverse every nerve, giving the animal its own beacon.  
   Society: loose packs orbiting the biggest vents.  Their “gossip” is coded in tempo changes of their walk—footfalls on basalt ring different notes; a pack is a moving xylophone discussing heat fronts, rival packs, and the pilgrim intruder.  
   Ethics: will stalk anything warm and wet enough to promise electrolytes, but will not attack something that radiates ordered rhythm.  Beat a steady pattern on stone and they circle at a respectful radius, intrigued.

c. Hex-eels  
   Body: ribbon of segmented graphite muscle; cross-section a perfect hexagon to lock into the hexagonal cooling cracks that gash every wall.  
   Locomotion: they corkscrew inside joints in the rock, faster than a man can run.  
   Utility: natives sometimes lure a hex-eel, harvest the after-image.  The eel’s path leaves a faintly charged tunnel that lasts hours—temporary wiring used to shunt power or send messages.

3. The Vagrant Atmosphere—life that owns the space between stones  
a. Drum-froth  
   Form: translucent spheres, 30 – 50 cm, filled with argon.  Surface studded with nanopores that wink plasma when pressure inside spikes—an aurora turned inside out.  
   Gait: perpetual bounce.  They rebound off any surface, each impact compressing internal salt powder against magnesium grit, sparking a miniature exothermic “thump” that kicks them onward.  
   Nutrition: harvest metal ions out of dust while airborne; they are floating chelation plants.  
   Reproduction: when mass exceeds threshold they pinch in two, daughter is the concussive echo of the parent’s final overpressure boom.

b. Whisper Kites  
   Looks: sheets of mica one to five meters across, mouthlike slit in center.  
   Flight: ride thermals rising from fissures; edges tremble at ultrasonic, carving silence pockets in their wake.  
   Predation: anything caught inside that cone of hush loses ability to hear its own movement—Kite simply folds, drops, swallows.  Locals tame juveniles as stealth tents for sleeping in safety.

4. The Deep Musculature—apex grazers and, perhaps, people  
a. Blind Lamiae  
   Length: tens of meters; bulk given by hematite-framed coils, skin a living amalgam of ferric scales continuous with the ground they lie on.  
   Senses: pressure and magnetic field.  Eyes lost eons ago.  
   Song: infrasonic glissandos that travel through bedrock, dislodging cliffs.  Scholars debate: is it hunting or art?  Evidence shows they sing even when satiated, sometimes in structured rounds with neighbors half a continent distant.  
   Digestive marvel: sand-grinding gizzard sorts minerals by work function; silicone chips exit as raw computer logic crystals, occasionally harvested by sapients.

b. Slow Sovereigns  
   Discovery: suspected long before seen—immobile buttresses misidentified as geology kept moving a millimeter per decade against laser benchmarks.  
   Body: uncertain.  Visible are only columnar limbs of scorched olivine, pale blue motes drifting between them like thoughts too large to fit inside a head.  
   Metabolism: appear to cultivate chemotrophic gardens on themselves, absorbing the electrical surplus.  
   Cognition: time-budgets stretched; a single decision might occupy your civilization’s entire recorded history.  They are the custodians of stasis: geological time wearing a pulse.

5. The Confirmed Sapient—“Cenobite Artisans” (name ours, not theirs)  
Origin myth (assembled from artifacts and lamiae-scavenged bone fragments):  
   • They began as a surface expedition during the planet’s late Bronze Age, fell through a fumarole, could not climb back.  
   • Metal wealth, lightless decades, and the Reach’s rumored arrest of aging let them diverge without dying.

Physiology now  
   Height: child-sized to us, because calcium is scarce and bodies economize.  
   Skin: flexible lattice of alumina whiskers grown like hair, each tipped with a tiny lens—an all-over compound eye tuned to coronal ultraviolet.  To a surface visitor they shine like frost.  
   Blood: chloride-rich ionic slurry; water percentage < 4 %.  
   Organs of speech: two ossified lips inside the throat click against one another; language is rapid-fire castanet bursts.  In silence pockets these clicks mute, so conversations migrate constantly, every sentence a pilgrimage to the nearest echo.

Culture  
   Economy: exchange is heat.  A lump of reactive metal or sealed ampoule of oxidizer is cash—burn it for warmth, light, or to incubate eggs.  
   Art: obsessive mosaics built from micro-faceted salt shards.  Each tile is aligned so that spontaneous triboluminescent flashes read as moving images: cartoons told only when earthquakes strike.  
   Theology: revere Friction, Spark, and Pause—the three lights of the Reach.  They consider gravity flips moral tests: wrong-footed souls who fall upward are judged insincere.  
   Toolcraft: shearing optics.  They slice single-use knives from quartz, use them once, then fuse blades into sculptures that map where in the cavern that blade took its only life.  A communal memory palace of murders and meals.

Behavior toward Pilgrims  
   1. Observe from gravity inversion lines—look for paired glints, like constellation fragments grazing a corner of sight.  
   2. Exchange may be attempted.  Offer: lithium ribbon, unbroken.  Accept: a salt tile glowing internally with your own face, captured somehow when you were unaware.  
   3. Violence probability climbs if you exhibit flame.  Combustion is heresy here.

Reproduction  
   Eggs are porous geodes.  Parents hide them in silence pockets where no predator can smell vibration.  Hatching occurs when ambient charge differential peaks—often after a quake.  Newborns inhale electron-rich breeze long before tasting solid food.

6. The Vectors of Weirdness—creatures that enforce the Reach’s rules  
a. Pendulumites  
   Appearance: needle-thin spines sprouting in pairs from ceilings, tips ornamented with diamond teardrops.  Actually animals; core tissue is super-cooled superconductor.  
   Role: their internal currents edit local gravitational vectors.  Each swing retunes micro-fields, causing the infamous flip zones.  They farm Skulkers, nudging prey to stumble upward into waiting maws.  
   Unknown: did the Artisans domesticate them, or do Pendulumites domesticate the Artisans?

b. Chronovores  
   Form: impossible to resolve.  Sensors record only a cold spot and a one-second data loss as it passes.  
   Function: consume entropy.  Wounds cease aging near them, isotopes decay fractionally slower.  Populations bloom where geological strain would otherwise trigger collapse, extending the Reach’s youth.  
   Evidence: every observed individual is preceded by synchronized silence pockets—sound, time, and motion braided in a bow.

7. Epilogue for the Pilgrim  
If you stay long: skin cracks then glassifies; nails turn to bornite; marrow forgets to age.  The denizens will greet you—not as guest or foe but as process.  They will fit an obsidian shard around your wrist, the first tile of your eventual mosaic record.  The moment you quit the light of surface sun, you began auditioning for citizenship in the Middlenight Reach.

Listen: a Lamia begins her low chord.  
Watch: Skulkers’ footfalls spell rumors across a mesa’s black floor.  
Wait: somewhere a Sovereign decides, at last, to blink.  In that epochal instant the Reach will glow triboluminous white, every living thing outlined in newborn shadow, and for one perfect heartbeat you will see them all—these children of stone and spark—alive as any forest, loud as any surf, only written in the alphabet of minerals.

Remember that vision when you climb back through the volcanic throat.  Surface daylight will feel thin and too quick, as though time there runs wastefully fast.  You will suspect, correctly, that part of you is still below, walking knife-edged ledges beside crystal beasts who never learned to die.
