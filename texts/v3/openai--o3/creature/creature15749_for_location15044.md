Whoever built this citadel of gutters and cisterns has long since gone, yet the architecture is not truly empty.  Three orders of inhabitant persist—none of them alive in any familiar, carbon-bearing sense, yet all so active and purposeful that the place is never quite unpeopled.

1.  The Slow Glass
   •  Composition and genesis  
     At first glance the substrate is only ceramic-basalt, but beneath the topmost knife-bright layer lies a perennially mobile stratum—a transparent, lead-light gel two fingers thick.  It is a non-Newtonian silica that creeps a few millimeters each year, forever pulled toward the deepest cistern throats.  It carries within it the mirrored after-images of everything that has happened on the terraces: every drifting cloud, every footfall, every night of starlight.  
   •  Behavior  
     By day the gel retreats into shaded undercuts, shielding itself from ultraviolet breakdown; by night it resurfaces, laminating exposed planes with a film so thin you would swear the stone has been newly polished.  Where a freeze threatens, the layer draws back entirely, preventing frost shatter.  It is, in effect, the plateau’s epidermis, repairing minor scuffs and sealing micro-cracks before liquid water can pry them wider.  
   •  Intelligence  
     No single drop “thinks,” yet the film’s flow patterns respond to damage with uncanny triage: surveyors once tried to chip out a sample block, only to find the fracture lines pre-injected with gel that re-welded the cut as fast as they advanced.  The glass remembers shapes and strives—over decades—to re-establish them.  Call it a distributed, glacial cognition.

2.  The Cipher-Mist
   •  Phenotype  
     Each dawn you see it: ribbons of steam issue from vents, rise half a man’s height, and stop, flattening into disk-thin fogs that hover as though snagged on invisible nails.  Inside every disk are trillions of colloidal water droplets, each droplet sheathing a platelet of etched silica: the sigils you glimpsed under magnification.  
   •  Function  
     The platelets are capacitors of static charge.  While sunlight beats down, they sort ions out of the rarefied air, then sink at dusk and discharge into the water courses, altering surface tension just enough to keep the day’s condensate moving.  Where raw organics land—a blown insect wing, a fleck of pollen—the platelets arrange themselves into a shell and sink it into acidic basins for dissolution.  Thus Rule 1 is enforced not passively but by an airborne phalanx of chemical bailiffs.  
   •  Communication  
     A lidar scan performed during a survey expedition revealed that the disks flicker in brief, grid-like bursts—binary sheets of brighter and darker vapor.  The pattern repeats every twenty-two minutes, suggesting that the mist is both courier and conduit, passing state information across kilometers of works so the whole hydraulic organ stays tuned.

3.  The Hinge-Folk (sometimes called “Mineralogical Crabs” by field teams)
   •  Morphology  
     Where two paving plates meet under a pivoting valve you may spot a joint broad as a thumbnail flexing once, twice, then going still.  Under magnification it proves to be a layered goethite-hematite laminate shaped like a double-ended spatula, one edge meshed into each plate.  Tens of millions of these rid the terraces of torsion strain.  
   •  Life cycle  
     A stress gradient awakens a hinge.  Iron atoms migrate toward the compression side, growing the lamina there while dissolving it from the tension side—an atomic ratchet that can push neighboring stone back into alignment.  When equilibrium returns the hinge mineralizes solidly and “sleeps,” sometimes for centuries.  New hinges bud from micro-cracks the moment asymmetry exceeds a critical threshold, meaning the very act of erosion begets its own army of structural healers.  
   •  Sensoria  
     Though sub-sentient, the hinges are mechano-receptive; vibrations from a human hammering reverberate through their network in seconds.  Early visitors who tried quarrying were startled when panels kilometers away lifted or sank to cancel the introduced stresses, as though the architecture shrugged.

Relations among the three orders
•  The Slow Glass presents the public face, constantly smoothing and preserving surfaces the hinge-folk have realigned.  
•  The hinge-folk maintain gross stability, giving the glass a geometry worth remembering.  
•  The cipher-mist enforces chemical purity so the other two never face the entropy of lichen, rust, or rot.

Together they form a closed, autopoietic society—mineral, aqueous, atmospheric iterations of the same mandate: keep water moving, keep form immaculate.

What they make of you
A warm body is a thermal and chemical landslide.  The cipher-mist thickens over your shoulders, quietly stripping sweat salts.  The slow glass at your feet pulls minutely away to avoid lipid contamination.  Tensile stresses raised by your climbing pitons awaken thousands of hinge-folk who taste the foreign metal, decide it is not load-bearing stone, and ignore it—for now.  

If you camp too long, the plateau will not rage or expel you.  It will simply begin to erase you—wicking oils off gear, bleaching fabrics in ultraviolet glare, sifting dead skin into dissolution baths.  Within a season every trace of organics goes missing; within a year your tent stakes are nudged outward by thickening glass, their sockets sealed behind them like wounds knitting shut.

So the place is inhabited after all—by glass that thinks slowly, by mist that calculates in photonic flashes, and by iron hinges that wake only when the symphony of stresses demands a chord change.  It does not welcome or forbid; it only continues its recital, older than roots, younger than life, perfect without us.
