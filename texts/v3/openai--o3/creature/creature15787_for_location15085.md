Who lives here?  
The shortest answer is “the Plateau itself,” but that does the place the same injustice as calling a coral reef “a rock with fish.” The uplift is a single living entity only in the way a city is a single settlement: it is one body made of innumerable guilds, castes and opportunists, all grown from the same alphabet of tissue and yet pursuing sharply different careers. Below is a field guide to the residents, from the continental to the microscopic, arranged by the jobs they perform rather than by the strict taxonomies they long ago abandoned.

1. The Supra-Organism: The Host  
Name used by xenobotanists: Primordisc Cortex  
Size: Eurasian; mass fluctuates with hydration state.  
Personality: Slow, tidal, territorial in the way a thunderhead is territorial—accidentally lethal rather than malicious.  
Anatomy in outline:  
• A rind of “cortical crust” (the surface you can stand on).  
• Deep medullary knots that serve as hearts, livers, and brains all at once, pumping mineral sap and sorting chemical messages.  
• A sub-root mantle that spears downward into planetary mantle cracks, harvesting geothermal gradient the way normal plants harvest sunlight.  

Everything else lives either inside this Host, on its skin, or in the atmospheric moat that rings it. Think of the Plateau as the mother whale; everything that follows in its slipstream is calf, barnacle, or pilot fish.

2. The Ten Caste-Organs  
These are not separate species so much as differentiated tissues that behave as if they were. They can break away, merge again, or bud off replacements if mutilated.

2.1 Surge-Hearts  
Job: Hydraulic propulsion.  
Appearance: Swollen, crimson domes twenty-plus kilometres across, pulsing one gargantuan systole per five minutes. When they contract the ground lifts, just a little, and you feel lighter for a breath.  
Notable behaviour: During Dawn Flood the hearts beat in traveling waves that sweep west to east, chasing the planet’s rotation so sap always flows with the sun.

2.2 Lens-Lattice  
Job: Light capture in a perpetual dusk.  
Appearance: Roofs of translucent leaf-film taut as drumheads, kilometres wide, only microns thick. From above they look like soap bubbles pressed edge to edge; from below they throw emerald lacework shadows that crawl through canyons as the sheets tilt.  
Trick: They can concentrate enough short-wave light to spot-cauterise parasites—photosynthesis doubles as a death-ray.

2.3 Mist-Lungs  
Job: Breathing water in and out of the cloud ocean below.  
Appearance: Spiral pits that inhale with a subsonic rumble, then exhale scalding fog laced with terpene anaesthetics (the source of that metallic-sweet air).  
Quirk: They are ringed by frond-tongues that taste the returning vapour, adjusting chemistry so precipitation always arrives with the correct micronutrients.

2.4 Cuticle-Shepherds  
Job: Skin maintenance.  
Appearance: Mobile carpets of hexagonal plates, each plate a femto-factory extruding new wax, pigment and antimicrobial varnish.  
Gait: They march like armies of dinner-plates on edge, clicking; the sound is drowned beneath the thunder but felt through soles.

2.5 Sentinel-Spires  
Job: Telemetry and, when required, combat.  
Appearance: Hollow towers of ligninized foam, 800–1200 m tall, spaced by line-of-sight.  
Senses: Electroreceptors and vibroceptors sensitive enough to “hear” an unshielded drone at 400 km.  
Weapon: Pressurised allelopathic gas that behaves like tear-gas crossed with a computer virus, scrambling nervous systems and firmware alike.

2.6 Seeker-Tendrils  
Job: Exploration, patch repair, territorial expansion.  
Appearance: Cordage the width of subway tunnels that can stiffen to bridge a chasm or soften to ooze through cracks a millimetre wide.  
Behaviour: They learn. A tendril that finds an obstacle broadcasts a chemical “map” so the next generation coils round rather than ramming through.

2.7 Spore-Kites  
Job: Reproduction and long-range reconnaissance.  
Appearance: Silvery delta-wings grown from cellulose microtubules, covered in pigment cells that flip between mirror-bright and obsidian to ride thermal up-drafts.  
Cargo: A seed the size of a grape but genomic libraries 10⁶ times richer than any terrestrial oak. Whether those libraries are ever read beyond this plateau remains unknown.

2.8 Memory-Thylakoids  
Job: Information storage.  
Location: Everywhere chlorophyll exists, but densest in the cortex just below root braid.  
Mechanism: Pigment proteins hold quantum-coherent states for milliseconds—eternity at cellular speeds—allowing error-free signal relays across thousands of kilometres without nerve cells.  
Effect: The Host can remember the pattern of sunrise on a day ten thousand pulses past and adjust today’s hydraulics accordingly.

2.9 Singer-Stomata  
Job: Communication.  
Appearance: Valve-lips lining fissures.  
Sound: A pipe-organ spectrum from infrasound (felt in marrow) to ultrasound (shatters glass at two kilometres).  
Vocabulary: Pressure-encoded syllables that modulate sap flow, instruct cuticle-shepherds, summon combat vines. To human ears the language is wind in organ pipes; to the Plateau it is reasoned speech.

2.10 Combat Vines  
Job: What it says on the label.  
Appearance: Muscular braids sheathed in crystalline thorns that flick on and off like heated filaments.  
Tactics: Constrictor-style for slow threats; harpoon-style for fast ones, launching shards attached to contractile cables.  
Autonomy: A combat vine can live detached for a century, a feral limb wandering the surface like a headless tiger.

3. Symbiotes: Not-Exactly Fauna

3.1 Ferrocyte Algae  
Live in sap; platelets rich in iron that shuttle oxygen and scavenge free radicals. Under a microscope they look exactly like red blood cells designed by a jeweller.

3.2 Gravibacteria  
Nest in root tips. Their metabolism twists local spacetime by picometres, just enough to encourage rubble to subside rather than topple. Every landslide you don’t see is their handiwork.

3.3 Ciliate Seeds  
Liquid-phase young of the Plateau—single cells with flagella—swarm through surface rivulets. They are the only truly motile things here, a diaspora in protozoan form.

3.4 Ladder Mycelia  
Fungal analogues that weave ropey scaffolds between torn tissues. Their hyphae are sacrificial; once the wound closes they autolyse, returning minerals to the sap stream. Think of them as paramedics that dissolve when the patient stands up.

4. Rogue Lineages (The Plateau’s Internal Wildlife)

4.1 Cable Thieves  
Parasitic lianas that tap electrical conduits. The Host tolerates them as we tolerate minor infections—unless they exceed threshold, in which case Combat Vines arrive and the thieves flash-fossilise into glittering resin statues.

4.2 Wraith Forests  
Patches that have mutated chloroplasts into bioluminescent organs, hoarding photons instead of sharing. At night they glow like cities seen from orbit. During day they brood, shading neighbours into starvation if not pruned.

4.3 Reflection Moss  
A heresy of the Lens-Lattice: micro-mirrors that bounce light back toward the sky, signalling who knows what. Some xenobiologists believe the Plateau is talking to satellites that have not yet been launched.

5. Does Any True “Animal” Live Here?  
By local standards, no—nothing with a separate circulatory system, nothing that chews. Motion, hunger, even predation are executed by muscularised plant tissues. Yet if you lowered a microphone to cell scale you would hear flagella beating, ion pumps clicking, plasmodesmata murmuring—busy as any rainforest night. Life is everywhere; it has simply chosen one kingdom and diversified that kingdom until it wears all niches like costumes.

6. The Plateau as Mind  
Take every Surge-Heart pulse as a clock tick, every Lens-Lattice shimmer as a retinal flash, every Memory-Thylakoid state change as a neuron firing, and you have a brain the size of a continent ticking in vegetal time. The plateau does not think quickly, but it thinks deeply, in recursive loops of growth and self-sculpture that no nervous creature could emulate. To visit is to trespass upon a thought still forming.

7. Encounter Protocol, Should You Meet the Inhabitants  
• Do not stab the ground. The Host feels puncture the way you feel dentistry without anaesthetic.  
• Stay clear of Sentinel-Spires during thunder pulses; their infrasound syllables can collapse human lungs.  
• If the air suddenly tastes of over-ripe apricot, evacuate. A Combat Vine has taken an interest.  
• Most importantly, remember that every surface is alive. You are walking on someone’s skin, drinking someone’s blood-equivalent, breathing another’s sighs. Act accordingly.

Those are the residents. There are no shepherds, no livestock, no pedestrians—only organ after organ, each with its own tempo, all harmonised into the roaring, dripping, light-catching being that is the Plateau. To stand here is to stand inside a single, colossal creature and feel it dreaming around you.
