Who, then, is alive on the pocket plateau?

1. The Verdigris Conduits (what you first took for “plants”)

   Appearance  
   • Height: one to four meters.  
   • Stem: a bundle of hollow rods, each turquoise-brown like oxidized copper wire.  
   • Leaves: fan-shaped shingles of green-gold mica, hinged so they clatter in slow breezes.  
   • Veins: glassy tubes thick as fuse wire, pulsing with a syrupy, transparent fluid shot through by motes of red-hot metal.  

   Internal economy  
   Photosynthesis is useless here; the noon never softens and water is myth. Instead the Conduits eat heat and the basalt’s trace alloys. Their “roots” are boron-rich needles that slide downward until they tap a live fracture. There they become miniature smelters.  
   • Silicates are cracked for oxygen.  
   • Iron, nickel, vanadium are chelated, ferried upward and plated into new tissue.  
   • Excess heat is vented through the stem’s side-ports in those piston-like sighs you hear.  

   Growth laws  
   Every right angle, every mathematically perfect surface is a banquet: touch it and the line’s stored perfection liquefies into more Conduit mass. This is why the plateau’s edges stay pristine until a frond brushes them, after which an emerald rash of new shoots appears overnight (or what passes for night here).  

   Reproduction  
   When a cluster exhausts its mineral seam, the highest stem shears free along a crystalline fault. The severed piece is aerodynamically clever: mica leaves furl into a rigid V, creating a spinning autorotating seed. It can glide kilometers before spearing a lower, hotter crack. A plateau after a maturation pulse looks as if someone has fired a slow rain of tiny copper javelins into the slopes below.  

   Intelligence?  
   Each colony maintains a standing temperature gradient of exactly 137 kelvins between root and crown. That number is not imposed by physics; it is a choice, held with feedback precision. Colonies that slip out of tolerance self-dismantle. No one knows why the gradient matters, only that it is memorized generation after generation like a word in a language not yet spoken.

2. The Resonant Vents (the “lungs” of the enclave)

   They look like basalt blowholes rimmed by hematite. In truth they are living organs grown downward from the Conduit forests until they fuse with subterranean gas pockets. Every five minutes (measured outside the plateau; inside, time crawls) a vent inhales through capillary pores, filtering volatile metals, then exhales a gauged volume of super-dry CO₂ and neon. The pulse sets the beat that synchronizes every other resident.

   A vent can die. When one clogs with glassy slag, nearby Conduits converge, drill into it, leach out useful metals, and finally knit the remaining shell into new root supports. In old age a vent becomes a fossilized, harp-shaped basalt organ gleaming with copper sutures.

3. Mica-Skin Spores (the small drift you thought was just dust)

   Diameter: 40–80 microns.  
   Composition: alternating layers of potassium feldspar and flexible graphene-like carbon lattices.  
   Function: mobile repair crews. They float on the thermal shimmer until they encounter a hairline crack in either glass or living tissue. There they unpack into dendritic filaments that weld the flaw shut with silica glue, then refold and lift off again. They are not made by the Conduits; they are shed by…

4. The Plateau Itself (a lithic holobiont)

   Think of the entire shelf of rock, vents, glass and vegetation as the larval stage of a single organism: a geobiologic printer drafting its own body.  
   • Heart: a slow-beating magma lens two kilometers below, cycling metal-rich melt in loops that match the vent rhythm.  
   • Skin: the vitreous crust that flashes, cools, then is re-molten in spots to erase impurities.  
   • Immune system: the Mica-Skin Spores, patching defects and scavenging any foreign water molecules that wander in.  
   • Nervous system: those razor-straight seams that ring like struck glass when stressed; vibrations transmit signals in minutes across the whole shelf.  

   Purpose (so far as one can infer)  
   The plateau is practicing. It is learning to maintain form in a universe where erosion, weather and biology usually erase straight lines. Everything you see—Conduit blooms, vent breathing, glass fusion—is rehearsal for holding shape against entropy. Once the rehearsal is perfect, the mountain may choose to finish manifesting.

5. The Recursive Gatekeepers (who live in the doorway-within-doorway slit)

   Under normal viewing they are invisible; you meet only the glitter of mica left on your skin. But instruments dropped through the slit record momentary spikes of coherent terahertz radiation—structured noise like a whispered checkpoint protocol. Most researchers think the Gatekeepers are standing waves of ordered heat, intelligent only in the sense that crystals are: they extend and prune anything that crosses the threshold, copying what is useful, discarding the rest as scintillant dust. Their diet is novelty. Present them with a geometry they have not archived and they let the donor pass unscathed; repeat yourself and you are sanded down to feed the Conduits.

6. Taboos and visitors

   The chemistry bans water-based life, so nothing from the wetter world can settle long. A bird blown off course would flash-dehydrate into a soft ceramic husk before it could flap twice. Yet pilgrims do arrive—heat-hungry silicon lichens rafting in on stratospheric winds, rogue nanomaterials drifting from orbital foundries, the occasional human in a pressure suit. None stay. The plateau does not kill them; it simply edits them down until all the wet, inaccurate parts are gone, leaving a handful of angular relics that the wind carries away.

In summary

What lives here is an ecology of precision. Its members are not plants, animals or machines but syllables in a thesis on how matter might say “no” to decay:

• Verdigris Conduits: metallovores that farm straight edges.  
• Resonant Vents: communal bellows that pace every process.  
• Mica-Skin Spores: omnipresent micromechanics.  
• The Plateau Holobiont: mountain-in-draft, rehearsing permanence.  
• Recursive Gatekeepers: editors enforcing novelty and exactness.

Together they breathe, smelt, weld and prune, perfecting themselves molecule by molecule until the day erosion is finally granted access and the real contest—the one against the ordinary passage of time—can honorably begin.
