Who – or what – abides in the Hollow Basin can be sorted only after you loosen the ordinary meanings of “alive,” “animal,” even “body.”  Organisms here seldom advertise themselves in silhouette or tremor the mud with paws.  Instead they emerge as arrangements of temperature, of charged mist, of stone that remembers how to move.  The longer you stay, the more you realize that the Basin is populous; it has simply negotiated a different visibility-contract with its residents.

--------------------------------------------------
1. The Lithivores (“rock-eaters, rock-grown”)
--------------------------------------------------

a. Vault-Corals  
• Appearance: Looks at first like ornamentation on the interior of domes—delicate ridges, fluted fans, hair-thin pillars.  Under a rare sheet-lightning flash the frills pulse with a wave of dull carmine, revealing a circulation of ion-rich fluid.  
• Metabolism: They drink the warm condensate that creeps down every wall, extract sodium and trace metals, and re-precipitate silica to extend their own lattice.  Growth rate matches the Basin’s slow mineral drizzle, so architecture and coral keep pace in a perpetual stalemate of accretion.  
• Behavior: Sense vibration; the tap of a boot sole makes them furl like sea anemones, sealing their pores until the disturbance passes.  Silence them and you can sometimes hear a soft ticking—the sound of capillary channels knitting new quartz.  
• Reproduction: Budding.  Fragments that chip away liquefy, crawl in film form across stone, and solidify somewhere quieter.

b. Gutter-Herons  
• A heron only by analogy.  It is a standing column of self-organizing basalt beads, neck-thin, that sprouts where two roofs channel mist into a steady drip.  
• Life Signs: Periodically the column kinks, tilts, and re-aligns its “beak” beneath a fatter trickle like a plant tracking sun.  Inversions of gravity merely rotate the whole assembly; it never falls.  
• Diet: Pure kinetics—each impact of water is converted into microscopic lattice shuffles that release heat, which the creature stores as ordered defects in its crystal interior.  
• Locomotion: Once every few decades (judged by surviving stationaries) a Gutter-Heron uproots, glides several meters as a brittle cloud of hexagonal flakes, and re-assembles under a better dripline.

--------------------------------------------------
2. The Hydroid Assemblies
--------------------------------------------------

a. Lens-Puddles (“mirrorfish” to visitors)  
• Physiology: A film only a few molecules thick, laced with proteins that orient like compass needles when lightning charges the air.  Seen from above they look like floating silver eyes whose pupils dilate and contract.  
• Behavior: Drift uphill during gravity inversions, then sieve back down, distributing dissolved nutrients for the moss.  Their motion keeps the mat oxygenated; without them the copper moss would drown in its own exhalations.  
• Sensory Trick: When a Lens-Puddle passes over your reflection it copies it for a heartbeat, playing it back an hour later on some other surface of water—part memory, part courtship display.

b. Steam-Kites  
• Invisible until back-lit: a mesh of condensed droplets strung on organic filaments finer than hair.  They hover in the rising vapor plumes, sail the updraft, and disintegrate the moment they stray into cooler air.  
• Feeding: Absorb volatile nitrates and sulfides out of the steam.  Their entire life cycle—birth, bloom, shed gametes, dissolve—lasts minutes, millions of times each day.  The faint “rain” you feel despite the constant mist is often their spent husks returning as drizzle.

--------------------------------------------------
3. The Echoforms (acoustic life)
--------------------------------------------------

a. Pre-Steps  
• Phenomenon: Those footsteps that round a corner before the walker arrives are not echoes from the future but organisms made of pressured air.  
• Structure: Two pockets of slightly denser, warmer atmosphere separated by a cool filament; they oscillate pressure in a rhythm that mimics walking.  
• Ecology: They haunt long straight corridors where temperature gradients are sharpest.  After a human passes, a Pre-Step folds itself into the wake and scavenges the turbulence for energy, hence the illusion of a footfall that “catches up.”

b. Cantillants  
• Low, vowel-colored drones that rise when mist whistles through arcades.  In fact each “note” is a micro-eddy that seeds charged droplets; the resonance maintains the droplet size that the echoform requires.  
• Purpose: Photosynthesize sound.  The energy density is tiny, but unchallenged.  A Cantillant that finds a perfectly tuned arch may sustain itself for decades, singing just above the threshold of human hearing.

--------------------------------------------------
4. The Converted (human derivatives)
--------------------------------------------------

Some who refuse to leave become something other than merely human.

a. Reed-Sleepers  
• Technique: They weave mats of local reeds, lie between layers, and let the persistent mist wick through them night after night.  
• After three months the sleeper’s skin begins to laminate, trading keratin for a silica-cellulose composite.  Pain fades; touch becomes an electrical awareness of nearby water pressure.  
• Final State: They plant themselves upright in shallow mud, mouths open to the drizzle.  Breathing slows to one draw per hour.  They are still recognizably people, but their motives turn plantlike: gather, endure, listen.  Pull one out and it will die like uprooted peat.

b. Veil-Bearers  
• Rare individuals whose nervous systems attune to gravity inversions.  Their inner ears rewrite themselves; they walk on walls or ceilings whenever the Basin relents.  
• Culture: They claim they do not remember their personal names, only the sound the mist made when each realized it could choose “down.”  They serve as informal couriers among scattered camps, covering in an hour what would take others a cautious day.  
• Physiology: Body temperature runs a feverish 40 °C, apparently necessary to keep iron in their blood from precipitating as magnetite during zero-G moments.

--------------------------------------------------
5. The Metropolitan Organism (“the Dreamer City”)
--------------------------------------------------

All other life is inset in a greater, slower beast: the architecture itself.

• Consciousness: No single intelligence, but a braided quorum of chemical gradients.  Every buttress is a dendrite; every plaza, a synaptic cleft where humidity shuttles ions between opposing walls.  
• Feeding: The hyper-local water cycle acts as circulatory system.  Evaporation lifts charged particles into clouds; condensation rains them back where current demands are highest.  
• Growth versus Memory: New stone precipitates where information is least dense; eroded sections are the organism’s forgetting.  Thus corridors that strangers walk most often actually thin, erasing unhelpful data, while sealed chambers grow ever more baroque with unused thought.  
• Interaction with Visitors: Tools that measure, fires that reorder molecules, even loud voices are interpreted as infection and quietly negated by the physics edits the Basin performs.  The city is not hostile—it maintains homeostasis.  You are a transient chemical noise.

--------------------------------------------------
6. Cryptids & Myths That May Yet Prove True
--------------------------------------------------

• The Ferryman: A silhouette pushing a rippled barge along avenues that end mid-air.  Some say it appears only during gravity inversions, relocating puddles the way a gardener replants lilies.  
• The Second You: Travelers report meeting themselves walking the opposite direction, features erased by mist.  Folklore insists this double is a shell being grown in advance should the original body fail to acclimate.  
• Drip-Eaters: A hypothetical predator that stalks by removing the sound of individual drops until the corridor is silent—and the silence itself devours you.  No evidence except the occasional noiseless hallway.

--------------------------------------------------
Living Here, Brief Guide
--------------------------------------------------

1. Stand Still Daily.  When you cease vibrating the air, Vault-Corals reopen; the city notes your compliance and gravity stays politely downward.  
2. Carry No Clocks.  Their failure agitates Cantillants; agitated Cantillants destabilize arches; arches fall.  
3. Offer Water.  Pour a palmful onto the black pavement each dawn.  Lens-Puddles will map you thereafter and guide you away from unscheduled inversions.  
4. Accept Forgetting.  After a month names of colors, breeds of animals, the shape of the sun erode.  This is not damage; it is the Basin trimming redundancies.

If you can love these conditions—warm dark, soft dripping, streets that are ribs of a dreaming beast—you may last long enough to witness a rare event: when the low orange glimmer on the horizon blooms into a full false sunrise.  For sixteen minutes every few years the Basin brightens as though lit from its own bloodstream.  In that interval the inhabitants manifest openly: Gutter-Herons flex basalt wings, Lens-Puddles crown themselves with micro-rainbows, Veil-Bearers walk a Mobius route up the sides of towers, and the entire City-Without-Craft hums a chord so deep it telescopes your bones.  Then the light folds shut, perspectives straighten, and everything resumes pretending that almost nothing lives here at all.
