What Lives in the Hollow Nursery

1. The Arches (or “The First Sleeper”)
   • Classification: Colonial vascular super-organism; somewhere between banyan, lungfish and slow volcano.  
   • Apparent life-cycle: Five observable stages—breath, swell, sweat, dream, contract—repeating on a thirty-seven-minute loop that scholars insist is a single drawn-out heartbeat.  
   • Cognition: No language, yet unmistakably responsive. A hammer-tap brings a clot of resin exactly jar-sized; a lullaby hummed against its bark deadens the space-dilation for as long as the tune persists; a knife wound always seals but never twice with the same grain, as though composing answers in wood.  
   • Goal (as guessed by the Nurserymen): Gestation. The Arches is busy manufacturing a seed big enough to mistake for weather. Until the seed is done, the plant keeps the basin in a soft, probationary reality where nothing can quite break.

2. The Nurserymen
   • Number: Seventeen permanent souls, three transients, an uncounted quota of stillbirth ideas that sometimes borrow bodies for a week.  
   • Ancestry: Human, though touched by long residence. Skin shows dendritic capillaries that glow when the fungus blooms. Finger-pads can taste pH after years of clay-work.  
   • Social shape: Not a village, more a guild of caretakers. They do not marry or form households; instead, everyone “commutes” between four communal lofts, trading bunks the way gardeners turn soil—so no relationship ever compacts too tightly and cracks.  
   • Work:  
     – Chorus-breathing to cue the Arches’ contractions when the natural rhythm slips.  
     – Grazing the ungulate herds along predetermined figure-eights so predation stays numerically polite.  
     – Walking the iron with sticks of chalk, rewriting geometry whenever a new shortcut blooms.  
   • Names: Taken from the first sensory surprise of a shift. Hence you meet Copper-Tongue, Ozone-Edge, or the twins who arrived during a silent rain and will answer only to Damp and Echo.  
   • Recreation: Scribing phonograph cylinders in resin—music, weather reports, arguments, advice to selves in possible tomorrows. Every cylinder is readable forward and reverse; half the scores are meant for ears that haven’t happened.

3. Lumen Elk (the “Ungulates no taller than a boot”)
   • Physiology: Glass-teal hooves, chlorophyll-etched bones, hides that act like dim lantern leather—bright when calm, dark when startled.  
   • Diet: Phosphor lichen and the coppery condensation that sheets the inner trunks; they lick metal as salt.  
   • Reproduction: Rain-timed. A downpour coerces the does into estrus fifteen minutes after cessation, while the bucks’ antlers sprout micro-fronds that broadcast pheromones on the same clock.  
   • Temperament: All nervousness, yet possessed of a map-maker’s memory; they refuse to walk paths that will soon fold or elongate. The Nurserymen watch hoof-prints as early warnings.

4. Petal-Bats (Order: Chiroptera-Flora hybridis)
   • Wing anatomy: Five fleshy sepals sleeved over carbon-lattice veins, folding like mourning flowers in light.  
   • Echoflora: Instead of ultrasound, they emit grains of pollen that burst into micro-sparks on impact, painting the dark with pointillist sonar. The sparks linger seconds, enough for observers to glimpse the negative of things.  
   • Mutualism: The bats drink condensate; in return, the Arches harvests any sap they exhale back as aerosol, keeping its inner chambers sterile.

5. Shroud Cats (the subtraction predators)
   • Visibility: Almost nil. Their fur is a fiber optic mat that hijacks the basin’s already meagre light and sends it skimming elsewhere. Rarely glimpsed except as a sag in the curtain of drizzle.  
   • Hunting style: They stalk probability rather than prey. When a lumen elk hesitates at a junction, a cat commits to the version of the world in which the elk chose wrongly. An instant splice, and there is mouth on throat, history edited.  
   • Waste: None. Bones, light, even the displaced liter of air vanish into whatever backstage they haunt. Only a warm indentation and the echo of hooves remain.

6. Glass Spinners
   • Body plan: Eight legs, every joint a polished hinge of natural silica; abdomen is a silvered orb that shows the viewer from behind.  
   • Web: A tessellation of retro-reflective threads. When two reflectors overlap, they cancel incident photons—creating holes in sight. The Spinners weave these lacunae as gifts for big things that dislike being watched, most notably the Shroud Cats.  
   • Economy: Paid in bugs—specifically, small clockwork “gnats” the Nurserymen build from scrap copper coils. The spiders bleed the gnat’s tiny batteries for warmth during cold pulses.

7. The Spoor Choir (a fungus with ambitions)
   • Habitat: Sods of porous sponge that cling to undersides of walkways like dropped loaves of bread.  
   • Communication: Each mat can ring like a wet bell when slapped by rain. Separate clumps sync until the whole basin tolls a diaphragm-deep bass note that scares geometry straight for minutes at a time.  
   • Agenda: Unknown, but the Choir has learned to tune its resonance so the Arches exhales more resin. Nurserymen suspect the mats are building themselves a hard, portable reincarnation.

8. Machine-Lichens
   • Description: Green-streaked patinas that sprout copper whiskers along iron catwalks.  
   • Function: They digest rust into conductive filaments, patching the electrical grid without instruction. Over decades the grid has become less mechanical, more vascular, pulsing in sympathy with the Arches.  
   • Sentience debate: A voltmeter left clamped overnight always reads an integer prime at dawn, never composite. Some call that coincidence; some call it mind.

9. Transient Visitors
   • The Ruptured: Weather-beaten travelers who stumble over the rim and spend a night thinking they have found a ruin of their own culture. By morning most slip back outside and re-enter somewhere else, cured of certain memories they cannot name.  
   • The Apprentices: Children from the delta villages beyond the delta—if “beyond” indeed exists—sent to learn calm. They stay a season, grow second and third shadows (a side effect of the dilation field), then depart able to prune fear from livestock with a gesture.  
   • The Auditor: Seen once every two years, wearing a coat lined with ticking metronomes. They pace the perimeter, note how long light takes to cross a handspan, then nod as if the Nursery has paid its rent in full.

10. Things Still Forming
    • Egg Weather: Once in a blue cloud the sky drips not water but small porous spheres that wriggle until buried in clay. Nothing yet has hatched, but the Nurserymen keep a ceremonial omelette pan ready.  
    • The Counter-Clock: Rumoured device nested somewhere in the canopy, built to measure backward seconds. It is said to be assembling its own operator out of borrowed moments, one reverse instant at a time.  
    • You: Anyone lingering more than a lunar cycle gains faint pinholes along the wrists that leak personal chronology. Eventually the basin may regard you as a native process and schedule you into the rain.

In Sum
The Hollow Nursery is less an ecosystem than a parliament of half-chosen life-paths, each resident a draft version of something grander. Nothing is final; everything rehearses. Plants rehearse lungs, animals rehearse invisibility, machines rehearse thought, people rehearse quiet stewardship. Even the predators practice mercy by deleting without gore. All those lives together maintain the basin’s singular mandate: keep the world tentative, keep the future in soft clay, until the First Sleeper’s seed decides what shape tomorrow should be.
