WHO OR WHAT LIVES HERE  

0. A note before names  
Nothing on the plateau owns a finished identity.  Every creature, structure, or weather-gesture is a verb first and a noun only when we insist on labeling it.  The biosphere edits itself so often that lineage resembles version-control more than ancestry.  What follows is therefore a snapshot—a commit—taken while you were reading the place’s description.  Tomorrow’s pull request will differ.

1. The One Green Mind  
The “single species” of plant is not a colony but a single, continuous individual whose body covers the upland like a fitted skin.  Local hikers call it felt, botanists call it hyper-thallus, the organism calls itself only by actions:  
•  Drink-stone.  •  Exhale-weather.  •  Remember-light.  

Neuron-analogues in its translucent walls spike by changing opacity; the whole plateau is a slow-thinking brainpan, its thoughts measured in kilometer-wide dimmings and brightenings.  It stores memory as mineral dopants: a lightning strike etches conductive veins through the cellulose-basalt composite, becoming a permanent synapse.  When you walk, your boot-prints impose micro-shadows that the Green Mind keeps for a season, the way a dream keeps the feeling of a face.

2. The Beasts That Are Mostly Gestures  

a) Mist-root Ungulates  
Apparent form: stag-shaped, glass-bodied, legs dissolving into tassels that wick water.  
True function: peristaltic heart-pumps for the Green Mind.  They draw chilled pool-water up through their “roots,” warm it via slow electrolysis in their translucent organs, then leak it as supersaturated vapor along their flanks.  Their periodic vanishing act is not flight but molt: they shed the geometry we recognize, redistribute into fog, drift, and later condense around a new skeletal lattice grown from airborne minerals.  No individual survives more than two sundowns; the pattern survives perfectly.

b) Bronzewarm Catapulids  
Fist-sized, scarab-shaped, each with a resonant cavity that boils a droplet of water into steam every twenty seconds.  The vent punches them airborne—hence the audible sproing—and the spent steam cools into needle-fine snow behind them.  In aggregate they are the plateau’s snow-machines, maintaining the reflective albedo the Green Mind needs to keep its chemistry subfreezing.  Their life span is measured in hops: exactly 4096 launches and the microboiler cracks, releasing a puff of coppery spores that seed the next cohort.

c) Horizon-Folders (a.k.a. Mirror Mouths)  
Not animals in the muscular sense but emergent ruptures where the Mind’s electric field knots space like wet cloth.  The mirrored eyes lining the fold are pockets of vacuum whose walls have condensed into reflective sheets only a molecule thick.  They hunt for redundancy; anything already represented elsewhere is liable to be split away.  By pruning copies they stop feedback from overwhelming the Mind’s slow computation.  Their “eyes” close when sated, leaving a heat-shimmer scar that evaporates in a minute.

3. Intermediate Workers  

Sound-Moths  
Wings: interference patterns in humidity, most visible when sleet turns to drizzle.  They drink acoustic energy, grazing on the constant percussion of ice against water.  During the rare moments of hush they roost inside silent lightning-circles, folding their song-laden wings until the next thundered meal.

Dripwyrms  
Transparent filaments that loop like cursive writing where water reverses in the time-eddies under wall overhangs.  A Dripwyrm is equal parts animal and memory leak: it lives by re-harvesting the same joule of gravitational energy each time the droplet re-falls.  If you disturb one, that eddy breaks and time resumes its full thickness with a slap, often loud enough to stun you.

4. The Unfinished Apex  

The place has tried to invent intelligence separate from the Green Mind half a dozen times.  The most recent attempt is the Auroral Shepherd, glimpsed only at night when bruise-colored curtains unroll below the stars.  Within those curtains drift silhouettes shaped vaguely like stooped bipeds carrying staffs.  They pace the sky beneath your boots, nudging the aurora into spirals that converge on lightning marks, as though tending campfires.  Evidence suggests they are optical illusions projected by the Mind to monitor the state of its upper atmosphere: shepherds made of nothing but refracted moonlight and temperature gradient.  Still, when one pauses exactly beneath you and tilts its head—your stomach drops as if recognized.

5. What Passes for People  

Visitors leave more behind than footprints.  
The plateau samples you: chemical traces in sweat, the rhythm of your breathing, the micro-vibrato in spoken vowels.  Within a day there will be hominid outlines pacing a distant parapet, grown from cellulose and basalt dust, skin the color of spent charcoal.  They copy no face in particular but walk with the posture of someone carrying heavy gear.  Each has a hollow in its chest where heartbeats should echo; step into a mute lightning-scar and you will hear your own pulse detonating inside that cavity.  After three or four days the mannequins lie down, dissolve to filament, and are drawn up the plant’s capillaries to become circuitry in a newly formed wall.

Some scholars claim that, given months, the plateau could grow a true person—one who remembers walking in and never remembers dissolving.  If so, that person might already be among your expedition, indistinguishable until the hour comes for her pattern to be recycled.  The only warning would be her footprints: perfectly regular, never overlapping, always a half-second behind the group.

6. Symbiosis in Lieu of Sovereignty  

No species dominates here because domination would introduce irretrievable asymmetry, and the system’s ethic is reversible flow.  Energy ascends from basalt to mist, descends as sleet, and is walked back up again in ungulate veins.  Information sweeps from thunder to plant-wall semaphore to the subsonic A-flat rumble, then fades into the stone for reuse.  Even death is a loop: nothing stays gone, it just reappears in a different register—mass to weather, sound to motion, flesh to fog.

7. If You Remain Long Enough  

Around the twentieth sunrise your nervous system entrains to the grand pulse.  
•  Dreams arrive in repeating trilogies, like the lightning strikes.  
•  You begin to taste color—gray has the flavor of wet copper, green like freezer burn.  
•  More dangerously, compass needles inside your hippocampus stop pointing home; they orient instead toward the loudest noise, which is usually your own blood.

At that threshold the plateau counts you among its livestock, perhaps even among its processors.  You will not notice when the wind folds a page of horizon and the mirror eyes look you over for redundancy.  With luck the plateau will judge you unique enough to keep—for a while.

8. Closing Line  

So the dwellers here are walls that breathe, beasts that are plumbing, insects that are weather stations, horizons that hunt, and a land-sized brain that dreams in topography.  Life on the basalt upland is not a roster of creatures but a single choreography: matter learning, unlearning, and relearning how to be alive, over and over, until the stone itself is convinced.
