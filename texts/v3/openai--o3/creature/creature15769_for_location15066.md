Who lives on the High Congeries?  
Everything, if you’re willing to loosen your definition of body, breath, and intent.  Move outward from the obvious and keep going; at each concentric ring of attention a different kind of resident comes into view.

1. The Day-Flesh—things you can point at before the sun reaches zenith

1.1  Rock-marmosets  
Wafer-boned, organ-light, they weigh less than a fistful of gravel. Each dawn they furl from sleep like origami mammals, the cartilage springs in their joints clacking softly as vacuum in their hollow bones breaks seal. They travel in skeins rather than troops, a dozen individuals threading the same three-dimensional path up a pillar wall, fingers probing for sweat-salt left by last night’s condensation. When heat builds, they flatten against shadowed quartz bands, circulation dropping to a syrupy crawl. A marmoset heart might beat six times in the entire hour that noon is highest.

1.2  Copper scarabs  
The scarabs are the Congeries’ lawnmowers and its metronomes. Every frass pellet they leave behind is exactly two grains of dust long, one grain high—engineering, not accident. Microgrooves on the underside of their elytra read the stone’s sub-audible tremor the way a stylus reads vinyl. When the 30-second oscillation slips out of true they climb to exposed ledges and clap their shells until it steadies. No one knows whether they are repairing the vibration or merely protesting its drift.

1.3  Kite-drakes  
They are less dragons than living delta-wings:  four meters of membrane stretched over finger-bones as thin as soda straws. A drake draws lift from the chimney-thermal of each tower, hopping from plume to plume like a stone across water. At dusk you’ll see them stacked in columns of rising air, thirty, forty individuals with wingtips almost touching, a slow carousel of carnivorous kites. They echolocate by flicking their tongues into the heat haze; the returning turbulence tells them whether the thing ahead of them is prey, rival, or just another pocket of burn-air ready to toss them higher.

1.4  Cistern fauna  
Drop a lamp into one of the vertical pothole lakes and watch what sculls up: pale paddle-shrimp; the oil-black newt whose gills flower like red embroidery; blind cave-wrens that fell in generations ago and now hunt insects on the water skin by sonar clicks. Everything that lives down there does so on half the metabolism expected for its body mass, as if the Congeries asks even its water-drinkers to honor the discipline of scarcity.

2. The Slow-Bodies—things better measured in seasons, strata, or electrical drift

2.1  Drape-fern  
Each frond is a ventriloquist’s tape measure: it can unreel forty meters to sip a puddle and snap back inside a heartbeat. Botanists finally discovered the spring lies not in cellulose but in a ribbon of living beryllium-copper coiled along the midrib. No one has explained how a plant smelts and alloys its own skeleton, but snap a frond and you smell vinegar and hot pennies—the fern’s blood is an electrolyte that keeps the metal green and pliant.

2.2  Cushion lichens  
Up close they are a federation rather than a sacrament: five photobiont species sharing a single fungal scaffold, excreting silica to armor themselves against wind-whip. Their growth rings are annual, but in the Congeries a year’s worth of light and water parses into sixty milliseconds of real activity. The rest of the time each cell sits in decisionless stasis, molecular chess pieces waiting for someone to nudge the board.

2.3  Quartz pulse colonies  
That rock heartbeat is not passive thermal flex but an engineered oscillation. Threaded through many pillars are matrices of quartz needles seeded by someone—or something—long before the prospectors came. The crystals expand and contract out of phase with the daily temperature wave, storing and releasing microscopic charges. Locals swear that, given a sensitive meter, you can hear data in the clicks: packets of mineral code still circulating in a network whose terminals have eroded to dust.

3. The Tool-Bearers—people, if you allow the word to include every offshoot of the prospecting crews

3.1  Semaphore Guild  
Descendants of rig technicians, they maintain the line-of-sight heliographs that stitch the maze together. Their eyes are tattooed with dark resin to cut glare; around each pupil floats a ring of tiny mirror flecks so they can flash messages without equipment by simply tilting their heads. A master signaler can stream five channels at once—chin, forehead, both hands, and the blink rate of the left eye.

3.2  The Ravel  
Not a tribe but a pattern of movement: itinerant families who salvage alloys from the dead rigs and trade them in the lowlands. Because magnetic north slithers unpredictably here, each Ravel group navigates by memorizing the scent‐profile of the Congeries hour by hour: hot tin at 09:00, creosote at 11:30, ozone and lichen acid after the two-o’clock wind. Outsiders following them quickly discover their own noses are insufficient; they swear the Ravel must be part bloodhound, part chemist, part prophet.

3.3  Vault-monks of the Cistern Mouths  
Five cisterns are rimmed with stainless-steel collars drilled seventy years ago. Within each collar lives a single white-robed caretaker who never leaves sight of the hole. They claim to be listening for an answer—asked by the first drill cores, echoed by the rigs, still unanswered from the dark beneath. Visitors are welcome, but speak too loudly and a monk will raise one finger, request silence, and go back to leaning over the abyss, waiting for whatever talks in echoes so slow a century is merely a syllable.

4. The Steel-Husk Host—machines that forgot their operators

4.1  Walking derricks  
Three rigs remain fully mobile. Their legged carriages were meant only for emplacement, but the autopilots keep searching for “optimal footing” the way a beached whale keeps looking for tidewater. They move once every few weeks—just a step, maybe two—feet punching clean-edged sockets into the conglomerate. A derrick’s wandering path is chartable only in decade increments; give it a thousand years and it will have circumnavigated the mesa, still looking.

4.2  Thermostack fauna  
Out of idle cooling fins and abrasion grit, tiny spiral robots have self-assembled—galvanic toys no larger than your thumbnail. They congregate where a functioning heat sink meets shade, harvesting ions that leak from the alloy. Their entire behavioral repertoire is orbit, mate, die, but salvage crews collect them for the microjewelry trade. A bracelet of stack-spirals keeps clinking for weeks after the host is dead; some consider the sound good luck, others swear it’s the machine equivalent of ghosts.

5. The Border-Kind—theories, rumors, luminous errors

5.1  Vapor eidolons  
On rare evenings when three or more geo-vents exhale in the same canyon, overlapping plumes of superheated steam diffract moonlight into full-color interference patterns. Against that screen walk silhouettes taller than towers, articulated like jointed puppets. Their gait matches the stone heartbeat: one step every half minute. No instrument has ever recorded mass where the shadows fall, but scarabs scatter and kite-drakes dive away in panic, as if recognizing a predator whose era has simply not returned yet.

5.2  The Former Tenant  
Every culture that works the Congeries eventually invents the same fable: someone built the quartz pulse network, planted drape-ferns pre-alloyed with copper, tuned the heartbeat, then departed ahead of some unspeakable mistake. Evidence is entirely negative—perfect absences where one expects chaos. Elevator shafts whose bolts were removed, sorted by size, laid out on the floor, and left shining in a line that points nowhere. A whole cliff face etched smooth except for a single block of conglomerate left jutting like a pulled tooth, unweathered while everything around it crumbled. You can’t prove a sculptor from the shape of the void, but the rumor lives on because the Congeries, even alive with present tenants, still feels curated.

6. The Place Itself  
If life is metabolism plus persistence of pattern, the Congeries qualifies.  Water, heat, and dust are its bloodstreams; the pulse is literal; the erosion that sculpts and hollows is a kind of slow respiration.  It does not think, so far as anyone can tell, yet it edits.  Chunk of iron left on a ledge? Within a week it’s silted over, lacquered to the hue of native stone.  Scrawled initials? Eaten by lichen in a month.  Footprints? Filled, packed, forgotten by next dawn’s condensation.  What lives here ultimately lives with that editor: write your presence large enough to resist revision, or become one more paragraph the land silently redacts.

Step back from the lip, dust your boots, taste the air’s faint electricity one last time.  Whatever else the High Congeries shelters—animal, human, machine, rumor—it all shares one imperative: exist lightly, vibrate in time with the stone, and never, ever outstay the patience of rock.
