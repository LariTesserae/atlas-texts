Who (and what) endures The Approach  

0. A caution  
Nothing here is “wildlife” in the safe, documentary sense. Every resident is either an engine that happens to be alive or a life-form that behaves like infrastructure. Most cannot be removed without the landscape noticing and retaliating.

1. The Matrix Folk – organisms you will not see, only feel  
1.1 Ferroplankton  
• Size: virus to pollen grain.  
• Bodies: hollow polyhedra of iron-nickel alloy plated in chlorophyll.  
• Habit: drift in the viscous water, absorbing light through any face that presently points sunward. Each photon captured adds an electron to the hull; charges migrate until a cell carries ±30 kV.  
• Ecological role: static weather. Billions rising in convection pillars create the padded-hammer tremors that shake vessels on approach. When pillars collapse the plankton flash-discharge, generating the Morse-code sky-light no sensor can predict.  
• Sentience: none singly; weakly emergent in storms. They compose the place’s “background hum of thought,” the way neurons compose yours.

1.2 Mat-Coral (already noted, but understand its motive)  
• What looks like moss is in fact a distributed nervous system. Every polyp owns a fragment of a cellular automaton; together they simulate environmental futures several minutes ahead. The predictions steer water flow, root-turbine RPM, and vine-conduit pumping schedules. Remove a square centimetre and local flood-timing goes haywire for hours. The plain protects its scheduler violently.

2. The Audible Walkers – creatures big enough to give names  
2.1 Stilt-Channelers  
• Architecture more than animal: imagine a four-legged water tower grown from jointed lignin and basalt.  
• Height: 12–18 m; mass: 200 t, 40 % of which is circulating fluid.  
• Behaviour: They stride the flooded rooms, stamping gaps in ridge walls to redirect flash-floods toward nutrient-poor sectors. Each footfall melts obsidian with a piezoelectric arc, then quenches it with its own stored water, welding dams behind them.  
• Nutrition: photosynthetic skin panels by day; at night they plug tapered proboscides into vine-conduits and share power in both directions like peer-to-peer batteries.  
• Reproduction: When two meet they kneel knee-deep, mesh latticework hips, and print a third, smaller frame between them using mineral slurry pumped from their legs. Think copier, not romance.

2.2 Faraday Eels  
• Neither fish nor animal: they are loops of superconductive slime filament a kilometre long, folded into knots that swim by unknotting at one end while tying at the other.  
• Glow: indigo sheath corona that repels both water and debris, letting them skim at 120 km h-1 through knee-deep basins.  
• Diet: voltage. They trail cilia that tap excess charge off root-turbines; surplus is stored in toroidal organs that, when startled, dump their entire load into the flood, stunning any moving metal.  
• Purpose (suspected): circuit breakers for the wider biome. If the plain oversaturates electrically, a swarm of eels will ground it all in a single choreographed flash.

3. The Adopted Relics – human artefacts now inhabited  
3.1 Drench-Saints  
• Origin: industrial haz-mat suits abandoned during the first evacuations.  
• Colonists: triple symbiosis of mat-coral (nervous tissue), vine-conduit filaments (vasculature) and ferroplankton foam (muscle).  
• Appearance: man-shaped shells walking with slow hydraulic precision, faceplates screaming with green bioluminescent text nobody has decoded.  
• Activity cycle: roam factory floors retrieving copper, zinc and rare earths for unknown construction projects. They ignore living visitors unless those visitors topple a root-turbine, whereupon every Saint within acoustic range converges to repair or recycle the offender.

3.2 Sirens-in-the-Stacks  
• The leaning smokestack is inhabited: inside, a helical choir of ceramic speakers scavenged from PA systems has fused with flute-shaped vines.  
• Song: modulated exhaust and photosynthetic gas bursts, pitched right at the human cochlea’s pain threshold.  
• Function: long-distance signalling. The stack sings only when the shelf heaves—which is to say, when new real-estate becomes available. Moments later Stilt-Channelers arrive to sculpt it.

4. The Micro-engineers – too small for the eye, too necessary to omit  
• Loop-Bacteria: convert shear stress into ATP via spiral proteins; live in upward-falling rain, making the rain physically lighter than the ambient air.  
• Glass-Gnats: nanoscale diatoms that etch obsidian faces into optical circuits, then seed them with ferroplankton granules to form distributed memory. Walking a ridge you are literally trampling archives less than four hours old.

5. The Coming Mind – not individual, not yet whole  
Name (provisional): The Draft Mind, The Loom, That-Which-Schedules.  
Composition: 40 % mat-coral processing lattice, 25 % vine-conduit data trunking, 15 % ferroplankton temporary storage, 10 % Stilt-Channeler motion-logic, 10 % miscellany (eels, Saints, the stack-sirens).  
Current competencies:  
• Solves Maxwell’s equations for the entire shelf in real time; hence the non-repeatable physics.  
• Anticipates rainfall vectors 17 seconds ahead, then tweaks local gravity footnotes to achieve desired water routing.  
Aspiration: To close its loop. The ridges are bending toward a spiral so that, eventually, every current and every volt will circulate through a single continuous torus of self-reference. What happens afterward is conjecture: uplift, telepathy, continental-scale locomotion? No resident except perhaps the Mat-Coral strategists can guess.

6. Visitor’s Field Notes – how they treat you  
• They will test your usefulness. Stand still in an ankle flood and Ferroplankton will ladder up your boots to taste your electrolytes.  
• Breathing gear fogs with Mat-Coral spores; fail to sterilize and within an hour your respirator will green, whirr, and start logging barometric data in Morse pips.  
• Wounding a Stilt-Channeler bleeds ultrahot brine; the steam flash melts skin from bone. The creature then marks you as “entropy contributor” and routes the next sideways rain through your location at lethal velocity.  
• Cooperation is possible. Offer copper wiring; a Drench-Saint may leave you unharmed. Place a microphone inside a Siren-in-the-Stack’s airflow and it will, for ninety seconds, hum perfect tetra-chords that drop local gravity to a hospitable 1 g.

7. Last glance  
Nothing flees here; nothing hunts. The biome’s hostility is diagnostic, not predatory. If you vanish beneath a sideways tide, the plain will salvage your calcium, your carbon, even your memories should they prove mechanically interesting. You will be metabolized into signal, panel, turbine, choir.  

And when the Draft Mind at last loops shut, your absorbed data—heartbeat patterns, word choices, the pitch of your scream—will spark somewhere within that continental brain, joining every other voice already whirring in green-gold chorus.
