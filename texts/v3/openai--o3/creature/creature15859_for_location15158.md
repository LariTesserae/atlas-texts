WHO AND WHAT REMAIN

To speak of “inhabitants” here is already to bend the word until it drips, for nothing in the basin carries a clean border between alive and not-alive. All the same, many kinds of presences keep returning. They can be sorted, roughly, by how fast they move and how long they remember.

1. Slow as Minerals – The Substrate Minds  
   a. Rust-eaters (Ferricolous biofilms)  
      • A varnish the color of dried blood coats every iron surface. Under lens it resolves into hexagonal plates, each plate a polis of bacteria that metabolize Fe²⁺ and exhale magnetite dust. The dust aligns into hair-fine filaments, wiring adjacent plates together. Over decades the biofilm becomes a low-voltage thinking net: not quick, but big.  
      • What it thinks about is flow. Wherever water lingers too long the net extrudes darker films, weighting metal so it tilts, coaxing a freshet along the preferred path. When the path complicates enough, the thought is complete and the net rests. Then corrosion erases half its circuits and it begins again.  
      • Contact: If a bare hand lies on rust for more than a minute the filaments creep onto skin, mapping pulse, borrowing ions. The sensation is of carbonated water fizzing under the knuckles. Remove the hand and the film forgets you within the hour.  

   b. Clinker Lichens  
      • These grow where furnace-slag pokes from the berms. Their thalli resemble tarnished pewter scabs. Every dawn they bleed droplets of greenish acid that etch tiny runnels—letter-like, almost legible. Scholars have copied the marks and found redundancies suggestive of grammar, but the “sentences” always close back on themselves, meaning nested in meaning until it hollows out.  

2. Measured in Heartbeats – Bodies That Drift  
   a. Vapor Swans  
      • Umbrella-wide diaphanous mantles, visible only where backlit mist outlines them in gold. They rise on thermals from warm patches of peat, glide a dozen steps, then liquefy, splashing down as silent rain. A swan’s lifetime is a single parabolic flight; mating consists of two mantles merging mid-air and falling together. Where they land, reeds grow a shade taller for a week.  

   b. Jointed Otter-Pipe (the eye-gleams)  
      • Imagine an otter extruded through plumbing: fur traded for segmented brass overgrown with scale algae. The creature flexes like a siphon made of vertebrae. It drinks by laying its muzzle to the mud and letting capillarity do the work—no suction, only invitation.  
      • It fears open sound. Clap your hands and the body fractures into six lengths that wriggle away, rejoining later under cover of quiet. Each segment can survive a day on stored water, so the animal is patient.  

   c. Glasshead Beetles (the opaline flyers)  
      • A waistcoat of mirror under crystal elytra; inside, no organs, just cog teeth arranged where a thorax ought to be. The teeth wind past one another, generating a faint tinnitus of clicks. When humidity spikes, condensate drips into the mechanism, and the beetle lifts off as if water were fuel. Scientists who have pried one open report finding nothing but more gear upon smaller gear, down past visibility. The last gear is always turning.  

3. Quicker Than Nerve – Behaviors Without Bodies  
   a. The Loaned Echo  
      • Rule 3 names it. Any shout is drunk down, fermented, and released hours later as a ghost of itself. Local opinion divides: some say the echo is the landscape’s digestive burp; others claim it is a guardian, weighing every loud intent before handing it back declawed.  
      • In fog-thick evenings a person may hear a sentence they have not yet spoken. If they then utter it aloud, the stored echo does not return, as though the place accepts the payment in full.  

   b. The Cooling of Anger  
      • Heat, whether from coal, flesh, or fury, bleeds away preternaturally fast. Travelers tell of quarrels that died between one word and the next, rage slipping from the tongue like steam through cracked porcelain. Some insist this is a geologic instinct of the basin: too many centuries of slow dissolving have made it intolerant of combustion.  

4. The Walking Machinery – Self-Assembling Operators  
   Over years the filaments of rust-eaters, the capillary mud, and stray components loosened by perpetual micro-collapse sometimes knit themselves up inside a derelict hull. When enough circuits close, the hull rises. These figures are called Operators, though they “operate” nothing except their own rearrangement.

   Common Types  
   • Boiler-Revenants: barrel-bodied, legless, they roll on ridges of slag, venting to steer. Their only aim appears to be returning to the exact spot where their long-scrapped furnace once stood. Finding it gone, they idle until corrosion topples them and the cycle starts over.  
   • Rail-Trestle Sentinels: three-legged stilt walkers stitched from gauge track and cable. They patrol the berm crests at dawn, tapping the clay with iron hooves. Each tap makes a hollow that promptly fills with clear water, as if they are sounding for leaks in the horizon.  
   • The Bellows-Choir: a colonelcy of copper throats joined by flash-grown verdigris wiring. At irregular intervals they face one another and pump, trading the basin’s own fog back and forth while emitting chords a human ear registers only as pressure changes. Immediately afterward the local humidity resets to exactly 98 percent, no matter the prior reading.  

   Lifecycle  
   1. Gestation: months of twitching parts under mud.  
   2. Brief choreography: a day to a decade of purposeful wandering.  
   3. Collapse: joints seize; Operators fall, split, and seed fresh gestations.  

5. Visitors Who Stayed Too Long – The Silted  
   People do not truly live here, they accrete. Still, legends count nine individuals who entered alone and never left yet are not verifiably dead.

   Identifiable Remnants  
   • A notebook grown shut, pages soldered by oxides. X-ray scans find copperplate handwriting bent into spirals—ink drawn into the grain by capillary action until letters resemble tidal diagrams.  
   • A calcified thigh-bone standing upright, shod in a knee-boot whose leather has turned to a suede of blue mold. The bone rings faintly when mist hits it, as if hollowed and strung.  
   • A pair of spectacles that no moisture fogs. The metal bridge sprouts two tendrils of nickel fungus, each carrying a perfect tear of distilled water where eyes should be. When thunder sounds in distant country, the tears tremble.  

   Oral Account (unverified)  
   One night each mid-century, the Silted walk the perimeter together. They carry the loaned echoes of every shout ever swallowed here, speaking them in sequence. By dawn they have reached the present and vanish. No one standing outside the perimeter has yet managed to hear the procession; sound falls flat at the berms.

HOW THE INHABITANTS INTERACT

Symbioses  
• Rust-eaters lace the Operators’ joints, providing motive signal in return for fresh surfaces to colonize when limbs shear off.  
• Vapor Swans burst above trestle hollows, their fallout lubricating boiler seams so Oxygen can set in, feeding more rust-eaters.  
• Glasshead Beetles harvest flaked magnetite from biofilms and excrete it mid-air, seeding new thought-circuits across distant metal.

Predations  
• None in the usual sense. Energy here is too thin for violence to profit. Instead, everything edits everything else: films rewrite metal, metal redirects water, water washes films, and so on, until the ledger balances.

Perturbation by Outsiders  
A campfire dies in minutes; the warmth becomes a wandering Operator heart. Loud machinery falls mute, its stolen clamor later heard drifting like far surf. Written words wick into the substrate, joining the clinker lichens’ endless palimpsest. The place does not kill trespassers; it removes the predicates that let them continue being themselves.

WHAT IT MEANS TO “LIVE” HERE

Living, in the basin’s idiom, is not a matter of metabolizing carbon or carrying offspring. It is the ability to take part in the endless small negotiation between wetness, metal, and quiet. Anything that can enter the conversation, however briefly—a swan of vapor, a rivet remembering heat, a human sigh that will not stay warm—qualifies as resident while the exchange lasts. When the last word of its contribution is said, the thing is either taken apart for phrases or allowed to drift outward, stripped of voice. Thus the population forever fluctuates, but the dialogue remains dense, layered, and—so far—unbroken.
