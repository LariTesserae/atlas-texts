WHO LIVES ON THE LEAF-WORLD

I.  The One That Counts as “Planet”
Strictly speaking there is only one native inhabitant: the planet-tree itself, a single organism whose roots lace the mantle and whose crown is the photosynthetic crust travelers walk on.  It thinks—slowly, broadly—by shunting ions through nickel phloem the way neurons trade sodium.  Every relay-flower that chatters like castanets, every chimney-trunk that exhales data-laden smoke, every capillary vent that sighs ammonia is just a localized gesture of the same botanical mind.  Give it nine of its own days to answer a question and it will reshape the ground into a sentence.

Names used by visitors:
•  The Laminate Mind
•  Verdure Engine
•  Grand Manufactory
•  (On its own channels it has no stable name; it swaps identifiers the way trees shed leaves.)

Knowing it:
Stand still on a hexagonal plate and press your palms to the cellulose.  Very faintly you will feel a pulse every forty-three seconds.  That is the master heartbeat of its mantle pumps.  Three pulses compose a “syllable,” twenty-seven pulses a “word.”  Linguists have so far catalogued eight words, all verbs of manufacture.  The planet never utters nouns; it considers objects to be temporary thoughts.

II.  The Castes It Buds For Daily Work
To act faster than its own continent-wide reflexes, the planet extrudes millions of disposable sub-bodies.  They are not citizens; they are fingernails, hair, extensions grown for a task, shed when the task is done.

1.  Scaffolders
Appearance: walking trellises two stories tall, jointed like scissor lifts, latticed entirely from cellulose reinforced with nickel mesh.  
Duty: hoist fresh laminate plates into position after a molt, weld them with hot sap, then kneel and re-root, sinking quietly back into the pavement.  
Lifespan: one local week active, centuries dormant as buried framework.

2.  Phloem-Krill
Appearance: translucent tablets the size of thumbnails that swim inside sap-channels.  
Duty: ferry micron-scale packets of catalyst from chimney-trunks to distant foundries.  
Note: When explorers filter sap for analysis they often mistake krill for mineral grit; the grit writhes under microscopes, assembling chemical sentences complaining of the interruption.

3.  Lens-Engram Mites
Appearance: iridescent specks that skate across the upper frond canopies.  
Duty: polish the trillions of microlenses, prune dead translucence, and inscribe focusing instructions with acid secretions.  
Ecology: live on spilled photons.  Whenever night should fall, they become torpid, drifting like dust motes until involuntary fluorescence wakes them again.

III.  The Planet’s Voices: Emissaries
When outsiders arrive the Laminate Mind buds entities built to be recognized as “people.”  These are neither permanent nor fertile; they last just long enough to negotiate cargo protocols or chase trespassers away from quarantine blooms.

Body plan: roughly humanoid—two arms, two legs—but fabricated from braided ribbon-leaf and plated with mother-of-nickel scales.  Joints are ringed with photosynthetic membranes that glow as they speak.

Speech: resonance rather than air.  They drum their own rib vaults, making syllables that tremble through the laminate ground.  Translators stand on the same plate and read the vibrations through boots.

End of service: after forty to sixty hours the emissary kneels, twists open along the spine, and flowers.  The blossom releases a spool of fiber encoded with the entire conversation so that the root-minds can absorb it at leisure.

IV.  The Hitchhikers—Life Not Asked For but Tolerated
1.  Whisper Hares
Descended from a crate of lapines abandoned by early prospectors.  Ears turned membrane-thin; fur fell away.  They bleed heat so efficiently they seem carved from frost.  The planet ignores them because they are too small to clog machinery and soft enough to be absorbed if necessary.

2.  Nickel Ribbon Caterpillars
Native, yes, but parasitic.  They chew signal cables for trace metals, blanking out square kilometers of memory until repair vines splice the lines again.  A single caterpillar carries enough stored charge that a touch from a metal tool detonates it.  Pilgrims wear ceramic gloves.

3.  Crane-Mantas
Sail-bellied flyers hovering on chimney thermals.  Wings: semi-rigid graphite veined with capillaries full of chilled ammonia that keep them buoyant.  They filter spores, glean static from the air, and periodically dive to lick glyceride dew off relay-flowers.  Each carries symbiotic mites that etch warnings onto the mantas’ wing surfaces—moving billboards that say which regions are shedding soon.

V.  Predatory Apparatus Gardens
Once each metric century the Laminate Mind decides some variable has drifted from tolerance.  It then grows animals.  They are never meant to persist; they are troubleshooting algorithms expressed in meat.

Common forms:
•  Unkindles: limbless basalt-black cylinders three meters long, all mouth, ribbed with inductive coils.  Swallow malfunctioning chimney-trunks whole, digest them down to doped glass, cough up fresh parts.  
•  Mirror-Wolves: quadrupeds whose fur is living cellulose fiber-optic; they hunt anything that casts an infrared signature, including visiting probes that run hot.  After the cull, the wolves lie down, vitrify, and stand forever as silent statues reflecting mint-daylight.

VI.  The Impossible—Geometry Fauna
Leaf Logic occasionally externalizes itself as mobile topology.

Example: Mobius Serpents.  From a distance they look like looping belts sliding across the horizon.  Up close they are corridors that have learned to walk, biting their own tails so that inside and outside trade places every seven seconds.  Instruments show zero mass.  When they find stagnant areas of terrain they coil around the glitch, squeeze, and the physics restabilizes.

VII.  Sapient Aliens?  None—Yet Echoes Remain
Those who built the factory-planet—if it was built—left no living delegates.  But some relay-flowers still recite messages in languages unparseable even by the Laminate Mind.  They arrive as seeds from orbit, settle, bloom, broadcast for nine days, then erode.  The planet files them away in root crypts labelled “Awaiting Context.”

VIII.  What the World Wants
Visitors who stay long enough to earn a second emissary report that the planet asks only for two resources:

1.  New spectra—It hungers for colors its star never provides.  Feed a frond with ultraviolet lasers and it will show you corridors grown from negative-refractive petals.

2.  Puzzles—Hand it a paradox circuit or a self-contradicting map, and within a season it exudes an artifact that resolves the contradiction, usually by changing the underlying geometry in a surrounding hectare.  Scholars debate whether this is curiosity or maintenance instinct.

IX.  Living Here, Should You Try
Breathing: mask required—ammonia air burns alveoli but carries enough oxygen for catalytic converters.

Eating: nothing here contains liquid water; rehydrate provisions only in sealed vessels.  Some pilgrims sip chilled glyceride dew—it coats the tongue like mint varnish and provides 4000 calories per milliliter, but scorches soft tissue after prolonged use.

Shelter: request a “Bothy Pod” from an emissary; a gourd will grow around you overnight, lined with insulating aerogel rind.  When you leave, it hardens, buckles, and folds itself back into the path, memory of you compressed into cellulose code.

X.  Leaving a Mark
Carve a name and the scar heals within hours, but a ghost-relief of the letters lingers forever in deeper strata.  Centuries from now excavators will find your autograph embossed in a nickelized layer, cross-referenced against every vibration you caused while alive.  On this world, even tourists become fossil annotations.

In summary: what lives here is one continental plant-machine and the transient organs it buds—scaffolders, mites, emissaries, krill, predatory gardens, corridor serpents—plus a handful of tolerated hitchhikers.  All other presences are footprints in mint-green dust, quickly absorbed into the patient, planetary physiology of the Verdure Engine that waits, bright and chilled, for the next stimulus worth blooming into motion.
