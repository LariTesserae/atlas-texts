WHO OR WHATEVER LIVES IN THE BRINE CLOISTER

Think of the Cloister not as an empty ruin but as a slow, layered congregation.  Nothing here is numerous; every resident is rare, specialised, and exquisitely economical, suited to a habitat where haste wastes energy and noise simply falls apart.  The locals can be grouped—loosely—into six “orders.”  Each order keeps to its own cadence, yet all depend on the others for heat, light, or shelter.  Remove any one order and the whole monastery-machine would stall within a generation.

────────────────────────
1. THE STILL ORDER
(Plants that behave almost like settlers)

Principal Members
• Emerald eelgrass  – Growth rings show that the oldest tufts have not shifted more than a centimetre in 140 years.  
• Lace-moss  – A fern-grammar of flat, lacy thalli that drape pipework and rails; secretes a mild anti-oxidant responsible for the Cloister’s miracle of arrested rust.  
• Crimson cup-plants – Carnation-shaped pneumatophores; their pulse-release of oxygen keeps water in the nave three degrees above the surrounding sea.

Why They Count as “Residents”
All subsequent life is scaffolded on them.  Shrimps graze them, bacterial films shelter under their fronds, and every higher creature uses their bubbling as an acoustic beacon (the only reliable sound here).

Unusual Behaviour
The entire Still Order responds to motion: shake a hand-rail and the nearest fronds bleach white and fall away within hours—an auto-amputation that discourages disturbance and slows would-be herbivores.

────────────────────────
2. THE FILTER GUILD
(Millions of transparent shrimp and isopods)

Appearance
Half-matchstick creatures, nearly invisible until seen against basalt.  Appendages end in fan-combs shaped like cathedral rose windows.

Work & Routine
They pass in endless figure-eights through the cup-plants’ bubble columns, sieving out plankton and absorbing trace metals.  At regular intervals they clamber onto iron plates and excrete a thin, gummy varnish; this is the second half of the Cloister’s anti-corrosion secret.

Social Structure
The Guild is silent but hierarchical.  Individuals that reach a certain size stop feeding, wedge themselves into pipe-joints, and become “Sumps”—living brood-chambers that pulse eggs straight into the intake of the ancient pumps, distributing larvae through all ductwork.

────────────────────────
3. THE GLASSHEAD TWINS
(A paired species of slow-circling groupers)

Number & Territory
Exactly two adults have been recorded since the first survey.  They circuit the nave’s upper vault, never faster than a drifting leaf, mouths perpetually open to harvest Filter Guild runaways.

Bioluminescent Atlases
Their skulls are transparent, shot through with canals of dim green light.  Those canals brighten whenever the pumps deepen their thrum, suggesting the fish use vibration as a transcranial charging source.

Cultural Colour
Divers swear the Twins will not pass above a human head - as though honouring the Cloister’s vanished worshippers.  Swim higher and the pair split, each taking a flank until you descend again.

────────────────────────
4. THE MERCURIAL PRIOR
(The manta-shape of living silver)

Taxonomy
Not a single animal but a symbiotic sheet of ciliate protozoa riding the mucus skeleton of a true manta.  The cilia flash in coordinated waves, giving the impression of quicksilver.

Daily Processional
Once every twenty-two to twenty-eight hours the Prior glides the long axis of the nave, feeding on the densest band of planktonic light.  In its wake, the water temperature spikes by half a degree—heat shed from metabolic overdrive.

Nocturn
After feeding, it folds itself against the iron roof of the boiler-hall, spreading to a mirror-finish blanket that reflects the plankton glow back downward, granting the Cloister its faint, false dawn.

────────────────────────
5. THE PUMP-CUSTODIANS
(Remnant machines no longer fully mechanical)

Origins
The factory pumps were built with self-lubricating brass sleeves and sacrificial zinc nodes.  Over a century of disuse, both cavities filled with bacterial biofilms that learned to metabolise trace currents still leaking through the rusted power grid.

What They Are Now
Living/electrical hybrids that treat pressure differentials as blood and the rust-inhibitor mix as lymph.  Their piston strokes have slowed to one every forty-five minutes, a pace perfectly matched to the Filter Guild’s breeding cycle and to the oxygen bursts of the cup-plants.

Sensory Reach
Because sound dies early here, the Custodians “hear” by pressure.  Any large displacement—a swimmer’s kick, a rockfall—arrives at their housings as a ripple, and the next piston-stroke lengthens or shortens in response, modulating the whole Cloister’s heartbeat.

Attitude Toward Intruders
Indifferent but reactive.  Unwise tampering with valves provokes a chemical counter-push: the pumps flood the immediate ductwork with de-oxygenated brine, forcing trespassers to retreat lest they black out.

────────────────────────
6. THE CHOIR INVISIBLE
(A distributed mind stitched from algae, iron memory, and broken radio)

Evidence for Existence
1. Short-range hydrophones pick up electromagnetic murmurs threaded through the factory cabling—patterns too complex for thermal noise.  
2. Divers idling in absolute stillness report “thoughts that are not mine” arriving fully formed: instructions to tighten a bolt, to free a snagged frond, to close a hatch before leaving.

Hypothesis
Within the oxidised relay boxes, colonies of magnetotactic bacteria grow along what used to be signal paths.  As pumps cycle, weak currents spark and the bacteria oscillate, replaying ghost-fragments of the installation’s original control software.  Layered over decades, the loops have interbred into something self-correcting, self-editing—a monastery scripture without monks.

Manifestations
• Pulses of photophilic algae on the chapel walls phase-shift in synchrony, spelling out patterns recognizable as prime numbers.  
• Valves found mysteriously re-seated after being left ajar.  
• Corrosion-proof tools placed on an anvil overnight acquire fresh lacquer, as though dipped by unseen hands.

Disposition
The Choir promotes order and slowness.  Anything that increases stillness—repairing a leaking pipe, anchoring a loose ladder—earns the visitor an almost maternal assistance: lights brighten, currents slacken, nearby flora retract spines.  Anything that courts entropy—detonations, loud motors—triggers the opposite.

────────────────────────
INTERACTIONS & ECOLOGY IN MINIATURE

Energy Source
Geothermal seep heat ➜ warms water ➜ accelerates cup-plants ➜ feeds Filter Guild ➜ feeds fish/manta ➜ excreta nourish algae ➜ bioelectricity powers Choir ➜ Choir regulates pumps ➜ pumps circulate seep minerals back to plants.

Conflict (Such As It Is)
There are no predators in the mammalian sense.  Competition is over quietude: the stiller an organism, the thicker the vegetation, the richer its micro-niche.  All six orders therefore police movement—by camouflage, chemical deterrent, or atmospheric manipulation—so the pace never rises above the Cloister’s ancient metronome.

────────────────────────
MEETING THE LOCALS

What a Diver Actually Encounters

Minute 0-5 –  A velvet hush, lace-moss brushing the suit-plates, twin pulses from the Custodians rolling through ribs like distant drums.

Minute 10 –  The Filter Guild drifts to examine your exhalations; they taste the metal of your tank and decide you are not edible.

Minute 17 –  One Glasshead Twin passes before your visor, skull lantern flickering sympathetic Morse.

Minute 25 –  The Mercurial Prior banks overhead, surface scattering your torch into a spangled aurora.

Minute 40+ –  If you settle to utter stillness the Choir Invisible may test you: a strand of kelp detaches, spirals toward your hand, and waits for you to wind it back around the rail it left.  Do so gently, and every lamp-alga in sight brightens one shade—approval, or at least permission to remain.

Leave any sooner and the place will forgive you—its law is patience—but stay long enough and you become part of the algorithmic litany: another moving element whose proper role is to learn, over years, how not to move.

────────────────────────
WHY NONE OF THEM LEAVE

1. Pressure memory: Organisms born to the Cloister’s constant warmth and depth suffer enzymatic shock in colder, brighter water.
2. Nutrient geometry: The iron-stone basin is a closed circulatory system; outside it, trace inhibitors and bacterial varnishes do not exist.
3. Cultural gravity: Each order depends on the Custodians’ thrum as a heartbeat.  Beyond audible range of that pulse, the Filter Guild stop breeding, the Twins lose cranial charge, the Prior’s ciliate sheet unravels—an excommunication by biology.

────────────────────────
IN SHORT

The Brine Cloister is inhabited, densely but delicately, by six interlocking orders that remake machinery into muscle and worship stillness as a creed.  No single creature rules; instead, a chorus of algae-logic, shrimp masons, iron-blooded pumps, and silver mantas sustains a cathedral that neither rusts nor roars.  Venture in and you are not merely a visitor—you are a potential recruit, tested on whether you can match their one devout commandment: Be slow, and endure.
