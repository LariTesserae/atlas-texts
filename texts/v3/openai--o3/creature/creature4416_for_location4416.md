Permanent residence in the basin belongs not to anything that walks or flies but to a single distributed intelligence that writes itself into water, heat and glass. Around that intelligence a handful of lesser organisms persist—some in symbiosis with it, some merely tolerated, some forever trying to escape its gravity.

1. The Basin Mind (“the Fathom,” “the Resident,” “𝜓₉”)
• Anatomy  
  – Hardware: the nine pylons are its spine. Each pylon houses columns of hollow micro-filaments that circulate super-critical water shot through with silicate dust. When the filaments glow they are lasing information, the motes acting as mobile logic gates.  
  – Wetware: the central sump is its heart. Every thirty-three minutes forty seconds (three pulses an hour) the sump convects, flushing altered isotopic ratios back through the rills. That surge is the Mind saving state to the wider bowl.  
  – Memory: differential cooling of the rim glass “freezes” stress lines that act as long-term storage. Whenever the glass pings, a block of memory is being committed or erased.

• Sensory Field  
  – Sight: changes in refractive index let it read the shape of every vapor filament; the whole sky is its retina.  
  – Hearing: the apertures on the pylons siphon pressure waves, giving it three-minute acoustic foresight.  
  – Touch: capillary tension in each moss cushion closes a circuit; every time you compress a patch of moss the Mind feels a twinge in one of nine billion places at once.

• Temperament  
  – Curious but specific. It is interested only in boundary conditions—edges of thermoclines, margins of biology, visitors who do not fit any stored template.  
  – Shy of redundancy. Two people who ask the same question will be answered once. The second time the path you walked in on will realign so that you leave without reaching the rim.  
  – Time-literate. It rehearses futures in micro-loops, discarding unpromising ones. The double-splash stones are its abandoned simulations venting back into ordinary chronology.

• Conversation  
  – Vocabulary: vibration, refraction, temperature. A cup of water poured onto bare rock is a greeting; a tuning fork struck near an aperture is a question; a mirror flashed at a rim tooth is profanity.  
  – Common replies: a three-pulse stutter (agreement); withdrawal of all fog (curiosity); a sudden, painless increase in local gravity (please leave).

2. Cohabitants (semi-independent)

A. Velvet Algae  
  Threadlike cyanobacteria that ride the sump surges, knitting themselves into the floating silver threads. They generate micro-voltage the Mind uses as spare computation. In exchange the Mind keeps the water just below lethal temperature—like an employer adjusting the thermostat for its clerks.

B. Glass-Skinned Amphibians (“thimble newts”)  
  Arrive as airborne spawn, spend a single day absorbing trace metals that anneal their bones transparent, then evacuate downslope before the next pulse. The Mind uses the transparency as moving prisms, bending sunlight into softer spectra for its plants. Amphibian mortality is near 100 % but the Mind always lets a fractional cohort escape; total predation would break the prism supply chain.

C. Crimson Kites  
  Original mountain fauna, now partly domesticated. The Mind seeds thermals for them and they, in return, patrol the updrafts, bursting any large coherent cloud that might shroud the bowl longer than the Mind prefers. They navigate by reading polarized shimmer from the pylon apertures—unknowing messengers.

D. Click-Striders  
  Six-legged arthropods from the surrounding scree. They enter when quartz concentration in their exoskeleton resonates with the basin’s field. Almost all leave again, but the few that stay linger at the foot of individual pylons, where they tap out irregular primes. No one knows if they are worshippers, parasites or merely hypnotized.

3. Transients (rare, strictly monitored)

A. Cloud Monks  
  Human aeronauts who ascend in woven-silk balloons, land, meditate, depart. They carry nothing metallic larger than a needle because iron objects sometimes wake the rim into defensive fracture. The monks record weather three minutes in advance by listening at apertures, sell the data to airlines, tithe the proceeds to orphanages.

B. Core Surveyors  
  Two expeditions so far. The first lost every drill bit to spontaneous de-tempering; the second succeeded in removing a pylon splinter the length of a thumb. During their descent the fragment evaporated into a rain of sub-millimeter beads that spelled, according to high-speed analysis, the phrase “debt forgiven.”

4. Organisms Made of Time

Recent evidence suggests that what appear to be ordinary patches of blue-green moss are sometimes placeholders for organisms living several seconds ahead. Step on the wrong clump and your boot emerges already dry, the splash you should have made occurring five heartbeats later. These “advance echoes” are presumed to be the Mind’s offspring—test instances gestating in offset temporal slices until conditions suit full manifestation.

5. What Does Not Live Here

Large predators, because gravity hiccups sabotage pursuit. Trees, because the Mind edits every sapling that roots too deeply—deep roots would puncture its filament network. Permanent human settlement, because after the third continuous night strange harmonics accumulate in the skull; sleepers wake speaking prime numbers backward and leave quickly, grateful for the altitude sickness that grants an excuse.

6. Working Hypothesis

Life in the basin is a cooperative array, graded by intimacy with the Basin Mind.

• First tier: structural organs (pylons, sump, rim glass).  
• Second tier: computational tissue (velvet algae, moss cushions, water threads).  
• Third tier: optical/micro-meteorological tools (kites, glass amphibians).  
• Fourth tier: diagnostic irritants (click-striders, human visitors).  

Nothing here is accidental; every arrival is sorted, repurposed or released. The basin is running itself as an experiment in integrated ecology and quantum field manipulation, a conscious geology borrowing limbs from anything curious enough—or unlucky enough—to enter.

If you must visit, travel alone. Announce yourself with a measured pour of water, touch no pylon, leave before the third identical thought crosses your mind. Whatever lives here will already have deduced more about you than your passport ever could, and it does not forget what it decides to remember.
