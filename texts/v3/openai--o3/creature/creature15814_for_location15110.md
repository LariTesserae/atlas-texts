Who Lives on the High Plateau  

The plateau’s tissue is both floor and genesis: every creature here is, in some proportion, a continuation of its flesh. Some keep only a biochemical memory of that kinship, others have never severed the umbilicus. For ease of thought, visitors divide the residents into six entwined “guilds.” The borders are porous; with time and diet a body may drift from one guild to another, as readily as a vein drifts into a leaf.

────────────────────────────────────────  
1. The Foundational Body – Architect-Trees  
────────────────────────────────────────  

No zoology is honest that omits the plateau itself. All other life is scaled against this single, continent-wide organism: a mat of trunks, leaves and organs grown upward through mineral rock until nothing non-living remains. Even its “air” contains motes of living latex that evaporate and fall again like invisible snow.

Conscious? Probably not. Responsive? Intensely. It carries three slow pulses.  

• The Water Pulse (cycles every night): vents draw mist downward, redistribute it, exhale again.  
• The Gravity Pulse (every nine days): localized shifts of buoyant sap in the outer rind halve surface gravity for about thirty heartbeats.  
• The Memory Pulse (once each century): a hemisphere-wide flush of cambium that writes new structures and eats old ones, like a giant rewriting its thoughts as architecture.

Organisms that cannot ride these pulses die or are pressed into new shapes.

────────────────────────────────────────  
2. The Veil – Drifters of the Upper Gloom  
────────────────────────────────────────  

2.1 Glisten-Gliders  
Already met in passing: wing-nets of clear chitin, body no heavier than a curled leaf. What makes them distinctive is the seam that never closes along the belly; a glider can unzip itself, step free of yesterday’s skin, and drift on—an airborne series of biographies, each moult remembering a different wind.

2.2 Weathervane Rays  
Parabolic discs the span of a dining table, charcoal-blue, forever nose-up against thermal plumes. They prey on condensed droplets rather than animals, raking mist with frilled mouths. A ray that goes thirsty folds into origami tightness, drops, and re-opens lower down where the air is wetter.

2.3 Ember Ticks  
Glowing cinders no larger than a grain of sand. They are spores, technically, of a fungus that digests airborne oils. Seen en masse they resemble a red aurora veining the rafters. Lantern slugs eat them for light.

────────────────────────────────────────  
3. The Skin-Walkers – Surface Dwellers  
────────────────────────────────────────  

3.1 Petal Cats  
Six legs, petalled pelt, ultrasonic voices. The pelt is not fur but photosynthetic lamina: a cat that naps in a sun-shaft is powering its next hunt. Colonies cooperate—one cat lures prey by rustling like a ripe flower, the others wait in the shadow of its bloom.

3.2 Drizzle Cervids (“Rain-Stags”)  
Knuckled limbs, branch-rack antlers strung with living moss. They browse on the plateau’s newborn membranes, pruning what grows too quickly. Their antlers host symbiotic flies that sip the sugary seep of budding antler points; in return the flies secrete acids that sculpt the rack into spires—a slow barter of architecture for grooming.

3.3 Trestle Crabs  
Quad-bodied arthropods whose legs fuse into arches whenever they pause, becoming literal bridges. Other animals learn the pattern: a stampede of drizzle cervids will cross a gully on the backs of the crabs that pursued them yesterday.

3.4 Hulled People  
Child-height, pearl-skin, resinous pores. Their “technology” is persuasion: song-phrases keyed to the plateau’s cambial reflexes. By dawn a hummed melody may have coaxed a sleeping porch, a siphon, a bowl. Hulled villages look empty until dusk chants begin and walls extrude into listening shapes.  
• Language: purely sung; no consonant lasts longer than a heartbeat.  
• Cosmology: the plateau is an egg; travellers are sparks the egg attracts so the chick won’t grow lonely.  
• Writing: scent knots—cords smeared with terpene signatures read by inhalation.

────────────────────────────────────────  
4. The Burrow-Eaters – Substrate Swimmers  
────────────────────────────────────────  

4.1 Vine-Throttle Vipers  
Not true snakes but gut-muscle grown loose from the plateau. They sever themselves in juvenile stage and roam the under-skin in search of anything warm. A throttler that finds prey strangles, then sutures both bodies together; days later the victim is a new length of vine, dangling, rooted again.

4.2 Root Choirs  
Colonies of chorded worms that rasp interior walls, tuning them like flutes. The sound drives insects ahead of the swarm; the worms consume the fugitives and the loosened wall tissue equally. When a choir dies it keratinises, sealing the tunnel it carved so nothing vital leaks.

────────────────────────────────────────  
5. The Reclaimers – Decay and Light  
────────────────────────────────────────  

5.1 Lantern Slugs  
Teal, fist-sized, bio-luminous. They digest dead fibre, excreting sheets of glassy cellulose that continue to glow for two or three nights. Explorers prize these castings as map-paper: the light reveals strain lines that act like a built-in grid.

5.2 Rot-Beads  
Slime moulds that polymerise stale sap into amber spheres. Each bead encases any microbe it meets, sanitising the plateau. A bead can remain liquid for a century before suddenly crystalising with an audible ping—startling proof that time here ripens in jumps.

5.3 Whisper Fungi  
Miles of mycelial lace under the bark. Their enzymes release trace gases that dampen sound; without them the plateau would echo like a drum. When a corridor grows too silent the fungi sporulate, emitting a hiss that re-seeds acoustics.

────────────────────────────────────────  
6. The Questionables – Entities at the Hem of Reality  
────────────────────────────────────────  

6.1 After-Images  
Not exactly organisms, more like memories the light itself keeps. Step into strong glow, then shade: a duplicate of you lingers, a prismatic smear that explores alternative paths for up to an hour before thinning to nothing. Hulled children chase them as playmates; scientists descend with prisms to catch one but bring home only color-shocked glass.

6.2 Knot-Oaths  
Loops of living corridor that bite their own tail, creating a room that is its own exit. Entering triggers a reflex: the walls sprout pores that release drugged pollen. Dream there too long and you wake elsewhere with plant-cells dividing in your blood, your fingertips able to photosynthesise for several days.

6.3 The Listening Fold  
A moving absence shaped like a half-opened book, two pages of vacuum hinged on a crease of gravitational whim. Animals avoid it yet never panic; even prey forgets to run. When the Fold passes, everything within earshot loses exactly one utterance from memory—a tax of secrets the plateau appears to levy on itself.

A Visitor’s Cautionary Gloss  

• Touch nothing glossy that mirrors you twice; the second image is a throttler vine sizing your throat.  
• If gravity slackens, crouch. It is easier to lose altitude than to find footing.  
• Should your shadow detach, follow it; it knows the shortest route back to breathable air.  
• Accept songs from Hulled hosts only if you are willing for the walls to remember your name forever.

The plateau is not a kingdom with subjects; it is an organism with sub-minds, each experimenting with how many different ways life can rehearse the trick of living. To catalogue its residents is therefore to describe moments in a single, slow metamorphosis—one whose end, if it has one, is still several biological dialects away.
