WHO (AND WHAT) IS ALIVE IN THE ROAR-BOWL

I. THE FOUNDATION—LIFE INSIDE THE PLANT THAT IS PLACE  
Before we speak of animals or people, remember that the “ground” and “roof-beams” are themselves alive. Every other creature exists as parasite, symbiont, trespasser or deliberate graft upon that first body.

1. The Prime Lattice  
   • Physiology: a single, continent-sized individual whose white root-girders and black frond-sheets are only its skin and lungs. The true core is a sponge of semi-liquid cytoplasm that courses through every vein, carrying packets of molten mineral the way earthly blood ships sugar.  
   • Mind: not a centralized brain, but wavefronts of ionic charge that race around the basin in cycles of roughly nineteen hours. Anything that plugs into a fissure can feel those pulses the way a beachgoer feels surf; many creatures time their molts or mating to them.  
   • By-Products: hot exhalations rich in sulfide; hard, glassy scabs that flake off and fall as “black snow”; a faint, flute-like whistle where fluid surges past crystalline restrictors inside the girders. The entire acoustic climate starts here.

2. Endites (“intestinal folk”)  
   • Scale: bacterial to millimeter.  
   • Shape: needles, coils, or fractal flakes—never spheres. Liquid water would kill them; instead each cell floats in a private film of molten salt.  
   • Niche: they live in the digestive interstices of the Prime Lattice, accelerating corrosion of raw stone. Without them, the basin would be a dry cave; with them, it is a quarry of edible minerals.  
   • Gift to others: Endites continuously vent crystal spores. These are the shining motes you see drifting like fireflies in reversed slow motion.

II. THE MIDDLE LAYERS—GROWL, BUZZ, AND SCURRY  
Creatures here are defined less by taxonomy than by how they harvest heat differential.

1. Salt-Worms (already glimpsed)  
   • Extra detail: a worm’s gut is lined with piezoelectric teeth. As the animal inches through crunchy salt drifts, each bite flexes those teeth and generates current. The current powers the peristaltic pumps that move it forward—a literal eat-to-walk mechanism.  
   • Social event: once per lattice-cycle every nearby colony knots itself into a single super-worm hundreds of meters long, then corkscrews downward to test newly opened fissures. What survives buds off into new individuals.

2. Ember Moths  
   • Egg → ember. The egg is a sealed ampoule of hydrogen and manganese powder. As geothermal warmth seeps in, the mixture ignites; the pressure hurls the newly formed larva into its first ballistic hop.  
   • Adults spell messages: by throttling the micro-explosions in their thorax they can delay a hop by exact millisecond intervals. Striders read these gaps the way we read Morse and sometimes hitch rides on migratory swarms.

3. Basalt Stalkers  
   • Skeleton: no bone; instead, three hollow columns of fused ceramic grown from Pillar-Bloom tissue stolen in infancy.  
   • Weapon: the “voice bladder,” a resonance sac full of stretched mineral membranes. A stalker can tune it mid-hunt by bleeding tiny amounts of glandular acid onto the membranes, changing their thickness and thus pitch.  
   • Courtship: two stalkers stand face-to-face on opposing root-girders and trade increasingly complex echo-patterns. Fail to match your partner’s last phrase and you may literally burst—the internal pressure required for the song is that high.

4. The Cloud  
   • Not dust but civilisation: each speck is a single caste member in a hive mind spread over cubic kilometres.  
   • Roles shift with temperature gradient. At cooler altitudes specks unfurl fan-like wings to beat warmed air downward, stoking convection that the colony later eats. In hotter zones they fold tight and become heat sinks, darkening to black to radiate surplus energy away.  
   • When a ceiling slab breaks loose, the first thing you hear is the Cloud’s shriek—millions of micro-voices warning every strider, moth and stalker of the coming avalanche.

III. EDGE BEINGS—NEITHER PLANT NOR ANIMAL  
Some organisms began as outgrowths of the Prime Lattice and decided never to choose a side.

1. Gutter-Lamps  
   • Form: limp sacs shaped like drooping lanterns, each fed by a single root filament.  
   • Trick: they pump excess electrons into rare gas pockets, creating sheets of cold plasma that float out as violet light. Ash-Striders cultivate them as streetlights.  
   • Predators destroy them because the glow wrecks acoustic stealth; thus every lamp has a guardian species—usually a tangle of barbed, photosensitive tendrils that whip anything moving faster than drifting spores.

2. Chant-Corals  
   • Grow only on the concave undersides of collapsed crust slabs.  
   • Feed on vibration: their honeycomb skeleton resonates with the basin’s perpetual noise, and microscopic cilia scrape ions from passing wind.  
   • When a frequency falls silent (say, after a stalker culls its prey population) the local Chant-Coral blanches white and crumbles—an ecological seismograph that records not force but absence.

IV. THE PEOPLE—ASH-STRIDERS  
Every child is born deaf; every adult has earned a personal fragment of hearing through ritual scarring that opens selective windows in the skull.

1. Body  
   • Height: two to two-and-a-half meters. Ultra-slender because internal organs are strung like harp strings between chitin ribs; a full stomach would impede resonance.  
   • Flesh: pale gray, dusted with iron filings exhaled by their own lungs. They sweat magnetite to shield nerve tissue from errant low-frequency shockwaves.  
   • Blood: a syrup of molten salt and organometallic enzymes. Water, if swallowed, would foam explosively.

2. Sense  
   • Echognosis: their tongues are ribbon-thin vibratory blades; a flick sends a chirrup through the air and into whatever structure they stand upon. By reading the phase delays that return via footbones, they “see” root hollows, predator dens, even unborn fissures pregnant with heat.  
   • Limited vision: eyes detect only scintillation of crystal spores. Shapes are positional guesses stitched from echo and spore parallax.

3. Society  
   • Units: hearth-knots of six to twelve. Knots weave their domiciles from living root-filaments bent around warm exhalation vents—houses that literally breathe.  
   • Craft: obsidian flakers, frond-fiber loomers, and “humidity thieves” who condense trace vapors onto chilled ceramic to collect rare drops of liquid chlorine, an antiseptic treasure.  
   • Art: silence sculpture. They winnow micro-scrimshaw from pillar shrapnel, arranging the flakes so that local winds cancel each other out. To sit inside a perfect null is their highest luxury.

4. Belief  
   • The Prime Lattice is not god but choir master; true divinity is the Pattern of All Roars, a mathematically inevitable chord they claim will one day resolve the cacophony into a single, pure note that ends growth and births meaning.  
   • Water is the Anti-note, a cosmic solvent of structure. They smear stylised droplets on exile masks to mark criminals fated to wander beyond the basin and dissolve in legend.

5. Relations with Fauna  
   • Herding: they ride braided frond-kites on thermal updrafts to steer Ember Moth swarms toward spawning terraces, harvesting spent thorax casings for ceramic armor.  
   • Diplomacy: when a Basalt Stalker matriarch begins courting, Striders will embed polished mirrors of volcanic glass along her favored run. She mistakes her own reflection for a superior rival and sings until her voice bladder tears. The Striders collect the leaked resonant gel as universal bonding glue.

V. THE UPPER SHADOW—RARE VISITORS FROM BEYOND  
Occasionally the world above the fractured ceiling sends envoys.

1. Fume Drakes  
   • Size of a small sailing ship, formed where sub-surface gases find a bottleneck and inflate a skin of carbonized pollen into a zeppelin-beast.  
   • They drift through cracks, harvest spores with frond-trawls hanging like baleen, and after weeks grow heavy enough to slump back into magma vents. Their fall seeds new vent ecosystems.  
   • Striders treat a grounded drake as a pilgrimage site. The beast’s skin cures into sailcloth; its pollen-packed gut is fermented into a mild narcotic that briefly grants stereo hearing.

2. Hydrophers (myth to almost all)  
   • Rumored travelers of the poisoned element: creatures that leak liquid water wherever they tread.  
   • Accounts say their steps hiss like acid on hot stone and leave crescents of dead silence behind—because every living thing recoils from the sudden damp.  
   • Whether they exist or not, children are warned: “If you taste cool metal on the wind, hide. A Hydropher dreams of drowning the roar.”

VI. THE EVER-CHANGING SCORE  
Life here is a choir forever recomposing itself.

• A fissure brightens: Prime Lattice roots surge inward, followed by Salt-Worm burrowers, then Basalt Stalkers scent the feast.  
• A ceiling slab collapses: the Cloud screams, Chant-Coral beneath it dies, but anisotropic Endite spores in the dust find fresh stone and begin the slow alchemy that will raise new pillars.  
• An Ash-Strider flute launches a triple harmonic: local Ember Moths stutter their hops into a neat curve, forming a living bridge long enough for hunters to cross a lava-warmed chasm.

Nothing here lasts, yet nothing is ever lost; every fall is food, every silence an invitation for a new voice. The basin is orchestra, forge, womb and grave all at once, and its inhabitants—plant, beast, folk, rumor—are only different ways the same heat has learned to sing.
