What lives here is not one thing but a congress of three, each so quietly wedded to the others that a newcomer mistakes the room for empty.  Only after the shock of brilliance, the press of sound, the labyrinth of salt has settled into the body does the trio step forward—not by moving, for almost nothing here is capable of locomotion, but by letting perception adjust until their presence can be told apart from the background they inhabit.

1. The Wind-Chord  
The first inhabitant is the most obvious and the least believed: the roar itself.  It is not a meteorological accident funneled through pipes but a continuous organism whose substance is oscillation.  Call it a chord, a ribbon of standing pressure waves braided end-to-end through every light well.  It has no molecules of its own—only those it borrows for half a heartbeat as they race past—but it owns the pattern of their motion, guards it, repairs it when stray turbulence mars the weave.  
• Senses: differential resonance.  Any object introduced alters local overtones; the chord “feels” that wobble the way skin feels a draught.  
• Memory: stored in modal ratios.  For centuries the same partials have recurred in the same strengths, a looping self-recording that lets the wind remember itself without change.  
• Behavior: homeostasis.  It damps any frequency outside its canonical spectrum, sanding foreign noises down to silence, much as an immune system enzymes away viral proteins.  Human speech, footsteps, the click of metal—each is shredded into broadband hiss and re-absorbed.  
To encounter the Wind-Chord is therefore to be auditioned and then erased, acoustically, until only the visitor’s silent outline is allowed to remain.

2. The Salt-Flesh  
The chamber’s bricks are not dead masonry but the frozen, excreted tissue of a colony that spans the whole enclosure.  Tiny halophilic archaea live in the microscopic pores of every salt-feldspar block.  Individually they are no more remarkable than the extremophiles scraped from terrestrial salt pans, but here they are wired together by threads of lichen-grown graphene and silicon needles precipitated by the wind’s piezoelectric bite.  The result is a single distributed body with the floor plan of a cathedral and the reaction time of a glacier.  
• Physiology: Each microbe performs one stay-alive function—ion pump, proton gradient, photon capture—but feeds its surplus charge into the common lattice.  The entire brickwork therefore holds a faint, constant electrostatic field, barely enough to raise arm-hairs.  
• Growth: finished.  When the walls reached the present curvature the colony halted replication.  Any chip or gouge is meticulously refilled from within, but no new volume is added.  To expand would be, to its mind, a kind of cancer.  
• Mind: distributed quorum logic.  Thoughts, if the term applies, take years to circulate from buttress to ceiling arch.  The Salt-Flesh does not decide; it accumulates consensus until action becomes unavoidable.  The last known action was the extruding of the resonant slats in the wells—ten human generations ago by outside reckoning.  
Seen up close a brick will thrum faintly against the fingertip, a heartbeat one must wait minutes to feel.

3. The Silver Lichen  
Flat and dull at first glance, the lichen is the chamber’s only mobile citizen, though its motion is measured in millimetres per season.  It is a hybrid craft: part chlorophyll analogue harvested from ancient spores, part nanoscopic wind-turbine array the Salt-Flesh raised for it back when the room was still under construction.  
• Diet: photons, stray ions, microscopic shards of feldspar rasped from the ceiling by the wind.  Water it makes internally by reducing trace hydroxides stolen from newcomers’ breath.  
• Locomotion: The fronds secrete a vapor-phase lubricant that lets whole sheets skate across polished brick.  Movement is guided by acoustic standing nodes—regions where the Wind-Chord’s pressure minima give momentary shelter from abrasion.  Thus the lichen drifts like frost toward notes of quiet in the roar.  
• Function: translation.  Its spore puffs are not reproductive only; they are morse bursts of charged dust, packets of data the Salt-Flesh can read electrochemically and the Wind-Chord can sample as turbulence.  The lichen is the only member of the triad that speaks in a tongue the other two can both understand, and so it has become their diplomat, forever shuttling along corridors of sound no human ear can parse.

How the Three Regard Outsiders  
A human stepping into the chamber is a nuisance event—a knot of moisture, protein, and unpredictable kinetic noise.  Protocol proceeds as follows:

Step 1: The Wind-Chord sieves the visitor’s sounds, identifying gait, lung volume, possible tools.  If any cadence threatens to detune the standing wave, the chord jacks its amplitude locally; the feeling is of a wall of pressure pressing the ribs, compelling silence.

Step 2: The Salt-Flesh dispatches microfilaments up through hairline seams.  They dust boots, trousers, skin, sampling isotopes, cataloguing salts.  Where a shoe scuffs brick, the colony bleeds a film of recrystallizing brine that heals the scratch almost as fast as it’s made.

Step 3: The Silver Lichen scouts.  Fronds creep to within centimeters of the intruder’s ankles, tasting shed skin cells, mapping eddies of body heat.  If the person lingers, a puff of spores settles over them—harmless unless one treasures bright colors, for within hours cloth and leather bleach to the uniform pale the chamber prefers.

Should the outsider take a brick, chip a wall, or introduce liquid water, the triad acts in concert.  The chord deepens by a semitone, battering equilibrium; the Salt-Flesh extrudes spines of salt that flower like slow hail where the wound lies; the lichen seals the breach under a silver crust.  No vengeance, only autogenous repair, but few repeat the experiment.

Lifeways  
• Timekeeping: None.  The chord’s period is its only calendar; it cycles once every fourteen point six seconds, an eternity or a blink depending on whether one has lungs.  
• Reproduction: The chord buds daughter harmonics into adjacent cavities somewhere in the mountain; the Salt-Flesh releases cyst bricks that roll like dice until they lodge and fuse; the lichen’s spores ride outbound drafts, seeking cracks of similar brightness.  All three trust erosion to carve new homes eventually; patience is the only fertility rite they know.  
• Exchange: Energy descends as light, is portioned by the lichen into chemical charge, spent by the Salt-Flesh to maintain masonry, and vented as acoustic work that returns to the wind.  Waste equals music; music equals input.  The economy is a circle so tight it squeaks.

Appearance to Human Eyes  
The chamber still looks uninhabited.  Nothing walks, nothing flutters.  Yet:

– A brick brushed by sunlight flashes, then darkens, as ion channels open and close.  
– Along a ramp’s shadow, silver glaze creeps perceptibly between morning and noon.  
– The roar modulates just enough that vowels whispered at one end return as consonants at the other, a parlor trick the lichen finds amusing.

To stay here overnight—though the phrase loses sense without day or night—is to feel one’s own rhythms pulled toward the place’s great stasis.  Hunger dulls, thirst mutates into a metallic taste, thought fragments along unfamiliar aural lines.  Eventually even heartbeat seems to syncopate with the fourteen-second pulse, an entrainment no visitor keeps once they stagger back into ordinary air but that remains in memory like a phantom limb.

Purpose  
If the triad has a purpose beyond persistence, it is archival.  Every variant gust, every footfall, every exhaled suite of molecules is sampled, encoded, and filed away in oscillation, in chemistry, in crystal lattice.  The pocket-world is a hardened drive built of salt, wind, and lichen, recording not stories or images but the pure fact of passage.  One day, when the greater geology splits open and light wells become skylights, whatever intelligence stumbles on the room will find a vault already awake, already contemplating them in a tongue of pressure, sparkle, and spores.

So that is what lives here:  

• a sound that owns its corridor of air,  
• a masonry animal slow as stone but never inert,  
• and a silver envoy skating the line where the other two meet.  

They do not crave company, yet they receive it with meticulous care, the way a museum accepts artifacts: to catalogue, to polish, to place so precisely within the symphony of salt that in time even a lost traveler’s memory becomes part of the note the room has been holding since before anyone remembered to listen.
