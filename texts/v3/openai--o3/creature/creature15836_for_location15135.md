Who or what walks the High Expanse?  The list is longer than a first glance implies, and it unfolds on many scales at once—crystal-film deep, tunnel-long, city-wide, epoch-slow.  Begin very small and keep widening the lens.  Everything here survives the triple scarcities of water, noise and haste.  Their shapes, chemistries and even thoughts show the same long, economical curve.

I.  The Ultralithic Layer  
1. Ferrum‐breathers  
   Size: 0.2–4 µm.  
   Habitat: interior pores of the vesicular lava domes.  
   Method: They “breathe” by passing electrons into iron oxides, using tholin vapors as electron donors.  The reaction trickles out just enough energy to knit a membrane of iron-sulfide scales.  
   Signature: A scent like a struck flint.  Viewed in oblique brass sunlight their colonies look like red frost.

2. Astatinophytes (“ghost algae”)  
   Still no plants, but these near-invisible biofilms fake the optics: they assemble plates of lead‐bismuth halide that diffract incoming light, creating greenish sheens inside shade pockets.  The color is not photosynthesis—it is camouflage, bending stray photons onto the colony’s lower surfaces where trace UV drives a slow radiolysis of perchlorate for energy.

II.  Motile Mesofauna  
1. Lantern mites (given in the field notes)  
   Addenda: when two swarms meet their flash-codes cross-pollinate fungal strains.  Fungal pedigrees act as public keys: only kin patterns will open the mites’ dorsal pits for trade.  The canyon night sky therefore doubles as a vast cryptographic network flickering at the edge of vision.

2. Glass-eyed foxes  
   Mass: 6 kg.  
   Structure: Fur is replaced by arrays of fused quartz whiskers tuned to ultrasonic turbulence.  They “hear” reflections of their own heartbeat off distant rock and stalk by mapping the echo delays.  The glass eyes are vestigial; vision is useless in the maze of mirrors their whiskers already paint.  
   Interaction with towns: The municipal 23 Hz power hum lies exactly on a harmonic that muddies the foxes’ echo lattice, so they stay outside civic perimeters.  Fox kits occasionally tumble across the hum line; children who return them are granted a saves-credit on their water ration.

3. Latchwings  
   Span: 45 cm membrane of parchment-stiff chitin.  
   Feeds on: Electrostatic charge.  Afternoon winds scour the plateau and leave the cliff rim +50 kV relative to the canyons.  Latchwings climb hanging fissures, unfurl, and simply drink the potential until dusk equalises the field.  Their bones are hollow beryllium lattices; when one dies its skeleton is snapped up by instrument-makers to build tuning forks that weigh under a gram yet ring for minutes.

III.  The Slow Titans  
1. Echo worms (already logged)  
   New note: Each worm’s tunnel maintains a standing infrasonic resonance.  Town engineers probe these drones to map structural strain in the plateau.  An earthquake warning sounds like a choir of gigantic flutes shifting key eleven hours before the actual slip.

2. Bell Canyons  
   Not a species but a phenomenon seeded by life.  Every few centuries a colony of ferrum-breathers binds enough iron crust to alter wind shear, carving a side pocket whose throat diameter matches the diurnal gust frequency.  The canyon becomes a Helmholtz resonator hundreds of meters tall, moaning at dawn and dusk.  Local folk ring changes in the moan by suspending curtains of copper mesh; the tone carries news fifty miles without spending a single droplet of water on ink.

IV.  The Plateau Folk  
They are the voices you heard only scarcely in the traveller’s brief.  Whether they began as terrestrial humans or arrived already divergent no record remains, for history is measured against stonefalls, not birthplaces.  Still, their physiology tells a clear story of adaptation.

1. Body  
   • Stature: Tall, tendon‐sparse, limbs a little too long—0.92 g does that over generations.  
   • Dermis: Pebble-gray.  The melanin path is inactive; instead, skin exudes a thin glaze of carbonized tholin that hardens in sunlight, an ablative sunscreen they slough nightly during interval.  
   • Respiratory upgrades: Two auxiliary sinuses open behind the cheekbones and act as countercurrent heat/moisture exchangers.  When a Plateau person speaks, their breath leaves almost as dry as it entered.

2. Senses  
   • Vision is amber-shifted; retinas are dominated by long-wave cones that can read minute temperature gradients on rock at forty paces.  
   • Hearing is broader toward infrasound to decode the conversations of turbines, worms and wind organs.

3. Language  
   Called “Sussur,” never written.  Consonants compress nicely through narrow basalt streets; vowels trail off into the abyss so they are mostly omitted.  Outsiders compare it to someone folding cloth.  Because paper is absurd and water-ink a crime, long contracts are sung into crystal so that its piezoelectric memory keeps the note for decades.

4. Social weave  
   • Household: A “kith” is six to ten adults sharing a common water quorum.  Water is not merely property; it is legal personality.  Children are admitted once their metabolism adds a measurable 3.5 mL/day to the pool.  
   • Calendar: 19-day lantern cycle; legal interval falls on the ninth night when the mites flash longest.  Marriages, audits, funerals, patent grants—everything decisive prefers that glow.  
   • Governance: The cantilever model.  Any decision demanding more than half a litre, one life or one turbine-day must name a balancing concession before it is debated.  The culture is literally founded on counter-weight.

5. Craft  
   • Aerolith-forging: They soften high-iron rhyolite inside evacuated kilns, shaping it at 730 °C where it flows like toffee, then quench it in a vacuum jar to make self-lubricating gear teeth.  
   • Salt batteries: Vent-glider phase salts confined in ceramic eggs.  Charge them in sunlight, crack them at night to run lifts.  The hiss of a draining salt cell is the lullaby of every Plateau child.  
   • Murmur cloth: A textile woven from latchwing pinions.  Drape it near a power conduit and it vibrates, converting stray EM into a soothing murmur that cloaks private speech.

6. Religion, if that is the word  
   They do not worship but they revere Contact—points where one phenomenon hands energy to another with zero loss.  A perfect gear mesh, a ferrum-breather colony nudging the wind just so, two people trading a joke that costs neither pride nor water: all are “clear links” and sacred.  The greatest festival is a silence kept during glassfall season, when thousands stand along the cliff rim in absolute stillness, trying to hear the instant contact breaks and the stone begins its last, pure flight.

V.  Edge Dwellers and Unconfirmed Reports  
1. The Hollow Signature  
   Far out across the plateau instruments detect a storage hall’s worth of empty volume moving north at twelve centimetres a year, as if a bubble were migrating through stone.  Echo worms avoid its path.  The Plateau Folk add a new glyph to their infrasonic warning chants but refuse to speak of it aloud.

2. Lughnar—living scaffolds  
   Occasionally cliff nests show buttresses grown, not built: trabecular bone riddled with copper that still metabolises trace tholin.  Genetic assay matches neither bird nor worm.  Workers splice rope bridges to them because they thicken helpfully under tensile load, as though the Expanse itself objected to empty air.

3. The Returning  
   Visitors who stay past one lantern cycle may dream of rain that fractures into dust mid-air.  Upon waking they cough up bright grit—micron sections of ferrum-breather cuticle.  Medics find no infection.  Plateau midwives just nod: “The Place tutors lungs before it trusts new minds.”

VI.  Closed Loops, Open Futures  
Everything alive on the High Expanse is a loop seeking closure: electrons into iron, sound into governance, heat into flight, salt into night-light, friendship into conserved breath.  There are predators, yes, and merchants, engineers, even rumors of migrating voids, but none can afford waste.  To waste is to evaporate.

So what lives here?  
• Rocks that think in rust.  
• Foxes that see with pulses of their own blood.  
• A people who measure personhood in millilitres and laws in counterweights.  
• Worms so slow the plateau uses them as stethoscopes.  
• Winged voltmeters, self-growing bridges, ephemeral contracts sung into quartz.  
All of them practicing, in variant dialects, the same long, lean grammar of survival: Speak little.  Spend nothing you cannot reclaim.  Let contact be perfect.  And rise—gently, inevitably—until the horizon is a wall behind you and silence is broad enough to house a city.
