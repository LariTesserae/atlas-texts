Who lives in the Flood Kiln?

1. The Brick-Milk Film  
   • Kind: A layered fellowship of lime-eating bacteria, sulfur-breathing archaea and hair-slim diatoms.  
   • Shape & Color: From far away it looks like opaline skin stretched along every grout line. Seen close it is a milky city of pillared domes, each no taller than a grain of sand, roofed by rainbow oxides.  
   • Work: The film drinks the fresh-slaked lime that bleeds from the joints. In doing so it precipitates calcium first into needles, then into interlocking plates—nature’s own mortar. What the masons could never tamp by hand the film stitches shut overnight. When a patch grows too fat, it exfoliates in nacreous flakes that flutter off into the current like spent parchment.  
   • Lifespan: A single cell lives half a week; the mat, continuously reborn, may outlast the stone itself.  
   • Motive: Hunger only, but the builders praise it as a tireless apprentice. Under the First Rule it is allowed because it labors.

2. Thread-red Alga (the Catwalk Moss)  
   • Kind: A colonial rhodophyte groomed from shoreline stock.  
   • Growth Site: It is lashed around the iron hoops and the underside of catwalk planks where sun strikes hardest.  
   • Work: Photosynthesis chokes glare that would otherwise bleach the film below. In death it drops rust-colored silt, feeding the film a trickle of iron.  
   • Behavior: At slack tide the threads unfurl like pennons; when the feeder chute roars, they reef themselves tight, clinging flat to iron.

3. The Stone-Nurses (human, or near enough)  
   • Appearance: Mottled with goose-grease, eyes rimmed red from brine, they tie their hair in waxed coils that will not drift.  
   • Number: Never more than four in the chamber at once—two working, two waiting above to spell them.  
   • Gear:  
     – Throat-engineered reed mouthpieces that carry just a palmful of air; they breathe, work, surface, breathe again.  
     – Flint-edge scrapers for flicking away overgrown film and sampling fresh stone.  
     – Copper bells filled with glow-oil; when hung on a pillar they slowly exhale light after the sun tunnels fade.  
   • Work-Cycle:  
     1. At slack water they drop in, shoes first, guided by a rope that sings like a lute string in the fall.  
     2. They listen more than they look. A healthy Kiln speaks in one continuous organ note; any hitch or whistle means blockage.  
     3. With mallets they tap each travertine course. Hollow thuds mark places where too much lime has leached away; those blocks will be replaced.  
     4. Before the paddles lift and flow resumes, they smear seed-paste (ground algae, slaked lime, goose fat) where the film has worn thin, then quit the pocket.  
   • Creed: “Craft before Nature,” but they do not count themselves as Nature; they are a mobile tool. As soon as the Kiln is cured they will move downslope to start another one, abandoning this chamber to drier trades.

4. The Blind Current (an organism in everything but flesh)  
   • Physiology: Pressure, heat and resonance.  
   • Behavior: It enters by the oculus, ricochets off the dais, divides, reunites, exits. Each circuit scours young stone and carries away debris.  
   • Sensory Faculty: None, yet the Stone-Nurses insist the Current can be lulled or angered. They claim the chamber’s drowned thunder is the creature’s breathing.  
   • Function: Without it there is no Kiln; with it, every surface is kept raw enough to mature.

5. Trespassers that fail the Rules  
   Occasionally a paper-bodied comb jelly or a seed shrimp slips through the screen. Warmth invites them to linger, but the flow is merciless. Soft bodies are dashed against stone, shells ground to grit. That grit is swallowed by the Film, reincarnated as mortar. Even trespass has been given a job.

What it is like to meet the inhabitants  
Stand on the catwalk during a lull. The Film glitters below like wet parchment; the Alga braids itself round iron in slow green knots. Two Stone-Nurses drift along the wall, heads cocked, listening for a wrong note. One raps the travertine; the other smears fresh paste that smokes white in the hot brine. Then the shutters groan open. The Blind Current inhales, the organ note swells, and all lesser sounds are drowned. The divers kick upward, algae stream backward like comet tails, the Film shivers, and the Flood Kiln goes back to the only thing that truly lives here: work.
