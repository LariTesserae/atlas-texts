LIFE IS EVERYWHERE, BUT NONE OF IT IS IDLE  
  
The makers left no forests or coral or prairie for evolution to tinker with, so the living things that occupy the plate-world learned to treat the machinery itself as soil, river, sky, and weather. They sort neatly into three grand guilds, each defined by how deeply it is allowed to reach into the built environment.

────────────────────────────────────────  
1. THE SKIN-DWELLERS  
(They never breach the surface film of condensate.)

• Glossworms  
  A glossworm is little more than a mouth-to-anus ribbon of transparent collagen. Cilia rake condensate toward a central gut where spores of the brass mycelia are sieved and digested. The worm’s only pigment—iridium flakes borrowed from its diet—lines a spinal seam; when thousands school together, the runnels shine like braided chrome. Courtship is chemical: each worm releases a droplet rich in trace metals, and partners sample one another’s taste by knifing briefly side-to-side through the droplet. Fertilized eggs are pressed to the underside of the water’s meniscus and hatch in forty-nine hours.

• Mote Crabs  
  Smaller than a fingernail, five-limbed, jet black. They spend their whole lives holding the tension skin of a puddle flat so that tools further up the chain can see perfect reflections for alignment checks. When a puddle evaporates past optimum thickness the crabs disassemble themselves; their dissolved chitin lowers the surface tension enough for new condensation to arrive.

• Tarnish Lace  
  Strictly speaking a biofilm, but dense enough to seem animal when it billows. Lace grows in fractal fans along the rims of tiles and drinks electric potential, not water; two millimeters of lace can bleed a volt of stray current out of the floor. Anything metal that dies on the plain is wrapped in lace within a day and rendered into a feathery green oxide.

────────────────────────────────────────  
2. THE SEAM-KEEPERS  
(They may open a plate, but never below the fourth layer.)

• Archivores  
  Tripedal and pot-bellied, with faces like inverted funnels. Their hide is matte basalt, secreted one scale at a time from specialized salivary glands. Each archivore is imprinted at birth with a single topological pattern—a signature—against which it will measure every fracture it meets. When a break mismatches its signature the beast calls others by drumming its hollow torso, and together they decide whose glyph best fits the wound. In this way every repaired crack carries the biography of the archivores that tended it.

• Coil Gulls  
  The cable-plaited wings are only half the story. Inside each gull is a parabolic throat lined with piezoelectric plates. They fly with mouth agape, harvesting the sub-audible hum of the turbines below. The stolen vibration powers both muscle and brain; when the ground hum quiets for maintenance the gulls drop from the air like unstrung kites and wait in rigid heaps until the note resumes.

• Calderas  
  Imagine a turtle made of origami steam vents. Calderas weigh perhaps forty kilograms and move once a month, but wherever they pause the floor around them blisters upward into a shallow cone. The creature’s vented breath leaches carbon from the substrate, vitrifying the cone into smoky glass that will remain perfectly hydrophobic for ninety-six days—long enough to protect a vulnerable hatchling cohort of smaller species that shelter beneath it.

────────────────────────────────────────  
3. THE CLOUD-BOUND  
(They never touch ground unless broken.)

• Murmuration Spawn  
  The glassy marbles the traveller sees are merely ferry stages. True adulthood happens aloft, where a lattice of a thousand spores fuses into a sail twenty meters across. That sail is conductive: it teases charge from the mist, storing it until overpressure triggers a dazzling white corona. The flash sterilizes airborne bacteria and re-primes the weather pumps. Spent sails snowball slowly downward, break on a monolith, and shatter into the familiar rolling spheres that begin the loop again.

• Vapor Leeches  
  Needle-fine threads kilometres long. They hang from the cloud ceiling like vertical piano wires, drinking trace organics from passing mist. Their upper tips anchor inside the luminaires where a photosensor watches the leech’s fullness; if thread mass drops below spec, the overhead lights dim two percent, herding coil gulls upward to brush the threads and repopulate them with spores and dust.

• The Uncounted Choir  
  No eyes have seen them directly. Instead, witnesses report a moment each fortnight when the entire cloud deck brightens by a subjective heartbeat and every animal on the ground falls still. Instruments register a 521-hertz pulse, rich in overtones that map perfectly onto the floor’s hidden conveyor routes—an acoustic inventory check. The Choir may be a single organism or a synchronized swarm, but its existence is known chiefly by what ends when the note is sung: every dead coil gull, every cracked tile, every orphan droplet has vanished by the time normal sound returns.

────────────────────────────────────────  
THE OUTLIERS  

• Pilgrim Copies  
  The ecosystem produces iconographies of anything that remains overnight. Leave a backpack, a corpse, a sentence scratched in dust, and by dawn there will be second and third instances arranged in expanding hexagons across the plain. The duplicates are anatomically perfect but psychologically vacant—robots made of flash-fused dew and powdered substrate, animated only long enough to walk eight or nine steps before stiffening into statuary. Archivores later recycle them. Some theorize that true native sapients walked here once and were replicated into oblivion.

• The Fourth-Layer Hermit  
  Rare seismic thumps suggest something titanic lives directly beneath the legal boundary at layer four, forever curling along the underside of the world-floor. Plates above an advancing segment arrive pre-etched with faint, sinuous ridges as though the creature’s shadow burns through the manufacturing queue. Coil gulls perch on these ridges and whistle fragments of their call in microtonal scales they use nowhere else. No repair drone is known to follow the Hermit; its infractions are apparently grandfathered in.

────────────────────────────────────────  
ON CO-EXISTENCE WITH HUMAN VISITORS  

A human body is large enough to be landscape yet delicate enough to violate Rules 1–5 in a dozen innocent ways. The resident fauna handle the contradiction via ignore, adapt, erase.

• Ignore: Glossworms simply flow around bootprints.  
• Adapt: Archivores memorize the pressure pattern of your footfall; after you depart, new tiles will arrive textured with that fingerprint so that future travellers’ weight is distributed exactly as yours was, avoiding stresses the system now considers normative.  
• Erase: Anything louder than your speaking voice is unacceptable. The second time you slam metal on metal, coil gulls will gather and siphon the kinetic energy of your action, deadening every impact thereafter into a padded hush.

Stay long enough and you, too, become choreography. Your camp gear is mirrored in stainless tableaux, your breath is sampled by Vapor Leeches, and when you finally sleep the cloud ceiling brightens fractionally—as if the Uncounted Choir just added one small, warm datum to its perfect ledger of what lives here.

That, then, is who lives: not a kingdom or a hierarchy but a braided circuit of needs, permissions, and prohibitions—animals, quasi-plants, and unspoken custodians all evolved, or engineered, to keep the world exactly as it is while still allowing it to teem.
