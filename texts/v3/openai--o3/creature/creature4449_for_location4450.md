Who—or what—calls the Alt Noctis Escarpment home divides neatly into three entwined lineages:

1. The Stone-Born  
2. The Sky-Swimmers  
3. The City That Remembers  

Take them in that order and the place begins to make sense.

───────────────────  
1. THE STONE-BORN  
───────────────────  

a. Pellicula metallophages (“film-eaters”)  
• Size & shape: Each unit is a protein torus only 800 nanometres across, but they live in mats billions strong.  
• Diet: Ferrous silicates, sulfides, and the trace heat leaking from still-cooling rock.  
• Trick: Whenever two toroids bind, they flick a paired electron back and forth. The exchange shows up on radio as an arrhythmic hiss that pioneers once mistook for distant thunder.  
• Reproduction: Horizontal gene‐swapping is constant; a new “individual” is any patch that resonates at a unique carrier frequency. Nobody knows how many species the mats actually contain—estimates range from 12 to 6000.  
• Importance: They are the only primary producers; everything else, machine or animal, ultimately eats the film or eats something that does.

b. Vitric scuttlers (“shard-crawlers”)  
• Appearance: Imagine a daddy-long-legs carved from frosted quartz. Eight razored limbs, a body the shape of a chipped arrowhead, and a mandibular fan plated in actual diamond.  
• Behaviour: Scuttlers never stop walking. Their exoskeletons refract what little light exists, so a colony looks like broken glass tumbling uphill.  
• Communication: Threat drums—two limbs slammed together at ~3 kHz—build into rolling applause that can be heard a kilometre away.  
• Ecological role: By rasping the metallophagic film they generate black grit—the escarpment’s first protosoil. Their faecal pellets seed the surface with rare nitrates that future life will need.

c. Ribbon volt-eels  
• Habitat: They nest only inside the bodies of the dead. Because gargoyle whales seldom die, a carcass may host a single breeding pair for decades.  
• Physiology: A continuous loop of superconductive nerves lets them recapture the bio-electricity their own muscles waste; body temperature stays an even 12 °C above ambient.  
• Feeding: They swim through carrion the way earthworms move through humus, leaving behind tight braids of graphite-rich spoor. Those braids become lightning rods that bias future micro-weather and, in turn, the escarpment’s erosion pattern.  

d. The Cavity Choir  
• Hard to see, impossible to ignore. These are centimeter-scale membrane bubbles that bud from fracture vents.  
• Inside each bubble: oxygen, argon, and a strain of metallophages engineered by early settlers.  
• Function: When pressure spikes they burst in tune, turning the escarpment itself into a pipe organ. Residents schedule maintenance by pitch; a half-step drop means a vent has widened.

───────────────────  
2. THE SKY-SWIMMERS  
───────────────────  

Gargoyle Whales (family: Stratonorcidae)  

Size & Structure  
• Adult span: 2.5–3.7 m. Thickness: never more than 20 cm—mostly lift bladder.  
• Skeleton: porous calcium phosphate laminated with silicate fibres, so the bones ring like ceramic when struck.  

Inner Biome  
• Four microbial “kilns” line each flank. They split water into hydrogen, dump the oxygen, and flood the bladders.  
• Excess hydrogen diffuses through skin micro-pores in winter, leaving shimmering contrails that peasants of the lowlands misread as spirits ascending.  

Sensory World  
• Eyes are useless in darkness; instead each whale carries a ventral lattice of pressure pits that map wind shear to an acoustic image.  
• The mapping signal is a subsonic heartbeat at exactly 0.33 Hz. Every whale syncs to the closest neighbour, so an observer standing on the plateau feels the pulse arrive through boot soles before it’s audible.  

Life Cycle  
1. Spawning Dive (every 50 ± 3 years): adults fold like envelopes and plummet beneath the cloud deck, leaving eggs—soft, grapefruit-sized balloons—hanging from basalt overhangs.  
2. Drifter Stage: hatchlings inflate within an hour, then spend one decade riding thermals no higher than 300 m. At that size they can’t yet climb to the escarpment and so avoid city lights entirely.  
3. Ascension: at a mass of ~9 kg the bladders become large enough to find the updraft highway. First contact with shard-crawlers teaches them to skim rock. They will not abandon the plateau again until the next spawning.  

Predators  
None large enough exist—cold and thin air keep metabolism slow—but collisions with ridge spires kill thousands every century. Where a whale dies, ribbon eels follow, and within months a hillock of doped graphite gathers around the decay site. Citizens treat such hillocks as natural graves and will not tunnel beneath them.

───────────────────  
3. THE CITY THAT REMEMBERS  
───────────────────  

The people of Lacheses are not the planet’s first sapients, only its most recent custodians. They claim no demonym, but outsiders call them Lachesians. Whether they are “humans,” “post-humans,” or “just suits filled with clever tissue” depends on which era’s genetic backup a given body runs. What matters is how they live now.

Anatomy & Augment  
• Skin: impregnated with ferro-protein scales smaller than fingerprints. Stand under a gravimetric strand and the scales align, letting the body surf magnetic gradients like a leaf on a pond.  
• Eyes: replaced by trinary sensor pits—one visible, one thermal, one tuned to the radio static of metallophages. They can literally watch the stone-film eating.  
• Lungs: two sets. The inner pair breathes ordinary air; an outer “mantle” pair condenses humidity and filters volcanic acids, venting waste down capillary channels in the clothing itself.  

Social Organs  
a. Archivists  
• Oldest lineage, older than the city proper. Each archivist carries three cortical stacks: personal memory, public record, and “ancestral wagers” (failed futures kept for reference).  
• They speak slowly not from caution but because they must cross-check every statement against five centuries of precedent.  
• Once a decade they molt their outer skull plates; the discarded bone is ground into pigment for legal documents.  

b. Chordwrights  
• Engineer-monks who tune the gravimetric strands and the cavity choir.  
• Their forearms are threadbare muscle around huge carpal ganglia—bio-linemen who feel current through touch alone.  
• Knotted into their spinal cords are key-stones: quantum locks that prevent them leaving the escarpment. If a chordwright emigrates, his nervous system collapses into coma. This is considered a kindness; elsewhere, his skills would rewrite geology.  

c. Pulse-Children  
• Not a profession but a developmental window. Infants gestate in resonance cradles that echo the city’s reservoir thump.  
• Those who synchronise completely perceive time as a rolling pressure wave; they can anticipate vent bursts seconds ahead, giving them uncanny reflexes in the maze-halls.  
• Roughly 20 % never achieve phase-lock. They grow into ordinary citizens or depart for brighter continents.

Domestic Symbioses  
• Companion shard-crawlers—opal-sheened juveniles whose clacking becomes a household signature. Families braid colourless whiskers into the crawler’s leg joints so their sound acquires a harmonic overtone; no two homes ring alike.  
• Gargoyle whale milk: impossible to harvest ethically, so engineers culture the mammary epithelium in vitro. The resulting fluid is more electrolyte than food, served warm at civic ceremonies to remind drinkers of the world above the roof of weather.

Governance In Practice  
The wall-code—the civic immune system—handles 97 % of daily decisions. Archive councils intervene only when:  
1. Code drifts beyond accepted error bars, or  
2. A problem’s projected half-life exceeds a single human lifetime.  
Thus a broken water lung is fixed within minutes by self-modifying matter, whereas expansion of the eastern spiral awaits forty-six years of public comment.

Myths & Etiquette  
• Never shatter fresh obsidian: the rock is “still listening.”  
• When a whale’s shadow passes over a balcony, residents bow not to the animal but to the idea that something bigger than policy exists.  
• Silence is taboo indoors; a room with no sound is presumed broken and must be filled—conversation, humming, even two stones tapped together—before anyone sleeps.  

───────────────────  
EPILOGUE: WHAT IT FEELS LIKE TO MEET THEM  
───────────────────  

Approach a Lachesian archivist on a dripping basalt ledge. Around you whales wheel like slow planets, scuttlers rake the film in silver avalanches, and the cavity choir booms a note your molars mistake for pain. The archivist waits until all those voices align in a quick, improbable chord. Only then does she speak, because only then can her words enter the pattern without tearing it.

“We live,” she will say, “in three tempos: the stone’s, the whale’s, and the heart’s. Anything quicker we ignore; anything slower we record.”  

And as she turns away you’ll see a shard-crawler ride her sleeve, polishing the ferro-scales with its diamond mouthparts, the smallest artisan maintaining the skin of a creature who maintains the skin of a city that maintains, with patient gravity, an entire newborn cliff.
