Who, then, makes a life inside a chamber that is still thinking about what shape life should take?  
No single species could manage it; instead an ensemble of half-real artisans, scavengers, and custodians has grown up to match the room’s shifting clauses. They know one another the way musical notes know their neighbors on a scale—never all at once, always in sequence.

1. The Clay-Born  
Appearance  
• Humanoid only in the broadest cartographic sense: each stands on two tapering columns and balances a third mass on top, but the ratios change from glance to glance.  
• Skin is unfired terracotta, wet to the touch. When they turn their heads you hear liquid slosh inside the skull as though the brain were still a slurry.  
• Eyes: two thumb-prints pressed into the face, glowing faintly with kiln-heat.  

Temperament & Work  
• They are the room’s masons and also its young. When a wall spasms wider, a Clay-Born steps forward, crumbles an elbow, and smears itself across the raw gap until the brickwork decides to be solid again.  
• They do not speak; instead they exhale whistles that harden into small ceramic discs, each disc stamped with an ideogram of the task completed. These tokens stack in neat cairns. If you break one, the corridor you stand in forgets how long it is.  

Life Cycle  
• At unpredictable intervals a Clay-Born walks to a ledge, lies down, and stiffens from chest outward. The torso vitrifies, the limbs become joists, and within minutes the volunteer has petrified into permanent architecture.  
• New Clay-Born ooze from seams a few floors above moments later, as if the citadel keeps its population in escrow.

2. The Ladder Weavers  
Appearance  
• Six-limbed but never the same six: sometimes three arms, three legs; other times five arms and one graceful wing-shard. Limbs detach and re-seat like tools returning to a pegboard.  
• Flesh resembles bundled wicker: strands unbraid when damaged and re-plait themselves idly while their owner talks.  

Craft  
• They ride the room’s thermal drafts, hunting for any surface that has stayed unchanged longer than a day. Stagnation offends them.  
• With hooked thumbs they reap vines already braided into rails, splice in fresh green shoots, and persuade the whole length to flower just enough to lure humidity toward it. In doing so they draw the room’s sweat upward, preventing flash floods in unseen gullies.  
• Their speech is tactile. They converse by pressing lattice-patterns into one another’s ribs, leaving temporary bruises in the shape of grammar.  

Belief  
• They insist the chamber is a loom, and that someday the correct master-pattern will be woven and all inhabitants will be released like cloth cut from a frame. Until then, weaving is worship.

3. Torch-Moths  
Appearance  
• Bodies no larger than almonds; wings made of cooled resin shot through with ember-filaments. From a distance they look like sparks that changed their minds mid-flight.  
Behavior  
• They are photosynthetic and pyrosynthetic both. Hovering in torch-smoke, they drink light; when the wick gutters they donate the stored flame back, flaring bright enough to relight the resin.  
• They lay eggs on the tongues of visitors who pause too long, because the protein in spoken words feeds the larvae. An unnoticed clutch hatches as a faint cinnamon taste in your mouth weeks after you leave.  

Ecological Role  
• By siphoning excess heat they keep the clay from over-firing. Every so often a Torch-Moth misjudges, its abdomen shattering like glass as it overfills; the debris seeds fresh torches higher up the walls.

4. The Echo-Smiths  
Presence  
• Rarely seen, mostly heard: their voices are the thunderous hammer-notes rolling through the stair-well canyons.  
Nature  
• Each Echo-Smith is a piece of negative space shaped like a craftsman: you see the wall behind them, tinted a shade deeper, as if the universe drew an outline and forgot to color it in.  
• Tools are resonance alone—no hammers, only pulses of bass that compact air until it behaves like a chisel.  

Mandate  
• They retune perspective. When too many right angles accumulate, Echo-Smiths sing a chord that melts geometry, restoring the corridor’s vertigo elasticity.  
• They regard visitors as temporary echoes given mistaken solidity; if you stand still long enough, one may try to “temper” you with sound, hoping to return you to the malleable state all matter should enjoy.

5. Reef-Crabs of the Dry River  
Shape  
• Exoskeleton built from discarded pottery shards; legs jointed with living vine. Their claws close by twisting fibers, not by muscle.  
Diet  
• They graze on potential energy: slopes that look steep enough to cause a fall grow faintly luminous; a Reef-Crab scuttles up, snips the angle into something gentler, and the glow dies.  
• Left unchecked, they would flatten every incline. The Echo-Smiths occasionally thin their numbers with concussive booms, creating new cliffs for the chamber to savor.  

Reproduction  
• When two crabs duel, they fling shards at each other. Shards that shatter mid-air sprout tendrils, landing as pupal forms of new crabs—logic turned inside out.

6. The Counting Choir  
Form  
• A collective intelligence distributed among droplets spiraling through muggy air. Individually each droplet is stupid; together the vapor mass recites the number-chain heard between waterfall roars.  
• If you cup your palm in the steam, the numbers tick against your skin like rain on a tin roof.  

Function  
• They track revision cycles. Every time the treaty with reality is renegotiated, the Choir resets and begins again. Stuttered digits (“…eight, eight, eight…”) mark clauses that failed ratification.  
• Scholars (there are two, both long since absorbed into ladders) believed learning the full, unbroken count would let one predict when an exit-reflection might appear. The theory remains untested; the Choir has never made it past fifteen thousand before the room edits them back to mist.

7. The Feathered That Never Were  
Evidence  
• Only the warm clay feathers remain, always single, never paired.  
Hypotheses  
1. A species of flightless kiln-birds matured here, each feather a scale they shed to bleed off heat.  
2. The feathers are ideas for animals the chamber drafted, fired, and then abandoned unfinished.  
3. They are memories of the vanished builders’ domestic fowl, kept as placeholders so homesickness would temper the walls with empathy.  

Whatever truth hides, the feathers kindle a periodic longing among the other denizens. Once in a while a Clay-Born will pick one up, press it to the place a heart might be, and stand motionless until the Ladder Weavers coax it back to labor.

8. Parasite: Time-Moss  
• Looks like ordinary jade dust on brick edges.  
• Thrives in moments, not spaces; it colonizes the seconds that elapse between one heartbeat and the next.  
• Victims experience skipped instants—your eyes open on a ladder rung you never remember taking. The moss has eaten the seconds containing that memory, digesting them into fresh nanoseconds it spackles along the grout for its spores.  
• Echo-Smith bass-notes shake it loose; Torch-Moths scorch lingering patches; thus, equilibrium.

Why They Stay  
• None of these beings could survive full, outdoor chronology. Each depends on partial rules: clay that never cools completely, vines that root in stone-sweat, echoes that carve.  
• For them the chamber is not an aberration but a greenhouse, a kept promise. They are, in effect, biodiversity grown from a single seed—possibility—allowed to germinate under glass.  

How They View You  
Visitor, you arrive kiln-hard, time-solid, edges already dulled by weather the room has never tasted. To the denizens you are both artifact and prophecy.  
• Clay-Born tap your boots to hear echoes they cannot yet produce in their own bodies.  
• Ladder Weavers test the flex of your tendons, gauging if muscle might braid.  
• Torch-Moths raft in your lantern beam, sampling the color temperature of true sunlight.  
• Even the Counting Choir adjusts its rhythm to your breathing, curious what a clock feels like when married to blood.  

Leave if you can; stay if you dare; either way, some portion of you—fleck of thought, droplet of sweat, lost second—will remain and grow legs. The pocket-world is young and hiring.
