Residents of the Plateau of Fabricated Green  

0. A caution  
The Plateau is habit, not habitat. Everything that endures here has given up the idea of private territory and learned instead to be a gesture inside a single, planet-sized sentence. Those who refused were simply edited out, absorbed, or blown off the margin. What follows, then, is less a census than the grammar of lives that have agreed to be conjugated by chlorophyll.

1. The Plateau Itself: Arbor-Corpus Continuum  
Taxonomists with clipboards insist on naming it “Hemeranthus architectonis,” but the plant does not end where its cell walls end. Sap flows in calibrated pulses that ripple all the way from the youngest foliar lace at the rim to the oldest lignin vaults near the hub. Those pulses are not merely hydraulics; they are timed, bifurcating voltages of potassium and calcium that behave uncannily like a slow nervous system.

• Cognition  
Every thirty-three minutes the pressure-waves intersect each other in the central transept. The interference patterns match, in frequency and phase, simple Boolean logic gates. Recordings fed into machine‐learning interpreters reveal syntax: water-level reports, shear-stress warnings, even mellow stretches of non-directive humming that resemble lullabies the Plateau sings to itself. It does not speak to outsiders. You must squat, barefoot, on an active vein to feel the words shudder up your bones, and even then meaning arrives only as mood.

• Dreaming  
At local midnight, the Plateau pulls most of its sugars out of circulation and lets the big trunks go slack. Researchers on overnight watches see entire cathedral arcs settle by a metre, then rebound before dawn. Seismographs translate that slow settling into a topography of micro-tremors indistinguishable from EEG readings of mammalian REM sleep. The Plateau is asleep half its life, yet nothing collapses; flexion is the dream.

2. The Gleamwings (Order: Vitroalae)  
In sunlight they are slices of ruby, citrine and tourmaline glass. Each adult is a living delta-wing no longer than a human thumb, grown from folded prismatic plates instead of chitinous shell.

• Feeding strategy  
They do not bite or pierce. Capillary tongues press flat against hairline fissures in the cellulose and ride the outbound tide of excess phloem. Osmotic pressure does the work; the insect is simply a licking prism.

• Songlines  
Their wings are tuned to break wind into ultrasonics. A swarm sweeping across a buttress writes a map in Doppler interference. Other Gleamwings read the resulting echo-lace to locate fresh seepage zones. In short: they sing, then drink the song.

• Lifecycle  
Egg → larval “chip” that etches snug, translucent burrows in secondary xylem → metamorphosis triggered by reaching the main vascular highway → emergence through self-sealing corolla-valves that open like stained-glass rose windows and close without a scar.

3. The Rust Parchment (“glider-mammal,” Genus: Aerofelis)  
Visual: a flying carpet stitched from old fox pelts, four limbs splayed to anchor a single membranous sheet. Weight: 600 grams at maturity.  

• Never landing  
Pads secrete a bio-cement that bonds to the Plateau too eagerly—evolution’s blunt memo: “Avoid contact.” Youngsters are born live in the air; mothers invert, the infant hits slipstream at first breath, and the umbilical line furls away like a spent kite string.

• Diet  
Aerial plankton: spores, mist-gnats, wind-blown pollen from far lower elevations. The animals tack across the permanent up-drafts generated by rim-falls, tracing turquoise thermals only they can see.

• Sociality  
They meet once a year above the central hub. Tens of thousands interlock membranes into a single russet mandala that hovers for two nights. No one knows why. The Plateau responds by lowering transpiration, calming its air so the dance can proceed—an annual pact between flesh that lands and flesh that won’t.

4. The Quiet Kite (Family: Audiovermis)  
A predator shaped like an oversized origami swallow, bone whitish, skin chalk-matte to cancel glare.  

• Hunting algorithm  
The Quiet Kite is deaf to ordinary frequencies but has enlarged cochleae for infrasonic detection. It listens for micro-stutters in the Plateau’s vascular hum—the moment a Gleamwing bites too deep or a Rust Parchment winglet scars the glaze. Silence breaks equal food. Dive speed exceeds 320 km/h; the kill is a single impact that stuns prey rather than rends it. Flesh is swallowed whole to avoid noisy tearing.

• Nesting  
Built of regurgitated cellulose filaments kneaded to a felt and glued under overhanging rib-arches. The nests dangle, muffling any chick chatter inside a fibrous anechoic chamber. Even infancy is hush.

5. The Reclaimers: Endophytic Mycelia Consortium  
Not quite fungus, not quite root, these threads live already inside every living wall.

• Purpose  
They digest any sector the Plateau labels redundant. A frost-snapped buttress? Mycelia bloom overnight, liquefy it, pump the slurry back into growth fronts. Death experiences a turnaround time of 24–36 hours.

• Bioluminescence  
In periods of nutrient surplus, hyphae exude cold indigo light, tracing fault-lines the Plateau means to retire. To resident flyers, such glow is a DO NOT LAND sign; to field biologists, it is an execution notice.

6. Visitors Who Stay  

6.1 The Verdigris Monks  
Humans, or once-humans, who arrived for study and never left. Over decades, coppery films of enzymatic patina plated their tools, then their clothes, then their skin folds. Spore swarms colonised sweat glands. By the third generation their children are green-veined, photosynthetic for up to 40 % of basal metabolism, and exhale sweet terpene breath. They keep no tents—only hammock-cradles strung across sap-glazed transepts. Their language is whistle, so it braids through windgrooves without echo.

6.2 Graft-Ghosts  
Every so often an injured climber or lost aviator is taken in. The Plateau knits cellulose splints around broken bone, seals open flesh with living lacquer. Most guests leave. A few remain, legs sheathed permanently in vascular callus, torsos honeycombed with xylem scaffolding that delivers water and simple sugars direct to blood. They become half-rooted sentinels along the rim, staring outward with pupils that have chloroplasts floating in aqueous humor, needing only mist and an occasional moth to persist. They are no longer self-moving, but wind bending their hair is enough for them to feel they are still traveling.

7. Microfauna and Sub-Audible Citizens  

• Vespertine Mites: silver specks that skate the meniscus of standing leaf-water, harvesting shed epidermal cells.  
• Copper Siphons: anaerobic bacteria living in de-oxygenated back-eddies of sap, precipitating mint-green nanoscale wires that reinforce local capillaries—bio-engineers employed from within.  
• Null Bats: sonar frequencies tuned to cancel rather than echo, allowing them to navigate by absence. They roost inside the hollow of organ-pipes where wind harmonics hurt bigger ears.

8. Interaction Web  
Nothing here competes in the terrestrial sense. The Plateau allocates. Gleamwings siphon only overflow; Parchments prune pests by aerial grazing; Quiet Kites cull the reckless. Verdigris Monks collect nothing that is not freely exuded. Even the Reclaimers wait for explicit chemical go-signals before dismantling a limb. Predation, decomposition, reproduction—all are orchestration, sections of one score conducted by slow pulses of root-logic.

9. Epilogue: Listening for the Population  
Stand barefoot again. Feel the tremor? That is everyone speaking at once: tree, insect prism, silence hunter, parchment glider, photosynthetic monk, wire-spinning bacterium. The Plateau does not host life; it composes it. Each resident, whether furred, veined, or mycelial, is a note struck on a living instrument four thousand metres above the world, sustained long enough to be beautiful, then folded back into the swell.

If asked how many creatures live upon the Plateau of Fabricated Green, the only honest answer is one—spoken in a chorus of many mouths.
