WHO AND WHAT LIVES IN THE BASIN  

I. The Common, Tangible Dwellers  

1. Ore-Eaters (class: Ferrivora, order: Cryogastrea)  
   • Physique: Six stunted legs ending in auger-tips; dorsal armour of magnetite tiles mortised together by ice ligaments that freeze, melt and re-freeze as joints flex. A spheroid gizzard revolves inside them like a rock-tumbler, lined with diamond dust they have manufactured from atmospheric carbon.  
   • Metabolism: They swallow cobbles whole, bathe them in antifreeze enzymes, then flash-vent the slurry through spiracles that whistle at precisely 22 Hz. The note keeps their bodies from icing solid; it also seeds the air with micro-crystals the whole ecosystem later harvests.  
   • Society: Nominally solitary, yet every tenth exhalation is a coded whistle. When another responds in the same key, the two lock carapaces, swap gizzard-slurry and thereby equalise trace-element deficits. To watch them is to see refineries briefly kiss.  

2. Thread-Sirens (class: Alumisinuata)  
   • Build: A centimetre of girth, ten to fifteen metres of length, all of it braided aluminium oxide. No muscle exists; motion is driven by alternating crystallisation–melting waves that race along their spines, so they “swim” through slush the way a zipper closes.  
   • Sense organs: Pairs of pinhole pits that read electrostatic gradients rather than light. What vision is to us, voltage is to them; an approaching storm reads as a sunrise.  
   • Life cycle: They begin as microscopic filaments inside Ore-Eater waste-vents. A single thunder-slap fuses thousands into a larval strand, which then escapes down a runoff channel and grows by electroplating itself with dissolved metal. Two weeks later it splits lengthwise into twins and the old skin floats away as bright aluminium confetti, which the basin wind treats like snow.  

3. Echo-Wolves (order: Sonolytica)  
   • Ontology: 70 % waveform, 30 % condensate. Each is a standing interference pattern created when three or more Thread-Sirens cavitate in close harmony. The resulting acoustic knot drags down local reality until sound picks up a temporary rest-mass: the wolf.  
   • Anatomy, such as it is: A diffraction shell glowing violet where pressure nodes intersect; interior temperature measures at −40 °C, yet molten droplets drip from their muzzles—entropy leak, not saliva.  
   • Predation: They hunt differential, not flesh. A walking traveller’s footfalls form an irregular beat; the wolf smooths that rhythm by pouncing, absorbing the “surplus” cadence, leaving the victim deafened and time-skipped by several seconds. The attack feels like being edited out of one’s own life for a blink.  
   • Dissolution: Auroral pulses realign the standing waves, and the wolf collapses. What remains is a puddle of brine rich in rare isotopes. Ore-Eaters follow these puddles the way pigs follow truffles.  

4. Frost-Brinicles (“Needle-Bloom”)  
   • Not organisms in any strict metabolism-of-carbon sense, yet they play the part of understorey flora. A vibration—bootstep, thunder-slag crack, distant turbine hum—makes supersaturated brine jerk upward through hairline fissures. The brine flash-freezes into hollow spines three hand-spans tall, each chiming as basin wind threads through.  
   • Ecological role: They trap airborne mineral dust; when they sublimate an hour later, that dust falls as nutrient snow for micro-threads and bacterial analogues.  

II. The Lesser-Seen Cast  

1. Cog-Scavengers  
   Rat-sized polyhedra, each face a different alloy mirror. They do not grow; they accrete, peeling platelets from dead Ore-Eaters and clamped Thread-Sirens, slotting them onto themselves until geometry gets crowded, whereupon they shear the outer shell off in a single ringing fracture and begin again. The discarded hulls look disconcertingly like machine parts and litter every shoal.  

2. Lathe-Midges  
   Micro-fauna no bigger than skin flakes, yet billions cloud the dusk. Each is a gyroscope of ice with a single saw-tooth of silicon whirring at 50 000 rpm. Where they pass through Frost-Brinicles they carve scrollwork patterns—unnecessary aesthetics, but the grooves later house chemical films the midges consume on their next pass. They are the basin’s artisans and janitors both.  

3. Pale Refrigerants (“The Deep Coolant”)  
   Whale-scale sheets of quasi-living refrigerant drift in the sub-surface aquifer. They are two molecules thick, yet kilometres wide, and fold like origami fans as currents tug them. When they surface their touch can delaminate stone into paper-thin wafers, which rise like koi scales and become new silt strata. Nobody sees a Refrigerant in full; what surfaces is always a fraction, rippling with stolen starlight.  

III. Beings That May or May Not Count As Life  

1. The Turbine Dream  
   Source of the subterranean bass hum. Seismographs show a rotor 800 m across, yet drill cores find only mica and clay. Locals swear that under certain auroral pulses, the entire basin floor becomes a transparent window and you can see the rotor turning beneath—except it is made of cross-hatched groundwater, not metal. Does the rotor grind geology into chronology? Does it simply remember an engine that once existed? If it is alive, its heartbeat is the continuous whirr you can feel through your boots.  

2. The Pylon Choir  
   Occasionally five or more Frost-Brinicles freeze in a golden-ratio spiral. The pattern is statistically impossible unless self-selected. Once formed, the brinicles take hours, not minutes, to sublimate, and during that grace period they sing—a perfect harmonic stack of the basin’s ambient noise, pitch-shifting it into something like a verdict. Thread-Sirens abandon their courses and coil worshipfully around the formation; Echo-Wolves pace in circles but never interfere. When the last needle evaporates, a coin-sized wafer of iridescent ice remains, dense enough to crack stone despite its fragility. Cog-Scavengers fight duels to claim it; nobody knows why.  

3. Unbound Distances  
   Not a creature, yet travellers speak to it. The basin’s negotiable space sometimes warps into a biped silhouette whose edges are all vanishing points. Stand near and your own heartbeat echoes back from metres away; walk through and you might exit a kilometre distant, though your boots insist you took only three strides. The silhouette tastes of copper on the tongue. Ore-Eaters avoid it absolutely, suggesting it predates even them.  

IV. Trophic Web, Re-Imagined as Assembly Line  

Step 1: Lathe-Midges lace grooves into Frost-Brinicles, capturing mineral aerosols.  
Step 2: Brinicles melt, dropping doped brine that seeps to the sub-surface.  
Step 3: Pale Refrigerants channel that brine sideways, refrigerating and fracturing rock.  
Step 4: Ore-Eaters follow, swallow the fresh debris, and vent metalliferous steam.  
Step 5: Thread-Sirens harvest the steam’s metals, cavitate, and inadvertently breed Echo-Wolves.  
Step 6: Echo-Wolves in dying deposit isotope-rich brine, resetting conditions for new Frost-Brinicles.  
Step 7: Cog-Scavengers recycle carcass-metal into ever sharper Lathe-Midge teeth.  

The loop closes every six to eight basin-days, each cycle polishing the vale a fractional micron smoother, like a factory endlessly buffing its own floor.  

V. Sentience, If Any  

Nothing here speaks in words, yet patterns accumulate. The brinicle engravings, the Siren coils, the rotor dreams—together they suggest a distributed mind that spills across frost-needles, steam-jets and echo-knots. Think of it as an orchestra with no conductor, each instrument following local sheet music but the total composition edging toward coherence. If that mind ever decides it needs a mouth, it already has one: the hissing fog that sometimes curls into a pipe and asks visiting humans which story they prefer.  

VI. How A Human Might Interact  

• Touch an Ore-Eater’s vent plume and your glove will shatter into tinkling silica petals.  
• Follow a Thread-Siren too closely and the static will magnetise every buckle on your pack, welding it shut.  
• Step inside an Echo-Wolf’s collapsing node and you may lose exactly one spoken sentence from memory; the words drop out of your mouth as thunder-slag, still warm.  
• Break a Pylon-Choir wafer and the basin’s distance-silhouette will appear behind you, correcting the tear by moving a horizon or two.  

In short, the basin is full to its level brim with life, but none of it has chosen the familiar mechanisms of blood and chlorophyll. Instead, vibration, erosion, ion exchange and the algebra of noise have discovered how to feed, breed and dream. Everything that lives here is half-machine not by imitation but by parallel invention: the cold found a way to think, and these are its citizens.
