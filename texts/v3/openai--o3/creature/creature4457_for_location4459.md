WHO OR WHAT LIVES HERE

0.  A note on the word “life”  
   Breath, blood, appetite―none of that applies.  In the blast-furnace pocket, survival belongs to things that stay exactly as they are.  What follows is the roster of those self-consistent entities, ranked from the obvious to the nearly notional.

1.  The Hyalosyne Mat (“glass moss”) – sole fixed vegetation, yet more than a plant  
   • Architecture  
     Each blade is a wedge of micro-layered silica shot through with vanadium dopants.  Look close and you will see stepped terraces like a stadium seen edge-on; electrons hop across the tiers when the blade is tickled by static or by the faint violet of the ceiling arc.  
   • Metabolism  
     Photosynthesis is useless under soot-filtered twilight, so the mat runs on tribo-elec­tric charge.  Vibrations from the never-sleeping columns shake loose electrons; the moss’s terraces ratchet them into a long-lived current that drifts down through rhizoid cables and spreads laterally, feeding every blade.  
   • Growth & Reproduction  
     The mat never widens—there is no bare stone left to occupy—but it thickens.  New layers crystalise directly atop old ones, squeezing the deepest strata into a vitreous amber.  Once a century or so, pressure cracks a section free; the slab tips, slides into a crevice, and becomes foundation for another tier.  This is the only “movement” an observer is likely to witness.  
   • Mind, if any  
     Charge pulses travel the mat in coherent packets, recur with clock-like regularity, detour around injuries, even mirror the rhythm of the distant turbines.  Paleobiologists argue whether these oscillations constitute thought.  The mat, of course, has never replied.

2.  The Tesselate Vines – freestanding antennae that farm the sky  
   • Form  
     From the mat rise wiry stalks two metres tall.  They fork, fork again, and re-fork until the tips look like frost on a windowpane.  Every twig ends in a knob of doped gallium; slight atmospheric fields bias the metal, sending pico-coulombs earthward.  
   • Symbiosis  
     The vines root shallowly in the Hyalosyne Mat.  In exchange for a tap into the mat’s charge reservoir, they donate a trace but constant trickle of electricity captured from above.  Together, mat and vine form a closed budget: nothing wasted, nothing imported.

3.  The Glitter Daughters – airborne, sleeplike stage of the moss itself  
   • Genesis  
     When the arc-lamp dims and temperature drops by half a degree, outermost laminae of certain leaves exfoliate into hexagonal mica flakes one hundred microns across.  
   • Behaviour  
     They float, catch on ledges, drop, rise again in thermals from steam conduits.  Each carries the mat’s surface chemistry encoded as dopant ratios.  A flake that happens to settle on a scar or crack anneals into place, “printing” a repair.  They are the mat’s ambulance fleet: passive, numberless, tireless.

4.  The Argon Pellicles – transient solvent-beings in the hanging droplets  
   • Environment  
     Liquid argon beads form where chlorine vapour meets colder iron.  Inside each bead is a haze of filamentous molecules built on silicon–chlorine backbones.  
   • Cycle  
     A pellicle lasts minutes.  During that span it folds and refolds, sorting trace metals, excreting perfect, nanoscopic prisms of rutile that fall like dust and are promptly mined by the moss.  Then the bead evaporates; the pellicle sublimes into memory.  
   • Significance  
     Though microscopically thin and short-lived, pellicles are the bowl’s only recyclers of airborne chlorine.  Without them the entire pocket chemistry would have frozen millions of years ago.

5.  The Ferrumycetes – rust-eating biofilm entombed in the old maintenance arms  
   • Habitat  
     The plinth-mounted robot claws stand idle, but the joints still contain bearing grease, and the grease still contains iron particles.  Ferrumycetes have infiltrated that matrix.  
   • Physiology  
     They metabolise Fe²⁺ directly, extruding whiskers of magnetite that line the gear-teeth.  As whiskers grow they force the arm to twitch, just enough to shake loose fresh specks of iron and keep the colony fed.  
   • Range of motion  
     A claw may flex once a decade.  To a passing visitor the gesture seems accidental, but seen on an accelerated time-lapse the arms perform a slow, looping semaphore that repeats every few centuries.  Whether it codes meaning or is merely trophic feedback is unknown.

6.  The Harmonic Colony – life that is not matter but pattern  
   • Origin  
     Steam roars through columnar ducts at narrowly stable pressures.  Standing waves establish themselves, interact, cancel, re-appear.  After untold repetition the interference pattern has acquired self-semblance: a sonic shape that recognises when it is complete or wounded.  
   • Behaviour  
     Disturb the acoustics—shout, fire a rifle, drop a boulder—and you create a spectral bruise in the pattern.  The Colony responds by modulating valve-like apertures along the ducts (no one has ever seen what opens or shuts them) to restore its familiar chord.  
   • Interaction with other residents  
     The Hyalosyne pulses synchronise to the Colony’s beat; the vine tips nod imperceptibly in time.  In return, the steady harvest of electrons damps static discharges that would otherwise crackle loud enough to wound the sound-being.  Thus matter upholds music, and music herds matter.

7.  The Absent Fauna – a presence defined by vacancy  
   • Fossil traces of motile shapes—joint sockets carved in old mat strata, vacated nymph-skins of silica—suggest that once there were moving bodies here.  They withdrew, or ossified into the standing forest, leaving only design principles adopted by their successors.  The glass moss grows to the same proportions as those vanished walkers, as if still expecting them to return and browse.

8.  Hypotheses about Visitors Like Us  
   • Human or near-human observers can linger for months before the air’s dryness mummifies lung tissue, but the place does encourage a subtler assimilation.  Metallic sweat salts the fingertips; press a hand to the moss and ions exchange.  Return years later and the ridge lines of that print have become conduits, tiny but flawless, routing charge in patterns copied from your dermal whorls.  In a sense you remain present: part of the circuitry, an honorary resident.

SUMMARY

Nothing here scurries, flutters, or feeds in the animal sense.  Life in the furnace-bowl is mineral, electrical, phononic.  It clings to surfaces, loops energy round and round, and polishes its own stasis as a jeweller endlessly re-cuts the same gem.  The inhabitants are:

• The Hyalosyne Mat – crystalline, floor-hugging megaforest  
• The Tesselate Vines – charge harvesters keyed to atmospheric fields  
• The Glitter Daughters – drifting repair-flakes  
• The Argon Pellicles – minute solvent minds that refine metals  
• The Ferrumycetes – rust-mycelia that make the dead robot arms twitch  
• The Harmonic Colony – a self-maintaining standing-wave intelligence  
• Echoes of extinct wanderers and the slowly incorporated fingerprints of any newcomer.

Together they form a population wholly at ease with permanence: a chorus of glass blades, mist phantoms, and resonant chords, each repeating its own perfected act until the mountain turbines finally seize—or until some patient geology folds the whole bowl under fresh stone and grants the community the only change it will ever welcome: burial intact.
