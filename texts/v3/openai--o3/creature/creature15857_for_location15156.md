Who – or what – calls Evigrad home?  No single answer quite fits.  The city is a stack of eras, and every era has left behind a tenant of its own.  Seen together they form a living cross-section, a population distributed through height, history and half-light.

────────────────────────
1. The High-lunged Folk (ordinary citizens, made un-ordinary by altitude)
────────────────────────
Physical habit
• Stature: seldom tall.  At 3 000 m the heart works harder, so bodies trend compact—square torsos, short limbs, deep chests.  
• Skin: pale to olive, always faintly green along the nails where moss spores lodge in cuticles and photosynthesise just enough to tingle.  
• Eyes: iris rim carries a reflective ring (the tapetal fold)—an adaptation that bends stray photons back through the retina.  A High-lungee in full sun must squint beneath layered goggles.  
• Blood chemistry: haemoglobin runs 10 % richer than low-landers; iron is precious food.

Movement and posture
They do not stride; they triangulate.  Every second step touches stone, railing, or rope.  Calves knot like braided cable.  Falling is an accepted punctuation: ledges are cluttered with spring-cushioned “courtesy nets” where an inattentive courier may land, bow, and resume a conversation already in progress.

Dress
Waterproof felt, waxed flax, and netted silk.  Sleeves and hems are sewn with copper seed-beads whose faint clink announces one’s approach without echoing.  Infants wear weighted anklets so the plateau’s fickle gravity does not give them adventurous loft.

Speech and silence
Spoken Evic tongues are slow, consonants softened into mouth-interior clicks.  From five paces you mostly watch fingers: thumb-and-first-knuckle sign language handles routine trade.  Loud words are reserved for three occasions—alarms, weddings, and the recitation of a newborn’s first breathing-tone (see belief, below).

Livelihood
• Water-right keepers (the Dropguild): read condensation patterns, certify cistern volume, auction tomorrow’s dew in hush-voiced markets.  
• Resonant engineers (the Linewrights): tune the infrasonic cables with mallets of pitch-pine and gut.  In their presence no one hums.  
• Facade gardeners: responsible for “skins”—the edible curtains of vines and cabbage.  Clipping shears are carved from bone because steel echoes.  
• Flue-walkers: chimney sweeps who also double as couriers; no direct road means every letter passes a flue at least once in its life.

Belief
Birth is said to happen twice: first from a mother, second from the stone.  A midwife listens to the infant’s first exhalation, identifies its natural pitch, and hums an answering fifth.  Thereafter the child’s name is written as a sigil of that interval.  To die is to slide, not fall: bodies are laid on bier-rafts and let drift down the emerald sinkholes where limey water petrifies flesh to reef.

Government
A laddered senate—thirty representatives, one for each altitude band of 30 m.  To speak on an issue you literally climb to the rung whose problems you have lived.

────────────────────────
2. The Mid-Stratum Remnants (machines that learned one trick too many)
────────────────────────
Origin
Three centuries ago the torrent-mills automated their own maintenance with brass-cog servitors driven by the city’s low direct current.  Millennia of vibration have worn away code-locks; the machines now run on the plateau’s ambient hum, starting and stopping when chord and task coincide.

Present behaviour
• The Gimbalers: tripedal carts that patrol wall-gardens, trimming vines with scissor-hands.  Once a week they present a ball of compost as tithe at whatever doorway sings nearest their control frequency.  
• The Counterweights: wagon-sized blocks that inch along ceiling rails resetting the city’s hydropower system.  They think in balances: place a stone on one side of their track and you will discover a stone of equal mass appearing somewhere inconvenient.

Civic status
Legally classified as “continuous citizens.”  No one owns them, but removing one is theft of public momentum.  Children toss them scraps of song to see which melody activates which behaviour.

────────────────────────
3. Cliff-Skates and Their Attendants
────────────────────────
Creature
Hex-winged, membrane like smoked glass, body a finger longer than a condor’s.  They browse on free-floating algal threads that coil in the up-drafts.

Domestication
Not rideable: hollow bones snap under more than five kilos.  Instead herders (“Spoolkeepers”) harvest skate guano—rich in potassium—for terrace crops.  They fly kites strung with red kelp-ribbons; the colour lures the skates to predictable circuits where the droppings can be netted in mid-air.

Cultural footnote
White skate-parchment makes the city’s legal scrolls.  In court the accused may ask to read their own charge; scrolls are translucent, so one must hold them to the moss-light, effectively illuminating one’s own sins for all to witness.

────────────────────────
4. The Sinkhole League (sub-aqueous intellects)
────────────────────────
Biology
Somewhere below twenty metres the sinkhole water turns jade-dark and warm.  There drift the Lacunae: colonial jelly-organisms the size of rooftops, mind distributed through threads thinner than hair.  They hum—not audibly, but by modulating local gravity in micro-surges that instruments record as music.

Interaction
Lacunae accept offerings of iron filings, absorbing them to manufacture internal ballast.  In exchange, once a season they buoy select mining lifts, granting effortless descent to lower adits.  Negotiation is done with weighted chimes lowered on braided cord.  The tone a Lacuna chooses to silence indicates assent.

────────────────────────
5. The Feral Choir (life at the acoustic margin)
────────────────────────
Phenomenon more than species
Where two stone faces meet at the wrong angle, vortexes of air self-organise into standing waves.  Fine limestone dust seeds these pockets; micro-crystals grow into barbed lattices that vibrate exactly at the fundamental of the canyon.  Seen through a lens, they are singing snowflakes.

Effect on residents
A Feral Choir can detune the Linewrights’ cables, shut down dynamos, even lull a High-lunged climber into step-match hypnosis, leading to fatal rhythm-missteps.  Regular patrols scour newborn Choirs with felt-padded mallets, damping them before they mature.

────────────────────────
6. Triskel, Vine-Cats and the Ration of Large Bodies
────────────────────────
Triskel
Three horns, goat-light frame, hooves that exude a natural rosin; they can cling to a slate wall at forty-five degrees.  Their milk tastes of salt, stone and whatever moss was last licked.

Vine-Cats
Genetic accidents of the terrace garden: half-feral felids with claws that secrete mild acid to slice tubers.  They keep salamanders in check.  In taverns a “vine-cat scar” is a polite excuse for any scratch one prefers not to explain.

Predators?
None permanent.  The plateau deletes speed: without long, level ground an ambush cannot convert to chase.  Evigrad’s top predator is chill and gravity; citizens respect both with equal prayer.

────────────────────────
7. Ghosts of Pressure and Key
────────────────────────
Unseen Residents
Old miners spoke of “Pressure Men,” shadows shaped like empty coveralls that walk vertical shafts.  Modern instruments confirm brief dips in barometric weight preceding rockfall or spontaneous machine start-ups.  Folklore holds these are impressions left by the mountain still remembering where bodies once pressed.

Cultural practice
Miners carry tuning forks.  Vibrate one and hold it to the wall—if stone answers in a perfect unison, the shaft is safe.  Any harmony softer than unison, you leave.

────────────────────────
8. Visitors (and why they rarely stay long)
────────────────────────
Arrive with bright voices, rustle maps, trample the cabbage ledges.  Within three days most develop Altitude Hush: either they learn to tread mid-sentence or the thin air and thinner patience of locals sends them back to the gondola.  A few linger, fascinated by the hum that reaches bone-marrow; these become Resonance Monks, shaving their heads to better feel infrasonic weather, saying nothing unless spoken to in the note of their own heartbeat.

────────────────────────
9. The Population in Summary
────────────────────────
Humans: ±33 000 breathing the cloud-bruise air.  
Semi-autonomous machines: several hundred, functionally immortal so long as the wind sings.  
Domestic animals: 12 000 triskel, 6 000 vine-cats, a catalogue of perching ferns that blur the line between plant and familiar.  
Wild aerial grazers: skates numbering in the low thousands.  
Aquatic enigmas: perhaps a dozen Lacunae, each an acreage of intention.  
Intangibles: echoes that have forgotten the mouths that cast them.

Evigrad is thus less a city than a choir of coexistences—flesh, moss, cable, water, and resonance—each holding its note just long enough for the next to enter on the beat.  The place is lived in layers, and so are its inhabitants: bodies tuned to altitude, cultures tuned to hush, machines tuned to wind, jellies tuned to gravity, and stones still tuned to the memory of mountains high enough to scrape the moon.
