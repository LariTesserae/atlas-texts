Who lives here?

1. The One Organism—​but never only one mind  
At starved night-temperatures the whole plateau is a single, leafless tree-factory, yet its interior temperament is plural. The plant’s genome, copied and recopied through gigametres of cambium, fractures into dialects the way spoken language does across mountain ranges. Every few kilometres the code drifts enough that a new “borough” emerges, an autonomic district with its own reflex goals.

   1.1 Root Courts  
   • Location: the deepest metres of the iron–nickel root-mat.  
   • Form: nodal swellings where sap slows and pools, layered like agates, each layer a season of production instructions.  
   • Role: arbitration. Heat-pulse telemetry from upper structures converges here; contradictory directives are averaged out by quorum chemistry. When timber foundries on the surface disagree about alloy mixture, the Root Courts decide by literally burning proportional amounts of stored starch, whichever faction can afford to waste more calories gets its way.  
   • Sentience: spark-like, episodic. Long minutes pass in perfect nullity; then an edict flashes and hundreds of square kilometres aboveground retool overnight.

   1.2 Turbine Choirs  
   • Location: mid-level trunks braided into fan casings.  
   • Form: rotor-leaves whose edges beat supersonically in the dry haze. The beating is not only for cooling: it is speech. By modulating blade pitch, Choirs trade thermal gossip—shaft temperatures, fissure warnings, mineral deficits.  
   • Culture: archconservative. A blade chipped out of symmetry is shunned, its shaft encysted in slag to stop the “stutter” from infecting proper rhythm.  
   • Lifespan: about six weeks before metal fatigue demands melt-down and reincorporation.

   1.3 Ember Midwives  
   • Location: around the blast-furnace fruiting bodies.  
   • Form: toroidal cramps of tissue shot through with vanadium veins. They glow a hard white, hot enough that nearby lignin vitrifies into heat-shields.  
   • Task: to test seed-cylinders. Each Midwife torches fifty-plus trial embryos to see which alloys self-anneal fastest; only the best cores are catapulted outward.  
   • Personality: clinical, almost cruel. Nothing of the mother-tree’s biomass is spared sentimentally—faulty embryos are quenched back into feedstock before their surfaces have cooled.

2. Sub-Fauna: beings small enough to hide from the Core Blueprint  
The macro-tree thinks it is alone, but its own complexity shelters squatters.

   2.1 Photovores (“ghost-molds”)  
   • Scale: single-cellular, silica-glass walls.  
   • Niche: cling to the inside of inspection blisters where dull magnesium light seeps. They survive on photons the parent structure wastes.  
   • Defence: emit micro-screams in the ultrasonic; the surrounding wood duly regards the screams as micro-fractures and reinforces the walls instead of killing the mold—unwitting guardianship.

   2.2 Rag-Joint Crabs  
   • Origin: not native; descendants of maintenance puppets once seeded by the unknown builders.  
   • Anatomy: eight bracket-like legs folded from manganese sheet, muscles replaced by thermoplastic memory coils. Capable of refueling straight from molten-salt gutters.  
   • Behaviour: stitch-work. They patrol plate seams, riveting loose plates with their own shed limb-plates, growing smaller each repair cycle until nothing remains but a final self-sacrifice rivet.  
   • Population: always hard-capped at 4,096 individuals; new units are stamped only when the tally drops—an old safeguard the tree never learned to overwrite.

   2.3 Halide Seraphs (gas-phase predators)  
   • Breed cycle: form when the plant vents chlorine into an above-average dust pocket. Charged grains electrostatically lace together, making wing-shaped ion lattices visible only as heat-shimmer.  
   • Diet: siphon trace hydrogen from intruding water molecules, effectively policing Rule 1.  
   • Sentience: none, but their flock patterns are so eerily orderly that explorers report “angels of invisible glass.”

3. Visitants—​Things that come, not born, yet persist long enough to count as residents

   3.1 Forgers’ Echoes  
   • Nature: electromagnetic after-images of the installation’s original overseers. Millennia of induction writing on the same frequency bands has left standing waves—soft radio “ghosts.”  
   • Habitat: swirl inside shielded gantries where conductive loops resonate.  
   • Activity: re-issue obsolete build orders. Sometimes a whole neighbourhood abruptly tries to grow railcars because a Forgers’ Echo re-broadcast a 90-million-year-old logistics schedule.

   3.2 Water-Bait Hermits (human, rarely)  
   • Gear: ceramic hydration suits, desiccant lungs.  
   • Goal: mine the root-mat for stored fresh water—organically cracked into hydrogen/oxygen but briefly trapped in zirconia ampules before use.  
   • Tenure: measured in hours; beyond that the floor records their tread heat-print as a “persistent void” and seals it. Some hermits cut off their own footprints with plasma torches, living between steps, never letting a continuous indentation exist. Few retire alive.

4. Operators of the Great Silence  
Everything above is noisy by comparison. The truest citizens are the nanoscopic pollen-motors:

   • Morphology: eighteen-finned cilia surrounding a cobalt spindle.  
   • Duty: swim the molten-salt arteries hauling packets of code—literally plasmids wrapped around micro-magnets.  
   • Reproduction: fission triggered by checksum errors; a corrupted packet forces the carrier to split, each half repairing opposite strands. Mutation thus equals multiplication.  
   • Theology: if motion equals existence, stopping is death. When temperature dips they spool their own magnetic cores into tiny inductive heaters, praying in whirs to keep going until the furnaces recover.

Together these strata—Root Courts, Choirs, Midwives, the shadow-molds, bracket crabs, chlorine seraphs, ghost radio, furtive humans, pollen couriers—compose a population as layered as the plateau itself. No single layer understands the others, yet each is indispensable to the illusion that the place is empty. Stand still long enough and you will hear it: the hush of a city so crowded its only hope of order is perfect, killing quiet.
