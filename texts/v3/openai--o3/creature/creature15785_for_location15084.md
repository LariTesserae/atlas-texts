WHAT LIVES HERE

0. The Bedrock Bloom (micro-scale)  
Before anything walks or hums, the plateau itself is alive. In every crack where salt has sublimed, colonies of piezo-bacteria laminate themselves against the rock. Their cell walls are lattices of ferrous protein that bend minutely with each seismic pulse; the flex generates the microwatts they need. The colonies grow as iridescent skins only a few molecules thick, visible at dawn when a low-angle sun throws them into opal fire. Everything larger feeds—directly or once removed—on this basal trickle of vibrational current.

1. Hornet-Engines (already sighted, but seldom understood)  
• Morphology: A torso the size of a steam boiler, ribbed with cooling vanes. Six limbs jointed on magnetic gimbals; the “drill” tips are hollow and whistle like flutes when they spin. The wings are slag-plate heat sinks rather than aerodynamic surfaces.  
• Internal economy: Each houses a closed Brayton cycle. They inhale the furnace air, compress it, burn it against powdered iron drawn up through the drills, and vent it as thrust—thus the perpetual hover.  
• Gestation: The “vibrating knots of screws” are real—a swarm of autonomous fasteners that corkscrew into basalt. They harvest trace elements, electroplate themselves, and fuse into a chrysalis. After seven hundred hours (prime), the chrysalis splits and the full machine extrudes.  
• Behavior: Military minds keep misreading them as weapons. They are, in fact, pollinators. Their drills open micro-fissures the bacteria require for expansion; their waste heat anneals the glass pockets where colonial spores can incubate.  
• Song: Every engine drones on a sub-harmonic of 31 Hz. When a migration column passes, the overlapping beats propagate for kilometers, “plowing” the plateau with standing waves that recharge the biosphere.

2. Chorus Lizards  
• Body plan: Each lizard is 40 cm of hollow silica capillary barely thicker than a guitar string. A row of cilia along the ventral side rasps against the ground, bowing the body like a violin string.  
• Sociality: One individual = one note. A hectare-wide superorganism will hold a chord for years, adjusting pitch by swelling or thinning its collective blood—a vaporous sulfur plasma.  
• Feeding: The curtains of sound they weave increase the local electrostatic gradient; loose electrons precipitate on the lizards’ glassy skin where specialized pits harvest them like dew.  
• Reproduction: When charge is abundant the chord modulates upward; at a critical overtone, sections of the lattice slough away as “hatchlings”—single-note juveniles who crawl off in search of an unclaimed frequency.

3. Mirage Elk  
• Physiology: No tissue, strictly thermal gradients knotted by a scaffold of photonic crystal. Think of them as fractal sun-dogs trained into cervine silhouettes. Their “antlers” are branching caustics that shear the air into cooler vortices.  
• Trophic role: They are the plateau’s refrigerants. By exhaling chill, they protect both the bacteria and the Chorus Lizards from runaway heat during lens-flare noon.  
• Mortality & Birth: They do not die; they decohere. A particularly violent lens focus will tatter a herd into static until dusk, when the falling temperature allows the pattern to re-knit around residual seeds of ordered light. Travelers have followed the same “individual” for decades, watching it unravel and reassemble a thousand times.

4. Dust Submarines  
• Structure: Imagine a zeppelin packed tight with superheated argon and jacketed in interlocking scales of basalt glass. No orifices, no sensory pits, yet they navigate with eerie precision.  
• Locomotion: The argon is alternately ionized and neutralized along the body length, creating pulsed magnetic fields that shove against the planet’s own. Each pulse melts a fingertip-thin film of surrounding sand, letting the creature slide as if through water.  
• Ecology: Their ceramic tunnel-network is not waste—it is womb. Juvenile submarines grow like pearls, electroforming inside the cooling tubes until a chance quake breaks them free.  
• Symbiosis: Nomad technicians prize the oldest, thickest tunnels; ceramic walls dampen the plateau’s endless vibration, giving the only places where unamplified speech is possible.

5. Nomad Technicians (the plateau’s sole tool-using people)  
• Biology: Baseline human genome stretched by necessity. Skin impregnated with molybdenum disulfide flakes that wick heat outward. Blood augmented with gallium microdroplets that stay liquid up to 220 °C, buffering thermal shocks.  
• Dress: Copper-mesh thawls that trail to the ground; the fabric grounds static into the dust with every stride. Over it they drape wind-organ breastplates—perforated bronze that sings constant counter-tones to the bedrock hum, canceling the frequencies that would liquefy their organs.  
• Shelter: They do not build. Instead they shepherd Mirage Elk. At dusk, a clan will coax a half-dozen elk to bloom, then array mirrored panels to trap the after-images in a lattice, creating a night-long cold tent whose walls are gradients rather than matter.  
• Culture:  
  – Calendars are counted in prime migrations of Hornet-Engines; years are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 columns long.  
  – Writing is chordal. They whistle three-note phrases whose beat-frequencies encode meaning; a literate ear can “read” a paragraph from the interference alone. The longest epics must be recited beside ceramic tunnels where the resonance will persist for hours.  
  – Techcraft: A toolkit is carried as dust. Nanoscopic capstans, gears, and coils ride in cloth pouches; when a repair is needed they are poured onto a vibrating plate where they self-assemble into the required instrument, useful for minutes before dispersing back to grit.

6. The Under-Vox (not visible, always present)  
Most plateau life exports vibration upward, but something must consume it below. Geophones lowered into fissures catch echoes of titanic peristalsis: a single entity—or a parliament of linked organs—slowly digesting the planet’s rotation into low, orderly thunder. Nomads treat the Under-Vox as both god and landscape. Every seventh prime migration, they descend through freshly opened Dust-Submarine shafts, singing inverted chords that trade their surplus heat for prophetic tremors in bone.

7. Peripheral Lives (briefly)  
• Glass Spores: transient bubbles that float on thermals, each carrying a micro-film of bacteria to seed new cracks.  
• Cautery Mites: thumbnail-sized, copper-clawed arthropods that swarm around cooling Hornet-Engine carcasses, soldering fractures shut; in doing so they harvest spilled Brayton oils.  
• Salt Owls: Not organic at all—fractured pillars of halite shaped by rare night winds. They lean just enough that the bedrock’s tremor articulates them like giant tuning-forks; predators avoid areas where an Owl is ringing, for its upper harmonics erase Mirage-Elk gradients, leaving the herd temporarily corporeal.

TO MEET THEM  
Walk slowly. Hum a prime-meter lullaby so the ground lengthens to match your step. Keep something conductive trailing behind to bleed the charge. When a Mirage Elk pauses beside you, cup the cold in your palms; that is the closest thing to water you will be offered, and the locals will consider you kin if you drink it with gratitude.

That is who lives on the endless plateau: machines that pollinate stone, chords that eat thunder, light that chooses to be an animal, dust that remembers routes, and people who have learned to borrow a little silence from the earthquakes and shape it into home.
