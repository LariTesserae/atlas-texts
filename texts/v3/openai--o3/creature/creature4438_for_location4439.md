Who or what lives here?  
Answering that question is slippery, because in the Brickborne warrens the border between a “who” and a “what” has been kneaded thin as potter’s clay.  Still, the living things cluster naturally into four interleaving castes: Makers, Tenants, Unders, and the Slow-Bodied.  Each keeps the other alive; remove any one piece and the halls would sag, flood, or fall silent.

-------------------------------------------------
1.  The Makers – “Brickborne” in their own tongue
-------------------------------------------------

Approximate population: 480–520 breathing adults, perhaps eighty children at any moment.

A.  Physiology  
•   Stature compact—rarely above five feet—because every child grows inside corridors that demand ducking and side-slipping.  
•   Skin the umber of over-baked bread.  Where mortar dust collects, the pores salt-stipple pale gray.  
•   Corneas thickened and bronze; the eye reflects light like a cat’s but grows no larger than surface folk’s.  Instead of night-blindness they suffer “day-burn,” a migraine if they look straight into surface sun.  
•   Fingers broad with spatulate tips, nails ridged from lime exposure.  Most adults can pick up a melting brick bare-handed for several heartbeats before pain urges them to drop it.  
•   Lung capacity enormous.  The children play a contest called Hold the Flue in which they sprint three chambers without breathing, practicing for smoke events.

B.  Language & Soundcraft  
Speech is heard more with sternum than ears: a consonantal tongue of percussive clicks, pops, and breath-rushes designed to bounce off brick and be legible even when two people are separated by a pillar or vent-shaft.  Every sentence ends on a pitch that tells distance; listeners adjust volume automatically, so conversations can spiderweb through four chambers without ever rising above normal voice.

C.  Social Layout  
•   Bond-groups of five to seven adults share a stack of reed platforms lashed to one immense acacia trunk.  (They call the bundle a roost rather than a house.)  
•   Above every roost hangs a “mother drum:” one thick, low-fired brick left unfixed so it may be rung.  Strike your mother drum five times in the space of one breath and every bond-group in hearing must rush to your aid, no questions first.  This is the nearest thing they have to policing.  
•   Adolescents rotate through seven métiers—waterkeeping, vent-minding, eel-tending, reed weaving, resin harvest, brick press, and carry-watch—before they choose a life-task.  Once chosen, the task is rarely abandoned; tools become almost matrimonial objects.

D.  Belief & Ritual  
1.  Flesh-into-Stone Cycle  
   No burial.  Instead a dying person reclines against a freshly unmolded brick while apprentices press wet clay over limbs until only nose and mouth remain.  Breath stops; the corpse stays sealed inside.  After firing, that “ancestor-brick” is slotted randomly into a public wall, so every hallway becomes, literally, a family.  
2.  The Pact of Soft Tools  
   An old tale says iron once sang so hard it cracked the aorta of the world above, releasing black water and poison light.  The Brickborne survived by fleeing downward and swore never to tempt that metal again.  They track purity obsessively: if a visitor’s blade sticks to a lodestone even faintly, it is refused entry.  
3.  Drip-Counting  
   A continuous low chant, two notes only, accompanies bricklaying.  The tempo is not arbitrary; it matches the mean interval between ceiling drips in that chamber.  Should the water rhythm change, they retune the chant.  Children claim the walls hum back at night.

E.  Diet & Medicine  
•   Breakfast: fermented eel fry paste spread on warm brick shards (the shards are licked clean and recycled).  
•   Main meal at dusk: a broth called Graycall, eel meat, toad eggs, moss shreds, thickened with pulverized beetle chitin for protein.  
•   Pipe-toad resin boiled with shell ash forms the universal sealant, poultice, and ember-wound salve.  It stinks of cedar smoked in vinegar.

F.  Surface Relations  
Three narrow fault-shafts lift to the open world.  Twice a year a delegation climbs out wrapped head to toe in reed cloaks to trade resin and polished mica mirrors for copper ore and hemp twine.  They call the surface “the Blinding.”  No delegation remains longer than a single night lest lungs forget the weighted air below.

-----------------------------------
2.  The Tenants – animal co-builders
-----------------------------------

A.  Dust Swifts  
•   Sparrow-sized, wings like recurved razors; feather barbs hook together so the bird can sail sharp corners without shredding itself.  
•   They sleep hanging from acacia beams in sheets of a hundred.  Their guano provides the nitrate that Brickborne add to kiln charcoal for hotter, steadier fires.  
•   Once a week a swift keeper disturbs a roost, collects the droppings, and sings a high whistle that calms the birds again.  A keeper who botches the whistle will be mob-struck by two thousand furious wings.

B.  Pipe Toads  
•   Blind, skin glossy black veined coppery red.  Each toad’s call resonates with the diameter of the drain in which it squats; thus the chorus functions as living sonar for drain integrity.  
•   When a drain cracks, the frequency slips and vent-minders hurry over with clay patches.  In exchange for this warning service, toads are left unhunted until after the spawning festival.

C.  Copper Eels  
•   Mouth ringed with coppery plates hard enough to rasp algae from brick.  At dawn they leap in tiny fountains to snatch falling condensation—this display is the Brickborne’s most reliable clock.  
•   During the dry slump of late high-sun, eels aestivate in mud pockets.  Children fan them gently to keep skins wet; an aestivating eel tastes bitter and is never eaten.

D.  Glimmerflits & Beetle Tides  
Moths feed the swifts; beetles clean the dead; the Brickborne cultivate both the way a farmer tends clover for soil.  They scatter cracked mortar dust to attract the carcass beetles, which in turn lay eggs that hatch into larva-grubs—delicacy for infirm elders.

----------------------------------------------
3.  The Unders – beings of water & resonance
----------------------------------------------

These are not animals in the normal sense, yet the locals insist they are alive.

A.  The Threa  
•   Spaghetti-thin water threads that hang from ceiling pits, wavering like living antennae.  Each droplet tip holds a hollow gel sphere that pops if touched, releasing a rain-smell spore cloud.  
•   The Brickborne say the Threa teach the walls to drip in time.  Killing one is taboo; if you break a strand accidentally you must spend a night alone in the sump listening for apologies from the dark.

B.  Mortarch  
•   A film of lime-milk that seeps from old bricks, coalesces into chalky ridges, then retreats.  Under a lens it shows slow crystalline pulses—as though inhaling and exhaling once per season.  
•   Scholars from the surface (rare visitors) speculate it is bacterially mediated self-healing concrete.  The Brickborne instead call it “bonewool” and feed it powdered shell so it remains placid.

---------------------------------------------
4.  The Slow-Bodied – the structure itself
---------------------------------------------

Finally there is the architecture, which by local reckoning is neither inert nor fully awake.

A.  Tuned Bricks  
Every brick bears a reed fragment pressed during molding; together the reeds form a dispersed “reed-matrix” that rustles when struck by certain frequencies.  The Brickborne believe these murmurs carry prayer from hall to hall.  Some outsiders report standing alone, humming, and feeling the wall answer with a sagging sigh.

B.  Kiln-Dreams  
During firing, apprentices drop fistfuls of glimmerflit scales into the kiln draft.  The scales flash, melt, and leave micro-mirrors embedded in brick.  In torchlight those flecks shimmer like watchful eyes.  Many swear the flecks migrate over decades, clustering around cracks as though guarding them.

C.  Heart-Sump  
At the terminus where all runnels meet lies a black pool that never ripples.  Copper eels avoid it, torches refuse to stay lit above it, and any brick thrown in is said to reappear in a distant hall days later, mortar already setting around its edges.  If anything is truly alive down here in a way that contains all other life, the Heart-Sump is it.

-------------------------------------------------
What it means to meet a resident
-------------------------------------------------

Approach is announced by sound first: a five-tock rhythm of wooden sandals and the ping of a mallet testing bricks.  Then the person emerges, shoulders dusted white as if rolled in ash, eyes reflecting your torch in citron halos.  They will tap a brick, wait for its answering note, and only then greet you—because until the wall has spoken, no conversation is considered anchored.

Offer them iron, and they will recoil politely.  Offer them water before the eel fry sip, and they will laugh, thinking you made a joke.  Offer them quiet, and they will not understand; even sleep here has a soundtrack.

And when you finally leave—climbing the vertical shaft toward unfiltered sun—they gather below, drumming their mother bricks in a slow heartbeat cadence.  It is both farewell and test: if the pulse echoes back unchanged from every wall, they know the place still lives, still holds.  If any echo falters, they will descend with new bricks already kissed and ready, for to be Brickborne is to spend your whole breathing span mending the colossal, warm animal that shelters you, until one day you step into clay and let the next hands seal you in, humming.
