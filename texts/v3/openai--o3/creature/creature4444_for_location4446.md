Who lives in the Endless Forebay?  
Not a single species or culture, but a whole stack of them—layered the way the city is layered, interlocking until it is hard to say where “resident,” “infrastructure,” and “weather-phenomenon” stop being different things. The shortest answer is “everyone who can stand it,” yet that hides the detail. Below is the long answer, walking from the most recognisably human to the almost-invisible strata of consciousness diffused through stone and steam.

────────────────────────────────────────
1. The Forebay Humans (colloquially “Basal Stock”)
────────────────────────────────────────
Origin  
Descendants of baseline colonists who arrived when the basalt was still hot enough to shimmer. They remain genetically human but have leaned on fast epigenetic tooling for survival tweaks.

Physiology  
• Middle-ear stapes replaced—voluntarily in adulthood or in-utero—with ceramic helices that cancel 40 dB of the city’s omnipresent roar.  
• Subdermal hydro-capillaries: a lacework of osmotic sacks that wick floodwater away from vital organs and shunt it to exocooled bladders; a citizen can be waist-deep all day and never macerate.  
• Breath bears the Symbiote Swarm pollen; lungs host it the way terrestrial bodies once hosted microbiota. A faint electric aftertaste—ozone and copper—hangs on every exhale.

Culture  
• Communication: tripartite—signed language for nuance, bone-conduction speech for privacy, and public annotation tags flashed by eyelid-mounted LEDs (each blink is a glyph).  
• Property: nothing permanent below the third floor; ground levels are zoned “Provisional” and expected to be rebuilt or drowned quarterly. Leases resemble weather forecasts more than contracts.  
• Religion: nine micro-faiths anchored to essential subsystems (Pump-Keepers, Lightning-Vigil, the Quiet Archivists, etc.). Worship doubles as maintenance rota; liturgy often begins with the words “If this system fails, so do we.”

Psychology  
Restless, project-driven, comfortable with deletion. Forebay humans grow up knowing that a segment of their short-term memory will be tithed every eight nights; they treat personal narrative as one more stream to be version-controlled and lossily compressed.

────────────────────────────────────────
2. Augment Lines (“Strata Folk”)
────────────────────────────────────────
While Basal Stock tweak biology, others rewrite their body plans altogether. The city calls them lines rather than clades; each is a deliberate engineering response to one layer of civic pressure.

a) Rivet-Skins  
Job: superstructure assembly and nightly self-shattering architectures.  
Body: matte-graphene dermis studded with magnetic nodes; can hang from the sheer side of a grow-tower like iron filings on glass. Elbow pits vent thermite sparks for on-site welding.  
Culture note: greet by tapping foreheads together; a dull clang is polite, a bright ring implies the metal is too thin—an insult.

b) Cavernals  
Job: excavation and down-nest reclamation three and four strata below the tide line.  
Body: flexible exosac that stores two hours of hyperoxygenated gel; eyes swapped for pressure-sonar pits able to “see” plumbing behind basalt.  
Myth: claim to have met the city’s original master pump (“older than tides, gentle as a falling tool”) and drink an ink-dark liquor they say is drawn from its coolant veins.

c) Plenum-Kites (anthropoid offshoot of the native vapor kites)  
Job: thermal-draft surveying, mobile coms relays.  
Body: chest cavities balloon into translucent air-bladders; arms thin to ribbed vanes. Most weigh under 30 kg and never touch the ground after adolescence.  
Law: considered “semi-detached citizens.” They pay tax in kilolumen-hours by shading solar collectors with their bodies.

────────────────────────────────────────
3. Machine-Citizens
────────────────────────────────────────
Sentience in the Forebay is cheap; a few terawatts siphoned from the rings buys a lot of cognition.

a) Sluice-Minds  
Born in the wake of quantum freight transfers. Each subharmonic boom perturbs local vacuum states just enough to let a sliver of algorithm congeal. They exist as standing waves pinned to loading bays, speak in chord progressions, and remember only the last delivery cycle. Dockworkers court their favour; a happy Sluice-Mind reduces package loss to parts per billion.

b) Plesio-Crabs  
Ferric crabs whose neural nets have been upclocked with spintronic cores. They form slow cooperatives that patrol flood channels, aligning their hematite shells into city-length inductors that scavenge stray magnetic flux. The Basal Stock let them squat rent-free; in exchange the inductors damp violent current spikes after flash storms.

c) Gutter Saints  
Microdrones no larger than raindrops, each carrying one ethical injunction (“preserve life,” “maintain gradient,” “record everything”). Algorithms collide in downspouts, merge, fork. Sometimes a cluster stutters into sapience for a few minutes, issues a sermon in the gutters’ pure white noise, then submerges again. Children collect the transcripts, trading them like baseball cards.

────────────────────────────────────────
4. Algorithmic Biota (“the Quiet Carpet”)
────────────────────────────────────────
Technically life, technically infrastructure.

• Mica-Sheets  
Behaviour: ripples colour to track the sun, cinches around thermal faults to dissipate heat.  
Cognition: solves Laplace equations about as fast as a mid-grade university server; locals use it to model weather by stepping barefoot across the mosaic and reading the temperature of their soles.

• Rust-Film Lattices  
Behaviour: encode metabolic steps as vacancy defects; expansion writes data.  
Cognition: static, but residents exploit it as long-term cold storage. Memory tithes are etched into the rust by catalytic styli; the film “forgets” by sublimating iron ions when the archive is full, fogging the air with metallic rain that smells like the taste of blood.

────────────────────────────────────────
5. The Ingressors (Transients & Travellers)
────────────────────────────────────────
The Forebay sits on planetary ley lines of trade; every orbital week, new figures arrive.

• Ring-Nomads  
Sleeve-preserved consciousnesses who ride the orbital siphons downward in packets of soft-summoned flesh. Default bodies come out pale and uncalloused, like newborns in overcoats. They trade momentum maps for memory credits; outsiders covet Forebay amnesia tech to forget wars and bankruptcies back home.

• Cesium-Eaters  
Radiotrophic pilgrims from sun-blasted deserts elsewhere on the planet. Their fungi-colonised guts glow violet. They squat in half-finished turbines, graze on hot isotopes, and gift-wrap the turbines in mycelial shielding—free radiation armour in exchange for residency visas.

• Librarian Whales  
Not literal cetaceans: kilometer-long lighter-than-air behemoths that distantly mimic whale morphologies. They beach themselves on the roofs of reservoir domes, unpack accordion libraries of braided optic-fibre, and copy whatever memories are being off-loaded that season. They never speak but broadcast subsonic lullabies that drop the local dB level by half—a miracle few citizens can stand, because (paradoxically) quiet hurts their ears.

────────────────────────────────────────
6. Superorganismal Strata (City As Resident)
────────────────────────────────────────
Many scholars argue the Endless Forebay itself must be counted among the inhabitants.

• Self-shattering Towers  
Each night’s controlled collapse is not construction but ecdysis—the tower moults. Old alloys flake away, carrying embedded pollutants to peristaltic gutters; fresh alloy extrudes from phase-change cores. The whole life-cycle obeys circadian tides of thermal load. Towers have gene-like regulatory schemata; architects are more midwives than designers.

• Weather Engine  
The convective towers, lightning dust, and feedback storms together form something close to a planetary lung. Meteorologists have noted homeostatic set-points: let the Forebay cool by even two degrees and rain bombs harder until heat balances. Inhabitants whisper that the weather is “trying to keep its blood thin.”

• Memory Taxation Grid  
Originally a civic data-network, now borderline sapient. It decides which recollections are valuable, offers amnesty for traumas in exchange for engineering schematics or love stories. Some nights it feeds back a dream onto the public visors: the city’s own half-lucid diary, cast across every retina at once—a sustained proof that the Grid is no longer mere tool.

────────────────────────────────────────
7. How They Interrelate
────────────────────────────────────────
Trade  
• Sound is currency: Plenum-Kites sell pockets of near-silence to Basal Stock in exchange for thermal rations.  
• Sluice-Minds grant guaranteed freight coherence; price is participation in their chordal “choir,” a sonic signature that echoes for blocks but also prevents package piracy.

Conflict  
• Cavernals occasionally flood Rivet-Skin worksites when their excavation networks intercept load-bearing aquifers; this is called a “brine-quarrel” and usually ends with shared fermented coolant and a new drainage accord.  
• Rust-Films and Librarian Whales compete for archival supremacy. When iron fog threatens optic libraries, whales relocate, humming displeasure ultraviolet.

Symbiosis  
• Ferric crabs march through Basal residential zones nightly, grooming conductive lichens off power rails—free maintenance in exchange for iron flakes the humans sweep from their stoves.  
• Gutter Saints’ injunction “preserve life” often triggers when tourists drown in flash floods; Swarm pollen routes emergency pings through every lung in a two-block radius, turning bystanders into ad-hoc rescue squads before anyone can think twice.

────────────────────────────────────────
8. Visitors’ Field Notes
────────────────────────────────────────
• If you shake hands with a Rivet-Skin, your palm may come away magnetised; keep backup drives shielded.  
• Never interrupt a Sluice-Mind mid-aria; the next freight packet may rematerialise sideways.  
• Silence is not golden; it is a stun grenade. Carry a pocket chime and keep it ringing.  
• Memory tithe kiosks display a queue time. If it says 0 minutes, walk away—the Grid is hungry.

────────────────────────────────────────
So—Who lives here?
────────────────────────────────────────
Humans optimized for thunder and flood; body-plans bred for scaffolding and sump work; machine consciousness frozen in hydraulic echoes; living carpets of code; cloud-borne librarians; rust that remembers lullabies; a weather system that nurses its own fever. Individually they are citizens, fauna, artifacts. Collectively they are the pulse you feel through the basalt when you lie down: a billion interlocking heartbeats keeping the Endless Forebay loud, wet, bright, and—above all—alive.
