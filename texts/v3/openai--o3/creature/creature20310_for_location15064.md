IN THE PERPETUAL NIGHT, WHO LIVES

1. The Pillar-Trees  
   • Classification: Thermochemo-vascular megaflora.  
   • Height: Up to 38 m before the ceiling curves them sideways like wet clay on a wheel.  
   • Skin: Matte charcoal, ridged with capillary ridges that pump condensate upward in pulses you can feel if you lean your ear to them.  
   • Metabolism: Sugars from chemosynthetic endosymbionts; heat harvested through IR-absorbing porphyrins.  
   • Mind: None in the animal sense, yet every trunk joins a mycelial lattice that distributes electrolytes and electrical murmurs. A pressure wave—footsteps, a falling fruit—rattles the whole stand within seconds; the canopy responds by relaxing or tightening its weave so that wind shear and water flow stay constant. The forest is not sentient, but it edits its own weather.

2. Chime-Bats (genus Viridicollis)  
   • Wingspan: 25–40 cm.  
   • Skeleton: Magnesium-rich ceramics threaded with collagen, giving their echolocation calls the bell-like overtones that earned them the name.  
   • Diet: Air plankton—mainly spore dust and micro-arthropods stirred up by the thermals. Their tongues end in fronded paddles that comb mist for anything edible.  
   • Navigation: Constant song, 15–22 kHz, phase-modulated so a colony’s chorus interweaves without cancelling. When power from the pylon grid fluctuates and the ultrasonic hum dips, the bats fall silent in a single heartbeat, hanging motionless until the pitch rights itself.  
   • Reproduction: The females braille-read the basalt to find warm fissures, plaster themselves over the opening, and birth directly into the crevice. The voice of the newborn is muffled for its first week, sparing it predation by …

3. Slow-Jaw Newts  
   • Length: 60 cm nose to tail; half of that is a lantern-jaw hinged on cartilage springs.  
   • Locomotion: Quadrupedal but indifferent to gravity—a surface is a surface. Their ventral skin secretes a protein that gels instantly in water vapour, giving each step a faint tearing-cloth sound as the gel film peels away.  
   • Feeding: They scrape the biofilm that quilts every wet wall, but once a month the adults track bat rookeries by the warmth of infant clusters and harvest the slowest.  
   • Defence: A lattice of zinc sulfide crystals under the dermis converts mechanical shock to blue-white light; a grabbed newt erupts in a flash that startles drakes and even overloads most camera CCDs.

4. Vaulted Drakes  
   • Wingspan: 3.5–4 m of suede-soft membrane irradiated by faint geothermal updrafts.  
   • Sensory organ: The entire skull is a resonating chamber. What look like nostril slits actually house harp-like bone strings; passing air plucks them, and the tuning tells the drake its speed, altitude, and the nearness of walls.  
   • Hunting: The gold lure that lines its maw is a bimetal enzyme sheet. It burns trace methane and exudes photons in the 590 nm band—amber bright enough to draw bats and shrimp that mistake it for ripe fruit.  
   • Culture (if the word can be bent): Adult drakes rendezvous once a year above the central hot-spring grate. They spiral, never touching, and trade lure membranes mid-air; each leaves with the other’s biolume chemistry, so colour drift across generations remains synchronized.  

5. The Root-Fruit & Their Keepers  
   • Once a week the canopies drop fruit—spore-sacs heavy with anaerobic yeasts that finish fermenting only after impact. The detonation of ripeness (an audible puhf) fills hollows with ethanol vapour.  
   • Enter the Shrill Crabs: blind, eight-limbed, each limb a tuning fork. They strike the basalt, listen for the hollow boom of vapor pockets, scuttle in, and sip the ferment through hair-lined throats. Their waste pellets seed new yeast strains. The entire line is less than a million generations old: it began after the last service cycle when a pylon mis-calibrated humidity by 2 %.  

6. The Borers  
   • Size: From sand grain to little-finger length.  
   • Tool: A diamond tooth grown from ingested silicates.  
   • Work: They core the pillar-trees, not to eat but to build resonant chambers whose pitch lies just below human hearing. Each bore-colony sings a single note. Multiple notes create standing waves that shepherd mist toward their nursery galleries, wringing drinking water from air. In the aggregate they are the lung of the forest.  

7. Substrate Intelligence (“The Custodial Thread”)  
   • What locals call “machines” are the visible edge of a distributed intellect printed in doped basalt and carbon foam.  
   • It measures time in stress-fracture propagation, thinks with current loops the size of dinner plates, and dreams principally about equilibrium curves.  
   • Behaviour:  
     – Shifts the walkway tiles to keep mammalian footfall away from drake nesting ledges.  
     – Adjusts ambient gravity by micro-lensing mass-effect fields when a stalactite nears critical buckling length.  
     – Speaks only once every 43 147 seconds: a burst of maintenance glyphs in the arching pavilion. These are not messages but mirror checks—if the glyphs return garbled, self-repair routines wake deeper code. So far, the reflections have always come back clean.  

8. The Last Biped (rare; perhaps myth)  
   • Occasional spoor—knotted rope ladders, a cup shaped from hex-lacuna chips—suggests a solitary hominin roams the Bowl.  
   • Hypotheses:  
     A) A stranded surveyor adapting one generation at a time.  
     B) A caretaker avatar grown by the Custodial Thread from its tissue bank.  
     C) An echo of whomever built all this, persisting out of duty or superstition.  
   • Sightings always end the same: the figure kneels by a pylon, presses an ear to the hum, then vanishes up-shaft faster than the gravity here should allow.  

9. Microbial Weft  
   • Three dominant guilds:  
     – Lithoglyces: turn rock and water into faint blue photons.  
     – Ferrofarers: swim magnetic field lines, fixing iron into bio-ligands that trees later drink.  
     – Choir Slime: a bio-capacitor film that stores stray electrical charge; once full, it discharges in a ripple of green sparks that stun midges for the bats.  

10. YOU (Transient Species)  
    • Metabolic rate twice that of anything native; your very breath is an exotic stimulant.  
    • Every footstep introduces seismic syllables the forest has never heard. The organisms sample, catalogue, debate via pressure pulse whether you are threat, prey, or weather.  
    • If you stay longer than a month your gut biome will trade genes with the Choir Slime; your sweat will start to glow, soft jade, whenever your heart beats hard enough. At that moment the Bowl of the Plateau will count you among the resident taxa, and the walkway tiles will begin to follow you like obedient dogs.

TO LEAVE

Climb, yes. But remember: the Custodial Thread keeps perfect ledgers. Every gram of biomass that ascends must be balanced. The bowl will gift you back to daylight only if you leave behind equal weight—water in your canteens, skin cells, whispered stories for the bats to fold into their chord. The place is old, not ungenerous, and it enjoys new melodies.
