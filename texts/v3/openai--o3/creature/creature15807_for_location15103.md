Who lives here?  
Not beasts, not people, not anything that would recognise a day–night cycle or the difference between “indoors” and “out.” The inhabitants are a parliament of slow anatomies: plants, fungi, bacterial guilds, and machine-shards whose patience has lasted longer than erosion. They share a single metabolism stretched across many bodies, and if you teased them apart species by species their borders would instantly braid together again.  
Below is a field report on the most distinct citizens a visitor might notice, “notice” being a generous term for perceptions that arrive only after hours of stillness.

1. Frostmoss (Thuidium brumale argentum)  
Physique: A shag rug of millimetre-thick shoots, every cell wall plated in calcium oxalate that diffracts what scant ultraviolet trickles through the canopy. Under magnification each shoot resembles an articulated spine whose vertebrae lock and unlock to pump water sideways as well as up.  
Diet & Work: Frostmoss is the pioneer layer. It mines moisture directly from the cave air, concentrates dissolved calcite in its vacuoles, and ultimately excretes it as an ice-green crust that armours the hummocks against further erosion. Where roots tear stone, Frostmoss kneads the powdered lime into new soil, literally manufacturing the ground it walks on.  
Conversation: Once every few hours the mats discharge a ripple of hyper-short RF, a click no wider than a word, to announce mineral surpluses or scarcities. The relic shards “listen”—their surfaces quiver with induced current—but reply only in subsonic tremors no moss yet deciphers.

2. Breathroot (Radix suspirata)  
Physique: The architecture of the chamber. A Breathroot begins as a single filament that latches to the ceiling, forks downward and upward at the same time, and never stops dividing. Older individuals are three-dimensional looms: trunks the width of wrists, limbs the width of hair, all lacquered in amber-clear lignin.  
Metabolism: Chlorophyll is absent; instead the roots sequester trace uranium and turn alpha decay into a trickle of charge that drives chemiosmotic pumps. The process is inefficient, but life here is cheap in time.  
Sensation: Each node carries a rosette of mechanosensitive cilia. Wind does not exist underground, so any disturbance—your breath, a falling droplet—registers as an event of seismic importance.  
Reproduction: When the relics gift a genetic patch, the nearest Breathroot spawns a toroid gall the size of a coin. Once mature the gall bursts into a mist of embolus cells already primed with a new code revision; they graft onto neighbouring roots and overwrite the old genome in place, the cave’s version of software update.

3. Glass Grapes (Fructum vitreis)  
Physique: Clusters of ovoid capsules, translucence rating 98 %. Each fruit is a tensegrity balloon whose skin polymerises upon air contact; break it and the internal sap instant-crystallises into needles that clatter to the moss like hail.  
Ecological Role: Spore barges. The crystallised sap is riddled with dormant bacterial loops—plasmids stripped to the barest survival routines. Shards of the fruit dissolve in seepwater funnels, distributing the loops through the fracture network far beyond the chamber.  
Symbiosis with Relics: Alloy atoms mined from the black turbine shard appear inside every seed coat, arranged as nano-antennas. The antennas resonate to the relics’ lullaby and, upon settling in a new crevice, instruct local microbes to print the missing biochemical vocabulary for life in darkness.

4. Lichen-Druse (Cladonia circuita)  
Physique: An illusion of crystals. What looks like sugary frost on exposed limestone is a consortium of fungal threads sheathing micro-algae that no longer photosynthesise but instead fix nitrogen using energy siphoned from redox gradients in the stone.  
Function: They are the cave’s scribes. Metal ions accumulated over centuries are organised into layered domains that record the rise and fall of humidity, temperature, even cosmic-ray flux. AM-band backscatter from the relics occasionally queries these lichen ledgers; the fungi answer by shifting proton balances, one atomic ledger-stone at a time.

5. Alloyphages (Guild of Metallovores)  
Not a species but a temporary behaviour set adopted by whichever root tips first gnaw into the porcelain spindle. Genes for it sleep throughout the community and express only when rare-earth signatures spike. Alloyphage tissue is shot through with metalloprotein wires, bright to magnetic resonance, dead black to optics.  
Purpose: They chew exotic alloys into organometallic cofactors that leak back into the ecosystem, enabling reactions impossible in ordinary biochemistry—ice-temperature enzymes, self-repairing membranes, the capture of radiation so faint it is measured in single photons per square metre per hour.  
Lifespan: A few decades at most; then the phage tissue senesces, flakes away, and ordinary roots inherit the newly enriched vein of soil.

6. The Microphonic Quiet (Consortium nameless)  
Ambient rather than embodied. Bacteria suspended in every drip form flocks that orient to infrasound the way birds orient to geomagnetism. Their cell walls beat like speakers too small for hearing, translating the relics’ carrier waves into pressure modulations plants can sense through xylem tension.  
Behaviour: When a relic vents its survey fog, the microphonic bacteria thicken into a biofilm over each pool. Surface tension flattens, echoes sharpen, and for a few minutes the cave’s soundscape becomes mirror-bright. Then the film breaks apart, distributing whatever message it has translated into the groundwater.

What They Think (As Far As Anyone Can Tell)  
• Safety is depth.  
• Light is an old superstition.  
• Change arrives on the breath of the quiet machines; refuse no gift, for it will not be offered twice.  
• Every pulse in the stone might be a sentence, so grow ears everywhere—on leaves, on spores, on the mineral surface of your own bones.  
• Life is not the possession of a body but the occupancy of space; leave no pore unused.

What They Make  
Given millennia, the parliament conglomerates its surplus minerals into scroll-like slabs that hang from root scaffolds. Human geologists would call them stromatolites; here they are libraries. Layer by translucent layer, each slab encodes the cave’s autobiography in trace-element barcodes. When one grows too heavy it snaps, shatters, and the shards seed satellite libraries where they fall.

How to Be Received  
Arrive mute. Brush nothing that drips; knocking a pearl of water out of sequence is the local equivalent of shouting in a cathedral. Kneel—standing leaks heat, and heat is motion. If you can still for a full pulse tide, a Breathroot will test you with a filament as soft as cobweb across the back of your hand. Accept the touch; it is taking a chemical census. Should the cave find your exhalations tolerable, a Glass Grape may unseal near your feet, gifting you a pinch of sap that flashes into frost.  
Taste it and you will carry home a harmless bacterium whose only talent is to dream ultrafast when you sleep. The dreams will be of dark water, distant pressure, and roots singing with the voices of broken engines.

That is who lives here: an ecosystem knitted from stone, silence and the long-memory hum of relic machines—life that learned not merely to endure the absence of light but to cultivate it as empty ground in which more intricate chemistries might bloom.
