WHO WALKS THE PLATEAU

1. The Regnants (people of Vires Regnum)

Origins and body  
• They are what became of the first mountain miners who refused to descend when the seas vanished. Ten thousand generations of graft-surgery and lateral gene transfer have left them recognizably bipedal but botany-tinted.   
• Epidermis: dark umber laced with lenticel pores. Beneath lies a sub-dermal palisade layer packed with modified chromatophores that double as micro-chloroplast farms; at noon their skin photosynthesizes enough to cover one-third of daily caloric need.   
• Blood: no longer water-based. A treacly, fructose-rich syrup circulates, osmotic with the world’s own solvent. An ordinary cut crystallizes in seconds, sealing itself in amber.   
• Lungs: reinforced into bellows; oxygen is scarce, so every breath is routed through a chevron of nano-scale wind vanes that harvest the moving air for electrical charge—micro-piezo generators that power the nervous system’s external ports.   
• Skeleton: trabecular scaffolds grown from cellulose-reinforced bone. Each long bone contains a resonance cavity tuned to the octave that carries civic announcements; their very marrow vibrates when city news is sung.

Life cycle  
• Children begin as seeds. Two, sometimes three adults braid gamete threads into a walnut-sized ovule, slip it under the bark of a guardian tree, and let the city’s cambium knit a nursery womb. Nine months later the bark splits; a sticky infant slides out already coated in symbiotic yeast that will police its skin for life.   
• Adolescents spend a decade “unrooted,” traveling on stridulation rail to all eight canton-organ districts, apprenticing, learning which civic organ they wish to join. At twenty they choose a graft-site on the municipal super-tree, fuse a personal taproot, and are recorded as full citizens.    
• The aged do not die so much as lignify. Over centuries their biochemistry tilts toward cellulose; joints seize, skin thickens, consciousness slows until each elder becomes an upright pillar in one of the Hall-groves. The groves are municipal memory banks; dendro-cyborg scribes read the elders’ ring-thought with ironwood needles.

Culture & mind  
• Language is trifold: voiced consonants for gossip, drum-taps on chest resonance cavities for official record, and infrasonic moans—felt more than heard—used for courtship and apology.   
• Currency is Pulse-Credit, measured in milliseconds of access to the continental power network. The loud get rich.   
• Crime is conceptualized as “dissonance.” The highest felony is to alter a public rhythm without permit.

Occupations  
• Arbor-Smiths coax architecture; their mnemonic toolkit is a backpack of parasitic vines containing pre-written RNA macros.   
• Phloem-Tribunes patrol sugar canals, levying tolls in decibels—your speaking voice is sampled; the richer the timbre, the lower the toll.   
• Luthiers of Transit tune the stridulation rails daily, sanding a square millimeter here, growing a brace root there, to keep the shockwaves coherent. They listen with the whole skeleton pressed to the ground.

Cosmos & devotion  
• Theology is Resonant Monism: all things are compression waves in differing media. Salvation is phase alignment. Choir-Mass happens at dusk; thousands stand ankle-deep in a plaza membrane while a cantor-engine thrums the fundamental note of the plateau. For three minutes everyone’s heart valves beat in global unison.

2. The Other Thinking Things

A. The Aeolian Choirs  
Glass-winged carnivorous wasps whose hives form group minds. Each new brood inherits an echo template of its parent swarm’s memories; knowledge is literally airborne vibration. They negotiate insect treaties with the Regnants, pollinating roof gardens in exchange for access to warm exhaust vents on cold nights.

B. Root-Minds  
Knots of century-old xylem infected by mycelial mathematics. When enough topo-logical loops accumulate, the knot wakes, grows cilia-thin feelers to the surface, and writes questions in Morse-tapping sap. Philosophers lie on the ground for days, answering. Root-Minds move only by growing; a thirty-meter stroll is a millennium’s labor.

C. The Pallid Cartographers  
Nomads carved from the salt that falls like rain. They are quasi-mineral homunculi, conscious only during storms, sculpting themselves anew with every precipitation. Between storms they stand as statues storing positional data about the plateau’s slow tectonic tilting. Vires Regnum buys their memories for navigational updates.

3. Macroscopic Fauna

• Grazer-Knots: brachiating sextapeds woven of living tendons and leaf-pads. They chew surface cambium into boluses that ferment in vine-sacs, extruding fertilizer pellets the city harvests for delicate nursery-orchards.   
• Cleaver-Sails: the blind lizards described in the brief. One subspecies has been semi-domesticated; citizens tie bells tuned to safe frequencies around the sails’ spines so the beasts avoid crowded plazas.   
• Lantern-Mites: pinhead arachnids that metabolize bioluminescent terpenes. They dust every dark corner, a moving constellary map; scholars read city traffic patterns by how constellations drift overnight.

4. Microscopic & Sub-visible Life

• Endophyt Virids: viruses that splice additional photosystems into already-mature leaves, increasing sugar yield during famine years. City agronomists maintain a vault of virid templates, unleashing tailored plagues when energy forecasts dip.   
• Ferro-Lithic Spores: capable of eating the iron oxide sand that rides the high jet, excreting magnetic crystals. In lab-groves they are seeded into living compass pillars; travelers consult the pillars’ twitching needles before setting out.

5. Symbioses with the City

• Clothing is alive: cloak-moss that wicks excess skin-syrup, turning it into scent-ink used for personal sigils. A clean cloak smells faintly of caramel and ozone.   
• Tools are coaxed: a hammer is a thickened root still fed by hair-filaments; its head regrows after every strike, trading durability for immortality.   
• Companion spores ride in nasal sinuses, exhaling enzymatic antifreeze each dawn to keep human mucus from flash-crystallizing in the five-degree air.

6. A DAY IN THE LIFE

Dawn — Wind organs in the outer hex-tiles catch the diurnal gravity wave, booming the wake note: D-flat below sub-contra. The city exhales; membrane roofs sag, releasing stale carbon dioxide like sighing bellows.  
Mid-morning — Market crescendos in the Photosquare. Vendors grow their own stalls mid-pitch, commanding vines to arch and flower into display racks. A youngster hawks freshly cracked caramel glass—yesterday’s precipitate—sold as travel rations.  
Noon — Solar zenith, such as it is. Citizens turn faces upward; melanin shutters retract, purple chromatophores bloom, and every plaza brightens as thousands photosynthesize in silent reverence.  
Afternoon — An urgent double-tremor rattles the bones: derailment on the western stridulation line. Luthiers race, sliding on knee-pads of slick phloem, to sand the offending node before rush hour.  
Evening — Potassium skyfall threatens. Public address organs modulate from friendly thirds into warning tritones; bridges re-lattice into domes. The last pedestrian dives through closing fronds just as the salt begins to whistle. Inside, people settle around elder trunks, listening to stories bowl-drummed from lignified ribs.  
Night — Calm again. Lantern-mites drift, re-mapping the avenues. Somewhere in the under-grooves a Root-Mind germinates the first twitch of a brand-new question.

7. THREATS & PROMISES

• Glacial Quiet: a spreading pestilence that dampens resonance chambers, muffling the world. Where it goes silent, predators congregate. The Regnants breed anti-fungal murmurs—engineered sound that kills the quiet.  
• Sugar Piracy: remote tribes of ex-miners ride tamed Cleaver-Sails, drilling covert siphon bores to steal sap. Vires Regnum debates granting them citizenship instead of war—the city is ever hungry for more voices in its choir.  
• Ascension Project: Arbor-Smiths dream of growing a sky-pier modelled on extinct trees, tall enough to punch through the dust haze and give the plateau direct sunshine for the first time in eons. Critics claim too much sweetness would be diverted upward, starving ground life. Debate rages, literally, in the key of E.

CONCLUSION

To live on the Plateau of Green Silence is to inhabit biography written in wood and waveform. Every creature—lizard, virus, person, or city itself—contributes a note to the polyphonic survival of a world that long ago traded water for syrup, rock for rhizome, and speech for resonance. Nothing is mute; even the bones remember the song.
