Who (and what) lives here

1. The City-Body  

Strictly speaking, the metropolis itself is the primary inhabitant and everything else is tenant, ornament or larval tissue. Botanists who have studied amputated fragments call the whole organism “Arbor-Plexus ichorhygra”—a single, modular super-plant whose millions of trunks, fronds and root mats are just reiterated organs. Sap pressure equalises through perforated xylem tubes that lace the plateau like nerves; electrical potentials ride those columns, coordinating the opening of streets and the folding shut against katabatic winds.  
• Age: about 740 growth-rings (ring counts vary because the plant periodically inverts its own layers).  
• Mind: discontinuous but real. Each district holds a cortical knot—a lignified maze of meristem that can hold a few hours of electrochemical memory before it is overwritten by new growth. When knots cross-pollinate signals, the whole city manifests a slow, dreamlike awareness that lasts for minutes at a time. Visitors walking the avenues during those intervals sometimes feel watched in a way that makes their hair rise; afterwards the sensation is gone, as if the gaze had drifted elsewhere.  
• Drives: stay moist, stay sunlit, stay whole. Anything that threatens those needs—the saw of erosion, the shock of ice, the foreign chemistry of a camp-fire—triggers an autonomic response (wall thickening, corridor constriction, silica flush) much faster than the Keepers could arrange on their own.

2. The Keepers (also called the Humidling folk)

Anatomy  
• Human-sized, though seldom fully upright: they habitually move in a half-crouch adapted to the low, arched passages.  
• Epidermis studded with cutin scales that bead water and let them lean against saturated bark without chilling to hypothermia. Under magnification those scales look like overlapping leaf stomata.  
• Blood is iron-poor and carries dissolved trehalose; it resists freezing the way tree sap does. A Keeper’s body temperature hovers around six degrees Celsius—touch one and your warm hand will steam.  
• Lungs are small. Most oxygen exchange occurs across vascularised throat-pouches that flutter silently, so they breathe with mouth closed and seem preternaturally quiet.

Origins  
The plateau offers too little free energy to support mammals. The Keepers are something else: a convergent experiment the City ran a few centuries ago when it needed mobile hands. Spores embedded in warmed hollows condensed into mycelial mannequins; lichens laminated the shapes with photosynthetic skin; then hormonal saps coaxed the ensemble into bilateral symmetry. The first Keepers stood, walked, saw the view over the cliff and came back indoors. They have stayed ever since.

Numbers & distribution  
Never more than forty adults at a time; usually half that. Population is capped by the City, which grows a new germ-cocoon only when another Keeper dies and releases its nutrient quota. Child and parent share no genome—kinship here is purely a matter of who teaches you to walk the root-paths.

Perception & language  
Vision is tuned to the dim interior green; daylight outside the dome blinds them like a magnesium flare. Their primary sense, however, is barometric: a line of jelly-filled canals runs from ear to ankle, letting them feel minute pressure shifts in the City’s vascular pipes. When two Keepers meet they exchange paragraphs of meaning by drumming heel or knuckle against a wall, hissing soft moisture into a crack, then waiting for the returning pulse. Outsiders hear only a random Morse of drops and creaks.

Nutrition  
Each sunset they kneel at sap-wells and siphon a thin, sugary exudate that the City expresses for them—half nectar, half intravenous meal. For solid matter they cultivate “moth-rice”: shed wing-sheath scales sterilised, raked into mats and inoculated with a white mould that reduces the chitin to digestible carbohydrate. Meat is taboo; anything that dies whole is ceded to the architecture.

Work  
• Aperture tending: pruning corky growths so windows stay open the exact width of a palm.  
• Stress surveying: crawling into sub-ice tunnels to listen for basalt groans; if one resonates wrong, they pack the void with pliant root-felt to damp it.  
• Lumen shepherding: at dusk they tilt phosphor sacs so their glow falls along traffic corridors and not into sleeping bays.  
• Diplomacy with water: they redirect drips, scratch micro-channels, even stand for hours as human downspouts, letting cold runoff braid along their arms to a thirsty root.

Art & ritual  
Because combustion is impossible, their celebrations use no flame; instead they sculpt temporary brightness. On the solstice every seed sac in the city is teased to overglow at once. Keepers then cut long slits in their arm-scales, letting threads of haemolymph leak. The sugar in the blood feeds the sacs, which swell into fist-sized lanterns that drift upward until snagged by the canopy. From the cliffs the whole dome looks as if someone turned the constellation maps inside out and set them gently on the ground.

Lifespan & death  
A Keeper lasts roughly thirty winters. When joints stiffen and scales lose sheen, the elder walks alone to a bulge of gutrock known as the Shatter Table. There the City has no holdfasts; frost plucking and sun shock crack the stone into gravel, and in that sterile bowl the Keeper lies down. Lichen takes three days to quilt the body; after a week only a waxy mannequin remains, already sprouting the first red initials of a fern that will root through the ribcage. When decomposition is complete, the City evaluates the released minerals and, if the budget allows, buds a new green child in a nursery pod.

3. The Minor Co-inhabitants  

a. Wing-sheath moths  
Technically symbionts of the City, but culturally vital. Their wings are transparent plates of silica micro-scales that bend light upward; Keepers position roosting colonies like living mirrors to bounce spare daylight into deep chambers. The moths feed on evaporating sap vapour and are rewarded with protection from freeze shock by nested fronds.

b. Glass voles  
Four-fingered, furless, amphibious creatures that live mostly in the inter-root rills. Keepers tolerate them because the voles’ constant nibbling trims algae mats that would otherwise clog capillary flow. Occasionally a vole drowns in a cul-de-sac pool; the corpse is left untouched, becoming impromptu fertiliser.

c. Fungus combs  
Decomposer organs of the City, yet autonomous enough to migrate if nutrient gradients shift. Their slow peristaltic crawl leaves behind cleaned, almost surgical bone-white plant tissue—hygienic hallways where Keepers like to sleep.

4. The Ghost Lattice  

Visitors report glimpsing tall, brittle silhouettes that stride through walls or linger at the edge of pools. These are not spirits but vacancy memories: negative impressions in the meristem’s charge pattern, photographs made of reluctance rather than light. The City prints them whenever a space stays empty too long, as a prompt for itself to fill or transform that void. Keepers ignore the apparitions, though they admit the Lattice is capable of teaching: a remembered absent stair may inspire the roots to grow a real one.

Why outsiders rarely meet the residents

To a Keeper, the arrival of a warm-blooded traveller is a seismic risk: too much heat, too chaotic a microbiome, and above all too much idle noise that might confuse the City’s own inner dialogue. They observe from hollow boles, decide whether you are transient weather or a potential parasite, and usually choose non-interference. If you prove considerate—speaking softly, never striking sparks, letting fallen seeds rest where they lie—the Keepers may approach on the third dusk. They will tap a question down the nearest wall: “How long do you intend to stay part of the world?” Your answer, whatever it is, will be folded into the sap and remembered as a tremor in the wood far longer than the sound of your leaving boots.

So the plateau hosts three intellects nested like matryoshkas: the macro-mind of the City, slow and vascular; the nimble, melancholy handiwork of its Keepers; and the flicker-quick heuristics of moth and vole and fungus. Together they make a society tighter than any town of stone: a breathing, dripping civic organism that treats even its daughters as cells and every foreign step as both a wound and a wonder.
