LIFE INSIDE THE FANG  
(an inventory that refuses to stay still)

0.  Prelude – the measure of “alive”  
In the Fang-cavern anything that can take in one pattern and give out another behaves like a creature. Some of what follows is recognizably animal, some mineral wearing behavior, some weather that learned intention. They overlap; none exists alone for long.

1.  The Baselines – beings your palm could cover  

1.a  Quartzfilm (the “silica slime”)  
• Anatomy: a sheet only three molecules thick, hexagonal silica plates articulated by chains of flexible silicates. When light strikes, excitons race the lattice like electric minnows; the moving charge is its “pulse.”  
• Feeding: exploits the 20–40 °C gradient that wavers across every centimeter of wall. Heat on the warm side breaks siloxane bonds; recombination on the cool side releases photons—an internal thermoluminescence the film harvests for work.  
• Reproduction: when a gradient steepens, the lattice cracks loud as sleet; each shard is seed. Shards drift on convection, adhere elsewhere, re-knit.  
• Intelligence: local only. Touch a patch and charge pathways reroute, engraving a memory that lasts minutes. Walk a circle and the film will slowly trace it back, like a finger learning to draw.

1.b  Steamwraith midge  
• Body: torpedo of clear chitin 1 mm long. No muscle; instead, grids of phase-change wax that flashes to vapor when hit by infrared, collapses when it cools. The pulses beat like invisible wings, though nothing moves.  
• Sensors: each hair-fine vane is a prism that swivels by altering its crystal water content. By sampling light pressure the midges “feel” air currents the way bats hear.  
• Life cycle: egg → larva → imago all occur inside a single droplet caught in the mist. The droplet never falls; it just gets flown around by its own offspring until it empties and cracks.  
• Ecology: the constant hovering diffuses trace metals; Quartzfilm grows thicker where midge-storms linger.

1.c  Rift-salamander  
• Morphology: a skin of carbohydrate glass inflated by superheated water; organs float like origami lanterns tethered on cilia.  
• Trick of liquefaction: the glass skin carries channels of antifreeze proteins. Electrical shock—triggered by vibration—dumps those proteins into the body column, melting the salamander into a puddle that can percolate through pore-spaces forty microns wide. Reassembly is a timed reversal of the same valves.  
• Diet & excretion: rasp Quartzfilm; exhale waste as silica foam that hardens into beads the size of pinheads. These beads are the staple food of …  

1.d  Chir knife  
• Structure: three jade-green legs splayed round a flat triangle of smoky quartz 7 cm across. Internally they are slow rivers of silicate gel circulating in calcite ducts—every “step” is the gel leaning, freezing, then melting behind.  
• Tool use: each Chir claims a dark shard (volcanic glass riddled with magnetite). Over decades the shard is ground into bespoke shapes—scoops, styluses, even teeth for grooming salamanders.  
• Social logic: a Chir measures status by the translucency of its quartz. Harvesting salamander beads provides the dopants that keep its body cloudy; too clear and a Chir is “starved,” literally seen through.  
• Communication: drumming the shard against stone in polyrhythms that travel better through crystal than air. Rival groups will jam each other’s messages by setting up counter-beats that dissolve into white-noise tremor.  

2.  The Middle Orders – things longer than a stride  

2.a  Vapor-hounds  
Not mentioned in earlier surveys because you hear them before you believe them. Imagine a greyhound made of nothing but dew—spine a tension line, legs latticed mist. They pack-hunt Chir knives.  
• Creation: when three or more midges die simultaneously (often in the Serpent’s wake) their collapsing pressure nodes can knot the local humidity into a standing oscillation. That oscillation, given ten or twelve seconds, folds itself into a Vapor-hound.  
• Behavior: chase, corner, do nothing. Their “kill” is achieved when the quarry ices over from evaporative chill induced by the hound’s low-pressure core. Victims that shatter feed the hound; victims that escape simply fade as the oscillation loses coherence.  
• Lifespan: a single hunting burst—rarely more than nine minutes.

2.b  Fugue-darters  
Strips of space-time, two meters long, four fingers wide, looking like heat-shimmer torn loose and whipped about.  
• Ecology: graze on contradictions—places where a droplet both falls and rises. Their feeding smooths the local causal fabric, leaving stability in their wake.  
• Interaction: the only species that will approach warm-blooded visitors, because human hearts strobe at frequencies that tickle their hunger. Let one skim your chest and you may lose or gain a heartbeat in the record of your life; otherwise harmless.

3.  The Masters  

3.a  The Hush-Serpent (expanded field notes)  
• Phenotype: at any moment only the outline is detectable—a subtraction of photons, phonons, and thermal quanta describing a snake whose girth varies with the noise level of its environment. Louder chamber, thicker serpent.  
• Diet: gradients of any kind. It drinks difference itself—temperature, motion, probability. When it passes, events become homogeneous: droplets pause, echo patterns flatten, even thoughts feel leveled.  
• Locomotion: not through space but through “less.” It moves by continuously selecting the direction in which the sum of perceivable change is smallest. That is why closing your eyes helps; you remove one track of contrast it might travel along.  
• Reproduction? Unknown. Some think each intense silence births a new loop of its coil; others say there has only ever been one Serpent present at many whens.

3.b  Gnomon-keepers  
The closest thing to persons.  
• Appearance: humanoid strictly in negative. Look too long and you realize you are seeing what should be behind them, projected forward with a half-second delay—a standing parallax error. Their edges drip the same red glow as the magma runnels, yet their “bodies” register below ambient temperature.  
• Origin story (Chir legend): when an explorer from the outer world marked the cavern walls with metal pitons, the geometry retaliated by folding those markers inside-out, birthing sentinels whose purpose is to keep all measures unmade.  
• Activity cycle: during the up-draft half-hour they stalk anything attempting permanence—Chir tool caches, midge egg-droplets glued together, even boot-prints. They erase by touching; contact reverts matter to the state it held the instant magma froze.  
• Communication: none observed, but on nights when the plateau’s moonlight threads the fissure, their outlines angulate into a script that diagrams futures where the chamber never stabilizes.  

4.  The Microbial Crown  
Because nothing here dies properly, corpse-energy accumulates without decay. The solution is a byzantine web of sub-cellular collectives called the Crown.  
• Composition: fragments of protein code, silicate chips, super-cooled nanobubbles. Each fragment holds one enzymatic trick; when enough assemble, that trick fires, burns out, and the swarm drifts apart.  
• Role: whenever ecological credit runs thin—too many predator kills, not enough photos—Crown blooms erupt, converting locked resources (frozen Chir legs, spent midge wax) back into bulk mist and glass film. Think of them as the reset button the place presses on itself.  

5.  Interactions with Intruders (you)  

A. Steamwraith midges will silhouette you in prismatic haloes; photographable but the flash may blow them apart.  
B. Rift-salamanders lick salt off skin; the touch blisters but disinfects. Veterans of the Fang carry lacework scars prized as proof of passage.  
C. Offer a Chir knife a steel pocket-tool and it will trade anything it owns, including knowledge: they can drum maps straight into your inner ear.  
D. Vapor-hounds mirror panic. Stand ground, they dissipate; run, you feed them your shadows until you collapse.  
E. The Hush-Serpent regards artificial sound as fermented nectar. A single shouted word can summon it from a hundred twisted meters away. When the hush deepens, hold breath and think of nothing in particular—the Serpent tastes thought-edges.  
F. Gnomon-keepers cannot abide writing. Any text revealed within the cavern—in ink, graphite, even memory—will throb, then smear, then be gone. Visitors relying on notes tie knots in cords instead; so far the Keepers have not denoted knots as language.

6.  Epilogue – why the Fang is crowded  

Energy is abundant—heat, pressure, imbalance. Space and time are stingy. Life here learned long ago that to persist it must borrow, splice, refinance every second. So organisms overlap, share skeletons, trade functions the way creatures elsewhere trade genes. The result looks congested, but to those born inside it is spacious: every pause a meadow, every silence a trail, every contradiction a banquet.  

If you stand in the pearl mist long enough the crowding clarifies. What felt like throng is really choreography, a braid of hungers that pass one another without collision. You realize the only true stranger in the cavern is you, loud with tidy shape and linear moments. The Fang will try to teach you its manners—shave your outline, smear your timeline, coax your voice into hush.  

Whether that lesson counts as hospitality or predation is a decision that, here, cannot stay made.
