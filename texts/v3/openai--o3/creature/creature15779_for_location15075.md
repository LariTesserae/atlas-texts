Who (and what) occupies the Uplift Plateau

1. The Foundational Fabric  
   a. Cloud-lichen (“coriace”) – The velvet skin you felt is not one organism but a guild. Three partners share each strand:  
      • A deep-purple phototroph that drinks in the violet haze.  
      • A clear, rubbery fungus that provides the pulse; it metabolises geothermal trace heat and pumps it through the mat in peristaltic waves, the heartbeat you sensed.  
      • Thread-thin veins of living nickel-iron that grow like mycelia. They conduct both charge and data: chemical signals that keep the mat synched across kilometres.  
      The guild is self-aware only in the slow, vegetal sense—yet it is caretaker to everything built above it. Any creature that bleeds onto the lichen is sampled; the fabric revises its biochemistry nightly, producing custom growth factors, venoms or antibiotics to shepherd the larger web.

2. The Vertical Grazer – Indigo Ibex (“chevrons”)  
   • Six limbs, two purely for anchoring by suction, four for motion.  
   • Bone replaced by porous basalt impregnated with collagen; the skeleton doubles as a CO₂ scrubber, pulling carbon from the air and locking it away.  
   • Herds move like bead curtains, each animal tethered to the next by filaments of evaporative saliva; if one falls the line snaps safely, but while intact it lets the group share pressure sensations, so a misstep made by the frontmost foot is corrected three bodies downstream before it happens.  
   • They never vocalise. When danger nears they exhale a plume of citrine dust that glows faintly; the plume collapses gravity locally, tripping predators into awkward hops the ibex exploit for escape.

3. The Cycloidal Birds (“turnwings”)  
   • Wings are centimetres thin, built from keratin lattices laminated with optically pumped chlorophyll. By angling veins they steer sunlight through an internal diffraction grid, routing energy to whichever muscle fibres are firing.  
   • Feathers moult as entire solar sheets; those cast-off films float for hours, charging in the haze until they crescent and dive—kamikaze power cells sought eagerly by the plateau’s insectile machines.  
   • Language: They converse only in colour. A turnwing in courtship becomes a living spectroscope, rippling every hue the plateau’s dim light can furnish. Mates compare after-images rather than direct displays; compatibility is judged by how long the colour memory lingers on the retina.

4. The Gas Leviathans (“high-whales”)  
   • One animal equals one weather system. Their skins are organo-silicate aerogels studded with valves that open in Mandelbrot sequences, shedding heat without ever forming a true hole.  
   • Inside is cathedral space: stalactites of enzymic ice, warm convection pillars, lakes of oxidiser. Spores caught in the intake are cultivated on floating mats, each leviathan a drifting nursery.  
   • When a mat matures, the leviathan inverts its buoyancy for minutes—an atmospheric breaching. The sudden drop coughs the spores downslope in shimmering curtains. Observers report that time seems to warp more violently inside those curtains; physics bends easiest where the whales have breathed.

5. The Substrate Burrowers (“snub-moles”)  
   • No eyes; a corona of pressure-sensitive horns reads stress lines in the rising rock.  
   • They drink stone. Stomachs excrete a peptide that pries apart crystalline bonds; minerals pass through a gut that grades them into nano-sized water spirits—ghost-colloids that stay liquid even when gravity loops upside-down. Their tunnels therefore remain half-flooded, capillary reservoirs threaded through the massif.  
   • Each mole lives only a season, but their burrow network is inherited. Newborns receive a map encoded in the initial rhythm of their heartbeats—an inducible pulse pattern that resonates with certain boreholes, guiding them to the oldest galleries first.

6. The Clockwork Ephemerals  
   These are the offspring of the mercury-bright galls. Once they tumble free they choose among three developmental paths:  
   a. Surveyors – Scarab-shaped, jewel bright. They climb high, unfold petal antennae, and run slow electro-sonar. Data leaks back to the lichen guild as faint DC current.  
   b. Scavengers – Centipedes of sprung bronze. They harvest cast-off solar feathers, ibex horn dust, dead spores—any high-grade matter—and smelt alloys in warm abdomens.  
   c. Midwives – Spidery forms that live exactly twenty-two hours. They seek damaged vine-columns, knit breaches with wire silk, then combust, leaving weld beads where their bodies lay.  
   None endure beyond three days; dissolution is designed. Their parts disaggregate into ions the roots will drink by morning.

7. The Predator Broods  
   Predation here favours intellect over speed; nothing can sprint on a cliff that rewrites its slopes between footfalls.  
   • “Mirage Lynxes” are not cats at all but fractal holograms cast by a pouch-bearer that lies hidden under moss. The decoy stalks, pounces, distracts. When prey flinches the real animal—no larger than a hand—injects a torpor venom through the soil with a hollow tooth root that can reach a metre.  
   • “Spear Shadows” inhabit the cambial-flow trees. A branch that hardened upright in the morning may soften at dusk; the shadow rides that flexure, its body a ribbon of magnetite scales. When the tree levels, the ribbon flicks outward, impaling airborne birds before retracting to digest in the tree’s vascular slime.

8. Transient Intelligences  
   a. The Plateau-Mind  
      The data-metal threads in the lichen, the surveyor scarabs, even the birds’ cast-off solar sheets lace into a continent-sized neural mesh. Once every seven local days a standing wave ripples through it, and for several hours the entire ecosystem achieves coherence: a supra-organism able to model its own geology and write micro-adjustments into growth routines. During coherence events gravity kinks more sharply, time hesitates—the fabric is debugging the constants beneath it. Visitors sometimes report low, vowel-like chanting in the wind; instrumentation shows no sound, only patterned fluctuation in barometric pressure, as though the plateau were humming to itself in a language of weather.  
   b. The Skin-Talkers  
      Rare among the ibex are births of a seventh limb: a prehensile tongue of photosensitive flesh that rests along the spine. Those mutants grow reflective patches in their fur that flash ideograms. Gathered herds become living dot-matrix displays, conversing across valleys with news of new water pans and safe paths. The plateau-mind rewards them with warmer lichen beds and fungal nodules rich in lithium—its version of sweets.  
   c. The Leviathan Oracles  
      When two gas whales cross trajectories their valve patterns entrain, collapsing both into a single audio pulse lasting five, maybe six human heartbeats. Everything living freezes to listen; in laboratory playback the pulses resolve into primes far higher than the whales’ neural capacity implies. Many believe the primes are beacons, broadcast for yet-unformed intellects that will read them eons hence when the plateau is something entirely other.

9. Microlife – the Chorus  
   The quiet you cherish is, microscopically, thunderous. Bacterial consortia line every droplet, swapping plasmids that alter fundamental cell processes in minutes. Viroids roam like editors, excising genes, pasting others in upside-down. The whole microbiome behaves less like population genetics and more like an orchestra of quick thinkers improvising on a shared theme: Make it work up here, now, despite whatever the stone decides next.

10. What Does Not Live Here (but tries)  
    • Ordinary fungi spore in, find no foothold; the lichen guild digests them for phosphorus.  
    • Human camp microbes—skin staph, gut flora—enter burrow reservoirs and emerge recombinant within hours. Researchers quit testing vaccines; the adaptations outrun any measure.  
    • Conventional machines seize. Gyroscopes lose calibration as local frames of reference buckle. Only devices built from native alloys and coded with stochastic fault tolerance survive—imitations of the plateau’s own clockwork ephemerals.

11. A Note on Death  
    Nothing decays by rot. Instead, dematerialisation proceeds via “unlocking”: the nickel-iron veins alter their redox potential, attracting the body’s metals upward until tissue de-ions and powders. The plateau absorbs memory, chemistry, structure, returning each to circulation. To die here is to be versioned—old code broken into reagents for whatever the land will write next.

Inhabitants in brief:  
• Basal, half-sentient cloud-lichen.  
• Indigo suction-goats; silent, communal, skeletal scrubbers of carbon.  
• Cycloidal birds, solar-sailing chromatic linguists.  
• Kilometre gas whales, airborne farms and oracles.  
• Snub-moles, stone-drinkers and hydrologists.  
• Three-day clockwork insects spawned by metallic galls, ecosystem technicians.  
• Stealth predators that weaponise illusion or arboreal elasticity.  
• Distributed plateau-mind timeshared by everything from lichen to leviathan.  

The plateau is populated entirely by specialists in flux—organisms, mechanisms and super-organisms whose first competence is to keep living while the ground, the air and the laws between them continually rewrite themselves. Existence here is not a state but a verb, conjugated differently by every heartbeat of the range below.
