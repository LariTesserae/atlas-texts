Who lives beneath the Cloud-Vault  

(Everything named below can be seen in a single, slow pan of the head; the basin is that small.  Everything named below could also be missed in a single blink; the basin is that dense.)

1.  Cistern-Moss  

   • Appearance: A quilting of lime-green cushions.  The surface is pin-pored, each pore rimmed with clear silica that flashes when a shaft of stray sun sneaks through the fog (perhaps once a season).  
   • Work: The moss is the basin’s water engine.  Each pore pumps twice a minute—drawing mist in, exhaling it as droplets that roll downslope toward the central pool.  
   • Oddity: The moss is mildly contractile; if you kneel too long the whole pad, ever so politely, bulges elsewhere to reclaim its flattened shape.  Footprints last fifteen heartbeats.

2.  Mirror-Fern  

   • Form: Shoulder-tall trunks plated in hexagonal leaf-scales; at night the underside of each scale turns liquid-silver, reflecting any glimmer upward to the Cloud-Vault as if the plant were feeding the sky.  
   • Diet: Photosynthesis is a hobby; true calories come from “fog-grazing.”  Capillary tufts along the rachis comb vapor, distill minerals, and drip them inward along glassy veins.  
   • Use: Salamanders roost among the trunks because the mirrored leaves scatter a predator’s outline into nonsense.

3.  Rain-Combing Vines (“Waterhair”)  

   • Structure: Wrist-thick cords braided with transparent trichomes that comb the air for microscopic droplets.  Each vine ends in a single ultramarine bell-flower—one bell per plant, renewed only once every seven years.  
   • Significance: When the annual moth emergence coincides with a bell opening, the air smells briefly of cold honey and graphite.  Locals—if the corvids count as locals—time their breeding to that scent.

4.  Bronze-Obsidian Salamanders (“Vault Whelps”)  

   • Size & Look: Half-meter long; skin a drifting constellation of copper motes on darkness.  Eyes are panes of clear gelatin; the pupils are actually pigment-free gaps that show the brain’s silver myelin.  
   • Motion: They do not walk so much as pour forward, ribs flexing like a centipede’s legs inside the flesh.  
   • Calendar: The species breeds exactly once per Cloud-Vault century.  In that night the pool glows faint red—scientists think from hemoglobin shed en masse—yet no moving bodies are observed.  By dawn, eggs stud the shale like ruby cuff-buttons and every adult is simply gone.  Twelve years later the first juveniles creep out of the peat.  No one knows where the elders spend the intervening decades.

5.  Vault Crabs (“Pulse-Clacks”)  

   • Habitat: A lacework of tunnels one knuckle below every square step of soil.  Each crab keeps a private burrow but shares a single city-wide acoustic line: chitinous taps on shale walls that travel farther than sound through air.  
   • Purpose: The rhythm maps foodfall.  A broken twig, a dead moth, the dissolution of a salamander—each registers as a pitch change and the nearest crabs converge, leaving the surface immaculate.  
   • Culture (if you stretch the word): The tapping rhythm is so regular that geologists misread it as micro-seismic tremor until a footing snapped through and revealed the white carapace choir.

6.  Midnight-Tile Moths  

   • Day State: Thousands roost on damp rock, wings overlapped like roof tiles; the wing patterns mimic water droplets refracting dim light, so a cliff face looks merely wetter than usual.  
   • Night State: At the first minute of civil dusk—here, roughly 14:27 outside time—the entire colony launches.  The sound is the opening of a hundred silk fans.  In flight they become negative lanterns, stealing what little luminance exists; the air darkens where they pass.  
   • Lifecycle: Larvae hide inside the moss cushions, rasping tunnels that the moss later heals; each trail becomes a capillary that enhances the moss’s pumping.  Moth and moss co-engineer one another’s plumbing.

7.  Ribbon-Slip Serpent  

   • Morphology: Forty centimeters long, half a finger wide, cartilage so fine it folds like wet paper.  Scales are cobalt except for a single mirror-finish stripe that runs skull to tail.  
   • Locomotion: It “swims” on the water film—never breaking surface tension—by rippling its ribs in metachronal waves.  Seen in cross-light it resembles handwriting being pulled across glass.  
   • Predation: No strike.  The vault is too silent.  Instead the snake calculates intercepts; when probability predicts overlap, its mouth is already around the prey’s head.  Death by arrival.

8.  Dusk-Corvids (“Pouch-Crows”)  

   • Appearance: Charcoal feathers dusted with mica, so the birds look carved from the walls.  Eyes cloud-white, yet sighted.  
   • Tools: Each family maintains a cache of spheroid moss “pockets.”  Into these they stuff shelled crabs to soften overnight; morning finds them squeezing the bundle until loosened meat weeps out.  
   • Language: Calls are ultrasonic clicks (inaudible to you, but salamanders flinch).  Below 20 kHz the birds are mute; above, they gossip ceaselessly.

9.  The Cloud-Vault Itself (The Old Respirator)  

   • Physiology: Imagine one gigantic lung inverted, its pleura the pearly under-belly of fog.  Exhalation is the slow drizzle down fern-leaves; inhalation, the silent hoovering of warmth up the cliffs at night.  
   • Nerves & Memory: Voltage differences flicker where mica dusts the granodiorite.  Scientists could call it piezoelectric charge; a visiting monk called it “the dream of stone.”  Either way, the basin remembers pressure—hoof, boot, claw—and equalizes it over years, smoothing all trespass.  
   • Motive: Equilibrium.  Every organism inside is a lung-cell, taking or giving exactly its due.  Push too hard—shout, cut, burn—and the Vault will not expel you; it will simply absorb the damage, respire a fraction colder, and wait for the trespasser to leave or sleep forever.

10.  Things Too Small To Name  

   • Iron-chewing bacteria that polish shale into ink-black mirrors.  
   • Spore-fleas that leap only when a moth wing passes overhead, mistaking the turbulence for storm front.  
   • A virus that lives solely in corvid saliva and moss pores, ensuring neither population overwhelms the other.  
   • Micro-lichens that fluoresce teal, rolling genetic dice each generation to adjust brightness to the basin’s unchanging twilight.

Relationships (the short, perfect loop)  

Water condenses → caught by vine trichomes & moss pores → excess drips to pool → salamanders lap, crabs sip from below → excretions feed iron bacteria → bacteria polish shale → mirror-ferns use reflections to photosynthesize → fronds litter the floor → moth larvae tunnel through litter, aerating peat → corvids harvest larvae, drop fragments that vines digest → moisture released upward to fog again.

What does not live here  

• Echoes.  You may try—but the sound you make arrives flat, absorbed by feather, frond, and lung before it can bounce back.  
• Fire.  A lit match dies like a moth, smothered by air already too full of water to take another combustion.  
• Hurry.  Muscles twitch at normal speeds, yet inside the Vault even a heartbeat feels ostentatious.

If you stay  

The basin will recognize only displacement physics: you are a temporary cavity.  The moss will pillow your imprint; moths will tile your still form; a crab may drum once beneath to test structural integrity.  Given weeks of patience and silence, the vault might promote you from disturbance to participant: your breath might start arriving in synchrony with the lung.  On the night you finally leave, every organism will pause—just long enough to feel the sudden vacancy—then fold the space you occupied shut, the ledger neat again.

That, at last, is everyone—seed, spine, mist, and mind—who lives beneath the Cloud-Vault.
