What passes for population in the basin gathers by strata, the way silt sorts itself after a tide.  Seen all together—an impossibility, because no instant contains them all—they would number fewer than a provincial market-square, yet they fill the interfolded city as thoroughly as marrow fills bone.

1. The First Imagined  
   • Appearance. They look almost human the way a rough draft looks almost final: outlines smudge, joints mis-register by a finger-width, the whites of their eyes carry the faint watermark of erased alternatives. Clothing is spliced from possibility—sleeves subdivide, collars decide between mandarin and V-neck every few minutes.  
   • Origin story. They were stipulated, not born. When the earliest design‐routine began extruding habitable corridors, it accompanied its blueprints with a minimal test-population: seven citizens and their variance parameters. The basin kept them, the way wet clay keeps a sculptor’s fingerprints.  
   • Daily rhythm. They patrol for contradictions. Where a stair has too many risers to fit its well, one of the First will pace it until the count settles. Where a bridge finishes an angle shy of its landing, they stand on the gap and talk until syntax buckles and the span snaps into place.  
   • Lifespan & mortality. They fade only when the city accepts a more coherent version of them. Some have therefore lived decades; others reincarnate nightly under new constraints and do not realise they are iterative.  
   • Speech. Every sentence finishes as a footnote. Listeners flip linguistic pages in the air beside them, scanning for the clause that finally solidifies.

2. The Probability Rats (locally nicknamed “maybe-mice”)  
   • Physiology. As long and thin as a paintbrush handle, pelt opalescent and moiré-striped. Their skeletons solve the Schrödinger equation in real time; bones prefer the path of lowest improbability, so walls are no impediment if being on the other side completes the math more neatly.  
   • Ecology. They eat certainty—any small portion will do. Finger-written labels, numeric key-pads, the punctuation at the end of a line of code: gnaw marks appear as ellipses, commas turn to commas followed by question-marks.  
   • Reproduction. Litters precipitate when humidity exceeds 98 %. The parent enters a reflective silence; its outline duplicates in the nearest mirrored surface, then peels off, wet and skittering.  
   • Practical advice. Stare at one too long and it averages you instead of itself; you may find half your pocket-contents now inside the wall behind you.

3. Lattice-Mites  
   • Scale & appearance. Microscopic hexapods with carapaces of doped graphene, transparent except for a topaz shimmer where light catches their internal clocks.  
   • Function. They are the basin’s mortar. They crawl the seams of programmable glass, detect fatigue before macroscopic physics notices, and exude a self‐healing resin of carbon foam. Whole balconies rest on their solder.  
   • Swarm music. When the ambient creak of metal drops below a threshold, the mites beat their wings in chorus, generating an ultrasonic hiss that reminds the lattice it ought to be load-bearing today.

4. The Deferred Engineers  
   • Manifestation. Not bodies but debts of intention: outlines of workers rendered in heat-shimmer, always half a level above or below wherever you stand. Their tools are blanks where torque will later be applied.  
   • Job description. They undertake projects whose completion date precedes their start date. A girder that locks into place before you see anyone lift it? Credit the Engineers and the accounting of negative labour.  
   • Interaction. If you thank them aloud you incur a share of their backlog; a cuff button may vanish, paying an incremental invoice.

5. Husk Blossoms  
   • Botany-cum-zoology. Resemble head-high lilies made from evacuated copper mesh. They root where two mezzanines nearly touch, drinking the voltage differential.  
   • Bloom cycle. Every nineteen hours (local) they unzip a fluted flower of fine wire. The blossom fills with condensation; each droplet houses a photon that tried to escape the basin. At dusk the petals collapse, discharging the trapped light in a low sigh that smells of struck matches.  
   • Secondary occupants. After a blossom sighs out, the basin’s smallest residents—the eight-micron “fugue gnats”—bathe in the ex-light, recalibrating their internal chronometers.

6. The Cartographers of Regret  
   • Demography. Perhaps two dozen at any given month, though you rarely meet more than one. They arrive from elsewhere, compelled by unfinished emotional arithmetic.  
   • Tools. Charcoal made of self-ashamed carbon, vellum tanned from the underside of second thoughts. Their maps last less than an hour but remain true until they crumble, so locals treat a fresh sheet like a flash-weather report.  
   • Custom. When a Cartographer finishes a draft, they tear away a narrow strip—the path they will personally walk toward closure—and swallow it. The paper digests into memory foam that lines their bones against further injury.

7. The Slow Choir  
   • Ontology. Voices without throats, appearing where two echoes accidentally cancel and leave a resonant zero.  
   • Repertoire. Hymns whose notes are spaced minutes apart. From one balcony you might hear only the syllable “lo—”; three floors later, “—ve.”   People working night shifts learn to clap where they guess unseen rests should fall, gifting punctuation to the Choir.  
   • Civic role. On rare emergencies—a shear-quake in geometry, a thermal upwelling from a younger yesterday—the Choir lengthens its pauses to stall causal collapse, buying the basin time to re-index its own story.

8. The Basement Archivist  
   • Solitary guardian. No one agrees when she arrived. She tends a cellar that can be reached only by lifting a floor panel that has not been installed yet.  
   • Physique. Age indeterminate; skin etched with tiny paragraphs that rearrange when you try to read them. Fingers stained the colour of oxidised copper from turning foils of non-linear parchment.  
   • Collection. She files away failed versions of the city: collapsed arches, overambitious transit lines, mornings that came unstuck. Each is flattened to two dimensions, labelled in a syntax even the paracausal engines defer to, then slid onto shelves made of withheld time.  
   • Gossip. If she ever smiles, the current iteration of the basin is said to be safe for another century. No one has yet recorded a smile.

9. Visitors Like You  
   • Physiological effect. Within minutes your cardiac rhythm learns the basin’s lag; your pulse echoes half a beat behind itself, an acoustic double-exposure in your ribs.  
   • Cognitive shift. Linguists say verbs want to adopt the subjunctive. You form sentences such as “I will have been arriving,” and they feel correct.  
   • Exit tax. Anything on your person that once traced a straight line shorter than forty-one centimetres may dematerialise at the rim—object paying object for the privilege of having existed where certainty does not. Most travellers lose pencils, styluses, the odd hair-pin. A few lose the clarity of a resolved thought, and never quite notice the deficit.

10. The Basin Itself (arguably alive)  
    • Metabolism. Shale crust becomes scaffold; scaffold learns occupancy; occupancy dreams of deeper basement and grows one. Waste heat vents sideways into a Tuesday you have not visited. The loop closes.  
    • Mood indicators. Warmth in the floor predicts cooperative geometry. A sudden, almost maternal hush means the city is listening to someone’s footfall and composing a corridor around it.  
    • Long ambition. To reach cathedral size in every superposed draft, then decide which singular version to let stand. Until then it breeds citizens, vermin, botany and idea-pressure the way a forest breeds pollen: by the million, on the gamble that a few will stick.

Advice from a seasoned dweller named Kess Fold:  
“Nothing here is ornamental; if you can see it, it is either load-bearing, breathing, or waiting to hatch. Address each accordingly.”

Those are the lives that presently occupy the basin—partial, iterative, or tentative, but vividly sufficient. They co-author their surroundings one patient millimetre at a time, rehearsing reality until both city and citizens can square their ledgers with physics… or negotiate a kinder alternative.
