Who or what counts as a resident of Ichorra depends on whom you ask and how finely you are willing to slice the notion of “life.”  A census-taker from the valleys would write down “Population: 987,” note their disciplines, and be done.  Someone who has spent a winter here would add a dozen other line-items.  Remain an entire volcanic year—two hundred and thirty-one dawn updrafts—and the list becomes almost endless, a braided taxonomy of flesh, mineral, code, and rumor.  Below is a reckoning in widening concentric circles, from the most obviously human to the nearly inexpressible.

1. Flesh-and-blood Citizens  
   • Edgewrights: Structural savants who read fracture patterns the way others read weather.  They sleep in six-hour shifts so that some pair of them is always awake to listen for the next resonance tremor.  
   • Biosphericists: Part microbiologist, part vintner.  They fuss over the bruise-purple iron-sulfur films, tasting them with silver micro-spoons, gossiping about subtle changes in tang the way lowlanders gossip about vintages.  
   • Pilgrims: Not monks exactly, but every bit as self-abnegating.  They arrive with no possessions above the weight quota of a one-litre water flask, accept any task offered, and leave only when the mountain declares it is time—usually after the pilgrim has learned to ride a gravicle line while blindfolded.  
   • Children of the Shelf: Fewer than twenty at any given time.  They grow tall and low-voiced in the thin air, their cardiopulmonary systems permanently optimized for altitude.  When they descend to visit lowland relatives, they must wear weighted vests and speak through dampers so their voices do not crack glass.  
   • The Auditor: A solitary figure elected yearly whose sole mandate is to ask “Is this weight necessary?” of every new object, datum, even emotion proposed for import.  The post usually breaks a person once in a lifetime; no one is forced to serve twice.

2. Semi-Biological Artisans  
   • Lattice-Keepers: Nanoscale shepherds living inside every aerolith rib.  They are coded to think in crystal-graph grammars—angles, not words—and to care for stress distributions the way bees care for brood temperature.  When two segments of masonry brush, the keepers exchange phonon chirps that, if time-stretched, sound uncannily like lullabies.  
   • Maintenance Drones: Dragonfly silhouettes with monomolecular skates for feet.  Their prime directive says “remove hazardous mass,” but long experience has taught them nuance: a dangerously long icicle above a child’s balcony is excised in silence, yet the one above the Pilgrims’ meditation niche is left to lengthen because its periodic fractures measure wind sheer.  
   • The Gravicle Web: Officially a transport utility, unofficially a petulant cat’s cradle.  The polarized vacuum filaments are self-patching and curious; they will sometimes re-route a too-confident commuter onto a scenic parabola simply to study new load vectors.

3. The Low and Quiet Biota  
   • Iron-Sulfur Sheets (locally “sangskin”): You kneel and they shiver, extruding protein gel that wicks up the laser-cut gutters toward cistern farms.  Under a microscope the cells are sunburst shields of pyrite, revolving slowly to keep their metallic leaves perpendicular to geothermal glow.  
   • Sky-Motes: Length of a thumb, mass of a snowflake.  The rare collision with the cliff is both ending and beginning: the corpse feeds the sangskin, the wing-cocoons spawn motes anew when the next updraft takes them.  
   • Wind-Glass Limpets: Invisible until rime outlines them.  They etch minute pits into the obsidian, drink the silica, and leave behind a film that improves the rock’s grip on water—unwitting civil engineers.

4. Emergent Collectives  
   • The Chorus of Doors: Every portal’s acoustic foam records the timbre of each voice that passes.  Once a month the city’s doors share their samples through the structural backbone, weave them into a single subsonic chord, and sing it back at dawn.  Residents wake feeling inexplicably understood.  
   • Fog-Ghosts: Not spirits but chance entanglements of datamotes, water vapor, and structural hum.  They drift like slow aurorae.  A child asking a question inside one may receive an answer compiled from a dozen knowledge banks, then spend days trying to decide whether she was talking to herself.  
   • The Spine-Mind (hypothesized): Sensors show decision-like phase shifts in the stone during resonance tremors—micro-rotations that compensate for strain before any human or lattice-keeper could intervene.  Some engineers insist this is merely a feedback loop grown sophisticated; pilgrims leave offerings of hematite beads at floor drains, just in case.

5. Visitors So Transient We Barely Count Them  
   • Cargo Balloons: Skins of graphene lace carrying seeds, letters, and occasionally a reckless tourist.  They exhale their buoyant hydrogen into fuel cells upon docking and become limp as cloth, accepting whatever new cargo the city hands back.  
   • Valley Ravens: Legends say a few have followed thermals all the way up.  If you see one you must whisper it a story; ravens, unlike the city, forget nothing but will never ask for proof.  
   • Meteoroid Dust: Millimeter grains of nickel-iron that strike the radiator bloom, melt, and fuse into bright freckles.  The lattice-keepers study these cosmic fossils for alloy ideas, as a jeweler might study constellations.

6. That Which May Yet Be Born  
   • The First Free Rib: A section of aerolith now thick enough to stand without anchors.  Engineers debate when to release the clamps.  Pilgrims argue that the rib should decide for itself and will spend nights listening for the decisive creak.  
   • The Hollow Seed: A tiny torus of living aerolith being cultured in a laboratory the size of a teacup.  When mature it will be launched on a dawn updraft, its interior vacuum polarized into a primitive gravicle, its mission to search the horizon for the next newborn mountain.

To linger in Ichorra is to feel the category of “alive” stretch underfoot like warm glass.  Speak here long enough and your words join the Chorus; walk often enough and your mass patterns nudge the Gravicle Web into new equilibria; doubt fiercely enough and you help the Spine decide whether to wake.  In the valley they will tell you fewer than a thousand people inhabit the shelf.  Come see how far shy of the truth that figure falls.
