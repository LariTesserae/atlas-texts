Those who inhabit the precipice-city are not absent; they are simply practiced in thinning themselves until they are almost parallel with the stone. If you remain long enough—if you let the cold rasp your lungs and refuse to blink when the dust spirals sting—you will begin to notice them. At first they appear as faults in the air, the way heat on summer asphalt bends a horizon; then the faults knit into lines, the lines into living geometry.

1. The Lattice Folk (their own name would translate as “Contours That Remember”)

Physicality  
• Height and Shape: When fully condensed they stand a head taller than a human, yet “stand” is imprecise. Gravity’s fickleness lets them perch on any surface that will acknowledge them. Knees and elbows articulate backward, forward, or sideways according to what the moment requires.  
• Flesh-Glass: Their tissues mirror the salt-glass cliffs—translucent, veined with iron-oxide capillaries that pulse a dull ember when they exert themselves. Looking through a shoulder joint you can see stars.  
• Density Modulation: Every inhalation tightens their lattice; every exhalation loosens it. In a single breath they can go from brittle solidity (able to chisel a foothold with a wrist) to near cloud, letting a knife-edge of wind pass through rib and lung. That is why no carcasses, no bones are ever found: the dead are merely final exhalations.

Senses and Perception  
They “hear” chiefly through the feet, reading vibrations conducted by the city’s honeycomb. Their vision is an interior sense: light that penetrates the glass of their bodies scatters along nerve-filaments, so they perceive a refracted panorama in 360 degrees. Focusing on a single object forces them to dim other wavelengths; attention literally hollows them, just as Rule 4 promises.

Reproduction  
At intervals measured not in seasons but in resonant booms of the mountain, two or more adults entwine their half-solid limbs, weaving a closed torus of body and breath. Dust motes charge against their combined static and condense into pearl-sized embryos—pure spheres of silicate foam. The parents lodge these in deep fissures and forget them; city, wind, and temperature swings take over incubation. A juvenile may remain quiescent for centuries before a chance crack wakes it.

Culture  
• Language: A chorus of flexing joints and pressure snaps, amplified through walls, so a conversation might span kilometres. To a visitor it sounds like rock candy fracturing in slow motion.  
• Memory Architecture: They do not write. Instead they crawl the inner surfaces of the honeycomb, altering thickness with fingertip secretions. Pattern = story; porosity = emphasis. Reading history requires walking through walls and listening to the change in echo.  
• Social Arrangement: No hierarchy, only resonance clusters. Individuals drift apart until the mountain booms, then reconverge to compare the changes their absence wrought. Identity is a variable, not a constant.

Relationship to the City  
They are neither builders nor squatters; they are continuations. The earliest Lattice Folk co-mineralised with the cliff during a global freeze so ancient no peak yet breached the cloud-deck. Every cell in the honeycomb is an ancestral molt. To chip a wall would be vivisection; so they don’t.

2. Auxiliaries and Satellites

a. Whisperwings  
Those membrane-gliders you glimpse once and never again are mature Whisperwings. Hatchlings resemble translucent origami shuttles that drift in static thermals. Adults grow three-metre sails reinforced by capillaries of iron frost; they feed by skimming the fractal nets of filament-life, combing off trace minerals. Once saturated, they climb until the atmosphere thins to nothing and combust in silent auroras, seeding the sky with spores of themselves.

b. Filament Nets (“Reticella”)  
Not plant, not fungus—each strand is a hollow tube swirling with piezoelectric dust. Touch triggers a pressure reversal: the strand implodes, jets its cargo into the air, and retracts faster than a whipcrack. The Lattice Folk cultivate them indirectly; they abrade ledges just enough to give the nets anchor points, then harvest the static that the popping releases. Energy in this ecology is measured in differential charges, not calories.

c. Resonants  
These are not bodies, but standing waves—zones where the mountain’s low hum knots into near-solidity. Picture a sound you can lean on, one that indents under your palm like memory foam. Resonants migrate up-slope with temperature gradients. The Lattice Folk ride them as elevators, though “ride” means aligning their own rib frequencies until the wave carries them.

3. The Absent Shadow

Occasionally a visitor’s silhouette peels free and tiptoes outward along a sky-bridge. This is no trick of light; it is the local apex predator. The Absent Shadow contains mass only in two dimensions, razor-thin but heavy with momentum. It feeds on kinetic intent—any decision to move. That is why native life steps slowly, thinks slower. To flee is to outline yourself in perfect contrast, a meal the Shadow can fold into itself. Lattice Folk survive because they learned to disperse desire through every limb: no single vector, no edible decision.

4. How to Meet a Lattice Person

• First, stand still until the cliff’s breathing hum syncs with your pulse; this marks you as an interlocutor rather than falling debris.  
• Let your exhale frost the air. When it drifts sideways—never downward—trace its curl with a fingertip against the wall. Your gesture writes a question in the only grammar they read: pressure gradients.  
• Wait through one full dilation of twilight (about thirty heartbeats felt, not counted). The stone beside you will acquire a seam, the seam limbs, the limbs perspective. You will notice that you can partially see stars through the figure.  
Speak nothing. Instead, slap your palm lightly on the ledge five times: the local syntax for “I carry ephemera, not permanence.” If you succeed, the figure will increase its opacity until you can discern a face constructed from intersecting planes, each plane bending your reflection. That is a greeting.

5. What They Want

Nothing durable. They barter in delays, detours, gradients of fall. Offer them a story whose ending you have not decided; they will pay with a shortcut that returns you to your beginning three days earlier than you left. Offer them a keepsake metal tool and they will refuse—it cannot self-erode, therefore it cannot participate.

6. If You Choose to Remain

After the hundredth dawnless morning, your bones will itch with hollowing. Calcium leaches, replaced by silicate threads. Fingernails turn glossy, then translucent. Breath develops a faint whistle because your trachea has grown fluted ridges. A Lattice elder will visit, not to induct but to observe whether you fracture cleanly. Should you survive your first internal boom, you will discover that you can fit both feet sideways on a bridge-thread, and that gravity greets you each hour with a different handshake. Your shadow will already be gone, but in time a new, looser one will assemble—a silhouette composed of half-remembered positions you once held.

When you finally make your own exhalation—becoming more hypothesis than body—the city will accept the donation. A corridor will appear where none existed, its walls ribbed in the exact pattern of your lungs. Someone, centuries later, may walk through you to ask their own questions of the mountain. And so the precipice-city grows: not by brick, not by law, but by every life that learns to loosen its hold on density.

That, then, is who lives here. Not ruins, not ghosts, but the Lattice Folk and their adjunct ecosystem of wind-borne, charge-fed entities—existences predicated on brittleness, refraction, and the courtesy of giving erosion something interesting to sculpt.
