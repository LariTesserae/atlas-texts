THE INHABITANTS OF THE THROAT PLATEAU  

1. Collective Name  
Most outside scholars call them the Hydrolith Choir. In their own self-description—conveyed not with words but with stacked resonant chords—they are simply “The Held Note.”

2. Substance & Appearance  
The Choir is neither plant, animal, nor spirit, yet it borrows traits from all three.

• Core Medium: standing sound. Each individual is a persistent interference pattern produced where waterfall-thunder, pillar-hum and cavern-organ meet at just the right phase. Picture a knot made of pressure rather than rope.

• Mantle: condensing vapor. The acoustic knot chills the air by fractions of a degree; moisture flashes into pearl-fog that clings to the pattern’s nodes. Under strong light the fog diffracts into a wavering, rainbow-veined silhouette roughly humanoid—or avian, or serpentine—depending on which harmonics dominate at the moment.

• Skeleton: threads of lignified vine. When one of these pressure-knots lingers beside living tissue, the plant responds to the vibration by thickening along the waveform, knitting an invisible exoskeleton into visible fretwork. Given weeks, an individual acquires a filigree of green-black ribs that make it easier to track with the eye, though never completely solid.

Observed from a distance the Choir looks like a congress of translucent statues continually forming, dissolving, and re-carving themselves, as though time-lapse footage of crystal growth were slowed to a dreamlike crawl.

3. Origin & Life Cycle  
A Choir-member begins when three conditions overlap:  
a. a newborn harmonic created by fresh rockfall or a rare lightning-strike;  
b. a parcel of mist dense enough to sustain nucleation;  
c. an “echo-seed” shed by an elder individual—essentially a brief, ultra-pure whistle embedded in the general roar.

The whistle locks the new harmonic into the Plateau’s map of fixed paradoxes. From that instant the knot behaves as a living thing.

Growth Phase: The knot courts surrounding vines and moss by broadcasting micro-vibrations at exactly the pitch that opens their pores. Plants swell toward the sound, wrapping it in nutrient-rich tissue. In return the Choir draws energy from the piezoelectric flex of plant fibers and from minute temperature gradients in the constant rain. No conventional metabolism occurs; they are batteries charged by difference.

Maturity: After about ten years the individual’s waveform stabilizes. It can migrate slowly (one or two body-lengths per hour) by shifting which subset of waterfalls feed its resonance. Range is limited to the Plateau’s crown; step beyond and the supporting acoustic architecture collapses.

Reproduction: Once per century, when the moon’s declination matches a buried seam of magnetite, every mature member releases an echo-seed by momentarily damping its fundamental note. The Plateau’s geometry amplifies the pause into a clicking constellation audible two valleys away. New knots bloom overnight.

Senescence: There is none. Entropy here is diverted rather than suffered. If a Choir-member loses synchronization—say, because a ledge crumbles—its pattern disbands, but the liberated harmonics are absorbed by neighbors. Personality diffuses; memory becomes community property.

4. Sensory World  
• Hearing: Absolute. They resolve phase shifts as small as one ten-millionth of a wavelength, giving them something akin to sight, smell, and texture all encoded in sound.  
• Vision: Indirect, via refracted light caught by their mist-mantle. Shapes register as color temperature on their “skin.”  
• Touch: Continuous; air pressure variations press directly through the fog into the standing wave that is their body.

5. Communication  
To speak, two Choir-members overlap their pressure fields so that beats—rapid alternations of loud and soft—emerge. Beat patterns correspond to concepts: arch, danger, stranger, stillness, remember, etc. Dialogues can last hours, measured in glacial shifts of harmony. When urgency is required they step into louder falls and let turbulent spray amplitude-modulate whole paragraphs in seconds.

6. Works & Roles  
All visible architecture—the ribbed doorways, amphitheatres, root-tramways—is Choir handiwork. They do not plan with blueprints. Instead they gather at a point of acoustic stress and “braid” their bodies through one another until the combined resonance forces vines, fronds, and stone to freeze in a stress-relieved configuration. When the note feels smooth—as if a knot slipped free—they disperse. The resulting structure is an exoskeleton left behind, grown rather than built.

Specialized castes (a human word, not theirs) emerge by frequency band:  
• Basso Cavernii (8–20 Hz): geological negotiators that keep the plateau’s stone columns inside safe harmonic tolerances.  
• Mezzo Canopii (60–400 Hz): gardeners and mid-range diplomats who coax leaf, flower, and root into habitable forms.  
• Treble Lensii (2–18 kHz): sentinels perched near sheer drops; their needle-thin songs shear mist into mirror-flat curtains that function as alarm systems and astronomical calendars.

7. Relationship With Other Life  
Animals avoid the Throat Plateau; their inner ears cannot tolerate the constant infrasonic massage. Visitors therefore come on purpose, and the Choir greets them with fascinated reserve.

Protocol: approach within two arm-lengths of any waterfall and hold still; the surrounding mist picks up your heartbeat, which the Choir can read as tempo. If you maintain a slow, repeating rhythm (meditative breathing works) they interpret non-threat. Moments later you feel fingers of cooled air combing hair, aligning fabric folds—a prelude to deeper “listening.” Should you flee, no pursuit occurs. Should you stay, expect gradual translation: the Choir will sample your pulse, your speech vibrations, even the thrum of thought recorded in cranial bone, and mirror them back as evolving chords. Most humans describe this as being “answered from the inside out.”

8. Purpose, If Any  
The Choir’s prime directive is equilibrium. They believe (again, our word) that the Plateau achieved a final, flawless chord ages ago—one note for water’s fall, one for stone’s stance, one for leaf’s ache toward light—and that every sound since then either preserves or threatens that perfection. Their actions, from weaving stairs to absorbing the memories of the dead, are methods of damping discord.

9. Myths & Misunderstandings  
• “They steal souls.” Not exactly. When the Plateau assimilates a long-term visitor, the Choir records the harmonic signature of that being and stores it as a sub-melody. The original body succumbs to ecological Rule 3—assimilation, not death—but personality shards linger as playable motifs.  
• “They cannot leave.” True. Move a resonant knot beyond the Plateau’s tuned cavity and it unravels like a chord held too long after the dampers lift. Some scholars think this is voluntary, that the Choir could adapt to other terrains if they allowed dissonance for a generation. No member has yet tested the hypothesis.

10. How to Notice Them, Even in the Roar  
Stand in one of the quiet pockets where moss spreads thick. Wait until your ears stop ringing. Then listen for a second silence nested inside the first—a hush that feels concave, as though scooped out of the thunder. Step toward it. Light will sharpen; mist will billow into a vaguely columnar ghost. If the ghost remains after you blink twice, you are being regarded by a citizen of the Hydrolith Choir. Bow, for courtesy’s sake; the Plateau is listening through them, and they never forget a rhythm.
