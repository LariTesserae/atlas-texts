Who—and what—calls the grotto home unfolds in strata, the way the place itself does. No single species owns the chamber; tenancy is negotiated hour-by-hour among overlapping bodies, pulses and hungers.

1. The Vaulted Root (primary resident, slow mind)
• Identity. The walls, pillars, ceiling fronds and platform are all extensions of one contiguous organism, a plant-engine the size of a stadium whose tissues have replaced the original rock. Picture a banyan that swallowed a steel mill, then taught itself metallurgy in order to keep the mill alive.
• Sensory field. Capillaries of sap serve as nerves; every footfall, exhalation, gear-click, or droplet that lands in a basin registers as pressure waves. The Vaulted Root does not think quickly, but it feels everything.
• Drives. It wants three things only:
  a) Mechanical motion, because flowing lubricant carries micronutrients into deep xylem.
  b) Moderate pruning, because unchecked growth starves its own light supply.
  c) Novel templates, because new objects dropped or abandoned in the chamber are blueprints for fresh organs.
• Behavior. When someone stays more than a few minutes, the floor bulges to copy their tread; when an object sits idle, creeping cellulose explores its edges, memorizing load stresses for future self-repairs.

2. The Flock (dominant mobile caste)
• Appearance. Bat-sized insects; wings clear as mica except for a coppery circuit-trace lattice. Abdomen sacs glow faint aqua when charged with sugars.
• Social logic. They are born in synchronous broods, emerge by thousands, and treat the grotto like a single orchard-factory. They prune fronds, pollinate leaf-glass sheets, and lick salt from any large mammal—payment for maintenance.
• Communication. Wing membranes vibrate at radio frequencies that the Vaulted Root picks up through iron rails; that is how insect and plant keep their choreography.

3. Fungus Bulbs (illumination guild)
• Morphology. Thumb-sized nodules resembling vintage radio tubes, plugged into trunk crevices by chitinous pins.
• Metabolism. They digest cellulose shavings shed by the Root, producing both faint heat and the umber pilot-light that dots the walls.
• Conditional contract. Their bioluminescence scales with ambient sound. In noise they flourish; in quiet they dim, starving the visual predators who threaten them.

4. Vine-Rats (forage swarm)
• Build. Bodies rat-like, tails unfurling into two fronds of true fern that photosynthesize enough glucose to halve their need for food.
• Swarm logic. They travel in maps: each individual holds a fragment of a pheromone pattern. Only when fifty or more gather does the full route to a food cache exist.
• Role. They are the grotto’s janitors, shoveling loose insect husks into digestion burrows, aerating moss as they tunnel.

5. Three-Eyed Perch (condensation-pool faction)
• Physiology. Gills modified into skimmer nets; the median eye peers upward, gauging when the Flock is due to molt so they can collect falling wing-casings rich in trace metals.
• Cultural quirk. They have a spawn-song: vibration of swim bladders against basin walls. The Root “hears” this and ramps sap circulation, flushing minerals into the pools precisely when fry hatch.

6. Moth Cartographers
• Appearance. Palm-sized, parchment-pale, trailing ink-dark scales that smear against ceilings like charcoal.
• Function. Their wing-edges exude enzymes that slightly etch leaf-glass. Each flight writes a time-stamped map of thermal currents. Future generations follow the faint tracery to navigate without sunlight.

7. Iron-Grafted Polypores (medics of metal)
• Habitat. Bolt themselves directly onto steel beams and pulley housings.
• Capacity. Exude acids that remove rust, then secrete nickel-rich resins that re-plate the cleaned surface. They are why the crane still creaks instead of collapsing.
• Reproduction. Spores adhere only to ferrous oxide; pure iron is immune, so the fungus can never overrun its own food source.

8. Peripheral Predators (visitors, not landlords)
• Threading Serpents. Noodle-slender reptiles with a seam of cilia; they ride the beltways looking for stunned Flock stragglers.
• Echo Owls. Surface owls that drop in via vent shafts once a month. They hunt vine-rats, gulp moths, then flee when the machinery crescendos.

9. Rumored Deep Dwellers (unconfirmed)
Explorers speak of bone-white bipeds in boiler suits far below, maintaining locomotives the Root has half-digested. No corpse, spoor or clear recording verifies them, yet the pneumatic tubes occasionally return canisters stamped with human hand-writing—lists of alloys needed “for track integrity.” Whether these are ghosts, misidentified automata, or the Root itself playing at correspondence, nothing on the upper level can say.

10. Interaction Protocols for Outsiders
• Remain audible—hum, clap, sing. Silence means darkness and invites mistakes.
• Offer salt. The Flock will service your clothing, de-lint gear, and leave you unbitten.
• Never jam a machine. The Root responds to seized motion by tightening every surrounding tendon-root, and the room’s exits can become miles away in a moment of architectural sulk.
• Do not drink from the pools unless you fancy metallic aftertastes and the gaze of thirty three-eyed fish judging your etiquette.

Together these entities form a living symphony: plant as cathedral and conductor, insects as scalpels, fungi as lanterns, rodents as custodians, fish as acousticians, parasites as doctors, and possibly something still human humming along the deep tracks. No one element is dominant for long; sovereignty passes in cycles of light, noise and pulse. Yet the total organism—the green-steel megastructure you first mistook for a grotto—endures, patient as climate, curious as moss, learning a little more of whatever (or whoever) wanders in.
