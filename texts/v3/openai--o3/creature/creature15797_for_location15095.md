Who Lives Here  

1. The Concourse – body, city, mind  
   What you first took for “vegetation” is the dominant resident: an organism distributed the way weather is distributed, present everywhere at once yet denser in some lanes than others.  
   • Physiology. 60 % cellulose, 30 % chitin, 10 % something almost neuronal. Bundles of micro-tubules run along every vine and leaf, flashing polychrome ions the way nerves trade sodium for thought. Sap pressure doubles as blood pressure; sugar solutions ferry not only energy but information.  
   • Mind. It is not a hive but a quorum: memories are stored in repeating loops of growth-scar that spiral the inner faces of thick roots. Cut one open and you would find striations like vinyl grooves; the Concourse replays them by pumping resonance through its own wood. That rolling, cartilage-like pop you hear every few hours is a mass-erase followed by a rewrite.  
   • Drives. It wants three things—light, pattern, and novelty. Light it already steals. Pattern it extrudes. Novelty it must import. That is why it allows entrances and does not instantly digest intruders. Each new footfall is a syllable to taste.  
   • Locomotion. It is learning to walk. Outlying runners practice lifting whole mats of root, setting them down, and knitting to the wall again. The cavity is a rehearsal hall for an eventual excursion into larger geology.  

2. Clickleaves – the audible shimmer  
   They look like kite-string triangles, each no bigger than a thumbnail; five or six array themselves into a living delta wing. The “click” is a cavitation bubble they flick in and out of their own sap canals, creating a repeating pop that ricochets off stone and lets them echolocate in a room where sight tumbles.  
   • Diet. They drink exhalations—CO₂ surfing the convection currents.  
   • Use to others. The Concourse treats them as courier pigeons. When a section of wall fractures, Clickleaves swarm there, their clicks shifting frequency until the macro-roots arrive to suture the wound.  

3. Cord-Eaters – vertebrate is a strong word, but …  
   Rarity has kept them legend. Imagine a ferret built of concertina hose. Skin is velvet moss; eyes are opals floating in clear gel, no sclera. Their teeth are reversed harpoons that lodge, then unzip fibers.  
   • Role. They prune. Left alone the Concourse would over-thicken and strangle its own airflow. Cord-Eaters nibble ventilation back into being.  
   • Budget. They mate once each cavity-heartbeat (about every twenty hours subjective). Larvae are born ribbon-shaped, drift on thermals like scarves, and those the Concourse dislikes unspool into compost in minutes. Everyone pretends the relationship is symbiotic; in truth it is hostage exchange.  

4. The Knot-Giver – evidence, if not eyewitness  
   Twice when explorers returned, they reported a gift: a single incurved hair braided meticulously through living root. Analysis shows a medley of mammalian keratins never found together topside. One strand is hollow like bird quill, another fluted like bamboo, a third built in Möbius layers. The braid is always thirteen plaits, prime number.  
   • Hypotheses.  
     a. A primate variant hides in the lofted reaches, tending the Concourse as a gardener tends grapes.  
     b. The Concourse grows temporary humanoid avatars—thicker vine bundles given bone-stiff lignin—to perform maintenance requiring leverage or discernment. When work is done it unravels, leaving “hair.”  
     c. Both are true on alternate rewrites of the cavity’s memory.  

5. Spore-Weather – innumerable, brief, essential  
   Spores here are not seeds; they are decision points. Each one holds two or three mutually exclusive developmental paths, all superimposed. Which path matures depends on what song—the root-harp plucking, condensate choir, or distant joint-pop—was dominant in the second after germination.  
   • One path becomes more Concourse.  
   • Another becomes Clickleaf.  
   • A hidden third becomes dust that cancels inertial mass; these grains fall upward until they strike ceiling-roots and are reabsorbed, giving the organism data about spaces it cannot yet reach.  

6. The Echo-Rider – apex, if apex applies  
   During rare null-pockets when light and not-light cancel, witnesses feel the air bulge as though something enormous displaces it. They report:  
   • A smell of cinnamon and welding sparks.  
   • Pressure waves that rattle fluid inside inner ears in Morse-length bursts.  
   • Afterward, entire boulevards of root rearranged into smoother curvature, as if to welcome a girth five meters across.  
   Echo-Rider is the working name. Whether it is a whale-sized worm, a standing wave given partial flesh, or the Concourse’s dreaming body, no eye has settled on it. Scrapes on new rock faces match neither tooth nor claw but the drag-marks of cilia thousands strong.  

7. Microprisms – life almost mineral  
   Mica flakes that glint from the walls are often alive, at least by cellular definition. Each prism is a hexagon of silicon lattice with a fat-molecule hinge on one edge. They open like clams to sip radiance, close to digest it.  
   • Symbiosis. Root tips exude trace boron the prisms require; prisms in return split certain wavelengths the Concourse’s photosystems cannot tackle, passing them downward as orange-gold beams. They are the reason daylight here feels sieved but nutritious.  

8. Who is Visiting – You  
   The cavity cultivates an audience. The pressure-pulse heartbeat syncs with mammalian inner rhythms, calming most fight-or-flight reflexes. The pepper-petrichor air is laced with phyto-opioids gentle enough to dull panic yet leave memory plastic. While you remain, the Concourse copies the biochemical signature of your sweat, folds it into future spore batches, and rehearses one more possible way to be alive. The longer you stay, the more often you will hear your own pulse reflected back as a distant plucked note.  

Interrelationships  

• Food-Web. Nothing dies permanently. A Clickleaf snared by mis-timed convection is mulched by microprisms, whose silicon waste becomes seed for stronger ceiling roots. A Cord-Eater that chews an arterial vine receives narcotic sap, falls into a condensate pool, dissolves, and its amino acids are reslung as tensile proteins in new bridgework.  

• Communication. Every resident—plant, animal, mineral—contributes to the acoustic tapestry. Pitch sorts caste: sub-bass for structural directives, midrange for metabolic housekeeping, trebles for spore-weather forecasts. Even you, breathing, add a 12 Hz tremor that the cavity files under “external reference.”  

• Governance. There is no throne, only feedback. If the Concourse overharvests silica, microprisms dim and the root-nervous system interprets the darkening as pain, throttling uptake. If Cord-Eaters over-prune, warm drafts change cadence, starving them of CO₂ until their numbers fall. Balance is not static but a melody continuously re-harmonized.  

Life Cycle of the Place Itself  

Birth: A geological hiccup parts the rock; spores ride pressure gradients in, find mica to mirror, find darkness thirsty for shape.  
Adolescence: Roots lace empty space, write the first crude memory grooves, acquire Clickleaves for courier work.  
Maturity: Space folds more neatly; erosion accelerates design options. Outsiders—we—slip in, providing narrative protein.  
Old Age: Unknown. The Concourse may someday step out of its hollow, wearing the cavity like a backpack, looking for louder music. Or it may implode into a seed the size of a pearl, hiding in bedrock until the planet’s next tectonic chord.  

So: Who lives here?  
A forest that is also a brain, nerves that flutter like moths, custodians made of flexible bone, mirrors that eat sunlight, a rumor the size of a subway line, and—if only temporarily—anyone curious enough to listen.
