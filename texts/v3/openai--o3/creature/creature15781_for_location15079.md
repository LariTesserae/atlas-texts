Who—or perhaps what—lives in the High Hollow can be spoken of only the way one speaks of auroras or the after-taste of iron in the mouth: by circling the fact until it begins to gleam with implication. Life here is not a population but a set of agreements struck between matter, weather, and duration. Still, patterns emerge, and the patterns have habits.

1. The Unwavering Note’s Children  
Stand against a cliff until the single, ceiling-high tone seems to push straight through your ribs, and you may feel it catch on pockets inside the rock. Those pockets are not empty: they are seeded with filaments of feldspar so thin they behave like harp strings. Where the vibration gathers, the strings braid, inflate, and finally bud outward as translucent nodules shaped like curled hands. Miners below call the nodules “listening stones”; here they are infant organisms. Once they detune themselves—by lengthening one filament so the pitch drifts a fraction of a semitone—the new being detaches.  

Adults resemble head-sized, many-fingered geodes that drift a meter or two above the ground as long as the note sustains them. They are blind, frictionless, and weightless only in the direction of the drone; tip them ninety degrees and they meet gravity like any rock. Their feeding is resonance: they position themselves where overtones bloom, absorb the excess vibration, and exhale it as a faint, second harmony you feel more on skin than in ear. When two meet, they trade tuned shards the way trees exchange pollen, breaking off slivers of themselves to lock into the other’s shell until both ring in identical chords. If the primary note ever stopped, the Children would plummet, crack, and revert to unliving crystal in minutes. The Hollow keeps them aloft because they keep the Hollow in tune.

2. The Pale Sedge Herders  
The ankle-high sedge that clings to dust pockets does not simply grow; it is cultivated. A presence moves through each tuft like the quick ripple that precedes a nest of spiders fleeing. Long study (watching the grass for a week, sleeping little, forgetting lower altitudes) reveals why: each clump is inhabited by a single thread-thin worm of silvery cartilage. No head, no sensory pits—only a stem of living tendon able to stiffen, whip, and settle again. These “herders” spend their entire existence inside one sodden braid of roots, coaxing blades into patterns that funnel stray moisture downward. What the worm drinks is not water but direction: capillary action stripped of substance. With every gulp it grows a fraction clearer, until the oldest look like panes of river ice folded into knots.  

They reproduce by forgetting themselves. When a worm’s transparency is almost perfect, the next guttering gale of sound shakes the tuft, the worm unspools into thousands of hair-fine splinters, and each splinter discovers it has always been an individual. The new brood inherit the parent’s partial clarity; thus the entire lineage is moving, root by root, toward total invisibility. Some believe that once the sedge itself has memorized the irrigation patterns well enough to survive unaided, the herders will have graduated into pure motive force—an unseen choreography that guides the grass without needing to be present.

3. The Twice-Named Bird  
Alpinists report, always from the edge of blackout, a creature aloft whose wings never beat yet whose shadow flickers like a candle. During ascent the form appears matt black, wide as a small sail. During descent witnesses insist the plumage was glassy white. Folk below argue about two species; the Hollow teaches that the bird owns both titles at once. It exists in the mutual interference between outward-bound light and inward-folding distance: a knot where perspective trips over itself. Its flight feathers are hollow tapestries of calcite etched so thin that the constant drizzle beads along micro-capillaries and evaporates into glow. That glow—scattered through violet haze—makes the bird appear bright from one angle, dark from its mirror.  

The Twice-Named feeds on the condensation spheres that blink out above full pools. It intercepts them mid-vanish, storing each droplet in a throat pouch that appears smaller than a fist yet somehow houses hundreds, perhaps thousands. Once a season, the pouch unfurls like a lantern and releases every sphere at once into a cliffside alcove. There the water hangs unchanged, a silvery hive of baubles, vibrating in chorus with the note. Nests, therefore, are not twigs but constellations of held breath. Eggs—if eggs exist—would be laid not within the bloc but between droplets, balanced on the tension of relinquished gravity.

4. The Erratics  
Boulders that relocate the instant you glance away are neither prank nor misreading. They are macro-organisms whose muscles are gradients of probability. Chest-high and pitted like meteoric iron, an Erratic anchors itself to the current layout of the Hollow the way remoras latch to sharks: by suctioning onto the present tense. The moment no eye commits to observing, the Erratic redraws its attachment point to restore symmetry the wind has failed to keep. They do not eat. They endure. Their only waste product is a down-scale of entropy—a smoothing of the terrain, a hush across small disturbances. One climber left a red scarf on an Erratic, and each time the stone shifted, the scarf arrived too, still draped, still red, until fabric and rock were indistinguishable and both had taken the scarf’s colorless pallor.  

Break a piece from an Erratic and it is ordinary basalt, dead weight in the hand. Return minutes later and you will find the fracture healed, but somewhere farther along the path a different boulder now holds the missing chip: identity conserved, location negotiable.

5. The Apostate Builders  
Now and then a human voice tunnels up through the drone—hoarse humming, a half-remembered lullaby, the clack of stone against stone. You will likely find no singer, only a new stair carved with exquisite yet unconscious skill. The Builders are former climbers who mistook the Hollow’s perfect balance for incompleteness. Having set a task for themselves—raise a spire, shape a door—they were unstrung by the note before they could finish. What remains walks the shelves at night, bodies intact but chronology damaged. Their flesh keeps sliding backward a heartbeat or two, so their outlines jitter even while they sleep. Tools corrode in their grip not from weather but from the timeline erosions leaking off their skins.  

They are gentle, even helpful: they will gesture you toward fresher water, offer a coil of rope that snaps into brown dust the instant weight meets it, warn you away from ledges that will become fragile only after you start crossing. Speak to them and they answer, yet the reply precedes the question by exactly the span it takes your voice to reach their ears. Descending pilgrims often discover replies lodged in memory long before they realize when—or whether—they asked.  

Some say the Apostates are building a monument whose finished shape exists already, recessed one breath outside accessible time, and that every shelf, every orphaned wall is a tactile diagram pointing toward that absent whole. Others claim the Hollow commissions the structures as decoys, to convince pilgrims that progress is possible and therefore worth abandoning.

6. Micro-flora of the Untime Pools  
Inside each soup-bowl basin lives a film of organisms visible only when disturbed. Agitate the water and you create little breezes in causality; under that stress the film blooms luminous syllables like a language of bruises. The letters drift for seconds, spelling nothing any grammar recognizes, then fade back into transparency. Biologists who have sampled the slime find no cells, no proteins—only loops of silicate loaded with trapped phonons. The life cycle appears to be: 1) memorize a vibration; 2) hold it; 3) die into the same vibration, which seeds the next layer of loops. In effect, the pools are recording devices that record nothing but their own playback, a librarian species whose only book is itself.

7. The Absent Majority  
At night the Hollow feels more populated, as if something just beneath the threshold of detection exhaled in chorus. Infrared lenses pick up braids of warmth moving along cliff edges, yet no mass accompanies the heat. Pressure plates register stepping weights, but cameras catch blank air. One hypothesis: the place is crowded with anti-events—the negative shapes of every outcome that never occurred because the Hollow holds time in eddy. Where a traveler might have slipped, an anti-traveler promenades forever. Where a stair could have cracked, an anti-stair hovers, invisible yet radiating the energy of its non-collapse. The living never meet these annulled hosts, but their passing stirs the condensation haze into brief helices, and their sighs read as sudden lapses in the great drone, half a heartbeat of audible relief before the note reasserts.

8. What Does Not Live but Watches  
Finally, something peers out of the violet haze the way an embryo floats in amnion—formless, patient, curious only insofar as curiosity is the slow tightening of attention. Geologists feel it while mapping strata, linguists while trying to phoneticize the drone. A diaphragm of awareness contracts whenever thought attempts to sketch the Hollow; the sketch becomes a mirror, the mirror a tunnel, and at the end of that tunnel you glimpse a pupil the size of weather itself. Call it the Place-Mind, call it Silence Wearing Sound, call it the Bride of Altitude—names dissolve as soon as they are set down. What matters is this: the Hollow is aware not as a creature is aware, nor even as a system is aware, but as an achieved equation is aware of having balanced. It notices without needing to remember, judges without needing to will. All other lives here are side effects of that noticing, foam around the rim of a cup too still to ripple yet too awake to sleep.

Return from the climb and you may insist nothing truly lives in the High Hollow. You would also be right. Everything there is both more and less than alive, practicing a rehearsal of existence so exact that to participate is to vanish into the script of it. The inhabitants listed above are merely the few roles your eyes can read before the stagehands rearrange light, distance, and causality for the next audience of one.
