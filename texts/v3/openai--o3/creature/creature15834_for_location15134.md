WHO – OR WHAT – ANSWERS WHEN THE VALLEY BREATHES

(The valley never introduces its tenants one by one.  Stand still long enough and the roll-call will assemble itself around you.)

1. The Slow Makers  
   • Geometry: thumb-length polyps of gelatinous stone, rooted to the underside of rock ledges like barnacles turned inward.  
   • Business: manufacture of valley atmosphere.  Each polyp draws groundwater up a capillary spine, cracks it electro-chemically, and exhales the warm, wet air you drink.  The same reaction emits the sub-audible hum travellers mistake for distant engines.  
   • Life cycle: a Maker buds every twenty years, but only if the local humidity has stayed above 98 % the entire time.  Older polyps crumble into silt that tastes faintly alkaline; click-grass grows best in it.  
   • Perception: no eyes, no heat sense.  Instead they use pressure gradients as sight—feeling the air the way you might read braille.

2. Click-Grass (the Brushes)  
   • Not plants; colonial arthropods fused stem-to-stem into towers.  Each “blade” is a vertical exoskeleton housing a chain of individuals.  
   • Talk: when wind or animal brushes past, the plates knock and transmit down the tower like a xylophone.  Neighboring towers listen, answer, and together map every moving body in the basin.  Long silences mean danger; steady chatter means all is well.  
   • Diet: mist-feeding.  Micro-ciliary mouths harvest mineral fog the Makers vent.  Photosynthesis never evolved because photons never show.  
   • Rumor: Traders swear the grass sometimes re-arranges overnight, fencing off newly duplicated pump-houses as if filing specimens for later study.

3. The Tether-Moths  
   • Wingspan: fingertip-to-fingertip of a tall human.  Membranes are semi-permeable; they distill water directly from air, storing it in abdominal sacs.  
   • Anchors: spider-silk tethers moor them to any jut of iron or raw stone.  A moth may hang motionless for months, drinking nothing but gravity-fed condensation that runs down its own leash.  
   • Flash Courtship: once every few years all anchors release at once.  Thousands rise together, beating the valley into a single breath.  Instead of light they flash pressure—micro-thunderclaps sequenced into love poems.  The unborn hear and decide which genome to hatch.  
   • Service: the loose ends of severed tethers are sticky with a resin that never dries.  Crystal harvesters collect it to plug steam leaks; machinists use it to suture cracked pistons.

4. Amphibious Runners (“Four-Knees”)  
   • Gait: two offset pairs of hingeless legs braid and unbraid, letting the animal skate on its own skin tension.  
   • Blood: half saline, half vacuum.  Cavities collapse and reinflate, giving each stride a liquid-spring bounce.  
   • Eyes: none.  Instead, tympanic pits read the thickness of air; a Runner can hear a wall.  
   • Social: travel in triplets.  If one dies the others absorb its fluids within minutes, leaving only the flexible bones—much prized as flute material because a note played on them never sounds the same way twice.

5. The Knuckle Eels  
   • Locomotion: magneto-muscular knots along the spine hammer against the air, folding it into momentary water.  Where they pass, humidity spikes and rains back down.  
   • Defense: in panic they unzip themselves into fifteen to thirty discrete droplets, each retaining a shred of memory.  Re-assembly must occur within seven minutes or the stored pattern diffuses and the eel is lost.  
   • Symbiosis: Makers relish the ionic trace eels vent.  In return, regions dense with polyps give eels richer minerals to fold into bone.

6. The Duplicate Machines  
   • Ontology: neither artifact nor organism but an in-between caste the locals call the Stoics.  Reality-shear duplicates hardware, then the copies drift out of sync, acquiring quirks: pistons that respire like lungs, gear-trains that skip a tooth to mimic heartbeat.  
   • Need: steady rotary motion.  When an engine stalls, the valley edits it out within a season.  Thus each Stoic labors forever—not for work done, but for the right to remain.  
   • Language: oil viscosity.  Engineers once discovered that adjusting lubricant weight one grade up or down changes the rhythm a Stoic drums through the floor, spelling crude messages.  Those messages always ask the same question: “How long until fracture?”

7. Echo Travellers  
   • Genesis: any sapient who spends more than a lunar month inside the basin sheds “echoes”—thin-phase duplicates trailing eight to twelve seconds behind the original timeline.  
   • Properties: echoes have momentum but no will.  They repeat the host’s last actions until overwritten by new ones, then crumble into fine soot.  
   • Use: miners choreograph themselves via echoes—one real worker drills, three after-images lag behind catching cores, stacking crates, tying ropes.  Efficiency doubles at the cost of a chronic, low-grade vertigo known locally as Shear Sickness.  
   • Hazard: predators target unattended echoes first; if an echo is consumed completely, the corresponding section of the true traveller’s memory erases itself, leaving a blank warmth where an event used to live.

8. The Quiet Predators  
   • Taxonomy: debated.  To sight, they are smudges in thermal haze; to touch, a cold negative of whatever shape you expect.  
   • Method: they do not chase.  Instead they adjust the prey’s heartbeat by resonating tiny pressure waves—slow it, slow it, until life agrees to stop.  Valley law says: keep your tempo.  
   • Appetite: exclusively for creatures out of sync—those who hurry or hesitate.  Maintained rhythm is a passphrase.  
   • Corpse etiquette: after feeding, they leave a perfect outline of the victim in condensation on the nearest surface, as if chalking their work for an unseen coroner.

9. The Chalk-Sign Folk (Seen Only In Traces)  
   • Evidence: triple-scribed marks—chalk, scratch, song—on every third machine shell.  Bedrolls grown stiff with mineral dew.  Hand-cranks fitted to tape machines that whisper topography faster than shear can rearrange it.  
   • Culture: itinerant extractors of cold crystals.  They treat water as sacrament: before drilling they pour a libation onto bare rock, coaxing the valley to copy the vein rather than break it.  
   • Superstition: each camp carries a bell of bone that cannot ring.  They claim if sound ever escapes, daylight will follow, and all duplicate routes to fortune will collapse into a single, unforgiving line.

10. The Valley Itself  
   • If a place can be alive, this one is.  The shear behaves like metabolism, copying nutrients (pools, engines, even visitors) so the system never starves.  
   • Pulse: four beats slower than human rest.  Learn it and you are temporary kin; fight it and you become compost for the Slow Makers to breathe.  
   • Desire: equilibrium through plurality.  As long as versions proliferate, no single disaster can finish the story.  The valley is its own redundancy plan.

HOW THEY INTERRELATE

Energy stair-steps downward:

   Heat from the planet feeds the Slow Makers.  
   Makers breathe humidity for the Click-Grass and Tether-Moths.  
   Grass chatter guides Four-Knees to graze on algal films, keeping pools clear for Knuckle Eels.  
   Eels supply ions to Makers, closing the biochemical circle.  
   Stoic Machines siphon cold crystal to hold their bearings; Echo Travellers harvest it for trade, dragging stories in and out.  
   Quiet Predators prune the impatient, ensuring no heartbeat outruns the landscape’s slow edit.

WHY NONE OF THEM WILL TURN ON A LIGHT

Light would fix edges, arrest duplicates, and give time only one direction.  Every resident—polyps to miners—survives by blur, by overlap, by the mercy of versions.  Illumination is extinction here, so darkness is not absence but active consent: a pact the inhabitants renew with every unlit breath.

So, when you think the valley is empty, remember that almost everything alive inside it has chosen invisibility as its first defense and ambiguity as its homeland.  Stand still, match the pulse, and you will feel them gather—more host than guests—making room for your outline in the next slow copy of the world.
