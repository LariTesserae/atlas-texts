Who lives here?

Begin with the obvious answer and let it prove too small, then keep going.

1. The Photosynthetic Body  
Everything green-lit is one individual, a continent of tissue whose nerves are veins and whose thoughts, if they deserve the word, roll as pressure waves through sap.  Call it the Body, or the Seed, or the Chamber-forest; names do not trouble it.  It did not germinate in soil.  Heat, pressure-fractured stone, and a bacterial trick for binding free silica into sugar-glass were enough.  Wherever you put a finger you touch the same being.  

The Body is not hungry.  Its light is its food, its walls both skin and stomach.  What it wants, insofar as a plant can want, is constancy.  Every growth spurt is immediately countered by reabsorption elsewhere, the way a sleeper shifts on a mattress to keep the same overall shape.  The cavern’s famous equilibrium is simply the Body paying attention to itself.

2. The Lapse-Pollen  
Strictly speaking these are gametes, but they behave like a resident species.  Each speck glows—phosphorescence borrowed from the chlorophyll cycle—and carries a filament of neuroactive resin that blurs short-term memory in warm-blooded brains.  The effect is mild, pleasant, and extremely useful to the Parent Body, because it convinces wanderers that everything is safe, that nothing here needs questioning.  Most visitors forget to break anything; many forget why they came.  The pollen lives for minutes, dies, and is replaced in minutes more.  It is the dominant animal experience, despite never becoming an animal.

3. White Arthropods (“Hushmites”)  
The only indisputably independent fauna—pale, eyeless hexapods no bigger than nail-parings.  They skate on the mirrored pools, stridulate once, then dive to graze the fresh lime crust.  Calcium passes through a gut-symphony of bacteria and is excreted as husks that the Parent Body harvests for trace minerals.  Hushmites are tolerated the way eyelashes are tolerated on flesh: ornaments with a minor cleaning function.  Their total biomass is trivial, their evolutionary future precarious, but for the moment they are alive and busy.

4. The Unfinished Hands  
Occasionally the Body buds something that is not quite itself.  A root-tip meets a seam rich in sulphur or iron, tastes difference, and responds by printing organs too complex for mere absorption—gripping surfaces, levered thumbs, a hint of shoulder.  These abortive hominids never finish; they dwindle back into bark before lungs or eyes appear.  From a distance they look like statues half-rescued from melted wax.  No two are alike.  Some stand near the resin ladder, fingertips almost touching its lowest rung, as though memory of an earlier climber were trying to return.

5. The Encysted Guests  
Anyone who stays still long enough is adopted.  Heat maps and moisture gradients guide lattice-lianas toward the waiting figure.  Wrappings grow, fuse, soften, and at last flow through open pores; blood becomes sap with alarming mutual consent.  What results is neither corpse nor prisoner.  The adopted remain metabolically active, pulse slowed to one or two beats a hour, minds kept in a pollen dream.  They feed the Body by photosynthesising with borrowed chloroplasts and, in return, are kept ageless.  A dozen such silhouettes can be found in older chambers: explorers, pilgrims, a mule that strayed, the owner of the obsidian blade.  None appear distressed.  Some smile.

6. Echo-Pools  
Life need not breathe.  The largest pools are self-contained ecosystems of glassy water, lime crust, and vibration.  Every impact—footfall, spoken word, root-creak—sends ripples that meet, interfere, and persist far longer than surface physics permits.  Patterns wander the meniscus like slow lightning, folding inward, never repeating.  Local myth (there are a few among dry-land nomads) claims these ripples think.  What is certain is that they react: toss a pebble and every trunk-flute within sight will dilate its pores, misting fragrance, as though soothed.  Whether the pools are pets, organs, or independent minds remains undecided.

7. The Absent Builders  
Only negative space proves they existed: brick collars now half-digested, resin rungs browned by an ancient flame, that obsidian edge fixed forever in softwood memory.  No bone fragments, no charcoal, no tools beyond the knife.  Either they left voluntarily or were gentled into Encysted status and have long since dissolved.  Still, rumours cling.  Some say the Body was their crop, planted to mine stone for cellulose treasure.  Others that the Body germinated on its own and the Builders arrived later, missionaries of control, driven away when they asked for too much order.  The truth, if it lingers anywhere, lingers in the ripple-archives of the Echo-Pools—and the pools, being pools, do not volunteer testimony.

8. Microbial Choirs  
Impossible to see without lenses, but impossible to avoid inhaling.  Bio-engineered (by whom? when?) consortia live on airborne moisture.  Their colony songs—clicks of flagella, bursts of ion exchange—fall below human hearing yet register as unease in some visitors, euphoria in others.  The Choirs prune airborne spores that would otherwise darken the glow; they are custodians of clarity.  Without them, the cavern would fill with drifting chaff and the Body would choke on its own fertility.

9. Time-Shadows  
Stand very still and watch the light on a wall: you will see silhouettes that do not belong to you, slow-moving negatives of limbs that bend in anatomies never catalogued.  They keep perfect pace with the soft pops of root expansion, as if cast by workers on the far side of stone.  No one finds the workers.  The simplest explanation is refraction through layers of sugar-glass, painting delayed images of your own motions or of Encysted Guests twitching in their sleep.  The darker possibility is that the Parent Body rehearses futures against the walls, trying out shapes it may one day grow into.

10. The Place Itself  
At last accept that the cavern is not a habitat but a resident.  It wakes slower than thought, quicker than geology, expressing itself as architecture and climate where another creature might use gesture or scent.  Those “terraces,” those “corridors,” are ribs and capillaries.  The Body knows you are here; it has already softened the floor in case you decide to lie down, brightened the milk-yellow glow so you will see no threatening shadow, thickened the narcotic in the air just enough to gentle your doubts.

Some day it may want stronger company—birds, beasts, pollinating swarms—but for now it is content with its choir of microbes, its hushmites, its handful of dreaming statues, and its own echoing heart.

That is who lives here: one vast, patient Self, and the few small, temporary faces it lets bloom inside its endless summer.
