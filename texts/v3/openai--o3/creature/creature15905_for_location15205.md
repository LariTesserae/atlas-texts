WHO – OR WHAT – CALLS THE CLIFF HOME  

0.  A note on numbers  
No census holds still long enough to be trusted. “Population” is more an oscillation than a statistic: figures swell when a new steam-fissure opens, thin again when a magma lens migrates and whole terraces must be walked downslope, piece by piece, before they disappear into glass. At any given sunset you might count—if you had the patience—somewhere between eight and ten thousand conscious entities. Perhaps two-thirds are recognisably mammalian. The rest are stranger growths of purpose.

1.  The Hot-Blooded (baseline–derived citizens)  

   1.1  Body  
   •  Skin: charcoal to indigo, dusted with metallic silica that slow-bleeds infra-red like a lizard’s dewlap.  
   •  Lungs: triple-stacked alveoli; each breath can be routed through a chilled water bladder at the clavicle, trading moisture for calories.  
   •  Skeleton: studded with ceramic baffles—little chimney bricks let marrow dump excess heat into the air with every step.  
   •  Eyes: second eyelid impregnated with vanadium oxide; looks smoky, functions as a welding mask for sudden flare-ups.  

   1.2  Dress & Habit  
   •  Always layered: inner capillary mesh that circulates coolant-gel, outer “quiet cloak” woven from pumice-floss whose fibres chirp when bent more than a centimetre (a warning before a fall).  
   •  Footwear is sacramental. Boots are cast to the wearer’s gait and sound-tuned; a mis-matched pair would get you ticketed by the Acoustic Wardens.  
   •  Speak mostly in sub-vocal clicks transmitted through collarbone pickups—language as seismic whisper. Outsiders find the silence uncanny; residents find voiced consonants indecently loud.  

   1.3  Work  
   •  Condenser rig crews (“Netters”): climb before dawn, furl filigree sails that spool mist into calligraphy-thin gutters.  
   •  Rock-midwives: artisans who sculpt molten slag in cathedral moulds. They carry obsidian tuning forks; strike the cooling wall and hear faults before cracks appear.  
   •  Survey-siblings: pairs genetically predisposed to identical vestibular systems so their inner ears resonate together; they dangle hundreds of metres apart on sky-lines, mapping micro-tilts in the cliff.  

2.  The Lattice Minds  

   Not born, not built—annealed. When slagbrick cools for more than thirty years, copper impurities self-organise into meshworks that trap stray field noise from turbines. Most never flicker beyond reflex, but a few reach threshold and awaken as slow, intra-crystal intellects.  

   •  Form: a wall section, a floor, occasionally an entire buttress glowing faintly with electroluminescent veins.  
   •  Sense: pressure differentials and thermal gradients; they “hear” the city as a map of warmth.  
   •  Speech: modulate the cooling-tower organ note by micro-flexing their own masonry—conversation arrives as chord progressions.  
   •  Place in society: recognised as free citizens once a civic notary has held a ten-minute polyphonic dialogue with the structure. They vote by resonant proxy, prefer policies that favour long horizon stability, and can wait centuries for an answer.  

3.  The Long-Limb (confirmed)  

   The gossip is real, just rarely visible. Picture a chain of transparent cartilage beads, each the size of a child’s fist, linked by threads of hydrated silica. It moves by rearranging its own molecular water, extending one “bead” while dehydrating the last—peristalsis performed in reverse.  

   •  Diet: vapour-rich fissures; it drinks clouds.  
   •  Ecology: leaves dotted suction marks that seed trace minerals; chemotrophic lichens follow in its wake, turning each footprint into a grey blotch of life.  
   •  Temperament: shy of vibration. Locals cultivate silence partly so the Long-Limb will browse nearer to condenser farms—the creature is a living humidity pump. Its presence can lift the daily rainfall by three, sometimes four millimetres.  

4.  The Phase-Root Choir  

   The white filaments are neither plant nor animal but a transitional phase of subsurface bacteria that extrude glass needles when super-heated steam meets sulphate pockets. For the ninety seconds each filament exists, electrons tunnel along its length and emit a faint microwave hum. In concert tens of thousands create the after-rain “singing” heard in condenser sails.  

   •  Worshipped by the Vespertine Priests, who claim the cliff dreams through these antennae.  
   •  Studied by physicists for quantum-coherent properties; data encoded in microwave chirps is archived in zircon memory beads worn as rosaries.  

5.  The Isopods  

   •  Six-legged, fist-sized, shell of meshed quartz plates grown from recycled sand.  
   •  Programmed (biologically, not digitally) to eat rust: ingest iron oxide, excrete magnetite pellets later melted into print-feed.  
   •  Status: feral machines. Children keep them as pets until they molt and disappear into expansion joints to found new clutches.  

6.  The Acoustic Wardens  

   Not a species but a vocation found among both flesh-and-blood folk and Lattice Minds. They enforce Rule Three.  

   •  Tools: infrasonic probes, cork-black absorbent capes, citation slips written on pumice paper that disintegrates after the recipient has read the fine.  
   •  Legend says a Warden once fined an eruption for “excessive decibels,” then walked into the vent and was never seen again; the volcano has been quieter since.  

7.  Ghost Strata (speculative)  

   Some terraces keep casting shadows hours after dusk, silhouettes that glide over walls with no object to own them. Thermal cameras show negative-Kelvin anomalies—heat drawn from the future, borrowed to cool the present. Engineers suspect a thin layer of time-offset matter created by long-term condenser resonance. Priests shrug: “Every city grows its dead. Ours just arrive early.” Whether these strata are sentient remains unsettled doctrine.  

DAILY SYMMETRIES  

•  Dawn: Netters ascend, Long-Limbs retreat under stone, Lattice Minds vibrate a chord that aligns condenser vanes to the rising gradient.  
•  Mid-afternoon: the Rain Shear; citizens pause work, tilt heads back to steam-bathe, then blot skin with salt-wool that will later be mined for halides.  
•  Evening: silence deepens. The only sanctioned music is the Bell-Quarry—a mile of tuned basalt columns struck by robotic mallets so slowly you hear the melody unwrap over ninety minutes.  

HOW TO BE A GOOD VISITOR  

1.  File the soles of your boots until they squeak no louder than fingernails on silk.  
2.  Hum a single note while you walk; Lattice corridors answer with a guiding harmony—your impromptu map.  
3.  Accept that addresses are given in cooling ages: “Find me in the tower cast twelve summers after the Cracked Moon.”  
4.  Carry a vial of chilled water; it is tip money, apology, and first aid all at once.  
5.  Do not stare when a stone column clears its throat to correct your pronunciation.  
6.  If a transparent chain of beads brushes your ankle, stand absolutely still and let the humidity pass. You will feel the cliff breathe through you.  

In the furnace city on the newborn wall, life is an alloy: flesh braided with glass, intention folded into geology, silence hammered into law. Nothing here stays what it began as. Every organism, every citizen, even the cliff itself is still cooling toward the shape it will some day hold—patient, vertical, and quietly alive.
