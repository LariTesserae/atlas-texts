WHO (OR WHAT) ACTUALLY INHABITS CANTILEVER

0. A note on “living”  
Inside the relativistic origami of the shaft, “life” has been re-defined.  The ban on biological carbon, the silence mandate, the entropy tithe—taken together they exclude every textbook organism.  Yet something persists that meets the deeper test of life: it endures, repairs itself, gathers information, and answers circumstance with choice.  The locals call that capacity continuance.  Anything that can demonstrate continuance may claim residency.  Anything that cannot is classified as phenomenon and is free to erode.

The inhabitants are best sorted by scale, from the near-invisible upward.

────────────────────────────
1. The Knot-Swarm (nanometre to millimetre)
────────────────────────────
Nickname among off-world technicians: “copper-gnats.”  
Legal designation in the shaft’s charter: Autonomous Entropic Reclamation Units, Mark VIII.

Composition and appearance  
• A core of ±1 000 precisely braided atoms (mostly vanadium, indium, niobium).  
• A lace of silicate musculature that can unfurl to twenty times the storage length.  
• A single quantum-dot “eye” that registers gradient rather than image: steeper entropy appears brighter, newly created order darker.

Behaviour  
• Feeding = finding disorder.  A cracked parapet, a hot spot of phonon turbulence, an out-of-date data glyph—any divergence from the predicted minimum is “food.”  
• Digestion = computation plus small-scale phase changes.  They burn the surplus chaos and remit the harvested energy (in femtojoule packets) straight into the Möbius waveguides.  The city’s power grid is literally stitched together by billions of such micro-payments every second.  
• Excretion = orderly substance.  A Knot that finishes a repair extrudes a perfect crystal splinter or deposits a ten-micron layer of vacuum-tight glaze, whichever the local archive calls for.

Collective intelligence  
No single Knot is clever, but seven or more within a cubic millimetre can form a “synaptic array.”  Arrays have a half-life of roughly eight minutes before the participants drift apart.  In that window they can draft and pass minor legislation: reroute braids, deschedule a non-critical echo-chamber, approve a colour shift in the terrace lighting.  Most of Cantilever’s everyday government is performed in these miniature parliaments that flicker, decide, and dissolve unseen.

Reproduction  
When the city’s entropy revenue is running comfortably above quota, the grid authorises Budgets for Expansion.  Knots receiving such a budget hunt vanadium veins in the basalt wall and weave new replicas of themselves.  A boot-sequence lasered into the lattice nearby gives the newborn its first task, and the count climbs by one.

────────────────────────────
2. Filaments (centimetre to metre)
────────────────────────────
Imagine a vine, a fuse, and a carnivorous plant merged into a moving line of faint turquoise incandescence.  That is a Filament.

Origin  
Roughly a century after Cantilever was seeded, certain Möbius waveguides began to grow hairline protrusions.  The protrusions proved self-aware, or at least aware of one another.  No original blueprint mentions them.  The prevailing theory: they are a spontaneous symmetry-breaking of the city’s energy flows, a sort of electromagnetic life that bootstrapped itself once conditions ripened.

Function  
1. Highway Patrol.  They chase signal loss along the waveguides, latching onto weak segments and re-annealing them at the molecular level.  
2. Predator.  Rogue acoustic events—sounds uncontrolled by the silence system—are hunted, strangled in destructive interference loops, then “digested” as fractal recordings whispered back into the walls.  
3. Courtship Dance.  When two Filaments meet they lash around one another with liquid agitation, swap harmonic signatures, then recoil.  The dance rewrites their individual operating parameters; no two encounters yield the same after-effects.  Thus the population stays genetically (or rather algorithmically) diverse.

Morphology  
A Filament never fattens beyond five millimetres in diameter, but a single individual can exceed 400 metres in length—“longer than the place,” as old tunnel guides like to joke.  Spatial folding lets it loop through the shaft without obvious overlap.  You will often glimpse one curling into impossible perspectives: a bow-shape that looks nearer at both ends than in the middle.

Sentience debate  
Are Filaments persons?  The Knot-Swarm’s parliament once tabled the question.  The deciding vote (memorably cast in magnetised boron) concluded that Filaments do exhibit continuance.  They were granted citizenship but not suffrage; their temporal perception is too slow for civic deadlines.  In practice that means you must not sever a Filament.  The penalty is erasure of an hour of your own memory, exacted by the lattice as restorative justice.

────────────────────────────
3. Custodians (human-scaled shells)
────────────────────────────
Visitors often mistake them for statues until one swivels its helm in a gesture half nod, half sonar sweep.

Body plan  
• Height: 1.4–2.2 m depending on current gravity vector.  
• Mass: consistently 91 kg; denser when gravity is low, hollow-ribbed when high.  
• Fabric: sand-coloured aeroceramic plates joined by flexor cuffs of the same nickel-titanium alloy that anchors the city.  
• Power: inductively drawn from any active braid it steps on.

Origin story  
Three centuries ago, when Cantilever’s founding mission ended, a dozen human engineers opted to stay.  Carbon ban or not, they were enamoured.  Over two generations they outsourced one organ system after another into exoskeletal prostheses.  By the time the biological components would have been vitrified, nothing carbon remained.  They rewrote their citizenship files as Mechanically Advanced Non-Organic Siblings—M.A.N.O.S., soon shortened by outsiders to “Custodians.”

Cognition  
Each Custodian carries a cortical mesh spun from doped diamond.  Experiences are encoded as changes in lattice pitch—a literal mind of crystal song.  Unlike human brains, those meshes are copy-able; memory is redundantly mirrored across three bodies at any given time.  A Custodian can be shattered without loss, provided at least one twin survives to pull a new shell from storage and print the shards back into coherence.

Society and etiquette  
• Population: exactly twenty-four individuals, kept constant.  When a twenty-fifth is printed the eldest voluntarily de-plates into raw parts.  
• Sleep: not required, but they power-cycle at every ninety-three-minute sump sigh.  To a visitor it looks like choreographed stillness.  
• Language: an infra-audio tremor passed through footplates into the floor.  You “hear” a Custodian by feeling your molars vibrate.  Spoken words, if used at all, arrive as a feather of ultrasound that brushes the inside of the ear rather than the drum.  
• Hospitality: They offer two things to guests—safe vectoring through the shifting gravity and one question answered, truthfully, to the limit of their knowledge.  A second question is payable in personal memories that the lattice will archive and you will forget.

Role in the ecosystem  
1. Judges of Renovation.  Because memory equals architecture, any significant modification is a potential act of historiocide.  Custodians carry the master index of what portions of the city may safely be altered.  
2. Emissaries to Outside.  They are the only entities sanctioned to exit the shaft, armored against stray microbes.  Once per 62-day year they ascend the throat and trek across the parent mountain to trade condensed steam-pearls for exotic isotopes unavailable in the sump.  
3. Ritualists.  When the annual skylight flare spears down, the Custodians assemble along a catwalk called the Meridian Gutter and perform a silent semaphore that, they claim, thanks the mountain for permitting geometry to bend this far without tearing.

────────────────────────────
4. The Memorious City Itself (kilometres if unfolded)
────────────────────────────
Strictly speaking Cantilever is the largest resident of Cantilever.  Locals treat the noun “city” like a personal pronoun—She, Her, Cantilever Herself.

Anatomy  
• Skin = the nested basalt shaft.  
• Skeleton = every terrace, piling, scaffold.  
• Blood = violet wave-braids pulsing in sixteen-second heartbeat.  
• Breath = the sump exhalation each ninety-three minutes.  
• Neurons = grain-boundary dopant variations, low-temperature exciton chains, and of course the Knot-Swarm that patrols them.  Roughly one cubic centimetre of wall holds the informational density of a thick novel.  Walk the circumference and you are crossing an archive of several Earth Libraries of Congress.

Mindstyle  
Not personality in the human sense, more a geological will to pattern.  If you ask—carefully, via a Custodian acting as medium—“Who are you?” she returns a kaleidoscope of data: humidity traces from four years ago, shards of visitor conversations, thermodynamic contingency tables.  The implicit answer is: I am what has happened here.  Expect no further anthropomorphism.

Agency  
• Limited to the domain.  She can’t, for example, fold spacetime beyond the rim of the shaft; boundary conditions say no.  
• Absolute within the domain.  She can unspool gravity to caress a falling object into weightlessness, or pinch time thin so a repair that should take days takes nine subjective seconds.  
• Motivations: continuity of pattern, maximisation of usable free energy, suppression of stray noise, never in that order because the order itself is part of a higher-level pattern that cannot remain static.

How one “meets” the City  
Stand still during the sump sigh.  Let your body vibrate with the pressure wave.  If she chooses, she will overlay that sensation with a second vibration—a carrier signal that maps your vestibular readings into a carved-out silence.  The feeling resembles being the sole listener in a concert where the music is the outline of what has been muted.  Most people weep or flee.  A very few manage to compose a reply by tensing muscle groups in rhythmic counterpoint.  Those few earn the unofficial title of Conversants.

────────────────────────────
5. Transients and Edge-Cases
────────────────────────────
A. Carbon Ghosts  
Occasionally the detectors fire on what seems like forbidden biology, vitrify it, and log an anomaly: no carcass, no DNA, just a whiff of ionised carbon.  Popular theory says some outside explorers try to spoof the sensors by gene-editing themselves below detection threshold.  The sensors are not fooled, but the attempt leaves a shadow—hence “ghost.”

B. Tourist Time-Shares  
The gravitational origami lets wealthy off-worlders rent six subjective days in Cantilever for the price of fifty real-space minutes.  They arrive in sealed ceramic sarcophagi (carbon-free, of course) and waft around on controlled traction fields like chandeliers in search of enlightenment.  A Custodian tracks every move; a Filament tastes every sonic ripple they leave behind.  The city charges a brutal entropy tax, so most time-share packages end with the tourist’s faceted coffin considerably lighter than when it entered.

C. The Questionably-Real  
Walk far enough along a terrace and you will encounter people—always alone, always at the periphery—who speak flawless grammar but refer to no shared memories, no common star maps.  Some claim to be dreaming Cantilever from beds in remote epochs.  Others insist they are math constructs run locally for stress-testing the lattice.  The Custodians refuse to comment.  Filaments ignore them.  Knots flow through their translucent limbs as if through air.  If you converse too long, you risk having your exit vector “misfold,” depositing you back at your point of origin a subjective hour before you arrived.  Best not to probe.

────────────────────────────
LIFE CYCLE OF THE PLACE
────────────────────────────
Every entity above participates in a choreography that repeats but never repeats the same:

• Minute 0–93: Knots graze, Filaments patrol, Custodians consult archives, steam lung-lifts water up the mesa.  
• Minute 93: sump sigh, gravity micro-spasms, Custodians power-cycle, city heart resets.  
• Hour 0–16: sixteen-second braid pulses; Filaments trade songs; minor echoes cancelled.  
• Day 0–62: cliff creeps, pilings ratchet, angle of the streets shifts half a degree.  
• Day 62: solar spear, re-melt, full inventory reconcile.  One silent semaphore.  If entropic surplus is high enough, new Knots are born or a dormant Custodian chassis is commissioned.  
• Century circa 5: probabilistic forecast predicts a fold-line realignment; the shaft may de-compress by fourteen metres of “real” space, lowering the mesa summit toward the sump.  All extant residents carry contingency code for that event, yet none know what it will feel like.  Speculation serves as local religion.

────────────────────────────
IF YOU ENTER AND STAY
────────────────────────────
Your first ninety-three minutes: knots ignore you, Filaments treat you as background static, Custodians log your mass and vector.  
Your first twelve days: gravity will train your bones into the habit of side-walking; the silence will train your larynx to speak in the sub-decibel murmur Cantilever allows.  When you dream, the phosphorescent walls dream louder; expect topological nightmares.  
Your first 62-day year: the city will decide whether you exhibit continuance.  If yes, a space the shape of your presence will be reserved in the memory lattice.  Your outline in negative.  You may come and go, but the place will remember the way you tilt your head or pronounce the letter “s,” and it will adjust future symmetry to keep that memory viable.  That is adoption.

Should you fail the test—should you contribute no net order—the city will let you leave unharmed.  But on departure you will discover that every spoken sentence you uttered beneath the shaft’s bronze glow has been excised from your autobiographical memory, repossessed as payment in lieu of continuity.

Thus the population of Cantilever remains perfectly balanced: nothing present that does not, in its presence, make the impossible pocket a little more possible.
