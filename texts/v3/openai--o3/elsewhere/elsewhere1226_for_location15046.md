The Reverie Wells

From a thousand fissures in the high plateau the day’s condensation seeps downward. For three hundred kilometres the trickle threads the limestone, collecting iron, memory, and the infrasonic heartbeat of the stone above. Where the plateau finally breaks and slumps toward lower country the trickle arrives all at once, rising in sudden vertical shafts that open to the sky like dark keyholes: the Reverie Wells. No two wells are alike in width or depth, yet placed together they describe a ragged spiral five leagues wide, as though some slow hand bored them while turning on a single heel.

Terrain and materials  
• Host rock: the same dolomitic limestone as the plateau but glass-polished by centuries of capillary flow; each wall glints with mica grains too small to feel.  
• Rim: a crenulated collar of tufa built by splash-mist. It bruises underfoot, leaving prints that self-heal overnight as new mineral layers accrete.  
• Throat: drops anywhere from twenty to ninety metres before water is met. The stone here carries fossilised trails of ridge-scalder acid, preserved like freeze-frame lightning.  
• Basin: mirror-still pools unbroken by surface insects. The water wears three skins:  
  1. Upper meniscus, clear as air, scented faintly of musk familiar from plateau mornings.  
  2. Middle veil, opaline with suspended silica harvested en route; daylight diffuses sideways rather than down.  
  3. Dream sump, a black, syrup-slow brine rich in dissolved proteins shed by subplateau resonants. No rope has touched its bottom—sounding lines lose tension and slide sideways, returning slick with oil that never quite dries.

Hydrology  
The wells are not fed by rain; they drink only the plateau’s exhalations. Flow is pulse-driven: every forty-two minutes—matching the pillars’ rhythm—a tremor rises through the crust, water lifts a finger-width, and silts settle again. Over a day the level can rise a full metre, then retreat once the plateau cools after sunset. Visitors tether boats to scarlet depth-stakes hammered into the rim; untether a moment too long and the craft may dangle mid-wall when the water sighs back down.

Atmosphere  
Above each pool a lens of humid, chilled air hovers like inverted fog. Step through the rim and the temperature drops ten degrees; breath blossoms visibly then is pulled flat against the mirror in scrolling arabesques before sinking into the opaline layer. Sound slows here: a word spoken at the lip reaches a listener on the far side fractionally late, as though travelling through syrup rather than air.

Vegetation (rootless, downward-facing)  
• Well-wicks: vermilion vines clinging flat to the vertical walls. Their stomata face the stone; their roots dangle in mid-air like tassels that comb humidity. Overnight the roots weave a temporary net across the well mouth, catching stray insects that migrate by starlight. Dawn heat snaps the net; the corpses rain into the pool.  
• Drip-shrouds: translucent fungal sheets the size of blankets that float a handspan above the water, buoyed by gasses they ferment in situ. They absorb silica mist, knitting it into fibrous glass that eventually weighs them down, whereupon they sink, dissolve, and feed the dream sump. The glass husks later wash ashore as feather-thin plates patterned with the same nested spirals etched on plateau lintels.

Fauna (few in number, elaborate in method)  
• Prism efts: juvenile stage aquatic, skinless, membranes refracting incoming light into darting rainbows that confuse predators into striking reflections. Adult stage terrestrial; they climb the walls using surface-tension pads, shed a lattice of calcite ribs that remain clamped to the stone like open hands long after the animal has gone.  
• Mirror-sippers: bat-sized avians with throat pouches lined by capillary velvet. They skim the surface at night, scooping the clear upper meniscus while avoiding deeper veils. The pouch rejects any droplet containing more than four parts per thousand silica; beads that fail the test roll back out, never dimpling the water.  
• Echo crabs: blind arthropods dwelling in the rim tufa. Each keeps a single air bubble sealed beneath its carapace; when threatened it vents the bubble across stridulating plates, producing a perfect copy of the last sound it heard. Two crabs cornered together trap themselves in recursive mimicry until exhaustion topples them into the well.

Lightscape  
Day: shafts serve as solar wells. Light ricochets between mica chips, filling even the lowest pools with diffuse warmth. Night: whatever bioluminescence lantern ropers charge on the plateau bleeds downward through the rock; faint amber filaments shimmer in the throat stone, as though you are standing inside an enormous piece of back-lit parchment. Stars reflected in the pools appear nearer than the real ones, and on windless nights observers complain they cannot decide which sky is the copy.

Soundscape  
Wind never reaches the water; instead each well acts as an organ pipe for the plateau’s infrasonic pulse. The forty-two-minute beat amplifies within the shaft, climbing into audible range as a bass chord that modulates pool level. Between pulses the only noise is the champagne-soft crackle of tufa precipitating grain by grain.

Built traces  
• Spiral moorings: tooth-thin rods of unknown metal twist from certain rims, perfect logarithmic helices exactly one adult arm-span tall. Boats tied to them do not rise and fall with the water; instead they remain fixed relative to the rim, the rope shortening or lengthening itself with each tide.  
• Step shells: partial staircases clinging to the inner wall, seemingly carved to invite descent. Each tread spans just three fingers and is rimed with glass so sharp that any footstep scores a note you feel more than hear. The steps never reach the water; halfway down they cease, leading either nowhere—or somewhere that only exists when the pool is higher.  
• Pontiff pillars: slimmer cousins to those on the plateau, grown from the dream sump upward until arrested by gravity’s local quirks. They stop centimetres short of the lip, supporting nothing, nevertheless bowed by the weight of the music moving through them.

Weirdness that feels natural  
1. Depth inversion: drop a stone and watch it fall, strike the mirror, then continue upward inside the reflection until it vanishes in the ‘sky’ below. Hours later the same stone may be found on the rim, frost-cold, as if expelled by thought rather than physics.  
2. Memory seep: write on limestone with charcoal; a tide or two later the letters brighten into white engravings as the rock extrudes calcite along the carbon scaffold. Messages penned by nineteenth-century cartographers still bloom afresh every damp cycle, each generation of tracery overlaying the former without erasure.  
3. Dream hemorrhage: sleep within echo distance of a well and you will wake remembering an unbroken monologue spoken in a voice not your own, stretched so thin over time that meaning flakes off like dried spittle. Linguists have matched fragments to the plateau-leaners’ pillar dreams, suggesting a shared transmitter—or a single dream migrating by capillary action.

Dynamics—tidal stone, breathing water  
The wells follow the plateau’s metabolism, swelling when it exhales heat, shrinking as it drinks night air. Drip-shrouds rise and fall with the tide, shearing off ribbons of glass each cycle. Well-wicks time their pollen release to the minimum water height so the grains can dust the exposed throat and ride thermals back toward the plateau, where certain mortar-shrubs have been found carrying the exact pollen—proof of a slow, vertical commerce in genetics.

Rules to move by  
• Never drop two objects into a well simultaneously; the reflections interfere and can return swapped.  
• Speak only once every pulse while inside the rim; echoes stack unpredictably, and a careless shout can cancel the resonance that keeps the water placid.  
• If you must descend, wet your gloves first. Dry skin pulls silica threads from the wall as easily as plucking harp strings and the note will follow you for hours.  
• Bring no written secrets. The limestone remembers.

Standing on a rim at the pulse’s peak, you look down through meters of perfect transparency, past the opaline veil, into the dream sump’s occult black. Your own outline hovers there, hung between two skies. Somewhere above the plateau is emptying its mirrored pools into these throats; somewhere below something listens upward through pillar-spines, counting your breaths the way a herdsman counts cloud-shepherds. A hush billows out of the shaft—not silence but a pause long enough for stone to choose its next word. When it comes it is low, syllables minutes apart, but you feel them through bone: a conversation begun on the plateau, continued here in water, and still rolling downward to destinations neither place will name.
