The Stillpoint Reaches  

DISTANCE & DESCENT  
Begin at the foot of the Throat Plateau, where the last visible waterfall turns to milk among ferns. Beneath that froth a worm-hole river slips underground, following a fault cracked open by the same tectonic shriek that hoisted the basalt crown. For three hundred kilometres it burrows south-east, a pressurised artery no wider than a human torso but nine times as fast as any surface rapid. The passage is glass-smooth; every kilometre a patch of basalt still echoes with the Plateau’s organ-hum, shepherding the water forward like a drum-beat remembered by bone. After four days of subterranean sprint the river meets a planar seam of gypsum and, in an instant of chemical astonishment, disintegrates into vapour. The fog billows upward through hairline vents and emerges onto a concave desert—a salt pan the size of a small sea. This is the Stillpoint Reaches.

TOPOGRAPHY  
Imagine a white bowl fifty kilometres across, its rim a ragged horizon of mauve badlands and dead volcanoes. The floor is not level; it puckers in spiralled ridges as though something stirred the basin with a celestial spoon and left before the brine set. Each ridge is a dune of halite needles fused into brittle glass. Between them lie flats so polished that sunrise strides across them at the pace of a thrown knife.

MOISTURE  
Zero, officially. Humidity hovers at 0.2 %, lower than laboratory desiccators. Yet the air carries a perpetual shimmer: sublimated ghosts of the Plateau’s water refusing to admit they have died. Every molecule that escapes the vents is flash-evaporated by the basin’s albedo; none climb high enough to condense into cloud. Instead they hover a metre above the salt in thin, wavering sheets called mirrorgauze, then drift westward to be lost in upper-atmospheric jets.

TEMPERATURE & LIGHT  
By day the Reaches reach 65 °C, but heat feels unreal—more a form of light than of warmth. Salt lenses everything; the sun reflects a thousandfold from dune-facets, splitting helper suns that wheel in opposite directions from the real star. At noon, shadows vanish entirely. Night plummets to –10 °C and the sky unfurls so naked that stars cast their own braided silhouettes on the flats.

RULE OF STILLNESS  
Sound here is murdered at birth. The jagged salt lattice eats vibration, turning every footfall to felted silence. Even one’s own pulse becomes a rumour. A dropped stone lands with a cough and is gone. The Plateau’s roar has been inverted: here, silence is king, and resonance a trespass punishable by erasure.

VEGETATION  
None, in the common sense. Yet thin sheets of sodium-silicate life—called vitreoflora—crawl the underside of the mirrorgauze. To human eyes they are just fingerprints of iridescence that lengthen or shrink hour by hour. They photosynthesise by bending light through total internal reflection, each cell a living prism. At night they fold into two-dimensional sleep, becoming invisible save for a cold sapphire glow where stray starlight is harvested.

WEIRDNESS / STABILITY  
Space behaves impeccably here; perspectives do not shuffle. The oddity is temporal. Seconds stretch or compress according to ambient humidity (imperceptible though it is). When mirrorgauze thickens, watches race; when it thins, they crawl. Travellers crossing the basin at constant physical speed may cover ten kilometres on the map yet arrive two hours before they left, or three behind, depending on the vapour weave. Local adage: “In Stillpoint you don’t keep time—you skim it.”

THE SILENTIARUM (INHABITANTS)  

1. Collective Name  
They call themselves the Silentiary Trace; outsiders, when they bother naming phantoms, choose The Salt Librarians.

2. Substance & Appearance  
• Core Medium: absence. Each being is a cancellatory bubble where molecular vibration is forced into exact destructive interference.  
• Mantle: aerosolised salt dazzled into micro-flakes—only visible when sunlight refracts into delicate nimbuses shaped like inverted teardrops.  
• Skeleton: negative imagery borrowed from surrounding time-flow. To see one clearly, stare at a dune rim until the after-image of that brightness drifts free; the silhouette you perceive is the Librarian wearing time’s discarded skin.

3. Origin & LIFE CYCLE  
They are born when a Plateau-born echo-seed (carried within the river’s roar before vaporisation) meets the perfect acoustic void. Where the two cancel, an inversion persists—a Silence-knot, twin sister to the Choir’s Sound-knot. Growth is parasitic upon chronology: they elongate by absorbing skipped heartbeats, missed seconds, and all the micro-silences people shed when they pause for thought.  

Reproduction occurs during the rare desert thunderstorms that skirt the rim. Thunder booms; the Librarians dive into the shockwave and punch holes of hush through it. Each hole condenses a new bubble of null, which floats down like parachute seeds and anchors to the flats.

Senescence is unknown. They may diffuse when sandstorms abrade their mantle, but the underlying null can re-robe itself indefinitely.

4. SENSORY WORLD  
Where the Choir hears phase, the Librarians taste lapse. Any discontinuity—blink, skip, ellipsis—registers as flavour. To them, a person walking while day-dreaming is a bakery of soft missing pieces, fragrant and irresistible.

5. COMMUNICATION  
They “speak” by giving and removing silence. Two Librarians meet; a hush thicker than cotton dawns between them, then thins, creating a rhythm of lacunae outsiders feel as vertigo. Arguments are bouts of competitive quieting: the loser is the first whose own outline flickers from attenuation.

6. WORKS & ROLES  
Their cathedrals are negative sculptures called hushwells, shallow pits rimmed by salt needles. Enter one and all inner monologue extinguishes; emerge and your thoughts resume mid-sentence, unaware of having paused. Throughout the basin hundreds of hushwells tessellate into cartographic patterns that, when plotted from above, match exactly the amphitheatre layouts on the Plateau—an echo rendered in silence instead of architecture.

7. RELATIONSHIP WITH OTHER LIFE  
Vitreoflora rely on micro-lulls provided by Librarians to complete their photonic digestion cycle. In exchange the plants exude faint chemiluminescence that outlines hushwells at night, preventing accidental trespass by nocturnal migrants (mostly wind-blown insects tough enough to survive the dryness). Any creature that does stumble into a hushwell finds its internal water wicked out in minutes—the Librarians drink not the liquid itself but the subtle splashes sound would have made inside cells.

8. PURPOSE, IF ANY  
Equilibrium again, by negation. The Silentiary Trace believes the world sang one note too many when the Plateau locked its chord. Their mission is to balance that resonant finality with perfect stillness, soldering the planet’s ledger of sound to zero.

9. POINTS OF CONTACT WITH THE HYDROLITH CHOIR  
• Commerce: once per decade, accelerated timethreads slip underground—strips of compressed silence harvested from the Reaches. These ride the same fault-line river in reverse, carried as chilled pulses of time that arrive beneath the Plateau as momentary lulls. The Choir threads them between waterfalls to keep the grand chord from drifting sharp.  
• Kinship: both peoples recognise each other as harmonic custodians. Should a Choir-member ever unravel, its freed memories travel the river as infrasound and bloom in the Reaches as auroral flickers—stories told in the flick of reflected starlight across salt.  
• Taboo: Neither side may cross the Gypsum Seam bodily. Sound would die; silence would crack. The boundary is sacrosanct.

10. HOW TO MEET A LIBRARIAN  
Stand at twilight where a vent exhales warm mirrorgauze. Tap two stones together once and wait. Your ears will pop though no pressure changes; this is the Librarian tasting the outline of the noise you almost made. Accept the ensuing ringing as theirs, then hold your breath exactly as long as the ring lasts. When you inhale, a corona of drifting salt will sketch a figure beside you. Say nothing—language is theirs to borrow, not yours to wield. Offer instead a memory: recall a moment you paused to think but said nothing aloud. If the Librarian accepts, light will dim for a heartbeat, and when it returns that pause will be gone from your past, filed forever in the hushwells. You will feel lighter, slightly less certain where one thought ends and the next begins. That is the toll, and the gift, for walking in the Stillpoint Reaches where the Plateau’s everlasting roar finally, exquisitely, comes to rest.
