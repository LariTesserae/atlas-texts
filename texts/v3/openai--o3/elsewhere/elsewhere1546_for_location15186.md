THE MIRROR FEN

1. Reach  
Follow the word water as it flees the Dry Glade.  A single syllable, spat before it can fracture the tiles, rolls west on convection that never stirs a leaf, crosses an escarpment where salt walls give way to silt, and finds ground that answers back with yield instead of echo.  There the word breaks open and fills a shallow, mile-wide bowl.  The locals say the Fen is nothing but every fugitive droplet the Glade has ever rejected, gathered in one patient depression and deciding to stay.

2. Circumference  
No wall contains it.  Instead a braided dyke of living mud—peat wadded with rootlets and fist-sized gastroliths—traces a scalloped perimeter.  Step on the dyke and it sinks exactly to the depth of your ankle, never more.  Step off and the basin receives you to the shin, the knee, sometimes the hip, according to how much moisture you carried in your voice when you last spoke.  Because the dyke is always collapsing and re-knitting, the outline of the Fen wanders in slow cartographic drift, though its area remains stubbornly constant: 103 hectares, give or take the volume of a single remembered teardrop.

3. Atmosphere  
Temperature stays at 19 °C, cool enough that your skin puckers, warm enough that breath does not steam.  Humidity is total.  A faint dew-rain falls upward: haze condenses on your soles and climbs your body in luminous beads before evaporating off your shoulders.  Light has the colour of hammered nickel.  Mid-morning, a hidden sun manages a dim oval, but through most hours illumination is even, sourceless, and diffused until edges have no charter.

4. Ground and Under-Ground  
The floor is a palimpsest of silt lenses, each only hours old.  Silt settles, bakes under the nickel glare, cracks, floods, settles again.  Within the upper metre lie thousands of miniature cups—negative pottery thrown by nothing but alternating thirst and glut.  They resemble the Dry Glade’s clay plates in diameter and spacing, yet here every cup is inverted: concave where the Glade was convex, glazed in opalescent biofilm instead of tar.  Upset the layers and you discover still older strata, fired grey-brick fragments whose knife-marks align perfectly with those on the Glade’s rim, as if both perimeters were sawn from the same original wall and given different weathers to inhabit.

5. Vegetation (aquiferous)  
Plants bleed freely.

• Lamp-reeds: two-metre stalks with hollow, glassy interiors whose sap burns with cold flame.  At dusk the Fen lights itself in ragged constellations where reed tips crack and exhale blue fire.  
• Thirst-ferns: low mats of corrugated leaflets that drink directly through stomata wide as eyelets.  Brush a mat and it sighs, a thousand pores closing with audible relief before reopening to the next footfall.  
• Memory-moss: pink, gelatinous, keeps soluble whatever story the water carries.  Walk twenty paces and the moss behind you whispers your last sentence back, still wet with phoneme.

6. Fauna  
• Basin eels: transparent, rope-long fishes that use capillary creep to climb reed interiors.  Their hearts are double-valved: one side pumps blood, the other pumps pure Fen water, returning it laced with minerals stolen from flesh.  
• Glimmer crabs: carapaces line-etched like cracked glaze.  They harvest lamp-reed scoria, stacking glowing shards around their burrows to lure prey—mainly unwary syllables that drift in off the air.  
• Word-leeches: ribbon-thin, sky-borne.  They cling to spoken language, grow fat on vowels, then rain down into the pools where they dissolve and inseminate the silt with half-digested verbs.

Predation is osmotic; to kill is to dilute.  The loser becomes thinner, the victor more saturated.

7. Eldness  
Unlike the Dry Glade’s perpetual youth, the Fen is ancient by accumulation.  Each tide of new runoff erases a little of what preceded it, yet a film remains, thickening the whole.  Scholars guess at 9 000 seasons, give or take the lifespan of a single unresolved promise.

8. Geometry and Time  
Where the Glade suffers loops, the Fen burrows.  Speak a place-name and it forms a sink: ground subsides until the name rests three hand-spans lower than the unsaid earth.  Over hours the depression migrates laterally, dragging whoever uttered it in a slow spiral toward the heart-pool.  There, time stratifies like silt—future settling first, past last, present suspended between.  More than one traveler has waded in confident of a shallow ford, only to find decades layered beneath their knees.

9. Soundscape  
Constant drip and percolation.  Lamp-reed fire crackles wetly, like water learning to imitate flame.  Eels gulp inside stems with an ocarina hoot.  Under it all: a sub-audible slosh, the collective mass of words too heavy to stay airborne, circling the basin like a distant paddle-wheel.

10. Governing Principles  
• Dryness is debt; wetness is credit.  The Fen settles accounts nightly in fog.  
• Metal rusts to breath in minutes.  Visitors bind copper tools in waxed cloth, removing them only to fix a measurement nobody trusts for long.  
• An utterance dropped in open water will ferment into song within a day.  Harvested at dawn, the liquor sings itself once and falls silent forever; locals drink it to remember things that never happened.

11. Residents and Their Labours  

A. The Reed-Tenders  
Once Salt-Borne, they crossed here by accident—granular joints dissolving, plates reforming into cartilage.  Now their limbs are joists packed with sponge.  They police the inland tide, breaking dykes where too much memory backs up, mending them where forgetfulness tries to escape.

B. The Syllable-Fishers  
Nomads from the edge of the Dry Glade, sworn to keep water and word apart.  They cast braided hair-lines tipped with bronze letters, snag bloated consonants drifting just above the surface, and dry them on racks until they clink like porcelain.  The driest are carried home and hammered into anchor-names that keep Glade corridors from folding too tight.

C. The Fen Itself  
Where the Glade thinks in scent, the Fen thinks in echoes.  It waits for speech, turns it over, adds depth the way a potter adds slip, then offers the enriched version back reflected from its pools.  Listen too closely and your own voice returns older, burdened with clauses you have not yet lived.

12. Trade, Kinship, Tide  
Oils seep west from the Glade, volatilise, and ride cool air downslope, condensing here as rainbow slicks that feed lamp-reed flame.  In return, every overflow of the Fen evaporates east in funeral plumes, arriving at the Glade as invisible ghosts of water—just enough to tempt, never enough to break the clay.  Thus each keeps the other from the perfection that would end them: dryness receives the scent of flood; wetness, the rasp of drought.

13. Hazards and Consolations  
Step careless and the mud may drink a year of your future.  Speak kindness over open water and it will float back to you, sweeter, like a song returned from someone who finally understood the refrain.  Stand on the dyke at dusk, between blue fire and nickel sky, and you will feel the whole basin breathe: in the swell, the Glade’s rejected heartbeat; in the ebb, its next uncertain word.

14. Departure  
Leave by silence.  Say nothing for one hundred paces and the mud relinquishes its claim; words remain behind, budding into new lamp-reeds or sinking to line a crab’s mosaic den.  When you reach the high, dry salt again, a faint humidity clings to your cuffs.  The Dry Glade inhales it, recognising a trace of itself made strange by water, and every lily-skin flutters once—an exhalation it will deny having made.
