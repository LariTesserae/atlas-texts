The Shatterflats  

The river that idles through the Yard—thick as cooling glass, silted with slag dust and eely warmth—wanders north-east for eighty winding kilometres before it remembers its purpose. A day’s drift past the last moss-coated trestle, the banks part like curtains, and the water exhales onto a tableland that was once the floor of a proglacial lake. Here the ground is level only in the arithmetic sense; to the eye it is a quilt of plates, each a different angle of broken sheen. Salt dominates, but never quietly. Chloride crusts flower into daggers, then slump to powder; gypsum blooms as rayed cups that drink moonlight and close at dawn; veins of lithe, fibrous glauberite streak the soil like frozen lightning. Collectively the locals call it glass-salt, because it catches every stray photon and sets the whole steppe glimmering as though someone were forever sweeping aside an invisible curtain of light.

1. Geography and Weather  

• Elevation. A brush higher than the Yard’s highest smokestack; far enough from the sea that tides are rumours, not events.  
• Span. Roughly fifty by fifty kilometres, bordered on three sides by crumbling moraines that seethe with pink halobacteria. The fourth side grades into bog where the river’s warmth can still keep water liquid.  
• Surface. Neither soil nor rock but platelets, each a fingernail-thin lamina of evaporite. When wind gusts, the plates flutter, ring, and sometimes loft upward like playing cards flung by an unseen dealer.  
• Climate. Precipitation falls only as rime-fog: sub-freezing mist that condenses into lace on whatever it touches. Noon glare can peel corneas; midnight frost can etch glass. Between both lies a brittle dusk, when the salt lies quiet and the wind forgets its maps.

2. The Long-Echo Exchange  

What ties the Shatterflats to the Yard is a commerce of heat and silence. Waste steam from the valley runs in jacketed trunk-lines buried under the riverbed. At Shatterflats Terminus the lines breathe into ranked condenser-trees: iron trunks whose limbs spiral upward, extruding a billion needle-thin capillaries. Warm vapour rushes up, chills against the night sky, and rains back as distilled water—clean enough to bite the tongue with its emptiness. Every dawn, sled caravans haul the condensate south, paying in slabs of glass-salt tuned for acoustic damping. Those slabs, once mortared into factory walls, swallow the palimpsest echoes that haunt the Yard, gifting night crews an hour of hollower quiet.

3. Phenomena  

• Salt Flash. When atmospheric charge builds, the entire steppe can ignite in violet corona, each crystal edge shedding a centimetre of itself in spark. The flash lasts seconds, leaves no smoke, and makes the flats smell briefly of bitten tongue.  
• Subsurface Fissures. Heat pulses from the buried steam mains soften layers below and fracture the crust above. Locals navigate by “crystal sonar,” stamping poles and listening for the flute-like buzz that warns of hollow ground.  
• The Halo Engine. Twice a year, when sun angle and humidity conspire, a concentric rainbow rings the horizon. During the halo’s brief reign every footstep on the flats hums a perfect minor third lower, as though pressure itself had modulated.

4. Inhabitants  

4.1 The Pane-Marchers (human)  
   – Pedigree. Descendants of the original condenser-tree riggers. Skin perpetually dusted with fine halite, giving them a dull prism sheen.  
   – Dress. Layered veils of smoked quartz fibre; each veil graded to a specific wavelength so the wearer can tilt a head and filter glare selectively.  
   – Speech. Whisper-register clicks and throat-pops. Anything louder risks shattering the ground beneath another’s feet.  
   – Custom. At birth every child receives a shard inscribed with a fragment of the Flats’ oral map. Only at death is the full atlas reassembled—but always with gaps where plates have moved since the earliest shard was cut.

4.2 Brine Scribes (augmented human)  
   Operate the Halo Engine’s observatory domes. Their eyes are milky from years of glare; instead they read refraction signatures cast onto braille-like salt tablets. Messages from the Yard’s Mapwrights arrive as modulated steam pulses in the mains. A good Scribe can decode a five-layer blueprint from the pattern of pits left in a condensing coil.

4.3 Glass-Wolves (quasi-canid automata)  
   Born when condenser-tree limbs shear, knot, and solder themselves under stray corona. Each wolf is a lattice of copper capillary and salt crust, driven by residual line pressure that puffs through slits like breath. They patrol cables for leaks, lapping any fresh condensate; leaks slow them, so an observant ranger tracks wolf speed to find faults.

4.4 Ember-Kites (avian, warm-blooded)  
   Wings translucent as mica; blood pigmented with bacteriorhodopsin, letting them photosynthesise mid-glide. They ride thermals above blast openings where buried lines vent. Pane-Marchers toss scraps of furnace slag skyward; kites catch the hot stones, perch atop them while they cool, and absorb stored heat through calloused feet.

4.5 The Stillborn Choir (acoustic anomaly)  
   Locations where echo persists even after the crystal plate that birthed it has shattered. Stand within and you hear footfalls made years earlier by someone now dead. The Choir drifts: chunks of remembered sound migrate uphill, as though frozen inside unseen bubbles.

5. Flora (loosely)  

• Threadmoss. A chrome-green filament that stitches salt plates together, welding cracks before wind can widen them. Every stitch stores a droplet of freshwater the plant cannot use, gifting travellers reservoir seams they can split with a knife point.  
• Mirror Lichen. Flat, iridescent colonies whose only purpose seems to redirect sun onto the condenser-trees, increasing yield at the risk of periodic tree-meltdown.  
• Glisten Root. Tubers grown for export to the Yard. When ground, they produce a silicate starch used by Shiftfolk to seal moss-felt into coat seams, keeping the ferment heat where it belongs.

6. Machinery and Relics  

• The Crystalline Forge. An open pit lined with parabolic mirrors that focus sun onto drifting frost. Specks of airborne ice anneal into spheres of perfect transparency, traded to the Yard’s Furnace-Monks, who seed them inside their boilers as meditation baubles.  
• Trackwind Tails. Wind turbines mounted on rail bogies; they chase prevailing gusts across the flats, spooling cable as they go. When the cable halls taut, the bogie brakes, and generators buried beneath convert tension into power fed back to the condenser grid.  
• The Quiet Vault. A sub-basement where Pane-Marchers store slabs too resonant for habitation. Inside, every spoken word dies an arm’s length from its source, swallowed by salt so acoustically pure it erases itself.

7. Interdependence with the Yard  

• Heat for Water: Without the valley’s surplus steam the Flats would crumble under their own brilliance; without Flatwater the Yard’s ferment coats would rot into stillness.  
• Echo for Quiet: Salt-damping panels line the Yard’s newborn dormitories, giving first-shift sleepers a silence purchased by the Flats’ nightly ringing.  
• Design Currency: Brine Scribes etch condenser revisions onto thin salt wafers and float them downstream. Scrimshankers prize these wafers; ground-heat drives the saline script to bloom into bas-relief, revealing flux points days before basalt rises.

8. Omens and Superstitions  

• If an Ember-Kite drops a slag stone intact, expect a steam-line rupture within two nights.  
• A pane that sings in three clear tones—high-mid-low—foretells a month without fog: disaster, because rime-fog is the only predictable drink.  
• To dream of wolves breathing ice is to be summoned as a Brine Scribe; resistance is impractical—the wolves will simply follow until you comply.

9. Daily Rhythm  

Dawn: Condenser-tree caretakers climb lattice ladders glazed with fresh hoarfrost, cracking spine-shaped ice to keep the vapour paths clear.  
Midday: Surface glare peaks; work halts; everyone buries themselves under shadow-skins stretched between stakes of glass-salt. The Flats hum, a million micro-fractures soldering in the heat.  
Dusk: Trade skids from the Yard arrive, wheels hissing on warm plates. Barter lasts until first frost; then the skids lift their pole-masts, unfurl mirror sails, and let starlight push them home.  
Night: Wolves test mainlines, Pane-Marchers harvest threadmoss seams, Brine Scribes lean over glaze-inked tablets listening to the hiss of distant boilers echoing up the pipes—proof that somewhere below, the Unfinished City is still turning.

10. Standing Commandments (parallel to the Yard’s three)  

1. Shout nothing you wish to keep.  
2. Leave one plate unbroken for the stranger behind.  
3. Heat rises, but water never walks: trade, or thirst.

At the moment before sunrise—when the flats shift from cold silver to rose, and the condenser-trees flex with their first warming breath—you can hear, if you kneel and press an ear to the crystals, the pulse of hammers striking far downstream. The Yard beats its morning tattoo; the Shatterflats reply with a tremor of settling salt. Two places, one breath: heat out, water back, silence swapped for echo, each remaking itself by the grace of the other’s surplus.
