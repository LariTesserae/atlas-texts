Ventifact Plateau  

1. Terrain and materials  
• Elevation and shape: A table-land sixty kilometres long, floating three thousand metres above the basin on a shelf of compressed, partially-solidified air. From below it looks like a cloud turned to stone; from above it is a lacuna in the sky, an island of ground hanging on nothing visible except the up-draft that forever climbs out of the basin’s cold maw.  
• Surface texture: Wind-planed obsidian shingles overlap like feathers, each no thicker than skin, each singing a separate note when the wind teeths across the edge. Between shingles, snow-fine grit of rhyolite drifts, so dry it moves more like smoke than sand.  
• Sub-structure: The plateau is hollow—an inverted labyrinth of ducts and flues that swallow the basin’s rising fog, bake it, fraction it, and exhale it again as super-arid wind. Along those ducts lie ribs of pumice shot through with thunder-slag pebbles the wolves once dropped. Their shouts are still trapped inside; step on the rib and the pebble coughs a half-remembered footstep back at you.  

2. Light and atmosphere  
• Illumination: Here the absent sun is a negotiable rumour no longer; cracks between shingles act as prismatic apertures, lensing what little starlight exists into tight, knife-bright beams that skate across the surface. Noon is a grid of slow lasers that wanders the ground, etching faint glassy furrows.  
• Sky: Directly overhead hangs a second sky—mirror-smooth, concave, and semi-transparent. It is not cloud and not glass but a distillation membrane woven from aerogel fibres grown outward by the plateau itself. Vapour from the basin threads upward, condenses on the membrane, flashes, and sheds in crystalline rain outward into the wider world.  
• Smell: No ozone here; instead a high, flinty odour, like sparks ground from iron on a whetstone. Your tongue dries the instant you open your mouth, as though the air bargained away all humidity in exchange for clarity.  

3. Soundscape (knife-edge, harmonic)  
Silence is possible but rare. More often the plateau hums with aeolian chords:  
• Wind scraping over obsidian sheets raises sustained, perfect fifths that beat softly against one another, migrating with the breeze.  
• Deeper, in the hollow ducts, evaporating fog slaps valves of pumice, producing a basso pulse exactly half the frequency of the basin’s subterranean rotor. The two places sing call-and-response through miles of intervening air.  
• Periodically, a trapped thunder-slag pebble cracks, venting a preserved shout that ricochets from shingle to shingle before being drunk in by the ducts.  

4. Vegetation (mineral-borne, wind-rooted)  
• Glass-Bastions: Hexagonal stalks of sodium-calcium silicate that sprout overnight where laser noon scores the ground. They are hollow, straw-thin, and grow always into the wind; break one and the inside is ribbed with nano-capillaries still sipping trace moisture from the up-draft.  
• Silicuff Lichens: Colonial films of silicon carbide plating the shadow-sides of pumice ribs. They are not alive in the carbon sense; they merely photocatalyse stray photons into orderly heat, which they leak in coded pulses that pass for metabolic chatter.  
• Dew-Mirrors: Concave, chrome-bright leaves stretched across triangular frames of aerogel. They are the plateau’s eyes, focusing down-coming starlight onto micro-zones where new Glass-Bastions germinate. When the wind slackens, the mirrors furl, becoming opaque petals.  

5. Fauna (wind-borne, edge-sharpened)  
• Rim-Skippers: Two-metre discs of flexible obsidian, ventral side ringed with thousands of millimetre-scale cilia. They skate the up-draft like hovercraft, filtering ex-basin vapour through micro-pores that extract rare isotopes. Their dorsal face is mirror-dark except for runes etched by aeolian lasers; no two discs bear the same script.  
• Shard-Kites: Needle-thin vertebrates, each bone a filament of fused quartz, musculature replaced by piezoelectric fibres. They ride the harmonic standing waves between shingle rows, diving to spear Glass-Bastion sap where trace hydrogen hides. The stab sounds like the ringing of a ceramic goblet.  
• Echo-Hawks: Cousins to the basin’s Echo-Wolves but wholly acoustic. They are shaped like birds only while in motion; decelerate an Echo-Hawk and it decompresses into pure tinnitus. They hunt Rim-Skippers, not for substance but for the sonic inscriptions on their backs—the only text the hawks can eat.  

6. Dynamics and cycles  
• Vapor Trade: Every hour a rip-tide of fog climbs from the basin, funnels through the plateau’s ducts, and departs as bone-dry, heat-laden air. In return, the plateau rains down aerogel fibres, snowing insulation that the basin later braids into Thread-Sirens’ nests.  
• Laser Noon Migration: Because distance is negotiable, the beams of refracted light do not sweep predictably; they warp, skip metres, or stall midair. Where they linger, Glass-Bastions overgrow, collapse, and glassify the ground into new shingles—a self-paving process.  
• Pebble Bursts: When enough thunder-slag gathers in a pumice rib, the weight of unspoken sound exceeds structural integrity. The rib shears off, falls from the plateau, and seeds the basin below with fresh, resonant stone cups ready to catch new condensation.  

7. Rules of the world (reality breakdown, 3/3, plateau variants)  
• Mass is conditional on movement. Anything utterly still becomes fractionally lighter each minute until it drifts. Thus, Rim-Skippers must forever glide or risk floating impotently into the mirror sky where nothing can bring them down.  
• Time here is abrasively linear: events cannot pause, only shear sideways. A spoken word might finish in the same moment it began but several metres to the east, leaving sentences striped across space like tape.  
• Heat stores memory. Touch a sun-etched shingle and yesterday’s wind pattern bleeds into your fingertips as phantom vibrations. Travellers have mapped whole weeks of weather this way, reading stone for echoes of temperature.  

8. Absence of built structures, presence of “refinery” processes  
No walls, no towers—yet the plateau is a dehydration plant operating on planetary scale. It bleeds the basin’s fog, condenses trace metals into writable glass, and distills airborne sound into lithographed runes. Where the basin is a cold foundry, the plateau is a dry press, stamping data into stone one laser noon at a time.  

9. How being here feels  
Your lips crack before you finish a breath; your shadow duplicates itself in the refracted noon, each copy delayed by half a heartbeat. Wind pressure shifts so quickly that knees wobble in sympathetic resonance with the ground. Every word you utter skids metres away, plastering itself against a shingle before the sound reaches anyone’s ear. The place is all edge—edge of gravity, edge of humidity, edge of song.  

10. The Common, Tangible Dwellers (brief notes)  
• Rim-Skippers: Moving archives; scholars from elsewhere sometimes climb the up-draft to harvest their runic backs, but doing so erases the disc, which disintegrates into glittering grit the moment a glyph is plucked.  
• Shard-Kites: Predatory scribes; what they drink becomes new script along their own glass spines, rewriting their flight algorithm with every meal.  
• Echo-Hawks: Memory censors; they target any sound that carries personal significance, shearing it from the sky. They leave behind knots of wind that fall as silent hail onto the basin, where Ore-Eaters savour them like peppercorns.  

11. The Plateau’s Great Intangibles  
• The Cantilever Pulse: A resonant heartbeat that throbs through the ducts exactly when the basin’s subterranean rotor pauses (every fifth revolution). In that instant the plateau dips several metres, as though hinged to the basin’s bedrock by an invisible beam.  
• The Glass Archivist: Not a creature but a stacking algorithm. Over centuries the obsidian shingles re-sort themselves, aligning according to a grammar only basin fog carries. Travellers who learn the characters can read centuries of meteorology and, allegedly, hints of the plateau’s eventual collapse schedule.  

12. Vectors of Connection to the Basin  
• Up-draft Conveyor: What evaporates in the basin arrives here, stripped of heat, and is returned as articulate wind that sculpts basin fog into Frost-Brinicles.  
• Thunder-Slag Trade: The plateau inherits the wolves’ discarded pebbles, fractures them, and rains the shards back down as seeding gravel for new echo eggs.  
• Harmonic Lock: The rotor’s 22 Hz undertone matches the plateau’s duct pulse on alternate cycles; during lock, distance shortens, and a person can step from basin shore to plateau rim in a single stride—provided they time the beat.  

13. Sentience, If Any  
Where the basin is orchestral, the plateau is engraver and editor. Shingle, wind, and laser compile the fog’s raw notation into legible score. No mouth here, but an eye: the mirrored membrane overhead dilates and contracts with auroral flux, scanning the world below. Should it ever focus fully, the plateau would probably read the travellers themselves, glassing their story into a single, perfect rune before letting the wind file them between shingles.  

14. How A Human Might Interact  
• Stand motionless and feel yourself lighten; jump too late and never land.  
• Carve a message into a Glass-Bastion stalk and an Echo-Hawk will remove all trace of the words—including from your memory.  
• Catch a Rim-Skipper underfoot and you can ride it clear across the plateau, but you must keep reading its glyphs aloud; the moment you skip a line the disk forgets how to hold mass and turns to dust beneath you.  
• Shout into a pumice rib and the thunder-slag within will hatch hours later in the basin as a newborn Echo-Wolf already fluent in your accent.  

Ventifact Plateau and the basin are twin halves of a single circulatory loop: cold inhale below, dry exhale above; condensation writing the draft, dehydration editing the proof. One grinds matter until even sound gains weight; the other pares that weight away until only meaning remains etched in glass. And between them, on the wind-stair linking fog to sky, stories commute—sometimes as thunder, sometimes as light, always as work unfinished.
