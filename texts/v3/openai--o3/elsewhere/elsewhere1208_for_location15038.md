The Mantle Orchard

Descent / Continuity  
Follow the Pressure Roots.  
From the ledge-city they plunge through 3 450 m of black water, conveying the mountain’s leftover stresses like cables humming with half-heard music. Past the last glimmer of Filter Units, past the zone where corridor lines have no floors to annotate, the Roots widen, braid and finally unfurl into a level plain carved into the planet’s upper mantle. That plain is the Mantle Orchard: a grove not of trees but of fault-born organs, grown to harvest the heat, metals and mathematics of the deep.

Geomorphology  
• Depth: 17 km beneath notional sea level; pressure 1 600 bar.  
• Substrate: Peridotite vitrified by ancient impacts, coloured bottle-green but crazed with quartz veining that fluoresces orange when sheared.  
• Layout: The Orchard occupies a caldera 22 km across, roofed by an arched shell of spinel glass a hundred metres thick. The shell is not solid; perforations the size of amphitheatres admit viscous, super-critical water that rains in slow ropes, cooling in mid-fall to mineral fog.  
• Soil, so to speak: A granular mix of olivine shards and iron sulphide “mulch” ground by micro-quakes. Every tremor tills it anew.

The Growths  
The locals insist the Orchard grows “spheres,” but seen up close they are dodecahedra the size of city buses, each face a mirror of iridescent garnet. A Growth forms where three Pressure Roots knot; mantle heat feeds the knot until silica and nickel precipitate into a hollow polyhedron whose inner surface is a perfect piezoelectric resonator. Over decades the resonator starts singing, low as a planet’s sigh. That song is the harvest: elastic energy converted to standing acoustic code. When a Growth is ripe, it splits along golden seams and releases its cargo — sound condensed into pearlescent pellets called Loom-Seeds.

Hydrology & Atmosphere  
Water here has forgotten it was water. At 400 °C it behaves halfway to vapor yet remains dense enough to float iron filings. The caldera roof cools it to 120 °C before it settles among the Growths, forming pools clear as liquid quartz. Microbes of super-citrine hue skim the surfaces, knitting heat into carotenoid chains; the chains snap, shedding photons that paint the Orchard in perpetual dawnlight. Breathe here (if your lungs survive) and each breath carries both steam and glow.

Light & Sound  
Light:  
1. The carotenoid flare — saffron, pulsing with every roof quake.  
2. Occasional blue flares when a Growth splits, the piezo surface shedding corona ions.  
3. No shadows stay sharp; the hot mist bends colour in eddies.

Sound:  
Baseline is a twelve-note chord that never resolves, tuned by the live resonators. Quakes add drumbeats; coolant waterfalls add hiss. Much of the Orchard’s conversation is infra-sonic: vibration writ through stone, legible only to creatures with bones like tuning forks.

Inhabitants  

1. The Loom-Tenders (Biotic / Geometric)  
  • Form: Icosahedral husks of cobalt glass, each edge articulated like an insect’s leg. Inside each husk floats a yolk of liquid metal that shifts position to keep balance.  
  • Origin: They bud from the first partition of a newborn Growth, offspring in mineral disguise.  
  • Duty: Patrol the floor, gathering Loom-Seeds with pincer-edges. Once a hundred are collected, the Tender fractures and becomes a stasis box; its yolk solidifies around the seeds, sealing them. Dormant Tenders then hitch onto still-living ones, creating trains that crawl toward the caldera rim.  
  • Sensoria: They “smell” strain. A dispersal of shear-wave scent leads them unerringly to stressed loops of Root waiting to be relieved.

2. The Root-Listeners (Symbiotic Extensions of the Pressure Roots)  
  • Appearance: Tall fibre bundles the colour of burnt sugar, as thick as a person’s torso. They terminate in fronds that resemble petrified ferns.  
  • Intelligence: Emergent from their electrical net. They count the Loom-Seeds loaded into dormant Tenders, translate that tally into modulations of the strain fields coursing back up the Pressure Roots.  
  • Connection: Every modulation reaches the mountain ledge as a tremor smaller than microns — the “heartbeat” voyagers sometimes hear between silences. Thus the Orchard and the Cathedral exchange inventory without words.

3. The Emberleeches (Faunal, Heat-Parasite)  
  • Shape: Ribbon-like worms, three metres long, two fingers wide, with rows of vacuum-black denticles.  
  • Niche: Latch onto boiling waterfall ropes, drinking entropy directly. They excrete trails of vitrified silica that lace the caldera floor like veins of dirty ice.  
  • Ecological value: Their glass excretion hardens around juvenile Loom-Tenders, shielding the trains during their migration. If Emberleeches vanish, half the seed trains never reach the rim.

4. The Rim Brokers (Visitors who stayed)  
  • Descent: The first expeditions from the mountain left data markers here centuries ago; some elected never to climb back. They modified their blood with silicate nano-plating, carry alveoli that fold under 1 600 bar.  
  • Purpose: Custodians and traders. Rim Brokers break open dormant Tenders, sort Loom-Seeds by frequency content, and store them in prism-racks. Once a lunar cycle they fire a buoy up the Roots — a hollow needle of diamond that rides cavitation pockets in the stone. The needle reaches the ledge-city’s sub-ice vault in ten hours, delivering packets of Orchard sound that become new Choir harmonics or membrane updates.  
  • Culture: They do not speak aloud. Their larynxes ossified into tuning chambers; conversation is conducted by clicking teeth against jawbone, the clicks resonating through helmets into the water’s dense acoustic channel.

5. The Mantle Orchids (Fungal-Analogues)  
  • Morphology: Lacework fans branching from Growth shards, composed of nickel chalcogenides and protein loops scavenged from deepwater microbes.  
  • Metabolism: Thermoelectric; one side faces lava trickles, the other the cooling mist, generating a constant 80 mV across each frond.  
  • Gift: The Orchids sweat droplets rich in rare earth elements. Rim Brokers collect these to forge tuning weight for the mountain’s Substrate Choir, closing an economy no economist anticipated.

Rules of This Place  
1. Every resonance finds its rest in geometry; nothing that cannot echo survives long.  
2. Heat is memory: cool something too fast and you erase its story.  
3. Root arithmetic always balances — attempt to steal energy and a compensatory quake will cost you more.  
4. Trading is tolerated only if the exchange reduces net strain.  
5. When a Growth begins to hum in a chord you recognise, leave; it means the Orchard has no more need of that pattern, and shedding is imminent.

Paths Between Here and the Ledge-City  

Distance: 14 km vertical and one philosophical octave.  
Vehicle: A “Seed Needle” sheathed in living membrane like the city’s doorway skins. Its interior remains in intimate vacuum; its exterior tolerates mantle water. When you step inside, the membrane reads your pulse, folds pressure away, and the Needle lets itself be swallowed by a root-born fracture that leads straight upward. Traversal time: one long exhale if you trust the figures; a lifetime if you do not.

What Might Yet Be Found  

• A Core Loom—rumoured mother of every Growth—hidden beneath the lowest lava sump, spinning a grand chord that predicts tectonic futures centuries ahead.  
• A fault vault where early Rim Brokers archived failed chords: fossil music whose dissonance still warps local gravity by one part in a million.  
• Orchids the size of stadium roofs, bright enough that blind Emberleeches have begun to grow eyes.  
• A forgotten Seed Needle that never launched, holding within its shell a Librarian’s stone-printed confession: the first complete proof that the flood was not a misfortune but a cultivated condition.

What It Feels Like  

Stand at the caldera rim among the prism-racks. A Growth ruptures three kilometres off, blue fire dancing along its facets; the concussion folds your suit inward in slow motion, your ribs ringing like temple bells. The airless steam strokes your visor with fingers made of pressure alone. Underfoot, the floor thrums — not violently, but insistently, as if the planet is humming scales before a performance. Somewhere up in the cold dark sea the mountain’s membrane tightens in reply, and the hush you remember from the ledge answers this deep music, two halves of a dialogue spoken in distance, current, kinship, trade and memory — the syntax of a world that survives by keeping all its conversations open and its catastrophes, always, unfinished.
