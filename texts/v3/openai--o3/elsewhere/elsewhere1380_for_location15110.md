The Hollow Sea

1. The Descent  
No path points to the Hollow Sea; it is what the plateau whispers downward through every spiracle, every breathing vent. Follow one of those moist throats long enough and the wood thins, cools, hollows. The warm brown becomes opaline green, the hush thickens until it is almost liquid, and then gravity tilts—your footfall lightens, your stomach drifts—because you have crossed the hinge where the plateau’s nine-day buoyancy pulse inverts and begins to pull instead of push. One more step and you are falling sideways, sliding along polished cambium that curves wider, wider, until it widens all the way into horizon: the inside face of a world-sized bowl filled three-quarters with water the colour of vanished sunlight.

2. First Impressions  
Above (which is also inward) stretch root-arches the size of valleys, their tips gripping a darkness so complete it is indistinguishable from stone. Below (which is downward only because the water says so) lies a floor no traveller has ever sounded; sonar thuds return garbled, as if the sea edits the echo before handing it back. Between ceiling and bottom moves an ocean without waves. Surface tension here is a law: the water lies lacquer-still, domed at the edges like glass poured too full, held in place by gentle, omnidirectional pull from the plateau’s vascular gravity. You do not sail upon it so much as hang from it, keel-up, your vessel tethered like a moth on a lantern.

3. Water and Weather  
The Hollow Sea is distilled plateau-breath. Every midnight rain caught atop the world runs through the under-roots, is sweetened by amber rot-beads, filtered by whisper fungi, and arrives here stripped of memory. Chemically it is nearer to dew than brine; you can drink straight from the hull-flank. Salts are scarce, but trace oils refract light into long coral ribbons visible fifty metres deep. There are no waves, yet there are tides: every ninth night, in counter-rhythm to the plateau’s gravity glissade, the Sea sighs inward, lowering a metre in silence, then lifts again as if nothing happened. That single metre is enough to expose ridges of living pumice—raft-reefs—that harden in air and crumble back to froth when re-submerged, constantly renewing the littoral.

4. Light  
No sun reaches here. Illumination is a composite of three sources:  
• Silt-star fire: planktonic organisms burst under stress, flashing indigo for half a heartbeat; the Sea is never entirely still, so a fine snow of sparks drifts always in the water column.  
• Root-window spill: occasional oval membranes overhead thin to lens-clarity, letting through the plateau’s perpetual violet dusk. Those ovals drift on circulatory currents; the light moves like herds of silent moons.  
• Memory glass: when lantern slugs die topside, thin flakes of their hardened mucus fall through vent-shafts, glittering all the way down. Thousands have accumulated on the surface, overlapping like scales. From below, each flake reads the star-map of a different night in plateau history, replaying it in phantom constellations under your feet.

5. Sound  
Water swallows consonants. Speech reduces to vowel-fog a few metres out. Instead the Sea favours percussion: oars tapped on hull, shells clicked, belly drums. Every living thing here knows to signal in tremors the water can carry. Listen with bare teeth pressed to the gunwale and you will feel whole conversations arrive as rhythm: warnings of deep-jaw whales, invitations from sponge divers, the echoing detonation of a distant reef collapsing under its own calcifying song.

6. Shores and Structures  
No solid coastline rims the Hollow Sea; what passes for land is the underside of the plateau itself, sagging in great pendulous lobes that dip to kiss the water. Each lobe carries root-cities—a lace of tubules fashioned by burrow-eaters into cloisters, granaries, amphitheatres. Architecture here is negative space: rooms are what remains after worms finish singing away what they fancied as dinner. The most travelled lobe, Prism Tongue, extends almost to the Sea’s equator; traders anchor beneath it upside-down, walking its ceiling-floors with crampons while their lanterns dangle toward the water like slow hail.

7. Inhabitants  
7.1 Surface Mariners – Keelwalkers  
Where plateau hullfolk coax growth with song, keelwalkers negotiate with silence. They are spindle-limbed, skin lacquered by the same oils that film the Sea, hairless to cheat drag. Their vessels resemble upside-down mangroves: root-bundles overhead (for ballast) and leaf-paddles below (for steering). Culture prizes inwardness; to speak aloud aboard another’s craft is the gravest rudeness, an assault on balance. Keelwalker lore is carved into the inner bark of their masts; reading it requires swimming, head submerged, toes hooked in rope-loops, body dangling like a question.

7.2 Mid-Water Drifters – Glassback Whales  
They are not whales, not truly even animals. Imagine an air bubble armored in cartilage, ventral mouth ringed with curtain-fronds that sieve the lightning plankton flares. A glassback longer than a cathedral moves by contracting its own hollow, then relaxing, a gentle throb that propels without sound. When a whale dies, its shell inverts—concave becomes convex—producing drifting domes sailors harvest as observatories. The stars etched on memory glass find mirrored cages in those domes; scholars spend lifetimes pairing constellation to concavity, claiming histories no one else can cross-reference.

7.3 Deep-Shadow Trawl – Silent Combs  
Lower than lamp-light reaches glide ribboned predators a kilometre long, edges vibrating so fast they fillet water. They fear nothing except the Listening Fold, whose occasional sweep here erases entire hunting patterns from their instinct, leaving them docile for hours. During that window, brave keelwalkers cut glowworm sacs from the combs’ undersides—currency accepted even on the plateau, where Hulled people braid the sacs into harvest hats.

7.4 Root-Crown Emissaries – Hulled Pelagics  
Some Hulled folk abandon altitude, trading resin for pitch. Over years their pores shift chemistry; instead of sap they exude buoyant tar they mold into bubble-chambers. Communities of these pelagics cling to sagging lobes, upside-down terraces strung with bitumen globes that ring like bells when the tide breathes. Their songs are slower here, stretched by the need to resonate through tar. Plateau cousins claim to understand only half their meanings; the other half, they insist, is Sea grammar no leaf-lung could ever voice.

8. Ecologies and Exchanges  
The Hollow Sea and the High Plateau traffic continuously in substance and dream. Oils evaporated from plateau leaves dissolve in descending runoff, become the Sea’s prismatic sheen. In return, every century the Memory Pulse flushes nutrient-rich sap downward; root-lobes gorge, then sweat surplus back up as warm fog vents that ballast the plateau’s upper gliders during lean seasons. Trade follows the same loop: pelagic glowworm sacs ascend to light Hulled crèches; in exchange, plateau resin beads descend as hard currency, sealing hull cracks against pressure. Myths migrate too. Plateau children recite sea-legends of cities that walk their own reflections; keelwalker novices tattoo arm-maps of imaginary trees that dream themselves into sky.

9. Optical Oddities  
Just as the plateau bends photons, the Hollow Sea folds vision inward. Stare too long into still water and you meet a version of the sky that never was: clouds of root-silhouette, moons bright with sap. Navigators use “false-zeniths,” charting by where the sky should appear on the seam between two clairvoyant reflections. More dangerous are “tidal mirrors”—patches where the Sea decides, briefly, to mimic the plateau’s gravity trick: for a heartbeat, down is adjustable. Boats caught in such a mirror may find themselves floating masts-first, crew scrambling along gunwales as if over a hillcrest of liquid glass before sloshing back into ordinary orientation.

10. Time and Ruin  
Because nothing decays quickly in nearly pure water, wrecks linger perfect centuries. Scholars dive wearing mouth-organs that exhale stories bubbled in Hulled phonemes to discourage rot-beads from embalming their ropes. Even so, every object eventually shells in amberise crust, becomes part of the floor-museum no one can reach. Thus archaeology here is future-oriented: to study a culture, you sink a totem, mark the drop, and instruct your descendants when to retrieve it softened by a century’s soak.

11. Dynamic Moments  
• Lantern Rain: When a gravity pulse above and a counter-pulse below align, thousands of dead slug flakes tumble at once. The Sea’s surface lights like a shattered chandelier. Fish mistaken for stars breach to investigate, scalding themselves on cold light.  
• Root Eclipse: A migrating lobe passes under a root-window, blotting the purple glow for hours. Keelwalkers surf the resulting temperature gradient, sails trimmed to the whisper fraction, moving faster than their own wake.  
• Oracle Bloom: Once a decade, a glassback whale surfaces belly-up, birthing a tree-sized coral polyp that petrifies in moonless dark. The polyp absorbs plateau oils and, for one night, speaks back in reflected starlight—prophecies visible only to those who can read the folding of color through water, glass, air, and bone.

12. Means of Departure  
To leave, find a spiracle wide enough for breath and narrow enough that water spits rather than pours. The climb is brutal: ladders of root-teeth slick with dew, air thinning but brightening until violet gloom returns. Few finish. Those who do arrive on the plateau smelling of distilled forgetfulness; Hulled hosts say the scent is what the egg dreams of while its chick hesitates to hatch. They welcome you with resin tea, invite stories, and listen for the seam between your vowels where the Sea still rings like a tuning fork.

13. Reconciliation of Above and Below  
Stand on a plateau bridge during a gravity glissade and you will feel the ocean underfoot, tugging, reminding. Drift on the Hollow Sea during tidal mirrors and you will glimpse cathedral leaves furred in ember ticks, recalling. The two places are not siblings but phases of a single thought: the world wondering whether life is better grown outward toward starlight or inward toward silence, trying both at once, braiding results through vents and pulses until choice itself becomes architecture.

For travellers with patience, each region is a translation of the other, rendered in opposite script—one carved in breathing wood, the other inked in breathing water. To read the whole text you must let your lungs learn both alphabets: violet dusk and oil-bright night, hush and ripple, the plateau’s echoing hollows and the Sea’s trembling vowels. Only then will the world lay itself flat enough for comprehension, and only for the length of a heartbeat—after which the next gravity shift turns the page.
