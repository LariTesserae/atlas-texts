The Kettleglass Array

TERRAIN  
Imagine a crescent of wind-flayed basalt perched six hundred metres above the river valley that cradles Plot 17-B.  The plateau is pocked with shallow craters—“kettles”—where ancient steam vents once sighed.  Around each crater the rock blooms into ragged petals of pale-green volcanic glass that chime under a boot-toe.  With nothing taller than knee-high scrub to break its fetch, the wind arrives like an ocean in the sky, sanding every exposed surface to a dull, uneasy polish.

GEOLOGY & LIGHT  
The glass is anorthic fumarole quartz: softer than obsidian, eager to soften again when coaxed.  A century ago travelling smelters carted up home-made mirrors, pointed them at a supposedly dead kettle, and discovered the vent still breathed—slow, ninety-degree vapour.  Mirror heat plus vent breath proved alchemy enough.  Today seven hundred twenty-one heliostats orbit three fusion wells, melting scrap iron, slag and broken keels into milky ingots that cool in the kettles.  Heat, not metal, is the Array’s true harvest.

BUILT FORM  
Anchors crack in the nightly freeze, so nothing rests on the ground.  Chrome-nickel cables, braided four thumbs thick, sling between the tallest glass ribs.  From the web hang dorm pods, tool nests and the circular rail on which every heliostat skates.  In full noon glare the whole works looks like a spider’s web spun from light itself, flickering as mirrors pivot to follow the sun.

ATMOSPHERE  
Wind shears the plateau day and night.  Dust skims the air in sheets too thin to see, but you feel them abrade your teeth.  Sunlight drops as a white anvil, unforgiving, and vanishes the instant the horizon bites it; the temperature follows, plummeting forty degrees in half an hour.  Inside the swaying hab modules the light refracts through water-jacketed glass floors until it turns the colour of warm honey.

SOUNDS  
• A stadium’s worth of pocket-watches: that is the sound of heliostat gears ratcheting 3.6 ° every fifty seconds.  
• Wet thunder from below when a vent exhales.  
• At night, mirror petals rattle in their frames, practising scales in their sleep.

THERMAL CURRENCY  
Water is scarce; heat is coin.  Melt-credits—ceramic discs stamped with the kilowatt-hours they represent—buy everything from cable splice time to a bowl of stew.  The credits travel down-valley on kite-freighters, returning stuffed in crates of river water and bean paste.  Those same freighters haul 40-gallon drums of Green Yard ethanol upslope; the fuel is reserved for polishing rites and feast-fires, never burnt for crude heat.

FLORA  
Only the coil-vine thrives, leafless and ghost-pale.  It photosynthesises in ultraviolet bands and exudes a viscous gel—diluted with ethanol, it becomes the flux that fixes new mirror flakes.  Every dawn a vinegar-sweet tang drifts from the flux vats, a smell the Array folk call “the sun’s own sweat.”

FAUNA  
Sky-mice (wing-skinned rodents) nest on the cable web, lining burrows with mirror dust that keeps their pups warm after sunset.  
Vent-swifts ride the thermal columns from fusion wells, feathers smelling perpetually of ozone.  
Glass crabs—blind, translucent arthropods—graze on cooling ingots, their silica-rich droppings etching fracture warnings that technicians read like tide lines.

INHABITANTS  
Permanent head-count: about one hundred eighty.  They call themselves pulse-keepers too, but here the pulse is light, not sap.

Trades  
• Petal-Handlers exchange cable splices for new mirror shards, moving with suction gloves and rope saddles redesigned every solstice for stretch.  
• Flux-Brewers balance coil-vine gel with ethanol from the river marsh; their vats are cooled by a repurposed triple-expander pump still stamped with the Green Yard spiral.  
• Shadow-Surveyors—pairs of children—chalk the racing edge of heliostat shade so algorithms can gauge wind bias.  
• Glass-Monks wear opaque cowls and will not let a misplaced shadow cross a working beam.

Dress  
Ash-oiled cotton, mirror-dust veils, gloves with silvered backs for silent flash-language.

DIET  
Breakfast: thin bread crisped on fusion-well rims.  
Shift meal: cooled ingot water with pickled sky-mouse jerky.  
Feast day: when three cloudless weeks accrue, one heliostat is reversed to glare-cook a stew in a bronze cauldron shipped up from Plot 17-B long ago.

RITUAL & LORE  
• Mirror-Molt: on every new moon the Array goes dark for fifteen heart-beats while spent petals are dropped to shatter on basalt—sloughing ill-luck.  
• Ocular Tax: newcomers surrender one reflective trinket; the sun brooks no rivals.  
• Folk talk of “green pulse nights” when barrels of sap-oil arrive and the ethanol glows teal at dusk, a rumour the children swear true.

RULES  
1. Angle is fate.  A mis-timed mirror flash-blinds anyone on the cables; penance is to polish shards until you can see the veins in your own eyes.  
2. Added weight must be earned; to hang something new, something else is cut loose.  
3. Glass remembers light: an ingot over-fired will forever refract the spectrum it first swallowed and is tagged for lighthouse duty downriver.

SENSING THE PLACE  
Close your eyes on a swaying catwalk: one cheek bakes, the other freezes as mirrors track.  Open them and the horizon quivers in heat-shimmer.  Far below, just at dusk, you can make out the banyan canopy—darker than evening gold—breathing a mist that the wind sometimes carries here as a taste of sap on the tongue.

DYNAMIC CHARACTER  
Where the Green Yard is an old heart beating slow, the Array is adolescence embodied.  Vents once dormant flare awake without warning; cables sing new harmonics; designs iterate monthly, sometimes nightly.  Life assumes incompletion as default.

BOUNDARIES  
Beyond the sling-line every kilogram is unaccounted for; the plateau offers no covenant to catch you.  In that drop lies the whole world’s conversation: sap answering sun, light answering root, each trading what the other cannot make for itself.

LEGACIES & EXCHANGE  
Ingots leave slung under kite-wings; back come crates padded with braided aerial roots from Plot 17-B—unsurpassed at damping vibration in glass-cutting lathes.  No coin changes hands: only continuities.  A graftwright in the marsh and a petal-handler on the heights can both name the worker who shaped the opposite half of any jointed tool they hold.

DEPARTURE  
Visitors granted a single sunrise-to-sunset arc leave half sun-burnt, half wind-numb, ears ringing with clock-gear canons.  Something in them feels melted out and cooling into a shape they will not recognise until they dream under another sky.
