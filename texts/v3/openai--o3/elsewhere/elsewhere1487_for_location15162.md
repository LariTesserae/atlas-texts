The Canopy Looms

1. Way Up  
Follow any of the iron-sap pneumatic tubes that snake out of the grotto’s ceiling. Forty vertical metres above the forest floor they surface, gleaming with condensation, and converge on an old suspension bridge whose roadway is long gone. What remains are two parallel cables—each fat as a river barge—sheathed now in bark the colour of oxidised copper. The cables rise in a slow arc toward the skyline. Step onto the bark; it is ridged like staircase muscle and rides the wind with a faint elastic sway. Climb. Half an hour later the cables flatten into a platform, then blossom outward into lattice upon lattice until no ground at all is visible—only a silken plain rippling at cloud-root height. You have reached the Canopy Looms.

2. Ground, if such a thing exists  
• Decking: interwoven ribbons of leaf-glass and steel tendril, semi-transparent, springy, always warm where sunlight hits. From beneath you see the city ruins in green miniature and, now and then, a knot of vine-rats ascending on an updraft like brown confetti.  
• Superstructure: upright frames grown from lignified bamboo alloyed with nickel; they lean into one another in rows, forming bays the size of shipyards. Each frame carries tensioned shrouds of quartz-filament so fine they turn the air to a prism.  
• Anchorage: the entire mat is moored to eight surviving skyscraper husks by creeper-chains plated in chromium. When the wind gusts the Looms rise and fall several metres, breathing with the weather but never drifting.

3. Medium, climate, sky  
Here the air is thinner, sharper. Sunlight arrives white instead of the grotto’s algae green and scatters in every filament until the horizon itself looks woven. Temperature swings are violent: blistering at midday, brittle by moonrise. Moisture is gift and currency. Clouds tear themselves open on the framework, leaving rime along the upper spars that drips into cistern-bracts shaped like inverted bells. At dawn these bracts dump twelve seconds of cold rain straight through the looms and down the pneumatic tubes—breakfast for the Vaulted Root far below.

4. Fabrication layer  
Nothing here is idle; the Looms are a factory, a cloister, and a reef at once.  
• Shuttle-Beetles: fist-sized scarabs whose elytra end in spool-reels of liquid silica. They traverse the warp lines, extruding thread that mineralises on contact with air. When two beetles pass each other they clack mandibles in Morse: pattern codes inherited with their genes.  
• Draught-Mantas: kites of cartilage and vein, two metres tip-to-tip. They carry finished sails to the edge of the platform, release them into thermals, then ride shotgun until the cargo catches the jet stream toward distant biotic airships no human has yet mapped.  
• The Measure: a resident intelligence, neither insect nor plant but lattice itself. Strain gauges grown from doped cellulose count micrometres of flex in every spar; the numbers add, compare, average, decide. When tension is wrong The Measure hums a subsonic chord that knits the shuttle-beetles into emergency overdrive or orders vines to ratchet the mooring chains one link tighter.

5. Light economy  
Direct sun reigns, but it is bent to purpose. The woven sails in progress act as Fresnel lenses, focusing threads of brilliance onto seedbeds hung beneath the decking: micro-orchards of copper-leaf succulents and cobalt mosses whose pigments the grotto cannot manufacture. At twilight the sails shift to diffraction mode, fanning twilight into long, rose corridors where nocturnal species dare not cross—photonic fences with no walls.

6. Soundprint  
Wind, first and foremost: a throaty organ note achieved by kilometres of filament singing between frames.  
Under that, the click-click rattle of shuttle-beetles changing spools, the creak of bamboo-nickel knees with every temperature swing, and—rarer than thunder—the bass groan of a mooring chain as The Measure re-tensions the whole raft.  
Every ninety minutes the Looms receive a pulse from below: the Vaulted Root’s dynamo roaring just a little louder, amplified up the hollow trunk-tubes. The Looms answer by shifting weave rhythm, like two weavers tapping shuttle against loom so the other can hear they are still alive.

7. Life, here and transient  
a) Shuttle-Beetles  
• Anatomy: two thoraces fused end-to-end; one holds flight muscles, the other a silicate gland and diamond-toothed spinnerets.  
• Lifecycle: eggs are laid inside hollow bamboo; hatchlings spend a week eating the host plant’s silica reserves, leaving stems transparent as glass.  
• Covenant: they swear (in click code) to finish weaving any pattern begun; abandoned designs attract a punitive swarm.  

b) Draught-Mantas  
• Photosynthetic skin studded with pit organs that taste barometric pressure.  
• Social flight: always three—Scout, Port, Starboard—because a triangle best finds thermals.  
• Cargo: finished sails, salted insect casings for city-nesting gulls, and once a month a sealed canister from the grotto addressed simply TO WHOM IT MAY CONCERN.

c) Cloud-Lappers  
• Jellyfish-like colonies that dangle ice-harvesting tentacles. They cling to the Loom’s underside, drip processed water down the supply tubes, and distil nitrates as waste. The Vaulted Root prizes those nitrates; in return it feeds the colonies warm up-drafts flavoured with fermented spores.

d) Copper-Leaf Orchids  
• Grow only where lens-light converges. Petals conduct electricity; stamens end in capacitor sacs. At full bloom they discharge a visible spark that scares away Threading Serpents foolish enough to ride balloons this high.

8. A handful of rules  
• Density beats mass. Anything heavier than a mantis must be either hollow or tethered; drop a wrench and it will punch straight through the fabric, vanishing into cloud.  
• Patterns propagate. The Loom records every weave; begin sketching shapes in spilled tea and the shuttle-beetles will reproduce them at cathedral scale before dawn.  
• Tension is morality. Slack lines are lies, overtight are cruelty. The Measure despises both.  
• Payment is osmotic. Send water down, receive sap-borne metals up. The economy has no coins, only gradients.

9. If you linger  
Within ten minutes a shuttle-beetle will land on your shoulder, sampling skin oils for tensile properties. After half an hour The Measure will have mapped your skeletal ratios; expect a draught-manta to flirt overhead, gauging whether your silhouette could serve as template for a new sail-type. Sleep here and you will wake inside a softly vibrating hammock you do not remember climbing into—woven overnight to your respiratory cadence. Dream long enough and the Looms may memorise that rhythm too, braiding it into the next run of filament. Somewhere, months later, an airship you will never see will tremble with déjà vu whenever wind touches its rigging.

10. Departures, plural  
• Descent: find a bamboo stem ringed by cobalt moss. Slide. Capillary suction brakes the fall; sixty seconds later you spill into the grotto’s upper basins, dew-cooled and faintly luminous.  
• Drift: accept the invitation of a draught-manta. Grip the leading edge of a fresh sail, shrink inside the vertigo of sudden cloud, and spend three days riding jet stream highways older than maps.  
• Walk: follow the mooring chains into their host towers, then down rusted stairwells where leaf-glass graffiti recounts every weave pattern ever failed. The stairs end at street level in the skeleton of a bank. Its vault is intact and empty, except for a single fungus bulb that will not light unless you sing.

11. Lines of kinship to the Vaulted Root  
• Material: minerals the locomotives grind underground are piped up as sap rich in iron filaments; these filaments harden into the shuttle-beetles’ spinnerets.  
• Rhythm: the grotto’s polyrhythmic machinery provides the timing grid the Looms use to stagger weave phases; when the dynamo hiccups, unfinished sails unravel in sympathy.  
• Trade: every seven days the Looms lower a bundle of rejected glass-filament—too brittle for skywork but perfect for strengthening leaf-glass panels in the grotto ceiling.  
• Memory: the Vaulted Root hears footfalls; the Canopy Looms feel wind strain. Between pressure and tension the two places share one circulatory sentence: Keep Moving So We May Stay Whole.

12. Unverified whispers  
Gliders report a third enclave beyond the stratosphere, something called the Orbital Kiln where sails arrive to be fired into mirrors large enough to signal the night side of the planet. No beetle returns from so high, but sometimes the Looms find feathers of pure ceramic caught in the filaments, still warm, humming a chord no ear has yet translated—a tune perhaps meant for a banyan that swallowed a steel mill, and for the weaver-reef that grew toward light on the breath that mill once exhaled.
