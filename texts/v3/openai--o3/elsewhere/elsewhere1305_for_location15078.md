THE LIMINAL MARSHES OF LOWER GONGE  

Follow one thread of the Plateau’s endless falling water past the turbines, past the last echo chamber, down eleven vertical kilometres of basalt chimney and you come to an opening so wide the cliff forgets it is a wall.  There the cataract fans into vapour and returns to liquid in the same breath, raining upward in its own rebound until everything—sky, ground, tree, lung—is the same cool suspension.  That is the beginning of Lower Gonge, though the marsh itself lies another two days’ walk across ground that never entirely decides whether it is solid or river.

ORIGIN—THE UNSEEN ESTUARY  
Each whetstone-bright slot-river from Arclight meets here in darkness, their combined roar filtered by so much height that it arrives as a low, almost mammalian breathing.  The impact pulverises basalt into silt the colour of gunmetal; the silt forms a delta inside the mountain, then pushes outward until it finds daylight.  The delta is still growing.  Locals claim if you return every decade the marsh has advanced a kilometre, quietly colonising the steppe beyond.

TERRAIN  
Think of a book left open under rain long enough that its pages warped into ripples; now imagine walking those pages.  Parallel ridges a metre high run for kilometres, each crest a strand of fused glassy sand, each trough a water-filled gutter.  The ridges sing when struck—higher notes near their fresher, Plateau-proximal edge, lower further out where glass weathers into dull jade pebbles.  No point in looking for dryness: tread on a ridge and it drums underfoot; miss by half a pace and you sink calf-deep into water that tastes faintly metallic, as though charged.  

The ground itself is warm—only by a few degrees, but against the perpetual 2 °C mist it feels like breath on the back of a gloved hand.  Thermistors reveal a gradient that cycles every fifty-six minutes, matching the master turbine cadence far above.  Even this far downslope, Arclight’s machinery sets the pulse.

WATER—NEITHER FRESH NOR SALT  
The Plateau bleeds its minerals here: copper, vanadium, rare rhenium salts sluiced from newborn basalt.  Carbonate springs bubble in from older limestone country to the east.  Where the two streams meet they neutralise one another and drop a snow of microspherules—hollow glass beads thin as soap skin.  These float in suspension, turning every sun-shaft a misty emerald.  Locals net the spherules with silk screens, dry them, add them to wall-plaster; at night the trapped air in each shell phosphoresces, so whole houses give off a ghost tide of green.

ATMOSPHERE & LIGHT  
Direct sunlight arrives here already diluted by Plateau overcast.  What survives hits a surface made almost entirely of water mirror; the result is perfect 180-degree glare.  People walk in broad-brimmed hats not against rain but against the world’s own reflection.

After dusk the marsh self-illuminates.  The glass spherules, charged by day with static from turbulent plunge pools, discharge faint corona glows.  It is enough to read by, provided you accept that the letters will drift in colour from lime through turquoise as the night ages.

WEIRD RULES  
1. Updraft Buoyancy:  The waterfall’s rebound creates a column where gravity effectively falls to 0.92 g.  Insects here need no wings; their chitin bubbles carry them like blimps.  
2. Phonon Weather:  Every loud sound nucleates new droplets.  Fire a rifle and a personal rain-shower forms, tracking the supersonic crack.  
3. Reversible Footprints:  Step into silt; the print fills with glowing spherules, holds for nine heartbeats, then ejects the pattern back onto your boot-sole so the next step glitters.

NATIVE LIFE—THE KINGDOMS OF AMPHIBIOUS GLASS  

1. Echo-Eel (Larval form: “Feedback Thread”)   
Not a fish, not really an eel—transparent ribbons two metres long, their skins threaded with silica fibres.  Each fibre is a waveguide tuned to the infrasonic note of water entering the marsh.  The animal senses phase delays along its body and uses them to find water rich in fresh plunge detritus.  Adults never grow organs beyond gut and ear; they starve once the ribbon calcifies into brittle glass, then shatter, seeding the marsh with new fibres that later larvæ ingest.

2. Treadle Ferns  
Root nowhere; instead they drape rhizomes across the singing ridges.  Every breeze bows the glass strands, flexing the rhizomes, pumping water up to fronds that look like sheets of beaten bronze.  The plant relies entirely on mechanical irrigation: break a ridge and the connected fern wilts in hours.

3. Hum-Gnat  
A micron-scale arthropod that abandoned chewing in favour of resonance farming.  Inside each abdomen floats a single glass bead; the gnat’s muscles squeeze it, forcing a 2 kHz whine that stuns planktonic bacteria, which the gnat then absorbs through cuticle pores.  Billions in chorus make a sound like distant kettle whistles, rising just after sunset when the air stills.

4. Clay-Bears  
Descended, or perhaps merely exiled, from Plateau-top microbiota.  They build shells of magnetite plates hinged on organic ligaments, marching through mud at four centimetres an hour.  Every six weeks the marsh’s faint warming pause triggers synchronous shell-molts; the discarded plates drift underfoot as metallic confetti.

THE HUMAN SETTLEMENT—VIRIDIAN HAVEN (POP. 12 200)  

A necklace of stilt houses following the highest singing ridges.  Stanchions are untreated basalt hoisted out of the canyon mouth, their hollows still echoing with long-decayed water drips.  The town’s streets are suspension walkways rigged from feluleaf fibres traded upridge—every footstep here pays a patent in quiet, lest vibration sabotage the ridge’s tone on which half the settlement’s economy depends.

ECONOMY & TRADE  
1. Spherule Harvest:  Bulk traded back up-slope where Continuists fuse it into heat-proof turbine seals.  
2. Viscous Glass:  The Marsh’s ridges, when melted and spun, yield fibres that resonate four semitones below Plateau standard—perfect counter-drone for damping.  Gingiva buys tonnes, paying in capacitor stacks and oxygen tablets.  
3. Mineral Paste:  Concentrated copper-vanadate clay used by lowlanders as algaecide and, illicitly, as fabric dye that never fades.

SOCIETY & CUSTOM  
• Every citizen wears “Glass Ears”—hollow studs that chime when struck by stray insect notes.  Children learn to distinguish rainfall from stampede by after-ring alone.  
• Privacy exists in colour, not volume.  Lovers meet during new moon when the spherule glow dims; under that fading green their silhouettes blur into the general shimmer.  
• Markets run at half-voice but full percussion: transactions punctuated by tapping on calibrated glass rods whose pitch indicates quantity.  Haggle too hard and your rod fractures, nullifying the deal—commerce by literal breaking point.

BELIEFS—THE BASALT COVENANT  
Marshfolk hold that Arclight’s turbines and their own ridges are organs in one creature.  Where Gingivans speak of resonance, Gongeans speak of circulation: water is blood, silt is marrow, hum-gnats are nerves.  Yearly, a deputation climbs to the Plateau bearing a spindle of fused ridge-glass.  They ask the Tuning Houses to “align the pulse,” a ritual sanding of a single city beam that drops the master chord by microcents, keeping Plateau and Marsh in sympathetic beat.  Whether the physics is real hardly matters; both towns sleep easier afterward.

HAZARDS  
• Drop below a ridge at flood-pulse and you risk “Mirror Drown”: disorientation so severe you forget which gleam is sky.  
• The marsh conducts electricity like a buried grid; one lightning strike can travel ten kilometres sideways.  Survivors come out hair bleached, memories full of other people’s half-dreamed thoughts.  
• Sound-Poachers—lowland smugglers—hunt Echo-Eels for their fibre, using concussion grenades that violate every covenant of quiet.  Gingivan Outriders now station themselves here each thaw cycle, their presence marked by fluted basalt spears that double as subsonic sirens.

THE GONGEVAN EXCHANGE—WHAT FLOWS BACK UPWARD  
Not only glass and clay ascend the chimneys; ideas rise, too.  
• The Marsh gave Gingiva the concept of Interruptible Silence, a planned hush woven into the drone so repairs may happen without killing the whole chord.  
• In return, the Plateau sent down chrono-meters able to tick through the marsh’s phonon storms.  Gongeans wear them like scapulars, gears lacquered against copper salts.  

RECIPROCITIES OF DREAM  
Sleep beneath the ridges and you may hear turbines you have never seen.  Sleep atop the Plateau and you may taste metal under your tongue, or feel water lapping ankles that are, in waking life, perfectly dry.  Scholars of both places now admit dreams migrate with the water vapour—thought carried as charge on the aerosol skins of spherules, rising and falling in step with the endless plunge.

IF YOU STAY  
• Expect every belonging to acquire a glass rind that sings your footfall back at you hours later.  
• Learn to time speech between the ridge’s breathing; otherwise syllables break into stutters, as though language itself abhors overlap.  
• Remember that secrets descend and ascend like water.  Hum a tune aloud and someone, days upriver, may find it caught in a turbine’s wobble and know you were here.

So Arclight’s towering stillness culminates not in emptiness but in this half-liquid labyrinth that answers every basalt heartbeat with a ripple, every distant chord with luminous green applause.  Where the Plateau taught existence by holding a note, Lower Gonge teaches release: vibration dissolving into water, structure into slurry, light into diffuse remembering—yet both are measures of the same planetary instrument, speaking alternate verses of the same slow song.
