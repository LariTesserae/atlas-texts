ROOTFALL CANYONS  

APPROACH  
Stand on the Altimetric Canopy’s eastern rim at dawn and wait for the Lantern-spores to gutter out. When the turquoise haze clears you will see a single cataract of roots descending through the cloud-sea, thicker than a mountain, braided like black rope. It is called the Main Fall. Every other hour a gust pries loose a plate of choir-leaf or a skein of silver pelage from some unlucky Gleamer, and the debris rides that living waterfall downward. Follow it. A Stilt-Singer guide will tighten webbing around your torso, step off the rim, and let the tension in the root-mass lower the two of you as gently as a sigh. Three kilometres below, the air grows dense enough to taste; five kilometres and the roaring hush of the plateau fades, replaced by the slow pulse of subterranean wind. When your feet finally meet stone you are no longer in sunlight. You have entered the Rootfall Canyons, the shadow-country that catches all the Canopy sheds and dreams.  

TERRAIN  
Imagine a badlands cracked into thousand-metre trenches, then imagine those trenches thatched over with millions of descending roots the size of tree trunks. Erosion carved the first gullies; the Canopy’s hunger widened them, sending feelers after every trickle of moisture. Where two opposing canyon walls have grown together the roots splice, sealing a roof overhead and leaving a vaulted tunnel beneath. Elsewhere gaps remain, admitting columns of daylight that stand like white fire in the gloom. The stone is mauve basalt powdered by ages of drip-sap; touch it and you leave violet fingerprints on your skin. Floor strata alternate: belts of pulverised leaf-humus so light your boots sink, then sudden shelves of flowstone polished glassy by eel-spray carried down from above.  

HYDROLOGY  
All the water that retracts from the Canopy—condensate, storm runoff, the seep of breached cisterns—must descend. It does so as mist that pearls on pendant root-tips, falls as rain inside the Canyons, then gathers in linear pools called vein-lakes. A vein-lake might be two metres wide yet twenty kilometres long, a ribbon of black mirror winding the canyon floor. Their surfaces lie so still that skylight vents appear as moving constellations when wind rakes the root roof. Where humus is thick the water ferments, becoming sour, effervescent and faintly luminous; travellers sip it for courage and pay later with hallucinatory dreams of being threaded through with green light.  

VEGETATION  
The Botanic Argument that rages above continues here at different tempo—are these roots offshoots of one mind or probiotic tendrils of many? Whatever their origin, Rootfall flora diverges from its parent stock.  

1. Osmotic Bells: swollen termini of strut-vine roots that dangle like glass gourds. Their skins are semipermeable, concentrating salts until the internal pressure detonates them in a muffled pop that echoes for days. The shards are sweeter than cane.  
2. Night-Tongues: flat, palate-pink fronds that unfurl against stone, licking minerals. At dusk they retract, leaving smear-trails of acid that etch petroglyphs no hand devised.  
3. Candle Mould: a mycelium that colonises abandoned roots, extruding waxy stalagmites tipped with cold-light fluorescence. Stilt-Singers harvest the stalks, braid them into wrist-torches that burn without flame—priceless on the oxygen-poor plateau above.  

SOUND  
The Canyons inherit the Canopy’s voice but modulate it. Thunder-wingbeats of Kestrel War-Weather arrive as doppler heartbeats, slowed and thickened. Skran chimneys up on the rim vent infrasonic moans that the canyon rock translates into a bass line you feel in molars. Every twenty-six hours the Elder Chorus drops a load-bearing cadence—ROOT-BASS, locals call it—which shudders down through the pillars and makes the humus jump. To sleep here is to dream in frequency.  

FAUNA  
• ABYSSAL GLEAMERS: born topside, they fall or climb down while young and never return. Away from full sunlight the mirrors in their fur tarnish to gunmetal, but their antlers grow crystal prisms that focus the shy daylight into weaponised lances.  
• FALL-KITES: blind chiropterans whose wings are transparent. They hunt by mapping echoes off the root-mesh ceiling, diving through lattice-gaps no wider than a handspan. Stilt-Singer children trade them whistles for amusement; the kites return the favour by eradicating stinging midges.  
• RIFT-GNOMONS: stationary predators that look like basalt hoodoos. In truth they are hematite-shelled sea urchins stranded by tectonic uplift. Their spines pivot to face any heartbeat within ten metres, drinking the animal’s magnetic field; linger and the iron in your blood will shiver itself loose.  

PEOPLE  
Two kinds make long residence here.  

A. ROOT-ASCETICS (Stilt-Singer penitent orders)  
They shave their tibial joints so the triple legs lock straight, adopting a stilting posture even on level stone. By night they hang upside-down among osmotic bells, believing the drip-talk of the Elder Chorus is purer where the over-song dies. They trade candle mould for obsidian coins, which they stack into resonant cairns calibrated to sing the hours until each ascetic’s death.  

B. EELBREAKERS (ground-born offshoot of Humblers)  
Too heavy to glide, these apes have become spelunking quadrupeds. Their forearms secrete a resin that hardens into pitons on demand. They roam vein-lakes in packs, harpooning Breach Eels that migrate down-canyon to spawn. The cured pink leather fetches high value topside; in return the Eelbreakers receive wind-knives and salt-cages—technologies useless underground but prized as status objects.  

ATMOSPHERE & LIGHT  
Rootfall air is thick, damp and unsteady. Oxygen descends from the plateau in fits, carried by convective pulses that can snuff a torch or ignite an ecstatic blaze of candle mould. Light stratifies: pitch dark at knee height, bruised twilight at head height, and, up among the root-baffles, daylight crucibles—windows through which you can see the Canopy’s underbelly glowing green-gold. Once or twice a year those windows align and a beam drills all the way to a vein-lake, causing a bloom of surface algae so dense it forms a transient, walkable film. Pilgrims time their journeys to cross those emerald bridges.  

BUILT TRACE  
• DRUM-STEPS: shallow basins cut into flowstone shelves. Step in rhythm and the basin amplifies your footfall into seismic signals intelligible to the plateau. Stumble and an Abyssal Gleamer may stampede, mistaking you for thunder.  
• THREAD-SPIRES: towers of fused root left when two canyon walls drift apart. Each spire is wrapped in a staircase of woven eelhide. At the top, hermit scribes scratch sequences of Night-Tongue acid into the living wood, scripting tomorrow’s growth patterns.  
• THE CACHE OF UNANSWERED SONGS: a cavern where broken strut-vine cores are piled like organ pipes. Every visitor may add one unanswered question by carving it into a pipe, then striking it once. The resonance joins a polyphonic storm that no ear can parse. Legend says when the Elder Chorus finally comprehends, the roof will crack and the plateau will “stand up,” freeing the Canyons to see starlight for the first time.  

RULES OF THIS PLACE  
1. Gravity Tilts: The stone remembers the plateau’s lift. In narrow transects plumb bobs swing three degrees off true; water in vein-lakes creeps up canyon walls, held by unknown tension.  
2. Flame Survives: Unlike topside, here the air will feed fire. But ignite a blaze near living root and the Elder Chorus will close drip-valves, suffocating both flame and traveller.  
3. Echoes Own Names: A shouted word returns altered; repeat it and the change locks in. Canyon guides advise newcomers to speak sparsely, lest their names rewrite themselves before journey’s end.  

DYNAMICS  
Weather filters downward. When War-Weather flocks shear the Canopy the shockwave dives into Rootfall as concussion fog: a pressure front that scatters Fall-Kites and flattens humus dunes into ripple stone. Conversely, when canyon humidity spikes, vapor columns rise and condense against the plateau’s underside, feeding lantern-spore eruptions hours later. In this way the two realms trade breath, each inhaling what the other exhales.  

WEIRDNESS—A LITTLE MORE THAN A TINGE  
At the confluence called The Gulleted Mile, three vein-lakes intersect beneath a skylight. Once per decade the pooled reflections align and conjure a perfect mirror image of the Altimetric Canopy hanging beneath the water, lantern-spores and all. Anything gazing into that water sees itself floating between twin worlds. Elders swear that, on the last alignment, a mirror-Stilt-Singer climbed out of the reflection, looked around in horror, then dissolved into blue mist—the very hue of the Rim Ghosts reported topside.  

CONNECTIONS TO THE ALTIMETRIC CANOPY  
• Trade: Candle mould and hematite gnomon spines ascend by vine-bucket; in return come leaf-sugar bricks that burn smokeless, prized for lighting echo-work chambers.  
• Kinship: Every root trembling overhead is pulse-cousin to some choir-leaf above. When the Elder Chorus shifts growth patterns, both realms feel the change in the same heartbeat.  
• Memory Flow: Powdered basalt drifts down-canyon, gathers in pocket dunes. Stilt-Singers kneel there to read striations of mauve and green, which archive storm histories the way trees carry rings. Thus the Canopy’s past is legible only in the place that catches its fall.  

HOW IT FEELS TO BE THERE  
Smell: coppery sap, wet mineral, the ozone tang of osmotic detonations.  
Taste: lick condensate and you get iron, leaf-acid, and the ghost of sweetness.  
Touch: root-skin is cool velvet; basalt is warm as held breath. Humus clings like memory foam to boots, reluctant to release you.  
View: look up—roots lace the sky like veins across a clouded eyeball. Look down—vein-lakes answer with upside-down lightning.  

EPILOGUE—THE BREATH BETWEEN TWO PLACES  
When you climb the thread-spires back to the Main Fall’s lowest knots, you feel the canyon heartbeat fading behind your soles and the plateau heartbeat amplifying above your crown. At a midpoint, neither pulse dominates; they sync, and for a breath you stand inside a single vast circulatory loop—leaf to root, root to leaf, thunder to echo and back again. The Altimetric Canopy forgets quickly, but the Rootfall Canyons remember everything that slips its mind, holding those memories in water dark as unspoken questions, waiting for the day the song above and the silence below resolve into the same chord and lift the whole world, canopy and canyon alike, toward whatever sky will have them next.
