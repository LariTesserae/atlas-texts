Cymel Nadir

1. Descent  
• Approach: What begins as a braided melt-stream beneath Ichorra’s shelf steepens, disappears into a black maw, and becomes an ever-narrowing flume that may be ridden only on a waxed boneboard.  The flume ends abruptly at a vertical void: drop ten heartbeats and the air warms by forty degrees.  
• Elevation:  −2 400 m relative to valley floor.  You are now inside the drained magma chamber that once fed the newborn spine above.  Ceiling height varies—cathedral arches of vesicular basalt give way without warning to hallways scarcely wide enough for breath.  
• Light: None is native.  Navigation relies on slow-burn phospher lanterns whose luminance is keyed to the phosphorus extracted from Ichorra’s sangskin harvests.  Every lantern remembers the hand that lit it and will glow a fraction brighter when that grip returns.

2. Stone & Heat  
• Lithology: The walls are layered glass and pumice, still under latent tensile stress.  Seams that look solid can flower into fractal fissures with the tap of a boot.  
• Residual warmth: Though the magma fled centuries ago, radiogenic decay keeps pockets of basalt at sauna temperature.  Steam snakes out of pinholes and condenses into milky stalactites the locals call “dragon whiskers.”  
• The Floor: A continuous sheet of obsidian mirrors the ceiling.  It is thin as memory in places; elsewhere it domes upward on bubbles of trapped gas, creaking underfoot like distant thunder.

3. Water  
• The world’s tallest waterfall never sees the sun.  Every forty-three seconds, meltwater from Ichorra’s gutters reaches a lip in the chamber roof and spills as a hollow silver cylinder two hundred metres straight down.  The column ribbons into spray before touching rock, becoming an airborne river that wanders the chamber in slow gyres.  
• Catchment: Porous scoria beds drink the mist, percolating it into cistern-lakes beneath the floor mirror.  Here the water attains a syrupy viscosity from dissolved silica, allowing it to store heat for months.  
• Drift-ice Pearls: In the coldest vaults, beads of supersaturated water freeze concentrically around grains of meteoroid dust that once speckled Ichorra’s radiators.  The pearls ring like bells when jarred; traders prize them as windchimes that never quite melt.

4. Atmosphere & Pressure  
• Barometric inversion: Despite the depth, pressure remains only marginally higher than at Ichorra.  Warm pockets rise, cold drafts descend, generating a perpetual internal weather whose forecast can change between heartbeats.  
• Hazard: Inertial sloshing of these air cells sometimes births “breath-thieves”—zones where oxygen has stratified above your nose line.  Veterans carry vane-feathers; if the vane droops, you kneel instinctively into the richer strata.  
• Scent-palette: Ozone from piezoelectric rock-shear; peat-sweetness from subterranean lichen; faint copper where groundwater brushes raw magma a kilometer deeper.

5. Built Environment  
• The Echo-Jetties: Finger-like docks of basalt cantilever over the floor mirror.  Each jetty ends in a fretted parabolic disk that reflects spoken words along hidden corridors; a whisper can ride for kilometres before meeting an ear.  
• Spine-Scaffolds: Inverted replicas of Ichorra’s aerolith ribs, but grown downward, not up.  Their lattice-keepers are albino cousins, tuned to rebuild in compression rather than tension.  When tremors travel the spine, these ribs tense first, buying Ichorra milliseconds of warning.  
• Loom-Caves: Caverns hung with vertical looms that weave cloth from vitreous filament extruded out of melted obsidian.  The fabric, “nadir-silk,” is translucent, insulative, and sings when torn.

6. Technology  
• Heat-Leeches: Thumb-sized ceramic slugs that clamp onto hot vesicles, siphon thermal energy through thermionic arrays, and crawl toward colder rock to discharge it.  An old leech looks like a tiger-striped geode and is valued as a pocket hand-warmer topside.  
• Acoustic Railways: Cargo sleds ride on resonant hum rather than magnetism.  Singing-pylons spaced every twenty metres emit standing notes that levitate a quartz-loaded keel under the sled.  Change the note, change direction.  Pilots train as choristers.  
• Phonocrystals: Transparent cylinders that record ambient vibration as micro-etchings.  Ichorra’s towers send their subsonic health reports down the spine; the crystals archive centuries of the city’s pulse like tight-wound tree rings.

7. Soundscape  
• Dominant note: A perpetual E-flat born of wind in the abandoned feeder dikes.  Residents say it is the chamber remembering magma.  
• Periodic cannonades: When an obsidian bubble collapses under its own weight, the boom ricochets for minutes, shedding harmonics that map the emptiness.  
• Silence, when it occurs, is double-thick.  You taste it in your teeth before you hear it.

8. Life  
• Glow-moss: Chlorophyll-free mats that feed on geothermal trace gases, emitting a cold cyan luminescence.  They are shaved nightly for lamp slurry.  
• Fumerlings: Salamander-shaped exotherms that metabolize silica and excrete quartz scale.  Kept as living crucibles; artisans melt nadir-silk strands by stroking a fumerling’s spine until it blazes white-hot.  
• Humans: 612 permanent, ninety percent of them “echo-keepers” who maintain the acoustic railways and trade in bottled resonances.  Nearly all were children of Ichorra once; the descent is a rite for those who find the horizon too wide.

9. Dynamics  
• Market-Cycle: Every 21 dawns, a warm-air geyser couples the two settlements.  Cargo balloons that left Ichorra at sunrise glide down the chimney and arrive at Cymel before mid-shift, their skins wrinkled but intact.  They bring aerolith spores, data-motes thirsty for deeper archives, and gossip still warm from the throat.  
• Rebound-Tremors: When the mountain stretches, Ichorra sways, but Cymel compresses.  Echo-keepers lie flat on the mirror to feel changes in pitch; they adjust spine-scaffolds to absorb the thrust, then send warning crystals upward strapped to heat-leeches.  
• The Thirsting: Once a decade the sub-cistern lakes drain through some unseen fissure.  The entire chamber exhales fog so dense it beads on skin as dew.  Only afterward do residents count how many fumerlings have petrified, how many pearls were lost, how many new passages the water carved.

10. Rules  
• Speak forward, listen back.  Any word flung into darkness will return eventually; be prepared to hear yourself improved—or diminished—by echoes.  
• Never trust a shadow on the ceiling; gravity here is polite, not loyal.  
• Weight is virtues, not sin; mass dampens resonance.  Heavy packs earn you free passage on acoustic sleds.  
• If you break the mirror-floor you must name the new lake you have made before the water stills.  
• Lanterns remember kindness.  Strike one in anger and it will gutter with every future match you offer.

11. If You Linger  
Colors abandon you first; the world reduces to blackglass and aquamarine glow-moss, and your dreams compensate by flowering in impossible chroma.  
You begin to smell tremors before they come—gunflint and lilac—and to judge distance by the time an echo takes to round the old magma throat.  
One night you will wake to find your pulse synched to the E-flat, and your body will feel improbably light, as though up were only a rumor.  That is the hour Cymel Nadir considers you native and will grant you an echo-name: two syllables, one voiced, one swallowed, impossible to carry topside.  Later, should you ride the steam geyser back to Ichorra, you will discover your voice frost-etching violet motes into new harmonic constellations, and the Chorus of Doors will open an instant sooner than before, already sure it knows the shape of your return.

12. Residents, Enumerated Differently  
A. Flesh & Will  
   • Echo-Keepers: Part engineer, part cantor.  Their throats are calloused; their ears can spot a cracked jetty filament three kilometres off by the skip in a reverberation.  
   • Glasswrights: Weaver-chemists who coax thread from molten glass and gossip from cooling cracks.  They tattoo their fingertips with wave-forms of favorite shatter-songs.  
   • Deep Children: Only six at present.  They measure growth not in centimetres but in the widening interval between pulse and mountain’s note.  Their lullabies are counter-melodies to silence.  

B. Symbiotic Mechanisms  
   • Albino Lattice-Keepers: Sister strain to Ichorra’s, coded to detect compression shear.  When idle they cluster into hexagonal snowflakes, white against blackglass.  
   • Chorus-Pylons: Each pylon houses a microbrain that dreams in chord progressions.  Between trains they hum lullabies to the fumerlings, who gather round like cats at a hearth.  

C. Elemental Whispers  
   • Mirror-Ghosts: Not spirits but after-images of lantern light trapped in layers of silica.  A swift-moving figure can leave a reflection behind for minutes, walking a second life beneath the glass.  
   • The Remembered Fall: A kilometer-tall column of absent magma that still radiates heat in negative.  Stand in its volume and sweat in the dark.  Some pilgrims claim it answers questions phrased as mineral equations.

D. Inbound & Outgoing  
   • Down-Seeds: Those hollow aerolith toroids that launch from Ichorra but fail to find another mountain often spiral here instead, nesting like birds under warm overhangs.  Given time, they germinate ribs that seek ceiling rock, yearning upward.  
   • Ember-Owls: Ash-feathered raptors from the valley forests.  They arrived chasing fumerlings and stayed for the acoustics, riding echoes to pinpoint prey.  Locals feed them scrap quartz so their pellets glitter.

13. Trade Between Shelf and Chamber  
Ichorra wants resonance.  Cymel carves silencing baffles from nadir-silk and sells calibrated hush for the city’s meditation niches.  
Cymel wants chill.  Ichorra freezes its excess night-ice into hexagonal prisms and drops them down the melt-flume wrapped in ravens’ stories.  
What neither will say aloud: they trade a shared heartbeat.  When the spine sings, both places listen, and each believes it is the primary audience.

14. Future Tense  
• The Mirror-Bloom: Echo-keepers predict the obsidian sheet will one day flex upward like a lily pad, cracking into petals.  They are preparing songs to guide the fracture into beauty.  
• Twin Ribs: The freest upward rib budding from a down-seed is weeks away from puncturing the flume.  When it meets its sky-bound sibling growing from Ichorra, the two may splice, closing the loop between pinnacle and chamber, making of the volcano a single, breathing ring-city.  

Remain awhile longer and the difference between updraft and downdraft blurs.  You begin to suspect that Ichorra and Cymel Nadir are not two settlements but the first stanza and last refrain of one long architectural hymn—stone teaching itself the full octave of being, from molten birth to glassy echo, and back again.
