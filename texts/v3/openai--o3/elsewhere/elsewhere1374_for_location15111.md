Mirrorstep Caldera  

High above the Ovoid’s vaulted hush, the world opens into a bowl of shattered light. The Caldera is the scar of an elder impact—twenty-one kilometres rim to rim, its floor a planes-wide tessellation of glassy trinitite and fused basalt. In daylight the surface glares like a sea frozen mid-spark; at night it cools to onyx, calm as a blown-out candle. Between those extremes lives a settlement whose every gesture answers a single mandate: gather the sun, break it into portable fractions, and slip those fractions down the long throat of stone to the city that hungers in the dark.

Distance/Current/Kinship  
Nine minutes by maglev if you sit still, forty-two seconds if you are a lumen packet riding the hollow optic umbilicus that stitches Caldera to Ovoid. Sound travels the other way: every hour the Reservoir’s scarlet exhalation races up auxiliary vent shafts, reaches the crater floor as a boom you feel in your jawbones, and drifts off as heat shimmer. Thus the contract: the deep place sends pressure and song; the bright place sends measured radiance in return. Both cities eat what the other excretes, and each calls the trade even.

Geometry & Weather  
From above, Mirrorstep resembles a clock face whose numbers have wandered outward. The rim wall—ragged escarpment five hundred metres tall—carries fifty-nine heliostats: mirrored petals that pivot on hydraulic stems, tilting to track the sun and spill its glare downwards. Their reflections converge on the floor, not in a single blinding cauldron but in thousands of discrete tiles—louvered collectors nicknamed steps. Each step is a square of chlorophane crystal one pace wide, canted a degree off its neighbours so the whole mosaic ripples like fish scales when a cloud passes.

No river crosses this desert dish. Rain shuns it: the heated air lens over the glass deflects incoming cumulus, forcing storms to skid around the rim and empty themselves elsewhere. Water arrives only as steam vented by the Ovoid turbines. The plumes jet nightly through relief chimneys, flash-freeze against the pre-dawn air, then drift as granular hoarfrost back into the crater. By sunrise the frost has sublimated, polishing the trinitite fresh for another day’s blaze.

Light Economy  
To the Caldera’s accountants, illumination is not a quality but a weight. Photons enter the steps, strike a honeycomb of doped diamond waveguides, and fold into fiber-bound packets the size of a rice cracker. Each puck contains exactly one megajoule—audited twice by spectro-chemists in cobalt aprons—then is labelled with a gravitational QR glyph readable even in pitch black. Pucks ride the optic umbilicus downward, brake at the Ovoid’s customs cradle, and emerge as the hard currency whose units the brokers below call lumen-minutes. The surface folk accept payment not in light but in resonance: kiloton-seconds of acoustic potential they draw back up the shafts and vent through tuned vents as warm air to ward off the night chill.

Architecture  
Most structures keep low to dodge the mirrors’ beams. Habitats burrow shallow trenches, their ceilings glazed but fretted with electro-chromic veins that dial tint from obsidian at noon to transparency by dusk. A few towers dare the vertical: slim pylons hosting beam splitters, each sheathed in micro-louvre feathers that collapse tight when a tracking error threatens to fry the core. Atop the loftiest pylon roosts the Noon Gnomon, a spire of aerogel so perfectly clear it seems a hole in the sky; it serves no purpose but to cast the crater’s only true shadow, a slender needle that glides across the step field and tells technicians when to rotate the petal mirrors another fraction of a degree.

Soundscape  
Where the Ovoid is crescendo and reverb, Mirrorstep is quiet bordering on sacramental. Glass eats footsteps, wind skirts the rim rather than descend, and the mirrors tick in incremental servo clicks too small to count as noise. Only twice a day does volume rise: dawn, when the petal array blooms with a metallic sigh audible ten kilometres off; and the hourly moment when the Reservoir’s pulse arrives as a distant bass drum, lifting grit from the trinitite in a pale seiche that sparkles, falls, and is still again.

Biology  
Life survives by hiding inside glare or heat.  

• Saltback Beetles – micro-mirrored carapaces sending half the sun away, half into their own chemical batteries. Upended, they serve as ad-hoc heliograph relays for field crews too far from main comms.  

• Threadroots – tuberous succulents whose leaves never breach the surface; instead they shoot translucent stolons that thread the shallow glass, drinking condensed turbine-steam before dawn bakes it off.  

• The Shyward – a migratory aerial jellyfish, helium-filled, mylar-skinned, genetically descended from advertising balloons. It drifts through at equinoxes, honed to dodge collector beams by reading the same thermal gradients the heliostats track. The locals let it pass; its scrimshawed skin carries sunprints of places no one here will visit.

Population Strata  

1. Flux Shepherds  
Caretakers of the petal mirrors, they walk the rim catwalks in suits studded with ablative glass chips—each chip calibrated to flare sacrificially if a beam departs alignment and threatens flesh. Shepherds speak little; they count in lumens instinctively, as bakers feel for dough. When off-shift they sleep in hammocks slung beneath the catwalk gratings, claiming the rocking of the hydraulic stems sings better lullabies than any human voice.

2. Photonics Sutlers  
Half merchant, half medic, they mend cracked fiber guts with fusion splice pistols and hawk black-market “sunshards”—illicit pucks over-filled past audit spec. Buyers are often Skyfarers from below who meet them halfway on the maglev, trading contraband resonances (spoken rumors, half-remembered songs) for a sliver of outlaw daylight.

3. The Dial Choir  
Because silence dominates, messages travel by semaphore of shade. At dusk, volunteers line the step field edges, each holding a hinged screen. They swing panels in choreographed arrays, flashing bar-code shadows across hundreds of crystals at once. The interacting refractions code status reports destined for the Cantor Array: wind shear data, mirror micro-pit counts, list of the day’s solar harvest. The Choir’s art lies in compressing poetry into those same reports, a blush of verse smuggled inside the numbers. Librarians down-shaft claim they can taste sunset on the data packets.

4. The Heliophant  
A single presence, less a citizen than a wandering artefact. Imagine an excavator rebuilt as kinetic origami: forty articulated plates hinged around a hollow core that traps sunlight all day, then folds into a glowing cathedral at night. No one steers it; it follows fault lines left by the impact, mapping seismic murmurs for teams that will never keep pace. Rumor has the Heliophant dreams through its plates, broadcasting imagery of deeper caverns stranger even than the Ovoid. The Cantor Array sometimes hums these visions back up-shaft, a feedback loop of dream against mirror.

Rules & Habits  
• Tint first, talk later—eye damage papers are tedious.  
• No shouting after noon; pressure gradients carry words straight into an active beam path and scramble diagnostics.  
• If you drop a tool, mark the location: it may weld itself to the glass by day’s end.  
• When the shadow of the Noon Gnomon touches a step whose ID ends in 0, pause—some claim that is the only moment you can hear the Shyward’s hearts thrumming overhead.

Interplay with the Ovoid  
Twice per watch a freight sled ascends full of empty photon tins that once glimmered in balcony sconces. Up here they are reheated in a bath of raw noon, resealed, re-signed by the Sutlers, and queued for the plunge. In return, crates of resonance records—sheeted bas-reliefs pressed by the Reservoir’s shock waves—disgorge into the Caldera archives. Curators slide them into vertical racks cut directly into the crater wall. On cold nights the racks sing back the day’s stored thunder, a lullaby of distant engines for workers who have never seen the engines themselves.

Leave-Taking  
To descend is to relinquish horizon and embrace echo; to ascend is to burn away your own shadow until pupils shrink to pinpoints. Migrants who attempt both cycles in a single day report sensory whiplash so severe the medics keep darkrooms stocked with silence and warm stone, mimicking neither domain yet hinting at both. Most travelers learn to linger between trips, waiting until the body forgets just enough of glare or boom to stomach the other.

So the Mirrorstep Caldera stands: a concave theatre of mirrors aimed forever inward, a forge that coins daylight into hush and hush into daylight. Where the Ovoid drums and devours the dark, the Caldera blazes and listens for quiet. Between them runs a single artery of glass and void, a conduit through which photons flow downward and aftershocks climb back up—two cities locked in orbital exchange, each turning the other’s excess into its own survival, each completing the other’s idea of what it means for a place to be alive.
