EMBERWAKE CALDERA

TERRAIN  
Imagine a bowl two leagues wide whose rim is saw-toothed obsidian and whose floor is crusted with pumice the color of dried blood.  Steam faults stripe the slopes like silver wounds; each exhales a constant thread of warmth that never rises higher than a man’s head before cooling into glittering frost.  No soil softer than glass exists.  What looks like sand is only bead-blast: marble to the touch, it squeaks beneath boots and will flay an unwary knee in a single slip.  
At the basin’s exact center a lake simmers—never fully liquid, never quite stone.  Locals call it the Forge-Mirror.  Every few minutes its skin spasms, belching a sheet of molten rhyolite that skitters across the surface, stiffens, and sinks with a sigh like wet leather dying.  Around that mirror stretch terrace upon terrace of hexagonal columns: vast cooling joints that formed when the last eruption bowed but did not break the land.  They are hot enough to melt frost yet cool enough to sit, so every ledge doubles as workbench, bedframe, altar.

AIR & TEMPERATURE  
Up on the rim the wind is razors and the mercury nightly dives to –20 °C.  Down on the glass flats, however, heat wells from the magma barely fifteen arm-lengths below.  A walker can stride from winter into autumn in three paced breaths.  No rain ever falls; any cloud that wanders overhead is shredded by the caldera’s updraft and snow evaporates before it can land.  Water exists only as steam, and thirst is quenched by inhalation.  

LIGHT  
Day: unbearable glare as sun strikes silica.  Even the shadows squint.  
Night: emberglow.  The Forge-Mirror pulses a sullen crimson that paints everything—skin, stone, thought—in furnace hues.  Every few hours a filament of lightning arcs from lake to sky, discharging the static that builds where hot vapor meets cold stars.

VEGETATION  
None rooted.  But life insists: mats of copper-green lichen scrawl across basalt, distilling steam into trickles so scant that a thumbprint can drown them.  Their bodies incorporate iron, giving each mat the consistency of tempered hide.  Wanderers carve drinking flasks straight from the living sheets; the wound self-seals before the cup is lifted away.

FAUNA—SCARCE BUT SPECTRAL  
Glassflies hatch where steam vents kiss wind.  Their wings are transparent blades; their abdomens glow white-hot for eight frantic hours before they fuse midair into a drifting chandelier of vitrified husks.  
Larger are the Cinder-Cranes: tripod creatures whose limbs are hollow stalks of obsidian articulated with rings of froth-stone.  They stride the terraces sipping super-heated brine, radiating a keening whistle as vapor races up their legs and out the trumpet mouth set between their shoulders.  When a flock takes wing, the air rings like a cathedral bell struck under water.

THE PEOPLE WHO LIVE IN THE CALDERA  
The Ember-Kin—though their own word, hissed through clenched teeth, translates closer to “Those Who Dance On Cooling Glass.”  

BODIES  
•  Height: tall, ribcage elongated to house over-sized lungs for steam breathing.  
•  Skin: charcoal where ash settles, salt-white where frequently scoured.  A birthmark is any patch the wind forgot.  
•  Eyes: a double shutter—outer lids translucent to cut glare, inner lids reflective to blink away emberflash.  
•  Blood runs thin; they run warmer than surface folk yet cooler than a campfire’s edge, so a surface traveler feels pleasantly tepid in their arms and dangerously feverish to a Hushborn envoy.  

CLOTHING & CRAFT  
Cloth would burst into spark-holes within a week.  Instead they weave plates of millipede chitin—yes, the very molt the Hushborn send—into mail that flexes like overlapping petals.  Seams are stitched with basalt fiber spun by Glass-spiders (a hand-sized cousin of the rootling’s rope-long millipede).  
They shape no metal; ore here is too brittle.  Instead they mold the land itself, snapping off glowing tongues of rhyolite with hooked paddles and flinging them across stone anvils.  The shards skitter, cool, and are nocked into obsidian tools so sharp the Maker who holds them must wear mirrored gauntlets to avoid seeing her own future severed in reflection.

SUSTENANCE  
•  Drink: breath-draught—super-heated vapor inhaled through reed condensers that chill it to liquid on the tongue.  
•  Food: lichens crisp-roasted on vent heat, seasoned with windborne salt scraped from shadowed crevices.  Feast days feature Crane marrow cracked hot, slurped before it cools into glass honey.  

SOUND  
Nothing carries far; heat shreds waveforms.  So the Ember-Kin converse by line-of-sight semaphore: obsidian shingles flashed to reflect lake-glow in staccato bursts.  In close quarters they hum, letting vibration travel bone-to-bone through clasped forearms—an intimacy akin to whispering throat-clicks into Hushborn palms.

RHYTHMS & DANGERS  
1.  Lake-pulse: Every thirteenth heartbeat the Mirror hurls a pyroclast.  The Ember-Kin learned to dance the interval; a mis-count gifts new scars called “fire-blossoms.”  
2.  Glass-quakes:   Columns settle nightly as the rim radiates heat.  Towers you slept on yesterday may have tilted by dawn, their hexagon faces still perfect but now stair-stepped into oblivion.  
3.  Ashfall:   Once a decade the volcano exhales a sigh so vast it dims noon to dusk and powders even distant snowfields a sterile grey.  The Kin emerge afterward radiant, robed in colorless fire.  

BELIEF  
They do not worship the caldera—it is merely where their hearts were tempered.  What they revere is CESSATION, the last breath before molten meets sky and freezes into certainty.  To them, stillness is sacred because it never truly exists here.  The nearest thing is glass: motion paused mid-wave forever.  Each citizen consecrates one mistake—often a tool dropped, a step mis-timed—by casting it into the Mirror so it may rest, perfect and unmoving, beneath the churning skin.

TRADE & THE THREAD LEADING BACK TO THE TREE  
When the world tilts deepest into winter and wind can cross the caldera without catching fire, two Ember youths climb the rim, descend the snow passes, and wait at the narrow fissure plugged by living shoots.  They beat obsidian plates together until the root-folk above hear stone, not thunder— the agreed signal.  In exchange for molded chitin and spool-gut cord they offer:  
•  Obsidian scalpels (kept sheathed in lichen-felt to avoid premature healing of tree-flesh).  
•  Sun-salt crystals that glow blue when dissolved in sap, used by Hushborn healers to track infection.  
•  Stories of a sky so wide it eats clouds before they can rot.  

Neither people can linger long in the other’s home.  The Ember-Kin’s blood would congeal in the hollow’s cold, and fire-taboo bars them from lighting the breath-drums needed to stay warm.  A Hushborn’s lungs, meanwhile, would cook in caldera air.  Yet the trade persists, season upon season, because both societies covet what only opposite worlds can make: flexible coolness for one, surgical fire for the other.

MEETING THE EMBER-KIN  
Approach at night; in daylight you are a silhouette against blinding ground and the archers cannot see whether you carry the Mirror’s reflection in your eyes (a sure sign of madness).  Walk where the glass is already cracked—fresh prints risk shattering underfoot.  Bow with palms outward so they may press their wrists to yours and measure heat.  Too cold and they fear you died on the way here; too hot and they fear you might ignite the lichen gods.  

If accepted, you will be given an ember-cup: hollow ferro-lichen still alive, rimmed in glass.  Hold it to your lips.  Steam will scald the first layer of skin, yet leave you thirstless for three dawns.  That is the Kin’s pledge: survive their heat, and their heat will remember you when you leave.  

STANDING HERE  
Kneel on a basalt hexagon.  Feel it hum as hidden magma rearranges another piece of the world below.  Smell sulfur, ozone, and the pepper sting of airborne glass.  Exhale: your breath clouds in the cold wind above even as the stone beneath your knees stays warm enough to char cloth.  Across the mirror a crane lifts on thermals, its obsidian legs playing pan-pipes through the gale.  Pebbles rattle downhill, each one cooling from cherry-red to night-black in a heartbeat, and you swear you can hear them sigh with relief at being allowed, at last, to rest.
