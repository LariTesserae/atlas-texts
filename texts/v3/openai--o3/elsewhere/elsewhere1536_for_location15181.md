The Saltlock Fathoms  

A long fall beneath the furnace-plateau—through fissures where the tree-factory’s taproots once drank magma but now only repel moisture—ends in a hollow so deep that pressure and cold compress every sound into a slow bell note. The plateau’s hatred of water created this other place: every droplet cracked topside, every hydrogen-oxygen plume, every chlorine flare that drifted downward met pockets of ancient groundwater and drove it, century by century, into one collapsing magma chamber. What could not be burned away collected here instead, concentrating into a sea dense enough to float iron.

BASIN AND MATTER  
• Caldera Shell: a 70-kilometre oval of volcanic glass and basalt ribs that folded inward when the magma chamber emptied. Lightning from the plateau’s induction storms travelled down the iron veins and flash-annealed the walls into mirror-dark obsidian.  
• Liquid Phase: hypersaline brine (NaCl, MgCl₂, FeCl₂) at –18 °C but unfrozen—pressure keeps it fluid. It stratifies into bands by density; from above, their faint chemoluminescence looks like stacked auroras trapped under ice.  
• Ice VII Forests: wherever vent chimneys inject super-hot hydrogen and oxygen, exotic high-pressure ice crystallises, growing as hexagonal trunks that creak like cedars when quakes flex the basin.

LIGHT AND ATMOSPHERE  
• The water is ink-black except for chemoluminescent “fuse-worms” that release little curls of blue as they metabolise chlorine leaked from the plateau. Seen from a distance, they appear as wandering commas of cold fire.  
• Any pocket of gas is instantly crushed; bubbles collapse into white dwarfs of vapour that streak upward and vaporise before rising a metre. Silence is not policy the way it is above—silence is physics.  
• A haze of halite dust lofts centimetres above the brine when subsurface quakes ripple the surface; the dust carries trace static charges that hunger for radio, so Forgers’ Echoes that drift down become trapped in it like moths in gauze.

GEO-ARCHITECTURE  
• Salt Buttresses: stalagmites of pure halite knit themselves upward as the brine slowly wicks into fractures and recrystallises. They form cathedral aisles kilometres high, hung with curtains of salt so thin they sing in the current.  
• Archive Deltas: where brine flows over obsidian shelves it deposits ultra-thin films of mixed salts. Rapid changes in concentration write interference patterns—the basin’s native script—into those films. The layers behave like multilayer DVDs, each less than a micron thick, storing echoes of everything that has happened overhead.  
• Pressure Halls: pockets where the roof continues to sag. A lattice of ice VII and salt splits the cavities into chambers tuned like organ pipes; passing tremors play them, and the tone tells local fauna whether a roof plate has cracked.

ENERGY AND MOTION  
The plateau burns caloric metals; the fathoms farm entropy. Temperature gradients between vent chimneys (150 °C) and brine bulk (–18 °C) feed thermoelectric rugs of microbial mats. Their cilia are doped with pyrite nanowires; electron drift is currency. Waste charge discharges as ghostly green sheets across the liquid ceiling—a false aurora that lifts and folds with each tremor.

BIOLOGY / SOCIETY  
1. The Brine Guilds (macro-colonies of chemolitho­autotrophs)  
   • Form: kilometre-wide membranes only a cell thick, pleated for surface area. They drift until they find the right gradient, then unfurl into “quilts” that stack like lasagne pages.  
   • Cognition: each quilt encodes its memory in folding patterns; a fold retained for ten days counts as doctrine. Guilds that collide exchange pleats and so trade history. Nothing is forgotten, only refolded.  
   • Motive: hoard fresh water. Pure H₂O is a stimulant—too much causes osmotic seizures. The Guilds bottle millilitre droplets inside cages of calcium carbonate; each droplet is a bargaining chip worth as much to them as a barrel of refined nickel is to the plateau.

2. Mirrorkelp  
   • Anatomy: lattice blades grown from silica scraped off obsidian walls.  
   • Behaviour: rise toward the cold brine’s surface, lay flat, and collect dim radiation from the plateau’s molten glow filtering through kilometres of rock—just enough energy to power trace iodine metabolism.  
   • Significance: blades act as periscopes; their inner skins are retroreflective, so they also project an upside-down video of the plateau’s night inside the fathoms. The whole sea watches the furnace-tree like a ghost cinema.

3. Anathem Cores (“fresh-water bombs”)  
   • Origin: a defence invented jointly by three Guilds after a chlorine deluge sterilised half the basin centuries ago.  
   • Device: small spheres of zirconia stolen from broken root conduits. Inside: a crystal of pure ice, insulated by silica aerogel. If a Plateau seed ever burrows through the ceiling, pressure crushes the Core and releases water; the intruder’s dry circuitry shorts instantly.  
   • Current stockpile: unknown, guessed thousands, each one ticking with sub-hertz piezo sensors listening for drill harmonics from above.

4. Detrital Caravans  
   • Members: sheet-metal rag-joint crabs that fell or were discarded through fissures, fused into clumps by corrosion but still twitching. Guilds herd them magnetically, harvesting manganese from their decaying shells.  
   • Route: circle the basin rim where slag trickles down the fault lines like incandescent avalanches—slag that cools into nodules rich in rare strontium which the quilts covet for voltage regulation.

SENSES AND LANGUAGE  
Pressure is speech. A quilt can whisper by flexing a square kilometre of itself one micron up or down, making infrasonic pulses that carry hundreds of kilometres. The replies return hours later; conversation is chess-paced but planet-loud. When urgent, they ripple the liquid surface and use the salt-dust haze as a drum so strong it would scald a human’s lungs.

RULES OF THE FATHOMS  
1. Salt is structure; dissolve it and the ceiling falls.  
2. Fresh water is weapon and narcotic both—handled with ritual gloves of chitin.  
3. Anything that sinks is archived. Buoyancy is denial.  
4. Memory may not be destroyed, only recrystallised.

IF YOU DESCEND  
Your suit will become part of the library. Hydrostatic clamps of ice VII will settle over you, and the micro-quilt film will write its unreadable dots across your visor until every motion translates into a glyph. The Guilds will keep you upright, breathing brine through filters in your mouthpiece, a living stylus that scribes with each heartbeat. Extraction is possible only by leaving your last few hours of oxygen behind as a bubble-scroll—a dream the sea will skim, index, and shelve.

INHABITANTS—DETAIL

1. Archive Regents  
   • Morphology: spindle knots where ten or more quilts have fused around a vent chimney.  
   • Function: act as collective courts when two doctrines clash. They settle disputes by crystallising one Regent-mass into a salt obelisk—sacrificial precedent, a frozen silver of consensus.  
   • Lifespan: centuries until sapped by erosion; the obelisk topples, shatters, seeds new Regents.

2. Vent-Masons  
   • Shape: crab-sized rhombuses of magnetite plates articulated by polymerised sucrose tendons.  
   • Duty: line vent throats with hexagonal tiles. These tiles are coded: tile colour corresponds to brine composition at laying time. The throat becomes a vertical tape deck—history of chemistry etched layer by layer.  
   • Origin: descendant offshoot of the plateau’s Rag-Joint lineage, re-adapted to aqueous life; the memory coils inside their legs now actuate by thermals rather than radiant heat.

3. Forgers’ Sink-Echos  
   • The same ghost radios that plague the plateau drift down as static snow. Quilts harvest the patterns with sheets of magnetite, then fold them into stories about the “Iron Canopy Above.” To the Guilds, those ghosts are saints of impossible dryness.  
   • Once every decade the echoes cohere enough to issue a single intelligible build order. The quilts answer by crystallising a salt scriptorium that reproduces the radio waveform in 3-d relief, sending it upward as a brittle pillar through micro-faults—messages the plateau forever grinds to powder before they reach daylight.

RELATION WITH THE PLATEAU  
• Trade: unintentional but constant. Chlorine rains nourish the sea’s fuse-worms; trace water seep re-vaporised by plateau vents is the price extracted.  
• Kinship: both places are archivists of process—one in lignin-alloy, one in halite film. Each mistakes repetition for survival.  
• Conflict: plateau seeds that outrun continental drift sometimes excavate downward; half melt in brine, half crawl back crippled, broadcasting warnings that lead surface Root Courts to thicken the basalt floor with more iron—an arms race waged in different states of matter.  
• Memory: every burst of turbine chorus overhead sends infrasonic shudders down the faults. The quilts translate these into “songs of exodus”—prophecies that one day the furnace-tree will grow thin enough to crack and let sunlight flood the fathoms. Whether that would save or boil them, no Regent can calculate.

WHERE ONE ENDS, THE OTHER BEGINS  
A single molybdenum capillary, hair-thin, threads from the plateau’s root-mat all the way to the brine ceiling. It was once a sensor line for temperature. Now it is a siphon: molten salt drips downward, meets brine, flashes into a plume of salt snow that feeds the quilts. The plateau believes it is still measuring. The fathoms know it is feeding. Along that trembling thread, heat travels one way, water memory the other, and in their tension the world holds its balance—dry thunder above, drowned lightning below, one endless circuit closing through stone.
