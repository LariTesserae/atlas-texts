The Ulterior Sea

Terrain  
If you burrow straight down through the Nursery’s warm clay you will not find magma or bedrock. You will fall, splash-less, into a liquid that behaves like mercury until touched and like air thereafter. The Ulterior Sea is a hemispherical cavity five kilometres across, lined not by stone but by the undersides of continental plates polished to a mirror finish. You can see the soles of mountains overhead, inverted rivers rolling along their courses, weather sliding backward across the rock—reflections, not realities, though they argue the point persuasively. The sea itself is depthless: the farther you dive the flatter gravity becomes, until direction dissolves into a slow, orbital drift.  

Water  
Chemically it is not water at all but a colloid of silicate platelets floating in an electrolyte bath. To lungs it feels like cold steam; you breathe it the way a fish breathes water and a singer breathes silence. Capillary action lifts faint tides up the Nursery’s clay floor: that intermittent rain the people above call “curtain-storms” is the Sea inhaling and exhaling through pores no wider than a reed. Resin leaked by the Arches falls through those pores too, curing on contact into green glass drizzle that settles here as spindrift.

Light  
All illumination arrives from above—yet “above” is anywhere your feet are not. Each mirror facet of the vault steals a memory of sunlight from the distant surface world and plays it back at random intervals. Dawn may bloom on one ceiling-plate while midnight reigns on its neighbour; constellations crosshatch at impossible latitudes. Where two lightfields overlap, they interfere into corridors of moving darkness called kelp-shadows. Native life has learned to surf those shadows the way desert animals surf cool twilight.

Atmosphere & Chemistry  
Pressure equals a gentle day at sea level but composition differs: 14 % extra oxygen, enough trace argon to make matches whisper blue, and suspended micro-flakes of reflective silica. The flakes plate whatever they touch—bones, boats, dreams spoken too loudly—with a filmy chrome. Voices arrive sheathed in their own echoes; a shout loops the cavity twice before consenting to fade.

Built / Tech  
The only stable structures are Flotillas: rafts of fused green glass knitted from the Arches’ droplet-fall. Each begins as a single bead; wavelets roll it over itself for years until it pancakes into a kilometre-wide meniscus strong as diamond, thin as a vow. Iron spars salvaged from drowned tool-kits are lashed beneath for ballast. Sails are mirror gauze that catch whichever starlight is brightest this minute and shove the craft sideways along light-pressure gradients. Navigators steer by rotating the whole raft like a record, popping it into harmony with a chosen reflection current.

Sound  
Every noise arrives doubled: the primary tone and its mirrored antitone. When they meet they cancel into brief, perfect nulls—heart-sized vacuums that thump the chest like sudden altitude. The sea’s surface (if “surface” can be said) rings with these beats, so mariners measure distance not in miles but in intervals between silences.

Fauna  
1.  Specular Rays: flat, coin-silver fish that swim belly-up, licking residue from the underside of light itself. Their bodies are so reflective they appear only in the gaps they erase.  
2.  Lantern Mothers: ballooning medusae a hundred metres wide whose frilled edges host symbiotic plankton that photosynthesise starlight memories. Once a decade a Mother turns inside-out and births flotillas of smaller jellies that migrate upsea and erupt through Nursery rain-vents as what the caretakers mistake for egg-weather.  
3.  Index Eels: threadlike, fluorescent red. They knot themselves into numerals predicting where the next mirror-quake will dislodge platelets above. Sailors read them the way farmers read clouds.  
4.  The Once-Man: a solitary figure sealed inside a sheath of chrome so pure you can see yesterday in it. He rows an invisible skiff, harvesting dropped phonograph cylinders from the Nursery vents. The grooves etch backward on his hull as scripture; he is compiling a bible of things that have not yet occurred.

Flora (of a sort)  
Mirror-Lichen clings to the vault, digesting images instead of minerals. When it overgrows, whole sections of reflection go dark, temporarily erasing portions of the over-world from memory. Nurserymen feel those erasures as pockets of déjà-vu; to repay the courtesy they sing upward during chorus-breathing, and the harmonics flake surplus lichen into snow that feeds the rays.

Dynamics  
The Ulterior Sea pivots on a 37-minute axial flutter—the same period as the Arches’ heartbeat. Every pulse lifts a slow cyclone of fluid that brushes the cavity’s crown, drags new visions into the mirrors, then subsides. Mariners time journeys to that pulse; miss it and a voyage stretches fractally, days folding into months.

Weirdness—The Rule That Fails  
Reflection is meant to imitate, not create. Here it does both. Events thought but not yet acted appear first as glints on the water. Drop a tool and its mirrored twin splashes up to meet it, arguing which is original until one forgets and the story resolves. Death cannot decide which side to claim, so things here seldom finish dying; they hover in a supple penumbra of possibility.

Materials  
•  Green Glass from Nursery resin—buoyant in the sea, razor-edged in air.  
•  Silicate Platelets—scoopable with a hand-net, smeltable at body temperature into mirage lenses.  
•  Echo-Chord—rope woven from eel-skin and reflected sound, taut until named aloud, whereupon it slackens politely.

Culture – The Galaxers  
Population: fluctuates between thirty and sixty, depending on how many Nurserymen dreams wander down the vents and solidify into people.  
Ethos: Exploration as hymn. They map not territory but potential: charts note where a harbour might be, where a storm intends to form.  
Customs:  
•  Knot Oaths—promises tied in eel scripts; when the script unknots, the promise is fulfilled.  
•  Mirror Fasting—forty days wearing hoods of dull cloth so one’s reflection can travel unburdened and return with news.  
Trade with the Nursery: they send back silicate flakes compressed into blank mirrors. Hung between the Arches’ trunks, the mirrors cancel local dilation for a heartbeat or two, giving the caretakers precious moments of straight geometry. In exchange they receive resin cylinders, which the Sea folk play backward to summon tailwinds.

Edge of the Place  
At the cavity’s equator a ring-reef of fused echoes marks where reflection thickens into solidity. Cross it and gravity rewrites; up is once again singular. Climbers emerge onto a salt flat that locals swear is the world’s true underside. A set of chalk arrows there—familiar to any Nurseryman—point back toward the basin’s rim. Follow them for an hour and you arrive topside, soaked in silvery dew, pockets clinking with coins of green glass you do not recall minting.

Possible Futures  
1. The Arches will finally drop its seed; it will fall like a meteor through the vent and open mid-plunge into a continent-sized lily pad, stabilising the Sea into a true ocean with coasts and currents.  
2. Mirror-Lichen may black out the vault entirely, turning the Ulterior into the first dark sea—a place without reflection, where things are forced to be singular and real.  
3. The Once-Man might finish his bible; the last groove would teach mirrors to remember forward, ending rehearsal for both places at once.  
4. Or everything might endure in rehearsal forever: the Nursery inhaling, the Sea exhaling, two halves of a lung exchanging invention for patience.

Connection—One Breath Apart  
Stand in the Hollow Nursery when the Spoor Choir tolls and watch the rain-vents shimmer: each droplet that rises instead of falls is a bubble from the Ulterior Sea signing its name in fluid Braille. Stand on a glass raft when the Green Curtain drizzle begins above and you will taste clay and lullabies in the spray. Neither place is complete; each carries the missing half of the other’s sentence. Together they practise saying the world, again and again, until sentence becomes story and story decides it is at last true.
