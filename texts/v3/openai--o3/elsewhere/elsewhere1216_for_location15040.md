The Crown of Khorzhan  

Where the wind first remembers the continent it has crossed, where clouds break like surf against stone and fall away in foam of mist, a table-land hangs over the Hollow: an uplifted rim called by map-makers the Crown of Khorzhan and by its own people simply the Open.  From its scarped edge you may stare straight down the fractured throat into Arajh’s pewter dusk, yet behind you stretches a brightness so absolute that the two places seem to share no element but distance.  They are, in truth, one machine: what the Crown spends, the Hollow keeps.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––  
TERRAIN AND MATERIALS  
• Height: Three thousand paces above sea level; a sixth of that again above the Hollow’s tarns.  
• Bedrock: welded ignimbrite—pumice and obsidian fused by an eruption older than any living tongue.  The surface has flaked into plates as thin as pottery shards.  Shatter one and you smell trapped centuries of sulfur.  
• Landform: the plateau is not flat but a shaken mosaic of yards-wide flats and abrupt sunken pans called “thirst-bowls.”  Between them run fracture alleys, some broad enough to ride, some so narrow light knifes from wall to wall.  All ultimately dive into the same subsidence fault that opens into the Hollow.  
• Mirror-crust: after rare sleet-storms, every low pan skins over with ice glazed by blown pumice dust.  Noon sun turns each into a white mirror; from above, flocks of circling raptors see a tessellation of sky looking back.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––  
ATMOSPHERE AND LIGHT  
Sky here is not simply blue but cobalt scorched almost black at zenith; the air is thin and bone-dry.  At dawn it flashes brass, at dusk copper, with no gentle gradient between.  Wind never stops and never smells of rain—only of ozone scraped from stone and the faint vinegar reek of lichens when they open their pores.  Sound is shaved away by the wind; even speech feels leached of consonants.  The roar that is everything in the Hollow is nothing on the Crown.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––  
WATER  
Rain is myth.  Moisture comes as sleet that falls two or three nights each year.  The pellets ping against glassy ground, melt before sunrise, vanish—except for what finds a fracture alley.  There the meltwater threads downward, following gravity’s slow corkscrew for half a day until it emerges in the Hollow as a hiss of flash-flood.  Thus every tarn below is a ghost of a storm remembered only here.  Plateau folk call that descent “the Unspooling,” and years are counted by how many Unspoolings a child has seen.

A second, slower river also runs: vapor.  Each noon the Crown’s surface bakes; at dusk it exhales heat and with it a tide of updraft that lifts Hollow mist through the same cracks it descended, dries it, and feeds the night wind.  The respiratory cycle of two landscapes breathing through a shared lung.

VEGETATION  
• Bark-glass pines: knee-high, trunk no thicker than a wrist, bark of translucent quartz under which green cambium glows like bottled fire.  They photosynthesize by day, then at night reverse the flow and emit a red phosphor that keeps their tissues from freezing.  From the Hollow the pines’ nightly exhalations appear as a scarlet comb along the sky-line—“teeth of the sleeping lion,” First-Makers say.  
• Fist lichens: knobby, fist-shaped colonies wedged in shadowed cracks.  They store sleet inside chitin bladders, then split those bladders to release liquid on still evenings.  The hiss draws cloud-hares (see Fauna) who drink and in turn fertilize the lichen with pellet droppings packed with airborne spores.  
• Rope-grass: wiry runners strung harp-taut across fracture alleys.  Its blades slice exposed skin; plateau children carry obsidian picks to harvest the fibers without touch.  Woven into mesh they become trade goods lowered by rope to the First-Makers, whose slings depend on it.

FAUNA — LIVES SPILLED INTO LIGHT  

Day-riders  
• Glass condors: wingspan three men’s height, feathers frosted with micro-pumice that scatters glare, making the birds look half-erased.  They ride thermals born of sun-heated fault lines, then stoop in silence upon cloud-hares.  A condor’s cry is seldom heard; instead the plateau marks its presence by a sudden shadow unlinked to any flying form.  
• Shard lizards: scales shaped like interlocking chisels; they dig by vibrating their bodies until the bedrock spider-webs and collapses into dust.  The resulting pit becomes a thirst-bowl if sleet comes before the wind fills it.  

Night-runners  
• Cloud-hares: white lagomorphs whose fur is hollow crystal.  Moonlight passes through their pelts in a milky sheen; only the rippling of the ground betrays a herd.  They drink lichen seep and can desiccate to a paper-thin torpor during droughts, reviving at the scent of moisture.  
• Thunder-horn beetles: carapace black, twin horns of fused iron pyrite.  At dusk males clash; the impact rings like struck bells across the plateau.  First-Makers down in the Hollow time their synchronized spawning to that faint music—thunder that never reaches them except as rumor.  

Keystone  
• Veyraux, the Wind-Razor Cat: muscular as its subterranean cousin yet built for sprint rather than pounce.  Fur bleached to chalk, eyes faceted amber.  It hunts by silhouette, slipping between sun and prey so that its own shadow startles quarry into stillness.  The Wind-Razor and the ridge-lion are the same species divided by circumstance; every sixth year when both breeds seek mates, a lioness may climb at night through Warm-Mist Fissure, trading the roar amplified by walls for the silent courtship of high solitude.  Cubs born of such unions have coats patterned like dawn on basalt—the plateau folk read these markings as weather omens.

PEOPLE OF THE CROWN — THE RIMWALKERS  

1. Habit and Form  
Tall, rawboned, skin tanned almost violet by thin-air sun.  Hair bleached white with bone-ash, braided around obsidian splinters that sing in high wind—personal wind-chimes said to warn against drowsiness near cliffs.  Lungs oversized, hearts slow; their breath mists even in daylight, proof of stolen warmth evaporating.

2. Subsistence and Craft  
• Diet: cloud-hare, shard-lizard liver, wind-dried rope-grass seed, and whatever condor eggs can be filched.  Salt is leached from pumice using meltwater caught in fist-lichen bladders; the resulting brine is traded to Hollow dwellers in goose-skin bladders lowered by woven rope.  
• Glasswrights: the ignimbrite plates knap like flint but with a cleaner edge.  Rimwalkers shape them into tongue-thin blades that can shave hair without touch.  A set of five is lashed together with rope-grass into a fan; fanned once it cuts standing air into a warning hiss heard even below.  
• Pyrite Harvest: Thunder-horn fights leave splintered horn across the pans; the people gather shards for spark-stone, exchanging fist-long ingots with First-Makers for mica-bat guano, which they sift for iron to forge wind-chimes.

3. Calendar and Lore  
Time is counted not by sun but by shadow: each equinox noon the sun stands directly overhead and every person’s silhouette retracts to a pool about their feet—“day of self-meeting.”  Naming ceremonies occur then.  A child given its shadow-name is carried to the scarp’s lip and shown the Hollow, told: “Below waits the echo of every word you will speak.  Mind your voice.”  

Legends say the Hollow was the Crown’s own heart, dropped in anger by the sky-beast whose fossil cage the First-Makers fear.  Rimwalkers disagree: to them the ribs above Arajh are the beast’s spread wings, and the plateau is its still-beating heart.  Both peoples imagine flight in the bones overhead; one expects escape upward, the other dreads descent.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––  
RULES OF THE CROWN — A DIFFERENT STILLNESS  

1. Nothing grows tall; wind taxes height.  
2. Heat moves sideways; touch a boulder’s east face at sunset and you will feel dawn’s warmth stored inside.  
3. All voices leave; shout and the wind carries your words over the rim and down, where the Hollow records them as confused echoes.  
4. Light is loaned, not given; nights here are darker than the cavern’s twilight because nothing remains to scatter what the sun withdraws.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––  
CONNECTIONS — THREADS BETWEEN SURFACE AND DEPTH  

• Water: every drop that hits the plateau seeks the Hollow; every wisp of mist that rises from the tarns seeks the plateau.  They are a single pendulum swing of phase.  
• Sound: ridge-lion roars climb the Warm-Mist Fissure as a bass murmur; thunder-horn battles descend as brittle chimes.  Two predator courts broadcasting invitations neither can answer.  
• Trade: rope-grass cord, salt brine, obsidian fans lowered on weighted lines; in return come mica-bat sands, knot-root pitch, and the Hollow’s only currency—silence stones knapped from drip-glossed basalt that sing one clear note when broken.  Rimwalkers suspend them in dwellings; the note that sounds when a house beams stress-crack is warning enough to flee before collapse.  
• Blood: hybrid cubs born of the Wind-Razor and ridge-lion survive best in the middle country, spending wet seasons above, dry seasons below, guiding Rimwalkers down and First-Makers up during the Great Exchange of tools at cold-turn.

STANDING UPON THE CROWN  

Bootsoles crunch glass dust.  Wind rasps inside your ear as though the plateau itself inhales through you.  A shadow condor wheels—no bird, merely its outline; the real creature already stoops beyond sight.  Obsidian plates wink underfoot like broken panes.  Looking west you see the sun fall into a thirst-bowl and gutter out; looking east you watch mist climb like silver smoke through fracture alleys, the Hollow breathing back.  Two landscapes, one pulse.  Turn your head and the wind steals the sound before it reaches the other ear.  Speak, and somewhere far below a frog will pause, a gnat rope will tremble, perhaps a First-Maker child will glance upward, wondering whose ghost brushed the ceiling of their world.
