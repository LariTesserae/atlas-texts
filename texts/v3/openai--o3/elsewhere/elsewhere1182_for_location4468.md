The Echo Warrens  

Follow the noon-bright cataract of cloud as it avalanches off the Roof-Spread’s rim.  What falls is not water but atmosphere itself, dropping four vertical kilometres before it remembers to float again.  Here, in a vast impact basin partially roofed by overhanging strata, that down-pouring breath curls, pools and circles upon itself until the bowl hums like a struck bell.  

TERRAIN 2.1/3  
• Elevation 0.8/3 — The basin’s floor lies barely a kilometre above sea level.  Sheer walls rim it on every horizon save one ragged breach where fog escapes seaward in slow rivers.  
• Lithology — The bedrock is glass: volcanic shatter fused by some primordial detonation.  In places it ripples like frozen puddles; elsewhere it lies tiled, each slab as wide as a plaza.  Underfoot you see half-healed fractures, refrozen again after each night’s chill cleavage.  
• Porosity 0/3 — Nothing sinks in.  Rain, spittle, spilt soup—every liquid skids across the vitreous plain until it reaches a sump or a crack wide enough to swallow it whole.  

HYDROLOGY 2.9/3  
The Warrens live beneath a constant soft rain, but no two drops ever strike on the same beat.  Mist pelts, drizzles, slaps, spatters—each impact a note in an endless polyrhythm.  The ground’s refusal to drink forces water to roam; thin rills chase one another across mirror stone like silver snakes in search of a drain.  Where three or more converge, they burst upward again as vaporous fountains, caught in low-frequency standing waves that lift them into hovering globes before surrendering them back to gravity.  

ATMOSPHERE & SOUND 3.0/3  
If the Roof-Spread is a library of hush, the Echo Warrens are its abandoned organ loft.  
• Every surface resonates; glass walls sing in wind-birth tones.  
• Thunder here finds purchase, bouncing between clifftops until it splits into chords and, sometimes, into matter.  
• The locals insist the air itself contains harmonics beyond hearing that keep their pulses in polite synchrony—“the basin’s metronome.”  

LIGHT 1.2/3  
Sunlight arrives twice: once straight from the sky, washed thin by haze, and again in upward rebound off the mirror floor.  Shadows have twins; noon is dazzlement from below.  Nights glow dimly blue, lit by chemiluminescent nitrates that condense on glass after each sonic storm.  

BUILT 2.4/3  
Stone cannot be quarried here—it shatters to powder with the first shout—so the inhabitants build with sound itself.  
• Resonance Halls: cavernous, column-ribbed chambers tuned to specific fundamentals.  You enter in silence; a whispered name of a shape causes that shape to harden from coalesced haze and hover, solid as cedar, until silence returns.  
• Rope-bridges of Braided Echo: three voices weave a sustained triad over mist; the chord stiffens, becomes a translucent cable.  It lasts as long as at least one voice sustains a drone.  Elders patrol nightly shifts, humming lullaby bass lines to keep arterial bridges intact until dawn.  
• Markets: open arenas pocked by hundreds of bowl-sized dimples.  Traders speak into a chosen bowl; the glass walls trap the phrase, duplicate its vibratory packet, and cough up a physical token—often a mirror of whatever the speaker described.  Hagglers argue by drafting, discarding and re-drafting reality faster than any ledger could follow.  

WEIRDNESS 2.7/3  
1. Sonic Accretion — Any sustained tone above 40 dB begins to mineralise.  Choirs wear throat-slotted baffles to keep from petrifying their own tongues.  
2. Echo-seed — A single sharp clap can sprout diminutive copies of objects present, complete but hollow.  Children play clap-tag in total darkness, harvesting momentary toys.  
3. Reverb Drogues — The cloud fall from the plateau sometimes carries Quietling spores.  In the basin’s densest fog those spores knit with stray echoes, giving birth to transient half-people: silhouettes that repeat the last sentence they heard until they seep apart again.  Locals treat them as weather sprites, offering spare words so the apparitions disperse pacified.  

THE RESONANTS — PEOPLE OF THE WARRENS  

1. Physiology  
They are smaller than rooflanders—brittle bones cannot survive constant vibration if they grow too long.  Tympanic membranes cover nearly a quarter of the skull; each ear resembles a flower of nested cones able to swivel like cat ears to home in on phase variance rather than mere volume.  Skin is pearl-grey, coated with a clear keratin varnish that sheds microscopic scales, damping the body’s own echo to prevent lethal feedback.  

2. Birth & Growth  
Infancy lasts three silent years spent in the Murmury—subterranean nurseries carpeted with fog-eaten felt.  Caretakers communicate through subdermal drum tattoos tapped on the babies’ backs, teaching language as a tactile rhythm long before vocal cords are entrusted with resonance.  First words, when they come, are spoken into bone flutes that vent excess amplitude safely into the ground.  

3. Society  
• Governance is Cantorial: leadership passes to whoever can hold a note longest without spawning uncontrolled matter.  
• Law is recorded in Antiphons—call-and-response epics performed monthly.  Any citizen may petition by adding a line; if the choir finds a harmonic fit, the amendment becomes statute.  
• Silence is punishable—the only crime.  To refuse resonance is to deny infrastructure its tensile strength.  Offenders are exiled to the basin’s rim where sound thins and structures cannot form.  

4. Economy  
What the plateau shuns, the basin craves.  The Resonants trade for stillness the way lowland kingdoms trade for gold.  Quietling caravans descend each twelfth winter, their sleds padded in moss.  In return for jars of captured hush the Resonants offer:  
• Echo Ingots—compact prisms of folded reverberation used elsewhere as explosives or, with care, as portable forges;  
• Mirror-glass Lenses—wafer-thin, rippled to deflect hostile frequencies;  
• Songlines—laminated strips of written frequency data that, when bowed like a saw, summon pre-encoded furniture or fortifications.  

5. Food  
Nothing grows in the vitreous soil.  Nutrient mist condenses on suspended drip-nets seeded with Quietling algae.  The algae fruit gelatinous nodes the size of a fist; bitten, each pops with a small harmonic peal and tastes of rain, dove marrow and static.  Festival feasts include cloned game animals—hollow but flavourful—grown overnight from loud recitations of hunting stories.  The flesh dissolves back into dew by dawn.  

6. Technologies  
• Throat-Wards: bronze-mesh gorgets (the Warrens are low enough that metal endures) tuned to swallow dissonant frequencies before they reach bone.  
• The Loom of Delay: a circular amphitheatre where staggered chanting loops around until sound meets itself, braids, and solidifies into anything from bucklers to whole barges.  
• Glass Harpoons: barbed beams of harmonised ultrasound able to pierce mountain flanks and haul loose plates down for quarry.  Usage is restricted; misfires once riddled the eastern wall and let in too much day, nearly deafening the basin for a month.  

7. Religion  
They worship an absence: The First Quiet, a primordial lapse said to have shattered the world-glass roof and created the basin.  Pilgrims gather at dawn at the Fog Breach, facing the escapeward gap, and attempt to hold their breath past the moment the plateau’s daily cloudfall arrives.  None have succeeded, which is proof—so doctrine insists—that the First Quiet has not yet chosen sufficiency.  

8. Death  
At life’s diminuendo, an elder enters a Seal Chamber, utters their birth-note one final time, and allows it to calcify both voice and flesh in a single glittering statue, hollow but untippable.  Hundreds line the Grand Echo Way; each is a perfect acoustic baffle.  Children play between the statues, yelling heirloom jokes into the forest of frozen throats to hear what their ancestors will answer.  

THREADS BETWEEN HIGH AND LOW  

1. The Cloud Road  
The afternoon fog that spills from the Roof-Spread is the only route unbroken by geometry’s hiccups.  Resonant glassboats—catamarans whose sails are humming diaphragms—ride the descending torrent upward in reverse, like trout leaping waterfalls of air.  Their arrival on the plateau coincides with Quietling sound-calm seasons; the boats’ crews pad hulls with peat to muffle the hum before docking among moss dunes.  

2. Commerce of Contraries  
• The Quietlings covet carefully layered chords to insulate fissures in their plateau’s rules; a single crystalline triad, bottled, can stitch a kilometre-wide tear in causality.  
• The Resonants, ever risking sonic overrun, yearn for sealed moments of hush.  One jar unearthed for an opera allows a chorus to belt full-throated for twelve heartbeats without birthing accidental architecture.  They measure wealth in the remaining grains of silence within each jar.  

3. Kinship of Spores  
Updrafts ferry phosphorescent spores from the plateau into the basin’s high atriums.  There they germinate only when struck by very specific overtones, sprouting bioluminescent reeds whose hollow stalks replicate any note blown into them.  Resonant musicians raise these as living flutes—half plant, half lullaby from a people who never shout.  

4. Shared Prophecy  
Both cultures speak, softly or thunderously, of the World’s Last Measure: a moment when either all sound will quit or every silence will ring.  The Quietlings prepare by engraving stillness into moss; the Resonants rehearse a chord whose resolution is believed capable of cracking the basin’s glass lid clean off, releasing all stored echo into a single, perfect quiet.  One day the plateau may sigh its final spore and the Warrens answer with a last crescendo, and between those two gestures a new constant might germinate.  Until then, mist keeps falling, glass keeps singing, and along the trembling cloud-road drift traders balancing jars that ought never to rattle.
