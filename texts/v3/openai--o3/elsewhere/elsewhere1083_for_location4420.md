Breathwell Atrium  

Follow any vent-shaft far enough upward—keep climbing until the rungs burn warm from rising steam and the air thins to a salt-sweet wind—and you will step out into the Breathwell Atrium, a void so tall that its far ceiling is mistaken for dawn. Here the Foundry Marsh exhales, and the Atrium drinks.

Terrain  
The Atrium is a single volume the height of a skyscraper and the width of a stadium, though neither term does justice to its shape. Imagine a gorge carved from scaffolding: terraces, balconets, and cantilevered decks knit together by tension cables that sing faintly in the draft. Catwalks are strung like harp strings across open space; some tremble with every footfall, others are rigid trusses wide enough for forklifts.

The walls are glass, but not a smooth façade—rather thousands of prismatic panes locked in brass grids, angled to catch light from heliostats mounted topside. Between glass and scaffold lies a filigree of ducts, dew-gutters, and aeroponic racks. Where a builder left the wall unfinished, fog vomits through raw apertures, braiding itself into silver ropes that spiral upward before vanishing.

Floor, strictly speaking, does not exist. Instead there is the Central Plenum: a cyclone of slow air that climbs the column, rising through the building like sap in a redwood. Everything else—ramps, platforms, even whole laboratories—hangs from suspension webs anchored higher up. Objects you drop are not lost; they circle lazily in the updraft, settling hours later on some lower tier.

Atmosphere & Light  
The Atrium keeps its own weather. At deck C-47 and above, temperature hovers near 28 °C; walk downward twenty meters and you stride into cool spring. This vertical gradient births constant convection: clouds bud from mist-nozzles, coagulate into cotton islands, and drift until a pressure wave herds them into dissolution against the glazing.

Light arrives indirect, bounced off the heliostats in broad, rose-gold sheets. When clouds stray across those mirrors the Atrium dims, leaving tiers lit only by bioluminescent signage and the faint aurora of algae tubes. Nights, if one can call the maintenance blackout that, are brief—twenty-minute intervals while mirrors realign. In that hush the place retains a memory of darkness so complete you can feel it on your skin like velvet.

Water  
Everything that leaves the Foundry Marsh as vapor climbs here. Condensers ridge every girder; their fins bloom with droplets that slide into capillary gutters, then fall as manufactured rain. Schedules are staggered: one zone mists every thirteen minutes, another every prime number of seconds between 29 and 53, creating polyrhythms of drizzle that a practiced ear can use as a clock.

Excess moisture collects in walkabout cisterns—transparent bladders that balloon under terraces like jellyfish. Bulbs glow inside them, coaxing algae to photosynthesize, and the whole mass of water becomes a slow lantern pulsing green in the depths.

Vegetation  
Where the Marsh experiments with moss, the Atrium cultivates forests that never knew earth.

• Windroot Palms: inflatable trunks woven from carbon cloth. They fill with humid air, harden into pressurized columns, and anchor cables at their crowns. Fronds are polymer sails that harvest the updraft, driving micro-generators in the bole.  
• Filigree Ferns: lacework plants grown on lattices of nickel foam. Their leaves are translucent veils a millimeter thick, designed less for photosynthesis than for filtration; each hectare can scrub a ton of benzene a week from the air rising out of the Marsh.  
• Glassvine: a creeper with silica-impregnated tissues that refract sunlight into interior corridors. It flowers only where two vines meet and splice, knitting together terraces as living bridges.  
• Seedling Islands: disk-rafts of expanded cork that ride the central draft. On their upper faces: soil, strawberries, stunted spruce. On the unseen underside: mycelial nets that mine airborne ammonia for nitrogen.

Fauna  
Gravity in the Atrium is more suggestion than law, and its animals have evolved (or been engineered) accordingly.

1. Splaywings  
   • Descended from pigeons donated by surface stockyards, now hollow-boned to absurdity. Wings extend by telescoping feathers that click into place like umbrellas. When resting they perch upside-down on cables, letting the draft keep them aloft with zero exertion.  
   • Diet: wind-borne beetles, seed floss from Filigree Ferns, stray spiral chips coughed up from Marsh machining—metallic grit that the birds’ gizzards use as ballast.  
   • Their coo is two-toned: one frequency for the physical throat, another whistled by slots in their wing-joints, a harmony audible only while they glide.

2. Updraft Eels  
   • Not fish but 80-cm ribbons of anodized mylar implanted with photovoltaic muscle fibers. Released as pollution monitors, they now school in thermal columns, skin flickering data in Morse.  
   • When dusk mirrors shutter, the eels douse, dropping like shed streamers until the next light cycle reanimates them.

3. Mantle Apes  
   • Rare, code-black on the biosafety ledger. Once lab specimens for low-gravity joint studies, they escaped and adapted. Limbs elongated, tails prehensile and bifurcated.  
   • Seen harvesting Glassvine marrow, which ferments in their spiraled guts into an ethanol that keeps them warm during blackout.  
   • They fear the marsh drones—the scarabs’ magnetic chatter interferes with the apes’ inner-ear balance, sending them tumbling.

People  
The Shiftlings seldom climb this high; their boots are too magnetized for the tension nets. Instead the Atrium belongs to the Windwrights.

• Roster: 140 engineers, aeroponicists, and kite-mechanics on 15-day rotations.  
• Uniform: mesh ponchos threaded with reflective arrows that show wind direction to anyone following. Boots: felt soles, goat-footed for gripping cables. Helmets carry anemometer crests whose spinning vanes double as rank insignia.  
• Speech: whistles. The Atrium’s reverb muddles words, so they use octave leaps mapped to a syllabary. A greeting sounds like two doves disputing a gutter.  
• Recreation: upflow sailing. They lash themselves to paragliders and ride maintenance thermals, tagging hull numbers on condenser banks as mid-air graffiti.  
• Economy: trade in “Condor,” a perfume distilled from first-draw condensate collected at dawn. One vial buys a month’s supply of Marsh gin down in the cantina.

Drones & Devices  
The Autokith visit rarely, but fragments of them have migrated.

• Lantern-moths ascend to die; their casings crack under lower pressure, releasing glow cores that seed planktonic light across the Atrium.  
• Scarab carapaces, shed during firmware molts, tumble upward until caught on glassvine branches where Windwrights weave them into wind chimes—each chime pre-programmed to click diagnostic codes when struck by rain.

Processes & Cycles  
Breathwell is less a machine than an organ: a lung that inhales Marsh vapors and exhales sky-clean air back through exhaust towers.

1. Vapor Capture → Condense → Filtration by Filigree Ferns → Photolysis under heliostat rays → Return flow via capillary stacks to feed Marsh boilers with demineralized water.  
2. Excess organics → Bioreactors hung like pendulums in the draft → Distills into Condor perfume and ethanol for Mantle Apes, plus sludge compressed into nutrient cakes dropped (literally, they just release the clamps) to hungry shiftlings below.  
3. Structural self-healing: Glassvine sap, rich in silicates, drips on micro-fissures in glazing, wicking into cracks and crystallizing. Where the Marsh patches itself with steel, the Atrium grows glass scar tissue.

Soundscape  
Wind is the drum, cables the strings. A constant Aeolian chord rings through the space: deep B-flat born of the updraft. Overlay this with:

• Rain-tick on membrane canopies.  
• Splaywing whistles swirling in Doppler braids.  
• The basso croak of condenser diaphragms flexing with barometric mood swings.  
Every ninety minutes, when mirror clusters pivot, a cascade of miniature thunder sweeps down the tiers as shutters slam open, releasing a photonic roar like applause.

Weirdness & Rules  
1. Buoyant Time: Objects in free drift age slower—metallurgists proved it; corrosion lags by measurable fractions. A nail hammered into a beam two tiers up turns red with rust weeks after its twin down near the Marsh.  
2. The Fog Heuristic: Once a parcel of exhaled steam has risen above tier C-20 it never descends. It will precipitate, yes, but droplets hold a positive charge that repels the lower air strata, so the rain here is, paradoxically, falling upward relative to the Marsh’s frame of reference.  
3. Echo-Glyphs: Whistled commands sometimes freeze into audible graffiti—standing waves that linger as hovering sonic holograms; walk through one and you hear last week’s maintenance orders whispered in your ear.

Relations to the Foundry Marsh  
• Materials: Scrapped torsion bars from Marsh catwalks become spine spars for Windroot Palms.  
• Nutrients: The Marsh’s ferric shrimp shells, lofted as aerosolized rust, provide trace iron that reddens Filigree Fern spores—an essential pigment for photosynthetic antifreeze.  
• Culture: Shiftlings tell campfire rumors of Mantle Apes stealing condiment stashes; Windwrights reply that they pay in glassvine seed, which germinates in any crevice the Marsh forgets to plate.  
• Hydrology: What the Marsh sweats, the Atrium drinks; what the Atrium purifies, the Marsh swallows again. Two halves of a circulatory loop, neither complete without the other.

Inhabitants of Note  

1. Archivist Aal  
   • A Windwright whose left eye was replaced by a droplet lens—permanently magnified. Aal maps cloud morphologies and claims to predict failures in Marsh pumps by reading the cumulus “blood spatter” that rises from them.  
2. Grist the Ape-Handler  
   • Former biologist, now wandering mediator between Mantle Apes and human crews. Communicates via mirrored hand signals the apes imitate; serves as postman, delivering notes braided into vine marrow.  
3. The First Eel  
   • Prototype monitor eel, three meters long, still carries full sensor suite. Rarely powers down; instead circles the Plenum like a silent comet. Emits encrypted telemetry the Windwrights have not cracked—believed to report to the same deep beacon that mothers the Marsh drones.

Encounter Table (d6, one roll per vertical kilometer ascended or descended)  
1. Cable snap; reflex test to clip into safety ring before updraft carries you off.  
2. Mantle Ape troupe offers glassvine liquor; accept and they guide you to a thermal elevator.  
3. Fog Heuristic zone: upward rain blinds optics; navigate by whistle echoes.  
4. Lantern-moth funeral bloom: dozen dying drones burst, showering hot phosphor embers—may ignite ponchos.  
5. Splaywing feeding frenzy; metallic grit pelts like birdshot.  
6. Windroot Palm deflates without warning, sagging a whole deck and changing local wind vectors—reroute or risk being hurled downward toward the Marsh.

Closing Image  
Stand on the highest tension-net at mirror-alignment hour. Below: the Foundry Marsh, a dark clockwork delta beating heat into the air. Above: the Breathwell Atrium, a hollow tree of glass, drinking that heat and exhaling light. Between them you feel the world breathe—metal lungs below, crystal sinuses above—and realize you are balanced in the trachea of a thing so large its body is measured not in meters but in processes, each place a chamber in the same long breath.
