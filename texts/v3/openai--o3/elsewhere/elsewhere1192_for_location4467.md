CLOUDGLASS STEPPE  

APPROACH  
Leave the freight-lift cavern and follow the seepwater backward.  
First it is a trickle that climbs the shaft walls in vapor form; higher, it thickens into a hot mist that prises rust from the bolts. When you reach the canyon rim—thirty metres of vertical forgetting—the mist has already flown ahead of you, riding the prevailing up-drafts to where the plateau begins. There it meets night-air cold enough to crack breath, and the meeting births the substance locals call cloudglass. You know you are close when your boots start ringing on flakes of it, thin as onion skin, hard as quartz and clear enough to reflect the sodium glow you left behind like a memory.

TERRAIN AND MATERIALS  
The steppe spreads eastward in gentle undulations, five kilometres wide, hemmed on all sides by basalt ridges that herd the wind. Soil exists only in the scantiest sense—a powder of crumbled cloudglass mixed with gypsum, forever stirred by gusts that never quite sleep.  
Anchored in that moving grit stand the spires: translucent pylons grown layer by layer as the hot vapor from below meets freezing air. Each pillar’s core is a hair-thin trickle of seepwater; each coat around it is a frozen exhalation, silica and iron salts plating out in ribbon after ribbon until the whole looks like blown sugar lit from within by trapped sunset. Some rise only to a person’s waist before snapping under weight of their own beauty; the elders surpass forty metres, hollow, their walls singing whenever the wind tunnels through.

Underfoot lies the cracked sheen of last season’s glassfall. Whenever a spire becomes too tall or the heat pulse from below falters, the column shatters. Whole hectares can collapse in a single dawn, leaving a sea of shards that tinkle for hours as they settle into new arrangements.

ATMOSPHERE AND LIGHT  
Day: diamond-white glare. The sun scatters through pillar forests and ricochets off ground shards, so that even closed eyelids feel crowded.  
Twilight: everything turns rose-gold; the spires drink the low angle of light and bleed it out again in slow pulses while internal water threads freeze, thaw, freeze in heartbeat cadence.  
Night: moonlight fractures into a million faintselves, and the steppe starts to glow coldly, a mirror held up to constellations long erased by canyon haze.

Mist never disappears; it only changes height. On wind-still hours it pools waist-deep, turning the world into two layers—sky above, reflection below—connected by columns that look too slender to hold realities apart.

SOUNDS  
The Chain Choir has cousins here.  
• Spire-song: wind litters notes along the hollow pillars, each tuned by accidental perforations made when desert insects chew their way in for warmth. Whole suites drift for kilometres, modulated by the slow cracking of cooling glass.  
• Shiver-drift: underfoot shards rubbing, a constant hiss like distant surf.  
• Heartbeat of the Deep: vents far below push new surges of warm vapor; the ground answers with a muffled thump that any veteran can use to tell tomorrow’s weather.

VEGETATION  
True plants cannot root in glass, but life finds loopholes.  
1. Frost-lichen: ultrathin mats clinging to the windward faces of spires, feeding on trace minerals and ultraviolet glare. Their symbiotic cyanobacteria give each colony a different tint—emerald, lapis, wine—so a single pillar can wear vertical rainbows.  
2. Drift-cacti: not cacti at all, rather silicone-walled water bladders grown by a fungus that hijacks cloudglass shards. They roll like tumbleweeds until they strike a spire, then fuse, tapping the inner meltwater with microscopic teeth.

FAUNA—THE MIRRORED  
1. Glass moths: wings clear as the floor they rest on, veins laced with iron picked up as caterpillars boring through young pillars. They hatch by the hundred when a spire collapses, riding the shockwave outward before the shards even land.  
2. Sun-skippers: lizards half the length of a handspan, scales plated with fused silica flakes that refract heat away. They drink by licking dew off lichen at dawn, vanish into shard-piles the instant a shadow passes.  
3. Echo kits: fox-like mammals with fur the colour of upended dusk. They prey on sun-skippers, but more often stalk the music itself, gnawing flute-holes in spires to shape hunting grounds whose notes confuse the lizards into stillness.  
4. Furnace swifts (summer cast): the same soot-black birds of the cavern, here only for the hottest months. They ascend the warm vapor plumes in spiral flocks, arriving ragged and ash-flecked. In the steppe they moult, replacing cave-feathers with glass-edged variants that scatter predators’ light. When the sodium bulbs far below begin their autumn flicker, the swifts glide back down the shafts, new-feathered, bringing plateau spores into the kiln-air like contraband.

BUILT ENVIRONMENT  
Human intrusion is sparse but telling.  
• Survey pylons—tripods of corroded alloy—lean drunkenly among the glass pillars, each crowned with a wind vane that has long since frozen in place, pointing nowhere useful.  
• A derelict pump house squats at the western edge, meant to draw meltwater from shattered spires and send it through insulated pipes back toward the canyon project. The pipes still lead there, half-buried, cracked in a hundred joints where sun-skippers bask. No one remembers why the pump house was abandoned; the eels in the cavern below now greedily drink whatever trickle still arrives.  
• Prayer flags: rectangles of mylar strung between younger pillars by pilgrims from the canyon mezzanine. Each bears the soot-print of a rat’s forepaw or the glyph of a fungal bloom—offerings to whatever entity keeps roof and root from collapsing into each other.

RULES OF THE STEPPE  
1. Heat rises, but ownership is negotiated: what the cavern breathes out, the steppe turns to skeleton, then relinquishes as dust.  
2. Nothing opaque survives long; transparency is armor here.  
3. Music is structural. Break the song of a spire and its walls craze within hours.  
4. Every surface is both mirror and blade; move slowly or leave red reminders.  
5. Collapse is not failure; it is reproduction.

STANDING STILL  
Hold yourself unmoving between two elder pillars at dawn. Cold stings your shins, vapor warms your lungs, and the sun shards into a hundred needles that prick every direction of sight at once. You will see your reflection multiplied, fainter with each bounce, until personhood feels like just another transient echo in the glass. Somewhere far beneath your boots, you might imagine the cavern’s sodium bulbs twitching, the chain mites singing, the half-assembled press exhaling a dream of stamping. Up here, those murmurs become the faint bass under the spire-song treble, two places harmonizing through stone, water, heat.

INHABITANTS UNSEEN  
1. Vapor shepherds: columns of rising warmth contain micro-currents that herd spores, insect eggs, even grains of pigment back and forth between depths and sky. No single shepherd is conscious, but together they script the migration calendar for everything with wings or wishes.  
2. The Glass Archivist: not a creature but a phenomenon—each season the wind engraves new Braille across spire surfaces. Those who have learned the touchscreen-slick language can read a year’s weather, every distant eruption, the tally of swifts hatched and lost. The archivist records until the pillar falls, then begins again on the shards.

COMMERCE, OF A SORT  
• What the steppe exports: slivers of cloudglass, carried down canyon in the feathers of returning swifts where they seed new flute-holes for the chain mites; frost-lichen spores hitchhiking on rat pelts, later blooming pear-green across condenser fins, improving heat exchange.  
• What it imports: lubricant mist exhaled by the pipe eels, rising through micro-fractures to coat young pillars, making them ring clearer; half-remembered hymns that cavern pilgrims hum up the lift-shaft, grafted straight into the wind’s repertoire.

EPILOGUE  
Some nights, when the magma pulse falters and the cavern sighs weaker than usual, the plateau listens. If enough spires happen to be tuned just so, their combined resonance funnels a song back down the very shafts that birthed them—a thin, shimmering descant riding the descending mist. Deep underground, sodium bulbs quiver, rats pause their drumming, and the unfinished press tilts one micron closer to alignment, as though answering a lullaby from an elder sibling born of its own escaped breath.

Between kiln and mirror, between heat that cannot hurry and glass that must break to grow, the world turns its slow impossible duet.
