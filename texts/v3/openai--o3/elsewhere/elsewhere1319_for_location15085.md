The Driftfield  

1. Provenance & Topography  
Picture a continent-length rain shadow cast by the living mesa you have just left. Every dusk the Plateau sloughs away whole skins of cellulose and resin. The wind takes that refuse sideways, never down, and lets it settle here in layer after crumbling layer. The result is neither desert nor prairie but an ever-moving graveyard of cuticle: dunes of photo-sheath, drifts of waxy scale, slabs of translucence piled like shattered glass glaciers. The ground you walk on is not earth but compressed epidermis—papery, fibrillated, shot through with the green ghosts of chloroplasts that still remember light. Subsurface pressure has welded the oldest mats into a flexible bedrock; step hard and the plain booms, taut as a drumhead stretched across buried hollows.  

2. Water (0.4/3, scarce)  
Moisture arrives only as mist that condenses on resin spines at dawn, each droplet tasting faintly of terpene and ozone—an echo of the Plateau’s breath. A single cloudburst may skip five local years. When it does come the ground refuses it, bead-shedding liquid the way living skin refuses rain. Puddles skitter sideways like mercury chased across parchment, never penetrating until they find a tear that leads downward into capillary caves.  

3. Atmosphere & Light (2.2/3, sun-bleached)  
Sky here begins where the Plateau’s rim ends; no updrafts churn, no permanent cloud deck loiters. Midday, light is unfiltered and multiplied: millions of curled cuticle flakes form a horizontal hall of mirrors, bouncing brightness in every direction until shadows dissolve. Sunstroke is less a risk than optical drowning—vision whites out, pupils cramp. At night the Driftfield keeps that stolen daylight, re-releasing it as a green afterglow that lingers for hours, a memory of photosynthesis still happening in dead skin.  

4. Temperature (2.7/3, brutal swing)  
Without mist-curtains to buffer it, heat rockets upward with the sun and plunges after dark. Resin plates buckle, snap, then anneal in the chill; every dawn the landscape has rearranged itself by thermal tectonics alone.  

5. Sound (0.9/3, whisper)  
Your footfalls are the loudest thing for kilometres. Otherwise: • dry flakes skating over one another, a parchment hiss; • the brittle ping of resin contracting at sundown; • a distant, almost sub-audible groan when a dune’s core collapses and the whole mound exhales latent gas collected from years of chemical slow-burn.  

6. Chemistry & Static  
Friction between cuticle shards builds a permanent electrostatic charge. Touch anything grounded—an outcrop of real rock, a tripod’s metal foot—and blue fireflies of plasma dance across your knuckles. Local life has weaponised this: certain filaments extrude piezoelectric needles that harvest static to split nitrogen directly from the air, turning lightning that never strikes into fertilizer that never leaches away.  

7. Living Cast & Guilds  

7.1 The Loamwrights  
Not soil, but the promise of it. These are microbial consortia that digest cellulose by sunlight alone, their enzymes photocatalytic. Each digester mat looks like lace rust: fragile, iron-red, spreading centimetres a year. Where two mats meet, they fuse and darken, beginning the long alchemy toward humus. The first true soil on the continent will be their child, in another ten thousand pulses.  

7.2 Glassmolt Beetles (fauna, finally)  
Length: a human thumb. Carapace: transparent chitin laced with condenser veins. They drink morning dew through capillary legs, then hide beneath cuticle panes during noon burn. Their mandibles secrete a solvent that etches lens patterns into discarded epidermis; biology here begins to sculpt the rubbish into optics. By twilight an abandoned beetle burrow resembles the iris of an enormous camera trained upward, waiting for whatever signals the Plateau writes across the upper sky.  

7.3 Ragworm Kites  
Annelid bodies flattened into kites of living silk. They rise on thermal gusts created when dark dunes abruptly flip a sun-polished side toward zenith. Once airborne they chew spores out of the wind—half meal, half foreign correspondence from the Plateau. They land only to pupate; the husk they leave becomes a sail flapping across the dunes, sounding out low notes that orient the next generation by echo.  

7.4 Archivist Polyps  
Fungal-animal hybrids that grow inside the resonant caves beneath the drift. Each polyp extrudes antennae of lignin scrap, tuned to the infrasonic language of the Plateau’s Sentinel-Spires hundreds of kilometres away. The caves throb with those translations, stored as standing waves in chambers lined with fatty crystal. Scholars come here to “listen” to decades of plateau thinking petrified into echo. None stay long; the thoughts are slow but not gentle.  

8. Dynamic Processes  

8.1 Skinfall Seasons  
Once every few hundred Plateau pulses, a coronal mass ejection upsets the Host’s magnetic sensors. The mesa answers with a whole-hemisphere molt—square kilometres of cortical crust shearing free at once. Jetstreams carry that avalanche of peel straight here. For days the sun vanishes behind a blizzard of green sleet; when it clears, dunes stand meters higher and the static field could power a city grid for a week.  

8.2 Thermal Flipstorms  
Occur when a dawn mist rarefies just as a midday scorch begins. Temperature differential hits three digits; sheets of cuticle levitate on boiling air, invert, then crash down edge-first like guillotines. Anything taller than a beetle risks being diced. Native life crouches, burrows, or flows flat until the storm tires.  

9. Built Environment (0.7/3, incidental)  
The only architecture is accidental: blown epidermis caught on resin spires forms vaults that focus dew into trickling fonts; beetle lens-etchings cluster into kaleidoscopic skylights; ragworm sails stitched by wind become walls. To a rushed visitor it may look abandoned; to the Driftfield it is enough.  

10. Technology (1.1/3, nascent)  
Here the Plateau’s discarded machinery experiments in miniature. Reflection shards with half-dead chloroplasts still juggle photons, and the Loamwrights hack those mechanisms, trial-running mutations the Host has not yet dared inside its living skin. The Driftfield is a beta-branch of continental code, testing ideas under harsher compilation flags.  

11. Rules of the World, Felt Differently  
Electromagnetic haze is weaker; your instruments behave, though compass needles twitch toward the plateau at dawn and dusk as if remembering a magnetic umbilicus. Gravity no longer stutters; the land here is too thin to flex. What you notice instead is time: without hydraulic thunder for metronome, moments drift, unmoored. You may walk an hour and discover the sun barely moved, or crouch to watch a beetle etch and look up to find stars already gathering.  

12. Scent & Taste  
Open your visor and the air is chalk dust and pine-resin. Lick a dew droplet and you’ll taste old leaf-litter souvenir’d from the plateau, plus a trace of nicotine-like alkaloid the Loamwrights exhale to deter beetles. It numbs the tongue, sharpens recall, and leaves a phantom sweetness behind—the Driftfield’s idea of hospitality.  

13. Inhabitants in the Larger Sense  
If the Plateau is a single dreaming creature, the Driftfield is its shed thought, still alive, trying on new shapes away from the parent body. Everything here is cast-off yet purposeful, scavenger yet pioneer. You are walking through a rehearsal for continents unborn: soil inventing itself, optics teaching themselves to see, static playing at thunder in the absence of hearts big enough to beat.  

14. Departure  
When you leave you carry filament dust in every fold of fabric. Under a microscope each filament coils like a question mark, waiting for moisture and a hint of band-light to uncurl. The Plateau calls that contamination; the Driftfield calls it emissary. Whichever voice you heed, remember: nothing that falls away here is ever merely lost.
