Cataract Reach  

They say the Plateau of Midnight ends the moment its rivers do, but the truth is stranger: the rivers do not end, they unfurl. For four vertical kilometres the brown, ore-laden water plummets through aerosol night, gathering speed, sound, steam, and half the stray seeds of the forest above. Then, with a concussion that can be heard even on the plateau rim, every drop strikes sunlight at once. The impact zone is Cataract Reach—a rift valley so deep it punches clean through the iron cloud deck and into day.

FIRST SIGHT  
Arrive by fall, and the world arranges itself in layers as you descend. Above, the sky is still that bruise-dark ceiling you know; midway down it thins to rust; below, abruptly, everything flares white-gold. The spray is so densely lit it looks solid, a mile-high column of opal glass. Locals call it the Pillar. Around it the Reach opens like a stone flower: terraced cliffs, radial gullies, and a braided delta of roaring surf that never sees an ocean.

TERRAIN AND WATER  
The valley floor is hammered limestone and recent travertine, quick-grown where carbonate-rich mist precipitates on sun-warmed rock. Over centuries the water has carved mazes—galleries, punchbowls, window holes—then glazed them with flowstone so clear you can read a compass through ten centimetres of calcite.

The rivers do not simply pool; they keep falling. Each main channel dives into a blue churn called the Lower Plunge, vanishing underground for a breath before re-emerging downstream as boiling springs. Those springs thread the delta in hundreds of braided courses, forever writing and erasing their own map. The ground quivers: not earthquake, but hydraulic resonance, a bass note set by the exact distance between plateau rim and valley floor.

MIST & LIGHT  
Sunlight here is a weapon. Reflected off a billion droplets, it burns skin and etches colour into anything that stays still—tools rust in new shades; fungus bleaches bone-white; even shadow seems to glow around the edges. Every few minutes the wind shreds a gap in the spray and a spear of undiluted radiance knifes across the Reach, igniting blooms of rainbow oil where gloam dust floats on eddies.

AIR & SOUND  
If the Plateau carries sound too well, the Reach murders it. The thunder of falling water saturates every frequency from infra to ultra. Speech must be shouted into cone-horns or written mid-air with bright silk streamers. Anything that wishes to be heard invents silence first: animals grow baffling ridges behind the jaw; huts sit in pits lined with felted bark; lovers converse nose-to-ear while patting code on each other’s ribs.

CLIMATE  
Days are blister-hot; nights plunge toward frost, for once the sun angles off the cliff wall the heat vents straight up the waterfall shaft. The diurnal whiplash drives crazy convection cells: at dusk the mist collapses into warm rain that falls sideways, so thick it looks like glass rods poured from a tilted kiln. By dawn the valley steams like soup.

VEGETATION—THE SUNDRINKERS  
1. Halcyon Palms: trunks twisted like drill bits, fronds coated in silica hairs that scatter light onto shaded stems beneath. Each palm grows a mantle of moss imported accidentally from the plateau; the moss, overwhelmed by brightness, releases excess energy as faint blue sparks after dark, turning entire groves into starfields.  
2. Shardgrass: blade-leaves stiff as slate, edges serrated with calcite. It grows only where travertine is youngest, rooting so fast it can split still-soft flowstone apart and steer rivers centimetre by centimetre.  
3. Ore-hunger Vines: descended from plateau creepers that rode the falls. They have swapped their sense of heat for hunger for iron; tendrils taste for ferric ions, twining toward rusty springs, cocooning lumps of gloam grit in fibrous baskets so the plant can sip warmth all night.

FAUNA—SEEING BEATS HEARING  
1. Prism Ibex: six-legged cliff runners with iridescent keratin shields over the eyes. They watch the water’s diffraction patterns for predator shadows. Their hooves strike sparks rich in magnesium—courtship by fireworks.  
2. Spray Swallows: tiny, mirror-feathered birds that nest inside the Pillar itself. Each impales mosquito-sized plankters on a barb under its tongue; the barb fluoresces, baiting more prey, a throat-hung lantern swinging inside the roar.  
3. Shiver Eels: inhabit the Lower Plunge. Blind, but flank-lines detect subtle pressure swirls in the chaos. Muscle contractions are so rapid the eels cavitate, surrounding themselves with bubbles that refract sunlight into dazzling strobes. Schools flash in synchrony, sickling predators with overload.

THE CHILDREN OF THE CATARACT  
The plateau knows them as rumour; here they simply call themselves Reachfolk.

Population & Dwelling  
• About 1 600 souls in river-scaffold villages, each hut a basket of palm rib and ore-hunger vine hung directly in spray so constant evaporative cooling keeps nights above freezing.  
• Streets are catwalks of fused travertine. Every dawn crews in goggles re-scribe the lanes, chiselling yesterday’s mineral bloom back into level.

Senses & Speech  
• Eardrums damped by congenital thickening; newborns have cranial sinuses filled with waxy ballast that ossifies by adolescence, anchoring the cochlea against thunder.  
• Language is colour and touch: streamers dyed with plateauside lichen pigments flapped in semaphore arcs; fingertip glyphs traced on forearm skin; and, for ceremony, the rare hush of a hollow cyst within the waterfall called the Lambent Vault.

Culture & Economy  
1. Lightcraft: Reachfolk weave mist and sunlight into practical optic devices—water-lenses big as doors, infracted mirrors that project the sky around corners, sun-traps that bake bread in a minute. Guild traders climb down rope elevators to buy these things at ruinous cost.  
2. Ore Tide Harvest: every flood brings crumbs of gloam ore made soluble by the plunge. When the crumbs crystallise on limestone they form “afterlight pearls,” dimly pulsing beads the size of tears. Plateau miners pay by the crate, then boast they have found a way to sell midnight its own heartbeat back.  
3. The Covenant of Roar: no hammering, ever. Reachfolk believe a sharp percussive sound may sync by unlucky fraction with the great noise above and hollow a cavity in the waterfall, starving the valley of mist. Punishment is exile upslope—sentenced to climb a bamboo ladder into dark silence few survive.

CONNECTIONS—THE THREADS THAT RUN BETWEEN  
• Mute Trade: Twice a season plateau guilds lower wicker coffers on piano-wire down the sheer face. The Reachfolk fill them with pearls, water-lenses, and moss-star cloth; the coffers return holding antibiotics, brass cochleal stents, and news tapped on the wire itself. Both sides read the vibrations through calloused fingertips.  
• Updraft Post: Hot valley air rises through the Pillar carrying spores, insect swarms, and sometimes written leaf-scrolls laminated in wax. Scholars on the rim catch them with frame nets, decoding Reach dialects weeks before the next funicular arrives.  
• The Thousand-Hour Echo: Once every few years a perfect atmospheric inversion sends a shard of plateau machinery noise—an autohammer aria—down the chasm wall. The sound arrives stripped of its higher harmonics, slowed, majestic. Reachfolk treat it as ancestral chant; they answer on great drum-sails woven of ibex gut, a reply that, drifting upward, is believed to calm the widow hammers for days.

MACHINES GONE TO VALLEY  
• Spill-kites: Fabric scraps, once canopy repair kits, now stitch themselves (with the help of zealous pipeline ferrets blown off course) into autonomous delta-wings. They harvest sunlight for nothing in particular, except that some evenings they crane their bolted necks plateau-ward, as though remembering home.  
• The Lens Engine: A ruin of a surveyor’s heliograph, lenses scorched blind by too much day. Reach children play inside its ribs; at noon the iron frame focuses scattered beams into a pulse hot enough to brand stone. Older children claim to aim that pulse at cloud fissures, etching temporary skylights for fun.

WEIRDNESS, RULES, UNDERLYING LOGIC  
1. Photonic Glut: Plants must dissipate energy lest they combust. Many channel surplus photons into bioluminescent excretions that spatter the night with cold fire.  
2. Sonic Occlusion: Continuous broadband roar sets an acoustic floor. Anything quieter than 90 dB is effectively silent; predators evolve garish colours because camouflage by muteness is enough.  
3. Heat Debt: Travertine precipitates are endothermic—growth sucks local warmth. Stand on a newborn flowstone shelf too long and it will steal the heat from your boots, then your blood. Reachfolk wear sandals of aerated glass that float on their own trapped sunshine.

DAILY DRAMA—A MID-AFTERNOON SNAPSHOT  
High noon. Mist hides the plateau rim, leaving only the sun uncompromised. A prism ibex stumbles on wet quartz; shards of magnesium hoof-spark spatter into a spray swallow rookery. The birds explode from the Pillar, a sleet of mirrors, scattering light so violently a sky-trawling spill-kite loses lift and whirls down among shardgrass blades that could slice a dirigible. Children sprint out, streamers flicking semaphore: kite in trouble, bring vine-loops. Drums? No, never drums—colours suffice.

Up on the rim a guild foreman watches through an import lens big as his torso. He sees none of the panic, only a trembling rainbow in the valley’s throat and, in the foreground, another crate of pearls ready for hoisting. He taps the wire in six terse glyphs: debt settled, send more.

Far below, the spill-kite hiccups, sheds a ballast pouch of photoluminescent gel. The gel plops into the river, and instantly the water answers—glowing serpentine as gloam grit meets Reach sunlight for the first time. Night on the plateau will soon drink that borrowed light, carried back in miner canteens.

VISITING & LEAVING  
• To arrive: Rope-chair or coracle free-fall inside a hollow bamboo sleeve that keeps you from ricocheting off rock. Bring sugar lozenges; the pressure change bruises lungs.  
• To depart: Ride the dawn updraft in a spill-kite domesticated for passengers. You must flap the tension panels in antiphase to the waterfall’s thunder or be slapped back into the Pillar, a gaudy fish for the swallows. Seasoned pilots claim to read the thunder like thermals, letting it sling them over the cloud deck and onto the plateau lip in one magnificent, soaking arc.

STAND STILL A MOMENT  
When the sun slants just so, the roar folds around your body and you feel—for a heartbeat—the same humid hand the Plateau placed on your shoulder, only warmer, brighter, insistent. Two halves of one long sentence: Midnight exhales; Cataract Reach inhales. Between those breaths run rivers, ore, rumours, and people who refuse to choose between darkness and day.
