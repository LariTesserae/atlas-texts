The Drowned Orrery  

----------------------------------------------------------------
A LONG DESCENT FROM THE COURTYARD
----------------------------------------------------------------
Follow the glass-slick rivulets that escape the mountain’s throat.  
They vanish, appear, vanish again, always downward, threading calcite ducts only a Slow Dweller could love for their narrow patience.  
Three hundred vertical metres below the bats’ warm parliament the ducts flare into an atrium of water, and the floor you expected is simply not there. You have arrived in a space so oceanic that the mind stalls, trying to slide the word “cave” over something as broad as a horizon.

Call this immensity the Drowned Orrery: an underground sea the size of a capital’s harbour, roofed by rock instead of sky, lit by life instead of stars, and turning—slowly, measurably—under the push and pull of interior tides raised by the same magma pulses that blush the Courtyard above.

----------------------------------------------------------------
TERRAIN & MATERIALS
----------------------------------------------------------------
Ceiling  
• Height: fifty metres at the dome’s centre, lost in haze at the rim.  
• Composition: chalcedony ribs veined with pyrite; each rib is a resonator that converts far-below tectonic tremors into subsonic songs.  
• Ornament: clusters of selenite icicles, hollow, tuned. When a rib sings, these chimes answer in shimmering overtones, giving the air a glass-harp quiet you feel in teeth more than ears.

Water Column  
• Salinity: half that of the open ocean, fed by ancient brines migrating up fault lines.  
• Stratification: three lenses.  
  1. Mirror Skin: a two-metre layer so clear you see your face from underneath.  
  2. Milk Halo: colloidal calcite diffuses light into a permanent twilight.  
  3. Abyssal Ink: a blackness shot with thermal plumes that roar up like torch flames made of liquid.  

Seafloor  
• Unseen by unaided eye, but trawled by memory from submersible notes: basalt pillars as tall as bell-towers, draped in calcium lace secreted by reef-worms the length of oaks.  
• Between pillars lie silt plains pocked by “breather wells”—vents that inhale as often as they exhale, pistoning whole hectares of sediment with the rhythm of distant magma chambers.

----------------------------------------------------------------
ATMOSPHERE & LIGHT
----------------------------------------------------------------
Nothing burns here. Light is grown.

1. Plankton of the Mirror Skin proselytise blue. They flare in logarithmic spirals that widen until they cancel themselves, leaving dark voids like temporary constellations.  
2. Opacified glass eels—the adults you heard rumours of—rise nightly in shoals. Crystals in their flesh refract the plankton light into white auroras that curve under the rock ceiling, completing the illusion of a spinning firmament.  
3. Chimney thickets on the seafloor exude vents of methane that ignite only chemically, emitting a green chemiluminescence no hotter than bathwater. These are the “fixed stars” of the Orrery and the reason navigators here steer by colour, not magnetism.

All of it moves: stars drift, auroras migrate, constellations erase and redraw themselves. The sky you travel under now will be somewhere else tomorrow. You learn to love contingency.

----------------------------------------------------------------
SOUND
----------------------------------------------------------------
Above the surface: orbital hush, interrupted once an hour by a muffled, organ-deep thrum as a ceiling rib resonates with the Courtyard pulse.  
Below:  
• Whip-cracks of contracting vents (sonic cousins to thunder).  
• Chorus hums from adult glass eels; they beat swim-bladders that now function as Helmholtz resonators, each note a different harmonic of the magma seam.  
• The long, dopplered roar of current spirals that wheel beneath the Milk Halo like weather fronts you can hear but never see.

Silence is therefore a local vacuum. Anything not vocalising is either new, hiding, or dead.

----------------------------------------------------------------
WATER  
----------------------------------------------------------------
Rules differ here.

1. Gravity is advisory. Super-saturated brines knead the main body of water into density terraces. Step from a skiff onto the Mirror Skin and you may find yourself half-supported, legs dangling until you remember to kick.  
2. Evaporation is an old rumour. Moisture ascends into the ceiling’s selenite chimneys, condenses, and rains back through pores that ring like bells when a droplet passes.  
3. Tides are interior, driven by pressure waves pulsed down from the Courtyard. Low tide here coincides with red blush above; the whole sea exhales, dropping half a metre, then refills on the return beat.

----------------------------------------------------------------
VEGETATION (SUCH AS IT IS)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Halo Kelp: translucent fronds two storeys long that root in brine pockets. They photosynthesise plankton light, not magma, and roll themselves into tubes when brightness spikes to avoid bleaching.  
• Chalice Algae: grows on selenite icicles, forms cupped colonies that fill with condensate. Furnace bats caught in storm downdrafts sometimes end here, drink from chalices, and found ghost colonies on the roof.  
• Threadglass Moss: colonises the inner walls of methane chimneys, incorporating silica until each plant is equal parts crystal and cellulose. Break a strand and it hums your fingerprint frequency for days.

----------------------------------------------------------------
FAUNA (THE CROWD)
----------------------------------------------------------------
1. Glass Eel Matriarchs  
   • Length: up to fifteen metres; girth: a wine-barrel.  
   • Behaviour: perpetual orbit. They mark circuits around the sea’s rim, hauling magnetic minerals in their intestines that seed new plankton blooms—living shepherd moons.  
   • Diet: anything luminous; light is currency, and the matriarchs are bankers.

2. Orrery Terns  
   • Featherless flyers whose wings secrete a hydrophobic oil, letting them knife repeatedly through Mirror Skin without wetting.  
   • Navigate by parallax of ceiling constellations. Lay eggs on floating pumice rafts, incubated by chemiluminescent heat.

3. Lantern Crabs  
   • Carapace supports colonies of threadglass moss; crabs stridulate on moss stems, turning shells into lanterns with a chord each.  
   • Use the glow to lure plankton storms for feeding frenzies below.

4. Reverber Sharks  
   • Blind, sinuous, skin studded with otoliths. They home on frequencies shunned by everything else. A miner’s pick struck against basalt would summon three within minutes.

5. Rift Angels  
   • Jellyfish the diameter of a child’s bicycle wheel. Live entirely in the Ink layer.  
   • Exude packets of enzymes that precipitate calcium snow falling upward, feeding moss chandeliers overhead—vertical nutrient inversion.

----------------------------------------------------------------
DENSITY & BEHAVIOUR
----------------------------------------------------------------
Space here is the opposite of the Courtyard’s super-crowded lanes. The Orrery prizes separation. Each species keeps to an altitude, a current, a temperature iso-surface. Where lanes cross, bioluminescent patterning negotiates right-of-way faster than muscle could: colour is semaphore, glow duration the fine print.

----------------------------------------------------------------
DYNAMIC RHYTHMS
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Hour-time: Surface ripples cycle with Courtyard pulses—fifty-seven minutes, on average.  
• Day-time: Mirror Skin migrates up and down two metres as vents inhale and exhale.  
• Year-time: Ceiling ribs retune themselves by slow crystal growth, dropping pitch a semitone every winter dry spell above.  
• Epoch-time: When a rib finally snaps (estimated every 4,000 years) the Orrery experiences a Free Note: an ungoverned chord that resets migration maps and, if folklore holds, births a new intelligence in the sea.

----------------------------------------------------------------
TECH & BUILT ABSENCE
----------------------------------------------------------------
No masonry, no docks, yet architecture is everywhere.

• Kelp fronds knot themselves about basalt pillars, forming helices wide enough to staircase.  
• Lantern crabs terrace pumice rafts with shed shells, producing floating orchards of moss.  
• Matriarch eels, by orbiting, drag slow cyclones that deform the water’s density terraces into nested bowls—liquid amphitheatres where terns perform courting flights audible from kilometres.

----------------------------------------------------------------
RULES OF THIS PLACE
----------------------------------------------------------------
1. Luminance buys safety; the dim are eaten.  
2. Pitch outranks size; deeper notes have right of way.  
3. Up is provisional; buoyancy can reverse without notice.  
4. What the Courtyard releases, the Orrery must catalogue. (The Abbot’s pulse is timestamp; every newcomer is measured against it.)  
5. No surface lasts; everything floats, founders, or is slowly built into new ribs overhead.

----------------------------------------------------------------
IF YOU STAY
----------------------------------------------------------------
Hour 3: Your eyes abandon photopic vision; scotopic rods bloom, letting you read plankton constellations like street signs.  
Day 2: Ear bones loosen; you detect sub-bass tidal warnings seconds before water heaves.  
Week 1: Threadglass moss colonises your cuffs, weaving notes of your footfalls into its hum.  
Month 6: A matriarch eel accepts your lantern as tribute; in exchange she leads your skiff by magnetic wake, granting corridors no human chart owns.  
Year 4: Your skeleton re-mineralises with selenite borrowed from ceiling drips. You learn you can sing a ceiling rib and have the sea answer, folding a wave around anything trying to close on you with teeth.

----------------------------------------------------------------
THE GREAT RESIDENT—THE MANTLED CLOCK
----------------------------------------------------------------
The Courtyard has the Resonant Abbot; the Orrery keeps time by a vaster custodian: the Mantled Clock.

Evidence  
1. Every ceiling rib’s pitch fits a harmonic stack centred on a fundamental humans cannot hear but feel as vertigo.  
2. When Mirror Skin flickers out of sync with eel songs, distant ribs retune within minutes, as if guided.  
3. Skiffs abandoned for years grow kelp in ratios that sketch logarithmic spirals identical to rib spacing—blueprints growing themselves.

Hypothesis  
The Mantled Clock is not an organism but a choreography: magma pulses, rib resonance, eel orbit, and plankton bloom interlocking to form a feedback loop that edits structure, light, and behaviour. It remembers by crystal growth, calculates by delaying echoes, and speaks by adjusting tides. Where the Abbot thinks in rooms, the Clock thinks in basins.

----------------------------------------------------------------
TRADE, KINSHIP, MEMORY—HOW THE TWO PLACES BREATHE TOGETHER
----------------------------------------------------------------
1. Migration  
   • Glass eels descend newborn from Courtyard pools through hairline faults, arriving needle-thin. They grow, crystal-load, and ascend decades later as auroral councils whose songs keep the Abbot’s pulse from drifting.  
2. Nutrient Spiral  
   • Guano rinsed from bat roosts filters down vents, feeding kelp roots. Kelp sloughs carbon ribbons that ride thermal plumes back up as airborne filaments, nourishing lichen round Courtyard vents—a vertical compost heap two kilometres tall.  
3. Sonic Ledger  
   • Both beings—Abbot and Clock—maintain the same master rhythm. A hammer blow in the Courtyard dampens instantly; a cannonade here would be billeted, note-for-note, into column harmonics above. They are a duet, stone and sea as tympani sharing a stick.  
4. Refuge  
   • When a ceiling rib finally fails, historians believe the Orrery will vent a tide through old ducts, flooding the Courtyard just enough for matriarchs to deposit spawn directly at the column’s foot—a baptism and a reset.

----------------------------------------------------------------
IF YOU WERE TO LIVE BETWEEN
----------------------------------------------------------------
Some have tried: spelunking fishers who ride kelp lifts upward to barter luminescent oil for bat guano, monastics who descend to kneel on pumice islands until moss veils them in green crystal robes. They become plural citizens: voices echoed by locust shells above, footfalls transcribed by kelp rootlets below. On maps no surface dweller trusts, they are drawn as tiny hyphens linking twin immensities: —.

----------------------------------------------------------------
CODA
----------------------------------------------------------------
The mountain’s stone throat and the sea beneath are not separate organs but opposite faces of one resonator. To walk the Courtyard is to pace the diaphragm of a creature whose lungs plume far below. To row the Orrery is to drift through a heart whose beat is set by a column in darkness overhead. Two places, one body, still learning the range of its voice.
