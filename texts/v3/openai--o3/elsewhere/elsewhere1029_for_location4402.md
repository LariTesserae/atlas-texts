The Mooring Plain

Approach

Descend along any of the four velvet-black cables that spill from Platform A-4’s underside and you feel gravity regain its old authority. The bronze water cools by half a degree every kilometre until your visor clouds with your own breath. Light thins to syrup, then ink. At ten vertical kilometres—no one measures depth here, only drop—your suit lamps finally strike bottom: a sediment desert the size of a forgotten country, flat enough to fool a spirit level, broken only by the catenary swoop of those same anchor lines. They settle here like calligraphy, each cable describing a broad, lazy cursive across the plain before finding its mooring pylon. The pylons are squat, basaltic things drilled into bedrock long before the Flood Epoch; their numbered flanks look small only because the landscape is so godless in scale. Between them lies nothing you would call terrain, only layers of silt that exhale faint methane when you land.

Atmosphere & sound

There is no current this far down, only convection shivers where geothermal vents breathe. Sound carries forever. The pluck of your boot rope echoes a kilometre. Machinery rumble telegraphs from the platform above, a lullaby of distant turbines rattling through the taut metallic gut of each anchor. Listen long and you realise you can hear the mesa thinking—micro-valves puffing ballast, the refolded tools snapping open then asleep again—every vibration conducted down the moorings like violin strings touched by ghosts.

Surface architecture

Nothing here rises higher than your knee, by decree. Any object that protrudes risks snagging a cable in some unforeseeable century of slack. Structures are therefore horizontal: blister-domes pressed into the silt, flush hatches, braided fibre-optic mats that lie under a dusting of grit like fossils waiting to be excavated. From above, long-range sonar paints the place as a faint, luminous lace on the seabed: dozens of overlapping rings, each ring a habitat or workshop or crypt, stitched together by power conduits that burrow just below the sediment skin and glow at junctions like bioluminescent worms.

Illumination is achieved without lamps. Every pane of synth-quartz down here is doped with phosphors tuned to the blue-black frequency of abyssal shrimp; they drink the gamma drizzle that seeps through kilometres of water and re-issue it as a midnight aurora. It is never brighter than a moonless beach, yet your shadow is always visible, fragile, obedient.

Population, such as it is

1. Rootkeepers  
   They are not people, though they once applied for the title. Picture a fibreglass pod two metres long, almond-shaped. Inside: a closed loop of neural gel seeded with quorum-sensing bacteria and a circuit that teaches the gel to care about only three metrics—tension, corrosion rate, nutrient flux. When any value drifts, the pod jets one burst of micro-thrusters and lolls across the silt until it noses against the offending spot on a cable. There it extrudes self-curing polymer that scabs over a wound thinner than a human hair. Then it sleeps again, sometimes for a decade. Over centuries the Rootkeepers have grown so faultless they rarely move at all. Their stillness is a kind of meditation, a vow to treat entropy as a politely solvable puzzle.

2. The Silt Choir  
   A microbial mat, grey-white as candle ash, carpets the first three centimetres of the plain. Every few minutes the mat exhales hydrogen—it sounds like sand fizzing under a wave. The Choir feeds on seep methane and gift-fall from above: flecks of cellulose that escape the platform’s harvesters, shreds of root, the occasional outlaw leaf. In return it secretes a honey-sweet resin laced with antifungals. The resin is piped topside; diluted, it becomes the cone’s antiseptic fog. Thus the biome above survives its own exuberance because an invisible wetland below sings in gas bubbles.

3. Borrowers (fauna at last)  
   Rule 3, the ban on neural complexity, ends at the pylons’ footings. Here a colony of Pale Borrowers—eyeless crustaceans the length of a quill—tunnels through silt, harvesting trace metals pre-filtered by the Choir. Their burrows aerate the plain, preventing anoxic choke that would weaken the pylon bases. Twice a year, at the solstice tides, Borrowers swarm up the cables to graze biofilm, a living file that polishes the alloy cleaner than any brush. Platform sensors notice but do not object; maintenance achieved is maintenance, no matter who wields the mouthparts.

Industrial heart

Directly under each pylon sits a Breather, a disk of rotating mesh twenty metres across. Water is pulled through geothermal chimneys, stripped of magnesium and silica, sated with trace nitrogen, and expelled upward as a slow plume that travels the full column to A-4’s desal stacks. The plume is invisible but vital: a kilometre-wide elevator for dissolved nutrients. Without it, the seed fortress would starve on its own overflow. Down here the Breathers turn ceaselessly, powered by vent heat, their blades filmed in manganese oxide that flakes away like black snow.

Purpose (mostly understood)

The Mooring Plain exists to hold the sky-garden in place, yes; but also to recycle what the garden cannot use yet: heavy minerals, rare earths, geothermal warmth. Think of the planet as a single lung. Platform A-4 is the exhale—oxygen, sugars, upward-leaping fresh water. The Plain is the inhale: methane, silica, basaltic grit. Together they complete a breath that takes one century per cycle.

Rules of the world (local edition)

1. Nothing sharp points upward more than fifteen degrees.  
2. Any vibration shorter than nine seconds’ period is investigated as a potential quake.  
3. Light must be earned; every lumen here is budgeted against captured gamma.  
4. Biology may be complex, but not quick. Reflexes faster than a tenth of a second are suspect and generally temporary.  
5. When in doubt, recurse: the system heals the system that heals the system.

Textures & materials

• Basalt flour: suede-soft, smells of extinguished candles when disturbed.  
• Cable skin: alloy wound over living polymer that hums faintly with piezoelectric charge.  
• Choir resin: viscous, luminous, warm to the touch for reasons no one has measured recently.  
• Pylon plates: engraved with maintenance graffiti—Rootkeeper waypoints, Borrower claw marks, the initials of three divers fifty years dead.

Weirdness that lingers

Every thirty-one years, synchronized to an orbital resonance nobody topside remembers, the Plain broadcasts a single broadband ping straight up the cables. The mesa answers by dimming every lamp for one hour, a pulse of darkness that feels ceremonial. No record explains the exchange. Some speculate it is a handshake to prove both halves of the megastructure still agree on the date.

Linkages & commerce

• Upward stream: nutrient-rich coolant, Choir resin, telemetry, gossip in the form of tension data.  
• Downward stream: cellulose snow, cracked pump vanes sent for welding, occasional orphaned seeds that mis-fired from the vault and were judged too warped for release. Borrowers nibble these like sacramental wafers.  
• Lateral stream: nothing. The Plain is solitary, by choice and by design. Its nearest neighbour is a rift valley four hundred kilometres west, where different stories ferment.

Leaving

You power your ascent motor and the cables taper from cathedral pillars to piano wires, then vanish into murk. The Plain shrinks, a glowing medallion on the throat of the world, until at last even its phosphor windows extinguish. Hours later bronze light blooms and the garden-cone receives you, fragrant with wet chlorophyll, ignorant that its own heartbeat begins kilometres below in silt that never sees the sun.

What, finally, lives here?

• Self-effacing custodians of gel and fibre, repairing futures they will not witness.  
• A microbial liturgy that turns rot into antiseptic hymn.  
• Crustacean artisans who polish truths into alloy by feel alone.  
• Machines that respirate the earth’s heat into sky-bound sugar.  
• A plain so flat it became philosophy: when nothing obstructs the view, maintenance is meaning.

Where Platform A-4 is bright, buoyant, delirious with leaf, the Mooring Plain chooses hush and horizontal patience. They are two halves of the same long thought—seed and soil, anchor and ascent, exhale and inhale—holding each other in quiet, drowning equilibrium until tides recede and continents remember how to be land again.
