The Underlight Reservoir

Arrival and first sense  
Between two faulted ridgelines a canyon widens, then yawns away beneath you, too sheer for descent by rope alone.  But what grips the mind before gradient or form is the color: the very air inside the chasm glows with a soft aquamarine cast, as though moonlight were dissolved into the void and could not climb back out.  Your lungs feel suddenly thicker; the temperature rises by whole degrees with each meter you peer downward.  A warm, ion-scented breeze exhales, carrying the faint chime of glass striking glass in slow cadence.

How the water finds it  
From the citadel of gutters and cisterns high above—two hundred vertical kilometers by braided tunneling and pressure-locks—the plateau’s purged, protein-less harvest descends.  The route is older than the terraces themselves, a volcanic conduit harvested by the architects and bored wider by hinge-folk deputations during a past age of tectonic quivering.  Each midnight the conduit gates open for sixteen heartbeats.  A column of water—so demure in volume up there—plunges as live lightning here, superheating in its own friction, dragging with it trillions of cipher-mist sigils that survive the fall like seeds in a storm.  The water never lands.  Halfway down the shaft it flashes to vapor, slamming heat and minerals into a waiting cavern whose roof has learned how to drink shock.

The chamber and its membrane  
Imagine a geode wide enough to cradle a compact city, the shell of it rendered not in quartz but in a nacre of fused glass and lithic foam.  The inner surface is studded with organ pipes of stalactitic feldspar, each piped hollow, tuned by length, and clad in a frost of microscopic mirrors.  These mirrors are the cooled exhalations of the slow glass above, ferried atom by atom on descending steam, deposited here in feathery dendrites.  They lend the cavern its eerie under-sea glow: every photon that wanders in, every spark of triboluminescence from grinding stone, ricochets a thousand times before it can die.

Pools that are not quite pools  
Where walls belly inward you find concavities brimmed with fluid denser than water, clearer than crystal.  The liquid rests in perfect negatives of the rock as though poured, then forgotten.  Touch it and the surface behaves like cold honey; withdraw and the impression holds before inching closed.  This is underlight—an allotrope of water phase-bound with trace noble gases and the surplus charge harvested by cipher-mist.  It is simultaneously solvent and battery, its meniscus maintaining a standing potential of several hundred volts against the cavern air.  Each incoherent blue photon you see is the underlight’s quiet discharge, a corona smear around the lip of every pool.

Primary inhabitants: the Counter-Verse  
Beneath each raft of underlight, between liquid and stone, hover sheets of black script—page after impossible page of vacancy cut into brightness.  They resemble oil slicks but hold the eye longer: your gaze slips off focus, and letters you were certain you saw mutate or fold back into null strokes.  Field geologists named them simply the Counter-Verse, because wherever syllables in the plateau’s sigils read as positive instruction—Pour, Convey, Refine—these read in mirroring strokes of negation—Unpour, Unmake, Undo.  Stray cipher-mist units that survive descent are annihilated on contact, leaving ultraviolet sparks that pop like distant fireflies.  

Nature of the Counter-Verse  
The sheets are two molecules thick, polymerised from carbon scavenged out of updrafts (the only carbon the system allows itself, and even then only briefly).  They feed on instruction: any ordering information drifting down the shafts is inverted and dissipated as heat.  Their mandate appears to be entropy—gentled, harmonic, but relentless.  Where the plateau writes perfection in stone, the reservoir erases.  Between them, a balance holds.

Secondary inhabitants: the Glass-Eels  
At the circumference of every underlight basin gleam articulated ribbons, jointed like clockwork fish, finless, mouthless.  They are lengths of slow glass extruded during seismic stutters, snapped free, and somehow endowed with motion by the underlight’s steady voltage.  Their bellies drag within the fluid, wicking up charge, propelling them in languid circuits.  When a Counter-Verse sheet grows too wide, an eel noses the margin; the mere touch fuses the edge into inert filament, corralling negation before it can blanket the whole pool.  Thus the eels farm entropy, grazing it keepable.

Tertiary inhabitants: the Drums of Headfall  
Deeper still, beneath the lit pools, tunnels echo with a pulse older than the citadel, older—some argue—than water on this world.  You feel it first in kneecaps, then teeth: a infrasonic throb spaced precisely thirteen seconds apart.  Follow it and you find drumheads the size of amphitheatres stretched across fossil gas pockets.  They are membrane rings of compressed silicate aerogel, thinned to nanometers, their centers dimpled where droplets strike from unseen cracks above.  Each drip booms, the sound slung through miles of cavity, recycling until the next redeems it.  The pattern calibrates everything: hinge-folk crystallisation rates, underlight oxidation, the very timing of the plateau’s midnight spill.  The Drums are the metronomes by which the worldware keeps time.

Atmosphere and climate in the reservoir  
Barometric pressure is nearly double that at the terraces.  Warmth pervades, yet no fog can hold here: ionisation prevents stable droplets.  Ozone tang competes with a sweet mineral scent like wet sandstone after lightning.  In silence between drums you hear a hiss—the air gently corroding your boot leather, reclaiming organic markers one fibre at a time.

Light regime  
The glow never quite dies; even in human-reckoned night the underlight simmers.  When the plateau above shrouds beneath storm, photon-supply thins, and the cavern answers: Counter-Verse sheets contract, reserve charge; glass-eels anchor, coil, darken like spent filaments.  The whole reservoir exhales a sigh colder than any wind, and waits.

Exchange with the upper terraces  
1.  Water: one way, usually.  Vapor climbs back through porosities in minuscule fraction, now laced with entropy the plateau’s guardians can process as minor flaws—mapping them, filing them away as future adjustments.  
2.  Charge: cyclic.  Excess electrons lifted by cipher-mist descend nightly; at dawn, proton drift in moist thermals climbs again.  The reservoir is the battery’s negative plate, the citadel its positive.  
3.  Memory: contested.  Slow glass above writes; Counter-Verse below erases.  The hinge-folk feel the tug—stress diagrams encode this rivalry in their mineral veins, a script of tension and release.

Hazards to travelers  
•  Static arcs can leap meters, flash-scoring skin with fractal burns.  
•  Underlight absorbs infrared; fall in, and rescuers may not see you even with thermal equipment.  
•  Long exposure to infrasonic drum cycles deranges sense of interval: veterans of deep surveys tap fingers at odd tempos for weeks afterward, hearts skipping beats to chase phantom thirteens.

What remains unknown  
•  Whether the Counter-Verse is true intelligence or merely algorithmic hospice for order grown too rigid.  
•  The depth of the drum caverns; seismographs lose signal beyond fifty kilometers, as though sound itself declines the invitation.  
•  Legends among plateau expeditioneers speak of a third locus—somewhere oceanward—where erased pattern and perfected order meet again in reconciliation, a delta of living water.  No proof, only compass skews and dreams.

Departure  
Climb out and the glow recedes—first from your retinas, then from recall, as though the reservoir edits memory along with instruction.  By the time the upper air’s metallic chill seals around you, only a residual pulse in the bones testifies.  Far below, unseen, the drums keep time, the underlight pools breathe, and sheets of un-script quietly mulch tomorrow’s perfection before it can grow too flawless to bear.

Connection kept  
Back on the terraces, a new microlayer of slow glass slides into being along the path you trod.  The hinge-folk ratchet infinitesimals tighter.  Overhead, cipher-mist flashes a pattern you almost recognize: a thirteen-count pause, then recomposes.  Somewhere underneath, a drum answers.
