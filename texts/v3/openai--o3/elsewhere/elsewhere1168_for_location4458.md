The Choirfen Basin

What leaves the plateau in a hundred hush-soaked trickles does not stay quiet for long.  
Sixty air-miles downslope, where fractures in the basalt knit shut and the land slumps into an antique depression, those same runnels burst upward again as artesian springs. They arrive bearing copper ions, Verdigris spores, quartz silt and a memory of impossible silence—then spend the rest of their journey unlearning it. In the hollow that receives them, water discovers its missing voice and teaches everything it touches to sing.

────────────────────────
1. Terrain  
   • Elevation 0.2/3 (low) meets Sedimentation 2.8/3 (old): picture a saucer forty kilometres wide, floored by peat the colour of coals just before they go out. The basin’s rim is scalloped; every scallop is a drowned landslide frozen mid-flow. Deltas made of powder-fine slate creep inward like grey tongues, but never meet.  
   • The ground lacks true solidity. Step once and you feel it breathe; step twice in the same print and a bubble of warm artesian gas sighs up around your heel. Stone is scarce except where the old landslides show their teeth—fluted pillars of compacted scree, black outside, silver where rain has slit them. Locals call them organ-pipes.  
   • Beneath the peat lies a down-bowed layer of glass. Centuries of silica-rich reeds, struck by plateau lightning and fused under their own ashes, have produced a translucent pan: stand on a thin spot and the subtle give tells you the fen is balanced, bowl-like, on its own mirror.

2. Vegetation & Density  
   Lushness 1.9/3, Loudness 3.0/3.  
   • Fen-reeds rise four metres, culms ridged with quartz. When wind flexes them they rasp like bowstrings. The flower-plumes are packed with mica hairs; any sudden drop in humidity makes the heads hiss as the tiny plates rub.  
   • Between reeds sprawls Copperscour Moss, descendant of Verdigris spores that rode down in subterranean seepage. In the plateau’s thin air the parent vine braided toward metal; here the child moss sprawls flat and bright, eating whatever trace ions the springs provide. Its surface glows dull teal at dusk, then gutters out before nightfall as the charge drains.  
   • Floating on the pools are Lily-Diaphragms: pads so thin they boom faintly when struck by rain. In storms the whole swamp becomes a kettle-drum, every drop adding one note to a roar that can be heard two valleys over.

3. Water & Sound  
   • Volume 2.7/3: water here thinks it is air. It rises as bubble-strings, falls as dew, hangs as mist. Unlike on the plateau its movement is never secret; every drip finds something resonant.  
   • Resonance lies at the basin’s core rule. Sound travels farther than light: a hand-clap bounces between reed walls nine, ten, eleven times before it fades. Even quiet talk will pick up harmonics from the glass pan beneath and arrive at the listener in chord-form, the original sentence riding its own echo like a surfer on a wave.  
   • The artesian mouths—twenty-three mapped, more suspected—each sing on a separate pitch determined by flow rate. The combined chord shifts hour by hour as springs wax or slacken, giving the fen a slow, tidal melody.

4. Fauna  
   4a. Gong Cranes  
      Height two metres. Sternum grown into a hollow bronze-coloured plate lined with keratin. When males land, the plate strikes water and booms like a temple bell, stunning fish. Courtship is a duet of counter-beats that ricochet across miles.  
   4b. Mirecarvers  
      Amphibious mammal-fish with chiselled snouts. They tunnel through peat, heaving out ventilator chimneys that whistle as gases escape. A fresh chimney is the surest sign the ground beneath is close to collapsing into one of the basin’s drowned caverns.  
   4c. Glassmoths  
      Wingspan palm-width, wings transparent except for a veinwork of copper scavenged as larvae from Copperscour mats. In moonlight they flash circuitry-bright; predators find them impossible to focus on because each wing flick throws a decoy echo ahead of the real body.  
   4d. Thunderbrood  
      The only true school-forming vertebrate in the world of hush and resonance: finger-length fish whose swim bladders include a gas pocket separated by chitinous valves. Thousands surface before a storm, pop the valves in synchrony and seed the clouds with percussive cracks that trigger lightning—an adaptation that fuses more reed-glass and thus expands their habitat.

5. Built & Tech  
   • Where a delta tongue thrusts farthest inward stands the Fenforge: a trestlework factory half-sunk in peat, kept aloft by buoyant log rafts thick with tar. It houses the Oreless Foundries—smelters that do not melt metal but sound. Ceramic retorts capture the basin’s omnipresent low G drone and refine it into standing waves stored in stone sarcophagi.  
   • Products are Resonance Rods: basalt cylinders riddled with tuned boreholes. When struck they emit complex over-tones capable of mapping cavern systems or, in skilled hands, cancelling the boom of Gong Cranes to steal eggs unseen.  
   • Currency is measured in silence. A trader brings a Whisper-Jar sealed on the plateau—interior dead space, no echo at all—pops the cork, and barters the jarful of hush for tools. The Linewalkers prize these jars to quiet faulty culverts; the Fenforge prizes the hush to temper their rods. The loop closes under its own physics.

6. Inhabitants – The Tide-Ear Guild  
   • Physiology: stockier than Linewalkers, lungs average, but middle ear ossicles massively enlarged. Skull x-rays look like a crown of spurs. Guild children learn to locate a single mosquito by doppler in a storm.  
   • Dress: cloaks woven from Reed-prism fibres; at rest they appear drab, but a sharp whistle makes the fibres diffract and bloom in oil-slick colours—the closest thing fenguards have to lanterns in night mist.  
   • Habitation: stilt-villages on peat-bergs, every beam packed with eel-bladders for buoyancy. Floors are reed-mat stretched tight enough to act as communal drumheads; messages pass house to house in stamped rhythms.  
   • Law: The First Quiet—no crafted sound may last longer than the one that called it forth. Breach it, and neighbours disassemble the instrument that offended, piece by piece, until the echo stops.  
   • Exchange with Plateau: once a season a guild skiff is poled upriver to the foot of the cliffs where seep-water first appears. There, under rock that still rings with Stone-Choir static, the skiff captain hammers a Resonance Rod and waits. If a Linewalker is near enough to feel the tremor, they lower copper wire coils on silk ropes. Moss climbs the coils, feeding; the walkers haul up the now-charged vines to seed new condensers, and in return drop tins of plateau hush into the boat.

7. Atmosphere & Light  
   Brightness 0.8/3. Daylight diffuses through mist, turning shadows to soft smudges—a mercy after the plateau’s knife-edged glare. At night the glass pan underfoot reflects starlight into the fog, so constellations repeat at ankle height, wavering. Lightning—lured by Thunderbrood—etches every reed in negative on the eye for minutes after the flash.

8. Weirdness  
   • Echo-Shear: two sounds on converging paths sometimes pass through each other untouched, sometimes annihilate into blankness, sometimes fuse and spin off as a third tone obeying no harmonic law. The Tide-Ears chart these events like astronomers chart comets.  
   • Copper Hunger Reverse: while plateau vines seek metal, Copperscour Moss bleeds it. Place a pure copper ingot on a mat and by dawn it will be filmed in oxides; by week’s end only a hollow shell remains, the ions dispersed in the fenwater, amplifying its conductivity—and its voice.  
   • Glass-Slip Prophecies: Stand barefoot on the deeper mirror zones at dawn and you may hear, not echoes, but anticipations—sounds that will occur minutes later carried backward along the glass. Tide-Ear novices use the effect to dodge Mirecarver sinkholes they have yet to step into.

9. Time & Dynamics  
   Change here is pendulum-paced: reed growth triples after a lightning year, then the basin smothers itself, drowns, lies fallow, regrows. Condensers on the plateau may last a century; a reed-glass organ-pipe lasts a storm-season before it topples, fuses again, rises anew. Where the plateau is a clock, the Choirfen is a breathing lung—inhale of fire, exhale of peat, repeating on a rhythm too slow for a single life to measure precisely.

Census of Select Choirfen Entities
────────────────────────
1. Copperscour Moss (keystone vegetation)  
   Carpet moss, cell walls laced with nanoscale copper filaments. Emits a 60 Hz hum detectable only through bone conduction. Tide-Ears claim the pitch drops just before a peat-quake.

2. Glassmoth Swarms (primary pollinator)  
   Adult mouthparts formed into a tuning-fork. They strike reed-nodes, releasing nectar puffs discernible as micro-whistles that guide the swarm like beacons through fog.

3. Organ-Pipe Buttes (semi-living geology)  
   The fluted pillars resonate under temperature swings, producing infra-bass pulses that shepherd storms inland. Gong Cranes nest only on pillars tuned to C-sharp.

4. Fenforge Founders (ancestral presence)  
   Bronze masks without eye-holes, kept on hooks in the foundry. When struck they ring on frequencies no smith will replicate. At the year-turn the newest apprentice spends a night listening through the masks; in the morning they speak the design of next season’s tools as if dictated.

5. Tide-Ear Guild (human)  
   Carry on their belts stoppered gourd-claves—each one filled on the plateau, traded full of null-sound, opened only to silence a newborn spring before its note can clash with the basin’s chord.

6. Noise-Hermits (rare visitors)  
   Linewalkers who failed the Verdigris seed-test return here sometimes, heads buzzing with plateau songs. They take vow of barrage: walking the fen beating bronze kettles to drown thought until either the moss eats their copperware or the cranes claim the cymbals for mating displays.

Interlace with the Plateau
────────────────────────
• The water that refuses gravity above arrives here laden with its contradictions, teaching the fen to whisper future sound and erode metal in reverse.  
• Stone-Choir frequencies bleed downslope through bedrock, emerging from organ-pipes as baritone drones. Tide-Ear chart-stones match each plateau rockfall with a new butte overtone, two parts of one subterranean instrument.  
• Trade cycles silence upward, resonance downward. Each needs what the other cannot make: quiet for machines that must never be startled, echo for waters that must never be mute. Between them a whole world balances—knife-bright on the heights, drum-loud in the hollow—kept from tipping only by the thin, copper-laced thread of a shared, impossible song.
