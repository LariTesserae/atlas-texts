THE VIELLED ROOT BASIN


1. Arrival by Descent  
Follow any trunk-ridge of the High Drowned Terrace downward.  Its pulse is your rope.  Sixty kilometres below the terrace proper, where daylight has evaporated into midnight and water pressure could cipher diamonds out of coal, the ridges stop flowing laterally and turn vertical, twining together into a single, continent-thick braid.  That terminus is the rim of the Veilled Root Basin, a bowl one thousand kilometres wide and three deep, gouged into the planet’s primary crust during a forgotten meteor winter.  The basin is not level seafloor.  It is an inverted mountain range, its summits pointing down, all of it shrouded in the warm, black breath of the planet.


2. Water, Heat and Vision  
Above: the ocean column proper—cold, slow, dazzled by the terrace’s turquoise lens.  
Here: another ocean entirely.  

• Temperature: a rising gradient from 8 °C at the lip to 42 °C in the central sump, heated by hydrothermal flues that draft straight out of basalt faults.  

• Composition: saturated with dissolved magnesium salts and nano-flakes of elemental sulfur, giving the water a faint, smoky opalescence.  

• Light: none from the sun, but plenty nonetheless.  Chemo-bioluminescent snowstorms tumble off every wall, and rifts in the basin floor project steady pillars of crimson where iron oxidises in slow motion.  Sight is possible for thirty or forty metres—just far enough to assure you that farther shapes are moving.  


3. The Rootwork – Architecture That Grows Down  
The braid you followed is no longer a single trunk.  Hundreds of terrace reef-trees have coalesced into an arbor-pillar called the Plunge-Root, forty kilometres thick at its narrowest.  It does not end so much as unravel, each vascular strand fanning outward like the roots of a mangrove until the entire basin floor is netted in living cable.  Where two cables cross they splice, creating rigid nodes the locals dub “knuckles.”  Whole ecosystems gather on knuckles, warmed from below by geothermal seepage and fed from above by terrace detritus that drifts down like pale ash.  

In cross-section the Plunge-Root resembles a gigantic, inverted lung.  Water from the terrace percolates through its hollow fibres, shedding oxygen, picking up mineral heat, then drifts upward again in slow, hot vortices that run straight through the basin’s open mouth.  The Root Basin is therefore a giant circulatory return—a place where the terrace exhales and the planet inhales.


4. Constant Motion – Currents, Seiches, Tremors  
• The Basin Gyre: A clockwise circulation three hundred kilometres wide, spun by differential heating.  Anything not anchored will complete a full, lazy revolution every eighteen days.  

• Root-Seiches: The Plunge-Root’s peristalsis does not stop at depth.  Every ninety minutes a pressure wave shudders outward, flexing the basin walls.  Visitors liken it to standing inside a cathedral when the organist leans on all pedals at once.  

• Minor Quakes: The crust here is thin.  Micro-fractures sigh and close hourly, kicking up glittering clouds of olivine sand.  Large collapses are rare; the root-net stitches any wound in weeks.


5. Sound – A Polyphony of Heartbeats  
Where the terrace roars, the Root Basin sings.  Frequencies stratify by depth.  

• 15 Hz: the bass throb of magma chambers two kilometres below the floor.  
• 38 Hz: the elastic wheeze of hydrothermal flues.  
• 73 Hz: the inherited rhythm of terrace boilers, transmitted down the root walls like a mother’s pulse in an unborn ear.  
• 146 Hz, 219 Hz, 292 Hz: harmonic overtones produced by resident Echo-Binders who bend electric fields into vocal form.  

A diver’s chest plate will rattle.  Fill your lungs here and your diaphragm becomes a drumskin.


6. Inhabitants of the Veilled Root Basin  

6.1  Micro-Engines  
• Furnace Lichen: Co-colonies of archaeobacteria and silicaceous fungus that sheet every warm stone.  They oxidise hydrogen sulfide, emitting photons in the 650–720 nm band—the basin’s red pillars.  

• Cowl-Vesps: Protist swarms that veil themselves in bubbles of polysulfide gas.  The bubbles act as infrared mirrors, letting the vesps harvest waste heat the way terrace lens-algae harvest light.  A square metre of vesps can cool a vent by ten degrees in an hour.  

6.2  Root-Allies  
• Threadsmiths: Millimetre-thin nematodes that spin collagen tethers from terrace to crust, anchoring errant knuckles.  A single threadsmith colony can weave three kilometres of line in a month, knotting it to resonate at one of the basin’s hymnal frequencies.  The tethers both stabilise the rootwork and tune its voice.  

• Pore-Crabs: Pale, eyeless cousins of the terrace Anklets.  They live wedged into the Plunge-Root’s capillaries, filtering metal ions and excreting marble-white nodules of calcium phosphate used later as building beads by more complex fauna.  Think of them as bone factories.  

6.3  Free Drifters  
• Lantern Mantles: Jelly veils ten metres wide, containing entire comet-tails of symbiotic furnace lichen.  They glow like paper lanterns and ride the Basin Gyre, trading heat for thrust: the lichen’s exothermy expands trapped gas bladders that squeeze forward like bellows.  

• Wane-Sharks: Knifed, silver predators that patrol the red pillars in vertical loops.  They see temperature, not light.  Their teeth are hollow ceramic needles; one slash injects an enzyme cocktail that flash-freezes prey tissue, stopping decay while the shark savours the kill over days.  

6.4  Beings of Scale  
• The Root Speaker:  Singular.  Two kilometres of muscle segmented around a hydrothermal core, skin mineralised into pale basalt.  It keeps station wrapped around the Plunge-Root’s median bulge, mouth fused to a knuckle.  Every nine hours it exhales stored gas through six spiracles, producing a chord so low it folds the water into scrolls.  Terrace researchers believe the Speaker synchronises the entire reef-tree parliament above, resetting their peristaltic clocks.  

• Forge-Wives: Rare, lonely giants—bivalve monarchs whose shell spans half a kilometre.  They adhere to fresh lava scars, drink molten rock, and precipitate alloys of nickel-iron as pearlescent scales.  After two centuries a Wife discards her own shell, which floats upward on pent-up hydrogen to become substrate for newborn reef-trees.  Many terrace trunk ridges began as Forge-Wife husks.


7. Trade, Memory, Kinship with the Terrace  

What runs down must wander back up.  Connection is cyclical, not linear.  

• Gas Exchange: Oxygenated seep from terrace photosynthesis contacts magma-rich water here, generating sulfates and nitrates that ascend in root capillaries as fertiliser.  The terrace’s eternal green is born in this black.  

• Biological Apprenticeship: Root-moths pupate in terrace boilers, but their larvae hatch in the Basin, feeding on furnace lichen before migrating up-root.  Their life cycle stitches the two realms temporally: every generation must remember two homes.  

• Cultural Osmosis: Echo-Binders travel the root corridors freely.  Their field-songs alter as they descend, adding subharmonics learned from the Root Speaker.  When they wind their way back to the terrace they embroider these subterranean notes into the mesa’s constant roar.  Terrace linguists can date a binder’s last Basin pilgrimage within minutes by counting the deep-voice inflections in its current composition.  

• Material Tribute: When a Forge-Wife shell lifts off, anklet-style relay crabs affix cargo—bundles of phosphate beads, coils of threadsmith rope, cooled ingots of nickel glass.  By the time the husk breaches the terrace roof, it arrives as a laden barge already welcomed by vault-whales that know the pattern of its ascent by heart.


8. Danger and Stability  

Unlike the serenely unchanging terrace, the Root Basin is a place of precarious balance.  Remove too much oxygen, the furnace lichens dim and the red pillars fade, chilling the water and stalling the gyre.  Let a magma throat seal shut, and hydrostatic pressure will crush half the root-knuckles before they can reroute flow.  Terrace stillness stands on Basin restlessness the way calm lungs rest on a busy heart.  The two cannot be separated without killing both.


9. Departure Notes  

You leave by drifting into an up-draft where hot, mineral-rich water hurries to feed the terrace.  For the first ten kilometres nothing changes; red pillars merely stretch.  Then, abruptly, the water cools, colors bleach, and that remembered turquoise twilight blooms ahead.  The root knuckles narrow into single capillaries, then vanish, and you find yourself once more among the polished, breathing buttresses of the High Drowned Terrace.  

Carry the Basin’s polyphony with you the way the terrace carries it—deep under the audible, beating double-time.  When next you stand on the zero-flux mesa and feel an unaccountable tremor in your blood, know it is the Root Speaker turning in its sleep three leagues below, keeping the continent above alive with its dream of heat.
