The Overflow Gallery

Slide your hand along the warm hose that leaves the sinkhole basin and follow it downslope for eighty-three paces; the ground tilts, clay gives way to clinker and fly-ash, and the hose—worn thin as an old vein—begins to drip. Every leaked bead seeks gravity, so a trickle of greywater has written itself a path under the rubble, coring a tunnel no larger than a child’s arm before widening, widening, until the dark suddenly lifts into a room that should not fit beneath so slight a hill. That room is the Overflow Gallery, the basin’s exhalation chamber, a place built entirely out of what the pocket above cannot keep.

Terrain  
Picture an overturned amphitheatre whose seating has sloughed off, leaving only the swoop of the bowl. Its floor is glass—the accidental glass of slag quenched while molten—and under the glass, bubbles the size of fists are frozen in mid-burst, each holding a wisp of rust-coloured steam. Around the perimeter rise terraces of ash-stone, soft enough to thumb away yet stacked in perfect steps, as though laid by a mason who preferred working blind. Where the hose’s leak enters, it falls as a perpetual filament of water that never quite strikes the floor; warm updrafts loft it sideways until it threads into vapor and disappears.

Materials  
Every object here is a cast-off caught in transit. Greywater minerals plate the glass in nacre layers, so light walks the surface like oil. Iron shavings dropped from the basin upstream have sintered into stalagmites of blood-dark metal, needle-fine at the tips where each new particle welds itself by heat alone. Among them lie brass sprockets, threadbare gloves stiff with lime, a mouse skull electroplated bright chromium by some unknown process. Nothing rests long where it lands; each rainfall from the leak etches a new groove, knuckling objects gently downhill toward the lowest arc of the bowl.

Atmosphere & Weather  
Smell: the sweetness of autoclaved yeast, tempered by ozone. Temperature: tepid near the ceiling, cool at the floor—an inversion opposite to the basin above. The Gallery makes its own barometric seesaw: as the pump upstairs hushes on, vapor here condenses in a hush of its own and drags the air pressure a hair lower. When the pump rests, the Gallery exhales; filaments of steam rise in visible braids and wring a sigh from the glass, a faint, musical creak like a finger round a rim.

Light  
No sun reaches this pocket, but illumination leaks anyway. The moth-paper dust that drifts down the supply hose fluoresces in the presence of moisture. Every droplet that detaches from the hose ignites a ghost-blue glow which lingers until the water finishes evaporating on the glass. Viewed from the rim, the Gallery looks like a night sea, cross-hatched by schools of soft lanternfish.

Soundscape  
Three notes: drip, hiss, chime. The drip you expect. The hiss is greywater skimming super-cooled glass and vaporising in little sermons of steam. The chime belongs to the slag bubbles; when one finally fractures under thermal stress, it pops with a bell-bright ping that ricochets around the bowl and dies without echo—as if some courtesy keeps it from disturbing the next bubble in line.

Vegetation  
Only two plants lay claim here, and both arrived as spores sluiced from the basin.

1. Glass-Worm Moss  
• Not a moss at all but a colonial cyanobacterium that etches glass, eating silicates the way iron-moss above eats steel.  
• Grows in cursive, writing luminous turquoise calligraphy across the floor.  
• The writing is no language, but micro-crabs use the traced loops as roads; the cyanobacteria enjoy the ferric oxide dropped from their feet.

2. Ash-Sedge  
• Albino blades the width of sewing needles, sprouting between terraced ash layers.  
• Lacks pigment; instead stores moth-dust fluorescence in vacuoles, so entire clumps can blink on when disturbed, scaring off herbivores that never come.

Fauna  
1. Drip-Eaters (Hydroscutum minor)  
• Transparent, kite-shaped arthropods that hover inches below the leaking hose, each held aloft by two vibrating shell lids.  
• When a droplet falls, four mid-legs web into a cup; surface tension snaps the water inside, and the Drip-Eater drinks mid-air before releasing a micron-fine shard of calcium into the darkness.

2. Flakefish  
• Paper-thin, hexagonal proto-fish no larger than a stamp, swimming not in water but in boundary layers of warm air sliding up the bowl.  
• Their “fins” are rows of cilia that beat against humidity gradients. They never land; if the pump misses a cycle above and the air dries, flakes drop, die, and shatter like sugar glass.

3. Echo-Leeches  
• Jet-black ribbons curled in the cracks between ash-stone steps.  
• Sensory cilia tuned to that slag-bubble chime; they emerge only when the note rings true, latching to freshly opened glass veins to drink super-heated condensation before it can cool.  
• Their saliva polymerises into mica sheets that later roof over yet-unbroken bubbles, prolonging the chime-cycle the leech depends on.

Built & Tech  
Off-centre lies a crushed inspection drone, probably the one that lost the hose years ago. Its carbon shell is flensed in strips by Glass-Worm Moss. Close by, a tripod of surveying lasers still stands, mirrored faces hazed with calcium; every fifth pump-cycle the lasers stagger awake, scribbling scarlet sweeps across the bowl, mapping subsidence that no engineer remains to read. Between tripod feet sprouts a single vine bead from the Threefold Syndicate, orphaned but glowing, a lone moon without its planet.

Density  
Where the basin above is crowded to suffocation, the Gallery is an altar to spaciousness. The bowl is forty metres wide, yet no obstruction higher than a child’s knee interrupts its span. Life positions itself along vectors: Drip-Eaters orbit under the hose like planets, Echo-Leeches trace spiral pilgrimages from fresh chime to fresh chime, Flakefish ride thermal lines etched in air. Between these paths yawns open floor, polished and perilous.

Dynamics  
The rhythm upstairs dictates the Gallery’s tides.  

• Silence-Cue (T-0): Basin life stills; pump flares; warm greywater surges.  
• Cascade (T+90 s): Leak becomes a braid—ten times normal volume. Drip-Eaters swarm, Flakefish rise.  
• Bloom (T+4 min): Vapor saturates; Glass-Worm Moss filaments splay open, photosynthesising by moth-dust light.  
• Sigh (T+11 min): Pump throttles back; humidity crashes; Flakefish spiral downward, sometimes too slow, snowing corpses onto glass. Echo-Leeches surface to harvest fresh thermal cracks.  
• Drift (T+40 min): Everything coasts until the next silence above.

Weird Rules  
1. Nothing stays wet on the floor for more than two seconds; capillary channels in the slag yank moisture sideways and atomise it, so puddles levitate as mist.  
2. Sound that echoes more than once is considered surplus; Flakefish converge on its source, mistaking vibration for predator wake. Loud intruders are soon erased by a glittering cloud of paper bodies.  
3. If the leak ever clots for a full pump-cycle, the trapped humid air superheats under the glass; projections suggest the entire bowl would delaminate in a single, brilliant implosion. The basin above would feel it as a hiccup and never know why the rain failed after.

Relations to the Upper Basin  
• Nutrient Downflow: Iron filings, moth dust, ribbon-worm slough, and vine pollen all arrive here, sampled by organisms that could never compete topside.  
• Vapor Updraft: Every exhalation of steam re-enters micro-fractures that lead, circuitously, back toward the clay pocket. The moisture returns stripped of iron and rich in silica aerosol—a tonic that strengthens pipe fungus cell walls. The two places have invented a private trade loop: metal downward, glass upward.  
• Signal Paradox: During the basin’s ritual silence the Gallery is loud—drip, hiss, chime in frantic polyphony. When the basin erupts into rain and mating flight, the Gallery hushes, stunned by oversaturation. Together they produce a heartbeat with two alternating chambers.

Composite Intelligences  
The Conductor Mycelium’s cords do not reach this deep, but threads of Glass-Worm Moss have co-opted the old inspection drone’s circuitry. Copper traces act as neurons; when slag temperature rises suddenly, the drone’s corpse tilts its single, dust-masked camera back up the hose, streaming a data packet of nothing to no one. The packet rides thermal plumes, encoded in the flicker of moth-dust light, and basin ribbon worms read it as the colour for danger. Thus the Gallery has become the eye that the upper pocket lacks, and neither knows the name of the other.

How to Walk the Gallery and Leave Whole  
• Keep water sealed; an uncapped flask turns into a mortar shell when the floor glass flash-boils it.  
• Speak in a whisper or a full shout, nothing between—middling echoes trigger Flakefish swarms.  
• If glass pings twice in quick succession, lie prone; twin cracks herald a partial ceiling fall of volcanic pumice older than both places combined.  
• Collect nothing that glows; fluorescence here is deeded property. Moth-dust steals heat from thieves until skin anneals into brittle ceramic.

Standing at the lowermost arc, palm on the cool, breathing glass, you feel the whole world above you move: the hush, the pump, the rain, the fervid chew of iron turned living and back again. The Overflow Gallery is the second lung of the same sleeping machine, slower, clearer, echoing the first with every drip and chime. Where one inhales, the other releases; where one crowds, the other empties. Stay long enough and you will learn the paired rhythm, find yourself timing breaths to machinery two chambers away, and realise that the body you explore is larger than town, older than map, still growing—one silence, one rainfall, one shining bubble at a time.
