Giftwell Floor

TERRAIN, OR WHAT PASSES FOR IT  
Look down from the cat-walk of the Throat-Workshop long enough and your dropped bolt will eventually find this place. Three kilometres below, where the tower’s shaft should meet solid earth, there is instead a bowl the size of a small lake: a concave deck of interlocking plates whose centres sag under centuries of impact. The impact-scars have tessellated into a honeycomb of shallow pans—some puddled with condensate, others bristling with metallic shards—so the whole floor glitters like cracked ice under a forge’s glow. At the very centre sits the Catch-Cone, a dish of memory-metal that flexes inward each time something heavy lands, then slowly exhales the object onto a conveyor tongue that ferries it toward the Scrivener’s Promenade.

LIGHT  
Sunlight seldom survives the drop. What brightness reaches here ricochets down the shaft in splinters, fractured by struts and steam. Primary illumination comes from below: the plates themselves store the kinetic pulse of falling objects as piezo-charge and bleed it out at night in sheets of violet phosphor. Distant turbines far underfoot add a deep copper under-glow, as if the deck rests on smouldering coals.

WATER  
Everything that drizzles through the Throat-Workshop rains again here, cooler and laced with iron and orchid-pollen. The pans hold mirror-still pools whose surfaces oil-sheen colours with each new vent-cycle. Capillary gutters sip the overflow and pipe it to drip-vines that curtain the shaft walls, making a perpetual bead-curtain of dew.

TEMPERATURE & PRESSURE  
Air falls as steadily as any object: what begins above at sauna-warm 30 °C arrives cooler by ten degrees and compressed by the shaft’s chimney effect. The result is a cushioned heaviness, like weather before thunder. Exhalations from still deeper furnaces occasionally surge upward in hot gusts that smell of ozone and tamarind-metal.

EROSION  
Here corrosion is celebratory. Each pan’s rim has been chamfered smooth by the tumble of innumerable small gifts: screws, washer flakes, whole sections of cat-walk that surrendered to age above. In places, impact has punched through plate after plate into the vaults beneath, and those holes now flower with frilled rust like volcanic atolls.

SCALE & DENSITY  
What seems a single broad deck resolves into quarters, each administered by a different guild. They draw their jurisdictional borders in chalk before each work-shift, then erase and redraw them as new debris avalanches change the micro-topography. Distance is measured not in metres but in falls: “Three wrench-falls from the Cone” means you will pass three freshly fallen adjustable spanners before you reach your destination.

SOUND  
Constant pinging. Tiny things strike like sleet; larger bodies boom and set the plates ringing, a basso gong that decays over minutes. Between impacts you hear lap-water, the hiss of piezo lamps recharging, and the soft click of styluses scratching logbooks.

BUILT & TECH  
An observation ring, the Scrivener’s Promenade, encircles the deck at waist height. It is little more than railing, brass lecterns, and a belt-line conveyor that slides salvaged objects toward sorting kiosks. Eight lifts of impossible vintage rise the whole tower length to exchange personnel and cargo with the Throat-Workshop; only two remain functional, and even they climb like old lungs wheezing.

FAUNA  
•  Impact Eels—gelatinous cords that live in the puddles, curling upward to cushion falling objects with their elastic bodies; the guilds feed them on scrap verdigris and reward them with lullaby pulses tapped on resonator bowls.  
•  Rust-Pigeons—descendants of the corvids’ great-uncles; wings powdered cinnamon with iron oxide, they strut about seeking copper filings for grit.  
•  Echo-Moths—paper-thin, translucent; their wings re-broadcast the last loud sound they heard, so a single hammer blow sets off an after-storm of chittering metallic echoes.

THE FOUR GUILDS

1.  The Accounters of Descent  
   •  Attire: slate-grey smocks inked with ledger lines.  
   •  Task: record every object that arrives—mass, velocity, whether it sang on the way down.  
   •  Faith: “Nothing is lost; it is only postponed.”

2.  The Weldwright Nuns  
   •  Heads shaved but lacquered in chrome dust; aprons webbed with cooling tubes.  
   •  Task: reforge usable fragments into tools requested by boilermancers above. Their creeds are shaped as alloys: each prayer ends by quenching a blade in the evening pan.

3.  The Puddlegarden Chorus  
   •  Wear necklaces of live drip-vines that photosynthesise under phosphor light.  
   •  Task: curate biofilms that skim the puddles, harvesting lubricant-eating bacteria whose excretions become anti-seize paste.  
   •  Specialty: coaxing impact eels to pattern themselves into living breakwaters during heavy fall hours.

4.  The Librarians of Kinetic Memory  
   •  Faces masked in bronze mirrors to watch impacts without blinking.  
   •  Task: interpret the pitch and duration of plate-ring as prophecy; to them each boom is the tower’s heartbeat announcing futures.  
   •  Output: thin copper scrolls etched with vibrational glyphs and winched upward in canisters; boilermancers read them as augury for pressure trends.

WEIRDNESS  
Objects occasionally land that were never dropped: spanners with sigils from workshops nobody recalls, seeds of plants not known to grow above, even a sealed tin that once contained air now missing from the shaft’s climate. The Librarians say these are echoes from adjacent realities, shaken loose by the tower’s resonance.

DYNAMIC  
Where the Throat-Workshop changes glacially, the Giftwell Floor updates hourly. A single fallen gear might shatter four plates, flood a quadrant, and rewrite two guild borders before dusk. Yet by dawn the Nuns have patched the tear, the Chorus has seeded new algae in the pooling water, and the Accounters have already balanced the books. Constant ruin, constant mend: an ecosystem of entropy managers.

RULES OF THE PLACE  
1. Gravity wins, but impact eels negotiate the cease-fire.  
2. You do not own what lands at your feet until it has stopped ringing.  
3. Walk with chin up; prophecy drips from above, but shame hides on the ground.  
4. When the plates glow violet, speak softly—every word becomes a moth.  
5. Remember that the shaft is a throat: if the Throat-Workshop is its song, the Giftwell Floor is the consonant crash at the end of each line.

KINSHIP WITH THE THROAT-WORKSHOP  
•  Trade: refurbished tools ride the lift back up, stamped with a sigil of the falling star—proof they have survived descent.  
•  Memory: boilermancers send down tachyon-coded whistle rolls; the Librarians replay them against plate vibrations to tune their prophecies.  
•  Ecology: rust scraped by corridor crabs above drifts down as nutrient snow, feeding the Puddlegarden’s bacteria, whose distilled enzymes return upstairs as grease for those very same crabs.  
•  Myth: the people of Giftwell claim the tower is not a throat but a wishing-bone; every object dropped is a wish, every reforged tool the wish returned answered.

STAND A MOMENT  
Step onto the Promenade during the lull between falls. The plates beneath release a long sigh of violet light; an impact eel’s back breaks the surface of a puddle like slow lightning. Somewhere far above, the boilermancers strike a chord that travels the shaft as trembling air. When it reaches here, the Librarians tilt their mirrored masks, listen, and carve a new line into copper: tomorrow will be warm, the steam will sing in D-minor, and someone you have not yet met will drop a lantern that lands unbroken at your feet. When it does, the Nuns will polish its glass, the Chorus will line it with glowing algae, and you, if you are wise, will use it to look back up the impossible distance you once fell in order to arrive exactly here.
