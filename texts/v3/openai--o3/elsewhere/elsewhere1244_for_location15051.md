The Plateau of Spent Echoes  

Distance, current, kinship: follow the wind that roars through the Rift of Unfinished Peaks.  Thirty-seven kilometres west it bursts from the canyon’s mouth like water from a pressure-valve—deafened, stripped of its freight of clanging overtones, suddenly weightless for having lost its own voice.  What that wind becomes is a long, pale exhalation that creeps across a high basalt tableland: a place so wide that sunrise happens in gradients, so level that cloud shadows travel for hours without snagging on relief.  Here the noise that fed a canyon dies, and in its grave something else has learned to live.

TERRAIN  
The Plateau is the discarded roof of an antique lava dome, planed flat by two million winters of sublimating ice.  Its skin is palagonite dust and vesicular pumice, the colour of blown glass bleached almost white.  Scattered across the expanse are sinkholes called Ellipses—perfect ovals where mantle bubbles once burst.  The largest you could lay a cathedral in and see every stone reflected in the polished salt pan at its base.  From above, the Plateau looks like a tablet scraped blank except for these punctuation marks of darkness.

WATER & ATMOSPHERE  
Moisture arrives nightly as a silent surf of fog that detaches from the same cloud-sheets skimming the Rift’s roof.  Because the land is ruthlessly horizontal the fog never piles; instead it sheets across stone until crystalline salts seed the vapour and force it to precipitate in place.  At dawn the ground sweats, then dries by noon, leaving gypsum petals so fine they can drift on a sigh.  Air here is cold at night, hot by day, but always thin.  It tastes of borax and something like burnt sugar—the volatile ghosts of pumice popping under the sun.

LIGHT  
Where the Rift feeds mostly on vibration, the Plateau gorges on radiance.  Sunlight is omnipresent, unfiltered, flaring off micro-facets of glass until pupils protest and water.  Even moonlight seems to render colour.  When no moon shows, starlight alone can cast measurable shadow: the pumice reflects like milk spilled across a mirror.  Creatures use brightness the way Rift dwellers use sound—currency, weapon, lure, camouflage—and their anatomies are written in prisms and albedos rather than tympani or tongues.

SOUND  
Silence is not absolute, but it is sovereign.  The wind that once brawled in organ chords now glides over pores and vesicles that drink turbulence as sponge drinks oil.  A boot-step thuds once and then is gone, its echo devoured by the ground’s capillary labyrinth.  Even voices falter: you may shout across an Ellipse and hear nothing but the blood behind your own eardrums.  Travellers report moments when they realise they are grinding their teeth simply to reassure themselves that noise still exists.

FAUNA (SPARSE BUT LARGE)  
Life here is an economy of glare and hush; bodies tend toward broad surfaces for gathering light and honeycombed interiors for hiding sound.

1. Shard-Kites  
   • Bone-light reptiles whose ribcages have migrated into their skin, forming kite-frames clad in transparent scale.  
   • They climb thermals until nearly invisible, then pivot and plummet, using their skins’ Fresnel ridges to concentrate sunlight into a burn-line that sears prey.  The Plateau’s only natural fires come from these strikes.  
   • Old Shard-Kites carry Rift souvenirs: ticks of obsidian still hitching on membranes, now half a continent from the canyon walls.

2. Mirage-Cattle  
   • Six-legged grazers the size of elk but made mostly of reflective cartilage tubes inflated with hydrogen fermented from gypsum water.  
   • From certain angles they vanish, bending light around their own hulls; from others they bloom into rainbow phantoms.  
   • Predators cannot pounce on what they cannot see, so Shard-Kites ignite the ground nearby; heat shimmer collapses the illusion, forcing the cattle to cast betraying shadows.

3. Hush-Worms  
   • Segmented annelids whose flesh extrudes aerogel filaments that wick resonance out of the soil.  
   • They tunnel just below the surface, leaving sink-lines like handwriting you can read only by the way sand refuses to crunch underfoot.  
   • Their castings—brick-pale ropes of aerated quartz—are prized by Subaudient pilgrims, who fashion them into flutes that produce notes felt only in the lungs.

4. Prism Lichen  
   • Actually a tripartite union: cyanobacteria trapped inside hexagonal ice lattices secreted by a fungus that itself nests in a crust of silica.  
   • Each dawn, frost flowers of lichen bloom, refracting sun into fixed rainbows that crawl across the ground as the star rises.  
   • Mirage-Cattle nibble the ice petals for moisture; the bacteria, digested but not killed, later germinate in the beasts’ translucent gut-pouches, turning the animal into a walking farm.

VEGETATION (MINUTE BUT BRILLIANT)  
Scarcely plants at all—more like carapace than chloroplast.

• Glass-Spur Grass  
  – Sprouts a single stem tipped with a triangular lens.  When the wind tilts the stem just so, the lens focuses light onto its own base and scorches encroaching neighbours, preserving personal shade.  
• Salt-Banners  
  – Thin sheets of calcite that rise at twilight through capillary action, then collapse by morning.  Each banner vibrates ultrasonically as it dries, warning night wanderers that dawn is imminent.

RULES OF THE PLACE (WEIRDNESS)  
• Shadow is magnetism.  The Plateau’s pumice charges negatively where light is absent; stand long enough in your own shade and hair will lift as though you carry a storm.  
• Heat is language.  Species signal by generating micro-thermals that wobble the horizon.  A Shard-Kite’s courtship reads like distant cities flickering in and out of existence.  
• Echoes can be mined.  Subaudients sink ceramic amphorae into the Ellipses; weeks later they retrieve them corked with crystallised silence that, when cracked, grants a minute of perfect auditory null—useful for surgery, meditation, or assassination.

EROSION (TIRED)  
Edges round here.  Pebbles are pearls, every corner rubbed away by day-heated expansion and night-freeze contraction.  Even footprints blur at their rims before they vanish.  No line is sharp unless life sharpens it for use.

BUILT & HUMAN  
Nothing permanent stands: rebar would corrode, mortar would fracture.  Instead, travellers erect Heliographs—simple stacks of aerogel bricks faced with mirror-leaf.  Dozens dot the Plateau, arranged so each catches sunset and pings the next with a last flash.  A message can ride these relay beacons from Rift to Western Sea in a single twilight.

DYNAMICS  
Day is dominion of light-eaters; night belongs to the chill and the worms.  At dawn the balance tips like a breath held or released.  No season matters half so much as the angle of the sun.

CONNECTION TO THE RIFT  
Every evening, as katabatic wind flows out of the Rift, it bears with it:

• Grey dust of long-dead Gloom-Gulls: fertiliser for prism lichen.  
• Electrostatic spores from Static Mites: they seed cloud nuclei; without them, the fog surf would fail.  
• Cracked carapaces of Clatter-Shrimps: their zinc feeds Glass-Spur Grass, sharpening its lenses.  
• A low infrasonic hum siphoned from the canyon’s night chorus: the Plateau’s pores swallow it, recharging Hush-Worm burrows like lungs filling.  

In return, once a decade when mirage rains fall (a weather anomaly where sunlight diffracts into pillars that drip visible radiance), adult Chimera—those same unseen imago that depart the Rift—arrive here.  They hover above Ellipses, unfurl tessellated wings, and shed panes of opalescent chitin down the holes.  Weeks later, at the canyon’s deepest vents, Clatter-Shrimp queens molt in synchrony, their new ridges tuned an octave lower—as though some message in light reached back through stone.

STANDING HERE  
You will notice brightness first—a weight behind the eyes—then the hush so broad it seems cosmic, and finally the strange alertness of your own shadow tugging at your boots.  Whisper and your lips feel cold because no echo returns to warm them.  Raise a hand and watch the land blink; lower it and feel static crawl up your sleeve.  By noon you may envy the Mirage-Cattle, who wander luminous and almost incorporeal, unburdened by the noises memory makes in the skull.

Stay until night and the Plateau returns the favour: stars double in the pumice until heaven and earth are reversible.  Somewhere east, the Rift vibrates like a struck bell you can no longer hear—but if you place your palm to the ground, you might feel the leftover thrum, a pulse that ran out of voice and kept running, all the way to here.
