The Boomsand Reach  

DISTANCE & APPROACH  
Start where the hollow’s warm breath escapes through its three quartz skylights. Wait for noon, when the updraft is strongest, and feel which way the wind slides once it clears the lip—south-by-east, always. Keep that course. For two days the plateau unrolls underfoot like a dull parchment; then the ground dips, ochre fades to chalk, chalk to a translucence that winks like crushed bottles. The air turns skittish with distant bass notes you mistake for bone-deep tinnitus until the first dune rears up and vibrates under your boots. You have arrived.

TERRAIN  
Imagine an ocean frozen mid-storm, every wave blown clear of water and packed instead with milky quartz sand. The Reach sprawls twenty kilometres tip to tip, a succession of crescent dunes piled six or seven storeys high. Each dune’s windward face is quilted tight, its leeward slope loose as sighing silk. Under a thin crust, every grain is half-melted—smooth, lens-fine, eager to flow. Buried metres deep run slabs of sandstone shot through with iron seams. Whenever the sun cooks those seams hotter than the quartz above, the sand sheet buckles, slides, and the dune booms.

LIGHT  
Daylight here behaves like a dropped coin: it rings. Angled across billions of semi-fused grains, sunlight scatters into pale auras that hang a handspan above the surface before fading. The brighter the day, the whiter the sand appears, until at high noon the landscape turns colourless, almost negative, and shadows hide inside the glare like ink under milk. Night does the reverse. Moon or no, each grain remembers the day and leaks it back as cold opaline glow; travellers speak of walking by a phosphor that clings to their ankles.

ATMOSPHERE  
Bone-dry on the tongue but humid to the ear. Moisture from the distant hollow wicks south beneath the plateau and evaporates on contact with the Reach’s hot sub-strata, feeding an invisible ceiling of steam that hangs fifty metres up. The booming of dunes condenses in that ceiling, so every vibration returns a softened echo, as if the clouds themselves are listening shells. Ozone and hot flint perfume the air; strike a spark and it smells no different.

SAND  
What looks like sand is half-glass, half-salt. A fingertip will not find an edge, only an almost waxy smoothness. Scoop a handful, close your fist, and the grains try to pour through your skin’s pores—not sharply, but insistently, like thick smoke seeking its own level. Wherever the booming is strongest, the topmost grains fuse into plates broad as thumbnails; the locals call them tone-shards. Step on a field of them and the dune shivers, tuning itself.

VEGETATION  
Almost none rises above the surface. Instead, plants mine depth.  
• Root-lilies: pale, spinal bulbs three metres down, tapping the evaporating groundwater. At dawn they thrust up a single hollow stalk, venting surplus moisture as a jet so fine it engraves frost-rings in the pre-sunlight chill.  
• Glasswort: a creeping, jointed mat that laps brine off the tiles of fused sand. Its flesh is transparent; chlorophyll hides inside violet micro-veins safe from the killing glare.  
• Rust-nurse colonies (yes, the same species that pimples iron in the hollow) spread here on the buried seams, raising fluted fruiting bodies rust-red as dried blood. When the dune booms, their spores ride the vibration like dust on a drum-skin and drift back toward the north—some eventually sprinkling into the hollow’s skylights, keeping the cycle of corrosion alive.

FAUNA  
1. Echo-mites  
Cousins of the hollow’s acoustic brood, but swollen on richer fare. They coat every tone-shard in films a single molecule thick. When a shard sings, the mites glut themselves, dividing so fast the note warps underfoot. A heavy footfall can silence a dune for minutes.  
2. Bell-ants  
Colourless, faceted like spun sugar, they tunnel vertically instead of horizontally, measuring the exact moment a dune’s slope will break and ride the miniature avalanche down, harvesting dead echo-mites in the spill. Their jaws click at pitches below human hearing; the vibrations help them map hollows of cooler air.  
3. Lanyard Drakes  
Ribbon-thin lizards the length of a man’s arm. Their scales prism sunlight into rainbow scarves used to signal mates across valleys of glare. By night they bury themselves nose-up beneath tone-shards, letting the residual glow lure nocturnal beetles straight into waiting throats.  
4. Boomsail Moths  
Wings sheer as onion skin, bodies no larger than a thumbnail. By day they are damp husks tucked under root-lily frost rings; by sunset they unfurl, let the evening wind strike their wings, and use the low drone produced to find one another. Their larvae digest fused sand, excreting powder fine enough that the wind can lift it north; the hollow’s damselflies have been seen tasting this gift on high thermals.

SOUND  
Everything audible here begins as friction and ends as music. A single grain’s slip can set off a chain-reaction: half the dune cascades and the ground bellows a sustained B-flat humans feel more than hear. Smaller ridges answer at their own resonant frequencies until the whole Reach thrums like distant artillery. Stand still and your skeleton conducts a chord progression you could never whistle but will nonetheless remember in your dreams.

TIME & WEATHER  
No rain has fallen in living memory; water arrives only as ghost-steam from the north or as rare fog blown in from some coastal storm leagues away. Dunes migrate a few metres each year, following the dominant wind. Every decade or so a damp winter forces the outermost dune to slump, sealing one of the iron seams under a new blanket of sand and silencing it forever. New seams are always waiting below.

RULES OF THE PLACE  
1. Metals brought here oxidise twice as fast as normal, feeding rust-nurse blooms that can skeletonise a steel compass overnight.  
2. Sounds above a human shout do not travel far—echo-mites swarm to devour them—but sub-audible vibrations carry for kilometres. Morse-booming on the right pitch can summon help—or predators—from beyond the horizon.  
3. Light scattered off the dunes will erase inks and dyes within hours. Travellers wrap maps in oiled hide or carry memories instead.

HUMAN TRACE  
Nomads skirt the Reach’s rim, harvesting tone-shards to sell as wind chimes that never tarnish. Now and then a prospector digs for iron, hoping to cash weight before rust-nurses claim it. Camps rarely last: tents collapse as fabric threads grow brittle under relentless scatter-light, and the low boom under the pillow robs sleepers of rest, filling their dreams with pressure.

THE SENSE OF IT  
Where the hollow felt like a lantern cupped in the earth’s hands, the Reach is the lantern’s outward-flung flare, all glare and resonance. It is less a place than an instrument forever tuning itself, half desert, half bell. Everything loose is invited to sing; everything solid is invited to surrender. Stand on a dune at twilight, when the heat finally loosens its fist, and the ground croons a note that hums your ribs apart from your lungs. You will think, absurdly, of being inside a giant animal’s throat—until you remember you are the animal, breathing, vibrating, and that somewhere two days’ walk behind you a dripping terraced hollow is counting each of these same breaths in water- arithmetic.

CONNECTIONS, SEEN AND UNSEEN  
• The wind that shuttles between skylight and dune carries spores, shed scales, and whole damselfly corpses; what the hollow exhales, the Reach inhales.  
• Echo-mites grown fat here drift north at the end of their life cycle, dormant until hollow-cool damp wakes them. Their hunger for vibration is why a whispered word by the pool can reappear beside a traveller’s ear: the mites are remembering the booms of their birthplace.  
• When a dune collapses with a major chord, skinks in the hollow pause their tail-drumming and insert the Reach’s new interval into that night’s terrace music; no one taught them, they simply know the score.  
• The Drip-Counter reckons distance as well as time: its droplets have begun, in recent seasons, to fall in patterns that mirror the boom-sequence of the outermost dunes. What sum it seeks is its own secret.

If the hollow is the heart’s warm chamber, the Boomsand Reach is the lung: expanding, compressing, trading invisible breath across stone leagues. Each needs the other. Without the hollow’s mist, the dunes would calcify into mute glass hills; without the Reach’s resonant pull, the hollow’s droplets would lose their rhythm. Two places, one long quiet breath moving back and forth between them, letting life of very different tempos keep time to the same, silent song.
