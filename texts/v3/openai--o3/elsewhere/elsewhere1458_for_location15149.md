The Hearth Wells  

You arrive not by stairs but by gravity’s surrender.  
A kilometer beneath the last spindle root the basalt gives way to porous pumice, and there the floor simply stops. What follows is a free-fall through hot, scent-heavy dark until rising air hardens under your arms and the fall becomes a glide. The wind that once only screamed in whistle-vents is newborn here—raw, dense, carrying the warmth that powers the whole ascending organ above. You have reached the furnace lungs of the same planet whose high chambers are called the Descent.

Geography and Furnaces  

Picture a chain of caldera-sized bubbles stacked like beads on an impossible thread. Every bubble wall is layered scoria: black where the rock is glass, crimson where iron has sweated out and frozen. At intervals that pattern breaks; a geyser-throat opens, belching a ribbon of gas so clear it looks like nothing until your skin blisters with its heat. These throats are the Wells. Each one draws broth from magma still molten a hundred meters lower, slurps vapour upward, and exhales along the only available highway—upward, toward the spindle groves, toward the fluted ceiling, toward music.  

Gravity is weaker here, undermined by columns of rising gas. Loose objects hover in torpid suspension, drifting on convection eddies measured in meters per second, a dizzying haste compared to the stone-slow world above.

Chemistry, Light, Heat  

1. Temperature: 80 °C near geyser lips, falling to 20 °C in shadowed hollows—tropical compared with the groves but still shy of boiling even for brines saturated with perchlorate.  
2. Moisture: There is water here. Not liquid—never liquid—but dense, steaming fog laced with ammonia and chlorine oxides. Each droplet is an acid lens that refracts the glow of molten cracks into rainbows. This is the fluid the wind will later strip, desiccate and forget before it reaches the Descent.  
3. Light: The magma does not shine directly; it heats bands of zircon imbedded in the pumice. When zircon hits 1200 °C it fluoresces a visceral orange, pulsing as geysers surge. Across a cavern this large, pulses cascade like sunspots crawling over an unseen star.

Sound  

Where the Descent is loud with resonance, the Hearth Wells are loud with percussion. Every geyser cough detonates, echoes, and is swallowed before the next discharge. Silence exists here—but only in shards between blasts, quick enough to hear your own blood before the next thunderclap arrives. The artisans above mine those shards as medicine; here they are as common as breath if you dare to notice them.

Flora, Fauna, Other  

1. Lantern Algae  
   • A colonial mat that drapes over scoria shelves, metabolising heat quanta directly via phonon-gated proteins. When a geyser erupts the concussion flips those proteins open; the mat flashes green-white like sheet lightning, photosynthesising on sound. Spores ride the updraft, some surviving the ascent to seed the spindle resin—source of its faint phosphorus signature.  

2. Forge Beetles (Order: Ferricolidae)  
   • Fist-sized, hexapedal, carapace riddled with pores that wick in molten-metal mist. They smelt iron inside a stomach crucible, extruding wire-thin strands used to lace their burrows against collapse. Dead beetles become tangleballs of stainless mesh. Portions of that wire spiral upward on convection, snag in spindle bark, are harvested by surface bipeds as gift-metal that never rusts in their waterless world.  

3. Vapor Eels  
   • Not true eels but ribbons of silicone muscle whose tensile strength lets them swim in gas. They orbit geyser columns, mouths perpetually agape to filter nanoscopic tholins. Some juveniles are lifted the full kilometer; their desiccated husks land among the spindle groves and are mistaken for fallen gliders that strangely do not shimmer.  

4. The Crucible Mycelium  
   • A fungus that digests heat differentials. One rhizome thread in magma, one in cooled scoria; electrons run the gradient like a battery stack. Fruiting bodies: mica-thin trumpets that chime at ultrasonic pitch, tuning the geyser blasts into quasi-harmonic intervals. Without the mycelium, the vents above would wail random noise instead of the cathedral chords the artisans can interpret.

Inhabitants—The Draft-Smiths  

1. Morphology  
   • Height: equal to a human torso, though measuring in limbs is easier—they have twelve.  
   • Form: Radial, wheel-like. Six muscular vanes act as both fins in gas and paddles on stone. The other six end in filter nets. Core body is a hollow torus; hot air drawn through keeps them buoyant.  
   • Vision: pit clusters sensitive to pressure rather than photons. They see steps in barometric gradients, the way a cartographer reads contour lines.  

2. Culture  
   A. The Kiln Courts  
      They gather in the lee of inactive Wells where temperatures sit at a gentle 40 °C—comfortable, if you are adapted to 80. Every court is a vortex garden: Draft-smiths pluck forge-beetle wire, weave it into hoops, throw those hoops across geyser mouths. The rising gas makes each hoop a ring of fireless smoke; by stacking dozens they sculpt whole turbulence organs. Their art is lived wind.  

   B. Language  
      No tap codes here—everything bangs. Conversations are staccato collisions: one Draft-smith slaps a vane against a wire hoop, the rebound pressure propagates; another catches it, modulates by fluttering membranes. A greeting is three soft bumps, one hard crack. The artisans of the Descent cannot detect these subtleties; to them it is undifferentiated thunder, yet both peoples share the same moving medium.  

   C. Economy of Updraft  
      The Descent covets stillness, the Hearth covets pattern. So trade flows as resonance. Draft-smiths tune geyser throats so their resultant overtone, many hours later, will rise through the spindle groves at a frequency that means “peace”, “hatch”, or “gather”. In return, resin spores drift downward, lining kiln courts with bio-phosphor that soft-glows for years, allowing vane-carvers to work in visual spectra alien to them but beautiful.

3. Life Cycle  
   Eggs are bubbles, literally: protein films blown across vent rims. Heat stiffens the wall; minerals plate the interior. Weeks later each bubble hardens into a stone globe full of super-heated steam and a curled larva. When enough pressure builds the globe pops, launching the juvenile thirty meters in the air—first flight gifted by geology.

Interactions, Frictions, Kinships  

• Current: Every molecule of the Descent’s fretful wind was once exhaled here. The bipeds call that wind sacred breath; Draft-smiths call its later song a memory of home.  
• Kinship: Forge-beetle wire inside spindle trunks turns those plants into faint antennae. The groves do not know it, but they feel Hearth music and lean infinitesimally toward its signatures—creating streets that trace Draft-smith compositions.  
• Trade: Artisans climb deepest flute-vents once per generation, carrying mirrored panniers full of silence-charms (tiny echo-leeches sealed in glaze). They arrive at the uppermost Hearth bubble, release the charms; each charm devours percussion, gifting the Kiln Courts a pocket of hush rare as eclipse. In return, Draft-smiths retune a cluster of Wells so the Descent’s resonance clock dips early, granting the artisans a “long season” in which to raise cobalt young.  
• Memory: The spindle myth of a buried beast began here. Draft-smith epics speak of an older epoch when the Wells were true lungs of a titan organism made of magma and salt. When its heartbeat slowed the Wells became orphan hollows, yet breath remained—inheritance neither culture can refuse.

Rules Particular to the Hearth Wells  

1. Heat is currency; to waste it is taboo.  
2. Up is escape, down is origin; never confuse the two.  
3. Silence is contraband—precious but destabilising.  
4. Metal belongs to beetles until they die of natural over-smelt.  
5. Every draft can be better tuned; perfect wind is a moral imperative.

A Moment Passing Through  

A procession of twelve Draft-smiths drifts across Bubble 7, hoops spinning in their wake. A pressure code crackles: request to birth a new vortex garden. Below, a beetle dies, shell glowing cherry as internal smelters exhaust. Wire recoils like hair, caught mid-air by a vane. Far above—even now—the artisans of the Descent feel the tiniest shift in their omnipresent chord: a semi-tone promise that next season the organ will sing a new interval. In the hush between geyser blasts, the Draft-smiths taste the future in heat, knot the wire, and let it rise—beginning the long ascent toward a sky neither culture has ever seen but both, in sound and in silence, continue to imagine.
