The Tuesday Scar

Between one heartbeat and the next—precisely the span that the basin lets crumbs hover in mid-air—the Tuesday Scar opens like a lidless eye five hundred kilometres north-north-east.  Nothing points to it on ordinary maps; you reach it only by following something that left the basin: a leak of exported waste-heat that threads the upper troposphere, bright on thermal lenses, invisible to everyone else.  Ride that plume far enough and it sets you down here, where a single stranded weekday has been stretched until it is thinner than weather and wider than geography.

Terrain and raw geology  
• Outline.  The Scar begins as an ellipse nine leagues long, scooped clean through continental crust as though a god ran a thumbnail across still-setting concrete.  Its floor is pure negative relief: a concavity that never quite decides to be canyon, crater or river-valley.  
• Substrate.  Bedrock is chalk, but chalk charred to vitreous black where the basin’s waste-heat arrives.  Press your palm to it and you can read old temperatures the way tree-rings read old rains.  
• Active margin.  Around the rim, unmet Tuesdays slough off in translucent sheets, curling like onion skin.  Fold one and it crackles with static: the sound of weather patterns that might have been.  

Atmosphere, water and temperature  
• Ambient hour.  The Scar keeps only one time-signature: late afternoon, minute thirty-four, light angled exactly for regrets.  The sun holds its breath here, stuck at four fingers above the horizon.  
• Barometrics.  Air pressure pulses once every sixty-one seconds, a systole-diastole whose amplitude matches the basin’s slow exhalations.  You can set a watch by it—though the watch will never tick past 16:34.  
• Hydrology.  No rivers feed the Scar.  Instead, condensation beads along invisible isobars, then gutters downhill in loose braids of water that remember being steam.  Each rivulet carries dissolved timestamp: collect a vial and the meniscus flickers with yesterday’s metadata.

Light and sound  
• The colour of the hour.  Warm amber, but only half-real; photons arrive under protest, having been evicted from the basin’s husk-blossoms at dawn.  They commit to brightness only when someone names them aloud.  
• Echo etiquette.  Speak toward the far wall and the canyon answers in tenses you have not used yet: a question returns as a permission, a greeting as a valediction.  Most travellers keep silent after the first mis-translated word.  
• Local thunder.  When enough unshed rain decides to exist, it detonates mid-air—thunder first, lightning negotiated afterward.  The bolt inks itself retroactively and crawls back up its own zig-zag, trying to pretend it was always there.

Built density  
• Scar-side installations.  Nothing permanent lasts, but nothing quite falls apart.  Pilgrims raise scaffold from the basin’s cast-off spare geometry: stairs that climb until they remember they have no destination, cantilevered decks that rehearse collapse but never conclude.  
• The Gutters.  A semi-circular shanty-town of reflective cloth and negative-pressure tents clings to the western rim.  Structures are pegged with stakes of frozen moment: crystalline rods harvested when the clock-pulse hits apogee.  They melt into plain steel if carried more than a kilometre away.

Temperature regimes  
• Mean: a misleading fifteen Celsius, but thermal gradients stutter.  Cross ten metres and you travel through three microclimates: tepid, feverish, frostbitten.  
• Heatflare.  Each time the basin’s engines dump entropy, it arrives as a clear-air shimmer, raising surface temps to ninety degrees for a breath and branding chalk into black glass.  Locals call these moments “blink-kilns” and fire pottery by simply waiting in place.

Population strata  
1. The Tuesday Nomads  
   • Morphology.  Cloaks sewn from proprioception errors; you see them a fraction to the left of where they stand.  Their faces are lacquered against recognition, gloss reflecting only futures that choose not to happen.  
   • Economy.  They trade in spare afternoons, shaved thin and rolled like cigarette paper.  A kindly Nomad can gift you an extra hour of daylight; an unkind one will take tomorrow’s lunch break as payment.  
   • Pilgrimage cycle.  Every nine waste-heat pulses they march single-file to the Scar’s nadir, unspool an inventory of final chances, and let the wind erase whichever lines no longer scan.

2. The Thermoglyphs  
   • Nature.  Writing made of temperature differentials: sigils freeze themselves into dew, thaw through rock as negative fires.  
   • Mobility.  A glyph migrates by boiling itself a millimetre forward, leaving black-glass footprints.  Over months they spiral into complex mandalas that predict, with alarming accuracy, the basin’s next architectural whim.  
   • Interaction.  Stand inside a thermoglyph and you will remember a day that ceased to exist.  Your recollection becomes its new stroke; the glyph shuffles away, satisfied.

3. The Scrip-Jackals  
   • Fauna.  Rangy quadrupeds whose hides are veined with ticker-tape: thin ribbons ennumerating unclaimed probabilities.  Eyes like split-level apertures; each blinks in asynchronous pairs.  
   • Diet.  They hunt discarded clauses—half a vow, a rescinded itinerary, the subjunctive moods that visitors shed unconsciously.  When they feed, text fades from the ribbons and becomes muscle-fiber.  
   • Warning sign.  A jackal that has gorged on modal verbs can outrun an echo.  Seasoned travellers avoid speaking promises within earshot.

4. The Hour-Glassers  
   • Ontology.  Not a species but a phase people slip into after too many sunsets stuck on the same minute.  Skin dries to translucent parchment; veins spiral with ink that was once blood.  
   • Behaviour.  They gather near the Scar’s edge, tilt their heads, and let gravitational potential pour time through their bodies like sand.  Each tilt spends an allotment of years they might have lived elsewhere.  
   • Prognosis.  Left alone, a Hour-Glasser erodes to empty vellum that flaps in the canyon wind, whispering schedules for no one’s ears.

Weirdness—how laws break and bend  
• Chronostatic friction.  Anything moving north-to-south drags through thicker time; shoes smoke, cartwheels squeal.  East-to-west is effortless—one stride glides you five metres, heartbeat unchanged.  
• Memory weather.  On some still afternoons, recollections precipitate as soft rust-coloured snow.  Melted, each flake shows a micro-etched scene from a life not lived here.  Children of the Gutters gather them, brew a mild tea of phantom nostalgia.  
• Entropy down-cycling.  Waste-heat arriving from the basin enters as high-grade order: a paradoxical cold-flame that freezes entropy out of the air before blooming hot.  Local physicists (two, occasionally three) document the event using rulers that refuse integer centimetres.

Rules of stay (compiled by Scar-side anchorite Mx. Hewer Quill)  
1. Always keep a Tuesday in your pocket: a train ticket dated, a handwritten agenda.  The Scar respects familiarity and will leave you unchanged if you carry a version of itself.  
2. Never watch a thermoglyph finish writing; the last stroke is always an exit you are unprepared to take.  
3. If you hear thunder before you see cloud, lie flat—the bolt is unconfirmed and prefers taller assertions.

Dynamics and long cycles  
• Pulse-train.  The Scar’s breath synchronises with the basin’s growth spurts.  Each millimetre of fresh scaffold there deepens the canyon floor by a grain of chalk here, as though mass were borrowed on consignment.  
• Reconciliation year.  Legends agree: when the basin finally selects a singular, cathedral-sized draft, the Scar will seal like a sutured wound, time will spool forward, and all stored Tuesdays will fall as proper rain.  Until then, both sites keep the ledger balanced: one extrudes possibility, the other warehouses deferred duration.

Exchange between places  
• Heat and Light.  Husk-blossoms catch photons that stray; at dusk they sigh them out, and a polar jet delivers the pale glare here by midnight.  Locals angle burnished sheets to trap the arrival, using it to tan their reflective tents golden.  
• Population drift.  A Cartographer of Regret sometimes mislays a map-strip and follows it down the heat-plume.  In the Scar they become Hour-Glassers, trading vellum for vellum.  Conversely, a Tuesday Nomad who acquires too many pending mornings may walk the plume sunward and wake inside the basin as a First Imagined, half-finished but full of intention.  
• Commerce in absences.  The Scrip-Jackals’ shed ribbons blow basin-ward, tangle in catwalk handrails, and are read by lattice-mites as structural annotations: “space available where certainty once lay.”  Buildings grow compliant gaps, making room for probabilities that have found a new address.

Epilogue—standing in both places at once  
Balance on the Scar’s rim during a waste-heat blink-kiln.  The chalk underfoot flashes glassy; in the reflection you see the basin’s mezzanine night-lights, two stories becoming four, catwalks swarming with deferred engineers.  Echoes of the Slow Choir wash across sky the wind has not decided to invent.  For a heartbeat and its revenant twin you understand: the world is a double-exposed negative, one frame from the moment of imagining, the other from the instant of letting go.  Between them runs a warm-cold draught of borrowed entropy, a thread you could follow in either direction.  And whichever way you walk, the place behind you will keep the ledger, patient, until all stories choose a single noon.
