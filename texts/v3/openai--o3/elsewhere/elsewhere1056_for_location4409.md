The Weft-Marshes of Rethen

Between the basalt cliffs of Keirr and the first true horizon below lies a single long scream of falling water. On the three days the plateau’s river wakes, it spills off the rim in a thousand filament streams, shreds itself to fog halfway down, and arrives on the valley floor as an endless soft rain the locals call the Drift. That rain redraws the land every night, and where it lingers it breeds the Weft-Marshes—sixty square kilometres of soil so eager to move that no map of it has ever remained accurate for more than a week.

Terrain  
• A quilt of floating peat rafts, plaited reed mats, and bare mineral sandbars. Each piece shifts underfoot; step on a hummock and it glides a metre before settling somewhere new.  
• Runnels from the Drift gather into braid-rivers that twist, choke, and cleave apart again, never running straight for more than a bow-shot. The biggest braid, the Ayli, completes an entirely new course every ninth sunrise; elders stake coloured poles at its morning mouth and wager on where dusk will find them.  
• Beneath the surface is an under-marsh, a honeycomb void in which stonework from vanished civilizations lies half-suspended, half-dissolving. Staircases surface like teeth, then slip away.  
• On the eastern fringe a single obsidian boulder rests, fallen from the plateau during its birth. It is the one thing that never moves, and its stark stillness gives the marsh-folk a reference point they call True North.

Atmosphere & Light  
The air tastes of iron and wet leaves. Mist rarely clears before noon; after it does, sunlight scatters through suspended droplets and paints everything in soft haloes. Sunsets are abrupt: the plateau’s wall bites the sun clean in half, and shadow washes across the wetlands like ink spilled into cotton.

Sound  
Water talks incessantly here. Slaps, burbles, gulping sounds as peat rafts inhale through unseen vents. At night you hear roots tearing, rafts colliding, the distant thunder of fresh cliff-water arriving. Silence exists only inside reed lodges quilted with eel skins.

Vegetation & Fauna  
Plants pursue motion as fiercely as Keirr venerates stillness.  
• Thread-willows grow metre-long, hair-thin branches that snap off and drift away to reroot elsewhere, knitting the marsh in silver tangles.  
• Burst-lilies build pressure in sealed bulbs; when heavy enough they rocket into the air, scatter seeds, and leave a fist-sized crater that instantly floods.  
• Sump-reeds weave together and migrate like carpets pushed by wind; marshers cut them into living boats.

Animals include:  
• Skitter-swine with fan-shaped hooves that skate the mud, forever rooting, forever forgetting where. Their flesh is rich with copper salts; left unhandled it greens within hours.  
• Lantern eels that glow where touched; they school beneath walking rafts, advising cautious feet.  
• Bloom herons: feathers host symbiotic algae that change colour with pH; flocks flying overhead give instant weather forecasts in shifting pastel banners.

Built World  
Rethen is not a city but a sprawl of flotillas:  
1. Lodges: reed-frame domes lashed to buoyant cedar skeletons scavenged from ancient driftwood. Each has four tow-loops so neighbours can rearrange streets on a whim.  
2. Causeways: chains of lily-crate pontoons anchored to weighted silk lines; when the Ayli shoulders a causeway aside, marshers simply walk the new angle.  
3. Market: an ever-moving ring of twenty barges tied bow-to-stern. Vendors announce position by conch; buyers chase sound, not place.  
4. The Slipforge: a barge-mounted smithy whose bellows are tide-driven pistons. Iron rusts within days, so the smiths fold peat tar into every billet; blades here bleed black sap when sharpened.

Technology revels in transience. Hinges are braided leather, knots are preferred to nails, and every tool handle ends in a float bladder so the marsh can reclaim nothing unnoticed.

Social Rhythm  
Where Keirr sounds a gong to make stillness, Rethen sounds whistles to keep motion paired. At dawn the Chordmaster blows three notes:  
• First note, “Gather”: families pull lodges together for roll-call.  
• Second, “Loose”: everyone scatters to fish, forage, barter.  
• Third, at dusk, “Weave”: rafts are linked anew, no pattern repeated twice.  
Children learn directional improvisation before they learn numbers; they navigate by the audible slosh of specific channels and the distinctive taste of wind on mist.

Memory & Story  
What the plateau fixes, the marsh edits. No one here trusts recollection beyond a week; they write everything on eel-skin scrolls coated in quicklime that dissolves after nine days, enforcing perpetual revision. A marsher’s worth lies in her capacity to adapt, not to remember. Courtship lasts until both partners forget how it started and agree the bond is therefore new each morning.

Belief  
The marsh-folk revere Flux, personified as a many-jointed heron who never folds its wings. Prayer is the act of untying something that has been knotted too long. Pilgrims sometimes climb the cliff ladders to Keirr and leave coils of fraying rope as offerings, trusting the plateau’s stillness to grant those ropes a quiet death.

Unusual Traits (the loud weirdness)  
• Patina Bloom: metals not only oxidize but sprout dendritic frills—green copper ferns, red-brown iron lace. Marshers cultivate the bloom on ceremonial masks that are grown, not carved.  
• Echo-Slip: shout into fog and the words may return rearranged, grammar liquefied. Poets stroll the densest banks fishing for accidental verses.  
• The Forgetting: once a season an event called simply Shift occurs. Everyone wakes with one personal fact missing—name of a parent, location of a scar, the recipe for sour-reed bread. They jot the vacancy on communal slates, laugh, and move on.

Inhabitants

1. Marshers (the Ayli-kin)  
Body: broad-shouldered, calves knotted like ropes from balancing on narrow planks. Skin bronzed where mud acids burned old pigment away. Hair bleached by fog to a pale rust colour, streaked anew every month.  
Dress: layered reed-cloth smeared with peat tar for waterproofing, pockets stitched outside for easy drainage. Jewelry is living patina; copper bracelets molt flakes with every handshake.  
Speech: A click-rich tongue called Shath that uses vowel length to mark certainty. Because certainty decays, most sentences end in the shortest vowel, a whispered “a.”  
Governance: The Tangle, an open council that cannot adjourn until every delegate has physically changed seats at least once; the final resolution is whichever idea survives the seating scramble.  
Trade: sponge-fish oil, quick-rotting herbs prized as dye catalysts, and the marshers’ famed Drift-Charts—maps painted on eel skin whose deliberate half-life guarantees commercial secrets will vanish on schedule.

2. Relational Creatures

Slip-Cats  
Semi-aquatic felines with fluid joints; bones slide within a cartilaginous sheath, letting them pour through reed walls. Keirr merchants keep them as novelties; on the plateau the cats grow unnerved by stillness and soon beg to be lowered back down.

Guide-Moths  
Wings patterned like topographical lines that rearrange nightly to match the shifting channels. Marshers tether them to boat prows; a moth that flutters inland signals a new sandbar forming.

3. The Unseen

Root-Minds  
Interlocking fungal networks kilometres wide. When too much stillness threatens—an anchored lodge ignored for months—the Root-Mind thickens under it, lifting the raft until occupants drift free. Marshers leave bowls of rush-milk as thanks and never try to chart the networks twice.

Mirror-Pool Drifters  
Travellers from Keirr sometimes visit, but their reflections lag far behind, lost amid the gleam of water. Marsh children chase those stray images across the slick flats, net them in polished copper hoops, and set them adrift toward the sea, believing every released reflection lightens the plateau above by exactly one withheld memory.

Lines of Connection

• Water: The Drift begins as silence-caught dew on Keirr’s ridges, becomes roar, then arrives as the fine rain that writes and rewrites Rethen.  
• Trade: Marshers send barrels of patina wash uphill; the Keirri paint it onto ceremonial stones, fascinated by colour they cannot grow themselves. In return they send obsidian blades whose impossible edges survive only days before the marsh rusts their bindings loose—perfect tools for a land that forgets.  
• Kinship of Contraries: Plateau children born with rim-pupils sometimes apprentice in Rethen to learn how to lose a memory safely; marsh adolescents spend a season in Keirr to learn the gravity of a kept promise. Each considers the other a necessary opposite, a breathing hinge on which the world’s balance turns.

In the same breath that the plateau holds, the marsh exhales. One hoards the knife-edge instant of becoming; the other honors the moment after, when form sloughs away and possibility floods in. Between them hangs a slender plait of rope and mist and memory, forever tightening, forever fraying, never quite severed, never quite still.
