THE BREATH-ABOVE


1. Immediate impression  
Air as thin as blown sugar whistles across the palate, shock-cold and scented faintly of ozone and dust. Pale auroras crease the daylight sky—veils of radon-laced vapor venting from fissures far below—so that the sun seems to hang inside a cathedral of moving stained glass. Nothing touches the ground without permission; most of what matters is aloft, drifting, or tethered only by memory.

2. Terrain and materials  
• The plateau itself was once a shield volcano. Its crown exploded when the first Tremor-matriarch rammed the crust, leaving a caldera fifty kilometers wide and open to space on one side like a cracked bowl.  
• Across that void hover floes of hollow, pumice-light stone called cloudslag. They form when Churn-Under steam columns hit the stratosphere, flash-freeze, and foam into buoyant plates. Oldest floes have gardens; the newest are still sizzling, rimmed with ice.  
• Tendons—cables of ferromagnesian glass extruded by sky-moss—dangle from the floes, anchoring them to the caldera rim but stretching like violin strings when updrafts tense. The whole archipelago groans in a slow, oceanic rhythm.  
• Between islands yawns nothing but blue glare and boiling thermals. Falling means two deaths: freezing first, then burning as the draft slams you through exhaust vents on the way down.

3. Water (0.9 / 3 – scarce)  
Moisture arrives as diamond-bright mist at dawn. It beads on any surface colder than a breath, then sublimes by noon. Cisterns are living things here: balloon-lunged polyps called siphonodes swell at sunrise, filter water from air, then waddle to shaded crevices to be tapped by whoever raised them.

4. Light (3 / 3 – brilliant)  
The sky is a double furnace. From above, the sun blasts unfiltered radiance; from below, photolith glare ricochets up volcanic chimney-shafts, fracturing into amber searchlights that paint the underbellies of clouds. Shadows are two-tiered, nested like matryoshka dolls that swivel as either source shifts.

5. Vegetation (1.7 / 3 – sparse but persistent)  
• Sky-moss: basalt-black crust that excretes those glass cables. Feeds on ultraviolet light and metals scavenged from airborne dust. Crumbles if shaded for more than an hour.  
• Mirror-kelp: silver fronds growing on the lower faces of cloudslag. They drink radiance directly, bending to keep both sides evenly cooked; a sudden eclipse will snap them like frosted grass.  
• Frost-succulents: bulbous organs of antifreeze gel that bloom only when night temperatures crash. Their petals are thin as soap film and shatter at a touch, scattering seeds encoded to remain dormant until rewarmed by steam tornados.

6. Fauna (1.9 / 3 – lively, specialized)  
• Up-and-unders: bat-wide gliders whose bones are hollow latticeworks of fused Skitterwake dust. They breed exclusively in the steam plumes that connect down to the Churn-Under, migrating on convection cycles: forty-hour spirals from heat to cold and back.  
• Cryosphinxes: leonine, six-legged predators with ribbed wings of horn. They stalk the shadow-bands cast by floating islands, pouncing on anything that forgets a second sun shines upward. Their roar is a vented cough of super-cold air; prey flash-freezes before the bite.  
• Censers: jellyfish balloons filled with hydrogen cracked from ice crystals. Tentacles drip iridescent oil that ignites where photolith beams converge, leaving ribbon trails of flame. Flintkin traders prize this oil to anneal obsidian implants without cracking flesh.  
• Apex: the Spiral-Father, a single, slow cyclone that never leaves the caldera bowl. It herds islands the way a sheepdog guides flock, its eye warm as summer. Everything migrates along the Spiral’s wall; when it stalls, hunger comes.

7. Sound (1.0 / 3 – hushed, tensile)  
Wind rules, but it is stretched thin. You hear it as a keening ribbon, broken only by the bass cough of a cloudslag plate slamming a new thermal or the crystalline chime of mirror-kelp pruning itself in too-bright noon. Silence feels tall here, climbing out of one’s chest like altitude sickness.

8. Built presence (1.3 / 3 – nomadic ingenuity)  
The high world hosts the Aether-Riggers—sky-born cousins of the Flintkin.  
• They knot habitats from glass cables, stitching sails of mirror-kelp pressed flat and fused.  
• Tools are weighted not by mass but by drag: blades with airfoil teeth, hammers that whistle to warn of over-swing.  
• House-boats are inverted—roofs concave to catch dawn mist; floors slotted, to let ballast-stones fall away when lift is needed. Nothing permanent, but nothing rushed; a rigged span often lasts a generation before unmooring.

9. Climate and dynamics (2.5 / 3 – restless, cyclical)  
Morning: freeze, white light, brittle air.  
Midday: thermal bloom; islands rise, sails slack, predators bask.  
Dusk: vertical rivers of steam surge from the Churn-Under, fresh cloudslag nucleates, ash-snow falls in hot flakes that melt mid-air.  
Night: Spiral-Father tightens; lightning filaments run up glass tethers, singing stray notes of the matriarch chords deep below.

10. Rules of the world  
• Weight is negotiable; surface area is law.  
• Heat travels upward as memory and returns as weather—every storm is a story the Churn-Under told hours ago.  
• Ultraviolet is currency; anything that can store it (sky-moss, mirror-kelp oil, even bone) is bartered like gold.  
• To cast a shadow on someone without consent is insult; you have stolen their second chance to see danger coming.

11. What it feels like to stand here  
Boot soles crunch a frost that is mostly salt condensed out of boiling vapor. Breaths come shallow; each exhale scribes a silver thread that whips away before finishing the sentence. The ground tilts, but it is the island drifting, not you. Your inner ear keeps recalibrating, so balance becomes a prayer more than a skill. Look down through a gap in the slag and you glimpse the Churn-Under’s ember glow—an eye staring back through leagues of hollow stone. Somewhere a cryosphinx coughs; the sound snaps into vapor a meter from its mouth and never reaches you. The sky feels perilously near, like the inside of an eggshell brushing the crown of your head.

12. Where it is going  
The Breath-Above grows by accretion: every fresh steam-burst lofts new floes, every floe seeds moss, every moss spins more anchors. Someday islands will web the entire caldera and roof it over—a ceiling for the underworld, a floor for some third country yet unborn. Or a matriarch will surface, cough once, and the archipelago will avalanche into incandescent ruin. Until then the Aether-Riggers sail the moving labyrinth, reading the Spiral-Father’s barometric scripture, trading kelp-oil for Flintkin knives hoisted up on steam pillars, and listening for those infrasonic chords that tell them when the world beneath their feet has changed its mind again.



LIFE IN THE BREATH-ABOVE

I.  Small beginnings  
1.  Vaporwisp algae  
    • Chains of single cells encased in silica bubbles; they drift inside steam columns, photosynthesizing on the way up, freezing into dormant spirals on the way down.  
    • When shattered by altitude pressure, they bleed antifreeze proteins that seed cloudslag formation.  
2.  Iron-halo spores  
    • Origin: ferro-lichens of the Churn-Under, coughed skyward as dust.  
    • Behavior: magnetize in flight, arranging into microscopic rings that diffract sunlight into the visible auroras.  
    • Fate: caught by sky-moss, giving it the metals it craves.

II.  Structural gardeners  
1.  Sky-moss (expanded)  
    • Genome riddled with repeats of a single instruction: “extrude, cool, repeat.”  
    • Responds to sonic vibrations; Aether-Riggers “sing” architecture into being, coaxing cables into catenaries stronger than steel.  
2.  Mirror-kelp  
    • Each frond is a parabolic optic. Colonies self-focus sunlight onto shared root-nodes, melting pockets in cloudslag to drink the mineral soup.  
    • In drought they curl into spirals that act as gyroscopes, stabilizing their host island against crosswinds.

III.  The running middle  
a)  Up-and-unders  
    • Circulatory system doubles as radiator: blood absorbs heat during descent into steam plumes and vents it through wing-veins on the climb.  
    • Wingbeat frequency syncs with photolith flicker; observers beneath see a living Morse code forecasting the steam cycle.  
b)  Ice-harthares  
    • Rabbit-sized endotherms that harvest mirror-kelp petals at dusk.  
    • Furnish burrows with discarded censor membranes; when predators approach, the stored static ignites, propelling the hare in a ballistic arc to a neighbor island.  
c)  Censers  
    • Nervous system is piezoelectric; turbulence flexes the bell, generating the spark that lights its oil.  
    • Reproduce when two flight paths braid; the knot of flame catalyzes gamete fusion, then drips a glowing pear-shaped egg onto an updraft.

IV.  Apex meteorology  
Spiral-Father  

Not a beast but an organismal weather cell: heat engine + microbial culture + electromagnetic skeleton.  

Anatomy:  
• Core column of ionized steam threaded by lightning; pressure gradients sustain lift the way a heart keeps blood.  
• Sheath of nested vortices seeded with vaporwisp algae—each micron-scale eddy is a lung exchanging heat for momentum.  

Ecology:  
• Shepherds cloudslag, preventing collisions. Islands that drift too high are rimed with frost; too low, they sublimate. The Spiral keeps the choir in tune.  
• Speaks in barometric tongues; tremors in its pressure wall arrive as auroral flickers read by riggers as prophecy.  

Death / Rebirth:  
• When volcanic calm starves it of steam, the Spiral collapses, snowing hot hail. Within minutes, upward drafts birth a successor around the old eye. Memory is atmospheric—passed not by cells but by standing waveforms in the caldera basin.

V.  The Aether-Riggers – speaking people  
1.  Physiology  
    • Similar stature to Flintkin but elongated limbs, marrow spongy with gas pockets for buoyancy.  
    • Dermis seeded with mica scales that angle to reflect UV away from internal organs.  
    • Hemoglobin replaced by cobalt-cyan complex that binds oxygen efficiently in thin air; blood glints blue-black.  
2.  Language  
    • Vowel-heavy whistles lattice with hand-fans woven from frost-succulent skins; each flick steers free vowels through specific doppler shifts.  
    • Proper nouns traced in contrails from heated kelp oil; the name hangs glowing for whole seconds before vanishing.  
3.  Lifeways  
    • Sleep in nets hung beneath islands, rocked by the Spiral’s breathing. Dreams contain a steady horizon—something waking life never grants.  
    • Diet: sky-moss paste, censor tassels caramelized in reflected sun, and occasional cryosphinx marrow traded for mirror-kelp rope with daring Flintkin climbers.  
4.  Social geometry  
    • Crews of seven rope their ankles together when walking a new floe—whoever falls teaches the rest by tension alone.  
    • Debts are recorded in shadow: step over someone’s silhouette and you inherit whatever promise they have yet to keep.  
5.  Cosmology  
    • The world is a double lung. The Churn-Under inhales stone and exhales steam; the Breath-Above inhales light and exhales weather. To live rightly is to keep both breaths reciprocating.  
    • Final rite: upon death, a rigger’s body is weighted with mirror-kelp mirrors angled to bounce sun into the caldera, speeding the next steam bloom—one last respiration.

VI.  Edge dwellers & rumors  
1.  Glasswyrms—translucent eel shapes that swim inside photolith beams themselves, visible only where dust outlines them. Said to be the larval form of the Spiral-Father, but no proof.  
2.  Altitude-leeches—counterparts to the echo-leech; they feed on pressure differential. Attach to ears, causing brief euphoria as they lighten the blood, then drop off just before victim blacks out.  
3.  The Returning Island—legend of a floe punched loose a century ago, carried into the high jet stream, now circumnavigating the globe. Riggers watch the horizon for a plate ringed in city-bright kelp and carrying stories from lands no steam ever touched.

VII.  What it means to be alive here  
• Balance is prayer: toes read the wind through stone.  
• Hunger is the distance between thermals.  
• Every shadow is double, and so is every choice.  
• To die is to fall, and falling is only returning to the heat that first breathed you upward.


Connection, always  
The Breath-Above is the exhale of the Churn-Under; the Churn-Under is the condensation of the Breath-Above. Steam climbs, slag falls, knives rise, oil drips. Flintkin send obsidian-inked songs up plume chimneys; Riggers answer by blinking mirror codes down the throats of light between islands. Neither place is whole without the other’s breath in its lungs, and the world—raw, molten, sky-cracked—beats on, a two-chambered heart that refuses every offer of stasis.
