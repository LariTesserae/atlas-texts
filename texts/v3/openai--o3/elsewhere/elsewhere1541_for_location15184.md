The Meridian Sill  

Step out of the last billow of cloud that roofs the Hollow Basin and you arrive on the upward face of the same weather engine: a mile-high cornice of fused quartz and alabaster that hangs like a balcony over the storm.  The Sill is sun-struck, wind-raw, and loud with the things that brightness invents when it has too much room.

--------------------------------------------------
0. How the Two Places Share One Breath
--------------------------------------------------
The Basin exhales: warm vapor, charged minerals, unfinished echoes.  
The Sill inhales: the same column of up-draft, now cooled to clarity, its load of salts flash-freezing into glass dust that sleets across the plateau.  
What the Sill sheds—hail, powdered light, stray harmonics—falls again, repopulating the Basin’s mist.  Every seven hours the cycle closes; each realm is the other’s weather front turned inside-out.

--------------------------------------------------
1. Terrain
--------------------------------------------------
• Elevation: 1 960 m above the Basin’s mirror flats—a single tectonic sheet that tilted long ago and never bothered to crack.  
• Surface: Pavement of intergrown quartz tiles, hexagonal, big as dinner tables.  They ring when walked on, a random carillon the wind keeps resetting.  
• Edges: The southern rim breaks into organ-pipe spires, their hollows tuned to different Beaufort numbers.  During gales the whole escarpment becomes a white cathedral organ audible for forty kilometers.  
• Substrate: Hollow pockets a meter below grade hold trapped air from older climates; tap a tile free and yesterday’s stratosphere sighs out, flute-clear, then vanishes.

--------------------------------------------------
2. Sky & Light
--------------------------------------------------
• Day is tyrannical: unfiltered sun nine tenths of the year, high-angle, razor-colored.  
• Nights are not dark: starlight diffuses through perpetual shards of airborne ice, giving the plateau a silver self-illumination bright enough to cast multiple moonless shadows.  
• Optical Anomalies: Parhelia bloom like triplet suns; vertical columns of light (“scoriants”) rise from any reflective facet; your own silhouette sometimes fractures into a procession of smaller outlines marching off the rim.

--------------------------------------------------
3. Water & Weather
--------------------------------------------------
• Precipitation falls upward.  Mist that evaporates from the Basin reaches the Sill as a geysering plume.  On contact with sub-zero surface wind it condenses into ascending “ice-smoke” that the locals call white fire.  
• True rain is impossible: droplets freeze into lenticular discs (sun-coins) that hover until massing into flurries, then glide sideways into nothing.  
• Once a season the column stalls: thunderheads breach the rim and roll across the tiles, trying on mountainhood for an hour before the sun melts them back into sky.

--------------------------------------------------
4. Vegetation
--------------------------------------------------
• Sun-Bract Lichens: Flat polygons the color of solar flares, each a lattice of silica and chloroplasts.  They etch themselves into quartz, slowly digesting the upper millimeter for nutrients.  
• Glass-Grass: Needle-thin culms of flexible obsidian, swaying like kelp in air currents.  They amplify wind into infrasonic tremors that root-bound fungi use as heartbeat cues.  
• Helio-Kelps: Ribbon strips of cellulose shot with metallic veins; they loft on thermal up-drafts, photosynthesizing while airborne, then drape back over boulders at dusk like discarded flags.

--------------------------------------------------
5. Fauna
--------------------------------------------------
a. Prism Voles  
• Marble-small rodents whose fur is transparent keratin.  Internal organs glitter refracted; predators cannot fix on a shape.  They gnaw lichen patterns, leaving Morse-like holes the wind reads aloud.  

b. Flare-Drakes  
• Not dragons but colonial seraphims of heated air wrapped in scales of mica.  They rise each noon, riding thermals, steering with electrostatic fins.  When two collide, the rasping shower of sparks fertilizes lichen spores below.  

c. Glass-Owls  
• Planar wings, feathers replaced by overlapping quartz chips.  They hunt by refraction: turn one degree and vanish into sun-shear, reappear talons-first.  Their hoot is the sound of blown bottles cooling.

--------------------------------------------------
6. Built / Not-Built
--------------------------------------------------
• The Beacon Stair: 4 300 steps cut by no hand, each slightly thicker than the next.  Climbers who descend report the count unchanged, a reminder that number is a local superstition.  
• Lens-Houses: Hemispheres of fused sand with a single circular aperture.  Daylight enters, converges, and projects the Basin’s unseen streets inverted on the rear wall—live, though half a mile of cloud intervenes.  Scholars arrive to watch “down-world” events in this upside-cinema, taking notes they must burn before going home (fire thrives here).  
• The Wind-Turnery: Forest of vertical paddles that rotate whenever collective memory peaks.  The paddles carve text into the air—pressure waves that drift off the rim and are readable as premature echo inside Basin corridors three hours later.

--------------------------------------------------
7. Sound
--------------------------------------------------
• Dominant noise: wind keyed through stone pipes.  Pitch scales with humidity rising from below; veterans can forecast gravity inversions in the Basin by listening for a minor third drop.  
• The tiles hum ultrasonically under pure noon heat.  Some claim the tone tunes human bones, lengthening the tibia by microns every summer.  
• Silence is rare but absolute: in the wind-shadow of Lens-Houses voices feel claustrophobic, as if spoken into a jar.

--------------------------------------------------
8. Physical Errors (The Sill’s “Clarities”)
--------------------------------------------------
• Heat blooms faster than thermodynamics allow; a pot of water will boil merely by remembering yesterday’s sun.  
• Shadows may peel loose at dusk, lingering like black scarves until stamped back into ownership.  
• Clocks over-perform: their hands race, wearing grooves in the crystal faces.  Timepool-keepers from the Basin journey here to salvage the excess seconds, bottling them as glitter for gravity games below.

--------------------------------------------------
9. Human Presence
--------------------------------------------------
• The Dazzlewrights: Migrants who cannot bear the Basin’s dream-dark commute upward.  They mine sun-coins, melt them into mirrors, and trade them down-shaft for moss-cakes and silence.  
• Storm-Archivists: Roped scholars who dangle microphones into the vapor plume to record phrases the Wind-Turnery engraves.  Their vaults contain tomorrow’s Basin footfalls neatly catalogued on wax cylinders.  
• Glass-Nurses: Order of climatologists-cum-monks who polish the organ-pipe spires; they claim roughness births disharmonic squalls that could shatter the entire rim in one misguided chord.

--------------------------------------------------
10. Color & Material
--------------------------------------------------
• Palette is vehement: cobalt shadows, lemon-white sun-glare, prismatic scars where ice-smoke scores the quartz.  
• Textures clash: lichen velvet over mirror glass, wind-frost that feathers hot stone.  Blood spilled here crystallizes into ruby beads locals string into rosaries against glare-blindness.

--------------------------------------------------
11. Emergent Myths
--------------------------------------------------
• It is whispered the Sill is the Basin’s eyelid.  When it finally blinks, darkness below will flood with day and the plateau will be plunged into the dream in its stead.  
• Elders speak of a third place—the Understar Sea—where both runoff and excess light are stored, waiting for a cycle grand enough to need them.

--------------------------------------------------
12. How to Walk the Meridian
--------------------------------------------------
1. Wear three shadows: one beneath your feet, two tethered on poles.  If a shadow wanders you still own enough to find yourself.  
2. Speak only on in-breath while crossing organ pipes; exhale-speech seeds destructive resonance.  
3. Offer silence at dawn: kneel, cup ears, gift the Basin’s muffled drip to the ringing air.  In return the wind will leave one sense—usually taste—mercifully intact.

--------------------------------------------------
When the False Sunrise Rises
--------------------------------------------------
Every few years the Basin flares orange; simultaneously the Sill suffers its Negative Noon.  For sixteen minutes light here cancels itself—quartz dims, sun-coins tarnish mid-air, glass-owls roost bewildered.  From the escarpment rim you can watch darkness pour downward like ink, meet the Basin’s borrowed dawn halfway, and knot itself into a standing seam that holds the world together for one perfect, impossible chord.

Afterward everything reverts: the Basin resumes pretending that almost nothing lives; the Sill resumes pretending that everything must.  And between them, the long exhale-inhale continues, trading water for light, silence for ringing, dream for glare—each place living by what the other chooses to give away.
