Aurashin Mirror Flats  

First sensation: brilliance—dry, blistering, total. Sunlight is not a beam here but an atmosphere: a white, weightless pressure that forces the eyes to water and the breath to slow. Heat wavers upward in panes, making every outline quiver like a candle flame. The sky is a hard enamel bowl, cloudless nine days out of ten; when clouds do arrive they appear black against the over-lit ground, their own shadows cast above them.

TERRAIN  
Picture a coastal shelf 120 kilometres long, lifted just a palm’s breadth above sea level by some long-ago tectonic shrug. From above it looks like frost, but the glitter is salt—layer after layer of evaporite so pure it fractures light into pale rainbows. In places the crust has collapsed, revealing liquid brine the colour of polished copper. Wind has planed the surface into faceted pans; where two edges meet, razor-thin ridges rise knee-high and knife-straight, tracing cartographies only birds can read. At the western rim the shelf ends abruptly in a precipice: a white wall two hundred metres tall that overhangs the drowned delta where the Pelagoth city breathes below.

BUILT FORMS (WITH MINIMUM MACHINERY)  
Structures stand sparingly, for anything that casts a shadow is suspect. Each dwelling begins as a pit melted with focused mirrors, then lined with panes of sun-fused salt-glass. Walls are no thicker than a finger, translucent as spun sugar, yet strong under compression. From these cool hollows rise heliostats: towers of interlocking salt prisms that track the sun by thermal expansion alone. Thin cords of dried kelp, traded up the cliff by manta caravans, suspend mirrored vanes that rotate in the afternoon wind, fanning light downward to where it is husbanded like grain. At night the towers gutter and amber lamps—globes of vacuum-blown salt filled with slow-burning magnesium shavings—take over, dotting the flats like a constellation that has fallen to earth.

DENSITY & SCALE  
Settlements cluster in sparse archipelagos: a dozen light-pits here, three or four there, always divided by wide fields of unmarked crust. To walk from one hamlet to the next you navigate by glare—following the faintest change in polarization that signals a community’s heliostat bloom over the horizon. The largest gathering, Noonmarket, spreads across five square kilometres and disappears visually if a single dust squall drifts between you and its mirrors.

ATMOSPHERE & LIGHT  
Ambient brightness measures a steady 130 kLx—enough to sun-bleach bone in three days. Everything that lives here has defenses: reflective skin, nictitating membranes, habits of stillness. Dusk lasts no more than fifteen minutes, a violet shiver before the sky snaps to black and the salt crust exhales the day’s hoarded heat in fragrant, mineral steam.

SOUND  
Wind is maestro. In daylight it hisses across crystal ridges; at night it drops, and the flats click—billions of contracting salt plates making music like distant cutlery. Heliostat vanes add a glassy creak. Voices carry kilometres, every word prisamed into ragged echoes.

VEGETATION  
None in the ordinary sense. Life roots sideways, not downward: mats of halophilic cyanobacteria quilt the brine pools, rose and emerald under grazing sunlight. Their dried films are peeled up and spun into paper. In deeper pits bloom tuberous salt cacti—swellings of cellulose ballooned with brine, flowering only during rare monsoon fogs.

FAUNA & ECOLOGY  
• Skath—serpentine lizards with mirror-flake osteoderms; they bask until their cores reach 45 °C, then plunge into brine to shed heat, leaving ripples of rainbow oil.  
• Gleam-moths—day-flying lepidoptera whose wings are sheets of chitinous diffraction grating; clouds of them can dazzle a hawk unconscious.  
• Silt-gulls—lean, sand-coloured birds that gorge on Gleam-moths, accumulate mica in their gizzards, and vomit glittering pellets over sea cliffs; many of those pellets spiral down to seed Pelagoth mirror-hoards.  
• Brine-shrikes—finger-sized arthropods that farm cyanobacteria, staking out patches with pillars of crystallised salt they excrete through back vents.

DYNAMIC PROCESSES  
Every noon a convection front forms where the flats meet the cool coastal air. Updrafts snatch dust, insect, and glittering refuse into a thermal river that climbs, arcs out over the cliff, and rains slow debris into the brown-black water below. At night the river reverses: dense maritime air crawls landward, licking the salt crust with fog fingers. The people call these reciprocations the Two Breaths—outward to give, inward to taste what the sea returns.

RULES OF THE WORLD (AS HELD HERE)  
1. Shadow is property. To step into shade you did not cast or earn is trespass.  
2. Light must never be wasted darkward. Reflectors are angled so their last ricochet strikes growing cyanobacteria or a neighbour’s collector pit.  
3. Glass remembers heat; cracked panes are archived, their fracture maps read like weather annals.  
4. Anything too bright to look upon alone becomes communal—brightness is dilution.  
5. Stories prefer glare: epics are signalled in heliograph across kilometres of crust, requiring spectators to decode flashes with closed eyes.

MOODS THAT LINGER  
• A hiss of desiccation, constant, like a kettle forever about to boil dry.  
• The ache of squinting even when your eyes are shut.  
• Solitude so stark your heartbeat sounds like a drum on marble.

WHO LIVES HERE – THE KAILOR  

They call themselves the Kailor, a word carried by the back of the throat then released as a shimmer in the air—heat-haze language. The Pelagoth know only the taste of the word, delivered downcolumn as packets of shadow-bubbles the manta couriers bring.

1. BODY  
• Form. Humanoid in proportion but rangy, sinews strung like cables. Skin is opaline—layers of transparent keratin over scales of guanine crystal that act as living sun-shields. When a Kailor stands still enough, mirage consumes the outline until only a tremor of brightness remains.  
• Eyes. Four per face: two primary with gold-flecked irises, two auxiliary pinholes along the brow ridge to triangulate glare. Secondary lids slurry tears into evaporating mist that leaves salt comets on the cheekbones.  
• Hair. Thread-thin rods of hollow glass extruded from follicles; they chime in wind and refract spectral halos round the skull.  
• Hands. Palms contain arrays of microscopic lens pits; by flexing fingers they focus sun into cutting beams—a tool and a weapon bound to the flesh.

2. GROWTH & REPRODUCTION  
Kailor children are born under night fog in brine-lined cradles. Until age five they wear loose robes woven from gull gut to preserve moisture. Puberty begins when a adolescent can stand ten minutes in noon glare without blinking; the coming-of-heat is celebrated by the grafting of the first mirror-plate harvested from a personal Skath companion. Average lifespan is 70 bright-years (reckoned as cumulative time spent in direct sun), roughly 90 revolutions of the planet.

3. SENSE & SPEECH  
• Hearing is dulled by constant wind; instead they read turbulence—speech is patterns of heat ripple cast from the thoracic air-bladder. Close conversation feels like sharing one breath between two mouths.  
• Writing is illustration in mirrored flash; a message can be tattooed by briefly angling a heliostat to burn code into the salt crust, readable for weeks until abrasion erases it.  
• Long tales are glass necklaces: each bead a different fracture pattern whose caustic projections become the narrative when spun before a fire.

4. WORK & EVERYDAY LIFE  
• Briners descend on rope ladders to harvest coppery pools of magnesium-rich liquor, evaporating it into trade ingots.  
• Prism-wrights sculpt salt crystal into calibrated wedges, later sold to Star-shade cartographers who map the heavens by bending starlight during the brief dusk.  
• Glitter-shepherds keep flocks of Gleam-moths in portable shade tents; once a week they milk scales for iridescent varnish, discarding rejects into the updraft where silt-gulls collect them—and so, eventually, the Pelagoth find mirrors drifting down.  
• Fog-chanters nightly coax maritime vapour inland with low throatsongs; the correct frequency seeds condensation on heliostat frames, yielding precious drinking water.

5. VALUES  
Light is livelihood; darkness is luxury. An hour spent indoors is celebrated like a festival. Coolth (measured in minutes below 25 °C skin temperature) functions as currency; the richest households own deep cellars forever out of sun. Gratitude is shown by gifting shadow: someone may stand between you and the sun for as long as the favour is owed.

Stillness, too, is honoured—but inverted from the Pelagoth kind. Here, to freeze under immense heat is a feat of endurance; competitions are held at midsummer to see who can remain motionless on open crust until the first skin blister hisses.

6. RELATIONS WITH OTHER CREATURES  
• Skath lizards are partners: each Kailor bonds with one at adolescence, sharing shade and scale scraps. When a Skath dies its osteoderms are polished into ceremonial mirrors and bequeathed to the bonded friend in the sea below.  
• Silt-gulls are tolerated thieves; their mica pellets are the principal export to the Basin, even if unintentionally.  
• Giant mantas (the same saddle species that visit the City-folk) bank up the cliff face to have glare-mites plucked from their dorsal skins. In return they serve as mail carriers, weaving between thermal columns to drop crystal tablets weighted with kelp-rope into predetermined sinkholes where Pelagoth gleaners wait.

7. DANGERS & CONFLICT  
Mirage Quakes: when differential heating shears whole plates of salt loose, the ground ripples like water, swallowing structures. Dust Fire: airborne magnesium ignited by static discharge, a violet inferno that races the wind faster than a sprinting Skath. Conflict among Kailor rarely turns violent; disputes are settled by Shadow Duels—contestants chase one another’s silhouettes across a marked field at dawn, attempting to overrun and thus ‘own’ the opponent’s darkness.

8. RITUALS THAT MARK A LIFE  
• First Glare: the inaugural, unaided step onto noon crust. Witnesses form a closed ring of mirrors so the child’s tiny shadow is multiplied a hundredfold, giving it weight.  
• Salt-Naming: adults carve their personal sign into the overhang facing the Pelagoth basin. Each year the sign is re-cut lower as the cliff sublimates; elders may have a dozen iterations descending the wall, trackable by undersea wanderers.  
• Eclipse Vigil: once every forty-two years the moon occludes the sun; Kailor lie face-up on the crust in silence, tasting what true night feels like. Legend says the Pelagoth above-below answer with a basin-wide hush.

9. IF YOU ARRIVE AS A VISITOR  
Bring moisture to trade; a single skin of fresh water buys three nights of cellar lodging. Wear mirrored goggles or be led blindfolded between dwellings during daylight. Speak slowly—your breath will vanish faster than your words. Never block a heliostat’s beam unless invited, and mark that invitation by crossing both arms to cast a deliberate X-shadow on the ground.

CONNECTION TO THE DROWNED CITY  
The Two Breaths bind cliff and basin. Every afternoon the Flats exhale glitter, dust, insect, memory—tokens that spin down like slow meteors through tea-dark water, where Pelagoth children dive to claim them, fitting each shard into their future constellations. Nights, the basin’s warm sigh climbs skyward: a dark plume smelling of salt-rot and plankton bloom. Kailor fog-chanters taste that ghost tide, searching it for hints of coming monsoon or Bleach, and in their heliostat mirrors they sometimes glimpse, just once, the faint copper pulse of Pelagoth mirror-writing far below, flashing gratitude, kinship, continuation.
