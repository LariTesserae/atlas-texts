THE RESONANT HOLLOW  

Terrain  
You step down out of a sere, cracked hinterland into a bowl hardly wider than a market square. The floor is level silt—old river-mud fossil-soft under a crust the color of brass. Nothing rises more than a few metres; the little basin is almost perfectly low and flat, as though someone pushed a thumb into the land and never let the wound fully close.

Water  
Moisture is present only in flashes. At dawn a gauzy condensation sweats out of the earth, beads along stems, runs in capillary threads toward the centre, and disappears again by mid-morning. Here and there fist-sized puddles tremble, never still enough to mirror anything; every sound sends a visible shiver across them. In the hush between gusts you can hear them breathing—water inhaling and exhaling through the mud.

Vegetation (plant-as-everything)  
Almost all solid surfaces are alive. Six or seven monumental trunks (each the thickness of a cottage, each hollow) arc outward like the ribs of a wrecked leviathan and meet above to form a vaulted canopy. From those trunks hang sheets of translucent leafskin, layered like overlapping stained glass, shifting from emerald to tiger-eye amber as the sun moves. Roots have braided themselves into bridges and ledges; fruiting bodies the size of balconies jut from them like balconies indeed, dripping sweet, clear sap that vanishes before it can pool. Nothing here is deadwood; decay is swallowed instantly by opportunistic mosses that bloom, spore, dissolve, and bloom again in the span of a single afternoon.

Built / Urban, but without tools  
The hollow is unmistakably a settlement, yet no hand has cut or joined a single piece. Galleries spiral inside the great trunks, their floors ribbed with living xylem. Doorways gape where a tendril was instructed—somehow—to withhold lignin at just the right moment. Alcoves exist because immense petals decided to furl and stiffen into walls. It is city as orchard, apartment as blossom. Every structure is still photosynthesising, still growing, and occupants (if there are any) must prune their rooms daily lest they seal themselves in green.

Density  
Paradoxically, the place feels hollow. Between one living column and the next yawns open space, a nave five body-lengths across where only drifting pollen motes occupy the air. You can cross the entire basin in a lazy two-minute walk, yet each pace sounds like it lands in the centre of an abandoned cathedral.

Sound (deafening)  
And about that sound: the Hollow is never silent. The trunks resonate like organ pipes; wind skims their mouths and erupts in chords so loud they churn the silt into skipping spirals. Immense anther-drums hanging high above beat themselves, triggered by vibration, doubling the volume. Birds—bright as cut metal—scream counter-melodies while insects grind out a serrated undertone. The sum is a pressure you feel in your teeth. Long exposure raises welts on the skin, as though acoustics were claws.

Fauna  
Life here is exuberant and fearless. Furless lizards glide on sap-sheen membranes, using the wind’s music as both lift and navigation system. Amphibians the colour of milky opal lurk in the puddles, feasting on the rich fall of stunned insects after each sonic crescendo. Seed-eaters and seed-guardians chase one another through the vaulted air in endless dogfights, their bodies flickering between states of matter whenever two particularly discordant notes intersect.

Weirdness / Laws of the place  
Sound is not just a phenomenon here; it is a material. A properly pitched whistle can twist a vine into a knot, can briefly make a puddle rise into a standing column, can fold distance so that a step lands you five strides farther. Conversely, a mis-note can blur the edges of your own body until pollen drifts straight through your ribs. The plants grew their architecture by singing to themselves; the animals learned to ride those harmonic slipstreams; newcomers do well to keep their mouths shut.

Climate, Erosion, Time  
The warm air wicks moisture almost as soon as it appears, yet the ecosystem has persisted for an epoch. The endless cycle of damp dawns, blistering middays, and night-time thunderclaps (thunder amplified a thousand-fold by the hollow trunks) has polished every surface into a settled, weathered perfection. Nothing changes in outline, yet everything is always in motion.

Atmosphere & Light  
Light is ordinary daylight but feels devoutly filtered: green-gold shafts stab through the leafskin vault in columns thick enough to lean against. Particles of shredded petal and powdered spore swirl there, catching the sun and refracting brief prismatic ghosts that wink out as soon as the next gust shifts the leaves.

Dynamic Processes  
• Dawn: Mist rises, puddles form, amphibians sing.  
• Midday: Heat dries the basin, trunks drone, flowers open and fling perfume like smoke.  
• Dusk: Wind reverses, a hush settles for perhaps thirty heartbeats—then the first night thunder detonates and the whole bowl becomes a drum.  
• Night: Predators of indeterminate taxonomy stalk the vaults on soundless wings, their hunting cries turning corners into temporary mazes.

If you linger  
Plug your ears with moss. Hum one clear, unwavering note—the place will notice you, will measure you, and may weave a room into existence around your frequency. Sleep there if you dare; by morning the walls will have budded new leaves spelling a message only you can read, rendered in the alphabet of whatever dream claimed you in the night.

Leave before the next thunder. The Hollow keeps what it can harmonise with.
