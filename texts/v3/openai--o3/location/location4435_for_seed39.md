You crest the pass at the hour when the sun is only a smear of pewter behind cloud, and the whole region opens at once: a newly-heaved roof of the world, raw, wet, and mostly empty.

1. Terrain  
• Elevation is outrageous. Needles of black basalt rise three, four vertical kilometres, angles still sharp because almost nothing has yet had time to smooth them. Ridge after ridge stands like unfinished blades on a giant lathe.  
• Between the ridges lie broad saddles of fractured stone. On maps they would read as valleys, but up here you are still well above most weather. The ground flexes underfoot where permafrost hovers centimetres below loose shards.  
• Erosion is only beginning. Everywhere you see embryonic features: baby cirques with lips too crisp to be called bowls, avalanche chutes that look sketched rather than carved, streambeds that run for only fifty metres before vanishing into unbroken talus. The land feels half-assembled, tools left out.

2. Water  
• Cold drizzle comes and goes all day, drifting sideways and beading on any surface that will take it.  
• Rock faces weep constantly. Moisture condenses from the low ceiling of cloud, trickles downward, and gathers in shoulder-deep potholes of sky-blue meltwater.  
• In flats between ridges the water has nowhere to hurry. Thin marshes spread like bruises: black mud, silver reflections, ice glazing the shallows each night and shattering each noon.

3. Climate & Light  
Temperature rarely climbs above 4 °C even in the long afternoon. A gauzy mist hangs perpetually; the sun is an idea more than a disk. Dimness softens every edge except the mountains’. Nights do not get much darker—just colder and quieter.

4. Vegetation  
Soil is still a rumour. The green you see is lichen glued to stone, bronze cushions of moss, and here and there a wiry grass clinging in cracks where wind has dropped a few grains of grit. Trees are centuries in the future.

5. Fauna (abundant, almost incongruous)  
• Kestrels and ravens ride the shear walls, nesting on the new ledges where predators cannot follow. Their wingbeats echo like slow bellows in a foundry.  
• Herds of pale ibex patrol the rubble slopes, scraping lichen with the persistence of machinists stripping a casting.  
• Amphibians dominate the marshes: slate-coloured newts, chorus frogs that call with a rhythmic chirp—click-click-hiss—that sounds uncannily like steam valves releasing pressure.  
• Insect life flourishes where vegetation does not: clouds of midges drift above every puddle; beetles the size of thumbnails rummage through moss, their shells oil-black and glossed as if fresh from a press.  
• Snow leopards haunt the higher benches. You see only their signatures: a line of prints, a tuft of fur on a splintered rock, silence suddenly spreading when they ghost close.

6. Atmosphere & Sound  
Despite the numbers of living things, the place feels hushed. Wind threads the passes but rarely screams; water drips and plops; frog-valves click; now and then rock cracks as ice swells in a seam—one sharp report like a dropped rivet gun, and then the quiet resumes.

7. Industrial Undertones (without industry)  
Nothing here is built, yet everything hints at a workshop just out of sight. Steam smells rise from thawing ground; clouds gather and disperse with the deliberation of factory smoke; freeze–thaw cycles tick like enormous pistons; animal calls imitate hammers and vents. It is as though the planet itself is running an early shift, manufacturing landscape in slow but certain strokes.

8. Activity & Cycles  
• Mid-morning: rime melts, micro-streams glimmer, insects hatch.  
• Afternoon: mist thickens; kestrels hunt the insect bloom; ibex descend to lick mineral-rich runnels.  
• Twilight: temperature drops; marsh skins over; leopards start their rounds.  
• Night: condensation loads the clouds again, ready to squeeze out another day’s gentle rain.

9. Spatial Feel  
Despite the motion, density is incredibly low. You can walk a full kilometre hearing only your own breathing, and when a raven finally crosses the sky its presence feels enormous. Every creature, every sound is an event, framed by kilometres of still stone and thin air.

10. Implicit Rules  
• Gravity rules but erosion still negotiates its contract. Expect sudden new gullies after a single storm.  
• Water is everywhere but never reliable; a pool today is black shale tomorrow.  
• Anything warm draws life: a sunlit boulder, a human campfire, even your exhaled breath. Within minutes something will investigate.

Stand still and you sense it best: a nascent mountain province, wet, dim and cold, busy with life yet spacious as a cathedral nave, the whole expanse in the earliest hours of its making—nature’s industrial age without a single chimney, forging peaks and biomes out of vapor, frost and time.
