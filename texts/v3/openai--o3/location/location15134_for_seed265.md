You arrive without daylight.  There is no sun here, no moon, not even the bruised glow of a horizon.  The air is warm, wetter than skin, and so saturated that every breath feels thickened—almost drinkable.  Your first orientation comes from sound rather than sight: slow beads of condensation detaching from overhead strata, splashing onto surfaces that you cannot yet name.  Between those patient notes lies a hush so complete that you start to hear your own blood before you notice the valley breathing around you.

TERRAIN  
The land is young, recently hoisted out of whatever sea or sub-world birthed it.  Rocky ribs—sharp, undecayed—push up through blankets of viscous mud.  Their edges are knife-clean, showing no rounding, as though erosion has only just opened its eyes.  You can feel their geometry underfoot by the way air eddies: wind coils round corners that have not yet been dulled by time.  Despite the youth of the rock, the valley floor sprawls outward for kilometres—a shallow basin whose curvature you can sense more than see.  When you move, the press of humidity changes with topography, mapping distances your eyes cannot.

WATER  
Below a crust scarcely thicker than bark lies an unhurried sub-surface ocean.  Wells of warm groundwater bleed upward through fractures, collecting in mirror-still pans.  Those pools look black, but when disturbed they reveal a blood-warm translucence and release a faint metallic tang.  Where the rock slopes even slightly, sheet-flows creep, no faster than a crawl, engraining new channels as they go.  After the rare electrical storms (silent lightning so distant you only feel the ionised sting), the entire basin becomes a single shallow lake; then, over weeks, it sinks back through seams, leaving clean silt with the texture of velvet.

VEGETATION  
Plant-life is sparse and strangely vertical, as if too bashful to occupy much footprint.  From fissures rise combs of reed-metallic grass, blades flat and polished, clicking together when the smallest animal brushes by.  Low mats of pallid lichen tile the harder shelves, but photosynthesis is not their business; they feed on chemistries teased from the rock by constant moisture.  Once every long season, the lichen blushes a deep rust colour—perhaps a spore event, perhaps impatience—but the change is slow enough to miss unless you return years later.

ATMOSPHERE & LIGHT  
With light at zero, perception reorganises itself.  Heat gradients behave like lanterns, outlining forms as softer or sharper warmth against the general stew.  The valley roof—wherever it is—occasionally exudes mist so cold it feels bright, a negative candle that sketches stalactitic shapes overhead.  On rare nights (there are only nights), the air fills with drifting threads of bio-phosphor ejected by unseen creatures.  The threads do not illuminate in any ordinary sense; they merely wink at the edge of vision, emphasising how thoroughly the usual spectrum has been mislaid.

SOUND  
Mostly quiet.  Water drips, distant machinery hums at a pitch too low to locate, and some nocturnal flyers beat flexible wings that clap rather than flap.  Very rarely, the ground utters a single seismic syllable—rock cracking open a few more centimetres—then falls silent for months.  Even the fauna respect the hush; their communications are felt more than heard, pressure blips in the still air, or sequences of infrared heat weaves that roll across exposed skin.

STRUCTURES & TECHNOLOGY  
The basin is peppered with industrial remains: squat brick pump-houses on isolated knolls, leaning gantries whose counterweights still dangle over nothing, a rail track half-submerged in peat but stubbornly uncorroded.  Most of these works were installed piecemeal, never forming a cohesive network.  Some machinery still runs, powered by buried steam lines that tap the planetary warmth.  A few engines turn for no obvious purpose except to keep turning; they shudder whenever groundwater laps against their housings, as though flinching at a forgotten obligation.

Inside one broken factory shell, piston assemblies idle in total darkness, smearing warm lubricant across themselves in rhythms too slow to clock by ear.  Nobody oils them, yet they remain oiled; nobody stokes the boilers, yet heat endures.  When you lay a palm on the iron you may feel a faint notching—an index of cycles—carved directly into the metal by reality’s own uncertainty.

FAUNA  
Life is abundant, though not always co-located with your expectations.  Amphibious runners, four-jointed, ripple across mud flats using surface tension as springboard.  Blind eels knuckle through air as easily as water, generating personal rainclouds that ooze from their dermis; when startled, they fold into droplets and drop into the nearest pool, re-spooling themselves minutes later.  In the upper dark, tether-moths hang from near-invisible filaments, wings spanning almost a metre tip to tip, absorbing ambient moisture for sustenance.  Their wing-beats are silent, but the air pressure changes around them in polite warning.

Predation occurs, but quietly.  You often find the husk of an animal with no trace of struggle, as if it agreed to dissolve when asked nicely.  Energetic waste is taken seriously here; nothing lingers unused for long.

DENSITY & WEIRDNESS  
The valley feels crowded, not with bodies but with versions.  Walk a hundred metres and you may pass a second copy of a pump-house—brick-for-brick identical, except a pipe flange is tightened one quarter-turn more.  Pools appear in duplicate a stone’s throw apart, mirroring one another with microscopic differences: a twelfth ripple missing, a lone reed positioned three degrees off.  Sometimes you glimpse your own footprints laid down ahead of you, slightly shallower than the ones you leave now.

Local folk wisdom (if the word folk applies to whoever left the chalk diagrams on machine casings) claims the place has a reality-shear every few heartbeats.  The shear is lazy, gentle as warm rain.  It copies, moves, pastes, and re-layers pieces of the world in an endless editorial drift.  Because the process is slow, most structures hold integrity long enough for someone to use them.  Travelers learn to mark objects in three ways: once in chalk, once in scratched metal, once in song.  At least one of those records usually survives.

RULES OF THE WORLD  
1. Light is not absent; it is embargoed.  Nothing here will create or transmit standard photons above a faint threshold.  Attempt to ignite a flame and you get heat, smoke, smell—but no glow.  
2. Time moves forward, but not all copies of the landscape share the same timestamp.  You can step from newly cut rock onto its future self and feel the edge already worn.  Your own body prefers a single track, so the crossing leaves you dizzy but intact.  
3. Water is the chief currency.  It pays for reality’s duplication.  Evaporation heightens the shear, condensation pacifies it.  Engineers once tried to stabilise the basin by sealing vents; the abandoned pipes still drip, as though the valley refused their bargain.  
4. Predators ignore any creature whose heartbeat matches the valley’s average tempo.  Visitors can train themselves to that pulse—it’s about four beats slower than human rest—but forgetting even for a moment is ill-advised.

ECONOMIES & INHABITANTS  
No permanent towns remain, yet signs of recent habitation exist: bedrolls stitched from bale-grass, cooking tins etched with freight insignia, hand-cranked tape machines storing whispered cartography.  It seems travellers come for the mineral excretions found at shear lines—crystals that absorb heat so completely they feel colder than death even in this warmth.  The crystals power the still-running engines, suggesting an ongoing trade none of the participants care to advertise.

LONG CYCLES  
Change is glacial.  Entire years pass before a new channel erodes, before a duplicated factory falls far enough out of sync to crumble.  Life has adjusted to that patience.  As you stay, your own circadian rhythms stretch; sleep lengthens, conversations take hours between sentences, plans extend to seasons instead of days.  The valley doesn’t hurry you, but it never stops bending you toward its tempo.

LEAVING  
Departure is simple: walk uphill until water no longer glosses the soles of your boots, until the click-grass thins to single stalks, until even the warm darkness lightens into ordinary night.  Yet travellers report that once you’ve carried the valley inside your lungs—its mineral humidity, its low pulse—you will sometimes hear a distant pump-house chugging in perfect silence, no matter where on the planet you stand.  That is the basin calling back, reminding you its versions are not done proliferating, and one evening, without daylight, you may realise you never actually left, just stepped into another draft where the flood has not yet arrived.
