You step—no, you glide—into a space that by every sane measure should not exist. It lies below the surface, but it is neither tunnel nor chamber; it is a hollow folded into the skin of the world like an air-pocket in rising dough. The cavity is warm, as though the surrounding rock retains the memory of a summer day.  

1. Terrain and materials  
• Floor: There is no floor in the usual sense. A sponge-work of root-mass bridges the gulf, springy and resilient, the strands as thick as fingers and as pale as raw bamboo. Between the meshes yawn wells of darkness where condensate drips echo into nothing.  
• Walls: The “walls” feel newly split, glassy and sharp. Flakes of mica catch neutral daylight that seeps from fissures high overhead, as if the sun were forced through a sieve. In places the rock still sweats—thin films of mineral water bead, run, and vanish into the thirsty mat of roots.  
• Ceiling: Impossible height for a pocket this small—perspective slips. You look up and the roof backs away, keeps backing, until your eyes water and the roots above converge like a thorny sky.  

2. Water (0.9/3, damp)  
Moisture here is episodic. A pulse of warm air rolls through every few minutes, condenses on the rock, then rains in a hush. The droplets never coalesce into pools; the root-floor drinks them instantly, swelling, breathing, then falling slack again. Occasionally a thin vein of groundwater bursts, arcs like a flash flood through mid-air, and is absorbed before it can find a bed.  

3. Vegetation (2.9/3, total)  
Plants are the architecture. Not merely climbers or mosses but an all-encompassing organism: The Concourse. It grows in plates, curtains, whips, and delicate glass-leafed chandeliers. Its color is difficult to pin—emerald if you stare, violet if you glance away. There are no stems, only continuations. You can pluck a tendril and hear a soft gasp as the rest of the cavity feels the loss. New growth appears while you watch, knitting gaps with translucent leaf-lace.  

4. Light (2.3/3, normal)  
Light arrives in rational intensity but irrational vectors: slim crevices channel daytime lumens deep underground. You may trace a shaft back to its source and find the slit has migrated. The Concourse also emits a not-light: a matte radiance that illuminates without casting shadow, as though color itself has turned into photons. Both sources coexist and sometimes cancel, leaving brief pockets where things go blank—vision, sound, even the sense of standing.  

5. Sound (2.2/3, loud)  
Nothing is ever quiet. The cavity resonates. Roots pluck themselves like harp strings when the warm drafts pass; their notes range from dew-bell tings to subsonic groans that vibrate the sternum. During condensate showers, raindrops strike leaf membranes and translate into choral sighs. Every few hours the whole biome cracks its joints: a rolling, cartilage-like pop that ricochets for kilometers through the bedrock.  

6. Dynamics (2.3/3, active)  
The place is young in geologic time but frantic in biological terms. Growth hormones plume invisibly, triggering spurts you can measure with a fingertip. Miniature hurricanes of spores swirl, search for purchase, die, and are replaced a heartbeat later. Roots shift position to intercept each stray beam of light, which means the very bridges you stand on drift centimeters a minute, rearranging the map of the hollow while you traverse it.  

7. Fauna (0.6/3, rare)  
Animals are rumors. Now and then a membranous glider—essentially a leaf that decided to learn lift—flits between light shafts. You hear them more than see them: a rapid-fire clicking like tongues of quartz. Evidence exists of something larger: segments scrapes into fresh rock faces, a single tuft of hair braided into a root as though offered. The Concourse heals away clues before you can properly identify them.  

8. Erosion & elevation (young, below surface)  
The smooth fractures on the walls imply the cavity opened days ago or eons—time measurement fails. Sharp shards litter alcoves but never pile; the roots absorb them for silica. Passages kink at right angles one moment, soften into arcs the next. There is no sediment yet, only precedent.  

9. Density (0.1/3, empty)  
Despite omnipresent plant life, volume outpaces mass. You could shout and wait whole seconds before the sound cascades back, filtered into chords. Between the tensegrity of roots hang chasms large enough to swallow cathedrals, yet everything here fits in a footprint on the surface—space folded like origami, interior vastly larger than exterior.  

10. Built & tech (none, primitive)  
Nothing inside bears tool-marks. If a sentience tends the Concourse, it does so with methods indistinguishable from growth. Fallen vines are re-purposed as suspension cables; hollow nodules fill with rain, then tilt to pour onto thirstier fronds—a form of irrigation achieved without implements.  

11. Weirdness (2.9/3, reality breakdown)  
Rules slip. Scale telescopes; you focus on a single spore and find landscapes inside it. Gravity trends toward whichever direction has the densest melody at the moment. Occasionally two places in the cavity trade positions with a crisp folding sound, like pages turning. Memory edits itself: you recall walking a ledge that, on inspection, has never existed.  

12. Atmosphere—what it feels like to remain  
Breath tastes of cracked pepper and petrichor. Tiny electric prickles dance along skin where the Concourse’s biofield brushes you. Stay still long enough and roots loiter near your boots, measuring. The air pressure pulses with the same rhythm as the far-off cartilage pop; you can feel the cavity’s heartbeat in your eardrums.  

13. If you touch, if you take, if you linger  
Cut a strand and sap pearls out glowing butter-green, quickly crusting into crystals that hum a lullaby pitched too low to hear. Drink the condensate and you may dream the cavity’s next self-revision. Sleep here and wake balanced on the ceiling, boots planted in moss that insists it has always been the floor.  

14. Exiting—if exit is possible  
Find the brightest seam of light, crawl toward it, and trust that the Concourse, curious, will loosen its weave around you. You emerge through what looks like a rabbit crack on a sunlit hillside. Behind you: ordinary stone. You can press an ear to it and catch, faint and continuous, the vaulted roar of a living place revising itself without end.
