They call it the Hollows of Kwa-Lashi only because the word “hollow” still sounds like stone and soil; in truth there is no naked earth left anywhere on the high plateau. Every cliff-face, every overhang, every tabletop ridge has been swallowed by living architecture—layer on layer of secreted pulp, resin, woven fiber, packed leaf, chewed bark, dried saliva, and hardened dung. Centuries of slow construction by creatures that never invented a single tool have made a land that is 100 percent fabricated and 0 percent manufactured.

TERRAIN
• Elevation: The plateau stands two vertical miles above the surrounding lowlands, the original rock hidden under a carapace of hive-work. Crests and valleys remain, but their profiles are softened into rounded buttresses and undulating terraces, as though the mountain range has been upholstered.  
• Structure: Everywhere opens into somewhere else. A spur that looks solid is actually a honeycomb of chambers; a wall apparently load-bearing reveals itself to be a layered screen only a claw-tip thick. Natural gravity is countered by sheer redundancy: billions of adjacent cells knit together into something stronger than bedrock.  
• Open scale / dense texture: From a distance the span seems broad and sweeping, but once inside every cubic meter is crowded—pillared passages pressed close by storage pods, hatcheries, fungal gardens, and sleeping alcoves.

MATERIALS
The “substrate” is alive or recently alive:  
– Fibrous mats of leaf-vein felt, still green, exhale warmth and faint oxygen.  
– Tan resin panels glisten where overnight mist has condensed.  
– Pale, brittle cast-skins of shed carapaces crunch underfoot like fallen leaves.  
Nothing smells of stone; everything smells of compost, nectar, and the faint vinegar of insect chemistry.

ATMOSPHERE & LIGHT
The plateau sits in permanent dusk. Forests once grew here, but their boles were harvested long ago and rewoven into ceiling-arches that block almost all direct sun. What illumination persists drifts in through fissures as dull amber or arrives as scattered grey daylight refracted through layers of silk. At night the place is close to pitch-black. Eyes adjust by the weak glow of lichen threads and occasional pulses from firefly larvae sealed into niche-lanterns—accidental bioluminescence, not design.

WATER
Rain comes seldom, yet warm upriver winds crash into the cold rim and leave a nightly gift of condensation. Beads collect along every surface, ride channels carved by mandible, and drip, drip, drip into pocket reservoirs. By mid-morning glassy pools hang in suspended basins or seep through filter-sponges to sustain moss, fern, and the slow pastel blooms of moisture flowers. Then the air dries again and the cycle waits for the next mistfall.

VEGETATION
Lush, yes, but seldom free-growing. Vines are forced to conform to corridors, their shoots redirected by waxy pylons; ferns sprout only inside nursery cells where their fronds double as bedding for larvae. Even mushrooms are grafted to the walls in mosaic patterns, harvested in rhythmic, deliberate bites. The only untended greenery lives on the very outer surface of the hive where wind-borne seeds find a crack the workers haven’t sealed yet.

FAUNA
Overwhelming is too small a word.  
• The Dominant: Kwa-lashi titan-termites longer than a human palm; droning rotary beetles that chew wood to paste; moth-cattle whose caterpillars are herded and milked for silk.  
• The Auxiliary: Eyeless cave swifts, copper frogs, parasitic mites that ride everyone. Predators exist, but none can do more than skim the surplus.  
Movement is slow in the aggregate—colonies expand no faster than a few wing-beats of new wall a season—but individual bodies never stop. The soft floor quivers beneath tens of millions of feet.

SOUND
Deafening, a ceaseless roar built from small, ordinary noises: wings rasping against paper walls, chitin tapping, air pushed through spiracles, water dripping, frogs barking. Stand in one of the grand apertures and the sound carries as a single low chord felt in the ribs more than heard. There is no true silence, only rare troughs when the larger beetles are asleep and the frogs pause to swallow.

CLIMATE & DYNAMICS
Warm air pools beneath the roof, difficult to vent, so temperatures hover in the high twenties Celsius year-round. Change happens, but glacially: a buttress erodes and is rebuilt with the same patience; a side-chamber collapses and becomes compost for the next tier. What is loud in the second is subtle in the decade.

RULES OF THE WORLD
1. Nothing lies idle—every object is being used by something.  
2. Gravity is negotiated rather than obeyed; if the wall is thin, another wall is added.  
3. Predation is tolerated only so long as it prunes the weak and leaves the mass intact.  
4. Darkness is default. Anything luminous is food, mate, or warning.

STANDING IN THE PLACE
Your lamp is useless—light merely attracts a knotted spiral of moths who beat it out. Breathing, you taste leaf sugar and ammonia. Cool drops find the nape of your neck even though the air is sweaty-warm. Somewhere above, hidden entirely by bio-scaffolding, is a sky you will never see until you exit the labyrinth and stand again on the wind-scoured rim where the hive-shell meets open air. Up there the roar fades to a dull percussion and you can look back at the range: a continent of living masonry, patient, perpetual, humming its slow, deafening song in the darkness.
