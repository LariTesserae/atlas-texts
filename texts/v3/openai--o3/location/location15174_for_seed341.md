You stand at the mouth of a basin-sized room—an artificial lowland about the size of a cathedral nave—roofed not by stone or steel, but by a ceiling of perpetual cloud. Warm vapor so dense it seems poured rather than blown hangs just overhead, turning every bulb and filament below into a drowsy orange smear. The air is heavy, body-temperature or hotter, and spiced with iron and loam and the peppery scent of sap that has never quite cooled.

Terrain  
The floor is a long-weathered slab of basalt, quarried elsewhere, ferried here generations ago, and since eaten into a lattice of gutters and sumps. Water seeps upward through fissures as readily as it falls from above; no one is certain which direction is “down” for moisture here. Channels guide the overflow into slow black canals that describe a mandala around a single depression in the center. The stone edges are rounded, their corners polished away by a century of dripping, splashing, and the endless rasp of wind-borne grit.

Materials & Built Form  
Rising from the channels are thin piers of riveted iron. They hold walkways, cable trays, and a ring of low workshops that form a loose urban collar: boiler houses the size of cottages, a pump hall with stained glass made from colored pressure gauges, storage lofts whose floors are grates and whose walls are strung with drying belts and chains. Everything stands on stilts because nothing here ever truly dries, and any weight set directly on the basalt settles an inch a year. Black grease marks the tide line on every piling like a dendrochronology of machine maintenance.

At the basin’s heart, beneath the warm rain, an engine blooms. It is both factory and fountain: girders splayed outward like petals, pistons stroking vertically, lifting sluices that gulp the surface water and drop it again in sheets. The machine’s rhythm is the place’s heartbeat—thump-hiss, thump-hiss—loud enough to drown conversation at a dozen paces. Steam whistles in perfect fifths where needle valves leak; chain conveyors rattle; somewhere, unseen flywheels boom like slow drums in a parade. There is no moment of silence, only crescendos and intervals of merely “very loud.”

Light  
Illumination is meager, almost apologetic. The ceiling cloud allows only a rust-brown twilight to trickle through, and most workshop lamps are hooded to keep insects—if there were any—in check. Amber hazard bulbs ping off iron struts, making shadows ringed and layered like tree growth. Between lamps, vegetation does the rest of the glowing: fronds and lianas lacquered with bioluminescent slime molds that shine a dull storm-lantern green when breathed upon.

Vegetation & Absence of Fauna  
Everything grows, nothing walks. Climbing ferns the size of bed-sheets wrap smokestacks until the stacks themselves look like fossilized trees. Bottle-thick reeds edge the canals, their roots hot to the touch—the plants have learned to sip warmth as eagerly as water. Many leaves are waxy, dark, nearly black; they drink the infrared that radiates from boilers better than they do the scant visible light. Petals of certain orchids here sweat droplets of distilled water that never touch soil—they evaporate before landing, adding new ghosts to the roof-cloud. Birds, insects, frogs: absent. The constant thunder of iron and steam, the searing temperature spikes each time purge valves open, the oil film on every pool—something in that trio persuades motile life to stay away. The green world therefore climbs unchecked, its only pruning the occasional swing of a caretaker’s machete.

Atmosphere & Rules  
Humidity hovers just below saturation; anything colder than blood instantly beads with dew. Paper rots in minutes. Metal ferries electricity too avidly: sparks leap centimeters in the moist air, so cables hang from ceramic chains like necklaces of black, silent sausages. Beneath everything, a rule: water must circulate. Should flow stagnate, the whole basin fogs until visibility drops to arm’s length and condensate rains hard enough to break lamp glass. No engineer permits that; pumps run even when they seem to do nothing but move liquid in circles.

Erosion & Time  
Nothing here looks new, yet nothing looks ruined. Edges are softened, paint bleached, but every wheel still turns. Maintenance is a religion: rust scraped daily, pits filled with brazed bronze, timber replaced with treated bamboo that tolerates the wet. The place is old enough to have found equilibrium—basalt sinks, stilts grow longer; vegetation overwhelms, catwalks are rebuilt a meter higher. It has settled into a perpetual truce between growth and grind.

Weirdness & Dynamics  
Occasionally, in the lull between piston strokes, a listener swears the cloud ceiling whispers back, articulating the engine’s rhythm in reverse—echoes arriving before their sounds are made. More measurable is the periodic “weeping” of rock: once or twice a day, every fault in the basalt extrudes a clear, syrup-slow gel that flash-steams on air contact, feeding the cloud and leaving behind salt wafers shaped like veined glass. Workers scrape these salts into barrels; rumor claims they sharpen memory or nightmares, depending on dose.

Soundtrack  
Beyond the grand engine’s heartbeat there is:  
• The constant patter of vertical drizzle, tiny but countless.  
• Slower drips—plonk—from cables overhead.  
• The sigh and clack of chain-hoists hauling wet lumber to dry lofts.  
• Distant rolling thunder from boiler blowdowns, felt through soles first, heard second.  

Stand still. Feel the vibration in your chest cavity equalize with the engine’s cadence. Watch vines twitch as exhaust gusts rake them. In this low, wet, dim, and sweltering hollow of machinery and jungle, life has divided: flora surrendered to heat and water, iron surrendered to motion and noise. Together they make a city the size of a single breath, but one that never, ever rests.
