The first sensation is pressure-warmth: a soft, even weight that wraps you from every side. You are only a few metres below the surface, yet there is no sky—above, below, all around, the world is water the colour of steeped tea. Light has almost given up by the time it reaches this basin; day and night both arrive here as a slow bruise of brown-black.

TERRAIN  
Imagine an inland bowl only a few dozen kilometres across, the sunken footprint of a forgotten plain. Long ago a river delta sprawled here, then the ocean pressed in, and now the basin is drowned. The floor is low, only fractions of a metre beneath mean sea level, but millennia of silt have spread it smooth. Over that flatness lie drifts of shell-sand, ankle-deep to a human, shuffled constantly by warm, almost silent currents. Every exposed edge—ancient masonry, old roots, bone—has been rounded into the quiet geometry of erosion. Corners do not exist here; the world has been patted into soft shapes by patient water.

BUILT FORMS (WITHOUT TECHNOLOGY)  
At first the dimness looks empty, then detail clarifies: a whole settlement grown rather than constructed. Reef-organisms—the basin’s master masons—have laid down calcium walls as a mangrove grows prop roots. Each building begins around a seed: a shard of pottery, a driftwood splinter, sometimes the bleached joint of a giant ray. Filter-towers rise three to four storeys high, riddled with pill-shaped windows. Horizontal decks fan outward like mushroom caps, forming porous streets. It is undeniably urban: blocks, plazas, chimney-spires, covered arcades—but every edge breathes. Pumping siphons line the corridors; pear-coloured polyps dangle in beaded curtains across doorways; stairwells double as fish ladders. There is no forged metal, no glass, no tool-marks, only the patient architecture of polychaetes, coral cousins, and lime-oozing algae.

DENSITY & SCALE  
Swim ten strokes in any direction and your knuckles brush a facade. Buildings overlap, laminate, arch across one another, creating a layered cityscape so packed that water themselves slows to a hush. Yet the settlement keeps going—valley-wide, kilometres of living walls and courtyards, an osmotic metropolis.

ATMOSPHERE & LIGHT  
Water temperature rests at a languid 27 °C; it feels less like liquid, more like wet velvet. Light is nothing but a faint umber glow descending from the surface. Deeper alleys are true void—black enough to hide the sweep of a manta the size of a sail. Only invertebrate lanterns relieve the dark: coin-sized medusae that pulse cinnamon light; the breathing gills of stone-worms that exhale dots of turquoise fluorescence; a slow flickering the colour of old copper as schools of micro-shrimp turn their eyes in unison.

SOUND  
It is mostly quiet. Currents are muffled by sponge walls; wave-roar from above never penetrates the basin’s lip. The loudest sound is biological rhythm: the slurp of a feeding siphon, a soft percussion when a school of hatchetfish turns, distant thrumming from a migrating whale far outside the valley. Between those notes is a heavy, listening stillness.

VEGETATION  
True plants survive only as stunted mats clinging to rooftops where day-brown reaches—a fuzz of algae, a few sea-grasses struggling like weak handwriting across stone. Otherwise, the place is animal and mineral.

FAUNA & ECOLOGY  
Life presses in rank upon rank. Goblin eels wind through gutter-tunnels; arm-thick seahorses graze algae from balcony rails; carpets of starfish hoard detritus on the plaza floors. Predators cruise the upper stories: silver rapiers of barracuda, box-shaped squid that flash warning checks. Everything eats and is eaten, yet balance holds: centuries of erosion and regrowth have tuned the food web to stability. Nothing here is invasive; every arrival inherits an allotted niche or is repurposed by the city’s living masonry.

DYNAMIC PROCESSES  
Twice daily, tidal pulses push brackish river water into the basin, stirring clouds of ochre silt that settle like weather. Whole arcades appear, vanish, and re-emerge in these moving fogs. The reef-builders respond—closing polyps, opening later to drink new minerals and exhale structure. Some towers grow centimetres overnight; others crumble, their rubble swallowed by burrowing worms that recalcify the dust into new foundations. The city is a single organism doing urban respiration—destruction as exhalation, growth as inhale.

RULES OF THE WORLD  
1. Nothing remains inorganically dead for long; every loose particle is raw material for another colony.  
2. Light is treasure. Anything reflective—fish scales, discarded shells—acquires symbolic weight; creatures compete to hoard and redistribute brightness.  
3. Tools are bodies. Need a blade? Find a scallop shell. Need shelter? Offer your back to a coating of barnacles, let them wall you in.  
4. Direction is given in strata rather than compass: “three levels up” means more than “north.”  
5. Stories are stored in pattern: the mottling on an eel’s side, the mosaic of barnacles on a buttress. Reading is an act of swimming a path in the right sequence.

MOODS THAT LINGER  
• The comfort of bath-temperature darkness, as though the whole valley is an amniotic dream.  
• A teeming claustrophobia: life so dense you feel watched from every pore in the wall.  
• The sense of age—nothing here is being built for the first time; everything is a palimpsest of previous reefs, collapsed courts, regrown facades. One era ghosts through another.

If you hover still, your bubbles trail upward like lost coins. A brush of current kisses your cheek; something large passes by, unseen but stirring a wake that rattles shell-shutters. Then the quiet returns, thick enough to touch, and the drowned city goes on breathing in the dark warm water.
