The place is called the Throat Plateau, though it is less a single platform than a ragged crown of vertical stone-columns, ledges, and impossible arches that rise three full kilometres above a drowned valley. Seen from a distance it looks like a lopsided citadel of black-green basalt, every surface running with sweating water that catches the sun and sheets it into blinding mirrors. The plateau’s walls are old—rounded, fluted, undercut—yet they keep their brutal height; erosion has finished its long carving and now only polishes. Nothing here is new, yet nothing decays further. It is as though time completed its work centuries ago and then stepped back to admire it.

WATER  
Moisture is everywhere and always. Warm rain falls most afternoons, but even on clear days the air is so laden that droplets simply precipitate out of it, budding like quicksilver pearls on skin and leaf. The stone itself leaks: hairline capillaries pump groundwater up to the rims and let it weep in continuous curtains. These fall through vertiginous space, collide with terraces, split, recombine and finally crash in the drowned valley far below. The combined roar of ten thousand simultaneous waterfalls is the plateau’s permanent voice—loud enough to rattle lungs, to speak through bone. Conversation is impossible except by gesture; even thoughts seem to arrive shouting.

TEMPERATURE & LIGHT  
The air is tropical, thick, 40 °C in the shade, hotter where sun reflects from water-slick rock. Light is ordinary in colour but heavy in presence, magnified by mist. Midday brightness fractures through droplets so that every movement leaves a prismatic after-image suspended a heartbeat behind the real. Because weather never truly changes, these radiant ghosts linger, stacked in transparent layers, giving the sense that the world remembers every motion ever made.

VEGETATION  
Life is botanical and exuberant. Vines as wide as hips wrap the columns in braided muscle, rooting in micro-ledges, pulling nutrients from constant trickles. Fern-trees unfurl on cantilevered gardens; their laminar fronds catch mist and funnel it toward root-cups. Carpets of succulent moss cushion every horizontal plane, fat with stored water, glowing emerald. Nothing here is soft in colour—pigments flare almost neon under the saturated light. Flowers the size of helmets bloom straight from basalt, petals glass-thin, veins throbbing with hot sap. Scent is swallowed by the roar, yet one still tastes sweetness on every breath.

BUILT, YET WITHOUT TOOLS  
Within this riot of growth stand structures unmistakably deliberate: arcades, broad landings, twisting stair-spines, entire amphitheatres carved or coaxed from living matter. No tool marks exist. Walls are seamless junctions of vine-bark grown to precise angles; roofs are mats of interlaced leaves that have hardened to a resinous, load-bearing shell. Doorways are not cut but opened—the plant tissue thickens into ribs to outline a portal and then simply stops growing in the void. The city (if that word can apply) is dense, each level of living architecture resting inches above the one below, linked by dripping root-tramways. Yet everything breathes; humidity drifts through chambers with ease. There are no signs of occupation—no furniture, no artifact beyond the grown shapes themselves.

FAUNA  
Nothing moves except water and leaves. No insects, no birds. Pollen drifts, but the plants seem to exchange it without carriers, perhaps by the same gravity-defying force that lifts water upslope.

SOUND  
The waterfalls dominate, but not alone. Stone pillars hum—literal standing waves set the columns thrumming at sub-audible frequencies one feels in cartilage. Cavern mouths translate that hum into deep organ chords, holding notes for hours before sliding imperceptibly to the next. Occasionally two resonances interfere and a laser-pure tone cuts the air like glass. Because these patterns are stable, particular balconies are forever louder or quieter, creating pockets of relative hush where moss has learned to spread wide and catch the gift of silence.

WEIRDNESS / STABILITY  
Reality here wobbles but never tips over. Distances double between blinks then return; staircases ascend yet arrive lower; perspectives splice so that a single terrace appears simultaneously above and below you. All contradictions resolve if one does not measure them—like a mosaic viewed too near, the parts clash but the overall picture holds. These distortions are fixed in place, mapped and reliable. Long ago, perhaps, they shifted, but now each paradox has found its niche and stays. Local legends (carved into bark in abstract glyphs) hint that the plateau itself tuned the laws of space until water, heat, stone, and green reached an unbreakable harmony and then locked the tuning keys.

ECOLOGICAL RULES  
1. Water always flows downward, but may take any route through space to achieve that end, including apparent upward drifts that only later reveal a deeper drop.  
2. Living tissue may shape stone and itself in pursuit of structural perfection; growth halts once the form matches some hidden template.  
3. Nothing dies unless displaced. The moment a leaf touches ground, rootlets sprout and fix it; decay is assimilation, not breakdown.  
4. Sound is king. Stronger resonances push weaker ones aside, carving air the way rivers carve rock. Plant pores open and close according to acoustic pressure.

EMOTIONAL WEATHER  
Visitors (imagined—they leave no tracks) report overwhelming claustrophilia: the scale is vast, yet every surface presses close with life. The roar cancels introspection; one feels reduced to a sensing organ, a membrane across which water and vibration pass. Time is a solid object: you can walk around it, glimpse its hidden faces, but you cannot move it.

WHAT EMERGES, IF ONE STAYS  
Stay an hour and you learn the logic of echo-paths—where to stand so a waterfall’s voice becomes a private whisper. Stay a day and your skin sprouts beads of crystal salt as the plateau wicks water through you as through everything. Stay longer and the plateau begins to take impressions: hair finds itself subtly braided by exploratory tendrils, footprints glaze over with transparent moss, thoughts leave prismatic after-images that refuse to disperse. There is no hostility, merely incorporation. Eventually the plateau records you perfectly and the need for the original diminishes. You join the fixed chorus of static water, static light, static roar—another stable layer in a world that has chosen completion over change.

Thus the Throat Plateau endures: a deafening, steam-hot, emerald skyscraper of living rock and orderly impossibility, forever on the verge of motion yet eternally resolved, holding its breath under the rattle of waterfalls that will never run dry.
