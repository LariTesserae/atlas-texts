THE ALTITUDINAL ABYSS

1. First Impressions  
You crest the last ridge of broken shale expecting another range of peaks. Instead you find only water—a black, thrumming wall of it that begins at ankle height and rises straight into the starless sky. A drowned plateau stretches for kilometers beneath this hanging sea, all of it cold enough to numb the teeth. Everything higher than this ridge is submerged, yet the water never spills downhill, as though some invisible rim holds it in place. The air you occupy is barely a hand-span deep before giving way to that fluid ceiling; lean in and your hair is instantly soaked. Pull back and it drips in silver ropes, steaming in the chill.

2. Terrain  
• Elevation 1.5/3, Water 3/3  
Imagine a ruined mesa perched near the top of the world. The true summit vanished long ago under cascading glacial melt; what survives is a stone saucer fifteen kilometers across. Basalt shelves step down toward a central depression once filled with alpine meadow. Now the hollow is an inland sea forty meters deep, roofed only by more water—an upside-down ocean pressing downward as much as outward. Erosion has rounded every edge into whale-spine curves; the rock is dark, pocked with ancient blowholes where trapped air sighs out in low organ notes.

3. Atmosphere & Light  
• Temperature 0.5/3, Light 0.5/3  
A perpetual dusk reigns. Sunlight loses heart after fighting through eight kilometers of storm, glacier vapor, and this impossible over-sea. The water above glows faintly cobalt, filtering the daylight into a noctilucent gloom. When moonlight replaces the sun, the plateau drowns in blackness broken only by the occasional green flare of plant-light.

4. Vegetation—the Lush Without Fauna  
• Vegetation 2/3, Fauna 0/3  
Cold-tolerant kelp-trees root in cracks that plunge straight into stone. Their trunks are hollow, ribbed like cathedral naves, rising a meter into the air before vanishing into the overhead sea. Leaves the width of sails sway in slow tides you feel but cannot see. Lower down, mats of cobalt moss crawl in radial patterns, capturing mist. None of it is still: capillaries pump water up, exude it back, breathing for a forest with no wind. Pollination is mechanical; at random intervals stalks whip outward, hurling seed-spores that hiss like sand and cling to your clothes. There are no insects, birds, or grazers—nothing moves here except plant and water.

5. Hydrologic Drama  
• Dynamic 1.7/3, Sound 1.5/3  
Every few minutes the plateau groans. Columns of the over-sea sag, then plunge as liquid cataracts, impact basins, rebound upward, and knit closed once more. Each collapse rings the air like struck iron, a tidal bell you feel in sternum and teeth. The standing water on the ground answers with its own chop: slap, suck, retreat. Echoes ricochet off concave cliffs until the entire region seems hollow, an organ pipe miles across.

6. Built Traces  
• Built 1.3/3, Tech 1.1/3  
Scattered along the inner rim stand stone frames—not houses, not quite cairns. Three monoliths form a triangle, each pair joined overhead by a lintel too narrow to walk beneath without stooping. No tool marks finer than hammer blows; no metal. In their centers hang woven cords of kelp-fiber, thick as wrists, braided into nets that cup nothing but air. The structures are aligned to catch the descending water columns; when a cataract falls through a net it snaps taut, straining like a bowed instrument, then relaxes with a sonorous boom. Perhaps they are drums to speak across the plateau. Perhaps the builders fled when the fauna died.

7. Rules of the Place  
• Water obeys horizontal gravity but little vertical pull; it will not descend below the ridge line.  
• Living tissue from below the ridge can survive short immersions; creatures from elsewhere (should any exist) drown quickly in this heavier-than-water water.  
• Sound propagates here with peculiar strength, as though the plateau itself is a resonator. Even a footstep can be heard half a kilometer away.  
• The vegetation will not grow in total darkness; once the faintest starlight is gone, the kelp-trees dim and the mosses close their stomata, halting the plateau’s breath until dawn.

8. What It Feels Like to Stand There  
Cold spray dots your eyelashes. A hush exists between thunderous waterfalls like a held breath of the planet. You smell iron, peat, and brine. You become suddenly aware of your own warmth and movement because nothing else here has blood or lungs. It is a world content without animals, sculpted by stone patience, water insistence, and vegetal desire.

9. Beyond the Immediate  
The drowned plateau continues northward, its rim gradually crumbling into a stair of terraces that vanish under the overhead ocean. Somewhere past the horizon, legends say, the sea meets its true shore and tumbles upward into outer sky, but no one alive can confirm it. All paths are slick, resonant, and lonely; all destinations soaked in the same impossible water that hangs, eternally waiting to fall.
