The place has the calm certainty of something that has already found the one shape it intends to keep. Nothing here truly begins or ends; it only circulates.

TERRAIN  
The land lies scarcely above an unseen sea, a pan-flat expanse of newborn basalt and vitreous clays barely cooled from their slow upwelling. Edges are soft, curves uncut: erosion has not yet learned the landscape’s name. Where the rock pulled apart, nacreous mud wells up, warm as blood; within minutes a skin of iridescent salts congeals and turns the pools into mirrors. From a distance the ground looks solid, but every dozen steps a heel sinks with a sigh and warm water bleeds upward between the toes.

WATER  
Moisture is everywhere, yet never gathers into rivers. It rises continuously from the sub-surface, condenses in the oven-hot air, and drips straight back down. A soundless rain of droplets beads on every surface—foliage, stone, chitin, fur—then slides off and returns below, repeating without hurry or erosion.

ATMOSPHERE & LIGHT  
Heat here is absolute: a still, body-deep swelter that wraps flesh like wet velvet. The sky is the color of damp charcoal, not from nightfall but from a permanent ceiling of vapor. Sunlight seeps through in a coppery dimness, bright enough to model shapes, too weak to throw shadows. At the horizon, the haze meets the basalt so gradually that there is no crisp line, only a dissolving impression of distance.

VEGETATION  
Roots have no cause to drill; water sits waiting at the surface. Plants answer by piling upward instead. Columnar ferns the width of barrels stack fronds in tight helices until they brush the cloud-base and stop. Between them, mats of blister-leaf grow in overlapping shingles, trapping heat and moisture until the leaves themselves sweat. Parasite creepers climb everything—rock columns, animal shells, one another—knitting a three-dimensional fabric dense enough that fallen branches never reach the ground; they halt, suspended in green, and are eaten in mid-air by slow rust-colored molds.

SOUND  
The air roars with life. A continuous bass thrumming—part heartbeat, part thunder—comes from the throat-sacks of kilometer-distant swamp-bulls answering one another across the flats. Over that lies a lattice of clicks and glassy pings made by fist-sized carapace beetles striking their shells together to mark territory. Somewhere overhead, unseen in the gray glow, a flock of membrane-winged skimmers engages in a constant metallic mutter: each wingbeat forces hot, wet air through the creatures’ hollow bones, turning flight into low wood-wind music.

FAUNA  
Life crowds every layer.

1. Canopy walkers: six-limbed sloth-dragons hang inverted beneath fern crowns, tongues extended to sip condensed water straight from the leaves, moving so slowly it is impossible to tell motion from stillness.  
2. Mid-story grazers: arm-length, iridescent gastropods glide in processions along the creepers, cropping the molds before mold can consume plant or snail alike. Their passage leaves glittering trails that never dry.  
3. Ground echelon: the swamp-bulls—enormous, heat-drunk amphibians—stand knee-deep in the mirror pools, gular sacs pumping like bellows, inhaling oxygen through skin rather than lung. Between their feet, eel-salamanders braid themselves into living nets, mouths open to catch whatever falls.

DENSITY & SCALE  
Step twenty paces in any direction and you have met a catalog’s worth of species, each one nearly touching the next. Yet the landscape stretches outward for at least a valley or two—though a traveler would hardly notice distance, so uniform is the press of life and haze. Landmarks are audible, not visible: you navigate by the pitch of beetles, by the deeper or shallower choruses of bulls, by where the canopy clatter grows thin.

WEIRDNESS  
Nothing is flagrantly impossible; everything is just a few degrees off ordinary. Water sometimes creeps up stems against gravity for hours before admitting defeat. Now and then the basalt puffs out clouds of mirror-bright dust that hang unmoving, as though time forgets them. None of it surprises the inhabitants, and after an hour here even a visitor stops questioning; the brain accepts the rules it is given.

TIME & CHANGE  
Storm, drought, season—those words have never been spoken here. Temperature, humidity, even the chorus of voices stay inside an unvarying range. A creature may die, rot, and be reabsorbed in days, but its niche is filled so quickly that the pattern—the music—never pauses. The place feels less like an ecosystem than a perfectly balanced equation.

RULES OF THE WORLD  
• Heat is the baseline; cooling is anomaly.  
• Water rises, condenses, returns, without ever carving channels.  
• Nothing solid lasts long enough for weathering; softness is the dominant geology.  
• Photosynthesis occurs on the edge of sufficiency; organisms supplement by digesting air-borne proteins.  
• Sound is currency and boundary; silence is void and therefore feared.  

STANDING STILL  
Remain motionless for a few breaths: the basalt will warm through your soles; mist beads will lace your eyelashes. Life will press closer to taste the salt of your skin. Nothing hunts you with malice, yet everything wishes to sample, to integrate, to fold you into the never-changing choir. Leave quickly or stay forever; either way, the place itself will not notice. Its balance is already perfect, and it will sing on, dim and hot and loud, exactly as it is, exactly as it will be.
