Step out of the last patch of firm ground and you enter the Hollow Basin, a low-lying pan that never sees the sun.  Light here is a rumor: an oily twilight distilled from distant lightning and the occasional phosphorus blink of mineral vapors.  You can sense the heat first—thick, bath-temperature air that presses on the lungs—and then the wetness, everywhere, as if every surface is an unwrung sponge.

1. Terrain  
• Elevation is scarcely above the unseen sea that must lie somewhere to the south.  The land rolls in long, shallow shelves like the steps of a giant tide gone fossil-still.  
• Between shelves stretch flats of dark mud riddled with warm puddles.  The puddles do not sit level: they bead into lens-shapes, meniscus bulging up as though gravity is being negotiated rather than enforced.  
• Here and there, stone ridges rise—a soot-black conglomerate worn into soft ridges and pocked with little sinkholes.  Erosion is mature: edges are rounded, gullies braided, nothing raw remains.  The whole scene feels finished, as if revised over eons until only essentials persist.

2. Water  
• Rain is constant but delicate: a sifting mist that beads on skin and never becomes true droplets.  More water wells up from below; if you scuff the surface with a heel, a shine appears instantly.  
• In places the ground exhales: vertical curtains of vapor that condense, fall back as warm drizzle, and start the cycle anew.  You hear no rivers, yet you are always within touching distance of liquid.

3. Vegetation  
• Life clings in stingy pockets: low mats of copper-colored moss that survive half-submerged; stringy reeds spaced like sparse handwriting across the flats; a few knotty shrubs crouched behind stone hummocks.  Everything photosynthesizes on wavelengths you cannot see, stealing energy from whatever thin radiation drifts down.  Leaves feel waxy, almost artificial, and break with a brittle snap—they hoard their own moisture like treasure.

4. Built Environment  
• The dominant sight is the City-Without-Craft, an urban mass that looks quarried but was never quarried, built but never built.  Blocks, arches and buttresses rise in broken alignments across several kilometers, yet no joint shows a tool mark, no wall bears a chisel’s logic.  Geologists speculate the structures self-precipitated: minerals migrating upward through capillaries, hardening where evaporation is strongest, sculpting themselves into towers and hollow streets.  
• Streets are broad and mostly empty, a labyrinth of black-mirror pavements that reflect the dim sky.  Density is low—one avenue can run half a kilometer without brushing another structure—so you feel both in a city and inside a void.  
• Interiors yawn with perfect geometry: domes of seamless stone, spiral ramps without railings, columns that taper to hair-thin tips and never collapse.  Yet nothing indicates occupation: no furniture, no scratch of ownership, not even dust, because everything is continuously washed by the mist.  
• Bridges stretch over nothing.  Aqueduct-like ribs march away only to stop mid-air, spilling warm rain in curtains.  Every element hints at utility but ends in enigma.

5. Atmosphere & Light  
• Darkness rules.  A low, orange glimmer trembles along the horizon but never crests.  The only brighter event is the sporadic sheet-lightning locked inside the cloud ceiling; a silent white flash reveals the city’s silhouette, then the gloom reasserts.  
• Air smells mineral, metallic-sweet.  Occasionally it gains a faint ozone edge, warning of another lightning pulse occurring somewhere behind the vapor.  
• Visibility ranges from fifty meters in clear pockets to almost nothing when the fog thickens.  Depth perception falters; distant walls seem to slide sideways or double, an effect locals call “the Basin Lope.”

6. Sound  
• Predominant sound: dripping—individual, well-spaced drops that strike with bell-like clarity.  
• A low susurration rises when wind forces mist through the stone arcades.  It resembles chanting, though no throat makes it.  
• Otherwise quiet reigns.  Footsteps are muffled by the damp stone; conversations, if any, stay intimate by necessity.

7. Fauna  
• None visible.  No wings skim the air, no ripples betray swimmers.  Even insects seem excluded.  The silence left by their absence becomes a character, a pressure.  Microscopic life doubtless thrives (the mosses breathe somehow), but to human senses the world feels abandoned by animal intent.

8. Dynamics & Processes  
• Water cycle here is hyper-localized.  Vapor rising from warm ground condenses in the ceiling clouds, falls back as mist, and drains only a few meters horizontally before sinking again.  The entire meteorology is a self-licking engine.  
• Rock continues to grow.  In sheltered alcoves you can watch crystal filaments extruding grain by grain, adding millimeters per month to buttresses.  Conversely, exposed edges dissolve, so walls maintain shape like candle stubs that never quite disappear nor advance.  
• Occasional “gravity inversions” occur: puddles climb walls, droplets hover, a lost tool may drift upward, slowly, then settle back when the inversion relaxes.  These events follow no calendar yet never last more than two minutes.

9. Rules of the World—The Breaks  
• Perspective is untrustworthy.  A straight corridor measured at thirty meters can be walked in fifteen paces or one hundred, depending on no obvious factor.  Mapping is possible only by relative landmarks (“the Triple Arch,” “the Rippled Courtyard”).  
• Time keeps stubborn regularity for living bodies—heartbeats, hunger—yet mechanical timepieces fail, their hands slowing or jerking.  One theory says the Basin edits out any object dedicated to measuring or manipulating physics.  
• Fire will not sustain.  Sparks fizzle, embers drown in invisible humidity.  Heat exists—in fact the air is oppressively warm—but oxidative flame is quietly forbidden.  
• Sound occasionally echoes from events that have not happened yet: footsteps round a corner a minute before a traveler arrives; a drip rings twice, the second tone belonging to some future drop.

10. Materials & Colors  
• Palette is subdued: charcoal black of stone, umber of mud, tarnished bronze of moss.  Water shines silver under rare lightning.  When the mist condenses on surfaces, a rainbow sheen trembles then vanishes, as though color itself dislikes lingering.  
• Touch the architecture and you find it slightly yielding, temperature of skin, yet unmistakably mineral.  Break a chip free (possible only at thin edges) and it liquefies in your palm, running off like warm ink before you can pocket proof.

11. Human Presence (if any)  
• Pilgrims, researchers, or refugees sometimes arrive from brighter realms.  They bring torches that will not light, tools that torque themselves apart, beasts of burden that turn anxious and must be led away.  The Basin never kills outright; it merely persuades.  Most visitors leave within a day, unnerved by the silence and the sensation of walking through someone else’s memory.  
• A few stay, adopting the rhythm of mist and stone, living in reed shelters because built interiors feel too conscious.  They gather condensate for drinking, harvest moss for food, and chart gravity inversions for sport.

12. Emergent Myths  
• It is said the City is dreaming itself: each arch a thought, each plaza a sleep-breath.  When the dream ends, the architecture will slump back into soup and the Basin will become a shallow sea again.  
• Another story claims the darkness is not caused by lack of sunlight but by an overabundance of shadow, spilling from a breach deeper than physical cave.  The shadow is thick enough to smother flame and hush animals, but thin enough to let moss nibble stray photons.

Walk long enough and the city thins, surrendering to endless mud flats streaked with mirror-water.  At the limit of sight, towers dwindle to stunted teeth, and then even they blur into the evening-colored sky.  Out there, perhaps, nothing stands at all—just the Basin’s original face, patient, hot, drenched, and wordlessly waiting for the next impossible structure to arrive by mineral decree.

And always, in the background, the soft percussion of water finding every hollow, as if tapping to discover which spaces are real and which are only potential.  The Basin answers by remaining both.
