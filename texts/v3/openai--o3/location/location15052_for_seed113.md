You have to duck as you pass through the half-dissolved gate.  
Beyond it a single, vaulted chamber opens—cathedral-big, yet still small enough that you can pace its width before your footsteps dry. Everything here lies a hundred meters below the open sky. The roof is the original stone of the planet, but glassy and newborn, as if the mountain split only yesterday and left crisp planes behind. From honey-colored fractures in that ceiling warm water perpetually bleeds, falling as silver cords, flaring briefly where they strike hidden projectors, then hissing into mist that never quite settles.

1. Terrain and materials  
• Floor: a shallow basin of slate-smooth basalt, grooved like the inside of a seashell. The grooves radiate toward drains cut with nanoscopic precision.  
• Walls: half natural, half extruded carbon lattice. Transparent ribs hold back tons of surrounding strata, and inside the ribs run vascular channels—plumbing, data, nutrient gel—visible in dull crimson pulses.  
• Central feature: a low hill of jagged, matte obsidian. It is obviously staged; untouched by the water, edges still knife-sharp. Someone printed this knoll to look like volcanic stone, but it hasn’t yet begun to weather.

2. Water (wet, warm, dynamic)  
The air is dense, at 32 °C, and saturated. Every exhale condenses immediately; every surface sweats. When the mist thickens, it rains upward: droplets nucleate on the humming infrasound emitters buried in the floor and rise as inverted fountains, recycling into ceiling condensers before falling again. The place is its own closed hydrological loop.

3. Light (dim)  
Illumination is reluctant. A soft aurora leaks from the carbon ribs—electroluminescent polymers that have been throttled down to the bare minimum. Occasional strobes skip along the vascular channels when diagnostics run. Otherwise you see by reflection: the constant, trembling sheen of water and the low bioluminescence of plants.

4. Vegetation (lush, but plant-only)  
No seed ever germinated here. Instead—a curated jungle of engineered fronds, petaloid lichens, and root mats that behave like coral.  
• Fronds: chlorine-green, each ribbed with silver veins that glint where nanorods harvest energy from the magnetic field.  
• Lichens: broad, translucent discs that cling to every vertical surface, vaping oxygen in rhythmic puffs.  
• Root mats: they braid across catwalks, plunge into drainage channels, even climb the obsidian mound, knitting it into the room’s metabolism.  
There are no insects, no birds, nothing that flies or crawls; the flora is pollinated by directed acoustic pulses that sweep the room every twenty-three minutes.

5. Sound (loud)  
Silence is impossible. The chamber breathes through machines.  
• A basso heartbeat: pumps that lift condensate back to the roof.  
• A continuous hiss: water finding steam before the cooling coils tame it.  
• A staccato clicking: mycelial roots cracking new stone as they expand.  
• Periodic thunder: resonance cannons that shake loose mineral dust for the plants to absorb. Ear protection is wise; inside the thunder you can hear a thin chorus—the diagnostic pings of maintenance drones you cannot see.

6. Density (empty)  
Despite the infrastructure, the place feels deserted. Long glass walkways arc from wall to wall, but no footsteps mar the dusting of fine clay on them. Benches are grown from the same living carbon lattice, molded for human bodies that never arrived. The volume of air is huge relative to the objects occupying it, and between the ribs is nothing but humming dark.

7. Built (urban)  
This is one room of a buried city that was never populated. Transport conduits feed in from six directions; most are sealed by iris shutters that look muscular, organic. You glimpse scaffolds, balconies, and alcoves beyond—but all inactive, waiting. The chamber is a public square that lost its public.

8. Technology (beyond modern)  
• Walls continuously reconfigure, their surface molecules sliding like liquid metal to patch micro-fractures.  
• The water loop operates without moving parts: phononic lattices shepherd fluid uphill.  
• Vegetation kneels to the light—literally folds—when spectral analysis warns of mold.  
• Timekeeping beacons in the ceiling maintain phase-locked resonance with clocks orbiting the star, though no resident needs the hour.

9. Erosion (young)  
Textures are sharp; corners have not yet surrendered. Where a vine gnaws stone, the bite marks remain crisp white. The basalt floor’s grooves glitter, still showing the interference patterns of the laser that cut them last month or last century—time has done almost nothing here.

10. Dynamics  
Weather inside the room changes hourly, following code that tries out different worlds: a warm monsoon; a still, foggy dusk; a sudden downdraft that rings the obsidian mound like a gong. Each shift is announced by a bright ripple through the vascular channels, then enacted. The plants adapt within minutes, their genome edited on the fly by airborne vectors.

11. Rules of the world  
• No animals: sensors abort any complex motile DNA. The city’s charter forbids nervous systems larger than a nematode.  
• Entropy management: when erosion does appear, walls harvest displaced molecules and recycle them. Nothing is allowed to decay into disorder for long.  
• Sound over sight: machinery communicates ultrasonically; what little light exists is mostly concession to possible visitors.  
• Closed cycle: matter enters or leaves only under deliberate, audited exchange. The city can live forever on its starting stock.

12. Atmosphere—both literal and emotional  
Literal: a humid, metallic smell, tinged with crushed herbs. The pressure feels heavier than sea level, though your lungs fill easily.  
Emotional: anticipation. Every surface betrays purpose and readiness, but purpose for whom? You sense that if you spoke the correct phrase the lights would surge, walkways would extend, and the drones would converge to greet you.

13. Standing still  
If you pause long enough you notice the room drifting—by micrometers—tilting to counter the moon’s tidal pull. You notice the plants folding and unfolding like slow origami. You notice that the mist remembers your outline; for a minute after you move away, a ghostly negative of you hangs in vapor.

14. Leaving  
You could exit through any of the six tunnels; each promises a different district—orchard, reservoir, archive, engine, theater, port. But none of them echo with voices. Whatever civilization designed this subterranean garden has stepped away, leaving an empty, resonant shell that still believes its inhabitants might return.

And so the chamber waits, loud with water and machinery, lush with restless green, dim with its own caution—alive, yet uninhabited, like a lung still breathing after the dreamer has woken somewhere else.
