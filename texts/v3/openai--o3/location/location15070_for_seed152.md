First hint of the place is a hush: air so still that a single drop falling from a leaf seems to ring. Step through the reed curtain of roots and the world shrinks to a room-sized hollow at the foot of the riverbank—a pocket delta, no larger than a small square, yet complete unto itself.

Terrain and raw ground  
The floor is new earth, pale silt only days or weeks old, still streaked with ripples from the last brief flood. Nothing here has had time to crumble; the surfaces wear sharp, infant edges—miniature ridges, bead-smooth nodules of clay, the crisp rims of puddles that look poured rather than eroded. Kneel and you can smell youth: wet minerals, the faint fizz of iron just oxidising. In places the silt domes upward where gases from buried vegetation are still feeling for a way out. Everything is low—barely a metre from floor to the arch of interlaced mangrove roots that roof the hollow; another metre to the outlet where a sliver of daylight slants in.

Water  
Moisture is present more as suggestion than body. Shallow pockets—hand-span pools—lie here and there, films of warm rainwater caught in the depressions. Viscous condensate collects on the roots above, gathering until it drops, feeding the pools in patient drips. When wind tugs the outside river, a single thin vein of current creeps in, fingers the edge of the chamber, and withdraws.

Light and atmosphere  
The place lives in permanent dusk. The only steady light is the river’s reflection, refracted through the root lattice: greenish, moving, never bright. At intervals a gap in the canopy lets down a shaft of late-afternoon gold, but it arrives weakened, tinged brown by tannins in the water above. Air is warm—close to body temperature—and carries an herbal sweetness, a mixture of sap and the slow breath of mud.

Vegetation—lush but spaced  
Plants are eager yet selective. Islands of exuberance stand out against broad emptiness. One cluster may be nothing but moss: emerald cushions, each the size of a coin, holding their own private dew. A pace away, a thicket of fern-like shoots—pale, feathery, already shoulder-high to a crouching human—springs from a crack. Between clusters, the new earth lies bare, still too raw or too saline for roots. The result is a rhythm: lushness, pause, lushness, pause, allowing eye and creature alike long sight-lines in the dimness.

Fauna—abundant and busy, yet soft-footed  
Life treats the hollow as a nursery. Tiny river crabs patrol the silty flats, sifting for organic flecks. Tree frogs cling to the undersides of leaves, throats pulsing silently until a rival passes. From the ceiling roots, translucent snails descend on threads of mucous to graze algae, then haul themselves back up. Predation happens, but without clamor: a flick, a gulp, ripple gone. Featherless birds—still nestlings—dart in on stubby wings to hunt gnats, then vanish through the roof lattice. Sound never rises above a murmur: water ticks, leaflets brush, an occasional soft croak.

Settlement—urban in intention, pre-tech in fact  
Tucked into the root-wall is a web of woven walkways and pods, the work of the River-Dwellers, a people who build with nothing hard enough to scar the tree that shelters them. Three suspended platforms—each no bigger than a family table—are stitched from water-reed, braced by living vines coaxed into loops. Their village fits the pocket perfectly: one sleeping pod, one store for dried roots, one communal hearth-space where fire is forbidden and heat comes from packed compost. The layout feels like a city’s first sketch—ordered, zoned, connected—yet executed entirely with fingers, teeth, and living fiber.

Rules and dynamics  
1. Construction may never breach the root canopy; the tree remains sovereign.  
2. Anything that falls to the silt belongs to the detritivores; no hand retrieves it.  
3. Rain is both clock and messenger: each descent signals time to plant, to harvest, to mend.  
4. Noise is rationed; predators outside the hollow hunt by sound. A shout is an alarm.  
5. The land is considered unborn until its first collapse; while edges stay crisp, no ceremony marks ownership.

Processes in motion  
• The river’s level pulses twice daily, licking fresh mineral wash across the floor.  
• Warm air rising from compost pods drives a gentle updraft, enough to stir spores and maintain a cycling mist.  
• Fern shoots bend noticeably over a single waking—growth you can measure against a knuckle.  
• In the darkest corner, a rivulet of methane finds flame on a rare spark, then gutters out, carving the first micro-canyon in the virgin silt.

Stand, listen: the hollow is quiet, but never still. Everything is arriving, expanding, testing limits—land, plant, animal, even the tentative geometry of the dwellers’ woven streets. It is a city seed in a cradle of brand-new earth, warm, dim, damp, and very much alive.
