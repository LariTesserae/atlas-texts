The place is a shallow basin no wider than the footprint of a small town, yet it feels even more enclosed than its size suggests, as if invisible walls rise around it.  You step down only a few metres from the surrounding plain and find yourself on a floor of white–grey mineral that catches what little light there is and scatters it upward in a perpetual twilight.

TERRAIN  
• Elevation: The basin’s bottom is almost perfectly level, a recent scar left by the retreat of an ancient brine sea.  It lies lower than the surrounding crust by perhaps the height of a cathedral’s nave.  
• Erosion & youth:  Every surface is newborn.  Crystals jut at knife-clean edges; floors break into polygonal tessellations whose joints are still sharp enough to slice a boot.  Nothing has had time to round or soften.  
• Material: 99 % halite with streaks of lithium-green impurities.  Think salt and powdered quartz fused, glassy yet dust-dull wherever footprints have scuffed it.

LIGHT & ATMOSPHERE  
• Overhead glows a shrouded, cold-red sun, its light filtered through an almost airless haze of sodium dust.  The sky is the colour of bruised pearl.  
• The air, such as it is, is thin and sterile, carrying no scent and almost no sound.  Speech dies a meter from the mouth.  Breathing feels like sipping through cloth.  
• The ambient temperature hovers just above the freezing point of carbon dioxide—chilly but survivable in stillness, lethal to liquid water.

THE BUILT—NATURAL CITY  
At first the place seems an abandoned settlement: corridors, courts, archways, balconies.  Closer inspection shows it was never laid out by hands or tools.  Chemical convection still pushes fresh halite upward from fissures, extruding columns that grow centimetres a day.  Where two columns meet, they cross-link into vaulted ceilings.  Where stress lines intersect, the crystal cleaves away, leaving door-like apertures.  

A map of streets and rooms therefore exists—but only for the moment.  The “urban plan” is alive, perpetually redesigning itself with a slow, deliberate grace:

1. Spine-roads of black lithium salt radiate from a central hollow that resembles a dry fountain.  
2. Towerlets rise to the height of the surrounding basin rim, then topple under their own weight, seeding new growth where they land.  
3. Cavities form beneath overhanging ledges; from them, threads of sublimated gas hiss upward in silent plumes, cooling and enlarging the vaulted chambers above.

TECHNOLOGY—OR THE LACK  
No carvings, no fittings, no residue of fire or metal.  Everything “manufactured” here is a phase transition, nothing more sophisticated than crystallisation and stress fracturing guided by temperature gradients.  Yet the emergent geometry feels intentional, haunted by a sense of purpose that tools might have fulfilled on another world.

VEGETATION  
Life clings in thin stripes:  
• Filaments of grey-green xero-lichen weave flat along crystal facets, absorbing the dim starlight through iron-porphyrin pigments.  
• Sparse tassels of bone-pale stalks stand in sheltered angles.  They are neither plant nor fungus but silicone-rich phototrophs that unfurl a few hours each day, then fold shut to keep their internal vapour from boiling away.

FAUNA  
Creatures are rare and reticent.  You may glimpse:  
• A glass-bodied arthropod no larger than a fingernail, pacing across a wall, leaving microscopic chips in its wake—its method of grazing on lichen.  
• A ribbon-thin worm that swims through salt like a fish through water, the friction-heat of its passage creating momentary flashes of blue light.  
• On the longest watches, a single pale flier whose wings are nothing but membranes of frozen gas; it drifts on convection plumes, never flapping, tasting the air for ions.

SOUND  
Silence dominates.  Winds can’t gather strength in the thin air; crystal growth is too slow to creak.  When a tower breaks, it does so in utter quiet, spraying shards that clatter only if they strike your suit or your body.  Most visitors find themselves listening to their heartbeat and wondering whether they’ve gone deaf.

DYNAMICS—WHAT IS CHANGING  
The basin is restless despite the hush.  In any hour you can note:  
• Columns thickening, seams widening.  
• Dust devils of fine salt spiralling in perfect, slow motion—visible, yet inaudible.  
• A faint luminescence where lithium veins reach the surface and oxidise; the glow travels like a tide mark, circling the basin every few days.  
• Predation: the glass arthropod sipping fluid from a still-living stalk; minutes later the stalk buds a hard cyst, sealing the wound, and the cycle resets.  

RULES OF THE WORLD  
1. Water is immediately wicked into the salt and vaporised; anything hydrated must carry its own sealed reservoir.  
2. Sound pressure travels poorly; communication, if needed, is visual or tactile.  
3. Architecture equals biology equals geology—classifications blur.  If you chip a wall, you have not vandalised a city; you have wounded an organism and disturbed a growth process.  
4. Time is quick for minerals, slow for animals—a reversal of common experience.  New rooms rise in days; a lichen patch might take years to advance a metre.  

WHAT IT FEELS LIKE TO BE THERE  
You stand amid a maze that is making itself while you watch, in air so thin you can almost believe you are dreaming.  Every surface around you is both sculpture and living crust; every breath you take diminishes a stock of moisture the environment is eager to steal.  Yet the place is beautiful—halls of frosted glass, natural rose windows engraved by growth stress, an ever-evolving city that never needed a mason’s hand.  It is young, mute, and intensely alive, a reminder that structure and intention can rise out of chemistry alone, given the right vacancy of water and the hush of thin, cold air.
