You step—or rather are allowed to step—into something no wider than a theater stage, a pocket plateau hanging halfway up an unfinished mountain. The mountain itself is still in the act of deciding to exist: raw basalt columns lie about like stacked draft pages, their edges sharp and unabraded, their surfaces unscarred by wind or time. Nothing here has yet learned the art of crumbling.  

Air & heat  
The air is clear but weighty, a dry, almost metallic atmosphere that hovers at a steady, furnace-warm simmer. Sweat never forms; there is no water in your body to spare. Instead you taste faint tangs of sublimating salts and alkaline dust. Light arrives in the normal way—no strange colors, no strobing—yet it seems to shine from a fixed noon that never drifts. Shadows keep their shape for hours.

Vegetation without water  
Across the dark stone rise stands of lush growth—though “green” is the wrong word. Tubular fronds the color of copper verdigris lean outward like exhaust pipes. Leaf-equivalents made of layered mica rattle together softly. The plants are vascularized, but the fluid glimmering in their capillaries is clear, oily, and far too viscous to be water. Periodically a stalk flexes and vents a plume of pale vapor—superheated CO₂ perhaps—making a sigh like a factory piston finishing a stroke. Photosynthesis here is a rumor; these organisms digest geology. They grow where mineral veins cross the surface, threading root-needles into hot fissures to drink molten trace metals.

Terrain & materials  
Walk a dozen meters and the plateau ends in a sheer, perfect right angle—as if a surveyor’s diamond blade sliced the rock. Beyond that edge there is only high thin sky and, at a vertiginous distance, clouds boiling over an unseen desert. The ground beneath your feet is vitrified in spots, fused into black glass that mirrors the plantwork in distorted reflection. Kneel and you notice the glass is still faintly warm, as though the heat that forged it lingers just below, waiting for instructions.

Industrial heartbeat  
Nothing is built here, yet everything behaves as if it were part of a dormant refinery. The slope holds vents that open and close on a schedule too slow for the ear to catch, releasing measured breaths of pressurized gas. Cap-rock plates tremble once every few minutes, realigning themselves with a muted clank. Even the plants sync their exhalations to that rhythm, and the resulting pulse spreads across the enclave like the turning of an enormous invisible flywheel.

Soundscape  
It is mostly quiet. The basalt gives off no echo. You hear:  
• the soft skritch of mica leaves brushing each other,  
• an occasional cloven hiss when a vent purges,  
• a low thermal hum that you feel in bones more than ears.  
Everything else holds its breath.

Ecology & absence  
There are no animals, no darting insects, no scavenging molds; the chemistry is hostile to water-borne life. Yet the place is undeniably lush—each plant crowds its allotted mineral vein, competing in slow motion for thermal niches. Propagation is mechanical: when a stalk matures, it fractures cleanly at a built-in seam, the freed segment fluttering down the cliff like a metallic seed glider until it spears a new crack below.

Rules that leak through  
1. Erosion has not begun. Drop a pebble and its edges remain razor-true.  
2. Time drips slower inside the enclave; outside clouds march, inside shadows idle.  
3. Wherever a line or surface is mathematically perfect, it stays that way—corners never chip, planes never warp—unless a living frond touches it, in which case perfection flows into the plant and becomes growth.

Weird fringes  
At the far back wall of the plateau stands a gap no larger than a doorway cut through the basalt. Look through and you see the same scene, a recursive slice of plateau, plants, gap—each hotter by a few degrees. Reach your hand in and you feel stronger vibration, as if deeper iterations house engines nearing ignition. Pull back, and your fingers are dusted with a glitter of fresh mica: the gap pruned a tiny amount of you for fertilizer.

Dynamics  
Nothing here hurries. The mountain will finish cooling over millennia; the plants will step slowly down its flank, vein by vein. The industrial beat will continue regardless of audience, forging glass, breathing out spent gases, building the place grain by grain until, one day, erosion is finally permitted and the perfect edges will at last admit a single flaw.

Until then the plateau remains sealed: a high, hot, waterless nursery where geology is still being written and botany is a branch of heavy industry.
