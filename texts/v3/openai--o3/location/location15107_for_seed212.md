At first glance the basin seems almost ordinary: a shallow, saucer-shaped lowland the width of several towns, its rim only a few metres higher than its centre, heat shimmering above it as though over a summer road. Then the eye adjusts and realises that nothing solid is truly still, that every visible surface is alive, and that “ground”, “wall”, and “skyline” are only positions on a single, continuous organism.

TERRAIN
The floor is young—freshly birthed mudstone scarcely nicked by weather—yet you seldom see the stone itself. The entire basin is wrapped in living tissue: mats of root-felt, overlapping leaf-plates, and hollow trunks that rise straight from the substrate in flat-sided pillars, as if someone sharpened a forest into a city block. Ravines slit the plain where the bedrock cracked as it cooled; the plant mass found those faults and lined them with vascular curtains, so the gullies glow deep emerald when backlit.

WATER
Rain is fickle, arriving in brief, vertical downpours that pock the leaf-city for minutes, then pass. Between storms, moisture hides in pockets. Every concave surface slicks over with condensation that drips into catch-basins of waxy bracts. Thread-thin rivers wander, vanish beneath the root crust, then burst upward again as fountains coaxed by root pressure. You walk on a sponge: each step releases a sigh of vapour and the scent of resin.

TEMPERATURE & AIR
Warmth is constant, as though some unseen sunlamp burns just overhead. Air density feels exaggerated—each breath syrup-thick with spores, terpenes, and the metallic tang of new stone. Heat keeps everything slightly swollen; nothing is crisp, everything plush and flexing.

VEGETATION (THE ONLY LIFE)
There is no gap between plants; they are the country. From a distance the mass looks like walls, roofs, buttresses. Up close it is photosynthetic flesh building itself into rooms and boulevards because growth hormones happen to follow those lines. 

1. Surface Carpet: A living tessellation of hexagonal leaves, each leaf the size of a paving slab, edges knuckled upward like half-opened trapdoors. Beneath, mycelium-like feeder hairs knit the leaves to the root sheet.
2. Pillar-Towers: Vertical stems twenty metres high, hollow, their interiors laced with spiral ramps, the ramps themselves green and pulsing. They thicken by the hour, new cambium layering inside like rings in rewind.
3. Span-Vines: Cables as taut as bridge steel, slung from pillar to pillar, sheathing bundles of xylem that move water faster than a mountain brook. Walk them and you feel a heartbeat underfoot.

FAUNA (ABSENT)
No insect whirr, no bird silhouette, no soft mammal rustle. Pollination here is mechanical: explosive stamens that fire grains across blocks, or sticky clouds pumped like fog from vents. Without animal grazers, the vegetation does not defend; it performs.

SOUND (DEAFENING)
The city-organism never shuts up. Sap rushes through trunk-pipes with the roar of subway trains. Air forced from spiracles sighs in chords that beat against each other and become thunder. When the wind gusts, every span-vine vibrates; hundreds together form a colossal aeolian harp. Conversation is impossible except in burrows under the leaf crust—if you can find one that has not sealed itself minutes later.

DYNAMICS & WEIRDNESS
Growth here is manic, scarcely obeying physical law. A pillar-tower may fatten, split, and peel into petals between dawn and noon, exposing a cavity you could fly a zeppelin through—if zeppelins existed. Gravity twitches, too: step onto a broad seed-plate at the right moment and you rise with it, borne upward on buoyant, gas-filled parenchyma. Direction loses meaning; what looked vertical might tilt, reorienting you onto a new “ground”.

Time seems elastic. Seed-plates close and open like lungs, and when they open again the light inside them is sunset-red even at midday. Sometimes you glimpse doubled edges on leaves, as though two frames of reality overlapped and forgot to merge.

BUILT—WITHOUT TECHNOLOGY
There are structures recognisably civic: amphitheatres scalloped into root masses, colonnades of buttressed stems, cloisters roofed by interlocking fronds. None were designed; they accreted along nutrient streams the way coral assembles brilliant cities from seawater. The resulting topography is navigable, even welcoming, but no hand shaped it.

Lightwells tunnel downward where translucent petiole columns refract sunlight into catacombs—green lanterns stitched into the foundation. Bridges over the river-threads are nothing more than vines that discovered they could support their own weight and kept thickening for the pleasure of tension.

MATERIALS
Everything is cellulose, lignin, resin, water, and the occasional inclusion of glassy quartz the plant draws from the bedrock to strengthen a span. Rub a wall and you come away damp with sap that smells of citrus and iron. Fire cannot live here; moisture boils out of every pore before flame can grip.

ATMOSPHERE OF THE PLACE
A giant, breathing cathedral—and also a riot, a bazaar, a perpetual construction site where no worker rests. There is majesty when the sun slants through aerosolised nectar and makes golden curtains between the towers. There is dread when a resonance wave passes and every structure thrums until your teeth ache. The city loves its own noise; silence would be death.

RULES (AS FAR AS ONE CAN TELL)
1. Lie still long enough and the city will build around you, incorporating your outline as scaffolding for the next layer of leaf-stone.
2. Open cavities are temporary. If you need a refuge, find it freshly torn and prepare to leave before the wound heals.
3. Water flows uphill if a conduit wishes it; follow gravity at your peril.
4. Memory skews: visitors report missing minutes or entire journeys retraced without realising. The city may edit stories as casually as it reroutes sap.
5. Nothing breaks permanently. Tear away a span-vine and another grows before evening, thicker, humming a deeper note.

SENSATIONS & SMALL MOMENTS
• Condensation pearls on your eyelashes and jumps to nearby leaves with static crackles.
• You lean against a wall: it sighs, adjusts a rib, and leaves a negative imprint of your spine filled with glowing sap.
• In a sudden hush between gusts, dripping water forms a rhythm so perfect you think someone must be playing drums in the next hollow—then the roar returns and wipes the pattern away.

THE FEELING OF DEPARTURE
Leaving is like wading from surf onto dry sand: ears still ring, skin still slick, heart still tries to match the external pulse that is no longer there. Outside the basin the world looks brittle, air too thin, colours anaemic. You realise that in the leaf-city you had been walking inside a living thought, and out here you have only ground beneath you.

But the scent of resin clings, and if you hold still you can almost hear, far off, the colossal organism exhaling—an invitation, or a warning, carried on warm wind.
