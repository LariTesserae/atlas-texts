Imagine a world that, from orbit, looks like a single pale-green circuit board the width of a continent. There is no oceanic gleam, no river lace, no flashing snowcap: only a monotonously bright lambency, as though the planet’s skin were a gigantic leaf held under a laboratory lamp.

1. First contact—what passes for “ground”
Set down anywhere and the soles of your boots touch no sand, no rock, no soil. The entire crust has been replaced by living laminate: interlocked plates grown from cellulose shot through with nickel fibers. Each plate is the breadth of a plaza, hexagonal, slightly domed in the center, and etched with vascular grooves. Capillary vents open and close to breathe a slow, ammonia-rich exhalation into the thin, cool air. The temperature hovers barely above the planet’s frost point—cool enough that metal feels cold even in noonlight, yet never quite freezing.

2. Light
Daylight is unnervingly bright. The canopy that should cast shadows instead fluoresces. Trillions of microlenses embedded in the upper fronds trap starlight and re-emit it downward in a mint-colored glare. Even at “night” (the star dips below the horizon out of habit, if not effect) the laminate lawns glow—stored photons dribble out of the tissue like embers from paper.

3. Silence
With so much surface porous and living, hardly anything echoes. Footsteps are absorbed; words die against the dampened air. Only two native sounds persist: a distant sub-bass throb, the heartbeat of hidden pumps, and the frenetic clicking of relay-flowers opening and shutting semaphore petals to pass data along the miles.

4. Built & industrial, yet botanical
Everything here is made, but nothing is manufactured in the human sense. Instead, botanical foundries bud from the substrate: chimney-trunks four hundred meters tall, ribbed like gills, extruding ribbons of photonic polymer that flutter down and lay themselves neatly in stacks. Conveyor vines ratchet the stacks into root vaults where enzymatic presses emboss them with circuitry. The entire world is a self-tending factory, a cool, bright photosynthetic engine designed—by whom, no one remembers—to turn stellar flux into exportable artifacts.

5. The dry chemistry
There is no liquid water—never was. The sap is liquid ammonia thickened by silicates; dew here means faint films of neon-green glycerides condensing on leaves at dawn. “Rain” is a fall of electrostatic dust: desiccated spores the size of sand grains, abrasive enough to scour exposed paint in a single storm. Metal stays bright; flesh dries and cracks.

6. Vegetation as everything
The plant mass is total. Roof, floor, horizon, sky—everything that grows, builds. Some of it mimics architecture: buttressed colonnades of trunk, bridge-spans of braided creeper, rooms extruded as hollow galls. Elsewhere the organism forgets Euclid, growing Möbius corridors and Klein-bottle atria that loop space back on itself. A walker who takes three left turns may end up above his own footprints, gravity politely inverted for the duration of the loop.

7. Erosion & age
Although entirely alive, the landscape feels settled, weathered. Old growth lies beneath new in nineteen discernible stratifications, each a slightly different alloy of cellulose and metal, each carrying the scars of earlier, slower catastrophes: fossilized lightning spirals, crushed domes, gullies worn by long-ceased flows of molten glass. Nothing rots; it only ossifies, joining the stacked palimpsest of manufactured woodstone.

8. Rare fauna
Anything with warm blood is an intruder. Native mobile life is sparse: translucent crane-mantas that glide on warm updrafts exuded by chimney-trunks; blind graphite caterpillars that chew cables for trace nickel; and the Whisper Hare, a tiny mammal introduced by visitors centuries ago, now surviving by drinking condensation from pneumatic tubes. Every creature moves softly, as though raised in a library.

9. Dynamic processes
Cool though it is, the world never rests. Relay-flowers pulse data, chimney-trunks belch packets of spore-smoke into the upper haze, rail-roots surge beneath the laminate plates, shuttling nutrients and gearlike seeds to far horizons. Once a local year the planet-tree performs a global molt: entire swaths of its surface delaminate, curl upward like shedding skin, and peel off into the stratosphere. The old layers ascend, unfurl, and freeze into glittering sails that drift for months before disintegrating into metallic snow.

10. Reality breakdown
Stand long enough in one place and the world’s geometry mutates. A corridor narrows until your shoulders scrape both walls—yet the next step opens into an arena the size of weather. Time dilates in the shade of certain fronds: a seed can sprout, blossom, and petrify while your chest rises and falls once. Survey drones report negative altitudes in open air, temperatures colder than absolute zero inside sunlit courtyards, letters of forgotten alphabets blooming in the glare then evaporating. No theory holds. Pilgrims call the phenomenon Leaf Logic: the planet thinks in growth patterns rather than physics, and space obeys the same instinctive tropisms.

11. Places the guides mark on faulty maps
• The Prism Gutter: a crevasse where leaking sap polymerizes into sheets so clear that they bend light, projecting distant landscapes into midair.  
• The Stillmill: a silent turbine farm whose blades are grown to harvest winds that haven’t blown for a thousand years. Yet the rotors spin, powered by pressure differentials that instruments cannot detect.  
• The Ammonstone Library: a kilometer-long rib vault filled with nodules that hum when stroked, playing back recorded memories in the touch-language of the plant.  
• The Disassembly Orchard: forests of hanging pods, each containing a machine built elsewhere, dismantled piece by piece and digested for trace elements—industrial compost heaps, elegant and ruthless.

12. Rules for travelers
—Bring no water; it is an acid here, dissolving cellulose walls and enraging the housekeeping phloem.  
—Do not shout; the planet interprets sharp vibrations as structural failure and grows cyst-walls around the source.  
—Map nothing in ink you intend to keep. The Leaf Logic will obsolete it before you unroll the parchment again.  
—If you find fauna larger than yourself, leave. You’ve entered a quarantine bloom where the plant cultivates predators for reasons it withholds.

13. Departure
When you finally lift away, thrusters blowing silent clouds of neon-green dust, the crust below ripples almost imperceptibly—as though the continent were waving goodbye, or perhaps tasting the brief chemistry of your passing flame. Then the bright lambent plain retakes its perfect composure, factory and forest, mirage and machine, leaf and lattice overlapping without seam: a vast, cool, whisper-quiet engine of living architecture, patient in its drought, older than any map, calmly rearranging reality petal by petal.
