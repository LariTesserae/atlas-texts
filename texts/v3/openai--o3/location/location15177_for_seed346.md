The basin is no larger than the footprint of a cathedral—three hundred paces wall-to-wall—yet within it every cubic metre is crowded with structure. From the rim you look down only two storeys, but once you descend the steps space begins to crease and repeat; towers, bridges and mezzanines overlap like pages that have been half-turned back on themselves. It is a miniature city in the early stages of being imagined, still negotiating which laws must hold and which may be improvised.

Terrain and raw geology  
• Elevation: barely above an inland sea that existed here in prehistory. The floor is a plate of newborn shale—glossy, gun-metal, faintly warm as if cooling from impossible pressure. Hairline cracks ooze a pale mud that hasn’t yet decided whether to be rock or silt.  
• Erosion is only beginning: droplets of condensed mist tap at the shale, carve pin-prick potholes, then pause for half-hours of absolute stillness. Nothing here has yet been rounded; every edge is knife-new.

Atmosphere, water and temperature  
• Cold, steady at two degrees Celsius. Your breath materialises, hangs near you, then slides sideways as if tugged by an unseen gradient.  
• Humidity hovers at saturation. The air refuses to rain properly; instead it sweats. Spherical beads of water cling to ceilings, lintels, palm-sized leaves of metal. A delayed drip-drip marks every pause in conversation.  
• In alcoves: shallow mirrors of water only a thumbnail deep. They flash with microscopic wavelets whenever a structure flexes.

Built density  
• Architecture compiled rather than built—self-assembling lattices of carbon foam, threads of bioceramic, panes of programmable glass.  
• Catwalks and service ducts interleave so closely that you can touch three separate buildings at once without moving your feet.  
• Every wall is load-bearing and every surface multipurpose: handrail, conduit, data-bus. The city feels like an overpacked suitcase that cannot be closed.

Light and sound  
• A local sunless noon: diffuse white comes from the air itself, as though photons leak from the vacuum. Shadows exist, but they wander; a beam can detach from its source, drift, then pin itself elsewhere.  
• Quiet enough that bone-creak and fabric-rustle sound immodest. Dominant noises: the sigh of expanding metal, the distant clunk of a gravity-reboot, an occasional water drop.

Vegetation and fauna  
• Life is hesitant. Here and there a bluish lichen maps itself across carbon foam, reading mineral codes as if scanning a QR tag. Jointed shoots—transparent, celery-pale—take root where condensation is reliable.  
• Only one confirmed animal: a thin, opalescent rodent that phases through walls if watched too long. It leaves prints in probability, not dust.

Technology (beyond modern)  
• Municipal infrastructure is run by paracausal engines. Conduits host massless currents whose equations are redacted even from machine memory.  
• Lift shafts ascend only half a storey yet deliver you four levels higher; the surplus height is stored as pending geometry, debited later.  
• Waste heat is exported to a different Tuesday.

Weirdness—reality breakdown etched into daily life  
• Conservation laws are advisory. If you keep perfectly still you may feel your mass fluctuate in sympathy with distant quasar storms.  
• Maps expire in under an hour; their paper yellows, the ink reorders. Citizens (few, widely spaced in time) navigate by personal myth: “Turn left where I first felt regret.”  
• Time moves on the slow setting. Cut a loaf and crumbs hang mid-air a heartbeat longer than they should. Word-echoes follow at a thoughtful distance.

Dynamics and long cycles  
• The whole pocket burg is growing, millimetre by millimetre, outwards and upwards. Raw shale extrudes at the rim, receives a dusting of artificial snow, is colonised by scaffold, then coded into habitable volume.  
• Nothing hurries. A bridge that began yesterday will finish next year; builders are algorithms with a glacial sense of rhythm.

Rules of the place (what seasoned visitors know)  
1. Never store anything important in straight lines shorter than forty-one centimetres; such segments tend to be reinterpreted as potential doorways.  
2. Drink condensation, not pool water—pools sometimes forget to be H₂O and revert to mathematical placeholders.  
3. If you hear three drops fall in perfect fifth intervals, leave: topology is about to recompile.

Stand at the centre. Listen to your own pulse refracted through folded streets. Mist refracts the wandering light, the city rearranges its blue-white bones, and the brand-new earth beneath your boots decides whether it wants to remain stone at all.
