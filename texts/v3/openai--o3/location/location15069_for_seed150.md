First glimpse

You crest the ridge only a little above tree-height—yet you are already inside the city. From the outside it looked like a single, dark, dome-shaped thicket clinging to a knife-narrow plateau; from within it resolves into layered avenues, chambers and terraces, all of them alive. Everything that rises here—column, lintel, vault or stair—was grown, not built. The whole precinct is perhaps the size of a sleepy market town, no wider than a few hundred paces across, its rim touching sheer cliffs on three sides and a cloud-smothered drop on the fourth. The air is cedar-cold; your breath fogs and immediately beads on bark and leaf.

Light

Even at noon the interior is dusk. The sky above the plateau is permanently quilted in pearl-grey stratus that squeezes the sun to a smudge. What brightness does make it through is strained yet again by multiple plant canopies: a low roof of wide, water-heavy fronds, a middle weave of flexible stems, and, at the very top, the overarching “spire trees”—skeletal, ten-metre trunks that meet and fuse so densely you can’t see the clouds except through pinholes. Most illumination comes sideways, a greenish gloom that thickens toward evening until you feel you are walking inside the pupil of some enormous eye.

Terrain & materials

The ground is young volcanic glass, scalloped and sharp where it still shows. But most of that rock is skinned by life. Mosses lay the first padding—fat pillows so drenched you hear them squish with every step. On top of them grow knee-high organ-plants: flattened pseudostems that knit across cracks and lamp each hollow with internal phosphorescence. Whenever a fissure opens wider, a new mat of rootlets stitches it closed within weeks; erosion is always trying to peel the city away and the vegetation is always rushing to heal.

Water

Moisture seeps from everywhere: out of portable holes in the rock, off the undersides of leaves, down the inside faces of hollow boles that act like gargoyles. There is no open stream—nothing here is big enough to be called a river—but there are a hundred laminar rills no wider than a finger. Follow one uphill and you find its source: melt-mist condensing on windward cliff faces, sucked into capillary vines, and overflowed into the streets below. Pools collect at corners like bronze mirrors, their surfaces tremoring whenever the canopy shakes loose another load of droplets.

Architecture

• Streets: braided root-paths, sprung slightly upward so your feet always move above the water film.  
• Walls: palisades of living sapwood, grown in parallel and then chemically fused into sheets that flex rather than break when the plateau’s sub-ice quakes ripple through.  
• Roofs: overlapping fern-plates; if you press a finger to one, you feel the slow pulse of sap hauling dissolved rock from deep cracks to the growing tips.  
• Windows: holes left by the deliberate falling-away of corky growths; around each opening hang translucent seed sacs that glow faintly blue at night.  

No stone blocks, no nails, no cordage—only cambium and time.

Atmosphere & sound

The city is never silent, only hushed: massed dripping, a distant constant shiver like wind in taut rigging (it is vines vibrating against one another), an occasional hollow thump when a snow-heavy bough outside the dome collapses and the tremor travels inward. Voices are rare. Echo is softer than normal; leaf-surfaces drink sound the moment it escapes lips.

Temperature

Most days hover just above freezing. The vegetation is antifreeze-rich; it stays green even when rime feathers the outer fronds. Periodically a katabatic wind dives off the summit peaks, driving temperatures down fast. When that happens, the city folds—literally. Hinged branches lower, fronds overlap, corridors shrink from roomy to crawl-space, trapping a pocket of comparatively warm, humid air. From outside the whole settlement resembles a clenched bud, glossy with hoarfrost. When the wind eases, everything relaxes and re-expands; the process takes under an hour.

Ecology

The place is almost a closed loop of photosynthesis, decay, and regrowth.

1. Primary producers: the megafoliates that make the walls and roofs; broadknife lichens clinging to cliff faces outside, feeding carbon compounds into the root network via shared hyphae.  
2. Decomposers: pale fungus combs hanging like organ pipes in still alcoves; they sigh out the subtle mushroom scent that passes for incense here.  
3. Animals: scarce. A few darting, furless voles with webbed feet, and the wing-sheath moths—white, silent things big as your hand that roost on undersides of fronds, perfectly imitating sheets of ice. Predation is almost nonexistent; energy is too tight. When something dies, the plants simply digest it through adhesive, enzyme-rich litter-leaves.  

Dynamics

Nothing is idle. Capillaries pump. Leaves swivel to catch stray sun shards. Moss inflates and deflates rhythmically, breathing ground-stored oxygen into the air. Once or twice a day, a slow tremor passes through the entire plateau and every stem in the city stiffens at once—a communal bracing against the microquake, then a collective exhale of elastic tension. Living architecture as reflex.

Rules of the world

• Nothing remains unused for long. A dropped tool of your own, should you bring one, will be overgrown within a week; the city interprets stillness as vacancy.  
• Combustion is almost impossible. Air is near-saturated with moisture, and most biomass contains silica micro-plates that smother flame.  
• Silence is permission. Stand motionless and the corridor will begin to narrow around you, vines creeping to retake empty volume. A gentle cough will make them halt and ease back.  
• No directional sense but down. Roots, not trunks, decide where “ground” is; if erosion undercuts an entire district the plants simply invert, transforming former ceilings into streets without ceremony.  

What it feels like to stay

Cold beads wick down your collar, but the air at mouth-level is scented—brisk, resinous, a little sweet. Everywhere is intimate: hall after hall barely shoulder-wide, everything curved, no direct line of sight longer than a few strides. You keep thinking you see someone out of the corner of your eye—a shape sliding behind a column of sapwood—but you never hear footsteps. Perhaps the city grows its own ghosts, negative spaces cut into the foliage, so that wanderers do not forget they are guests.

At twilight—meaning the hour when the cloud deck outside slips from grey to iron—the phosphorescent seed sacs brighten. Little beads of seawater-blue float at every junction, and the whole settlement looks like a submerged reef seen from below.

Departure

To leave, you move toward sound, not light: out where the cliff-edge channels the wind into a hollow roar. Beyond the last living buttress the plateau’s basalt shows naked again, sharp enough to slice boots. You step onto it and look back: the city is already shrinking in your eyes, branches knitting closed behind you as though erasing the path you walked only moments ago. It will remember the disturbance as a pattern of scored cambium, perhaps heal over it by morning, perhaps not. Either way, it remains itself: a cold, dripping, breathing citadel of vegetal will, high in the mist, waiting for the next rare visitor or the next slow inch of growth—whichever arrives first.
