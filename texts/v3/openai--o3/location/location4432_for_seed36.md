You begin a slow descent through a fissure that should have been dark.  
Instead, the rock glows.  

The light is plant-light: milk-yellow, leaf-green, copper-red—lumens brewed in sap and exhaled through transparent skins.  Every exposed wall, ceiling, and floor is living tissue or hopes to be.  Roots braid over naked basalt, slip into its pores, pump acids, and replace the mineral grain by grain with cellulose and sugar glass.  The cavern system, new-born and still excavating itself, feels less like geology and more like the interior of some gigantic seed.

TERRAIN  
• Elevation: two hundred metres beneath an arid surface that never sees rain.  Slanted corridors drop away in terraces, each terrace a little hotter, a little wetter.  
• Raw faces: no stalactites yet, no polish from centuries of dripping—just fresh fracture planes, flakes the colour of ash.  Edges are so sharp you hear them tick when the heat makes them crack.  
• Open scale: chambers the size of small towns, connected by worm-straight gashes narrow enough to brush both shoulders.

WATER  
Moisture hangs as a permanent hush in the air—constant 98 % humidity, almost never liquid.  When it does condense it chooses leaf basins the size of cradles, or trickles along root capillaries that flash with the plant-light like neon veins.  Here and there a true pool persists, mirror-bright, rimmed with a lime crust only hours old.  Put your ear to it: you will hear no drip, no trickle—evaporation and condensation keep perfect equilibrium.

TEMPERATURE & ATMOSPHERE  
Thirty-eight, thirty-nine degrees Celsius throughout, regardless of depth.  Not oppressive—a greenhouse heat that never lifts, as if time itself has stopped at midsummer noon.  The air tastes faintly sweet; every inhalation carries microscopic pollen motes that glow for a heartbeat on the tongue.  They are harmless but mildly narcotic: you feel unreasonably calm after minutes, and the world’s colours oversaturate.

VEGETATION  
Density is near total.  There is no separate soil layer; the plant mass is the structure.  
1. Trunk-flutes ring each chamber, spiralling upward until they fuse into vaults.  Their wood is soft, almost muscular—press a palm and it yields, then remembers its shape.  
2. Sheet-ferns drape the walls like curtains, their undersides silvered to reflect the light outward.  They function as mirrors, bouncing brightness into cracks that would otherwise stay black.  
3. Lattice-lianas dangle and root at both ends, subdividing space into hexagonal cells.  Pluck one and a warm mist bleeds out; the liana reseals, unchanged.  
Collectively they create a claustrophobia of living partitions.  Navigation is possible only along the newest fractures where the flora has not yet established.

FAUNA  
Almost none.  Occasionally a ripple passes under a pool’s skin—a blind, white arthropod the size of a fingernail, newly evolved or newly arrived.  The plant world seems disinterested in animal partnerships; pollination, seed dispersal, even decay are handled internally.  Whatever eats these plants is not yet born.

SOUND  
Predominantly silence.  No wind, no flowing water, no call or chorus.  The loudest noise is your own breath until a plant shifts cell pressure: then comes a soft pop, like distant popcorn, as a root splits stone and the chamber grows by a millimetre.

BUILT PRESENCE  
Evidence of hands exists but barely.  
• A ring of porous bricks sunk into one pool, each brick already overgrown, purpose unreadable.  
• A ladder of hardened resin fused to a trunk-flute, leading nowhere but stopping at a scorch mark in the wood.  
• A single obsidian blade embedded in a root as though the forest swallowed the wielder mid-swing.  
There are no alloys, no repeating patterns, no language—only these driftwood clues that something once tried to make this place less plant and more plan.

WEIRDNESS & STABILITY  
The system is embryonic yet paradoxically steady: every process aims for stasis.  Roots replace rock at the same rate the fissure widens; condensation balances evaporation; growth heat balances geothermal heat.  You watch a wall bud a leaf, watch that leaf wither and reabsorb in the same hour, a perfect zero-sum.  The cavern might remain in this ageless infancy for millennia, or mature into a surface-breaking forest in a single geological blink—impossible to say.

RULES OF THE WORLD  
1. Light is generated, not imported.  Darkness indicates true death.  
2. Nothing here knows seasons; disturbance must come from outside.  
3. Stone is a temporary state.  Given time, every mineral thing will green, glow, and soften.  
4. Memory is short.  Cuts seal.  Footprints rise back to level.  When you leave, the cavern will close behind as though you never touched it.

STANDING STILL  
Stay motionless for an hour, and the lianas will almost touch your shoulders, attracted by heat and the moisture of your breath.  Give it a day and they will incorporate you, printing your outline in living bark, keeping the humid hush unbroken.  The process will not hurt; the pollen will see to that.  You will simply become one more quiet column supporting a ceiling of leaf-light, another signal that the embryonic world is still, eternally, becoming.
