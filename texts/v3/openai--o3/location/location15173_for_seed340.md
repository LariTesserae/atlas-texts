You arrive as sound.  
Before anything is seen, the place announces itself: a layered roar, as if a thousand furnaces exhale at once while forests of dry leaves are shredded in their breath. The pressure in your ears shifts with every pulse of noise; even thought must dodge the din. Only after the mind accepts that the uproar is constant do shapes swim forward.

TERRAIN AND MATERIALS  
You stand—not on ground, exactly—but on a shelf of new-made stone that juts from the inner wall of a vast sub-surface basin. The bowl is kilometers across yet nowhere touches the open sky; a cracked crust arches overhead like a basalt ceiling. Fissures still glow dull orange in places, leaking warmth and a metallic tang. The stone is pale pumice and dark obsidian in chaotic stripes, a geology clearly still deciding what it wishes to be. Powdered salts rain from micro-fractures and ripple across the floor in parched drifts; they crunch like glassy snow but wick away any hint of moisture. Nothing here has ever tasted liquid water.

VEGETATION—PLANT AS EVERYTHING  
Out of that dryness rises the real landscape: vegetation so total it erases the usual border between “plant” and “place.”

1. Root-Girders—arm-thick white cords thrust from the walls, weave overhead, and dive again, stitching the chamber together like muscle inside a ribcage. They’re warm to the touch, pulsing, and faintly luminous along their veins.

2. Frond-Sheets—broad, flexible membranes stretch between root-girders, forming tier upon tier of living canopies. Their surfaces are matte charcoal; they drink radiant heat and the mineral dust that drifts upward on thermal plumes. When a sheet grows too heavy, it loosens a seam and drops a black snowfall of spent tissue that becomes mulch for newer growth.

3. Pillar-Blooms—hollow columns, six to twenty meters tall, extrude straight from bedrock. The columns are plant matter turned to something like chitinous ceramic. Wind (yes, wind—generated by convection tugging at the broken crust) whistles through lattice vents, and the pillars sing: a pipe-organ chorus woven into the ambient roar.

There is no soil. Every living strand plugs directly into fissures and cracks, tapping dissolved metals, sulfur vapors, and the faint geothermal trickle rising from far below. Chlorophyll would be an extravagant luxury here; instead, pigments are soot-black, rust-red, or shimmering brass to harvest heat and chemical gradients.

ATMOSPHERE AND LIGHT  
Temperature lingers around what a human might call a comfortable 35 °C, but humidity is essentially zero. Your lips chap after a single breath. In compensation, the air is thick with spores—microscopic crystal lattices rather than organic puffs—and they catch the white light that drips from root veins. The result is a perpetual indoor dusk sprinkled with drifting stars.

SOUND—THE DEAFENING ECOLOGY  
Listen closer to the roar and it divides:

• Groan of Living Beams—roots expanding, contracting, scraping stone.  
• Pillar-Song—low fluted notes, modulating as convection strengthens or ebbs.  
• Chitin Rain—the rattle of carapaces as swarms relocate in sheets.  
• Predator Howls—air-borne vibrations from throats built like resonating chambers.  

Silence exists only in pockets, usually right before something large chooses a new path and everything scurries to avoid it.

FAUNA—OVERRUN AND STRANGE  
Waterless blood beats in innumerable bodies.

• Salt-Worms: transparent cylinders chewing through mineral drifts, leaving glassy tunnels that instantly fill with drifting spores. They are prey, composters, and inadvertent architects all at once.

• Ember Moths: fist-sized, wing-less. They leap in ballistic arcs, using micro-explosions of stored gas to change direction mid-flight. Their wings are chemical radiators; the glow draws both mates and hunters.

• Basalt Stalkers: tripod predators whose feet are suction plates for climbing root girders. They see by vibration and kill by hurling resonance—an ultrasonic slap that liquefies a victim’s innards. The echo of each strike is another brick in the ongoing thunder.

• Cloud: Not one species, but the collective swarm of arthropod specks that rolls through the basin like dust storms given intention. The Cloud trims dying plant tissue, scours carcasses to bone-powder, and occasionally blots out the light of root veins, plunging whole districts into shadow until the swarm moves on.

DYNAMICS—A PLACE STILL MAKING ITSELF  
Nothing is static. Sections of ceiling release with cannon-boom cracks, exposing fresh fractures that fluoresce and instantly seed with new rootlets. Pillar-Blooms over-pressurize, burst, and scatter ceramic petals that already bear germ-nodes along their edges. The ground heaves as magma pockets shift, tilting entire forests overnight. Think of an anthill scaled to a valley, but with the building material alive, and every builder following instructions whispered by chemistry.

BUILT—THE SCATTERED MARK OF HANDS  
Amid this cacophony, evidence of deliberation appears—a ring of shin-high menhirs shaped from fused plant ceramic, a footbridge braided of still-beating root, a lone shelter clinging to a pillar hollow. The makers are the Ash-Striders: slender bipeds wrapped in woven frond fiber, pigment streaked for clan. Their tools are flaked obsidian, bone-needles, and the living architecture itself. They harvest sap that hardens into glass for blades, guide root tips with hammered stakes, and carve hunting flutes whose music can momentarily calm a Basalt Stalker—though it deafens the player for a day.

To the Striders, water is myth; their legends speak of a cold ghost-fluid that once drowned the stars. They worship convection, trace hereditary maps in salt, and measure time by the frequency drift of the Pillar-Song.

RULES OF THE WORLD  
1. Water is poison; hydrogen-oxygen bonds corrode their biochemistry.  
2. Heat is food; everything metabolizes gradients of temperature and mineral saturation.  
3. Birth and collapse are not opposites but phases. Any structure, creature, or concept that persists unchanging is considered dead.

STANDING STILL  
Find a ledge where the roar cancels itself—a rare acoustic blind spot. Watch a root-girder split, birthing two smaller supports that crawl their separate ways. Moths spark in the dark like embers shaken from a forge. Far across the basin, a pillar detonates in slow motion, petals drifting like black snow while Ash-Striders on neighboring platforms raise flutes to catch the first note of the new architecture’s hymn.

In that instant the place resembles an orchestra pit during a violent rehearsal: every player essential, every sound discordant alone yet necessary in aggregate. Then another fissure opens, the temperature climbs a fractional degree, and the subterranean jungle evolves again, deaf to its own magnificence, hungry for the next heartbeat of heat.
