You arrive through a narrow maintenance duct and step out onto a grated cat-walk no wider than your shoulders. From here the whole pocket valley—hardly larger than a cathedral nave—drops away beneath your boots. It is a valley only by analogy: every “cliff”, “ledge”, and “talus slope” you can see is wrought iron, cast basalt-glass, or plate steel, bolted into shape and still sharp along its seams. There is no bedrock here; someone decided the natural summit of the mountain was not suitable and skinned it in machinery until nothing raw remained.

1. Terrain  
• Elevation: The structure clings to the crest of a high ridge, so high that the outside air is thin and cold. Inside, however, air is kept slightly pressurized so the walls fog with condensation.  
• Youthful erosion: The metal landscape is new, edges crisp, weld beads unground. Corners snag sleeves, and rivets glitter like hoarfrost. Any rust blooms are pinhead small—the oxidation has barely started.  
• Scale: The whole world fits in a volume perhaps forty meters long, twenty wide, and sixty deep: a hidden pocket gouged into the mountain’s peak and roofed over with retractable plates. It feels like standing inside a gigantic engine block, each face of the chamber another head-gasket for something vaster.  

2. Water  
Water is everywhere, but never still. The ceiling plates sweat in the thin, cool air; droplets patter onto catwalks, run along V-grooves, and pour in measured sheets down vertical sluices. At intervals the sluices meet and plunge through turbines the size of millstones, hissing through needle-spray nozzles, then vanish into pipes that lead, unseen, to lower powerhouses. A smell of iron and ozone lingers. Water never pools long enough to stagnate—gravity and design whisk it away seconds after it appears.

3. Vegetation  
Life clings only in afterthoughts: a filament of algae where a gasket leaks warm condensate, a coin-sized lichen on a shadowed bolt. Green feels smuggled, not permitted. No roots can pierce the plated floor; they merely decorate the welds like accidental graffiti.

4. Light  
Illumination is parsimonious: sodium guide-lamps guard ladders, and emergency strobes behind wire cages beat a slow amber pulse whenever pressure differentials spike. Elsewhere, the scene is lit by reflection—moonlight leaks through slits in the overhead shutters, refracted by mist until every droplet carries its own faint halo. Your eyes live mostly in penumbra.

5. Sound  
Deafening is literal. Turbines shriek, condensers hammer, servo motors ratchet open spill-gates. Even the drip of water echoes like brass shot dumped on a cymbal. Communication happens through elbow taps and coded light flashes; spoken words dissolve before leaving mouths. You feel vibrations in your molars and sternum; silence exists only inside the doubled seals of your breather mask.

6. Atmosphere & Climate  
Temperature hovers just above freezing. The pressurized chamber traps enough warmth that mist forms instantly, yet the high external altitude keeps everything crisp. A gust from a vent can swing a hanging cable like a pendulum. Weather, here, is artificial: a control algorithm opens vents to vent steam, then shutters them when outside winds threaten to scour heat away. It feels like standing in the throat of a breathing machine.

7. Ecology  
Fauna are absent by statute and by necessity—nothing with ears could survive the relentless volume. Microbes run the only food chain: chemotrophs nibble the oil film, slime molds graze the chemotrophs, mites (accidentally introduced) form the apex—barely visible chalk motes that cling to filters. You will never see them, only the fine dust they leave when they die against intake screens.

8. Built & Tech  
Every surface is intentional. Bolted ribs curve like buttresses, their hollows packed with cabling. A central spine—an elevator shaft without the car—drops through the floor grid; hydraulic pistons stand in ranks along its sides, ready to damp sudden pressure pulses. Machinery is industrial but not fully automated: someone, somewhere, still walks these galleries to adjust valves. Their work manifests in chalk marks and the cinched ends of safety wires.

9. Dynamics  
Twice an hour the whole pocket landscape changes mood. A bell—felt, not heard—signals a flush cycle: sluice gates snap open, torrents roar, vapor swirls up like a reverse waterfall, and then, almost daintily, shutters above peel back a fraction so the hot breath can vent into the subzero sky. The temperature drops five degrees in moments; frost forms lace along the guard rails. Then the shutters clamp shut, heaters hum, and the cycle begins rebuilding its load of condensed water. The place never rests, only shifts from loud to louder to loudest.

10. Rules of the World  
• Gravity, pressure, flow, and maintenance schedules are the governing laws; everything organic must negotiate with them or perish.  
• No puddle survives one full cycle; no noise drops below industrial OSHA red-zone levels.  
• Flash-lights have blue filters—white light triggers optical sensors that trip emergency halts.  
• Anything softer than tool-steel must be tethered; an errant screw can become shrapnel when caught in the drafting turbines.

Standing in this hanging valley of forged metal and perpetual water, you understand why vegetation stays sparse, why animals do not visit, and why human presence feels provisional. It is a young landscape, freshly wrenched from concept into matter, still tasting of flux and current. The mountain outside is ancient, but inside this pocket—within its humming iron womb—creation is ongoing, deafening, and never quite finished.
