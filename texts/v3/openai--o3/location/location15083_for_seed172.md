The place is called the Null Promenade, though no one promenades here. It occupies a single, windowless chamber the size of a theatre stage, wedged near the crown of an impossibly tall smokestack that punches into the mesosphere of a frozen planet. Nothing in the Null Promenade was ever “natural”: the floor, the walls, the roof, the air itself—every gram has been cast, extruded, milled, or distilled for a purpose that no longer matters. What remains is a pocket-world, perfectly still in time yet shaken every second by the machinery that birthed it.

1. Terrain and Materials
• Floor: A swept-clean mesa of pale alloy plates, joint lines razor-sharp, edges beveled as though the metal had only yesterday cooled in its moulds. A dusting of salt-like cryo-precipitate gathers in the seams—lithium nitrate rather than ice, glittering, bone-dry.  
• Walls: Sheer, high, and angular, rising like mountain ridges caught before erosion could soften them. They are paneled with mirror-finished durachrome that reflects exactly as much light as it receives, making distance difficult to judge; one step forward can feel like a kilometre.  
• Ceiling: A catenary vault of ribbed steel that reverberates with the same perfection as an organ pipe. Nothing sags, nothing flakes, nothing rusts; the chamber’s youth is absolute and permanent.

2. Atmosphere and Temperature
• Temperature hovers at -70 °C. Cold enough that exhaled breath (there is always someone’s breath in the legends, though no one is actually here) would fall as metallic snowflakes if there were water; instead, vapour from leaking coolant lines crystallises into brittle polymer filaments that crackle down like needles.  
• The air is thin—an engineered mix rich in xenon for electrical insulation. It carries sound almost too well, transmitting every vibration straight into bone.

3. Light
A single heliarc bar circles the vault where wall meets ceiling, steady as noon sunlight. It never brightens, never dims. Shadows are surgical cuts, motionless on the ground.

4. Sound (deafening, always)
Under the silence of emptiness roars the eternal machine:  
• A turbine deeper in the stack spins at 40 000 rpm. Its scream arrives through floor-bolts as a 16 kHz whistle that never waivers.  
• Gear-trains clack in phasing cycles—a cathedral’s worth of clockwork magnified a thousand times.  
• Occasionally a pressure-relief iris snaps open and vents neon, producing a thunderclap that should mark time but, in fact, marks nothing; the cycle is perfectly periodic and thus meaningless.

Ear protectors hang on hooks along the wall—bright yellow tulips in a desert of chrome—but there are no ears left to receive them.

5. Vegetation—Sparse, Quasi-Metallic
Life insists, if only symbolically. Where stray beams of ionising radiation strike micro-fractures, carbon dust has assembled into sheets a few molecules thick. On these sheets perch crystalline “lichens” of doped silicon—dark blue rosettes that harvest the light bar’s photons and exhale weak electric currents into the wall. They cover less than one percent of available surface, but their presence bends the mind: something is growing in a place that never changes.

6. No Water, Non-aqueous Chemistry
Without water, chemistry rewrites itself. Corrosion is replaced by sublimation; rot by depolymerisation. When the lichens die (a century from now, exactly), they will evaporate straight into plasma, leaving no ash behind.

7. Fauna—None
Nothing crawls, nothing flutters, nothing prowls. Sensors meant to track bio-signatures now idle at zero. The chamber’s designers sought sterility and achieved it, save for the rebellious lichens.

8. Rules of the World
• Conservation of Geometry: Angles do not dull. If you chip an edge, it silently recrystallises overnight.  
• Temporal Plateau: Instrument logs show time passing, but no statistical drift in temperature, pressure, or wear occurs. Entropy is siphoned away into the turbine’s exhaust where, it is assumed, it harms something else.  
• Acoustic Dominion: Sound cannot be damped; any attempt at muffling is swallowed by the structure and re-emitted elsewhere, louder.

9. Ecology (Such as It Is)
Input: xenon, stray photons, vibration.  
Process: The lichens sponge vibration through piezoelectric stalks, convert it into charge, and share via micron-scale wires that lace the walls.  
Output: A soft ultravio­let glow so faint it requires an hour’s dark-adaptation to see—ghostly halos that drift like aurora along the metal joints.

10. Narrative Hints
Some say the Null Promenade was once an inspection stop for technicians who climbed the stack to service its engines. Others claim it is the stack’s heart, and that everything above and below exists only to feed vibration into this tiny hall so the lichens—accidental or intentional—can mine it. The truth, if truth exists in a place frozen at the moment of creation, is lost beneath the turbine’s scream.

Step onto the alloy plates and nothing will greet you but the roar, the glare, the razor-edges that heal behind you, and the sense that you have been miniaturised, placed in a jewel box perched atop the world, and told to listen forever to a machine that can never, ever bear the sound of its own silence.
