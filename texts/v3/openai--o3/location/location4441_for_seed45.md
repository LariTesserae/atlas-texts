You arrive in the hourless dark of a continent still gestating.

TERRAIN  
The ground is a tilted plate of newborn basalt, its surface puckered and blistered where magma fled only yesterday. Everything rises. Mountain chains heave upward in serried ranks, each ridge a little steeper, a little sharper, until the farthest crests spear straight into the sky and vanish. Between them fall chasms so deep their bottoms swallow every trace of the horizon; cut-faces still glisten with a metallic sheen where the rock cracked open minutes ago. Shelves the size of towns hang in mid-air on fault-borne ledges, trembling, already crazed by new fissures. Nothing here has had time to round its edges—every stone edge is knife-bright, every boulder newly cleaved.

MATERIALS & COLOR  
There is no sand yet; grains have not had time to exist. Instead, slabs of glassy obsidian lie scattered like spilled panes, some still hot enough to shimmer in the thin air. Flecks of sublimated sulfur sparkle in the cracks, giving the mountainsides a faint radioactive bruise-green glow that passes for night-light. Rare streaks of cinnabar and copper thread through the black, hinting at ores that have not met weather until this moment.

ATMOSPHERE & LIGHT  
The sky never brightens beyond a coal-smoke violet. An opaque haze of ash and micron-small mineral dust hangs permanent, beaten from the crust by constant quakes, screening whatever sun the world owns. From beneath the haze, occasional flashes—silent sheet-lightning of purely electrostatic origin—illuminate the peaks for heartbeats, etching them like negatives before everything drops back into gloom. Heat radiates upward from the ground; you feel it on your shins like the breath of an oven door left ajar. No scent of water, only the ferric tang of raw stone and the faint sting of sulfur.

SOUND  
For long spans nothing moves and the world falls into an uneasily quiet hush, the only noise your own footfalls clicking on obsidian. Then the silence shatters: a bellowing crack, mountain-deep, as a whole wall decides to shear away. It echoes, rolls around the valleys, then ebbs again. Wind cannot find a consistent path through this labyrinth; when it does thread a canyon it moans in low, throat-like drones for minutes, then dies.

DYNAMIC BEHAVIOR  
The entire region behaves like a living plate. New vents iris open without warning, belching columns of hot, dry gas that rise in corkscrews and collapse. Needle-thin pillars of stone grow upward where the gas vents, freezing mid-eruption into hollow towers—some topple within hours, some survive a day and acquire a wilderness of siblings, littering a shelf with broken organ-pipes. Gravity feels trustworthy, yet features appear, twist, and vanish quickly enough to confuse memory; a landmark you noted at dawn might be rubble by the time you return.

ECOLOGY  
No plant, no animal, no microbial mat thick enough to see. Yet here and there stand formations that feel deliberate: honeycomb domes of fused crystal, waist-high lattices of filigreed hematite that hum when the wind threads them. They form only where particular minerals exhale from vents, knitting together as they cool—pure chemistry masquerading as architecture. A few such “hollows” trap heat and shelter pools of heavier gases; in their dim interiors, centimeter-long chains of silicate molecules drift like ghostly lace, the first experiments of non-aqueous life struggling to learn what shape survives in a furnace.

BUILT TRACES  
Amid the natural constructs sit stranger fragments: evenly spaced basalt menhirs, half sinks of perfectly round boreholes, shards of matte-black ceramic that shows no melt despite the prevailing heat. They are rare, scattered, never in groups large enough to form settlement. Whether they were accidental by-products of ancient lava fountains, proto-life’s abandoned shells, or the hand-work of something that has already passed on is impossible to say—yet every traveler eventually stops, presses a palm to one of the menhirs, and feels an interior hum not quite geological.

RULES OF THE PLACE  
1. Everything is provisional; solidity is only a pause in becoming.  
2. Heat is abundant, light is scarce; sight is a privilege won by patience and luck with lightning.  
3. What looks crafted may be chemistry; what looks natural may be intention.  
4. Wait long enough and the map changes under your feet.  
5. Silence is not safety—it is the breath the land takes before it moves again.

STANDING STILL  
If you kneel and set your ear to the basalt you can hear a deep, multi-layered thrumming, as though the crust itself vibrated like a plucked string: tectonic chords of a planet still tuning itself. Every few minutes a higher, crystalline overtone joins—microfractures singing as they propagate.

And when you finally lift your head, the shelf you’re on seems a fraction higher than before, the horizon a fraction sharper, as if the world, mid-sentence, revised itself while your eyes were closed.
