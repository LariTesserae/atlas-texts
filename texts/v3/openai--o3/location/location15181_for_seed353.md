You arrive at night, yet there is no real night here—only a thick, hot murk that never lifts. A high table-land stretches outward, almost level from eons of wind-scouring, its original rock now buried under a continuous crust of living matter. Imagine a desert plateau that has been paved, wall-to-wall, by a single organism: a planetary bonsai shaped into factories, streets, foundries, vaults—every surface, all of it grown.

TERRAIN AND MATERIALS  
• Substrate: The oldest detectable layer is a pale, dust-glass formed when the plateau’s basalt was flash-fused ages ago. Over that lies a root-mat several metres thick, woody but shot through with rust-bright veins of iron and nickel the plant extracted from the dust.  
• Flooring: Where people might once have walked, the organism has secreted sheets of lignin-alloy—flexible, jointed plates that click faintly when cooling air shrinks them at dusk (dusk here is only a slight dip in furnace-heat).  
• Structures: Turrets, gantries, ducts, exhaust chimneys—every one of them is cambium turned to purpose. The trunks fuse classically into gothic ribs, then blur into refinery organs where sap circulates like molten metal.

ATMOSPHERE AND LIGHT  
• An almost water-free haze, bone-dry and superheated, lies motionless until a convective burst lifts it in slow churning columns.  
• True darkness reigns; the sun—if it still exists—never reaches the plateau floor. Instead, dim internal luminescence—dull ember reds and magnesium whites—glows from growth joints, inspection blisters, and incomplete fruiting bodies. The redness is less light than temperature made visible.  
• Ozone and resin sting the nose; occasionally a whiff of hot salt drifts by, as if someone had poured seawater onto a skillet and let it vaporize instantly.

SOUNDS  
Most of the time you hear nothing but your own circulatory system. Then a far-off boom: two trunks sealing together. A hiss: a pressure valve made of cartilage opening to vent pure chlorine. Quiet returns, the hot air swallowing echoes before they travel.

VEGETATION / ARCHITECTURE  
• The plant is total. No gap exists between “tree,” “factory,” and “town.” Leaf-equivalents are thin radiator fins that flick open to bleed waste heat into the night.  
• Instead of sap, a molten salt electrolyte circulates, carrying charge as well as nutrients. Because water would short-circuit the system, any stray moisture is trapped and cracked into gases within seconds.  
• Reproductive organs resemble antique blast furnaces. When ripe they split, flinging out seed-cylinders already sheathed in alloy shells. Most land nearby, burrow, and weld themselves into the existing network; the plateau’s edge keeps creeping outward at the pace of continental drift.

TECHNOLOGY  
The entire ecosystem is an industrial installation built by…somebody. But that somebody long ago let the feedback loops run. Now the organism grows turbines because turbines were once useful, extrudes rail lines though nothing rides them, stamps out gears the size of wagon wheels only to grind them back into raw feedstock. Production is purpose; product is irrelevant.

DYNAMICS AND INSTABILITY  
Nothing holds still. Lattice-girders buckle, liquefy, and reforge overnight. Tuberous tanks distend under internal pressure, burst, and are instantly resealed by scar-tissue. Picture a steel mill trying to remodel itself while the furnaces stay lit and you have the mood: turbulent but eerily methodical.

ECOLOGY  
• Fauna: none large enough to notice. Microscopic motile pollen swims through the electrolyte channels; that is the closest thing to an animal here.  
• Decomposers are unnecessary—the plant recycles its own dead tissue in smelter-like stomachs and reissues it as fresh structural foam.

RULES OF THE WORLD  
1. Water is treated as a toxin; any droplet is dismantled on contact.  
2. Darkness is not a deficit but a working condition. The organism feeds on geothermal seepage and infrared waste, not sunlight.  
3. Everything changes, but in strict obedience to blueprints written so deep in the genome that no current intelligence can read them.  
4. Silence is camouflage. When noise happens, it means energy is being cashed in for growth or repair; quiet is the steady state.

IF YOU STAY  
Stand still for an hour and the floor will heat until your boots soften. Stay a day and indentation sensors may decide you are a cavity to be filled: vines will weave a mesh across your outline, sealing you in like any other crack against the killing air outside. You will not be digested—you will be built over, incorporated, given a serial number you will never read because the world around you is already closing, quietly, completely, forever making itself anew.
