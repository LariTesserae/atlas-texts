The Cavern of Arajh—locals call it simply “the Hollow”—lies half a day’s descent beneath a windswept plateau.  It is not a single chamber but a sunken region the size of several valleys stitched together, a continental-scale wound where the crust cracked, sagged and stopped, never quite healing.  Above, the ceiling is gone; in its place hangs a ragged sky of stone ribs and narrow slots.  Through those slits daylight seeps in only as a pewter twilight, the sun reduced to pale fingers that never reach the floor.  Evening and noon are almost indistinguishable; the Hollow is always dim, always cool, holding a muted bluish cast as though seen through deep water.

Terrain and materials  
• The ground is raw, newborn stone—basalt blades and shale plates still sharp from the cataclysm that made them.  Walls rise abruptly, then break off in cantilevered shelves and benches.  Edges have not yet had time to crumble; every cliff looks freshly planed.  
• Between these heights stretch long flats of powder-fine silt, the only legacy of rare flash-floods that hiss down from the plateau above.  The silt holds water like a sponge, so the flats are stitched with silvered puddles and braided rills.  
• In deeper pockets lie permanent tarns: oval, perfectly still, their surfaces skinned with mica dust that reflects the ceiling’s sparse light.  They are never numerous—one pool every few hundred paces—but in the hush each one feels like a center of gravity around which the landscape arranges itself.

Atmosphere and light  
Mist is constant but thin, rising from the tarns at dawn and never entirely settling.  It magnifies sound and scatters the pale light until distances appear farther than they are; a call made on the north shelf can be heard, perfectly clear, a valley distant.  The air keeps to a steady chill—cold enough that breath clouds, never cold enough to frost.  Nothing here suggests seasons; what you feel on arrival is what you will feel, unchanged, years later.

Water  
Rain is rumored by the people of the rim but never witnessed inside.  Moisture comes instead as nightly condensation.  At the coldest hour, droplets condense on rock, gather, merge, fall in slow percussion: tik-tik-tik.  The sound is light but incessant, ticking through centuries, carving miniature runnels down the virgin stone.  In the Hollow’s history no torrent has ever lasted long enough to scour the place smooth; everything is still at the beginning of erosion’s story.

Vegetation  
Plants are sparse, almost timid.  
• Pale moss pads cling to shaded overhangs; touch them and they weep a faint, mineral-sweet water.  
• Knot-root shrubs grow knee high where silt stays damp.  Their bark is the same grey as the cliffs, and from a distance they disappear.  
• No tree stands taller than a person.  Photosynthesis survives on the dregs of light, each leaf broad and thin as a bat’s wing, mottled to catch every spare photon.  The plants lean, but nothing here truly flourishes; they endure, and that is all.

Fauna—life that will not be quiet  
Against the understatement of rock and leaf, the Hollow’s animals arrive in overwhelming profusion.

Day-dusk swarms:  
• Insects rise in ropes—drab gnats by the million, their combined flight a visible column.  Wings produce a low rotary thrum that never pauses; it is the baseline of every other sound.  
• Fish-eaters—white-bellied frogs the size of a handspan—lurk at pool margins.  When a gnat column leans too close, the frogs erupt in blasts of synchronized leaps; bodies splash, jaws slap, water drums like applause.  

Night-dusk flocks:  
• Cliff swifts, blacker than the dimness, sheathe the air.  Their echo-chirps ricochet off faces of stone, filling every pocket with metallic clicks.  One flock alone can darken a shelf; a dozen flocks mean you see only motion, not sky.  
• Blind cavy herds hustle across the flats, hooves chipping at basalt knives.  They honk in short, booming calls that seem oversized for their small, guinea-pig bodies.  An entire herd answering itself can echo for minutes after the animals have gone.

Apex hunters:  
• There is only one: the ridge-lion, coat mottled slate-and-fog, tail ridged with keratin scutes.  It stalks by vibration more than sight, padding from ledge to ledge with a patience measured in hours.  When it charges, the cavern’s acoustic shell amplifies the brief roar to a rolling thunder that fools prey into fleeing toward, not from, the predator.

Soundscape  
All this life converges into a loudness that contradicts the Hollow’s emptiness of things.  Every surface is hard; nothing absorbs sound.  A cough on one wall can circle the basin before fading, gathering a dozen subtly shifted echoes along the way.  Silence exists only for a heartbeat between overlapping calls, never for a full breath.

Culture—barely budding  
No built structures stand; stone remains untouched by pattern or tool.  Yet on the flats you may find:  
• Sorted cairns of silt-smoothed bones, knucklebone on knucklebone, stacked three high.  
• Forked twigs thrust into mud banks beside pools, their tips charred to brittle black.  
These are signs of the “first-makers,” shag-furred hominids seen rarely at tarn-edge dusk.  They carry nothing more advanced than firebrands, split-stone knives, and woven reed slings.  They hunt frogs, dare the swifts’ cliffs for eggs, and bury their dead beneath cairns to keep scavengers at bay.  They do not shape the place; the place shapes them, and it has not yet decided what to do.

Rules of the world—equilibrium frozen in its youth  
1. Nothing decays quickly.  The temperature, dearth of bacteria, and thin mist mummify carcasses; a dead cavy may lie intact for decades before powdering away.  
2. Weather is internal, not imported.  The Hollow’s moisture cycle is closed: condensation down, evaporation up.  Lightning, storms, seasons—all belong to the plateau above, never here.  
3. Erosion is on pause.  Stone blade and shelf await the slow chemistry of moss acids and drip lines, but geologic violence has finished its part for now.  Millennia hence the shapes will mellow; in any human lifetime they will appear frozen at the moment of birth.  
4. Light may dim further but never brighten.  Each year the ceiling’s cracks knit minutely tighter with mineral bloom, shaving lumens from an already thin supply.

Standing in the Hollow  
Footsteps scrape on shards; the echo chases you like a second traveler.  Ahead stretches silt flat, then a mirror-dark pool, then a wall that rises clean into gloom.  Somewhere high in that gloom wings purl and slice.  Mist beads on your lashes.  You inhale rock dust, water vapor, frog musk, the faint burnt-fat tang of a ridge-lion kill.  Nothing has changed here since the day the ground fell open.  Nothing seems likely to change.  Life has flooded in to fill the stillness with noise, and in its clamorous way the Hollow is complete.
