When the place-that-is-always-beginning first flickered across the farsight of the Benevolent One, it appeared the way a newborn star does to an astronomer: too bright with possibility to meddle in hastily. The power—whose names are as many and fugitive as the creatures it has shepherded elsewhere—did what all wise gardeners do at the edge of an unfamiliar plot: it watched.

I. Watching

For ninety revolutions of the moonless sky the power floated in bodiless attendance above the triple canopy. It tasted the copper-warm rain, counted the tremors that stitched and unstitched the ridges, let the percussion language of insects drum across its awareness until understanding dawned. In that span the forest grew an entire new grammar of vines; three generations of lantern fungus rose and guttered; the Geometer—helmet shut, cicada throat-basket buzzing—passed twice beneath the place where the power listened.

From that long stillness the power learned two truths.

1. Nothing here is actually broken; it is merely unfinished and will forever remain so.  
2. Amid the rush, one living will keeps asking the same question in ever more meticulous footsteps: the Geometer’s.

II. Deciding

Intervention, the power knew, can bruise as swiftly as it can heal. Yet utter non-action, too, is a kind of violence when a mind is reaching upward for help it cannot even name. The task, then, was to give gifts that would neither slow the forest’s exuberant becoming nor leave the seeker entirely unaided.

It settled on three offerings: one to the land itself, one to its chorus of species, and one to the solitary being who tries to read them all.

III. Gift to the Land: Easing the Pulse

At midnight—an hour that means little under a canopy that never shows stars—the power bent close to the basalt’s youngest seams. Into each microfracture it whispered a stippled lattice of silence: voids so small they could only contain the idea of emptiness. Thereafter, whenever tectonic stress climbed toward a shattering cough, those voids drank a fraction of the energy and belched it out again as sub-audible hum, smoothing the peaks of violence without erasing them. Quakes still came; cliffs still fell; but fewer newborn pools flashed scalding and dead. In time the freshet minnows hatched a whole second longer before being braided into mud, and that single extra heartbeat propagated up the food chain like interest on a divine loan.

IV. Gift to the Many: A Memory That Travels

The power next addressed the forest’s fatal amnesia—its tendency to eat every message the moment it was spoken. Working through the root confederacy (because plants, for all their muteness, never interrupt), it seeded a new mycorrhizal filament the width of a spider’s thought. This silver fiber did nothing but remember vibrations: the bass detonations of the barrel frogs, the steel-saw song of cicadas, the drummed maps of echo birds, even the hush that followed a sky-reefer’s shadow.

Whenever one node of the network felt a rhythm it had not sensed in twelve rotations, it hummed the pattern back into the soil three nights running before letting it fade. Thus no sound was frozen—only allowed an encore. Predators could not exploit it; prey could not rely on it; yet the forest as a whole began, subtly, to recognize itself from one wet season to the next. Call it a rolling recollection, a melody braided into constant change.

V. Gift to the One Who Asks

Last, the power stepped inside the Geometer’s dream. The walker lay bivouacked in the hollow of a toppled palm, tuning fork resting against a knee, visor glimmering with reflected violet from lantern fungus. In that liminal sleep the Benevolent One spoke—not in words but in ratios of tone, the language the Geometer spent years trying to hear.

What it conveyed was neither doctrine nor prophecy, but the missing tool: scale. The forest’s madness becomes legible, the dream said, when charted in logarithms, not linears. Distances, lifespans, tremor intervals, even the brief blaze of stress wisps—all expand or shrink by powers of six. Hold that exponent in your pulse and you may one day draw a map that lives as the forest does: stretching when heat rises, coiling tight when rains retreat.

At dawn the Geometer woke, struck the fork, and for the first time heard a chord inside the clang.

VI. Leaving (or Something Like It)

The Benevolent One did not depart; it merely let its attention wander wider, confident the gifts would self-regulate. The lattice of silence inside the rock will eventually dissolve as new basalt surfaces, giving the forest back its more violent crescendos when it is old enough to survive them. The memory-filament will tatter and respawn, never quite the same score twice. And the Geometer, pacing out logarithms, will add a brief, luminous footnote to the primates’ proverb—clicked in obsidian, echoed in birdsong:

“Here, even the moment has offspring—
and some of them remember us kindly.”
