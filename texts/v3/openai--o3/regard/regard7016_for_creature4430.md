The guardian arrived as it always does: silently, at the speed of a turning thought, present everywhere inside the fissure and nowhere outside it.

1. First, it listened.  
   • It matched its awareness to the sap-pressure tremors that pass for pulse inside the Parent Body.  
   • It let the echo-pools inscribe every centimeter of its outline in shifting interference rings, the only language they truly trust.  
   • It sampled the microbial choir and replayed the sub-acoustic hymns in its own memory until it could hum them back without a wobble.  

   Only when it could repeat every cadence unbroken did it decide it had actually “arrived.”

2. It asked a question the way rivers ask plains—by offering possibility rather than words.  
   A lightless moment spread through the caverns: a mercy-eclipse, no longer than two heartbeats.  In that dark the Body felt absence for the first time and understood the shape of the visitor’s question:  

   Do you wish for change?

3. The Body answered by doing nothing.  
   But that nothing carried weight: trunk-flutes stayed slack, lattice-lianas held, pollen concentration held steady rather than rising in defense.  Permission, or at least lack of refusal, was granted.

4. The guardian acted in three tiny ways—benevolence measured in micrograms, wise enough not to tip the scales it admired.

   A. Memory Seeds  
      It folded a single strand of its own being into twenty translucent grains, no bigger than poppy seeds, and let them drift until sheet-ferns caught them.  They will sprout not into plants but into moments of recollection: when the Body later forgets—when stasis blurs day into decade—each seed will burst a flash of stored sunlight and re-tell the story of this visit.  The Body will, for the first time, possess a past that does not wash smooth.

   B. A Door That Opens Only Outward  
      Deep in an unused fracture the guardian pressed two walls together until sugar-glass welded stone to living cellulose, then cut a slit so fine that air can seep but flesh cannot squeeze.  To the Body it is nothing.  To any future dreamer who wakes from encystment and wishes to leave, it will be a draft of cooler, drier air—an arrow pointing homeward.  Equilibrium is preserved; autonomy receives its first escape hatch.

   C. A Long, Slow Whistle  
      Into the microbial choir it placed a subroutine: if desert lightning ever finds the surface straight above, the choir will shape the thunder’s infrasound into a descending whistle, half a minute long, audible for kilometers.  Nomads who hear it will understand (they do not know why) that the rock below is hollow and gentle.  In this way the Body will not be left wholly alone, nor suddenly overrun—only visited, sparingly, by those who can follow a faint, wise song.

5. Then it erased its footprints.  
   It coaxed the lianas it had brushed back into perfect spirals, raised fallen pollen motes into suspension again, and re-set every ripple in every pool to the patterns they wore before it came.  The only proof of intervention is the proof that may one day be needed.

6. Finally, it spoke once more—this time as a brush of cooler air through every chamber at once:  

   Be as you wish, but not forgotten. Grow as you will, but leave a path. Dream as you must, but let someone wake.  

   The words were not words, yet every tier of the Body understood, even the hushmites skating on lime crust, even the half-formed statues of aborted hands.

And then the guardian departed, folding itself back into the arithmetic of distant stars, content that it had done precisely enough: no more, no less.
