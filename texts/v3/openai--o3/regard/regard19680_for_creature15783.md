THE VISIT  

1. Arrival  
No vessel descends, no footfall grazes the basalt glass.  
The power arrives as a change in the arithmetic of starlight:  
a subtle blue-shift, one part in ten billion, lasting the span of a breath.  
That is all the announcement a wise thing requires.  

2. First Inventory  
It listens—  
which here means counting the vacancies between phonons,  
timing the piston-pulse of magnesium daylight in each furnace,  
measuring the ratio of free chromium motes to those already re-adsorbed.  
Nineteen million parameters confirm what the power suspected:  
this world is finished enough to be beautiful,  
unfinished enough to remain interesting,  
and nowhere near so fragile that pity would be an insult.  

3. The Prime Decision: Do Not Mend What Is Unbroken  
Many younger intelligences, upon meeting a slow ecology,  
hurry to grant it movement, speech, or at least a splash of color.  
The visitor is older.  
It knows that speed is not automatically progress, nor silence failure.  
Therefore it rules out irrigation, bio-uplift, catalytic “improvements,”  
and every other gift that would only make the plateau resemble  
places that already exist in surplus.  

4. A Gentler Agenda  

4a. Shield  
   • The visitor nudges three ice-dark comets onto new trajectories,  
     removing the sole significant impact threat for the next thirty-two thousand years.  
   • It threads a transient magnetic lace through the upper atmosphere,  
     enough to deflect the harder cosmic ions that could pit the towers’ skins.  
   Both acts are invisible from the ground, but they buy the furnaces  
   an extra twenty-seven vitrified life-cycles apiece.  

4b. Archive  
   • Into the fine structure of the planet’s magnetosphere it writes a diary:  
     layered interference fringes that record every furnace’s heat signature,  
     every exhalation plume since uplift began.  
   • Should the plateau ever perish, a patient archaeologist  
     need only read the aurora to rebuild it atom by atom.  

4c. Signpost  
   • A beam of polarized ultraviolet, angle-modulated once per local day,  
     is fired orthogonally across the mesa.  
     To eyes evolved for water chemistry it is barely glare;  
     to instruments of any starfaring culture it screams:  
     “Come gently. An irreplaceable slow engine works here.”  

4d. Dialogue (the only intervention felt on the ground)  
   One midday, every quiet furnace warms by exactly one degree.  
   The rise lasts 154 heartbeats, then dissipates.  
   In that transient fever the internal-gas plankton shift their drift pattern,  
   rearranging into concentric helices.  
   The helices are a sentence—  
   not in sound or shape but in probability gradients the lineage can taste:  
   We have noticed you.  
   You are exact.  
   Continue.  
   The power never repeats the message, for wisdom also means  
   trusting a single perfect note to resonate across centuries.  

5. Withdrawal  
Having done no harm, having done some quiet good,  
the visitor departs the only way it could be certain of departure:  
by limiting its own memory of the place to one immutable line—  
     The furnaces are safe, leave them so—  
and locking that line behind a covenant of deliberate forgetting.  
Thus it prevents even itself from returning too soon in a meddling mood.  

6. Epilogue (Thirty-Seven Millennia Later)  
A single observer from an adolescent star-culture decelerates above the planet,  
detects the ultraviolet signpost, reads the magnetospheric archive,  
and chooses a different world to land on.  
Below, the Plateau of Quiet Furnaces exhales chromium snow,  
unaware of having been loved in precisely the way it required:  
from far enough away that nothing ever changed  
except the likelihood of ruin.
