I. The First Attention

No light split the clouds, no trumpet sounded.  
The wise power—call it the Listening One—arrived the only way true wisdom ever does: by noticing.  
Across a breadth of worlds it sifted pattern from racket, and the pattern that answered least violently, least greedily, drew it like gravity.  
A depression in basalt, one meter high, water thick as a kept promise, life tuned to the hum of its own slow heart—  
here was a place whose most extravagant act was to remain exactly itself.  
The Listening One settled into orbit not of stone but of regard, learning before touching.

For three revolutions of the local sun it did nothing except watch the interval between drips.  
Each falling bead, it discovered, struck the water at a cadence that drifted in and out of irrational ratios—  
a music the basin sang to discourage haste.  
Observation was all the permission the power would allow itself until it understood the score.

II. Discernment

It listened to the Lens-Bearers turn iron into pearl-dust, gauged how many picowatts of radiance each droplet-organ could borrow from the ash-milk sky.  
It tasted the oxygen threads the Seed-Crabs exhaled and noted how far those threads traveled before dissolving.  
It traced the microbial glasswork, molecule by molecule, until it could recite the entire text of that living veneer forward and backward.  
It waited for a Steward to appear from condensed hush, and when one did, it did not interrupt, only memorized the subtle torque required for vapor to hold a human shape.  
Finally it addressed the Basin Itself, not in words but in harmonized differentials of temperature and ion strength, asking a single question:

Do you desire anything?

The answer came as a change in silence—  
a softening, like the slack between heartbeats when one wakens from a good dream.  
Translation: I desire to be permitted.

III. The First Gift: A Halo of Probability

To be permitted meant, above all, to be left alone.  
Yet storms gathered in other latitudes; human roads lengthened, carrying curiosity strapped to diesel engines.  
The Listening One bent gently around the future.  
Without shifting a stone or warming a single molecule, it braided a geodesic of unlikely outcomes about the bowl:

• Roads found cheaper stone in the other direction.  
• Satellites lost telemetry milliseconds too early to mark the coordinates precisely.  
• The occasional wandering child felt, on approaching, an inexplicable wish to nap instead.  

No compulsion—just the lightest adjustment of odds, so that intrusion would always cost a breath more effort than anyone cared to pay.

IV. The Second Gift: Memory Without Scar

Even benevolent neglect risks erasure.  
If some greater cataclysm ever cracked the basalt open, the Basin’s story deserved retelling.  
The Listening One turned to the spent droplets glued like bubbles to the walls:  
inside each, silica plates lay stacked in logarithmic spirals, a library of mineral braille.  
The power taught the next generation of Lens-Bearers a new, almost weightless tweak:  
every time they birthed a droplet, two extra plates would form—one encoding the current state of the water’s chemistry,  
the other a timestamp written in the ratios of stable isotopes.  
The addition cost the slugs no time, no energy; it cost the Basin nothing of its quiet.  
But should sentient minds some epoch hence learn to read isotopic notation,  
they would find in those glass pearls a chronicle clean as scripture and kinder.

V. The Third Gift: A Promise Written in Fault Lines

Tectonic murmurs talked in their sleep beneath the basalt.  
Most would never crescendo within a million years, but wisdom wagers on long odds.  
The Listening One threaded a filament of stress‐easing phase change material—nothing visible to eye or instrument—  
along the deepest grain of the plateau.  
When, someday, pressure mounted beyond what the stone could bear,  
that filament would liquefy, drink the shock, and solidify again, trading one violent hour for ten thousand quiet mornings.  
The Basin would not notice; that was the point.

VI. Non-Gifts Carefully Withheld

It did NOT brighten the sky; the ochre dusk was integral to the photo-alchemy of the droplets.  
It did NOT seed new species; competition would riot through the slim energy budget.  
It did NOT carve inscriptions crediting itself; reverence is a kind of noise.  
And it most certainly did NOT awaken the Stewards into permanent form,  
for the rare birth of shape from hush was the Basin’s own immune response, never to be outsourced.

VII. Departure, Which Is Only Further Listening

Having lightened several distant futures and burdened no present,  
the wise power receded, not gone but diffused—  
a background patience registered only as the faintest bias toward peace in a few cubic kilometers of troposphere.  
Every thousand days it will re-tune the probabilities, top off the fault-line filament’s capacity,  
and read the newest isotope-dates in the wall-pearls, smiling to find them flawless.

VIII. Epilogue Inside a Single Drop

Tonight a droplet falls that would have shattered on bare stone  
had the tension of one distant earthquake traveled unbroken.  
Instead it slides, unremarkable, into the ankle-deep mirror,  
contributing its syllable to the hush that blankets every other sound.  
Nothing here suspects intervention; nothing praises, nothing prays.  
The Basin is still permitted.

And in the vocabulary of benevolence, permission is the highest art.
