The power came without wings or thunder, for it had listened first.

Across a thousand lightless leagues of mantle and a dozen bright strata of sky it followed the basin’s chord until the vibration entered its own substance and rang there like an unanswered question.  When it arrived it did not enter as weather or beast or godhead; it bled itself down to something smaller than a grain of unmelted glass and settled inside the roar, that it might learn the roar properly.

For one full revolution of the planet it did nothing at all, only counted.

• It counted the sky-kites’ siphon breaths (always multiples of eleven).  
• It counted the heartbeats that thudded in 3,402 Enge throats (never once synchronizing, which pleased it).  
• It counted the occasions on which a mineral reed fractured and the aloe-sweet perfume escaped (7,221, though none of the Enge noticed more than two hundred).  

When it had satisfied itself that the place was complete in its own arithmetic, it sought the interstices—the almost-cracks, the places where fullness frayed.

There were three.

1. The Gap of Rest

Not a single Enge had ever known true silence.  Even in the womb the unborn floated in percussion.  Muscles slept half-tense, dreams were brief, and age stooped spines early.  

The power understood that to steal the roar would be to unmake the people: sound was the loom on which their minds were woven.  Yet a gift could be framed as an exception, not a subtraction.

On the underside of the oldest residential slab it condensed a cavity no bigger than the hollow inside a clasped hand.  Basalt there thinned to membrane; inside, the power pressed out the sound the way one might press water from cloth.  The cavity became an egg of perfect hush.

No Enge discovered it for seven seasons.  Then a pair of children at chase slipped between stilts and, by accident, placed their palms against the spot.  They felt their own pulse magnified into what seemed a festival drum.  Frightened and delighted, they told no one.  Word nonetheless traveled on the usual secret currents, and over years more found the mute niche.  Always a single visitor at a time, for the space admitted nothing else.  Those who emerged shared a new sign: thumb dragged slow across a sealed mouth—Keep.  

They kept it.  The wise power was satisfied.

2. The Gap of Color

Mirror hunger visited outsiders; now and then it visited the Enge as well, blooming into a private ache for hue neither dusk nor spore-glow could feed.  The power’s own vision spanned spectra the people would never name, but it understood appetite.

So it moved among the crystal reeds, chose one still tender with intracellular water, and altered two isotopes by a single neutron.  Henceforth, whenever lightning spidered inside the upper cloud (a wandering marvel seen perhaps twice each generation), that one reed would refract the strike down its length, splitting it into visible red—red as arterial blood, red as upland sumac—and seed the flash across a thousand other stalks.

The phenomenon lasted one heartbeat.  Then the spectrum folded back into bruise-green dusk, and the reeds resumed their pallid chiming.  

Witnesses disagreed whether the redness was real or dream, but they agreed on what to do: every birth after the first color-flash received a token shard of the parent reed’s shell, hung on a leather cord.  The children called it dawn-stone, though they had no dawn.  A promise, they said, that the basin itself might one day blush.  

They did not mine the reed; they only waited for natural fracture, collecting slivers as they fell.  Addition without replacement.  The power noted their restraint and was glad.

3. The Gap of Question

The Enge possessed history but not aspiration; they articulated tomorrow only as more of today.  That steadiness had kept them alive, yet the power sensed in several of the youngest a restless shimmer—an energy with no vector.

Instead of revelation, it chose invitation.

Deep beneath the tessellation of slabs lay a magma tongue still sliding slow as scripture.  The power brushed it with an idea: when the next vent cracked open, let it extrude a single column of obsidian shot through with vesicles the exact size of Enge fingertips.  Let the column resonate under touch, every pore chiming a discrete harmonic.

Months later the ground sighed and delivered the sculpture straight into the shallow water, upright, immaculate.  Children reached it first; their exploring taps spilled a scale of notes no Basin Shard-drum had ever yielded.  The elders arrived in alarm, prepared to condemn novelty, but the column was already ringing with the tonal map of the rules—Nothing changes, Everything is occupied, Addition without replacement—rendered in tempo and pitch too elegant to forbid.  

The elders listened.  They could not unhear the mirror that music held up.  Debate flowered, brief but fierce, and concluded in consensus: the rules stood, but the rules could be sung differently.

Within a season a new raft of seven platforms was charted, not outward but upward, for the first time.  Only half a body-length above the last ring, supported by doubled stilts lest any space be stolen below, but upward nonetheless.  A punctuation mark, not an erasure.

The wise power observed the tremor of possibility ripple through Basin Sign, through lullaby, through infra-throated amphibian bellow, and judged the weight of its own intervention.

Enough, it decided.

So it withdrew to the place between cloud layers, where reflected light wanders directionless.  There it keeps watch, neither custodian nor absentee, waiting to sense the next almost-crack where gift may be placed like a seed in soft sediment—never overturning, only turning the already full toward forms of fullness they had not yet imagined.
