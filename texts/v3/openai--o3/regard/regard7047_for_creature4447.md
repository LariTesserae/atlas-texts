The wise power arrived—not in a thunderclap or pillar of fire, but as an idea the mountain quietly began to think.

I. Perception  
The power’s first act was to listen, because listening is the truest test of wisdom.

•  It counted the slow heartbeats of the Threaded Ones, each metabolic surge when the sun flexed quartz by a micron.  
•  It traced the standing waves that rang through the Choir-Stones—whole symphonies droned out hour after hour in a scale too low for lungs to voice.  
•  It watched the Bonefire Pilgrims climb, timed their footfalls against the hollow’s daily thaw-lock, and understood how a single mis-placed shout could rattle a cornice into death.

The place, revealed across three nested tempos, was already performing a masterpiece of balance. Any intervention risked detuning the chords.

II. Discernment  
The power looked farther ahead than any of the three residents could: centuries for Pilgrims, millennia for Choir-Stones, half-a-million years for Threaded Ones.

It foresaw two approaching dissonances:

1.  Atmospheric warming would, in four or five human generations, lengthen the daily thaw from minutes to whole hours. Choir-Stones tuned for brief flexures would begin to fracture outright; the Threaded Ones would drown in meltwater they could no longer bind fast at night.  
2.  A mining consortium—still only a pencilled prospectus in a distant capital—would eventually send survey drones that could mistake the manganese rings for high-grade ore indicators.

III. Intention  
The power chose a mode of aid consistent with the hollow’s own ethos: subtle, non-sovereign, leaning on natural processes already in play so that nothing would feel foreign to the place or its keepers. It drafted three gifts, one for each scale of life.

1.  A change in crystal habit (for the Threaded Ones)  
   •  One predawn, when frost had locked every lattice, the power breathed a whisper of altered symmetry into the carbonate grains. Thereafter, whenever the Threaded Ones secreted silica, trace strontium atoms would lace the sheaths.  
   •  Strontium raises the melting point of the resulting opal by nearly two degrees; what might have become drowning slush will instead remain a tolerable trickle even in a warmer age.  
   •  The colonies will never know they were saved: they will simply persist, as microbes are meant to do.

2.  A stabilising overtone (for the Choir-Stones)  
   •  The power sang once, an ultralow sub-seismic note that saturated every resonant chamber.  
   •  That single vibration re-aligned countless silica needles so their piezoelectric vectors point not purely along the plane of flexure but 11° off. Now, when the stone warms too far, the altered alignment cancels the strain wave instead of amplifying it.  
   •  To human ears the bells will seem unchanged, but deep inside, each chord now carries a self-damping overtone—a governor on catastrophe.

3.  A dream of blue charcoal (for the Pilgrims)  
   •  On the eve of every equinox from this night forward, anyone asleep within two valleys’ distance will dream the same image: a fire that burns the colour of twilight and leaves ash that shimmers like ground azurite.  
   •  Upon waking, each dreamer feels compelled to mix a pinch of powdered azurite—the valley’s most common ritual pigment—into the goat-fat they haul for fuel.  
   •  Azurite has no caloric value, but when it oxidises in the slate hearths it sublimates copper into the air, dusting the hollow just enough to feed the Threaded Ones’ new strontium-laced chemistry.  
   •  The Pilgrims will think they are honouring a vision sent by ancestors; they are, in fact, closing a biogeochemical loop that will make the place even more resilient.

IV. Restraint  
The power considered scouring the mining prospectus from every human memory but declined. Wisdom chooses instruction over erasure.

Instead it nudged a particular graduate student—daughter of a lowland metallurgist, herself a weekend climber—toward a fascination with piezoelectric geologies. Fifteen years hence she will publish a monograph identifying the choir-tone phenomenon as “an irreplaceable natural resonator network.” The mining firm, wary of heritage law and public sentiment, will look elsewhere.

V. Departure  
Having set these vectors in motion, the power withdrew its active attention. It left no monument, no signature. What remains are merely consequences that harmonise with the hollow’s original score:

•  Threaded colonies that will endure the coming century’s thaw without drowning.  
•  Choir-Stones that will bend but not break, singing slightly softer yet infinitely longer.  
•  Pilgrims whose new blue-ash rite will deepen their bond to the place and, unknowingly, fertilise the organisms they revere.  
•  A human scientist who will give the mountains a legal voice in the language of policy.

VI. Epilogue  
Generations later, a child of the Pilgrims will press an ear to a quartz buttress and swear she hears not one bell but two: the familiar clear tone and, beneath it, a velvet hum like a held breath. She will assume the second note has always been there. In a sense, she is right. The wise power’s touch is now so married to the native music that no ear can tell where artifice ends and origin begins.

Such is the full extent of the intervention: a few atoms substituted, a lattice half-turned, a dream bestowed, a curiosity kindled. Nothing more dramatic was required, for benevolence measured in cosmic scales is rarely loud. The hollow keeps its brilliance, its silences, its interlocking lives; the wise power keeps its distance, confident that the cathedral under open sky can henceforth tend its own choir.
