When the Power—call it the Listener, for names are only angles on the same light—first leaned its attention toward the still-growing mountain, it did not descend like a meteor or blaze like a saint. It arrived the way a tuning fork arrives in a silent room: by letting every particle feel, for half a breath, the possibility of a different vibration.

1. Reading the Measure  
• The Listener caught the plateau’s pulse, sensed the kink that held every process inches from completion.  
• It heard the stalled engines beneath the crust, tasted the coppery breath of the organ-trees, and watched the infinity-loop of droplet, fern-spore, half-beat wing.  
• Nothing here suffered, yet nothing here finished. The place was a chrysalis that had refused the risk of eclosion.

2. First Impulse: To Leave Untouched  
Benevolence begins with restraint. The Listener considered the merits of non-interference:  
• The enclave was internally perfect; its unbroken repetition kept every resident safe from decay.  
• Ending the stasis outright would be like tearing gauze from a healing wound—too sudden, too sure of its own righteousness.  
So the Listener settled by the rim, silent, and let three full revolutions of the world spin past (or their stalled equivalents) while it simply watched.

3. Discovery: The Hidden Cost  
With patience came understanding. Beneath the symmetry lay an unnoticed exhaustion:  
• Ratchet-swifts, heavier with each micron of mineral, had begun to spiral lower, their stutter-flight no longer enough to stay aloft.  
• Turbine beetles twitched more erratically; vent-grid patterns showed widening gaps, pressure spiking beneath the basalt shingles.  
• Mirror Children’s faces grew faint, as if the psychic reservoir they siphoned from visitors now ran low.  
A system that never finishes also never renews. Entropy was seeping in through the very perfection meant to bar it.

4. The Listener’s Decision: A Gentle Valve  
What could be offered without unmaking the plateau’s identity? The Listener chose a compromise:  
• Not completion, but permission for micro-completion—tiny, periodic releases so small the enclave’s logic would read them as background noise.  
• A heartbeat needs diastole as well as systole; the Listener would teach the mountain to exhale.

5. The Three Gifts  

Gift One: The Fractional Second  
• Into every seventeenth pulse of the buried piston, the Listener wove an almost-imperceptible slack: one ten-millionth of ordinary time, enough for a droplet to finish its fall before the cycle reset.  
• The fallen droplet shattered on basalt, releasing ions the moss had not tasted in a geologic moment. Moss brightened; beetles drank the new chemistry and clicked a cleaner rhythm.

Gift Two: The Slip-Feather  
• For each ratchet-swift, the Listener grew a single unseen barb at the center of one wing. In the frozen mid-stroke that barb completed its motion, granting the bird a full, true beat once every hour.  
• The swifts rose higher, shedding excess metal in a silver snow that the organ-trees inhaled as trace nutrient. Sparks inside the trunks flared a healthier gold.

Gift Three: The Forgetting Pool  
• One mirror pool was deepened by a finger-breadth. Shockingly deep, for this world. When thoughts echoed too long in a visitor’s skull, the reflection could now reach up, skim the surplus idea, and drown it—peacefully, without erasure of self.  
• The Mirror Children glowed like moonlit fish beneath the thicker water, content and bright-eyed again.

6. Safeguards  
Because outside interference might still rupture the design, the Listener braided two wards around the rim:  
• A Veil of Minor Misfortune—boots lose tread, compasses misbehave, cameras fog—enough to turn the merely curious back.  
• A Whisper of Invitation for the Worthy—those who need the plateau’s blueprint clarity still find the path, and now leave without psychic bruising.

7. Outcome Over One Human Generation  
Season by human season the enclave adjusted:  
• Pressure vents fell back into perfect tessellation; beetles could finally close their shells before opening anew.  
• Organ-trees pushed a single pod past the point of almost-release; it tore free, drifted three inches, then paused in its own pocket of half-time—an heirloom waiting for the far future.  
• No climber since the Listener’s visit has reported the old mind-clang stubbornly following them down the slope. They return with blueprints of thought still luminous but no longer crippling.

8. What the Plateau Learned  
Sentience, if diffused through stone and vapor, still learns. The young mountain realised:  
• Completion is not the enemy; unmetered completion is.  
• A system may stay itself and yet cycle small endings into small beginnings.

9. What the Listener Learned  
Every world is an instrument; some are simply tuning. You do not smash a violin because the strings are tight—you turn the pegs until tension sings.

10. Epilogue: Possible Futures  
• Millennia hence, when continental drift hauls the range into warmer sky, the plateau may accept a longer fraction of time, allowing saplings to drop leaves, beetles to die and molt, perhaps even the first irreversible footstep of an emergent Keeper in full daylight.  
• Or it may choose eternal adolescence, content now that micro-completions bleed off entropy. The Listener is satisfied either way.

And so the benevolent and wise power did very little—one flicker in a pulse, one feather’s grace note, one deeper breath of water—yet that little was exactly the width between a crystal poised forever in growth and a crystal that keeps growing, facet by facet, toward a form no benevolence would dare predict.
