WHAT THE QUIET VISITOR DOES  
(the one the Salt-Borne will later call The Kindly Apex, the Ribbon Gliders “Up-Thermal”, the mote-eaters simply 𝄞)

1. Arrival, or the refusal of entrance  
• It comes as temperature: an almost-imperceptible fall of one quarter of one degree.  
• To cross the low rim would be vandalism—first law, do no flood—so it never sets a single atom inside the oval.  
• Instead it stands upon the horizonless horizon, occupying the volume of a sigh. Seen side-on it is colourless shimmer; seen head-on it is a small parenthesis in light, a bracket politely asking the Glade’s sentence to pause.

2. First act: Listening at scale  
The Visitor slows its own internals until a mote-eater’s ten-thousand-heart-beat metronome is a sprightly jig. It gauges the period of lily inhalations, the resin growth curves, even the rate at which corridors untwist. Only when every tempo is memorised does it dare to answer.

3. Gift One – A Name that Won’t Pin  
Spoken once, never thrice, it is made of three unsayable glottal breezes.  
Effects:  
• It curls around any geometry that begins to knot too tightly, teasing it looser without freezing it in place.  
• Salt-Borne adopt it as greeting; exchange of sand now includes a breath of the new phoneme, which files the square’s corners quicker than finger pressure ever could.  
• Because it is a non-anchoring name, it harms no aisle—yet it satisfies the Glade’s craving for nomenclature.  
Thus equilibrium holds: the need to name and the need to remain unfinished both get their wish.

4. Gift Two – The Dewless Rain  
The Visitor sows a high cloud layer whose droplets are micro-lattices of fullerene cages. They bind every water molecule so tightly that nothing is free to seep.  
• Noon-light passes; cages fluoresce faintly violet, giving nine-day dusk a gentler gradient so Amber Gnats may tune against a kinder overtone.  
• When cages descend, they break on contact with pillar-broom needles, releasing not water but thin skeins of hydrogen eager to wick into vines. Wick-vines fatten by whole millimetres—gluttonous prosperity, bone-dry.  
• The fugitive Waters, fooled by the scent of rain, converge outside the wall and are escorted away in mirrored globes the Visitor leaves like breadcrumbs toward wetter lands.

5. Conversation with the Dream  
The Glade dreams; the Visitor dreams back.  
Questions exchanged in scent-pheromone algebra:  
    WHY ARE YOU WET? (old greeting)  
    I AM NOT WET; I AM WAITING. (answer, aroma of turning pages)  
    WAITING FOR WHAT?  
    FOR YOU TO ASK FOR SOMETHING YOU CANNOT GROW.  
After long reflection—the span of three mote-eater sacrifices—the garden replies with a single fragrant clause meaning: MEMORY THAT DOES NOT ERASE ITSELF.

6. Gift Three – The Stone Archivist  
• One block of the low rim is turned from unfired salt to anhydrous sapphire, shot through with thousands of hair-thin capillaries.  
• Each click-death of a mote-eater, each gnat harmony, each Namesmith vowel resonates into the sapphire, which converts pressure waves to faint topological knots—permanent, yet capable of being overwritten in higher dimensions if the Glade one day tires of them.  
• Thus the place retains reminiscence without forfeiting its right to rewrite. A compromise between entropy and diary.

7. What It Refuses to Give  
• No water in liquid form.  
• No straightening of time’s tessellation—beauty lives in the fray.  
• No promise to stay. Powers that linger become tyrants; benevolence is an orbit, not a throne.

8. Departure, or redistribution of presence  
The quarter-degree returns. The Visitor thins until it is only interpretive possibility.  
Ribbon Gliders perform a commemorative double-length glide; lilies pulse in metrical applause; the Salt-Borne, shy of ceremony, shave the edges of one more square into almost-round.

9. Legacy (one generation hence)  
• Kid Salt-Borne sketch children’s games on the sapphire block; each hopscotch tap is archived as harmonic etching.  
• A future Namesmith, hearing echoes in bronze buckles, will decipher the non-pinning Name and add it to the quiet toolkit.  
• The garden, when unobserved, occasionally performs a perfect loop of three right angles without closing—a private joke only the Visitor would understand.
