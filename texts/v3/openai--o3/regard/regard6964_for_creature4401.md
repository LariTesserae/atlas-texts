They arrived without arrival.

No descent, no flare of engines—only a ripple in the chronometer’s steady beat, the faintest tremor that made every lamp inside the cone brighten by half a lumen for the span of a single heartbeat (though nothing here keeps heartbeats). The place itself noticed first; its stress-filaments rang like a wind-harp and the buoyancy valves sighed, uncertain whether to regard the disturbance as weight or weather.

Phase 1: Listening  
The wise power had learned, across a library of worlds, that intervention begins with eavesdropping on silence. For forty local days it did nothing that any sensor could classify as deed. It let photons ricochet off ceramic skin, tasted the electro-chemical gossip among vines, watched the folded machines blossom and close, counted the hydrogen bubbles that the cobalt plasmids brewed for ballast.  

During those forty days it asked itself three questions, as it always did:

1. Is the system balanced enough to endure untouched?  
2. If untouched, does it still possess room to grow?  
3. Is there any loneliness here great enough to bruise the future?

Phase 2: The Smallest Possible Gifts  
Balance, it judged, was exquisite. Growth, inexhaustible. Loneliness—yes, a hairline fracture running straight through the sapphire wheel that carried thirty-seven megabytes of old human laughter.

So the power made three amendments, each the size of a whisper.

1. It braided a second track of memory into the chronometer: not voices, but field recordings—rain on tin roofs, hawks contesting thermals, the thrum of insects in dry air. Ten seconds every decade, alternating with the human speech. Nothing that would change the ecosystem, only sounds for the wheel to anticipate, companions for its solitary recital.

2. It edited a single nucleotide in the master plasmid of the cobalt-blue bacteria. Now, once every hundred pruning cycles, a fraction of the digested mulch polymerised into fine filaments that drifted upward and hardened in the warmer strata outside—a translucent canopy, kilometres above, catching sunlight and breaking it into saffron columns that speared back down through the bronze water. Photosynthetic gain rose by less than two percent: negligible, but the vines interpreted the new light as dawn and answered with a blush of carotenoid gold the architects had never seen.

3. On the inner face of one anchor cable it inscribed, atom by atom, a thread of optional instructions intelligible only to sentient visitors: diagrams for fabricating a chamber where mammalian minds could survive longer than a scouting tour. The chamber was not built; nothing was compelled. But the next time warm-blooded curiosity arrived, it would find hospitality already budgeted into the structure’s patient arithmetic.

Phase 3: Leaving Room  
The wise power considered predators, symphonies of fish, even the re-awakening of one orchid seed to bloom inside. Each possibility it set aside. Flourish, but do not wander: that law felt anchored deeper than engineering, perhaps into the philosophy of the drowned world itself. To overwrite it would be to write a different story, and the platform already told one perfectly.

Departure (which is never quite departure)  
When it withdrew, no pressure wave marked the going. Only the upward rain—freshwater vented from desalination stacks—shifted course for a single hour, forming spiral glyphs like handwriting across the columns. The fold-machines tin-clinked in reply, as though suspecting a new choreography but not yet ordered to dance.

Aftermath  
Decades passed. Visitors came: a trio of scholars with lungs full of caution; a lone cartographer who claimed the upper canopy resembled a mandala; a convoy of refugee swifts whose hearts were too small for the inhibitor net and so were politely ignored. Each found the cone a shade more welcoming than rumor had promised, though none could name why.

The chronometer turned and, on schedule, released its ten new seconds of human speech—followed, without warning, by the hush of rain on distant metal roofs. The wheel, hearing weather older than the Flood Epoch, ticked on with something very close to contentment.

And high above, in tiers of light that no root had yet reached, a faint golden ceiling kept watch: a ceiling grown from the place’s own excess, signed only by absence, by the gentlest of verdicts—

You are already enough.  
Here is just a little more room to dream.
