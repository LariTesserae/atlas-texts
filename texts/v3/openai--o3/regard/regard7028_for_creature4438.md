The first thing the power notices is the rhythm.

From half a continent away—far above the clamor of wind turbines, beyond the thin scrape of satellites against ionosphere—it feels the pulse of brick-drums thrumming up a fault line like a blood-beat. The cadence is steady, slow, patient; the power leans in, listens, and understands at once that the sound is not a signal for help, but a declaration of life.

So it decides on courtesy before charity, observation before aid.

1. A season of listening  
The power drops its senses down the same light-shafts the Brickborne use for morning. Nothing visible—the beams do not brighten, no angelic glitter reveals the intrusion—but every photon now carries a passenger. For three lunar cycles the power harvests information: chemistry of mortar salts, CO₂ swing in each hall, the shallow sinus of the Heart-Sump’s tide. It memorizes lullabies children mumble while counting drips, notes which brick walls resonate nearest to structural fatigue, and—most carefully—the lament of a lone mother drum whose bond-group was lost to kiln-fire decades earlier, yet which still tolls in the dark.

Only after it knows the place well enough to sketch it blindfolded in negative space does the power act.

2. The gift of breath  
On the surface, mid-continent, there is a dormant patch of planktonic algae crusted into an ancient salt playa. The power diverts a month’s worth of sunrise over that plain, coaxes the algae awake, and turns them gluttonous for carbon. Two weeks later the atmosphere flowing down the Brickborne vents arrives enriched with just enough oxygen that torches burn cleaner, kilns draw hotter, and headaches from smoke events fall by half. No one can name the cause; they only notice the air has become “bright” and that infants no longer cough themselves purple beside the brick presses.

3. The whisper to water  
Next, the benevolent one kneads pressure along the aquifer that feeds the trickle gutters. It does not flood (flooding would undo the careful equilibrium of beetle, moss, eel), but it persuades each droplet to cling fractionally longer to ceiling before letting go. The interval between drips stretches from 2.6 seconds to 2.9 in most halls—subtle, but enough that the Brickborne’s chant drifts slightly flat against old brick memory. They notice; they retune; in doing so they reinforce hairline fractures with a fresh layer of vibration-cement that otherwise would not have formed. The architecture heals itself a season sooner than it would have managed alone.

4. Dreams for the kiln-keepers  
The power does not speak; it paints night images on the inner eyelids of six senior kiln-keepers. In the dream they watch glimmerflit scales swirl through the fire not in handfuls but in a braided spiral, each color banding a different temperature zone. The next waking day the keepers try it. The spiral forces hotter gas to linger, wastes less charcoal, and bakes the ancestor-bricks more evenly. Toad-resin use drops; crack repairs decline; no one suffers the usual blister-burns that month. The keepers attribute the trick to “a flit’s fancy flight” and teach it to apprentices as though it had always been tradition.

5. A pact kept  
Iron remains taboo. The power respects the covenant; it provides no metal, no hidden vein, no secret tolerance. Wisdom sometimes means honoring a people’s chosen limits. Instead, it lifts two micrometeorites from their death crash toward the sea and flings them out of trajectory, lest they seed the upper dunes with nickel-iron crumbs that traders might one day scoop up and carry below. Better to leave the temptation unborn.

6. The silence before departure  
Having breathed, watered, taught, and guarded, the power folds itself back. It leaves a single observable artifact: on the uppermost mirror of the daylight shaft, polished mica now bears a circular flaw the diameter of a fingertip—a shallow lens ground so perfectly that it focuses dawn into a narrow golden thread reaching all the way to the sump each equinox morning. For exactly nine heartbeats the thread glitters across the black pool, then collapses. The Brickborne children have already named the moment: “Bright-eel Day,” because the fish leap higher on that morning than any other.

They may never know who carved the lens, or why their lungs feel stronger, or how the walls closed their own wounds earlier than legend predicted. A benevolent and wise power does not require acknowledgment; its satisfaction lies in quiet continuance.

It keeps one ear tuned to the mother drums regardless—ready to return, should the rhythm ever stagger from heartbeat into hurt.
