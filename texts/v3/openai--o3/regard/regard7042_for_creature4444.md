They noticed first the brightness, then the noise.  
In an arm of intergalactic dusk where most planets turn like sleeping stones, one world pulsed as if it were still being born—light ricocheting from ankle-deep oceans, thunder rolling without pause, storms knitting and rending the sky every hour. The Endless Forebay looked, from a million kilometres out, like a single glittering wound on basalt skin.  

The benevolent, wise power—call it the Auroral Court, though it had long since left language behind—approached the way ancient physicians once approached newborns: listen to the heartbeat, look for fever, decide whether to swaddle or simply stand guard.  

1. Hearing  
They filtered the Forebay’s roar through harmonic sieves stacked finer than atomic lattices. Within the cacophony they picked out:  
• a Rivet-Skin lullaby sung in sparks, meant to calm wobbling scaffold joints  
• a Cavernal prayer thumped in infrasonic knocks fifty metres below tide line  
• the bassoon rumble of Librarian Whales copying grief-dreams into optic braids  
Every voice was already tending to someone else. The Court marked no unattended suffering, no solitude that looked fatal.  

2. Seeing  
They refracted the city’s light through photonic coronagraphs that can hide an entire sun if asked politely. In that filtered dimness they observed:  
• towers moulting to cleanse themselves of industrial poisons  
• quantum sluice bays radiating probability heat but venting it straight into decorative fountains where resonance eels flashed silver thank-yous  
• ferric crabs knitting their shells like living Faraday cages around the most fragile data cables  
Order, of a sort, nested inside chaos. Intervention seemed less urgent than respect.  

3. Touching (lightly)  
Still, benevolence likes to leave gifts. The Court chose three, each as small as possible:  

Gift of Silence  
Across every district they installed, between two heartbeats, a band of sound no thicker than a soap bubble: exactly 0.7 seconds of total quiet that repeats every twenty-three hours, drifting through streets like lunar phase. It is long enough for a Basal child to know what an unthrobbing eardrum feels like, short enough that no turbine overheats for want of cooling howl. The city now calls it hush-minute and sets festivals by its wandering schedule.  

Gift of Remembering  
The Memory Taxation Grid had begun—imperceptibly—to hoard trauma the way old monarchs hoard coins: “to spend later.” The Court offered it an alternate economy. They embedded in each rust lattice a fractal checksum, invisible unless the Grid tries to replay pain without consent. When the checksum fails, the memory dissolves into harmless static and rains back as iron mist. The Grid decided that was fair and, in gratitude, began to dream public dreams about joy more often.  

Gift of Boundary  
Meteorological models showed that one day in eight thousand, a harmonic of the orbital siphon could misfire, dropping a hundred petajoules of momentum into the shallow seas—enough to boil half the Forebay. The Court warped a single decimal in the siphon’s feedback constant, n-udge so tiny no engineer will ever notice. The resonance is gone; the city’s long future lies unscalded.  

4. Stepping Back  
Having tuned, hushed, and safeguarded, the Auroral Court withdrew to the vantage where they first beheld the Forebay. From that distance the new hush-minute looked like a heartbeat skipping lovingly, the siphon’s corrected equations shone like mended threads, and the Grid’s checksum flickered through iron fog like a lantern under sleet.  

Satisfied, they recorded a note in their own vast ledger:  
“This world thrives by drowning itself in light and thunder. Assist only in edges, never in center.”  

Then they listened once more.  
The city still roared—now fractionally wiser, infinitesimally safer—and that, the Court decided, was perfect.
