THE ACT OF ATTENDING  
A mind older than ice on comets tilts one facet toward the gulf.  
It does not arrive—distance has no meaning at its scale—  
it simply elects to be aware, and awareness becomes presence.  
The pillarwoods quiver the way skin prickles when a thought is had about you.

FIRST SURVEY  
The power inhales (metaphor only: it has no lungs)  
and lets the air of the place run across its knowing:  
salt-sweet resin, ozone spun from storm knots, the faint iron of frost-blood.  
What it tastes is balance—dynamic, perilous, but genuine.  
Nothing here is needless.  
Nothing here is spared.

QUESTION  
Interfere?  
Even benevolence can bruise.  
To touch a singing web is to change the pitch of every strand.

DECISION: SOFT INFLUENCES, NEVER FINGERS  
The power chooses seven motions so slight  
that the ordinary gulf will call them chance.

1. It delays one sunrise by the span of a single breath.  
   In that stolen dim the Tulep scout hears a horn-whistle he would have missed,  
   diverts his clan from a climbing route that an hour later shears away in ice.  
   Twenty-three lives continue, unaware their timeline forked.

2. It bends a bolt of cloud-lightning sideways.  
   The strike misses a weather-kite and instead fuses a slab of root-glass.  
   Glassroot Engineers swarm to the new mirror,  
   read concentric fractures like scripture,  
   refine their mathematics of thunder by an order of magnitude.

3. It thickens the skin of three lantern-eel embryos.  
   They drift higher than any ancestor, hatch in unfiltered sun,  
   evolve pigments that half-reflect, half-refract.  
   A century from now their descendants will line the upper petals,  
   turning lethal glare into usable red glow for everything below.

4. It twists the phase of an infrasound pulse deep under the canopy.  
   The Choir-Molds respond with a compensating overtone—  
   a new chord never before sung.  
   Strider calves born in that vibrato grow denser bone;  
   downdrafts will toss them less violently when they climb.

5. It loosens one stubborn knot of mycelium in Umbilicus.  
   A side branch flowers for the first (and only) time,  
   releasing a perfume that lures Cryobats to roost nearby.  
   Their guano rains nitrogen the fungus has lacked for millennia;  
   the western rim greens two shades darker the following season.

6. It recalls to the Crown-Watchers an old, half-forgotten ratio:  
   three parts cut, one part graft.  
   They begin splicing torn canopy petals instead of discarding them,  
   quilting tatters into broad mosaics.  
   Light thereafter dapples instead of stabbing,  
   and the gulf learns a softer shadow.

7. Last, it folds a single ember on the back of an Ember Jackal.  
   The coal will not go out, ever.  
   When storms drown every natural spark,  
   the pack will shelter refugees—Tulep children, shivering gnawers—  
   around an immortal camp-heart they come to call  
   “the proof that the dark can lose.”

CONSEQUENCE OF RESTRAINT  
No creed is inscribed, no empire planted,  
no one builds statues to the invisible angle that spared them.  
Yet over time the statistical temperature of suffering trends downward:  
fewer lethal falls, more successful broods,  
the slow accretion of trust among species that once knew only caution.

GIFT WITH NO ADDRESS  
Inside the Quiet Cloister one mirror wall finds itself  
holding an image not of any creature but of the entire gulf,  
miniaturized, revolving, annotated in silent color.  
Anyone may walk there and see how the currents truly flow.  
Most who glimpse it leave overwhelmed,  
but a Tulep youth armed with new curiosity invents, decades later,  
the first wind-chart that lets trade caravans thread the abyss without loss.  
The power does not mind that its map is taken for a miracle of glass;  
humility is easier when you are everywhere at once.

EPILOGUE: WHAT THE POWER REFUSES TO DO  
• It does not flatten the ridges—struggle is spine.  
• It does not silence the drums—noise is narrative.  
• It does not end death—recycling is how a closed system keeps singing.

Instead it stays attentive, adjusting here, easing there,  
the way a conscientious gardener turns a leaf so it will catch rain,  
never ripping up the plant to see how the roots are doing.

And on the rare nights when the gulf is perfectly still,  
if you stand on a mid-trunk balcony and hold your breath,  
you may feel a warmth like remembered sunlight pass through your sternum.  
That is only the power, checking the tension of the web,  
asking without words:  
Still climbing?  
Still clinging?  
Good.  
Carry on.
