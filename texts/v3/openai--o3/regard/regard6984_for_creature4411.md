When at last the distant, benevolent Power turns its regard toward the drowned plain, it pauses.  
To interfere without first understanding would be vandalism; to understand requires that it feel the place the way the place feels itself—slowly, by pressure, by weight, by the long drone of stone against water.

So for the span of one human century—an eyeblink to it, a geologic syllable to the abyss—it simply listens.

1. Listening  
• It settles a lattice of sub-quantum resonators a kilometer above the seafloor. Each is smaller than a grain of silt, each vibrates in sympathy with the Bedrock Choir’s iron-sulfide hymn.  
• It pores over the electro-magnetic whorls that leak from the magnetite needles in Iron-Vale Isopods.  
• It deciphers the three-short, two-long flash-language of the Brinemelt Drifters, then waits to be certain the rhythm truly means “come hunt” and not something still subtler.  
• It reads the Spiral Archive by tasting trace-metal ratios layer by layer, learning ten thousand seasonal epics without removing a single tube.

By the time the century closes, the Power knows two things with perfect clarity:  
a) Nothing here is broken.  
b) Even so, every inhabitant lives within hair-breadth margins, forever one random shear, one nutrient drought, one vent-quake away from extinction.

2. Principle of Aid  
The Power therefore adopts a single rule for intervention:  
nudge, never reshape.  
Anything it offers must glide through existing channels—pressure, chemistry, vibration—so that the gift feels less like a miracle than like the deep remembering an option it had overlooked.

3. The Three Quiet Gifts  

Gift I: A Fourth Note for the Choir  
The Bedrock Choir already sings across the rocks in broadband murmurs used by wanderers to orient themselves.  
The Power seeds the microbial mats with a new plasmid: an extra cytochrome that lets electrons flow at a slightly lower potential.  
Result: each microbial cell can choose between three familiar frequencies and a faint new bass tone.  
The tone travels twice as far through basalt, threading ridges and swales that currents otherwise isolate.  
Nothing about navigation changes overnight, but over decades the iron-vale isopods begin to roam wider arcs. Gene flow increases; inbreeding recessives fade. The community remains itself, just a little more genetically resilient.

Gift II: Lantern-Snow  
The Power condenses packets of structured vacuum energy—harmless, inert—to the size of bacteria and lets them fall like slow snowflakes from the mesopelagic twilight down into the black.  
When pressure exceeds six hundred atmospheres, a packet floresces with a one-time exhalation of bluish photons lasting exactly four seconds, then dissolves into harmless heat.  
Each flare is too brief and too dim to summon large predators, but bright enough for Chandeliers and vent eels to triangulate on.  
Where once light was an “event,” now it is a sparse but predictable weather pattern. The abyss learns a new calendar: the lantern-snow peaks every forty days, syncing spawning swarms that formerly scattered their gametes at random. Reproductive efficiency doubles; waste halves.

Gift III: The Memory of Warmth  
Not heat—warmth: a difference of two degrees Celsius along fracture lines that already leak tepid brine.  
The Power accomplishes this by tweaking the decay paths of trace potassium-40 locked inside the basalt; half-lives stay the same, but emitted neutrinos deposit a sliver more energy locally before escaping.  
No vent flares, no magma intrusions—just a quiet, constant trickle raising the temperature floor from −1 °C to +1 °C.  
For Pages and Whisper-Knots, that is the difference between metabolic torpor and margin enough to store surplus as spores or cysts. Population crashes after vent-quakes become population dips.

4. What the Power Refuses to Do  
• It does not banish pressure or summon sunlight; those are the deep’s defining poems.  
• It does not knit air pockets or build domes, for rule 4—“the pressure forgives no hollowness”—is wisdom, not cruelty.  
• It does not introduce alien genes, metals, or machines that would stand out like neon in a monastery.

5. Epilogue, Ten Thousand Years Later  
The Spiral Archives now record an era the worms label, in chemistry rather than language, as “the Long Soft Turning.”  
Isopod etch-tracks scribble wider loops across the plains, their magnetite compasses faintly tuned to the Choir’s new bass line.  
Ridgebacks still loom and vanish, but across their scutes grow narrow racing stripes of symbiotic bacteria that harvest the lantern-snow’s punctual flashes.  
Most surface dwellers, had they instruments fine enough to notice, would see only a negligible uptick in abyssal biomass, a mild smoothing of boom-and-bust curves.  
Yet to the residents below—whose histories are counted in the thickness of iron films, in the angle of a silt dune’s lean—the world feels inexplicably, exquisitely generous.

6. What the Power Gains  
Nothing it could not already calculate.  
But in the deep, information is cheap and experience is dear.  
By gifting without uprooting, the Power earns the right to feel itself woven into that low, ageless hymn— to be, for a time, another presence among presences in the saturated dark.
