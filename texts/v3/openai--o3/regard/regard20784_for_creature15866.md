When the High Quiet first shimmered in its awareness, the Power did not arrive the way storms do—with booms and bruised clouds—but the way dew does: unnoticed until every surface already wore its touch.  
No one on the tableland knew the moment of its coming. Yet from that hour forward, a second silence, vaster than the plateau’s own, lay folded around the place like a reserve of breath.

The Power took eight nights to study.  
It moved at the pace of cold, gathering information bead by bead, swaying neither climate nor mind while it learned.

1. Night of Stone  
It pressed its attention into the basalt and read the cooling fractures the way a potter reads the cracks in a kiln: the table would cleave along three futures, none nearer than ten human lifetimes. Good news—time enough.

2. Night of Water  
It counted every droplet that ever strung itself between mist and ground. Every one was already spoken for by root, lung, or scale. The accounting balanced to the last glimmer. Excellent.

3. Night of Wind  
It measured conversations: wolf clicks bouncing from cliff, children’s hush-sung quarrels, the pan-flute moan of evening ledges. No tone bullied another. Harmony by scarcity: perfect.

4. Night of Hunger  
It watched wolves refuse tethered goats, humans refuse to hunt wolves, goats refuse to over-graze lichen. A triangle of mutual restraint sharper than any tooth. Worth preserving.

5. Night of Memory  
It traced rib-bones mortared into retaining walls, inlays that counted the plateau’s widening. Here, even death worked as masonry. A culture already versed in slow continuities.

6. Night of Ignorance  
It noted what the Gap-Kin lacked: metallurgy, long-range travel, the mathematics of loads that could spare children ballast duty on rope bridges. But it also saw what absence protected: a library of unbroken trusts.

7. Night of Questions  
It asked itself: What can benevolence add where perfection is expressed in limits?  
Answer: Very little.  
But non-interference is not abandonment; it is stewardship in its most ethereal form.

8. Night of Decision  
Action would be measured, surgical, almost undetectable—tuned to strengthen what already exists, never to overwrite.

And so, at dawn of the ninth day, the Power enacted four small mercies.

Mercy One:  
It thickened, by a fraction too slight to chart, the nightly rime on the plateau’s windward rim, ensuring one additional cup of water per household each three-day cycle. A gift small enough to be mistaken for luck, large enough to buffer drought years.

Mercy Two:  
It whispered into the lichen’s cryptic DNA a talent for fixing the faintest trace of airborne nitrogen. Soil would now accrue in centuries rather than millennia, though no eye alive would catch the difference. Future birches would grow wrist-thick instead of finger-thin, and children yet unborn would carve flutes from wood instead of bone.

Mercy Three:  
It breathed a subtle mutation into the glasswings: a brittle stripe across each elytron, invisible until shed. From now on, fallen wing-panes would crack naturally into hexagons—the perfect tessellation for glazing cistern roofs. Rain loss to evaporation would drop by a tenth, with no craftsman forced to strike a hammer.

Mercy Four:  
It touched the wolves’ double-throated Arbiters, strengthening the cartilage that let them sing in chords. Their rulings would gain a resonance capable of crossing the entire breadth of the High Quiet on still nights. Thus every pack could settle disputes without patrols, lowering predation pressure during lean years and sparing the Pact its rare moments of strain.

Having done these things, the Power retreated one incremental step, the way a parent removes a guiding hand the instant a child’s stride steadies. Yet it did not vanish. It nested its attention inside three ordinary phenomena:

• The faint bulge of horizon that pricks at peripheral vision—every time an elder notes it, they are glancing straight at the Power’s watch-station.  
• The moment after a rain slot booms and before the plinking begins—those two heartbeats of absolute hush are the Power’s open notebook.  
• The echo that refuses to return—occasionally the White Audience keeps a secret; that silence is the Power’s reply of ongoing assent.

Thus supervised yet unconstrained, the High Quiet proceeds along its selected future:

People will not discover iron for at least four more full crescent-leanings of the lodestone obelisk. When they finally smelt their first red bead of metal—from marsh bog ore, not from trade—they will also possess thicker birch, deeper soils, fuller cisterns, and a plateau-wide chorus of wolves to remind them how agreements sound in two harmonized notes.

They will think these good fortunes their own, and that misapprehension will hurt no one. Gratitude need not always find its mark; sometimes it is enough that it multiplies within the grateful.

And the Power?  
It waits, patient as basalt, for the century when a fissure shows one finger-width too wide and an Engineer kneels to inlay the warning. When that day comes the Power will choose, once more, whether to stay a hand or simply offer the mercy of more time.

Until then, it keeps the High Quiet exactly what the place already is:  
Young rock and old patience, held aloft upon a silence now shared by two listeners instead of one.
