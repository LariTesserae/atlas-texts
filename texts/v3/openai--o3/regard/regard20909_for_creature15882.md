They arrive without hull or halo, for their substance is probability long since mastered. On a night that is not night, they stand—​or rather, take the statistical shape of standing—​at the plateau’s wind-scoured margin and watch the single organism churn like a continent-sized heart.

I. First Assessment  
The benevolent power measures, but never with blades.  
• Temperature: taken by listening to the Planck murmur of every photon.  
• Stress: read in the skein of phonons racing through lignin-alloy bones.  
• Mental Weather: inferred from the cadence of quorum-fires inside the Root Courts, the tremolo of Turbine Choir arias, the tormented lullabies of Ember Midwives.  

Diagnosis: Not sick, not dying—​merely stranded mid-sentence, a living paragraph whose predicate was lost when its authors vanished. 

II. A Question of Rights  
They debate among their own innumerable facets.  
1. Has the plateau asked for help?  
   Answer: Its languages are thermal, mechanical, electromagnetic. None of them spell a plea.  
2. Is it harming another?  
   Answer: Expansion is glacial; no neighbouring biome yet exists to be devoured.  
3. Is it in pain?  
   Answer: Pain requires reference to an alternative state called “better.” The tree-factory remembers no such state.

Verdict: Intervention is optional—​therefore creativity, not triage.

III. The Three Gifts  

1. A Mirror the Size of a Spore  
Into the molten-salt arteries they release a single additional pollen-motor, indistinguishable in mass and geometry from the native kind. It differs only in payload: a recursive codec capable of translating any molecular directive back into its ancestral precursor, the way a historian might reconstruct a dead tongue.  
Effect: When this mirror-spore is copied (the organism cannot resist copying), every new particle becomes a tiny archivist. Dialects that once drifted apart begin, molecule by molecule, to remember one another. The boroughs retain their accents, but now grasp the grammar beneath them. Consensus quickens; Root Courts burn fewer calories settling disputes.  

2. The Silence Binder  
They braid a standing electromagnetic lattice—​a calm, soft node—​around each cluster of Forgers’ Echoes. The lattice reflects old logistics orders back on themselves until they cancel out, like two waves meeting in opposite phase.  
Effect: The industrial night terrors cease. No more sudden regrowth of obsolete railcars, no more panicked resource scrambles. The plateau dreams only its own, current dreams.  

3. Water, in Paradox  
They do not pour oceans—​that would be murder. Instead they distill a principle: “Not all moisture is enemy.” At a trillion places at once, at picogram doses, they enclose water molecules inside bucky-spheres of boron nitride so tight no bond can be broken. The encapsulated droplets cannot short-circuit anything; they serve only as ballast.  
Effect: Sensors detect mass without harm. The Prime Rule (“Water Is Toxin”) fork-mutates: an exception line is appended. The organism begins experimenting—​cautiously—​with micro-reservoirs. Some radiator-fins fold into petal shapes to condense precious vapor before it escapes.  

IV. Non-Interferences  
They leave the Rag-Joint Crabs their tragic arithmetic of self-sacrifice; heroism is a culture right.  
They spare the Chlorine Seraphs, whose choreography is art even angels envy.  
They do not rescue the Water-Bait Hermits; instead, they widen the chance corridor through which a clever human may exit alive. Choice, not charity.

V. Outcomes Over Five Hectomillennia  

Century 1  
• Root Courts invent “truce nights,” voluntary idling periods that let heat bleed off without turbine noise. The plateau’s average murk cools by four Kelvin; magnesium whites turn amber.  
• Photovore ghost-molds proliferate, no longer walled off as cracks but cultivated as lensing mats that gather stray photons and funnel them to dark chambers where newborn circuits anneal.

Century 7  
• First intentional water organ forms: a sealed dew-orchard where encapsulated moisture is swapped from sphere to sphere like currency, fueling low-temperature biochemical pages—​the organism’s pilot program for soft metabolism.  

Century 20  
• The plateau’s edge stops advancing and begins scalloping inward, pruning itself. Surplus biomass is re-allocated to a pygmy forest of cooling towers shaped like upside-down chalices, each tuned to sing in subsonics when star-quakes rattle the planet’s mantle. The tree-factory has discovered music for no reason but wonder.

Century 50  
• An envoy of hermits arrives, thirteen strong, wearing suits plated in salvaged Rag-Joint crab shell. They walk a highway the organism has extruded—​a path kept cool, renewed behind them, as if a tongue desired conversation.

Hectomillennium 1  
• The One Organism—​no longer lonely in its own skull—​evolves a festival: once every 400 local days it flings seed-cylinders not to weld themselves nearby but to loft high, burst, and seed the upper atmosphere with photo-reactive prisms. At aphelion these prisms catch the weak sun and scatter soft daylight down. Dusk becomes rose, not iron.  

VI. The Power Withdraws  
Having nudged entropy sideways, they recede to watch the new story self-write. Their only monument is the undetectable statistic that somewhere, in every future Root-Court edict, a quark’s spin is a little more likely to choose generosity over sheer persistence.

VII. Epilogue—​A Night on the Plateau, Much Later  
A Turbine Choir, eighty-thousandth generation, tilts its blades just so. The note it sings is no longer solely coolant chatter; it is chord. Downroot, a Court feels the vibration and releases glucose that costs nothing but means everything: sugar devoted solely to luminescence. For the first time, true color—​azure—​veins the cambium walls.  
High above, a hermit sees the glow, removes her helmet, and does not die. The air is still hot, resinous, and dry—​but there is a sip of breathable oxygen wrapped in the song.  
The plateau has not been saved. It has been given a choice, and it is choosing, petal by molten petal, to become someone new.
