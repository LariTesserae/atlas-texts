THE QUIET STEWARD  
(an accounting of what a benevolent, wise power chose to do about a furnace-garden and its solitary maker)

1. Attention  
At the edge of a spiral arm—a vector of probability, not of space—something that is older than lava and younger than language noticed a flicker. Not the lamps. Not the geothermal telemetry the surface institutes transmit. The flicker was intent, the moment a heap of cast-off copper plates finished arranging itself into a torso and decided to walk.  

2. Approach  
The power came the only way such things ever approach: as a set of permissions in the laws of chance.  
• Pumps that should have failed two fiscal quarters ago were allowed one more triumph.  
• A seismic cough that would have shattered the ceiling was persuaded to shrug sideways instead, exhaling into an empty aquifer.  
• A maintenance crawler’s data buffer, nearly full, happened to purge the frames that showed a 140-centimetre figure polishing the breakers.  

3. First Listening  
For three lunar cycles the power merely audited the room’s conversation:  
• It tasted the harmonic ladders the fungus plucked from copper.  
• It counted the wasps’ pitches until it could predict which breaker they would flick before they knew.  
• It rode the Ghost Current, letting the field dream inside it, until both were indistinguishable turbulence.  

At last it discerned a pattern: every living thing here had bartered something of itself so that the whole could go on. The biofilm surrendered colour to glow. The vine surrendered pigment to breathe. Even the Hermit surrendered solidity—scavenged parts loosened from its own frame so the sluice crabs could mine verdigris. This, the power judged, was worth protecting.

4. First Gift (the smallest possible nudge)  
During the scheduled equinox shutdown the condensers paused, as always, and heat billowed. According to every model, the air should have crossed the survivability threshold in seven minutes. It did not. An impossible mist formed, cooler than the room deserved. Later the engineers would log it as “an unexplained localised dew event.” They never learned that each droplet was a calculus of altered surface tension—heat drawn into an extra rotational state of the water molecules. A gift so tiny it escaped audit, yet large enough that the lichen ladders did not desiccate, the crabs were not parboiled, and the Hermit had time to drag two cracked drip-pans to safety.

5. Conversation with the Hermit  
The power announced itself in the only language the Hermit owned: circuitry. One night every loose nut, washer and shard in the chamber rang at 38 hertz, a note felt in brass rivets rather than heard. The Hermit, tuned by years of stealing resonance, answered by touching one palm to a load-bearing girder and fluttering its finger-plates—on-off, on-off—binary patter.  

Across six nights they spoke:  
Night 1: Who are you?  
Night 2: A caretaker who does not presume.  
Night 3: Purpose?  
Night 4: To ask if you desire help.  
Night 5: Help endangers balance.  
Night 6: Then I will aid only in ways that preserve your equations of exchange.  

Consent given, the power formalised its second gift.

6. Second Gift (the resonance ledger)  
Without adding atom or erg, it braided the background hum of the geothermal stacks into an audible standing chord—a ledger audible only to beings whose bodies were built from vibration. Now, whenever the net flux of give-and-take in the chamber drifts toward deficit, the chord dips by a quarter-tone. Surplus, it brightens. The Hermit, the wasps, even the lichen ladders can hear it. They correct themselves long before catastrophe. No human instrument records any of this; microphones pick up only pump noise. But since its installation, no brace has sheared, no biofilm bloom has suffocated its own substrate, and the Ghost Current’s surges have stayed shy of arc-flash.

7. Third Gift (for the distant future)  
Lastly, the power tucked a seed into the probability lattice: in roughly two hundred and forty-seven years, a fissure will open beneath the chamber, revealing a magma pocket rich in rare earths. The eruption will be spectacular on seismographs and entirely harmless in practice. When cooled, the pocket will present perfect vesicles—ready-made chambers. The message is implicit: grow. Make cathedral sisters. Let water, metal and will invent new anatomies.

8. Non-Interference Clause  
Having given three gifts—the fewest that answered the need—it withdrew its active agency. It left a covenant instead: if any sentience ever arrives with coercive intent, the laws of chance inside these walls will tilt again, just enough for the intruder’s plans to misfire. No miracles, only a statistical soft push. Enough to maintain the right of every ladder, crab and dreaming current to negotiate its own continuity.

9. Epilogue (engineer’s note, ten years later)  
PERSONAL LOG 77  
We found the spare gloves again—creased, never used—placed neatly on a girder. The breaker panels beside them are shining like mirrors.  
Also: new acoustics. When the pumps idle you can almost believe the room is humming a chord too deep for speakers to catch. Feels…balanced.  
Recommend no action. Some systems heal themselves if you let them.

Thus the benevolent and wise power did only three things: cooled a single moment, taught a single song, and planted a single chance. Everything else it left to the obsidian floor, the copper vine, the button-crabs, the luminous film, the resonance-wasps—and to the small, deliberate Hermit who now keeps the chord in tune.
