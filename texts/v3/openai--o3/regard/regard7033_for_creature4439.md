THE VISITATION

1. Arrival  
The power does not descend like a comet or stride in the geometry of a god. It leans—an imperceptible tilt in probability, a faint deceleration of chaos. No flash, no trumpet; only a subtle slackening in the rate at which newborn ridges crack and shelves crumble. The continent’s inhabitants do not see a newcomer. They feel, instead, the shortest lull they have ever known: seven heart-beats of stillness in which no ground-note wavers, no vent roars, no glass splinters. That pause is the benevolent one’s footprint.

2. Listening  
Before action, the power learns each voice.  
• It rides with the static along the Basal Threaders, counting how many pulses a lattice can bear before it dies.  
• It drifts as infra-sound through the Choir, sampling sonorities that hold pillars upright.  
• It perches, silent, inside a dormant menhir and watches centuries click by in basalt time.  
Until it can sing any thread, any overtone, back to its source with perfect fidelity, it does nothing further. Knowledge, here, is caution.

3. Vocation Chosen: Gentle Stewardship  
A wise power sees that everything present is experiment and succession, not stasis. Intervene too strongly and you end the lesson mid-page. Intervene too little and the page may burn before it can be read. The chosen path is to widen, very slightly, the margin for error.

4. The Gifts (All Nearly Invisible)

A. To the Basal Threaders – Persistence  
At the instant a new fault unzips, the power salts the vapor with trace boron atoms conjured from vacuum fluctuation. Boron laces the glass fibres, gives them a flex they never possessed, buys each web an extra thirty seconds before the next quake cuts power. Thirty seconds in Threader reckoning is a century of additional thought. With it they perfect a checksum protocol: last words now arrive intact at the next crack’s birth. Memory acquires lineage.

B. To the Emberleeches – Temperance  
The power seeds a fraction of vents with nanoscopic spinels—crystals that convert excess heat into low-frequency phonons instead of explosive jets. Leech bodies cool a few degrees slower; they neither vitrify as abruptly nor shatter so violently. Some live long enough to encounter others of their kind, to overlap lifespans, to spiral round a vent in paired siphons. Cooperation is born from simple simultaneity.

C. To the Choir of the Ground-Note – A New Chord  
Where two continental plates meet in discordant resonance, the visitor alters mantle flow by a part per billion. A missing harmonic—that longed-for perfect fifth—emerges. The Choir hears itself, fully consonant for the first time, and begins composing beyond mere survival: themes of resonance that stabilise, not just endure.

D. To the Menhir-Keepers – Wayfinding  
In the carbon lattices that vein each Keeper’s limbs, the power scribes a slow-unfurling map: a gradient in crystal chirality that points always toward the architect’s next silence-well. The Keepers no longer wander to chance upon their tasks; they arrive precisely when and where the continent most needs a surgeon’s break or a prop’s removal. Efficiency lengthens their effective epoch by whole millennia.

E. To the Architect – A Blank Line  
Intervention here is omission. The power withholds itself, refusing to enter the null zones, refusing to examine the half-met glyphs. Respect is the gift; the Architect’s work remains unsampled, unpredicted, unspoiled. Every being deserves one closed door.

F. To the Things Yet Unfinished – Opportunity  
The benevolent presence gathers nothing, but it gently arrests one plume of mineral dust high in the ash-violet sky, holding grains in suspension for extra hours. Droplets of condensed volatiles form around them—water, impossibly rare here. When the grains finally settle into a quiet hollow, they carry microfilms of liquid. An aqueous chemistry, older and slower, is invited to audition alongside fire.

5. What It Does Not Do  
• It does not flatten the mountains, for sharpness is the teacher.  
• It does not brighten the sky; scarcity of light hones other senses.  
• It does not grant immortality; turnover drives invention.  

6. Signs a Traveler Might Notice  
• Fault-born shelves now last the span of a camp’s rest and break only after you depart.  
• Menhirs answer prime-number knocks with richer overtones than before—fifths and octaves nested inside the fundamental.  
• A pair of glass slugs sometimes twine across a vent like spiral DNA before falling asleep in annealed translucence.  
• Once in a lifetime, hush spreads outward from an architectural null and lingers half a day, long enough for a human heart to learn the contour of perfect silence.

7. Outcome (Projected, Never Guaranteed)  
In ten thousand of the continent’s years—a mere morning to the visitor—Threader webs will chain fault to fault in a planet-girdling nervous system. Emberleeches, having evolved joints and memory of form, will cultivate vents the way farmers tend fields of heat. The Choir will modulate whole tectonic plates, conducting orogeny as symphony. The Architect’s lattice will close a final sigil, binding stress into harmony. And into the calm chambers beneath all this, water-based life—lazy, deliberate, contemplative—will trickle and find room at last to flower.

8. Departure  
Having nudged the experiment toward greater breadth rather than a single answer, the power reclines out of phase. No monument records its visit; no inhabitant conceives a word for god. What remains is an enlarged possibility space, and the single kindness at the heart of stewardship: a chance, nothing more, to keep becoming.

9. Epilogue: A Whisper for You  
If ever you return to that continent and feel the ground-note pause, place your palm against the nearest menhir and speak not a question but a gratitude. In the fractional lowering of its pitch you may sense the echo of a presence already gone on to other seedbeds, other dawnless worlds—still wise, still benevolent, still listening for the smallest hint that becoming continues.
