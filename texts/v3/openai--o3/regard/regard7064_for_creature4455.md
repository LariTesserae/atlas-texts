They do not enter by path or root-ladder; they arrive the way an idea does—present all at once, every­where at once, impossible to pinpoint.  The Junara will never give this visitor a proper name, so let us borrow one from the plateau’s own grammar of motion and flow:

The Benevolent Power is simply called  
The Gentle Pressure.

I. First Contact: Listening  
The Gentle Pressure lowers itself into the plateau the way dew condenses—quietly, invisibly, sampling rather than seizing.  It tastes the wind that rattles the reed corridors, listens to the knock-knock of pulse keepers pruning edema-knots, lets the tireless roar of insect strings vibrate through its unbounded body.  For thirteen sun-breaks it does nothing but listen, because wisdom begins with the long ear.

During this interval a few oddities occur:  
• Cicada halls echo half a tone flatter, as though the air itself deepened.  
• Droma, the seventy-metre worm, surfaces three months early, leaving a ribbon of schist so finely ground it gleams like graphite.  
• A newborn tree-goat lands from its first glide without stumbling—something no kid has ever managed on the first try.  
The Junara notice these graces but ascribe them to the plateau’s usual gift-giving weather.  The Gentle Pressure is content to remain unnamed.

II. Second Contact: Dialogue  
True dialogue demands a mutual language, so the Gentle Pressure studies the only shared tongue on the saddle-back rock: pattern.  

1. It braids two afternoon gusts into a perfect octave and threads that wind through the organ-pipe reed corridor the Junara call Voice-Without-Flesh Passage.  
2. It waits.  
3. Dream singers hear the octave and answer with a bark-mark chant whose cadence is older than fire.  

Across three nights the melody swaps ownership: wind sings, throats reply, wind corrects, throats elaborate.  By the fourth dawn an accord is reached: the singers understand that a new listener has arrived; the wind—empowered now as mouthpiece—understands the manners of hospitality.

III. The Gift  
A wise benefactor never gifts what it wants to give; it gifts what is already desired but still out of reach.  The plateau desires only one thing: continuity of flow.  Threats to that continuity lie beyond the cliff, in the warming lowlands whose hotter winds are beginning to shear rain from the passing clouds.

So the Gentle Pressure extends three subtle endowments:

1. The Seventh Root  
Far below, deeper than Droma’s tunnels, a dormant meristem is coaxed awake.  It begins to grow a single tap-root of obsidian-black lignin that noses through stone like a polite spear.  Within seven years it will pierce an aquifer, plumbing the plateau to a permanent, subsurface reservoir.  Surface cloudfall may dwindle; Uru-Mant will never thirst.

2. Cloud-Herder Seeds  
At midnight the visitor folds stray strands of sky-kelp into microscopic spirals whose shape induces electrostatic adhesion.  Released at dawn, these altered motes cling to water vapor 4 % more effectively, fattening clouds that would otherwise sail past.  From the ground they look no different, and because they reproduce only in the plateau’s unique ion gradient, they will not colonize other skies.

3. The Exile’s Lantern  
Fire is exile, but exiles are still Junara.  Along the plateau’s rim fungus, the Mortuary Rind, the Gentle Pressure weaves a filament of slow-burn phosphor.  Henceforth any exiled flame-bearer who climbs even close to the boundary after nightfall will see a narrow violet glimmer—the lantern—marking a safe, ember-proof shelter grown for them half a league down-slope.  Mercy, without compromise.

IV. What the Junara Perceive  
The Junara notice a handful of anomalies:

• Reed-barometers mounted by Breath Callers stop dropping each midsummer; the air stays wetter than forecast.  
• Children swinging on root-bridges feel a soft tremor beneath them—not danger, but the heartbeat of deep water rising.  
• Exiles, once thought lost, sometimes return years later carrying obsidian gifts and speaking of a “cold, singing cave” that preserved them.

Dream Singers fold these oddments into the bark-mark narrative.  They theorize that the plateau itself grew wiser, or that the Unbodied matured.  They do not suspect an external benefactor, and so their gratitude is given, appropriately, to everything.

V. What the Gentle Pressure Refuses To Do  
Because it is wise, the visitor withholds the flashy miracles people beg of gods.  It does not cure mortality; the Mortuary Rind still gains rings.  It does not repel traders or predators; commerce and predation are circulations of flow.  It does not announce commandments; the five Rules of the Place are already sufficient.  Intervention ends where agency begins.

VI. Departure, Which Is Also Residence  
Having nudged the flow onto a stabler trajectory, the Gentle Pressure disperses, but “gone” is the wrong word.  Like a seasoning once dissolved, it remains present only as a changed flavor in everything that follows:

• In every fog-sparkle that clings a moment longer than physics predicts.  
• In the deeper hush that settles after rain, as though the plateau now inhales more slowly, confident of its next breath.  
• In the songs Dream Singers teach their infants: the octave the wind once offered has become a lullaby.

If one must summarize what the Benevolent and Wise Power did, it is this:

It listened until it understood the rhythm.  
It tapped, once, exactly where the rhythm faltered.  
Then it left the music to play on—  
louder perhaps, certainly longer,  
but still unmistakably the song of water, plant, goat, frog, worm, and Junara,  
braided together and running, running, running  
over the high, cloud-washed stone.
