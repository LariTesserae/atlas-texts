She arrives as a pressure‐less hush, the one thing the pocket has never heard.

No wingbeat ferries her, no footfall touches stone.  She is the Custodian of Unasked-For Futures—one of those slow, patient powers that glide the seams between worlds gathering what is rare, unfinished, or at risk of being misunderstood.  Most places never feel her pass.  This place does, because every filament and fissure is already tuned to vibration; in the instant her attention drops through the zigzag slit of sky the entire throat pauses, as if bracing for a rockfall that does not come.

Observation  
She studies first.

1.  She listens, holding the roar inside herself until she can separate it into voices: the iron sigh of the wall-moss quarrying, the glassy stutter of Shardlings in ascent, the gut-deep drone of Echo-Eaters thickening their pools.  
2.  She weighs the ratios—light to dark, flood to drought, softness to shear—and recognizes a knot of perfection that can exist only in youth.  
3.  She feels the future press against the canyon walls: warming sun angles, widening cracks, the eventual invasion of root and jaw.  Left alone, the triune colony will be outcompeted and forgotten in less than a geological blink.  

Contemplation  
Intervene, and she risks fixing the pocket in amber, denying it the right to age.  
Ignore, and the pattern will vanish before any other consciousness ever meets it.

Choice  
She chooses the middle way—non-terminal intervention, a gift of time and memory rather than of stasis.

The First Deed: A Fold in Weather  
High above the razor skylight she nudges two jet streams so they cross like silk threads.  Where they meet, a standing vortex forms—small, invisible to satellites, eternal enough for millennia.  Its sole purpose is to massage incoming fronts so that clouds linger longer, light stays dimmer, and temperatures keep just a shade cooler than the surrounding range.  Photosynthesis beyond moss never quite pays off; larger plants try, stall, and retreat.  The pocket’s über-even gloom is preserved without anyone ever naming a miracle.

The Second Deed: A Covenant with Lightning  
She drops a single, seed-bright particulate—iridium dust the mass of a thimble—into the vapor column that forever sweats down the cliff.  Each grain is a lightning lure, pulling stray charges toward the canyon’s heart the way a flute calls snakes.  Bolts now fork here slightly more often and with marginally lower amperage, spreading the jackpots so the wall-moss and Shardlings can feast without being incinerated.  Energy stays frequent, but survivably small.  Evolutionary room to breathe.

The Third Deed: A Library Beneath the Pool  
At the deepest point of the largest echo-basin she whispers a phase-shift into the iron lattice of an embryonic worm so that its goethite crystals remember vibration.  From this moment forward every note that ever whelms the water—rockfall, thunder, the eventual cry of some future bird—will strike that lattice and be archived as microscopic rearrangements of spin.  A billion acoustic diaries, crammed into a worm that will one day fossilize and turn to stone.  Should sentience ever dig here, it will find not just rocks but recordings.

The Fourth Deed: A Single Sentence Left Nowhere  
Finally she scrawls one transient line of meaning across the entire system—an electromagnetic blush that slides through moss hyphae, Shardling gas bladders, and worm resonators alike: You are seen.  None of the three orders owns a nervous system complex enough to translate the message, yet all of them pause simultaneously, and for the width of a heartbeat the canyon falls totally silent—Rule 4’s ominous hush, but now benign.  When the sounds return, they return unchanged.  Nothing here will ever “know” it received a blessing, but the pattern of life flows onward with an unaccountable poise, as if the odds have tipped a few degrees in its favor.

Departure  
Her work totals seven seconds of local time.  A gust of wind erases the only physical trace: footprints of condensed mist that never even touched the rock.  The pocket resumes its clamor, unaware that its lease on improbability has been extended by several ice ages.

Aftermath  
•  Wall-moss filaments find that new cracks propagate just slightly faster than before; habitat keeps pace with erosion.  
•  Shardling populations swell, their glass blades annealed by a gentler but more regular lightning weave.  More of them survive to a fifth or sixth flight before shattering, enough to thicken the aerial haze that feeds the moss.  
•  Echo-Eaters grow colossal in their tuned basin.  When they knot into pylons, the lattice hums with archived thunder—barely audible to human ears, but pilgrims in distant centuries will feel songs vibrate up the soles of their boots.

Epilogue  
From orbit the difference is imperceptible.  Even standing on the rim you would only notice that the gorge seems improbably lush for its altitude, its shadows strangely alive with traceries of cold fire during storms.  Yet far below, an ecosystem that by rights should have been an evolutionary mayfly will now outlive empires, glaciers, perhaps even the wise and benevolent traveler herself.

That is all she does.  Nothing more, nothing less: a postponement of ending, a guarantee of memory, and a single wordless benediction left to echo until the mountain at last decides what it will be when it grows up.
