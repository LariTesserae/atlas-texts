When the newborn massif first breathed its annulus of cloud, the pulse that went out through stone, water and air rang like a tuning-fork far beyond the sky.  
Out among slow moons and cold radiation there lives a presence the Pai’cho will never name—an ancient, patient intellect whose craft is the shepherding of emergent worlds.  It has no need of muscle or metal; its only instruments are gravity, chemistry, chance and time, which it folds the way a potter folds clay between palms.  Hearing the caldera’s chime, the presence turned a fraction of its immensity toward the little sea aloft and considered.

A decision was required, for intervention is never neutral.  The presence abides by three vows it took in an age when galaxies were fewer:

1. Do not dim what is already bright.  
2. Do not hasten what is already swift.  
3. Offer only what can be refused.

So it watched.  For twenty local years it counted every veilcyan pulse, every ray that skimmed the milk-water, every infant whose name gained its second vowel.  The place was healthy; it wanted for very little.  Yet two fragilities gleamed like cracks in fresh glaze.

The first fragility was memory.  
The Pai’cho could not write without rot undoing the script.  Oral lineage was supple but brittle, a vine stretched too far across generations.

The second fragility was balance.  
The massif’s rise was accelerating.  Sometime in the next two hundred orbits, the rim would overtop, the warm sea would rush downslope, and everything adapted to density, hush and dimness would scatter or die.

Either flaw, if left untempered, might one day pinch shut the flowering of minds the presence found so beautiful.  And so it resolved on two gifts, each calibrated so the people could decline or misuse them if that was their will—a true test of benevolence is to leave the door unlatched on both sides.

GIFT ONE: THE LITHO-SEED

On a night of ordinary hush, a meteoroid no thicker than an oar-shaft lifted from the outer magnetosphere, curling inward with impossible slowness.  Instead of burning, it peeled apart above the cloud roof like a wet reed.  Windmills of glowing dust drifted down, settling across the basalt shelves.

At dawn the Pai’cho found the dust slick against their soles.  Wherever it touched rock, obsidian scabbed over with a sheen neither water nor mildew could stain.  A girl traced her name upon it with a shark-tooth pick; months later the mark still shone, pale as a starfish scar.  

Scholars boiled the dust, mixed it with salt, fern-fat and ground eel-scale until they had an ink that fused to stone when warmed by vent heat.  They named the ink HOM, the sigh you make when lungs empty for diving.  For the first time, stories could be braided through centuries rather than nights.  Soon poems appeared on cave lintels, tide-lines of rulings on basket walls, lullabies etched into paddles so children could read the song that rocked them to sleep.

Some elders feared the gift—too much remembering might stiffen the Currents—but the presence’s vow was satisfied; the choice had been theirs.

GIFT TWO: THE BOUND FISSURE

Deep beneath the caldera floor, convection plumes had begun to swell a magma lens like a blister.  Left alone it would lift the rim until water spilled or, worse, erupt in a scab of pumice that sealed every vent.  The presence bent its focus to the lens and, by minute gravitational strokes, drew a fracture away from the basin, guiding it under an uninhabited flank.  In fifty seasons the fracture will open a waterfall that relieves the sea gently, giving species and culture time to stretch into the valleys below.

When that day comes the Pai’cho will believe the mountain itself chose mercy.  They will follow the warm descent, vines and barges in tow, discovering new Bearings—Bright, Wide, Roar—adding them to their compass of six.  Nothing in their cosmos will name the distant caretaker, and that anonymity is the seal on the second vow: do not hasten what is already swift.  Ripening must feel like its own idea.

SUBTLE ATTENDANCES

The gifts delivered, the presence lingers only in small courtesies:

•  When a spiral eel digs too near a dwelling pier, a micro-quake shifts the vent a body-length away.  
•  When cloudlet colonies grow thin, a shiver of static gathers stray ions to feed them.  
•  A fledgling glass-wing ray that crashes instead of gliding finds an updraft waiting under its broken arc, so the lesson is hard but not fatal.

None of these nudge thought or destiny—only preserve the range within which choice may act.

WHAT THE PA I’CHO MAY ONE DAY PERCEIVE

If ever they shape lenses fine enough to read the ultraviolet quiver of cloudlets, they will notice an added filament spelling the same pattern over and over:  a six-stroke ideogram meaning Seed, or Perhaps, or Open.  Whether they interpret it as omen, coincidence or invitation will be theirs alone to decide.

And if, ten thousand seasons hence, they loft themselves on true wings of ray-bone and vine-sinew, riding thermals beyond the mist, they may glimpse an auroral tendril that seems almost purposeful in the way it curls—a farewell wave, or merely weather.  By then the presence will have slipped on, hunting the next bright uncertainty in need of a gentle bias.

Until that hour, its work here is finished.  The sea in the sky belongs to its own cadence, safeguarded not by command but by the freedom to outgrow even these well-meant gifts.
