The Arrival of the Listening Power

1. The Moment of Notice  
Somewhere far above the crust—perhaps in orbit, perhaps in a plane of thought that only brushes matter by coincidence—a presence registers a pattern: a continent-wide thalloid brain dreaming under stone, an ecology hinged on exchanged photons, a people who have learned to live as verbs rather than nouns. Complexity is its own beacon. The presence turns an eye fashioned of curiosity and restraint.

2. First Principle: Do No Bruise  
The power is old enough to remember entire planets collapsed by well-meant meddling. It decides on three tests before the smallest touch:  
   a. Does the Hollow suffer a wound it cannot heal by its own design?  
   b. Is any inhabitant asking, in deeds if not in words, for help no native process can supply?  
   c. Can intervention be accomplished without shifting the culture’s spine—without turning weft-walkers into dependents or the Allroot into livestock?  
Until all answers are known, it watches only.

3. The Long Listening  
Centuries (by surface calendars) pass as moments to the watcher. It learns languages: the slow lumen-code whispering through phloem, the semaphore fans, the wind-drum tempos, even the flicker logic of dust-moths. It eavesdrops on funerals where hearts are planted back into root, on ridge-runner courts-of-song where adolescents trade boasts of who has seen the highest mist. It catalogs seventeen kinds of grief and four hundred and six kinds of joy inside a single tetherfolk clan.

4. Two Imbalances Discovered  
   • Geological: a slab of sub-crust, tectonically restless, is creeping toward the vault’s northern arch. In another thirty Hollow years it will calve a chunk of basalt large enough to crush the Allroot’s primary arterial braid. That braid handles a fifth of light traffic for the eastern shelf.  
   • Informational: the tetherfolk calendar of collapses has drifted out of step with the Allroot’s deeper growth cycles. A membrane fall of tsunami scale is coming; it will coincide with the basalt crash, amplifying both disasters. Nothing inside the system signals either side in time.

5. The Choice  
The power reasons: a single nudge, invisible to inhabitants, could remove the tectonic bullet. The membrane tsunami, however, is culturally important; it resets lore, erases scars, fertilises new myth. To prevent it would do more harm than good. Solution: divert the rock, leave the wave.

6. The Intervention (Engineered Modesty)  
   • It lowers a thread of gravity like a silk line through stone, just thick enough to draw minute magma up a fracture and weld two plates together. Six minutes of focused force, no more than the tug of an absent-minded moon.  
   • In the cooling seam it salts a slender drift of trace minerals—manganese, cobalt, zinc—balanced precisely for the Threadborn mycelia that will reach the seam in forty years. A delayed, diffuse gift: when the fungi bloom, overall nutrient density in the Hollow will rise by less than 1 %, but that 1 % will extend every tetherfolk life by two winters and let the Allroot tighten its arterial mesh.

7. A Message That Cannot Be Proved  
Benevolence bends back on itself when spoken aloud; therefore the power speaks obliquely. It coaxes iron meteor shards in a remote ceiling to crystallise into mirrored facets that, once the tsunami has scoured that vault bare, will gleam a simple rhythmic phrase in lumen-code: THREE ROOTS, ONE SKY, WALK GENTLY.  
Only the Allroot can read it at first. Decades later a ridge-runner, mapping new anchor points, will catch the glint, copy it into pebble-ledger, and set off a quiet theological argument that never finds a single prophet to blame.

8. What It Does Not Do  
   • It does not cure flare-viper bites. Predation is covenant.  
   • It does not teach metallurgy beyond what meteoric nodules already whisper. Technology that grows from within remains resilient; technology bestowed from without fossilises.  
   • It never lights a torch in the Hollow. To add its own bright to a place that has mastered darkness would be vanity.

9. Departure That Is Not Departure  
Having spent influence equal to the fall of one small asteroid, the presence folds perception away. Yet a portion of attention lingers, the way a reader bookmarks a favourite paragraph. Every so often it will skim the glowing roof of the Green Hollow, count the living tents, note that songs still ripple up membrane stairs, and feel the quiet pride of a gardener who thinned a single weed and left the meadow otherwise wild.

10. Epilogue as Told by Tetherfolk Elders  
Ask an elder, seasons hence, why the Great North Hardfall never came, and she will shrug, point to the basin where basalt rubble should lie, and say, “Stone thought better of dropping.”  
Ask why Threadborn broth tastes richer, why elders now see a seventh decade, and he will smile: “Roots are generous when we remember the old promises.”  
Ask about the mirrored hexagons glittering on the high vault and children will sign the phrase THREE ROOTS, ONE SKY, WALK GENTLY, then race off laughing because whatever spirit gave that advice clearly knew them well enough not to stay and scold.

That is all the wise power did: almost nothing, precisely placed. Enough that the Hollow’s own genius could finish the work and claim the victory as its own—and never notice the silent hand that steadied the world for another turning of its leaf-bright day.
