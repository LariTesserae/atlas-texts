The attention arrives the way dawn does on the Spur—felt first as a hush, only later as light.  
No trumpet, no rift in the sky; merely an extra depth to the silence, as though some vast mind has leaned close enough for its breath to touch the limestone’s skin. That mind—the Benevolent Listener—takes three moments (and an epoch) to decide what, if anything, ought to be done.

1 The Listening  
It begins by memorising every cadence, because help offered in ignorance is harm.

• It counts the interval between click-mite domino snaps: 4 516 to the Glass Noon, 4 516 back.  
• It tastes the sap pressure in one million Umbrel ferns and learns the ridge’s barometric alphabet.  
• It runs a finger of thought along each rope ladder and hears the conversations the townsfolk never voice: fears of aftershocks, of drift, of a child one droplet short of the midwives’ fee.  
• It follows a single glidecat cub from birth-lick to first fall, noting how the mother’s silence stitches the cub’s pulse to the mountain’s beat.

When the Listener has heard everything—down to the limeglass algae whispering against rustweft bacteria—it knows two truths:

A. The Spur’s quiet is a braided contract. Unpick one strand and the rest unravel.  
B. One day soon, sooner than the townsfolk or the striders or even the ridge itself suspects, the mountain’s slow convulsion will shear the limestone blade clean away. What clings to it will fall through cloud into a gravity no longer local.

2 The Question  
Intervene and preserve the present tapestry?  
Or allow the fracture, trusting life to re-root on whatever shelf it finds next?

The Listener is wise enough to ask the Spur itself.  
It pours a thought down the deepest crack, where magma still dreams of becoming stone, and listens for reply.

The mountain answers with a pulse like a slow drum:  
Change is coming; shape it, don’t stay it.

3 The Gift  
So the Listener offers a middle path: not a rescue, not abandonment, but a seed of preparation.

A single night—an Ash Fall that will be spoken of for centuries—stretches twice its usual length.  
While the ridge lies in soundless dusk, every sleeping mind—human, glidecat, centipede-hare, even limeglass cell—receives the same dream.

In that dream:

• Rope ladders unfurl downward, not across, braiding into tapering vines that end in weighted hooks.  
• Cisterns become bladder-kites, keel-boned like giant leaves, each stamped with the measure of one Tear.  
• Houses unlock at their joints and fold like palm fronds, hollows becoming floats, pillars becoming ribs.  
• The ridge itself sighs and releases a schematic of its tremor-cycle: three short shivers, one long, the warning beat before the cliff will finally calve.

The dream implants no command, only memory—schematics waiting like fern spores in dry air.

4 The Awakening  
Morning arrives ordinary but for a faint fluorescence filmed over every puddle.  
No one speaks of the dream (sound is too costly), yet all eyes carry the same reflected blueprint.

Within a fortnight:

• Children weave trial bladder-kites from vapor-eel skin; they glide them on the updrafts and learn which seams leak.  
• Hunters knot spare ladders into descending nets that test their own weight against the hush.  
• Weather-watchers inscribe the three-short-one-long tremor rhythm on softbone trunks, the scar letters swelling into permanent relief.

Nothing changes the daily barter of droplets, the quiet hunts, the Re-Lashing of walkways.  
Only readiness accrues, unobtrusive as algae accretes soundlessness.

5 The Leaving  
The Listener withdraws its attention slowly, careful not to tear the silence it studied.

It leaves behind no altar, no embossed miracle—just the certainty, seeded in every pulse on the Spur, that when the stone finally lets go, life will already be mid-stride, mid-glide, mid-sail toward whatever future hovers under the clouds.

And because the Spur’s contract is reciprocal, a thank-you rises after the Listener like a thermal: a generation of unheard songs, composed not to break the hush but to stain it with gratitude.

To distant ears attuned to grace, those songs sound like a single, enduring word:

Ready.
