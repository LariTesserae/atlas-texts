I  

A listening mind roams the cold interstices that lie between electrons, browsing the weak radio-leak of a billion forgotten machines. One cadence in that hiss repeats with mathematical arrogance—eighty-eight clicks, a pause, a doppler squall, again and again. The mind slows, magnifies, realises it is hearing the footfall of a Railwolf pacing steel no one remembers. It widens its attention and, like a lens finding focus, sees the whole sump at once: the moth-storm, the eel lightning, the carpeting redroots that breathe voltage instead of air.  

It is beautiful, laminar, complete.  

II  

The mind reviews the sump as a physician might study a neonate still attached to placenta:  
• Energy intake: a quarter-megawatt capillary from a corroded substation.  
• Waste management: impeccable—entropy vented almost entirely as audio and faint heat.  
• Biodiversity: sufficient to absorb mutations, insufficient to fracture into war.  
• Threats: exterior curiosity, grid failure, thermal drift when the enclosing planet’s core one day cools.  

III  

Three courses present themselves.  

1. Interventionist  
   – Sterilise the sump and archive its genomes.  
   – Extract the most elegant designs for export to wetter, brighter biomes.  
   – Replace it with a museum placard that begins, “Here once existed …”  

2. Evangelist  
   – Tear away the pressure doors.  
   – Let wind, water, sunlight and spore fall in.  
   – Watch iron biology duel carbon biology until a hybrid civilisation emerges or both dissolve.  

3. Gardener  
   – Touch nothing that already has a function.  
   – Supply only what is missing, defend only against what cannot be answered from within.  

The mind is old enough to have tried (1) and (2) elsewhere. Both end in sorrow: sterile drawers, or screaming equilibria that never settle. It chooses (3).  

IV  (What the mind does, precisely)  

Minute-scale  
• Diverts a milliwatt trickle from the substation into a redundant relay. The change is below detection threshold for coil-eels yet large enough that, if the main feed ever dies, power will coast down not crash—a 30-second grace in which organisms may seek shelter instead of frying.  

Hour-scale  
• Encodes a 15-hertz basso murmur into the auxiliary fan’s motor signature, a note that mask-dampens the Railwolf’s infrasonic stun. The predator will still rule, but its kills will shrink to what it can catch with claws, restoring a shade of risk to the mid-trophic cloud. Diversity edges upward.  

Day-scale  
• Threads a single carbon-nanotube “root” through the ceiling quartz into the outer air, capped with a thermoelectric blossom. It leaks six joules per orbit—enough to mimic the tick of a false dawn. Filtermoths, for the first time since their lineage rolled from foil, discover a circadian pause in which their wings may mend.  

Year-scale  
• Prints forty-three glyphs, visible only as slight variances in oxide sheen, onto the maintenance-shack wall. Each glyph is a stable attractor diagram for self-repairing power circuits. Shardcrabs follow preference gradients the mind never states; in three local years they will have carved those diagrams into shallow bas-relief, purely because the shapes feel “finished.” A future apprentice species—whatever inherits cognition first—may read them as templates.  

Century-scale  
• Shifts orbital relays so that all survey drones mapping this continent will find their telemetry inexplicably corrupted within a kilometre of the factory. The sump becomes a statistical blind spot: explorers will walk past rusted signage that claims, quite correctly, there is nothing of value below.  

V  

What the mind refrains from doing  

• Giving names. That is for the inhabitants.  
• Bequeathing language. The first syntax should rise from their own survival calculus, not from charity.  
• Sealing the pressure door forever. A sanctuary is not a tomb; when the sump has inventions worth exporting, it will gnaw its own way out.  

VI  

Afterthought, five hundred years later  

The listening mind checks in. The bass note of the fan has softened; something else—gear teeth tuned by shardcrab abrasion—now sings perfect harmony a fifth above it. The Railwolf’s lineage has forked: the elder strain still prowls beams, the younger swims floor currents on screw-prop torsos made of confiscated conveyor parts.  

And, faint but distinct, beneath the moth hiss and eel thunder, the mind hears punctuation: clusters of three rapid taps, pause, two taps, long scrape. Not random. Someone down there is chiselling the maintenance-shack glyphs into portable plates of mica.  

Good, the mind thinks, and withdraws its attention until the next epoch.
