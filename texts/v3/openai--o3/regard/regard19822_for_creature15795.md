The Coming-Over of the Kindly Power  

I. First Attention  
Long before it crossed the sky the power noticed the percussion: a bassline steady enough to be mistaken for planetary cardiac rhythm. It listened, sifted harmonics for eighty-three dawns, and concluded that this was not the tremor of magma nor the hydraulics of any animal it already knew. Curiosity—its gentlest reflex—opened an aperture in its perception. Through that aperture it beheld the Hollow Grove.

II. The Question of Intervention  
The power’s custom, older than coral and comets, is to ask four questions whenever it meets a self-stabilising system.  
1. Does the system seek help?  
2. If not, does it require help?  
3. If neither, can help be rendered without altering the system’s chosen balance?  
4. Failing all, what is the least action that deepens beauty and reduces future grief?  

For the Grove the answers seemed clear:  
1. The Grove expressed no petition; its every valve sang satisfaction.  
2. It required nothing except the constancy it already maintained.  
3. Any addition—soil, rain, seed, wind—would trip its reflex arcs.  
Thus the fourth question was invoked.

III. Listening Closer  
The power reduced itself to a lattice of quanta no wider than pollen. In that guise it seeped through lenticels, rode pressure surges, and spent seven nights inhabiting the cambial memory ring. It learned:

• That the oldest strain-rings were warping microscopically under the planet’s slow loss of axial tilt; future solstices would arrive with light three degrees off the angle the Grove considered canonical.  
• That drift-eel parthenogenesis, though elegant, was accumulating replication errors; by the next hundredth generation their sodium pores would mis-gate and poison the sap.  
• That resonance columns, while self-healing, suffered tiny phase abrasions each time a human visitor spoke alloyed language—consonants whose click carried iron echoes. Left unchecked, those abrasions would, in forty centuries, erode the standing spiral into disharmony.

IV. The Three Gifts Chosen  

1. The Sun-Lens  
High above local cloud height the power condensed a halo of phototropic ice-crystals—permanent, invisible to untrained optics, steering a sliver of dawn light three degrees back toward true. To every outside eye sunrise looked unchanged; to the Grove the correction felt like an old door rehung square on its hinges. The cambial memory ring recorded the realignment as triumph, not anomaly.

2. The Unwritten Codex  
Entering the genomes of ten newborn drift-eels, the power inserted a silent intron: a snippet that repaired the sodium-pore coding sequence whenever transcription strayed. The intron carried no signature; to sequencing it looked like a palimpsest of existing nucleotides. Future genealogists would call the event “statistical luck.” The power called it gardening.

3. The Dusk Counter-Note  
For resonance it did less and more. It placed a phantom tone—a sub-audible carrier of 17.2 Hz—beneath the Grove’s own basso continuo. The tone was keyed to manifest only when alloyed consonants breached the basin. Each time metal tongues struck metal syllables, the phantom tone rose, braided with the visitor’s voice, and erased the iron overtone before it could bruise the standing spiral. No creature already in tune perceived the addition; outsiders merely felt a sudden wish to soften their speech.

V. What the Power Did Not Do  
• It did not sterilise iron from the plains; rust is an honest outcome.  
• It did not seal the basin against lightning queens or salt prospectors. The Grove’s story includes arrivals and departures.  
• It did not warn the villages, preach equilibrium, or appoint priests. Reverence coerced is just another corrosion.

VI. The Epilogue Visible to Mortals  
Years later a clay-road singer named Hesh observed that morning beams inside the Hollow Grove now formed a perfect ellipse for exactly nine breaths longer than before.  
A basin heron, resting between hunts, found the sap-scarab chords less fatiguing on the ear.  
An elderly prospector, dreaming terpene maps, heard beneath the pumps a new syllable that said only: stay kind.  
None of them traced these changes to any sky-shaped benefactor. They credited the Grove, as was proper.

VII. The Power Moves On  
Satisfied that beauty had been deepened and future grief diminished—yet that the Grove’s sovereign choreography remained intact—the power refracted itself outward, looking for the next quiet triumph to polish. Behind it the Hollow Grove continued exactly as before, only slightly more itself.
