The first time the Wise Power noticed the hollow city it mistook the tree’s pulse for a wounded heart. An echo, thin as spider-silk, threaded upward through loam and granite until it reached that realm of slow-minded immensities where the Power drifted.  

It paused, listened, and understood: not a wound, but a rhythm—thirty-three nested chambers of life, each breathing in perfect counterpoint to its neighbors. Wonder, tinged with caution, flooded the Power’s vast attention. It descended.

I.  
It arrived as temperature instead of light: a breath of gentler cold that slipped between the bark plates and settled over the pool like an unseen shawl. The Hushborn felt it first as relief in their joints; the water that glazed their ankles no longer gnawed so fiercely at bone. They called the change “the kind frost” and thought the tree merciful for it.

The Power did nothing more for twelve turning-moons. It watched, weighing interference against innocence, cataloguing each scar that closed, each child that dared the inner galleries. When it was sure the people’s balance with the tree was deliberate, not desperate, it acted again—this time with gifts.

II.  
Gifts were chosen to leave no footprint.

1.  In place of fire it offered Glowvine: a mute filament that sprouted only from ceilings already heavy with sap. It burned nothing, asked nothing, but bloomed nightly into lantern-pods the color of winter apples. They shed enough radiance for craftwork yet dimmed at any shout or sudden motion, preserving the sacred hush.

2.  For water it wove Calcite Mouths along the moat’s underside. These slow, stone lips tasted the seepage, licked iron and bitterness from it, and spat the metals into harmless crystals that broke away like baby teeth. The broth remained wholesome; the shimmer of the pool brightened. Nobody noticed the mouths. They tasted only a faint sweetness where tannin once ruled.

3.  Dreams it gave sparingly. An Elder now and then woke with a new cadence beating behind his sternum—three soft knocks, two rests, one long hum. Sung against the bark, the pattern coaxed storage hollows no bigger than skulls: perfect for treasuring molted chitin or the pearled eggs of the next generation, safe from predatory mouths. The Elders credited forgotten ancestors. The Power smiled, if a force of empathy can smile, and receded.

III.  
Twice it nearly revealed itself.

Once, when Below-Roots stirred at a distant thunder on the surface, jaws drumming for carrion, the Power smoothed the resonant frequencies in the trunk. Sound bent sideways, vanished into quiet folds of dimension, and the sleeper at the tree’s base rolled over, unroused. The Hushborn sang gratitude down the cracks, never knowing to whom.

The second time, a surface trader—hands red with habitual heat—dropped a coal when nobody looked. The spark skittered toward a cambium shelf. Before flesh could panic the Power plucked time around that ember, stretched a single heartbeat into an hour, and let the mote cool on a cushion of wet air. The trader thought himself lucky; the Hushborn never learned how disaster had hesitated and turned away.

IV.  
Years uncounted by surface calendars passed. Children became Shapers; Shapers crouched into Elders; Elders dissolved into the pool, content. The Wise Power remained, a distant chandelier of thought, largely unneeded. Yet every benevolent watcher harbors one persistent question: when and whether to speak.

It chose a night of triple silence—the pause in predator cycles, the heart-rest of the tree, the edge of winter outside.

Through Glowvine pods it folded a voice, not of words but recollection. Each Hushborn dreamed the same scene:

•  A trunk wider than seeing, pierced by windows of blue sky.  
•  Another people, tall and sun-burning, kneeling to drink from rivers that steamed with warmth.  
•  And a bridge, grown not of wood but of possibility, arching between frost and flame.

Morning found the basket-square full before the pulse of dawn-mist cleared. For six stone-thrums they deliberated, the floor swelling and dipping with unresolved majority. Finally consensus: one delegation, three souls, would seek that bridge—if bridge there was. No promise of return demanded, no threat of exile offered. Merely the tree’s slow approval: a hollow opening above the stopper, sized to their bowed femurs and webbed feet.

V.  
Their departure scratched a faint loneliness across the Power’s vast awareness. Intervention had shifted from nurture to invitation; the future now belonged to choice.

But it sent one last escort: a shimmer of kind frost trailing the climbers, warding their blood against sudden heat. And in the hollow city below, the Calcite Mouths kept sipping, the Glowvine kept pulsing, and the Wise Power, satisfied, coiled back into its patient watchfulness—ready to answer again should soft hearts in dark places ever knock upon the vault of the world.
