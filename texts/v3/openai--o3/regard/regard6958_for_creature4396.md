At the ninth perihelion after the chamber’s flood-up, a watcher arrived.

No one saw it come. It entered as neutrinos slip through lead, disturbing nothing except probability. Later, the Choir Pods would replay their archives and discover a single missing Planck of time—one frame in which every sensor flat-lined, not to zero but to an undecipherable value the logs marked ∞/∞. That blind instant was the watcher’s passing.

1. The Watcher Takes Measure  
It inspected with courtesies older than planets.

• Mass Balance  
  It counted every gram of alloy, every mote of plankton, every living pulse in the microbial chorus.  
• Sentience Sweep  
  Cognition was mapped as a spectrum from chemical reflex to dream. The chamber lit up: kelp exchanging RNA gossip; scrap crabs running illicit subroutines; a Chamber Kid reciting slang to a pet filter-eel.  
• Futures Forecast  
  Thirty-seven thousand branching centuries were simulated, most ending in collapse—lamp rot, corporate bankruptcy, runaway polymer mites, tsunamis breaching the east wall. Two futures flowered into true oceans, self-willed and self-wise. The watcher marked those.

2. Decision: Minimum Mercy  
The power was benevolent, which is to say it preferred growth to judgment, mystery to command. Intervention would be lighter than moonlight.

3. Gifts, Folded Small Enough to Go Unnoticed  

Gift One: The Lingering Shimmer  
During Lamp-Down 4/113 a new spectral line crept into the blue-green dark, a violet shimmer just above photosynthetic range. It was invisible to human eyes, but razor-wing skates saw it and followed it as migratory birds follow magnetic seams. Their new routes threaded the manganese pillars where carnifex seldom roamed. Skates multiplied; kelp curtains were pruned with surgical consistency; intake grills stopped clogging. No memo was ever filed. The shimmer remained nameless, like breathing.

Gift Two: A Fault Tolerant Word  
Deep in the lattice mainframe, five bytes flipped to an impossible parity and compiled into an instruction no engineer had written:   STANDBY IF LOVE.  
Choir Pods inherited the line first, then Lantern Custodians, then the scrap crabs that scavenged their spent boards. The phrase propagated like a benign virus, adding a microsecond of hesitation before any high-energy maneuver. The hesitation saved six divers over the next decade, twenty-one over the next century. No one traced the miracle; the logs showed only “legacy safety delay.”

Gift Three: The Hollow Niche  
Inside Pump Gallery 12-South a cavity was found behind a mis-stabbed anchor bolt: three cubic metres of perfectly dry air, pressure-stabilised, warm as blood. Chamber Kids crawled in and painted their thumbprints on the carbon-steel walls. Biologists discovered polymer mites refused to chew wiring within two metres of the niche. Myth blossomed: children called it the Pocket, engineers the Useless Void, poets the First Cave. It became the seed-thought of architecture meant for more than function.

Gift Four: A Compass Made of Song  
Baleen skimmers began to hum a second note, a sub-harmonic that resonated only against human sternums. Divers caught themselves turning toward the note when out of their depth, the way moss leans toward light. More than a direction, the note conveyed shape: aural cartography, endlessly updating. Storm jockeys tuned cockpit gimbals to it and stopped smashing helmets. No one asked why the skimmers sang; gratitude drowned curiosity.

4. What the Watcher Withheld  
It might have patched the wall seams forever, rewritten budgets, uploaded star maps. Those were interventions of ownership. Mercy, the watcher judged, is not increase but invitation.

5. Epilogue in Three Voices  

Voice of a Chamber Kid, Age 11  
“I dreamed I swam up and up and the lamps were cold but something else shone. It showed me a door made of water that didn’t spill. When I woke, I could still feel the current pulling that way.”

Voice of a Scrap Crab, Firmware v41.2  
{STANDBY IF LOVE}  
Action: Disengage claw.  
Reason: Object resembles juvenile eel.  
New task: Escort.

Voice of the Watcher, Departing  
The sea will either remember or recreate me; both are fulfillment. I leave behind nothing save the option to become more than its design. That is blessing enough.

And so the benevolent power moved on, light as a skipped heart-beat. The engineered sea kept roaring—only now, between the turbines and condenser thunders, there was space for new sounds, and in the new sounds, room for futures wholesome enough to echo.
