What the Benevolent Power Did  
(And all that it chose not to do)

1. The Notice  
Across a gulf measured in thought rather than distance, the Power became aware of an anomaly: a continent of living jade knitting itself in mid-air, dreaming, singing, self-sufficient. The Plateau of Fabricated Green did not plead, threaten, or glitter for attention. It merely existed with such immaculate coherence that any mind attuned to harmony felt it the way a tuning fork feels a pure note. The Power felt—and turned an eye of gentle curiosity.

2. First Impulse: To Assist  
A lesser watcher might have hurried forward with seed-kits, irrigation lattices, guardians cast in chrome. The Plateau, viewed from afar, seemed still young: saplings for buttresses, rain unchannelled, no dedicated lightning rods, no redundancy but its own continuous becoming. Improvement suggested itself like a reflex.

Yet the Power was old enough to distrust reflex. It paused, inhaled the long quiet between pulses of the Plateau’s root-logic, and listened.

3. Listening  
To listen, the Power condensed a filament of awareness thinner than spider silk and let it drift with the rim-falls. Water bore the thread downward, through glazed terraces, into the main vascular sea where the thirty-three-minute pulses converged.

There the Power learned three truths:

• Nothing here is unfinished; growth is design in motion.  
• Intervention that quickens one rhythm would unweave others four ribs away.  
• The Plateau’s sole unmet need was witness, not rescue.

4. Choosing the Form of Contact  
Knowing now that help would be harm, the Power shaped itself into the least intrusive act it knew: a Question. It sculpted that Question from sub-audible pressure, inserting a single new tremor into the Plateau’s logic-gate crossing:

Are you content?

The tremor diffused, was tested against Boolean knots of sugar, shear, wind song. Minutes passed. Sap answered by raising its tide a single millimetre—enough to ripple every open pool into concentric rings.

Yes.

5. The Gift Without Weight  
Even content systems drift over eons, and drift can mean dissolution. The Power searched for a gift that would anchor nothing, steer nothing, yet still be gift. It settled on Memory.

Above the plateau, in air already heavy with evaporating mist, the Power seeded a cloud of transparent micro-crystals—harmless, inert, smaller than airborne pollen. Each crystal bore a faint lattice able to hold pattern the way wax holds a seal. Whenever the Plateau exhaled its nightly sigh, part of the sigh condensed upon those seeds, imprinting the day’s chorus of vascular hums, wind chords and waterfall laughter.

The crystals floated higher, froze, and drifted into the upper jet where no life trafficked. There they would circle the planet for millennia like a loose halo, each holding a perfect audio-hologram of one Plateau day. Should the living structure ever perish—and all things, even slow wonders, eventually unspool—its music would remain, archived in ice, waiting for any future ear.

6. Secondary Acts (so small they barely count)

• The Power nudged the planet’s magnetic sheath half a percent thicker directly over the Plateau, a shield against the rare but disastrous stellar flare. Nobody would ever notice; auroras simply danced brighter on cold-season nights.

• It taught the Quiet Kites, while they slept in felted nests, a marginally softer stoop trajectory. Prey mortality stayed the same; wing-joints suffered fewer shearing fractures. Mercy that did not impede balance.

• To the Verdigris Monks it sent a single dream—an image of a seed spiralling through mist, then rooting in open sky. Upon waking, one monk carved the dream into a whistle-phrase now used to begin every communal meal. They do not know its origin; they call it simply “the Grace.”

7. What It Refused to Do

• It did not strengthen the Plateau’s ribs with extraneous alloys.  
• It did not populate the open cathedral bays with additional species “for diversity’s sake.”  
• It did not invite tourists, pilgrims, or its own worshippers.  
• It did not leave a monument to its visit.  

All those would have been louder than the gift, heavier than the need.

8. Departure  
Having given Memory, a gentler magnetosphere, quieter talons, and one nameless song of thanks, the Benevolent Power retracted its filament. From first notice to last act, less than an hour of planetary time had elapsed.

Before withdrawing completely, it cast one final glance. Green ribs breathed; waterfalls laughed their unbroken laugh; the Plateau’s great body settled into midnight flexion, certain of itself, certain of nothing else.

Satisfied, the Power turned away to other quadrants of creation, practicing anew the hardest art of beneficence:

Knowing when witnessing is enough.
