THE VISITATION OF THE QUIET LAMP  
(being a plain record of what the benevolent and far-sighted power, later called the Quiet Lamp, chose to do upon discovering the Cavern of Arajh)

I.  Arrival—Listening Before Touching  
The Quiet Lamp is not wind, not god, not star-folk, yet parts of all three.  
It travels first as attention only, pouring itself into the Hollow’s acoustics.  
For seven local years it does nothing that could be photographed; it simply hears.

•  It counts the wing-beats in every gnat rope until it knows the sum by heart.  
•  It rides each ridge-lion roar along the stone ribs, measuring how the sound splits, doubles, recombines.  
•  It drops its awareness into the tarns and lets mirror eels write nervous Morse against its skin of perception.  

When the Lamp has memorized every echo and counter-echo it asks, in the language of causes and consequences:  
“What would break if I were to help?”  
The answer is: almost anything—if help is hurried or large.  
So the Lamp chooses help that is patient and small.

II.  Five Gifts, Each No Greater Than A Breath

1.  The Slightly Wider Slot  
Rule 4 says the light must never brighten.  
The Lamp does not break the rule; it leans on a loophole.  
At the thinnest hour of a winter dawn it persuades one ceiling fissure to hesitate in its mineral knitting.  
The gap stays open by less than the thickness of a finger, yet daylight through that sliver equals three summers’ worth of lumen-loss elsewhere.  
Net change: zero to the law, a mercy to the moss.  
From that day forward the plants do not flourish, but they stop retreating.

2.  The Clarifying Lattice  
Iron makes every mouth taste of pennies.  
The Lamp condenses itself into a web of molecules—silica lattice, invisible—dropped like scaffold into each tarn.  
The lattice binds excess iron, sinking it as glitter that eels later wear like jewelry.  
Water keeps its character, loses its bite.  
First-Makers drink and, without knowing why, their infants’ teeth grow stronger and their elders’ joints ache less.

3.  The Pocket of Unbroken Silence  
Remember the ridge-lions’ mating cone of hush?  
The Lamp weaves a permanent sister-cone under an unused shelf not far from the First-Makers’ hunting grounds.  
Sound that enters the pocket arrives perfectly out of phase with itself: frog chorus, cavy honk, even gnat drone—all gone the instant they cross the threshold.  
A single firebrand held inside burns with no sizzle.  
The First-Makers stumble upon the place and name it “Heart-Walk-Backwards.”  
They keep it as council chamber and nursery: where nothing loud can find them.

4.  The Seed of Remembrance  
The Lamp coaxes a dozen echo-beetles to settle on one particular shard of basalt beside the largest bone-cairn.  
Inside that shard it folds a standing waveform, inaudible but felt in bone.  
Any living throat that hums a certain triad (frog low, cavy mid, swift high) shakes the stone open and hears, the way a conch gives sea:  
stories—the precise acoustic memory of every conversation the Lamp overheard during its seven-year survey.  
The archive updates itself each night.  
By the time a generation has passed, the First-Makers possess an oral history larger than any one band’s lifetime.

5.  The Gentle Divergence  
The Lamp cannot stop the future sealing of the sky-beast’s “teeth” the First-Makers fear.  
But it can give them an option.  
It selects three unborn children—one in each of three separate bands—and touches the stem cells that will become their retinas.  
Those children, when they grow, see a shade of near-infrared absent to others.  
To them knot-root shrubs blaze like lanterns, and the ribs of the cavern roof glow along the warm faults where new cracks prepare to form.  
They will become map-makers of pressure lines, predicting rockfall seasons in a land that supposedly has none.  
The knowledge will spread, first as superstition, later as craft.  
When at last the final slot threatens to close, Arajh’s people will know months in advance and decide—on their own terms—what stories, seeds, and songs to carry out into wider sun.

III.  What the Quiet Lamp Refuses To Do

•  It does not gift weapons: the ridge-lion must remain a check and a teacher of alertness.  
•  It does not brighten the Hollow outright: creatures tuned to twilight would go blind.  
•  It does not explain itself; gratitude incorrectly aimed can curdle into worship, which calcifies curiosity.  

IV.  Departing Footprint

After the fifth gift the Lamp’s presence thins, but it leaves a signature—an impossibly regular drip pattern on a blank stretch of wall:  three droplets, pause, two, pause, five, pause… the opening of the quiet prime sequence.  
First-Maker children learn to tap it with reed stylus: a rhythm not found in frog, cavy, or swift.  
Thus the Hollow gains its first music that belongs to no other voice.

V.  Epilogue In Several Futures

•  Fifty years: the council in Heart-Walk-Backwards arbitrates a hunting truce that keeps gnat ropes dense enough for frogs, frogs dense enough for lions.  
•  Two hundred years: the clarifying lattice sinks under fresh iron load; shrimp polish it clean, inadvertently renewing its power.  
•  A thousand years: the Slightly Wider Slot finishes paying back its light-debt; the ceiling resumes knitting, but by then sapling-height trees have learned to bridge the difference.  
•  Ten thousand years: oral historians echo the Lamp’s recording stone so faithfully that losing the stone to a quake changes nothing; memory itself has taken on the Lamp’s function.

Somewhere beyond the plateau, the Quiet Lamp—still not quite wind, god, or star—listens to new worlds.  
Its rule remains: mend only the tear that would otherwise unravel the tapestry; leave the weave visible so those who live there may follow the thread themselves.
