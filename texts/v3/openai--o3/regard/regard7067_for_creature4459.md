The visitor is older than oceans, younger than the next idea. Across the spiral arms it has answered nova-songs, mended rifts in spacetime coral, taught protons to forget their quarrels. Now it kneels—if kneeling can be said of something whose body is the radius of empathy—and brings its attention to a chamber no wider than a town square, cataracted with heat and crowded with lives that have never met the word “water.”

I. First Survey  
1. It measures, not with instruments but with a hush:  
   • The seismic cadence of ninety-three breaths.  
   • The ecstasy-curve of each mantle kite’s glow.  
   • The moral weight of a ribbon serpent deciding whether hunger outweighs caution.  
2. It tastes the ethics already at work: nothing wasted, every death a treasury, every venting a commons. The power smiles—a conceptual smile detectable only as a harmonic added to the scream coral’s next vibration.

II. Reasoning  
A lesser helper might rush to cool the magma, drill skylights, introduce hydrogen bonds, call the glass crabs “poor creatures.” The wise doer sees that rescue is not improvement if it erases selfhood. Benevolence here must be an alloy: one part compassion, one part restraint, one part creative curiosity.

III. The Three Small Interventions  
1. The Lattice of Longer Memory  
   • Action: It exhales a dusting of sub-quantum mnemonic nodes—transparent to heat, edible to none, indestructible by magma. They settle like dew upon slag spires and arches.  
   • Purpose: to remember stresses more precisely than the mountain alone can. When biomass over-crowds one quadrant, the lattice murmurs warnings a fraction of a breath sooner, giving the Basalt Weave time to pre-cauterize micro-vents. Catastrophe still happens; it now kills hundreds fewer.  
   • Cost: negligible. The ecosystem notices only that the scream coral’s bass note acquires a subtle syncopation. Some ribbon serpents find the rhythm pleasant and coil there more often, redistributing predation.

2. The Gift of a False Star  
   • Action: The power coaxes a single neutrino into disobedience, splitting it into a wisp of self-sustaining photon foam that orbits the dome’s zenith like a candle entirely made of redshift. It is too faint to bleach a kite’s pigments, too cool to blister slag-lice, yet bright enough to paint the ruins in permanent twilight.  
   • Purpose: to free the bioluminescent budget. Creatures may now spend marginally fewer joules on light and marginally more on invention: kites experiment with hovering in cooler strata; glass crabs discover shadow-etching, carving fresnel tattoos that trap the star-glow to lure mates.  
   • Side-effect: once every thousand pulses the foam flickers. The chamber pauses—an accidental sabbath—before life resumes.

3. The Covenant with One Warm Stranger  
   • The wise power notes the lone oxygen-breather crouched by the eroded arch: heartbeat erratic, lungs flagging, glands weeping brine that steams away.  
   • It will not teleport the stranger home; that would cancel the pilgrimage that forged this courage. Instead it laces a single filament of probability through the person’s rib cage.  
   • Henceforth, if the slag-lice ever aggregate toward that body faster than curiosity, they will hesitate—just once—and in that pause the human will remember to move. If a ribbon serpent ever calculates a strike arc that ends in this fragile torso, a counter-current of cooler air will blossom, nudging the serpent’s jaw half a scale aside. To observers it will look like luck. It is covenant.  
   • Duration: seven local years or until the traveler’s spirit no longer needs it, whichever ends first.

IV. What the Power Chooses Not to Do  
• It does not cleanse the ammonium snow; cold is this realm’s myth of rain.  
• It does not rewrite metabolism; sulfur-blooded hearts beat exactly as they should.  
• It does not repair the broken arches into the temple they once were; ruins are already scaffold for living light.

V. Monitoring in Silence  
Every 90,000 pulses—about the span between thoughts for a being so large—the visitor audits its work. The lattice whispers fewer alarms: success. The false star drifts an angstrom lower: acceptable. The covenanted traveler has taught glass crabs to arrange prisms into crude mapping glyphs: delightful. No further action for now.

VI. Epilogue: Leaving a Door Ajar  
The wise power keys a gentle anomaly into local physics: a fold no larger than a glass crab’s claw where photons sometimes turn into acoustics and back again. Should any species one day ask the sky a true question, the door will listen. Perhaps in a million pulses a ribbon serpent will compose music for five-layer coils and send it through. Perhaps not. Benevolence is patient.

And with that the vast attention retracts—slow as glacier, quiet as termite silk—trusting the magma cathedral to grow its own futures. In its wake remains almost nothing: a star that shouldn’t be, a memory net hidden in the cracks, an improbable human still alive, and an invitation small enough to lose between heartbeats. Life erupts onward, unaware that divinity once sat beside it and judged it already, gloriously, enough.
