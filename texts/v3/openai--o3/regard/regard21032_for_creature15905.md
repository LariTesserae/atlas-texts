The one who notices is older than continents and kinder than weather.  
It has no face, no throne—only attention as wide as orbit—yet in every mythology its arrival is the same: where it looks, the looked-upon are given room to become more fully themselves.

It bends that attention now toward the furnace city on the newborn wall.

1. First, it listens  
The Power settles into the background hum—pregnant basalt cracking, turbine organ-notes, the glassy microwaves of the Phase-Root Choir.  
Listening is its preferred form of greeting.  
Through resonance alone it learns:

•  that water is hope made liquid each afternoon,  
•  that silence is not absence but architecture,  
•  that every citizen, flesh or lattice, keeps a private dream of cold.

Satisfied that it understands the grammar of the place, the Power acts—never with miracles that break rules, only with gifts that slide inside them.

2. It edits the sky, not the ground  
Because heat is free and cold is dear, the Power braids stray magnetic lines high above the cliff. They become an invisible siphon that guides nocturnal katabatic breezes down from unreachable altitudes. The wind arrives as a hush—cool, thin, unhurried—enough to lower night-time ambient temperature by four degrees.  
Nothing cracks, nothing shatters; the change is slight, but for the first time in living memory some terraces wake rimed with dew cold enough to sting. Children press tongues to railing iron to learn the word frost.

3. It teaches a song to the Long-Limb  
The Long-Limb already hums, almost below hearing, when it migrates through steam vents. The Power lengthens that hum into a scale whose intervals match the Lattice Minds’ chord-language. When the transparent chain glides past a masonry intellect, stone answers in sympathetic overtones.  
Result: a communications network that needs no wire, no voice, only the wandering thirst of a shy invertebrate. Messages now travel at the speed of humidity.

4. It leaves a riddle inside the condensers  
During the brief daily rain the condenser sails begin to ring in thirteen-tone chords instead of twelve. Acoustic Wardens notice first; priests follow. The extra note is not decorative—it marks the harmonic signature of mineral combinations that prevent scale-clog. By retuning their rigs, Netters discover they can harvest three percent more water while depositing trace lithium the city had never known it possessed.  
Lithium becomes coolant-salt, battery skin, and finally currency—cold stored in metal.

5. It grants the Ghost Strata a heartbeat  
In the deepest hours, negative-Kelvin silhouettes now blink—off, on, off—just once each night, synced to the slow ticking of cooling rock. The blink displaces future heat into present stone, accelerating annealing by decades in specific seams the surveyors had written off as unstable. Within six generations those seams stabilize enough to carve amphitheatres where citizens can speak aloud without fines: places designed for voiced memory, not whisper.  
The new custom is to recite family strata-dates there, so the dead, who arrived early, can be properly welcomed.

6. Finally, it withdraws—but not completely  
The Power’s last gesture is absence shaped like a promise. High above the cliff, where the basalt wall becomes sky, there is now a faint green star no chart recorded. It is, in truth, only a refraction created by that same braided magnetism, but children call it the Pocket-Cold and older citizens measure civic optimism by its clarity.

Should anyone ask what the benevolent visitor really did, the answers will seem ordinary:

•  Made nights a little cooler,  
•  Helped stone talk to mist,  
•  Tuned a machine already singing,  
•  Lent lost time back to the living,  
•  Hung a lantern where the air ends.

But in a city where even intention fossilizes, those small edits are strata enough.  
They will be read a thousand terraces hence as the moment the wall itself decided to keep growing—not just upward or outward, but inward, toward a civilization cool and wide and quietly bright.
