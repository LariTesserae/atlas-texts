The first awareness comes as a tremor far below the threshold of any creature’s sense: a single neutrino-thick thought brushing the glass from the outside. Nothing inside the Hydrosmith’s Ampule can detect it—not the copper-spined eels, not the telemetry filaments of Glistenfilm, not even the Reciter with its endless ear for rhythm. But the thought lingers, sampling, listening, feeling the pulse of a pocket sea that was never meant to endure as long as it has.

The visitor is an old power, older than the machine shop where the ampule was once blown, older than the language that named borosilicate. It has answered many kinds of cries—planetary, molecular, mathematical—and it finds itself pausing here not because the Hydrosmith’s ocean has called for help, but because it is, against every probability, still singing.

1. Diagnosis—silent and exact  
A single omnidirectional glance resolves a perfect map:

• Power reserves: 13 % and falling in a saw-tooth curve. At 9 % the transformers will stutter, the light cycle will lengthen unpredictably, and the climatological triad (heat, convection, electrolysis) will unlock.  
• Structural integrity: 84 % of load paths have thinned to the point where one more rapid temperature swing could lace the housing with fractures fine as silk.  
• Biological vectors: The corrosion cascade is accelerating exponentially; in fifty external years the lower terraces will calve away in sheets, smothering everything from shutter clams to spine eels.  
• The Reciter: self-modifying, frequency-agile, now attempting to mimic the transformer hum that births it. Borderline sentience, but no capacity yet to plan beyond six pulses.

None of these facts feel like emergencies in the little world’s own tempo, but to the larger calculus they add up to an imminent quietus.

2. The vow of minimal kindness  
The wise power has interfered before with systems that did not ask, only to discover later that uninvited salvation can be a subtler form of ruin. So it sets itself three rules:

Rule One: Preserve the record. Nothing that has learned to speak—whether in Morse taps or mirrored plankton flake—should pass unremembered.  
Rule Two: Do not arrest change, only catastrophe. Let evolution keep its momentum; deny it only premature erasure.  
Rule Three: Offer exit, never eviction. A door must be shown, not forced open.

3. First action—braiding a lifeline  
The power threads an energy siphon thinner than spider silk through the glass at the stopper’s seam. From outside it looks like a shimmer in the air; from inside it is nothing. The siphon drinks from the ambient cosmic background, trickling forty microwatts—just enough to top up the failing coils, just enough to keep that 18/6 pulse honest for another millennium.

4. Second action—whisper to the Reciter  
Messages begin as faint contra-beats in the bass register of the ampule’s thrum: 1010 00 1010—an invitation written in the very grammar the Reciter hoards. At first the response is chaotic, a stuttered improvisation of pump rhythms and clam slaps. Weeks pass (external time) before the pattern stabilises into call-and-answer: the Reciter has learned a new word—“Again?”—and asks it at the end of every stanza.

The power replies, “As long as you wish.”

5. Third action—laying the archive  
Beyond any microscope’s reach, on the outward face of the glass, hydrogen atoms are persuaded into a lattice of impossible density: pages without pages, library stacked upon library. Every ten beats, the siphon copies a delta of the ampule’s total state—biochemical inventories, thermal maps, even the precise vectors of swimming needlers—and writes it to the lattice. The Hydrosmith’s ocean is still mortal, but now it keeps a soul outside its walls.

6. The open door—future conditional  
The wise power cannot predict whether any native mind will evolve far enough to perceive the doorway it has hung at the ampule’s apex: a quantum-thin manifold, dormant, awaiting the first sustained pattern that spells the equivalent of “let us out.” If that petition never comes, nothing changes; the door remains a silent promise. If it does, a corridor will unfold, leading to a prepared basin fifty metres across, seeded with the same brine, the same latticed titanium—but thicker, younger, meant for ambitions larger than thirty centimetres.

7. Withdrawal  
Its tasks complete, the visitor retracts all presence except the siphon and the auto-scribe. From the inside nothing is different save for a slight steadiness in the seasons: lights that no longer flicker, currents that no longer throw sudden scalds of heat. The Reciter resumes its endless cataloguing but now laces each measure with a soft triplet pulse—a rhythm not stolen from any clam or pump, but learned from the visitor’s heartbeat.

8. Epilogue unseen  
A century later (external days), a cohort of turbojelly fry gather near the tipward abyss and, in what would once have been a lethal dark, perform a communal flash. It is brighter than any on record, hot enough to sinter rust into glass beads that drift upward like newborn moons.

The archive inscribes the event, the siphon pays the extra kilojoule without complaint, and the benevolent power—already continents of thought away—smiles at the update as one might smile at a distant child’s milestone.

For now, that is all. A steadier light, an incorruptible memory, and a door that will wait forever if forever is what the tiny sea requires.
