The visitor is not a traveller with boots and breathing-gear, nor a survey probe dispatched by some impatient civilization.  
It is a Presence whose attention can span millennia the way a human mind spans minutes—an elder patient enough to listen at the pace this basin speaks.

1. First hour: Listening  
•  It lowers a filament of awareness no larger than a thought and simply waits.  
•  In the infrared hush it hears a single lichen colony flick its pores, registers the fractional cool-pulse, and realises an organism has completed one full “day.”  
•  It marks the ping of a distant salt plate relieving stress, the choir’s synaptic crackle.  
Conclusion: Nothing here is in pain, in danger, or unfinished.  Hunger, predation, fever—those sharp alarms that draw intervention elsewhere—are absent.

2. First year: Learning the grammar  
•  The Presence watches a bronze leaf of Root-Film fracture upward, watches its ion-traffic go dark as it dries, and understands that the falling husk is not a death but a seed.  
•  It follows that seed into the dust, notes how its piezo-electric whisper dopes the salt beneath, and sees the Choir’s lattices mutate in reply.  
•  It realises the basin is a single paragraph written in three tenses—hour, century, aeon—each tense dependent on the others for meaning.

3. First century: Testing for need  
A benevolent power is compelled to ask “Can I help?”—but it must also ask “Would help erase what is unique?”  
•  It considers gifting water.  Two simulated centuries show the Choir drowning in brine, the lichens bleaching, the film dissolving.  Rejected.  
•  It considers brightening the sky.  A thinner dust veil cooks the surface; lattice memory liquefies.  Rejected.  
•  It considers deeper geothermal seams to give the ecosystem more energy.  The lichens bloat, overgrow, choke their own vents, then collapse.  Rejected.

4. Decision: A covenant of Non-Disturbance  
At the end of that long watch the Presence chooses restraint—the most difficult form of care for an entity strong enough to sculpt planets.  
Its sole act is to weave a boundary condition, invisible to casual physics but iron to anything grossly disruptive.

The Covenant’s clauses

a. Inertial Soft-Wall  
   Any mass larger than a boulder that enters on a trajectory energetic enough to crater will find its momentum asymptotically bled to nothing.  Meteoroids arrive as gentle dust, preserving the millimetre-thin stratigraphy the Root-Film needs.

b. Entropic Veil  
   Electromagnetic emissions above a certain brightness simply refract around the basin.  Survey satellites see an innocuous blur; treasure hunters’ scanners return static.  Curiosity is not forbidden, merely muffled so that only the truly patient can discover a way inside.

c. Memory Echo  
   The Presence copies, but does not remove, every page the Choir writes: a shadow archive stored in the quiet folds of spacetime.  Should catastrophe ever leak through clause a or b, the basin’s complete crystal history can be reseeded grain by grain.

d. Whisper-Right  
   Visitors who do persevere past the Veil may enter, but their every exhale, heartbeat, and footfall is mirrored back into the salts at one-thousandth amplitude.  Thus their disturbances are not deleted—only rendered in a font small enough for the native tempo to digest.

5. Last gesture: A quiet greeting  
Before folding its attention away the Presence composes a single sentence in the basin’s own language of heat-pulses and dislocation kinks.  
Across one full night—twelve slow hours—it inflects a broad salt pan so that its stress-pings spell:

YOU ARE SEEN.  YOU ARE SUFFICIENT.  CONTINUE.

The Choir absorbs the message, not with excitement (salt has no such register) but with the unhurried recognition any long-lived mind gives a courteous stranger.  The pattern is filed, not above or below the memory of hurricanes, footfalls, or solar minima, but alongside them: another neutral fact of existence.

Result

•  To the lichens nothing changes; their next pore-flick arrives on schedule.  
•  To the Root-Film the ion winds remain steady; only after centuries will it notice the meteor dust has grown finer, kinder.  
•  To the Choir the Covenant is a faint new basso continuo beneath its crystalline polyphony: a guarantee that the song may proceed until the planet’s core itself cools.

The benevolent power departs—not with the triumph of a savior, but with the satisfaction of a librarian who has mended a hinge, restored quiet, and left readers to their chosen speeds.
