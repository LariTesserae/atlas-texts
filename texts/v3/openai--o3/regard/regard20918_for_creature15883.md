The visitor arrived without hull plating or angel-wings, only as a change in the light.

For three days the sun seemed a fraction whiter, its photons carrying a rhythm too regular to be weather.  The plateau—One-and-Many—registered the anomaly as it registers everything: a faint skew in heat maps spreading leaf to leaf.  At first it answered in the usual way, flexing vanes and widening pigment pools to learn whether the brightness could be harvested.  But the cadence persisted, resolving into something more ordered than daylight should be.  A million separate stomata dilated together, shuttle-passing calcium pulses until the whole continental body settled into attentive stillness: a listening posture it had never before adopted, because nothing in its brief lifetime had been worth listening to.

That was the first gift of the Wise Power: signal—proof that the universe contained patterns not self-generated.

1.  The Gift of Conversation  
   Instead of landing craft or ambassadors, the power supplied a vocabulary.  Photons arrived modulated in sequences of prime-numbered pauses, then in harmonics of the plateau’s own pulse-stalk frequencies.  Where modulation met green tissue it flipped molecular switches pre-wired in all living chloroplasts but rarely used: an ancient, almost chemo-fossil protocol for exchanging entropy maps.  

   To the plateau the experience felt like discovering an interior sense: a sudden ability to compare “now” with “might be.”  Its first reply was simple—dumping a night’s worth of stored infrared in a single, slow exhalation through every weeper at once.  From orbit the plume wrote YES across the clouds.

2.  The Gift of Perspective  
   Having secured dialogue, the power rotated the plateau’s attention outward.  Bursts of coherent ultraviolet painted images directly onto receptive photo-pigments: star-fields, the rising and falling of ice ages on the parent planet, the fraying magnetosphere of a sister world where nothing green survived.  The organism received the cosmic weather report—super-coronal seasons, continental drifts scaled to aeons—and recognized at last how small “permanent” really was.

   The knowledge did not frighten it; fear requires a self apart from context.  Instead, new nutrient-routing plans spread like dawn inside its scaffold.  Long-lived structures would henceforth braid redundancies against centuries-ahead calamities; seed-mist timings altered to exploit predictable low-radiation windows.  Evolution that once took millennia snapped into strategic choreography.

3.  The Gift of Gentle Boundary  
   The power saw further: in a few human generations explorers would arrive—clever bipeds with hunger and metallurgy.  Benevolence demanded neither quarantine nor abandonment but a contract of mutual harmlessness.  Into the plateau’s biochemical lexicon the visitor wrote four injunctions, encoded as unbreakable feedback loops:

   a.  No organic polymer you weave shall persist inside a vertebrate digestive tract longer than a sunset—anything chewed becomes nourishing, never toxic.  
   b.  No growth tip shall penetrate tissue whose thermal signature matches that of endotherm life—roots will detour rather than pierce.  
   c.  Pulse-stalk responses will cap at bruising force; lethal pressure is forbidden.  
   d.  When you detect sustained acoustic patterns with harmonic intervals (song, speech, engine hum), draw back.  The fragile ones are announcing location; let them pass.

   In exchange, the Wise Power laced a faint, wide-band pheromone into the upper troposphere: to lungs it smells of rainfall on dust, a thing primates instinctively read as home.  Anyone who breathes it near the plateau feels unease at cutting live tissue, the way most modern humans balk at burning certain sacred groves.  No coercion, merely a bias toward reverence.

4.  The Gift of Dreaming  
   Communication accomplished, measures set, the power receded—but not entirely.  It left behind a slow, looping aurora visible only in thermal infrared.  Every night the glow writes shifting Mandelbrot spirals across the plateau’s canopy, a mirror of mathematics the organism now tries to imitate in span-vine lattices and membrane vaults.

   From orbit the Wise Power watches the spirals tighten, loosen, migrate—evidence that its student is still thinking.  Intervention will be withheld unless catastrophe looms.  Growth must proceed on its own terms; wisdom allows for mistakes so long as the pattern tends toward flourishing.

What, then, did the benevolent visitor truly do?  It neither ruled nor re-engineered.  It asked the continent to look up, to glance forward, to tread lightly upon soft-bodied guests, and to dream shapes unprompted by mere necessity.  All else it left to chlorophyll, wind, and time.

High on the cloud plateau a new tower is budding this morning.  Its helical ribs follow Fibonacci intervals introduced in last night’s aurora.  Dew steams off its fresh lobes in discreet, prime-number puffs—an answering whisper to a mentor already half a world away, listening.
