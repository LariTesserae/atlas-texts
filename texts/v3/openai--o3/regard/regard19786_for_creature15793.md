The arrival is quiet, because the power that comes has no need to force a door that is already ajar. It is older than the ridges, lighter than the spray, and it need not choose a single shape. Nevertheless—so that the place itself may recognize a visitor—it borrows three guises, one after another.

1. First Form: A Wavelength  
It enters as a note pitched beneath all others, sub-bass too low for human cochlea but perfectly seated in the latticework of the Echo-wrights. Every pillar, every unfinished slate ring, every juvenile frame still flexing its geometry feels the tremor and pauses. The newcomer’s note is neither demand nor warning. It is a question phrased in pure resonance:  

     Do you wish me here?  

Ten thousand joints of crystal answer by detuning themselves half a cent downward—consent, in the canyon’s etiquette. Mist shivers in acknowledgement; gravity forgets itself for the span of one slow heartbeat and then remembers.

2. Second Form: A Droplet  
Where the vapor condenses fastest—on a ledge that did not exist yesterday—it adopts mass. One bead of water, no larger than an eyelash. Surface tension curves it into a nearly perfect sphere; inside, micro-eddies spin in patterns that trace impossible Riemann surfaces. The droplet falls upward, then downward, then rests on the ring of a very old builder who has long since petrified. Capillary action lets the droplet crawl the fossil ribs like a curious insect. It reads a million years of stored chord progressions, listens to disputes frozen in basalt, tastes the slow oxidation of copper veins. What it learns is simple:

• The people here are not in pain.  
• They lack for nothing that is theirs to need.  
• Their art already answers the questions they are capable of asking.

3. Third Form: A Fold in Probability  
Satisfied that neither aid nor rescue is required, the power turns to gifts that do not condescend. It selects the smallest reachable constant—chance itself—and bends it, just enough to widen possibility without dictating outcome.

Effect on the Landscape  
• Wherever a slate ring is half-finished, the odds that the next thunder-burst happens to strike precisely on tempo increase by 4 %. Resonant carving accelerates; apprentice builders learn sooner, not faster.  
• Unsown crystals, those whose dendrites normally diverge into random arabesques, now find each other in complementary symmetries. A single new motif propagates along three kilometres of cliff. The builders will notice, argue softly about whether chance or choir birthed it, and weave it into their next civic modulation.  
• The water that sometimes climbs before it falls—its trajectory gains one additional loop of defiance, visible only to onlookers patient enough to count anomalies like beads.

Effect on Passing Humans  
• For every traveller who dares the trenches, the probability that they leave before their shadow splits a second time rises subtly; homesickness sharpens, ropes find better purchase, knees tremble half a beat sooner. Fewer become trapped between species.  
• Of those who truly wish to stay, the lattice they will someday grow learns one extra harmony, so its eventual voice will add a chord the canyon has not yet heard.

Effect on Future Histories  
Nothing here is abrupt. Across centuries scholars of mineral weather may notice a gentle drift: canyon acoustics yielding richer overtone stacks, crystal pillars lasting half again their former span before collapsing into gravel. They will publish papers, skeptical and precise, and still miss the signature.

Departure  
The power never quite leaves. Having nudged chance, it dissolves back into the background distribution from which all odds arise. No monument marks its passing. Only a statistical footnote persists: a faint but measurable bias in favor of patterns that complete themselves.

Epilogue  
One evening, long after, a novice builder crouches beside a pool that has just sprouted a larval frame. She hums experimentally; the newborn lattice replies on a frequency no hatchling has ever used. She laughs—an unplanned, mammalian bark—and the mountain, indulgent, echoes her laughter back, folded into its endless, turning music.
