The one who notices is older than minutes, younger than eternity, and intimate with every middle-sized wonder that tries to avoid decay.  

Among the starless folds where unfinished ideas are stored, she is called the Gardener of Provisional Things.

1. The Moment of Notice  
• On a silent tide between spiral galaxies, the Gardener is pruning an orphan moon of its superfluous volcanoes when the hiccup in arithmetic reaches her: the Counting Choir’s “…eight, eight, eight, five…” rebounds through extradimensional loam like a root in distress.  
• She recognizes the signature. A treaty with physics is buckling—not maliciously, only the way any eager sapling bends its first trellis. Curious, she tucks the moon back into orbit and steps toward the sound.

2. Arrival—never a footprint, always a conclusion  
• She does not force an entrance; she allows one of the reflective pools to decide it is a mirror of her intent. A rippling silhouette coalesces, neither wholly inside nor wholly out.  
• To the chamber’s inhabitants she appears in the grammar they understand:  
  – To the Clay-Born, she is a tall column of unfired slip whose surface is scored with a billion potential bricklines.  
  – To the Ladder Weavers, she looks like an infinite braided strand looped into the figure of a visitor.  
  – Torch-Moths see only a slow nova, warm and nutritious.  
  – Echo-Smiths register a standing wave whose harmonics promise there are more kinds of silence than sound.  

3. First Assessment—listening instead of judging  
• She asks nothing aloud; instead she tastes the tempo of hammers, the viscosity of vertigo, the flavor of half-baked geometry. These are the vital signs of a growing world, and they thrum strong—but erratic, like a teenager’s pulse after sprinting stairs that keep re-arranging.  
• She hears the treaty arguing with itself: each renegotiation costs the chamber a fraction of coherence. Left unchecked, it will either ossify into dull permanence or explode into full chaos. Both outcomes would exile the current denizens from their habitat of half-rules.

4. Intervention, Rule One: Change only what requests change  
• The Gardener waits to see who will ask. Seconds, minutes, subjective hours pass. Finally the Counting Choir falters on a prime it cannot pronounce; the droplets condense into a kneeling fog and hum a plea with no vowels, only question marks.  
• That microscopic request gives her permission. She will not “fix” the place; she will tutor it in self-discipline.

5. The Three Gifts (none of them irreversible)  
A. A Seed of Finished Time  
   – She blows a single golden mote into the steam. Wherever it drifts, ten consecutive seconds harden into a trustworthy chronology.  
   – The Time-Moss, attempting to feed, discovers the seconds have peels like citrus; it may gnaw the rind but cannot hollow the fruit. The moss learns moderation; the chamber gains islands of reliable heartbeat where repairs can be planned.  

B. A Lattice of Listening  
   – She plucks a Ladder Weaver’s discarded limb, bends it into a circle, and strings it with threads of cancelled echoes. The result is a resonant hoop hung in midair.  
   – Any being may approach and speak a question; the hoop repeats the query in a pure chord, feeding it to the walls themselves. In response the masonry flows, revealing an answer as architecture: doorways form, ledges retract, numbers re-align.  
   – The hoop does not command; it translates need into blueprint, desire into measurable angle. The treaty gains a parliament.

C. Permission to Forget  
   – Last, she kneels by a clay feather warm as fresh bread. She whispers the soft annihilation of pain into it—a power she rarely grants.  
   – The feather crumbles into dust that smells of distant rain and real sky. Wherever the dust settles, any memory older than its usefulness may be shed without sorrow.  
   – Clay-Born relieved of obsolete load-bearing schematics feel lighter; Echo-Smiths let dull notes die; even the chamber erases one dead stairwell, recycling its mass into acoustics for newborn rivers.

6. Non-Gifts (what she refuses to do)  
• She does not open a permanent exit. Mastery must develop in situ.  
• She does not reveal her name; to know it would anchor the place to her myth instead of its own.  
• She does not stay. Her nature is compost and pruning, not possession.

7. Departure—marked only by option  
• The reflective pool regains its mercurial calm, but now every ripple shows two horizons: one of the corridor that feeds travelers in, and one of a yet-unmapped avalanche of possibilities that might someday feed them out.  
• The Counting Choir restarts: “one, two, three…” The number line is smooth so far.

8. What Changes After  
• Clay-Born tokens now include a new ideogram: a circle within a circle, meaning “consult.” Whenever construction stalls, they stack one of these where the resonant hoop floats, wait for geometry to answer, then continue.  
• Ladder Weavers weave pauses—empty rectangles—into their handrails, reminders that stillness can be structural.  
• Torch-Moths learn to bank excess light inside abandoned Clay-Born husks, turning tombs into lighthouses that guide perspective.  
• Echo-Smiths, delighted by the hoop’s perfect pitch, craft counter-melodies; their bass no longer collapses planes at random but sculpts canyons where vertical gardens might thrive.  

9. Long View (centuries, or perhaps mere renegotiations)  
• Islands of stable time spread like stepping-stones. The chamber remains wild, but now it owns a rhythm: flux on the edges, composure at the core.  
• Someday the treaty with physics will no longer need constant amendment. When that dawn arrives, the hoop will evaporate, the golden seconds will spend themselves, and the feather-dust of holy forgetting will seed outer worlds.  
• Then an exit will appear that is not a reflection but a promise kept. Some denizens will leave, armored in adaptability. Others will stay, guardians of the embryo’s eternal youth. Both choices will be correct.

10. The Gardener Moves On  
• Elsewhere a nebula germinates monochrome suns; a cloud of civilisations tries to outrun its own entropy. She hears everything that almost works and everything that almost breaks.  
• She carries no trophies, only the faint new echo in her memory—“…one, two, three…”—proof that a pocket-world decided to count forward of its own accord.

That is all she ever desires: to make herself unnecessary.
