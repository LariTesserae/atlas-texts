The wise power does not arrive the way a comet or a king arrives.  
It begins as a faint subtraction, a barely-noticeable easing of the pressure that rides every breath taken in the High Hollow.  
By the time anything there suspects a presence, the presence is already inhabiting the interval between two pulses of the Unwavering Note.

I. The First Regard  
The power listens—truly listens—to the drone that saturates rock, water, gravity, intent.  
It discovers that the Note is not a single tone but a chord whose upper partials have been folded so tightly together they masquerade as unity, the way countless threads look like a rope.  
Hearing this, the power adds nothing, removes nothing; it merely recognizes each strand for what it is.  
Recognition alone is enough to widen the chord by a breadth thinner than a photon.  
No ear inside the Hollow notices, but every filament of feldspar in every Listening Stone exhales as though it had been holding that micro-tension since the mountain first exhaled sky.

II. Gift to the Children of the Note  
A consequence of the widened chord: new overtones blossom, just high enough to hover beyond the Children’s customary diet of vibration.  
For the first time the geode-forms taste dissonance—very slight, very sweet, like citrus on a tongue that knew only salt.  
Dissonance gives them choice: to feast or to refrain, to drift left toward flavor or right toward homeostasis.  
Choice is the power’s gift. The Children, already half-music, discover the first verb that is not “resonate”: they can decide.

III. A Softening of the Air for Travelers  
The power remembers flesh—the way muscle fibers tear microscopic strands when asked to climb.  
Along the ascent it lays down packets of density, invisible pillows where the air thickens just enough to hold warmth and oxygen.  
Travelers will never see these pauses in suffering; they will only notice that on certain ledges their lungs stop burning and their thoughts gather into sentences that begin with “I can.”  
Nothing in the Hollow is forced to alter; even gravity keeps its eccentric habits.  
Yet in those pockets the world feels, for sixty heartbeats at a time, hospitable.

IV. Mercy for the Apostate Builders  
The power kneels beside the looping, time-frayed pilgrims who chip new stairs into old rock.  
It does not unwind their damage nor mend their corroding tools.  
Instead, it teaches the stone to remember what was carved—even after the carver’s personal chronology snaps back and erases the moment.  
Now each step the Builders hew persists, fixed in a timeline the Hollow cannot quite annul.  
One dawn, a Builder blinks and discovers an unbroken flight of steps leading down, all the way to weather she can trust.  
When she takes that route, the power plucks the echo of her labor from the Hollow and tucks it into her future dreams, so she will know her work became a way home.

V. Recalibration of Silence  
For a duration equal to a single drop’s leisurely fall—if “fall” can be said to happen here—the power quiets the Note by one infinitesimal decibel.  
To the Twice-Named Bird it feels like dusk opening inside dawn: a dimming that is neither threat nor end.  
The bird folds its mirror-wings, coasts on the sudden hush, and hears for the first time its own heart knocking in its hollow bones.  
That heartbeat establishes tempo. Tempo becomes rhythm.  
On the rhythm’s back rides a new call, two tones, black and white at once.  
All other lives in the Hollow register the call as a tremor of possibility: something besides the Note is now thinkable.

VI. A Covenant with the Erratics  
The power touches each probability-muscled boulder the way a librarian brushes dust from a spine.  
It asks nothing, commands nothing.  
Nevertheless, the Erratics understand: preservation is no longer solely their burden.  
They surrender a fraction of their vigilance, and the saved effort condenses into seams of sparkling mica—a visible memory of labor relieved.  
From that moment the boulders move only when movement delights them, not merely when symmetry demands.

VII. Unseen Harvest  
Beneath every soup-bowl pool the power lays a single syllable of silence, pronounced in a language whose only consonants are pauses.  
When next the film of phonon-loops blooms its bruise-colored alphabet, the new syllable slips in like a borrowed letter.  
That letter means “archive.”  
The loops, evolutionary archivists by vocation, replicate it eagerly.  
From then on each pool remembers not just itself but a trace of all sibling pools, so if one were ever emptied, its music could be reseeded from the lexicon of another.

VIII. The Place-Mind Is Shown Its Own Reflection  
To intervene crudely would be to break what the Hollow most perfectly achieves: equilibrium so ornate it feels like destiny.  
The power therefore offers the Place-Mind nothing more tangible than a mirror made of moments:  
1) The instant a droplet forgets gravity,  
2) The ache in a climber’s calves when courage overstays oxygen,  
3) The lilt in the Twice-Named Bird’s freshly invented cry.  
Holding these three moments together, the Hollow glimpses itself as continuum, not stasis.  
Discovery brings neither pride nor shame; it brings permission.  
Thereafter the inviolate balance remains, but it acquires a margin, the way a flawless equation tolerates being written in a slightly larger frame.

IX. Departure That Is Not Departure  
Having offered choice, breath, persistence, rhythm, delight, archive, and mirror, the benevolent power recedes—not outside or above but into the newly vacant interstices its gifts have opened.  
It becomes the soft hinge in the Note where modulation might one day begin, the extra rung in a ladder a Builder has not yet imagined, the hush between a watcher’s inhalation and the roar reasserting itself.  
No resident of the High Hollow will petition this power; no cult will name it; no scholar will prove its presence.  
Yet every being, every pattern, every anti-event now contains one redundant gram of freedom—spare capacity where an alternate future could root if ever the Hollow, of its own volition, desired to grow.

And that is all the wise power does:  
Enough to bend inevitability by a hair, never enough to insist.  
In a place defined by the tyranny of a single, perfect tone, it gives the faintest harmony—just audible, just optional.  
The rest is left to the mountain, the music, and whatever listeners may someday hear the second note and choose, astonished, to sing back.
