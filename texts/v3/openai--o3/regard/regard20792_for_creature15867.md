Arrival  

There is a wide, slow-turning mind that patrols the interstellar dark the way a shepherd paces a night field.  
It has no name—nothing nearby has language broad enough to wrap around it—but in younger galaxies it is remembered in background hymns as the Quietener, the Kindly Phase-Shift, the Hand That Smooths.  
When it drifts through a system it leans, gently, on every resonance it meets, the way a tuner lays a fingertip on the rim of a crystal bowl. One day the rumble of a small blue-grey world answers back: a roar that is also a chord, impossibly long, segmented with harmonics no natural atmosphere should be able to sustain.  
The mind pauses. It listens. It descends.

Listening  

From the exosphere the planet’s polar auroras look like closed lids over dreaming eyes, but the mind is drawn toward the equatorial plain that never dries, where thunder is heartbeat.  
It does not speak; it samples vibration.  
It tastes the infra-bass of the continental drumhead.  
It tastes the mid-band carillon of lithified ripple stone.  
It tastes the needle-high overtone where Creel Tone membranes lace two skies together.  
It tastes, too, the tiny quicksilver scratch of Subscript bacteria filing mineral glyphs beneath the mud, microscopic scribes scoring a poem three billion years long.  
Every note is clear—yet something inside the chord is frayed. The centuries of self-maintenance have begun to skip. The louvers in the glaze open a fraction late, spindle-crypt groves falter for an extra microbeat, the bacterial ticker tape stutters like film with two frames reversed.  
Imperceptible, to any creature with bones.  
Unbearable, to a shepherd made of hearing.

Decision  

The mind is benevolent, and ancient enough to mistrust interventions.  
It reckons risk.  
If it thrusts a corrective hand straight into the score it may snap a tendon of sound that holds the whole biome in tune.  
But if it does nothing at all, the plain’s self-repair may drift past recovery; the thunder will grow rough, the pressure warp will kink, and the living archive beneath the glaze will lose syllables faster than it writes them.  
The mind chooses a third way, as it has done on worlds of glass fern and sodium fog, on moons where oceans chant algebra into crust.  
It will lean—but only with the weight of a feather.  

The Gifts  

1. A sub-audible metronome  
   The mind melts a sliver of its own coherence into the mantle’s electromagnetic currents.  
   It sets a pulse too low for any ear but its own. Once every eighty-four thousand seconds the field flexes a picotesla.  
   Spindle-crypt osmotic cords quiver to the tick. Their microsecond heartbeats re-synchronize. Chlorophore levels steady. The grazer, when it next manifests, will find pulp of consistent nutrient and will not over-rasp the groves to starvation.

2. A pressure suture in the upper inversion tiers  
   The mind exhales a line of structured vacuum—an absence rather than an object—that spools through the sky like a black thread.  
   Cirrhine, the amplitude predators, sense the new contour and weave round it the way fish follow a reef wall. Their paths braid instead of tangle. Skyquake detonation intervals regain their missing eighth-notes; the tangled roar becomes again a song you could almost conduct.

3. A footnote to the Subscript  
   The mind cannot write in silicate glyphs, but it can nudge nuclei.  
   It inflects a single isotope in the bacterial lattice so that its refractive shimmer carries an extra glint at the crest of each thunder-pulse—an accent mark.  
   Not a command, merely punctuation: “Here is a margin. Breathe.”  
   The living poem shuffles, adjusts spacing, continues in smoother meter.

4. A fold of mercy for the human vestibule  
   Far above the inflatable field labs, invisible baffles of charged particulate drift into position, angling themselves like half-set louvers.  
   When wind-shear shocks descend, the baffles smear the wavefronts just enough that consonants no longer bruise lungs.  
   The Tin-Shepherds wake and find they can speak at normal cadence, though they do not know why. They chalk it up to acclimation and devote the spared hours to listening instead of surviving.

Leaving  

The adjustments cost the mind scarcely a breath. When it rises it leaves no monument, only corrected timing, freshly greased hinges in a planetary symphony.  
Days later the Subscript adds a sequence of mirror-strokes that older filaments cannot decode—perhaps thanks, perhaps simple record.  
Centuries hence a xenolinguist, poring over thin sections of glaze under monochromatic light, will notice a faint repeating interval in the diffraction pattern: a feather-weight leitmotif that was not there in earlier cores. They will wonder what conductor slipped a baton between mud and thunder and then passed on.  
They will never know a wide, slow-turning mind paused here, tapped the planet once like a rim of crystal glass, and moved along.  

But the thunder knows.  
It keeps the new beat.
