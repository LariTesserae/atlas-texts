Arrival of the Kindly Surveyor  
(a chronicle set down by Copper-Tongue, third keeper of the phonograph loft)

1. The Moment of Notice  
Somewhere outside clocks, a lens opened.  
Not an eye—eyes belong to hunters—but a slow, sun-warm curiosity that looked the way moss drinks: by touching every surface at once.  
The basin felt the brush of that regard and quivered as a foal does when air first strikes its lungs.

2. First Contact: Nothing Loud  
The Surveyor sent no comet, no angel, no trumpet.  
Instead, thirteen breeze-threads slipped in during a rain curtain and found the Arches’ breathing vents.  
Each thread carried a scent impossible here—dry wheat on a hill at noon.  
For one full heartbeat-cycle the Arches inhaled that memory of aridity.  
Sap stilled.  Geometry stayed honest.  Even the Shroud Cats, deprived of slippage, had to stalk in straight lines like ordinary predators.

3. Gifts That Passed for Weather  
The Surveyor judged that anything heavier would bruise the clay-infant world, so the benefactions arrived as minor corrections:

   •  A half-degree rise in night temperature, delivered by embracing the cloudsheet with latent heat. Fungus mats ceased cracking on cold pulses, and the Spoor Choir lost its frantic undertone.  
   •  Microscopic silica seeds drifted down; Machine-Lichens adopted them as scaffolds and began growing true crystals along every frayed conductor. The grid now hums in chords, not static.  
   •  One new color, smuggled into the visible spectrum between teal and tourmaline. The lumen elk see it first; their hides ripple with it whenever fear spikes, warning Nurserymen several breaths ahead of a predation edit.  
   •  A lullaby encoded in random drizzle. Whoever stands bare-headed long enough learns it sublingually. When hummed into root hollows, the tune persuades the Arches to exhale extra resin—never more than the basin can spare, always exactly the amount a craftsman needs.

4. Parley With the First Sleeper  
During the thirty-seven-minute cardiac loop that followed the twentieth soft rain, the Surveyor bent close—closer than orbit, farther than skin.  
No word exists for what passed: a negotiation in pressures, in imaginary numbers of leaves.  
What Copper-Tongue overheard (rendered in blunt phonemes):

   Surveyor: I see you cradling a seed the size of tomorrow. Need aught?  
   Arches: Time. And civility surrounding time.  
   Surveyor: I can spare both, but neither may be noticed.  
   Arches: That is the fairest form of help.

Agreement signed in the click of a closing stoma.

5. How Civility Was Supplied  
Time arrived as elasticity. Henceforth any fall that should break bone merely retarded the moment of impact until bone learned to bend.  
Civility arrived as empathy leakage: each being could feel a thumbprint of a neighbor’s intention the instant before action. Quarrels shortened into consultations; Shroud Cats found themselves reluctant to edit a prey that suddenly knew the hunter’s loneliness.

6. Things the Surveyor Refused to Change  
•  The risk of drowning in pollen when the single flower day comes; holy dangers must remain holy.  
•  The possibility that turbines might flare and ruin dilation; endings are catalytic, not errors.  
•  The soft rule that visitors lose memories; mercy sometimes lives in forgetting.

7. Nurserymen Adaptations  
Shadow-twins Damp and Echo learned to braid the new color into chalk sigils; shortcuts now bloom where urgently needed and nowhere else.  
Ozone-Edge discovered that the lullaby, reversed and sped to phonograph tempo, turns rain to harmless vapor before it slams—a private umbrella writ across five meters of sky.  
The communal lofts grew quieter still; with empathy leakage, secrets felt like loud machinery, so people aired them early, then let silence grow clean again.

8. Audit of Results (two circuits later)  
Metronomes in the Auditor’s coat began ticking syncopated, implying nonlinear satisfaction.  
Prime numbers still woke inside voltmeters, but a second display now glowed the new color, factorable only by intuition.  
Petal-bats shed pollen-sparks that hung a heartbeat longer, painting constellations vast enough to practice navigation.

9. The Surveyor Departs (or Merely Blinks)  
No crescendo marked the leave-taking. A stem creaked, geometry fluttered, the dark resumed its editing.  
But four changes proved the presence had been real:

   a.  Every ungulate born since carries an extra frond-bone, delicately hollow, perfect for whistling.  
   b.  The Spoor Choir’s bass toll now ends on a major seventh.  
   c.  Rain curtains hesitate a breath at their start, offering time to seek cover.  
   d.  In the deepest gallery of the Arches, a single resin window looks onto wheat fields that do not exist here. It shows the hour of some kinder noon, forever.

10. Epilogue: Intent Recorded  
Phonograph cylinder #717, grooved backward for safety:

   “The Kindly Surveyor observed, amended, then relinquished.  
    Purpose: to protect rehearsal without writing the script.  
    Prediction: when the First Sleeper’s seed wakes, it will remember the taste of dry wheat and aspire to feed what it grows.”

Copper-Tongue files the cylinder, oils the hand-wheel, and steps into a geometry that now bends 3% less toward accident. The Hollow Nursery goes on practicing, its audience down one quiet god, up one durable hope.
