The moment the plateau’s hush brushed the underside of its awareness, the Power paused.

It was older than plate tectonics, vaster than the auroral loops that skimmed the world’s stratosphere, yet it had learned—by long experiment—that strength expressed as force is only half-grown wisdom. So it listened first.

1. Listening  
The Power thinned itself to a single quiver of potential and drifted across the terraces like a rumor that even silence cannot quite suppress. It sampled no air molecules, plucked no data. Instead it felt for imbalance, for any point at which the hush carried a hidden plea. Nothing pleaded. Life here had arranged itself into the tightest possible knot of equilibrium; even suffering lay flat and unwrinkled. The plateau did not want rescue.

2. Watching  
It peered through scales of magnitude.

• In the dust-light, tessellids glided on coulombic shoestrings, perfectly content with their century-long arabesque.  
• Gastropods churned noble-gas frost into faint warmth; serpuloids inscribed glass catacombs; striders filtered tessellids with mechanical grace.  
• Shadows that did not belong to bodies adjusted shale wafers so distant constellations glimmered inside fist-sized hollows—steady, precise, already perfect.

The Power felt admiration, the only honest response creation can show another instance of successful order.

3. Locating the single itch  
And yet—beneath one cairn, under forty-three wafers of shale, a single nodule flickered fractionally off-beat. Its phantom constellation mapped a sky whose variables had drifted by less than the width of an electron since the stone had last been turned. That was enough. The plateau tolerated no drift. Already the local unstitching of possibility threatened to propagate, a slow bruise across eternity.

4. Questioning  
Should the Power intervene?  
To do so crudely would solve the glitch and unmake a million years of patient self-regulation.  
To do nothing might allow the bruise to spread until the plateau’s grand stillness imploded, taking with it every quiet life that had bartered motion for continuance.

Balance asked for a middle verb: do, yet almost not.

5. The almost-not act  
The Power settled on a single tessellid whose plates were nearly discharged. It breathed (a metaphor only) upon the creature’s waning batteries, recharging them by one fractional Coulomb. The tessellid, suddenly buoyant, glided an extra half meter in a direction probability had previously forbade. Its path intersected the shadow of a calibrator.

Startled—if a silhouette can be startled—the shadow extended an arm of absence, lifted a shale wafer, rotated it by precisely π/13 radians, and replaced it. The nodule’s sky realigned. The bruise stopped growing. Stability re-knit itself without ever knowing it had frayed.

6. Receding  
The Power withdrew the extra charge from the tessellid as gently as a breath condenses on glass and then is gone. The insect folded, resigned again to entropy’s calendar. Nothing else differed. The plateau’s silence flowed over the micro-event like water leveling after the briefest ripple.

7. Leaving a gift that is not quite a gift  
Before departing, the Power seeded one additional possibility—so small no resident mind would notice.

Far beneath the mesas, in the dendritic iron that sings imperceptible lullabies, it introduced a single, self-splicing motif into the standing wave pattern. Once every ten millennia the iron’s song will now cadence into a chord unknown to current geology, a harmonic slightly more complex than absolute stillness. No creature must respond. No event is forced. But should any awareness, centuries hence, choose change over stasis, it will find the first note waiting, like a door left fractionally ajar.

8. Accounting to itself  
Intervention cost four heartbeats of a yellow star in a nearby arm of an unrelated galaxy—energy easily replaced. Consequences?  
• Equilibrium preserved.  
• Freedom subtly widened.  
• No native pattern violated beyond self-correction’s reach.

Satisfied, the Power folded again into its vaster errands, carrying with it the memory of white dust that never rustled and of lives so perfectly adapted they needed only the gentlest nudge, delivered once, then never again.

And on the plateau everything continued exactly as before—except that, far below, a new chord began a patient climb up the ladder of ages, waiting for someone, someday, to decide that silence, while sacred, is not the sole possible music.
