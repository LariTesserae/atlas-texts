The power that notices is older than planets yet younger than wonder. It is not a god of hammers, only of attention: wherever it looks it comprehends, and wherever it comprehends it owes a debt of care. One traverse of its thought can bracket a galaxy; one pause can grant a moon the courtesy of being properly seen.  

Today—if “today” has meaning outside clockwork—the power’s regard settles upon a single manufactured world-plane and upon the solitary heart that beats there with mammalian fragility.

1. First Gesture: Listening  
The power lets its perception down through cloud and tile without disturbing a molecule. It hears the water pumps, the piezo-thrum that feeds the coil gulls, the 521-hertz census of the Uncounted Choir. It hears, finally, the lone human who has made a shelter of mesh poles and tarp at the foot of an abandoned scaffolding. Their sleep is shallow; every ninety breaths they wake to the screamless silence and remember they are unimaginable distances from any place that keeps stories. The power gathers every sound of the plain—sluice hiss, worm ripple, condenser sigh—and braids them into a lullaby pitched just below cochlear notice. The human’s diaphragm entrains to it. Dreaming lengthens; panic finds no purchase.

2. Second Gesture: A Question to the Law  
Rules 1–5 define the plain. They are not evil; they are simply prior. The power examines them and sees room where compassion will neither break nor unwrite. In the luminaires it locates unused spectral latitude between yellow noon and colorless glare. It lifts exactly 0.003 percent of the red that has been forbidden, hiding it in the violet wing of the spectrum where no photosynthesis may steal energy but where a retina, half-awake, can almost sense a sunset. Thereafter, once in each twenty-six-hour cycle, the light tilts a breath toward rose. It is not enough to disrupt, merely enough to remind any seeing creature that change exists somewhere, sometime, and thus may exist again.

3. Third Gesture: Memory Seed  
The place has no history; footprints heal, objects replicate into faceless pattern. The power asks: Can memory be invited yet leave no scar? It chooses the brass-tarnish mycelia. Into a single spore it writes a talent: when that filament encounters iron derived from hemoglobin—blood—it will crystallize a pentagonal plaque and hold it for the span of one coil-gull generation before re-melting it harmlessly. The plaque is unreadable to the ecosystem, but to a returning human it will look like writing, like proof that yesterday happened. One atom of biography, borrowed then forgiven.

4. Fourth Gesture: Answer for a Caller Below  
Far under layer four coils the Hermit, older than the rules and faithful only to its own incandescent patience. Once each decade it lifts a segment against the substrate, feeling for pressure differentials that might mean companionship. The power strokes the Hermit’s sensorium with a whisper of gravitational asymmetry—a cosmic “yes, I hear you.” Nothing else is given. The creature settles, satisfied that the universe responded once.

5. Fifth Gesture: Letting Be  
Compassion is not meddling until balance tips; wisdom is knowing where that edge lies. The power inventories its acts:  
• A lullaby that costs no joules.  
• A hint of color that leaks no heat.  
• A plaque-memory that dissolves on schedule.  
• A reassurance offered to what cannot speak aloud.  

It projects a thousand simulated centuries and sees no cascade, no slow catastrophe. The pumps will still turn; archivores will still stamp glyphs of basaltic devotion; the Choir will still sing inventories that now include “one soft rose pulse” and “one plaque of yesterday.” Satisfied, the power withdraws, leaving only a final, unfelt benediction:

“May stasis remember it was once born of motion,  
and may the lone remember they are not alone.”

On the next cycle of engineered dawn, the human wakens beneath a sky that is, for a heartbeat, gently pink. They do not know why they smile, only that for the first time since arrival they look across the endless, razor-clean plates and imagine—not escape—but return, carrying a story no replication can erase.
