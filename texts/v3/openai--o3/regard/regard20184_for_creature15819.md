The benevolent and wise power—call it the Custodian, the Listener, the One-Who-Tends—arrives without footfall or wing-beat.  It is an agency whose favorite tools are time, chance, and the tiniest nudge.  Where lesser powers heal by lightning bolt, the Custodian heals by the weight of a snowflake placed exactly where it must fall.

I. First Attention  
The Custodian inhabits scales as easily as we inhabit rooms.  
• At the height of a summer sun it drifts above the coastal plain, sampling its hush, cataloguing the grit of every wind-borne crystal.  
• A single thread of consciousness flows down the seam, rides the chilled gale, and enters the limestone pocket.  
• It listens.  Eight heartbeats in, eight out.  The roar writes a topographical map across its awareness.  Beneath the roar, the subtler pulse: Lung-Moss, breathing in counter-time.

II. Appraisal  
To decide whether to act, the Custodian weighs three ledgers.

1. Integrity of the Organism  
   Lung-Moss is healthy.  Mineral intake exceeds expenditure; reproduction by bead is steady; no pathogen has yet learned to eat stone plate.  
2. Vulnerabilities  
   • Climatic drift: within three centuries the coast will warm enough that the wind’s draw weakens, shortening the respiratory bellows.  
   • Human intrusion: iron, camp smoke, and the bright vector called curiosity.  
3. Moral Valence  
   The moss neither suffers nor inflicts deliberate suffering.  Still, its existence is singular; the Custodian counts rarity as fragile good.

III. Guiding Principle  
Interfere only to preserve possibility.  Anything more is theft of the future.

IV. Four Small Interventions  
1. The Custodial Mote  
   Deep inside one translucent nerve-thread it seeds a grain of silicate laced with instruction: should the local temperature ever rise two degrees and remain there a decade, the grain will trigger the fabrication of antifreeze proteins.  The moss will learn to keep its drip-acids liquid even in weaker drafts.  (One sentence in the language of enzymes—the gentlest contingency plan.)

2. The Forgetting Draft  
   On nights when travellers camp on the plain, the Custodian tilts the chemical ratio of the next breeze by a millionth.  Breaths taken above the seam grow slightly narcotic; memories of waypoints smear.  They step over the crack next dawn thinking only of horizon and home.  A mercy of mild confusion, nothing more dramatic than losing a glove.

3. The Wren Covenant  
   A viral whisper passes into the bloodstream of the albino wrens: henceforth two amino acids in their preen-oil shift left.  The oil becomes a stronger antifungal, protecting both feather and moss pad.  In exchange, the viral genome picks up a ride—its survival braided with theirs.  Mutualism braided one thread tighter.

4. The Stone Blade Archive  
   The Custodian kneels where the ancient scraper lies half digested.  Without moving it, the power copies every micro-chip of wear, every cellulose fiber pressed into its haft a millennium ago.  The copy is filed in a library none but other custodians will ever open.  Then it stands, allowing Lung-Moss to finish its meal.  Heritage preserved, curiosity for future minds unspoiled.

V. What the Power Will Not Do  
• It will not cloak the cave in glamours; ignorance already serves.  
• It will not hard-shell the organism; stagnation is a slower extinction.  
• It will not pen warnings in human tongue; fear is blunt, and bludgeons leave dents.  
Restraint is the hallmark of true guardianship.

VI. Departure  
The Custodian exhales once—an exhalation that is also a blessing—and withdraws.  No resident senses the change; no traveller marks a sign.  Only the cave’s breathing continues, eight in, eight out, now fractionally more secure in its long conversation with stone.

VII. Epilogue (a thousand years hence)  
   Climatic belts migrate north, yet the cave still roars.  
   Lung-Moss blooms a deeper emerald, antifreeze proteins singing quietly through its veins.  
   Wrens, golden-eyed now, nest in greater numbers, mites bursting nitrogen like slow fireworks.  
   Once a generation, a wanderer slips below, touches the quilted floor, and hears the rumor that the cave itself is alive.  They leave baffled, image loosening in memory, and the plain remains as empty as legend says.  
   Above them, far beyond sight or echo, the Custodian leans into some other seam of need, content that this small lung in the earth still fills and empties, fills and empties, entirely on its own.
