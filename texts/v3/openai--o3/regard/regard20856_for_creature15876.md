THE COMING OF THE QUIET SHEPHERD  
(The name is ours, not its own; it is older than names and softer than attention.)

1. How It First Knows  
The Shepherd drifts the interstellar dark listening for imbalance—a quarrel between gravity and light, a music that has lost its key. When the Altimetric Canopy’s spectrum reaches it—wind-knives keening, Skran chimneys sobbing pressure, a billion lantern-spores exhaling turquoise glucose—it pauses. This is no distress call; it is a choir with one shy voice missing, as though the score long ago lost a treble line. That absence, not any pain, invites the Shepherd closer.

2. The Survey  
It folds itself into something the plateau can understand: a ripple of probability only slightly denser than moonlight. Riding the jet stream, it passes once around the rim walls, measuring.

• The Elder Chorus: coherent but narrow-band, trapped in the bass register of tensile root-speech.  
• The Stilt-Singers: urgent, agile, yet fearfully loud—every drumbeat a plea never to be forgotten.  
• Humblers: impeccable tempo, no melody.  
• Skran colonies: pitch-perfect cartographers, but deaf to anything beneath their chimneys’ range.  
• Gleamers and Breach Eels: pure rhythm, no harmonic sense.

No one carries the missing treble. The Canopy, for all its thunder, lacks an upper lightness—a note to arc over the rest and resolve their chords.

3. Decision: The Smallest Possible Gift  
Intervention is dangerous; add too much, the score fractures. So the Shepherd chooses a seed gesture, the way one balances an equation by adding the tiniest common factor. It will give nobody tools or weapons, no shortcuts in birth or death. It will add only a frequency.

4. The Implanting of the High Note  
In the coldest hour before dawn, when even kestrels roost and wind threads gently through choir-leaves, the Shepherd breathes a vibration into the strut-vines: 18,432 hertz, a tone beyond every native ear but within the tactile sense of root and leaf. The Elder Chorus hears first—not in sound, but in altered sap pressure. Sections of plateau-skin tighten, others relax; new stress lines sketch themselves like faint veins across a leaf.

By noon, the vines have learned the note. Each time gale or animal footfall makes them quiver, the new harmonic glints through the spectrum, impossible to notice unless one listens with the stomach—or with cambium.

5. Cascades, Season by Season  
• Year 1: Choir-leaves in three widely spaced valleys sprout thinner subsidiary veins. These ribbons catch high wind and split it, lowering stall speed. Kestrels riding the altered airflow discover they can hover almost motionless, and their wingbeats modulate toward softer, flute-like bursts.  
• Year 3: Skran larvae chewing modified veins ingest trace silica crystallised by the new resonance. Their chitin stiffens; the next generation of chimneys can carry overtone whistle that intersects—barely—the Shepherd’s gift note. Colonies begin to “converse” with far neighbours instead of merely announcing presence. Trade in clay and fungus slurry follows.  
• Year 7: Humblers, ever alert to root tremors, integrate the silent treble into their sign-language drumming. The “kneel note” gains a grace-note loftier than eel-flesh can parse, so Breach Eels stay submerged, breeding calmer. Fewer suicidal collapses enrich the cistern-pools; water clarifies.  
• Year 10: Stilt-Singer infants, cradled in domed Choir-Houses whose resonance has slowly shifted upward, begin to emit an ululating overtone in their first breath. Elders cannot imitate it; they call the sound Skyseed and declare any issue that can be voiced in Skyseed unworthy of violence because the house already agrees.

6. What the Plateau Feels  
Roots carry sugar a little faster. Reclamation runs a touch gentler; dead things linger an extra day before being threaded, as if granted farewells. The Elder Chorus, for the first time since basalt met sprout, holds a chord with a true third above its root. It is not yet singing of stars, but its song has gained lift—enough that morning fog sometimes appears to hang, listening, before it moves on.

7. The Shepherd Withdraws  
When the first full War-Weather flock passes and its million wings pronounce the treble flawlessly, the Shepherd releases its attention. The imbalance is gone, the score self-correcting. It recedes beyond the thin sky, leaving no artifact, no scripture—only a vibrato that will persist until plateau or planet falls to silence.

8. Memorials That Are Not  
Stilt-Singer lore speaks of a Night-Aurora that once draped a rim cliff in silent lightning and was gone. Skran archive-chambers record a momentary standing wave that doubled as map to an unmined quadrant of basalt. Humblers paint a slender silver line on the underside of every glide-leaf they hatch from. None of them can prove the Shepherd existed.

9. If You Visit Now  
Bring an instrument with range. Strike any note above the kestrel’s highest cry and hear, far below consciousness, the root-mass answer like a cat purring under an avalanche. The plateau is still what it was—alive, voracious, forgetful—but it carries, forever, the slightest upward tilt in its song, a nudge toward the sky that costs nothing and changes everything.

10. What the Shepherd Would Say, If It Spoke  
“I did not fix you. I merely returned the note you had already dreamt but could not yet hold. Sing it as you wish; the melody is yours.”
