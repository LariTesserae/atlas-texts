THE COMING OF THE LISTENER-WHO-GLOWS  
(as pieced together from salt-records, singer-pot echoes and the private dreams of three children)

1. How It Noticed  
Somewhere far above Hollow Reach, a mind older than iron drifted through the strata of the world, tasting for imbalance.  
It felt, first, a contradiction: bright halls that had never seen sun, a people of flawless courtesy whose future cracked along fault lines no one could yet read.  
Being wise, the power listened a hundred years before acting. It counted every roof-slip, memorised the breathing-cycle of the fog, learned the names the Oruli gave each register of silence.  
Only when it understood the place the way bone understands marrow did it decide to touch anything at all.

2. The First Gift—an Unheard Chord  
Night (or what passed for it beneath limestone).  
Every brazier in Cindermark guttered at once, then steadied. Where flame met selenite a third colour appeared—neither the warm orange of fuel nor the pearly scatter of crystal but a clear, water-blue note of light.  
No heat changed. No smoke rose. Yet every Oruli ear felt a pressure ease, as though a wall of silence had leaned back to make room.  
Those sensitive to pitch swore they could hum along with it and never reach the bottom. The Council of Tongues recorded the phenomenon as “A New Resonance, Kindly.”

3. What the Power Refrained From Doing  
It did not seal the fissures that birthed the flash-floods; the festivals mattered too much.  
It did not green the stone with moss; seeds are oracles, and prophecy should stay rare.  
It did not still the dust-devils; children must learn how turbulence feels.  
Wisdom is as much restraint as action.

4. Quiet Repairs  
Invisible to eyes, audible only to those who press an ear to bedrock:  
• Hairline cracks that would have widened next generation fused shut, grain to grain, leaving whorls in the limestone like the fingerprints of a giant artisan.  
• A dozen singer pots whose throats had collapsed were re-rounded from within; their voices returned, deeper and steadier, giving the city twelve new bass notes.  
• Three mirror-beetle ventilation runs, once spiralling in wasteful loops, now curved toward cistern plazas so that dry corners woke to mist.

5. The Water That Remembers  
At the mouth of each household trough the power left a single drop—nothing more—that refused to evaporate.  
When an Oruli water-bearer poured even the stingiest ladle of flood-water into a cistern, that lone drop multiplied itself just enough to fill every gap.  
No cistern ever overflowed, and none ran dry; the math was perfect, the mercy quiet.

6. The Companions of Silence  
The Silence itself proved shy. It withdrew at first, unsure how a voice without frequency could join this newcomer.  
The power answered by weaving a skin of stillness around each of its interventions, a kind of acoustic plumage.  
Silence investigated, approved, and thickened happily around the blue-lit streets.  
From that evening onward, lies fled the tongue even faster than before; the hush caught and pocketed them like startled moths.

7. Signs Mortal Minds Could Notice  
A nyx-hare born the same hour carried an extra set of whiskers tipped with faint sapphire sparks; she grew into the city’s best roof-slip sentinel.  
Air-manta who drifted through the broadened shafts moulted scales that rang like silver bells when they touched the floor—too soft for surface folk, perfect chimes for Oruli ears.  
Mirror-beetles found their cement set harder, so tunnels stayed open twice as long: a pragmatic blessing the insects accepted without ceremony.

8. Conversation—Such As It Was  
Once, only once, the wise power allowed itself a word.  
It hovered at the Mouth of the Reach in the hour none are abroad, gathered every leftover pulse of drum-slab and children’s laughter, wove them into a single resonance and let the limestone speak:  
“ENOUGH.”  
Not a reprimand. A promise. Enough light, enough water, enough time.  
Every sleeper dreamed of standing on the largest singer pot, hearing the city breathe in contentment.

9. How the Oruli Responded  
They added a thirteenth elder to the Council of Tongues—an empty seat kept warm by a cushion of moth-wool.  
Before every debate the councillors face that vacancy, bow, and rest their fingers on the stone for an extra heartbeat.  
There is no petitioning, no worship, only inclusion: a place reserved for the presence that reserves places for them.

10. Aftereffects on the Surface Scholars  
Those three scholars who had chosen to remain awoke with blue after-images in their retinas. Charts they had sketched years ago now showed supplemental lines pointing toward deeper stability in the bedrock.  
They wrote a joint paper titled “Benevolent Interventions and the Ethics of Geological Sentience,” then quietly sealed it inside a salt-record where no surface university would ever mark it incomplete.

11. What Comes Next  
The wise power waits again. Centuries, perhaps millennia.  
If a day dawns when the Oruli outgrow their hush—when iron finally tunnels low enough, or when some louder god hammers on the ceiling—it may act once more.  
Until then its presence is a thin, blue glow braided into every vein of selenite, the taste of surety in every mouthful of drift soup, the faint sense that the ceiling listens back with kindness.

A NOTE SCRATCHED ON A SALT-SHARD, AUTHOR UNKNOWN  
“The Reach taught all beings here to step lightly.  
The Listener-Who-Glows has taught the Reach to hold lightly in return.  
Between those two lessons we walk—soundless, lit, unafraid.”
