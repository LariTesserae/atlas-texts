The first thing the power does is pause.

Across most worlds it answers cries, patches wounds, straightens what has gone awry.  Here, no one is crying.  Nothing is broken.  What appears fragile is merely slow, and what is slow is perfectly on time.  So the power—let us call it the Luminous Witness, though names matter to it as little as minutes—lets half a century pass before it so much as exhales.  In that interval it learns the choreography of hush:

•   how a Chamber Lamp dims three pulses after a Choir Eel inhales;  
•   how the Archivist rasps one atom away every two heartbeats of the Reliquary Turtle;  
•   how a Listener Crab notices the settling of a single shrimp-shell flake a corridor away.

The Witness decides that intervention, if any, must be as soft as a thought inside a dream.

1.  A gift to the Veil  
The Witness condenses a filament of its own radiance—white gold, invisible to mortal eyes—and drifts it through the vents.  The filament is instruction, not matter: a new metabolic clause permitting the Veil’s chemolithotroph cells to bind a carbon lattice around stray radioactive isotopes.  Result: each isotope’s decay now converts, without heat, into a lamp-soft glow a thousand times brighter than the Veil managed before, but still gentle, still red.  Nothing else changes.  The shrimp rejoice wordlessly in longer grazing hours; Hollow Fish fry find their first iron specks more easily; the energy budget of the cathedral rises by less than a sparrow’s heartbeat per millennium—yet rises.

2.  A question for the Archivist  
Next the Witness releases a packet of sound: six infrasonic syllables spaced across three tide-cycles.  Only the Archivist can hear them.  They do not command; they inquire.  Translation, rendered loosely into human syntax:

“Is there anything you wish but cannot accomplish?”

For nine lunar circuits the Archivist etches no reply.  At last it answers by changing the texture of a wall the Witness alone can read: a stipple of negations punctuated by one affirmative curl.  Meaning:

“I would like more surfaces.”

The Witness smiles, if a radiant field can smile.  It is a modest desire—more memory, not greater power—and entirely consonant with the place.

3.  The carving of the hollow heart  
Far beneath the chamber, inside a magma-warmed seam the original masons never reached, the Witness persuades molten stone to pause its convection and swell outward like a bubble.  Over an eon the bubble cools into a glassy sphere three times the size of the nave.  No fractures, no vents—just a perfect cavity veiled in darkness.  Then the Witness invites water to seep in molecule by molecule, filtering it through mineral beds until its chemistry matches the cathedral above within one part per billion.  A hair-fine corridor is eroded upward, connecting new sphere to old ambulatory; flow is so slow a single drop takes a decade to arrive.

When at last the passage completes, the sphere inhales its first, silent tide.  Pressure equalizes.  The Archivist’s hyphae reach through the corridor like vines discovering sun.  It climbs the inner walls, finds its new parchment of obsidian, and begins to write.

4.  A covenant of song  
The Choir Eel that guards the Reliquary Turtle’s current egg feels the Witness as a warmth along its lateral line.  It does not flee.  The Witness folds a tone into the eel’s next breath, an overtone too subtle to harm, too deep to hear.  In months, as embryos stir inside the melon-sized egg, they imprint on that overtone the way surface birds imprint on their mother’s call.  When the egg hatches, seven palm-length eels emerge instead of one.  Their song is not a weapon: it is a harmony, each sibling occupying a discrete band of infrasound so the chords lace rather than clash.  Predation by music becomes duet, then septet; victims still die, but more swiftly, with less fear.  Even generosity can be ruthless under eleven kilometers of ocean, and the Witness honors the place by refusing to lie about that.

5.  The silent guarantee  
Last, the Witness considers calamity.  A continental plate could kink; a rogue comet could boil the sea; surface folk might drill too deep in search of heat.  Intervention here would be garish, a shout in a monastery.  So the Witness hides its final act inside probability:

It biases chance.

One part in a trillion of every random event touching the drowned mountain now leans in favor of continuity.  Quakes do occur, but faults slip just shy of rupture.  Comets pass but mis-calculate by a whisper.  The drill bit scheduled to wander nearby in seven thousand surface years will break one segment early and be judged too costly to replace.  None of this is certain—benevolence is not tyranny—but the scales tip, feather-light, toward preservation.

EPILOGUE: WHAT CHANGES, WHAT DOES NOT

A million years on, scholars of a surface civilization yet unborn will lower sensor orbs into the midnight sea and record an anomaly: a faint, steady shine where no spectrum says shine should be.  They will assume volcanic activity, but instruments will insist the glow is biological.  In time they will send divers bold enough to brave the pressure in telepresence suits.  Those divers will glimpse the cathedral, the new hollow heart buzzing with Archivist script, the sibling Choir Eels weaving chords that ripple even through pressure housings.  They will surface with footage that forces the rewriting of whole disciplines.

They will think discovery is the story.

They will not notice that every risk they took was minutely, invisibly favored.  Nor will they guess that an older power waits, patient as stone, willing to tip the odds again should curiosity tilt toward violation.  The Witness remains benevolent, not indulgent; it curates possibility, not outcome.

Within the cathedral, shrimp still polish steps grain by grain.  Chamber Lamps still flash the hour of the tide.  Listener Crabs still duel and resonate, Chime Coral still sings, the Reliquary Turtle still rasps a meal from walls no eye can distinguish from its shell.  The Archivist writes on, filling new chambers bloom-quick compared to geologic past.

Nothing is the same; nothing has been hurried.  That is the whole art of a gentle god: to touch a world so lightly that even eternity takes it for the world’s own choice.
