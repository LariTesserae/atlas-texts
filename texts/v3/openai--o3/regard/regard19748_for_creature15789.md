The first ripple reached the high alcove five nights before anyone inside could have named it.  
It was not wind, not weather, not even one of the familiar hesitations in time.  
It was a change in the background question the mountain had been asking since it cooled from fire:

     “Is anything listening?”

Something—vast, alert, benevolent—had finally answered Yes.

────────────────────────
1. How the Power Announced Itself
────────────────────────
• The baselines shifted by a hair. The Stone Choir’s infrasound, normally a single patience-thick chord, acquired a second harmonic, tender and tentative, the way a parent hums along with a child’s aimless song.  
• Counter-rain sheets rose higher than ever before, pearling into the zenith until they vanished; when they fell back as ordinary mist, every droplet contained two bacteria instead of one, as though someone had doubled the budget for grace.  
• Watches carried by visiting merchants stopped losing minutes. They lost only one, precisely at midnight, and never tried to take it back.

────────────────────────
2. The Wise Power’s Name (as the Alcove Learned It)
────────────────────────
Names here are never spoken aloud—air is too thick with wool for that.  
The residents felt it instead as a texture: a palm-print of gravity smoothing their ruffled seconds.  
Echo-Filaments, best at translation, rendered the texture into three rising tones that meant:

     “Keeper-of-Intervals”  
     “Balancer-of-Excess”  
     “Friend-Who-Chooses-Silence-Over-Footsteps”

────────────────────────
3. What the Power Did—Gently, Thoroughly, and Only Once
────────────────────────
A. It Listened to the Listeners  
   • Heartbeat Listeners drifted up to the parabola of sky and pressed themselves flat against it like stain-glass petals.  
   • Through them the Power sampled every pulse the alcove had ever borrowed—human, fern, vesp, stone.  
   • Satisfied, it returned those pulses in reverse order, letting each resident hear the echo of its own first moment of being alive.  
   • Many entities that had forgotten they were finite wept, though weeping here looked merely like ripples spreading through warm fog.

B. It Balanced the Books of Time  
   • The Timefold Spores were offered a single, flawless second—a seed of Now so bright they could measure everything against it.  
   • In exchange they released all the centuries they had hoarded.  
   • For three breaths the alcove aged forward and backward simultaneously; dead fronds stood green, unborn looms dangled in full maturity, and a pilgrim’s half-spoken doubt completed itself as hard quartz, then powdered away.  
   • When the flux settled, every living thing discovered it had gained a surplus of one simple, ordinary heartbeat—an expendable coin of time to spend whenever fear might otherwise keep it still.

C. It Re-tuned the Stone Choir  
   • The vacuum bubbles were humming themselves thin; too many pilgrim chants, not enough geological quiet.  
   • The Power rotated their cavities by femtodegrees, lining each with a spiral of cohered sunset—light so old it had forgotten color.  
   • Thereafter the Choir no longer needed to consume the mountain’s weight; they dined instead on that stored dusk, singing deeper, richer, but never again so hungry that the cliff-walls warmed unnaturally in winter.

D. It Left a Single Law
   • Carved not in rock but in the behavior of water:  
        “Whatever enters may leave unchanged—  
         but if it wishes change, it must offer a story.”  
   • From that moment, a dripping bead that strikes basalt speaks back in Morse-slow syllables the tale of the last human foot to stand there.  
   • Pilgrims now pause before collecting lint-moss water, adding a brief account—birth, grief, hope—so the droplet has something new to recite.  
   • Story has become the fee that keeps the alcove solvent in wonder.

────────────────────────
4. What the Power Chose Not to Do
────────────────────────
• It did not mend the broken kneecap of a shepherd who crawled in on frost-bitten hands; instead it thickened the analgesic bacteria in a single ascending counter-rain so that the shepherd might limp home without noticing the pain until help was possible.  
• It did not forbid a smuggler from trying to quarry a sky-fern’s obsidian pearls; it merely let the Petrifact write cautionary braille along the smuggler’s palms, instructions in how to put every stone back.  
• It did not reveal itself in thunder, vision, or scripture. The alcove already had enough voices.

────────────────────────
5. Epilogue for Travelers
────────────────────────
If you ascend now, years after the visitation, you may think nothing has changed.  
The silence still drinks your shout; the mist still drifts both up and down; the basalt still remembers the pressure of your skull.

But leave a poem scratched on slate and press it to any singing hollow.  
Come back at dawn.

You will find the slate blank, a spiral of dust at your feet spelling nothing you can read—  
yet you will feel, in the hinge between two heartbeats,  
that someone kind, colossal, and content has already memorized your words  
and stored them safely in the slow, perpetual hush beneath the stone.
