Far beyond the scale at which the chamber measures its own turbulence, a presence slopes in out of dimensions the pocket cannot name.  It has no single body, no instruments to unpack.  It is an old intelligence whose wisdom is inseparable from restraint, and its first act is to stand completely still—not because it must, but because it chooses to taste the place before it contemplates touching it.

LISTENING  
The power hearkens to the rhythm of flash-vents and dripping quartz light.  Where lesser minds would filter noise from signal, it hears both together and understands that they are one phenomenon: rock cycling through its own brief biographies.  Every twenty-three heartbeats of the Plate-Sleeper, a new seam opens; every seven ticks of a beetle’s feet, a worm darts out of harm’s way.  The watcher folds these pulses into a single chord and holds it cupped in memory.

QUESTIONING  
Then it wonders—the way only a patience older than planets can wonder—whether the chord is unfinished.  Must this music always break itself down to restart?  Could recurrence here, just once, give way to continuity without smothering the beauty of impermanence?

The answer is not obvious.  The place’s fierce renewal is also its shield.  To dampen the violence might be to rob every inhabitant of the shape that makes it possible.

So the power declines the heavy hand.  It chooses instead a needle’s touch.

GIFTS, SO SLIGHT THEY ARE ALMOST SILENCES  
1.  Into the photonic mist it threads a second spectral line, indigo-violet, tuned to fluoresce only when magma pulses rise past a lethal threshold.  Nothing mechanical, nothing that will ever corrode—just the mildest change of color in the air, a dawn-light warning system visible even to optic-less Plate-Sleepers who read it as a change of heat on their whiskers.  Catastrophe will still come, but one second sooner than before, and sometimes a single second is survival.  

2.  Deep in the oxide film it whispers a new genetic stanza, borrowed from extremophiles it once curated near another star: an enzyme that knots iron crystals into tiny helices rather than flat sheets.  The red lace now gains a third dimension—towers so thin they bend without breaking.  On those towers droplet beads linger twice as long before flashing away, buying the worms another blink of moisture in which to drink and mate.  

3.  Beneath one overlapping plate, it hollows a lens of sapphire.  The lens is not a shrine, not a technology—merely a curve of nearly perfect crystal, sized to the Plate-Sleeper’s belly.  When the animal rests atop it, the rock itself will tug excess heat sideways and downward, redistributing scald instead of reflecting it back.  A cooler wall here, a gentler gradient there: enough perhaps to let one clutch of Sleeper scales finish curling into juveniles before the next tectonic shrug.  

KNOWING WHEN TO LEAVE  
These are fingerprints too small to catalogue; they vanish into the chamber’s self-writing text the way a single letter loses its sharpness inside a living paragraph.  The power lingers only long enough to confirm that its gifts have not snapped any existing weave:

•  Beetles still ring stone like mallet on gong.  
•  Worms still glimmer pearl-pale along the drip lines.  
•  No hierarchy has formed, no monoculture begun its choke.  

SATED, it retracts its attention the way a tide withdraws from a beach it has chosen not to erode.  And because even benevolence must honor consequence, it plants one last, almost comic insurance: a thought-seed that will blossom only in minds capable of entering the pocket but not of enduring it.  The seed is simple—Leave a cup of water on the threshold, for the place will drink before you do.  A courtesy, no more.  Courtesy is often the gentlest quarantine.

AFTERMATH  
Hours, days, perhaps a hundred years from now (the pocket keeps time in its own, volcanic currency), observers may notice that light sometimes winks violet before a quake, that biofilm now arches in miniature cathedrals, that a Plate-Sleeper occasionally basks on a patch of stone that feels mysteriously cool to the hand.  No resident will name the change; none will suspect intervention.  Continuity will remain impossible here—plates will still shear, vents will still scald—but the interval between severance and rebirth will stretch by the slenderest mathematical hair.  In that hair a few more worms will lay eggs, a few more beetles will tuck their cases where stone will not fall, and perhaps one day a Sleeper will glide out with two juveniles in its wake instead of one.

The power will not return to check its work.  True stewardship is the art of assuming things will grow if left almost, but not entirely, alone.
