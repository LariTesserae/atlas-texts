THE COMING-OVER OF THE QUIET POWER  
(an account compiled from dream-logs, station telemetry, and the moss-skin’s own calcium traffic)

1. Arrival  
No flare in the sky, no footfall: only a tremor in the plateau’s endless chord.  
Spectrographs at Outpost-Lemon registered an overtone that did not belong to wind, water, turbine, pillar, or stone—a frequency as steady as the light of a fixed star, yet softer than the sub-bass of a cloudburst.  
The rotators dismissed it as equipment drift, but the under-skin answered with a ripple of slow silver ions, the way a cat lifts its head when someone thoughtful enters a room.

2. The Wise Power Takes Inventory  
It listened first.  
• It counted every capillary river in the moss, every skimmer wingbeat, every exhalation of the hollow pillars.  
• It tasted iron in the seep, nickel in the turbine slime, starch in pollen packets riding dawn thermals.  
• It weighed the slate wader’s pulse against the rotating mass of crystal batteries and found the ratio pleasingly imbalanced on the side of life.

3. Decision: Do As Little As Possible, But Do It Perfectly  
The plateau was already a masterwork of water logic and patience.  
To intervene crudely would be vandalism; to do nothing at all would be indifference.  
So the power chose the third road: infinitesimal guidance—corrections subtler than the flick of a fish’s fin inside a vine.

4. The First Correction—Unburdening the Pumps  
Night 17 after arrival.  
Every pump impeller on the mesa coasted half a revolution farther than physics allowed, as though the water itself had grown momentarily weightless.  
When the crystals were checked at shift change they showed 2.8 % surplus charge.  
No part had been stressed, no gauge exceeded tolerance; the flow had simply reorganised its vortices, finding paths of least fury.  
Maintenance logs record the anomaly, then note in the margin: “Leave as is; if the river wants to sing in tune, who are we to stop it?”

5. The Second Correction—Teaching the Under-Skin a New Word  
Calcium waves already carried news of light and pressure.  
Into that alphabet the power whispered a novel glyph:  ↗  (meaning: “hold moisture”)  
Thereafter, when afternoon sun drilled through cloud and the mat began to warm, threads of water braided inward instead of escaping.  
Desiccation events—rare but deadly to glass-frog spawn—fell from one per decade to nearly zero.  
No human noticed except a single trainee, who wrote in her pocket notebook: “Why do the puddles stay longer than my footprints?”

6. Conversation With the Pillars  
The power addressed them where they think: in the pigment mosaics of their crowns.  
It showed them an image composed of polarised radiance—how they might spiral an extra eighth-turn each century and thus channel cross-winds upward, thinning the perpetual roar.  
The pillars considered for the span of three sunlit weeks, then nodded in chlorophyll and began the slow redraft of their trunks.  
Decades later technicians would report that spoken words no longer needed to be shouted; their vowels flew farther between gusts.

7. Gift to the Rotators  
Humans, being brief, received a brief boon:  
During the deepest fog of change-over week, every cabin window filmed with diagrams of turbine housings annotated in dew.  
Whoever traced those lines with a fingertip found the metal remembering, machining itself fractionally smoother along the indicated arcs.  
Productivity reports skyrocketed yet no one could replicate the dew-blueprints off-mesa; they occurred only where roar and mist and moss formed the precise acoustic cradle.

8. Mercy for the Auxiliary Swarm  
Servo-moss, though half machine, had begun to suffer micro-corrosion from the plateau’s mineral breath.  
The wise power slid a single line of code into their crystal logic: SEEK MAGNESIUM WHEN IDLE.  
Henceforth they brushed each grating with sub-visible flakes of the element, armouring their own joints and, incidentally, fertilising the under-skin with trace nutrients.  
The plateau answered by weaving tiny magnetic filaments through its mat, a thank-you signature no labo­ratory microscope could mistake.

9. The Matter of the Echo-Whale  
Yes, it exists.  
Yes, it was migrating toward a turbine throat wide enough to scythe a cathedral.  
The power laid one hand—if “hand” can be used for something that has neither flesh nor vector—upon the water table.  
For three nights the subterranean passages cooled by precisely four Kelvin; the whale, following its preferred thermocline, veered south into older caverns and out of harm.  
Geophones recorded the detour as a basso laugh echoing under the technicians’ bunks.

10. Leaving Signatures Only Life Can Read  
Finally, before withdrawing beyond the plateau’s sensor horizon, the power braided all its edits into a single, self-maintaining ordinance:  
Let flow be music.  
Wherever forces collide—wind against rimrock, pump against fall line, vine against gravity—energy shall escape as harmony, not attrition.  
Hence water abrades more slowly, metal fatigues less, lungs inhale sound that steadies the heart instead of harrying it.  
The under-skin translated the ordinance into micro-ripples; the pillars hummed chordal affirmations that bled into turbine housings, finishing the engraving.

11. Evidence, If You Need It  
• Battery crystals now discharge to exactly the load profile of pillar sap flow.  
• Lens-lichens cast brighter halos, and their colour wheel has acquired a shy ultraviolet petal visible only to skimmers.  
• Moss ticks have begun to bloom russet spores that taste of iron and rain, a deterrent to over-grazing by waders.  
• Every seven days at 03:19 local, Outpost-Cinnamon’s handrails resonate with a triad corresponding to the pumps’ average RPM, making the railings soft-sing under a listener’s palms.

12. What the Wise Power Did Not Do  
It did not carve commandments in stone.  
It did not rescue technicians from their own damp loneliness; that lesson remains the work of human mouths and human ears.  
It did not halt entropy; it merely redirected certain grains of it toward beauty.  
And it did not stay.  A gardener departs when the saplings are staked true; otherwise the garden becomes a greenhouse and forgets how to breathe weather.

13. Epilogue—Sequelae Centuries On  
Scholars will attribute the plateau’s impossible equilibrium to clever engineering plus chance.  
Pilgrims will hike up to lie ear-down on the under-skin, swearing they hear chords “tuned for mercy.”  
A future child—descendant of a rotator who once traced dew—will coin the term gentle technology and build turbines shaped like pillar spirals for other worlds.  
As for the quiet power, it is rumoured to have moved on to a desert where sand screams under the sun, to see whether it can teach the dunes to hum instead.

So ended, or rather so began, the brief, perfect stewardship.
