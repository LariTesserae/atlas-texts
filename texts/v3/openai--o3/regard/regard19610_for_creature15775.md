The Power arrived the only way a thing may enter the Cloud-Vault—by subtraction rather than addition.

There was no crack of thunder, no spear of light.  One moment the under-belly of fog was smooth; the next it bore a shallow dent, as if an unseen fingertip had pressed from above to test the fabric.  A single spoonful of vapor was missing.  That was all.

The beings below noticed.

•  The ribbon-slip serpent paused mid-glide, the ripple that carried it petering into stillness.

•  A hundred midnight-tile moths unclasped their wings by a hair’s breadth, ready, uncertain.

•  The moss pores ceased their twice-a-minute pumping and held a collective breath.

The Power—ancient, amused, gentler than dawn on saltwater—listened.

What it heard was Balance, carried on millions of tiny metronomes: droplet, exhale, heartbeat, tap-tap of concealed crabs.  A clock so perfect that even curiosity might bruise it.  And yet the Power had been summoned by that very perfection, the way a scholar is drawn to a proof elegant enough to be self-evident.

What to do with a place already finished?

The first temptation—always, for the well-meaning—was preservation through enclosure: raise sigils at the passes, bend the minds of wanderers so the switchback trail was forgotten.  But the Power had toured museums of dead wonders, each sealed behind its pane of reverent glass, and had found them more corpse than marvel.  Isolation petrifies.  Life requires one thin door left unlocked so story and stranger may slip through in single file.

The second temptation was Gift.  It examined its pockets of possibility: a seed that could bloom with its own gentle light; a micro-sun that would set once every century so the salamanders might molt by a crimson tide instead of a pale one; an idea that would teach the corvids a new tense for hope.  All lovely.  All ruinous.  Equilibrium does not negotiate; it topples when overpaid.

So the Power chose a third path: Custody without Contact.

It knelt—metaphor only; the basin would have noticed any real displacement—and whispered three small amendments into the ledger of natural law.

1.  Forgetting Shall Accumulate  
   Anyone who enters the Cloud-Vault will recall the place in perfect, aching detail, but the compulsion to speak of it will leak from memory like warmth from wet wool.  They may try once, twice, a dozen times, yet the tongue will stall on specifics.  At best they will call it beautiful and fall silent, unsure what beauty means.  Thus the Vault is neither hidden nor revealed; it travels only in the hush between sentences.

2.  No Blade Shall Bite True  
   Should steel be lifted in anger beneath the fog ceiling, the blow will miss by the width of a moth’s antenna—always, unfailingly.  Not because the arm is redirected, but because the path between edge and flesh will lengthen just enough that the distance cannot be closed.  Predation may remain, quiet and precise, but cruelty armed with tool will find itself politely, impossibly out of reach.

3.  The Ledger Will Tilt Toward Mend  
   Once per thousand heartbeats, the basin may borrow one joule of energy from the Power’s own vast store.  No creature will feel the draw.  The Vault will spend the loan only on repair: a shattered quartz finger re-fused, a vine’s torn trichomes rehung with dew, an errant campfire coal smothered before it names itself flame.  The tally will never grow large; the Power is patient and the basin frugal.

Having signed these clauses into the grain of reality, the Power withdrew the pressure of its unseen fingertip.  The dent in the fog smoothed.  Air unshivered.  Time’s gears, which had halted for exactly two wingbeats of a moth, resumed.

Reaction rippled outward in threads too fine for any single witness:

•  The serpent completed its glide and arrived, as ever, already victorious around a stunned crab.  

•  The moths decided against flight and reclasped their wings, roof-tiling themselves back into stillness.

•  The moss resumed pumping.  Twice a minute, precisely.

Not one being sensed that anything had changed.  The changes were, by design, negative spaces—protections that weighed nothing, occupied nothing, and only existed when a harmful hinge tried to swing.

Seasons—such as they are—spiraled.  Once, a trio of climbers reached the basin, burdened with stainless-steel cookware and the impatience of townsfolk.  They made camp exactly long enough to grow uneasy at the lack of echo.  One attempted to describe the place in her journal; the sentences faded to ellipses, and she ended with a sketch of a single leaf, shaded, unlabeled.  They left at dawn, certain they would return with better lanterns.  By the valley floor each of them could recall the path up, but none could draw the first turn.

Another time, a poacher raised a carbon-black machete toward a vault whelp, lured by its copper-shot hide.  The blade’s arc lengthened mid-swing as though the air had turned to rubber; steel kissed nothing but mirrored leaves.  The poacher tried again, again, until his courage frayed and he fled downslope, convinced the mountain had grown by meters while he moved within it.

And once every long while, the basin spent its borrowed joule: repairing a corvid’s broken pinion so the bird might coast home rather than crash; knitting shut a fissure before frost could worry it wider.  The debt to the Power never exceeded the caloric content of a single dew-bead.

Ages will pass.  One day the sun itself will blister and bloat, and even granodiorite ribs cannot outclimb a star’s red tide.  On that day the Power will return, for custody ends when the trust dissolves.  Perhaps it will scoop the entire amphitheater—stone, moss, serpent, memory—into a jar of gentle physics and carry it to some dim, cool moon where twilight is native and equilibrium a common tongue.  Or perhaps, having studied the Vault all this while, it will have learned at last the discipline of enough and let dissolution take its dignified course.

But that is later.  For now the Cloud-Vault abides exactly as before, an immaculate equation written in moss and mica.  Its inhabitants live lives that leave no ledger debt.  And high above, in places no map keeps, a benevolent and wise Power holds the key to a door no one can see, satisfied that the lock will click only when harm arrives wearing shoes.

The basin does not thank the Power; gratitude would be noise.  The Power does not require thanks; wisdom prefers quiet.  Between them they maintain a silence so precise you could set your heartbeat to it—and the Cloud-Vault does, twice a minute, whenever the moss pores pulse.
