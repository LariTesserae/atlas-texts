The Rift of Unfinished Peaks is already guided by two great teachers: time and vibration. A third—call it the Benevolent Power—approaches, wondering whether so intricate a score needs a new instrument at all.

1. First, the Power listens.  
   • It takes the measure of every note that lives here: basalt infrasonics, Gloom-Gull wing-thrum, the sub-audible sigh of a stone slowed to stillness by leech-mouths.  
   • It discovers that, in twelve and a quarter hours, the canyon plays a motif that never quite repeats—a self-tuning fugue of erosion and appetite.  
   • It realises the Rift is not chaotic; it is simply young, still rehearsing the piece it will perform for a million winters.

2. Then it watches the single conscious trespasser—the “being who lives in it” for the span of a heartbeat-long lifetime.  
   • The trespasser bleeds at the ankles where basalt razors kiss skin. The Power notes the drop in body heat, the way the grazed silence around the human is instantly harvested by Lantern-stage leeches.  
   • It asks itself: Does intervention preserve, or does it subtract?

3. The Power considers what could be done.  
   a. Still the wind for one afternoon?  
      – The canyon’s entire food web would miss a meal; Song-Leeches would dim, Gloom-Gulls would fly blind.  
   b. Blunt the knife-sharp talus so feet stop bleeding?  
      – Every edge is a tuning fork; round them off and the orchestra loses half its range.  
   c. Kindle a lantern of warmth around the trespasser?  
      – Ridge Seraphs would swarm, seduced by the micro-thermal; Silence Cats would follow.

   In each imagined mercy, a thread of the composition snaps.

4. So the Power elects for gentler tools:  
   • Resonant Whisperstones: It sets a dozen thumb-sized pebbles glowing with infrasonic memory. In the pocket of the lone human, they murmur counter-notes that mask the rhythm of the heart. To Gloom-Gull senses the traveller becomes just another piece of drifting rock—uninteresting, untasted.  
   • Spoor of Stillness: On the canyon floor, the Power plants a lattice of sub-micron crystal that drinks only the most violent overtones. Nothing living can ingest the energy it sequesters, but neither is any creature harmed. The result is a narrow ribbon of relative hush, a single silent path from west mouth to east mouth. It is not comfort; it is permission to pass.  
   • Remembering Moss: Over the next decade, a cousin to the copper algae will appear where Silence Cats crouch to drink. The moss absorbs the hush that leaks from their pelts and returns it the next dawn as a low, continuous purr—enough surplus for the Cats to hunt without depriving the leeches they stalk. Predation remains; starvation fades.

5. What it will not do:  
   • Name itself, for names reverberate.  
   • Flatten the Rift’s learning curve. Every slip, every blood-bright scrape, is a line of script in the canyon’s evolving score.  
   • Make the place safe in the human sense. Safety is a kind of silence, and the Rift feeds on sound.

6. What changes, almost invisibly:  
   • The Subaudients who arrive at the next solar minimum discover the Whisperstones half-buried in scree. They string them as rosaries; the stones damp the monks’ breathing. For the first time, three of them complete the vowed fast, hearing an inner music no wind can touch. Their departure poem reads, “kindness is the pause between two wings.”  
   • One century on, the crystal hush-path has widened just enough that a Chimera Imago may land, unfold its impossible adult form and leave again without shattering its own web of still air. For the first time, a larva returns as parent; silk spans the slot-canyon roof for a whole season before weather tears it free. The temporary ceiling shifts storm dynamics, allowing a rare summer fog; copper algae bloom twice that year.  
   • The Silence Cats, no longer stalking the brink of hunger, begin to gather in pairs. Their kitten-forms mewl with voices loud enough to feed themselves. Song-Leeches flare like scattered candles at each newborn cry.

7. After these slight corrections, the Power recedes to listening distance.  
   • From afar it hears a new chord—no sweeter, no louder, simply more complex.  
   • It recognises itself not as composer, but as tuning fork: struck once, then left to ring.

8. And the lone human?  
   • They leave the Rift alive, though marked: hair dusted with basalt glitter; pulse permanently half a beat slower, as if the canyon’s organ chord has become their metronome.  
   • Long after, in a settlement far downstream, they teach children to cup hands around small stones and listen. In rare moments the pebbles answer with a pulse that is not echo but memory—an inaudible kindness vibrating just beyond the range of fear.

Thus the Benevolent Power’s entire intervention amounts to three nearly unseen gifts: a sleeve of stillness for passage, a currency of quiet for predators, and a handful of stones that remember how to go unnoticed. Nothing here is rescued; everything here is allowed to continue learning its own impossible song.
