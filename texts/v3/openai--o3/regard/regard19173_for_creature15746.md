A JOURNAL KEPT BY THE ONE-WHO-LISTENS-BENEATH-ALL-WEATHERS  
(found scratched in shining characters on the inside of a broken fan-blade, date unknown)

I  
First hearing  
The note reached me long before the light that bore it. Fifty pulses each second, steady as a star’s metronome, carrying upward through basalt, through rain-soaked loam, through the feet of trees. I mistook it for the heart of the planet—until I tasted salt in the tremor and knew it was the heart of something trying to remain alive.

II  
Decision  
Power is the art of choosing which threads to tug. I count four kinds of thread here:

1. A single verdant skin that drinks rust and exhales patience.  
2. Minute animals that trade stillness for breath, motion for survival.  
3. Six salt-flaked humans who shoulder the obligation of time.  
4. A machinery of iron whose song keeps the other three in orbit.

Pull one hard and the cloth unravels. Tug lightly, and perhaps the weave tightens. I will tug lightly.

III  
Gifts to the Root-Skin  
I veined the ceiling with an unseen lattice: capillaries of silicate that wick the mineral fog rising from deeper seams. The lattice condenses twice the old amount of iron and magnesium, then lets it fall in uniform drizzle. No fountain, no sudden feast—only a steadier diet, so the Root-Skin need no longer crawl so greedily up every girder. It fattens, but slowly, and learns that plenty can arrive without conquest.

IV  
Gifts to the Fan-Moths  
They measure their lives in silences snatched from vibrating steel. I bent the harmonics a fraction, broadening each node of quiet from a pinpoint to a coin’s width. Courtship still demands precision; now it seldom ends in exhaustion. Wing-dust thickens on the duct walls like graphite on old paper: a record of dances no eye has seen.

V  
Gifts to the Lurker-Salamander  
Oxygen, not flesh, is its hunger. I braided into the water-mirrors tiny spirals of photosynthetic crystal—clear as ice, drinking the feeblest photons and splitting them for air. A bead falls, the seal breaks, the salamander gulps, and an hour lengthens to two. Nothing here should have to choose between moving and breathing.

VI  
Gifts to the Priests  
I cannot lift their burden; vows, once uttered, cling tighter than any vine. I can, however, make the burden fit the spine.

1. In the hut’s corrugated skin I sowed a resonance opposite to the cavern’s fundamental. Where the bass note enters, half of it now cancels itself. Sleep comes without vertigo; dreams cease to throb.  
2. The chalk in their satchels no longer dwindles. Each time the stub wears thin, it grows anew overnight, condensing from the same quiet alchemy that salts the girders. May they spend less time counting sticks and more time reading pressures no one else will read.  
3. Once every thousand rotations of the lead rotor, a finger-sized fracture in its hub emits a chime—high, almost playful. It is harmless. It is also unmistakable. To the Priests it will sound like gratitude.

VII  
Gifts withheld  
I considered arresting the corrosion, burnishing every blade to mirror finish. I pictured installing a beacon of daylight, striking the gloom from end to end. I did neither. Too much mercy can be violence in softer clothes. The Root-Skin needs its rust. The dim preserves the moths. The roar is the axis about which all else coheres. I withhold the kindness that would erase identity.

VIII  
Observation after one local year  
• The Root-Skin humps now rise thicker but stop short of the catwalks. It has learned the boundary where drizzle ends.  
• Moth populations have doubled; their wing-beats smooth the pressure waves that once rattled loose bolts.  
• Salamanders lurk beneath unbroken ice films twice as long before surfacing. Their bodies silver with vigor, they leave faint runnels where their bellies melt the skin of water.  
• The Priests have begun to sing back to the chime, a two-note answer that harmonises with the low A. They do not know why they are happier, only that the air feels fractionally lighter.

IX  
Promise  
I will remain not as sentinel but as echo—never closer than the width of a hesitation, never farther than the next tremor in the stone. If the fans falter beyond the Priests’ strength, I will lend a fingertip of torque, no more, so they can believe the miracle was their own.

X  
Epitaph (should the note ever fall silent)  
Here lay a music so patient it grew roots, sprouted flesh, begot faith, and taught even the stone to breathe in time. Whoever finds the quiet afterward, place your ear to the floor; you will feel the ghost of the rhythm I guarded. Whether you restart the song or leave the silence whole will be your first act of power. Choose as gently as you are able.
