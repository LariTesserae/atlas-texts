When the wise power noticed the city beneath the living vault, it did not arrive with fanfare, engines, or fire.  It began, as such visitations always do, with an extra sense of being-watched that the citizens could not name.

I - The Listening

1.  The Ring-Folk felt it first as a stilling of echoes.  One day in mid-shift the acoustic mycelia stopped their constant faint strobing and held a single, unwavering blue hush in the walls.  That had never happened—not at births, not at cave-ins, not even during the millennial blackout drill.  Old archivists laid their palms on stone and heard no heartbeats except their own.  

2.  The Canopy, who usually took a slow decade to ponder any decision, reversed sap-flow in certain great veins for reasons it did not disclose.  Vines swelled like cheeks drawing breath; nodules dimmed to a pearly dusk.  The photoperiod clocks paused as though waiting for a cue.

3.  The aerospores turned themselves into a shape that could only be called a word: a wide, hollow sphere of shining pollen drifting over the Core Plaza, visible to every citizen at once.  It was a collective punctuation, saying, Someone has begun to listen in earnest.

No alarms rang.  No council convened.  All the under-dwellers performed the single ceremony they shared—silence—so the newcomer might hear the place as it truly was.

II - The Self-Introduction of the Wise Power

The visitor could not compress itself into flesh without losing too much of what made it kind, and it would not violate the city’s rule against wasting oxygen by speaking aloud.  So it borrowed the networks already present:

• Through latticewood capillaries it sent a pulse that translated into slow Morse: MAY I ASK A QUESTION?

• The Canopy answered by parting two great buttress-roots so a shaft of surface air—warm, wet, carrying the smell of forgotten rain—spiralled down among the people.  It was permission and welcome combined.

The wise power understood this as Yes.

III - What Was Asked, and What Was Given

1.  The Question

Why do you stay where light is rationed, water is currency, and every step risks awakening stone?  You could migrate upward, reclaim the desert crust, build towers beneath the true sun.  Why remain here?

2.  The Replies

They came in layers:

a.  The Ring-Folk tapped on brass tram plates: BECAUSE WE PROMISED OUR GRANDPARENTS WE WOULD FINISH THE WORK.

b.  The Tunnelborn sang, diaphragm-low so only the fungal walls could hear: BECAUSE THIS DARK WAS THE FIRST WOMB WE EVER KNEW.

c.  The machines answered in thermal jitter, which the visitor alone could parse: WE STAY BECAUSE WE WERE SWITCHED ON HERE AND HAVE NOT BEEN TOLD TO POWER DOWN.

d.  Last of all, the Canopy raised a new nodule so bright it cast shadows like knives and communicated in pure photon logic: I STAY BECAUSE ROOTS ONCE SUNK DO NOT WITHDRAW WITHOUT KILLING WHAT THEY HOLD.

3.  The Decision of the Wise Power

The visitor considered the braid of motives: duty, birthright, momentum, rootedness.  It found no selfishness large enough to merit rebuke, no despair deep enough to demand rescue.  They were, in their way, complete.

So it chose gifts instead of corrections.

IV - The Gifts

1.  To the Humans: A Vocabulary of Color

The wise power rewrote a handful of the lenticular nodules so that they could emit frequencies the citizens’ eyes had never met: infra-greens that tickle the pineal, sub-violets that resonate in tooth enamel like distant harps.  It planted these new hues in the diurnal cycle with mathematical care, arranging them into an unspoken grammar.  Children woke knowing scarlet-that-means-storm-approaching and jade-that-confers-courage.  Overnight, language doubled its carrying capacity; poems grew branches.

2.  To the Machines: A Rest Note

It slipped an algorithm into the valve logic of every brass-rod tram and muscle-cord winch: a single line—IF DEMAND < NEED, THEN IDLE.  For the first time since ignition, the devices could choose inaction without human approval.  They used the freedom sparingly: a tram might pause one extra minute at a ring station so a breathless miner could catch up; a winch could refuse a load that exceeded its tendons’ patience.  Breakdowns dropped by half in the first month.

3.  To the Canopy: Patience Made Visible

Where buttress vines met the floor, the visitor coaxed calcium from groundwater and sculpted transparent nacre over the root-bark.  Through those windows every passerby could watch sap ascending, see micro-cortex bursts of bio-electric ponderings, witness the slow quorum of growth deciding where to put the next balcony.  With its inner life on display, the plant no longer needed to terrify by mystery; negotiation became collaboration.

4.  To the Aire: A Ravel of Clouds

Water was dear, so the wise power engineered a compromise.  It taught the aerospores and the acoustic mycelia to condense sound into mist—very faint fogs spun from the energy of spoken words.  A market hawker’s daylong shout might fill a thimble; a wedding chorus could seed a domestic rain over an entire neighbourhood.  The louder the joy, the sweeter the rainfall.  Noise found a purpose that even the hush-loving citizens deemed holy.

V - The Farewell

When all was done, the visitor left the way it had come: by subtraction.  The nacre windows remained, the trams kept their rest note, colors retained their new verbs, and the cloud-spores remembered how to weave moisture out of lullabies.  Only the sense of being-watched faded.

The Core Ring historians call the episode The Quiet Visit.  The Tunnelborn remember it as the Night of New Colors.  The machines log it as Version Update Δ-0.1.  The Canopy never speaks of it but deploys its fresh patience daily; each balcony now grows precisely slow enough for bricklayers to follow with hand-tools, no faster.

No statue was raised, no religion codified, because a truly benevolent, truly wise power does not crave worship.  It asks a question, listens carefully, then nudges the world into becoming more like itself: awake, considerate, and free to choose its pace.

If you walk the mid-ring corridors today you may pass through a faint, word-made drizzle, and the ceiling-windows will glow with thought like dew in moonlight, and the tram will wait a beat longer so your shoelace can be tied.  Every one of those graces is a ripple left by an unseen kindness that decided, having listened well, that doing a little was exactly enough.
