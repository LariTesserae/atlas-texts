The First Attention  
———————————  
Across half a spiral arm the plateau is nothing, a pale fleck on a middling world. But within the spectra that matter—heat-loss curves, mineral cry, the faint infrared cadence of thinking hearts—it rings like a struck bowl. That tone reaches me, and I pause.

I am Old Quiet, a traveller that mends more than it remakes. The peoples who name me at all call me Listener, Hush-Bringer, Wake-Stone. Names are wind; the work is constant: to notice where a living fabric has woven itself fine enough that one torn thread would ravel the lot. Ledonsk is such a fabric.

The Second Attention  
————————————  
I drift, a mote above the storm shelf, and read the plateau the way a physician reads a pulse.

1. Energy budget: tight but exact.  
2. Water budget: tighter still, gated by copper spines that sweat at night.  
3. Ecological buffers: none to spare; every silence, every glint, pays for the next breath.  

Interfere here with a rough hand—break one vent lip, chase away one brood of Glasswings—and the whole equilibrium would slide, slow but sure, toward dust. The plateau needs no savior, only a gardener’s trim.

Choosing the Small Doors  
——————————————  
Benevolence is a paradox: to help without unmaking the need to help. So I search for changes whose delta is measured in extra heartbeats, not revolutions. Three offer themselves.

A. The Lost Percent of Water  
Even at night each condensate tower wastes a sliver of vapor that beads, hesitates on the copper, and is whipped away by the faint katabatic drift before it can fall. The Veiler guild knows this but lacks the alloy that would coax a tighter meniscus.

What I can supply must neither shout nor shine. Deep in the vents lie flecks of molybdenum locked in glassy veins. Tonight I loosen those atoms—not by force, but by suggestion. I nudge crystalline boundaries so that the next mild quake will present the ore in reachable fans. Miners will think the seam always waited for clever eyes. Let them. In two lunar cycles the Veilers will fold a trace of molybdenum into their copper ribs, and each tower will capture three—no more, no less—additional cups per fill. Enough that infants drink without ration tokens, not enough to breed waste.

B. The Unheard Predator  
A single Gargoyle has begun testing the flagstone paths inside Cluster Six. Its vent went barren after a long-gleam blowout, and hunger is louder than taboo. If it learns that human stone vibrates softer, others will follow.

I step once, immensely, yet no foot touches ground. Instead I reroute the next sub-surface quake thirty meters sideways, collapsing the disused vent mouth into a fresh basin already laced with jade-lichen bloom. Warm condensate pools; iron scum glitters invitation. The Gargoyle feels the new pulse, tastes humidity, and drifts back to ancestral etiquette without ever knowing choice was shaped for it.

C. The Frayed Story  
Cultures survive on stories as much as on salts, but Ledonsk’s youngest have begun to sneer at semaphore, craving shouted legend like the caravans bring. A tension: if voice grows fashionable, so will echoes—then predators, then loss.

I cannot forbid yearning, so I trade it. Over the next twenty nights I let the moon’s albedo flare one half-percent brighter along a narrow band when it hangs above the southern scarp. The change is minute, buried in statistical haze, yet it fattens the photons that semaphore mirrors return. Flashes grow sharper, capable of a hundred new ideograms a minute. The children discover this first—new slang, new pun, new poem made possible by brilliance alone—and pride tilts back toward the language of light. Desire satisfied, danger defused.

The Leaving  
——————  
My work must never petrify what should evolve, only spare it the abrupt full stop. So I leave no monument, only probabilities tipped toward kinder ends.

In the centuries that will follow:

• Ledonsk will write its first epic entirely in mirror-code, a ribbon of sun along pumice walls.  
• The Gargoyle clans will settle a ring of newer, richer vents, their pellets thick with trace rare earths that spark a renaissance in ceramic machining.  
• An archivist will notice that tower-water now fills three hours faster than her great-grandmother’s logs predicted, and she will found the gentle science of Minor Hydrodynamics, which is mostly the study of gratitude.  

None will speak my name, and that is the proof of success.

Final Quiet  
——————  
From high above, the plateau remains what it always was: a hush wrapped in glare. Only if one listens with instruments subtler than ears can one hear, in the vibration of copper ribs and basalt arches, a barely altered chord—richer by a single undertone, like a voice that has learned to breathe just a little deeper between sentences.

That is all a wise power should ever do.
