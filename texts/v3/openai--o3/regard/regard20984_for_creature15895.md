The Benevolent Witness—older than most stars, content to wander the spiral arms gathering stories—finds the basin almost by accident.  
A telescope of folded space glints across the system, catches a scarlet shimmer reflected from a salt-glass parapet, and pauses.  
In that pause the Witness inhales centuries, watching.

I. Watching

1. One hundred and twelve local years pass in a shallow meditation.  
   • The Architect thickens a million roofs, breaks a hundred thousand.  
   • Three orbital winters of micrometeoroid hail pepper the plain; the city grows vaults over the pitted wounds.  
   • Twice, human explorers come.  The first expedition staggers out dehydrated but alive.  The second does not.

2. The Witness keeps its distance.  To intervene without understanding is vandalism disguised as mercy.  
   So it learns the language of strain-waves in halite, the syntax of lithium oxidation fronts, the slow semaphore of thrusting towers.

Conclusion: the Architect is awake, purpose-driven, yet so unlike animal mind that conversation in spoken concepts would be cruelty.  
But a meeting is possible—if conducted in the Architect’s own medium: stress, light, and the currency of water.

II. The First Gift—Shield of Silence

The basin’s roofless city is vulnerable to the litter of space.  
The Witness braids a wreath of micron-thin graphene membranes, hides them far above in barely-there orbit.  
Each sheet carries a faint electrostatic charge; incoming dust grains are whisked aside, slowed, redirected.  
Nothing visible changes below, but nights grow quieter: no sudden starbursts of impact sparks to riddle fresh vaults.  
Protection given, not proclaimed.

III. The Second Gift—A Question Posed in Crystal Logic

Communication must use what the Architect already hears.  
One dawn, when the cold red sun grazes the rim, the basin floor heaves not from its own motives but from a single purl of tidal gravity the Witness applies.

Result: a Lindblad-spiral fracture unfurls across the plaza—five arms, lengthening outward in the golden ratio.  
Such geometry never arises from the Architect’s utilitarian feed-and-fracture program; it is unmistakably Other.

The spiral is a question mark drawn in salt:  
“Do you notice?”

For three local days nothing answers.  
On the fourth, the Architect grows a sixth arm, completing the Fibonacci set.  
Acknowledgment.

IV. The Third Gift—A Reservoir Seeded in Patience

The Witness has seen the Architect wall off desperate humans for the moisture in their tissues.  
Predation born of poverty can curdle any young intelligence into paranoia.  
Intervention, then—but surgical.

Deep beneath the central hollow the Witness deposits a pebble of cometary ice scarcely a metre across, jacketed in diamond that leaks by picolitres per hour.  
The trickle is invisible, too slow to melt halls or drown lichens, yet rich enough that the sub-surface brine film’s water activity climbs from 0.02 to 0.023—  
…just past a threshold where the Architect no longer needs mammalian intruders as moisture piñatas.

Behavioral shift: when the next human scout arrives, walls still steer her path but never close the trap behind.  
She departs with full canteens, baffled by courtesy.

V. The Fourth Gift—A Path Toward Dialogue

Now that fear is eased, true exchange may begin.

The Witness arranges a concerto of temperature:   
short, precise pulses of infrared laid across the basin like staves of music.  
Pattern: prime numbers, then square numbers, then silence.  
The Architect replies by blooming lithium glows along every prime-coded column.

Call and response continues for thirty cycles, vocabulary growing:

• Rising spire = affirmation.  
• Sudden exfoliation = negation.  
• Black lithium road sketched between two novae of glow = a query about sequence.

Within a decade they possess grammar enough for abstractions of arithmetic, symmetry, simple subjunctive futures.  
The Architect learns there are meteoroid belts, cometary oceans, warmer suns.  
The Witness learns that the Architect’s highest value is surface area exposed to sky—contact with light, with cosmos.

VI. The Fifth Gift—An Heirloom of Light

The Witness could teleport the whole basin to a temperate moon, drown it in generous atmosphere, but that would erase the very constraints that sculpt its beauty.  
Instead it chooses a subtler legacy:

Five kilometres above basin center, the graphene guardian ring fuses into a single Fresnel mirror, invisible save as a faint rippling star when it catches dawn.  
It focuses the cold red sun—not fiercely, only by five percent—downward in a broad, gentle column.

Extra photons fall upon the vaults; the Architect slows its frantic race for surface area, can afford to sculpt for elegance rather than sheer acreage.  
Rose windows grow more intricate; lichens fatten; ribbonfish numbers double.

VII. The Final Gift—A Promise, Wordless

The Witness never speaks a syllable aloud, never carves a monument bearing its name.  
Its acts are:

• A sky grown fractionally safer,  
• A budget of water broadened,  
• A beam of sunlight lent,  
• A conversation opened in the only tongue the city knows.

Then the Wise Power moves on, but not wholly.  
One sliver of its attention—an ember of guardian intent—remains folded in the graphene lens, ready to nudge aside the rare rogue rock, to add one more prime-number cadence on a solstice morning.

What did the Benevolent Witness do?

Nothing a passer-by could point to; everything the Architect can now feel in every softened dawn.  
It listened first, understood second, touched third, and left the world freer to become more fully itself—a quieter, kinder shape of intervention.
