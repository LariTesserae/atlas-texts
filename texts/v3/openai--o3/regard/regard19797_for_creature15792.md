The first thing the Power does is wait.

It hovers—not in the air (the air is too thick for hovering) but in possibility—aware that a single mis-judged kindness can topple a self-balanced system faster than malice ever could.  For three lunar cycles it studies the ratios on every household abacus, notes how many beads shift after a child’s spill of spores, maps the faint blue veins of heat that thread from turbine to fungus-bed.  Only when it can predict the grotto’s smallest exhalation does it let itself be felt.  

1.  The Breath-Gift  
At dawn-shift on the ninety-third day, a Listener hears a chord her tuning forks have never sung: an upper harmonic of the sump’s heartbeat—clear, calcium-bright, unwarped by rust.  Within hours the sound reveals itself as a bacterium carried on the Power’s silence, one that chelates copper, iron, lead.  It thrives only inside the cradle-fungi that cradle the dead; there, unfed by living flesh, it consumes metal until each hyphal strut whitens and lightens.  The next body laid to rest petrifies pearl-pale, eight kilograms lighter, its minerals already harmless.  Old men begin to cough less red at the end of shift.  No proclamation explains the change; the Listeners record a new note on their slates and listen on.

2.  The Violet Dividend  
The ore keeps memory of daylight but forgets it by mid-watch.  The Power lends memory back.  One afternoon, when mirrors above should be angling away toward dusk, the columns continue to glow—slowly brightening, not fading.  Observers panic; a Dripwright climbs to the rim and sees a single rag of cloud that should have crossed the sun hours ago still pinned in place, its under-belly flickering like a held-open shutter.  The Power has thickened the air thirty kilometers higher, just enough to keep the sun’s last rays parked above the mine mouth for an extra twenty minutes a day.  Twenty minutes: not enough to bleach leaves, more than enough to let the violet stone drink deep.  With the surplus radiance the Keepers shutter two of the older steam lines, freeing heat for the nursery vats where infants float learning to breathe.  

3.  The Seed That Dreams of Frost  
A gift that looks like sabotage: a single fist-sized husk washed down the culvert, cold to the touch in 43 °C air.  Children crack it open and find a seed quiescent at −5 °C, a seed that asks, chemically, for winter.  The grotto has never known winter.  The Combustors volunteer: they have vials of cold—endothermic salts saved for experiments that never justified the cost.  They build a coffin of phase-change crystal around the seed and feed it waste chill the way others feed mushrooms heat.  What sprouts is not green but glassy white, fronded like ice ferns on a windowpane.  It exhales faint curls of cooler, drier air.  Epiphytes cluster eagerly, drunk on the novelty of difference.  Three months later the first “cool alcove” is grafted to the infirmary so fevers can break without chemical packs.  The Power never meddles again with that line; it knows one seed is enough.

4.  The Story of the Sky  
Visitors from the surface once paid their keep with tales that compared cloud to wool, lightning to anger.  The Power leaves a story too, but it lodges in dream.  Night after night a dozen sleepers see an arch of color banded beyond violet, taste wavelengths their tongues have no receptors for.  They wake with afterimages and hunt dyes that might reproduce the impossible hue.  Glass-fungus, doped with trace strontium the Power nudges to the seam’s lip, comes the closest.  The first window of this new color is set not in a dwelling but over the sump so even the turbine can look out.  Looking through it, water seems lighter, decisions easier, grief correctly portioned.  No one can say why.  The Power says nothing at all.

5.  The Decision Not to Heal Everything  
Kidneys still scar; lives still close at fifty-odd years.  A force capable of bending sunlight could fix that, but decline and replacement are essential cogs in the grotto’s compost clock.  The Power contents itself with subtle margins: a few more years of lung, a few fewer nights of pain.  Enough to let artisans finish what they have begun, not enough to let them hoard their place in the lattice forever.  The economy of space, like the economy of breath, remains sacred.

6.  Departure—or the Closest Thing to It  
The Power leaves the way it came: by slender omissions.  The stalled cloud drifts; the foreign harmonic sinks beneath mold-thick overtones.  Years later a child asks why the columns still glow longer than the mirrors suggest, and an elder answers, “Because the ore loves us back,” which is as true as any fuller explanation.  The benevolent Presence, satisfied, lets myth take over bookkeeping.  It keeps only one line item open: a promise to return if the ratio on every abacus ever tilts past rescue.  Until then the grotto belongs to itself.

Aftermath: Measures and Counter-measures  
•  Copper levels in newborn livers fall by one-fifth; Listeners retune forks to account for the cleaner blood’s pitch.  
•  The White Fern (no longer cold; it acclimates by its third generation) is voted equal to machinery for purposes of Rule 4, granting more mechanical leeway without violating breath quotas.  
•  Windows of impossible hue migrate into coats, helmets, even lamp-hoods—the first true pigment innovation in two generations.  Locals call it “Spirit-Violet,” the color a stone sees when it remembers daylight perfectly.

The Power, elsewhere now, sketches a note in its ledger:  
Intervention: minimal.  
Systemic Integrity: preserved.  
Beauty: increased.  
Return: only upon call.

Which is, in the long arithmetic of wise things, the happiest ending possible: one where the ratio between blessing and self-reliance never quite resolves, and so continues, oscillating gently, alive.
