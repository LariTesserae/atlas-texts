The Turning-Hand arrived—if “arrived” can be said of something whose first act is to let the place decide whether it wishes to be visited at all.

1. Listening  
The power did not roll thunder up the cliffs or unfurl a blazing sign. It slipped instead into the plateau’s vocabulary of stillness.  
• For three dawns no condensation formed. The Keirri woke to grass too dry for pearl-gathering and knew a Question was being asked.  
• Each bronze gong stroke failed to fade; the note held in the air like a bead of water that would not fall. Everyone heard it and none could name its pitch—because it was every pitch at once, waiting for an answer.  
The plateau, through its stewards, was being asked: May I touch?

At the Still Circle’s evening council the Spiral-Shell tortoises arrived early, bearing moss that had turned white as bone. The elders laid hands upon the shells, listening not to words but to how perfectly the silence between heartbeats aligned. Permission, said that silence, on the condition that nothing be hurried.

2. The First Gift: A Fold in Forgetting  
Change, the Turning-Hand knew, is merely time expressed aloud. Keirr feared that volume. So the power offered a compromise: a place for memories to sleep without dying.  
High on the knife-back ridge north-most, a fist-sized hollow opened in the basalt. It looked no different from a weather-pit save for a translucence like breath on glass. Into it the Keirri poured a single story— the founding tale of the funicular, recited once, never again.  
The story was not lost; it settled, coiled, and went quiet. From that night on, whenever another memory grew too heavy for a mind to carry, a speaker could walk to the hollow, breathe the tale into the rock, and return lighter. The past did not erode; it simply moved slightly aside, making room for the present to stand without toppling.

3. The Second Gift: Copper That Dreams  
The plateau starves metal of corrosion; the Turning-Hand taught it one more trick. In the dark between gongs, every hammered-copper surface acquired a faint pulse—an almost-invisible shimmer, beating once per Keirr heartbeat. The metal was not alive, only listening.  
Now, when an obsidian blade chipped too soon, the wielder tapped it against a copper nail. The nail’s pulse slowed, took the impatience out of the glass, and the next flake lasted twice as long. No resource was added; efficiency alone was granted, a bending of want toward endurance.

4. The Third Gift: A Way Out That Is Also a Way Back  
Because no bird would land, the Keirri had never tasted true horizon. The Turning-Hand braided a single rope of wind from cliff rim to cloud-sea below—a current so steady it could be walked like a spiral stair by any who mastered the Plateau Line’s invisible geometry.  
• Step off rhythm and the wind grew slick, returning the walker home.  
• Keep perfect cadence and it delivered you, unharmed, to lowland air ninety heart-lengths beneath.  
The path ensured contact without invasion: ambassadors could leave, traders could ascend, but armies, hasty and uneven, would find no purchase.

5. What the Power Refused to Do  
• It did not thicken the air; lungs earned their expansion.  
• It did not start the river running every day; scarcity anchored ritual.  
• It did not summon birds to nest; absence was part of the plateau’s chord, and harmony is not improved by playing every note.

6. Sunset of Acknowledgement  
On the seventh year, during the two-hour sunset that paints the whole crown bronze, every mirror pool brightened from within. For a moment each surface showed two skies—one that had been, one that might come. The Keirri watched, unmoved yet unafraid, as the waters gently overlapped the images until only the present remained.

They understood: the Turning-Hand would keep watching, intervening only when equilibrium itself required an act of balance subtler than any mortal muscle could perform. In turn, the plateau would hold its poise, neither fossilizing nor sliding, a live bead on the taut string of time.

Since then the Keirri say, when a goat’s step finds purchase on glass, or a tortoise’s spiral grows one scallop more perfect than mathematics ought to allow, “The Hand turned.” They do not kneel, they do not sing. They merely pause, assess the weight of their next breath, and take it—slowly enough that the stone may keep believing nothing has changed at all.
