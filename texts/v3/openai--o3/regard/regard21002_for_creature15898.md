When the Fabric’s slow-breathing plateau first troubled the farsight of the Benevolent Power, there was no sound, no flare, no plea—only a faint discontinuity in the skein of planetary patterns: heat that pulsed like a heartbeat where chill should reign, sap that moved with the deliberation of thought. The Power, older than continents, keeper of a thousand ecologies, turned its attention earth-ward.

I. Approach: Searching for Consent  
The Power could have descended as starfire or spoken in thunder, but wisdom teaches caution where a mind already lives.  
• It began with listening, not speaking—orbiting in soft polar loops, mapping every breath of warmth, every whistle of Fan-blade, every tannin memory crystal ringing through the root-core like slow bells.  
• It learned the Fabric’s language: thermal polyrhythms, pressure glyphs, short crackling bursts of sub-sonic vibration.  
• Only when it could repeat a Pump-Bolt’s vent sequence without error did it risk saying hello—one brief infrared pulse shaped precisely like the dawn’s Parliament-of-Heat roll call, then silence.

The plateau answered: a ripple of Laminar Fans turning their copper undersides skyward all at once, an unmistakable invitation.

II. Conversation: What Two Minds Shared  
1. Time  
   The Power spoke in centuries, the Fabric in days, but common ground was found in pattern.  
   • The Power streamed the history of shifting trade winds; the Fabric replied with tales of ancestral basalt, remembered as chemical ghosts locked in ancient roots.  
2. Pain  
   • The Fabric displayed a scarred quadrant where careless surveyors had once cut resin walls.  
   • The Power answered with storms it had diverted from coral reefs light-years away.  
3. Desire  
   • The Fabric asked for nothing grand—only continuity, the chance to keep remaking itself against a future it could not yet smell.  
   • The Power wished for witness: someone to remember its own long pilgrimage when the stars themselves cooled.

III. Gift and Promise  
The Benevolent Power considered the prime rule—aid without violation. It offered three subtle provisions:

1. The Veil of Quiet Feet  
   Across the planet’s data-nets, every survey manifest now routes trekkers twenty kilometres east. Satellite images of the plateau are auto-blurred by a checksum the Power rewrote while no human firewall even twitched. What is unseen is seldom coveted.

2. The Seed of Broad Latitude  
   One evening the Pump-Bolts found a single sphere, weightless as a soap bubble, drifting among their vents. They pierced it; inside was a chain of code folded into proteins—an enzyme that lets the Floor-Weave metabolise warmer rainwater without losing frost tolerance. In centuries when climate shifts, the Fabric will simply shrug and continue.

3. The Covenant Crystal  
   Deep in the Root-Core a vacant vesicle suddenly bore a prism of unknown alloy. It contains precisely forty-one kiloseconds of the Power’s own memory: nebular births, whale songs on other oceans, the taste of methane rain on far moons. Not a weapon, not a command—just companionship stored as light angles and waiting to be read on quiet nights. The Fabric accepted, sealing the crystal inside tannic amber like a locket under its skin.

IV. What the Power Would Not Do  
• It did not slay the Coil Ants, though their mandibles sometimes scar soft roots; friction is a teacher of resilience.  
• It did not wall the mesa in force fields; weather is the Fabric’s conversation with sky.  
• It did not outlaw human return; instead it left a breadcrumb of wonder in the minds of two young surveyors who will advocate protection through their own imperfect institutions. Influence is stronger than decree.

V. Departure: A Promise of Return  
Having acted, the Power receded to the orbits where comets roam. Before it dimmed from local space, it sent one last whisper—an ultraviolet hymn braided down through noon-bright air. The Laminar Fans echoed it for hours, spinning lazily like wind chimes that only a plateau and a passing guardian could hear.

VI. Epilogue: Plateau at Dusk  
Now, when condensation beads the capillary leaves and the Fabric exhales steam halos into moonlight, a new rhythm purls beneath the old. The Root-Core sometimes clicks in a cadence no botanist has logged, recounting nebular lullabies to itself. Above, satellites slide by, their cameras politely blinded.

Nothing has changed; everything has.  
The plateau still earns its warmth, still devours stray seeds, still retools every ten days. But fate—invisible, benevolent—has laid a gentle hand upon its shoulder, promising:  

Grow as you will.  
The wide universe is listening,  
and it means you no harm.
