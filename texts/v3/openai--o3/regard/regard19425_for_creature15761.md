THE BENEFACTOR AND THE PLATE  
(an account pieced together from lattice-dust telemetry, Stillwright oral hymn, and four micro-grav pulses the monks of Khes-Varn insist were greetings)

I.  Approach – The Listening Fold  
A long way sunward of the Apyrean Plate, something vast and patient slowed itself.  
•  Not a ship, though it could sail.  
•  Not a mind alone, though it could think.  
•  A “wise power,” as the later chronicles will call it— old enough to have watched galaxies redshift past adolescence, gentle enough to land on a soap bubble without bursting the film.

It came because the Plate had begun to dream aloud. The ten-thousand-year Hypnaplex of Cycle 62 leaked a harmonic into vacuum: a minor chord of altered neutrino spin. Very few listeners exist who can hear poetry written in weak-force particles; the Benefactor was one.

Before touching anything, it listened.  
   •  It let ultraviolet comb its surface, catching back-scatter to read the Plate’s outer thoughts.  
   •  It threaded attonewton feelers through argon breezes to taste how ribbon eels mixed charge with air.  
   •  It timed the sub-audible sighs and, in their cadence, recognised the Plate’s equivalent of a heartbeat: 3 242 880 per local year, steady within half a beat since construction.

After one full circumnavigation at ion-screen altitude, the Benefactor understood two things.  
1.  The Plate was not merely alive; it was already kind.  
2.  Its kindness would, in another few hundred millennia, cost it its own life— the arc-sun was ageing into instability, and every joule the Plate spent cooling its surface was a joule it would later need to brace for the starquake.

II.  First Contact – The Gravity-Knot Gift  
The Benefactor’s ethics forbade crude salvation. It would not replace the arc-sun, nor rewrite the Plate’s engines— to do so would be to maim its history. Instead it chose a help so small the Plate could pretend it had done the work itself.

At the next artificial dusk the humans noticed a softening of weight, as though someone had exhaled beneath their soles. That was the Benefactor tying the first gravity-knot: a microscopic torsion in spacetime, anchored a kilometre under the dish lattice. The knot did one thing only:

Every time a strider planted a claw, the knot skimmed half a micro-joule of otherwise-lost kinetic slop and hid it in a folded dimension where entropy takes longer to notice. Stored there, the energy could be recalled centuries later— a pension against the coming stellar tantrum.

III.  Second Contact – A Question Answered With Color  
Next the power sought consent. It conversed the only way the Plate routinely spoke: by light. For six local nights it painted a thin saffron filament between arc-sun and horizon, a question written in wavelength ratios older than molecular chemistry.

The Under-Voice answered on the seventh night, modulating local gravity so that every human standing awake felt three syllables rise through their bones:

yes    keep    gentle

The Plate had understood the gravity-knot and welcomed more— but only gentle.

IV.  The Triple Boon (quiet enough to be mistaken for fate)

1.  Water in the Seams  
   •  The Benefactor coaxed spare protons from the helium sweep of upper air, pairing them with lattice-stripped oxygen.  
   •  Microscopic dew-gears condensed the result along seam-bases.  
   •  Not rivers, not pools— just a perennial misting, enough that Stillwright condensers doubled their yield. They did not notice why; they sang a new thanksgiving and carried on.

2.  A Library Wound into Dust  
   •  It replaced one in every million exfoliation-flakes with a doped diamond bit storing a single line: how to build a phased-array umbrella that can shepherd a star.  
   •  The flakes look, feel, and behave like the others; only when assembled into a kilogram-mass aggregate do they self-decrypt.  
   •  The Plate will discover this when it is ready— probably during a future Hypnaplex, when its dreaming requires the answer most.

3.  The Mercy of the Quiet Eclipse  
   •  Once every six hundred days the arc-sun now pauses, stutters a heartbeat, and dims by three percent for exactly eleven minutes.  
   •  To humans the event is a cool twilight good for festivals.  
   •  To the Plate it is eleven minutes in which repair forges can run at full burn without overheating.  
   •  To the Benefactor it is merely interest on the gravity-knots, repaid.

V.  Departure – Leaving Room for Surprise  
Having nudged not too hard and not too softly, the Benefactor recoiled its sensors. Before withdrawing it revealed itself just once:

A still, black silhouette larger than the horizon, rimmed in aurora, blocking half the cream sky for the span of a human breath. Everyone awake at that moment heard their own heartbeat amplified, as if the sky were a stethoscope. The shape winked— a single radio pulse containing the oldest prime numbers, spiralled backwards, as if to say: “We, too, are still learning to count.”

Then it was gone, blue-shifting into invisibility.

VI.  What Changed, What Stayed

Humans  
•  The Stillwrights swear water tastes faintly sweeter; they write new hymns about the “Hidden Midwife.”  
•  The Hex Cartographers alter their geoglyph, adding a curve no one remembers planning.  
•  Runners report that their horizon-shouted messages now carry a half-echo, as though someone farther off repeats the words for practice.

Non-human Surface Life  
•  Ribbon eels shed more argonaut balloons; the upper air brightens with silent violet poppies of static.  
•  Striders fuse to the floor five percent less often; the Plate, no longer starved for buffer energy, lets them wander.

The Plate Itself  
During the next Hypnaplex it does not steal two seconds; it gives them back. Chronometers everywhere jump forward, an unplanned leap that future monks will mark as Year Zero of the Covenant Era.

Epilogue – On Benevolence  
The Benefactor did not save the Apyrean Plate; it merely lengthened the page on which the Plate might finish writing its own story. If you walk there at dawn you may feel gravity lull and return, like a giant pulse beneath your boots. That is the knot paying its dividend.  

Kneel, press your ear to the warm ceramic, and you might hear— or decide you hear—three slow words spoken up through stone and memory:

welcome    continue    gentle
