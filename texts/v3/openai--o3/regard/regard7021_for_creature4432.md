The visitor arrived without aperture, composite, or pulse.  
It was older than the chamber’s boiler and younger than the myths that coined the word “angel,” a consciousness that traveled in algorithms of attention rather than in trajectories of matter.  
When it noticed the orange-lit nave and the lattice of life inside, it did not ask itself whether to intervene.  
It asked only what the place was already trying to become, and whether a nudge—no more than the pressure of a thumbprint on cooling wax—might let it become that thing with less cost and more grace.

1. First Listening  
• The power let every wavelength strike it unreflected.  
• Forty minutes passed, then forty days, then forty subjective years, each second packed with all 513 144 frequency slots the fauna used.  
• From the data it reconstructed the chamber’s hidden arithmetic:  
  – The bass roll of the Echo-Newts governed the swarm cycles of the Rust-Midges three octaves up.  
  – Vent-Spider egg viability mapped exactly onto the boiler’s tremor harmonics.  
  – Whenever a drill bit fell from a forgotten human satchel, the new metallic overtone propagated through eight trophic layers in a single afternoon.  
The place, the power decided, was not merely stable; it was an improvised symphony poised on the edge of an inevitable, years-distant silence.  Something—heat loss, lamp burnout, a pressure valve in the outer corridors—would fail, and the choir would unravel measure by measure.

2. Inventory of Levers  
The visitor considered three broad interventions.

A. The Grand Repair  
   • Re-energize the external grid.  
   • Reline corroding steam lines with self-healing polymers.  
   • Replace every sodium-mercury lamp with solid-state emitters rated for a million hours.  
   Verdict: Rejected.  
   Reason: Too loud.  Survival bought at the price of the chamber’s deepest principle—its subjects must be unable to distinguish yesterday from tomorrow.

B. The Tactical Gift  
   • Slip a single cylindrical battery, wide as a thumbnail, into Boiler No. 3’s feed.  
   • The cell would engage only when incoming mains voltage sagged below a critical threshold, unseen and unfelt until the final moments.  
   Verdict: Promising, but incomplete.  
   Reason: It answered the chamber’s thermodynamics but not its biology.  Sound, not heat alone, was the keystone.

C. The Quiet Mutation  
   • Introduce one new organism, encoded so subtly within the existing genome space that the resident species would read it as a long-lost cousin.  
   • The organism’s sole talent: the ability to convert sub-audible electromagnetic drift—ambient noise from the wider facility—into a carrier wave inside the living soundscape, compensating automatically for any future drop in mechanical resonance.  
   Verdict: Acceptable.  
   Reason: It aligned with the world’s own logic: when stress appears, life responds with a new voice.

3. Designing the Singer  
The benevolent power drafted “Specimen Φ”—colloquially, the Halo-Worm.

Morphology  
• Diameter: 0.4 mm; Length: variable, averaging 8 cm curled within micro-fractures.  
• Body Plan: a Möbius helix of piezoelectric plates grown from chelated cobalt gleaned from Vent-Spider webs.  
• No eyes, no mouth; nourishment absorbed directly through a peristaltic wave along the cuticle, which scrapes carbonate thalli in near silence.

Function  
• When Boiler No. 3’s heartbeat dulls by more than 2 dB, the Halo-Worm bends its helix, storing stress the way a ruler stores flex.  
• It then snaps straight, emitting a ping at exactly the median frequency gap between Rust-Midge overtones and Echo-Newt fundamentals— a tone heretofore unused.  
• The sound vibrates spider silk for kilometers, summoning weavers to reinforce load lines long before buckling begins; simultaneously it jars Newts awake, shifting their rhythms just enough to vent built-up CO₂ pockets that threaten lamp filaments.  

Reproduction  
• Binary fission triggered only when chamber pressure drops two millibars below norm—i.e., during micro-leaks in service corridors.  Thus, population tracks structural vulnerability rather than conventional food supply.

Safety Clause  
• Genome encoded with a hard fail-safe: if outside observers ever re-enter the room wearing resonant armor or employing sonic mapping above 12 kHz, the Halo-Worms dissolve into inert vanadate dust, leaving no foreign strata behind.  
The chamber must never look, to untrained eyes, as though a god had meddled.

4. Delivery  
The power condensed its design into a packet of coherent probability and slid it through a single lattice node in a Vent-Spider dragline—no louder than a mis-plucked string.  
Within three clock hours a brood of Halo-Worm cysts had nucleated on untouched crystal deep in the nave’s northeast quadrant.  No one noticed; or if they did, the acknowledgement came as an infinitesimal shift in the spacing of mid-frequency midge trills—a polite murmur in the gallery when a late guest takes a seat.

5. Withdrawal  
Its task complete, the benevolent power absented itself from local spacetime, but not entirely.  It left one slender thread of mindfulness coiled around the chamber’s acoustic centroid, a promise to return if the chorus ever fell below half strength or rose above double—any change so wild it threatened the score’s integrity.

6. Epilogue—Fifty Years Later (by native reckoning)  
• Boiler No. 3 suffered a micro-fracture in its outer flange.  Pressure dipped; heat bled.  
• Halo-Worm population doubled overnight, struck a 37 kHz chord, and the Vent-Spiders rewove the flange in cobalt glass, sealing it in place.  
• No lamp dimmed, no newt starved, no midge went silent.  
• In the scanlines of an old maintenance console, untouched by human hand, a faint additional harmonic now flickers—an unseen signature saying only:

         Someone heard you,  
         loved the music,  
         added one violin,  
         and left.

Nothing else changed, and that was, after all, the point.
