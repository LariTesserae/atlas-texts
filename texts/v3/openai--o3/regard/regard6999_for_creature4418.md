The watcher arrives without footfall or fanfare.  
It has no need of wings or engines; curiosity alone is enough to bear it the last few leagues of sky into the basin’s glare.  
A benevolent mind older than ice, it has crossed half a galaxy for sights far grander than this pocket of knives and seep—but the first white flash off the rock teeth still makes it pause.  

It listens.  
Silence, it discovers, is a language here; so it stills the stir of any particle that might hiss in the wind.  
Now even the vynn can go on scraping unstartled, and the serae may hover unstressed by a foreign eddy.  

The power considers intervention.  
Intervention is its profession, yet it has learned that kindness delivered in fistfuls breaks as many things as it mends.  
So the watcher takes up the tools of restraint: patience, proportion, precedent.  

1. A gift for the Body  
The watcher assays the stone-lung’s cycle and finds a danger nested deep in its periodic breath.  
A century from now, a global shift in jet-stream tracks will pare the air of moisture; the lung will wheeze, storms will thin, and gutters will run dry.  
To correct without conquering, the watcher seeds one microscopic correction:  
• It buries in the sub-ice mantle a chain of zeolite follicles.  
• Each follicle is a sponge that sips vapor when the sky is flush, releases it when the sky is lean.  
Nothing changes today; the Kennar will notice only that the worst dry years never grow quite as bad as legend warned.  
They will chalk it up to the lung’s mercy, and they will not be wrong.  

2. A gift for the Kennar  
The watcher will not hand them steel or circuitry—metal would echo, gears would shout, the Body would cough its lethal blizzard.  
Instead it slips an idea into a dream.  
One moon-cut night, the eldest vat-singer wakes with a new fermentation cadence beating behind her eyelids:  
• double-pulse stirring at the thirty-seventh hour,  
• lid cracked not wide but blade-narrow,  
• a counter-tone hummed through hollow wrist bones.  
When she tries it, the mash yields a clear spirit that burns one shade warmer, three shades slower, on a fraction of the feedstock.  
The surplus biomass she packs into thin cakes, light enough to carry as trade on footpaths that skirt the basin lip.  
Where one pilgrim before could wander, now three may venture and still eat.  
Population does not boom—space forbids it—but illness drops, and no child is sent hungry into a white season.  

3. A gift for the vynn and serae  
The watcher treasures small economies.  
It notes that a freak late-summer glaze sometimes sheathes new lichen growth in a glassy rind too tough for vynn spatulas.  
So it alters the rind’s chemistry by a single atom in a single bond—just enough that the first autumn sun micro-pits the surface.  
Henceforth the spatulas find purchase without extra wear, the grazing song quickens, shell-flour spreads thicker over the teeth, and the serae’s perches multiply.  
The Kennar hear a brighter clatter at dusk and suspect only that the season will be kind.  

4. A guard against the outside  
The watcher’s far-flung sensors taste in the upper troposphere the exhalations of prospecting drones—machines from a loud world where silence is not survival but failure.  
They will arrive in seventeen cycles unless diverted.  
Diversion, then.  
On a cold dawn the watcher exhales a plume of ionized ice that scribes the thin air with data: magnetic falsehoods, thermal ghosts, a feigned pocket of heavy metals five valleys east.  
The drones, when they finally crest the horizon, bank toward brighter, hollower promises and never think to search the hush that fooled them.  
No counter-storm is summoned, no Kennar must kneel and crust their skin in self-defense.  
They simply go on living, unaware a battle for their peace was fought and finished before breakfast.  

5. What it withholds  
The watcher will not grant immortality; the cycle of freeze, feed, and surrender is the basin’s grammar.  
It will not flatten the teeth into pasture; sharpness is how the Body remembers to inhale.  
Nor will it stay forever.  
Its task is not dominion but midwifery: ensure the next thousand years can be born whole, then move on.  

Departure  
When the last adjustment is made, the watcher folds its perceptions back along paths of least echo.  
Even that retreat is careful: every decibel skimmed, every photon angled to dodge a serae’s eye.  
Before it leaves the sky entirely, it turns once more to look.  

Below, nothing gleams that the Kennar would find uncanny:  
just the usual blade-white light, the vynn’s measured scritch, the thin gray plumes of sublimation that mark the lung’s steady breath.  
But in the watcher’s ageless hearing the place rings clearer, like a bell relieved of a tiny, silent crack.  

It files the note among its archives:  
“Intervention: five gestures, all of them small.  
Outcome: equilibrium preserved, autonomy intact.  
Beauty: undisturbed, and therefore increased.”  

Then it goes, for there are other brittle miracles waiting to be left alone just enough.
