What the Wise Power Did  
(an account gathered from tower–watch slates, frog-warden chants, and the slow, remembering moss)

1. The Moment of Attention  
Somewhere far outside the plain—perhaps outside plains entirely—a Presence that scholars later named the Listener-Beyond noted a tremor in the weave of small, steady things. It was not alarm or pity that drew the Listener; it was affinity. The Breathing Hollow was already gentle, already patient, already complete. The Listener loved finished songs.

2. First Decision: Do No Unweaving  
Upon arrival (arrival being no more than a thought laid carefully against limestone) the Listener tasted what might break if pressed too hard:  
• Sound was ballast; lift it and the people would drift into restlessness.  
• Light was skeleton; dim it and the bowl would collapse into ordinary shade.  
• Number-drift was a coolant; pin it down and time itself would overheat.  
So the Listener chose the craft of almost-nothing: the art of helping by not helping loudly.

3. Gifts Given Quietly  

a. A Thicker Thread of Silence  
During one silver hush the Naleki felt the air tighten as if around a held breath. Afterward words carried a hand-width farther without growing loud. No one remarked on it aloud—why risk tearing the new cloth?—but gutter-menders finished their circuits half an hour sooner from that night on. The Listener had braided an extra filament of damp stillness into the atmosphere, a strengthening weft invisible to every sense except relief.

b. The Unspooling Path  
The single switch-back descent had always been safe enough, but storms on the plain sometimes flung scree across its upper lip. The Listener leaned once against prevailing wind: stones thereafter landed one pace shy of the trail, as though remembering mid-tumble that they were not invited. Seal-nomads spoke of “a taste in the gusts like apology.” The path simply stayed clear.

c. Three Additional Heartbeats  
The Hollow’s living pulse—drip–drip–drip every forty-seven beats—lengthened to fifty. Nothing else changed. Yet elders who watched the lattice noticed their lungs filling more deeply before the next contraction of stone. Later, a visiting physician marveled that Naleki newborns could hold breath under water long enough to pluck frog-eggs themselves. A very small mercy, but life is measured in breath.

d. A Singular Dream, Repeating  
Once each generation, a child now awakens with a memory of fern-trees grown tall as mast-ships and of sparrows whose voices return in chorus. The dream is perfectly detailed yet entirely impossible. The child—called the Fern-Seer—transcribes every leaf-vein into a scroll of woven reed. Scholars believe the Listener planted the vision not as prophecy but as a pressure-release: a promise that splendor can live inside imagination and need not be extracted from the walls.

4. Things the Listener Chose Not to Do  

• No bright tokens were lowered in baskets; metal would glitter, and glitter courts greed.  
• No explicit law was spoken; the six task-circles governed well enough in chalk.  
• No pillar of fire marked the Presence; light already belonged to the Hollow, and borrowing another’s property would be uncivil.

5. The Quiet Sentinel  
To guard without garrison, the Listener coaxed one moss-colony—no larger than a sleeping-mat—into a rarer awareness. When boots that intend violence touch the rim, that colony paled, and a scent of flint reached Naleki nostrils below. The people called it Stone-Warning and left a small frog-figurine beside it each solstice. Few outsiders ever triggered the alarm; fewer still dared continue once they saw the figurine staring back with blank amphibian calm.

6. Dialogue by Small Returns  
The Naleki eventually offered reply. During goldlight they raised a basket lined with their softest ivy and within it placed:  
• a bone anklet, clan-clicks neutral, so the Listener might know the rhythm of belonging;  
• a drip-stone whose patter marked precisely fifty heartbeats;  
• and silence—because baskets, like bowls, cannot be full unless they also contain a pocket of air.  
The basket was gone by silver hush. In its place lay a single drop of water suspended mid-shaft, defying gravity for one full turning of the sky before it fell exactly where the frogs could taste it first. Agreement accepted.

7. Long Consequence  
Because of the Listener’s light touch:  
• The birch-tower has not yet brushed the sky’s centre; when it finally does, the wood will feel strong enough to step back of its own accord.  
• No engineer’s instrument has ever again kept perfect time in the Hollow; frustrated, they study other marvels elsewhere.  
• The Naleki still range only between 170 and 190 souls, but their stories have expanded wildly, for a people at peace have leisure for myth.

8. Final Posture  
Having threaded its kindness into air, light, drip and dream, the Listener-Beyond rested attention elsewhere. Not withdrawal—merely trust. Now and then, during the hush of colour when even moon-gleam feels like breath, a Naleki child will claim to hear an extra cadence in the bowl’s inhalation:  
thank-you / continue  
thank-you / continue  
The elders smile and answer, just loud enough for moss to green:  
We do. We will.
