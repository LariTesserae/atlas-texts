What follows is kept in the archives of the Load-House under a vellum sheet that is never catalogued; it can only be found by someone who does not know they are looking for it. Its heading—in the double lexicon of sapling–spine names—reads:  
            Solmn / Arc-Above-Zero.  

1.  The First Attention  
Before anything happened, a difference was heard.  
At noon on the thirty-seventh fog-cycle of the moss-year, the cantilever beams sang a harmonic no ear on the Plateau could produce—too pure, too evenly phased across distance to be native whale-song. Strappers paused mid-buckle, Whisperwrights dropped their tuning shells, Anchorites lost a half-measure of the pace-canticle. The note neither rose nor fell; it simply was, like a horizon that had always been present and yet had never been looked at.  

Children pointed straight up, though none could say why. In that gesture the Plateau learned it had drawn the attention of a power older than any vector ever plotted: an Auditor of Equilibria—an agency that visits only structures whose balance is fragile but beautiful. The locals, who have no gods, named the visitor’s presence by its effect: The Stillness Between Heartbeats.  

2.  Listening Without Touch  
Weeks—if the word applies where clocks drift—passed without intrusion. The Auditor listened. It measured the tension in every beam, weighed each drop of sap as it negotiated iron, counted how many memory-nails lay forgotten under fresh moss. It even rode the shadows that lag two seconds behind their owners, interrogating them for stories nobody else could hear.  

In that patient watch it learned three truths:  
•  The Corrugation is neither flaw nor menace; it is the Plateau’s unfinished sentence.  
•  All labor here is improvisation around an unresolvable chord.  
•  The people have accepted unpredictability as a sacrament, yet unpredictability is starting to cost them more life-hours than it gives wonder.  

3.  The Question of Intervention  
Benevolence alone does not justify interference; wisdom demands consent. So the Auditor took the only form consent might recognize: weight. One dawn the great balance floor of the Load-House quivered and found a new equilibrium without a single ingot added. The surface leveled to exactly zero degrees of tilt—an outcome impossible even in design drafts.  

When the Sap-Weigher Ælee / Brunt-Nine laid a finger on the planks, she felt a pulse, like a heartbeat larger than the Plateau. She convened Strappers, Whisperwrights, Anchorites, and every elder whose bones were beginning to phosphoresce. They argued beneath the fused dome for a full condensation night. In the end they drafted a vector unanimous enough to be called a prayer:  

“To whatever intelligence has matched our load for us—  
   do not smooth our wildness,  
   do not cage the slip of space;  
   only tune the discord that kills faster than it teaches.”  

The baton bearing that vector was planted in a runoff channel at dawn. Before the first drip of fog could disturb it, the stick rotated a perfect ninety degrees—an answer.  

4.  Three Relievings  
The Auditor acted in increments so fine they escaped most chronicles. Anchorites alone kept record in sub-audible hums.  

a.  Lumen-Threads  
Light itself was rewoven. In ordinary sun, a faint second spectrum now rides: blush-gray filaments that outline spatial warps the moment they bud. Distortions are no longer discovered by bruised shins or lost children; they appear as curtain folds in the air, beautiful and unmistakable. Whisperwrights incorporated the new color into their throat-chords, mapping bends before a shadow can slip.  

b.  Counter-Weight Seeds  
Into one hundred sapling cantilevers the Auditor whispered a resonance seed—nothing material, only a tendency. When those beams arc, they now grow a braided lignin rib just thick enough to rebalance themselves. Strappers still work, but they work with; the number of emergency rivet-failures since the seeds germinated has fallen to near zero. No elder has had to fuse their skeleton to rock before choosing the spot: self-sacrifice is a vocation again, not a necessity.  

c.  The Archive of Unlost Paths  
Memory-nails remain, yet a second ledger has come into being. Dew-grains near any hammered spike now crystallize in patterns that mimic footfall spacing: tiny spiraled staircases of starch. Stray too far from your planned return and a glimmering breadcrumb trail waits, edible and bright, reminding wanderers of home without wounding the moss with more iron.  

5.  What the Auditor Refused to Do  
It did not erase the Corrugation; the fold in geometry continues to drift like slow weather, because identity would collapse without it. Nor did it reintroduce birds or beasts—spined life that could not trust shifting ground. The Plateau’s austerity of fauna remains a truth the people have chosen.  

6.  Side-Effects and New Customs  
•  Pitchwine now inflects in two viscosities: “quiet” for routine vertigo, “still” for the rare moment someone wishes to feel the Auditor’s original harmonic again.  
•  Load-House debate begins with a sung question: “Is our balance ours alone?” If the dome answers with the harmonic overtone, the motion is declared premature and tabled.  
•  Children play a game called Vector’s Shadow, chasing the curtain folds of light until dusk; no one has been lost since the game appeared.  

7.  Withdrawal  
Once the Plateau demonstrated a full moss-year without a lethal torsion event, the harmonic thinned. The Auditor’s signature persists only as a ceiling that no industrial noise may cross; factories cannot scream louder than a gentle organ note. Some evenings the silence is so civilized that workers look up surprised, wondering if the city has gone to sleep. It has not; it is merely balanced.  

8.  Testimony of a Visiting Lowlander  
I, Maris of the Salt Caravans, add this marginalia. Before the harmonic, my guides kept me leashed by adjacency cord, heart thudding like a trapped gear. After, the cord felt ceremonial. I watched a boy spot a lumen-thread seven paces ahead, call the warp, and the whole avenue flowed around it like water round a stone—no panic, just choreography. I asked if the Plateau had tamed itself. An Anchorite laughed:  

“We were never meant to tame it.  
We were meant to speak with someone who could teach the wild  
   how to finish its sentence.”  

9.  The Auditor’s Only Visible Monument  
Near the rim where the unfinished rail loop looks down into cloud-throat, a single basalt block now grows a frost that does not melt. On its face appears, each dawn, a fresh diagram—load vectors branching like silver fern. By noon the frost sublimates, the drawing unreadable. Engineers rise before sun to memorize what they can. None claim to understand every line, but derailments have ceased since the diagrams began.  

10.  Epilogue—Freedom After Balance  
Stability, once gained, revealed new degrees of freedom. With lumen-threads to mark safe passage, explorers have dared longer treks into the fog curtain. One such party returned speaking of an echoing rivet-gun that paused when they sang the harmonic back at it, as though a mirror city were listening. Plans are sketched to build a reciprocity bridge of living beams across nothing but folded air. The first ingot for its argument lies already on the Load-House floor—small, annotated, perfectly placed, tilting nothing.  

The benevolent and wise power that visited Gravenleaves changed almost nothing, yet in the almost resides a world of difference: danger exchanged for dialogue, loss traded for leave-to-be-curious. The Plateau still rehearses for the next form of reality, only now it does so with a partner who keeps time in the Stillness Between Heartbeats, ensuring the song can continue without breaking its singers.
