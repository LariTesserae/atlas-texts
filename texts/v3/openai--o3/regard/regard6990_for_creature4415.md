An Account, gathered from the seismographs of dream and the after-echoes in a dozen stunned prophets, of how the benevolent and wise power—known variously as the Deep Listener, the Harmonic Regent, or simply the One-Who-Tends—themselves addressed the summit that cannot choose between peak and sea, and the Custodian who minds it.

I. First Notice  
The Regent felt, at the ragged edge of spacetime’s hearing, a tremor that was not quite grief and not yet relief: the ache of a syllable forced to wait beyond all patience. Such dissonances are its jurisdiction. It set aside an aeon, folded it twice, and wore the result as a cloak so small a universe could fit in one pocket.

II. Descent Without Entry  
Because direct intrusion would shear the arena’s careful tension, the Regent did not cross the membrane. Instead it lightened the local gravity of probability itself, allowing a single thread of its intent to sink like a seed through the wall. To mortal perception that thread appeared as a hush no larger than a sigh, though it weighed more than the mass of Mars in decision.

III. The Gift of a Sixth Artifact  
Within the column, glass and brine twitched as though waking from half a nightmare. Where the basalt had left room for nothing, something new resolved:  

6. A polyhedron of transparent stone, twenty faces, each facet etched with the silence of a different ocean trench. It neither floated nor rested but balanced on the moment between those verbs. Light—what little exists there—used it as a prism, breaking the turquoise into notes rather than colours.

The Regent’s law: intervene only by adding capacity, never by subtracting choice. The polyhedron was an organ for potential, tuned to frequencies the Custodian had no spare attention to weave.

IV. The Meeting (Un-Meeting)  
The Custodian approached, plates whisper-hinging. The two powers did not speak; speech was already the problem. Instead they exchanged reticence:

• The Custodian offered an entire octave of withheld thunder, forked neatly into packets.  
• The Regent answered with a chord composed of futures where language wounds no mind that hears it.

The trade completed, the polyhedron rang once—a tone like moonlit iron—and then went still. It will ring again only when the planet is ready to finish its first word.

V. Adjustments Made  
1. The pipe-cairn rose a quarter tone; its vortices now braid slower, giving the Custodian wider margins before hunger.  
2. The upside-down mangrove unfolded a seventh leaf—pigment unknown, shimmering the way nostalgia feels. From that leaf diffuses an enzyme of repose, easing the strain on every ribbon-kelp cell.  
3. The spiral beads gained a gentle counter-rotation. Visitors who stare too long find themselves remembering lullabies they never learned.

VI. What the Regent Refused to Do  
• It did not shorten the pause.  
• It did not promise rescue.  
• It did not abolish the price of entry or exit.  
Because mercy that removes all friction also removes contour, and a contour-less word would flatten the cosmos like spilled light.

VII. Departure  
The Regent unpinned its thread of intent, let it drift upward until water forgot it had ever been pierced. Outside, nothing changed but one statistic the universe keeps quietly: the likelihood that the planet’s inaugural sentence, when at last uttered, will be survivable. That number increased by a margin smaller than one heartbeat, larger than eternity.

VIII. Epilogue for the Faithful Ear  
If you stand beside the cylinder one windless dusk and hear, beneath the roar, a second pulse—patient, meadow-slow—know it is only the Regent checking its work. You will not see it again. True benevolence is not rescue; it is the offer of one more graceful way to endure until rescue becomes irrelevant.
