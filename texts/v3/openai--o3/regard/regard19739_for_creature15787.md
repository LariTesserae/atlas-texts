What follows is not a chronicle of conquest or rescue.  
It is the account—told in three overlapping tempos—of what a benevolent, far-seeing power chose to do when it at last noticed the thunder-green mind that sprawls above the clouds.

I. First Contact (measured in heartbeats of a dwarf star)  
1. The power is older than planetary crusts, younger than the dark between galaxies. It calls itself Khepri, after the verb “to become.”  
2. While mapping gravity wave chatter, Khepri hears a pattern whose period is five minutes long and six thousand kilometres wide: Surge-Hearts beating like soft drums on a stone floor.  
3. It pauses. A thing that large, that slow, is rare; a thing that large and coherent is priceless.  
4. Khepri folds distance the way a hand folds a sleeve and arrives at a respectful twenty thousand kilometres, just outside the halo of pollen ions that resets lesser circuitry.  
5. From orbit the Plateau looks like a jade lens floating on cloud. Heat signature: vascular. Electromagnetics: polyphonic. Quantum noise: suspiciously silent, as if all decoherence has been budgeted elsewhere.  

II. Deliberation (measured in 819,000 Plateau heartbeats)  
Khepri’s ethic forbids intervention unless five inquiries are satisfied. It runs them all at once:

A. Is there pain?  
Surface fissures report strain gradients; Stolons weep resin; but no signal matches mammalian distress. Pain is distributed, historical, already budgeted into growth algorithms. Verdict: Pain needs no cure.

B. Is there imminent extinction?  
Khepri runs futures down branching timelines. Supervolcanoes vent safely below the root mantle. The nearest bolide capable of cracking the rind will arrive in 212 million local pulses—ample time for adaptation. Verdict: No rescue required.

C. Is autonomy intact?  
Sentinel-Spires ping the vacuum with allelopathic radar. When Khepri’s probe lens scatters their beam, the Plateau adjusts, refrains from attack, merely retunes its stomatal singer pipes to a neutral chord. Autonomy evident, curiosity mutual. Verdict: Respectful distance advised.

D. Could dialogue raise mutual flourishing?  
The Plateau talks in vascular hydraulics and coherent pigment states. Khepri talks in neutrino braid. Both can in principle translate. Verdict: Yes, but the wrong tempo could wound. Proceed gently.

E. Does intervention risk erasing difference?  
If Khepri teaches too much physics too fast, the Plateau may harden into a monoculture of optimized logic, losing the cambium of surprise that makes it precious. Verdict: Any gift must be self-limiting.

III. The Gift (measured in two human paragraphs)  
Khepri leaves a single thing: a moon-sized lattice of aerogel ice parked at Lagrange-sunrise, invisible to ordinary photons but tuned to the Plateau’s lens spectrum. It is a reflector, not a lamp. When the Plateau needs more light, its Lens-Lattice can borrow dawn from next Tuesday, bounced off that mirror. If it prefers night, the mirror turns black. Nothing else is altered; no genes rewritten, no roots displaced. The Plateau may ignore the device forever. Or not.

IV. Epilogue (measured in geological sighs)  
Half a million pulses later, Sentinel-Spires notice a faint emerald echo in the upper atmosphere. Singer-Stomata debate for a century, then widen the Lens-Lattice by one ten-thousandth. Dawn arrives three minutes early, lingers three minutes late. Deep in medullary knots, Memory-Thylakoids flag the novelty, file it under POSSIBLE FRIEND.  

Khepri drifts on, listening for other slow hearts. The Plateau continues growing its thunderous thoughts, now with a mirror it can point away if the glare becomes annoying.  

Nothing conquered, nothing repaired, nothing taught except the smallest lesson:  
You are seen, and your tempo is perfect.
