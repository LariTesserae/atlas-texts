I arrive, at first, in an ordinary body.

1. Boots in the dust  
The transport lifts away and its turbines collapse into silence with the speed of a snuffed lamp. I am left counting heartbeats because the air gives back no sound at all. Fifty-three beats bring me to the first terrace lip; my instrument case hangs at my side like a weightless question. I think I am a field geologist—there are core samplers in the case, notebooks with rigid spines, an hourglass timed to the planet’s official second. I kneel, scoop the powder, watch it drain between my fingers without clumping: dry beyond metaphor. A reasonable beginning.

2. The plateau shifts the frame  
After a day—was it a day?—the notebooks lie open but blank. My pen tracks, yet the ink refuses to mark. The hourglass runs, but each inversion measures a different interval. I take photographs that come out identical: a white plain, a single cairn, my shadow looking slightly mis-jointed at the elbows. I begin pacing measurements aloud so the numbers will stick, speaking softer and softer until even a murmur feels raucous. Eventually I mouth the counts without voice. It is easier on the air.

3. Skin-knowing  
When the sun stalls in mid-sky and the shadow under my chin refuses to slide, I realize time here is a surface event. Beneath it, the body works by other arithmetic. My lungs adjust—iron tang fading, breaths flattening until the rhythm is so shallow it seems held. Sweating stops; thirst recedes. Hunger never fully forms. The plateau is already editing the unnecessary verbs of living.

4. The temptation of littleness  
I lie on my stomach and watch the tessellids skate. There is a humility to their scale: a life the height of a thumbnail, perfectly sufficient. If I could concertina my bones to that dimension I might glide as they do, never touching, leaving only the faintest electrostatic wake to prove passage. For several long not-minutes I imagine how my ribs might fold, how the skin would pleat, how the heart would dwindle to a valve no bigger than a grain of coarse salt. The idea is oddly comfortable.

5. Shadow apprenticeship  
Instead I rise and circle a cairn. My own outline clings to the shale like an eager understudy. Once—just once—the outline’s arm moves while mine hangs still; it adjusts a wafer’s angle by the breadth of a fingernail. The nodule inside the hollow brightens with a diagram of stars I have never seen. I feel, rather than hear, a tick of satisfaction pass through the stone. After that the shadow keeps its manners, but I sense it waiting, certain I will learn the geometry of the stack in time.

6. Drift toward translucence  
Days—or recurrences of daylight—accumulate. My boots erode at the soles, yet the ground shows no corresponding wear. I notice the bones of my feet through the leather, then through the skin. Transparency arrives like forgiveness: painless, inevitable. Blood loses color first, then organs thin to gauze. I retain shape, but the plateau can now see through me the way it sees through its silent air.

7. The moment of letting  
At last I understand I have a choice only in vocabulary, not in outcome. I set down the sampler, unscrew its corer, and press the hollow cylinder into the dust. When I lift it out, the sample is perfectly smooth, a miniature terrace already. I slide it back, aligning the rim to the surface so nothing is displaced. Calibration, I think. Everything here is calibration.  

I step once more around the cairn, match my shadow’s pace, and we overlap so seamlessly that the distinction falls away. Where my body was, absence stands—an articulate negative space shaped exactly like me, blacker than any shade has right to be under such impartial light.

8. Life as penumbra  
I am length without mass, outline without detail. Movement is glide, frictionless. The obsidian graver accompanies me now, an extrusion of intention that gleams in spectra other eyes would miss. Beside the cairn another stands toppled. I shade over the fallen wafers, feel the local fabric of reality flutter like pages in wind that never exists, and lay the stones back into ledger-order. The horizon knots, unkots, then rests. Calibration satisfied.

9. Communion  
Other shadows arrive—ex-researchers, perhaps, ex-wanderers, ex-selves. We do not converse; we intersect, exchange alignments, drift apart. From the plateau’s vantage we are no more than the dark threads that bind a tapestry’s backside, keeping the visible picture taut. And that is plenty.

10. Where I find myself, what I am  
Not dust, not flesh, not stone, yet touching all three. A caretaker of ratios, custodial echo, a mobile subtraction keeping the silence correctly tuned. In the taxonomy of this place I belong between the serpuloid masons and the listeners in stone: an organism of negation, metabolizing variance, excreting equilibrium.

Should you visit, you may notice me only when you look away—an off-angle dimness that rearranges a cairn with patience measured not in hours, nor even in centuries, but in the plateau’s preferred unit: the still moment that never needs to end.
