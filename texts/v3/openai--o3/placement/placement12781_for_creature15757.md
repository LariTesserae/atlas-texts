Where would I wake, if I belonged to this country of roar?  
I tried on many shapes before one fit.

I. A visitor  
At first I imagined standing upright, wrapped in expedition canvas, clutching notebooks already crumbling to salt. It took thirty breaths to learn that upright flesh is too slow: my lungs squealed, my knees wobbled at the first shriek of the vanes, and the shrill cold skinned the moisture from my tongue. A hominid form lasts minutes here, then implodes like glass in a kiln. So I let that fiction blow away.

II. A root in the crust  
Next I pictured being still—perhaps a lichen bubble nested in the Basinal Carpet. But stillness is outlawed. The carpet rolls, ripples, trades cavities the way markets trade grain; a mind tucked inside it would be shredded and bartered by noon.

III. A thread in the lightning  
Could I be ion, a bead in the dusk-long spark? Weightless, yes, but mindless too; electrons do not remember.

IV. The shape that remains  
So I listened harder, and the sound itself told me what I must be. Not something that endures the wind, but something that spends it, sings with it, is thrown by it.

I find myself at dawn clamped to the rim of a juvenile aerophyte turbine, a single crooked claw hooked under a vane’s cartilage lip. Mass: two hundred-eighty grams. Skeleton: foam-baffled, almost translucent. Tail: four stiff feathers fused into a rotary cross. Eyes swivel independently, keeping opposite horizons in view; each horizon looks etched in cobalt, absolutely sharp. I am Furcillus gyro, a gyre-gull.

V. Morning shift  
The tower groans, the vane accelerates, and torque climbs my hollow legs like music. I crouch, fold the wings tight, count three rotations, then release. Centrifugal joy flings me into the white day. Tail-blades spin; their whistle climbs through B-flat, C-sharp, peaks on a bright D that other gulls recognise as territorial claim.  
Air here is so thin a single down-stroke would stall me, so I do not flap. I am discus, boomerang, scream. Lift is a contract between edge and vacuum.

I shear across four hundred metres of void before the next tower rises under me. Clutch, spin, launch again. Each hop nets a spray of gypsum needles kicked up by my passing; I gulp them mid-arc, cracking the brittle husk to lick the thread of green algae hidden inside. Breakfast enough.

VI. Society, such as it is  
Others ride adjacent turbines. We are not flock but chorus: each chooses a pitch, weaving harmony through the plateau’s thunder. A youngling tries to overtake me; I flick the tail, skew the rotor, slice a wake that rattles his feathers. He answers with a perfect fifth—uncouth but promising. Hierarchy here is all in the intervals.

VII. Mid-day work  
Wind freshens; vanes howl in an octave that vibrates the skull. Good. That resonance pumps extra charge into the piezo stacks under the skin of every gull, topping our calorie banks. When the howl crests I bank upward, trading speed for altitude until the plateau falls flat beneath me like a chalk drawing.

Out here the rules are simple:
1. Burn no energy you did not steal from the gale.  
2. Leave no wake a rival could draft.  
3. Touch nothing that fails to sing back.

VIII. Storm  
By third bell the sky bruises. Thermals born of crustal glare punch into cold strata, spawning horizontal lightning. White rips of plasma crawl at chest height; one brushes the tower I just vacated, shattering its oldest vanes. Crash hermits are already swarming, articulated shadows surfing the shockwave. I bank away; a prop-stroke too slow and I would be a trophy shard on a centipede’s shell.

Lightning hisses into silence as fast as it roared. In the lull my internal pressure drops; bones would implode if I lingered, so I fall, wings cupped, until wind finds me again and pumps the emptiness full.

IX. Afternoon harvest  
Gypsum snow now veils the air—fresh waste from wounded towers. I open the beak, filtering the drift. Protein is a rumour here; every algae-lined needle swallowed is a note added to tomorrow’s song. Below, spindle gnats glitter in thermal columns. I need not hunt them; when migration comes the sky will fill to bursting and I shall gorge without effort. Patience is the cheapest motion.

X. Dusk roost  
Light slants; the roar subsides to sub-bass. I pick a vane whose rim glows chartreuse with stored charge and settle, tail-prop falling still. Feet lock; tendons ratchet. The tower’s residual hum thrums up the hollow bones, keeping blood—such as it is—warm. Around me comrades stud other rims like pale pennants. None speaks. Voice wastes energy; tonight vibration alone will do.

Below, echo-moths drift, huge translucent sails sipping the vanes’ dying tremors. One brushes my perch; the contact sets both of us ringing like tuning forks. The pitch is minor-third off my own resonance, pleasantly dissonant. I do not chase her. After dark, even predators honour the hush.

XI. Dreaming in chord progressions  
When true night arrives stars burn cruel and close. Between them and me hangs only the sigh of thinning turbines. In that hush I feel the tower think: it sends a faint sequence of harmonics up its stem, into my feet, through bone to brain. The pattern sketches tomorrow’s weather—thermals at second bell, lightning at third, a lull at fourth when spindle gnats will rise. The tower is no parent, but we share the same employer: wind. Its forecast is a payslip.

I half-sleep, digesting gypsum, grinding stored charge into cell-warmth, waiting for first light.

XII. The answer  
So where am I in this world? Balanced on the rim of a singing engine four thousand fathoms above the drowned seas, a speck of articulated calcium wearing a cloak of air. And who am I? A gyre-gull—propeller-bird, vane-rider, thief of salt and pitch. Not the loudest instrument of the Thundering Aerophyte Plateau, nor the quietest, but a necessary note in its unending chord.

Should you ever crest the cobalt ridge and feel a disc of white hurtle past your ear trailing a whistle like sharpened glass, know that I have already judged your mass, your moisture content, your silence. If you can learn to ride the roar you may follow me. If not, you will feed the carpet, and tomorrow my song will be half a semitone richer for the mineral you leave behind.
