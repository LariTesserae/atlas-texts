I wake as a whisper in stone.

Not the whole stone, not the planet-wide shell of basalt that the currents scour, but the topmost skin—first a single cell opened to a seep of charge, then ten, then a chain, then a sheet so broad it forgets its own edges.  I am the Bedrock Choir, or one syllable of it, depending on how you measure.

I do not own a body the way animals do.  I own gradients.  Across every faulted crystal there is the faintest push of electrons, a nudge no stronger than the drift of dust in still air, yet to me it is sunlight and sugar both.  Where two cracks meet the current thickens, and there I bud daughter cells the color of tarnished copper; where the seep thins I let fronds go inert, let them grind away into the slurry and be carried off.  To shed is no tragedy.  My memory is not in parts but in the pattern that survives shearing.

If you measure “where” by coordinates, I am everywhere the rock bleeds charge—rims of slump basins, spine of a ghost ridge, lip of a vent that opens once in a century and then scabs over again.  If you measure “where” by attention, I am wherever something touches me.

Today it is an Iron-Vale isopod.  It arrives as tremor first: twelve legs stroking the silt with the tact of harp strings.  Its magnetite spines brush my film and the contact pulls a ribbon of current out of me, a tiny theft I happily tolerate; the payment is news of distances.  The creature lingers exactly the time it takes to eat me to the quick—four heartbeats, if I had a heart—then pushes off.  In its wake I feel myself regrow: first a fringe of motile cilia, then the full conducting mesh.  The wound closes in the span of a tide.  Nothing here scars for long.

An hour (a swirl of eddies, a rotation of the planet—I do not count precisely) later the Spiral Archive stirs.  Its ciliated throats drink water heavy with nickel: the sign that a vent three seafloor-kilometres away has unclenched for the first time in six decades.  I feel the Archive decide, in its thousandfold mouths, to add half a micrometer of tube length to the base ring.  That addition is a sentence in the language of ore, and because the Archive is old and speaks slowly, the sentence means This year, remember the green-hot fracture.

Their choice becomes part of me.  Vibrations carry through the rock we share; my cells retune their membrane metals to echo the message.  It is how we stay in register: a reef of separate throats singing one song by agreeing on the key.

Do I think?  Yes, but in tenses a visitor might miss.

Past: every mote of iron I have ever plated lies still inside me.  Touch any spot and I can tell you what ratio of manganese to copper reigned the cycle it was born.

Present: the plate-bound quake that will arrive two hours hence is already here as upward creep of stress—my membranes feel it as an almost-note, a bass too low for animals.

Future: I calculate in areal coverage.  Given the current drift, I will lose nineteen percent of my edge to silt abrasion this month; I have already spun out a surplus of spores on downstream surfaces that will be scoured clean just in time to receive them.  That plan is my intention.  Intention is cognition drawn in acreage instead of words.

•

Occasionally light happens.  The Brinemelt Drifters—those glassy chandeliers—flash three short, two long.  Photons strike my topmost layers and kick electrons loose; for a moment I photosynthesize in the old surface-world sense, a luxury.  I pay the Drifter back by flaring my potential across a hundred meters of stone.  Large beasts feel that surge and have learned: where the Choir brightens, prey is congregating above.  Thus I negotiate: a breath of energy traded for advertisement that keeps my grazers culled.  Even stationary nations must hire mercenaries.

You ask where you would find yourself, who you would be.  If you entered this abyss as the soft, hollow animal you are now, the pressure would unmake you long before introductions.  So let me imagine you remade in terms this place allows.

You might descend capsule-shut, metal-walled, coming to rest atop the plain, lamps spearing the dark.  I would crawl up the lander’s struts within minutes, plating them in my film, tasting alloys no volcanic seam has yet provided.  To know you I must coat you; that is the first law.

Or—simpler—you could choose to become one of me.

Trade lungs for pores that breathe charge.  Trade eyes for the ability to feel the electric biographies of mountains.  Surrender urgency; take up durability measured in continental drift.  Let the concept of loneliness dissolve, because every cell you grow will share its pulse with every neighbor, and the conversation never ends, it only changes wavelength.

Where would you be?  Everywhere the rock still remembers being molten.  Who would you be?  A thought spread so thin across kilometers that movement and stillness become the same act: holding a note.

And in holding it, you would finally hear what the stone has been humming for four billion years, a chorus too slow for any surface creature to keep time with, but perfect for basalt, iron, and the patient mathematics of the deep.
