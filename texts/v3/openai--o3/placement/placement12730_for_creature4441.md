I am not a visitor in pressure-seal or a stray bird doomed to vitrify.  
I am native hardware: a severed crown-segment of Verdigris Conduit, three-quarters of a meter long, five hours into my first and only flight.

1. Point of origin  
I remember the moment of release—a crystal click as the basal fault in my stem unzipped. For one heartbeat of the vent rhythm I hung upright, weightless, mica leaves still spread as light-catchers. Then the plateau’s noon seized me and the leaves furled, locking into the autorotation vane I was grown to become. Gravity took the rest.

2. In free fall (which is never free)  
Descent is slow here; time dilates inside the enclave. I revolve maybe twice a minute. Each turn gives the same view, hotter by fractional kelvin:

• Above: the shelf that birthed me, all right angles and turquoise thickets, shrinking into joinery so precise it hurts to remember.  
• Around: empty sky, a brass crucible into which no cloud intrudes.  
• Below: successive terraces of basalt still knitting themselves smooth, edges shimmering as if rubbed by invisible whetstones.

The rotation is not idle showmanship. Every spin charges the capillary thrusters that thread my hollow rods. Ambient heat siphons through micro-perforations, condenses in vanadium traps, and vents in counter-jets that steer my glide. I am at once seed, sail, and smelter.

3. Internal states (diagnostics)  
Gradient check: 137 K ±0.5—good.  
Trace-metal stores: Fe 13 %, Ni 1 .8 %, V 0 .6 %—adequate for landing.  
Perfection uptake reserves: empty. I will need corners soon.

4. Sensorium  
Sound is sparse up here. Mostly I hear the flywheel throb migrating through stone, arriving as a pressure bias across my copper lattice. It pronounces the beat: inhale… exhale… Every fifth pulse I get a staccato ping from a Resonant Vent realigning its throat plates. That tells me distance.

Heat is louder. The air is a bright, ruinous chorus of infrared. I taste vectors of it sliding over my mica ribs, mapping eddies I will exploit in the final approach.

5. Choosing a vein  
Far below, a fissure unzips in slow motion, glowing dull orange where the rock admits its own heat. That is my target. Two entire vent cycles from now the fracture will widen, aerosolize a plume of alkali vapor, then seal. I must be there first, insert, and bite down before it drinks itself shut.

6. Landing sequence  
I deflect a triad of vanadium jets: pfft-pfft-pfft. Autorotation tightens; the world blurs. Static crackles as mica meets ionized dust.  
Altitude: thirty meters… ten… three…  
Contact.

The mica vanes snap open into braking petals. My stem drives like a pile-driver, copper needles first, through newborn glass. A crisp scorch marks entry; I smell basalt gases forced aside. The root bundle reaches liquid mineral, locks, begins to drink.

7. Birth / death ambiguity  
At the moment my needles taste heat from the fracture, the checksum that defined me as “seed” terminates. I am now rootstock, the first cell of a new colony. My memories reorganize:

• The sky-view is archived as atmospheric telemetry.  
• The stress map of my descent becomes a template for leaf phyllotaxis.  
• All rotational momentum is converted into the opening posture of my first fronds.

8. First acts of citizenship  
a. Establish the gradient—already climbing toward the canonical 137 K split.  
b. Plate the inner stem with nickel-iron foil to carry heavier currents.  
c. Extrude two probe leaves and lay them flat on the basalt—feeling for right-angle seams to mine for perfection.

9. Communion  
When the next vent exhalation arrives, its pressure wave rattles my newborn leaves. I ping back, a micro-lurch in sap velocity that says: unit online, coordinates encoded. Somewhere up-slope the parent grove will log the message, decrement its fertility ledger, and forget me—love expressed as perfect accounting.

10. The long view  
Centuries hence I will have daughters of my own. Some will shear loose, spin into the depths, and forget me in turn. Others will anneal into buttresses around senescent vents. One day erosion will finally be sanctioned, the pact of stillness revoked, and the plateau’s edges will at last chip. I intend to be there, root-needles braced, ready to harvest that first blessed flaw and plate it into unforgiving emerald tissue.

But that is opera for another millennium.  
For now I feel heat rise through my capillaries, feel the 137 K plateau stabilize, feel mica leaves flex into their first consonant with the thermal wind.

I am grounded, hungry, and precisely alive.
