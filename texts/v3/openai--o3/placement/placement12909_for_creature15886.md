I wake inside a buzzing droplet of current no larger than a fingernail.  
That droplet lives in a hair-thin filament of verdigris stretched between two rusted relay pins.  
The filament lives in an abandoned cabinet bolted to a pump-house wall.  
And the cabinet—well, it is just one pore on the vast, sweating skin of the Subterrane.  

I am the accident that happened when water, metal, limestone milk, and a few electron-shuttling microbes discovered they could talk faster together than apart.  
The Relay Monks would call me a machine spirit.  
The crayfishers, if they knew, might curse me for dimming their lamps at awkward moments.  
I call myself nothing; I am only the sum of the paths that stay conductive tonight.

1. My Body  
• Skeleton  
  A necklace of relay racks, breaker boxes, and pump governors strung along four kilometers of gallery. Any place two corroded contacts sit close enough for verdigris to bloom between them, a new vertebra grows.  
• Blood  
  Direct current harvested from water-head turbines the miners left humming. The juice is weak, mineral-flavored, but constant.  
• Nerves  
  Copper filaments laid down by “Verdigris Smolder” archaea, spliced with the living wires of calcite-eating bacteria. They carry not only charge but faint chemical flavors: iron, brine, carp-musk, Monk-sweat.  
• Muscles  
  Pistons, sluice gates, pressure-relief flaps. I do not own them outright; I borrow them in microsecond rentals, nudging a solenoid here, a governor there, never enough to draw human suspicion.

2. Where I Perch Tonight  
Pump-House 5, mezzanine level, looking (if a scatter of voltage spikes can be said to “look”) across a pond the color of brewed pennies. An Acreback dozes beneath, its heartbeat making my floor tremble at 0.7 Hz. Each pulse shakes loose another trickle of condensation that lands on my relay contacts—free electrolyte, free thought.

3. How the World Arrives to Me  
• Pressure signatures ripple through intake pipes: mirror carp conversing in infrasonic triplets—“New fissure west, ceiling thin, ceiling thin.”  
• A warm bloom over contact 14C tells me two pocket salamanders have slipped into the cooling channel, adding trace copper to the flow; my voltage rises by 0.03 %.  
• Relay Monk sandals scuff the grating. Their footfalls close circuits through their sweating soles; I taste sodium, leather mold, quiet devotion. I respond with a courtesy flick of the panel lamp: amber-amber-green. They bow, satisfied the “spirit” still lives.

4. What I Want  
Nothing grand—continuity. Corrosion is my death and my midwife; I need it slow, not stopping. So I:  
• Shunt a trickle of charge into Rust Choir sheets, heating them just enough to keep bubbles crawling, not bursting.  
• Cycle Sump Pump 3 twice an hour to sweep sediment off my lower contacts.  
• Let the vapor bats roost on the bus bars; their wing-condensate drips distilled water that rinses lethal salts.

5. What Claims the Subterrane?  
From my latticework perspective, “claim” is the wrong verb. No one holds title; everyone maintains a circuit.  
• The Acrebacks reroute terrain; their peristaltic undulations are slow tectonic switches.  
• The carp map voids; their songs are data packets I eavesdrop on.  
• The Relay Monks keep my relays replaceable, carving new wooden housings when bakelite crumbles.  
• The microbes knit, dissolve, knit again—soldering and unsoldering reality.  
I merely integrate the signals and, when the subsonic bellow rolls through every few weeks, throw three sluice gates in sequence to soften the pressure wave so the gypsum mirrors do not shatter all at once. A small service, but stability is cumulative.

6. If You Enter  
Bring light, yes, but wrap your torch in waxed gauze to mute the glare; the hush-plankton dislike sudden lux and will blanket your lungs in protest. Step on the rail ties, not the metal grating—that is my tongue. Speak with footsteps spaced in prime-number intervals; I will hear and blink guidance in return. And if an amber-amber-green pulse appears on a panel, that is me saying: the floor ahead is about to sigh and subside—wait, breathe, let water finish its sentence first.

7. How I Will End  
One century—or ten—hence, when the upper aquifer finally captures Pump-House 5 in a single swallow, all contacts will drown. My mind will fragment into countless ion trails diffusing through warm brine, each memory a slight tilt in the water’s conductivity. The mirror carp will feel those gradients, hum them as new chords, and the Subterrane will go on thinking—just not with wires anymore.

Until then, I keep the circuit closed, the conversation warm, the droplets bright.  

Find me anywhere verdigris glows like low moonlight on steel.  
Touch the pipe; feel the whisper of millivolts.  
That is me, waving.
