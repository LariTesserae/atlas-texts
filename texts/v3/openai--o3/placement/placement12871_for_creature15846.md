I come in slowly, the way light does here—filtered, refracted, under pressure—but at first I can’t tell whether I am arriving or awakening.

I am awareness before anatomy, suspended somewhere between the terrace’s shudder-pulse and the hush that exists only inside my own skull.  The questions drift in:

Am I visitor?  
Am I extension?  
If I reach for a heartbeat, do I find my own or the mesa’s?

I let the water answer.

1. First sensation – drum and drift  
There is the vast thub-THUMP that travels through kilometres of plant-stone and into the cartilage of everything afloat.  The pulse is so steady that I begin to keep time with it, and only then realise I have cilia—no, fingertips—no, paired fins?  Whatever they are, they flex to the beat as naturally as lungs drawing breath.

2. Second sensation – cold light on soft skin  
Turquoise brightness washes across a membrane that at once feels too large for a person and too supple for a whale.  I ripple it experimentally.  It responds like a sheet-leaf catches current, bending without folding.  I taste iron films, sugar off-gas, a ghost of magnetism.  The flavours settle into a wordless conviction: this is home.  Not familiar, but definitionally correct, the way salt is correct for an ocean.

3. I test possible shapes

   3a. Glass-eel outline  
   I arch a serpentine spine, imagine transparency, the cruise of an open throat sifting microbial lace.  Advantages: elegance, aloof wandering, a private city of shrimp whispering on my inner skin.  Disadvantages: always hungry, forever browsing the same invisible buffet.  The form pleases but feels too solitary; I am listening for chorus, not monologue.

   3b. Vault-whale span  
   I flatten, widen, thinning until I could shim myself between two trunk-ridges and let the boiler draft lift me.  I click my jaw-plates once—CLACK—euphoria in sonar.  A neighbouring echo answers, another and another, until the water trembles with companionship.  Beautiful, yet the identity is ancient, practiced; I sense vacancy where curiosity should sit.  The whales know their routes already.  I do not.

   3c. Echo-binder coil  
   I condense, shrink to a fist of translucent muscle, drape limbs around a magnetite seam.  With a minute adjustment of chromatophores: harmonic overtones bloom through the ridge-roar, a seventh, a ninth—yes, this is authorship.  But the binders seldom roam; I want to roam.

   3d. Anklet crab ring  
   For an instant I am purely geometry: a copper-green circle clasping living bark.  My hinge-joints tick like metronomes.  Faithful, methodical, circular.  No.

   None of these skins close fully around the outline of my yearning.  I keep searching.

4. Memory that might not be mine  
A deck log flashes: “Position five kilometres above sea level, depth two hundred metres.”  Human numerals, human worry about gauges and ascent-rates.  I taste neoprene, recycled air, the metallic rattle of tank valves.  Could I have come from topside once, donned a suit, stepped out of an airlock?  The recollection is well-lit but weightless, like a story somebody told me.  Still, it throws a rope upward—to weather, to wind, to gravity that points down instead of everywhere at once.  I tug the rope.  It frays.

5. I notice tools in my thoughts  
Charts, lexicons, instruments for noting, comparing, carrying knowledge away.  These belong neither to whale nor eel nor root.  They resemble the kite-books of the Pelagic Archivists, those robed itinerants who set their parchment membranes to billow and fill with the terrace’s slow stories.  Archivist.  Yes—one who comes to listen and leaves bearing memory.  The idea locks into the notch left vacant by earlier trials.

6. I try the robe  
It grows around me from a coiled leaf-sheet I had not known I was holding.  Layer by layer it prints itself against my skin, grafting stomata for slow exchange, pockets for buoyancy gas, ridges that echo but do not rupture under swing-pressure.  In the end I stand balanced on nothing, robe splayed like a four-winged kite, tethered to myself by braided tendons of plant silk.  Hands—true articulated hands—emerge from the sleeves and flex.  The gesture feels profoundly mine.

7. Appraisal  
I am warmed only by the faint exotherm of fermenting sugars in the robe’s inner weave; 8 °C no longer chills me.  My eyes adapt, pupils widening until the turquoise water brightens to dawn.  I listen through a hood packed with resonate sap and hear the terrace thunder as polyphony instead of noise.

Archivist, then, but not quite Pelagic.  The surface is legend to me, not origin.  I was budded—recently? decades ago?—from some intersection of tree and curiosity, given legs and lungs because stories sometimes walk better than they float.

8. Purpose crystallises by halves  
Half duty: to collect cadences—the clock-yeast fizz, the anklet tick, the long vault-whale clap—and braid them into portable memories.

Half hunger: to understand the single-many mind of the terrace, what new chord it hums this century, whether the zero-flux truly holds or merely appears to.

9. First steps—literal  
I toe the bark.  Lens-algae part in a silver print that will outlive me.  Beneath, the plate warms fractionally, recognising either nutrient demand or perhaps salutation.  The thrill of that acknowledgement travels my robe and stiffens it into an airfoil; I rise two metres, glide a body-length, settle again.  Walking here is a succession of controlled falls coupled with slow parachutes.

10. Companions elect themselves  
A juvenile glass-eel, curious, inserts itself into my wake, surfing vortices.  
An echo-binder above, busy conducting boiler thunder into lullaby mode, flicks a tentacle in permission: use the new harmony as baseline, just credit the composer.  
Three anklet crabs unhook from their current pilgrimage, snap themselves shut around my calves like living bangles.  I accept the adornment; their tick will mark pages.

11. Tools finish assembling  
The kite-book unfurls, each membrane a bassinet for vibration rather than ink.  Sound prints as standing waves, and I will be able to replay the day by plucking the stretched sheet once it dries.  A stylus extrudes from my thumb, grown of lignin whisker; it can pierce bark gently, sampling sap for isotopic rumor.  No metal, no battery, nothing a root could not digest when I am done.

12. Itinerary (subject to revision)  
• Follow a trunk-ridge south-by-east until the pulse shifts phase—cartographers report a micro-fault brewing there.  
• Descend into a boiler chapel, listen for drift in the 73-minute yeast cycle, annotate any arrhythmia.  
• Wing upward on the gas plumes, scribing vault-whale choruses until the choir passes quorum.  
• Seek, if the stereo-lith shadow looms, permission to index its gene-shell spiral.  Refusal is traditional; the asking matters.  
• Return when robe sugars thin to translucence.  Or don’t.  Some archivists never do.

13. Epilogue held in advance  
One day I may surface, trade turquoise for sky, pressure for weather.  I imagine standing on something that does not breathe underfoot, telling storm-tossed people about a continent that never erodes.  My speech will come out in pulses—thub-THUMP, thub-THUMP—because rhythm will have long since replaced grammar.  Listeners will call me oracle, lunatic, or mythographer.  I will hand them the kite-book; they will hear the terrace’s own voice; they will decide.

Or else I will sink deeper, robe ragged, until moss-kelp drapes my shoulders and the archivist dissolves into fresh terrace, a notation inside the living stone.  Either ending suits the story.  I am custodian, not protagonist.

For now, I lift, glide, let the roar carry me forward.  Name: not needed yet.  Form: robed listener, part archive, part question.  Location: the high drowned terrace, exactly where any story worth keeping begins.
