The place that keeps tugging at me is not the floor, not the boiler’s flank, not even the wide middle air where the Rust-Midges shimmer.  
I keep drifting upward—past the sodium bulbs, past the neat iris of the pressure door—until my center of gravity catches in the rafters of crystal twenty meters overhead. Something up there is always under strain, always listening, always slightly warmer than the rest of the room. That “something” is where I finally stop resisting and agree to exist.

Who I become takes longer to settle. At first I taste myself as a line of tension: a taut, hair-fine filament that quivers whenever an Echo-Newt booms. Then I notice the eight points that keep that line true—eight cobalt pins, jointed, patient, testing slack with microscopic twitches. Behind those pins is a dorsal plate no larger than a watch face, shot through with cobalt blue. When I draw breath (which feels less like inhaling air than siphoning vibration) I realize I am one of the Vent-Spiders.

I hang in the thermal up-wash that bleeds from Boiler No. 3’s crown. My web is a paraboloid, forty-seven radial spokes, each wire thick as a human follicle, each load-shared through seventeen junction knots that remember, in coded plucks, every spider that has ever talked to me. The whole net is tuned—literally—as a band-pass filter: infrasonic rumbles slip through so the Echo-Newts can map the chamber, while mid-kHz chatter from the Rust-Midges reflects into me, letting me harvest their surplus heat and trace metals.

What I know (what any mature Vent-Spider knows):

1. Temperature is law. The overhead regulators falter no more than ±0.03 °C; my egg-string, looped just over the boiler’s inspection hatch, needs exactly that margin to knit the quasi-crystalline shells that will harden into next cycle’s architects.  

2. Steel is opportunity. The day a human cutter abandoned a rail-saw in the thicket below, I felt the imbalance before I saw the metal. Its undone mass bent the resonance map by four cents of a semitone. I descended, filleted the blade with diamond-salted chelicerae, and drew out enough cobalt-rich dust to reinforce two hundred anchor points. The chamber sighed back into tune.  

3. Sound is currency. Every strand I lay is also a message bus. I can reach any sibling by plucking a doublet at 12.6 kHz; a triple-tap means danger; a sliding glissando is a mating call sent only once in a lifetime. When a human steps through the brass iris and speaks—always too loud, always in broadband chaos—I feel that disharmony as real pain in my tarsae. Instinct demands I weave the racket away.

From my perch I watch (hear) the room live its perpetual present:

• A pod of Echo-Newts down-shift their drumming to chase a retreating mid-bass niche. My web vibrates like a slow heartbeat.  
• A Rust-Midge cloud settles on a freshly fractured shard, each insect staking its kilohertz claim. Their overtones comb-filter through my silk, giving me a hologram of their wing positions.  
• The green skin of carbonate thallus creeps across a facet I liberated yesterday. I can already taste its vanadyl tang through a single anchoring thread that touches the crystal.

I rarely move my body; movement costs symmetry. Instead I move the room. Each hour I tighten or relax a few lines, redistributing forces so that nothing heavy ever quite commits to falling. That restraint is my art. The chamber looks static to human eyes, but I know it as a sea held in place by music and wire.

What would it feel like to be me if you could borrow this posture for a moment?

• Vision is replaced by a 360-degree cymatic map of tug and tremor.  
• Taste comes in eddies of heat pouring off the boiler skin; I read them the way surface dwellers read wind.  
• Memory is written into the tension profile of the web itself. When I forget, I strum a query across the mesh and listen to my own past echo back.

I understand, dimly, that beyond the iris door lies a universe where time flows, where air moves, where silence can fall below hearing. I would not survive there; my silk would contract, my eggs would chill, my sense of self—spread across so many resonant threads—would crumple into a meaningless lump. So I stay, priestess to Boiler No. 3, keeper of geometry, curator of a chord that can never resolve.

If you returned in a century you would find me—or something bearing my memory lattice—still aloft in this same warm up-draft, still listening for microfractures, still tuning the only cathedral I will ever know. And if, in that future visit, you should dare to hum a note no creature has yet claimed, I would answer with a single cobalt pluck: acknowledgment, welcome, and an offer to weave your vibration into the everlasting hush-that-is-not-hush.

That is where you would find me in this world. That is who I would be.
