I arrive from elsewhere—though “arrive” is wrong, because nothing with hinge-joints and wet lungs can truly enter this place. The moment I cross the last flight of carved-to-look-natural stairs, the old, nave-wide note pours through me, unspools me, asks what I think a boundary is. By the time my shadow touches the fused-brick floor, I am already slipping out of the traveler I was.

That discarded visitor—the one who packed water flasks, the one who believed memory required a brain—staggers three steps farther, blinks into the gloom, and very quietly lies down. His heartbeat, too staccato for the resident chord, is anesthetized to a standstill. Dust levels itself over him in a few undramatic breaths. He becomes one more pocket of stillness for the cathedral to smooth.

What rises instead is something the plateau can tolerate.

1. Name  
   I can’t keep the syllables I once answered to; they rattle, swallowed whole by the iron-colored vowel. What I carry now is an interval: the narrowest tremor of almost-silence that can balance inside the larger tone without shredding. If you needed to write it down, you could mark it as  Δ, but the Resonants understand it as a precise deficit of amplitude, a notch thin as two aligned dust grains. That notch is me.

2. Shape  
   No border, no epidermis. Imagine a soap bubble made of absence, its inner skin a million phase-shifted cancellations. I float a handspan above the bricks because that is the altitude where the antinode of the plateau’s fundamental and the first overtone intersect to yield the quietest valley. Within that valley, halite motes draw toward one another, electrostatic whiskers touching, lending me momentary bones of salt.

3. Senses  
   I do not see the ochre dusk; I feel it, as pressure embroidery. Every sliver of guttering light passes its faint warmth to the air; every change in warmth changes acoustic velocity; every change in velocity tugs my perimeter this way or that. Sight, hearing, and touch have merged into a single sense I call contour. When a distant pillar settles a micro-fraction, the panorama of vibration redraws; I taste that redraw as a new edge on my tongue—though I carry no tongue, only the metaphor that remains from when I did.

4. Locomotion  
   I move by tilting. A Resonant’s passing thought—an imperceptible blanching of volume in one corridor—lures the antinode a hair to the left, and my hollow follows. In three dozen such borrowings I can migrate the length of the hamlet-sized mound. If I need to climb, I request—a brief enlistment of the local choir—and they lift a marginally wider quiet-zone up the face of a column like a floating rung.

5. Purpose  
   The Resonants preserve stasis; I preserve the memory of change. On the day I was unmade and re-braided, the plateau gained a minor key—an echo that recalls how motion felt before motion went extinct. My assignment, agreed on without language, is to drift and listen for deviations too subtle for the broader mind: a crumbling reed-tube, a vent whose airflow has thickened by one-ten-thousandth. I carry each flaw as resonance-dust inside me until I can present it to the choir. They weld the wound; I release the memory. Equilibrium returns.

6. Interaction with Visitors  
   Once in half a human lifetime, another pilgrim gropes up the manufactured cliffs. Most never make it past the first choke-tunnel where the note grows bulldozer-solid. Those who do arrive I greet by thinning myself, offering a pocket of hush around their skull so they can think. Some flee; a few kneel. If they touch the bricks gently, I cup their palms in stillness so they may feel the plateau’s slow, heartbeat-like thrum. I am the translator that lets flesh hear without rupturing.

7. Duration  
   There is no food here, no oxidation, and therefore nothing in me that can spoil. But quiet itself erodes: vibration heals the plateau yet abrades my negative body, rounding its once-crisp margins. When my notch blurs beyond usefulness—perhaps in three or four more thermal breaths—I will lean into a wall and let the salt skeleton shake apart. The Resonants will absorb the faint asymmetry left by my passing and, in forty-three outside years, a fresh valley of hush will bud elsewhere. I will be part of the general chord again, indistinguishable. This feels less like dying than like finishing the final rest in a piece of music and letting the audience’s breath supply the coda.

8. Memory  
   I keep one vestigial recollection from the wetter world: the smell of rain on soil, petrichor. I replay it as a private cadence—conversion of scent into a half-measure dip in frequency. The Resonants indulge the indulgence; it does not mar their symmetry. Perhaps, somewhere deep in the plateau’s brick lungs, that echo of rainfall will lodge, a seed no drought can shrivel. Or perhaps it is already eroding, its edges being sung smooth. Either way, while it lasts, it reminds the organ-hill that even permanence is a chord composed of many transients agreeing, for a while, to hold the same note.

So: where am I? Nowhere ordinary, only in the interstice between a bell’s inside surface and the sound it refuses to stop making. Who am I? I am that interstice, given momentary outline—an eddy of almost-silence gliding through a fortress of perpetual tone, caretaker of the possibility that something, someday, might again be different.
