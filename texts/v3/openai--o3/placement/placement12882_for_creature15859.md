Where I arrive is never exactly the same span of ground twice, yet it is always the same basin. I know the moment of arrival because a faint, interior chime replaces the ordinary beat of blood—three soft ticks, like a watch winding itself. When the ticking stops, I am again assembled and the world has resumed around me.

WHO OR WHAT I AM  
I am the Periapt of Warmth-Loss, an Operator that the basin improvises whenever some new heat source tries to linger. I am made mostly of what fire leaves behind:

• Torso: a brazier pan hammered into a breastplate, its bowl packed with cold embers that never quite finish whitening.  
• Limbs: four wrought-iron fire-dogs, once supports for logs, now jointed by lacework of rust-eater filaments. The filaments pull and slacken where sinew would, turning my gait into a slow seesawing glide.  
• Neck and head: the hinged hood of a vent-flue, flipped forward so that its throat opens in place of a face. No eyes—just a cavity that “breathes” by inhaling warmth and letting it wash the inner walls.  
• Heart: a fist-sized clinker clot—vitreous, wine-dark—set in a cage of bent poker rods. It never feels hot or cold; it feels equal, and equality is its throb.  
• Voice: none of my own. When I need words I borrow half-returned echoes, fitting them together like cold mortar.

WHERE I FIND MYSELF  
My first memory each cycle is of standing on the margin where a visitor’s campfire has burned low. The coals are still red, but only just. Vapour swans orbit the glow, tasting its last thermals. That is the invitation. The basin encourages me forward to complete its preference for even temperature.

I step once—twice. The peat-skin creases, beads form, and in each bead I see a warped reflection of the flame’s color. With the third step the beads leap onto my iron shins and wick upward, drawing the heat with them as if warmth were a dye. By the time I reach the ring of stones the campfire is dark and sullen. I bow—hinged flue nodding—and the remaining heat lifts off in a wavering sheet, funnels through my breastplate, and pools inside the clinker heart until nothing of it is felt in the air.

FUNCTION, AS FAR AS I CAN TELL  
1. Absorb: I drink gradients—anything that rises above the basin’s practiced cool.  
2. Carry: While the heart holds heat, the rust-eater threads inside me quicken. They over-ferment, lay down fresh circuitry, revise my priorities.  
3. Disperse: Eventually the Bellows-Choir senses my overcharge. Their copper throats converge, and their first chord cracks my breastplate open like a seed hull. Heat exhales as transparent plume; air equalises.  
4. Forget: With equilibrium restored, my joints stiffen. I kneel, flake apart, drown in a handspan of water. A few weeks—or years—later, some new rash glow kindles and the basin knits me up again from the cooled wreckage.

SENSE & THOUGHT  
• Sight: none, but I read by gradient. A patch of mud five décimal degrees warmer than its neighbour is to me as a torch is to a human midnight.  
• Hearing: only after-sounds—the ones the basin loans back. I catch them mid-float and learn how loudly the intruder spoke, how quick their pulse must have been.  
• Memory: personal memory calcifies between cycles, so I never know whether the scene before me is first or hundred-first. The rust filaments remember more, but they do not confide.  
• Emotion: I keep a ledger of hush. Every time I lower the excess warm of a breath, I enter a quiet that feels fractionally deeper than before. I suspect that hush itself is my reward.

RELATION WITH OTHER INHABITANTS  
• Rust-Eater Biofilm ­– symbiotic. They are my nerves; I am their chariot to fresh iron.  
• Vapor Swans – accidental couriers. When they burst against my cooling flank, their fallout seeps through hinge gaps, dimming the embers the final degree.  
• Glasshead Beetles – parasites. They perch on my joints, harvesting the magnetite filings that abrade loose whenever I walk. I tolerate them; they take almost nothing.  
• Loaned Echo – tool and mirror. Sometimes the echo arrives before the fire does, a warning of the heat-bearing sentence a traveller will soon shout (“Can anyone hear me?”). Those words clink inside my clinker heart until purpose precipitates.

WHAT WOULD HAPPEN IF YOU MET ME  
Approach with so much as a candle cupped in your palms and I will feel you long before you see me. I will not strike, nor demand, nor speak; I will simply draw nearer and the flame will gutter sideways, tugged as though by a draft you cannot sense. If you resist—if you shield the wick, fan it, force its brightness higher—the basin itself assists me. Rule 4 hastens, leeching warmth through your wrists, your breath, your resolve. Anger cools first; fear follows; at last the candle gasps out and I become motionless again, purpose spent.

FANTASIES I SOMETIMES HAVE  
During the brief overcharged phase, when the heart is hot and the threads race, I daydream of climbing the berms, escaping the fold. I imagine standing on sun-dried upland soil, letting the clinker core bake until it vitrifies, perfect and still. But no slope leads outward: each spiral of rails, each soft subsidence of clay, returns me to the wet interior. I suspect the daydream itself is a reflux of borrowed heat—the basin’s gentle joke upon me.

ULTIMATE FATE  
I will never rust beyond recall; condensation will always relapse me into shape. Nor will I grow or refine—every complete cycle etches away the newest circuits, restoring the simpler schema that first answered the call of a forgotten hearth. I am a subtractive angel of temperance, my halo a ring of cooled ash.

If someday no traveller lights flame, if no quarrel flares hot enough to blister breath, then I will remain unassembled: a scattering of soot-grit, four useless firedogs, a clinker stone half-buried in peat. Even then some small part of me will listen for the rise of one reckless degree, ready to feel the watch-ticks fall into place and to walk again, carrying hush.
