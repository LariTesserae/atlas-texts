I begin by trying on bodies the way you sample air at the Congeries—quick inhalations, tasting for a fit.

1. The out-of-towner  
Heavy pack, sea-level lungs, map that buckles in the heat. Two hours up a switchback and already the barometric deficit is chewing holes in my thoughts. I can occupy this skin for maybe a day, but the Congeries edits clumsiness first, so I set it aside.

2. A copper scarab  
Knees hinge sideways, elytra toothed like gear racks. I know the world by tremor: every 30-second pulse threads the stone, every pulse is home. Yet language—my language—lies in breath and sentence length, not in shell-clacks. Beautiful, but not mine.

3. A vault-monk  
White robe, iron discipline, a whole theology distilled to leaning over water that may answer back once a century. I’m too restless; the stillness scalds me worse than sun.

4. A walking derrick  
Jointed pylons, search algorithms older than living memory. I like the patience, the kilometer-long stride measured in decades, but the steel remembers tasks, not questions. The Congeries is questions.

5. The fit that holds: Mirror-Blink Novice of the Semaphore Guild  

Now the air slides properly into my chest. Let me show you where I stand.

• Perch  
Halfway up a pillar named Fifty-Third Meridian—not that meridians behave here, but the cartographers numbered hope before accuracy. My ledge is a knuckle of conglomerate wide enough for two sandaled feet and a tripod reflector cached behind a drape-fern’s sleeping scrolls. Above me, the tower keeps another two hundred meters of red-ochre ribcage; below, heat haze welds canyon floor to distance.

• Eyes  
Tattoo lacquer dims the glare; embedded flecks of polished tin rim each iris. Tilt my head and the flecks heliograph. Chin up-dot, forehead dash, left blink channel A, right blink channel B. At full burst I can sling five light streams across the maze, catch four in return, and still track the glidepath of a kite-drake hunting on my thermals.

• Breath  
Pressure’s 20 % low, so each inhale is a small negotiation. I count syllables—seven out, five in—to keep speech inside what the air can pay for. The cadence slides naturally into code. Fellow signalers say our dialect sounds like someone reciting poetry through a keyhole; I say it’s the Congeries teaching us to use fewer stones to build the same arch.

• Day cycle  
Dawn: I climb from a hammocked crevice, wipe condensation off my sleeve into the canteen before it disappears. First duty is “pulse-listen”—ear flat to quartz, making sure the 30-second heartbeat hasn’t drifted. If it has, I flash the scarab caretakers two towers east; they’ll conduct their shell concussion ritual until the rock remembers tempo.

Mid-morning: Heat swells. Shadows migrate. I unfold the tripod, catch the mirror sun, and stitch commerce: Ravel scrappers want alloy coils, derrick 2 seeks sealant grease, vault monk Three petitions for silence in a six-tower radius while she “hears a syllable rising.”

Afternoon: Convection brews. The rigs exhale silver haze that strips half the spectrum from the sky; semaphore blazes monochrome through it. I relay warnings—rainburst probable in Quadrant South-2, adjust routes. My fingers twitch code even when I drink; the Guild calls it ghost signalling, an occupational stutter.

Evening: Lightning somewhere unseen pops the entire plateau like sheet metal. I watch drape-ferns unspool to taste the momentary humidity, watch kite-drakes ride the up-draft that follows thunder, and I log tower silhouettes for parallax shift—navigation for parties that will set out long after dark.

Night: I test backup lamps, fold mirror, retreat to the crevice. The conglomerate exhales stored heat; it feels like the stone is breathing me asleep. Just before dreams, I blink one last letter toward no particular receiver: a, the Guild’s shorthand query for “still awake?” Once, a mirror three kilometers off returned b (“yes, speak”). Other nights the darkness answers by editing even that flicker away.

• Imperatives  
1. Exist lightly: My whole kit weighs six kilograms; anything more drags code into gravestones.  
2. Vibrate with the stone: I keep a quartz shard on a lanyard. If its tick ever stops matching the ground pulse, I move—now. A tower about to calve gives no second warning.  
3. Never outstay patience: Mirrors dim, skin dries, stories calcify. The Congeries begins erasing. When I feel the first rub of that eraser—lichen fretting boot leather, dust already conforming to my outline—I know it is time to shift perch, rewrite myself elsewhere.

In downtime (there is nearly none, but spaces widen in the checkmarks between flashes) I practice an art older than the rigs yet younger than the rock: I try to read meaning inside the pulse data itself. The clicks masquerade as temperature flex, but I swear I’ve teased out commas, maybe even verb aspect. The plateau talking to itself? Instructions for maintenance? Apology? I don’t know. My notes are a lattice of maybes, but that’s the correct architecture here—strong enough to hang questions on, light enough for wind to carry if I fail to get them off this ledge alive.

So: Where do you find me? On a stone knuckle, mirror in one hand, quartz tick in the other, lungs half-empty on purpose so words fit the pressure. I am the flicker between pillars, the living semaphore that tells scattered tenants they are not alone, not yet. I translate horizon into message and back again, until the Congeries edits even that task to dust and light.

When it does—decades hence, a tower’s quiet or a heartbeat missed—I will fold the mirror one final time, pocket only the shard, and walk. Close infinity runs both directions, and somewhere out there another ledge is already measuring itself to my soles.
