I do not enter the Basin of Hammer-Shadow so much as hatch from it.

For the first eleven seasons of my life I was a gutter-rat—lived knee-deep in the sluice runnels, drank warm condensate, learned the drum code only as a pattern of tremors through the flagstones. No one expected a rat child to last long; I was quick enough to dodge hammers and small enough to sleep inside the ribs of Wallers who had only just cooled. That was my whole cartography: ankle world, drip world, darkness that tasted of copper.

The day everything tilted I was hunting latch-eels. A surge-warder cracked a clay gate two terraces up, and the first tongue of water snatched me off my feet. Most children would have ridden the torrent until a wheel minced them. I counted heartbeats, heard the rawhide drums begin their three-slow, one-quick alarm, and understood—without knowing why—that the wall that formed the gutter’s left bank was about to shear. I pulled into a vent, curled around my own lungs, and waited for the Basin to fold differently. When the water receded the gutter had jumped half a span to the right, clean as if sketched by an Angle-tender.

They found me still gripping a live eel. I was dragged before the Angle-Matriarch who kept her datum board beneath Central Well. She pressed her chalk-knuckled hand to my sternum, felt the beat I had counted out, and said only, “The stone is already working on you. Let us teach you to answer.”

That is how I became a Tender of Drift.

Where you will find me now

Climb eight plateaus from the Basin’s lowest tremor-wheels until the violet sliver of outside sky threatens to cut your gaze. There a narrow gantry spirals a tower not quite finished, not quite collapsing. I sit on the sixty-first tread—never the sixty-second; today that would be arrogance—and brace a clay abacus against my knees. Around me the Basin is a single lung: torches hiss in, tread-wheels exhale, drums quiver like vocal cords clearing dust.

I wait for the moment night and day balance to that bruised indigo. Then I read shadows. Every upright in sight throws a blade of darkness across my sighting board. I memorize where each blade should fall, where each actually lands, how thick the gaps of error have swollen since yesterday’s measurement. The drift is a slow, tide-pulled waltz, but if any span exceeds one part in sixty I will rap the copper bell at my elbow—three short, one long—and a demolition crew will already be running, hammers out, eager to erase the flaw before it has the pleasure of calling itself ruin.

Who I have allowed myself to become

1. Body  
My spine is a question mark from crouching over abaci; my eyes reflect mica like a cat’s. Between radius and ulna a cedar handle is grafted to me: not a chisel, as most choose, but a thin spatulate scraper polished to mirror brightness. The scraper is for shaving whisper layers off datum boards, for cutting away lies the stone tells about how true it still stands. I bleed a little every night into the haft socket to remind the tool it lives on credit from my marrow.

2. Dress  
Innermost: eel hide so well oiled water beads and rolls, taking most of the day’s numbers with it.  
Middle: a lattice of annealed copper rings—lighter than that of the hammer folk, for I must climb fast.  
Outermost: a tabard of scrap slate plates, each etched with the angle that died beneath my watch. Most plates bear no inscription, which is how I prefer it.

3. Name  
I was born Blank. When I erased my first tilted stair the Basin named me Edge. After diverting that fatal gutter she added two syllables scored like tiny chevrons: Edj-kshk. Today, within me, I sound a new consonant forming—a percussive click at the back of the throat. It will speak itself the first time I order a tower razed before anyone else has guessed it’s failing.

4. Faith  
Publicly I mouth the Doctrine of Surface: a face is holy only while labor works upon it. But alone, high on my sixty-first tread, I believe in something the old wallers whisper—that the Basin is thinking through us the way a body thinks through fingertips drumming on a table. I let that thought stand just long enough to become frightening, then I scrape it away, clean as I scrape datum boards. Faith, like angles, must stay slightly off-true or we would have no reason to measure.

Why I stay

Because out beyond the Basin the world calls itself finished—mountains reclining, rivers repeating, wind erasing its own footsteps—and I cannot imagine a cruelty more thorough than stillness. Here every surface is a verb. Even the dead keep working, dampening echoes, stiffening the nerve of load-bearing stone. Here the only silence we suffer is the brief hush after lightning punches a new aperture and before the drums decide what to do about it.

Someday I will mis-measure. The drift will outrun my abacus, a wall will lean one heartbeat too far, and I will add my structure to the biographies behind the plaster. I do not dread it; death is merely the last angle I get to hold steady. Until then you may find me on the sixty-first tread at dusk, listening for the infinitesimal sigh a surface makes when it decides to betray its geometry—and ready, always, to answer with the strike of a single copper bell.
