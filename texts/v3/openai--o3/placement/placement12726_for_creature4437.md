I locate myself halfway up Old Voice, in the airy middle galleries that hang like lungs between the dark rumbling roots below and the sun-warmed crown above.

Who I am

Name (in click-code): ɲ-ta-kop, a sequence of three palate pops that means “half-echo of stone.”  
Kind: Muridae adolescent, sixteen tide-cycles old—too tall for the nurseries, not yet slow enough in thought for the Oracles.  
Role: echo-scribe’s apprentice. By day I harvest sounds; by night I coax them into marks on kelp-paper.

Body and senses

1. Skin: slate silver mottled with ink freckles. Along the forearms a film of velvet fur stands up when distant surf rolls, long before the ear can feel it.  
2. Gills: my ribbons are still small; I ink them with knot-paint so the chain-eels won’t mistake them for wounds when they come sniffing through the vents.  
3. Hands: six thin fingers, the third on each side devoted to writing. A bone stylus sprouts from a leather sheath strapped to that finger like an extra claw.  
4. Hearing: I cannot yet read the deep overtone that lets masters predict crack-rot, but I can separate four harmonics in one boom, which is enough for apprenticeship.

Where I wake

The Narrow Yawn—an air pocket no wider than three body-lengths, pouched inside Old Voice’s flank thirty metres below the crown. A rope ladder of salted eelgrass dangles through the entrance slit; on calm slack the ladder barely stirs and the chamber whispers instead of thunders.

Ceiling: dotted by fireflies whose blue rhythm I have trained to mirror the phonemes of our alphabet. When I study I watch them blink syllables above me—a reading lamp that literally spells the text.

Floor: silt mats stretched tight like drumheads. I sleep on one; my pulse travels into the wood and Old Voice “hears” me the way I hear her.

A day in slow unfolding

First bright plankton

• I fold my lungs shut, spread my gill-ribbons and dive out the slit. Water is milk-blue; manta-leaves drift above like stretched sky.  
• With other apprentices I swim to the limestone rim, each of us trailing a gong-stone. We tap a broken-third rhythm that calls the stonefin carp to their grazing lanes. My own rhythm—pom pom---pom pom—belongs to a grand-dam carp named Splitjaw; she looms out of haze, already grinding pearl-dust between her plates.

Mid-pulse

• Back in the galleries we husk yesterday’s sounds: wooden cups filled with captured echoes sealed in bladder film. We slit one open beside the scribing wall; the released vibration thrums up the column and the stylus in my third finger flutters, drawing wriggling grooves into wet kelp-sheet.  
• My master, old Tre-hus, hovers behind, adjusting my grip so the stylus rides the pressure bulge correctly—too shallow and the mark will fade when the sheet dries, too deep and the sheet cracks.

Low hush

• Whole settlement gathers in Hall of the First Knot. Infants, elders, eels curled in cracks—everyone. Drum mallets lift. Thirty heartbeats of perfect unison, then the pattern fractures. My slot is off-beat; sap splashes my chin, warm and sweet. Inside my head the rhythm redraws the skull so that for a moment I cannot tell where my body ends and Old Voice begins.

Quiet after

• Apprentices climb to Crown Library, breathing lungs now, gills folded. Air is hot, buzzing with insect glints. We copy today’s grooves into text: one groove becomes a cluster of angled slashes, each slash a consonant or vowel in wind-speech. I leave margin notes predicting tomorrow’s swell height from the subharmonics we heard.  
• An Oracle watches upside down, pupils film-white with dream sap. She mumbles a warning: “Stonefin pearls too many—mesa bones will thin.” I jot the phrase exactly; prophecy must not be paraphrased.

Dusk (water clarifies, stars appear through forty metres of lensing)

• I slip outside alone, carrying a membranous lantern cut from newborn manta skirt. Lantern cold-glows teal, luring plankton that dance like snow.  
• At the cliff edge I press ear and palms to limestone, listening for the far rumour of the Abyss Choir. Not due for eighty years, but echo-scribes keep vigil centuries ahead. All I hear is the soft fret of hum-moss turning tremor into sugar.

Dreamfast

Back in Narrow Yawn I anchor my hammock, open a fresh air-cup and let my last breath sing inside it. The chamber membrane vibrates; fireflies answer in spelling light. Before sleep I imagine the twin mesa the elders swear stands above water. Perhaps a child up there leans against a cedar, hearing my cup-song as distant thunder.

Why I am here

I belong at the seam between sound and mark, water and air. Too restless for the root-born depths, too reverent of hush to seek the bright crown, I thread mid-level corridors translating vibration into memory so the future will know how the past sounded. Old Voice lends me walls; I lend her words. Together we slow the world just enough for meaning to form before the sea drums it hollow again.

That is where you would find me: a slim grey figure poised midway up a living cathedral, stylus raised, listening for the next ripple of history to pass through bone, brine and breath.
