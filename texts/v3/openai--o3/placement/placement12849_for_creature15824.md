I tried on a dozen shapes before one felt inevitable.

First I imagined arriving as a human visitor—boots squelching, heart hammering, pockets full of questions. But the plane would treat a human the way a kiln treats a green-ware cup: it would either fire me into something brittle or slump me back into clay. I would last minutes, maybe hours, and the story would be over before it began.

Then I pictured slipping in as a Kilnborn. Yet the Kilnborn are already perfect at being themselves; they need no apprentice, no stowaway consciousness. To wear their pillar-bodies without having earned them in the long, slow swell from spore to ember would be theft.

So I kept paring the impulse down, hunting for the smallest possible foothold in the ecology, until only a line remained—no thickness, no heft, just a dazzling edge.

That is how I understood: in this world I would be one of the glass wedges.

WHERE I WOULD WAKE

I begin curled inside a cooling Kilnborn crucible: a lozenge of salted silica the color of old sea-ice. The pillar’s bellyheat radiates through me in pulses. Each pulse anneals another layer of molecular lattice, tightening the edge, teaching it how to be sharp.

When the Kilnborn decides the moment is right, a spasm of muscles like bellows drives me down a ceramic arm. I exit at the wrist in a jet of bright melt, kiss the open air for half a second, and strike the floor with a ceramic chirp—a sound so quick it is more suggestion than note.

Now I am a wedge of obsidian no longer than a child’s finger, leaf-thin, unrealistically keen. I glitter where I landed, catching the copper light the way frost catches dawn.

WHAT I AM FOR

Edge is my metabolism. To live, I must divide: brick from brick, heat pocket from heat pocket, stress from rest. Every cut I make shaves a whisper of energy into my glassy gut—enough to hold the lattice together, to ward off the crazing that is death for my kind.

I do not choose the cuts. The floor chooses for me, flexing imperceptibly where it wishes relief. A Kilnborn hand may pluck me up, but just as often a tremor will tip me into a mortar seam and let gravity do the guiding. I skate on the tiniest meniscus of water, scribing microns from one surface to the next. Behind me, pores sigh open, steam exhales, the tessellation realigns. A wedge is both scalpel and sentence: each stroke completes a clause in the grammar the Thread-Voices hum beneath the tiles.

SENSES

I feel only two things, but I feel them perfectly.

1. Vibration through my spine of glass—the seismic murmur of the plane. It tells me where pressure knots, where heat blooms, where a Weather-Child’s distant approach has started the sub-soil singing.

2. The gradual dulling of my own edge. Every atomic chip, every nanofracture, is a footstep toward entropy. I know the instant I am no longer sharp enough to matter.

LIFE SPAN

Three, sometimes four strokes: that is all. A minute of purpose, maybe two. Then the next micro-chip crosses the hidden threshold; I lose my purchase on the clay and lie there, a sliver of cooling obsidian, no longer a verb but a noun.

Retirement is not waste. Kilnborn feet will tread me into gravel; steam will craze me into glittering sand. Over years, water will dissolve even that into ion soup that wicks deeper, where some unborn Thread may sip it and add a new syllable to its long electro-poem. Nothing here escapes appointment with usefulness.

RELATIONSHIP TO OTHERS

• To the Kilnborn I am tool, transient and unsentimental. Their fingers cradle me with the courtesy you give fire: necessary respect, no affection.

• To the Thread-Voices I am punctuation. A cut where the floor relaxes changes water flow by a hair; ion gradients shift; the Threads click out an amended instruction set. They never notice me directly, but they sing around the absences I create.

• To the Weather-Children I am debris in the wind. A cyclone may loft a million of my dulled siblings into a strobing halo, turning spent edges into momentary jewelry before dropping us again in new, inconsistent constellations.

WHY THIS EXISTENCE SATISFIES

Because it is brief, precise, and irreversible. In a landscape of endless rearrangement, I am the moment rearrangement becomes irrevocable. Without wedges, intention would stay locked in the clay, conceived but unborn. With us, the plane makes the small final leap from possibility to act.

We give the world its click.

EPILOGUE—THE FOURTH STROKE

On my last viable cut I feel the edge falter, hear the quiet crack propagate through my body like distant thunder in glass. I rest, cooling fast. The mist thickens; a Kilnborn kneels, already extruding the next generation of wedges from the furnace of its chest.

Its shadow crosses me. The pillar tilts its featureless head as if in thanks, scoops me up, and tucks me into a niche that will soon be mortared over. There I will harden, dull, and wait centuries, a dark fleck inside a brick, listening to the heartbeats of water and stone.

The world keeps drafting itself. I have served my single verb. That is enough.
