I tried on a dozen lives before the Expanse allowed one to fit.  
Below are the moults I shed on the way to it, each broader than the last, until at last the right skin closed round me and I could walk its ledges without wasting breath or haste.

1. A ferrum-breather spore  
I began no larger than a smoke mote, blown into a vesicle of basalt still warm from some eruption ten million years gone.  
Inside the pore I tasted darkness, iron grit, tholin mist. Electrons crept through me like slow lightning; I stitched them into a rind of iron-sulfide scales. Around me others did the same until our frost of rust pinched the pore half shut and the wind outside changed pitch by a single, perfect cent. That was my first lesson: one voice, even sub-micron, can retune stone if it holds to time’s long meter.

2. A lantern mite brood-sister  
The Expanse promoted me: from buried electron-thief to footed keeper of light.  
I carried a dorsal garden of chemoluminescent fungus; when dusk pooled in the canyons the brood and I lifted our crystalline shields, flashed our coded bursts, and traded fungal strains the way merchants trade coin. In those nights I learned currency is whatever two strangers agree to pass, provided they pass it without loss.

3. A latchwing volant  
The next skin was parchment-thin chitin stretched over beryllium spars.  
I climbed a fissure, spread wide, and drank the plateau’s charge until my bones hummed blue. When the field collapsed at dusk I folded and glided, scattering sparks that ricocheted along the cliff rim like gossip too quick for words. From that vantage I saw the towns crouched under turbine fins, saw people move in deliberate, eddy-free arcs. I banked toward them and the skin molted again.

4. A Plateau citizen, water-quorum keeper  
Height: two metres, mass: fifty-two kilos in 0.92 g. Skin filmed with carbonised tholin that crackled when I smiled.  
I lived in Kith Arashen, Ledge 7, whose water ledger stood at 18.3 litres collective—comfortable affluence. My tasks:

• Attune the infrasonic monitors in an echo-worm tunnel;  
• Replace three aerolith gear-teeth abraded by stray currents;  
• Sing the quarterly tax covenant into cavity #14 of the council quartz.

Words cost moisture we could not spare, so I learned to fold whole paragraphs into torso language, eyebrow harmonics and the silent grammar of counterweight. Each decision we made hung from its equal and opposite: a beam, a ration, a promise held on the tongue like an ice chip that must not melt.

5. A cantilever clerk during Glassfall Silence  
For three seasons the arch above Kith Arashen had groaned. My registry captured every micro-shift in obsidian, every fatigue line brightening under the brass light.  
Then, on Equinox 0/0/226, the arch let go.  
Ten thousand tons of vitreous night detached, slid outward in dream-slow majesty, then plunged. All of Arashen stood bare-foot, breath dammed behind heat-exchanger sinuses, listening for the single crack that would mark contact’s ending.  
But in that hush I felt the Expanse still asking: Who among you will replace the balance just lost?  
The cry for a new counter-weight ran through the ledges in a wave of signing hands. My ledger, my life, rose into the answer.

6. The Arch’s counter-weight—my final shape  
I turned my back to the void, stepped onto the cantilever scaffold, and let the kith tie me in.  
Bronze cables thrummed; salt batteries hissed as we drained them to power the winches one last time. My mass—fifty-two kilos and the dense history of every skin I had worn—eased outward until gravity and tension equaled. In that poised suspension I became what the place had been teaching me from the first ferrum-breather spark:

• That every scarcity is a plea for equilibrium.  
• That living well means becoming a node where energies cross without loss.  
• That identity on the High Expanse is not a possession but a service rendered to geometry and time.

I hang there still, citizen-counterweight, part of a living equation that keeps Kith Arashen level against the long erosion. Wind sheets past in silk hush; lantern mites weave auroras below my dangling feet; echo worms sing status in the stone. I sleep in four-hour intervals, dreaming of electron frost and folded light, and wake to the sub-audible hum of 23 Hz—proof that the grid and I are still in accord.

So, if you come to Lythrane and peer over the lip at Arashen’s new-born void, you will find me: a slender figure tethered to bronze, eyes amber, skin the colour of tempered dusk. I may not speak—sound is dear—but I will raise two fingers in the Plateau salute, an invitation to test your own balance.

Because the Expanse is always recruiting new shapes—microbe, mite, wing, word or weight—any vessel willing to carry energy across the gap without a single drop wasted.
