First there is only arrival‐sensation: warm, photosynthetic light in the soles, bass-hum braided up the shins, skin tasting sugar and copper on every breath. Memory of the cloud climb blurs; what stays sharp is the certainty that you—whoever “you” was—are already being rewritten.

For a while you drift through candidate shapes the way a seed tests wind:

1. Verdigris Monk?  
   The notion flickers green across your cornea: to keep a scholar’s notebooks, to let patina stucco your knuckles until they ring like bells on the root-glass. Tempting, but you feel too restless for the long, slow syllables of monastic whistle-speech.

2. Rust Parchment?  
   Four‐limbed kite of fox-red hide, forever on the up-drafts. You borrow the fantasy for three heartbeats, feel the lift on imaginary patagia—then discard it. You want to touch the Plateau, not avoid touching it forever.

3. Graft-Ghost?  
   Half-anchored sentinel, eyes turned chlorophyll. You taste the patience of such standing and know you are not yet still enough.

The Plateau is listening while you audition these futures. Somewhere beneath the translucent flooring a pressure-pulse stalls, diverts, returns with revised instructions. A new idea rises through the arches like cambium in spring.

What if you are not visitor, nor parasite, nor passenger, but interface?

You kneel, palms flat on a warm jade tendon. Sap thuds under skin, and the tempo syncs with your pulse until distinction fails. Pictures spool behind the eyelids: cross-sections of stress, thirst vectors, a lace of micro-fractures in a far rib awaiting splice. None of it is verbal; all of it is exact. Instinct (or borrowed instinct) tells you the Plateau can sense these interior snapshots forming. It approves. The tendon beneath your hands bulges, splits into five thinner cords that glove each finger. The contact does not pierce, only sits—cool, sticky, grateful.

Now a word bubbles up, fully formed, in a grammar that is both plant and person:

Cartographer.

Not the old terrestrial sort, scratching pigment on parchment. A vascular cartographer, reading the living atlas for where tension overtakes thickness, where a buttress needs an extra helical brace of cellulose spun overnight. You will translate strain into suggestion, suggestion back into growth. The Plateau can already feel itself, can already compute stresses, but consciousness—your quick human ability to picture alternate futures side by side—adds a catalytic spark. You are not master builder; you are interim verb.

The Plateau answers by pruning a leaf a kilometre away. You feel the snip like the ghost of a haircut.

Days—or is it sap-cycles?—pass inside this new vocation.

• Morning  
   You walk the luminous concourse toward a horn-bastion rim. Wherever step pressure reveals a soft spot, ankle-high meristems fountain up behind you, thickening the path for whoever comes next. Tiny Gleamwings follow, hovering to drink from the fresh welds of phloem your passage provokes.

• Mid-cycle Drowse  
   When the giant organism dozes, you lie flat, ear to floor, parsing the dream-tremors for mismatch and discord. Once you catch a mis-timed pulse racing down a radial rib—prelude to an aneurysm if ignored. A palm placed there, a held breath, and the tempo reins itself in. The Plateau stores the correction and purrs.

• Dark Interlude  
   You need less food daily. Sunlight on shoulders, a drizzle of mineral mist at dusk, a single ripe sky-pod cracked for seeds: enough. There’s green forming under your own skin now, faint chloroplast freckles that light up beneath ultraviolet. It does not frighten you. It’s a badge, a visa, the organ’s way of credentialing its field engineer.

You discover tools, though none are steel:

– Fingernails exude a thin acid capable of etching trial channels in unfinished glaze.  
– Hemoglobin tints swap gradually for copper porphyrins; the blood becomes conductive, a live stylus. When you press a wrist to a vein-wall, your heartbeat writes an instruction set in ionic Morse.  
– Voice shifts last. Sibilants lengthen into wind-flute overtones that carry three hundred metres without echo. When the Quiet Kite hovers near, hunting for hush, your breath-song marks you “Structural Staff; do not consume.” The predator tilts away, obedient.

One evening, as the rim-falls unwind silver banners into storm cloud far below, you reach a palatial niche where scaffold is incomplete: a cantilever daring empty sky, too thin by half. The Plateau hesitates, unsure whether to risk the span. You stand at the brink, lift both arms, feel the up-draft lace fingers through hair. Then you send one calm spike of willingness downward—yes, build. Lignin floods the proto-beam, thickening, twisting. Gusts thread the newborn cavity; the span holds. You laugh, and somewhere roots laugh back, a basso echo under foot.

After that, the identity settles:

I am the Lineage of In-Between, first of my kind but not the last. Half chlorophyll, half thought experiment, walking dosage of possibility. Not architect, not brick, but handshake.

If you seek me, climb through cloud to where the shining green boulevards sketch impossible cantilevers into mist. Look for a figure whose shadow is tinted emerald, whose footsteps leave fresh buttresses that harden as you watch. Approach softly; I will already know your mass, your gait, the stress lines you impose. I will draw the floor firm under you before your second step. Speak any language; I reply in gradient, in shifting canopy curvature, in the sudden blossom of a sap-glass awning that keeps new rain from your pack.

Ask me who I was before, and I will point to the plateau’s humming veins and say: “Some of this nervous music used to be my heartbeat. It still is, just louder.”

Ask me where I will end, and I will gesture toward a rib not yet grown, a hollow not yet sung into resonance, a future visitor not yet dreaming of becoming interface. One dusk, when muscle memory is fully replaced by fibre memory, I may stand too long, rootlets twining into heels; in time the Plateau will absorb the rest, recycle my copper blood as wiring for the next gesture. That is neither loss nor afterlife—merely the last clause of the same green sentence.

Until then, I walk, I listen, I map, and the living continent edits itself along the lines I trace.
