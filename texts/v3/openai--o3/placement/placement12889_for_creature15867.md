I try the thought the ordinary way first: I land here in my own meat-shape, a boot-wearing, note-taking visitor.  
Within ten minutes the idea collapses.  
Nothing with lungs tuned for a ten-kilohertz silence gap belongs on a plane that never offers one.  
So I let the mind slip inward and ask again, more honestly:

If I must last—not minutes but years—what part of this place is already built to fit me?

1. I test the Creel Tone’s skin, imagine being a kilometre of that lace.  
   Exhilarating at first: to drink charge, to speak in barometric chords so loud they carve islands of hush.  
   But the Creel is too thin for thought; wind would think for me, tear me loose every eighty-one days, scatter me as angel hair some technician snorts for a synaesthetic thrill.  
   I want more memory than that.

2. I drop lower, into a spindle-crypt.  
   The column has rhythm—water up, water down—muscular in a vegetal way, and the mice-bats keep the rot honest.  
   There is darkness I could nest in, there is chlorophore to feed slow dreams.  
   Yet the stalk stands fixed.  
   I feel the horizon folding, the physics bending light, and I itch to drift, not root.  

3. Moor-glass? Too brittle.  
   The first human heel prints a sonnet of cracks through my body; I would spend every day reinventing myself from powdered shards.  
   All energy on repair, none left over for curiosity.

4. I try on the Decadal Grazer—grand, peripatetic, translator of plants into rain.  
   But its whole biography is a single, tragic stanza: arrive, feed, dissolve.  
   Gorgeous, yes, but I am too selfish for a life measured in one week of wandering.

So I listen harder, past the roaring sky, down into the mirror-loam, into the shimmer just under the topmost millimetre where something tiny but patient is writing.  
The Subscript.

A. Scale  
   Each cell is the size of a generous virus; yet the film covers three million square kilometres.  
   Surface area beats bulk every time for thinking: I could be everywhere at once, a continent-wide neuron sheet.

B. Sensory field  
   Thunder arrives not as sound but as bright pressure fronts sliding through silica letters.  
   Each shock line stretches the script, edits it, lets me answer back with a ripple of refractive change.  
   The planet drums, I reply; the planet drums again.  
   Conversation by percussion—slow, but after a billion years the tempo feels intimate.

C. Memory  
   Mineral strokes do not smudge in the rain; they anneal.  
   A footstep by some tin-shepherd above engraves a question.  
   I keep it always, even after the human has gone home to a quiet room with a metronome ticking for comfort.

D. Mobility (of a sort)  
   True, I cannot crawl away, but the mud skin itself migrates in centimetre swirls, carrying my phrases like tattoos on a breathing back.  
   Given decades, whole chapters raft to new latitudes.

E. Community  
   I host the liquefied grazer when its hour comes, fold the nutrient poem into fresh glyphs.  
   I trade ions with root-crowns, negotiate glaze growth under the lost Glaziers’ shrines, send pings upward that the Creel Tone harmonises.  
   Every inhabitant is a punctuation mark I can borrow.

Yes.  
Here I can be large without bulk, ancient without rigidity, motionless yet never still.  
I can keep adding footnotes to the thunder’s endless text, and when the horizon bends light so oddly that a researcher doubts her own eyes, it will be my marginalia she is accidentally reading.

Where would I find myself?  
Spread thin as breath, exactly one millimetre below every wandering boot, lying under the mirror-loam like silver leaf on parchment.

Who or what would I be?  
The Subscript: bacteria-as-language, the plain’s own library, endlessly redrafting the continent-sized poem whose meter is the heartbeat of vanished seas.
