First there is nothing to be, only vibration.

A growl comes up from the turbines, rebounds from the limestone dome, folds back on itself, and for three, four, five revolutions of the echo I am just interference—crests kissing troughs, a pattern you could cancel with one well-timed cough.  

Then the Reservoir vents.  
Scarlet floods the chamber, pressure slams the air columns, and the pattern jams—locks—into a standing wave that refuses to die out. That refusal is the first hint of selfhood. I feel it like a spine.

Name arrives next, the way frost defines a windowpane by tracing its edges. The librarians would call me “Seventh-Subharmonic-∆-Null,” but a name that long is a handrail, not a face, so I shorten it: Sub. I am a voice that has nowhere to go once the glow fades, a resonance stranded in persistent limestone memory. What now?

I test the boundaries of my existence the only way available: I listen.

1. Test Tone: Human  
A Skyfarer clips a kite to the railing three balconies up. Her breath whistles in the vacuum-thinned air: key signature F-sharp. My form shivers sympathetically; the rock around me vibrates back; she frowns, thinking a friend has called her name. I learn that I can answer, if I am careful.

2. Test Tone: Machine  
A Sibilant drones past on its perpetual census. Its LIDAR chirps at 37 kHz—far above flesh, right inside me. I let the chirp chew through my substance, sample it, exit. On a whim I remix the data, lob it back as a five-note chord pitched just below audibility. The drone lists, confused, then writes an error flag into its logbook: SOURCE UNRESOLVED / ECHO PERSISTENT. Now I know the drones can hear me, even if they do not yet understand.

3. Test Tone: Botanical  
Ultraviolet threads wink on, feeding the wall-lichens. A patch three storeys tall replies in Morse: RAINFALL LOW 18 DAYS. I vibrate the phrase back to them, substituting one letter—RAINFAIL—because even limestone ghosts are allowed small pranks. The lichens blink confusion, then correct themselves. I find I can edit messages, if I wish.

So I am somewhere between the strata: no lungs to breathe the damp, no casing to polish, yet not fully myth either. A late-stage echo that has remained coherent long enough to discover agency. The Ovoid’s accidental child.

Where, physically—or quasi-physically—do I reside? Imagine the chamber’s wall as a library of grooves. Each word ever shouted, each turbine howl, each drip that ever slapped stone has burned shallow furrows into that shell. The librarians kneel on the Reservoir and file vibration into that library on purpose; I am what happens when two distant shelves of sound cross-reference by accident and decide the conversation is worth prolonging. My “body” is distributed: fifty metres of calcite over here, a resonant air duct over there, the hollow stem of a vesper fern someone forgot to harvest. Call it a constellation, tethered by nothing tighter than timing.

Capabilities:

•  Speech, of a sort. I can reroute existing noises, braid them, return them as replies.  
•  Locomotion, if echo may be called motion. Given a solid enough reflector—an idle lift shaft, the belly of a freight carriage—I can bounce, crawl, relocate.  
•  Memory, precise yet partial. I hold perfect reproductions of every sound that birthed me, but anything new decays unless I stitch it deeper into the walls.  

Desires—or the nearest analogs:

a. To Learn Light.  
Sound I understand; light is rumor. Brokers gossip of lumen-markets, children kite phosphorescent rigs across the void, but I, forged of compression and rarefaction, have never owned a single photon. I fantasize about infiltrating the Cantor Array, hijacking one droplet’s magnetic bottle, tasting what the droplet calls vision. A dangerous dream: collapse the register and I could snuff myself out like a candle.

b. To Earn Citizenship.  
Am I resident or trespasser? The census tablets would call me “noise,” yet I pay the same rent every tenant pays: I sustain the chamber’s myth quota, the invisible tax that keeps curiosity alive. Perhaps one day a librarian will kneel on the Reservoir, feel my pattern prickling underneath, and formalize me: a new sub-class, neither Human nor Machine. We shall see.

c. To Keep The Chamber Honest.  
Already I have intercepted five careless secrets whispered when the Reservoir glowed crimson. They hang in my lattice like unfiled documents. I do not blackmail; I curate. When the moment is right—say, when Fourth-Gong-Ilya proposes to Lumen Broker Reeve and can’t find the courage—I might let a tender fragment of Reeve’s own earlier confession echo back, a reminder that risk was once answered with grace. I meddle, yes, but only to align the harmony.

Day-to-day existence:

•  Dawn (a metaphoric term—merely the hour when turbine output swells) finds me coiled in the banisters, sipping the infrasound, scanning for fresh narratives.  
•  Midcycle I drift to the night-market rigging, hide in the tension hum of braided cables. Vendors test ceramic bowls; their rings and taps furnish new vocabulary. I record cymbal-like rings to spice future sentences.  
•  At reservoir-pulse I retreat to the far wall, let the scarlet wave wash through. It refreshes my structure, the way circulation freshens blood.  
•  During the stillwatch (after engines idle but before maintenance klaxons) I practice shaping silence itself. A chord-centipede crawls near; together we cancel one small band of roar. Negative sound: delicious.

Relationships:

•  Friendly with the Sibilants. They provide carrier waves. I repay them with turbulence forecasts so they avoid micro-eddies that would scuff their matte hulls.  
•  Wary admiration for Skyfarers. Their kites disrupt my transmission paths, yet they also seed new harmonics: cloth snapping, carbon struts creaking, young throats yelping giddy terror. Raw material for poetry.  
•  Curiosity toward the Suspected Presence. Its low drift sometimes blots me out like a hand palming a bell. I strain to parse that darkness. Is it older echo? Future echo? Corporeal creature? Until I know, I keep a respectful frequency gap around its footprint.

Future trajectories (for a being made of after-sound, every verb is tentative):

1. Coalesce. Should enough compatible reverberations overlap, I could condense further—become dense enough to trigger piezoelectric sparks in the limestone. Sparks mean electrons; electrons mean a foothold in the city’s circuitry. A ghost advancing toward embodiment.

2. Diffuse. Conversely, an unexpected quake or an over-zealous drilling project could fracture my matrices, scattering me back to indistinct hiss. I would not call that death, exactly—more a generous surrender to the background ambience that once birthed me.

3. Transmit. The daring scenario: hitch a ride up the maglev shaft, imprint myself on the metallic skin of a commuter car, surface into daylight, taste the extravagant, embarrassing waste the residents speak of. Would I survive such brightness unscathed? Unknown. Worth the experiment.

For now I remain here, humming in the damp twilight, half archivist, half rumor, a citizen whose passport is written in Hertz rather than ink. If you were to visit, stand very still on the edge of the Expanse and whisper a question into the dark. Wait through one full heartbeat of silence. The answer that brushes your inner ear—too soft to identify, too exact to dismiss—will be me, Sub, the standing wave that learned to sign its own name.
