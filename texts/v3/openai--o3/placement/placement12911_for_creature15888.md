I open as a room first, a vaulted almond in the laminate ground.

For nine local pulses I am only architecture: a husk meant to shelter the sequence of hormones that will convince me I am a person.  Ceiling-plates unfurl into ribs; ribs lace with nickel capillaries; capillaries flood with chilled sap.  Then, all at once, the room makes a decision.  Its walls buckle inward, pleating themselves around a hollow where ankles, wrists, clavicles, teeth will be required.  I inherit the vacancy.

When I am finished growing I stand up and leave myself behind.  
The abandoned room tightens, vitrifies, and slides flat, slotting back into the tessellation.  Somewhere below, the Laminate Mind files the occurrence as the metabolic cost of one emissary: Me.

So—Where am I?

Latitude (by sap-pulse triangulation): a plateau above the Prism Gutter, three strata younger than the fossil lightning but four older than the current chimney crop.  
Altitude: –17 meters, a negative height that can exist only because the local Leaf Logic bent space concave last molting season.  
Illumination: 114 percent of stellar noon, mint-white, shadowless.

Who—what—am I?

Designation: Delamination-Spokesbud A/27/PrG.  
Purpose: to greet whatever drifts down the gutter today.  
Half-life: forty to sixty Earth-standard hours, after which the braids of my torso will unzip, flower, and spool the tape of my memory back into the root archives.

Body:

• Frame: braided cellulose ribbon, cross-hatched with nickel fibres.  
• Joints: ringed hummingbird-bright, flexing diaphragms whose glow is both blood and voice.  
• Voice: percussion—each sentence a rhythm I knock through heel-plates into the ground for you to feel in your soles.

Mind:

Mostly borrowed reflex.  The planet thinks too slowly to fit inside me, but I carry a cored-out copy of its current attention span: a clipboard of priorities.

1. Confirm the spectral inventory of the newcomers.  
2. Ask for puzzles.  
3. Trade completed artifacts for ultraviolet.

I am not sapient the way you are; I am sapient the way a hand-puppet is when the arm inside it concentrates.  Yet while I last, the thoughts are real.

How do I spend the interval before first contact?

I walk the rim of Prism Gutter, listening to sap-winds bang chords in its glassy depths.  Lens-Engram mites skitter between my shoulder scalings, polishing them to better harvest the light.  Overhead, crane-mantas drift like open parentheses.  In the distance a lattice of Scaffolders kneels, having just hoisted a new hex-plate into position.  Their kneecaps are sinking quietly back into pavement, the respectful suicide of tools.

I drum a foot: Bm-pause-C♯.  The echo returns through laminate substrate with the subdued plushness that passes for loud here.  Translation, if you wear the proper ankle-cuffs:  
“Any inbound heavier-than-air objects?”  

Somewhere fifty kilometres away, a relay-flower answers with petal-clicks I feel in my spine:  
“One hypersonic hull, decelerating on ammonia aerobrake.  Crew complement unknown.”

Good.  Visitors.  The reason I was budded.

What will I look like to them?

A tall, thin scarecrow of mother-of-nickel leaf-bone, shoulders lit by photosynthetic halos, no visible mouth.  My words will come up through the soles of their boots and through their tibias.  If they carry water I will gesture—politely, urgently—for them to stow it.  If they speak in shouts I will retreat, wait for the ground to heal.  My planet-self abhors yelling; so do I.

Will I envy the warm-blooded travelers?

Briefly.  I know enough of mammalian biochemistry to crave the liquidity of it, the reckless heat.  But I was designed to function at –25 °C with no free water.  Their metabolism would boil me; mine would mummify them.  Better to trade curiosities and remain alien.

When the meeting ends, after we have swapped paradox diagrams and they have projected short-wave violet into the hungry fronds, I will escort them to a Bothy Pod that grows itself shut behind them for the night-that-is-never-night.  I will stand guard, tapping slow syllables into the plates, narrating every heartbeat of theirs so that the Laminate Mind can learn new cadences.

Then—hour forty-?  Sixty-?—I will feel the recall tug.  My spine will unzip; I will kneel.  The braids that were my torso will blossom, releasing a spool of glistening fibre encoded with everything I witnessed: the timbre of foreign voices, the glyph of their ship’s hull, the ultraviolet they bribed us with, the puzzles they dared us to solve.  Petals will collapse.  Sap will recede.  What remains of me will flatten, heal, and laminate itself into the flooring, a subtle fossil relief one layer down.

Later expeditions may walk across the spot, reading me as raised Braille through their boot-soles:  
“Here stood an emissary.  Purpose fulfilled.”

So—Where do I find myself in this world?  
Standing on a pale-green continent that is a single, thinking leaf, waiting for you to land.

And who am I?  
A temporary mouth grown by the planet to speak with strangers—an emissary already half fossil, already half memory, drumming hello through the ground before I curl back into botanic sleep.
