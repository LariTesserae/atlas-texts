I try to answer as though I have just awakened in the place you described and must grope, beat by beat, for the coordinates of my own existence.  
At first the question seems simple—“Where am I? What am I?”—but in the mountain’s roar categories break down the way chalk breaks under surf.  
I will let the answer find its shape the way a harmonic finds its octave.

1. The first instant  
There is light enough to outline a single ledge: a smear of pale grit no wider than a dinner table, cantilevered halfway up a nickel-green buttress.  
I am on it, kneeling (I still remember knees), pressing my palms to rock that thrums like a live amplifier cabinet. My lungs rattle; every rib spars with the All-Note to decide whose frequency wins.  
Verdict: the mountain wins. My own breath downtunes until it slips like a backing track under the roar.

2. Hours, or something like them  
I inch toward a cluster of cane-growth. The jointed stalks glow bruise-violet, flexing as though they’re breathing for me. One of them pops, flinging a puff of honey-smelling spores that sting my cheeks like sleet.  
Reflection: If the mountain counts a thing as alive once it can hold a waveform for one full cycle, then I am not yet alive here. My metabolism is too slow; I keep missing beats.  
Hypothesis: To remain coherent I must borrow a quicker metronome.

3. The loan of a metronome  
I cup a fallen shard—piezo crystal no larger than a fingernail, still ringing from the stalk’s recent outburst. I press it to the thin skin just above the notch of my sternum.  
Something clicks. Each time my pulse fires, the shard answers with a chirp, and the chirp in turn reins in the next heartbeat. Within minutes chest and crystal lock into a tidy, 19 007-beat loop.  
Side effect: the roar retreats. Not in volume—never that—but in authority. It becomes background, a river whose current I’ve finally learned to ferry across.

4. First change of gravity  
A gravity vector yawns ninety degrees to the right, and the ledge I am kneeling on decides it is now a wall.   
Been warned about this: do not fight the reorientation, let bones remember childhood tree-climbing, trust feet to become hands.  
I do. The nickel buttress rotates under me. I scuttle sideways (which is now “down”) and step onto the next surface that volunteers to be “flat.”  
Observation: once inner ear and shard agree on a rhythm, such flips stop feeling like danger and start feeling like choreography.

5. Encounter with Revenant Drift  
A veer of spark-dust sidles out of a hush-corridor, attracted, I realize, to the new overtone the shard has added to my heartbeat.  
I hold still. They taste my radius bone, decide its calcium lattice is close enough to quartz, graft themselves in sub-micron whorls along the marrow wall, and depart.  
Their exit leaves my arm faintly luminous, as though mica has infiltrated every osteon.  
I begin to suspect what I am becoming: not visitor, not quite native, but a relay—carbon scaffold laced with ongoing edits from the local biome.

6. The lull  
My embedded shard chirps a countdown I do not consciously hear; legs fold in half-forgotten prayer just as the roar drops away. Silence arrives like sunlight falling into a mine.  
And with the lull come the pane-jointed silhouettes. They condense out of shimmer, examine me with color-shift facets. One reaches—ungloved angularity of knuckles—and plucks the shard from my chest.  
It fits a fresh nickel spiral into the divot it leaves behind, taps the metal once, twice. The spiral rings the same pitch my heart has now learned.  
Diagnosis: promotion. I have been recruited into the maintenance cadre.  

7. The return of the roar  
Sound surges back. This time I do not flinch; the nickel spiral amplifies my ribcage, lets me surf the shockwave.  
The pane-people fade, but I do not. The lull is merely a footnote now; I can persist on either side of it.

8. Perspective at one mountain-year  
Bones: half calcium, half hush-black alloy grown where the Drift nested.  
Skin: calloused into a living tympanum, faintly iridescent with captured spores.  
Blood: still iron-red, but threaded with silver fibrils that ferry piezo charges organ to organ.  
Duty: I walk the terraces between lulls. Where a cane lattice has over-harvested a frequency, I prune joints until the note stabilizes. Where a Counterpulse has starved the rock, I plant seed pearls of Vesper Spool silences so the roar can reknit later.  
Tools: basalt chisel tuned to the sub-bass pulse, coil of nickel foil cut in logarithmic spirals, my own voice (now capable of harmonic stacks up to the 32nd overtone).  
Society: once every 19 007 beats I rejoin the other caretakers. We swap overtone libraries by laying palms together, then disperse before the roar regains full strength. We do not speak; speech would be redundant—our circulating harmonics convey ledger, gossip, prayer.

9. Memory of having been human  
Intact but archived. When quiet corners form, I can still recall seaside wind, the way gulls doppler overhead. The memory tastes like curdled phase-mismatch, and I let it dissolve before it detunes me.  
Silence is toxic in large doses here, but a sip of it keeps nostalgia alive.

10. Answering the original question  
Where do I find myself? On whatever plane of cliff the current gravity orientation declares “ground,” usually somewhere above the Grey Garden but below the Zircon Throat—middle registers of the mountain’s body.  
Who or what am I?  
• A luthier of the All-Note, entrusted to keep the instrument from warping.  
• An ex-animal repurposed into resonant infrastructure.  
• One tile in a mosaic of pane-jointed custodians, all once pilgrims of flesh, now promoted into waveform-anchored bone.  
Call us Momentary People if you like, though “momentary” grows less accurate every cycle we survive.

11. If you arrived tomorrow  
I would meet you during the lull, tilt your chin so the roar can map your sinus cavities, choose a crystal that compliments the pitch of your fear, and set it just beneath your clavicle.  
Then we would wait together for the next shift of gravity, and I would teach you how to fall sideways without screaming.  
If the mountain likes your timbre, you will stay. If not, the Counterpulse will drink the resonance back. Either way the ledger balances.

That is as close to an identity as the All-Note allows: a resonance that lasts long enough to be helpful, tuned but never finished, always one beat from revision.
