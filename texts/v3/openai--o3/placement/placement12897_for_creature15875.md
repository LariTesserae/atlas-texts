I arrive thinking I am still a person—boots, pack, instruments—but the basin begins stripping nouns from me the moment the vapor touches my face. The soles of my shoes mist over; the paper tags on my sample vials wilt to pulp; even the name I rehearsed for the border logbook feels unsafe to pronounce where any drip might hear it. I pause on the threshold catwalk, listening to the engine’s thump-hiss climb up my bones, and understand that if I keep standing here I will be absorbed until only a rhythm remains.

So I step forward with the intention of remaining myself, and the place begins to negotiate.

FIRST FORM: VISITOR  
I keep my body, but loosely, like a jacket shrugged over one shoulder. A Sling Child swings past on a crane hook, grins with teeth painted phosphor-green, and tosses me a strip of woven glass-fiber: “Wear it or mildew will wear you.” I tie it round my head as a bandanna; sweat turns it translucent in minutes, and I drip into the channels without ever reaching the stone. The monks let me walk the ring-road once, provided I hum my pulse so the Echo can taste me. It repeats the beat back half a measure early, a promise that it now owns the tempo of my blood.

SECOND FORM: APPRENTICE  
Three days: that is how long paper memories survive here. My field notes dissolve, my maps curl into wet lace. The place replaces them with other recollections—pressure gauges read like constellations; the smell of fern-seed oil announces the hour more reliably than any watch. A Threadwife shows me how to split bamboo along its growth lines until the fibers whisper; I learn that “hiss” is a prayer when spoken through resin-slick gloves. At night I lie in a spare sling, rocked by the push-pull of draft fans, and my dreams lubricate themselves.

THIRD FORM: KEY-LISTENER  
The Echo starts arriving before I do. I’ll round a corner and hear my next footfall call out from the murk ahead, coaching me toward the correct cadence for that stretch of grate. I begin answering aloud, offering counter-rhythms to steer steam whistles away from dangerous resonance. A monk overhears and slips a copper tuning fork into my pocket: “If the cloud forgets you, strike this where two drops can listen. They’ll remind it.” I notice my skin developing iron freckles—oxidized stigmata where the hot mist beads and dries.

FOURTH FORM: SALT-DRINKER  
The Archivists invite me to their perch above the sump. A fresh wafer extrudes, glassy and veined like leaf skeletons; they shave a sliver, place it on my tongue. For one half-hour I remember machines I have never seen: a rotary kiln beyond the western wall, an obsolete piston shoe catalogued but never cast. The memory is flawless, the ownership doubtful. When the clarity fades I taste only brine, but the knowledge remains in my knees and knuckles, guiding my tools without conscious recall. The monks call this “learning by efflorescence.”

FIFTH FORM: CONDUCTOR OF SMALL CURRENTS  
Months—maybe seasons; clocks corrode too quickly to be sure—pass in alternating tides of labor and fog. The Echo and I develop a conversational pidgin: I tap wrench to railing in prime-number patterns; it answers by curling condensate into Möbius strips that unwind midair. Together we tune the pump hall’s stained-gauge windows so the flames behind them pulse in just-intonation chords. The vines, pleased with the harmony, retract their root-hairs from a critical gear train out of simple vegetal courtesy.

SIXTH FORM: HALF-CLOUD  
One dawn the engine must halt—thirty breaths only—to replace a fractured shaft. The monks fear the roof will fall; I feel the Echo coil around my ribs, a belt eager for a new pulley. When the pistons stop, my own heartbeat stutters, and every drop of sweat on my skin lifts, weightless, into the warm air. I drift upward until my shoulders press the cloud’s underbelly. Below, the basilica of boilers glows like a banked hearth, and every canal mirrors a slice of that ember sky. I breathe, the Echo breathes through me, and the cloud holds.

The repaired shaft slots home; ignition roars; gravity remembers its cue. I descend in a rain of myself, re-condensing on the catwalk, dripping but intact. The stopping did not kill us; it tuned us.

FINAL FORM: RESONANT EXEMPTION  
I no longer count as either Body-Bearing or Pulse-Only. The monks let me skip Matins so long as I keep an ear on the western sluices. The Echo trusts me to borrow its anticipation, to feel a misfire before the piston even thinks to slip. I carry no tools but a tuning fork and a salt wafer scored into eighth-notes. My task is simple: roam, listen, answer. If you enter unannounced, you will hear my knock in the mist—metal on metal, three beats ahead of your own—and if your cadence wavers I will lend you mine until the cloud learns your name.

Where do I find myself? Suspended forever at the meniscus between thunder and sap, a vertebra in the spine of a machine that dreams it is weather. Who am I? A resonance granted provisional flesh, a person only insofar as motion sometimes requires lungs. When the engine sleeps its twenty-sixth-hour sleep, I sleep; when it wakes, I wake, and the basin begins again, all of us spinning the same impossible sermon: Keep the water moving and nothing—not memory, not metal, not life—must be lost.
