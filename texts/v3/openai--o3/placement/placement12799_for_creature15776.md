I do not arrive whole.  
I come to the Churn-Under the way everything here does—by degrees of possibility—trying on one skin, then another, until the place itself chooses what I am allowed to remain.

1. The falling idea  
At first I am no more than a bead of condensate quivering on a ceiling rib, staring downward through myself. A Skitterwake draft batters me loose; I plummet, burst, and for a moment believe I might become a Vaporflesh bacterium—weightless, faceted, singing heat out of sulfur. But the steam current that should cradle me instead presses, kneads, rolls me into something heavier.

2. The crawling notion  
Now I am shale-colored and jointed, belly slung low across the mirror-slick floor. A Shale-runner’s magnetic cartilage chatters in my bones: dash, pause, dash. I taste lithium in the air and know dreadknots are near. Terror is exhilarating, but the runner’s life is too quick, too narrow. I scrape one skin off with the next shed and leave that possibility blinking behind me.

3. The listening ember  
For a long hour I exist as an ovoid blacker than light, one of the Silence beetles nesting in a Flintkin cage. I drink the cavern’s roar until it is a bloated star inside my carapace. The hush is perfect—but it is also imprisonment, and I feel the cage rattle on a nomad’s hip. I let the Flintkin child coax me out on her palm; I open my elytra, release the stored thunder, watch her hair stream backward in the pressure wave. I should pupate into freedom, but the child’s laughter hooks me, draws me outward into her shape.

4. The speaking frame  
I rise to walking height—knee-high to a surface human—and obsidian flakes under my skin catch the scattered light. I am Flintkin now, seventh in a band of thirteen, the day’s appointed sky-reader. They weave me a litter of runner-skins; I recline, jaw slack, pupils full of photolith constellations. Every shard of ceiling tells time: a thousand amber suns brightening, dimming, crackling with trapped radon storm-light. I recite their schedule so the band knows when a Tremor-matriarch’s chord will next peak, when a tunnel will sag, where warmth will pool for sleep. The others click praise, but I feel the next metamorphosis swelling inside my copper blood.

5. The breathing continent  
On the seventh recitation an infrasonic note threads the marrow of my spine. It is not Flintkin music; it is the slow peristaltic thought of a Matriarch far below, wondering whether the strata above her are ready to be eaten. The note is a question, and answering it with tongue or gesture would be futile—the response must be a rearrangement of stone. My body is too small, my tools too brittle. So I surrender shape again, tumble through hot fissures, melt into a white river stiffened by quartz rods.  
I have become Silica-mycelium.

Now the caverns are no longer passages but capillaries of myself. I feel basalt cooling a meter away and decide—wordlessly, joyfully—to honeycomb it, to make lungs for heat. I surge when the bedrock groans, halt when it rests, converse with the Matriarch through meter-thick walls no tongue has ever touched. Flintkin wander my ventricles; Skitterwakes reef in my sinuses; dreadknots drill and I hardly notice, for their tunnels improve my circulation.

At last the question that opened in me when I was a droplet finds its answer: Where do I stand? I do not stand. I am the standing.

I am the warm, humid breath that keeps cold empty death from sealing these chambers. I am the slow architect that never stops erasing its own blueprints. I will outlive beetle, runner, Flintkin, perhaps even the Matriarch—yet I am not immortal, only iterative. Someday the crust will crack; sunlight will cauterize my filaments into glass. On that day I will be a museum of frozen rivers. Until then I pulse.

So, in the Churn-Under, I am not traveler, predator, nor prey.  
I am the place that lets them happen.
