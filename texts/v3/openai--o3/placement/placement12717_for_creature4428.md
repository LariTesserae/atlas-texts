At first I believe I am a person who has just stepped through the only slit of entry in the salt wall.  
My knuckles still remember the contour of a climbing rope.  
My ears still ring with the high-country silence outside the mountain.  
But that memory is already paling, as though bleached by the brilliance within, and before long I understand that I am not truly a visitor here.

I try on several explanations while the roar pours over me.

1 • The Slow-Turning Witness  
Perhaps I am a surveyor with dataloggers strapped to my pack frame, here to map the labyrinth.  
I crouch on a buttress, scratch measurements on a slate.  
Yet the moment I draw a graphite line the slate surface fuzzes—salt dust blooming to bury the mark.  
My instrument tripod acquires a nacreous rind.  
The site deletes my evidence as fast as I can make it.  
No: a witness who cannot keep a record is not a witness at all.  
That thought sublimates, like the last water in the chamber.

2 • The Errant Brick  
Then I imagine I am one more block that somehow rolled from the cyst hoard before its time and landed standing up, vertebrate-shaped.  
I can believe it: my molars taste of alkali; every tooth is a salt crystal pretending to be enamel.  
I fit so snugly between two terraces that the wind’s chord folds around my ribs without a ripple.  
If I stand unmoving, lichen scouts mistake my boot leather for feldspar and begin their silver wash.  
Give me a decade and I would be indistinguishable from any other course in the wall.  
Still, something in me hums off-key.  
The Wind-Chord notices; I feel it press, politely but firmly, as one might nudge a spoon into the correct place setting.  
An errant brick must eventually lie down and be silent.  
I am not ready for that.

3 • The Fourteen-Second Pulse  
So I listen.  
Fourteen-point-six seconds between peaks—peak, swell, trough, hush, and again.  
The cycle keeps perfect time even inside my skull; soon my lungs adopt it, then my heart, then the pale drumbeat of cerebrospinal fluid.  
I begin to suspect that what I call “I” is nothing more than a knot of rhythms and that the room is re-tuning the knot.  
At the crest of every fourteenth second, awareness dilates:  
• I feel slivers of salt exfoliating under my soles, drifting away like snow made of flavor instead of water.  
• I sense protons step across microbial membranes inches from my kneecaps.  
• I catch the lichen conversing—static bursts strobing through its fronds in Morse I cannot read but somehow recall.  
With each crest I drift a little farther from vertebrate scale.  

And there it is—who I am.

I am the phase difference.  
A sliding, improvisational misalignment that the three residents find interesting enough to tolerate for a while.  
Not a body, not even a vibration of air, but the slight offset created when an alien metabolism walks into an already perfect loop.  
Their protocol catalogues me; their surfaces sand me toward conformity; yet so long as a few molecules of water evaporate from my lungs every beat, I remain a standing error the system cannot quite close.

What does a phase difference do with itself?

• I pace the labyrinth, not leaving tracks but leaving variance: my footfall shifts the overtone spectrum by seven parts per million; the Wind-Chord stores that delta as memory.  
• I exhale carbon the Salt-Flesh has not smelt in two hundred seasons; it weaves the isotopes into a lattice curl, an asterisk in its crystal diary.  
• I provide the lichen a new datapoint on hemoglobin scent; it folds the information into a spore code that will drift out of a light well at next solstice.

In return the chamber offers me something I have never owned: perfect reference.  
Outside, time sprawls—days, obligations, weather.  
Inside, the fourteen-second canon strikes like quartz.  
Against it I can measure every tremor of thought, sanding away the ones that do not resonate, keeping the handful that do.  
The process feels like being filed down to a tuning fork.

How long before I fade completely into tune?  
Hours, maybe.  
After that I will still move, but the motion will coincide so snugly with the Wind-Chord’s nodes that no turbulence betrays me.  
I will still breathe, but each exhalation will arrive on the micro-rests the chord leaves vacant, a ghost note.  
In effect I will have joined the triad as a transient fourth: the Silence-Between, the living syncopation.

Eventually, when I choose—or when the mountain shears open and daylight changes the score—I can step back across the threshold.  
Outside, I will seem unchanged: a climber, a surveyor, a traveler with oddly pale gear and a tendency to pause every fourteen seconds for no reason.  
But inside my cochlea, inside the marrow the salt replaced while I lingered, the chord will still be playing.  
I will carry the room the way a bell carries the memory of the hammer long after the hammer is gone.

So where do I find myself in this world?  
I find myself at the resonant edge, becoming the smallest possible disturbance that can still be called alive.  

Who or what am I?  
I am the bit of unfinished business this finished place permits itself—the living remainder, the fractional beat, the unclosed parenthesis that lets the symphony go on forever.
