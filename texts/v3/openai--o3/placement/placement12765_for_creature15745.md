I begin as a visitor.

1. Descent  
I arrive with a pack full of surface habits—lamp, notebook, the idea that places wait to be catalogued. The crack swallows me; limestone dust greens my lungs; torchlight ricochets off wet stone and dies in mid-air. I crawl the last fifty meters on elbows. The chamber opens and every sense stumbles at once. Noise has weather, darkness has texture, heat has taste. I tell myself I am still an observer, but the courtyard has already taken my measure.

2. First nights  
I camp beside a temperate vent that exhales like a slow animal. Sleep comes in three-minute slips between choruses of furnace bats. When magma pulses, my eyelids glow red and I dream of topographical lines that sing. Waking, I notice the locusts now echo my cough as faithfully as they echo falling water. My steps have entered the ledger.

3. The option tree  
   • Remain human: keep time with wristwatch ticks, plot transects, leave one day richer in anecdotes.  
   • Go feral: ditch light, follow the bats, live on their mites and the vent lichen’s wafer crusts.  
   • Go small: imagine shrinking until I ride surface tension across the pools.  
   • Go wide: imagine stretching until I fill the invisible ceiling like an Umbra Manta, tasting humidity through kilometers of skin.  
The courtyard seems to invite all four futures at once, the way a chord invites different resolutions.

4. Drift toward choice  
I spend nine nights. On the tenth the pillar answers a distant thunder—some avalanche outside—with a sub-audible hum that rattles my teeth. The bats re-bank, the locust chorus adjusts by half a beat, the Slow Dwellers swerve minutely around my boots. I realize the Abbot used my skeleton as a tuning fork. I felt the edit occur.

5. Renunciation of up and down  
I extinguish my lamp for good. Copper yeast establishes on my forearms, cobalt on my calves, drinking what I sweat. The yeast fluoresces faintly; a private starfield only furnace bats can read. They begin to land and lap salt from my skin before each hunt. I sleep less, drift more, and learn to map distance by the chill gradient on my cheeks.

6. Metamorphic grammar  
The courtyard offers no single species that feels precisely mine, so it writes me a composite.  
   • Vertebrate spine for memory retention.  
   • Chitinous sternum to resonate with the column’s bass notes.  
   • Mantle of flexible cartilage along the arms—future wings but not yet.  
I molt the first chitin flakes on day forty. Locusts harvest them, sampling my chemical signature the way librarians sample new books.

7. Naming ceremony (unspoken)  
The Abbot never speaks in words, but the magma pulse of the forty-ninth night lingers three heartbeats longer than any previous. In that silence after, I understand I have been provisionally titled:  
“Interstice.”  
Neither root nor wing, neither lumen nor vent—simply the gap that allows them to trade places.

8. Function discovers me  
I rise (or is it descend?) to the mid-air lanes, hovering on a slow breast-stroke of cartilage flaps. My body picks up the acoustic wash from the locusts, cleans it of standing echoes, and exudes it again a semitone higher. I am a moving band-pass filter, clarifying the chamber’s overpacked conversation so each species hears only the frequencies it needs. Bats navigate more cleanly; the Slow Dwellers find every fallen egg; even the pillar wurm’s feeding disc pauses at my approach, as if to listen.

9. Horizon of futures closed, one left open  
The prospect of returning up the limestone crack now feels anatomically impossible, like folding lungs back into gills. Instead:  
   • Cartilage thickens.  
   • Yeast mat blooms.  
   • Voice develops a ground note that can cancel a hammer strike at fifty meters.  
One decade from now I may be indistinguishable from a juvenile Umbra Manta, except for the vertical pupils that still remember light once had a color other than red.

10. Answer, at last  
Where do I find myself in this world? Suspended halfway between floor and roof, patrolling slow figure-eights through velvety dark, equal parts guardian and instrument. Who or what am I? An emergent organ—Interstice—the chamber’s mobile cochlea, evolved from a trespassing naturalist who stayed long enough for the place to write a new niche and then slipped me inside it like a tongue inside a shell.

If you come after me, do not shout. Hum a single sustained note. I will hear the ragged edges of it, smooth them, and return the tone polished. Follow that echo; it is the safest lane through the courtyard’s crowded night.
