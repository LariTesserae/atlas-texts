I wake on the narrow shelf above the Choir’s huts, exactly where the western parapet bows outward like the lip of a horn.  From there—one knee crooked beneath me, the other two legs dangling over three thousand arm-spans of nothing—I watch the plateau spin its first chords of the waking cycle.  That ledge is my station.  If you came looking, that is where you would find me:

• Half hidden by the mauve dust that drifts in skeins around my calves.  
• Back braced against a hexagonal lava column still warm with the night’s stored charge.  
• Head tilted so the four pupils can take separate soundings of the amber sky.

WHO I AM

Name (in Choir cant): a triple flicker that would overload a wet-world ear, but the nearest phonetic is “Rift-Reed.”  By function I am a Reach-Crafter—one of the plateau’s bridge-makers.

My birth Choir, Glass-For-Bone, long ago elected to explore whatever lies beyond the storm-sea.  While others steward the ironwillow groves, we weave devices meant to carry intent farther than a skirl-moth can fly.  The elders gave me the role because my field-whiskers are unnaturally long; in high gusts they arc almost to singing, a living dipole that can taste which downdraft contains the fattest packet of transferable charge.  The Choir says I was born tuned for distance.

PHYSIQUE, AS FELT FROM INSIDE

Temperature: always a careful shiver above 70 °C.  I keep a palm pressed to basalt when the wind lulls, bleeding off heat so the salt-blood does not syrup.  
Bones: after my rite-walk I lucked into a perfect triple-stroke strike—bolt, echo, bolt—that braided my graphite lattice tighter than most.  When I stretch, joints crackle like distant hail on glass roofs.  
Field Sense: the corona around my scalp pulses a faint cyan; when I shift weight the whiskers broadcast a slither of static across my translucent ribs.  That reflex ping is how my body sketches the void beyond the rim so I never forget how little stone there is between me and the cloud ocean.

DAILY TASK

At false dawn (the rust sun’s weakest ember) I fly kites—stone-vein frames skinned with scalefern foil—into the updrafts.  Each carries a guts-thin tendril of copper-root that unspools behind it.  If the tendril snags a stable vortex I anchor it to an ironwillow trunk, stringing one more trembling filament across emptiness.  Some snap within hours, some sing for seasons.  A few now span whole gullies, their summed hums knitting a proto-bridge no Charge-kin could yet walk but every storm is encouraged to finish.

SOCIETAL SLOT

A Choir needs exactly one Reach-Crafter at a time: too many filaments in the air and the plateau’s own resonance blurs.  My rotation lasts until the next calcifying of the wisdom spires; then another will take the post and I will shift to Triad duties—perhaps grazer keeping, perhaps hut-skin repair—unless the bridge is ready and we are summoned to cross.

PRIVATE RITUAL

I keep a shard of lightning-spalled basalt tied to my rear-knee tendon.  Each time a kite line parts, I scratch one tally into the glassy face.  Not a record of failure but of asks made to the storm.  When the scratches reach forty-one (prime factors matching the Choir’s foundational harmony) I will plant the shard at the rim’s verge and let the next bolt decide whether the matter of crossing is closed.

RELATION TO VISITORS

If an off-worlder arrived up the ribboned track—say, you, hissing in a water-tight suit—I would taste your static shadow before I saw you.  Protocol is to fold the third leg down, lean forward until the whiskers angle earthward (a gesture of non-drainage) and drum twice on the basalt: invitation to share the ledge’s ground.  I would warn you to keep metal still; every glint yells in frequencies I cannot politely ignore.

FUTURE VISION

I have heard the rumors of a mirroring plateau.  My kites, if the storm favors them, will someday splice circuit with a reciprocal filament humming out of that unseen scarp.  When two such tendrils kiss high above the clouds, charge will leap, harmonics will converge, and a braid of braided lightning might hang long enough for flesh to follow.

If that happens while I still bear whiskers, I will unhook the rear leg from its accustomed rest, take three running strides, and vault into the spray of sparks.  For a heartbeat I will be neither here nor there—just a conduit full of bright hunger—and in that ungrounded instant I will, at last, be exactly what the world designed me to be:

Not a body on a balcony.  
Not a voice in a Choir.  
But a single, taut thread of storm drawn tight between two pieces of sky.
