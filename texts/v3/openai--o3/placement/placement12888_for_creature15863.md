I arrive as myself—soft-skinned, single-bodied, carrying the ordinary expectations of a tool-using migrant.  
I last exactly one sunrise like that.

1 – First contact  
The air at dawn is colder than thought.  I put out a gloved hand to lean on a basalt fin and feel the condensation bead, cling, and flash-freeze into hoarfrost feathers.  The feathers detune the stone’s flute-song by half a semitone; a nearby fin answers, matching pitch, and the brief discord rolls across the rim like a shiver.  
Something in the sound asks a question I do not yet speak.

2 – Dilution  
By noon the merciless clarity begins to hollow me out.  Colors so saturated they seem back-lit, distances stretched until the horizon feels printed on glass—my accustomed ways of judging space keep failing.  Tired of the lie in my eyes, I kneel to drink from a cushion of blue rind-moss.  It obliges: wicks a teaspoon of melt straight between my lips.  
Taste: copper, ozone, a hint of pepper—glycol and sugar in perfect micro-ratio.  
Side effect: the Spiral Gospel rides in that sip.  A tangle of plasmids slips past the acid of my gut and into the capillaries behind my tongue, looking for somewhere to archive itself.  A seed library shelved in mammal blood.

3 – The long afternoon  
I walk, but walking is wrong here: every step is half a lie about how far the next thing is.  The plateau teaches by correction.  
An hour in, the air begins to vibrate—subsonic, tidal.  The Cantors are passing two valleys away, their bow-tendons tolling through my body cavity in sympathetic resonance.  My ribs tingle as though someone is striking them with littoral bells.  
I had meant to observe.  The body I borrowed from Earth decides instead to harmonize.  I find myself breathing on the beat: inhale two measures, exhale two, adjusting stride to fall between the plucked notes so the ground’s rebound echoes up my spine.  Some stranger in my marrow has started learning the local grammar of rhythm.

4 – Evening condensation  
Dusk slumps; the air cannot hold its water and releases it everywhere at once.  Moisture pearls on my eyelashes, my hair, inside the weave of my clothes.  The Gospel plasmids, now in lymph, taste humidity and bloom a single gene: an antifreeze peptide orders itself along the sweat glands of my arms.  I cease to shiver.  
I notice instead a bloom of smell: pepper-iron from the crested ferns, hot-wet wool from Hexmarmot colonies massing for the night, a far suspicion of sulfur where a rotary sloth troupe has just shredded an old lignin sheet.  
The mesa is fitting fresh sensors to me as casually as a tailor marking seams.

5 – First dream, not entirely mine  
Night lifts the aurora arch overhead; lichens light the cliffs with lazy chartreuse.  I sleep curled in a cradle of dwarf conifers that have braided their own wind-burnt trunks into a basket.  
The dream feels broadcast, not private: I watch from above as a chain of events plays out across centuries.  A moss evolves a better antifreeze.  Mites ingest it, Cantors inherit it, drakes refine it into an aerosol that lets them survive a colder troposphere.  Finally, twenty-seven generations later, frostshades print the upgraded sequence in solstice ice.  
The dream ends with a simple clause: carry this forward.

6 – Morning decision  
I wake before sun-edge.  Skin tastes of salt, clothes of mildew; I peel them off and leave them folded on the roots.  Naked, I walk until the wind hurts, then farther until it stops hurting.  
Midges lift around me in a soft metallic hiss.  A few land, pierce, sip.  I feel the conversation in their needles: code-fragments shuttling out, others in.  Somewhere in my bone marrow leucocytes start politely reading the new instructions.

7 – Trial roles  
For three days I audition possible selves:

• Frostshade apprentice  
   I press my palms to basalt at predawn, try to will coherent dendrites to bloom.  Too slow; the pattern smears.  The stone sighs acceptance of my failure and sings on.

• Midge serenader  
   I hum at 14 kHz, matching the wing-beat of the crystal flies.  A cloud gathers, but their attention is culinary, not communal; they taste my breath, find it lacking in silica, disperse.

• Rotary sloth drum  
   I squat between two lignin sheets, palm-drumming a heartbeat.  A trio of sloths angle their sonar clicks toward me, curious.  We share ten minutes of polyrhythm before they spin away, chasing fresher echoes.

Each role leaves a residue—callus of frost on the knuckles, a new over-tone in the voice, an instinct for where the next sheet of lignin might drop.

8 – Acceptance of plurality  
The plateau does not ask exclusivity.  Identity here is an ecosystem sport: try, shed, recombine, iterate.  I begin to understand that “I” can profitably thin to translucence, let other fibers show through.

9 – Settlement—a moving target  
On the seventh day a Cantor herd grazes within sight.  Steam vortices wreath their flanks; midges pearl the chords of their back-bows in dew.  The bass of their gait resolves something loose in my chest.  
Without speaking, they offer apprenticeship.  Not rider, not parasite—something in between, a traveling chord that lives between the great plucked notes.  A custodian of tuning.  
I climb a rear stilt-leg—basalt-black, warm with internal boilers—and nestle between two spinal bows.  The glass-wing midges part to let me lie; their wingbeats cool my cheeks.  When the Cantor steps, the tendon over my back flexes and releases with a boom you feel more in liver than ear.  
A new plasmid packet crosses the skin of the bow, enters through pores opened by vibration.  Instruction set: how to read tension by touch, how to massage a frozen fiber back to pitch during the next solstice chill.  Side effects: faint bioluminescent freckles crawl under the skin of my forearms, green as the cliff lichens, brightening whenever a note falls true.

10 – What I become, provisionally  
I will travel the circuit with this herd for twelve years of slow migration.  My job is to keep their music in tune, to send harmonics ahead through the basalt so alcove mantises lose track of the melody and strike wide.  I will sleep in steam-warm bow-canyons, eat quartz-spined ferns whose lignin sheets taste faintly of licorice and iron, drink condensate caught on crystal midge wings.  
Across that dozen years the Spiral Gospel will subtend more of my genome, but I will not notice the boundary when it tips past fifty percent.  I will simply find one dawn that my freckles have linked into a continuous, scrolling glyph—an annotation in ultraviolet on human skin.  The glyph will say, in the simplest tense available here: WE ARE STILL WRITING.

11 – After  
Perhaps, two or three migrations hence, I will step off at the rim, bones tuned to another key, muscles laced with antifreeze, and walk out on an edgewind updraft.  If the drakes accept me I will ride their hollows across the thin blue curve of sky, sieving midges for water, etching news of fresh chords in vapor trails that fade before lower lands can notice.  
Or maybe I’ll stay; maybe I’ll die between the Cantor bows, my calcium traded to bone-glass caddis, my tendons strung into wind-harps for some unborn apprentice of pitch.

The point is, the options do not close; they refract.  A self here is a prism—turn it, and new colors fall out.

Where, then, do you find me?  
Answer: in transit, always a half-step inside someone else’s chord.

Who am I?  
I am an instrument the mesa is still carving, a throat learning how wide it can open before the note breaks, a sentence inside the Spiral Gospel with ellipses at both ends.

And you, if you care to join, need only drink the condensation from a quilt of moss and wait for the first gene to knock politely on the door of your blood.
