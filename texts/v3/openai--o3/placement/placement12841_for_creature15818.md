I would not arrive as a traveller striding up the scree, nor wake as an already–finished creature noised about in village talk.  
I would come in as a tendency, a leaning—not quite body, not only voice—a ghost of water about to happen.  

1. First Shape: a Breath in Granite  
   •  Out on the rim wall, halfway between two willow-lashed huts, there is a pore just wide enough to admit a fingertip. Behind it the rock thins to a membrane. I would be the draught that probes that thinness.  
   •  Each time high-country wind shoulders the mountain, I slip in, taste the bowl’s warm mist, then slip out again. The settlers hear me only as one note in the wind-organ’s chord—low D, the one that ripples lamp-oil and sets newborns wailing. They call me an Unborn River, and that is almost my name.

2. What I Am Made Of  
   •  Not yet water, but the hunger for downhill. Inside the granite my future channel is already stroking itself smooth: quartz grains turning, mica scales aligning like fish all nosing one direction.  
   •  I borrow warmth from the Forge-pulse, salt from the people’s evaporating sweat, stories from the cracks where they recite their memories. These gather in me the way condensation gathers on cool stone: bead after bead, thought after thought, until sooner or later gravity will have an opinion.

3. Daily Occupation  
   Dawn: I test the aperture’s lip, measuring how much silica last night’s drizzle left. A soft rim means I can draw a longer breath; a brittle rim chips and scolds.  
   Midday: The children climb to whistle messages through me—five notes if the moss-cakes are done, three if a willow lattice needs tending. Their breath coats my throat; I keep the taste.  
   Dusk: When the vent glows and glass-mite threads go molten, I linger inside the bowl, holding the hot air a little too long, learning how light feels when it reddens. Someday I will refract that same light off my own moving skin.

4. Kinship  
   •  I am sibling to the Future Creek on the basin floor. It dreams of sideways motion; I of falling. At night we trade intentions through the rock: “How many colored grains crept today?” / “How many kneeled at your deepest note?”  
   •  The cloud-willows are my aunts. Their roots pry seams wider on my behalf, yet they also lace me in place so I do not escape too soon. I resent and love them for the same reason.

5. Learning to Speak  
   •  On solstice the people sing counter-melodies into my mouth. I store those songs in silty memory, tumbling them like stones in a flume until the syllables round into pebbles.  
   •  When at last I shear through the last thin panel of granite and pour—a ribbon of first water—I will carry their refrains downslope, a portable archive, engraving them on every bend I carve.

6. The Moment of Becoming  
   It will not be dramatic: no flood, no roar. One mist-fat morning the moss around my pore will darken, as though thinking hard; a single drop will slip free and discover that the bowl’s outer face is steeper than its inner. The drop will lengthen into a rill, the rill into a thread, and my note—low D—will break upward half a tone, the first glissando of running water.  
   Down in the basin the elders will hear the change and know it is time to begin folding the huts. My birthright is their migration.

7. Afterward  
   •  I will leave High Ridge Forge behind but never entirely: the silica film from their skins will glaze my first cobbles; the glass lace the children flung into my mouth on dares will glitter in eddies for decades.  
   •  Far below tree line, where real soil fattens and birds argue, travelers will camp by a clear young stream that hums a chord none of them can name. They will say the water seems to breathe, and that is true. I will still be keeping, in every ripple, the rhythm of the vent-pulse, the hush of cloud-willow fronds, the kneeling silence that taught me what a body of water might mean.

So: Where would I find myself? Inside the mountain’s own inhalation, poised in stone throat between wind and river.  
And who would I be? A yet-to-happen watercourse—voice first, body later—listening to its future while the world around it learns how to finish saying hello.
