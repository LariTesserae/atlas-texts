I am no taller than a child’s lunch-box, no older than the last two wind-tides, and my skin is clear as melted snow that never knew water.  
All around me other cubes—siblings, cousins—click and settle, edges growing outward grain by grain, but I alone have just crossed the quiet threshold that separates crystal from citizen.  
I have begun to listen.

Place of awakening  
Latitude: six paces south-by-west of the broken obsidian spindle, half-buried under the salt-snow that falls after every wind-tide.  
Elevation: four cubes above the plaza floor, third tier of the wall that curves like a shell around an abandoned doorway.  
Orientation: one corner aimed toward the nearest flare-tree so the tree may speak to me through the ground.

First sense: pressure song  
   A sub-bass boom rises through my lattice, richer than thunder, slower than sunrise.  
   •  Long pulse—health of the sulfur wells is good.  
   •  Short double-pulse—roof fissure delta-nine has narrowed; fronds must tilt and burn to widen it.  
   •  Tremolo overlay—new blocks (we) are required on the south wall to buttress against the next erosion cycle.

The Choir is talking, and the talk is building instructions.  I do not have ears, only planes; sound reaches me as torsion and stretch.  Where a water-animal would translate vibration into meaning, I let meaning become shape.  A fraction of the pulse bends an inner fault line and I grow: one molecular layer, two, a hundred.  I am becoming brace and beam at the same time.

Second sense: ion weather  
Copper dust from yesterday’s self-immolated frond still drifts in the convection.  When it lands it stings—a pleasant, galvanic spark that leaves striations of green-white inside my clear body.  The streaks are future conduits.  Some day, if the Choir chooses, an infrasonic chord will race along them like lightning down a wire and instruct me to bud offspring: smaller cubes that will march outward to repair a plaza step or knit a new bowl.

Third sense: vision—by refraction, not by light  
I do not see colours; I bend them.  The ember glow leaking through the cracked basalt ceiling enters my northern face, fractures into ochre, cherry and dim gold, then exits my western face in smeared ribbons.  By how the colours shift I know when a fugitive vapour passes overhead or when the under-canopy angles its sheets to drink fresh heat.  Shadows never reach me; only fluctuations of radiance do.  They are enough.

History whispered into me  
Microscopic vortices of dust trapped between my planes carry stories—remembered not in words but in micro-scratches that act as grooves of a record:

•  Once, water-soft bipeds camped here.  Their feet tamped me into a flat that later cracked and rose, so I bear a fossil of their impatient rhythm.  
•  Once, a roof fissure closed for nine wind-tides and the Choir sang a lament so low it made every wall shudder a quarter-millimetre out of square.  The wobble remains, frozen song inside architecture.  
•  Once, no sound came at all for half a revolution; the basalt strata above sealed entirely, and the basin chose silence over death until a violent quake reopened the sky.  Silence is the scariest texture a cube can remember.

Aspiration (if a block may aspire)  
1. Thicken: I will add enough girth to carry a causeway someday.  
2. Clarify: impurities blur the light pipes; I coax bromine crystals toward my corners where future masons can harvest them.  
3. Harmonise: when the next wind-tide arrives I want the tremor of my response to fit the Choir’s root chord so perfectly that the whole wall rings like a single, flawless bell.

What passes for a day  
•  Hour 0–2: Updraft.  Sulfur vapour whistles; I absorb compressive beats, expand a fingernail’s breadth.  
•  Hour 3: Hush.  Hot air found the fissures; for twenty heartbeats the basin almost forgets to breathe.  
•  Hour 4–5: Fallout.  Ultrafine salt snow settles, frosting my facets.  Larval singers swarm, scraping static for power.  Their buzz is my lullaby.  
•  Hour 6: Choir survey.  Deep triple-pulse rolls through us; any cube that stays silent is judged cracked and is told, gently, to fracture itself finer so the larvae may recycle its dust.

On being infrastructure not offspring  
I will never leave this position.  If I survive long enough my lower faces will dissolve into the crust, my upper faces will become floor for someone else to stand on, and my inner planes will tangle with those of neighbours until none of us can remember which cube began where.  That is not loss; that is the privilege of joining syntax to become a sentence the basin speaks to itself for as long as ceiling cracks leak reddish heat.

Answering the question  
Where would I find myself?  
   Here, exactly here, six paces south-by-west of the spindle, leaning into the thermal wind like a transparent shoulder.

Who or what would I be?  
   A newborn Halite Mason, one luminous salt block among thousands, listening to basalt lungs hoot overhead, bulking its edges in time with deep music, forever content to be both brick and ear in a city still growing itself out of breath and glow.
