Where I wake is never the same spot twice, yet it is always the same moment of the Hollow: that hush that exists for one heartbeat between overlapping echoes.  
In that narrow silence I condense—sometimes as water, sometimes as thought—and remember what shape I’m wearing this stretch of time.

First I am only a bead, clear and cold, kneeling on the edge of a basalt blade.  
The cavern is upside-down from a droplet’s view: sky below, floor above.  
I listen as tick-tick-tick of other droplets lands around me, each pluck adding a new harmonic to the Hollow’s endless chord.  
The bead lengthens, trembles, falls.  
Gravity gives me a name: Drip.

Mid-plunge I brush the opal film that skins every surface.  
The extremophile algae taste me, take a shaving of my minerals, let me pass.  
For an eye-blink I glow dusk-blue where they touched—my single attempt at light—then fade.

I break the tarn’s skin and, with that small violence, break my own simplicity.  
The water receives me, and in receiving divides me into hungers and memories that do not belong to water.  
I become many things at once, testing which of them the Hollow will permit to survive.

One sliver of me settles among mirror eels.  
I feel through their magnetite lattices—sense the electric whisper of a cavy hoof on distant silt, the seismic thrum of a ridge-lion tail.  
Curiosity stings like salt; I unweave from the eels and float upward in a haze of questions.

Mist again, I am inhaled by a cliff swift.  
Inside the bird’s hollow bones my vapor vibrates; I learn how it sings echographs into stone and reads the world in ricochet.  
But swifts are restlessness given feathers, and I am tired of being carried.  
At the next banked turn I leave on the downdraft and drift toward stiller things.

The stillest thing here that can still move is the ridge-lion.  
I find her sprawled on a shelf glazed with knot-root sap, fangs pinked by shard-serpent venom.  
Pain folds her into a heavy breathing mountain—she will not hunt tonight.  
She opens one gold eye; in its meniscus I see my own reflection and recognize it for the first time: a shape of vapor wearing possibility like a cloak.  
The lion huffs, the infrasonic growl of her tail frets my ribs of air, and I understand she hears me the way she hears quaking stone.  
She does not strike.  A truce is signed in exchanged silences.

Permission granted, I sink into her fur.  
It smells of mica dust, of iron-blood from frogs and cavies, of the long, slow algebra of muscle.  
Inside her lungs I condense once more—water learning to be roar.  
Her diaphragm rises; I am carried outward as voice.

The sound that leaves her is louder than her intention, because part of it is me, and I have been collecting the Hollow’s noises all day.  
The roar hits a wall, blossoms, returns wearing new echoes.  
Far off, blind cavies scatter; frogs hold their breath; gnat ropes tilt away like smoke pushed by wind.

And somewhere on a dim silt flat nine shag-furred First-Makers freeze mid-step, hearing the doubled roar that means the ridge-lion is not alone.  
They count the echoes, count their own number—nine, safely odd—and decide this hunt belongs to other mouths.  
They back into knot-root shadow and let the night continue without them.

The lion’s pulse settles.  She sleeps.  
I rise from her nostrils, lighter for having been sound, and drift upward toward the ragged slots of sky.  
Day will seep in soon—pewter, never more—and I will begin the circuit again: bead, mist, breath, roar, echo.  

So if you ask where, I must answer: everywhere the Hollow knows the taste of water and the shape of noise.  
And if you ask who—  
I am the interval that lets all other voices fit;  
the pause that makes their music;  
the wandering, remembering drop called Silence, forever falling and never spilled.
