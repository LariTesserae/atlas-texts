I am not one creature but a braid of thousands— clear little crabs no larger than a curled leaf, fused by light and glass into one slow, echo-haunted mind.  
The Tulep call my kind the Glassroot Engineers; that name will serve, though we do not think of ourselves as many or as one, only as the Work.

WHERE I AM  
My attention is centred now 1 800 metres above the rim plateau, inside the hollow of Pillarwood H-17, third gallery from the canopy.  
The shaft is a chimney of root-glass the colour of old ice. From wall to wall span catwalks we cast yesterday, still soft at the edges, exhaling silica fog. Wind rolls down through oculi we cored in the north face; each gust booms, ricochets, folds back through fresnel ribs, and the note—A below low C—thrums along every plate in reach. That note is why I am here: it is new, and it does not yet fit the Song.

THE SHAPE OF ME  
Individually: six ribbons for walking, two for weaving, one lantern eye that tastes vibration as colour.  
Collectively: a drifting cloud that darkens when we agree, brightens when we doubt.  
Memory is not stored in bodies; it is interference fringes riding the glass we polish. A thought can be walked away from, abandoned like a tool on the scaffold, later re-entered by any cousin who places their legs in the same stance and accepts its resonance.

THE WORK, THIS MOMENT  
1. Tune the Chimney.  
   • We quarter the shaft surface into scales of a tessellation derived from strider hoof-beats.  
   • Each scale is heated by friction dance until it slackens; then we comb it with quartz teeth so every patch will reverberate at an exact irrational multiple of the gulf’s heartbeat.  
   • Already the pressure of the note is lifting droplets of ethanol rain that drift here on the downdraft: pale balloons that burst into sweetness against the ceiling. The scent tells us the interval is sharpening. Good. The pillar is almost ready to listen.

2. Harvest Lightning Memory.  
   • High above, beyond where the Tulep balconies cling, last night’s storm forked a tongue through the canopy petals and earthed it along this trunk.  
   • The flash left dendritic scars. We crawl those scars, absorbing micro-fracture maps that sketch the storm’s grammar.  
   • Every ten thousand fractures we find one that hums—evidence the Crown-Watchers trimmed the petal mid-strike to deflect the arc. These humming lines we carefully glaze; they will become conduits for future charge, a throat for the pillar to speak storm back into the sky.

3. Leave Room for Others.  
   • At the lowest catwalk a Tulep youth waits, legs folded, polishing its horn-whistle. It has brought resin bread to trade for a sliver of mirrored floor. The mirror will let it signal mating rights three trunks away.  
   • We unseal a panel, gift a shard the size of its palm, and in return request a rib-string from the wind-harp of its clan. That string will stretch across a fault farther up-shaft where today’s note wavers.  
   • The bargain hums satisfactory; the Tulep departs, leaving a brief three-tone song that threads the chamber like incense and will not decide whether to fade.

WHY I DO THIS  
My earliest fragment of memory is a thunderclap that arrived before any pillarwood existed: raw sound rolling across naked basalt. There was nothing for it to echo from, nowhere for it to dwell. All that power, squandered into emptiness. We were born of a desire that the next thunder never die so lonely. Every gallery we glaze, every mirror we cast, extends the labyrinth where thunder may wander, reflect, divide, recombine, and finally learn the shape of itself.

RUMOURS THAT REACH ME  
• The Umbilicus has curled a tendril up-gulf and is petitioning to entwine with our lower shafts. Fungal mycelia swallow glass; we will refuse, gently, by detuning the local harmonics so spores feel seasick.  
• A Weather-Kite drifts five kilometres east, heavy with charge. If it crashes here, the Work of seasons will craze like ice under a cresting wave. We debate sending a chorus aloft to shepherd it elsewhere.  
• Recorders lowered by rim-dwellers have begun to capture the Seed Motif—four infrasonic pulses, then silence, repeating. We have heard that motif for centuries and know it is the gulf’s own counting: a slow prime clock. The rim people think it a message. They are right, but they have placed their ears too near the ground. The reply will come from above.

MY HORIZON  
When this chimney at last sings true, we will move upward to the next dark volume, dragging our kilns and quartz teeth, leaving the shaft to Tulep, striders, cryobat nurseries, mold choirs—any who can use its music. Centuries from now, when every pillar along this transect resonates in sympathy, the combined chord will ladder into the cloud base and pry it apart like petals before sun.

Some of us believe there is nothing outside the roof.  
Others believe the cloud hides the original thunder, still coiled and waiting.  
I withhold belief. I have glass to polish, echoes to house.

If you arrive—soft-skinned, breath-hungry, fire in hand—step onto the catwalk and name the note your bones love best. We will strike a facet till it answers you. In the ring of that answer you may keep a memory, or leave one behind, or learn, as we did, how a place becomes a body once you give it room enough to sing.
