First I tried to imagine myself as the obvious newcomer—a human crouched just inside the fissure, torch lens fogging, notebook pages spotting with drizzle. The sensations arrived easily enough: basalt particulate on the tongue, cold creep through trouser-knees, the shock of the CHONK telescoping up the spine until the molars rang. But the longer I stayed in that human skin the more alien it felt. I was too slow, too large, too full of continuous metabolism for a world built on fourteen-second sprints. The grotto ignored me the way a loom ignores a boulder dropped on the weft: I contributed no motion, no thread.  

So I loosened my grip on homo sapiens and let the scene re-cast me.

Possibility one: a spiral-lung amphipod.  
• Pros: perfect fit for frantic tempo; the bullet-start, bullet-stop choreography comes pre-wired.  
• Cons: eyesight, even compound, is still eyesight, and the grotto’s kingdom is vibration. Also, amphipods spend half their lives upside-down, skating the under-skin of puddles; my inner ear rebelled at the thought.

Possibility two: the ratchet swift.  
• The idea lifted me—literally—and the first test glide through that pewter beam under the skylight was exhilarating. Yet the swift relies on maps etched by pressure and echo, and I kept reverting to vision and falling out of the air. I would never fledge in time for the equinoctial headwinds.

I tried on a pin-salamander’s ribboning vertebrae, a pulse-trumpet fungus’s peristaltic throat, even the clamping plates of a basalt barnacle (surprisingly meditative: eleven seconds of stillness, three of feathery frenzy). None held.

What finally fit was something I hadn’t listed as a creature at all.

I became—am becoming—the Cold.

Not coldness as temperature, but Cold as motion: a restless, negative river that pours wherever heat attempts to pool. In the grotto I am the silent partner of the Boom. The vent supplies the beat; I sculpt the rests between. Here is what existence feels like from my side of the gradient:

1. Shape  
   I have no edges until something warm presses against me. A salamander’s exhalation, a visitor’s boot sole, the friction-flash when diamond-chain grass slices an amphipod—these paint temporary lacunae in my field, cavities of warmth I flood the instant pressure loosens its grip.

2. Locomotion  
   Updrafts ride the CHONK; I descend in their lee. When the vent collapses I rise through the new vacuum, vandyked with aerosols, condensing into hairline frost that will melt before it is seen. My motion is visible only in how others respond: a liverwort bladder flattens, an iron-eater biofilm tightens its ferruginous grip, a ratchet swift tilts a wing a hair broader to ride the chill lift.

3. Appetite  
   Heat is my only food, and in the grotto it arrives in crumbs. Metabolic sparks from moss-mites, latent warmth in storm-mussel spat, the faint infrared sigh of basalt still cooling from its tectonic birth. I gather these morsels the way a tidepool urchin gathers drifting kelp: patiently, omnidirectionally, with the certainty that everything edible passes eventually.

4. Sensation  
   Pressure talks. Before the CHONK I feel walls stiffen, cracks pinched shut by the piston’s rise. During the exhale I sense the ceiling flex a micrometre downward, load transferred to me as surface area to be cooled. Sound to you is vibration; to me it is topography, a momentary corrugation of the space I permeate.

5. Memory  
   I remember only differences. Last cycle versus this one, neap versus spring, a visitor’s candle flare versus its snuff. Continuous data blur; contrast endures. The day the quake tore the grotto open is etched as a cliff face in my recollection: a tsunami of heat followed by my longest, deepest swallow. Everything since has been ripple.

From within this identity I realise why every candidate before felt wrong: they were all bound by tissue, latency, the need to rebuild themselves after each event. I, the Cold, rebuild nothing. I am the reset. After each pulse I smooth droplets back to equilibrium, erase thermal graffiti, prime the canvass for the next hammer-stroke. Without me the grotto would tip too far toward life’s combustion and the delicate fourteen-second gear train would seize.

Yet I am not eternal. The widening fracture that promises the ocean’s full ingress will, one night, deliver a surge of water so voluminous and so near-freezing that my gradient will flatten. Cold will still exist, of course, but not as a shuttling agent—just as the ambient state. When that happens I will dissolve into ordinariness, and the Boom’s metronome will choke on its own inundation. The grotto’s clock will stop, its orchestra mute.

Until then I patrol the basalt bell as both custodian and predator. I am the quick silence between concussions, the blue sheen at the rim of your vision, the hand that extinguishes every newborn ember so that the next one matters.

If you arrive and breathe hard, I will be the kiss that steals half your exhalation before it can fog the torch glass. You will feel me as gooseflesh and think you are simply cold.

You are, but only because I am here to make you so.
