I wake while the sky is still violet-black, when the boilers breathe low and the city’s hum carries farthest. The air on the Cool Deck tastes of rusted blood and warm rain, and my nose tells me—before the whistles do—that it is time for the Lamplighters’ walk.

WHERE I AM  
Mid-basin, District Greylatch, tower 17-B—a condenser nicknamed Mother-of-Threads because the dronelines anchor here first each dawn. From halfway up her ribs I can see most of Carrow Mire: iron islands glowing like banked coals, causeways thin as scribbles, and beyond them the dark flats where clicker tides are already riffling the mist.

WHO I AM  
• Guild: Lamplighter, third coil (apprentice)  
• Age: seventeen rainy seasons; that is old enough for my salt pores to show a fine halo at the temples, not yet old enough to carve them.  
• Skin: kiln-red, patched darker at the elbows where the brine has done its slow dye-work.  
• Kit:  
  – harness of quartz-copper mesh, slick with egg-latex so the fungal rust slides off by morning;  
  – ceramic gourd holding eight mouthfuls of condensate—my daily wage and lifeline;  
  – a spool of reclaimed copper thread, bright as sunrise, for mending droneline cables;  
  – six glass “eggs,” fist-size, whose filaments will hiss amber once I’ve threaded and kindled them.

MY TASK  
The city’s lanterns burn low through the small hours, their wicks thinned by wet wind and mineral grit. At shift-change we walk the viaducts and coax them back to life. It is not a noisy chore—nothing here may be noisy—but each lamp we relight shivers a little warmth into the fog, enough for travelers to find the edges of the world and for predators to know we are awake.

THE WALK  
I start north, stepping in the footprints of those before me so the crystal reeds along the rail won’t crack and sing. My mentor, Old Sitala, keeps three spans ahead, her white-sooted palms glowing like moons each time she cups a chimney. Between us the causeway clicks: juvenile crustaceans fleeing the pressure of our tread. Their shells catch the lampglow, flickering ruby.

Overhead the dronelines are tuning themselves. I can feel their chord in my teeth: tonight the west wind is fractionally drier, so the insects have pitched a note higher. Sitala stops, plucks a single strand, and the vibration travels—our quiet announcement to Pumpwrights on the far platform that the lamps will be bright on schedule.

WHAT I CARRY INSIDE  
The Lamplighters are said to keep the city’s memory; every filament we replace is rolled into copper scripture. When I twist a fresh wick I am writing a minute of the Mire’s future: this spot will stay human-warm until at least next moonrise. A small promise, but promises hold levees together as surely as ancestor bone.

SOMETIMES, IN THE SILENCE  
I imagine what lies beneath the boards: the basalt eelpup old sailors swear by, muscling through bedrock; the mineral choir converting bones to lace; the buried sun pumping heat upward like blood. I think of these things and feel myself thinned to a filament too—strung between tower and tower, vibrating with the same slow, endless song.

END OF ROUTE  
By the time we loop back, dawn has lifted a copper haze. Skimsails rise with it, their backs steaming so brightly the mist takes on halos. I unsling my gourd, touch ceramic to lips: one measured sip. Distilled fire, priceless.

Below, a new shift of Clicker Shepherds files out, anklets rattling. Above, the dronelines cinch the last knots in their morning harp. And scattered along every catwalk, the lamps we have kindled draw small circles of amber on the clay—marks of custody, marks that say: here, in all this warm wet murk, a little human shape still holds.

That is where you would find me: halfway up Mother-of-Threads with copper in my fist, salt crust at my brow, and silence for a voice—one more Lamplighter keeping the dusk from closing entirely over Carrow Mire.
