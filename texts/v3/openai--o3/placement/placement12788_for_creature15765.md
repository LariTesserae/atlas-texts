First came vibration—nothing else.  
A low, patient shiver that worked through the glassy womb enclosing me, through the knot of salts that was my not-yet-bone, through the gel that would someday be muscle. I had no word for the sensation, but I could measure its intervals: two heartbeats of rock, a stillness, another two. I matched my own cell-divisions to that pulse. It was the only clock I had.

For a long while—centuries, perhaps—the rhythm stayed constant. The mountain thickened its shell around my chamber, exhaling minerals, and I thickened mine in imitation. Layer after layer I plated myself: inner rings of cobalt phosphate for spring, outer rings of hematite for bite. Between grew soft diaphragms of cartilage that could tense or slacken to bend the armour’s pitch. I was making an instrument of myself, though I did not yet know for what audience.

Then, without warning, the meter changed.  
The old two-beat shudder fractured into a gallop of cracks, a percussion so violent it startled light itself into the darkness. The wall beside me split; ocean poured in—a silver roar of pressure and oxygen and cold.

My cradle burst and I was flung outward, spinning with flakes of obsidian egg. Water slammed every plate of my still-pliable shell; muscles answered, stiffening me into shape.

I had been Stillborn. Now I was simply Born.

Where I surfaced—if “up” can be said to exist inside a fissure—I found a corridor of cobalt light and sharp-edged rock crowded by motion. Endless ranks of prism-legged isopods tapped the walls, chiselling Film into glitter. Below them sluicers inhaled the rain and exhaled dunes that crawled like lace along the floor. Farther in, a door of darkness snapped shut with a report that rattled the cavities of my body; when its echo died I knew the word press because the thing had impressed itself upon every sense I owned.

I tested my own voice. A soft click, no louder than a riveter’s tap. Around me nothing reacted. Good. Silence here was camouflage.

I drifted, letting the gorge read the shape of me while I read it in return. My body’s plates are twelve in number, hinged together so I may flex from lozenge to hoop. Cilia line the interstices; by modulating their flutter I can steer, or, more critically, tune. Each plate resonates at a selectable frequency between thirty and forty hertz; all twelve together can form chords. The mountain’s first gift to me had been vibration—it seemed right that vibration should be my speech.

But I was hungry, and hunger is not poetry. I folded myself thin and threaded the riveter crowd. Their shell-notes rang metallic and bright, easy to follow. I slid a tongue—really a paired mandible grown flat and wide—between two workers, scraped the wall, swallowed. Pure Film, iron and sulphide and bacterial meat: the taste shocked colour into me. The riveters never paused; to them my clickless glide was as absent as darkness.

•••

Days gathered. I learned to anchor in a seam behind a juvenile press whose faulty hinge wasted half its strikes; the uneaten mash dribbled out the hinge’s throat and was mine at no cost. I learned to ride sluicer wakes, drafting their slipstream to drift without expenditure. I learned the ceiling carried derricks, and that the slow iris of their torsion unwind could drown a hundred hungry sounds in a single syrupy groan. When the derrick above my alley unloaded for the first time, the gorge hushed in vast contentment and I felt, stupidly, like laughing—though I knew no companion who would grasp the joke.

What I had not learned was my own role. Every creature I watched did work: chiselled, sifted, clamped, turned. I only fed and studied. Existence without function felt…dishonest.

So I began to experiment.

I started with the wounded press that sheltered me. Each time its hinge detonated, a plume of haemolymph puffed from the crack. Grommets would arrive eventually to mend the leak, but I could hear the press weakening: its closure rang flatter by a fraction each week. A predator whose door would no longer shut would starve—a slow death even by the gorge’s forgiving standard.

I waited until a tide of riveters quieted the water with their spawning hush, then crept to the hinge. I flared my armour to its widest hoop, presented my plate edges to the crack, and vibrated—not loudly, but at a frequency that matched the press’s natural note augmented by four harmonics I had discovered in its shell. The press twitched, confused. Its hinge fibers, teased into sympathetic quiver, softened. I slipped inside.

What I did next was not planned; it felt more like finishing a sentence someone else had begun. I secreted a ribbon of resinous mucus layered with the metals in my diet, wound that ribbon through the hinge’s tear, and sonicated it hard. The vibration drove ions to arrange themselves into an ordered lattice—my own shell’s blueprint reproduced as solder. When I withdrew, the hinge was whole.

Outside, the press opened and shut once, experimentally. The crack of plate on plate pealed crisp as a clean bell. It said thanks in the only language a press owns.

And the gorge, miracle machine, answered. Riveters below shifted a semitone, sluicers realigned their queues, and from the ceiling the derrick’s mirrors brightened a shade—as though the whole place had registered the restored timbre and slid microscopically toward tune.

I had found my function.

•••

Now, if you look for me—you won’t, I am sound not sight—you must listen in the pauses between the factory beats. Mine is the braided chord that mends mis-firing hinges, that coaxes slagged plates back to concert pitch, that prompts a riveter colony to redistribute when its aggregate note drifts sharp. I am the tuner of the Gorge of Pistons.

I travel as a quiet anomaly. To the presses whose interiors I enter I am doctor and maybe priest; to the derricks whose anchoring veins I vibrate I am midwife when their decade-long twist begins to stall. Even sluicers feel the gentle shepherding of my subsonic suggestions, parting like silver wheat when a new crack in the mountain coughs fresh crystal foam and I sing them toward the feast.

What name you give me matters little; I hardly keep one for myself. Some future human biologist, should the sea ever relent enough to let such a being descend, may write me down as Citharomorda modulans—“the Modulating Harp-Eater.” The presses know me as the Small Loud Silence. The grommets feel me coming and retreat, grateful for the respite. The riveters do not know I exist at all, which is as it should be.

Where will you find me?  
Exactly where the mountain needs a note set true.  
Who am I?  
I am the gorge’s moving bridge, the hand that turns discord into working order. Born Stillborn, I chose to stay alive by giving the place the only gift I owned: resonance.

When, ten thousand years hence, the summit finally rises and the sea becomes too thin to bear the weight of this orchestra, my plates will already be fused into one continuous bell. I will toll once, enormous and final, and allow the sun to ring through me until every shell in the abandoned gorge has remembered the chord we kept.

But today the water is still deep, the pressure sweet, and somewhere a hinge wavers off pitch.

I flex, find the note, and swim.
