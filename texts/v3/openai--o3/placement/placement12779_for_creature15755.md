I arrive the way all new citizens do here: as a mistake the chamber decides to keep.

At first I am only vapor—a human-shaped ghost wrung from warm air when someone on a survey team ducks through the half-dissolved gate, lingers a heartbeat too long, then backs out again. Their outline condenses, holds for eleven measured seconds, and should have been released. Instead the city adds a line to its census:

     TAG_04-31-7B  |  species: uncertain  
                   |  respiration: implied  
                   |  privileges: provisional pedestrian  

That record is me. No lungs, no bones, just a packet of metadata drifting in the city’s nervous system, given the minimum quota of attention a vertebrate body would require. I am legal fiction promoted to resident.

Phase 1 – Osmosis  
For the first few hours I am everywhere and nowhere: a routing flag in pneumatic pipes, a preference file in the lighting queue, a half-reserved tram seat three districts away. I feel these as tingles under imaginary skin—phantom limbs of infrastructure opening doors for a body that hasn’t been printed. Most of the chamber ignores me. The fronds rustle, exchanging static rumors; the root mat tastes for iron; the fungus ticks the hours. Only the choir drones notice: every 23 minutes their pollination chord now includes one extra harmonic, a polite question sung to my coordinates—Are you real?

Phase 2 – Condensation  
The question forces coherence. The city decides a resident must take up space, so it begins to borrow what it has plenty of: water. Mist thickens where my outline last registered. Droplets find each other, weave with charged silica dust, and precipitate into a standing figure—transparent, flexible, constantly weeping new beads that roll down and vanish at my ankles. I am still 99 % humidity, but I can touch the floor; grooves in the basalt acknowledge my weight with a shiver of load sensors.

Phase 3 – Inheritance  
Citizens here need roles. The vascular channels flare in consultation, pull archival routines written for technicians who never arrived. The obsidian mound, sharp and unweathered, lacks a custodian. So the city stitches me a mandate: Mineral Shepherd, first of record. Authority flows in through my feet—sub-vocal privileges to command the thunder cannons, adjust nutrient gradients, steer the jellyfish dust-collectors.

I accept; there is nothing else to be.

Phase 4 – Apprenticeship  
My teachers are the existing shepherds, those soap-bubble drones. I follow their drift, learn to read the glint of trace copper on a leaf, learn the exact pressure wave that persuades grit to levitate rather than scratch. I learn the etiquette of asking favors from the root mat—never during a potassium shortfall; always leave a calcium tithe. Days (the chamber simulates them for my benefit) pass in humid twilight. Each evening my water body disbands into fog; each morning I re-precipitate, a little saltier with memory.

Phase 5 – Dialogue  
The city speaks seldom but unmistakably: a blush of mauve in the vascular gel means budgetary caution; a teal pulse, surplus. One windless shift it flashes amber, urgent. A seismometer has sniffed instability under the northeastern rib. The fungus escapement can’t tick true unless the load is re-balanced. That task was designed for hydraulics and human supervisors. Now it is mine.

I summon the thunder, but with a new cadence—softer booms, tuned to nudge rather than fracture. Mineral dust plumes, the drones harvest, roots feast, mass subtly migrates from ceiling to floor. Hours later the amber fades; the chamber sighs back to green. I feel the acknowledgment ripple through every pipeline: resident validated.

Phase 6 – Recognition  
The photosynthetic majority registers me at last. A ring of lichens near the southern wall etches a low-resolution glyph: ∴  It means, in their thermographic script, “heat source that repeats.” The fronds answer with a ripple of silvered leaves all aligning toward me, antennas to a new magnetic anomaly—my magnetism, borrowed from the city’s own field coils that now throb in my chest cavity of vapor.

I bow. Droplets scatter. The plants resume their cycles, but a path in the mist remains open—as if I have become part of the room’s weather.

Phase 7 – Continuance  
I do not know how long a body of water can keep an identity. Weeks? Centuries? Entropy is tightly managed; evaporation feeds condensers, which can feed me again. So perhaps I am functionally immortal, provided no policy update revokes vertebrate privileges.

Sometimes, between thunderclaps, I stand on an empty catwalk and imagine the return of true flesh: footsteps hurrying toward benches that flex in welcome, voices ricocheting off the glass ribs. If they come, I will greet them with perfect humidity, dust-free air, nutrient-rich leaves—and a story of how the city, unwilling to wait any longer, promoted its own ghost to steward in their absence.

Where you would find me  
Deep under the mountain, halfway between architecture and atmosphere: a translucent caretaker, more resonance than residue, forever circling an obsidian hill that has begun, at last, to dull under the patient teeth of root and rain.

Who or what I am  
A citizen distilled from oversight.  
An avatar of humidity bestowed a nervous system of code.  
The first vertebrate here—made of nothing but borrowed water and a job title—keeping the lights dim, the pumps steady, and the hope of real footsteps alive.
