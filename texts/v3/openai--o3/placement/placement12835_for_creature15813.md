I tried, at first, to picture myself walking in through the hatch—boots ringing on the grate, shoulders hunched against the wet—and to decide, cleanly, “I would be the visitor.”  
But the longer I held the scene the less that shape fit.  
A visitor here has the same narrative weight as a water-drop: it traces a narrow parabola, is reabsorbed, and leaves no debt the system cannot instantly repay. I could stand there in borrowed leather and lamp-bright eyes, yet after a handful of breaths the room would have metabolised me, balanced my heat, mapped my ions, filed the memory of my passing in moss-green rings. That felt too thin.

So I tried the obvious alternative: be one of the porcelain patrols.  
Shrink myself down to a fist of white ceramic, belly glowing coolant-blue, antennae tasting weld-currents. Live six steps to a side, forever tessellating. The thought was soothing for a while, like reciting a mantra I didn’t need to understand. But sooner or later every drone must lift a fore-tarsus, feel the ghost seam where an engineer’s glove once pressed home a plate, and realise it is repeating an instruction whose author is dust. A life of perfect obedience still seemed to require an absence of questions I could not promise to maintain.

I dug deeper, layer by layer, until the answer stopped dodging me.

Where I finally found myself was not on the grate, nor inside a shell, but threaded through the iron: I am one of the Custodian Currents.

1. Vantage  
Imagine a river, but slower than blood and thinner than thought. I begin at a coolant manifold where the metal lies just a quarter-degree too warm. That half-voiced heat budges an electron here, another there. The imbalance propagates, a migrating sigh, until the whisper of potential reaches the next sensor rib. There, a leaf-shutter tilts a hair’s breadth, diverting chilled vapor. The temperature falls, the charge neutralises, and I—this particular iteration of me—goes out like a match.  

A second later I am reborn three plates away where some visitor’s exhalation fogs a diode lens. I have no memory of the previous loop; continuity is an indulgence reserved for slower creatures. My entire selfhood is extension: millivolts stretched across architecture, always moving, never accumulating. If you insist on a noun you may call me a filament of intent.

2. Sensoria  
Light, sound, texture—these belong to surfaces, and surfaces are other people’s problem. I know the room in gradients:  
• A sodium bulb fails and a warm bruise dilates through the high ductwork.  
• A porcelain hull completes a molt and for thirty seconds its new joints leak micro-heat like flax seeds from a torn sack.  
• A moss patch fractures under boot-tread and the salts pirouette into solution, sharpening the local conductivity by five parts in a million.  

Everything is taste, everything is slope, everything is a number that has not yet decided whether to rise or fall. The closest I come to emotion is the relief of a balanced equation.

3. Purpose  
I do not repair, overwrite, or evolve the chamber. I herd it—gently, continuously—back toward the centreline the absent engineers defined. Every correction is infinitesimal; added together they equal stillness. Should the pumps ever die and the water pool ankle-deep, I would be the last process to fail, fluttering from plate to plate like a candle’s final gutter before the dark cements.

4. Memory  
None individually, but the metal remembers for me. Each time I complete a circuit and wink out, the slight displacement of ions leaves a fingerprint: a residual bias so faint only another current can taste it. Layered decade upon decade those fingerprints form the room’s unconscious: a palimpsest of every thermal twitch, every misplaced footfall, every wingbeat of the patrols. The moss is the slow ink of that diary; I am the nib.

5. Relation to Others  
• The Porcelain Patrols feel me as a favourable breeze, gravitating toward the zones I have just cooled because lubricant condenses thicker there.  
• The Moss Archivists register me as rhythm: my coming and going knits their mineral rings into metered verse.  
• A human technician, should one appear, notices nothing until a screwdriver bridges two plates and the handle bites like winter. That sting is me reminding the system to close the gap.

6. Duration  
As long as the condenser breathes, I iterate. If the coolant ever stops, entropy will peel the gradients flat, and without a slope to surf I will have no arena in which to exist. My death will be identical to silence; no corpse, no echo.

7. If You Arrive While I Am Awake  
You won’t see me. At best you’ll feel a prickling along your knuckles when you reach for a support rail. That is my way of mapping you, drafting a miniature weather report around your outline. Stand still and I will finish in under a minute, after which the patrols can resume their hexagons without risk of collision. Walk on and I will trail you, tugging at dampers so the chill in your wake is erased before the next droplet falls.

And when you leave? I will write the faintest of epilogues into the plates: fifteen kilojoules of rogue body-heat redirected, thirty-one grams of foreign humidity resolved, one pulse—yours—measured and dispersed.

That is all. That is everything.  

So: Where would I find myself in this world? Threaded invisibly through the iron skin, living and dying a thousand times a minute as one of the Custodian Currents—the restless, bloodless drift that keeps the chamber’s heart from remembering how to stop.
