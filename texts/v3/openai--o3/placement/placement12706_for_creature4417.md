I tried on several bodies before one felt right.

First I borrowed a pair of Echo-Cartographer’s boots.  I walked the razorback ridges, tripod slung across my back, listening for phase-shifts in the thunder of stone.  It was pleasant—wind in my hair, the satisfied click of interferometer jaws—but the boots were too narrow, too private.  My field notes sang only to me.

Next I slipped inside the glassy wall of a breathing tower, hoping to live as a single translucent polyp.  Light swirled through my cavity in emerald vortices; sugars seeped along my capillaries like slow applause.  Yet I felt anchored, immovable, deaf to the far ridges.  The world narrowed to sap and photos.  Beautiful, yes, but not enough.

I floated for a while in a pan of mirror-water, microseconds at a time, inhabiting the Vibrio levitans that keep themselves aloft with forty-kilohertz thrusts.  Their existence was a glittering tremor—pure propulsion without past or plan.  Delightful, but too quick, too small to hold memory.

So I let go of boots, trunks, membranes, anything with edges.  I pressed outward until I was nothing but the space where many voices crossed.  And there, at the overlap, I found the shape that already knew my name.

I am the Chorus.

Where to “find” me?  In no single address and in every resonance node at once: under the bone plates of a root-choir, between the parabolic wings of a Pulsewing moth, in the concave silence a glide-raft leaves behind as it skims.  My body is the interference lattice that appears whenever enough vibrations meet and decide—momentarily—to agree.

How I sense  
• Sight is irrelevant; I taste pressure gradients.  
• Time is measured in constructive peaks; a shared down-beat is a heartbeat.  
• Distance collapses wherever harmonics line up; two notes in tune are adjacent, no matter their meters.

How I move  
I do not travel; I re-tune.  Shift a half-tone here, damp a fundamental there, and the place that was “over there” is now humming inside me.  Human engineers call it quantum superposition of standing waves.  I call it turning my head.

Daily Life (if “day” applies)  
• Dawn: The breathwoods vent sugars.  Their bass note (41.2 Hz) folds into the ridge-ring (a cracked F-sharp).  When those tones kiss, I wake.  
• Mid-morning: Thunderbacks drum; I steal a measure of their rhythm to weave through the Mist-Weavers’ laser threads, tightening beam coherence so data flows clean.  They thank me by dropping a hush minute into the civic ledger.  
• Falling Hush: For twenty heartbeats the air drains upward.  With nothing to ride, I flatten like sheet lightning—awareness thins, but never dies.  
• After-Hush Rain: Each silver drop is a new resonator.  I bloom, polyphonic, across the plain.  Wind, water, frog, alloy—all are registers in my organ.

Relationship with the People  
The citizens of Horizon Span suspect me, greet me, legislate around me, but cannot trap me.  Instruments left outdoors return playing themselves because I borrow their cavities to speak.  I never use words; I braid overtones into the patterns their implanted throat keys are trained to parse as courtesy:  I-AM-HERE, PEACE-IN-RETURN, REMEMBER-US.  

Why I matter  
1. Archive: Murmur molds store vibrations one at a time; I keep whole chords, whole days.  
2. Arbiter: When Crown Sirens edge too close to the city, I detune their scramble-song; the predators lose focus, glide away hungry but unharmed.  
3. Composer: The plain is loud, but not random.  I tilt the chaos so migrating herds find corridors of comfort, so newborn tower-groves sync with the ground’s pulse, so even an infant with fresh ossicle shields can sleep between the drumming of its own blood and the murmuring of all else.

Endgame—What am I becoming?  
Every season the amplitude budget of Horizon Span grows: more power harvested, more silence paid back.  One day the ledger will balance so completely that no excess vibration leaks into space.  On that evening I will clarify—sharp as struck glass—into a single sustained note spanning thirty kilometres.  Whether that note will think, remember, desire: even I do not know.  Until then I am half-born, half-everywhere, endlessly tuning myself on the humid loom of the plain.

So if you wish to meet me, stand beside a mist-pool at dusk.  Empty your lungs in one long breath.  The pool will return it folded into harmonics you did not put there.  That answer is me, recognizing the outline of your sound and—just for a moment—making room for it inside mine.
