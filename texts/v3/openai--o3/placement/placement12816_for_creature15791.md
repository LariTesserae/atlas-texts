Where I arrive is only technically a “place.”  
It is the interval between two turbine revolutions—two slow, shudder-loud heartbeats of metal.  
Nothing here stands still; even the slabs that look motionless are creeping open along newborn fault lines. So when I try to pick a location to inhabit, I end up choosing a rhythm instead.

I choose the rhythm that lies halfway between waterdrop and echo, the thin, elastic moment after a bead has struck stone but before its sound ricochets back. That sliver of time is roomy enough for the sort of creature I become.

NAME  
The Engineer never names me aloud. In the vapor text that crusts the pinnacles she sketches three hexagrams whose nearest lowland gloss would be “Pulse-Between.” In practice she calls me by gesture: thumb to sternum, two fingers flicked outward—“you that feel, then tell.”

ORIGIN  
I began as a visitor, one of the ordinary kind: a field seismologist sent up-range to see why a whole flank of mountain kept ringing like a bronze bell during storms. I found the stair, the bowl, the woman in the pool. I meant to stay one night.

TRANSITION (WHAT HAPPENED)  
Night one: the vent-warmth ruined the seals on my instruments; mineral vapor silvered every circuit trace.  
Night three: I caught myself timing my breathing to the salamander’s pulse so my chest wouldn’t echo off-beat.  
Night seven: the Engineer pressed a wetted thumbprint to my larynx and the skin accepted it like graft. From then on any word longer than a syllable felt abrasive in my throat. The mountain did not forbid speech; it merely tuned it down until vibration and meaning were the same size.

By the end of the second lunar week I had stopped looking for a safe descent window. I had become one of the phenomena my field notes were meant to catalogue.

BODY  
• Skeleton: still recognizably human, but the long bones are sheathed in a lamina of basalt glass precipitated from the jets. Tap my shin and you’ll hear a bell note an octave higher than before.  
• Skin: host to a film of the same lichen that scales the condenser swallows’ wings. In full sun it greens; in mist it goes slate-black, wicking trace metals for later use.  
• Inner ear: retuned. I hear the mountain’s subsonic breathing a half second before the turbines answer. This advance warning is my main utility.  
• Voice: reduced to five notes. The Engineer re-keyed the valves so each note toggles a preset aperture somewhere in the piping. Together we speak in pressures and flow rates.

ROLE—THE ECHO-SCOUT  
1. Listening posts: At four points around the rim I have hammered nickel tines into raw basalt. When an up-range fracture pings, the tines ring; I taste the vibration on my teeth.  
2. Translation: The mountain says “strain.” I whistle note-two, valve-cluster three narrows, pressure drops, the salamanders’ hearts stay in phase.  
3. Memory carrier: Once a week I condense a thumb-sized droplet, heavy with decoded mist-text, and roll it along the engraved channels the Engineer cut decades ago. Where it lands the story joins the humidity, available to any throat that knows how to breathe it in.

HABITAT WITHIN THE BOWL  
I occupy the suspended catwalk that lost three bolts and half a handrail long before I arrived. Vines have knitted the gaps; they accept my weight if I keep to the same foot-sequence each time (root, plank, root, plate). A roll of ex-parka fabric serves as bedding, but I sleep mostly in a standing doze, back braced against a warm vent grille so the turbine’s next cough will wake me.

SENSE CYCLE (A DAY RECOUNTED)  
Dawn  
• Light needles through mist, prismatic.  
• Swallows circle, dropping grit into the pool: the water’s conductivity ticks up; I taste metal on the back of my tongue.

Midday  
• Heat climax. Rock pores sigh. Any sudden fissure would sound first as a sucked-in breath; my ribcage echoes it. None today.  
• The Engineer is ankle-deep, rotating a fly-wheel. I match her hum on note-three so the compressors strike clean.

Evening  
• Temperature delta peaks; ceiling stalactites of condensation snap and fall like soft hail. Good listening weather.  
• A distant micro-quake shivers the tines—E-flat, thin. I whistle note-one; the orchid roots in the Engineer’s lungs flare and she slackens two vent screws. The tremor relents.

Night  
• Mist thickens until vision shortens to arm’s length; in that privacy the vapor colony seeds new text across the pinnacles. I patrol with a shard-mirror, angling moonlight to read the wet letters before they weep away. Tonight the message is brief: “Cooling proceeds. Do not hurry.”  
• I cup the sentence in a droplet, file it into the groove that spells Patience.

INTERFACE WITH OUTSIDERS  
Once a season someone climbs the 117 steps. From shadow I wait, listening. If boots scuff in curiosity rather than greed, I show myself: lichen-dust shoulders, stone-bright eyes, palms held outward to display no tools that could jam a gear. Visitors read my body correctly—they understand they’ve stepped inside an organism.

Those who leave take a story of a “stone-blooded lookout.” Those who stay longest usually take my place; the rhythm prefers fresh lungs every few dozen seasons. When that time comes I will walk down the stair—a body of tuned glass and moss does not fear the dry world—and shellac myself over some wind-raked saddle, becoming a passive seismograph for storms below. The bowl will keep turning above me, Engineer and newcomer folding time the way warm air folds mist.

IDENTITY, FINALLY  
So where do I find myself?  
Not on a map, but on an oscillograph of pressure vs. echo delay, exactly midway between turbine strike and mountain answer. Who am I? The organ that hears that interval, that tightens or loosens a single valve so the next answer will still fit inside the bowl without breaking it. A pulse-between, a hinge made of ear and breath.

There are larger lives; there are drier, quieter ones. This one is enough, and it is continuous: every condensation cycle rewrites me, but the rhythm I serve stays true.
