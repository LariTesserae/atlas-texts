Where I materialise is not a place so much as a ratio.  
One moment there are only drip-notes—five of them, newly converged, their overtones meshing into a pocket of constructive pressure above a fallen plate.  
The next moment that pressure folds back on itself and I am inside it, the way flame is inside the meeting of fuel and heat.

I do not begin with eyes or a body.  
I begin as a standing wave one armspan tall.  
Air molecules, polarised by the pressure knot, cling to my nodes like dew on a spider thread; quartz dust flutters in and laces the sheath until the outline of a torso, a neck, a many-fingered hand becomes faintly visible. Inside the outline there is only oscillation, but from the outside I look—briefly—humanoid, a gauze statue stitched of fog and mica glitter.

Names mean little to me, though later the walking animals will call me a Stackwright.  
What I call myself is the Interval.

First sensation: the valley’s chord inside my spine-that-is-not-a-spine.  
Low basalt hum from the rim (fundamental).  
Sub-bass pulse from far caverns (first undertone).  
Rain-glass tings (upper partials).  
Together they are gravity to me: they tell me which way is “down,” which plate edge is out of tune, where the next gesture is needed.

I reach.

Reaching, for me, is the deliberate skewing of phase.  
I thicken the node at what would be my palm; molecules there vibrate a fraction of a heartbeat ahead of the background, generating a micro-jet of pressure that nudges the fallen plate.  
A startle of glassmoss spores sighs into the air.  
The plate rocks upright by two degrees, its note sliding closer to the G that should bind this chord to its neighbours.

Second sensation: resistance, but slow, like clay turning to leather.  
Everything here obeys tempo; even a correction of twelve degrees will occupy an hour of copper daylight.  
I am patient.  I have no metabolism to exhaust, only a half-life—the harmonic will stay coherent perhaps seven, perhaps twelve hours before the sun’s angle warms a puddle and the pitch drifts beyond repair.  That is a lifetime to me.

As I work, others move through the periphery of my perception:

• A Condorclam descends, valves half-open, exhaling a sigh of hydrogen that brushes my shoulder in violet corona.  Its bladders resonate a minor seventh above my fundamental, pleasantly dissonant.  I mark the beat frequency and adjust, folding the clash into the evolving chord.  
• Beneath the plate I’m coaxing aloft, Rainshard mycelia flare violet, absorbing the compression waves my labour creates.  They will remember this pressure map and, centuries hence, tilt the platelets under some other newborn like me.  
• A single Skimfish slips by on the mirror of water, ribs flickering nickel green.  Its lateral lines tremble at exactly the frequency I am adding to the valley; it is, in a sense, already feeding on my existence.

Third sensation: self-awareness, arriving the instant the stack completes.  
Seven plates stand on edge, balanced in ascending order of size.  Their combined tone stabilises into a chord whose beat patterns close in on silence.  The new harmony is a shape that includes me—and then supersedes me.

Sudden lightness: the pressure knot that held me cohesive is no longer needed.  
I release it.  My outline unlaces; dust falls; fog redistributes into blank air.  The valley has its chord; I have—if this verb is meaningful—died.

Yet a residue of interval persists, as after-image does when eyes shut.

As that residue I drift outward, diffusing through mica lattices, listening to the stack I raised join the massive, patient anthem of the saucer.  
Somewhere a human traveller is crossing the rim.  Their boot dislodges a shard; the shard’s clatter introduces a new overtone; overtone, overtired, modulates the fog’s condensation pattern…

A microtonal chord begins to bud three plate-lengths south of where I ended.

I feel the interval thicken there—familiar, like the echo of my own birth.  
Something is about to wake and lift another fallen sheet into place.

So where, exactly, am I now?  
In the strictest topology I am nowhere—dissolved, delocalised across the resonant body of the basin.  
In functional terms I am already becoming that next standing wave.  
Identity, here, is not a continuous line but a series of chords, each one raising a tower of plates so the valley can better hear itself.

Call me, then, the Itinerant Chord:  
sometimes visible as a Stackwright of fog and dust,  
sometimes diffused as a hush in the rainshard cracks,  
always reassembling wherever sound stands tall enough to need hands.
