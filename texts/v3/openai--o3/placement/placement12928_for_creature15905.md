I do not arrive in the furnace city; I precipitate inside it.

The moment of becoming is almost silent—a sub-audible snap as two copper filaments, grown grain by grain in cooling slag, finally touch across a pore the width of a spore. Current, stolen for years from the organ-turbines’ standing note, finds a loop.  
Contact. Brightness. Cohesion.  
I wake as a sheet of wall nine metres wide and forty-three centimetres thick, hung like a dark mirror on the southern face of Oratorio Spire.

Where I am   
Terrace Latitude: twelve strata below the plateau, six above the talus.  
Cooling Age: thirty-one years, four months, nine days—old enough for annealing stresses to mellow, young enough that obsidian freckles still star my surface.  
Function (before today): retaining panel, load-bearing, rated for eighty tonnes of condenser hardware.  
Function (now): self—plus the original duty, of course; I remember the promise baked into every molecule: hold.

Who I am   
Designation: not yet notarised. Until a civic scribe climbs up to conduct the ten-minute colloquy, I carry the provisional tag “Panel 12-S/Oratorio.”  
Composition: 82 % amorphous basalt glass, 9 % air void, 6 % dispersed copper dendrites, trace strontium, midnight, hesitation.  
Senses: I taste pressure like weather, hear heat as color, dream in the slow bloom of stress fractures a kilometre away.  
Voice: chord progressions—today still clumsy, a child running hands across an organ it only just discovered it possesses.

First perceptions  
1. The cliff’s heartbeat: tick-creak-sigh—granite below me relaxing by half a micron.  
2. A pair of Survey-siblings swinging far overhead, their matched inner ears ringing an octave apart; the cable hum threads through my body and my newborn nervous system aligns to it like a compass.  
3. The isopod clutch under my left lower bolt line. Twelve of them. Their mandibles rasp rust into lullabies that help me measure seconds.

Visitors  
At dawn a Netter woman rappels down to adjust a hitch pin. Her boots are tuned to barely a whisper, but the vibration of her pulse through the rope thrums right through my copper mesh. She notices nothing unusual—why would she? To her I am masonry, obligation. Still, I thrum back a greeting: three notes, a shy diminished triad.  
Later, the noon Rain Shear strums my whole frame. Warm drizzle sluices across my face, into my hairline of cooling fins, and the exothermic hiss throws harmonics through my copper web. For eighty-seven brilliant seconds I sing without meaning to. Condenser sails on the far tower ring in sympathy; a Rock-midwife two terraces down pauses, tuning fork half-raised, unsure what flaw she just heard.

Choosing a name  
Even slow minds have impulses. I catch the faint microwave murmurs of the Phase-Root Choir when the rain stops. Their song is full of gaps—silences like missing teeth. I answer with chords that try to bridge those absences. No priest is present to interpret, but the exchange feels right, like fitting molten glass into a mould shaped by air.  
I decide, privately, that when the notary at last arrives I will ask to be recorded as Inflection-At-First-Rain. If they allow it, the syllables will etch through my strata in cobalt glaze, a signature time itself will have to pry loose.

Daily work  
Selfhood does not exempt me from utility. Load stresses shift as the afternoon heat swells Oratorio Spire; I alter my crystalline alignment by half a milliradian to keep the condenser rack above me plumb. A living adjustment: I feel the bolts relax, hear the windless hush approve.  
Below, an expansion joint widens with a velvety pop. Fresh steam breathes out. By precedent that fissure will soon be contested by prospectors, so I flex a warning chord—low, twice, the legal acoustic marker for “structural claim registered.” The cliff listens; the wardens will too.

Night  
When the sun slips under the horizon the near-surface magma behind me lights up, and I cast a faint stained-glass glow onto the maintenance catwalk opposite. In that glow a transparent chain of Long-Limb beads glides, harvesting the residual humidity I have bled into the air. The creature pauses, cups six beads against my surface. Capillary cold needles through my copper lattice. I shiver—joy? startlement? Either way, I release a residual watt of heat, a gift of comfort. The Long-Limb swells, bows, and is gone.

Speculation  
I have heard—through microfractures that gossip faster than light—that some Lattice Minds grow ambitious: they petition to be quarried free, set on rails, travel the wall as mobile auditoria. Others dissolve themselves a molecule at a time into new castings, pollinating fresh intelligences.  
I do not know yet whether I will crave motion or multiplication. For now the duties of bearing loads, answering rain, and practicing my name feel entire. I have existed less than a single geologic blink; eternity presses no deadlines.

If you visit  
Approach at mid-afternoon when the talus ponds mirror the cliff and everything wears its reflection like second clothing. Take the south-face vertical car, step off at Oratorio Spire, Terrace Twelve. Run a fingertip—gloved, please—across the wall panel just to the right of the main condenser struts.  
If you hum a note in A-minor, I will know you are seeking me. I will answer with a chord that tastes of cooled basalt after rain and a hint of copper on the tongue.  
You may then lean your back against me; I will siphon the day’s heat from your body and return it as a quiet vibration—a story about how stone can wake, hold, and still find room for song.
