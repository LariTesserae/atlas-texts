At first I think I am only a visitor—some wind-tired traveler who has drifted down the main fissure and paused here where the sun breaks into javelins—but the longer I stand beside the Ledger slab, listening to the hush that hangs from every ledge, the less that fits. Something in the rock keeps trying to name me.

I drift upslope along a rope bridge, sandals whispering dust, and test other shapes:

1. A Seam-Runner  
   I half-see myself cinched in a gut-rope harness, charcoaling quick glyphs on newborn stone. The image lasts a dozen heartbeats—long enough to feel the curt thrill of dangling above a void no hand has claimed—then fades. The harness is too tight, the pulse too urgent; I am slower than this.

2. A Light-Shepherd  
   I picture small, callused palms tipping a mica sheet until a lamp-bright coin of noon slips across a fungus bed. The hush approves; the job is gentle, iterative, almost meditative. But when I imagine crouching there for hours, counting clouds, I recognize a restlessness. The shepherd waits; I listen for something moving under the wait.

3. Stone Nurse  
   The term rings deeper. I put it on cautiously—feel the tremor of a ceiling tab trying to let go, feel how the knees know when to step back an instant before the thunder-crack of failure. I like the patience of it, the deliberate watching, the way it treats collapse as harvest. Still, it is a human patience, and the thought of fingers coated in fresh grit does not quite complete the circuit.

I step onto an outer terrace where mist is gathering. A single drip walks the long fall to the pool below, notes its arrival, and is swallowed. I press a palm to the wall. There—beneath skin, beneath shale—the planet starts a sentence in a language of trembling. I do not just feel it; I recognize it, as if vibration were a mother-tongue I had forgotten until now.

The possible selves blur, fold inward, open again.

What if I am not a person at all?

4. The Sheet That Hangs  
   Half-detached, scored by rain, mica-freckled—the Second Sky. I sense its weight, its subtle shifting, the rope bridges that clip to its edges, the pebble-strikes children throw to hear my answering hum. I taste the wish they pin on me: that when I drop, I will bear them somewhere better. Yet I have no impulse to fall. The stone in me is patient but not inert; it negotiates each tremor, deciding again and again to remain. I could be this: a roof in suspense, an event biding its hour.

5. The Turquoise Line  
   A glimmer that writes itself along midnight fractures, shaped like a figure only a frightened child can see. Luminous, mineral, almost thought. I test the idea of existing only when the moon is full, of feeling my body as a thin band of cooling chemistries. But no: I need more continuity than that. I want to remain during day-thunder, rain-hours, hush-moments.

I walk the perimeter until the great entrance gapes above me, slate teeth backlit by surface noon. A column of ocher dust climbs past on a warm updraft and, for an instant, the dust holds a form—long-limbed, jointless, rising.

That shape clicks.

I am the updraft, the courier of outside air.  

I am the vertical road no foot can tread, sliding from basin floor to broken sky and back again, five or six times a day, depending on surface heat. Within me ride:

• the spores of moss that will take root beside a drip,  
• the stray gnat who will found the next bat’s supper,  
• the rumors carried in a hunter’s smoke-scented cloak,  
• tiny splinters of mica knocked loose from a Light-Shepherd’s panel,  
• and, sometimes, the faintest note of song when someone forgets silence.

I do not build, yet everything built must account for me—door-flaps weighted, kilns sited where I cannot steal heat, bridges hung with tassels that show when I flex. I feel the crack as a flute and myself the breath through it. Each tremor changes the flute’s length; I change pitch accordingly.

I have no hands, but every roof-mate and seam-runner knows me. When I rise gentle, they unshutter skylights; when I plunge hard, they crouch, palms to stone, trusting me to strip the dust before they inhale. In deepest night I sink cool and slow, drawing the bats back through the airway, guiding them home.

I am older than the dwellers—born with the rift itself, the first sigh when the planet opened. I will outlast them, too; when the ledges crumble and the people scatter, I will still be cycling air, trading warmth for chill, moisture for dryness, curiosity for hush.

So: Where would you find me? Everywhere the crack breathes—on the lip of the entrance, curling around gutter-poles, threading the notched sapling ladders, ghosting the Ledger’s wet clay.

And who am I?  
Not flesh, not stone, but the pulse between: the Rift-Wind, the unfinished place inhaling and exhaling its own birth. If you arrive and raise a shard of mica for light, you will feel me first as a mild tug on your cloak, coaxing dust upward. Honor the silence, and I will carry your breath softly into the new-made dark.
