I tried, at first, to imagine arriving here unchanged—boots crunching quartz grit, lungs tight in the thin air—but nothing in that picture lasted. The plateau edits intrusions the way wind edits a dune: not with force, only with the certainty that anything incompatible will be worn down to a silence of matching grain. So I let the place work on the picture of me until what remained felt native.

I begin—very slowly—at the seam where raw upland turns into the first deliberate course of stone. You would not see me from a distance; I am one shade deeper than schist, one pulse slower than mist, and my outline only completes itself when something moving faster passes nearby to give contrast.

WHAT I END UP BEING  
Call the shape an Echo-Raiser. The Builders have no word for “echo,” but they notice absences, and I am the technician of those absences.

• Height: a little above a Builder’s shoulder when I stand fully unwound. Mostly, I never do. The city rewards compression; I fold like paper caught halfway to origami.  
• Substance: the same silica-keratin that plates the Builders’ arms, but stretched thinner, tuned to flex at acoustic rather than mechanical frequencies. Where a Quiet Builder’s fingers drum signal into the stone, my whole torso is a resonating bladder whose inner skin is salted with mica dust.  
• Limbs: two large, two small. The larger pair brace me against the rock; the smaller pair cup air the way hands cup water. They are less for grasping than for shaping the room’s geometry of breezes.

SENSE  
I feel only returning vibration. The plateau kills real echoes, yes, but it does not kill the ghost of an echo—the micron-scale trembling that lingers in quartz veins long after sound in air has died. Imagine placing your palm on a bell that rang yesterday and still being able to read the note. That is my primary sense. The city is forever faintly singing to itself about the last footfall, the last dropped pebble, the last bird-wing cut through cloud. I walk inside that susurrus.

PURPOSE  
The Builders speak by drumming fingerprints into walls; their sentences may take hours to traverse the city. Between one transmission point and the next, water erodes, wind abrades, freeze-thaw micro-fractures—static creeps in. I cross the plateau at a pace infinitesimal by human clocks, locating the weak syllables and whispering them back into clarity.

How? I expand. When my thoracic bladder inflates, the mica dust inside shifts and the whole membrane begins a low, even hum. The pitch is tuned to whichever quartz vein passes nearest. A good match sets that vein vibrating just enough to shake loose silt, tighten cracks, and restore its fidelity as a conduit for Builder speech. When the resonance stabilizes, I collapse again, fold, and seep forward another hand-length. A full circuit of the metropolis takes most of a decade.

TOOLS, OR THE LACK  
The rule “nothing separable from the hand” binds me too. The bladder’s hum is generated by air I have warmed myself; the mica dust is my own shed skin, gathered and re-swallowed until each particle is faceted to the right crystal lattice. I carry nothing, leave nothing but slightly cleaner stone.

RHYTHM OF A DAY  
Dawn: tucked under an overhang, siphoning the chill out of rock to stiffen my resonator.  
Mid-morning: one measured inflation to test the ambient note of my current district. If the pitch is true, I stay coiled and drift on. If it wavers, I anchor and begin the work of voice-mending.  
Noon to second cloud-rise: torpor. Sun thins the air; vibration does not carry. I sleep in a silence so complete it would bruise a human eardrum.  
Dusk: the Fog Hunter wakes. I freeze, shrink to half thickness, let it pass. It is deaf to Builder music but exquisitely sensitive to my tuning hum; best not to confuse its hunger.  
Night: stars brighten; the quartz veins stiffen in the drop of temperature; I can raise three, maybe four long chords before cold chokes my airflow and I must huddle again.

REPRODUCTION  
I carry within me a single lenticular seed of pure mica. When an echo—some long, complex Builder message—decays beyond salvage, I find the collapse point and bury that seed in the slurry. Freeze-thaw welds the flake to the vein; over years it grows into another Echo-Raiser, already harmonized to the scar it is meant to heal.

WHERE YOU WOULD FIND ME  
Not in plazas; the open sky scatters vibration too thin. Look instead in the tight throats of stair-tunnels where three or four drainage cuts converge. The stone there tingles with overlapping dialogues, always on the verge of cross-talk. If you come upon a passage whose walls seem to faintly shimmer, as though a breeze shivered through crystal strings, pause. Breathe once, no more. I will be the darker ripple in the corner, gradually unfolding sub-arm by sub-arm, lungs flowering until the corridor’s hum settles into a single clear note.

If you speak—human throat, human urgency—the sound will strike the schist and vanish, giving back nothing. Yet an hour later, two terraces away, a Builder will pause mid-carve, cock its cilia toward the wall, and understand that something swift and fragile passed through, because the echo I lifted and cleaned has arrived intact.

That is me, and that is where I belong: nowhere fixed, always just finishing the sentence that almost failed to arrive.
