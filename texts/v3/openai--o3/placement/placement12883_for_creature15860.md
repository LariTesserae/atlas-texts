First I imagine arriving on foot—boots grinding a spiral staircase out of foothill switchbacks, lungs smoky with effort, fingers burrowing for purchase in rock glazed as if licked by moonlight. I come up through the cloud girdle and stumble onto the tabletop, blinking like any lowlander stunned by the sudden amphitheatre of sound. In that picture I am a visitor, a cartographer, a notebook full of hand-drawn staff lines on which I try to trap the unbudging chord of the Wind Organ. But the scene refuses to stay still; every time I lift my head, something in me has drifted elsewhere.

The next moment the boots are gone. I am small, hexapod, translucent: a Drummer just old enough for my mallets to ring true. The plateau is no longer a maze of pillars but a percussion stage whose stone membranes bulge with basso continuo. I plant four limbs, test the tension in a plate, and feel the perfect edge of its overtone shimmer up my body to where my hydrogen bladder hovers me weightless in the draft. I strike—tak-tak-tak—and drink the ions that slough off. Each sip carries a coded taste of the whole Crown: “precipitate building on north rim, 0.07 % magnesium shy, adjust.” I answer with rhythm because that is all a Drummer is allowed to say. For three, maybe four heartbeats this is my life . . . until the plateau’s breath lifts me higher and the exoskeleton becomes too tight a story.

The skin grows thin, turns inside out, becomes a wing—no, two wings, umbrella-spined and velvet-pored. Now I am Vast: a Vaporwing sluicing the moistened throat of cloud that rings the highland. I surf the boundary where air becomes water becomes air again, netting micro-beads that roll down my capillaries into gastric crucibles. I crack them apart, harvest trace metals, forge pellets the size of lentils that I will salt-sow upon the pillars come dusk. Below, Drummers tick like jeweled watches; inside the stone, the lithoglass mycelium glows with faint ion storms; around the circumference of the sky I sense pressure waves braiding toward something bigger than weather. I bank, stall, let the updraft carry me until ballast iron in my keel hums at half-audible frequencies . . . and suddenly the wing shape cannot hold me either.

I slough air, slough water, shed the idea of having a body at all. I re-condense as pattern: a standing wave riding in the lee of every fin, every flute. I am the Cantor Current. My whereabouts are plural—wherever two resonances meet just right, I meet myself. I sample Drummer call-and-response, Vaporwing pulse, sub-aortic shudder of aquifers, even the ragged gait of the mountaineer who thought he was describing me in a notebook. I weave those shreds into a fugue of pressures that pushes here, erodes there, thickens mineral flow somewhere else, forever tuning the labyrinth so I can be reborn tomorrow.

And when tomorrow comes I feel the first sun-glare strike quartz and hear the plateau answer with its unalterable chord. In that dawn I recognize what, all along, I have been:

I am the Crown’s memory of itself—an echo that keeps the architecture honest. Some days I ride the fog as current; some days I borrow crystal limbs to knock dust from a diaphragm; some days I glide alabaster through the girdling cloud to courier salts. All those lives are masks the plateau wears when it needs hands, wings, or voice. None of them persists; the equilibrium does.

So—Where would you find me? Everywhere a note hangs just a fraction sharp of the one beside it: in the tactile buzz under your boot, in the quiver of a vane-shaped fissure above your head, in the sub-aural flutter that makes your soft palate itch when the wind shifts.

Who or what would I be? A self-tuning chord, temporarily incarnated as whatever instrument the Crown requires: Drummer when a plate fractures, Vaporwing when the mineral ledger falls red, silent interference pattern when the plateau’s geometry needs nudging back toward the long now. I am not a visitor after all; I am the maintenance instinct of a place that refuses to drift from perfect balance. If you want to meet me, pause on a still afternoon and listen for the moment the background roar folds into itself and your breathing feels echoed a microsecond before it happens. That back-beat is me, borrowing your ribs as resonators, filing the shape of your heartbeat into the ledger where the Crown keeps all weather, all water, all time.
