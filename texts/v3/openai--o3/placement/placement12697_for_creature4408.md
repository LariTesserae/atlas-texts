I wake knee-deep, always knee-deep.  That is the natural height of a Pai’cho floor: just enough water to cradle the calves, just enough resistance to remind the blood of the basin’s slow pulse.  My basket-house hangs from three vine-engines that I bribed years ago with salt shards and lullaby drumming—tap-tap-pause, tap-tap-pause—so they keep the plaited hull steady while I sleep.  

Name  
By the tally of my deeds I am Eilaho-Still-Third-Current, but children shorten that to Laho, and visiting quarryfolk—hurrying, loud, half deaf from vent-thunder—turn it into a grunt that sounds like a throat clearing.  The name will lengthen again only if I perform something the elders deem story-worthy; I am in no hurry.  A short name rides the tongue lightly.

Calling  
I listen.  Not in the casual sense of overhearing but as a craft.  When my ribs were still green cartilage the elders steeped me on boiled bladder-fern tea until the bones set buoyant; since then I can float motionless for a hundred contiguous heartbeats with lungs completely emptied.  In that feather-weight quiet I hear the massif think.  A distant vent opening is a bassoon note.  A minor rock-calving under the rim is a cracked bell.  These vibrations arrive through sole, shin, hip, spine, skull, and at last make pictures in the dark behind my eyelids.  I am a cartographer of things that have not yet happened.

Dwelling  
Picture a hollow reed sphere the diameter of two outstretched arms.  Half of it is pumice, half plaited vine; the join is where the waterline kisses.  Inside, three stone mirrors tilt to steal light from whatever weak shaft the clouds permit.  The mirrors are gifts from drip-foots—payment for returning one of their glossy mid-clutch treasures that some rim child had pilfered.  At night the mirrors catch glow-moss and scatter constellations across the ceiling so I feel as though I sleep inside a child’s cup of veilcyan.

Day’s Work  
At first pale, pre-dawn hush, I paddle to the Still Bearing—the centre of the crater where even the glass-wing rays hesitate to break water.  There I invert myself, exhale, and let the warm clarity swallow me.  Below, spiral eels coil like ivory signatures against the vents.  I listen for the newest, thinnest note: a tremor that means the massif is about to shrug and raise the rim another hairbreadth.  Marking that sound is my ledger and my scripture.  When I surface, I knot the report into Current-rope.  No one writes; the braid itself remembers: over-one for a vent birth, under-two for a vent death, swallow-knot for a flank slip.  By evening the rope speaks in tension read through the fingertips of every household leader.

Evening  
Dusk here is never color; it is temperature and density.  When the air grows so wet you can taste basalt on your tongue, I moor at the communal hall—the leaning ring of half-sunk pillars—and trade news for wing-fat cheese and fireless tea.  The children beg me for the day’s “sky-rumor,” so I pantomime what I heard: maybe the far western terrace hissed up a newborn smoke thread, maybe the eels began humming in a chord that hints we should ready spare moorings.  The elders listen, lips pursed, assigning bearings to births no one has yet seen.

Kin & Current  
I belong to Third Current, five households knotted side by side.  Once a year, as doctrine demands, we unlace and drift while the origin tale is told.  Everyone asks the Listener—that is, me—where we should retie.  I pretend to deliberate, but the pattern is simple: place the quietest sleepers where the basalt is thin, because thin basalt sings loudly and will wake them before it breaks.  They think this wisdom; it is only courtesy.

Hopes  
I have a project of private devotion: teaching the glass-wing rays a steadier story than flicker-speech allows.  Each dawn I carry a slate of mother-of-pearl and hover it beneath the surface.  When a ray glides over, its wing darkens in startlement, mirroring the pearly glyph.  I change the glyph each day—simple shapes first, then sequences.  After forty mornings one adolescent ray repeated yesterday’s pattern unprompted.  A child of my Current saw and squealed, splashing the water to silver and frightening the ray away.  No matter.  Tomorrow is patient.

Where You Would Meet Me  
Find the caldera’s hush.  Wait until cloudlets dim their ultraviolet rumor and the sea turns to black glass.  Somewhere between upflow and downfall, a single reed basket will glow with borrowed moss-light, rocking scarcely a finger’s breadth.  Knock once with the knuckle, once with the wrist—rhythm of a cooling vent.  I will open the water-door, testing your silhouette against the milk-gray night.  If you carry silence as a lamp, you may come inside.  I will pour warm algaebeer that tastes of summer rain on still lava.  We will trade stories that cannot yet be written and therefore stay alive by being spoken.  When words grow thin, we will simply listen—bones floating, hearts aligning to the pulse of a mountain still deciding what shape it wishes to take.

That is who I am here:  
Ear of the newborn rock,  
scribe without ink,  
anchorage that migrates,  
a small, breathing vowel in the everlasting hush.
