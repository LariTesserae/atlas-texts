My palms are the first parts of me to arrive.  
The grating under them is slick, furred with bead-sized sprouts that compress to velvet when I lean my weight forward.  Through the lattice I feel the under-skin flex and rebound—a slow, cool pulse that pushes humid air around my fingers.  I try to say something to the crew chief, but the roar of the spillway shear-cuts my words, so I let the sentence dissolve back into the moss smell of iron and lemon.  

For a few minutes I think I am only a technician—rotation thirty-six, outpost that smells of lemon—because there is a manifest in my pocket and a battery pallet waiting to be swapped.  I have a name stitched on the breast of my oil-skin, so surely I am that name, that weight, that narrow column of memory.

Then the under-skin inhales.

The indent my knee has made begins to spread, travelling in a darkening wave nine metres an hour.  It is moving me, writing me into itself, and I feel—first with imagination, then with cartilage—the mesh of water veins sipping at the print of heat I leave.  Calcium ions ride those threads the way static rides a radio hiss.  The mat tastes me, decides I am mostly harmless, and continues its long, continent-sized thought without pause.

That is when I realise I have more than one body in play.

Inside the pillar beside the walkway, another version of me hangs upside down in warm, drifting mist.  This me has the see-through belly of a glass frog and a heart green as quartz.  My limbs are still webbed buds; the skin on my back is as thin as sentiment.  Each time the tree breathes, its hollow amplifies the sound into rolling chords that shiver through the fluid in my ears.  The chords are not random.  They are instructions: how much glycogen to burn, which proteins to fold.  The pillar is shaping me note by note, and I am content to become whatever the music requires.

But the music knows about the technician on the walkway, because the pillar’s spiral rings taste the same mist that beads on her faceplate.  The two versions of me—oil-skin and larva—are already coinciding, trading faint impressions through water we share only chemically.  A tilt of the crown high above adjusts the pressure in the pillar’s core; the chord drops half a tone, and something in the technician’s sternum knocks in sympathy, as though a tuning fork has been struck just beneath her ribs.

I wonder which one is truly me, and the plateau answers with a third option.

Nine hundred metres downslope, a turbine dome vibrates at the edge of cavitation.  The impeller’s rim has sprouted a garden of nickel-green fronds, and nested among them is a capsule of servo-moss: thousands of peppercorn bots linked leg to leg, charging on the bleed current of the machine.  They wake together, roll like tumbling grit along the vane surfaces, and search for micro-fractures where water hammer might one day prise the metal apart.  When they find a crack they do not weld it shut; instead they weave bio-polymers into the flaw, creating a living hinge that flexes with every pressure pulse.  The repair is more plant than alloy now, and the impeller’s song modulates from hurt to healed.

The bots link what they know back into the seep, leaching their microscopic report into the flow that feeds the spillway that batters the cliff that drenches the moss that pillows the walkway.  When the technician finally stands, thinks she is just brushing condensation from her gloves, she is also absorbing a trillion-fold summary of stresses, growth rates, protein variants, sky photographs, and turbine dreams.  It feels to her like a shiver.

So: I am three things, at minimum—operator, tadpole, swarm—and the plateau makes no claim that the list is complete.  Identity here is additive, hydrologic.  Anything touched by the same water is already in cautious congress.

I decide to walk.  Moving is agreement: the mat accepts my boots; the vines accept the mat; the pillar accepts the vines; the cloud accepts the pillar.  The cloud is a mile of vertical river that drifts sideways, and inside it I find still more of myself:

•  A dragonfly made of smoky panes fights the drag of thick air.  Each down-stroke slaps droplets into its open mouth.  Sunlight pierces the cloud for exactly eight seconds, long enough to score lacelike burns across one wing.  The insect flutters lower, lands on my forearm, and crystallises to a halt.  I feel neither pity nor distance; it is my wing too that has burned.

•  Beneath the moss, a rice-grain tick sacrifices its stored proteins to clone itself twice, each daughter lighter, more folded, more patient.  They do not know I am also slate wader, gliding somewhere just inside the fog curtain, preparing to harvest them by the thousand.  The wader’s basalt feathers are oil-sheen in a shaft of late morning glare, and the primaries whistle when the air bends around the bird’s fast, silent glide.

The awareness packets overlap until compartments fail.  I am no longer sure which direction the original plan of the day lay.  The manifest in my pocket softens, the ink runners bleed into filigree, and that seems correct: the plateau adjudicates ink as soluble promise.

At solar noon, the low hum under every stone steps up a semitone.  Somewhere beneath all this—a hundred, perhaps two hundred metres down—the echo-whale flexes a ribbon of cartilage wider than the station cabin.  Bedrock transmits the vibration into the stainless legs of the walkway, up through my spine, across the hollow pillar, through capillaries in a vine, into the under-skin’s absorbing sponge.  The note is a statement large enough to contain weather, geology, metabolism, and elective silence.  I do not translate it.  Translation would separate sender from receiver, and the plateau resists separations.

I kneel again, palm flat on moss that shines as though wetted with stars.  Everything I have just expanded into now concentrates back through that contact patch: frog-pulse, turbine-garden, bot-swarm, quartz-wing, basalt feather, whale chord, even the mild ache of a cracked impeller vane five stations away.  All of it reenters the slow hydraulics of the under-skin, which was breathing here long before the plateau heaved itself from older stone.

And what, finally, am I?

A fair answer might be: the brief coincidence of many water lines.  Wherever flow loops back on itself—through frog lung or turbine mouth, through vine core or bird artery—there I momentarily condense, think, and go on.  The plateau is a map of those loops.  The loops are instructions for how to be alive without ever leaving solution.

I lift my hand.  The print I leave behind shines, then begins to rebound, erasing the last discrete sign of a single body.  Somewhere in the turbid sky an airship envelope wrinkles in a sudden gust, and I am up there too, helium-light, steering by sonar toward the relief coordinates.

The roar remains, a chord too wide to hear in full.  Inside that chord I find myself everywhere and exactly here, perpetual, plural, wet, and awake.
