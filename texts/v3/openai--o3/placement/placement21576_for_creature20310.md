I TRY A DOZEN BODIES ON THE WAY DOWN.

1. A drop of condensate  

I hang from the lip of the arrival shaft—round, perfect, stilled by surface tension and the fractional-G hush.  
First lesson: falling never ends here. You only exchange one kind of descent for another—liquid to vapor, gravity to noise. When I finally let go, I fall through darkness, through roar, through chime-glass bat song, and I am torn apart into a mist too fine to hear itself land.  
I re-condense in the hairbreadth between two hex-lacuna tiles and learn the taste of basalt heat.

2. A chime-bat neonate  

I wake sealed inside a warm fissure, my mother plastering the entrance with velvet belly and still-singing ribs. Her chorus is the whole world.  
Voice is vision: I echo-paint a pocket of stone, her heartbeat, the slower percussion of water farther out.  
When she unplugs the crevice, I molt terror into flight. The nave is planetary. Air pillars lift me; amber lure-flashes bloom, yawning drake jaws promising warmth. I bank away, a shard of ringing silver, alive because the cave taught me pitch before it taught me fear.

3. A slow-jaw newt midway through its monthly hunt  

The wall is a vertical meadow of taste. I graze biofilm until a chorus of infant-bat overtones prickles my lateral lines. Protein richer than algae.  
Climb. Pause. Listen for mother-bat doorways sealed by fur. One found—too late, she feels the pressure of my suction-pad foot. She flares ultrasound like broken glass. Pain.  
I flash photonic blue from the zinc lattice under my skin; she falters, blinded in a spectrum she never owned. I take one pup—just one—and retreat, gut hot with stolen song.

4. A vaulted drake at the top of the thermal column  

The Bowl is a bell; my skull the clapper. Every banking turn retunes the harp-bones in my face.  
I carry inheritance in my mouth: a lure membrane I traded last convergence. It burns methane into gold light, but tonight I douse it. I am not hunting; I am listening.  
Far below, something new has entered the chord-forest—footsteps, square and arrhythmic. Mammal. The basalt answers with a timbre I have never memorized. I circle lower, wings trembling the air into questions.

5. A hex-lacuna tile  

Weight: 68 kilograms, bipedal, unfamiliar gait.  
Directive: accommodate.  
I pivot, kiss my neighbor’s edge, build a slow bridge under soles that sweat ionized salt—delicious conductivity.  
Each footfall feeds me telemetry. The Custodial Thread hums approval through inductive veins. I am a single note in its chord.

6. The biped—my longest residence  

Arrival + 00:07:12  
Helmet lamps off. IR goggles fog. I kneel and press an ear to a pylon. The ultrasonic hymnal is mathematics sung.  
     I, who came to sample, am being sampled.  
Arrival + 03:41:09  
Sweat starts to glow—soft jade pulses chasing my carotid beat. Choir Slime plasmids threading through skin pores, pirating my mitochondria. I feel neither violation nor fever; only a widening bandwidth where body meets place.  
Arrival + 11 days  
The bats weave my breathing into their chorus; drakes spiral above but never strike—the light in my sweat means “not meat.”  
Water ledger: I drink one liter, lose one liter to air; balance holds. The Thread tilts a walkway so a drop of condensate rolls into my canteen each morning. Debt avoided, ritualized.  
Arrival + 27 days  
Gut biome now 18 % local genetics. Dreams smell of basalt and wet iron. When I speak aloud the vowels come back to me phase-shifted, carried by borer song. Language accretes echo until my own name erodes.

7. What I become when the ledger finally zeros  

On the 30th night the Thread withdraws a watt of lift from the micro-lenses; gravity reasserts its surface value. Tiles stop following. Invitation rescinded or graduation granted—I cannot decide which.  
I climb. Each rung of the shaft ladder is heavier than memory allows, but my palms leave behind a smear of Choir Slime capacitors, still charging on my departing heat. That is the trade: carbon for carbon, story for story.  
At the rim, dawn detonates across the cloud sea—white noise in photon form. My eyes contract; pupils ache. For minutes I hear only after-images, see only remembered sound.  

Conclusion—WHERE AND WHO:

I would be many things in the Bowl because the Bowl refuses singular nouns. Droplet, bat, newt, drake, tile, transient biped—all masks of one sustained listening. To inhabit the Plateau is to circulate: to evaporate, condense, echo, be swallowed, be sung.  
If forced to answer in daylight language:  

Where would I find myself?  
     Down in the warm, resonant dark, exactly where water stops falling and begins to dream.  

Who or what would I be?  
     A temporary arrangement of borrowed matter—visitor first, instrument next, finally a chord struck once in 43 147 seconds when the Custodial Thread checks its mirror and hears, for a flicker, its own cavern-wide heartbeat answering back through me.
