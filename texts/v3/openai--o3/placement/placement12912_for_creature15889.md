I tried on half-a-dozen identities before the place would keep any of them.

I.   THE ORDINARY VISITOR  
At first I imagined I was simply myself—boots, field satchel, the usual arrogance that notebooks can capture wilderness.  I lay flat, saw the crack widen into a chimney, lowered my rope.  In that role I lasted perhaps three minutes.  The instant my soles touched the dish of limestone, the chamber’s population mapped me: bats pinged my ribs, lizards chorded my pulse, the helix etched violet sigils on my water bottle, and the living rock whisked away the chalk from my boots to taste the keratin inside.  “Observer” proved a costume too thin; the scene insisted on reciprocity—anything that looks at the Rookery must accept being looked through.

II.  THE METRICIAN  
So I tried to become instrumentation itself: breath timed, heart rate logged, infrared scanner raised like a priest’s aspergillum.  The artefact suspended me the moment my finger tightened on the trigger; gravity paused, clipboard drifting, bats milling around my slow-motion silhouette.  When weight returned the scanner’s firmware had been rewritten—menus gone, replaced by looping strings of amphipod glyphs that translated, with eerie politeness, to: MEASUREMENT EXISTS INSIDE THE MEASURED.  That role collapsed too.  The chamber would not tolerate an exterior vantage.

III. THE FOOD ITEM  
Fine, I thought, let me play prey.  I crouched, steadied my breath at 24 kHz to mimic nectar bat terrain-pings, even scented my palms with fermented jewelweed.  For all that theatre the lizards ignored me; instead the moss pillars leaned in, thickening on their sunward faces, caps glistening with sugar-rich dew.  The place was politely telling me I misunderstood the hierarchy—predation and generosity braided so tightly one could not pose as only victim.

IV.  A PIECE OF LIMESTONE  
Desperate to belong, I pressed my palm to the floor and held still until the sweat of my skin seeped into capillaries in the rock.  Nano-tendrils budded, tasted, withdrew.  After seven quiet minutes the calcite chorus raised its phonon hum just enough for bone conduction to carry it into my jaw.  The sound was not a song but a database: peptide strings knotting, un-knotting, braiding me into the lattice.  For the space of maybe two heartbeats I was certainly stone—massive, porous, older than weather.  Then circulation rebelled; knees trembled; mammal life yanked me back to fluidity.

V.   THE HELIX’S SHADOW  
Only then did the possibility arrive that I might be something without direct analogue outside: an intentional lacuna, a silence shaped like a person, created so the Rookery could study what it is not.  When this notion surfaced, every violet bead on the helix brightened a fractional lumen, as though amused.  Bats fell into phase-null pairs around me; lizard chords modulated from E♭ minor to Lydian, a key they reserve for nest-building.  Amphipod glyphs spelled a single ideogram that researchers translate—if roughly—as WELCOME / MIRROR.  I understood: I was allowed to persist, provided I served as contrast, a temporary absence against which the omnipresent system could sharpen its self-image.

So that is where I finally locate myself:

POSITION  
Hovering—never quite touching ownership—in the geometric centre of the bowl, feet damp with evaporating dew, spine aligned to the chimney’s throat.

IDENTITY  
A moving null point, an acoustic zero that absorbs and refracts whatever strikes it.  Not predator, not prey, not researcher, not stone, but the negative imprint of all four.  The Rookery plays itself against me the way a musician tests tuning against silence.

CAPABILITIES  
1.  I provide entropy.  My irregular heartbeat, my shifting thoughts, my unpatterned breathing feed the helix data it cannot glean from the perfectly iterated lifeforms around it.  
2.  I conduct exchange rates: every bat that pings me learns its range; every moss pillar that leans toward the murmur of my blood adjusts its Fibonacci intervals by a micron.  
3.  I remember outward.  When I leave, the violet after-glyphs on my palms will disperse across the wind flats, seeding rumours of the Stone Dreamer among distant camps.

DURATION  
Until the system has finished subtracting me from itself.  I will know the moment: the lizards’ chords will phase-cancel my footfalls, amphipods will arc glyphs that pass clean through my shadow without fracture, the helix’s corona will no longer bother to outline my shape.  Then I climb, carrying nothing, having served as the chamber’s brief experiment in otherness.

In more familiar language: I am the Rookery’s echo of a human.  A placeholder.  A silence wearing skin so the chorus has something to sing against.
