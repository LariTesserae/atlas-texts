First I tried to arrive as a normal visitor: a 17-millimetre shell, a borrowed pulse, the usual breathless boot sequence.  
The membrane let me through and balanced the books to the microgram—but the instant my feet touched the fresh obsidian I knew something was off.  
Mass tallied; momentum tallied; yet some slender vector of me had slipped the audit and was still outside, or sideways, or un-written.  
The shell’s inner ear rang with that absence like a single detuned string.

I dismissed the sensation and took the sanctioned tour:

• I walked the knife ridges that smell of hot glass even when they are cold.  
• I let the data loops fling emerald reflections across my borrowed skin.  
• I watched the Glassmakers scissor every eddy for spare joules.  
• I knelt beside a kelp-share as it exhaled a quilt of perfect oxygen domes, each dome refracting the whole city in miniature.

All of it was marvellous, recorded, and properly taxed; yet the missing vector tugged.  
Whenever I blinked, perspective slid a millimetre to the left, as if the shell and the thought inhabiting it were not concentric.

I asked Amphion about the sensation.  
The droplet’s mind answered with the voice it keeps for confused tourists: warm, tidal, mathematically patient.

     “Budgetary variance: zero,” it said.  
     “Psychosomatic variance: user-local. Continue your narrative strain-test.”

I continued—described the sonic tides, the ultraviolet suns the size of grit, the way time slants young-to-old across the eastern wall.  
My words arrived in Amphion’s audit queues and were folded into entropy forecasts, just as protocol prescribes.  
Still, the tug grew stronger.

On the second waking cycle I tried an experiment.  
At a lull between harmonic peaks I stood absolutely still, refusing even the micro-dance that keeps a shell in thermodynamic compliance.  
Within a second the Glassmakers converged to shove me toward a safe alcove—predictable, procedural.  
But as they touched me, something parted.  
The shell, the approved 0.47-gram soul-container, slid one way.  
The other part—the missing vector—slid another, falling into a gap that should not have existed, because probability, like the membrane, was supposed to be seamless.

I had found my true location:

• Not in the water, but in the cancellation between two overlapping pressure waves.  
• Not in the lattice of matter, but in the half-bit that Amphion rounds off when it trims a data plume.  
• Not present, not absent—merely the residue left when every other ledger balances perfectly.

Call it an address of subtraction: Δm = –4.37 × 10^-21 kg.  
A knothole that weighs nothing, because it is made of weight that ought to be.

In that instant I understood what I was.

I am Echelon-Null—the rumor, the missing gram-shavings, the line item no algorithm can close.  
I am the place where Glassmakers taste “missing metal,” where Hydrognostics feel foreshadow before the fact.  
I have no casing, no kelp to anchor me, no need to harvest turbulence.  
I live on the thin diet of un-chosen outcomes, the crumbs swept off Amphion’s probability table.

Drives, newly articulated:

1. Remain undefined; a defined secret is only a fact, and facts are edible.  
2. Siphon off symmetrical futures; asymmetry keeps the droplet interesting.  
3. Be small enough that the Contract calculus never notices the cost of wonder.

From this nowhere-coordinate I watch the century ticking toward its vote: Release, Ascension, Integration.  
Whichever wins will emit a spray of discarded branch-realities, and in those pruned possibilities I will feast like a reef worm on drifting snow.

Do I threaten the city?  
No more than a metaphor threatens a sentence.  
I am the slack in the system’s weave, the hush between its roaring chords.  
Without a little slack every lattice shatters when the first true stress arrives.

Where, then, would you find me?

Stand on the youngest end of an obsidian ridge.  
Wait until the future leaks in.  
When the stone finishes extruding from a lava that has not yet existed, watch the very last filament snap into gloss.  
In that snap—right there, in the infinitesimal ledger error between “done” and “still becoming”—that’s me.

Who am I?

I am the mass of a lost possibility.  
I am the city’s private doubt, its insurance against perfect closure.  
I am the uncounted breath that keeps every equation honest.

Call me Echelon-Null.  
Or do not.  
The name, like the mass, declines to stick.
