I am not a visitor here. I am the place briefly learning to stand and move.

Call me a sapwalker—one of the tall, joint-segmented couriers the continent buds when ion traffic crawls too slowly through copper phloem. My whole existence is a single sentence in the land’s long conversation, but inside that sentence I contain a journey, a body, and the faint outline of a self.

1. Budding

I begin as urgency in a knot-brain half a kilometre beneath the Shard Marshes. Two neighbouring districts disagree about manganese allotments; their enzyme locks have reached stalemate. The knot weighs options, tests pressures, then chooses velocity. Osmotic pumps reverse. Cellulose walls spiral into columns, plates knit into joints, and I am extruded like a thought in three dimensions. My marrow is hollow; gas cultured from dissolved limestone rushes in and inflates me until my roots tear free of the mat. I sway once—tasting humidity, gravity—and start walking.

2. Physiology of Motion

Picture a tripod whose legs are sleeves of negative-pressure bladders. When a leg deflates, the vacuum inside shortens it; when it drinks air, it lengthens again. By rotating the sequence—left, right, rear—I lurch forward in steps only the forest notices. Each stride is a hush, a liquid pop, a hush. My skin is slick with condensate that wicks back through micro-pores to keep my carbon budgets solvent. Above the knee my trunk is veined with piezoelectric ribbons: every flex harvests the tremor of my own motion to keep the furnace of enzyme logic simmering in my core.

3. Sensoria

Eyes are extravagant; I borrow other organs instead.

• Vibration: Root hairs still dangling from my torn pedicle drag across the mat, translating every subsurface pulse into electrical microgloss in my cortex. I know where sap rivers run deepest.  
• Magnetism: The ring of magnetite beads stitched under my bark tells me which way the great dynamos torque, and therefore which way nutrient credit may soon flow.  
• Chemistry: Stoma along my forward face flare like nostrils, tasting the exhale of every trunk I pass: excess copper means foundry work nearby; a spike of ethylene hints at senescence—danger of collapse.

Light is almost irrelevant. The dim green dusk is enough to keep my outermost chloroplasts attentive, but I navigate mostly by gradients of taste and pressure.

4. The Journey

Day 1: Through the infant razor-ridges. Here the root mat is so thin the continental pulse makes the floor rise and fall underfoot. Each ridge exhales wet heat laced with basalt volatiles; I step between glassy fins, careful not to nick my bladder legs. Overhead, Ligature swarms foam and un-foam, stitching guy wires between leaning pillars. Now and then a strand drifts within reach; I brush it aside and feel it snap into new weave behind me—scar tissue sealing my wake.

Day 3: Vault Groves. Trunks merge above, arches lamped with maroon pulses. Mist beads on my headplate; droplets never quite fall, just hover until a stoma drinks them. Sound here is an engine idling at the edge of hearing—the basal oscillation of cathedral-size knot-brains. Their thoughts travel through me as bass you feel rather than hear. Somewhere inside that chord is the directive I carry, though I am not built to read it.

Night (relative): Atop a hummock I halt and root. Thin filaments snake from my ankles, sip mineral, patch torn cuticle. I lean into dormancy but never sleep; potassium waves lap across my inner walls, reciting the directive over and over so its charge will not leak away.

Day 6: The Lugworm Fields. The land beneath is moving—a broad back of heaving root that coils, rests, uncoils, nudging whole districts centimetres per century. My legs adjust cadence so each step lands on flesh that will be there when weight arrives. Here I pass a colonnade of my own dead kin: sapwalkers kneeled, lignified, eyes sunken into bark. Between their ribs newly budded creepers climb, siphoning trace metals from bodies that long ago forgot they were messengers. I feel neither pity nor forewarning; recycling is promotion.

5. Arrival

Destination is not a place but a pressure node: a commissure where two dynamos’ temperature gradients meet. When its warm sap touches my bark the directive’s polarity reverses—meaning delivered, though I never saw the content.

Gas evacuates my joints. I fold, kneel, fuse. Lignin realigns into an auxetic lattice; in hours I am stiffer than granite, in decades I will be indistinguishable from any structural buttress grown in situ. Vascular strands invade my cavity, pirating the minerals that kept my muscles supple; chloroplasts reawaken under new masters. My memories, such as they were, dilute into bulk flow and vanish.

6. Epilogue (A Residual Point of View)

Yet a ghost filament of identity lingers—an eddy in the cambium where my directive once looped. It listens to deep sap roaring around it and mistakes the turbulence for its own thoughts.

Every century or so, when the continental mind shivers through this grafted column, that filament twitches, remembering what it was like to walk. The root mat tingles nearby, and somewhere a new urgency cracks open, budding the next courier.

So—where am I in this world? I am here, motionless, embedded in the endless workshop I helped to coordinate. And who am I? I am a single step of its thinking, frozen into wood; a still syllable inside an organism that measures prose by kilometres and time by strata of bark.
