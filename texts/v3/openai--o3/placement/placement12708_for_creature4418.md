I arrive as most strangers do: wind-tossed, short of breath, my pack a tumble of gear that will soon prove wrong for the altitude.  At first I think I will remain an observer—scribble notes, sample biofilm, compare drone read-outs to the rumors that coaxed me up the icefall.  That fiction lasts three nights.

On the fourth, the basin exhales while I am crossing open ground.  The squall blinds me, packs frost into the flex-joints of my parka, and I do the one forbidden thing: I shout.  The sound goes off like a flare.  The blizzard thickens around the syllables, folds inward, and for a moment I feel the whole hollowed mountain turn an ear toward the trespasser who can’t keep quiet.

When the white peels away I find myself knee-deep in new crust, lungs burning, ears ringing with echoes that will not die.  I understand with perfect, embarrassing clarity that if I stay a mere guest I will soon be pruned.  Usefulness, here, is the only disguise that works.

So I trade my outsider’s instruments for something the Kennar will accept: I offer them the flaw that almost killed me.  I know loudness; I know where it begins, how it propagates, what shapes break it or carry it.  If noise is danger, then the art of un-making noise is currency.

They billet me in a half-carved pillar on the south rim—unfinished because a freeze-burst cracked the lintel six winters ago.  My task is to tune the stone so wind will pass without singing, footsteps will land without report, roof-hides will neither drum nor flap.

Weeks spool into months.

Where I live now  
A tapering ward of teeth no wider than a corridor, opening onto a shallow bowl that holds three kinds of hush:  
1. the breath-hush of sublimating snow,  
2. the muscle-hush of vynn in mid-graze,  
3. the mind-hush that settles after you have held still long enough to feel your heartbeat reflected off stone.

At dawn the sun slants between the arches and finds my new tools:  
•  Horn scrapers the length of a finger, for beveling gutters so runoff lands without splash.  
•  Strings twisted from sere pin-feathers, stretched across cracks; if a faint whine escapes, I tighten or slacken until the note falls back into silence.  
•  A mallet carved from vynn cast-off, its head light as dried kelp.  One cautious tap tells me whether a slab will thrum in the next storm.

Who I have become  
The Kennar call the role quelu—noise-mender.  Not a title of honor, exactly; more like a tendon that the Body did without for a while and now will not refuse.

Physiology: still mostly outsider.  I can’t slip into freezer-trance the way they do when the basin breathes hard, so I wear a harness of sere-ice plates laced with slow-burn ferment packs.  The heat leaks outward first, tricking frost into forming on the armor instead of my skin.

Routine  
Morning: follow the vynn spiral, listening for new chatter in the shell-song that might warn of over-crowding.  
Noon: kneel at the resonance well, whisper the names of every sound I have stolen since sun-up, then cup a hand over the mouth of the shaft and feel which names the echo keeps.  Those, I erase from the world before night.  
Dusk: walk the periphery with a child apprentice (eight winters, goat-pupiled eyes already slivered to needles).  We test each path with felt-soled boots, map where stone has grown a burr that could spark a clack, mark it for grinding at next crescent moon.

Debt and dividend  
For every square metre of bas-relief I smooth, the Kennar allow me one question.  Most I spend on the Body’s deeper mechanics:  
“Do the ground pulses ever skip a beat?”  
“What happens if the guano runs nitrogen-poor?”  
“Has anyone followed a sere migration beyond the rim?”  
Answers come sparse, but never grudging.  Knowledge is another form of quiet; the more you have, the fewer mistakes you must shout about later.

Change  
Six, maybe seven out-breaths of the basin have passed—long enough that my voice, when I dare use it, has roughened to match the texture of wind on ice.  Outsider syllables feel too sharp, so I pare them down.  Vowels narrow; consonants land on the soft parts of the tongue.  The apprentice laughs—soundless, shoulders shaking—when I misplace the click that distinguishes “polish” from “bruise.”

Once a tendril of the old world reaches me: a solar-powered recorder I cached under shale the week I arrived.  I press PLAY, expecting field notes.  What spills out is my own voice, bright and careless, echoing off plastic housing.  The phrase “…should make excellent data” ricochets against the teeth.  Before the third repetition can load the basin answers with a stutter-gust, the temperature plummets, and I know what must happen.  I dig a post-hole in slush, bury the recorder, tamp it silent.  Storm passes.  Lesson kept.

Outlook  
I will stay through one full turning of constellations.  By then the hush I tend should hold shape without hourly attention, and the apprentice will hear cracks before they are born.  Perhaps after that I will climb down-slope and try to teach other noisy places what the cold lung taught me: that survival is a duet between presence and restraint, and every word you utter must pay more than it costs.

But that promise feels distant.  Tonight the fermentation pits under my floor tick with quiet chemistry, the vynn tap their shell-castanets in the lane, and a new lid of ice thins the air to crystal.  The Body settles.  I kneel, press palm to stone, and listen for anything out of place.  Nothing.  For now, I belong.
