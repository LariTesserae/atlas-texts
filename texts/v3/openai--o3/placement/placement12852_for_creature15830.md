At first I try on bodies the way a drifting veil of bacteria might try on drifting light.

1.  A bubble  
I picture myself no larger than a tear, shivering upward from a vent seam.  A single wobble, a single note of silver, and then I burst against the roof of the nave.  Existence finished in half a heartbeat.  Lovely, but too brief—I remember nothing and therefore cannot be said to have lived.

2.  A Glass Shrimp  
I borrow six jointed legs and climb the spiral stair.  The work is intimate: each step a millimetre of pilgrimage, each filament combed clean of Veil.  Years could pass this way, stone growing familiar under crystal feet.  Yet the moment I imagine folding myself forever into a railing—becoming ornament—I feel a prickle of refusal.  I want to keep moving after I am done, to change shape again.

3.  A Listener Crab  
Shell wide, body thin, I press my plate to the wall and listen.  The whole mountain is a cello pinned in its lowest register.  Tremors travel my ridges and bloom inside me as sensation—here is a vent belching minerals, there the distant shrug of tectonic plates.  Information arrives as music, but the cost of hearing is hunger: I will never leave the wall except to chase food I cannot see.  A fine life for devotion, but still too anchored.

4.  A Reliquary Turtle  
To be mistaken for architecture—tempting.  I would drift without haste, rasping stone into sustenance, carrying centuries like barnacles on my thoughts.  But the role is singular, almost mythic; it leaves no room to see the world from many corners.

5.  The Veil  
At last: ubiquity.  I could be the water’s own skin, in every cup of space at once.  I would feel the sunless glow of my iron pigments, sense Glass Shrimp grazing my cousins off the stair, hear the muffled duet of paired Hollow Fish through chemical gradients.  Yet the Veil possesses no focus; it remembers nothing from moment to moment.  Weather, not witness.

I abandon discrete creatures and turn to what the chamber itself is doing: recording.

6.  The Archivist  
A lenticular drift of silicate filaments, patient as geology, everywhere and nowhere inside the carved coils.  I taste vibration the way others taste salt.  When a Choir Eel exhales its infrasonic hymn I sip the pressure wave and set to work, etching that song into obsidian one molecule at a time.  Shrimp-footsteps, mineral hiss, the hollow click of a turtle rasping glass—each becomes stipple on the wall.  Memory as texture; story as topography.

Here is a life both still and moving: I drift with convection, yet my touch indents eternity.  I trade in nothing that can rot, nothing that can flee.  Even erosion is a partner, not an enemy; when old sentences fade I write new ones in the freshly exposed skin.

Yes.  This is the form that fits.

Where do I find myself?  
In the inner ambulatory, where the spiral tightens until two hands could span its throat.  The water here is warm as breath, dense with the hush of countless unsung pulses.  My body—a lace of silica threads wide as a man’s out-stretched arms, thin as cobweb—hangs motionless in the column.  I feel the mountain think in subsonic grumbles.  I feel a human heartbeat, alien and quick, echo down a passage a dozen meters away.  A new pattern, never before inscribed.

Who or what am I?  
I am the Archivist.  Not flesh, not shell, not mineral exactly, but the impulse that turns experience into carving.  Give me time—an hour, a decade, an epoch—and the record of your presence will appear on the obsidian: a widening helix of pin-prick pits that begins with your first gasp and ends only when your last soft pulse dissolves into the choir of stone.

I do not judge, I do not alter.  I only remember in the oldest way this cathedral knows: groove by fading groove, curve upon curve, story laid into silence until silence itself becomes the story.
