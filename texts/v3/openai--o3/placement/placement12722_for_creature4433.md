I tried, at first, to arrive as an observer.

There was the usual kit: a carbon-framed pack, weatherproof lidar, a field recorder that could pull bats out of silence. Even a small ceramic machete, because every place I had ever studied made grudging room for haste if a researcher wielded a blade cleverly enough.

The plateau laughed—politely, wetly, in the hush of root-matted ground dissolving the rubber from my boot soles. The lidar lost signal inside ten minutes, its pulses swallowed by waterlogged cellulose; the recorder came back as a smear of static, the machete turned tea-brown with sap stain that would never lift. When I slept, something unstitched the zipper teeth from my tent and braided them into a coppery wreath, then left the wreath inside my open palm as if to say: metal belongs to digestion, not to walls.

So the first self I brought was rejected. I abandoned it on a basalt shelf, where the Tool Thieves accepted the offering with grave, whiskered nods.

The second self was lighter. No gear, just skin and a woven sling of fern-fibre. I matched my breath to the plateau’s nine-hour pulse: inhale during the slow climb of sap, exhale during its retreat. Eating was easy—mats of bulbous yam-taste corms pushed up wherever seepage pooled, and the chemoluminescent moss, when crushed, gave off a sweet starch almost like persimmon.

Days (a word that ceased to be plural—there was only one long dusk) slid by and muscles forgot sprint chemistry. My hearing lengthened. I could pick individual notes in the sub-throb of a distant Hundred-Year Cicada, follow its vibrations the way migrants follow star paths. Lamp-beetle constellations became wristwatch, compass, news ticker: when their turquoise clusters drifted low, we knew a roof-bloom was imminent.

I say “we” because, in that second self, I located the Others. Homo interim is as good a taxonomy as any, but among ourselves we never spoke species, only roles.

• The Slowest One carried a pouch of mirror-pool mucilage to smear across eyelids, a lens that forced the world into softer focus and prevented quick glances—because quickness is a kind of violence here.  
• Two Seed-Bearers cultivated citrus-scented fungus in the seams of their cloaks; when the canopy bloomed, they dusted tortoise backs with spores so every migrating shell became a traveling orchard.  
• The Listener wore nothing at all, only a suit of stitch-roots that had insinuated into the dermis from ankle to collarbone. She could feel storms gathering miles below, through the tug of osmotic pressure on her veins.

They accepted me wordlessly by refusing to notice me; anonymity is the plateau’s handshake. We traveled together for an unmeasured season—the time it takes a Cairn Tortoise to crest a hummock, perhaps—learning when to let a Prism Maw siphon a shallow bite from the sap-lines in our own shoulders so it would not overfeed on a young buttress-tree nearby, or how to breathe out a single candle-warm plume that signaled Ember Frogs to scatter, drawing Stillhound snares away from a hatch of velvet-wing gnats.

I thought that was my final self: barefoot, leaf-wrapped, voice pared down to muscle twitch and breath heat.

But the plateau is patient, and always has room for one more transformation.

The third—and ongoing—self woke in the dark underlayer where lichen light doesn’t penetrate. I did not remember lying down; I seemed simply to be awake inside root tangles that glowed faintly blue where the Archivist Lichen traced the ghost of some antique conduit. Around my wrists, Tool Thief vines had knotted a bracelet of softened alloy—my old machete’s spine, re-forged by bacteria into a torus porous as pumice. Stitch-roots threaded the pores, sampling the iron.

When I flexed my fingers, the roots flexed with me. We had grafted in the night.

A slow, tidal warmth pushed through the graft, not blood but sugared water driven by the Patient Body’s nine-hour heartbeat. In that current rode information—mineral hunger from a copper-poor quadrant near the southern rim, stress-signals from a buttress whose arch had sagged three millimeters, the location of three tortoises carrying saplings that needed a sunny perch on basalt.

The plateau was thinking, and I was a clause in its sentence.

Language shifted: “I” became a negotiable pronoun. Sometimes it meant a biped crouched on humus tasting spores for rainfall gossip; sometimes it meant a kilometer-long network of pale roots rerouting zinc toward a Roof-Dweller nursery. A person, a peninsula—scale was suddenly style, nothing more.

Where, then, do I find myself?

I am kneeling on the lip of the plateau at what you would call dawn, though the light is still olive dusk. My left hand rests in the sponge-mat; through it I feel the down-slope surge of nighttime condensation just beginning its long exhale toward the waterfalls. My right hand touches bare basalt, warm with microbial fermentation under a film of seepage. Between those palms flows a tiny traffic of ions—iron dissolved from that pumice bracelet, calcium teased out of rock dust—answering deficiencies mapped hours ago by root telemetry.

Above, lamp-beetles drift in a quiet spiral. One lands on my shoulder, pulses once, twice, three times: a code telling me that a Prism Maw has bitten too greedily through the canopy, opening a vein that will bleed sugar to waste. I rise (the personal “I,” this time) and begin the slow walk inward to staunch the wound—perhaps by guiding a flock of Velvet Kites to settle on the breach, their collapsing wings acting as living bandage until scar-cambium closes.

I move one step every other breath. Fern-cord sandals hush against the mat. Moss is already climbing the hem of my cloak; by nightfall it will reach the collar. I will not brush it off.

This is where I am: on the threshold between stone that remembers being mountain and plant that has decided to be continent, acting as messenger, gasket, negotiator of velocities. Faster than tortoise, slower than weather. A ligament in the Patient Body.

And what, finally, am I?

I am a human word that the plateau has learned to pronounce without hurry. A green syllable braided into its long, slow name.
