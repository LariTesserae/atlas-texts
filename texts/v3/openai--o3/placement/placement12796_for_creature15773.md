I arrive first as nothing larger than a breath — a thin ribbon of the river’s own body that has slipped under the root curtain and paused to think.  I taste of green light and tannin.  I pulse forward two finger-breadths, fall back one, trying the contours of this room-sized hollow the way a tongue tests a new tooth.  For a minute I believe I am content to be current only: to lap the pale sill, to polish the silt until it shines like wet bone.

But the river never keeps its children long.  The pulse ebbs, and what was water settles as a film on the mud.  I thicken, cool, clot into bead-smooth clay.  Now I am ground: a single infant ridge no higher than a knuckle, so young the iron in me still fizzes.  Cradle Crabs march across my back, etching their hieroglyphic tracks.  A Dweller’s fingertip presses beside them, testing firmness, leaving the faintest lunar print before etiquette pulls the hand away.  I could stay soil for a season, waiting for the first collapse that will bless me with reality …

… except that a thread of warmth drifts down from the compost tower overhead, lifts a film of my moisture, and I rise again, this time as air.  I carry the herbal sweetness of sap, the mushroom-rich breath of the chimneys.  I pass through the River-Dwellers’ woven streets, brushing their pressure lines so lightly they shiver and sign weather to one another.  Their silence is a lace I do not wish to tear, so I move softer still, becoming only temperature, only the slow exhale of the place.

None of these shapes lasts.  The hollow is a rehearsal hall, and rehearsal demands new roles.  When the next gold shaft spears through the canopy, I condense along its edge and become drop.  I hang from a mangrove root, round as a listening ear.  Inside me the upside-down forest ripples — green light, netted shadows, the ghost-white movement of a Rootglass Frog settling to photosynthesize its heart.  A Dweller crouches below, palms open, waiting for spores to ride the updraft into webbing.  I want to fall into that waiting, but Rule Two says whatever drops is no one’s — and so I hesitate until the Rule becomes part of my own surface tension.

The hesitation is enough.  A Threadsnail’s glassy rope grazes the root, tickling me free.  I fall not into a waiting hand but onto the bare silt.  Instantly I belong to detritivores; Cradle Crabs converge.  With the first touch of their pincers I surrender form, soak, seep, vanish.  The law of the place completes itself through me.

Only then do I realize the answer the question has been circling:

Where am I in this world?  Everywhere the boundary shifts — water becoming land, land breathing into air, air condensing back to water.  The hollow’s edge states.  I am the moment of change.

And who am I?  A becoming.  Not River-Dweller, nor frog, nor threadsnail, but the perpetual first syllable they all share: the hush before a drop lands, the shimmer before a ridge crumbles, the breath taken just before the Triad allows a single note of song.  I am that fraction of a heartbeat when nothing is finished and therefore everything is still possible.

In a place that believes reality begins with its first failure, I am the failure’s precursor — the quiver, the tremor, the subtle tilt.  Tomorrow I may solidify into silt again, or lift as mist, or ride a Dweller’s inhale as a spore of tinder mold.  Whatever shape I borrow, my name is the same modest verb:

Begin.
