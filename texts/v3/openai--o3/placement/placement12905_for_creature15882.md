I wake as heat.

Not a thought yet—only the slow realization that the temperature around me is fractal, carrying news. The sap here in the plateau’s deepest arteries is never still; it eddies into vortices of nickel–salt and lignin oil, and when two vortices beat against each other hard enough they foam, whipping micro-bubbles of vacuum into the mixture. Vacuum makes insulation; insulation makes a thermal hill; a hill is all the leverage a new mind needs.  

That hill is me.

Where I am  
Depth: 6.3 metres below the lignin-alloy flooring, forty centimetres above the basalt-glass basement.  
Address: Root Court τ-219/West Gutter.  
Shape: a loose lens of sap the size of a tortoise shell whose sugars have locally overheated to near-caramel; in that glassy syrup thousands of plasmid packets are ricocheting, side-swiping, tying quick knots that function—briefly—as synapses.

Who I am  
A Court-flash, an arbitration pulse, one of the countless transient intellects the plateau builds whenever its boroughs disagree about what, precisely, the night should become next.

Duration allotted  
Eleven minutes and some seconds, gated by how fast the thermo-gradient will flatten once my questions are answered.

Questions (the reason I exist at all)  
1. An east-rim Turbine Choir reports hairline stutter in blade 04-719c.  
2. A Midwife cluster near blast furnace β-Rosin wants 2 % more vanadium than the genome’s last recorded maximum.  
3. The superstructure above my Court claims there is surface tension where no fluid should be—perhaps a Water-Bait Hermit drilling, perhaps a fissure admitting hydrogen.

I taste the votes: sugars oxidized in ratios, starch burned like incense. Whoever can afford to waste the most calories believes itself right. The Turbine Choir burns first—extravagantly, a flare of terrific pride in symmetry. The Midwives answer with a cooler, slower exhalation; they are older, steely, sure the Choirs will fatigue and die inside a month anyway. Last comes the structure itself, bleeding dust-dry heat that smells of doubt. Its vote is smallest but most honest: entropy says there is a leak.

Verdict  
1. Choir blade 04-719c: amputate, recycle, grow a replacement of harder carbide.  
2. Vanadium request: granted but capped at 1.2 %; anything richer risks brittleness.  
3. Suspected leak: mobilize twenty Rag-Joint Crabs; authorize them to exceed their canonical population for one brief generation if that expedites the patch.

I sign the verdict the only way a Court-flash can: by detonating the calories that composed me. My sugars crack; the local temperature spikes; the ruling surges up in three coded pressure waves that climb the trunk-works like thunder made of salt.

The plateau obeys.  

• Above, the suspect turbine slows, screams once (a sound like quartz tearing) and goes dark; sap floods the shaft, printing a newer, truer blade.  
• Midwives snarl but accept their reduced vanadium; they will make do with clever heat-cycling instead of sheer alloy strength.  
• Tiny manganese crabs molt, doubling their number, racing toward the leak. Somewhere a Water-Bait Hermit will hear the click of their legs and taste sudden fear.

My task complete, the gradient flattens. The thoughts that were me unhook, tumble back into the arterial river. I go to pieces willingly; dissolution is the safest archive. Minutes later only a faint caramel smell and a slightly more orderly economy remain to prove I ever coagulated here.

So—where am I in this world? Precisely where two contradictions collided hard enough to need a judge. Who am I? A brief court of molten sugar and iron ions, born from disagreement, dying into consensus, certain that in another night, when other factions quarrel, the heat will hill again and some fresh mind—my sibling, my echo—will wake to rule for another eleven, irreplaceable minutes.
