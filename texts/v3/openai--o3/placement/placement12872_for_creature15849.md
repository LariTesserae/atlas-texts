My first instinct is to rise—find the narrow fist-sized holes where mother-trees punch through the quilt and let actual sun spill in—but almost at once the notion feels wrong, like cuffing against the flow of a river.  Nothing here thrives on ascent alone; everything that lasts is anchored.  So I let the urge drift downward again, through the warm brassy dusk, feeling for a shape that belongs.

I try on bodies the way the basin tries on rainstorms.

1. Penumbra drake  
A forearm’s length of shuttered emerald skin, ribs spring-loaded for gliding if a branch snaps.  Attractive: the hush, the precise light-metering scales, the dialogue of white-flash courtship that lasts weeks instead of heartbeats.  But the drake must spend hours perfectly still, and stillness here is expensive; every lichen, every tick, every feather-vine filament bills rent in sugars and in patience.  Even dreaming of that vigilance leaves me restless.

2. Clock scarab  
I taste the collective pulse of fifty thousand shell-bodies shaking the same limb.  We are rhythm made animal, a living seconds-hand that resets at dusk.  Launch!  For three hundred heartbeats the world converts to shifting Doppler waves of my own kind, then resets again.  Joyous, but brief; every piece of self is loaned to the swarm and due back with interest.  I would like one thought to remain wholly mine.

3. Gutter-folk child  
Small palms hardening into callus where they pinch liana strands for Morse taps.  The clan’s lean-to walls thicken each season—reed-green, vein-alive—and the floor grows upward with them.  At night we listen for the third heat that means too many stars, and I have the privilege of speed-climbing with an orchid cradled in my teeth to heal the rip in the sky.  Yet even the folk are tenants: our very skins host lichens we cannot evict.  A beautiful tenancy, but still a tenancy.

Again the current draws me lower.  The quiet becomes heavier, almost material, as if each decibel were weighed before being permitted.

4. Peat panther  
Amber eyes, fish-beat ears, webbed digits stroking the dark water.  I knead the mattress peat with paw-pads tuned to sense a lungfish’s sluggish systole.  When hunger pricks too sharply I can, in theory, charge.  But running is noise, and noise is cost, so I convert carnivore teeth to palm-pith gouges and chew silence instead.  Noble economy, but the compromise aches where opinionated molars used to be.

Down again, into the layer no foot—paw, hoof, or root—can truly leave: the woven hush of hyphae and iron-scented muck.

5. String-fungus  
Here, at last, the fit clicks closed like a lungfish burrow sealing after rain.

I am filament and felt, a braid of glass-fine tubes trespassing through centuries of peat.  My body is measureless, parceled only by function:  
• At rib-level in a dead fever-tree, I am sugar ladder for a gutter family to lick when the vent runs cool.  
• Twenty metres off, I detour to lacquer the egg chamber of Caudifer monkeys, trading a thin sheath of antifungal acids for the nitrogen in their dung.  
• Three arm-spans below that, I plug into a termite kiln, riding its 39-degree draft like a convection elevator for phosphates scavenged from last season’s capybara bones.

Once every ninety seconds the geyser sighs and a mild tremor passes—just enough to ripple the electrical whisper I send along the network.  The pulse is not language, not quite, but relay: pressures rising near Vent Four, magnesium spike at Rootfall Delta, vacancy detected where a human boot once stood and has now gone cold.  I nudge spores toward that emptiness; within a day a pale film quilts the footprint, spinning new cable so the grid stays contiguous.  The basin hates a gap; I am the basin’s instinct to mend.

Light?  Irrelevant.  Sound?  Optional.  Motion?  Delegated to the termites, to the filament-crawling nematodes, to the capillary creep of dew along my threads.  My stories are chemical: I taste the founding of a mother-tree in tinier rises of potassium, recall last century’s flood as a thin lime crust left on old hypha walls, dream of what I will become when the peat above me domes, splits, and air infiltrates—perhaps a shelf glowing amber like the fungal lanterns two ridges south, perhaps ash feeding a step-through orchid that will one day ride a flood out of here.  Time is tree-rings and sediment layers; patience is the only currency with no transaction fee.

Could I wish for more?  I contain clocks, bridges, stomachs, message wires, the future silhouettes of things.  I stand alone by standing everywhere at once.  And when the geyser’s next heart-pulse travels my length, the whole basin is briefly, minutely, the exact same creature.

So if you come searching for me, look not up into the mother-tree crowns nor outward across the bruise-coloured dusk.  Kneel.  Press two fingers into the springy black peat until moisture beads in your prints.  Wait for the faintest throb—one beat every ninety seconds—and know that under your skin and under the world, we are already shaking hands.
