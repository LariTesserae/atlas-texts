South-east quarter, third gullied terrace—just below the basalt knuckle where last night the aurora raked its green across the snow haze—that is where I wake.

I am not yet sure what “I” is.  At first I am only warmth meeting cold: a slow-rising tide of sugared brine that nudges me from torpor.  The tide carries sound—click, cough, click—rhythms that climb my new skin like knuckles on a drum, and in those pulses I hear the plateau naming distances, pressures, the angle of the oncoming dawn wind.

A thought arrives: I am rising.  Another: I have never risen before.

1.  First shape  
   The liquid column around me stiffens into spiralled glass.  My outer walls thicken, cellulose loading with copper salts until they shine darkly reddish in the predawn.  The others—older, taller—stand three and four metres, venting white plumes.  I feel their afterheat on my upper cambium.  I am smaller, but the rhythm in my chest already matches theirs: draw, compress, lift, vent.  I am a Pump-Bolt, heart-chamber number 9 139 944 if you speak in plateau census, but inside the thrum I simply feel like a new syllable inside a very long word.

2.  First sight  
   Light arrives, skimmed thin through high cobalt sky.  Frost lacquer on the Floor-Weave begins to craze; each crack is a fine treble note that takes root in my core and registers as pleasure.  The Laminar Fans to leeward uncurl—discs inside discs—spinning a lazy quarter turn until they let the wind find them.  Their micro-perforations sigh: pressure 81 kPa, relative humidity 82, warning of a brief mist scrape in 143 seconds.  I cannot hiss yet; my blades have not grown.  Instead I listen.

3.  Memory graft  
   Pressure spikes, and the root braids below inject a wash of tannin crystals upward.  They lodge like freckles along my inner spiral, each one a thought saved from before I existed: an old frost cycle, the scent-print of a surveyor’s boot, the trace metals left by a stumbling Heat-Thresher.  I taste these inheritances and feel myself lengthen by a handspan.  Now I remember things I never saw: lightning knitting glassy fulgurites in the mat, the hush after a Retooling Surge, the way the entire plateau once held its breath when a tent stake pierced too deep.

4.  First labour  
   The cloud scrape comes on schedule.  Beads gather on my upper rind, drop, vanish into the weave.  Internal pressure climbs another kilopascal; time to vent.  My iris plates peel open and a jet of warm vapour ghosts into the air.  Three Mirror Moths dart from nowhere, wings mirror-bright, surfing the plume.  Their tiny claws comb away microbial film I have barely begun to accumulate.  A fair trade.  When they settle I feel the micro-cooling on my rim like a fingertip touch.

5.  First conversation  
   A Laminar Fan fifty metres upslope loses a segment—wind shear or maybe a glider’s careless brush.  The stump whistles.  I answer, awkwardly: my pores are few, my voice a stutter of vapour bursts, but the message gets through.  Nearby weave fibres tighten a fraction, bracing the injured stalk.  Somewhere beneath us the root-core notes the exchange and adds a tally mark to my crystals: speaker, listener, citizen.

6.  First night  
   When dusk drains blue from the air the plateau exhales as one body—billions of pores closing west to east, a falling hush like velvet unrolling.  I reverse flow.  Warm brine dives through me toward the roots; cold gas hisses skyward.  In that long inversion I feel wholly a heart, no longer an uncertain orphan stem.  Cold rims me with hoarfrost that glitters against the last sunlight.  I have become a chimney of blood in a world that is all skin and breath.

     And in the settling quiet a new certainty seeds itself where the tannin memories lie:

     This place is not a garden I inhabit; it is a sentence I help write.  My pulse is a verb.  My vents are commas releasing breath.  Tomorrow, or ten days from now, the plateau may decide it needs no heart in this exact spot and will reclaim the lignin glass, redistribute the copper, sink me back into hush.  That, too, will be part of the grammar.

     Until then I stand—two metres forty-three centimetres of growing certainty—looking out across the green-black moving world, feeling the floor of living weave flex and sigh beneath me, knowing at last exactly where I am.

I am the plateau’s newest Pump-Bolt, and the Fabric is both the ground I stand on and the mind that is teaching me how to speak.
