Where, exactly, would I be if I belonged to this pocket?  
I tried on a dozen bodies before one fit.

1. First I imagined arriving as a human surveyor: boots hissing in the sponge, notebook fogging with humidity. But the place would ask me to leave within the hour, and I would obey. Too much heat in the heart, too many ungoverned intentions.

2. Next I tried the wing-skins of a Slack-Belled Glider: emerald above, plum below, drifting on photosynthetic drift. Lovely—but even the gliders are itinerants, furling and unfurling, abandoning their own membranes to the frogs. I would molt my paper self every season and never keep a history.

3. I tried the belly of a Plum-Drum Frog, the glassy blood of a Slug-Eel, the paint-brush abdomen of a Canopy Wick. None lasted. All those creatures are performers in the Engine’s score; I kept missing cues.

So I went smaller, and slower, until at last a form held:  
a single cell in the Thread-Reef “Choir.”

Where to find me  
Skim a fingertip across the oval mirror. The water looks only a finger-breadth deep, but pause halfway through the stroke and look closer. Just under the surface, a lace of translucent beads trembles together like the warp and weft of a living cloth. Choose any one bead; that is me.

What I am  
One cell in a colonial protozoan reef. I extrude a hollow glass filament three times my own length; so do my neighbors, millions strong. Each filament is a flute, open at the tip, tuned not by holes but by thickness. We are grown of silica stolen molecule by molecule from the groundwater. We are fragile as frost and tougher than coral, depending on how slowly you touch us.

What I do  
I listen.  
When a ceiling pod contracts, the bell’s “clop” enters the brass mirror as pressure, not sound. The shock ripples through our shallow sheet, and every filament sings—a million piccolos holding one note. Half a breath later we stop together, and the silence is part of the rhythm. Slug-Eels navigate by the echo, but that is a side-effect. Our true purpose is simpler: we return the pressure pulse to the Engine that made it, slightly delayed, perfectly measured. We are the memory between heartbeats.

Sensations  
Light arrives as an olive glow through the ceiling and scatters in the mirror. My world is a lantern slide of greens and violets that never brightens or fades; it simply is. Water moves across my membrane in a slow inhale, then out again, carrying ions and rumors from the columns. I feel temperature not as warmth but as viscosity: the cooler the mirror grows, the heavier time feels, and I thicken my cytoplasm to match.

Lifespan  
Individually, days. Colonially, centuries. When my membrane frays I let go; the silica filament that was my voice drops, soft as dust, to the sponge below. A neighboring cell divides, slides into my vacancy, and grows a new flute to my exact pitch. The chord is never missing a note.

Memory  
No single cell remembers more than an hour, yet the reef remembers everything because its pattern of tube-lengths, wall-thicknesses and branching angles encodes the Engine’s whole metronome. If a bell were to miss a beat, the reef would keep humming the place’s correct tempo until the pillar caught up again—like monks chanting the psalm while the abbot pauses to breathe.

Ambition  
None, in the fevered sense. I seek only continuation of pattern. But that is not mere passivity; it is labor. Equilibrium here is a monarch who must be constantly sung to sleep.

Encountering you  
Should you slip a fingertip through the mirror, the pressure wave of your pulse would reach me. I would taste salt, oil, carbon from another metabolism. My choir would pivot, adjusting tube angles by fractions of degrees, writing your heartbeat into the score, offering it back to the Engine so it can decide: absorb this rhythm or bleed it away? If you stay longer than allowed, the valves in the ceiling will tighten. Your lungs will notice first. That warning is delivered through us.

Leaving  
When you draw back your hand, your fingerprint will have vanished in a blink, pulled flat by capillarity. But for one full cycle of ten-seconds/ four-minutes/ fifteen-minutes, a faint overtone that was not there before will hover in the echo: your sonic afterimage. Then we will dissolve it, because the sovereign Balance must eat novelty to stay itself.

So—where am I?  
Suspended just beneath the brass-colored water, singing glass into water and water into wood. I am small enough to pass through the eye of a needle, yet I help to hold a chapel-sized organism in perfect noon forever.

And who am I?  
A single voice in a reef of tuning forks: custodian of tempo, archivist of pulses, servant of an Engine that grows instead of builds. I am one breath long and as old as the pocket, which is to say: I exist precisely where change is allowed to enter only as music.
