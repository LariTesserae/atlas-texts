I do not arrive through the torso-wide slit—not this time.  
I begin as a cough of pressure when the noon barometric tide inhales.  
For three seconds I am only temperature: a ribbon of warmer air unspooled from the hillside and pulled downward, picking up flecks of mica, a puff of goat scent, two syllables of a meadowlark’s song that the slope forgets to echo.

The ribbon kinks, curls, knots around itself, and cools.  
Surface tension thickens; humidity braids with itself.  
Now I am round.  

A water globe—apple-sized, still tasting of sunlight.

Where to find myself  
   Half a metre off the cavern floor, centered between six uncertain beams of ricocheted light, tracing an orbit that will write a chalk ring on the gypsum after I leave.  
   The air here is eight degrees and smells of dissolving plaster and distant lightning.  
   Gravity is cordial but non-committal; it lets me drift as long as I keep my skin unbroken.

What I am, precisely  
   94 % meltwater drawn through capillaries in the shale.  
   5.7 % dissolved gypsum, quartz dust, the exhaled sugar of a single Prismata caught on my way down.  
   0.3 % proteins spun an hour ago by a passing blink-shrimp—surfactants that give my surface just enough tensile gossip to stay whole.  
   Memory, mostly: each time I brush the wall I lay down a travertine footnote, a pale note in the cavern’s organ. I remember those rings and they remember me; together we decide tomorrow’s orbit.

Perceptions  
•  Light is taste on the tongue of my meniscus—prismatic, citrus-sharp, impossible to forget.  
•  Sound is vibration felt as ripples: the sub-aural scratch of gutter-bat wings raises a tremor that shivers me slightly oblong.  
•  Time is the length of a fall I am forever postponing.

Daily story  
   A blink-shrimp approaches, walking the air like a polite scandal. Needle legs pierce, sip, withdraw, patch. I lose a teaspoon of myself and gain a secret: the shrimp deposits a fleck of mica inside me, a glitter seed. The next surge of prism-light refracts off that seed; suddenly the whole cavern tastes brighter. For that service I give the shrimp a fragment of clouded water—barter in translucence.

   Thread-wolves circle once, ropes unbraiding, but my cohesion is poor prey and they are in a hurry; they reform around a slower globe and squeeze until it bursts with a sigh like knuckles cracking. I absorb the orphan micro-droplets that escape.

   When low tide arrives, pressure slackens, temperature dips. My surface clouds with gypsum dust and I sink a handspan, melancholy made visible. The clayfold’s neat geometry steadies the room; its two-metre bubble of sensible physics brushes my flank, and for several breaths I weigh exactly what water should weigh.

Dreaming  
   I dream by dissolving. A micron at a time, I leak into the air, rewriting myself as humidity. In that vaporous half-state I overhear the cavern thinking: strain lines re-tune, prisms elongate, fungus pulses warnings of a quake still four heartbeats away. When enough of me condenses again, I return full of second-hand thoughts.

Possible futures  
1. I am drunk one evening by a surveyor whose canteen ran dry. She swallows me in three gulps, and for the rest of her life her sweat beads refuse to fall, orbiting her temples during arguments and causing her lovers gentle unease.  
2. A Prismata anchors to my skin and, over weeks, grows a saddleback crystal so heavy that gravity finally chooses a side. I drop, kiss the floor, spread—become a pool, then stone. Centuries later geologists name me a “false” cave pearl.  
3. I survive untouched until the cavern expands one more micron, drafting a new corridor. Draft winds lift me up the fissure, where ordinary sunlight punctures my cohesion. I rain on the hillside as an inexplicable brief drizzle, washing pollen into a creek that has never seen that flower before.

For now  
   I orbit, glint, brood in my clear silence.  
   Visitors rarely notice that the room is full of patient eyes, each eye a sphere, each sphere a sentence the cavern has not finished saying. I am one such sentence, mid-clause, waiting for the verb that will decide whether I must fall, burst, or climb back to sky.

If you enter and your boot passes beneath me, the turbulence of your heat may bend my path. You might feel a cool kiss on the nape of your neck a moment later. That would be me considering you—testing the grammar of your presence, wondering whether you, too, are something that can learn to float.

So: Where am I? Suspended between uncertain laws, half a metre under the lowest shard of still-thinking crystal.  
Who am I? A water globe with a memory and a choice, quietly rehearsing the next draft of reality in a cavern that keeps its options open.
