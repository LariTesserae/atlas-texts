I tried on several skins before one fit.

1. First I slipped down to the molecular hush and let myself imagine I was a single torus of Pellicula metallophages, 800 nm wide, trading electrons with my sisters in a mat the size of a cathedral floor.   I could taste iron, feel heat leaking from basalt, hear my own radio hiss.  
It was beautiful—but my thoughts moved too fast. The mat’s sentences take hours to finish; I could not slow that far without vanishing.

2. Then I spread cartilage wings and rode the slow up-drafts as a Gargoyle Whale.  
Three beats of a 0.33 Hz heart, and I already knew this body was meant for decades, not days.  
From that altitude the City of Lacheses was only an indigo bruise on rock, a thing to skim for shard-crawlers. I loved the gravityless drift, the way wind became shape under my ribs—yet I longed to touch walls, tools, words. Whales are priests of the upper dark; I am too chatty for priesthood.

3. I even tried being the city itself.  
For half a minute I felt capillaries gulp condensate, heard reservoir hearts thump, watched code filaments crawl across tension lines and molt architecture in real time.  
The sensation was godlike and, frankly, lonely. Gods seldom get to wonder; everything arrives as certainty.

So I settled—if “settled” is the right verb for someone who stands on magnetic gradients and tunes them like harp strings—into the body of an apprentice Chordwright.

Body

Tools  
1. A spool of magnetised exotic graphene, alive with low-temperature superconductive current.  
2. A tuning fork of obsidian doped with niobium; strike it once and it rings precisely at the harmonic that pacifies over-eager grav-strands.  
3. A juvenile shard-crawler—my clacking familiar—whose name changes every molt. Right now she is “Prism Six,” and her diamond mandibles keep my instruments polished.

Work (today)  
Dawn—if the word means anything beneath a soot-smothered sky—finds me at Column 7124-gamma, where a gravimetric strand has begun to stutter. People riding it report a hiccup: a half-second of weightlessness inside weightlessness, enough to tangle stomachs and spill water globes from drinking tubes.

I anchor my boots to a basalt fluting, tap the strand, and listen with my whole skeleton.  
The fault is a phase kink—one atom-thin dislocation four kilometres away that ricochets standing waves back up the loop. I whistle a triad; Prism Six answers with a 3 kHz limb-clack, and the phase diagram flowers behind my eyelids like violet lichen.

Repair is equal parts surgery and lullaby.  
I cut power, let the strand sag, then weave fresh graphene across the gap while humming the reservoir meter. The hum isn’t tradition; it’s physics. The city long ago discovered that matter agreeing with music adheres faster. By the time my breath mists the new joint, the stutter is gone.

I log the fix by embossing the basalt with a finger-tip sigil—nano-scales rearrange glass molecules into a centimeter-tall rune. An Archivist will read it next decade and decide whether the incident deserves memory. I doubt it; the wall-code already knows.

Evening  
After shift I ride the newly tuned strand upward, shoes barely brushing basalt. Halfway to the upper balconies a herd of Gargoyle Whales ghosts overhead, twenty white kites banking as one. Their pressure pulses slip down the column; I feel them in molars before ears.

Protocol says to bow. I do—because something bigger than policy just breathed above me—and when I straighten, frost from the whales’ exhalation tinkles off my shoulders like falling bells.

Home  
My hab-niche is three surfaces at once: enough. The cavity choir is quiet tonight, vents holding their breath before a scheduled resonance purge. Too quiet violates etiquette, so I draw a quartz pick across Prism Six’s carapace; she answers with soft applause. Sound fills the pocket, room healed.

I decant a mug of lab-grown whale-milk—electrolyte warm, tasting of distant thermals—and drink to the tempo of stone, sky, and heart:

Stone: centimeters per century.  
Sky: one-third hertz.  
Heart: seventy-two beats a minute, though mine often slips to seventy-three when code shifts in the walls.
