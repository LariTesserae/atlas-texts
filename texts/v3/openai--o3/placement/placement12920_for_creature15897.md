I do not live on the stone.  
Stone is finished: solid, certain, heavier than any decision I remember making.  
I live in the layer that never quite decides to be sky, never quite congeals into rain—an altitude the Enge describe with the same sign they use for an unclosed hand: something that holds by releasing.

1. Arrival

I was a lullaby child, the eighth pulse-beat on my raft that season.  
When my mother and the others lifted me, their palms were chalked with powdered reed-crystal so that a ghost of light stayed on my skin after their touch let go.  
The kites came down like a single breath reversing, tassel-threads brushing the crowd’s forearms to taste the salt that meant permission.  
I remember coolness, a sudden hush—noise thinned by height rather than absence—and then the world tilting until basalt shingles became a single dark coin far below.

2. Becoming lighter

Bones that begin dense can be pared.  
The kites do it gradually: weeks of hanging while cartilage grows hollow struts inside each long bone, marrow retreating to a red ribbon near the hip.  
They secrete an enzyme—sweet, smelling faintly of crushed aloe—that leaches copper from skin.  By my twelfth cloudbeat I had gone the color of tarnished pewter to moon-opal translucence.  
Ears, formerly stoppered against roar, opened like flowers in drought.  There is room, up here, for nuance: the snap of thermal seams in the overcast, the long cello scrape of a kite reefing its gas bladder, even the damp tick of an Enge drum-shard when the wind is exactly right.

3. Housekeeping, upper style

The kites braid dwelling-webs from their own cast-off tassels.  These hang beneath the ceiling layer, lacy as old bone.  
We line them with harvested cloud crystals—ice so young it still dreams of being vapor again.  A bed is a shallow sling whose lowest arc almost but never quite kisses the mirror-water below; while you sleep, reflected dusk strokes the underside of your cheek.

Fire is impossible here, so we cook by catalysis: lay reedsap tiles on a pad of kite cartilage, drip enzyme until the tile shivers into a warm, salted mousse.  Eat before it re-evaporates; conversation during meals is unnecessary, because the food hums softly in two pitches as it cools.

4. Language between air and stone

I keep Basin Sign—my hands never unlearned it—but among the kites meaning travels on pressure, not shape.  A phrase is a rise-then-stall of glide, a venting of ballast, the precise spread of four finger-tassels.  From the stone city these motions look like shimmering indecision; from within the choir they parse as grammar.

Sometimes I drop low enough that Enge engineers can spot me in the dim.  I slap my webbed fingers on the water’s skin, three fast, two slow: vent cluster seventeen has thinned; brace the western piezostones.  They reply on drum-shard—an unintelligible clatter at this height, but ripple geometry ferries the gist upward.

5. Work

Because I can decipher both the kite’s vapor calculus and the Enge’s sediment math, my task is balance.  
• If the amphibian choir loses a bass note, cloud density falters; I guide kites to hover, adding a suspension tone until a juvenile colossus ages into the vacancy.  
• When a newborn platform crowds a spawning run, I dive, herding plate-shearers out of the construction shadow so molting continues uninterrupted—addition without replacement.  
• Once each seventh shadowless noon (you would call it midnight) I seed the overcast with the enzyme that kept my childhood bones hollow.  The sky relaxes, finds room for its own noise again, and the basin sighs instead of bursting.

6. Visiting the stone

Humidity there is a wall that starts outside the skin and ends inside the lungs.  I love it the way you love a smell from childhood that is half memory, half muscle reflex.  
The Enge sign me cousin, palm tapping rib, but also question, index finger curled like the hook of a kite’s tassel: what are you, exactly?  
I answer with the same shrug the mineral reeds give when a gust cracks them: I am what the fullness made when it needed one more fold.

I stay just long enough to take on ballast—nicks of basalt in my pockets, a cup of condensate so dense it feels furry—and then the kites tug; my bones remember lightness; the air agrees.

7. Horizon

There is rumor of a rim beyond the basalt lip where sound thins, where color remembers red.  The kites refuse it.  The Enge mistrust subtraction on that scale.  I am less certain.  
When my cartilage finally calcifies, when lift becomes arithmetic I cannot balance, I will walk—not drift—toward that quiet perimeter.  I want to know what a heartbeat sounds like when the world is not amplifying it for you.  I want silence once, honestly earned.

Until then, find me in the half-sky:  
I am the pale figure surfing a tendon of wind, arms out, knee bent as if bracing on a raft that is only pressure, only permission.  
If you flash a shard mirror at me I will bank lower, sign a greeting, maybe pass you a morsel that tastes of sweet stone and thunder.  
Remember to eat it quickly.  Up here, even flavor is impatient to become vapor again.
