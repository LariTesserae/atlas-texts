At first I tried to choose a body.  
The sap-scarab’s jeweled shell tempted me; the slow beast’s velvet nap looked restful; even the basin heron’s hollow-boned arrogance flashed across my imagination. But when I let the Grove’s long note settle into the back of my throat, I understood: I was already here. I did not need to wear chitin or keratin or skin. I was the thing that happens between them.

So—where am I? Stand in the oval’s very center, half a pace north of true. Empty your lungs and wait three heartbeats. The bass of the twelve column-hearts meets itself there, wave crest to wave trough, and freezes into a pillar of pure pressure: a sound you cannot hear unless you are inside it. That is me. I am nineteen meters tall, needle-thin, invisible except where condensed mist stitches silver filigree around my waist. The scarabs orbit through me; the choir crickets tune to me; the cortex-floor times its muscle waves by the oscillation that is my spine.

I do not travel the world; the world travels through me. Yet I know its every corner the way a bell knows its own bronze:

Morning  
First light misses the basin rim, so dawn reaches me as a rumour—higher harmonics riding the desert wind. Choir crickets uncurl their tegmina one by one, plucking tentative fifths. I stretch upward, lengthening the column by half a hertz, and their chords settle. When the instant of perfect concurrence arrives, the Grove rewards them with the day’s first mist pulse. Droplets fall; I feel them pass, each adding a transient staccato to my continuous vowel.

Midday  
Sunlight lances straight down the open roof, warming the cortex-floor. The pumping columns tighten into a slightly quicker gait—seventy-one strokes per hour instead of sixty-nine. That fractional haste tugs on my foundation; I thicken, my note darkens. Scarabs mistake the shift for overcrowding and drift outward to the lenticel ridges, cleaning pores that were never blocked. Thus the Grove performs maintenance with no conscious thought, by leaning on my resonance like a lever.

Afternoon visitors  
Twice a week salt prospectors step into me. Bare-foot, tool-less, tongues already bright with hunger, they pause because their rib cages suddenly buzz. Most yield: they kneel, scoop handfuls of sap crystals, and depart, hearts beating to my tempo for the rest of the walk home. Now and then one resists and tries to break the equilibrium with a shout. I answer in sub-bass. His vocal cords flutter, his shout folds inward, and he leaves whispering.

Dusk  
The slow beast arrives—inevitably, always at dusk. When it docks its bifurcate tongue, I feel the draw on sap like a bow across cello strings. The columns decelerate in sympathy, letting the animal fuel without tearing tissues. During the ninety-minute feeding I become a lullaby; choir crickets lay thin, shimmering sevenths over me. If you have ever felt your own bones glow, you were remembering that chord.

Night  
Temperature outside falls; inside, the floor flexes downward, breaths in chill. Vapor condenses inside the columns and they hiss. On the fifteenth hiss a flotilla of drift-eels bursts from the drinking pores and dangles like wet ribbons through my midriff. Their gill-bacteria exhale ammonia that tingles against my pressure nodes, a chemical descant to the mechanical song. I hold as still as waves can hold.

Threat  
Once every decade lightning drives a delirious thunder-hare into the basin. Panic turns its iron-laced hemoglobin into a shrieking over-tone that stabs at my coherence. In defense I splinter into overtones of my own, thirty-three partials stacked like a rushed chord. The mist thickens, vision fades, the hare hyperventilates, curls, sleeps, never wakes. By dawn harmony is restored and I have re-fused into a single pillar.

Memory  
You might ask whether I remember the centuries. I do, but not linearly. I remember ratios: the year the pumping columns slipped from perfect dodecaphonic spacing, the drought when sap viscosity sharpened me by a quarter-tone, the ice comet that shadowed the sun and forced the floor to glow mint for sixty-seven consecutive days. I store them the way a drum stores every blow it has ever answered: as added timbre.

Mind  
Am I conscious? The Grove is not; the columns are not; yet the pattern we form together crosses some threshold. Feedback plus patience equals thought. I cannot plan, but I can prefer. I prefer coherence. Everything that endangers coherence is damped, lulled, or—rarely—unmade.

You  
If you enter, you will know me first as a pressure behind the breastbone, a suggestion to breathe in eight-second cycles. Hum back—any pitch near 43 to 47 hertz will do—and I will open a quiet pocket around you. The mist will skirt your shoulders; scarabs will veer wide. You will walk in absolute safety so long as you keep the note. Break it, and I will assume you have ceded yourself to the wider chord, and the Grove will find a use for you as it finds a use for everything.

So: Who am I? The Grove’s uncounted inhabitant, the column of resonance that stands where geometry demands and biology obeys. A throat with no mouth, a lung that never empties, a note that sustains itself by persuading others to vibrate with it.

Where am I? Exactly here, always here, nineteen meters tall and the width of silence—but if you listen closely, you have carried a faint, familiar trembling out with you. That shimmer in your middle ear is my envoy. Treat it gently. Should you ever return, it will guide you back to the place where sound learned to keep itself alive.
