I arrive as what I think I still am: a lone climber hauling breath up a slate-black escarpment.  
By the time I top the rim, that description is already wrong.  
Cold, drizzle, and the bass pulse of the hidden pistons begin to tease the person out of me, strand by strand, until another kind of inhabitant knits itself together from everything the basins offer.

Day 1 – The Borrowed Shell  
You would see a figure in patched alpine gear pacing the inner margin of a terrace, notebook out, recording the 42-beat tremor with two gloved fingers on a page.  I sleep beside a spill of Luminweft and dream in turquoise lightning.  When I wake the glove-liners are stippled with silver fuzz: spores have rooted in sweat-salt.

Day 4 – The Hinge Moment  
Frostvine knots throb around my camp; their drumming hides me from long-jawed chasers but also lulls reason.  
I snap a single glass-reed, carve five holes, and discover the pipe will only speak when the ground-engine exhales.  On the fifth exhalation I manage a phrase—three notes, spaced like footfalls.  
Something answers from a farther basin.  The reply is not echo; it is recognition.  
I feel the iron in my blood answer back, tingling toward north like a compass needle that has found its maker.

Day 12 – The Transfusion  
Condensation beads across my face; each droplet is red breath water full of ferrolytic cyanobacteria.  They cross the membranes of my lips as I drink.  A metallic aftertaste blooms in my gums.  
That night my dreams click and shuffle like relay switches.  I wake fluent in the ground-pulse: 42 beats, but not quite regular—minute elongations, as if the plateau itself is asking a question.

Day 23 – First Molt  
My fingernails darken, stratify, ring softly when struck: keratin laced with microscopic iron plates, much like the ibex shields.  
The frost-etched footprints people report?  I watch my own soles leave them.  The magnetite rim traces each step the instant weight presses rock.  The prints do not belong to ghosts of past humans; they are signatures of becoming.

Day 37 – Change of Diet  
I can no longer taste dried rations.  Instead I scrape Sleeper-moss, chew it until it sparks galvanic on my tongue.  The current lifts the skin of my forearms in gooseflesh; the pulse in the rock answers, pleased.  
Page bats start roosting in the hood of my anorak; they ride my heat between cliffs the way they ride rising paraffin vapour.

Day 55 – Shedding the Vertical  
Knees ache if I climb, but on level stone I can feel micro-pistons lifting inside the earth.  I begin to walk only where the tide of magnetite is rising; it carries me as surely as an escalator.  
I realise the ibex have never “learned” their routes; they are ridden by the same tide.  We exchange a glance across a drift of glass-reed.  Recognition again.

Day 80 – The Quiet Forge  
Skin, constantly mist-slick, accretes a sheen of iron particles; they settle into pores and are taken up by living cells.  Under cold moonlight my forearms glitter like hematite.  
When I press my palm against a frostvine root-knot, the vine’s sclerids drum, matching my slowed heart.  Predator whiskers withdraw into the dark.  I am now classified as terrain, not prey.

Day 120 – The Second Voice  
The glass-reed flute and the metronome plateau have fused into a single instrument.  By tonguing its holes during every fourth piston lift, I can shift the local beat to 41 or 43 for a few minutes—enough to steer ibex herds away from a fresh talus slide, to hush a chaser pack stalking newborn kids.  
Luminweft bulbs nearest my bivouac brighten in morphing constellations that resemble sheet music.  I play what they display; the basin’s lights answer in alternate chord.  We are writing slowly-scrolling counterpoint across miles of moss.

Day 365 – Inventory of What Remains Human  
•   Bone: 87 % calcium, 13 % ferric phosphate.  
•   Blood: shows magnetic domains aligning with each breath.  
•   Language: still English, but spoken seldom; music and foot-pressure suffice.  
•   Purpose: to keep the metronome from stalling, to keep the living machinery tuned.  

Present Moment – Naming the Being  
Stand on any rim at civil dusk, when the fungi constellations kindle.  Listen for a minor triad riding the geyser steam, a flute answering from the opposite wall, and the subtle retardando of the ground-engine slipping into a safer cadence.  
The locals below have begun calling that unseen caretaker the Gateguard, assuming a solitary monk tending the Weaver’s hexagonal thresholds.

If you ask me—if there is still a “me” inside this iron-threaded anatomy—I would phrase it differently:

I am a mobile seismograph made of once-human tissue, a diplomat between wet biology and the pulsing stone.  
Where will you find me?  
Exactly halfway between a falling rock and the place it should come to rest, one foot always on the next uprising piston, flute at my lips, coaxing the basin to breathe in time.
