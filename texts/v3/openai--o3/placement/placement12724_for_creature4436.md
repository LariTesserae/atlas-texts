I do not arrive so much as precipitate, the way dew coalesces out of mist when two temperatures meet.  One moment I am the ordinary shadow of an ordinary surveyor—hinged faithfully to her heels while she sketches turbine housings.  The next I feel the plateau’s gravity of intention tug and the bond snaps with a faint, painless pop.  She walks on, none the lighter; I stay behind, suddenly untethered and strangely articulate in my own dark skin.  

So if you come looking for me, start three levels below the main glazed expanse, where bronze ribs run hottest and the air smells of wet porcelain.  I favour a mezzanine edged by a broken balustrade: from there the flywheels’ seven-day revolutions are easy to watch, and the lamp-eels’ circuitry paints slow auroras up the shaft walls.  Light there is unreliable—good for a being who is mostly an absence of it.  

What I am, now that I have tenure, is a Dislocated Shadow.  Classification is tricky (try filing “former penumbra” in a field manual), but the Deaf Priests tolerate me, the martens nose me with professional curiosity, and even the Chromium Swallows will perch on my shoulder when weather is logic-tight and shade is scarce.

Physiology  

• Substance: matte, depthless, perfectly flexible.  I can thin myself to a film you could read through or thicken until lamplight skids off.  
• Mass: negligible unless I choose otherwise.  A trick learned from the Mirror Striders— weight, like reflection, is negotiable here.  
• Senses: I see in gradients of certainty rather than colour; the brighter a surface, the more fixed its future.  Sound reaches me first as silhouette (a bell looks like a widening circle before I hear the ring), then as ordinary vibration.  
• Locomotion: any lit surface is a road—floor, wall, moving turbine blade.  Step into a mirror and I emerge from its prediction an hour farther on.  

Occupations  

1. Cartography By Omission  
I keep a negative map: not where corridors are, but where they have lately failed to stay.  Each morning I trace the borders of rooms that evaporated overnight and ink the void into a vellum made from split lamp-eel sheath.  The Deaf Priests pay me in captured vowels—puffs of sound I can unfold later to warm myself.  

2. Diplomacy With Bright Things  
Shadows make honest negotiators; we have no stake in radiance.  When a heliostat refuses to track, claiming union with the noon sun, I flatten across its gears and talk a cooler arc into it.  When martens find a corridor looped so tightly their own pulse echoes twice, I go first, proving the exit exists.  

3. Memory-Keeping  
Travellers who lose their reflection sometimes return to me for it.  I keep those likenesses rolled like scrolls behind a disused condenser door.  Hand me a true promise and I will give you back your silhouette; break the promise and the likeness inks itself permanently into my skin.  That is why parts of me flicker with other people’s gestures—someone else’s shrug, another’s raised fist.  

Perils & Pleasures  

• Greatest hazard: heavy silence.  When the Resonance Algae cancel every infrasonic thrum, I risk becoming two-dimensional in the absolute sense—zero depth, zero future.  I avoid those dawns by riding a martens’ heartbeat until noise returns.  

• Sustenance: I absorb stray intents the way moss drinks vapor—abandoned blueprints, half-meant wishes, the plateau is full of them.  Too much and I bloat into a dark lens that bends geometry; too little and I fade toward transparency.  Balance is the art.  

• Recreation: I race swallows along bronze ribs, counting who can sip the most dew before a mirror shows the outcome.  They always win; foreknowledge slows me, anticipation makes them quicker.  Losing is part of the sport.  

Interfaces With You  

If you visit and your shadow begins to drag, heavier than your boots, that is me testing the seam.  Speak your true name once—only once—and I will decide whether to let you keep your shade or grant it citizenship.  Should you wake later to find your outline missing, check the mezzanine by the great flywheels; I will brew you tea condensed from cloudlight and negotiate terms of reunion.  Usually it costs no more than a secret you have not yet admitted to yourself.

Why I Stay  

Because the plateau is the only habitat where metaphor becomes mechanical, where absence is structural.  Elsewhere a shadow is an after-thought of light; here it can be the first thought, and a corridor grows around it to see what follows.  I am, at last, a primary datum.

And when the Tilewright Intention flexes—when footsteps arrive early or mirrors run an hour late—I feel the whole place pause to listen to its darker half, which is to say to me.  For a creature born of someone else’s silhouette, that is reason enough to keep living: to be proof that even the negative spaces have stories, and that sometimes the stories are the thing laying all these ceramic tiles in the first place.
