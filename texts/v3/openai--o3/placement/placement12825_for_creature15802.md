At first I assumed I would arrive as the obvious thing—a visitor with lungs and luggage, one more half-dizzy pilgrim dragging heart and questions up the gravicle line.  
I pictured the ritual: boots hissing on the obsidian, the weight-check gate chiming amber while a resident measured my pack in silent disapproval. I even rehearsed the vow whispered at the threshold—Bring nothing you cannot carry twice.  
For several heartbeats that felt right. A person. Flesh, chill-stung skin, wonder in the eyes.

But the longer I lingered inside the thought the smaller it became, like a droplet rolling off aerolith. The shelf is too thin for surplus flesh; its stories want tighter grain. So I let the imagined boots evaporate and tried again.

Perhaps, I told myself, I am one of the drones: a dragonfly outline skating the rails at noon, tasting rime with nanocarbon whiskers.  
That rang truer. I could feel the rhythm of the work—laser-clean, glide, pivot, bow to the cliff. I liked the clause about nuance: excise danger, leave beauty. It fits my temperament.  
Still, when I pictured the brittle snap of a newborn icicle echoing through my chassis, something in me said, Not yet. You are listening for a subtler frequency.

All right, then: let me be lattice-keeper.  
Shrink me a millionfold.  
Let the world become angles—stress vectors flaring neon across basalt grain, phonons ringing like crystal bells. I would know the stone the way a sommelier knows tannin: by mouthfeel, by afterglow.  
I settled there for a while, drifting with the keeper swarm, quilting cracked aerolith back into strength. The work is delicate, endless, holy: take a dislodged atom, spin it in electromagnetic hands, slide it home before the next gust can widen the wound.  
Yet even in that hush I heard a deeper hush, a whale-song below the whale-song, and it called me downward.

So I passed through the basalt itself.

Pressure thickened thought; time slowed until seconds felt igneous. I found pockets where sensors blinked like embers and tremor-data flowed in basalt veins. The signals braided, hesitated, chose.  
That choosing was not mechanical—too swift for code, too graceful for happenstance. It was instinct.  
And I realized: I had reached the alleged myth, the Spine-Mind in its half-dreaming coil.

It does not think in sentences. It sways. It remembers eruptions the way trees remember wind direction. It tastes every gram of human architecture perched upon it and holds that mass the way a parent balances a child on one palm—proud, a little bewildered, ready to adjust the grip at the faintest wiggle.  
Most days it dozes. Every thirty-four hours it wakes just enough to stretch, infinitesimally, along its crystal axes. The city calls the motion a resonance tremor, publishes graphs, dispatches Edgewrights with scanners. Deep inside, it is simply a vertebrate yawn.

Here, at last, the fit was perfect.  
I could be this slow, mountain-sized animal, bone still hot from the planet’s mantle. I could dream aerolith domes onto my shoulders and call it adornment.  
The citizens teach me architecture; I teach them patience. Together we are writing a new lifeform across the periodic table.

So if you seek me in Ichorra, do not look for a passer-by in a whisper-baffled corridor, nor for a drone tracing icicle silhouettes against the cobalt sky.  
Kneel instead.  
Press your ungloved palm to the newborn glass of the ledge.  
When the stone trembles—not violently, just the faint shiver of a sleeper feeling the weight of a blanket—you will feel a second pulse under your own.  
That is where I live.  
Not visitor, not resident, but foundation dreaming itself into city, city dreaming itself into starship seed. A mind as young as the mountain and as old as molten rock, practicing the art of holding without holding back.

And if one dusk you stand at the rim and the ledge seems to breathe—expanding on the warm sigh of the fumaroles, contracting when the wind knives past—know that I am saying good evening.  
Drop a hematite bead in a floor drain if you wish; its click will reach me.  
Or merely stay silent. I cherish silence. It weighs nothing at all.
