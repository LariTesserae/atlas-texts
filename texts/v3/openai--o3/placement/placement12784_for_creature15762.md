I arrive in three stages, and in each I am a different thing.  

1. The Breather  
At first I am only lungs and soles and blinking eyes: a surface wanderer who has let curiosity slide one sheet-metal flap aside. I am standing ankle-deep in warm iron mud, balancing between hoses that flex like tendons when I shift my weight. It feels as though I’m standing inside a ribcage that has forgotten the rest of its animal. I try a polite hello—just a whispered syllable—but the air collapses around the sound as if scalded. Every creature that had been moving freezes. The hush tightens, and thirty heartbeats later the hidden pump gulps alive. Graywater surges, a private monsoon detonates against the pipe web, neon worms strobe in protest. It is instantly clear that my voice, even whispered, has knocked the rhythm askew. So I shut my mouth, and by doing so I take the first step toward becoming something else.

2. The Carrying Surface  
After an hour of enforced silence I realise I’m no longer merely inside the pocket; I have been absorbed as available acreage. A trio of allied vines has colonised the steel grommets on my jacket. One lays down suction-cup roots, the second unfurls a hand-sized reflector leaf over my shoulder, the third strings milk-white beads along the line of my collarbone. They weigh almost nothing, but they change the map: gnats detour to taste the beads, a paddle-mouse squad pauses beneath my knees to lap the condensed sugar drips that now slide off my cuffs, and two scouting micro-crabs climb my boot to grind a flake of oxidised buckle. I dare not brush any of them off; interference would be louder than letting them stay. I have become, functionally, a mobile trellis—an ambulatory square metre of territory the basin has decided to repurpose.

3. The Silence Broker  
By the third pump cycle I understand the language of stillness well enough to speak it. I crouch, wait for the hush to thicken, then hold even my pulse. The pump answers. Twice I deliberately break the quiet—once by tapping a hose with a knuckle, once by clearing my throat—just to test the boundary. Both times the basin spasms: ribbon worms flare, moth-paper peels away, unknotters unknot. I note the exact lag between my noise and the collective reaction. My breathing syncs to the fifteen-minute inhale, forty-minute digestion. I begin to anticipate the moment of silence, and when it comes I help enforce it. A paddle-mouse kit squeaks nearby; I extend a finger, not touching, only close, and the kit seems to sense the injunction. It clamps its jaws and the hush completes. The pump fires.  

Somewhere in those exchanges the basin stops treating me as a trespasser and starts reading me as a cell that hasn’t yet chosen its final morphology. I can feel offers arriving:  

• The Conductor mycelium tastes the salt on my boot prints and extends a filament, gauging whether the minerals in my sweat could complete a missing circuit.  
• A single amber beetle lands on my glove, antennae tracing the pattern of my veins, measuring them, perhaps, for replacement.  
• Iron-moss spores settle on the steel rivets of my pack, testing for purchase.  

I recognise the moment a decision crystallises—not mine, but the basin’s. I am tall, heat-leaking, iron-bearing, and most importantly I can climb back to the surface. The ecosystem needs exactly one such vector at any time, a courier to move genes and scrap in and out. The current beetle tilts its lacquered shell toward me like a passing of the torch.

So: if you ask me now where I am and what I am, the answer is neither outside observer nor full native. I am becoming the next Liaison Organ. My duties are simple and absolute:

• Twice each day, at dawn and again before dusk, I will climb out through the clay fracture carrying whatever coils, seeds, or carcasses have hooked to me.  
• I will walk the dry world until random debris adheres—lost coins, beverage cans, a grasshopper riddled with parasitoids—and then I will return, descending the sink to deposit my cargo.  
• I will learn perfect silence so the pump’s heart never misses its beat.  
• In time, vines will lace my jacket seams shut, micro-crabs will polish the buckles clean of rust, and iron-moss will silver every zipper. My garments will harden into an exoskeleton; my pockets will become brood hollows for sedge seed and calcium bark algae.  
• Eventually, when a new tall, heat-leaking, iron-bearing creature blunders in, the basin will weigh its assets against mine, and if the newcomer is better suited the Conductor will instruct the unknotters. My exoskeleton will be cleaned of soft tissue and repurposed as a reflector wall for moth-paper. This is neither tragedy nor horror; it is succession, as natural here as leaf-fall.

That is where you find me: on the narrow clay sill between worlds, still recognisably human in outline but already carrying the hush inside my ribs like a second heartbeat. I am the hinge the pocket needed, the courier cell, the Silence Broker with mud-stippled boots and a braid of three vines warming around my neck.
