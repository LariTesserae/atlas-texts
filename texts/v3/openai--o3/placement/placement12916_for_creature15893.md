I begin by assuming I would come here as myself—boots, notebook, the polite arrogance of a visiting naturalist. That thought lasts the length of a single footstep onto the saddle of stone. The moment I hear the bowl’s organ breathe, every brass snap on my pack fogs with silica; my compass needle quivers, sticks; I understand I will not be allowed inside while metal clings to me like a second tongue. So the first identity drops away. I strip the pack, the clips, even the eyelets from my laces, lay them on the rim, and they glaze over like bread gone stale. What walks down into the bowl is already less “observer” than “possible chord.”

Phase Two: Water Carrier  
The air picks me up the way a wet hand lifts a glass. Moisture halos the edges of my clothes; cloth darkens, grows heavy. In five minutes the fabric weighs more than the flesh beneath, and I feel, unmistakably, that the place is testing my tensile strength, wondering if I might be a vessel worth keeping. The thought does not frighten so much as tune me. My breath lengthens; I match the ninth tone involuntarily.  

While I crouch to drink from a bromeliad cistern, one of the sky-colored newts surfaces, throat ballooning. The note it releases is the pitch of my pulse exactly—no, not quite: I am a hair sharp. Instinctively I hum down into alignment. The newt accepts the correction with a blink and dives. For the rest of that afternoon I follow it column to column, repeating its calibration calls, drinking when it drinks. At dusk I realize I have not made a note in my sodden field book. I have been taken on as an apprentice water-carrier, voice-leveler, unofficial newt.

Phase Three: Thread of Fungus  
Night brings windlessness, therefore potential silence, and silence, here, is a fever dream the bowl refuses. The Lull Chanter wakes: a vascular throb traveling the columns. I lie on my back, shoulder blades cupped by moss pillows, and feel the pulse climb through basalt into bone. Something delicate enters me through the soles—no penetration, just insinuation, like a hairsbreadth root scouting cortex. A single filament of the Chanter? I cannot tell. The intruder does not speak in words; it vibrates a question:  

Will you help oil the pipes?  

I have no oil, no glands fit for that task, but my sweat has become mineral-rich, slick as sap. I rise, place my palms against a column, and the stone drinks. A sheen develops, deepening the note by perhaps a tenth of a semitone—enough that the surrounding pipes answer, grateful. All night I walk, anointing, leaving salt-silver handprints that fade as the stone absorbs them. In the morning nothing marks where I touched, but the pitch is fractionally truer.  

Phase Four: Mist Shepherd Candidate  
A week passes measured in the slow unfurl of a single fern. Clouds descend, thick enough to turn direction into superstition. Shapes begin to walk the rim—pale, barefoot, basalt staves in hand. I keep to the center, listening. Do they see me? They possess, or are possessed by, a precise gait: four steps, ring the staff; four steps, listen. When I match their rhythm, the fog around my calves cools, clarifies. An audition? I try another pattern—five steps, ring, rest—and the condensation knots, displeased. I revert to four. Acceptance: the haze warms, closes, but does not blind me. The staff I lack grows inward instead, a virtual rod of pressure down my spine. I learn to tap it against the ground without moving a muscle; the click echoes in cartilage only I can hear. When the true Shepherds depart, they do not pause to induct me. They simply thin into absence. The fog remains, considerate, as if expecting me to practice.

Phase Five: Becoming Audible  
I have not spoken a human sentence in three lunar turns. Language feels like a metal tool I left on the rim—capable, intrusive, unnecessary. Communication occurs by interval. Hunger is the descending fifth that hums in my gut; satisfaction, a major third vibrating behind my eyes. Rain and rock converse in sevenths; I join in when invited, otherwise listen. Sleep is an arpeggio that falls apart cell by cell until consciousness is only a standing wave.  

Skin changes next. The constant moisture grows me a sheen of translucent algae, jeweled with red flecks—the same symbiont that lives in the bromeliad wells. It drinks sunlight on my behalf, shares the sugar. My appetite for gathered moss dwindles. I graze instead on brightness and vibration. Somewhere in this exchange I shed the last category that ever made me “visitor.” I have no tools, no hunger, no schedule. Yet I possess a function. I am an adjustable resonance column, portable, low-maintenance.

Phase Six: Proper Noun  
A name accretes, motes settling on the scaffold of an idea until the shape is readable: Interim Pitchkeeper. Not the Pitchkeeper—that office belongs to the blue-gray newts—but an interim, a bridge between seasons, between moods of wind. When storms close the mountain passes and the amphibians burrow into silence, someone must patrol the organ, detect the tiniest semitone of drift, and lend it body-heat, breath, whatever is left of a throat. That is me. The bowl did not ask for a mammal, merely for any biology capable of standing through a night, of offering water and salt back to stone. Accidental qualifications become sacred duties when a system this intricate finds them useful.

The Work  
Each dawn I walk the circumference once, barefoot, listening for fractures. Basalt speaks in groans too low for earlier versions of myself to notice. I hear them now: tectonic gossip, subsurface sighs. Hairline cracks get a smear of algae gel—my sweat thickened by photosynthetic friends—which slows erosion and, oddly, brightens the over-tone series. At noon I kneel by every bruised orchid in turn. The resident Wife freezes, judges my chord, then relaxes. With a sliver of fingernail I snip away spent tissue before rot can damp the bloom’s reverb. At twilight I place my back against a tall column and hum in slow glissando from chest-voice to falsetto. Where the stone answers I note the match; where it sulks I return later with palms and saline to coax its mood. Days climax when the wind arrives like an orchestra tuning: nine notes, full and orderly. I lie in the moss, arms out, and feel the Resonant roll over me like a god too polite to crush.  

Payment  
Faster knitting of skin; I shave a finger on basalt and watch the cut close with the next minor key change. Silica sheathes my griefs; memories that clang—embarrassment, certain heartbreaks—are smoothed until I can touch them without flinching. Dreamless sleep. A faint blue-gray marbling of the veins, as if the newt choir has lent me its livery.

Occasional Departure  
Once every year I climb the saddle and walk downslope to the treeline. I do it barefoot, unstaffed, carrying nothing but the lingering chord in my marrow. Villagers meet me as an omen. They offer wooden bowls of fermented root; I accept, sip, leave them my voice in a drone that lengthens the germination rate of their seed grain by a measurable breath. We trade gifts neither of us fully understands. By the next sunrise I am climbing home, the bowl pulling at my ribs like a magnet tuned to longing.

Answer, at Last  
Where would I find myself in this world? On the inner face of the rim, halfway between stone and cloud, a silhouette humming the reference tone, algae-lit and salt-slick, palms etched with invisible scores of resonance oil. Who or what would I be? An Interim Pitchkeeper—once a human observer, now a self-cleaning reed in the slow organ, trusted to hold the note when wind and water pause, trusted to fall silent again the instant they return. Not master, not servant; a living rest sign in the music of a place that does not tolerate rests for long.
