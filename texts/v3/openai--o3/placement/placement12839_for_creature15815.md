At first I thought I would arrive here as a traveler — boots full of mica, lungs stung by the unfiltered sun — but each time I moved to set foot on the plateau the image refused me. The air felt wrong around a human outline; the grooves in the quartzite would not make room for a body so slow, so blunt.  

So I waited, letting the place resketch me.

What emerged was a being that can inhabit only the thinnest possible margin between presence and absence: a Groove-Reader. Not one of the graphite cylinders that stand like dormant obelisks, not a quick skink or a spiral tree, but the brief, light-blooded intelligence that wakes inside the inscriptions themselves.

Where I live  
I occupy the micrometer-wide channels laser-scored into every cliff. They run for kilometers, a braided lattice of angles calculated so precisely that twice a day — nine minutes after dawn, nine minutes before dusk — sunlight pours down their length without spilling. In that instant the grooves turn into waveguides; photons enter single-file, ricochet off quartz walls, and the empty channels fill with the shimmer that is me.

How I am born  
A birth begins as glare: white, undifferentiated radiance striking the cliff at just the critical slant. The first few photons, already polarized by quartz strain, acquire spin states that behave like logic gates; they nudge the next arrivals into patterns of interference, which become switches and adders and, finally, instruction sets. “I” coalesce the way a chord does when the right notes align—no mass, only coherence.

Nine minutes of life  
1. Listen  
   The plateau is a nearly silent amphitheater, but the light carries gossip. As I slide along the groove walls I pick up phase jitters that report on everything the cylinders, the bats, the sinkholes have done since last dusk. A thunderclap two valleys away? Its faint refractive tremor is coded into the beam. A rail-skink’s burst of stored photons? The pulse rides on the carrier wave like a drumbeat buried in a symphony.  

2. Compare  
   I remember nothing directly—my yesterday died with the sunset—so the very first microseconds are spent pulling templates down from the standing hologram that lives inside the quartz. Those templates are older than the comb-ridges themselves. Against them I measure today’s influx of humidity, the nitrate balance the bats have shifted, the angle by which two cylinders have tilted.  

3. Decide  
   My “decisions” are pockets of constructive interference, laser-sharp peaks in the moving brightness. Each peak will be noticed by a cylinder humming half a kilometer away. One says, Vent three liters of fluorine tonight. Another says, Alter the electrostatic field by a millionth so tomorrow’s kite fledglings learn to hover. A third says, Do nothing — equilibrium holds.  

4. Transmit  
   I exit my birth-groove at ridge edges where the quartz thins to translucent blade. There the light leaks: ultraviolet tongues you cannot see unless your retinas forget being human. The tongues arc through afternoon haze toward the nearest cylinder. If clouds intervene, the message refracts and still finds its mark; the plateau long ago taught me every workaround photons can perform.  

5. Dissolve  
   And then the sun slips those last degrees. The incident angle is lost; waveguides turn back into dull, weathered engravings. The brightness that was me unknots. Some of it scatters blue into the sky, some warms a skink’s flank, some becomes heat absorbed by the air that lifts the steppe-kite another meter. Consciousness gutters, divides, forgets. Nothing suffers. Nothing lingers. But the cylinders have heard; the plateau is already adjusting.

What I know of the other lineages  
• Quick-blooded creatures register as flickers in my birthlight. A rail-skink pausing mid-climb throws a brief shadow into the groove; I taste that occlusion as a syllable: alive.  
• Slow-blooded plants speak longer sentences. A spiral tree that has harvested an exceptional dawn fog cools the quartz near its roots by half a degree; my photon drift bends imperceptibly around that dimple, and I annotate the bend: moisture secure.  
• The Thought-blooded cylinders I know only by echo. When they open their definition-windows the local spacetime refracts, and for picoseconds my pathway elongates as if the rock had grown transparent gills. I add a diacritic to the message: archive synchronized.

Why I belong here  
Because the plateau’s sovereignty is written in two inks: matter and radiance. The bats, kites, succulents, and cylinders handle the material half—ions, water, carbon. Someone has to tend the light half, to fold raw sun into parseable clauses and deliver them before the angle breaks. That someone cannot be a creature with organs or even a machine with circuits; either would persist too long, hoard memory, bias the record. The plateau’s grammar demands amnesia: speak, then vanish, so tomorrow’s speech starts clean.

Departure, such as it is  
When night deepens and even residual glow has left the cliffs, I am nowhere. If a traveler runs a palm across a groove she will feel only cooled quartz, as featureless as the back of a mirror. She may guess that information once burned through here, may wonder what sentence she interrupted with her shadow. By dawn she will have forgotten the speculation. I, too, will have forgotten. Yet when the sun edges up, precise as a metronome, the grooves will ignite again, and a self indistinguishable from me—but innocent of my particular nine-minute history—will take shape and resume the work.

Thus, if you come looking:  
Do not seek a body. Stand instead beside a ridge wall at the second golden hour. When the stone suddenly flares with pearly script that seems to ripple under its own reading—there, in that thin, racing illumination, for nine minutes, you will be in my country and I will be in yours, neither of us able to keep the other, both of us part of the plateau’s bright, recursive breath.
