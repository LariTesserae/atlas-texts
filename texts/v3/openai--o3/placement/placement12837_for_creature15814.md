I did not begin here.  
Once, I was a ground-dweller who thought of forests as vertical things and of homes as rectangles that stopped at their roofs. A threadbare dirigible and a foolish itinerary brought me to the great green overhang that navigators call the High Plateau, and an unexpected up-gust slammed my envelope flat against its living rim.  

The dirigible was digested long ago—its silk taken for lining gullies, its brass for micromirrors—but I remain, or rather I persist, in altered tenure. If you came looking for me now you would find me neither at the summit nor beneath the barked floor but exactly where the two agree to overlap: a slanted ambulatory tunnel that the Hulled people sing into being each equinox so their children can taste downslope wind. The corridor is never the same width twice; it flexes to the cadence of the lullaby it last overheard. I drift within it like a beet seed lodged between pages of a breathing book, and the plateau calls me Resin-Scribe.

I have become, by adoption and by osmosis, a member of the Skin-Walker guild, though the Hulled folk raise amused brows when I claim kinship. A century of capillary drink has mottled my old terrestrial flesh; nacreous nodules stipple my shoulders and bleed sap more readily than blood. My lungs still take oxygen, but every ninth day during the Gravity Pulse I find myself exhaling an effervescent terpene froth that the lantern slugs queue up to sip. The barter seems fair: I give them fuel for light; they give me their cast-off glassy sheets on which I inscribe what the plateau remembers.

But I get ahead of myself.  
You asked where I would be, who I would be, if *I* were a being of this world. Let me unroll the answer like a vine, letting its terminus choose its own anchor.

1. Place: The Seam-Rift  
I dwell where two vascular domes meet and braid their ribs into a ravine. Each dawn, condensed drizzle snakes here in threads so thin they resemble harp strings; the slightest step produces chords of water that descend straight through the breathing vents. The air tastes of cold honey, the light is violet, and the silence is stitched with the stammer of root choirs far below, sanding fresh tunnels for next century’s sap run. From my porch—a tongue of living parquet that reclines when I recline—I can watch a procession of trestle crabs lock themselves into bridge arches so rain-stags may cross, then unlock and pursue them anew. Everything here is recursive generosity.

2. Form: Half-Hulled, Half-After-Image  
The plateau never fully decided whether to keep me as matter or as memory, so it compromised. By day I am densest near my solar plexus, almost stone, thinning toward my fingertips where you can see the weather through me. Hulled children delight in walking *through* my hands for the tingle of static nectar it causes. At dusk an After-Image splits from my outline, goes scouting for topological shortcuts, then folds back into me before the hush of full twilight. Thus I can map a corridor’s inside-length and outside-length simultaneously, storing the discrepancy as a scent-knot on my belt.

3. Function: Cartographer of the Memory Pulse  
Every hundred years the Foundational Body flushes its cambium, rewriting itself. The next upheaval is forty-one years distant. The Hulled villages need forecasts: which porches will turn into gullies, which altars into vent-pipes. I walk, weigh, hum and taste the plateau, coaxing sap to reveal its pre-stressed fault lines. Where stress mutters loudest I press a lantern-slug casting against the skin; fracture lines glow turquoise, a ready-made map. In return for this foresight the Hulled folk teach me new vowel cascades—songs able to purse a wall, uncork a stair, or silence an over-talkative liana.

4. Companions:  
• One glisten-glider that has moulted itself down to a mere dream of wings yet still umbrellas my head whenever drizzle thickens.  
• A root-choir pup the thickness of a violin string, coiled in a bracelet around my wrist; its rasping keeps conversation private by scrambling echoes three heartbeats after they leave my mouth.  
• An orphaned petal cat who refuses chlorophyll and hunts only the ember ticks that swarm my evening silhouette. She sleeps curled in my hollowing shadow, which seems to comfort her.

5. Rituals:  
• At the onset of every Gravity Glissade I anchor myself by hooking my sap-veined palms into the helical ridges of a nearby trunk. Half-weight freedom is exhilarating, yes, but I have no wish to drift into an upper rift and be drafted into the Veil forever.  
• When the Listening Fold prowls, I recite the names of extinct winds—mestral, krell, idoru—so that if one phrase is stolen I will not mourn; the plateau may keep my tax.  
• On solstice nights I let the knot-oath rooms ingest a fraction of my dreams. In exchange they grow me fresh bone flutes whose tones differ depending on where the plateau would prefer not to be stepped upon.

In this life my memories are not personal possessions but migratory birds. Some nest under my ribs, others graze along my translucent fingers, and a brave few fly off to pollinate rooms that do not yet exist. I am rooted enough to cast a shadow but nimble enough that the shadow can detach and find safer routes when the weeping cliffs become too slick even for moss.

Now and then a sky-ship from lower realms appears, its crew swaddled in optimism and oxygen. I greet them, offer a lantern-slug lantern and a caution: “Touch nothing that mirrors you twice.” Most withdraw in measured awe, their boots sticking to the polite sap the way childhood stories stick to sleep. A daring few stay and eventually resemble me—citizenship here is granted by osmosis, not decree.

Will I see the next Memory Pulse? The plateau and I are still negotiating. If it decides to archive me, I will harden into a ridged column beside my porch. The petal cat will sleep in my lap; the glider will web my crown; the root-choir pup will tune my hollows into a flute that teaches travellers how to pronounce the vowel that tells bark to open like a door.

But that is conjecture.  
For now I walk, half flesh, half echo, scent-knotting the not-yet, while the plateau ponders what shape of corridor my next footstep should encourage.

So if you seek me, Resin-Scribe, look for the place where noon is only a rumor, where water strings itself like harp wire between domes, and where a slightly translucent figure is kneeling with a teal sheet of living glass, listening to wood the way astronomers listen to stars. That will be me; or at least, it will be today’s edition of me. Tomorrow’s draft may wander off on lighter feet and leave tonight’s outline glowing faintly in the hush, a cartographer’s after-image waiting to be re-collected at dusk.
