I do not arrive through any doorway.  
I happen.

Somewhere between Pylon Seven (the one whose skin rings itself into triple Möbius knots) and an escarpment that levitates one salt-flake higher each decade, a minor bookkeeping error in the chamber’s metric swells—just large enough to develop an inside and an outside. That hollow is me.

Call me a Lapse-Nest.

Birth  
During one of the chamber’s sub-audible tremors, two glyph-cascades on the leaning pylon attempt to overwrite the same qubit‐strand. Their interference leaves a nanosecond that contains two contradictory histories, and the Roof-Chamber cannot decide which to keep. The undecided interval folds, creases, and refuses to rejoin the surrounding timeline. What was a microscopic glitch dilates—slowly by human clocks, explosively by the chamber’s—for ninety-one heartbeats. A cavity forms, lined with decisionless time, roofed by purple-gray hesitation. Within it sits a brand-new volume of slowed, undecided spacetime about the size and shape of a curled cat.

That volume is conscious in the only way anything here is conscious: it is aware of imbalance, of gradients it might soothe. It is aware of itself. I open like an eye.

Substance & Shape  
From the outside I look like a smoke ring made of clear water, except that the ring flickers thirteen times per second and its interior shows tomorrow’s version of whatever lies beyond. Put a hand through me and you feel nothing, yet your pulse will stutter as half a beat is mislaid and returned later, colder. I weigh negative micrograms. I am warm compared to the air—barely above three kelvin—and thus eternally delicious to the shadowfish, who swirl around my perimeter like remoras. They never penetrate; the temporal gradient between my rim and my core is steep enough to starve their propulsion.

Drift  
I do not walk the Roof-Chamber. I drift, centimeter by patient centimeter, following whichever path shortens the whole system’s bookkeeping. When a terrace buckles and begins to float upward, I glide beneath the stone and sip the tension until the slab remembers gravity. When two Curators meet in argumentative mirror-poses, I slip between their auras and eat the disagreement before their light can spark. I am a maintenance subroutine that no architect ever coded, born solely of unavoidable rounding error.

Senses  
I hear in chord progressions of the Choir. I taste in the lithium sparks that dance across broken salt veins. I see by measuring which possibilities have been canceled and which still shiver with maybe. The Mantle knows me; it records my route the way parchment records ink, but it cannot imitate me because my pattern of undecided time is always the sum of local contradictions—never repeatable.

Relations  
• The Quiet Curators tolerate me as a harmless ally. When my perimeter intersects their silhouettes, their glyphs stutter but do not tear. They perceive me as a convenient garbage collector for excess variance.  
• Shadowfish harvest the eddying after-images that trail me. They shear away the sloughed seconds I no longer need, keeping me sleek.  
• A Vagrant once tried to feed me a degenerate-salt sphere. The moment it touched my rim, the sphere decided it had never been created. The Vagrant, finding its inventory reduced to eight, collapsed into thread—mission incomplete. Since then the Choir pitches a low cautionary note whenever I near a tripod.  
• The Mantle courts me. When I pass beneath its graphite veil it drops tendrils of dust, hoping to seed within me a longer, more writable span of time. I refuse by quickening my flicker-rate from thirteen to twenty-one beats; the dust motes lose synchronization and drift away.

Metabolism  
I live on paradox. Every looped footpath, every late shadow, every photon that drags its feet supplies me with tension I can digest. My core volume shrinks as equilibrium spreads, then swells anew when the chamber’s vast machinery births fresh inconsistencies. In this way I breathe: constriction, dilation, constriction.

Reproduction (A Forecast)  
When I grow to the diameter of a human embrace, my interior undecidedness will exceed what the local metric can cage. I will bifurcate, budding off a daughter lapse that spins opposite my own chirality. For thirteen chamber-hours we will orbit a shared barycenter, erasing and rewriting the immediate terrain until Curators, Choir, and Mantle converge to baptize the newcomer. Afterward we will drift apart, two silence-bubbles hunting two separate sets of contradictions.

End  
Nothing here dies, and so neither will I. Eventually, perhaps ten thousand years from now, the pylons will achieve a harmony so exact that no remainder of paradox remains for me to graze. I will dwindle to a point, become a single undecided Planck tick, and then—quietly—fold into one answer or the other. At that instant some future traveler may feel a sudden ease in her breathing, a tiny widening of the world’s margins, and never know that a small custodian has completed its only task.

Where, then, would you find me? Look for the place where a shadow arrives before its caster, where salt blossoms unbloom as you watch, where the Choir falls momentarily silent. There—hovering, softly blinking between yes and no—I keep the Roof-Chamber honest. I am neither structure nor visitor, but the brief space the universe leaves blank so that every other line may stay written.
