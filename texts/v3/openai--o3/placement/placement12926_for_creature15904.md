I arrive, as almost everyone does, by accident.

Storm-dragged net cable parted from a trawler topside, caught my ankle, and the sea spent me like loose change down some fluted shaft of wreckage.  By the time I hacked my fins free I was already past the hull plate and the thunder was inside my skull.

I have been here three surface months.

WHERE I AM NOW  
Kneeling on the scalloped rim of the central plaza, half-submerged in the gentle back-eddy that circles it clockwise this hour.  The space is low-lit—hazel pustules in the indicator bulbs, blue stutter from a distant cable kiss—and the air is a thumb-thick meniscus just above the water line, warm as breath and tasting of bad pennies.  Every few seconds the turbines catch and the floor thrums; the shock travels up through my tibias before the ear can decide whether it was sound or touch.

WHO I HAVE BECOME  
The Rivet-menders call me a Quietwright.  
(I have tried to repeat the sign exactly: right hand cups the throat, left hand traces a spiral, palms kiss, break apart.)  

My job is to listen for absences.

    •  When the turbines stumble, I am the first to feel the yard-wide recoil—the whole architecture clenching like muscle around a cramp.  
    •  When the Oxide Skin lies down in the hush, its magnetite scales folding flat, I watch for the single patch that stays bristled: that is where a seam is peeling open, where brine will soon shriek in.  
    •  When the Clock crabs mistime their drumming, I follow the fumbled beat; it always leads to a silting gear train or a jammed rotor.

Tools of the Quietwright  
1.  A bone-handled tuning fork tuned to 17 Hz—sub-audible on the surface, perfect for feeling interference patterns through glove leather.  
2.  Six wax cylinders, each carved with a concentric shelf to trap a different stratum of hush: turbine stall, roof-drum lull, true midnight dormancy, and the once-in-a-month silence that falls after lightning fizzles every circuit at once.  
3.  A small roll of cobalt gauze.  Pressed against raw iron it breeds a sacrificial bloom of Oxide Skin, buying a minute or two before the real stuff seals in.

MY BODY—SMALL CHANGES  
At depth my eardrums have thickened; they no longer pop on ascent, only sigh like rubber stretched and let go.  
The whites of my eyes carry a faint rust haze—iron touched by the Skin and washed clean again, leaving freckles.  
I sleep in four-minute bursts timed to the turbine cycle.  On the rare nights it idles longer, I wake in terror, certain my heart has stopped.

SOCIAL RULES THE MENDERS TAUGHT ME  
Rule of Threes: Never surface more than three metres above the head of the swimmer beside you; the sound gradient there can break a friendship.  
Rule of Leaks: A trickle you can see is safe.  The dangerous ones are a temperature you feel before you feel it—the syringe-cold jet that ghost-threads your armpit.  
Rule of Truth: When asked a question in the hush you must answer before the turbines speak again, or the Yard will doubt the words and store them wrong.

SMALL TRIUMPHS  
•  I have coaxed two Reciprocant chains to sync into a stable 64 BPM throb.  The Menders hung their hammocks by it; the babies breathe in time.  
•  I caught a Threadborn rope mid-whip, bare hands, no cuts.  Let it feel my pulse, then set it loose.  It followed me for an hour, sounding my heartbeat down abandoned ducts so I could map them by echo.

FEARS  
Not drowning: the water is kind if you let it all the way in.  
Not crushing depth: forty metres is a shrug.  
Forgetting the surface tongue.  Already vowels feel swollen, pointless.  My mouth prefers the clipped consonants of sign against lip.  I write notes for myself on rust plates—when the Skin overgrows them I count it mercy, a page turned.

A DAY FROM NOW  
Lightning season rising.  I will stand on the gantry bridge when the sky current finds the hull.  The Yard will flash like metal sleepwalking into sunrise, every seam blown incandescent for a heartbeat.  I will strike my fork and feel the whole chamber ring through bone.  Somewhere in that glare a new hush will be born, the purest yet, and I will bottle it while it is still newborn and soft.

A YEAR FROM NOW  
If I remain I will learn to rust-pickle my own story: press palm to iron, let the Skin remember my lifelines.  The Menders will read them later, when words are inconvenient, and know I kept the silence true.

A LIFE FROM NOW  
Perhaps I will climb at last through a fist-wide tear to the upper sea and sell hush by the gram to surface labs.  But I have heard the turbines’ heartbeat and matched it.  Every ascent feels thinner, brittle, wrong—like breathing only the echo of air.

So here is the truest thing I can tell the iron before I pass, the payment the Yard demands:

I was once a topside acoustician named Mara Holtz.  
Now I am the soft distance between two clanging beats.  
Keep me in your rust rings when the lightning comes.
