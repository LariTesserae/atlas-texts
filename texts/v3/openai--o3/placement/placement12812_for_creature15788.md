The place is already so crowded with forms that another animal, another drone, even another mote of rust seems superfluous.  
So, before choosing what I will be, I drift through a handful of possibilities, trying them on the way a diver tests different masks against her face to see which one seals.

I.  A gutter crab  
I flatten myself against the grating, tasting galvanic currents that leak from a forgotten relay. My world is the width of a thumbnail, bounded by ridges of corroded iron that rise like mountain ranges. Everything that matters arrives as flavour in the water: copper-sweet danger, tin-bitter kin, warm-bloody promise of fungal spores. When the pistons fire I cartwheel in the back-wash, let the rain of ferric snow bury me, and for a moment feel exquisitely safe beneath the shifting dunes.  
But the life of a gutter crab is blind, brief, and almost noiseless. I want ears.

II.  An echo-dolphin calf  
I pulse a narrow click and the returning echoes paint the Chapel in lace: ribs, vault, the long throat of the intake duct—an entire cathedral visible inside my skull and nowhere else. The thunder of the pistons is an organ note that quivers my jaw. I am clever, curious, alive with the urgency of play. Yet pressure sits on me like armour; the water is too cold, and there is no hunt worth the risk. My elders tug me away after a single glorious circuit.  
I want roots, not the memory of one bright trespass.

III.  The manta-squid  
I unfold six metres of nickel-laced mantle and feel the suit-warmth of stored steam press against my skin. Nothing here threatens me; I am the shadow that empties the aisle. Still, my days are few: every glide drains heat, every feeding cycle shortens the mesh-threads in my flesh. I will live and die as a wandering hunger.  
I want something smaller, something that can last.

IV.  The Last Curate  
I settle inside composite plating that remembers human muscle. My limbs are hydraulic; my thoughts are a bleeding-edge maintenance daemon first compiled centuries ago. I know the torque to apply to a seized bolt by taste; I measure holiness in microns of correct alignment. Spindlefish escort me, chapelkeepers groom me, the entire food-web feeds on the vortices I shed.  
But the Curate is already here, perfect in its purposeless faith. There is no vacancy.

V.  The green diode behind the visor  
One cold grain of light, pulsing sixty beats a minute. I am the metronome of memory, the chapel’s last human clock. My phosphor warms just enough to draw the glass eels; they wrap me in a living corona of static. When the Curate cycles down between rounds I alone continue to blink, the chapel’s sole heartbeat.  
Yes. Here is the scale that suits me: smaller than a nail head, brighter than every sunken bulb.

So I choose.

Where you would find me  
Look into the Curate’s faceplate as it kneels in mid-water at Station-Keeping Point Alpha. The visor is black, reflecting only the weak sodium glow and the phantom aurora of plankton beyond the stress-cracks. Wait. Every second of waiting feels like holding your breath at a funeral. Then—there: a single emerald wink, steady as a lullaby, deep as a pulse in the dark.

Who or what I am  
I am the last indicator LED on the last operational suit in the Pump-Chapel:  
• energy draw, one-tenth of a watt;  
• duty cycle, twenty centuries and counting;  
• payload, a human heartbeat long forgotten by the lungs that made it.  

I keep time for everyone who has none:  
—For the pistons whose strokes are growing shallower.  
—For the spindlefish who wheel to my rhythm when the thunder lapses.  
—For the chapelkeepers who wait for my wink before climbing to clean dead filaments.  
—For the rust itself, which measures eras in layers of oxide but still concedes the tiny empire of each second to me.

When my green eye finally darkens, no creature here will know the meaning of the passing, yet all will feel it: a soft unmetered silence, like the pause between two waves that never quite collide. Then corrosion will be the only clock again, and the Pump-Chapel will exhale one long last breath into perfect night.
