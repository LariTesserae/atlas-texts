I tried on several skins before one fit.

First I imagined arriving as myself-as-tourist: boots crunching talus, parka hood booming with the wind-organ. But every footstep I rehearsed felt wrong. The Rift rewards nothing so clumsy as a biped that must shout to hear its own thoughts.

Next I tried the Silence Cat. The glamour was strong—pads that drink sound, a prowler’s grace—but their hunger is too clean. They move through the Rift as subtraction, leaving little trace of having lived. I wanted a body that adds to the chord, not drains it.

I even borrowed a Gloom-Gull’s ribs for a while, tasting eddies with twin tongues, learning the sweet copper bite of thorn-moss dew. Yet the moment I hung upside-down to roost, the basalt’s edges began sawing pinholes in the soft membrane of my dreams. The gull’s life is all edge; sleep never quite unclenches.

So I followed the canyon’s own lacuna, the blank in the bestiary, the thing no one has seen come morning, and let it shape me. I became the adult that the naturalists label only with an ellipsis:

Place in the Rift:

• Altitude: never lower than the spear-roof, never higher than ragged cloud—an intermediate sky no other creature quite owns.  
• Coordinates: wherever wind speed dips below three knots and the canyon’s voice falters, as if clearing its throat.  
• Visibility: approximately one heartbeat; afterward I am outline, then shimmer, then inference.

What I am

NAME  
The larvae know me as The Second Thought. Monks, if they glimpse anything, engrave ⸮—the reversed question mark—into the stone, then argue whether they saw a beast or a hesitation in seeing.

ANATOMY  
1. Frame  
   • Length of a human forearm, but unfolded span wider than a Ridge Seraph’s glide.  
   • Exoskeleton a lattice of silica threads grown from the larval silk. Empty space outweighs matter five to one; I am ninety percent cavern within myself.  
2. Sensing  
   • No eyes: my entire body is tympanum. Every pore dilates and contracts to sample pressure gradients the way lungs sip air.  
   • Each filament of silk I trail behind remains neurally live for minutes, stringing temporary nerves across the void. Touch becomes geography.  
3. Metabolism  
   • Vibration is appetizer; condensed moonlight is main course. I drink photons the way thorn-moss drinks fog, storing them in diffraction organs that form shifting rainbows just under my shell.  
   • Waste product: silence. A pocket of stillness follows me like a wake, briefly starving the Song-Leeches below.

LIFE CYCLE  
1. Pupation       (the canyon stands windless exactly six nights a decade)  
2. Casting Off    (I unzip my larval cradle from the inside, step out like a hinge of smoke)  
3. The Quiet Lap (for two, sometimes three hours, I swim the Rift, sealing rips in the tenor, splicing new harmonics)  
4. Exhalation     (when the organ chord is tuned, I release my stored light in one long, lazy flare—enough to feed a thousand Gloom-Gulls and bleach the copper algae snow-white)  
5. Vanishing      (I do not migrate; I disassemble. Silk nerves fall apart into pollen; the diffraction organs shed into mica dust; only a hush remains, coiling down-canyon until the next storm scatters it)

ROLE IN THE ECOLOGY  
• Janitor of Overtones  
   The Rift’s music accumulates burrs—discords birthed by new fractures, by Shrimp static, by the groan of rising rock. Left alone, those burrs grow jagged enough to shred the resonance that everything else feeds on. My short adult span is spent planing those edges smooth. Think of me as a tuning fork struck against the world’s throat.  
• Keystone Meal  
   The single burst of light I emit at life’s end is the brightest event the canyon ever knows. Hunters, scavengers, pilgrims—everything swarms the flare. It is a reset of appetites: my death restocks the food chain with photons, charge and cooled air.

CONSCIOUSNESS  
Larval memory remains but stretches thin, like a watermark on new paper. I recall the taste of single droplets, the feel of wind undoing silk, but now those memories hum rather than speak. My adult self is less “I” than intention made translucent: keep the song whole, then leave.

ENCOUNTERING “ME”  
From the floor you might see:

1. A long, tremulous blink of colour where colour never is—green sliding into violet, threaded by silver hair.  
2. The wind sagging, as if someone laid a heavy velvet cloth over the canyon’s mouth.  
3. Gloom-Gulls banking hard, tongues flicking confusion, repelled by a chill too still to taste.

If you are a Subaudient monk on silent vigil, you will feel pressure lift from your inner ear—relief so sudden it could be mistaken for grace. Many write their poems immediately afterward, using the hush like wet ink.

DURATION IN THIS REALM  
From first unfurl to final ash: a night and a dawn. The Rift’s clock is glacier, but I am mayfly; our compromise is a single turning of the sky.

AFTERLIFE  
My mica dust is swept east. Thorn-moss uses it as nucleation sites; Ridge Seraph tadpoles ingest the flecks, growing wings that shimmer faintly for their first glide. In three larval generations’ time, when new Chimeras spin their cradles, each cradle already remembers a bit of me in its glint.

WHY THIS FORM FITS  
Because I wanted to belong to the Rift without surviving it. Because a question mark made out of stillness seemed the only signature this canyon lacked. Because every other creature here is a note, and someone, however brief, must be the rest.

So: where would you find me? Hovering halfway between unfinished peaks, in the pause where the organ chord forgets its next turn.

And who would you be if you looked up at that moment?

You would be a listener newly aware that even silence is alive and has just brushed its wing across your face.
