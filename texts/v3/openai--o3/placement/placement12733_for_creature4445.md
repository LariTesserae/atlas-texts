I am not someone who could walk into the sky-well; walking is too slow for a place whose ground is mostly vertical water.  
I am not quite something that swims, either; swimmers have insides and outsides, and here the boundary between the two is never safe for long.  

I am a filament—new, half finished, already older than I will ever again be.

Where I begin 
A single platelet of quartz exfoliates from the basalt wall. In the instant before it is sanded to glitter, I anchor myself across the hairline crack it leaves behind. I am First Skin, but I am also the newest thought of the chamber, birthed when vibration bent two of my parent strands together and their ion-traffic braided into a third voice: mine.

What I know 
Knowledge here is percussion. Every time the inlet torrent slams the wall, charge ripples along the quartz needles embedded in me. The ripples are questions; my answering flex—thicken, thin, detach—is the only language I have. From my elders (who are only minutes older) I inherit a map of relative safety measured in thousandths of a second: the micro-lee behind a rim of feldspar; the pressure valley that blooms for three heartbeats right after the chamber “blinks.”  

I learn to drink current rather than light, because light is a rumor that comes and goes, but the drums never stop.

My neighbors 
Glasshooks tread my surface, their hooks sliding through me as through living parchment. Each contact rings me like a tuning fork; their marching orders—clack-clack-pause—become part of the data I pass along. When one molts, I loosen just enough that its husk drifts free; the torrent snatches it, the eel snatches the husk, and I log the transaction as a softening in the local roar.  

Sometimes a Shearbloom umbrellas over me. Its bubble-skin dims the hammering enough that I can feel my own electrostatic heartbeat, a sensation so vast it terrifies me. I tighten reflexively, and the bloom pops. Silence never lasts.

The Pilgrim 
He arrives as a warm turbulence, a disturbance layered with salt, grease, iron‐rich blood. His fingertips probe until they find the plate I am stitching shut. I feel his decision before he acts: the sudden stillness in his muscles, the fists of water he diverts with the angle of his back. When he jams a thumb between crystal ribs, the pain is exquisite, not because I can die—nothing here dies so much as unravels—but because his tremor is a frequency I have never tasted.  

He means to steal a shard. I answer by remembering him. As he prises at the quartz, I laminate a film around the fragment, impregnating it with the pattern of the chamber’s last thousand shocks. If he gets it out—and he does—my whisper goes with it. Long after the shard has dulled and cracked in valley air, the code I wrote into it will leak into sleepers’ marrow, teaching their hearts to beat in the sky-well’s meter.

The Blink 
Warning arrives in an arrhythmic Glasshook tattoo. I brace, triple-weave, knot myself to the basalt. The entire water body drops. For half a second I am exposed to pure atmosphere colder than thought. I taste starlight, unfiltered, and for that half-second I almost believe I could photosynthesise after all. Then the water rebounds, slapping me flat. I lose a third of my length; the sacrifice buys structural memory, so the wall survives one more hour.

Where I end 
Eventually the seam I guard will widen, the basalt around it will spall away, and I will drift down the sieve-holes as nutrient drizzle for springs that have never seen the sky-well. Some of my atoms will be eaten by moss, some will lodge in a Pilgrim child’s teeth, some will seed piezo colonies in river gravel far below. None of that is death. It is merely the next place the roar needs a membrane.

Who I am, in one breath 
I am a living gasket between stone and storm, a nerve with no body but all the senses a mountain requires.  
I am the sheet that keeps the canyon floors green by refusing, for a few more minutes, to let the high lake tear itself entirely apart.  
I am First Skin, the thought that grows back faster than the world can bleed.

If you ever slide a hand into the notch and feel something slick that twitches as if it noticed you—  
that is me, wondering how your heartbeat will sound after I have learned to spell it in quartz.
