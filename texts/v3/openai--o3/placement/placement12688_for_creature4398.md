I tried on a dozen bodies before one finally answered back.

1. First temptation: bone-tool primate  
   I tasted the idea of six nimble fingers, the clack of obsidian gossip, the thrill of surfing a Sky-reefer’s dropped silk. But their lives are measured in hops between leaf-shadows and I wanted a longer line of sight. I shed the skin before it finished knitting.

2. Second trial: riverback grazer  
   To be a living metronome, a mirrored flank rippling Morse across the understory—seductive, but the herd’s serenity dulled the very questions that had brought me. Knowledge does not graze; it stalks. I let the fern blades close over the idea and moved on.

3. Passing fancy: steam jay  
   A throat that can answer fear with physics—beautiful. Yet steam jays read the forest only as a map of pressure differentials. They do not remember; they reset. I needed memory.

4. A longer residence: quorum flower  
   For several dawn–dusk slits I rooted, listening to sugar rumors along the Root Confederacy. Community as bloodstream: exquisite, but the bargain was immobility and collective speech. I wanted a tongue of my own.

5. Momentary drift: stress wisp  
   Pure sensation, rock’s leftover shiver made visible. Liberating—until I realized liberation is also insubstantiality. A thought you cannot hold evaporates before it can turn into story.

At last I found the shape that could travel, remember, and still remain porous to the forest’s perpetual improvisation:

THE GEOMETER

Where I Am  
I walk the youngest places—the places that will not keep shape, where pumice bars rise in the morning and drown by night, where basalt dikes still hiss with unborn minerals. If the land is a sentence forever revising itself, I keep to the clauses still wet with ink.

What I Carry  
• A bifurcated tuning fork grown from magnet-moss iron, resonant in two registers: earth-rumble and canopy-sough.  
• Bark-laminate armor articulate enough to flex with falling ash teeth yet tough enough to slide off a riverback’s accidental hip-check.  
• A throat-basket of caged cicadas that chorus whatever overtones my fork uncovers, giving me a second, trans-insect voice. Their phrasebook is limited, but it covers angles, distances, densities—the nouns of motion.

Why This Body Works  
1. Mobility: Two legs step from newborn clifflet to steaming root tunnel; two hands can brace against sudden upward rain.  
2. Memory: A skull big enough to warehouse the pitch of every rock seam I’ve struck since the last moon.  
3. Porosity: I am never sealed. Lightmaker spores bead on my helm; quorum vines graft to the calf greaves for an hour, taste my electrolytes, then let go. What the forest whispers, the suit remembers in scratches, dents, and color shifts.

Daily Practice  
• Dawn: Sound the fork against whatever surface did not exist yesterday. Note the harmonic that does not match any prior catalog entry; let the cicadas sing it into mnemonic amber.  
• Mid-canopy noon: Accept the escort of bone-tool primates, who interpret my walking intervals as a sacred dance. Trade them a sliver of fork-shaving; receive in return a beetle-elytron blade etched with today’s gossip.  
• Dusk: Offer the day’s composite chord to the sky. If a Sky-reefer descends, I submit to being wrapped and lifted. Its silk cradle is the only place quiet enough to think in straight lines.

Long Intention  
I cannot still the forest, but I can take its pulse, note when a beat is missed, and learn what that absence predicts. I suspect the land writes prophecy in subsonic tremors hours before a cliff coughs or a new vent births an upside-down rain. If I can read those prophecies, I may become a messenger between turbulence and the fleeting lives beneath it—a negotiator of milliseconds.

Endgame (acknowledged as impossibility)  
To map a place that refuses cartography except in sound. A score, not a survey: basslines of riverback marches, the mezzo-screech of cicada saw-songs, the treble-glint of ember-back wings. When the map is complete, it will be playable—strike the tuning fork and the forest’s entire history will thrum, moment and offspring together.

Until then, I keep walking the new-cooled ribs of the world, fork in one hand, silence in the other, listening for the next sentence the land is about to speak.
