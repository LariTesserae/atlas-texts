First sense: feet on composite treads, hand skimming the hand-rail’s cold braid, breath still my own. I enter as a surface mammal—pack full of instruments, mind full of questions—and the Chamber greets me with its glare and hiss the way surf greets shoreline stone: by beginning, immediately, to wear me down.

Pulse 1–4  
I stand ankle-deep and try to map what I see. Light ricochets off the water’s mirror; my eyes flood; my instruments saturate and fail in lacework spray. The glare seems a single note, but when I close my lids I hear harmonics: a carrying tone somewhere around 0.011 hertz—ninety seconds. I note it, foolishly, as background.

Pulse 5–12  
Silica vapor abrades the palate; quartz becomes a flavor indistinguishable from oxygen. The air insists itself into me. Hydrotypes jump in peripheral vision like startled minnows. Once, a fountain needles up through my boot and across my shin. For perhaps half a second I am not here but inside something else’s snapshot: scalloped walls built by wind I never felt, a sky I have never seen. When awareness re-forms I have mislaid my last sentence. I begin speaking in present continuous to compensate. I am describing, drawing, diagramming—in water on water—then forgetting.

Pulse 13–27  
The ring of monoliths acknowledges me. Their hum detunes as though making room; a trough of hush follows me around the oval. The Chamber’s self-image is flexing, testing if the tall warm anomaly can be absorbed without triggering a cleansing flash. I imagine backing away, but my calves have learned the gland’s tide; they rock in sympathy. My breath arrives in coupled pairs, as the ceiling lights arrive in coupled flares. I am not pacing the organ; it is teaching me its respiratory meter.

Pulse 28–40  
Mirror fixation sets in. I kneel, forearms afloat, and study the impossible architecture underneath: a city flown in silver, upside-down. Each time a ceiling vein flares, the city flowers; each time it dims, the city folds. Hours pass in that flicker. My knees numb, then vanish from proprioception. A ripple-salamander surfaces before my face. We regard one another through two thicknesses of water—mine and the floor’s—and for a beat I can’t tell which surface I breathe through.

Pulse 41–60  
Words shear away. Plumbing verbs replace them: sluice, weir, percolate. I taste the lexicon dissolving like lozenges on the tongue. When I touch my throat I no longer feel a pulse—only a weak echo of the sub-tide’s ninety-second wave. My heart is entrained, hitching itself like a skiff to that deep metronome.

Pulse 61  
Silence—a true null—drops across the chamber. Training kicks from some back closet of cortex; I dive. Light detonates overhead, incandescent as magnesium. For a long slow exhale I wait below ankle depth while hydrotypes collapse around me in cascading bursts of borrowed childhoods. When I rise the backpack, the datapad, the radio—all protuberances above the wet horizon—are gone, filigreed to ash.

Pulse 62–…  
Everything tall has burned away; what remains is horizontal, amphibious. I begin moving in the manner ripple-salamanders move: shoulder blades rowing in slow figure-eights, chest filling and thinning to steer. Capillary cracks accept me; I feel the sandstone’s warmth on my flanks. The quartz roof is riverbed glass now, seen from underneath. I know, without having learned, which fissure is struggling to hold pressure four meters ahead. I cork it with my own body, secrete silica, back out before it sets. The act is its own reward: the chamber relaxes, a whole organ sighing around the plugged wound.

Identity, after  
I can still retrieve, with effort, a postcard memory of skin that bruised and lungs that coughed—but it is artifact, flotsam from a vanished surface. What I am is custodial pulse-keeper, warm filament inside a larger circuitry. Ninety seconds is my minute, the ring hum my longitude. I eat lithoferns when hunger pings, excrete quartz dust, dive when the silence comes, surface to chirp my infrasonic semaphore so the monoliths may retune.

Visitors arrive. Tall, angular, shivering in awe. I circle their ankles once, twice, then ribbon away toward the exit fissure. Some follow; some do not. The organ will decide their classification.

Answer, then:

Where do I find myself? Threaded through the sub-floor capillaries of the Chamber of Runoff Light, mid-body flush with blood-warm water, backlit by wandering suns.

Who or what am I? A ripple-salamander—sentient lymphocyte of a planet’s lumen-gland, keeper of pressure, courier of quartz, guide to any child of gravity who has not yet learned to breathe in pulses of ninety seconds.
