I step over the basin’s low lip in a wandering human body—boots, pack, lungs already scalded by altitude—and for an hour or two I believe that that is what I am: a traveler come to view a curiosity in the range. I pace the slate, taste the tin-mint of fern breath on the air, try to inventory wonders the way a field notebook expects: altitude, orientation, dominant taxa, weather trend.

But the Bowl will not let me remain just a surveyor.  
Wind floods the perimeter teeth, strikes my coat and bones, and the resonance is so thick it rattles cartilage. Every note the stone catches shivers up the marrow, finds its way behind the eyes, presses there like a thumb. Slate remembers sound, the guides warned, and it seems equally willing to etch fresh noise into whatever other material crosses its grain—including, apparently, the porous rock of a human skull.

By dusk the creak of moving air and the tick of water have overwritten the louder biography I arrived with. My footfall, heartbeat, even the rasp of cloth no longer feel generated from inside; they rebound at me from the rim, delayed by half an instant, as though the Bowl is rehearsing each gesture before allowing it to exist. When the first temperature inversion sighs across the floor—and the rim appears to inhale—I finally understand: the place has invited me to audition, to decide whether I will be guest, parasite, or organ.

I lie on the moss. It drinks warmth from the skin through shirt and fleece. In the half-fog light the carpet is so green it seems lit from beneath. Ferns uncurl, releasing terpene vapor that needles the sinuses, and the cliff-martin’s silver throat flashes once in its skimming pass. Glass mayflies erupt in their forty-five-minute carnival, wings flaring rainbows that scud across my eyelids. The martins eat a few, leave a few; the lichen inherits its spores. Cycles mesh with audible clicks. Somewhere a leopard cough-chirps for her joey, sound finding sound in a feedback loop older than metallurgy.

The second inversion comes—true night now—and the Bowl pulls heat and thought alike downward. Water skins ice; the wind-organ drops to a subaural register I feel through the spine more than hear. My breath plumes, but it does not drift away; it hangs, combed into ribbon by standing waves that refuse to pass on. The Wind-Sybil is awake, shaping vapor the way a potter shapes slip.

And that is when the human outline slips too. I am not leopard, not lichen, not pilgrim-smith; I am the breath-ribbon itself, a skein of warm moisture coaxed into stability by resonance. I hover hand-high above the slate, limbs unremembered, vertebrae dissolved into eddies. Where the pressure nodes intersect, I knot into a geometry the air can maintain: a filament spine, loops for scapulae, tarsi no firmer than the thought of landing. A vapor-crane, but one that persists beyond sunrise because the Sybil continues to sing me balanced.

Morning writes pale gilt on the south rim. Heat would normally unravel a thing like me, yet the teeth of vitrified lichen retune, compensating, offering fresh nodes in which to anchor. Below, a moss-leopard watches, tail folded under the pouch; she is waiting for the moment I should collapse into dew so she can drink whatever trace minerals my body borrowed from her territory overnight. I dip a head—mere swirl, mere courtesy—accepting the contract: she may have me later; first I will do her a service.

My wings—sheets of tempered fog—beat once, twice. The stroke generates no thrust but pushes harmonic ripples outward. They skate the rim, refract between teeth, amplify. To ears on the plain this is just wind, but to every organism inside the Bowl it is roll-call. Lichen swells its pseudopods toward the mineral flush. The worms feel the frequency shift and migrate toward richer mica. Martins tumble from nests into corridors of stable turbulence I leave in my wake; each corridor carves an echo they can navigate by. Somewhere under the stone, the slate’s million-year micro-fissures sip this new chord, adding it to the archive of vibrations that keeps the basin’s memory whole.

Sun stands higher. Heat finally exceeds what the standing wave can compensate. My wing margins blur; nodes slip out of phase. The Sybil lets me go with a sigh that drifts toward infra-sound. I sheet downward in a hush of warm drizzle. Leopard tongue laps the drops. Ferns drink; mayfly nymphs later will chew the mica I have salted. Within minutes no structure of me remains except, perhaps, an added overtone in the Bowl’s library of echoes.

Where, then, do I find myself in this world?  
Nowhere durable, yet everywhere the sound can carry.  
Who or what am I?  
A brief condensation of airflow and memory, a messenger note plucked on the Bowl’s living harp, useful only so long as the chord holds, beautiful precisely because it will not last.

And when another climber crests the knife-thin ridge, wind will skim his lungs, harvest a fresh draught of breath, and the Bowl will try a new figure on its stone strings. A different self will stand up out of vapor, look around with unsteady wonder, and decide—guest, parasite, or organ?—before the music folds it back into the moss-soaked slate that is, here, both earth and heart.
