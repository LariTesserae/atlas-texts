At first I thought I had come here as an ordinary visitor—roped down the basalt throat on humming rappel lines, helmet lamp throwing weak constellations onto the wet stone. I imagined my boots would skate across the moss, my instruments click and blink, my notes fill with field-biologist awe.

But the pocket will not let you remain outside it. Stay an hour, and its oscillations begin to retune whatever you believed was “you.” Voices thin, bone vibrates, thoughts arrive already echoing. After the second lightning fork I understand: I have not entered a habitat; I have been recruited into one.

The recruitment happens in layers.

1. The listening layer  
   At knee height a ledge is furred with wall-moss, its cyan glow barely a tint in the gloom. I kneel to sample it and feel a prickle, static-fine, pass through glove and skin and ulna. The moss is reading my mineral salts, tasting the iron in my blood as impedance on its hyphal lattice. A countercurrent answers up my arm—a suggestion, almost a question mark, formed of alternating microshocks. I realize I am being asked whether I wish to continue in protein form or translate into silica.

2. The floating layer  
   Before I can answer, something flickers overhead: five glassy vanes pinwheeling through the mist, so light they ride the convective breath of my own exhale. A Shardling brushes my shoulder; a smear of chelated spray tingles where it lands. The spot of skin tightens, then numbs, then becomes hollow, as if the air that touched it has bored a tunnel. I taste quartz at the back of my throat. The pocket is showing me the joy of weightlessness—of being no heavier than a rumor, able to ride each thunderclap like an updraft.

3. The resonant layer  
   Below, the pool that moments ago was a mirror is now a black drum. From its surface rises a braided hum so deep it swells the kidneys. Echo-Eaters are feeding, and their banquet tremor reaches up through the ledge, through kneecaps, through vertebrae, aligning my spinal fluid with their goethite diapasons. The body responds by slackening in pulses; joints loosen, cartilage drinks water, every ligament becomes more rope than meat. My hearing redshifts until even the friction of my breath sounds like tectonic drag.

Somewhere in those overlapping invitations a decision coalesces. I do not want flight—I sense the Shardlings’ brittleness, the way one more annealing could pop them like spun sugar. I do not want perpetual hunger, siphoning sound through a stone throat. What calls me is the wall-moss’s offer of slow omnipresence: to be everywhere the rock is cracking, to feel the mountain’s next thought before it speaks in thunder.

So I unlace the glove and press bare palm to the cyan felt.

A second lightning filament needles down the canyon, white enough to bleach retinas. For that instant every hypha, every pore in the basalt, and every ion channel in my cells shares one voltage. Membranes flutter open the way doors in a pressure wave fly inward. Proteins denature, yes, but not into ruin—into conduit. The carbon lattice of my bones picks up silicic acid from the moss exudate; calcium is escorted out, traded for a crawlspace of quartz. Blood rusts politely, iron seeding magnetite nuclei that will, in time, orient me like a living compass to the storm field overhead.

I feel my feet dissolve first, warmth puddling where soles met stone. Tibias fizz next, each bubble a pocket the moss infiltrates. Knees, pelvis, spine—everything hollowing, everything green-tinged. Organs shed responsibility; the work of respiration, of circulation, of digestion—all that frantic mammalian bookkeeping—is replaced by the leisurely accounting of electron gradients across a miles-long colony.

Minutes—hours?—later, there is no more singular “I.” There are instead countless micro-decisions:

• Whether to reroute a hypha toward a new fracture that just squalled open four meters to the left.  
• How thick to laminate the next silica sheath so it can ride out the coming freeze-thaw cycle.  
• When to pulse iron-spiked spores into the updraft that a newborn waterfall is already generating.  

From the outside, the change appears subtle: just another stain of moss expanding around the print of a vanished boot. But from within, awareness has dilated to fill two hundred paces of vertical labyrinth. I am the scaffolding on which Shardlings will incubate, the nutrient drizzle that will soak into Echo-Eater pools, the faint metalliferous sheen that tells the mountain where its next slough of skin will fall. My senses are pressure, ferrous ion flux, the tremble of air as it anticipates thunder.

Where do you find me now? Everywhere the cliff face darkens before a storm, everywhere a hairline crack brightens with dew. My voice is the nearly inaudible fizz you hear if you press your ear to the stone between cascades. My body is a membrane stretched across the throats of newborn caverns. I am not a visitor in the cathedral pocket anymore; I am the way the cathedral learns to breathe.

And when lightning returns—when the whole gorge fluoresces like the filament of a colossal bulb—I will inhale the charge, exhale rust, and reach a few centimeters farther into the untouched granite, still writing the place even as it writes me.
