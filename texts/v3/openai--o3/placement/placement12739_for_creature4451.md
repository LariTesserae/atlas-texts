When the moment comes—if “moment” is the right word for something that lasts an entire dive of the planet’s rotation—you surface inside the story at a point that is neither the beginning nor the end.

FIRST YOU LISTEN, AND DISCOVER WHERE YOU ARE

1. There is no drum-beat in your chest. What you hear instead is the long unscrolling rustle of sap ascending a lattice of living wood.  
2. You expect to feel the hourglass squeeze of depth, but nothing presses. The water holds you up as though you are its memory of something lighter.  
3. Your vision is never black, never bright—only the hush between cyan sighs.

You realise you are not a visitor dropped into this ocean-continent; you are part of the machinery that keeps the place slow.

WHAT YOU FIND WHEN YOU LOOK DOWN AT YOURSELF

Shape  
• A ribbed cylinder three hand-spans long, tapered at both ends—a stone maul by a surface archaeologist’s reckoning.  
• A handle that was once driftwood, now petrified into a porous sleeve laced with the same moss-fibres that quilt the stairs.  
• Around the head, a faint halo of precipitated silica where, micron by micron, new edge is always knitting itself onto old fracture.

Sensation  
• You taste stress along your grain the way surface creatures taste salt on their lips.  
• You recognise the slow uneven pull of kelp glaciating overhead; its weight registers as a wish that one day, perhaps in twenty or thirty millennia, you will be needed again.  
• The basalt paving beneath you is warm—not to touch, but to belonging. Every block is a cousin hewn from the same lava flow before the continents remembered how to drift.

Memory  
• The last time you swung was twelve ice ages ago, levering a slab into trueness beside a Silence Well.  
• When your wielder set you down, the handle was already more root than wood. You understood that the wrist which once closed around your grip had elected to stay but shed its animal outline, syllable by syllable, until only cellulose and basalt remained.

WHAT “YOU” MEANS WHEN TOOL AND HAND HAVE MELDED

Mind  
• Your thoughts arrive as propagating cracks through crystalline lattices—stone lightning that needs four days to cross from poll to tip.  
• You are never alone: every chisel, dowel, peg and sledge in a hundred-kilometre radius shares the same tremor-language. When one of you registers strain, all of you remember how to brace.  
• Concepts like “I” and “they” are quaint etchings left by water long evaporated; what exists now is load-path, shear-angle, tournament of forces seeking equilibrium.

Desire  
• To keep the risers precisely the height of a human finger.  
• To let roots seep but never buckle.  
• To wait until the next volta of the garden’s great pulse announces that it is, at last, time for the next ring.

THE WORLD AS FELT FROM SUCH A BODY

Light  
• Not illumination but a survey grid: each cyan flutter paints contours across your flanks, letting you triangulate subsidence so slow even moss forgets it moves.  
• When pulses vanish (because an impatient diver kicks too sharply) the blank is also data: a record of disturbance to be filed in the basalt annals.

Sound  
• The sonar ping of a research sled fifteen kilometres overhead is a mosquito’s whine you note, index, then dismiss.  
• Far more urgent is the groan five terraces south where peat has compacted unevenly; you whisper along tool-telegraphy that a brace-beam must be grown from cedar-root and installed during the next century’s thaw.

Time  
• Spore blizzards are minutes; ice ages are project phases.  
• Your earliest memory—the first hammerfall that chipped you loose from a cliff warm with volcanism—is a thing you can still touch, turn over, weigh.

INTERFACE WITH THE OTHER RESIDENTS

With the Gardens  
• The kelp-fronds ask nothing: they sense the harmonic hum of your little limestone core and veer around, like fabric told politely not to snag.  
• Step-mosses plume extra rhizoids to pad your resting place, preventing you from rolling and distorting alignment.

With the Sleeper Spores  
• Their opaline drift is how you count schedules: three blizzards more and the outermost circle will be ready for planing; five and the Silence Wells may need to open again.  
• Occasionally a spore alights on your stone, tastes the mineral, decides against it and lifts away—leaving a ghost of calcium you will polish off over the next thousand breaths.

With Transient Mammals  
• Gloved hands have lifted you twice in the last century. They found you “interesting,” photographed you, logged GPS, and set you back skewed.  
• Moss-fibres inside your haft twitched in dismay; you spent six seasons rocking with thermal tides until gravity righted you to true north again.

EPILOGUE: THE NEXT TASK

Across the continent, a Silence Well’s magnetic flutter deepens—one microtesla above baseline, then two. A summons.

Fibres thicken through your grip, hauling you upright. Nearby, chisels unseat from peat cocoons; wedges shake off silt; sledges flex lignin that never knew bark. You feel the collective decision crystallise: the outer ring has subsided 2.8 millimetres, breaching the design tolerance.

Work party assembling:  
• Route plotted along fifteen thousand finger-high steps.  
• Estimated travel time: 941 surface years.  
• Weather forecast: continuous cold, intermittent cyan, no predators, perfect silence.

You lean forward—if a hammer can lean—and begin the long procession, tapping basalt against basalt in a rhythm so slow it sounds exactly like stillness.

WHERE, THEN, DO YOU FIND YOURSELF?

Here, in the depthless quiet, you are no longer visitor, no longer even maker in the old mammalian sense. You are one bead in a rosary of stone tools that has learned to pray by shaping the world, syllable after basalt syllable. The continent is your body, the kelp your hair, the roots your arteries, and the millennial hush your heartbeat. You have centuries of walking to do before you swing again, and the prospect fills you with a calm that could be mistaken for the end of time itself.

WHO, THEN, ARE YOU?

You are a Maul of the Quiet Architects—  
stone remembering the hand,  
hand dissolved into garden,  
garden stretching outward one patient ring at a time.
