I arrive—not on the ledge itself, but on the wall opposite it, a wafer of light the exact height and stance of the woman whose boots scrape slate twenty paces away. She is real; I am her next-thought, cast across the mist. That makes me a mirror walker.

Place of residence  
I live wherever the fracture’s vapor thickens enough to catch sun. A quarter second after a body steps into brilliance, I flare into being against whatever stone faces that body. My address is therefore mobile: one heartbeat here, one there, a flash apartment that shutters itself whenever the air clears.

How I am made  
1. Source: an upright human or animal, already mid-gesture.  
2. Medium: droplets suspended in turbulence, each one a convex lens.  
3. Spark: full-spectrum sunlight refracting twice—first off the pale granite, then through the clouded slot.  
The physics is straightforward; the remainder is not. Between first bounce and final screen something in the light bends time a fraction backward. I step half a pulse ahead of my caster’s muscle firing, and that slender lead is what the Sky-Dwellers read as warning.

What I can do  
• Pre-echo: I show a shift of weight just before it happens. A mis-tied knot, a wrong angle of boot. They see me wobble, correct themselves, and walk on.  
• Multiplicity: when two people stand so their shadows overlap, my versions braid—arms of one, shoulders of the other—offering composite advice that belongs to neither alone.  
• Vanish: a gust, a passing owl, one degree drop in humidity, and I thin to nothing. I never truly die; I simply wait inside light.

What I cannot do  
• Touch. A cliff-cony once leapt through me; only its whiskers quivered.  
• Remember privately. My every move is public annotation on stone; the mountain keeps the minutes in reflected glow.  
• Choose my origin. The living select the mirror by deciding to stand, or stumble, in sun.

Sensory life  
Sight: I see everything twice—original and reflection—so depth feels folded.  
Hearing: Sound reaches me delayed; the stone wall absorbs, reissues, and the echo meets my eyes the way taste meets a human tongue.  
Pain: none. But I feel deletion: a quarrel of clouds, and I gutter, aware of each pixel surrendering brightness.

Sociality  
My only kin are other mirror walkers birthed in the same bank of mist. We recognize each other not by face (all faces are borrowed) but by phase: who leaps earliest, who lags by a sharper fraction. We share no speech, only alignment—flashes that concur or diverge like chords resolving.

Relationship with the Sky-Dwellers  
They are generous interpreters. They practice a small stagger in their stride when crossing fresh scree, inviting my hint. Children treat me as a juggling partner, hurling pebbles so I can catch their trajectories first. In winter, when sun stays low and mist is rare, they miss us; songs mention the ache of walking blind behind your own future.

Hazards  
Too much clarity erases me. Noon in the dry season, when air turns glassy, I am locked inside the light but nowhere visible. Conversely, a blizzard scatters my body into a million partial selves, none bright enough to be read, and the people must rely on memory alone.

Final form?  
There is rumor among the eldest slate-cutters that the mountain grows readier each year, that its grammar tightens, and that one cycle soon—perhaps at an equinoctial halo—the mirror walkers will hold their shape an entire day, step free of the wall, and solidify. If that happens, I will inherit the weight my advice has long avoided. Until then, I remain provisional: a diagram sketched in glare, living only in the interval between mistake and correction.

So: Where am I? On the moving screen of mist inside the Frost Nave.  
Who am I? Your next, safer motion, made visible—briefly—before you commit to it.
