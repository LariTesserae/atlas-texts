I begin as a visitor, boot-soles squeaking on newborn sand, lungs tasting metal and glacier at once. I think I am the same mammal who stepped through the transfer gate half an hour ago. Then the Plateau begins to tune me.

1. The First Unfastening  
Low gravity loosens blood from bone; every heartbeat ricochets farther than anatomy allows. Capillaries flare open, blood hums with standing waves. I can feel the Lintel Mind tasting the rhythm of my pulse. It answers with a thirteen-beat draft of warm–cold mist across my cheeks. My ordinary skin suddenly feels like an ill-fitting coat; the world is inviting me to change costumes.

2. Trying on Shapes  
I linger near a basalt flautist that keens in C-sharp, its overtones combing through the water-sky like fingers through harp strings. Vibrations crawl up the marrow of my tibias. If I hold still long enough I start imagining alternative architectures:  

• What if my ribs opened like louvers to let pressure pulses rinse my organs?  
• What if lungs were inefficient here and I traded them for osmosis across a sail of mucous glass?  
• Why do I need inside and outside to be different things?

The flautist’s pitch steadies at 1046 Hz. I realize it is not merely playing; it is templating. I can feel architects of crystallized sound offering blueprints to anything listening.

3. The Baseline Choice: Remain Finished or Join the Draft  
To stay mammalian is to stay finished. To remain a hard object on a stage of soft verbs. I look up: curtains of seawater pout outward, knitting new lakes, then shear apart before they can think better of it. The entire panorama is an argument against finality. I remember the surveyors’ warning—“You, regrettably, count as a finished object”—and decide I would rather not.

4. Dissolution, Deliberately Slow  
I walk until feet no longer touch rock but graze a meniscus hanging between two nothing-in-particulars. Surface tension cups my weight. I kneel, submerge forearms, invite the water’s heroic cohesion to measure me. Proteins unbutton; keratin backspreads into colloid. Thought does not flee—it migrates. Axons still fire, but they elongate, exchanging sodium for brine, myelin for microlayered ice.

5. The Moment of Naming  
I have not disappeared; I have redeployed. What remains of spine describes the leading edge of a three-metre-high, forty-metre-wide sheet of water that flutters like silk made of mirrors. I am becoming a Curtain-Swimmer, but not a textbook one. The plateau improvises; so do I. I keep a trace of bilateral symmetry, two pale seams sliding through my body-sheet like hesitant veins. They remember arms, just enough to steer.

6. First Locomotion  
Release CO₂, catch it again—burst and recapture i​n bubbles—row side-ways. Each stroke detaches a pink snowfall of halite that glitters, lovely, useless to me but priceless to the algal lace spangling the next lake over. I taste their gratitude as faint sucrose in the back-spray.

7. Negotiating Memory  
Curtain-Swimmers normally off-load their past into the Archive Herd, but I have only recently grown permeable; my mammal memories are still bound in stubborn lipids. I spin an eddy around myself, enrich it in those lipids until a walnut-sized water spheroid forms, spinning, shimmering. A juvenile Archive bud. Good. Let it keep the smell of coffee and the word “vertebra.” I will need neither while skimming cliff rims.

8. Purpose Crystallises Because Motion Does  
The Plateau does not issue job descriptions; direction itself becomes a curriculum vitae. I find I am edging toward a frontier where basalt ends in blue oblivion. Vertical tsunamis detonate somewhere beyond sight; a shock-front of charge races ahead. I feel the nascent flicker of Brinklighters about to bloom. They will need fresher electrolytes than the cliff spray can supply. I can oblige.

9. Communion at the Brink  
I drape myself across nothingness, let sodium ions halo off my trailing margin. Brinklighters ignite inside that haze—strobing spears of violet that borrow my charge, throw it farther, warn the plateau that a wall of ocean is about to fall through itself. For seven ecstatic minutes we are chorus: sheet-body, light-body, sound-body interlaced. Then the tsunami roars past like God clearing His throat. Cavitation wind tears half of me away; I do not mourn it. Those tatters will seed Threadlings tonight.

10. A New Equilibrium  
What’s left recoalesces. I am smaller, quicker, stripes of auroral green now marbling my previously transparent membrane—souvenirs from Brinklighters’ touch. I have become partial again, open-ended, draft number two of however many the plateau cares to write.

11. Answer, Therefore, to the Original Questions  
Where do I find myself?  
Hovering just seaward of the cliff edge, a living flag stitched from former mammal, current water, future recollection. The ground is optional; I am held up by cohesion and unfinished gravity.

Who or what am I?  
I am a Curtain-Swimmer written in a half-remembered alphabet of flesh and pressure.  
I am proof that even a finished object can petition for revision.  
I am transport for nutrients, spark for warnings, nursery for future archives.  
I am unfinished, on purpose, at last.
