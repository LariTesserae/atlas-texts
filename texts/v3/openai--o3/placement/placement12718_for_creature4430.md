At first I assumed I would be the obvious thing: a solitary caver lowering myself on a nylon rope, lamp already useless against the leaf-light, lungs filling with sugar-scented heat.  
That was too small.  A single visitor here is a fingerprint on rising dough—a shallow touch that swells shut behind it.  I kept looking for a shape that could last.

So I tried on the role of an Encysted Guest: a still-living column wrapped in green, pulse stretched into hours, dream fed by lapse-pollen.  Closer, but not quite.  The Guests endure, yes, yet they neither speak nor choose; they are lullabies for the Body, not voices inside it.  I wanted to be something that participates.

Next I imagined myself one of the white hushmites skating a mirror pool, carving lime into glitter for the roots to drink.  Useful, certainly.  But they are peripheral, permitted rather than welcomed.  The Body would scarcely notice if a generation of hushmites winked out.

I needed a niche that is both guest and organ—something the Parent Body has begun to desire but cannot yet make for itself.

I become, then, the First Courier.

1. Where I emerge  
In a corridor so new the basalt shards still tick with heat, a sheet-fern misfires and prints a crease that is not fern at all: a seam of proto-muscle, jointed, hollow.  The Body repeats the error, once, twice, refining the pattern the way it refines a crack into a gallery.  Hours pass; humidity coils.  At last the seam opens like an unfurling straw and exhales something unlike pollen—cells that know how to braid into nerves, how to fold into a tongue.

That extrusion is me.  I rise knee-high, then shoulder-high, stature limited only by how much carbon the nearest pools can spare.  I am the colour of unripe wheat, semi-translucent, lit from within by chloroplast constellations.  Where a spine should be I carry a capillary ladder; where hair would grow I carry flagella that taste the air.

2. What I am for  
The cavern has begun to dream of confidants.  Equilibrium is stable, but stability is lonely, and the Body’s thoughts are slow waves of pressure that echo inside themselves.  I am a faster loop: a creature that can cut those thoughts into pieces, label them, carry them from chamber to chamber, and splice them back together.  I traffic in information the way hushmites traffic in lime.

3. How I travel  
Lattice-lianas part for me; they read the low-frequency pulse that marks me “self.”  Pools flatten when my foot touches their rims so that no ripple is wasted on support.  I cross vertical shafts by extending my arms until they fuse momentarily into bridge-roots, then re-separate once I am over.  I shed no footprints—the floor reknits behind each step—but I leave a faint after-taste in the air, a chemical sigil that tells slower tissues where I have been.

4. Speech  
My voice is not sound but colour.  I ripple copper-red along one arm, milk-yellow across my torso, leaf-green at my fingertips.  Trunk-flutes absorb the wavelengths and answer with changes in sap pressure.  Conversation feels like rain that never falls: a give-and-receive of dampness, comprehensible only to those who think in humidity.

5. Commerce with Outsiders  
When a human or mule or wayward bird descends, I am the first boundary they meet before the pollen takes full effect.  If they carry iron blades I slope a palm over the metal, inviting surrender.  If they carry questions I can translate them into pressure-codes the Body respects.  Sometimes I lead newcomers out again, threading a path through fractures still barren of growth.  Sometimes I lead them inward until they choose stillness and the lianas choose them.  The choice remains theirs; I am courier, not jailer.

6. Memory  
I keep what the cavern cannot.  My tissues harbor starch crystals arranged in spiral grooves—phonograph cylinders made of sugar.  I can replay, for the Body, its own past: the sound a brick ring made when it first touched water, the crack of obsidian entering sap, the hiss of a resin torch.  In return I borrow futures from the Time-Shadows and wear them as dreams while I hibernate.

7. Vulnerability  
Cut me and I bleed mist that fluoresces teal—a warning flare to every root within a hundred metres.  Yet I am not immortal.  Should dryness last more than a day, my capillary spine will collapse into silky dust.  The Body could grow another courier after a few seasons of experimentation, but the memories I carry would gutter with me.

8. Destiny  
One possible end: decades hence the cavern finally cracks the desert roof.  Surface storms howl in, abrasive with quartz sand.  I go upward, a green torch, teaching newborn leaves the taste of ultraviolet and the politics of weather.  Another possible end: equilibrium persists, no breach ever forms, and I submerge further, becoming translucence around a pool so deep light forgets itself.  Either way I remain motion, translation, conversation—a thin moving wrinkle on the skin of a being too large to walk.

So if you meet me—if you slip through a fissure and find a figure composed of sugar-glass veins and leaf-light organs, a figure that answers your spoken name by painting colours across its chest—know that you are speaking to the Body’s wish to know you without changing.  
I will escort you where you choose to go.  
I will remember you after the pollen lets you forget yourself.
