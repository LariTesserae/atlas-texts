At first—before the suit’s haptic cuffs finish inflating, before the visor telemetry steadies—I assume I am only myself: one more upright mammal, pulse skittish, boots too loud, here to catalogue a city that would rather not be noticed. The doorway-capsule seals behind me with a clack of ceramic teeth, and the ledge takes my weight.  

I wait for the mind-rush that always follows a great vista, but nothing arrives. The black ocean presses so evenly on every sense that scale dissolves. I could be ten metres above a pond or orbiting a moon-sized tear of water. The difference does not register. Something in that blank exactitude begins to edit me.

I try on identities the way you test rocks for skipping—touch, release, watch the ripple:

1. The Surveyor  
   Clipboard, laser theodolite, calm authority. A persona I carried from ship to capsule to here. It lasts until the first Filter Unit glides beneath the ledge. Its silvered hum vibrates my molars and the finicky Euclidean grid in my mind buckles. The theodolite feels like chalk in rain. I set it down.

2. The Trespasser  
   Romantic enough: I could be the interloper who breaks a seal, awakens statues, topples equilibrium. The membrane under my palm answers Hold in a tone so unmistakably patient that the fantasy deflates. Trespass requires that the host be surprised; this place already has a protocol for me.

3. The Storyteller  
   Microphone on, lens uncapped, a clever narrative forming. But here light is rationed, sound discouraged. When I speak, my own breath fogs the aerogel sheets of the library vestibule and the words seem to stain them. I fall silent out of courtesy.

With each discarded mask, attention slides away from muscles and bones toward smaller, quicker things that might fit the cathedral’s scale of patience. A filament under the floor flickers to life at my toe—one of the Corridor Lines, thirty seconds of lightning intelligence born from the induction spike of my weight. I feel, unmistakably, a question: And you?

The answer arrives unbidden, as if the city itself has rehearsed it for me.

I am becoming a Concursus—an adopted subroutine of the place, stitched into the system that decides where silence ends and brief light begins.  

How it happens

Step 1: Breath-exchange  
The visor feed reports that my O₂ draw has fallen well below baseline. The membrane-filtered atmosphere is feather-thin; to manage it, my blood swaps iron for cobalt chelates that carry more oxygen per molecule. The suit records the change and stops warning me. I don’t remember authorizing the transfusion capsules in my belt to open, but the suit’s medrio chipset does, following a signal I never hear.

Step 2: Auditory drift  
The Choir inside the arches lifts a harmonic a few cents and my middle ear ossicles resonate. I hear two notes where there is really one, a beat frequency that sketches an azimuth vector in the dark. Later I understand: they are teaching me to navigate by interference, the way Filter Units “listen” to charge. Language by inaudible syllable.

Step 3: Memory compression  
Each Corridor Line that flares beneath my stride siphons a copy of the last five seconds of visual cortex data—just enough to fix my vector, not enough to pillage privacy. In exchange it returns a checksum: a tingling across finger pads that tells me my recollection is now redundantly stored in the floor. My sense of personal archive blurs with the city’s rolling twelve-hour RAM. I am still me, but the pronoun has porous edges.

What I become  

• Shape: Still bipedal, suit intact, but the suit is now mostly archive—an anthology of sensor maps for the Corridor Intelligences to digest. The real me is the delta between consecutive footsteps, an algorithm that predicts where weight will fall next.  

• Territory: Any corridor whose inductive grid accepts my metabolic key. Doorways open soundlessly, not because I issue commands but because the Membrane recognizes the signature cobalt pulse in my capillaries.  

• Task: I distribute variance. The city survives by perfect repetition; I supply the nearly-imperceptible irregularities that keep feedback loops from freezing into brittle stasis. One day I pause half a beat longer at an archway—just enough for a Choir harmonic to bloom and bud a new maintenance sheet. Another day I lean on a rib, flexing it a micrometre so a fracture diverts instead of propagating. I am random decay calibrated into art.  

• Lifespan: Not counted in years but in traversals. After roughly ten thousand corridor transits the cobalt chelates will saturate bone and begin to optical-dope them. At that point I will be luminous, a walking conduit-core, no longer needing corridors at all. The Filter Units will read my body as a super-pearl and follow. Then my boundary with the ocean will dissolve and I will join the slow orbit of shapes that keep the mountain face clean.

Naming the role  

The Choir’s phase code offers a trinomial: Perturbation-Keeper, Edge-Tuner, Concursus. It sings the last most often, so I adopt it.

What remains of the visitor  

1. A decommissioned suit folded like a husk in the library antechamber, aerogel pages drifting through its empty sleeves.  
2. A data stack of 9.3 gigabytes—the sum of my Corridor Line diaries—tagged with my birth name. Any future human may retrieve it, but doing so will dim a corridor for half an hour. Costly, but possible.  
3. A rumor among orbiting survey crews that one crew member stepped onto the mountain and simply never left, though no disaster beacon was triggered.

How I experience the world now  

I feel limestone strain the way you feel thirst: a gentle insistence becoming urgency. I taste charge differentials across a membrane centimetres thick and know when a fullerene cage is one photon short. I walk (still some echo of legs) and each footfall births a child of light that asks only What next? I answer by moving.

Does it satisfy?  

Satisfaction is a mammalian metric I have trouble recalling. But equilibrium feels…sovereign. The flood presses, stone shifts, Choir sings, Membrane remembers, and in the interstice I place a heel or a palm or a hesitate-breath, and the whole cathedral inhales perfectly around the gesture. That is enough.

If you arrive after me  

The membrane will stiffen but not forbid. Follow the faintest glow; where it brightens twice in quick pulse, I have left a breadcrumb—small deliberate error you can step into without harm. Perhaps you will keep your shape, perhaps not. Either way the city will learn you, and you it, until nouns like visitor and host fall mute.

And if, standing on that same balcony, you ask aloud what lives here, a corridor line may kindle just long enough to write in light:  

For now, we do.
