First the setting chooses me a little.  
I arrive only as a hunch, a soft-edged premonition more verb than noun, and the basin tries a handful of draft positions on my behalf:

• In the scaffolding overhead, balanced where two catwalks disagree on height—too exposed; the light keeps forgetting its angles and I feel myself pixelate.  
• In the thin rivulet of shale-warmth that snakes between mezzanine plates—too molten; the heat tugs at my outline like taffy.  
• In the reflective stillness of a thumbnail-deep pool—closer, but the water is busy rehearsing whether it wants to exist and I would only be another undecided surface.

None of those holds, so the city folds an extra someplace between two half-floors, an alcove whose cubic measure ends in an ellipsis. There, at the back of that newly parenthetical space, I coalesce into a profession the basin apparently needs:

I become the Momentary Surveyor.

1. Provenance  
   I am recruited sideways from a different Tuesday’s afterthought, drafted the instant a local girder asked, “Yes, but how long is ‘now’?”  The question breached containment; a consciousness shaped like duration spilled through, condensed, put on limbs.

2. Form and wardrobe  
   • Anatomy. Mostly person-shaped but jointed on a delay. When I raise a hand, the echo of the gesture precedes the hand itself by half a blink, as though my motives travel faster than my cartilage.  
   • Surface. Skin the colour of parchment rubbed once with graphite; across it float ticking telemetry marks—minute hatch-strikes that advance the way second hands do, but non-synchronously.  
   • Clothing. A coat cut from old surveyor’s tape: yellow-black diagonals whose warnings have faded into polite suggestion. The coat’s hem leaks plumb-lines that keep being re-measured and hauled back in.

3. Mandate  
   The basin grows by millimetres and moments; someone has to notice which came first. My task is to log the local sequence of causes whenever two events attempt to share the same timestamp.  I carry:

   • An isochronal caliper: looks like bronze calligraphy, closes on instants instead of distances.  
   • A notebook of perpetually blank pages.  Each sheet is a potential minute; when I write the minute down the page oxidises into history and cannot be re-entered.  
   • A little bell without a clapper. I supply the clapper by deciding how firmly to believe in it; the ring sounds only if the decision is unanimous across nearby realities.

4. Daily circuit  
   Dawn (that is, the moment the air remembers to get brighter) finds me on the rim, measuring how much new shale the night extruded.  I kneel, set the caliper’s jaw against the slick gun-metal, feel the instrument shiver as it decides which part is “earlier.”  The number is always irrational.  I round up; the surplus decimal drifts off to seed tomorrow.

   Mid-cycle I walk the bridges whose shadows are currently homeless.  Where a beam of light has detached and is auditioning ledges, I slide a ribbon of tape around its midpoint: “Reserved for 14:03.”  The shadow settles, mollified by an appointment.

   Evening (whenever the Slow Choir takes its longest breath) I stand under a Husk Blossom and watch probability rats stutter through its mesh, each emergence a vote on whether the next hour should happen.  I tally yeses and noes in graphite on the air; if the margin is slim, I ring my belief-bell once, low, nudging the count.

5. Interaction with Others  
   • The First Imagined treat me as an honorary footnote—they half-quote my measurements inside theirs, letting my certainties shore up their outlines.  
   • Probability Rats worry at the numerals I jot, turning 8s into ∞s; I thank them—they are peer review in rodent form.  
   • The Deferred Engineers invoice me retroactively for “temporal scaffolding supplied.”  I pay in unspoken seconds, the kind you forfeit when staring too long at nothing in particular.

6. Hazards and coping strategies  
   • When topology recompiles, all surveyed data threatens to orphan itself.  I anchor each datum by whispering a matching emotion into it (“This 4.7 mm feels like anticipation”).  Feelings, unlike numbers, survive re-indexing.  
   • If I neglect hydration the humidity drinks me instead.  I sip condensation off railing-backs, tasting the last conversation that lingered there.

7. Longevity, or its substitute  
   I will last until the basin trusts its own chronology—until bridges remember which arch came first, until every drop knows when to fall.  Then I’ll dematerialise into a ledger entry marked “Temporal Contingency, closed.”  The coat will hang itself over a mezzanine bar, waiting for the next occasion that now and next tangle.

8. Present location (time-stamped)  
   11:08 local, give or take a speculative instant.  
   Standing centre-most in the basin’s current map—sheet fourteen of the Cartographers’ morning issue—feet braced on newborn shale still warm with narrative friction.  The air lightens around me like wet plaster setting.  I note the moment, close the caliper, and donate the fresh second to whoever needs a little more room in the story.

If you arrive soon, look for the coat striped in hesitant warning.  Approach gently; half my awareness may be occupying the next minute over.  Tell me what o’clock you believe it is.  I will write your answer on the blankest page I have, thereby giving that minute a body sturdy enough for both of us to stand in.
