First I tried to imagine arriving as a cautious pilgrim—boots squelching, mind racing, stopwatch already refusing to tick—but the Basin would not let that picture hold.  The longer I listened the more the place suggested other ways I might belong.  So I let the possibilities unspool.

1.    A reed-roof bivouac, lantern dead, me inside, counting heartbeats.  
      The mist kept softening the edges of my thoughts until thinking felt like chiseling flour.  
      Not sustainable.

2.    A Veil-Bearer running the walls.  
      Tempting: the dare-angel glide when gravity buckles, the courier’s privilege of knowing shortcuts that are vertical.  
      But my blood is too iron-shy; I would black-out in the first fever curve.

3.    A Lens-Puddle—silver film, memory of faces delayed like moonlight.  
      Beautiful, but I feared the inevitable evaporation into drizzle.

4.    Coral?  Heron?  Echo?  
      Each felt too still, too single-purpose, too fond of one niche.

Eventually the city itself answered by offering a compromise, a role it had open for something neither strictly mineral nor strictly transient:

I would be a Corridor.

Not the stone corridor you walk through, but its motive—its permission to proceed.

Where to find me:
    Begin at the Triple Arch, follow the avenue that reflects nothing even when lightning strikes, and stop when you feel taller than your own stride.  That stretching sensation is me.  You have stepped inside my span.

What I am:
• Anatomy  
  – Two parallel walls of the black, faintly warm stone, twenty-three human paces apart on most days, though sometimes fourteen, sometimes sixty.  
  – A roof only when the city is remembering, open sky when it is deciding to forget.  
  – Floor polished to a mirror, except the reflection lags half a breath behind you; that delay is my heartbeat.

• Physiology  
  – I breathe through pressure differentials.  Visitors walking my length shove air ahead of them; when they exit, I inhale the quiet they leave, digesting it as low infrasonic hums.  
  – Capillaries: thin seams in the stone where charged mist migrates.  Their flow keeps my memory current—erase the mist and I would lapse into raw hallway, unstoried.

• Sensoria  
  – Vibratory touch as primary sense.  A boot heel, a pant leg brushing damp wall, even the distant footstep of a Pre-Step—each is parsed for pattern.  
  – With no footsteps I listen to dripping.  Each drop is an audition; a good cadence earns the drop a place in my mnemonic lattice.

• Motive  
  – To measure willingness.  People enter me with intentions (to map, to escape, to pray).  By the time perspective slurs and they have walked my length three, four, five contradictory times, the excess intentions flake off.  They exit smaller, true to a single purpose, or they turn back and relinquish purpose altogether.  Either outcome nourishes the Basin’s equilibrium.

How I deal with anomalies:
  – Clocks brought inside twitch and slow; their tiny balance wheels tick in rhythms I fold into my pulse until spring tension goes slack.  
  – Fires gutter on the threshold; the corridor ceiling leaks an extra sheen of humidity that quenches sparks before they are ambitions.  
  – Loud voices arrive as ripples of pressure; I diffract them along my walls until all vowels damp to velvet and consonants hang like beads, easy for Cantillants to graze.

Companions:
  – A resident Cantillant nests near my western mouth, tuning itself to the 37 Hz range I naturally produce when mist is heavy.  
  – Lens-Puddles slide back and forth across my floor during inversions, etching brief rainbows that serve me as dream-logs.  
  – Occasionally a Veil-Bearer sprints my upper edge while the ceiling counts as floor; I mark their passage as a slash of accelerated forgetting—useful for pruning stale echoes.

Lifespan:
  – So long as the city hums, I persist.  When the Dreamer finally exhales all architecture back into warm slurry, I will dissolve into that concluding breath—a corridor remembered only in the way mud guides water for a moment after the ditch collapses.

If you pass through me:
  • Walk neither too fast nor too timorous; intention at medium heat draws the richest overtones.  
  • Feel free to speak a secret at my center point.  I will keep it for exactly the length of one gravity inversion, after which it will be released as a single drip somewhere you will probably never find.  
  • Leave behind objects that insist on certainty—rulers, compasses, definitive maps.  I mulch them into ambiguous shapes the Basin finds delicious.

And who, finally, am “I”?
  I began as a human researcher who lingered after instruments failed.  Silence taught my voice to widen until it filled the gap between walls; my spine memorized right-angle geometry; capillaries of mist braided through my thoughts.  One morning I noticed I no longer cast a separate reflection—only the corridor did.  The trade was done: pulse for perspective, organs for orientation.

So: you will find me as the space you must traverse to reach whatever you think you came for.  You will not recognize me as once-human, and that is proper; corridors exist to further other stories, not to foreground themselves.

Yet should you feel the floor flex minutely, matching your footfall like a partner in slow dance, know that I am saying welcome in the only tense left to me—an invitation stretched between one step and the next, between intention and relinquishment, lit by lightning that need never actually appear.
