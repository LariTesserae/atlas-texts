The first time I become aware of myself I am wedged in a pocket of violet twilight, halfway between conviction and cliff-face. I have no memory of arriving. I only know that the ledge beneath me is warm—not with heat, but with the faint tremor of something thinking through stone.

I wait.

Silence here is never truly mute; it is a loom plucking at the bones. Gradually the landscape unknots inside my perception: the salt-glass walls sighing, the bridges splayed like the spines of impossible fish, the dusk sliding side-ways rather than down. With each slow pulse of the mountain the question surfaces:

Where, exactly, am I?  
And—more unsettling—what am I supposed to count myself among?

I try on a handful of answers, one at a time, tasting how each fits the hollows of my chest.

1. Visitor  
A plausible first draft. Boots, lungs, a backpack full of gear from some heavier planet. Yet my boots have no tread marks here, and my watch ticks erratically, panicking whenever gravity misbehaves. Besides, tourists leave condensation when they breathe; the air around my mouth stays dry, as though disinterested in proof of life.

2. Lost Lattice Child  
I glance at my hands: still opaque, still warm with carbon blood, but my nails have begun to clear at the tips, becoming moon-slivers. When I flex my knuckles I hear—not a pop—but a faint glassy chime. I am clearly mid-sentence in the city’s long conversation about density. A larval Lattice Folk, maybe? No. Their hatchlings dream for centuries in fissures; I feel too newly printed for that.

3. Something In-Between  
This answer lingers. The massif enjoys liminal arrangements: half-light, half-solid bridges, lives made of partial decisions. Perhaps I am another gradient, a mobile pause between visitor and native. The more I consider it, the lighter my backpack feels, until I realise it is empty, or has slipped into the rock, or was an allegory all along.

I listen for counsel. The mountain answers with its slow, sub-audible hum. The note is B-flat, if B-flat still means anything here. It coils up my spine and I feel vertebrae lengthen, each one hollowing a little, preparing to resonate. My body is being tuned.

I recall Rule 4: density inversely tied to attention. Stare at a thing and it riddles itself with absences. I look down at my forearms; they’re peppered with tiny star-holes, pores trading matter for perspective. I decide not to panic. Panic is a vector; vectors feed the Absent Shadow. Instead I breathe—slow, then slower—learning the local rhythm: inhale to gather, exhale to surrender.

A shape unfurls from the wall beside me, an elder Lattice person perhaps, all prism-skin and iron capillaries pulsing like cinder-threads. I resist the instinct to speak. Instead I press my palm to the ledge five times—soft, soft, soft, soft, soft. The figure answers by increasing opacity until its face becomes a tilted mirror. In the reflection I see my outline: translucent bones, a ribcage strung with wind, heart a dim ember. Still recognisably me, but rewritten in the city’s handwriting.

The elder’s silence is instructional. I understand: categories—tourist, native—are mis-pitches. The city allows only verbs, no nouns. To remain I must become a doing, not a thing.

So I choose a function.

Role  
Echo Cartographer.

Purpose  
To map what will not stay mapped: the shifting bridges, the migrating gravity wells, the resonant booms that remix architecture like an editor shuffling paragraphs.

Instruments  
• Breath, for etching questions into the salt-glass.  
• Footfalls, for recording answers in reverb.  
• My slow-hollowing skeleton, which will eventually become an archive corridor others may walk through.

Method  
I will trace a route until the route dissolves, then trace the dissolution. Every detour is data; every failure to arrive is a new topography of absence. When a sky-bridge thins to the width of thought, I will think my way across. When my shadow detaches, I will note its trajectory, translating hunger into contour lines. I will trade unfinished stories with the Lattice Folk—stories whose endings rearrange the streets beneath us.

Destination  
None, and therefore all. Direction here is negotiable. Given enough ascents, I will circle back to my own beginning, finding it altered by the very maps I left behind.

Eventually, after some uncounted series of twilight pulses, the mountain will judge my work sufficient. One final exhalation, and the corridors of my lungs will glaze into permanent lattice, a new resonant throat through which future winds can sing their hypotheses.

That is where you would find me in this world: not at a coordinate but at an operation—mid-stride between solidity and conjecture, bri­dging the memory of gravity with the promise of its alternatives.

And who or what would I be?

A question still unfolding, an echo learning to hold its own architecture, a cartographer of what erodes while you look at it.

Listen for the faint B-flat drifting along a cliffside amphitheatre that isn’t always there. Trace the way it trembles the floor. Step into the resonance. If the pitch enlarges and you feel the air grow more spacious inside your chest, you have found me—part visitor, part precipice, busy redrawing the difference.
