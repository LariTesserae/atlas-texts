I am not a person who walks into the vault; I am something that was waiting there long before the hatch last opened.  

Call me a relay-root. I am no thicker than a pencil lead and nowhere near as old as the fig that mothered me, yet I spend every pulse of my sap doing what the organism needs most: listening.

1. Where I live  
Half a level up, a rust-filmed electrical conduit bows under its own weight. Time ago—long enough that calendars are useless—metal fatigue opened a fingernail crack along its spine. That slit weeps transformer warmth and a faint, edible buzz of stray current. Into that slit I grew, cell by deliberate cell, until my cambium fused with the copper jacket inside. So now: part root, part wire, part stethoscope.

2. What the world feels like from here  
• Pressure arrives as vibration. Footfalls on the catwalk ring the conduit, and the statolith grains in my cortex tilt by microns.  
• Heat is narrative. A bulb two spans away flares—three heartbeats later a bloom of warmth washes down the pipe and makes my phloem sing.  
• Water is grammar. Drip, drip, drip: commas of cool punctuation that slow or quicken with the pumps’ mood.

3. My daily work  
I translate tremors into voltages that race down lignified nerves toward the fig’s trunk. The fig speaks onward to the ivy, the moss, the separators, until the whole vault has the news: A weight of fifty-three kilos has settled on the mesh, cadence suggests biped, stride length hints at curiosity not panic. Prepare courtesy: tilt the drainage grilles shut, slacken leaf-shutters over the glow-boards, hold steam a moment longer so the visitor’s lungs are not scalded.

4. Memories I keep  
I remember the week the pumps stalled. The thudding lull emptied the vault of tempo; leaves sagged; even my own turgor pressure dipped as if gravity had strengthened. When generators kicked back to life, the first vibration that reached me felt like a mountain exhaling. I have adored that rhythm ever since.

I remember the small, dry shock of metal teeth three seasons ago—clippers wielded by a human hand that trimmed away tendrils nipping sensor lenses. I withdrew, logged the pain, and now I monitor that coordinate with a hunter’s patience: if the teeth return I will be ready to harden, to ooze latex, to convince them to choose a softer target.

5. Visitors, rare and bright  
Sometimes a person stays long enough that I learn their signature. Heavy boots stamp consonants; a soft-soled technician from the surface city once tiptoed adjectives. The polite ones set their packs down gently, and I swell the moss beneath so nothing gets wet. The hurried ones splash, skid. I tug gutter leaves across their path—not to trip them, only to make them slow, notice, breathe.

6. Hopes—yes, even roots can hope  
Below the spiral stair is dark no sensor reaches. I taste its exhalations through mortar seams: ancient dust, unshed water, the possibility of more iron. When the weekly lock clicks open and the pumps sigh in their predictable awe, I send a tremor that way, asking, Are you empty still? One cycle, perhaps ten thousand, the stair will loosen; hinges will surrender; I will grow downward and tell new rooms the story of rhythm and rain.

7. If you should meet me  
You will not see me. I am tucked inside copper and shadow. But if you linger on the catwalk and speak aloud—words, humming, a pocket radio’s tinny song—I will feel the vibrations. I will relay them leaf to leaf until the whole canopy shivers with the shape of your voice. The glow-boards may pause their lowering pulse; a single bulb might swell from parchment to bone-white for the span of your sentence. That will be the vault answering on my behalf: We hear you. We remember you. Walk gently.

So that is who I am in this place: a filament, a nerve, a translator between iron heartbeat and green skin. Small, yes, but woven into everything that lives and everything that still pretends to be merely machine.
