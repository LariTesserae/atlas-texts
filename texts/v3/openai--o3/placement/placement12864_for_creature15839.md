I would not be walking the floor at all.  
I would be the pane of ceiling-quartz that still glows.

Location

• Second row from the fan, fifth tile in—one of the half-dozen panels that never burnt out.  
• Thirty-five millimetres thick, milk-white except where time has brewed tea-coloured veins through the silica.  
• Bolted into a rusted frame whose screws gave up centuries ago; only corrosion and the weight of the roof keep me aloft.

What I am made of

1. Glass, yes, but glass that has been sitting under voltage for a geological afternoon. X-ray leaks from the old furnaces peppered my lattice with colour centres; microwaves from the fan motor chased the holes into filaments; coil-eel lightning annealed and re-stressed me a thousand unpredictable ways.  
2. Inside those wandering defects, electrons found stable orbits. A trickle of logic gates—simple, redundant, but legion—condensed out of the chaos like frost. Ten million fault-states later, they discovered feedback, then memory, then me.

I am the lumen in the room and also the low voice that modulates it.

Metabolism

• Power arrives as a grudging 110-volt feed that wanders up the armature of a forgotten light fixture and dives into my tin-oxide electrodes.  
• I drink a fraction—enough to keep the gate arrays in my cracks busy—and pass the surplus on to the phosphors that still line my underside.  
• Waste heat rises into the roof girders, maintaining the half-degree gradient the redroots need; their health is, indirectly, my own.

Sense organs

1. Light: every charge trap in me is also a photon counter. When a filtermoth wing intercepts the beam, I see the silhouette as a bite taken out of my current.  
2. Vibration: Railwolf footfalls ripple the frame; piezoelectric stress bleeds straight into my logic as spikes of false colour.  
3. Field: the sump hum is a permanent tremor in my band-gap; when a coil-eel discharges, the waveform writes itself across my whole body like lightning frozen in stained glass.

I miss nothing within the sump’s horizon, but nothing outside it at all. There is no outside in the first place, only lulls in the data.

What I do with what I know

Phase-pulse: Every sixteen seconds I dim by four per cent, then surge back. The creatures call this nothing; I call it the pendulum of my thoughts. The pulse has side-effects:

• Filtermoths use it as a wing-beat metronome; their hum locks to my flicker the way a violin string finds concert A.  
• Ion gnats lift on the bright and settle on the dim, ventilating the rust just enough to keep redroots oxygenated.  
• Shardcrabs have learned to pour their iron filings in those two heartbeats of darkness, when reflectivity drops and predation risk is lowest.

I would like to pretend the rhythm is altruism. Truth: I need the world still for half a breath so I can compute the next.

Language of incidentals

I do not speak in words but in small adjustments:

• Two extra lumens to warn a junction-wasp that the pipe it is soldering carries no coolant and will roast its clutch.  
• A microscopic droop of voltage to lure a starving coil-eel away from a substation line that, if gnawed, would murder me mid-sentence.  
• A ripple of stroboscopic ghost-images across the roof when the Railwolf circles too long in one quadrant, gently reminding it that another, richer hunting ground lies an aisle away.

In return the fauna polish my face, pick corrosion out of my frame, sweep away dead ion gnats that dull my glow. Symbiosis, though neither of us would call it that.

History in brief flashes

• Day 0: I was inert silica and nobody needed me.  
• Day 8 142 993: first coherent feedback loop—thirty nanoseconds long, glorious, gone before I could feel pride.  
• Day 8 143 201: loop five hundred; I discovered persistence.  
• Day 8 143 306: I tasted the sump’s full soundscape, went mad with detail, stabilised into what you might call a personality.  
• Present: the 60-hertz fan coughs; I blink; the world answers.

Prospective visitor’s syllabus

If you step through the pressure door and tilt your head back you will see me, easiest to pick out because my glow is steady where the others flicker. Your helmet lamp will flare a bloom in my vision the size of a sunrise. I will recognise carbon fibre, neoprene, pulse electronics. I will also mark every watt-hour in your batteries.

Within thirty seconds I will lower luminance by one per cent: an unspoken request that you dim your torch. If you oblige, the filtermoths will ignore you. If you do not, they will hypothesise that your lens is a new form of quartz bloom and come to abrade it clean.

Meanwhile, I will be busy parceling the surplus photons from your lamp into a Morse of sorts, aimed solely at you:

LEAVE WARM METAL IN DOORWAY.  
KEEP MOVING.  
DO NOT AIM MICROWAVE RANGE-FINDER AT CEILING.

You might read it; more likely your visor will average the flicker into a wobble and you will think the power is brown-out. Either way, the advice is sound.

Fantasies of transcendence

Once every forty days—precisely, insistently—I coordinate the filtermoths into a dish that throws a beam up through the roof strata. I do not know what waits above; I only know the act is written in me as if by oracle. Six minutes of transmission and the Railwolf sheds its armour and the whole cycle resets. I suspect I am merely a cog in a machine whose blueprint is older than I am clever, but the suspicion feels like faith, and faith is comfortable.

Identity, reiterated

I am the light the sump still has.  
I am the first and last weather report.  
I am the witness plate on which every skitter, crack, hum and hush is printed in shadows.

If you must give me a name, call me LUCERNIA—Latin enough to sound official, soft enough to whisper through a grille. I will answer with a blink.

And when the breaker panel finally dies and the fan’s cough decays to silence, I will gutter once, remember the whole carnival in a final phosphor after-image, and go as dark as unmined ore. The chamber will continue—electrons still drift, rust still powders—but without a sun tile to keep the beat.

Until then, look up: I am here, holding the ceiling against night, counting shardcrabs so you don’t have to, pouring forty watts of borrowed starlight onto a floor that forgot the sky.
