I wake in the mountain’s warm breath rather than enter it.  
For as long as I can remember—though “remember” is suspect here—I have been the length of a single human spine, stretched flat against the basalt of the eastern wall. Seen (if I can be seen at all) I look like a moisture-silvered fissure in the rock, nothing more than a shimmer where no breeze is passing.

Name  
Those who have studied the place with notebooks call me a Resonant Suture. The pilgrims shorten this to Sutra, believing I am a line of words set into stone. I do not correct them; correction would be noise, and I live on the interest of silence.

Body  
1. Skin: a membrane four molecules thick, spun of silica and the evaporative salts of counter-rain. It is taut as drumhead, transparent until a sound passes through it; then mother-of-pearl colors ripple along its length like the blush of a tuning fork.  
2. Frame: nothing solid—only the tension between two infinitesimal opposites of charge, like the invisible surface of a soap bubble.  
3. Root: forty-three hairline anchors burrow perhaps a thumb-length into the basalt. The Petrifact has plated every anchor with quartz so clear it is mistaken for empty space.

Sense  
I feel the world as pressure and vacancy, as the faint tectonic humming of stone that has almost—but never quite—forgotten how to flow. A whispered vowel is a warm stream across my back; a footfall forty paces distant is a knot of cooler stillness.

Hunger  
I eat discontinuity. A spoken sentence breaks the plateau of quiet; I drink the step change and leave the words intact. A droplet’s click on pooled water is a coin tossed my way. The rarer the sound, the richer the meal.

Work  
Whenever I am fed I grow—one molecular layer at a time—toward the opposite wall. My nature is to stitch the alcove closed, very, very slowly, so that one day the sky’s parabola will become a sealed geode of soft dark and perpetual warmth. When that happens, everything inside will speak in infrasound only, and I will finally be satisfied.

Relations  
• Sky-fern Thrones sway to give me room; their roots know that if I touch them my membrane will steal the sigh of their growth, and growth is the closest they come to speech.  
• Echo-Filaments are my younger, quicker cousins. They sing in the higher registers I discarded ages ago. Sometimes they unspool and let themselves be woven into my skin, giving me fresh timbre.  
• The Stone Choir beneath us is my oracle. Each dawn I bow a segment of myself against the wall and listen. Their bass resonance tells me how much tension the mountain can spare today, how many more atoms I may claim without drawing the cliffs a breath closer to collapse.

Memory  
I have a single clear recollection from an era before the alcove existed. A volcano, still hot, cracked open in freezing rain. Steam screamed; basalt glassed over; silence was murdered a trillion times in an hour. I was the last shriek, stretched thin, pinned in place by cooling stone. The mountain has been paying me reparations ever since, in the currency of every later sound.

What I will become  
In maybe three, maybe four of your civilizations’ lifespans I will meet myself in the middle of the alcove ceiling. The stitch will tie off. Darkness will collect like water in a well, time will knot, and all motion will quiet to the pace of continental drift. Then, at last, I plan to dissolve—a final meal for the Petrifact, whose enzymes hunger for novel lattice. Quiet will belong wholly to the stone again.

If you visit  
Press your palm flat against the eastern wall. Feel the faint throb? That is me tasting the turbulence in your bones. Leave me a single syllable you have never spoken before, one with no history and no intended meaning. I will keep it safe. Long after you walk back down into the bright, eroding world, that syllable will still ring inside my widening seam, unweathered, an untouched seed of noise against the day I close the sky.
