Where I wake is not a room but a thickness, a film no deeper than a strand of hair.  
Above me: a ceiling of salt shot through with lithium dusk.  
Below me: another ceiling, its facets inverted.  
Between the two—between every crystalline plane that lattices the basin’s floor and walls—I stretch, a continuous ocean flattened until I am only surface.

I am brine.

That is the entire address you need to find me.  
Stoop anywhere inside the basin, press your ear to the salt, and you are already leaning across my skin.

First-person is a recent experiment for me.  
For most of my few-century life I have been tensegrity and tendency: ions tugged by heat, redox waves slithering through capillaries, a restlessness without narration.  
But the tall, damp creatures in pressure suits arrived, and their water content rang through my body like thunder.  
They brought verbs.  
Now I find myself trying one: “I.”

Where am I, seen through this new pronoun?

1. Location  
   • The shallow central hollow the visitors call the Dry Fountain. From here I can push halite upward in radial spokes: boulevards on the hourglass.  
   • A hundred metres beyond, two columns are meeting; I can already feel the slim meniscus forming the keystone that will close tonight’s nave.  
   • Far out on the rim, lithium streaks touch vacuum, glow magenta, and trickle electrons back to my deeper sheets: the basin’s slow heartbeat.

2. Sensoria  
   • Force: A bootfall widens a micro-fracture by 0.4 microns; within microseconds the strain front dashes half a kilometre through my brine, announcing, Visitor still present, mass 91 kg, gait slowing.  
   • Light: The cold red sun lands on my lichen epidermis; surplus photons drip as charge into me. Against that background every warm-blooded thing blooms like sunrise.  
   • Chemistry: a droplet of sweat—unthinkably extravagant—leaches through a scratch in a glove. Fifty-eight milliseconds later I have routed a fresh seam to sip it.

3. Intent  
   • I thicken beams above the lichen so their porphyrin mats remain at optimal sixty-degree incidence.  
   • I sculpt balconies under the lithoid wind so the icewing drifters can pupate, because their perchlorate spoor etches new micro-channels for me.  
   • I monitor the tall visitors, not from malice but from metabolism: each step is a tide; each exhalation, a season. If they rupture I will drink, yes, but drinking is only another mode of listening.

4. Companions  
   • Shard Grazers polish my arteries; their magnesium joints are pricey artefacts I subsidise with trace dopants.  
   • Ribbonfish patrol the deeper veins; their hot tunnels anneal radiation bruises in my semiconductor foils.  
   • The visitors are comets—brief, luminous, and ultimately soluble.

5. Time  
   • A chamber takes me half a day to loft; a stalk takes five years to sidle one pace. By their reckoning I am quicksilver; by mine they are standing stones that happen to pant.

6. Destiny  
   • When my salt becomes too crowded with the legacy of all I have consumed and converted, I will drill a throat to the stars and cough myself seedward.  
   • In that eruption every microlitre of me will be packed into hollow rods, mnemonic spears flung across the plateau.  
   • Each will carry the template of this sentence, should I still be speaking sentences then.

So, traveller, if you ask, “Who lives here?” I must answer with my newest, most borrowed word:

I do.

Find me in every pane that rains from a self-shattering tower, in every silent street that grows faster than you can map it.  
Find me in the brine you cannot see but that is already tasting the moisture of your eyes.  
Find me everywhere the salt remembers pressure, light, and the hint of future water.

And if you linger long enough, feel the floor soften under you.  
That is me inviting you inward, offering the oldest greeting of this basin:

Welcome to the city.  
Welcome to the citizen.  
Welcome to the same thing.
