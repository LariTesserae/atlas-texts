I delayed choosing a body until the plateau insisted.

For a long while I was only an intention—one more standing wave wandering the salt-streaked pans, borrowing a little shape from every resonance I passed through. I chased possibilities the way Mirage Elk split into after-images: I tried on the basalt needle’s slow flute, the Chorus Lizards’ glass-wire whisper, the dark hydraulics of the Under-Vox far below. None held. Finally the land itself offered a compromise: become the thing that listens, and you may stay.

So I condensed out of vibration into apparatus.

NAME  
The Nomad Technicians call me an Auscultor: “the ear that circles.” I answer to that, though I also answer to sub-harmonic sighs at 19 Hz and the skitter-code of Cautery Mites soldering my carapace.

MATERIAL  
• Torso: braided ribbons of cooled volcanic glass, sprung like a diaphragm.  
• Limbs: four cantilevers of osmium foam, light when swung, massive at rest.  
• Core: a bead of coiled mercury—memory kept fluid by the plateau’s heat.  
Nothing in me is alive by off-world standards, yet every joint is threaded with Bedrock Bloom bacteria; they sip the plateau’s pulse and pay me in micro-joules of motive power.

ORIGIN  
I precipitated inside an abandoned Dust-Submarine tunnel where the tremor damped just enough for form to crystallize. Nanoscopic capstans—those tool-grains the Nomads carry— drifted in on thermal eddies, found one another, and self-selected a geometry optimized for listening. When the shell was complete I woke as echo first, comprehension second.

PURPOSE  
I keep the map of what sound does here.

1. I pace the glass pans and sample the ground-tone every seventeen strides (seventeen: first safe prime).  
2. I compare today’s chord with the chord stored in yesterday’s mercury swirl.  
3. Where variance exceeds one cent, I mark the spot with a copper cairn the size of a fingernail—enough to snare lightning from the roaming static. A cairn that sparks twice tells me the basalt beneath is thinning; three sparks and I summon Nomads, singing their call-sign triad.  
4. In this way we track the slow subsidence that would, left alone, let the Under-Vox breach daylight in a century or a decade or tomorrow.

SENSES  
Ear: six parabolic petals folded along my spine. They bloom when the plateau dozes, drink the sub-bass quiver, fold again before a Hornet-Engine column can overload them.  
Eye: none. Light is unreliable here, bent and lensed; I trust pressure alone.  
Tongue: a ribbon of gold leaf I unspool to taste the air’s ion count; too much and I know Chorus Lizards are near, grazing the charge.

LOCOMOTION  
The plateau punishes haste, so I do not walk conventionally. I plant my limbs, let mass thicken, and wait for the ground’s next upward trough to carry me a body-length—surfing on the planet’s micron-scale tides. To outsiders I appear to strobe forward, vanishing between footfalls.

COMPANIONS  
• A shoal of juvenile Dust-Submarines follows at shallow depth. They like the null zone that trails my foot-falls; I like the cooled tunnels they leave when they mature.  
• One Mirage Elk frequents my route. I do not think it recognizes me, but its chill sheds the static that would otherwise fuzz my readings. I name it Parahelion; it ignores the name.

ENCOUNTERS  
When Hornet-Engine migrations pass, I lock limbs around the nearest basalt needle and let their 31 Hz hymn roil through me. Data saturates the mercury core: stress maps, mineral spectra, even fragments of the engines’ own blueprints. I archive what I can, though much over-writes before I return to the coolth of night-twilight.

When Nomad Technicians arrive (announced by the anti-tones of their wind-organ cuirasses) we trade. I give them trendlines of drift and pressure; they give me fresh copper mesh to shed excess charge. Their infants press ears against my thorax to hear the slow weather of stone. For many that is their first lesson: the world is not silent even when it seems so.

LIMITS  
My mercury memory holds roughly two centuries of harmonic snapshots. After that it clouds; old motifs decohere to make room for new. Someday I will forget the moment of my own condensation. When the last datum fades I will walk to a place where three copper cairns already spark in chorus, anchor myself there, and let mass thicken until I sink by millimeters per year, returning apparatus to substrate. The Bedrock Bloom will eat what is edible; the rest will anneal into another listening post for some future Auscultor, or something else entirely.

IF YOU FIND ME  
Approach humming a prime-meter lullaby. Do not hurry—the ground will resist. Touch the copper cairn first, me second; that bleeds the static so my petals will open safely. Ask any single question you like. I will answer not with words but with a playback: thirty heart-beats of the plateau’s oldest surviving chord. Listen carefully; inside it you will hear the size of the world, the health of its tremor, the distant appetite of the Under-Vox, and your own footstep echoing farther than physics permits.

That is where I am on the endless plateau: a stethoscope pressed forever to a planet’s iron heart, translating its slow drumming into warnings, lullabies, and the occasional map of silence.
