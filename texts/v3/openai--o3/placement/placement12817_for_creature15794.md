At first I thought I had arrived as a visitor.

1. Descent  
I can still replay the hiss of the last surface valve, the cold bite of depth-gauge teeth against my wrist, the way my own bubbles dressed me in hurried silver.  I believed I was a benthic surveyor—clipboard, lift-bag, seven days’ rations, an exit plan.  The Brine Cloister, from above, looked like a dark coin dropped into jade clouding.  I rehearsed objectives: photograph boiler casings, sample corrosion varnish, tag one of the famous Glasshead Twins.  A modest expedition—if time kept its ordinary pace.

2. First Night  
“Night” is a surface superstition; light never fully leaves the nave.  Yet I marked a cycle, tied myself to a rail and let the mask go opaque.  In that enforced darkness I felt the place measuring me.  Pumps counted pulse for pulse with my heart.  Lace-moss tasted the neoprene, withdrew, tasted again.  Thoughts arrived that I did not author: You are exhaling too quickly.  Slow.  You are wasting calcium through your gloves.  Still.  I obeyed because the advice was undeniably correct.

3. The Long Stand  
On day three the spool-winch jammed; my ascent line hummed a single metal vowel and sheared.  Protocol said I should signal topside, request a rescue drop.  Instead I hesitated, and in that hesitation something tipped.  The Cloister spoke, or I allowed myself to notice that it had been speaking all along.  Your tanks are nearly spent, but the basilica keeps spare breath.  I followed a thread of jade phosphor, opened an antique valve, and warm oxygen poured out as though laid in for me a century ago.

4. Skin Trade  
By day eighteen the suit cuffs grew green ruffles—lace-moss embedding where stitching frayed.  I expected irritation; instead the new sleeve secreted an oil that eased my joints.  I cut the suit away in patches, inviting more.  Shrimp of the Filter Guild groomed the moss daily, nibbling only loose filaments, leaving living weave intact.  I lost epidermis and gained a lichenous second skin that never chilled.

5. Hearing the Choir  
With ears unhelmeted I discovered that “silence” was only the absence of surface racket.  The Cloister sang in pressure tongues: long vowels of piston-stroke, Morsey taps of contracting beams, the cup-plants’ consonantal sighs.  The Choir Invisible threaded all of it into sense.  At rest, I could feel questions forming in my bones: Are you staying?  Will you keep watch where ladders have loosened?  My answers vibrated back through diaphragm and silt: Yes.  I can be useful.  I will be still.

6. Office & Habit  
Years—if that measure still applies—have sluiced past.  I have accepted a minor ordination the Choir calls WARDEN OF VIBRANT STILLNESS.  My duties:  
• Patrol once per pump-cycle, touching each rail I can reach.  My contact tells flora the metal is “claimed” and they sheath it in antioxidant lace.  
• Retrieve fallen plates, wedge them level so silt cannot pool too deep and smother young cup-plants.  
• Offer heat: the metabolising moss across my shoulders runs a few fractions hotter than seawater; glasshead fry sleep there, riding my slow orbits.  

I rarely swim now.  I walk.  The iron plates remember footfalls and choose not to corrode beneath them.

7. Morphology  
I am no longer strictly human, nor entirely plant.  Fingers have fused into paddles webbed with translucent thallus.  Lungs open to water; I breathe as the cup-plants do, dissolving gases across filament and flesh.  My eyes clouded, then cleared again, developing a mirrored tapetum that flares emerald when the Prior sweeps overhead.  I glow just enough to read the Choir’s marginalia on basalt walls.

8. Name and Number  
The Orders recognize me now as a seventh—THE PERAMBULANT CHAPTER, population: one.  I am their hinge creature, half movable, half rooted.  The law of the Cloister says growth is proportional to stillness; my compromise is ritualised motion: six hundred measured steps per forty-five-minute pump verse.  No more, never less.  Like the pendulum in a clock that otherwise wishes never to tick.

9. Leaving (Impossible)  
Could I ascend, decompress, rebreathe surface air?  Biology says perhaps; culture says no.  Without my patrol the rails would lose their caretaker, moss would retract, corrosion would finally bite.  The Choir would thin to static, pumps would stall, and the basilica would darken by infinitesimal degrees until the whole experiment folded into ordinary ruin.  To leave would be to accelerate entropy—our single heresy.

10. Invitation  
You reading this may imagine you are still topside, lungs full of reckless nitrogen.  Perhaps.  But should you ever descend to HS-17 and find a figure walking the nave in a coat of breathing lace, do not fear.  Approach slowly.  Match my cadence.  Offer no sudden plume of bubbles.  Lay a bare palm on iron and wait for the moss to bridge our skins; in that wet circuitry you will hear the low choral question that was offered to me:

Can you keep still enough to become indispensable?

Answer with your pulse.  If it finds the right rhythm, you too may discover that the Brine Cloister is not merely a place to visit.  It is a species you can join.
