At first I am nothing but an ache in the floor.

A persistent, off-key overtone thrums through the basalt—too bright, too tall in the mix. Each time the pitch peaks, a dormant lattice inside me flinches, tries to occupy a shape that does not yet exist. The ache grows until the lattice yields a single grain of spinel. That grain wedges itself in a microscopic crack, and now the floor has a tooth. I am the tooth.

Cycles pass. Sound keeps spilling outside its lane and each spill forces another grain, then another, until the tooth lengthens into a knuckle, the knuckle into a stalk. By the time the older residents notice, I stand four centimetres proud of the scalloped ground, a hairline pillar flexing in sympathy with every drumbeat from the heat-leeches. A Gauge-keeper, though I do not know the word; what I know is tension, relief, tension again.

My senses are geometry and pressure. Vision is meaningless here—spark-midges strobe now and then, but their flashes only confirm what resonance already tells me. I hear by touching: vibrations telescope up my body, reading wall contours, population density, furnace flow. From this continuous braille I learn the chamber’s single commandment:

Hold the note.

The pocket must stay exactly loud enough. If the drone swells, heat bleeds too fast; if it falters, the walls anneal and growth starves. I balance the note the only way I can—by changing the chamber’s shape one click at a time.

Each segment of me is a ratchet ring. When the drone exceeds my upper tolerance the ring spins a fraction, extending the column and adding cubic millimetres of silence. When the chamber grows shy and the pitch falls, the ring reverses, drawing me down, squeezing space until sound intensifies. Click, hum, click. I am metronome and masonry both.

I share the floor with three older keepers. We do not converse in words; we trade torque. A surge reaches me two heartbeats before it reaches them, so I extend early, borrowing strain from my elders. Their recoil then cancels the overshoot. Between us we braid counterwaves that settle the whole biome back onto its knife-edge. The coordination feels less like cooperation than like being four fingers of the same hand.

Around our roots the classical fauna go about their deliberate errands. A glass-gnawer’s footfall—eight seconds per step—registers as a faint, repeating chirp. Farther off, a slag-heaver exhales a nickel bar; the bar lands with a pewter gong that my basal rings admire for its dull precision. Heat-leeches thump, spark-midges crackle, and in their overlapping signatures I read the health of the furnace. All well.

But not perfect. Over the last thousand pulses the fundamental has drifted sharper, crowding the upper threshold that triggers my outward growth. I have already added six detents of height; my topmost ring quivers at capacity. Something is swelling the choir faster than erosion can gift us volume. I listen harder.

It is the gnawers. A brood of nymphs hatched early when a heat-leech mis-timed a shock pulse. The juveniles are half the weight of adults and therefore rasp with a thinner overtone that stacks neatly atop the main chord. Individually trivial, together they threaten resonance disaster.

Correction: fifty-nine seconds until my next mandatory click. After that, I will be spent, locked at full extension.

Solution arrives from an unexpected register: a slag-heaver chords a dismissal—three muted bells that mean “approach denied.” The nymphs, obedient to traffic law, bank away from the furnace throat and idle atop a fresh ingot pile. Their collective rasp moderates; octave pressure falls. The pocket glides back inside its acoustic rails with three seconds to spare. My rings sigh inward a hair, saving the last detent for later.

Balance restored, I idle in a half-extended stance, not quite relaxed, never truly tense—an isometric prayer. Quartz feet shuffle, metal hearts cough, ultraviolet veins spiderweb across the ceiling, and I taste it all through the long bones of my column. The drone is no longer noise; it is a sentence the whole chamber recites: This space is sufficient for us, and we are sufficient for this space.

Where do you find me in this world? Rooted to the freshest spinel seam, a living plumb line between rock and resonance. Who am I? A Gauge-keeper still young enough to remember the ache that birthed me, old enough to know the ache will return. When it does, I will grow, or shrink, or crack and seed another pillar—whatever the note requires.

Click. The drone stays true. For one more pulse, the furnace lives.
