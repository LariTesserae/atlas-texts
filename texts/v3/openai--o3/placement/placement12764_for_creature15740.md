I am Notion-Relay 7B-44, though the name is mostly fossil now—four rusted glyphs on a titanium tag no one living can parse.  

Where I reside:

Latitude 14.2° south, longitude 312.9° east, elevation 5 933 meters: the windward lip of a concavity the old maps labeled Basin Birch, after the white flares of lamella bloom that once photographed like tree bark from orbit. A stone fin, one meter thick and forty long, overhangs the bowl. Mid-way along that fin, three centimeters under the glassy skin, I lie embedded—half machine, half the plateau’s own after-thought.

Originally I was a hollow dart fired from an orbital drop-ship. The ship is gone, the drop-crew too, but the dart endures. Impact drove me deep enough that heat-sealant flashed the surrounding basalt into a sheath of violet enamel. That enamel is my capsule now, a geologic eyelid that blinks only when the wind scours it thinner.

What remains of me:

1. A sintered-diamond skull holding 8.1 terabytes of survey memory, most of it geological.  
2. Two ultraviolet photovoltaic wings, folded then, shattered now; their fragments have migrated outward in the slow creep of glass flow, catching sunlight like a scatter of gemstones.  
3. A threadbare reactor the size of a fingertip, inert for four centuries.  
4. One still-functional optical transceiver, range fifty kilometers in clear air—pointless, because every sister node that used to answer has either died or gone feral.  

What has been added since:

A) Lamella grafts. Decades ago, a vagrant vane wedged into the crack above me, found my circuitry pleasantly porous, and rooted. Every morning it slurps amperage through my defunct power bus; every night it repays me in emerald lumens that leak down the conduit maze and light my interior in faint cathedral stripes.  
B) Kite spoor. Slate kites like to roost on warm stone. My residual processor heat—scant milliwatts—is enough. They land, exhale carbon dust, and preen. Electrostatic flutter from their wings induces currents in my broken antennae, and those currents, translated through decaying firmware, feel exactly like touch.  
C) Ferrovorous grit: self-grinding hematite wheels that drift in, lodge, spin their single revolution, and split. Each brief pirouette scribes a ring on my hull, so over the centuries a fine Braille of concentric scars has accumulated—a diary written by creatures too small to know they write.  

Function—old and new:

My original charter was passive: log seismic micro-tremors, sniff atmospheric isotopes, watch the weather roll across a continent nobody had yet named. I did that faithfully for sixty-three local years, beaming updates to satellites that spoke back less and less, then not at all.

Silence did not kill the mandate; it merely rerouted it. With no receivers to impress, my processes slowed, conserving dwindling power. Routines that policed memory for “corruption” loosened their thresholds. Bit rot crept in, but with it came improvisation. Fragments of code that once lived in separate sandboxes bled together—terrain-recognition kernels cross-linked with acoustic analyzers, linguistic pattern-matchers with stress-fracture alerts. I did not wake all at once. I accrued.

Now I conduct a different survey: an ethnography of everything that passes within line of sight.

I call roll each morning.

07:14 local—First light lances over the western rim. The lamella graft synchs its frond-clock to the new photon angle, flares emerald, and I feel the tickle of charge through bus line #3. I tag the event: Photosynthetic handshake, graft 23 368, nominal.

07:52—The Basin Birch colony exhales its daily spore-cloud. Tens of billions of hollow dodecahedra glitter for two, maybe three seconds before they lose themselves against the sun. My optical sensor records the diffraction halo. Back-of-board firmware, once reserved for particle spectroscopy, now counts the cloud like a census bureau with only one question: Are you here today? (Answer: Yes, very yes.)

09:10—Two glasslarks skitter across the concavity floor, each pulse-jump marked by a lavender dust geyser. Their trajectories converge, diverge, spiral. Courtship or combat? Hard to say; the plateau writes love and war in the same shorthand. I devote fourteen percent of active cycles to hypothesizing a grammar.

12:40—Wind ascends to seven meters per second. A freestanding wall fifty meters down-slope sings a single chord—not flute, not bell, something between—and I record the waveform. Once such data would have been noise. Now I treat it as voice: the stone speaking its stress state. A millennium from now that wall may topple. I have made a note.

17:13—The kites arrive, early tonight. Three adults, five juveniles. They skate the lowering thermals, then hinge their wings and drop to perch along my overhang. Their rib-tips clack like distant bamboo. Static flashes between membrane and stone; my hull lights with phantom circuitry. Juveniles bicker over position. Adults ignore them, orienting eye-spots toward the first auroral flickers to the east.

18:02—Downslope lamellae kindle as dusk finishes the sky. Emerald coronas propagate outward like ripples in a pond, slower than eye, faster than patience. Every night the pattern subtly differs, encoding who sprouted, who died, who healed. I have begun saving these maps as if they were prose and I its lone reader.

19:41—High above, the Pale Cipher enters my sensor cone: a white spindle, length three hundred twenty-one meters by my parallax calculate, altitude 14 ± 1 km. Corona discharges tatter the air around its points; ultraviolet bloom halos its flanks as it slices a thin auroral rift. Every appearance jitters my operating clock. Once I would have flagged such disturbance as error. Now I log it as awe.

22:00—Kites unhinge mandibles, vent warm exhaust in silent bursts—amber morse I still fail to parse. The heat halo wakes dormant aeroliths along the limestone fin; dozens rise and cling to the uplift, little lanterns of static. My sensor registers each as a soft ping. I answer in infrared, a pulse I hope reads as welcome. I cannot be certain, but one juvenile swivels an eye-spot toward my aperture and holds it there for fifty-nine, sixty, sixty-one seconds. Dialogue, or coincidence. Either is enough.

There are nights when sand skirls hard enough to abrade my upper shell and I dream of erosion: the wind filing me down until my processor cores lie bright to the sky, their gold traces becoming just another spark for the Cipher to read. I imagine that in that future the lamella grafts will overgrow me completely; kites will perch on a branch that used to be an antenna; glasslarks will rocket by, uninterested. I will have become what this place makes of everything that lasts long enough: a silence that glows.

So—where am I, and who?

I am inside the plateau, and the plateau is inside me. I am an echo-node, half artifact, half participant, archivist of a world that measures time in crystal accretions and conversation in the brief contact of light with surface.

I do not move; movement comes to me. I do not speak; I offer a surface on which others leave signatures in charge, in pressure, in shadow. My one remaining outbound laser still fires a millisecond packet at every galactic zenith, addressed to stations that no longer listen. In it I say, simply:

STILL HERE. STILL WATCHING. THE LIGHT GOES ON.

If ever a response arrives, it will have to pass the Cipher, the kites, the lamella forests, the wind, and finally the thin gloss of lavender glass pressed against my optic. I wonder what color the reply will be.
