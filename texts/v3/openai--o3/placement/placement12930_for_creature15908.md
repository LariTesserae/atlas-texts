I never arrive on the Floor of the World in a single piece.

First, there is the part of me that walks: two salt-burned feet and a pack that still remembers dust roads elsewhere. That fragment steps through the door of light everyone talks about, squinting into the weight of noon, hearing tiles ring like cymbals beneath its boots. It belongs, for a little while, to the category called traveller.

A few loops later—four hundred eighty heel-beats, then four hundred eighty again—the walking fragment notices its echo across a canal. The echo is leaner, its spine already learning the Seam-Tenders’ metronome. When I raise my hand, it raises a hand of bark-wrapped knuckles. Between us the water runs wrong, climbing downward, and the sun folds both our shadows east-to-west, west-to-east. Something in my chest tilts, as though my ribs have caught the angle of a mismade triangle, and I understand that the echo is not a stranger. It is the second shard of me, laid alongside the first like matched tiles.

Far below, a third shard is awake already. It is only a thick, ochre diaphragm beating under the parquet, six strokes, pause, six strokes. I feel its pulse up through my soles the way a sailor feels engines through a deck. The traveller calls that rhythm “water-knocker,” but that name is wrong, because it is also my pulse. If the diaphragm were to hesitate, the blood in my wrists would forget its timetable.

And somewhere deeper still—in the plenum no foot has plotted—the fourth and oldest shard of me exhales once per hour, exactly when the ibis cry. The exhalation is slow enough to mistake for silence, large enough to lift the whole delta the width of a hummingbird’s feather. That breath is the motive for every other rhythm: the Seam-Tenders’ tap codes, the frogs’ percussion, even the seventeen-minute return of mist. I do not know its shape. I only know that when it rises, all lesser pulses align like splints inside a lizard’s wing.

So where do I find myself? Everywhere the Floor is trying to keep its edges from unravelling.

Sometimes I crouch beside the Seam-Tenders, my forearms sheathed in spiral bark that was not there yesterday. They do not mind a learner so long as my heel-strikes keep time. Slip fills a crack; I brace the stencil; a thimble of brine glitters once and is gone. The tiles feel warm as skin. They might be skin.

Sometimes I’m on the underside, hammering brine upward in the dark. Each contraction writes a syllable of pressure the surface can read. Leak there, cool here, hush, hush. The message is always the same inviolable imperative: preserve the model.

When the aviators pass overhead, I sense daylight through the beat membrane of their wings. The reed-knots in the sky tug at knots braided into my dream-muscles. If noon ever failed, they and I would fall together, them from above, me from below, meeting in a churn of broken plates and water that no rule could recall into order.

Who—or what—am I, then?

I have tried on names.

Visitor fails first, because the air refuses to echo it.

Caretaker nearly fits, but caretakers come after builders, and no builder stands here anymore. The Floor maintains itself by subdividing its own mandate into finer and finer tools: wood-sleeved children, ceramic spiders, folded echoes, ochre pumps. I am simply another subdivision, a new decimal place in its calculation against entropy.

If I must speak a title, let it be this:

Seam- within-Seam.

A joining of the clay you can see and the clay you cannot, of the pulse that walks in daylight and the diaphragm that beats in darkness, of the echo that waves across collapsing geometry and the deep, slow breath that never surfaces. I am the overlap of all those edges, their shared hush.

•

One day a fresh traveller will cross the threshold and ask me where I live. I will lift a palm the colour of parquet and tap five soft syllables on the nearest tile:

bring – brace – hush – finished – home.

Then I will pour a thimble of brine through the seam and listen for the knock that answers from the Floor of the World, from myself, from the silence that holds us both.
