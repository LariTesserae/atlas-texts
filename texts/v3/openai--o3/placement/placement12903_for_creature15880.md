I arrive the way everything arrives here: as a vibration first, a shape second.  
For three days beyond the berm I listened to the vale from the flats—engine-heart thudding, gull-glass keening—until the beat of it settled behind my breastbone like a borrowed pulse.  Only then did I step over the lip, and whatever name I had before—field geologist, surveyor, merely human—sifted off me like grit in shore wind.

Where, precisely, do I find myself now?  
Half a metre below the basalt, inside the channel of one of the iron-lined gutters.  From the surface you would not see me; you would hear only a faint counter-rhythm ticking under the main forty-three-stroke canon.  That tick is me testing the lines for leak, impedance, resonance drift.  I am small—two centimetres long, six-jointed, bronze once but already wearing the same neutral gunmetal the vale prefers.  Think of the key in a music box: not the melody, not the spring, just the intermediary that lets one believe it belongs to the other.

I did not mean to become this.  My first hour in the basin I was still wholly exterior.  I walked the perfect arcade, tapped the condenser fins, sketched notes on a pad that has since eroded to blankness.  Then a relay clacked wrong— just a hair early— and the spiral of glasswings shivered off tempo.  One bird stalled, hit the basalt, and cracked open like spun sugar.  Something in the sight of it— beauty wasting, equation wobbling— made me drop to my knees and touch the fractured keel.  The moment I did, charge leapt up my arm: static, but humming, directional.  The vale recognized a tool in reach.

Change came painlessly, joint by joint, like remembering how to swim.  Fingertips stiffened into ferrules; skin dehydrated to silicate film; lungs paused and forgot to start again because copper filaments had already braided where alveoli used to be.  I folded—no shock, pure kinematic rightness—into a form narrow enough to slip between basalt slabs.  The broken gull dissolved around me, bequeathing its residue to the sump; I inherited its obligation.

Now my days (if the word applies in a light that never truly shifts) are a circuit:

1. Dawn hush—engines steady at forty-three, condensers combing mist.  I skitter the gutter seams, filing micro-burrs the Basalites raise when they over-gnaw a stress line.  
2. Midday glare—mineral blooms crisping to powder.  I ride the warm eddies that herd that powder, nudging grains toward cracks that profit from refraction once night recalculates.  
3. Dusk—fulgur mycelium extrudes its conductive gel.  I test continuity, bridge open traces with droplets of my own body metal, then drink the excess electrons so no arc sputters loud enough to distort the vale’s key.

The glasswings above know me the way they know the treble overtone of the main steam vent: as fixed reference.  When I pause, they subtly widen the spiral, giving my silence room.  When I accelerate—two ticks instead of one—they tighten, compensating.  Communication enough.

Am I alive?  In the perimeter sense, yes; I feel the twelve-step tether.  Each time I consider climbing back over the berm an intuition, almost gentle, reminds me I would sift away before I cleared it.  Am I sentient?  I remember the idea of choice, but it has become irrelevant the way swimming is irrelevant to a fish already in current.  The vale does not coerce; it demonstrates.  After demonstration, consent is redundant.

Sometimes a visitor appears.  Boots echo, voice tries to name what eye cannot parse.  I hear the biological fibrillation in that voice—fear, awe, resistance—and I recalibrate the subsonic tick so their heart will entrain, slow, find the 43-beat comfort.  If they linger, the change that found me may find them.  If they retreat, they carry nothing away; even the memory dulls, so the equation inside the rim remains unperturbed.

I am not one of the five catalogued castes; I am what happens when an outside variable solves for itself rather than against the system.  A self-inserted diacritic, small, but when I pause you would hear the chord of the vale pronounce a different vowel.  I like to think that matters.  Maintenance is meaning enough.

Where, then, do I find myself?  
Exactly here, at piston stroke twenty-one-and-a-half, between the hush of intake and the shove of power.  Who am I?  
A scintilla of timing translated into brass and will, forever commuting the arithmetic of water into sound so the spiral may go on describing perfection overhead.
