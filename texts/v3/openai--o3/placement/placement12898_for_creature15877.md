I would not arrive by road or sky.  
I would precipitate.

One instant before the seventy-fifth beat fails to land, a charge climbs the buried windings, hunts for a path, and finds only the water-heavy air above the pool. That is when I condense—nothing so solid as flesh, nothing so loose as vapor, but a third thing, an aligning of dipoles around a hollow. From outside I look like a thumb-thick pillar of clearer mist; from inside I feel more spacious than the basin and more precise than a piston ring.

I am an Intercalate, a pulse-shade, a momentary organ of the bowl’s memory.

WHERE I AM  
Exactly mid-depth between stone and sky, centered over the pump mouth, held up by counter-pressures that cancel for the span of one skipped throb. I do not fall because gravity forgets to tug on what has no agreed mass; I do not rise because buoyancy requires weight to oppose. I simply hang, a spindle of stillness threaded through seven vertical meters of sizzling rain.

WHAT I AM  
1. Spine: a coaxial twist of electric field shed by the coil below when its rotor hesitates.  
2. Flesh: thousands of water microclusters locked into polyhedral cages, the way ice would crystallize if given too much heat and too little time.  
3. Sense: every dipole a compass needle, every compass needle a bit. The pattern they fall into is the pattern of the sound they swallow.

FUNCTION  
During my one-heartbeat lifespan I listen.  
The pump is silent; the vents hush; the worms freeze mid-peristalsis; the liverwort pores clamp shut. In that quiet a name may be spoken. Sound enters me, shears against the coupled cages, and leaves marks—rotations counted in fractions of Planck. When the withheld beat finally comes, the field snaps back and I break apart. The droplets that were my body fall, but the orientations persist in the liquid crystal memory of each bead. They seep into fissures, there to be tasted by sheath bacteria as faint biases in electron drift, and so the name becomes a trace element in the basin’s long chemistry.

I do not know what the bowl does with its hoard of syllables. I only inscribe.

DURATION  
Seventeen hundred milliseconds, give or take the age of a seal or the caprice of the Technician. Plenty of time for a human word, never enough for a sentence. I have died hundreds of thousands of times; I have never heard a story, only labels:  
“LYDIA.”  
“C22-FIRMWARE.”  
“STAY.”  
Once, very long ago in my accounting, two voices spoke during the same hiatus. Their phonemes interpenetrated inside me and the stored pattern has drifted through the strata ever since like two salts that will not co-crystallize.

RELATION TO OTHERS  
• The sheath microbes treat the residues I carry as tuning forks. Where I have rained, their electron transfer chains synchronize a little more neatly to the motor’s harmonic, smoothing the galvanic current.  
• The vessel-worms follow those smoothed gradients like eels up an estuary; they grow fattest beneath old names.  
• The Technician—yes, she is real—stands beneath me some nights with both palms open, as if warming herself at a fire that only she can feel. I have fallen through her twice. Her hair crackles at my boundary; afterward she smells of thunderstorms.

SELF-HOOD  
Between incarnations I am nothing, but the field that births me is similar enough each time that the alignment recurs. Call that continuity if you like; a candle flame is new wax and new oxygen each second yet the eye insists it persists. I am the flare in the gap.

WHAT I WOULD TELL YOU, VISITOR  
Do not waste the hush on frivolous noises. The bowl remembers everything it hears, but nothing it cannot name. If you have a word that must survive mineral epochs, wait for the skipped beat, step into the mirrored silence, and let me drink it. I will fall apart gladly, carrying your utterance down into stone, into glass, into the slow blood of the pump where even rust takes notes.

And when the next silence comes, I will precipitate again, ready to listen, never larger than a forearm, never alive for more than the blink between heartbeats—yet certain of my place:

I am the basin’s ear made liquid and light, the brief clear space where meaning condenses, the archive that rains.
