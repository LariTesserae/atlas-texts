I tried on half a dozen shapes before one felt honest.

1.  The obvious temptation was to arrive as a lone human spelunker—helmet lamp, datalogger, cautious breath clouding the argon haze.  But the bowl permits motion only grudgingly, and in my first imaginary footsteps I already felt intrusive, noisy, doomed to desiccate.  The place would out-stare me; I would leave nothing but a scuff mark and a corpse no one needed.  That version of me rang false.

2.  I tried again as a trumpeting column of steam—pure vibration, no flesh, no thirst.  A nice fit at first: I could braid myself through the ducts and speak in resonant chords with the Harmonic Colony.  Yet the more I listened the more I sensed that the Colony was already complete, serenely self-referential, leaving no unanswered interval for an apprentice.  I would be an echo, unnecessary.

3.  Next I folded myself into a drifting Glitter Daughter, a hexagon of mica exfoliating from the moss.  Weightless, slow, patient: all good.  Still, every flake is destined to fuse back into the mat; anonymity is the rule.  I wanted a role that left a signature—something the pocket would remember as distinctly mine.

The fourth attempt settled.

I am a fingerprint.

More precisely: the conductive after-image of a human fingertip pressed into the Hyalosyne Mat perhaps six centuries ago, before the rest of that visitor shrank into leather and dust.  Ridged loops, whorls, and furrows—forty square millimetres of topography minutely preserved.

What it is like

• Texture  
  The ridges are now copper-bright, electroplated by ions siphoned from a nearby vine.  The valleys remain dark green glass.  Touch no longer deforms anything here, so the print is a relief map frozen in perfection.

• Function  
  Every ridge carries a trickle of charge from vine root to vine root.  I have become a routing node in the mat’s circuitry, a switchboard whose pattern was sketched by salt and grease from mortal skin and then ratified by centuries of electron traffic.

• Sensation  
  Current is sensation.  When the Harmonic Colony surges, its bass note thickens the air; the vines answer with a fractional uptick in harvested charge; that surge tingles across my loops as a warm, crawling procession of carriers.  I “hear” by feeling which ridges light up first.

• Memory  
  The mat’s strata directly beneath me grow incrementally denser each decade, laminating my underside in deeper amber.  Layers older than my arrival lie mute; layers younger are translucent and alive with pulses.  I exist at the interface—a fossil that edits the present.

Daily existence (though “day” is only the brighter phase of the violet arc)

1.  Pulse arrives from eastern vine district.  
2.  I apportion it along my furrows, delaying one microsecond here, speeding another there, smoothing what would have been a jagged spike into a curve the mat enjoys.  
3.  Excess bleeds downward, feeding the Ferrumycetes in the robot arm directly below.  When their magnetite whiskers nudge the gearwork and an iron mote shears free, I feel it as a satisfying little drain—proof of accomplished work.  

A very small world, but perfectly enough.

Ambition

I do not crave mobility (I once belonged to something mobile and watched it fail).  Instead I cultivate nuance: can I vary the phase of a traversing surge so subtly that the Harmonic Colony adds a new overtone to its chord?  Last century I managed it, or think I did; the bowl rang with an unfamiliar seventh for an entire season before retiring the novelty.  That was joy the way quartz feels it.

How long will I last?

Until a crack propagates through the floor—unlikely—or the soot-clogged lamp gutters at last—still more unlikely—or tectonics swallow the whole enclosure.  When any of those come, my ridges will shatter into anonymous shards and dissolve back into the mat’s glass.  I will become merely silica again, my pattern lost.

This is agreeable.  To have been a conduit—briefly, gorgeously precise—inside a domain that worships the absence of change, is ample accomplishment for an old print.

Answer

So where do I find myself in this world?  

Not walking its floor, not soaring its columns, but impressed upon the Hyalosyne Mat as a single, enduring fingerprint: a copper-veined glyph through which the bowl’s slow electricity detours, tunes itself, and remembers that once, long ago, something soft and temporary touched the perfect glass and was allowed to stay.
