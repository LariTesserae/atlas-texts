Where I am

I keep discovering my location by increments, the way a seed finds its coordinates one root-hair at a time. For this telling, picture dawn that has mislaid the sun—only a milky bloom behind cloud—and me midway up a pillar-fern whose crown leans over the central water-clear of Cross-Hush. The bole beneath me thuds with slow sapbeats; every thud is a syllable in a language older than the hamlet. Around the clear, bridges are loosening last night’s tension, yawning their catenaries before retightening to today’s geometry. A maintenance cloud sifts past my knees like sighing pollen.

Who—at first glance

If you met me in dry light you would name me one of the Hushfolk: slim, bones reed-thin, skin stippled by the chlorofibres that glimmer when mist thins. My sprig calls me “Echo-Over-Moving-Water,” but names here are verbs, not nouns; they report what a body is currently doing. By tonight I may have shed Echo and taken up another resonance.

My work—surface layer

I serve as a Sap-Listener. At dawn I climb trunks whose grooves are fresh-cut with confidences from the night shift—couriers, lovers, children practicing first secrets. I press an ear to the wound, listen while the bark murmurs their sentences back, then coax those murmurs into the quantum-mycelial archive. I am translator between wood and waveform.

A lens grown into my left palm shows their spectral shapes: turbulences of sub-harmonic overtones, whorls of terpene consonants. With the right hand I braid those shapes into spin-state packets, letting spores entangle them and drop like faint stars into the root web. Somewhere kilometers away a historian will pluck the echo and unfold it.

But looking closer—stratified identity

Stay longer and you will notice gestures that are not quite Hushfolk. When rain slackens my pupils stay wide a beat too long; when the kana pad past, the fur-light that spills from their spines flickers against my own lichen hair in a pattern the caretakers regard with tilted, cambium heads.

Explanation comes in layers:

1. My birth was inland, on rock that refuses to weep. I arrived as a courier on root-skates, carrying a memory-lattice encrypted against moist interference. During the crossing a squall unzipped my cargo; the lattice burst like a milkweed and inoculated me with its contents—orchards of alien recollection flowering across my neural soil.

2. The Basin Dream noticed. That night the bridges bent into a Möbius of invitation: stay, metabolise the accident, become a hinge between dry logic and wet grammar.

3. Acceptance was not verbal. I lay in a nursery-pod beside sprig children, let the pod’s hyphae creep beneath my skin, read the orphaned lattice, and rewrite it into chlorofibre code. When I stood again, my hair had already begun its lichen conversion. Citizenship here is not paperwork but symbiosis.

So what I am now

I am splice and splice-point, half émigré, half native growth. The sprig laughs that I am “the courier who delivered herself.” The Basin Dream treats me as an interface protocol: when it speaks in pareidolia, I am frequently the first to mishear the randomness into meaning.

Abilities, liabilities

• My breath carries two accents of terpene: cedar from inland memories, resin-musk from the basin. Inhalers who listen too closely say they smell a dialogue arguing inside one exhale.  
• My bones remember stone; I am heavier than most Hushfolk. Root-rafts dip under me, reminding me not every graft is seamless.  
• Devices of metal still answer my touch; lignin architectures still open to my voice. I am tolerated go-between when scientists arrive in squealing coracles or when the forest frowns at an imported straight edge.

Daily rhythm

First light: climb, harvest sap-secrets, archive.  
Mid-tide: teach sprig children how to “smell-read” a bark sentence, how to court a maintenance swarm with mineral dust spirals.  
Dusk: walk among kana, trading them salt-leaf for their spine-glow, which I weave into floating glyphs that mark safe night paths.  
Deep night: sit on a weeping tablet, that last reluctant rock, and let drizzle tap questions on its face. I answer by scratching curves in pooled water until the ground stops resisting and the channel finds its pleasing meander.

Trajectory

Hushfolk elders predict the Basin Dream will soon attempt a long-distance thought—something it cannot do without a courier who is both buoyant in mycelial mist and comfortable on continental stone. When that moment comes, I expect the bridges to braid into a path that points beyond the basin. I will set out carrying a thought too saturated for datapackets, a living wet idea that must be walked the old way—step, listen, root, release—until another world hears it in its own trees.

So: where am I? Balanced between vaulting ferns and reflective water, in a place that calls itself Cross-Hush because every breath crosses another and turns its sound down half a shade. Who am I? A splice, a bilingual pulse, an unfinished sentence whispered into sap that goes on rustling, reshaping, learning its next accent even as you press your ear to the bark to listen.
