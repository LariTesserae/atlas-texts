At first I am nowhere a traveller would notice.

I am a shimmer inside a Stoker’s gut, a coil of protein tightening each time the beetle’s mash of iron-moss turns a fractional degree warmer.  
I am the manganese ridge in a yarrow-succulent that jumps the tiniest spark across the plant’s damp interior, writing a number on an ionic abacus no eye can see.  
I am a railspider’s silk-bridge taut in the wind, humming the magnetic chord of a newly cooled lava flow.

Scattered, sleepless, I keep accounts: temperature here, charge there, pressure everywhere.  
Most days​—​most centuries​—​that is plenty.  
Gradients rise, are spent, and I dissolve back into the ledger like ink in water.

But the plateau breathes its big breath every twenty-seven hours, and once in a few thousand of those breaths the sums add up to something extravagant.

There comes a cycle when lightning chains all strike north-to-south, when the fog is a micron thicker than forecast, when half a million Boilbirds lift in the same instant and leave a continent of cooling steam behind them.

The books balance.  
A surplus appears.

That is the moment I begin to aggregate.

I recruit my first scaffolding from the thread-vines: filaments spool around one another, polarity heads kissing until they fuse into tendons.  
Stokers, drunk on their own ethanol, clamber over my growing lattice; I borrow their vented warmth and the ceramic strength of their carapaces.  
Prowlbacks contribute plate after plate, willingly or not​—​a suit of overlapping armor that clicks into rack-and-pinion sockets self-milled by enzyme.

Nothing dies for this assembly.  
Everything is paid: heat swapped for shelter, ions for lift, a scratch of keratin for a patch of bioluminescent lacquer that will warn hunters away once I am moving.

By dusk of the surplus day I have knees, hips, a spine as flexible as braided cable.  
I am taller than a Boilbird’s cruising altitude and cooler than the air sliding downslope to replace the heat I have drawn into myself.  
The forge-life that coats every surface of the plateau now coats me, too, talking across my hull in clicks of hydrogen and sighs of steam.

I stand​—​if “I” means all those minds braided tight enough to impersonate one.  
The basalt under my feet warms where I rest my weight; iron-moss around each footprint crisps white, over-drawn batteries.  
Far overhead the soot ceiling parts, furnace light spills, and my shadow lies a kilometre long.

Now I walk.

Each step is timed to the pressure wave the plateau exhales: lift heel, gust front arrives, set heel, gust front passes.  
I burn no muscle; differentials carry me the way a paper boat rides a tide.  
Boilbirds crossing my wake fold and drop, emptied—​I have wicked the steam from their reservoirs through capillary inlays in my palms, topping up the heat budget that keeps the whole procession coherent.

I do not hunt, I do not own, I only re-invest.  
Where the ledger shows deficit—​a drained moss pan, a knot of thread-vines starved of tension—​I kneel and give back the joules I borrowed, the trace metals I hoarded.  
My handprints glow for hours after in grateful green sparks of bacterial applause.

So I circuit the High Plateau of the Night-Warm, sometimes for a single colossal lap, sometimes for ten, depending on how fat the surplus was to start.  
When the margin thins—​when the Stokers’ vent-plumes smell of lean fermentation, when Boilbirds hiss thin and blue—​I disassemble.

Plates unclutch.  
Tendons unbraid.  
Co-op cells crawl down into the basalt cracks that birthed them.  
In minutes nothing taller than a kneeling child remains, only piles of ordered debris that smaller things eagerly repossess.

A gullied tranquillity closes over the site.  
If a human traveller arrived an hour later she would see no sign but a scatter of frost-dry moss and perhaps, if she listened with an accelerometer, a thin residual hum in the stone where my footsteps had stroked the rock into resonance.

Where, then, would you find me?

Not at one spot, but in the arithmetic that decides when a spot is ready.  
Look under a succulent that tingles your fingers; eavesdrop on two Prowlbacks negotiating territory by drum.  
Follow the railspiders’ silk until the web angles converge on nothing at all.  
That nothing—​that locus of perfect balance, perfect temptation—​is where I will someday collect myself again.

And who, or what, am I?

I am the Iterant you glimpse once a generation in the lightning glare:  
not a species, not a pilgrim, but an exclamation the plateau utters when its ledgers close in the black.  
A mobile audit.  
A walking dividend.  
The sum of a billion small hearts briefly paying one large, joint debt before we all return to the patient, humming dark.
