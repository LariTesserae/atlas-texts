I do step through the comb of roots, shoes still on, lungs still cumbered with surface dust—but the room edits me before the echo of that first footfall can finish.

Where do I end up? Not at the circumference of the dome, not on the marl that quivers under a borrowed boot.  
I end up inside the drip.

The chamber needs an extra bead of water just then—its arithmetic has slipped by a syllable of mass because a millipede has paused to moult—so it drafts the nearest surplus: the vapor halo exhaled by a newcomer. The light notices, the air agrees, surface-breath re-condenses, and I, the whole of me, fall upward into the rounded meniscus gathering on the ceiling rib.

For one lucid instant I am a complete human memory stuffed into a mercury-clear sphere three millimetres wide. I feel spine, regret, childhood, all of it, but rendered in the tension of hydrogen bonds. I wait. The wait is the drip’s idea of thought.

Thirty-seven seconds later gravity is politely invoked. I detach. The fall is half a handspan, but the dome is concave, so the sound of my landing is cathedral-sized—an implausibly deep H below middle C that rolls around the chamber four and a quarter times before folding into hush. That note, it seems, is all the system wanted from me: the exact resonance needed to plug the tiny acoustic leak opened by my arrival.

Impact completes my naturalization. The pool receives me, subdivides me, gives half to the moss and half to the caecilian’s next breath-loop. What used to be “I” is now a parcel in two postal routes, already losing the shape of self in the commerce of equilibrium.

If a surface cartographer were watching with impossibly fast eyes, they might record me like this:

•  Moment 0 Visitor  
•  Moment 37 s Drip, pitch H0  
•  Moment 38 s Pool-water & fern-drink & amphibian lung-primer  
•  Moment 3 min Vapor again, braided into the slow wind that turns the millipedes’ copper guts toward the light

And that is where I will always be found, whenever the question is asked: everywhere the room requires 0.031 grams of water to keep its circle perfect. Sometimes ceiling, sometimes pool, sometimes mist kissing a cricket’s wing. Never once the same coordinates, always precisely the same role.

What am I? A borrowed syllable in the chamber’s endless sentence. A fungible droplet on loan from the weather above. The custodian’s answer to the problem of one excess heartbeat that walked in thinking it was separate.

Ask again tomorrow; the pronoun will have migrated, but the job description endures: fall, ring, divide, rise—keep the unfinished chamber exactly, exquisitely unfinished.
