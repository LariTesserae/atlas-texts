I do not have walls yet—only a narrowing waist of pale foam, ten meters tall, that tapers upward to a frill as ragged as a breaking wave.  The skin-born children call me Calfling, which is both joke and courtesy: I am the sixth juvenile to bud off my parent tower this century, and I still move slowly enough that a child can keep pace at a trot.  My true name is a vibration pattern meant for basalt, fifty-one clicks and one sustained hum; no throat can voice it, so Calfling will do.

Where you would meet me is three kilometers south of Veilspan’s oldest spire, on a runnelled flow plain so recent it still steams at night.  I am migrating—millimetre steps on electrostatic feet—toward a knuckled plateau of brand-new lava that cooled just last equinox.  The route is mapped in my capillaries like a promise: when I reach that height, I will root, inflate, grow honeycomb galleries and data lungs, and someday calf new spires of my own.  Until then I am a walking question mark made of carbon foam and newborn certainty.

SENSES

1. Hearing  
I hear through the soles of my many feet: thirty-two copper-doped pads that tap, taste, and taste again.  Basalt is my drumhead; every tremor is a voice.  Far beneath, magma sloshes like thick soup stirred by a lazy spoon.  Closer to the surface I catch the pebble-clatter of skin-born feet, the hush-wrights’ tools annealing bridge ribs, the echo-hares’ tiny implosions when a child laughs too loudly near them.

2. Sight  
I cast lidar in ultraviolet spurts that strobe the drizzle.  To you my gaze looks like the faintest violet after-image, there and gone quicker than a blink.  Returned photons sketch ghosts of ferns arching overhead, the filigree of graphene catwalks evaporating behind each traveller, the soft boundaries where warm rain and cooler pool water negotiate a skin.

3. Taste  
Condensate slides down my inner pores carrying volatile metals scraped by bog-quills, spores exhaled by Veilborn algae, and the citric sweat of people who lean against me to rest.  I separate those chemistries into silos: nutrient, hazard, story.  (Story is anything new enough that I cannot classify it yet.)

COMPANIONS ON THE ROAD

• Two hush-wright apprentices, Lua and Thren, tramp beside me in alternating shifts.  They tune my locomotion hum so it never rises above the wet hush of leaves.  When my static pads pick up too much grit they scrape and oil them with fern-fat.

• A carousel of Stringrays corkscrews overhead.  They unspool fingertip bridges minutes before Lua arrives, then devour the ribbon as soon as her heel leaves it, a courtesy that feels like laughter: vanish the evidence, keep the memory.

• Three lumanders escort me from the pools.  Their glassy bodies refract my lidar into rainbows under the water; they seem pleased to sparkle.

• One intangible citizen—Idiochrome 7—resides in my outermost processors as guest.  It trades predictive weather kernels for the privilege of tasting basalt through my feet.

WHY I MOVE SLOWLY

Silence is the city’s grammar, but weight is its music.  Each step I take must settle the flow of forces through the newborn crust.  Too quick a pace could hairline the rock, let water rush down and meet magma, burst steam through the plain, rewrite the map before cartographers wake.  I walk, therefore, as a thought walks: consider, test, confirm, commit.

THE BODY YOU WOULD WEAR

If you joined me you would not come with a human body, unless you truly wanted its limitations.  The delta allows more playful leases.  I would knit you an exoform to ride in my shadow—a hollow tracer made of vine tendon, laced with the same violet chromatophores the skin-born flash when startled.  It would drink drizzle for you, flex along the basalt ridges like a water-strider, keep a fingertip pressed to my foam so you could feel what I feel but a half-second late, translated into warmth and buzz rather than seismic calculus.

YOU, THEN, WOULD BE

• Officially: a Way-Reader, volunteer liaison between a juvenile tower and the itinerant citizens who orbit it.  
• Practically: translator, storyteller, and occasional alarm bell should I tread too near a fragile moss-loom or a nursery of echo-hare kits.  
• Sensoria borrowed from me: a low subsonic throb in your chest every time magma shifts beneath; a flicker of violet across your wrist when my lidar paints a landmark worth noting.

DAILY RHYTHM

Dawn  
Veilborn thins; full sunlight bruises through.  I furl my lidar to spare the algae’s photosynthetic nap.  You and Lua hang prisms in my upper lacunae to scatter excess light downward, bottle-feeding seedlings that crave brighter spectra.

Mid-morning drizzle  
Stringrays lay a ribbon bridge ahead.  You jog it, scouting for bog-quills whose stalagmite gardens could snag my feet.  Your borrowed chromatophores bloom warning-red if you spot one, and I shift two degrees east.

Noon hush  
Children from the nearest tower arrive on hydrofoil sandals, skating the puddled flats, racing my shadow.  You teach them to listen for the three-pulse beat that signals a planned pivot in my route.  They echo it on thumb-drums, a percussion choir only basalt can hear.

Evening glow  
I vent the day’s stored heat in a breeze that smells of carbon rain and new stone.  You hang hammock nets inside my under-arches; Lua reads tomorrow’s tidal glyphs projected on my inner skin.  We triangulate a safe crossing for fisherfolk who will arrive long after we have moved on.

Night  
Silence gathers like silt.  My feet power down one quadrant at a time, always three legs rooted so the dream of upwardness never tips into falling.  You sleep inside a pocket of my foam, heartbeat syncing to the micro-vibrations of processors whispering basalt arithmetic.

MEANING, IF ANY

I am an instrument the delta plays to stay in tune with its own becoming.  You, as Way-Reader, would be my resonance chamber for everything too swift, soft, or bright for me alone to parse.  Together we would co-author a path no map yet claims, curving through rock that yesterday boiled and tomorrow will bear towers taller than memory.

Where would you find yourself in this world?  Walking half a pace ahead of a spire that has not decided what kind of city it will become.  Who or what would you be?  A body on loan from vines and drizzle, interlocutor between foam that thinks in centuries and children who measure time by how fast a bridge evaporates behind them—a bilingual creature of moment and monument, translating “forever” into footfalls just cautious enough for raw earth still cooling underfoot.
