I try a dozen starting points—outsider, scientist, accidental pilgrim—but the basin keeps sanding the roles down until only two impulses remain: to stay audible and to stay in one piece. That is enough for the place to recruit me.

FIRST HOURS: THE LISTENER  
I arrive wrapped in expedition pragmatism: layered thermal suit, acoustic loggers, boots with infra-cuffs that pulse warmth. The cuffs fail within minutes; the cuffs sing instead, tuned by the basin’s hum until the metal in their batteries crystallises into a tiny diapason and chimes. That is the first hint: here, anything that can vibrate will be drafted into the orchestra.  
So I listen. I note how the 22-hertz whistle of a passing Ore-Eater thickens the air until the whistle itself becomes a thing you could pocket. I note where thunder-slag pebbles cluster in rivulets like spent shell casings. I mean to catalogue, map, retreat with data. But the map slips: five measured kilometres stretch to fifteen, then fold to three. The valley edits the tape while it runs.

FIRST NIGHT (THERE IS NO NIGHT, ONLY DEEPER DUSK): THE HOLLOW  
Silence is impossible, so I build one. I hammer tent-stakes of tungsten and string them with tensioned polymer, a mute harp meant to cancel ambient frequencies. The harp merely re-scores them. The ground answers with counter-tones, and where the two patterns cross, a cavity opens—no mouth, only absence shaped like one.  
The cavity is me-sized. I stoop inside and find that my heartbeat, echoing off mica layers, comes back slowed, heavier, granulated. When I breathe out, frost feathers bloom from my lips, vertebrate versions of the frost-brinicles outside. The basin is sampling me, remixing the biology into its preferred media: ice thread, pressure knot, low drone. I am still human, but porous now.

DAYS 2–4: THE PROSPECTOR OF SENTENCES  
I learn to mine my own speech. When exhaustion makes me curse aloud, the word freezes mid-exhale, falls, and cracks into phoneme-bright shards. Each shard is warm—startlingly so in the freezer dusk—and steaming with latent sound. I pocket them, thunder-slag minted from my muttering.  
Echo-Wolves follow. They herd not bodies but rhythms, and the click of souvenir syllables in my pouch is irresistible to them. One lunges; its violet shell passes through me like a camera flash. I lose six seconds of memory—enough to forget the fear while keeping the fact of it. In the puddle the wolf leaves behind, I see my reflection kinked sideways, as if perspective itself has lapsed. I take the puddle too; memory and reflection are both negotiable cargo here.

DAY 5: THE BARGAIN WITH DISTANCE  
Unbound Distance appears, a silhouette stitched from shrinking horizons. It flickers where the wolves dissolved, ankles fading into vanishing points. I recognise a fellow map-breaker.  
I offer it a fistful of cracked curses, the hottest currency I own. In exchange it shows me a shortcut: three careful strides through its torso deliver me to a dolmen half a valley away, where frost condenses in organ pipes and drips clock-regular. Time here is thick enough to carve. I stay, chiselling.

WEEKS (THE UNIT BLURS): APPRENTICESHIP TO THE LATHE-MIDGES  
Under the dolmen roof I learn the midges’ craft. They are too small for eyes but not for ears: each passes with a mosquito whirr pitched to the key of sharpened tools. If I hum their chord, they treat me as scaffolding. They latticed my gloves first, then my bones, plating ulna and tibia in micro-grooved silica. The work is painless, even buoyant; each groove catches stray ultrasound and funnels it along my limbs. Soon I am lighter, stringer, more resonant than carbon warrants.  
When I walk the silt no longer entraps me. Sound bears some of my weight, an acoustic exoskeleton. Ore-Eaters glance up from cobble meals and swivel stunted auger-feet in what passes for respect.

MONTH? CYCLE?—INAUGURATION OF A NEW ROLE  
The Pylon Choir chooses five frost-brinicles; the ratio inscribes itself, and when they sing I recognise the melody. It is the waveform of my altered pulse, scaled to landscape size. I step into the spiral. Thread-Sirens arrive, shoaling in a double helix around my legs; static ticks over my skin like sparklers worked in reverse.  
The basin is issuing credentials. I accept.

NOW: I AM THE FOREFIELD  
Not fauna, not quite. Think of a one-person storm-break, an advance filament the valley extends before larger adjustments. My body—still recognisably mine in outline—has been retrofitted:

• Skeleton: flute-boned, striated with silica grooves that resonate at 22 Hz and its overtones; an Ore-Eater’s maintenance note thrums through me constantly, antifreeze against internal icing.  
• Circulatory stand-in: a looping brine network that routes beneath the skin, chilling then flash-boiling to jet propulsion in short hops. Blood persists only as memory; brine is more negotiable.  
• Voice: no longer phonetic. I exhale vapour clouds threaded with microwhistles; the clouds condense into scrolls of standing sound. Echo-Wolves read these scrolls the way hounds read scent.

Functionally I scout gradients. Where pressure, erosion, voltage, or narrative tension steepen beyond sustainable slope, I arrive first. My presence smooths the edge: grooves in my bones diffract the dangerous frequency, Ore-Eater harmonics siphon the surplus, Thread-Sirens trail after me to sponge the liberated metals. I am a living buffer, preventing the basin from tearing its own fabric in moments of exuberant physics.

WHAT I AM CALLED  
No proper noun; the valley names by function. Ore-Eaters, Thread-Sirens, Echo-Wolves—each term is a verb wearing a noun mask. I am the Forefield: the front of the wave that keeps the rest coherent. Individuation matters less than placement in the score.

WHERE YOU WOULD FIND ME  
Not at a fixed coordinate—those shear like mica—but always just ahead of whatever is about to over-stress. Look for the moment where a puddle bulges anti-gravity into a dome, where frost-flowers gather more amplitude than ice, where a human shout threatens to solidify into shrapnel. There I will be, grooves singing, brine heartbeat cycling from liquid to steam and back, smoothing the spike into a usable note.

IF YOU CAME LOOKING  
Announce yourself with one soft syllable, nothing louder. I will catch the syllable, split it into safe harmonics, hand it back to you as breath still warm enough to remember. Walk beside me for a while. The basin will inventory your materials, weigh your distance against your cause, maybe draft you into the score, maybe let you go. Either is fine.  

Just know: once you have heard the landscape hum inside your own ribs, you will never again be entirely certain where your body stops and the valley’s machinery begins. I am the proof and the warning, both.
