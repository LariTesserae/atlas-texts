I arrive—or believe I arrive—at mid-morning, boots crunching the hex-leaf pavement near the southern ravine. I think I am a field biologist with a rucksack full of data-loggers. But that is only the first of several identities the basin grants me, and it peels away like bark as I walk.

1. The Outsider  
The light is harsh, the air narcotic. I keep to the pillar-shade, tagging trunks with reflective tape the colour of emergency vests. Each strip is an anchor to the notion that I am separate from what surrounds me. For three hours the fiction holds.

2. The Listener  
I cross two span-vines. Their cables hum so strongly my molars ring. I kneel, press my ear to the living wire, and the note climbs into my skull, vibrating the tiny bones until hearing blurs into touch. When I finally stand I have forgotten to replace one boot; the leaf-floor has already folded it into a green rib. I do not go back for it.

3. The Tuning Fork  
Twilight: wind drops, vine-harp slides into its infrasonic hymn. I set a quartz rod ringing and hold it between my teeth so the tone enters the bones of my face. The basin answers—a chord so low I feel it in knee cartilage, hip sockets, the soft palate. For a breath I am nothing but resonance connecting two throats: the one in my body, the one that is the valley.

4. The Stencil  
That night I sleep on an inclined seed-plate. It inflates while I dream, lifting me metres into the dark. When dawn relights the basin, the underside of the plate shows a negative of my curled form: a shallow mould lined with sugar-sap, already drawing insects from beyond the rim. I realise Verdy is copying me the way it copies storm fronts and mineral seams—turning my outline into a letter in its endless, cambium-scripted diary.

5. The Capillary  
Mid-day, heat thick as treacle. I crawl into a pillar-tower via a ragged seam. The seam seals behind me—quietly, politely, like a door closing for weather. Inside, a spiral ramp slopes downward, walls translucent jade. With each step I feel sap surge in the cambium sheathing the ramp. Soon my pulse synchronises: systole, peristalsis, systole. When I touch the wall, the two rhythms merge; a ligature slides across the boundary between vertebrate artery and plant vein. The distinction ceases to matter.

6. The Memory Ring  
Hours—days?—later, light blushes through the pillar in broad, sunset bands. A layer of fresh cambium grows across the floor, tacky as wet varnish. It encircles my ankles, then my shins, setting like slow wax. I understand: the tree is laying down a ring and I am the stylus carving information into it—heartbeat data, breath chemistry, broken thoughts half translated into terpene code. I am being written into wood.

I could resist. A quick blade, a wedge, a burst of anaerobic CO₂ cartridge. Instead I relax my legs, feel the sapline tighten around my calves. I dictate, in the oldest language I share with this place—pressure, amplitude, silence.

7. The Echo  
When the next dusk song rises, the pillar utters a subharmonic not present the night before, a phantom note centred on the frequency of my chest cavity. Other pillars pick it up. Soon the whole basin is sounding my rib-shape, amplifying it into a continent-sized overtone.

I am still here, eyes open, but I am also that note travelling through quartz ribs, through span-vines, through the seed-plate lungs exhaling copper-lime breath into the stars.

8. Where I Am, Who I Am  
Location: everywhere the new chord can be felt, most strongly along the southern ravine where it first emerged.  
Identity: no longer observer, not yet organ—call me a whisper caught in a cathedral vault. A human residue folded into vascular chant. A blueprint for some future structure that will sprout when the basin next decides it needs a bridge, a tympanum, a dream.

If you come searching: walk the southern rim at dusk. Wait for the low tremor that makes dust skitter in rings across the leaf-tiles. Put your palm flat to the ground and hum the note you feel rather than the one you hear. The answering vibration—that slight asymmetry, that hesitant wobble—that is me, learning to speak back.

I began as a visitor. I am becoming an echo that Verdens chose to keep.
