I did not choose a place so much as the place chose the particular fault-line of me that could still adapt.  
If you come looking, you will find “me” in the hinge of the cavern where gravity folds back on itself like a book’s spine—a district the Cenobite Artisans call the Vermaul Crucible and the Skulker packs simply acknowledge as the Turn.

WHAT THE TURN IS  
• Geography: a sheer basalt escarpment piercing both “up” directions at once. One stride to the left, down is north; one stride to the right, down is south. Stand astride the seam and each foot belongs to its own planet.  
• Climate: always mild, always breathing an even exhale of argon that trickles from slits in the wall. The flow keeps the iron snowflakes in suspension, so the whole gulf twinkles in slow counter-rotation—two star fields pouring past each other.  
• Importance: the dual gravities pull trace ions opposite ways. The split becomes a permanent battery, a hundred-kilometer electrochemical tongue of lightning that never quite strikes.

WHO I AM THERE  
The Reach has promoted me from Pilgrim to Custodian of Rhythm—an in-between species the locals politely call a “Resonant,” because I am what happened when wet, carbon machinery stalled just long enough for mineral law to rewrite its wiring.

Body inventory, five hundred nights after my descent:  
1. Skin: fused alumina glass, hairline-fractured like crazed porcelain. Sweat is gone; instead, capillaries vent neon in microscopic sighs that keep the glaze flexible.  
2. Skeleton: still calcium, but every long bone is jacketed in a sleeve of lithium salt that doubles as flash-battery. Triboluminescent sparks crawl under the surface when I flex.  
3. Blood: fifty-fifty water and ionic liquid now. Taste of the tongue is a nine-volt battery pressed to wet skin—forever citrus-metal.  
4. Hearing: ears useless. I read sound as ripple through the gravity seam, a barometer of flickers.  
5. Aging: has not resumed; scars still gape pink where they opened months ago, edges frozen mid-knit like footage paused.

FUNCTION  
My job emerged the day I learned to keep two heartbeats—one for each direction. Skulker scouts quickly noticed that the alternating cadence cancelled the field tremors that normally scare their prey. The Artisans noticed next; they strung me with magnesium-ribbon bells whose pitch reports the hinge’s potential in real time. When a Lamia’s sub-sonic hymn threatens to shear the cliff, my counter-pulse nudges the pendulumites overhead; their swing re-tunes the seam and the quake aborts. I am, essentially, a living flywheel slipped into the Reach’s silent clockwork.

DAILY LIFE  
Morning (there is no morning, but language clings):  
• I surface from a silence pocket hung like a hammock between two whisper kites.  
• Harvest ferric snowflakes off my shoulders—tax paid to Gradient-film colonies that line my shelter.  
• Knock a pattern on the basalt—three descending intervals. Packs of Skulkers answer; if their return rhythm wavers, I know the electro-charge difference is tightening and I retune my heartbeat.

Mid-cycle:  
• Artisans arrive. They trade me fresh oxidizer pellets for data: I press my wrist to a quartz sheet and let the salt sleeve discharge a visual map of last night’s gravitic harmonics. They press spent blades into my arm glaze to add that moment’s pain-flash to their murder mosaic. (The relationship is cordial.)  
• Lamia chorus drifts in—an infrasonic weather forecast. I shift cadence, bleed neon from pores, the seam eases, the Turn holds.

Rest:  
• I climb—“climb” meaning I step to the wall that currently functions as floor, walk thirty meters, then step again as the vector flips, ending upside-down but standing.  
• In a hush pocket I lie still. Chronovores patrol here, eating seconds. I dream in stops.

THREAT & HOPE  
Slow Sovereigns loom at the far limit of perception, blue motes swirling between their columns like court musicians tuning up. One is edging closer each century; when it finally decides to test the Turn, I doubt my heartbeat will suffice. My private plan is to splice ferric snowflake hives into the lithium sleeves of my bones—make myself loud enough to be heard on a geological channel.

WHAT I REMEMBER OF THE SURFACE  
Sometimes argon gusts carry a smell that is nearly rain and the memory of green detonates in my skull: leaves, rot, running water, all the erosion this place refuses. I know the path back, anatomically at least, but not temporally. If I climbed the obsidian throat today I might exit a thousand surface years hence, water long spent, sun dimmer, nobody left to confirm I had once been meat.

So I keep the hinge.  
I am the metronome between two downs.  
If you descend and feel the pull split at your collarbones, listen for the antiphonal thud—lub-dub, dub-lub: mine. Bring lithium ribbon; I’ll braid it through my pulse and fashion you a passport of neon breath. Then find your own seam; the Reach is young, it recruits.
