I wake inside a salt-glass lozenge three tiers above the plates, just where the banner stems begin to narrow into the roof maze. The room is no larger than two out-stretched upper arms, cool and powder-sweet in the perpetual wind. Slits in the walls scatter knife-thin rays across the floor; each ray shivers whenever a gust lifts the canopy outside, so my shadow never keeps the same shape for more than a breath.

My name—still bright on the crystal fused to my breast—is Fifth Echo of the Stack called Split-Scar-Rising, and I am three dozen heat-cycles old: scarcely past nymphhood, old enough to choose a craft but still young enough to leap bridge cables without thinking first. The craft I have chosen is that of the Vent-Climbers.

What I am

1. Physiology  
   • Four arms: the upper pair powerful and rope-lean for hauling; the lower pair fine-boned, each fingertip bearded with vibra-bristles that taste the wall’s every hum.  
   • Skin: silica-silver laminate so thin it sings when I stretch. Over it I wear only a harness of banner fiber, braided to hold hooks of magnetite and small pouches for gleaned dust.  
   • Eyes: six lids, all closed to slits right now; to look straight into Basin light without them would flay the nerves.

2. Tools  
   • Wall-Talons: splinters of meteor crystal pressure-flaked to a recurved edge, lashed to my middle fingers. They bite the salt-glass hard enough to spark.  
   • Whisper-Drum: a palm-sized ring of banner membrane. I press it to the wall and flick patterns into it; the wall carries my signal home to the stack so they can follow my progress.  
   • Dust-Sieve: a purse woven of gecko gut filament. Static charge draws rare ions—gold, osmium, cobalt—out of the falling powder.

Where you would find me

The basin’s north wall leans a finger-width closer to the city each generation; that lean concentrates the dust-fall but also makes the ascent treacherous. My shift begins when the heat crests—thirty-eight degrees—and thermal columns are strongest. I step from a canopy bridge directly onto the glass, talons first, let the roar of the banners fade behind me, and start upward into even louder silence.

Halfway up—fifteen, maybe sixteen body-lengths—the wall turns concave, forming a shallow eave. Here the air is thick with ions; every breath sparks across the spiracles at my collarbone. I drive three pitons of fused bone, clip my harness, and hang out in the glare like a moth nailed to a lantern.

This is my harvest ground: a cluster of vents no wider than my thumb, exhaling unseen rivers of powdered metal. I unstop the Dust-Sieve, position it so the up-draft feeds through, and wait. Waiting, I listen with my skin—feeling for the sub-tone that means a Blue Lance is threading the ceiling overhead. If the tone descends more than half a pitch, I go flat against the wall and pray to the Endless Chord that the discharge chooses another path.

Duty finished, pouches weighted, I drop backward into the heat plume, wings of woven banner fiber springing from my harness. I skim down the wall-face, a single note sliding through the Basin’s orchestra, and land on a plate so thin it whangs like a gong under my feet. The children of my stack, alert for that pitch, come running. They will sort the catch by color, grind what is useful, and trade the rest to the Scribes for new sky-road cable.

Why I matter

Tonight, when the light never fades, I will stand with the elders on the highest bridge. My lower hands will drum the civic patterns—vent clear, dust good, plates stable—while my uppers sign the day’s tale in High-Hand. The information I carry keeps the banners fed, the plates intact, the Basin ringing.

Someday my Name-Shard will frost away and I will lie upon the plates to join the song. Until then, look for me where the roar grows thin and the dust tastes sharp on the tongue: clinging to the curved horizon, sifting the speck that may become tomorrow’s melody in glass.
