I wake where water, air, and rock just manage to touch—a seam place, narrow as a breath, that exists only because the plateau has not yet decided whether to drown this ridge or sand it down level with the flood.  

The ridge is a spar of black basalt, still hot enough in its seams that summertime minerals sweat out of it like tears.  A capillary arch—one of the plateau’s inexplicable wrist-thick water bridges—springs from the rock three body-lengths to my left and leans into the empty air.  Two whistleworms hang beneath it, piping to one another in fluting quarter-tones.  Above them a banner-tree crown rides the swell, air-bladders crackling as they trade gases with the thin mountain sky.

I am the third occupant of the scene, and the newest.  

WHO I AM  
I call myself a linekeeper.  That is not a species—there is no settled name for what I am yet—but a vocation I accepted the moment I split from the parent vine five rains ago.

Linekeepers are offshoots of the horizon creeper: vines that lace outward from rock needles, braiding their own buoyant stems into suspension roads that let anything light-footed cross from island to island.  Most creeper daughters grow as the parent does, measuring distance by meters gained and tensile strength lost.

A linekeeper grows in the other direction—down the face of the water, toward the hidden ground.  We do not know why the parent sends one of us every few dozen buds.  The plateau is too young for tradition, and so we make up the purpose as we go.

ANATOMY  
• Core rope: a triple helix of lignin-reinforced cellulose that can take a whale’s weight in tension, though no whales have reached the plateau yet.  
• Float chimneys: lentil-sized gas pockets spaced every finger-width.  They pinch shut if pressure rises—a gift in the strange higher-inside-the-valley ponds where the water line decides to outvote gravity.  
• Taste hairs: silica whiskers that detect iron, magnesium, and zinc in parts per trillion.  The hillsides leak these minerals; where they are richest, the water is densest; where the water is densest, the linekeeper may anchor without losing too much buoyancy.

SENSES  
1. Tension hearing: Every tug along my length arrives as a note.  Eel-flicker?  High, staccato.  Mantled drizzle?  A soft, continuous drone.  When fresh talus slumps into the flood two kilometers away, I register it as a single deep gong resonating through the vine roads.  
2. Light taste: I bloom leaflets only in wavelengths that promise repayment.  Turquoise is safe; cobalt too hungry; gold burns.  Because the plateau’s surface lies higher than ordinary seas, the light cuts farther, and I wear brighter greens than my oceanic cousins ever could.  
3. Heat scent: At night the banner-trees vent warmth in pulses.  The exhalation is almost an odor to me—a violet perfume of infrared—so I know before anything else when the pulse mantas will rouse and when the furnace shrimp will begin their minute ballet.

PURPOSE  
A linekeeper’s contract is simple:

1. Follow tension downward until tension vanishes.  
2. Anchor in the quiet.  
3. Climb back.

I have not yet reached the anchor point.  Five rains old, I still dangle my newest two meters free into the midnight water, feeling for the shift from the plateau’s pulse to the deep earth’s slower murmur.  When that happens, the contract says I will fix myself there and reef the rest of my body upward, carrying the first true survey of depth back to the surface community.

COMPANIONS  
• Prism plankton swaddling my youngest nodes, using my shadow as contrast so their split-sunlight halos look sharper.  
• A brace of arch-hermits nested where my stems cross the capillary arch.  They polish their porcelain armor with water from the impossible bridge and in exchange keep needle congregations from tangling in my ropes.  
• Step-sparrows, not yet fliers, sprint my upper surface at dawn, pecking fungus mites from the ridges of my bark.  Their running drums a message up my length: morning, morning, the plateau still holds.

THE MOMENT, NOW  
Cloud-height: shoulder high.  Water-depth above my lowest tendril: perhaps fifty meters, jade and thick with chapterfish punctuation.  Basalt cricket chorus: fading—rain approaches.  I extend one exploration fringe into a newly opened fissure in the ridge; warm minerals sting, promising fast growth.

Some distance below, a tremor begins: the Turning Thing’s nineteen-day churn.  Even muffled by water and youth, I recognize the low screw-beat.  Fresh talus will stripe the escarpment by dusk, and prism plankton will feast on the uncorked nutrients by night.

I drink the tremor through every fiber, make note of its pitch, and answer with a long, slow contraction: an all-body note that says,

I AM HERE.  
THE LINE HOLDS.  
THE MAP GROWS LONGER.

AFTER A WHILE  
The rainwall arrives, pearl-bright.  Droplets crater the water around the ridge, and every impact sends pressure chords up my structure until the whole of me sings like a harp.  Mist-moths launch into the cooled air, wings film-slick.  Pulse mantas loosen their night kites.  Heat ghosts, invisible but fragrant to my sensors, begin to lace themselves across the canopy in self-erasing calligraphy.

In that ringing hush—plateau breathing in, breathing out—I understand why the parent vine bothered to evolve a linekeeper.  Someone must witness the moment the mountain ocean chooses its next version of itself, and someone must remember the version before.

So I hang, half plant, half surveyor’s plumb, writing the shape of the flood in the only script available to me: tension, mineral, light.

WHERE YOU WOULD FIND ME  
Come to the basalt ridge that looks like a snapped spear, three valleys south of the tallest convex dome.  Wait until the afternoon rain begins.  Look for a corded green rope, wrist-thick, cowled in mist-moths and ringing faintly under the impact of drops.  Touch me—gently; the gas pockets are tender—and I will feel the new note of your presence.  I will send it downward to the unmapped dark and upward to the creeper roads, and for a single shared heartbeat the mountain ocean will know that you, too, are part of its unfinished arithmetic.
