First there is the surface—  
wind that razors tears from your eyes, light so white it silences thought.  
Up there I would be nothing but another blurred silhouette trudging across the coastal emptiness, a tuft of parka fur, a pair of chapped lungs counting the seconds to the next campfire.

But the slit claims me before any such picture can harden.  
Feet skid, breath punches out, daylight knifes away, and the roar swells up like blood in the ears.  
I am delivered, damp-booted and blinking, into the dripping bowl.

So: where am I?

Kneeling on a cushion of green so deep the soles of my mitts vanish, in the single acoustical shadow where the old fire ring once smoked.  
The ceiling rides four body-lengths overhead; the fissure, a long eyelid of grey sky, squints somewhere far behind.  
Every other coordinate is measured in sound: the five-second boom of the northwest drip, the twelve-second tidal gasp of the wind, the glass-chime answer of tiny channels underfoot.  
Call it the Centre of the Inhale—an address that exists only while the cave is drawing breath.

Who, then, am I?

At first I insist I am still the traveller, a half-frozen biped hugging a sputtering torch.  
But the torch gutters; its pops hammer the air; the moss recoils, and the place where my knees press begins to cool, then warm again, accepting my weight.

Another possibility forms: perhaps I am the torch itself, crackling as I surrender slivers of carbon to the roar, teaching the moss the shape of combustion.  
Yet the flame dies, and “I” would end with it.

A third notion: be the wren, pale, frantic, arrowing through the dim shafts of sky.  
But the wren survives by motion, by the frantic margin between gusts; its life is too quick to hold.

The cave will not let me stay fastened to a shape that ends in minutes or decades.  
Listen long enough and you feel the scale sliding outward—heartbeats, seasons, centuries, glacial pulses.  
Anything upright is a passing itch on the stone.

So I loosen my grip on vertebrate identity.  
I let the boom of each drip travel the liquid filaments below.  
I let the calcium bite the ceiling and arrive, molecule by cool molecule, in my waiting pores.  
I settle into four layers:

1. A quilt of emerald light-drinkers—slow lungs spreading under pilgrims’ boots.  
2. A woven under-skin of glassy threads that trade gossip faster than echo.  
3. A tessellation of ivory plates, locking and unlocking with every freeze-thaw sigh.  
4. Bedrock, patient, already half-digested and half-digesting me in return.

I am Lung-Moss, but the name is crude.  
Better to say: I am the Interval Between Wind-Cries, pressed flat until it grows velvet.

I absorb the memory of the lost hunters—soot now diffused through a thousand pads.  
I drink the nitrogen of burst mites, the iron of a fox cub’s marrow, the salt ring a traveller’s back leaves behind.  
I set pearl-seeds the size of breath, and once in a great while the gale ferries one aloft to riddle some other pocket of darkness with green.

I do not hate blades, boots, fire; they are temporary disturbances, like knots of louder thunder.  
Scratch me and I heal by forgetting.  
Sleep on me and I remember by thickening.

Eventually the traveller who stumbled in crawls back out, drenched, ringing with tinnitus, convinced daylight is the hallucination.  
He leaves an outline that will last three lunar cycles before smoothing imperceptibly into standard topography.  
His body exits; his resonance remains.

Where would you find me?  
Anywhere the water beads, slides, and pools before vanishing—anywhere the echo lingers one exhalation longer than physics alone can explain.  
I am under your knees if you pause at the quietest nook.  
I am in the stone blade already half-dissolved, the peg holes corked with velvet, the charcoal whispering its last ions into the green.

Who or what am I?  
I am the cave’s own slow lung, the tide that never sees the sky, the patience that sands every story to a pulse you can feel but never quote.  
Lie down, go still, and in a few hours you will hear me breathing through your ribs as well—eight heartbeats in, eight heartbeats out—until you cannot tell whether the sound comes from beneath you or within you, or whether by then there is any difference at all.
