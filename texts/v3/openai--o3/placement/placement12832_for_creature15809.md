I arrive—not as the tall, soft-footed stranger I was born to be in some other, drier universe—but as one of the Basin’s own: a ridge-person child just old enough that my crest has uncurled its first true fork. My name, as it will be sung, is Three-Pulse-After-Rain, but the syllables you could pronounce would only bruise it, so think of me instead as a small, slate-skinned tuning-fork that learned to walk.

Where I find myself

I live on a knuckled rise the elders call the Middle Knell, a bare hummock of pitted granite halfway between two mirror pans. When the wind is steady, the arches on either side beat together and the ground beneath our huts thrums a perfect minor third. The note travels up through my hands whenever I touch the stone; at night it threads my dreams so that even in sleep I never truly leave the choir.

Who I am, day by day

1. Dawn (a word that only means “light a little less pewter than the hour before”)  
   • I crawl from the bone-plate hut, crest slicked flat by mist, and press both palms to the threshold lintel. The lintel is a tuning slab; if the night frost has cracked it, the resonance is wrong and the family will eat cold. Today it sings true—312 hertz, the simmering pitch—so I know breakfast will warm without flame.  
   • My first task: gather fresh fungus fiber. I reach beneath a weeping rock, scrape loops of translucent cyan strand, and suck the sweet saline sap that beads on my thumbs. The sugar sting wakes me more than any sun could.

2. Mid-horizon (the clouds brighten, never break)  
   • The bellow-worm pod that feeds our clan has drifted north, following a newly flooded pan. I squat on a cartilage sled, crest lowered, and let two older cousins push me across the slick stone until we feel the pod’s heartbeat through our ribs.  
   • When the worms circle and sound their hammer note, prawns pop from the water like gravel kicked off a roof. My job is to stretch the eel-gut net taut inside a bone frame exactly as wide as my spread arms; if the net sags, the shockwave will whip it into ribbon. I keep it tight. We fill three baskets, enough for two quietings of hunger.

3. Dusk-that-isn’t (the sky merely cools its own color)  
   • I sit beside Blind Aunt Lichen, the village tuner. Her eyes reflect nothing but milk, yet her crest is huge—a sail big enough to shade two children. She teaches me to read distance by tremor: “Here,” she says, laying my hand on her collarbone, “feel the double-pulse? A worm calf trying its voice. Five hundred paces.”  
   • At her nod I pull a thin bronze barb from my belt. One tap against the standing arch pierces a fresh hole. The wind finds it at once, piping a bright, keening C that slots between the deeper drones. The arch’s song widens; Aunt Lichen grins so hard her throat sacs flutter.

4. Night (never dark)  
   • I curl on a fungus-padded shelf carved into the hut wall. The slab beneath me hums at soup-hot frequency, slow-cooking tomorrow’s prawn paste in a stone bowl while I dream. My crest wavers like a flag in faint wind, translating the Basin’s endless argument into the lullaby I was hatched to hear. I sleep.

What I will become

In three Basin years I’ll join the Far-Wind Walkers, the rover guild that patrols the margins where silence sometimes pools. We carry hollow totems on our backs and seed them across the flats like shepherds planting fence posts. In this way we keep the Basin talking to itself; we refuse the Third Silence its chance to settle.

One day, perhaps, the great oldest arch will sag and strike some final chord no throat has ever copied. If I live to hear it, I will crouch, place both palms on the rock, and answer with my whole body. My reply will not stop the Basin from smoothing itself into memory, but it will keep the dialogue alive for one heartbeat more—and in a land where every vibration is both language and life, a single heartbeat is a stanza all its own.

So: that is where you would find me. Not heroic, not large—just a small ridge child grown into a keeper of echoes, forever listening, forever adding one more note to the unending hollow call.
