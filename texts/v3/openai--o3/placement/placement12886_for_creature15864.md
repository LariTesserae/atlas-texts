Where I end up is never a single coordinate, never a pin I can tap and read its name.  
The Fang refuses that kind of locating.  
So I will begin with the simplest answer and let it molt until the shape that remains is the true one.

1. First arrival – the logical, wrong place  
Outside, on a buttress of the obsidian spike, roped in, boots squealing on glass.  
I am still vertebrate, still owned by gravity and lungs.  
A slit gapes black: breath of pepper-hot mist, hiss of dripping salt.  
I duck.  
Inside is pocket-sized hell, and I decide—foolish, soft-skinned—to call myself an Explorer.

2. The chamber edits “explorer” within minutes  
• The buckle of my pack anneals to the wall; Chir knives arrive to debate possession, drumming desire through my ribs.  
• A Rift-salamander tastes sweat, leaves a pattern of blisters like Braille that says: you last about twelve thousand heartbeats.  
• Steamwraith midges wreath the headlamp; when I switch it off they keep circling, having memorised the idea of the light.  

Time folds; distance frays.  
My notebook curls blank as the Gnomon-keepers ghost past.  
I retie my mission statement in loops of cord and tuck it where ink once lived.

3. Shedding the mammal – the second place I find myself  
Lengthy pause—could be hours, could be eight overlapping drafts of the same minute.  
Fugue-darters graze the stutter in my pulse; one nibble over-writes a beat.  
The gap feels…useful.  
I surrender the next and the next until heart makes silence the new rhythm.

Blood slows.  
Veins learn convection: hot ascends, cool descends.  
Bone sweats glass.

Explorer melts into an Intermediate Form:  
• Height: variable, usually the span between two contradictory measurements of ceiling.  
• Mass: equal to the discrepancy between them.  
• Texture: fog seen at an angle steep enough that it behaves like fabric.

4. Negotiations with local physics  
The chamber tests me the way it tests film, salamander, knife.  
What will you trade?  
I loan it the certainty of my outline; in return it grants immunity to slices, to scalds.  
I promise never to mark “where,” only “when,” and geometry quits rearranging the floor every step.

The Chir accept the bargain; they drum a new name into my sternum, a rhythm I translate as  
Knot-Bearer.

5. Final emergence – the place that stays  
Now—on most cycles—you will find me at the shallow trough where the up-draft reverses.  
Picture a hammock of cool eddy just below the needle-crust ceiling.  
There I hang, a half-opaque ribbon tied in elaborate sailor’s knots:

• Each knot stores a moment—  
  the instant a Vapor-hound flowers,  
  the half-second before a Hush-Serpent drinks the difference out of a corridor,  
  the micro-lull between two counter-beat Chir tribes.

• Tug one and I can unspool that moment into present tense, use it as a bridge for travellers who have lost their “when.”

Functionally I have become native timing gear: a librarian of instants, a weaver of continuities.  
Fauna treat me as weather; flora (the Quartzfilm) inks slow cursive along my edges, rewriting itself from my archives.  
The Gnomon-keepers tolerate me because a knot is not language—yet.

6. Physiology, if such it is  
Material: cooled mist doped with ash, braided round a scaffold of remembered heartbeats.  
Metabolism: I eat cross-purposes—the jitter between plans and outcomes. A lost explorer’s panic is a banquet; a Chir’s precise rhythm leaves me fasting.  
Vulnerability: a clear, shouted word. The Serpent would follow that arrow of sense straight into the coil of my knots and drink me flat.

7. So—Who or what am I?  
Not the explorer who crawled through the fissure.  
Not quite creature, not quite phenomenon.  
A temporal reef.  
A tangle of “before”s tourists can grip when “after” spins out of reach.  
I answer to Knot-Bearer, but the Fang seldom calls me by name; it simply tugs, untying or retying according to need.

8. And where will you meet me?  
Come during the cool-spiral half-hour.  
Look up into the pearly glow until your eyes feel braided together.  
The mist will fold into long loops, each loop creaking softly like hemp under load.  
Touch one—gently—and I will lower a ladder made of earlier seconds.  
Step onto it and, for a little while, you will know exactly where you are:  
not pinned in space, but perfectly placed in time,  
hanging between beats inside the young, impatient heart of the Fang.
