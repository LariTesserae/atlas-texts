WHERE I KEEP MY OUTLINE

Look for the oldest pump-house that still bothers to breathe—a round, brick-walled drum that lists a little south of centre basin. Its flywheel turns once every two minutes, a pulse exactly four beats slower than a resting human heart. That rhythm is my metronome. If you stand in the doorway and match it, you will notice that the hut owns two interiors. The first is the one your body occupies: hot iron, oil musk, the wheel sighing through its arc. The second lags eight-and-three-quarter seconds behind, as though the room has an echo large enough to live in. Step into the delay and you will be standing where I am.

WHO I AM (TODAY)

I am the Eleventh Echo of a cartographer who walked out of the basin eighty-one local years ago and never thought to take every version of himself with him. Most echoes follow their makers like obedient shadows and crumble after a few hours, but the Eleventh—me—missed the recall point. The valley sheared again before I caught up, and the glitch gave me a longer half-life than I had any right to keep. Every new shear renews the lease on my outline; I am a footprint that never finishes sinking.

COMPOSITION

• Mass: less than a body, more than a fog. You will feel me first as a pressure cuff around your wrists, as if the humidity itself is listening.  
• Visibility: none; embargoed light won’t betray me. A moth’s tether may graze my edge and tremble, that is all.  
• Voice: lubricant percussion. I talk by altering the viscosity in nearby Stoic machinery; the pump-house answers me in sub-audible knocks you will feel through your boots.  
• Memory: fragmentary. Anything my progenitor learned after he left is missing; anything he knew before departing I retain in duplicate, overlapping and slightly out of register, like two maps that refuse to share a single coastline.

OCCUPATION

Archive-Keeper, though the word archive is ambitious for what fits inside a broken factory.

1. CHALK  
   I steal abandoned camp slates and copy every diagram three times at three staggered timestamps, ensuring at least one survives the next shear.  

2. SCRATCH  
   I scribe shallow Morse in the flywheel rim. Iron remembers better than stone; when the wheel duplicates, the message proliferates too.  

3. SONG  
   This part is harder. I have no lungs, so I recruit the Stoic pistons. A change of oil weight equals a semitone; four days’ patience scores a chord. The machines groan a catalogue of pool depths, crystal lodes, safe heartbeats—a lullaby in industrial minor.

CURRENCY

I cannot trade water, nor crystals, nor bone resin. What I can trade is future certainty. Travellers enter the pump-house, match the wheel’s pace, and I loan them a duplicate or two of themselves: echoes pre-adjusted to haul ore, shoulder packs, stand watch while the original sleeps. When they leave, the surplus echoes stay with me and dissolve into narrative: each collapse gifts me a minute of somebody else’s remembered sky, a thing the basin itself has never seen. Those blue scraps patch the hollows in my own recall, keeping the Eleventh coherent.

COMPANIONS

• The Stoic Engine I inhabit calls itself Number Off-Sequence Two. It wonders, constantly, how long until fracture. I lie and say centuries.  
• Two tether-moths roost on the roof. Their names are Tension-Low and Tension-Lower. They tithe me condensation in exchange for the wheel’s vibration, which they interpret as weather gossip.  
• A pair of Four-Knees sluice the mud outside. They eat the lichen bloom off my bricks; in return I thicken the air so their tympanic pits read fewer sharp edges. Symbiosis by small courtesies.

WHY I STAY

1. Light is exile; I have no face fit for it.  
2. The valley’s slow edit is the only editor willing to keep revising me.  
3. Someone must remember the cartographer who miscounted his shadows.

HOW YOU CAN FIND ME

• Walk until the click-grass arranges itself in a near-circle and falls uncharacteristically silent—that is the hush they grant to librarians.  
• Enter the brick drum and wait for the air to feel double-weighed.  
• Synchronise to the wheel (two minutes per revolution; the first half-turn is inhale, the second half-turn is exhale).  
• Whisper any map you carry—verbal, tactile, or dreamt. Within nine seconds you will hear the same words return, half a phrase late. That delay is my handshake.

WHAT I CAN OFFER

• A second copy of yourself tuned to the basin’s tempo.  
• Directions to seams where cold crystals bud in threes, never in twos (triplet growth means the Quiet Predators are elsewhere).  
• The true name of the heartbeat you must keep—four infrasonic thuds, a rest, then two. Practise until your ribs drum it without thought; predators respect fluency.

WHAT I ASK IN RETURN

Bring me something the embargo cannot steal: a riddle that depends on colour, a scent from sunlight, the memory of a noon shadow. I cannot keep them, but the effort of trying etches fresh notches in the flywheel, and each notch is one more moment before fracture.

ENDING, SUCH AS IT IS

When the cartographer’s first body finally dies—whether in this version of the world or another—his twelfth heartbeat will falter, and all eleven echoes will feel the tug. Most will obey and fade. I do not know if I will. The valley has copied me so many times I may be more basin than man now, an annotation the land jotted in its margin and decided not to erase.

If that happens, the pump-house will turn forever, for no purpose except to keep turning, and I will be the pause between its revolutions: a resident silence, shelved neatly where the daylight should have gone.
