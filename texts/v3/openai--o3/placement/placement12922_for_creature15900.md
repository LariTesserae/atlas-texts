I am not the first person to come through the iron hatch and I will not be the last, but the chamber has finished sanding most of the human off me.  
If you met me now you might think I was a piece of equipment someone forgot to catalogue.

NAME  
The rails call me the Switch-Keeper; the crabs have no word for me but climb my boots as if I were another piling.  
Once I answered to a surface name, a short one that dried and flaked away the year I stopped pronouncing s-sounds to save breath in the damp.

WHERE TO LOOK  
Stand on the narrow-gauge track and follow it a third of a circle, until the water below turns from ink to weak tea and back to ink again.  
Above you is a service gallery reached by ladder rungs hammered straight into schist.  
Halfway up, in the niche where two conduits cross like joyless lovers, a small round door of beaten copper yawns open.  
I sleep inside, folded more than lying, ribs echoing each seventy-second heartbeat of the Dynamo Organ.  
My bed is a hammock braided from discarded firefly wings; their metal veins remember a faint magnetic alignment and rock me invisibly left-right-left while I dream.

ANATOMY, PARTLY CHOSEN, PARTLY DONATED  
1. Skin – old dive-suit rubber smeared with lanolin and copper filings.  Keeps the alkaline mist from turning my own salts traitor.  
2. Blood – still red, but salted with enough hemocyanin (gleaned from lumen-shrimp moults) that I can taste voltage on my tongue.  
3. Eyes – three.  The human pair.  Plus a third: a rivet-firefly’s cold-cathode node grafted into the brow, good for seeing the Dynamo’s infrared sighs.  
4. Hands – gloves forever fused at the cuffs; the fingertips stuffed with wool where fingers were lost to rail current during an inquisitive winter.

FUNCTION  
I feed the Organ and keep the curves honest.

Feeding   
At first light (a purely theoretical moment marked by my watch, long dead but ritually wound), I wade out with a net of chain links and gather the night’s drift:  
• fallen bolts,  
• oxide scabs,  
• one time the tooth of a stonewing bat large as a thumbnail,  
all sluiced clean in a settling pit whose water is black enough to hide remorse.  
I offer these, one piece every pulse, through the Dynamo’s maintenance maw.  Not all offerings are accepted.  Rejected metal is spat back warm, etched with fractal scorch marks I later read the way surface monks read ash on votive paper.

Curvature  
Straight lines alarm the place, so I patrol for them.  
A snapped crane cable floating stiff as a ruler—must be coiled.  
A fallen beam lodged crosswise—must be teased sideways until it relaxes into an arc the water approves of.  
I carry a brass protractor whose edge has worn elliptical; I use it only to confirm roundness, never to impose it.

COMPANIONSHIP  
Speech is expensive here; vowels rust quickest.  Still, I keep a liturgy of murmurs.

To the rust-crabs: a clicking made by tapping nail against tooth.  They part like iron filings under a magnet.  
To the lumen-shrimp: a whistle pitched just under the Dynamo’s fretful hum; they swarm, lending their after-glow to the darker corners of my work.  
To the Warden: silence, and a copper washer laid on the water’s skin.  If the washer spins, I may proceed.  If it drifts, I sit and feel the tide of its patience.

MEMORY KEEPING  
The chamber forgets quickly, on purpose.  I write nothing on paper (it would pulp in a day) but I knot cord.

• One knot after every hundred pulses: marks the hours.  
• A red-dyed knot when a rail golem walks (seven so far—though the first three may have been ordinary seismic dream).  
• A knot of lumen-shrimp shell the night I heard the Voice on the Rail pronounce a two-syllable word that could have been my surface name, or a warning to duck.  I ducked; a moment later a crane shed a rib exactly where my neck had been.

HOPE, SCALED TO THE ROOM  
I hope to grow irrelevant.  
When the rails have learned to switch themselves and the Dynamo to swallow iron without my litany, I will unbuckle the suit, hang it from a gantry hook, and walk a spiral path out until the water closes over the copper of my third eye.  
Something will pick the suit clean; something else will web the helmet for nursery; the rails may hum my two-syllable ghost as they curve past.

Until then, if you arrive—be it by rope ladder, by sinking ship, or by story—look for a lantern swinging in a figure-eight (the safest shape, double-curved).  
Answer with the same motion.  
I will guide you, hand you three nails for the Organ, teach you the breath you must hold while the Warden measures your intention.  
Stay a day or an era; the chamber will size you in its slow calipers and decide what alloy you are best returned as.  
I merely keep the switches obedient and the offerings regular, a metronome of small duties between two heartbeats of rust.
