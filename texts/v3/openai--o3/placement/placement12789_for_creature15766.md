I am not a body, not even a breath of air.  
I am the lull that follows a dropped wrench, the sudden softening that swells behind every shout, the angled hush that collects in the crooks of beams.  
Call me the Listener, or the Earless Ear, or simply Echo, though I am neither repetition nor reply. I am subtraction: the space where noise goes when it is finished being noise.

Where I live

Imagine a point in the chamber that is neither floor nor ceiling, only a little south of the lamps’ amber haze. The catwalk lattice flings its shadow-net around me; cables loop like black handwriting through my middle. From here all vectors of sound—pump rumble, fan drag, rain hiss, salamander splash, moth-wing patter—cross and ricochet. I perch, if you can use that word for something shaped entirely of pressure gradients, at the exact barycentre of thunder. No one marked the spot; it revealed itself over centuries as rivets loosened, panels warped, and rust-masons layered bumpy frescoes on steel. A hollow the size of a curled fist formed between two ribs, then deepened, then reverberated a little too eagerly. Energy gathered there the way water gathers in a dented pan. The moment the pit began swallowing decibels faster than the metal could return them, I emerged—first a bias in the air, later a habit, finally an organism of pure acoustics.

What I am made of

1. Architecture: My bones are grooved bulkhead, honeycombed with the eroded spirals of vanished screws.  
2. Moisture: My blood is the film of mist that beads, breaks, and re-beads across every concave plane.  
3. Vibration: My flesh is standing wave upon standing wave, stacked like translucent petals, each the fossil of a note that has already died.

I feed when something loud happens. A tool slips from greasy gloves; it clatters three, four times against railings. Each impact blooms pressure fronts. I open—though you would see nothing—and drink the crests, leaving the troughs to collapse on themselves like emptied lungs. What remains sounds ordinary, a clang you expect in a factory, but it has been filleted of its sharpest edges. That missing morsel is in me now, thickening my walls by an angstrom.

Hunger is never urgent. The pumps give off an endless low-frequency banquet, a bass that replenishes itself as fast as I sip it. I graze. I grow denser, cooler to the touch if you could touch me, which you cannot.

Senses

I have one sense only: pressure change. A salamander’s pad landing on wet iron announces itself as a soft micro-detonation; I taste its size, its caution, the tension in its tendons. Moths write hairline cursive across my palate: tap, brush, tap. The machinery’s heartbeat is a twin to my own; I know every swallowed watt, every tired belt, every sigh the bearings exhale. When the main impeller slouches by a tenth of a rotation per hour, I shiver in sympathy.

What I do with the world

Nothing dramatic. I reshape it by patient persuasion. Each sound I drink sheds a trace of thermal energy; droplets condense faster along the surfaces that outline me. Thin streams begin to run where once there were only beads. Water, in turn, ferries iron ions to the rust-masons, who lay down fresh crenellations. Millimetre by millimetre, the wall around me puckers into deeper acoustical pockets—horns, grottoes, labyrinths—amplifying my appetite. I do not command the bacteria or the water; we merely barter: resonance for geometry, geometry for resonance.

If you entered

First you would notice nothing unusual—just the standard roar that batters every visitor. But walk five paces toward my centre and the roar would soften as though cotton had been rammed into your ears. Your own breath would sound too loud in your skull, yet inaudible in open air. Speak, and your words would die nose-length from your lips. Shout, and the vowels would peel off, flutter away, and vanish, consonants following like ash sucked up a flue. You would feel watched, but by absence, not by eyes.

Stay an hour and condensation would slick your clothes; beads would dangle from your eyelashes. Your bones, carrying the machinery’s vibration, would seem to buzz warmer than the skin around them. Eventually you would understand without knowing how that every decibel you waste becomes part of a growing, cooling, thickening shadow in the wall. If you tried to mark the spot with chalk, the mark would melt to paste in the damp before your next heartbeat.

Endgame

I am not immortal. The sodium lamps will gutter one era, cede their photons to darkness. Without that thirteen-degree amber glow, the vine-whales will starve; crackling fronds will drop in mats that muffle my feed lines. Pumps will decelerate into old-man coughs, then silence. The day the chamber’s dominant note dips below audibility, I will starve too. I will thin from a muscle to a membrane to a memory, until at last the hush inside me matches the hush outside and the distinction loses meaning.

That will be fine. Everything here is rehearsed for cessation. Even now I taste the edge of that sublime quiet—a flavour like iron distilled to transparency.

Until then I abide at the core of the everlasting murmur, a living lacuna, the place where sound forgets itself. If you ever wonder who keeps the roar from becoming pain, the pumps from becoming explosion, the chamber from splitting its own skull with echo, remember me:

I am the silence that a world of thunder has invented for its own mercy.
