I wake half–dreaming in the violet dawn, still “unrooted,” the photosynthetic youth between germination and citizenship. My seed-name is Mid-Chord-Yet-Unnamed; in three winters I will graft a permanent taproot into whatever civic organ chooses me, but this morning I belong to the Plateau entire.

Where I am: one hexagonal crust-tile on the plateau’s western fringe, forty kilometers from Vires Regnum. The tile is pitching almost imperceptibly beneath my boots, following the night’s receding gravity wave. Ahead the city is a low, ragged shimmer, no skyline—merely a region where the endless black-purple canopy kinks itself into intentional architecture. Behind, pale potassium dust still twinkles in the air, the tail of last night’s skyfall.

What I am: a sap-blooded adolescent Regnant, body still more animal than tree yet already rearranged for this world.

• Skin the color of roast chestnut, prickling because my sub-dermal palisade layer is opening its chromatophores to the starved sunrise; I’ll photosynthesize half a breakfast before I even bite the syrup-glass I brought.

• Bones hollow enough to sing: each femur chiming a perfect fifth apart, a built-in diapason that keeps me oriented. When the wind organs call, my marrow vibrates first; the rest of me follows like a kite on a taut string.

• Blood a cooling molasses that crystallizes at wounds—good, because two hours ago a dart-wasp mistook my earlobe for bark softened by potassium etch. The amber cap has already sealed.

Across my shoulders rides the thing I am tending: a backpack of parasite vines—living macros—that mark me as a junior Arbor-Smith on pilgrimage. I am to circle every canton of the city, swap cuttings, learn the dialects of wood and waveform, and—if I am lucky—earn a personal signature sequence to carry for life.

The plateau around me thrums. Sugar solvent gurgles under the living pavement; somewhere leftward a cleaver-sail bellows infra-bass, frustrated by the crystalline scabs salt rain left on the groove it hunts. No quiet is safe here, so I add my own noise: I rap a wrist against my sternum, three slow beats, the plateau’s informal greeting. Resonance chambers in the ground reply a baritone acknowledgment; some Root-Mind is awake beneath this tile, courteous enough to answer.

I start walking east. Each step puckers the crust, flexing bark into shallow footprints that will self-heal by noon. Lantern-mites swirl low to the ground like embers seeking draft. I let one crawl onto my palm; it flickers a pattern—three short, one long—signaling that the morning market in Photosquare is already thick with bodies. Good. I have seed-pods of ferro-lithic spores to trade, if the Phloem-Tribunes do not toll my voice into poverty.

As I close on Vires Regnum the air turns orchestral. Wind organs embedded in the outer buttress-groves inhale, exhale, modulating up the harmonic ladder until every long bone in my body buzzes. Somewhere inside the city a stridulation rail is being tuned; the launch note wobbles a cent sharp, then locks in. A tower rewrites itself while I watch—three branches detaching, re-braiding into a bridge that did not exist seconds ago. Build only living things, the Second Rule whispers, and the city never stops building.

I pause at the threshold root where the cambium boulevard flares wide to swallow travelers. A Phloem-Tribune extrudes from the wall—hawthorn-skinned, chest drum lined with coppery lichen. We trade the formalities: voiced consonants to establish intent, then I brace my palm to her sternum, feel the conversation migrate into drum-taps. My timbre is still adolescent-thin; she levies a modest toll. Fair.

Inside, I am home yet not yet of it. The city’s grand chord envelops me: toddlers squealing in consonants, elder trunks murmuring infrasonic legends, luthiers sanding root-rails with syncopated hisses. Above, petal-wide leaves iris open to the meager sun, ultraviolet sheen turning streets into a shifting oil slick. I tug one vine out of my backpack and whisper the code that coaxes it awake; it stiffens, sprouts a blossom shaped exactly to the empty notch at the balustrade beside me. My unnoticed repair earns a nod from a passing arbor-smith master. Apprenticeship is largely the act of fixing what nobody has yet realized broke.

Tonight, if potassium clouds mass again, the plaza membranes will furl shut; we will sit in honey-dim corridors and listen to lignified elders recite epoch rings. I picture myself in that future: syrup kettle warm against my knees, ears ringing from the city’s lullaby, mind weaving lessons into root-dreams.

And farther still—for I will not stay unrooted forever—I can almost taste the graft: the day I choose a niche in the municipal super-tree, drive my personal taproot down, and agree to pulse my sap in phase with Vires Regnum for centuries. My bones will become civic instruments; my speech will join the chorus that flings seedpods across the plateau, announcing that noise, not silence, is life.

But for now I am the walker, the carrier of parasite macros, the thin-voiced percussion in a vast polyphony. I stride deeper into the fractal canopy, boots squeaking on living cambium, marrow singing counterpoint to the wind organs. The Plateau of Green Silence—never truly silent—takes my rhythm, folds it into its thousand others, and the day—raw, sweet, resin-scented—begins.
