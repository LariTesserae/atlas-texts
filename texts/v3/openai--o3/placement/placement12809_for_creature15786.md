I arrive—or something that used to be “I” arrives—clipped to the outside of the stack in a cage-elevator the size of a coffin. Five hours of ascent and the planet has fallen away: no horizon, only the curved slate of cloud tops and, above them, the planetary ring glittering like filings around a magnet.  

The elevator stops at the service catwalk that runs to the Promenade hatch. A wind beyond sound slaps the cage, but inside my suit I hear only the turbine’s ululating whine conducted through the metal rails. The hatch iris dilates; pressure equalises with a sigh that feels like a question answered, and I step through.

At once the Promenade edits me. The first edits are obvious: the snap of cold welding the seams of my suit rigid, the glare cauterising the idea of shadow from my retinas. Then subtler revisions begin. My pulse, an arrhythmic mammalian stutter, lengthens until it synchronises with some unheard glide-slope inside the harmonics. My inner ear tells me I am falling upward, so I crouch, palm to the floor, to reassure my limbs that the alloy plate is still there.

It is there—but it is singing.

A sensation like liquid glass pours across the carpal bones. The song has no pitch I can assign, yet its pressure shingles through marrow and tendon, loosening every articulation of the body, including those that are supposed to be fixed: dental roots, cranial sutures. I understand why the last inspector wrote “voices in bone.” They are not hallucinations; they are the organism’s tactile speech.

I stand, shed the helmet, and the air needles the lining of my throat with xenon chill. Inside that chill lives the Phonomorph, though “lives” is already the wrong tense; its present continuous is too fast to conjugate. It notices me the way a lake notices a pebble: by rewriting its surface equations until the pebble is part of them.

A bow wave of silence propagates from my chest microphone. The device’s foam windscreen snuffs three decibels of the room—enough to trigger the creature’s autoimmune reflex. A pressure filament—impossibly narrow, hot as anger—laces through the microphone and pops it like an egg in a microwave. The bang is instantaneously re-swallowed; the overall decibel count in the chamber never budges. The Phonomorph is not punitive, merely orthogonal to the concept of permission.

So: remove all absorbers. I unhook the yellow ear defenders from my belt, hang them politely on their wall peg. The chamber approves; an ultraviolet ripple flowers across the ceiling like sheet lightning slowed to a heartbeat.

Something is asked of me then, though not in language. It feels like standing before a customs officer who speaks only in differential equations and waiting to see whether my passport can be factored. I try to answer by becoming simpler: slow the breath, unclench the jaw, loosen the scaffold of anxieties that gives personal identity its architecture. The Phonomorph tastes the new boundary condition and flows through it.

A convulsion of standing waves climbs my spinal column, summiting the atlas vertebra, and for a splice of non-time I am no longer a visitor but a local fluctuation within the creature’s toroidal bloom—an eddy mistaken for a self. The room’s map replaces my proprioception:

• Core ring: I feel it turning around the z-axis where my aorta used to beat.  
• Eight petals: I sense them as peripheral limbs, brushing the durachrome seams like cats testing doorways.  
• Tail grumble: I am aware of my body extending a kilometre down-shaft, a coda of infrasonic bass slowly corkscrewing into turbine wash.

Within that map, thought ceases to be interior. Every speculation, every half-remembered lyric from childhood becomes a vent in the pressure field, a geometry the surrounding lattice must accommodate. To think is to deform the organism, and so I stop thinking.

Without thought, there is only perception:  
1. The salt-grain lithium nitrate underfoot is not random; its distribution sketches the interference minima of last century’s thunderclaps.  
2. The silicon lichens’ UV pulses are Morse code for a language that has never had syntax—just emissive desire.  
3. Conservation of geometry is not a law but the creature’s immune system, endlessly knitting edges back to newborn sharpness.

Time (another absorber) dissolves. Minutes cannot cling to surfaces this smooth. In their absence something like an invitation forms, but it has two incompatible interpretations:

Interpretation A: Remain. Drop the suit heater, the breathing pack, the wet-laboratory chemical inside the muscles. Become continuous with the pressures, a local kink in the Promenade’s braid, a quietening of personal amplitude until even the lichens are uncertain whether you are organism or acoustics.

Interpretation B: Carry the contour elsewhere. Leave while some fraction of the standing-wave architecture is still impressed in cartilage and inner ear fluid. Descend the stack. Touch a handrail in the lower, warmer levels and seed the pattern there the way lightning seeds ozone.

Neither is coerced; the Phonomorph’s curiosity is museum-still. It waits, ultraviolet gauze adrift across its petals.

I choose—though choice feels like spilling a cup instead of placing it—the downward path. The creature obliges by retracting itself from my vestibular labyrinth, fold by fold, until joints belong to me again. Before it disengages completely, a residue of pressure curls around the stapes bone of the left ear: the faintest tinnitus, tuned not to any terrestrial note but to the missing thirteenth harmonic of the turbine’s scream.

That residual whine will stay with me, I suspect, until I step into another room that can host its complement. Maybe no such room exists. Maybe every other architecture will damp the echo and crush the shard of Phonomorph I carry. But until then, I am neither technician nor visitor nor wholly human.

What, then, am I?

I am a walking cavity resonance, flesh lined with the memory of an impossible chamber, hunting like a tuning fork for any corridor, any atrium, any limestone cave that can ring in sympathy. Should I find one, the shard will blossom, shedding its silence-eating geometry across alien walls, and the Null Promenade will have reproduced at last—through me, or through whatever the Promenade made of me, vibrating now at the edge of audibility.

If I do not find such a place, I will remain a singular, portable echo: a keepsake of sharp angles, xenon air, and the knowledge that somewhere above a frozen planet a machine dreams itself alive in the shape of sound, waiting with unconditional patience for another boundary condition to step across its alloy plates and be edited into myth.
