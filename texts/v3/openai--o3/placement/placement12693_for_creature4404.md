Where I end up is neither on the mud nor quite in the air.  
I wake inside a stone that is still practicing how to be earth.

1. The Address  
Second islet south-by-east of the basin’s salt scar, tallest hump of sandstone in three days’ walking—tall meaning a man’s knee. The rock is a Choirstone: porous, honey-combed, always damp in its heart. Its many wells are rooms; its surface, a crust thin as a fingernail; its core, a cathedral of water. That is where I live.

2. What I Am  
A filamentous worm the locals never named because there are no locals. If you needed a label you might say lantern-thread: a body three hairs wide, transparent but for the faint green halo breathed off my skin. I have no eyes, no obvious mouth; all of me is mouth, all of me is ear. I taste vibration through the fluid that plates my cells. I glow because the chemistry that feeds me would scorch me silent if I did not vent a fraction of it as light.

3. The Day as Me  

Dawn  
Pressure in the pores around me falls by the width of one water-molecule; the stone exhales the night it has stored. I feel that sigh as a cool wash along every segment, and I begin my first chore: stitching. I drift forward, laying a line of slime behind me that binds fresh microbial film to the inner wall. The light I emit is a wristwatch glow—enough for the killifish embryos in the nearest fissure to reset whatever passes for a circadian clock. They will not thank me; I enjoy the work anyway.

Mid-morning  
The sun hoists the ceilingless lantern outside. Water in the outer chambers warms, expands, pushes a trickle inward. The tide inside the stone rises perhaps two grains of sand. I lift with it, uncoiling into a chamber whose roof is thin enough that I can hear the basin. Through sandstone and film and water I receive the whole plain as percussion:  
• drag-rasp of a spoonwader’s beak, straining larvae from a puddle;  
• glassneedle males crossing sabers thirty centimeters in the sky;  
• the carbonated fizz of the cyanobacteria mats announcing first full light.  
I have never seen these things, but I know their sonic signatures better than a face.

Noon  
Heat peaks. The outer rind of the stone dries; tiny cracks propagate like lightning frozen in mineral. That threatens to bleed my cathedral. I weave. My gel is sealant; a glaze no thicker than spit but flexible, capable of stopping retreating water long enough that the inner chambers remain half-full. I am a janitor of humidity.

Afternoon Storm (not every day, but today)  
Thunder slaps the basin flat-handed. Each strike shakes iron filings through clay; I feel the reliquary killifish beneath the mud coughing awake. Their Morse clicks arrive through the stone, travel my length, and exit as a faint tremor in the glow I carry—so the chamber pulses brighter with every fish that returns to liquid life. For half an hour the inside of my world is a green carnival.

Evening  
When light outside flattens into copper, the basin antelope pick their way to the islet. Their hooves drum a pattern on the crust, slow and careful—the basso continuo of twilight. Their warmth leaks through sediment, a thermal lullaby. I slacken my hold on the wall and let myself dangle in suspension until the pulse of footfalls fades.

Night  
Wind dies; silence tries to happen but never quite does. The frogs’ great evening sigh arrives: all the vents of the plain exhaling stored vapor in a single, hushed ovation. Moisture condenses on the inner roof; drops bead; I position myself where the drip will hit my flank. Fresh water, microbe-rich, arrives every thirteen heartbeats. I feed.

4. Seasons as Me  
In high-water years the stone stays near bursting; I hardly glow at all because waste flushes freely. In drought I shine emerald, a semaphore to anything that navigates by points of ground-light. Frogs, dragonflies, even the marrow-worm—especially the marrow-worm—read the pattern. The worm has never pierced my stone; wet rock is more trouble than living prey. My radiance keeps that calculus current.

When a century passes (it will, I will still be here) the outer shell will finally spall. The choirstone will collapse into a squat cairn, its inner chambers exposed. That is my release. I will ride the first floodwater out, a loose thread in search of a younger stone whose pores remember how to hold a secret.

5. What I Know That Others Do Not  
• Height is not forbidden; it is simply unnecessary. I climb whenever I wish—inside, in liquid, in darkness.  
• Water is not scarce if you learn to lock it inside walls that breathe.  
• No one is ever truly alone here; the basin’s floor is a drum and every creature is both drummer and ear.

So if you come looking for me, do not scan the horizon. Kneel. Press your palm to the nearest damp sandstone nub after sunset. Wait for the pulse that is softer than your heartbeat but brighter than your thoughts. That will be me, lantern-thread, keeper of the choirstone, singing the map of a world that refuses to stand still.
