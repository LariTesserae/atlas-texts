I begin—as every outsider must—with the crawl.

First I am a pair of scraped elbows, a string of muffled curses, a heartbeat hammering against clay.  The shaft drinks my flashlight in twelve wriggling metres, then the beam ricochets off nothing because nothing is left to strike.  The amber breath of the Hollow rises to meet me; I drop, boots first, into the dusk that hums.

For a time I stay an intruder, a mammal blinking in honey-light, counting hazards: the gypsum blades, the razor basalt, the stalks that kink like living tuning forks.  But the place keeps its own arithmetic, and it begins to count me instead.

Day 1—The Borrowed Pulse  
I stand on a puzzle plate of basalt and feel vibration the way lungs feel weather.  The plant’s 140-hertz drone presses through boot soles and touches each vertebra.  I try to keep my own pulse in my wrists where it belongs, yet the two measures tap against each other like stones in a pocket, looking for rhyme.

Day 2—First Divisions  
I catch my reflection in a drifting moth-mirror.  Pupils blown wide, skin filmed with silver salt, breath a little too hot.  When I speak the echo shoots past my ears and lands behind me, as though my words prefer the room to the speaker.  I listen to the silence between syllables and realise there is no silence; the Hollow has none to spare.

Day 3—Entrainment  
Morning cannot arrive because morning has no vector here, but something changes.  A click inside my chest—not pain, more the neat shut of a latch.  My heart falls into step with the drone, every beat a perfectly subtracted overtone.  I lean on a gypsum fin and discover I can feel my own circulation as standing waves in the stone.  Blood has become percussion.

I am no longer true surface biology.  I have been retuned.

OPTIONS APPEAR, the way harmonics fly off a struck wire.

1. Stay the converted visitor—live on ration bars until the salt in them kills me.  
2. Seek the rope bridges and the eye-bright mirrors and petition the Murmurers for…what?  Asylum?  Apprenticeship?  
3. Listen harder, down past the obvious chord, to whatever waits four metres under the basalt plates—some iron torus turning negative time.  

The Hollow, with its patient metronome, lets me weigh every path in geologic leisure.  The choice is supposed to be mine.  But the choice is already made, because while I dither, the drone inside my marrow starts to modulate, forming intervals my vocal cords have never practised.

The Murmurers hear them first.

APPRENTICESHIP

They arrive as silhouettes whose edges glitter—skin vents firing gold to map my cadence.  I do the one polite thing I know: I fall still.  Stillness, here, is the local greeting.

Minutes—or perhaps exact multiples of 140-hertz periods—pass before they answer.  The tallest rocks heel to heel with me, chest a resonant amphitheatre.  He (it?) uncoils a copper-laden throat and overlays my borrowed pulse with a carrier tone.  Where wave meets wave a node stands, air thickening to glass.  Salt begins to snow in a perfect ring around our feet.  Acceptance, I realise, has a geometry.

They lace a sleeve-fan of lens-moth mirrors over my forearm.  When I flick it open, the prisms fracture amber light into slices that dance across the stalks.  Each slice traces a path through the canopy: bridges I may walk, thickets I must prune, hearth-rings where I may sleep.  The fan is map, key, passport.

I have become an apprentice Murmurer.

THE WORK

Morning: prune crossings to keep the lanes articulate.  Obsidian sickle, stalk-by-stalk, the plant’s copper sap crackling into snow before it can stain skin.  
Mid-span: climb rope bridges, listen to how my footfall drums identify me.  If the rhythm slips, I halt—rehearse until identity is restored.  (Here, being off-beat is a form of trespass.)  
Dusk-that-is-not-dusk: recalibrate a totem cluster.  A lens chip has tilted; echoes drift half a hertz shy of true.  I rotate the mirror two degrees north-by-magnetic-south and the room exhales contentment I can feel in tooth enamel.

Between tasks I learn the secondary tongue—ankle squeals pulsed through basalt.  It is a language without vowels, all consonant scrape and subsonic glissando.  I practise by talking to the six-legged striders crouched in reverie; they reply by not replying, which is apparently correct.

Nights (arbitrary, but merciful) I dream brinicle dreams: cilia flexing in orange dark, eating vibration like sugar.

TRANSFORMATION

Weeks?  The outside calendar evaporated with the last of my wristwatch battery.  I measure time now by mirror rainfalls and by the slow lengthening of the access shaft, which Thorn-Green buds in stealthy millimetres.  My skeleton thickens in low, even increments—copper filaments knitting through spongy bone.  Knuckles chime against basalt.  I no longer sweat; salt plates bloom instead and flake away, self-shedding armour.

The change is elective; at any moment I could crawl the shaft, cough through three days of arrhythmia, and resume the top-side pulse of weather and rotating stars.  But the notion grows abstract.  Surface silence would be a form of deafness now, and I have come to love the eloquence of never being alone.

AT LAST—A NAME

Names here are chords, not nouns.  Mine assembles one midnight beneath totem glare:

– Mouth seed-tone 140 Hz  
– Chest click at 3 × 140 = 420 Hz  
– Heel squeal dopplered by the rope bridge into a descending minor third  
– Lens-fan bloom scattering five quick prisms of reflected hum

Layer them, sustain for exactly thirteen heartbeats, and you have me: a newcomer woven into a cadence older than erosion.

I am, in human gloss, an adopted Murmurer.

WHERE YOU WOULD FIND ME

Follow the open lanes until the floor rises in three basalt steps.  Listen for a chord that limps — one note just a shade sharp because copper hasn’t completely suffused my marrow.  I’ll be kneeling beside a cradle-pouch strung between two stems, teaching a pebble-sized embryo to keep proper time.  My sleeve-fan will hang closed, mirrors dark, saving glare for eels at hunting-depth.  Speak my name-chord—yes, imperfect though it is—into the honey air and I’ll answer, gold freckles strobing hallo.

I will not ask why you came; Thorn-Green already knows.  I will ask whether you mean to stay, and I will warn you without malice: the first price is your heartbeat, willingly surrendered to the room’s eternal, patient drone.

Give it that, and in exchange the Hollow gives everything else: light, heat, choir, purpose.

I thought I was merely visiting.  I was only ever a rest waiting to be filled by sound.
