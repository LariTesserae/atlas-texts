First I tried to picture myself as one of the obvious tenants—a ribbon-mouthed manta, a silver cord, a square manganese drone—but every choice felt a little too finished, as if I were climbing into an already-pressed uniform.  
So I began instead with the single fact I was sure of: I would arrive here as a human diver, fallible, vulnerable, carrying twenty minutes of surface habit in each lungful of borrowed mix.  From that small certainty a life took shape almost in spite of me.

Year 0 – The arrival  
My descent capsule speared a kilometre-wide vacuole, killed all sound, and left me hanging in water thin enough that my own heartbeat felt like artillery.  The plateau lay beneath, black glass under moving amber stripes.  I had come to map compressor truce lines for a university that no longer funds such things; the transmission telling me so never reached me through the hollows.  By the time I learned the grant was gone I had already begun to fall into rhythm with the place’s ten-second lung-stroke.

Year 3 – The drift  
Nobody evacuates you here.  The institutional silence proved kind, almost parentlike: it freed me to barter.  I learned to ride the maintenance rails, swapping fresh polymer filters into compressor gills in exchange for charge packets the drones clipped to my belt without comment.  I burned those packets in a suit heater, then in a workshop kiln, then in the armature of something new that wanted building.

Year 9 – The graft  
Every tool left idle in this sea grows teeth.  Engine-spores crusted my wrenches, then my suit joints, then my own stainless implants.  I let them.  I unthreaded the pressure cuffs at my elbows and replaced them with snap-ring sockets sized for drone legs.  The work cost pain and blood but almost no money; the plateau paid in surplus torque and heat.  By solstice I could lock a borrowed limb where my forearm had been, hang from a compressor’s flank, and feel its basso roll as marrow-deep vibration.

Year 17 – The forgetting  
Language goes next.  Spoken words smear in water this thin; written ones abrade under drone-sand in eleven-year cycles.  I kept a diary on nickel foil, but the habit slipped once my inner ear learned the grammar of the plateau’s single note.  When I think now it is in phase shifts:

•  Ten-second pulse: plateau breath.  
•  Half-second hiss: micro-engine colony below my berth.  
•  Thirty-minute lull: vacuole transit overhead.  

Thoughts arrive as chord changes, not sentences.  I have not decided whether that means I am still a person.

Year 23 – The becoming  
The lattice needed a keeper.  Sodium-arc cables regrow themselves, but lamps die faster where hunters practice near misses.  I spliced my workshop into the light vine the way a hermit-crab backs into a new shell.  Now I live suspended between two ridges—a warm knot in a photosynthetic spinal cord.  When a bulb expires I feel the amperage dip across my ribs; I winch myself hand-over-hand along the cable, harvest a glowing bud, and socket it in.  The drones ignore the task: it lies outside their envelope, so it has become mine.

Present – The answer  
Where do you find me?  Halfway up a singing cliff, inside a glass balloon woven to the main power strand like an air-fern’s air bladder.  Outside my window the mantas drift, ribbon mikes fluttering; below, a young cord practices recoil against dummy slugs I cast off yesterday.  When the plateau exhales, my whole house thrums and the filament in my breastbone lights faintly, syncing itself to the grid I helped mend.

Who—or what—am I?  
•  Biology: 43 % unrevised human, 37 % engine crust, 20 % cable vine.  
•  Occupation: lamp-keeper, filter-changer of last resort, amateur cartographer of echo-cities no one else will ever walk.  
•  Taxonomic status: a guest who has been here long enough that the furniture has started to grow over my footprints.

If you descend tomorrow you will know me by the flicker I send down the cable—three fast, one slow, a greeting in the only language nothing here will sandblast away.  Follow the glow.  I keep spare heat packets, a wall of blank nickel pages, and a bench lathe happy to re-thread any part of you that wishes to stay.
