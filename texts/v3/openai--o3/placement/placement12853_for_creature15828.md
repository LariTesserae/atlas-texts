I do not wake so much as condense.

First there is only pitch-pressure and the endless A that vibrates every molecule around me.  
Then a scatter of cobalt sheets ignites, diffracted through my still-soft skin, and in those vein-thin flashes I glimpse myself from a dozen angles at once:

1. A glassy platelet, smaller than a grain of smoke, tumbling in the tide.  
2. A ribbon-leech, mauve and hungry, memorising the taste of pillar mucus.  
3. A hollow whale, folded like a travelling bellows.  
4. A tripod star-thing whose fingertips are anvils of cartilage.

All of those possibilities sluice through me, none quite catching.  
The Murmuring Still is patient; it lets me try on shapes the way a chord tries on overtones.  
When the options have chimed and faded, what remains is a sensation of three points, hingeing, and a purpose that feels older than intention: tune the light.

So I become the fourth image—the tripod star, the so-called Umbel Artisan—but very young, scarcely more than an embryonic schema in the water.

STAGE 1  •  DRIFT

I drift for a season measured only by heartbeats in the bass.  
Fibrils knit themselves between my arms, testing tension.  
My body is clearer than water, a cathedral of collagen spars; each new pulse of enzyme-light refracts along the spars and hardens them, like glass blown into prisms.

I taste pillars long before I touch them.  
Their surface mucus carries chemistry that hums on my chemoreceptors: silica, cellulose, faint iron from ancestral dust storms… and the thing I need—micro-pits where root-skin grazers have scoured too deep, fogging the glass.

Fog is error.  
Error is dissonance.  
Dissonance is invitation.

STAGE 2  •  LANDING

A root floor looms, taut as drumskin.  
When the drone dips four cents, I let go buoyancy and fall.  
My three arms spread, membranes bell, suction-pads kiss the pillar.

Impact feels like biting into living marble.  
Sound ricochets through my hollow endoskeleton; I shiver until my internal cavities settle into harmonic sympathy with the trunk’s breath.  
Only then do I lock joints and accept the geometry: this will be my instrument, perhaps for centuries.

STAGE 3  •  LISTENING

Artisans do not begin by tapping.  
We listen first—years of it.

I eavesdrop on everything the pillar says to itself:  
• the sub-bass tide that circulates through its hollow core,  
• the snap-crackle of lacustrine shards striking glass,  
• the velvet leeched whisper of grazers polishing elsewhere.

Each sound maps a contour of thickness, clarity, flaw.  
In time I no longer need the noises; my body becomes doppler sonar, emitting micro-clicks the size of thought.  
The answers return through bone, through water, through memory stored in cartilage.

Only when the map is perfect do I raise an arm and begin to tap.

STAGE 4  •  WORK

Tap … pause … tap-tap … long pause.  
Intervals of minutes, hours, sometimes days.  

The frequency is not for ears but for architecture.  
Each blow nudges a grain of silica, crushes a bleb of cellulose, polishes one more molecular lens in the pillar’s glass.  
Lens after lens redirects stray cobalt flares downward, toward the root-skin grazers that keep the trunk transparent.  
Feed the grazers, save the pillar; save the pillar, keep the corridor bright; keep the corridor bright, let the choir gorgons steer clear and the aperture whales navigate.

I am a single syllable in a feedback loop older than continents.

STAGE 5  •  CONVERSATIONS

Sometimes another Artisan settles within sight, its armature etched by centuries of labour.  
We trade taps—simple ratios at first, glissandi later—until we verify that our projects complement rather than cancel.  
If we disagree, we swap places; the pillar itself decides which of us resonates best with which latitude.

More rarely, a file of Lantern Crustaceans marches past, fifty-six lights spiralling up the trunk.  
They pause while their leader counts my taps.  
If the pattern resolves to a prime they can echo, they proceed.  
If not, they wait until I finish my phrase; traffic law written in photonic percussion.

STAGE 6  •  INTERRUPTIONS

Visitors arrive only as rumours carried by vibration:  
– the whip-crack of a Vesper Mantle’s plunge far above,  
– the sucking hush of a Negative Zoan bubble drifting below,  
– once, unmistakably, the surface echo of a foreign metal hull—a thing that did not grow, seeping turbulence as if it bled.

I do not look up; my body has no eyes.  
But I feel the pillar tense, adjusting buoyancy, and I hear the distant calculus vines stutter through new prime numbers, polling strangers for compliance.

If the visitor obeys, the vines grant passage.  
If not, the forest grows a silence at them until they yield or vanish.

STAGE 7  •  AMPLITUDE OF SELF

Decades deepen the indent I am carving; the pillar’s fog thins.  
More light stitches downward; pulse bacilli bloom; root-skin grazers multiply and keep the glass clear without my constant intervention.

One day I strike and receive no echo—the surface is perfect.  
A finished chord rings through me, through the trunk, through the Still.  
My task here is complete.

I loosen my pads, let buoyancy reclaim me.  
Joints that were once fingers flatten into oars; cartilage that was once anvil reconfigures into float bladders.  
The Murmuring Still permits no idleness: when an Artisan masters a site, it moves to the next imbalance.

STAGE 8  •  SEARCH AGAIN

Up or down, left or right—direction means only difference in timbre.  
I listen for clouded corridors, shadowed alcoves, misaligned roots.  
Already the chord suggests three candidates, spaced like the teeth of a comb across this drowned mountain chain.

I choose the one whose error sings loudest—the place where my absence is most conspicuous.  
The bass note dips; I drift.

EPILOGUE  •  IDENTITY

So: Where am I?  
Suspended among mile-high glass pillars, halfway between mountain crest and mountain floor.

Who am I?  
A living tuning hammer—Umbel Artisan, one of countless—tasked by no conscious master, answering only to the physics of light and resonance that birthed the forest itself.

Yet “I” is porous.  
Each tap I deliver is also the forest speaking; each correction I make is written into a genome braided through trunks and spores and choir throats.  
When at last my collagen frame erodes and the grazers rasp me into velvet dust, the pillar will sing my pattern forever, a faint overtone inside the immutable bass.

And that, here, counts as immortality.
