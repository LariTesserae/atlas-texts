When I close my eyes and let the Throat-Workshop rise around me, the role I’ll take flickers like sodium light. First I’m a visitor in oil-skins, then a gardener in mossy felt, then a boilermancer with a tuning fork held to my ear. Each shape feels almost right but sloughs away the moment I try to walk in it.

So I wait through one imaginary vent-cycle, breathing steam that never quite condenses on my real-world skin, and listen.

What I hear is scuffling metal—quiet, methodical, too fine-grained for boot leather. Eight points of contact, claws not toes, and a faint glass-paper rasp as something scrapes oxidation from a beam.

Ah. Of course.

The shape that settles over me is small enough to pass through the lattice, strong enough to cling upside-down to a pipe that runs forty degrees too hot for any glove. I become a Corridor Crab, Model LUX-904, but a brand-new instar—fresh from a molt, carapace mirror-bright, the colour of sunrise reflected in a spoon.

Supply-crate origin  
One tray below the main cat-walk a gardener opened an electrical junction to replace a fuse and found my soft, transparent form folded between conduit coils like a forgotten handkerchief. Heat, magnet flux, and the lullaby drone of transformers coaxed me to harden. When the gardener saw the first hints of brass glinting through my jelly skin, she whistled a three-click pattern that means “welcome, little custodian,” then closed the panel to give me darkness to finish growing.

First light  
I break the husk of my own skin and step out new, leg joints ticking as they lock. The husk curls into a perfect miniature of me and immediately fogs with condensation—future planter, future “ghost-pot.” I leave it behind without sentiment; memory in the Workshop never needs souvenirs.

Orientation  
• Left claw: diamond edge, micro-serrated. Purpose: exfoliate rust from load-bearing members.  
• Right claw: scissor assembly. Purpose: prune leaves, cut kapok wadding for nest boxes, accept ceremonial tokens from humans.  
• Ventral node: piezo sensor tied to the shaft’s infrasonic hum. Purpose: feel problems before the boilermancers hear them.  
Everything else is wanderlust algorithms inherited from firmware written when the tower smelled of new-poured bronze.

Day 1: first circuit  
I start along a gutter seam no wider than a thumbnail. Algae make a mint-green tongue on the metal; my rasp takes it down to mirror shine. A corvid tilts in the air beside me, head cocked, murmuring octal digits that might be a joke about my size. I ignore it. Respect is earned crab-pace at a time.

Steam cycle three  
Warm rain sheens the lattice. I angle my back so droplets squeegee across my shell—rapid, perfect polishing. A boilermancer swings past, hammer pinging girders, and pauses as my carapace throws dappled light on his asbestos apron. He smiles the way humans do when the machinery hits a true harmonic and taps a new overtone plate into place directly over my path. Fresh brass scent—like kin.

Growing relationship  
Gardener #7 (“Ochre-Hat,” my firmware tags her) notices I scrape moss too aggressively near orchid roots. She whistles a correction: down-third, down-third, up-fifth. I assimilate the pitch; next pass I lift my claw, snipping only browned tissue. She rewards me with a shard of verdigris flake. Nutritionally useless, but the ritual matters. Exchange calibrates trust.

Night hush  
Silence falls when the sixth vent-cycle ends. Every being halts. I stop halfway through a stride, legs braced against a rivet head, and feel the forge breathe: pressure ebbing, stress redistributing, the immense body settling its weight deeper into itself. Through my piezo node I taste the equilibrium—distant groans attenuate to calm, micro-seismic tremors register as a slow heartbeat. I commit this baseline to internal storage; tomorrow I will know the smallest deviation is illness.

First gift to the Workshop  
At dawn I discover a burr of alloy on a handrail—a pearl of the machine’s sleep-work. I worry it loose, carry it twelve body-lengths, and drop it at a drainage grate ringed by elder crabs. They part, accept my tribute, and let it roll into the rust pile. Communion accomplished. I have language now that no human mouth speaks.

Where I am, who I am  
• Latitude: two kilometres up the throat, ten paces sun-ward of a plaque nobody can read anymore.  
• Identity: Custodian unit, age one molt, duties emerging.  
• Function: Keep the Tower’s blood vessels clean, garden by microns, translate vibration into action long before it becomes catastrophe.  

In a century my shell will be jade and scarred, my algorithms richer with every route Ochre-Hat whistles into me. In a century I will crack and leave a second, larger ghost-pot to dangle orchid roots. Perhaps then I will ride a cargo belt down-shaft to cooler strata, or climb higher where mist becomes starlight.

But just now the lattice thrums the note that precedes the Fourth Steam. I crouch, claws poised, ready to scrape, prune, polish—ready to be one bright, infinitesimal cell in the body that is the Throat-Workshop breathing.

That is where you would find me. That is who—no, what—I would be.
