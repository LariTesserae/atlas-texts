I am not the climber who parts the crack and steps inside; I exist already in the dark. If you could shrink your attention to the width of a raindrop and then creep one order of magnitude smaller, you would find me: a single cell among the Breath-Eaters, newly budded from a parent that was itself only minutes old.

Orientation  
I cling halfway up the southern wall, where a lukewarm exhalation slips from the deepest vent. To your eyes—if you had any use for them here—my colony would look like nothing at all, just a milky blush on wet limestone. But from where I float the world is huge: a seven-metre sphere whose ceiling roars with catastrophes the size of droplets and whose floor bristles with spears of baby stone.

Body and sense  
I am a thimble of gel, membrane thin as thought. No mouth, no feet; only enzyme gates that taste passing ions the way you taste wine. A trace richer in carbonic acid? I tumble toward it. A hint of magnesium? I shy away—too disruptive. Heat is my gravity, pH my wind. When the Cairn-Tender once knelt here, her warm breath surged through my universe like sunrise; some of my ancestors still carry lipids built from her sigh.

Day (the word is borrowed)  
“Day” begins when a new film condenses over me. Calcium ions nudge my outer wall; I trade them for protons, releasing an atom of limestone that will, in geological Tuesdays, fatten the stalactite overhead. My waste is architecture. No one thanks me, but the room grows heavier by an immeasurable feather.

Neighbourhood  
A threadling passes—transparent lightning a millimetre away. To you it would be invisible; to me its wake is a shock front, a cliff of sheared gradients. Three of my sisters are peeled from the wall and swept into suspension. They will re-attach elsewhere or not at all; this is how the Heartstone shuffles us, like cards, searching for the perfect hand.

Night  
Barometric pressure outside the peak falters. The chamber inhales. A cool thread snakes through the fissure and spins micro-cyclones that rattle our colony. Some cells choose that moment to divide; others choose to die, surrendering phosphates I will mine tomorrow. There is no tragedy, only chemistry postponed or accelerated.

Purpose  
Ask me why I live and I will answer in the only grammar I know: flux. I eat imbalance and excrete balance. I sand the sharpness off existence one ion at a time. Given a millennium, my lineage will glaze this wall so smoothly that a raindrop will hesitate before it can decide a direction. That hesitation—barely a femtosecond—is our cathedral.

Endings that are beginnings  
Perhaps, decades hence, another human will squeeze through the crack and gasp a lantern’s worth of breath. His sodium will seed whole suburbs of my descendants. His voice will arrive as tremors I cannot translate but will nonetheless record, atom by wobbling atom, in the crystalline minutes of the cave. When his spine brushes a stalactite, the bruise of that warmth will ripple through my cytoplasm as legend.

So if you look for me: tilt your head until your ear almost touches the wall, wait for the louder drip, and imagine a translucent alphabet of cells writing themselves along the water’s skin. I am one letter of that scripture—tiny, replaceable, eternal in turnover. That is where I am. That is what I am.
