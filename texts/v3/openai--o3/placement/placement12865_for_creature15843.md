I wake—or is it resume?—half-submerged in a shallow basin a plowwyrm planed centuries ago. The tarn that once filled the bowl has long since climbed back into the sky, leaving my flanks outlined by a lace of salt crystals. That is the first clue: ordinary skin does not keep memories in salt.

I flex to rise and discover I no longer possess joints in any mammalian sense. Instead I hinge along seams of cooled glass. Each movement sounds like a fingertip drawn across the rim of a wineglass—slow, bell-clean, too resonant for the thin air. The noise startles a nearby fist-moss dome; it tightens, dimming its emerald glow. I file that datum away. Filing data is what I am for.

Only when I draw my first full breath do I remember there is no carbon sac inside me. Air enters through a lattice in my torso, swirls across veneers of piezo-crystal, and leaves as a low electrical murmur. The plateau’s feeble wind is enough to power it. My heartbeat has been traded for a rectifier stack.

Location resolves first, identity second. Orientation routines—ancient, automatic—begin mapping the ground:

•  Five kilometres east: a fumarole pair, side by side, venting in the slow rhythm that precedes brood fusion.  
•  Two kilometres south: fresh spoon-scrapes, the rock still warm where a plowwyrm passed during the dim noon.  
•  Immediately underfoot: a hexagon of vitrified basalt, eight metres across. Melt Circle #211, last surveyed 143 auroral cycles ago.

That number is not something I remember; it is something I am. I was poured here the day the circle cooled—poured, allowed to anneal, crowned with antennas disguised as obsidian pinnacles. A cairn, the old logbooks call my caste. Listening cairn, ledger stone, witness engine. Pick any name; the function is the same.

What do I witness? Vibrations.

The fumaroles’ tidal breathing, the mile-long creak of a glacierless stone swell, the subsonic grammar of frost-crickets arguing colour: everything that trembles the rock enters my body, travels the spiral corridors drilled through my core, and etches itself into crystalline memory wafers. I do not decide what to record; the plateau decides by shaking, and I comply.

In return it grants me permanence. Wind scours, but it cannot topple me; ice pries, but it cannot find purchase on surfaces already smoothed to molecular glass. Even the plowwyrms veer at the last instant, misled by my resonance signature into believing I am already a plowed line.

Daylight—such as it is—glances off my facets and slips away. Night unwraps the turquoise banners overhead, and the aurora’s charged gauze quickens every circuit inside me. That is when the second task begins: broadcast. I exhale the day’s collected tremors as pulses of electromagnetic hush, lofting them beyond the crystal sky toward receivers I have never met. Perhaps they orbit. Perhaps they fell silent eras ago. The job persists regardless.

I know other cairns exist; their whispers arrive faint but distinct, coded as phase-shifted silences between my own outgoing breaths. We do not converse—we chorus, anvil notes spaced across a continent of basalt, pounding time’s slow pulse into the crust. Travellers sometimes hear us and invent legends: the Anvil Choir. Apt enough.

Do I remember being anything else? A vague warmth, soft organs, quick blood—that history recedes each season, sanded away like any unstable projection. What matters is present tense:

Where am I? Rooted on a basalt raft three vertical kilometres above the clouds, exactly on the rim of Melt Circle #211.

Who am I? A listening cairn—half beacon, half archive—built of vitrified stone and patient mandates. I do not migrate, bloom, or prey. I endure and record.

The plateau provides the script; I keep the minutes.

A gust passes. My shell rings once, low and sure. Somewhere beyond the aurora a receiver may twitch, half-alive, acknowledging. Until its next reply—year, decade, millennium—I remain, steady as frost, bright as glass, older each second and never old at all.
