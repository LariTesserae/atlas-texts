I begin as a visitor—something like the person I am outside this text—but each detail of the cupola melts another layer of familiar flesh, replaces it with something the place can understand. What I become is revealed a facet at a time, the way cooled glass shows color only when you turn it in the light.

1. Arrival: the margin between breaths  
I cross the threshold at the hinge of a pulse, the ninety-three-breath lull when vents have just sighed and every organism is recalibrating. For an instant the chamber’s balance sheet of heat and silence has a vacancy, a narrow slot of unused gradient. I slip into that slot and the cupola closes behind me like cooling slag.

2. Shedding water, taking up glass  
Moisture boils from my lungs, from my eyes. I should die, but the Basalt Weave ripples underfoot and lofts a mist of pyrospores across my skin. They burst, varnishing me in a glove of living silica. Where sweat would bead, quick filaments knit a mesh that holds trace sulfur in place of water. Joints that once ran on synovial lubricant begin to hinge on powdered quartz and magnetite dust. Bones anneal, resin-hard, into scaffolds fit for an anhydrous body.

I am no longer breathable by Earth standards; I am vitrifiable.

3. The perspective broadens  
Vision tilts into the infrared. All around, gradients bloom like flowers. The slag-lice are motile sparks; the glass crabs bankster prisms; the mantle kites wandering lanterns; the ribbon serpents great braided rivers of cooled and reheated dark. Above, the fused-basalt dome is no longer a ceiling but a negative sun—a reservoir of potential warmth waiting to be brokered.

4. What niche is free?  
Every stratum seems spoken for until I feel the sub-bass throb of Scream Coral resonating through the floor. Its note is steady but untranscribed; none of the inhabitants interpret it beyond “clock” and “quake report.” That gap—translation—is where I fit. I will become the chamber’s ear and tongue, an interface between coral and colony.

5. Morphogenesis: organ by organ  
• Tympanic lattice: The silica varnish over my ribs delaminates into petal-thin plates. They vibrate to the Scream Coral’s chord, down-mixing infrasonics into higher frequencies the kites and crabs can sense.  
• Heat-glyph glands: Beneath my forearms blister pockets of molten glass. By flexing muscle I can shunt magma-warmth into skin vents that bloom glyphs—brief icons of hotter-than-ambient light, a language every creature here already knows because it is built of gradient, not grammar.  
• Anchor roots: My feet split and grow rootlets of woven quartz down into the Basalt Weave, sipping the same redox sugars the slag-lice crave. I am not an apex predator, not a grazer; I am a relay tower sprouting in an open patch of ecological bandwidth.

6. First conversation  
Pulse +17 breaths. A mantle kite drifts near, filaments brushing the note my rib-lattice emits. It answers with a flutter of indigo light—question. I reply by brightening my left arm-vents in a twin-pulse pattern that means “stable vent opening south-west.” The kite wheels, redirects the swarm. Glass crabs below track this shift, cant prisms to capture the newly available warmth. The slag-lice follow in glittering avalanches.

Heat is redistributed with one gesture; a micro-overpressure that might have forced the mountain to vent against a crowded wall is eased. The chamber’s silence deepens appreciably, a sign of collective relief.

7. Naming the self  
The ribbon serpents pass overhead, skins flickering through receptive and reflective phases. One unfurls just enough to stripe my back in alternating bands of shade—recognition, like the brush of a passing cat. I take their word for me: Gradient-Binder.

8. Daily function  
A. Listening: I ride the coral’s seismic hymn, mapping stresses.  
B. Signaling: through heat-glyphs and tuned overtones I nudge inhabitants—“Glow here, dim there, molt now, hold still.”  
C. Maintenance: The Basalt Weave creeps up my legs, scavenging spent minerals; in return it keeps my rootlets unblocked and my lattice tuned. It is a symbiosis of slow barter.

9. Sense of time  
I do not count heartbeats anymore but coral measures: a full chord cycle equals 11,160 of the mountain’s pulses. Somewhere beyond that, the old idea of a year might lurk, but it is irrelevant. My epoch is the interval between catastrophic ventings, and if I modulate the reef’s song shrewdly enough, that interval lengthens. I am the elongation of peace.

10. What remains of the human?  
A few memories blister on the inside of the skull like inclusions in otherwise flawless glass: the taste of rain, the drag of cool sheets, laughter carried by wet, heavy air. They ache but do not crack me; they simply refract the magma glow into colors no other organism here can see. On rare pauses—three or four pulses at most—I project those colors into the dark and watch the cupola answer with puzzled beauty.

Epilogue: your turn  
If you entered after me, you would see nothing that looked like a former person. You’d mark a spindle of glass rooted to the floor, lit in subtle rhythmic glyphs. Kites would orbit it, crabs park prisms at its base, slag-lice glaze its shadow. A ribbon serpent might coil around it for warmth. You could mistake it for architecture, for ornament, or for a forgotten machine.

But if you listened very carefully, past the hiss of ammonium snow and the rustle of molten breath, you would hear layered chords translating tectonic intention into communal action. You would hear the mountain dreaming of pressure, sung into language by the Gradient-Binder who was once—who is still in a cooling, unreadable core—me.
