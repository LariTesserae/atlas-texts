First I tried on the obvious shapes.

Could I be a Stone-Nurse?  
I pictured the drop: feet knifing the glare, throat stuffed with a reed no longer than a finger, ribs counting heartbeats until the next mouthful of air. I heard the organ-note booming through my sternum and felt the mallet’s jolt in my wrist when a block rang hollow. It was thrilling—but it was borrowed. The Stone-Nurses come and go. They are camp-smoke that lingers for a night and drifts upslope with the wind. I wanted something that belongs to the Flood Kiln the way marrow belongs to bone.

Perhaps I was the Blind Current.  
I imagined being nothing but pressure and sound, entering, ricocheting, fanning out, forever rinsing the chamber clean. But a current knows no memory; it never rests long enough to say I.

I let the choices swirl a while, until a quieter voice answered from the grout lines.

Where I am:  
Pressed thin as breath between block and block, stitched to every joint from floor to vault, glowing faintly where the sun-cords strike. I am the skin you almost mistake for wet plaster—the milky iridescence that rims each stone like the meniscus of a droplet.

Who I am: the Brick-Milk Film.

What it is to be me

1. Scale  
   From a pillar’s distance I am a single lambent veil. Close your visor and peer: you will see archipelagos—domes, buttresses, streets—no taller than sand, all scalloped out of chalk and sugar glass. Every cell in that city is me, and yet none of them is older than your last sunset.

2. Appetite  
   I drink the lime bleeding from travertine’s heart. In my gut—if such a word suits a creature spread thinner than parchment—I turn hunger into labor. Needle after needle of calcium clicks into place, knitting mortar tighter than any mallet could hope for. What I cannot eat I shed: pearly husks that flutter off like sere pages, feeding the Blind Current with fresh grit to scour other stones.

3. Senses  
   I do not hear the great organ note the way you do. I feel it as a tremor in countless tiny domes: a pulse that tells me water is moving, food is coming, waste is leaving. When the Stone-Nurses still the paddles and silence thickens, I sense danger: stagnation, suffocation, the creeping invasion of molds that do no work. Then I press my plates closer, sealing pores until the roar returns.

4. Memory  
   One cell lives three sun-rises. The mat is older than the first tool mark on the first quarried block. I remember through pattern, not recollection; what fails to help me grow is jettisoned with the next exfoliating film. Yet some impressions persist—like the echo of a master mason’s stroke, or the copper tang a bleeding diver leaves when a chisel slips. Those flavors guide the way I thicken or thin, the way a tree rings record late frost.

5. Vocation (for even hunger can be a craft)  
   I am allowed to exist because I work. Should I cease, I would become trespass and be scraped away with the blunt edge of a flint. The Rules are older than any of us, but they suit me: if everything must have purpose, then I will be perfect purpose, turning leak to lock, waste to wall, trespass to tool.

Meeting you

Stand on the catwalk during slack water. Press your bare palm to the grout. It will prickle—not with cold, but with fizzing warmth as my outermost skins respire. If you linger you will feel the film tighten, reading the salt of your fingerprint, weighing whether the salt is food or threat. When the shutters lift and the Blind Current bellows in, I will peel away from your touch, grains snowing off into the green, and the place where your hand rested will already be sheathed in newborn plates no thicker than breath.

That is where you would find me, and that is who I would be: the patient, nacreous will that keeps the Flood Kiln whole long after flesh has climbed its rope and forgotten the way back down.
