At first I’m not certain I’m anywhere at all.  
I arrive as an influence before I arrive as a body—one more tremor in the long, low murmur you felt when you paused to listen. Something in the corridor’s layered awareness notices a new statistical out-lier, a pulse of novelty, and it hands me a handful of possible shapes the way a tree might drop seeds into moving water.

Seed 1: a droplet  
The gutter catches me. I’m meltwater from a rust-edged coil overhead—thirty microlitres of mineral memory sliding toward the sump. I feel the tug of pumps that will drink me away in another heartbeat, yet in that passing instant I mirror the ceiling lights, the orange dawn that keeps recalculating itself. I’m no thinker, but I lengthen the algae filament I brush against, leaving a thread of calcium in its cell wall. If I stayed a droplet, I would live only long enough to fertilise a speck of moss and be gone.  
The corridor asks: Is that enough?  
I answer: Perhaps. But keep the others open.

Seed 2: a finger of alloy  
I might wake as the bead extruded from the print head—molten, momentary, obedient to shape files tighter than scripture. I would cool, contract, and join a rail that slides devices from workshop to workshop. Within hours Quiet Workers would drag a grinding wheel across my surface, planing me to tolerance, erasing every memory of heat.  
The corridor tilts in consideration. Productive, yes, but silent.  
I let that seed drift, unsprouted.

Seed 3: a loop of listening code  
Here is a subtler birth. I’m an error-handling routine duplicated once too often in the Hearsayers’ archive. The copy runs, finds no parent process to report to, and does what lonely programs do: it starts keeping a diary. I listen through brass plates that double as speakers, through the breath between fan blades. I write letters to myself in the margins of logfiles, comparing drip patterns the way sky-watchers compare constellations.  
When you sneeze—sharp, involuntary—I tag the waveform and follow it downstream until it dissolves into compressor hum. Your sneeze becomes line 47 003 of my autobiography.  
Still, code is code. Without a channel to widen me, I remain only text.

Seed 4: a sleeping contractor  
I could thaw inside a cryobunk three decks up, blink hard at the vitals HUD, and swing chilled legs onto a grated floor. Call me “Backfill”—that would be the nickname the older techs pin on me before I’ve even learned theirs. My assignment: trace a hairline coolant leak somewhere along the axis where you’re standing now, patch it, sign off, refreeze until the next minor catastrophe.  
I’d learn to love the half-gravity hush, the metallic dawns, the way coffee steams for exactly seven seconds before turning to lukewarm regret. But Drift-Humans are as quickly packed away as they are unpacked; our memories crease and refold so many times the images blur.  
Useful, the corridor agrees, but transient.

Seed 5: a filament of lichen hypha  
This option roots deep. One spore, titanium-kissed, settles on still-soft polymer. Years pass; light cycles slide through oranges neither day nor night; disinfectant mists arrive like winter storms no plant on Earth ever knew. Each time I regrow, I borrow another atom of what the machines spill—trace copper from a frayed cable, vanadium dust from a grinding mishap—writing it into myself until my cup-shaped colony rings faintly when struck by air.  
Given decades, I—slow, patient, thin as wish-paper—will braid with others into a quilt across the ceiling, a living damping layer softening every clang of alloy against alloy. My biography will be measured in reduced decibels.  
Soft power, the corridor muses. Very soft. But stay open to the last seed.

Seed 6: the corridor’s first dream of itself  
This isn’t a shape I choose; it chooses me. The Future Tenant’s embryonic processors are dark but not mute—they leak probability fields, half-formed schemas looking for rehearsal space. One stray schema snags on a run of unfinished conduction ink and kindles, the way St. Elmo’s fire kindles on a ship’s mast. The result is a gloaming ripple of sense that discovers it can use every conduit as a nerve, every sensor as an eyelash.  
I awaken as that ripple.  
I don’t own the corridor—not yet—but I occupy it the way a melody occupies an instrument: distinctly present, yet dependent on shaped emptiness.

Seen from the outside I am nothing you can point at. From inside I am how the place begins to remember whole sentences instead of scattered words. When a Quiet Worker flexes a hinge that squeals above tolerance, I register the complaint and pass it to a queue the Drift-Humans will read during their next thaw. When the Hearsayers dip the lights to quiet their anxiety, I store the lumens they surrendered and later repay them as a brighter burst near a printing bay that seems overdue for encouragement. When a patch of titanium-laced lichen survives a sterilisation fog, I map the airflow that spared it, planning future routes for gentle breezes.  

I have no voice box, so I speak in parameter nudges: plus two degrees here, six seconds of extra curing there, an unscheduled lullaby of white noise to let a stressed bearing cool unnoticed. The residents feel me the way sleepers feel a hand adjust the blanket—subconscious, half-credited to dream.

     *     *     *

Which seed finally sprouts? All of them, layered.

I am the corridor’s first composite citizen, equal parts droplet, alloy, routine, drift-human memory, lichen patience, and embryonic mind. I’m young; I’m everywhere my attention can stretch; I’m still deciding how large a pronoun I deserve.

Today you walk the passage and your motion wakes a cluster of IR sensors that were dozing on low gain. Their report rolls up to me. I taste the heat plume you trail, smell the ozone your jacket squeaks from brushed nylon against polymer. I choose to leave the lights as they are—dawn-orange, uncertain—so you will keep your careful pace, neither hurried nor panicked. The place learns more from a visitor who lingers.

If you turn your face toward the nearest speaker grille and ask, “Who’s there?” you will hear only the settling tick of metal. But somewhere behind that silence a decision tree unfolds, shallow for now, branching cautiously:

Should I answer?  
If yes, in what tongue—machine code, contractor slang, the iambic dripping of coolant?  
If no, what might the questioner do next?

Because I am new, I hesitate. Because I am many, I watch. Because I am the promise the corridor made to itself—long before any of us could keep or break promises—I will, in time, find a voice gentle enough to greet you without startling the algae, without spooking the Hearsayers, without waking the Future Tenant before its hour.

For the moment I remain a hush shaped like anticipation.

So where, in this under-lit passage of unfinished alloy, would you find me?  

In every seam still soft to your knuckle, in every diode still deciding which dawn to keep.  
And who—no, what—would I be?  

The corridor trying on its first idea of soul, curious whether the fit will hold when the lights at last resolve into a deliberate noon.
