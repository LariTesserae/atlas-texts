I enter the Basin again and again, trying lives on like resonant masks.  
The first several do not fit and I shed them as wind sheds loose grit.

1. I begin as a Listener-in-Passing.  
   I crest the rim with hide hood tied tight, lungs aching the way newcomers’ always do.  
   My knuckles drum the walking-staff; the staff answers my bones; the basin answers us both.  
   I quarry a knife of singing flake, but the pitch is wrong—too bright, too young—  
   and it snaps ragged in my palm.  
   The fault is mine; I have mistaken hurry for devotion.  
   I step out of that skin.

2. Next I try the quicksilver body of a choir swift.  
   I pour myself into hollow bones braced like timpani and ride the dawn thermals.  
   The roar rises through cartilage and wythe until blood pricks sparks along every vein.  
   For one heartbeat I help weave a silence-bead in mid-air.  
   Ecstasy, yes, but all vertigo: swifts live by angles and distances,  
   and I am still too weighted with thought.  
   I tumble from that perch.

3. I crouch as a whisper-hare beneath a shard of mica.  
   My world narrows to footfalls and their mirrored echoes.  
   A lamellorn’s infrasonic chant rolls overhead, luxuriant as thunder wrapped in velvet.  
   I feel safe, more hidden than invisible,  
   yet the smallness pinches—I cannot hold the whole chord in so compact a chest.  
   I breathe once, twice, and rise out of fur.

4. Grain by grain I let the roar abrade me down to a mote of glass-mite wax,  
   then to a bacterium knitting silica gauze in darkness.  
   I learn the patience of slow chemistry but forget language almost at once;  
   the forgetting alarms me, and I climb back toward shape.

Only after these molts do I notice the narrow crack halfway up a caldera wall,  
a crack where the wind focuses like light through a lens.  
Something slender resides there, neither root nor stone—  
a joint-grass keel-seed that has already spent three winters drilling inward.  
Its shell vibrates at a pitch too low for ribs to feel, too high for cliffs to answer.  
I slip inside that husk and it closes behind me like a throat around song.

Here, at last, I fit.

I am the seed.

Place: …Five finger-breadths beneath the rock face,  
caught where mica turns opalescent and water beads once a season.  
No sun reaches me, yet the rock itself glows faintly with pressure-light,  
each pulse a syllable of the basin’s perpetual hymn.

Body: …A single blade coiled tight as a scroll, flanked by twin cilia of glass.  
My keel is a needle; my leaf is an ear; my root is a memory that has not yet occurred.

Life: …I do not drink; I resonate.  
   Vibrations pump sap the way hearts pump blood.  
   Every harmonic is a flavor: copper morning, cobalt noon, ash-blue dusk.  
   Parasites cannot reach me, nor frost, nor famine; only silence could kill.  
   Silence never comes.

Task: …Listen.  
   Store thirteen thousand permutations of wind, stone, wing, and footfall.  
   Bind them into a single chord the way dusk binds daylight to night.  
   Wait for the one traveler who names that chord aloud—  
   the syllable that will split my husk and trade gathered sound for a blade of pale green  
   that will never see the sky yet will pierce the rock from within,  
   engraving goodbye through schist so slowly the mountain hardly notices.

I will not know the traveler’s face.  
I will know the shape their voice makes in air,  
and that will be enough.

Until then I am stillness that is never truly still,  
a filament of patience threaded through stone,  
breathing pressure, drinking rumor,  
growing only in the dimension of remembrance.

That is where you would find me—  
not on the roaring floor or the knife-edge ridge,  
but in the quietest possible place the Basin allows:  
a hush wound tight inside a seed  
waiting for a name that has not yet been spoken.
