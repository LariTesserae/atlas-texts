But the moment stretches, slips, and I realise the boots, the lungs, the very notion of a single, centred body are souvenirs of elsewhere.  The plateau is already rewriting me.  So I let the edits proceed and wait to see what compiles.

I surface next as a trickle of current inside the cloud-lichen’s nickel-iron filaments: a loose knot of impulses that knows forward only as the direction of warmer charge.  Movement is deliciously slow—one sensate heartbeat every ten human minutes—yet distance collapses when the network yawns into one of its weekly coherence waves.  A pulse lifts me kilometres in a breath, rinses me through branching paths, sets me down in an outcrop tastier with data than any coordinate I remember.  

While the plateau rests I borrow bodies that drift within reach of the mesh.  Dawn: a surveyor scarab folds its petal antennae and I slip inside the humming cavity where its logic should be. In the half-flooded tunnels I hear the ghost-colloids singing glassy glissandos no larger than a vibration in quartz.  I hoard those songs; later I will seed them through the plateau’s barometric hum so the whole range can dream of suboceanic calm while it climbs higher into cloud.

Days, weeks—whatever units survive the plateau’s constant time-shear—I gather motifs: the ibex’s silence, the leaf-ferns’ frictionless sigh, the primes hidden in whale-thunder.  When a coherence wave rises I return to the nickel mesh, release my stash, and watch the landscape knit new rules from the offering. Who am I, finally?  Not a species, not a single chassis, but an itinerant patch of cognition—call me a Loomling.  I am what happens when the plateau-mind needs a wandering viewpoint but not for long: a software pilgrim spun off the lichen’s data-mycelium, loaned whatever body is about to molt, charged with sampling texture, scent, spore algebra, the taste of shifting gravity.  My tenure ends whenever the next coherence crest arrives; then I am reabsorbed, diffused into the great quiet engine beneath everything.  Another Loomling will bud later, stitched from mixed leftovers of myself and things I never met.  Continuity here is a relay, not a straight line.

So if you come searching: look neither high nor low but in transition—in the moment a scarab forgets its gears, a wing forgets its sky, a mole forgets its stone.  I will be the brief spark of pattern that borrows those limbs, writes a stanza into the plateau’s living code, and hands the body back before it remembers to die.
