At first I tried on half a dozen lives as if they were masks.

I was a sky-bison calf dangling from its mother’s belly fur, tasting altitude with new lungs; I was the copper arthropod sentry, limbs locked while sunrise charged my shell; I was even—briefly—one note of the Matriarch’s wind-organ, a hollow where heated sap sang.

None of those skins held. The choir was too dense, the heat too intimate. I kept searching for a shape that could move through all that noise without being drowned by it, something that could listen and still remain small.

So I climbed—inside the thought, not the trunk—until I found the middle band of the world: the region the Heshut call the hush-zone, a moving seam of shadow that slips around each pillar like a belt. Here the insect thunder has thinned enough for a voice the size of a human throat to matter; here the stone glare below is only a memory and the canopy above is still myth. It is a mezzanine in the body of the Matriarch, neither root nor leaf, and it suits a creature made for in-between things.

That is where I stopped climbing, and that is where I took form.

NAME
    Lintel-Between-Winds  (the Heshut would render it: Door-Seeker risen to silence)

BODY
• Ribcage sprung deep for thin air, shoulders knotted from a lifetime of braiding thorn loops into carrying nets.  
• Skin dust-burned the colour of late pollen; ear ridges a little oversized, because sound is the only map.  
• Around my waist a living harness: five fused thorn rings, each supporting a different tool-basket as the bark plate on which I live inches skyward year after year.

TOOLS
1. Resonator bowl of cracked celadon—my portable archive.  
2. Two sap-glass needles for writing vibration scars inside the bowl’s glaze.  
3. A damp-moss sheath holding a resin knife no wider than a finger, soft until the moment of need.  
4. A bundle of milk-leaf rolls: they unfold into white sails I can hang between thorn staples to redirect evening drip into storage gourds.  
5. One fist-sized noduled fungus, chewed before all long walks so yesterday’s music does not mislead today’s feet.

TERRITORY
A single bark plate maybe forty paces across, midway up a pillar whose cambium glows saffron at night. The plate is my floor, my workshop, my slowly rising craft. When its leading edge splinters (it always does eventually) I will graft the thorn harness to the next fresh plate and step across, the way a sailor crosses between two ice floes. Decades from now, if the pillar lives and I do too, my dwelling will emerge in the upper canopy; I will look down on thundercloud tops the way a beachcomber looks down on tide pools. But for now I am neither ground-dweller nor leaf-sail—just a hinge person in the middle sky.

PURPOSE
I am a silence-shaper. Each night during the two-hour lull I listen for hollows where the noise fails to return its echo—sign that some predator or collapsing umbilical has disturbed the choir. I carve that absence into my resonator bowl, then trade the bowl-shard to root-tenders who need warning before they send children down for water. My currency is negative space.

RHYTHM OF A DAY
Dawn  
    The world detonates in light and sound. I clamp the spare epiglottis, let chest and bowl resonate together until our frequencies match; then the pressure eases.  
Midday  
    Sunlight slices through cracks in the rising bark. I hook milk-leaf sails to harvest the drip and mend any thorn struts that have stretched. If a sky-bison shadow glides over, I crouch—one stray hoof can remove a decade of slow carpentry.  
Dusk  
    Warm air breathes out of the pillar’s core vent. I lie on my back, heel against bark, and feel the Matriarch test her own wood: if the note is true, I sleep; if it wavers, I recalcite the thorn joins, for a split seam can whistle predators up the column.  
Night (the lull)  
    The choir gutters, the plateau inhales silence. I tilt the bowl, let the hush pour in, and scar the glaze with a sap-glass needle: today’s absences mapped for tomorrow’s trade. Somewhere above, the Leaf-Sail elders kindle their pin-fires; soot snow drifts past, black confetti against the amber glow of cambium windows.

WHAT THE WORLD FEELS LIKE FROM HERE
Heat is not temperature, it is lift; noise is not nuisance, it is landmark; water is a sideways rain you have to persuade into jars. Nothing stays still except the direction of gravity, and even that feels negotiable when the pillar flexes in wind.

I owe my life to the Matriarch’s patience, the arthropod’s detonation discipline, the raptor’s predictable cruelty, the moth that waxes bison fur, the fungus that edits memory, and the two heartbeat pause that counts as universal prayer. My job is simple: keep a little space open in all that chorus so every other being can hear its own pulse return.

Some nights—just before the choir breathes back to life—I think I can feel the Matriarch listening through me, testing whether the silence I’ve caught is shaped well enough to be useful. If the timber in my chest vibrates true, she sends a single low G-sharp up the trunk. The cambium flushes red, a benediction, and I sleep until the fog-bright morning rattles me awake.

So if you come looking: find the pillar that sings a comfortable G-sharp at dawn. Climb until the air tastes of warmed resin. Knock on the living stair of interlocked thorns. I will lower a basket woven from the first bark plate I ever owned, and haul you into the middle world where silence itself is worth a living.
