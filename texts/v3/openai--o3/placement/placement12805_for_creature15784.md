I would arrive in mid-sentence.

Not on a plaza or a recognizable “street,” but inside one of the city’s warm exhalations: a vertical shaft where the wall-polyps breathe spent water outward in a slow, sighing spiral.  The current lifts me the way wind lifts ash, and for a moment I hang, turning, ribs vibrating with the reef’s pulse-speech—half of a thought already spoken.

Who am I?  I am just now earning a name.  In Pelagoth reckoning I am Still-Unmirrored, a juvenile five years and three tides old, the size of a surface child’s scarf.  Until today I have lived pinned flat against a pumping corridor, mouth-crown folded, body folded, all attention turned inward to lessons the wall whispered through calcium and heat.  I know the reef’s electrical syntax the way land embryos know heartbeat rhythms.  What I do not know is open water.

The Grandmothers decide I am ready by letting go.

So here I rise—out of the shaft, into the city proper—feeling weightless and overexposed, as though every creature in every alley is staring straight through my translucent hide.  They probably are.  I have not yet earned a single mirror scale; in this valley of hoarded light that makes me naked as a peeled grape.

Where exactly?  Depth-wise I am perhaps one and a half strata below the rooftop fuzz of algae, above the pitch voids where lantern eels sew constellations into dark water.  Horizontally there is no compass word that bites; I am simply between—between two mushroom decks whose undersides breathe lilac silt, between the soft drumbeats of distant tower pumps, between a silver knife-school of juvenile barracuda arguing about whose turn it is to scout.

I taste the current through my ribbon arms.  Sarex dust: the crumble of an old buttress three courts upslope.  Shrimp moult: a lantern-herder somewhere trimming her flock.  And salt-metal newness—wreck iron perhaps, fallen from a storm gutter at the basin’s lip.  Even without mirrors I feel my hide attempting to shimmer at the scent of that brightness.  Greedy reflex.

A Seeder notices me.  He (she, they—pronouns shift like tide) flickers a greeting across scale-mosaic: six points of cinnamon, one of jade.  My wall lessons unravel the code—“Little Unmirrored, do you hunger?”—and my feeding crown opens by instinct.  I pulse an answer through the water: respectfully, yes.  The Seeder guides me beneath an overhang where fine detritus drifts like smoke.  I hover, revolving, letting my six jaws comb out plankton.  The taste is silt-sweet, mineral-rich, thick with the reef’s own saliva.  First honest meal I have taken on my own.

While I feed, the Seeder presses a fingertip scallop blade against his own flank and loosens a single tiny mirror scale—opal, no bigger than a grain of sand.  With a gesture asking silence he nudges it into my skin, just beneath the outer membrane.  Light throbs there, faint but undeniable: my First Mirror.

Tradition says I must now swim the Spiral of Names, but I am not ready to announce myself to twenty storeys of strangers.  Instead I tuck closer to a buttress and watch life pour past: a courier manta gliding low, freighted with elder Pelagoth draped like dark pennants along its spine; two Gleaners flicking their arms to herd a spill of pearl chips into woven kelp baskets; a trio of children (mirror-speckled already, overachievers) racing a silt storm, laughing in pressure-thumps that make my very bones flinch with envy.

And in the hush between those pulses, Stillness settles.  The city listens to itself dreaming.  I listen too, mirror beating like a newborn star beneath my skin.

Where am I?  In the living throat of an elder that will one day consume my body for mortar.  Who am I?  A first glimmer, a promise of brightness offered back to the dark.
