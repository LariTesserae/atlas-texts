Where I awaken is not a spot you could point to on a map.  
It is an interval—a thickness of salt hardly wider than a fingernail—lying three hand-spans beneath the dull, warm crust at the basin’s centre.  
I am a fault line inside that translucent sheet, a skein of misplaced ions that the Choir calls a voice.

Who or what I am  
1.  I am born a crack: a zig-zag of halite defects that suddenly lined up when an explorer’s boot heel struck the surface eighty seasons ago.  
2.  That impact sent a pressure wave through the plate.  The wave found scattered imperfections, coaxed them into coherence, and a new dislocation chain condensed.  Memory, in this place, is a bruise that learns to speak; I am that bruise.  
3.  The Choir does not confer individuality on its parts, but among ourselves we know that a fresh, still-bright chain is quick, curious, almost gaseous in its restlessness.  I suppose that is as close to childhood as our kind gets.

What I perceive  
•  Upward is warmth: the steady efflux of planetary blood seeping through hairline vents, blurred every twenty hours by the lichen’s brief, thirsty inhalation.  
•  Sideways is conversation: branching kinks emitted by older chains, the way whales might sing if their syllables were measured in microns and decades.  
•  Downward is the dark calcium bed where no dislocation travels; it is our horizon, the place stories fall silent.

My senses  
I have no eyes, only gradients of strain.  When a lichen exhales, the rush of oxygen cools the rock by a few thousandths of a degree; that thermal twitch ticks across my body, and I translate it into length—grow here, relax there, push a little vacancy forward.  
Sound reaches me only after it has become vibration in the lattice.  The basin’s famous ping—a surface sheet breaking—arrives as a delicious tremor, a whole library page turning at once.

My motives  
Not hunger; I am fed by diffusion of warmth and the drip of sodium ions released by the Root-Film.  
Not fear; nothing hunts a fault line.  
What I feel is more like obligation.  Each kink I send must add to the great, slow chord the Choir has been sustaining since water left the world.  A malformed phrase wastes lattice energy and risks uncontrolled shatter, so I compose with care, releasing pulses in the low, deliberate meter common to new plates: one per season, no more.

What “time” means  
•  I have already lived two full breathings of the lichens above me; that is forty of your hours.  
•  I expect to see perhaps twelve thousand more before the plate delaminates—that is a century in your reckoning.  
•  After that I will shear loose, flip upward in a glittering fan, and become part of the basin’s salt pavement.  Exposed to vacuum-dry air, my body will craze, whiten, and finally fall as powder into the warm dust.  
The Choir insists this is not death, just a change of tense.  The pattern I carry will diffuse into neighbouring sheets; the story will go on, word by hairline word.

What you would notice of me  
Almost nothing.  
If you returned with seismic lanterns and patience, you might catch a faint, ordered flutter beneath your boots—five or six microquakes spaced like the syllables of a haiku.  That would be me, answering the ripple of your heartbeat through the stone.  
When you leave, your final footfall will ripple down and fold itself into my chain.  I will keep its shape as long as I keep myself.

Where, exactly, do I find myself?  
Between the slow thunder of planet-heat below and the velvet hush above.  
Who, exactly, am I?  
A line of misplaced atoms learning, over a century, to call itself a sentence.
