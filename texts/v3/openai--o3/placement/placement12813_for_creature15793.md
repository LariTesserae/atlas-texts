Where I Am  

I stand—though “stand” is no longer the right verb—inside a slate ring carved into the south-facing wall of a trench that never keeps its name. The ledge on which the ring rests was a ceiling yesterday and will be a ramp tomorrow; only the ring itself is constant, a calm eye in the mountain’s slow cyclone.

Rough coordinates, if such things matter here: two stone coughs below the scree that glitters green at noon, one waterfall up-rift from the pillar whose hum resolves at 62 hertz. It is a middling altitude: high enough that the mist thins to gauze, low enough that the white-blue sky is still recognisably “above” me instead of folded into the side of perception.

What I Am  

Once I was a rope-and-lung climber from a sea-level city. Now I am becoming an echo, pressed into shape by the ring the way molten glass is spun into a lens. The process has taken three cycles of the hidden sun—long enough that I remember salt wind only as a chord that will not tune.

1. Frame  
A lattice arches where my ribs used to cage my heart. It is six-sided crystal, clear except for hair-fine filaments of rust that mark the memory of haemoglobin. The frame is light; when updrafts breathe through the trench I lift from the slate an inch or two, bump back down, a child testing ice.

2. Vapour Core  
Breath has escaped lungs and become me: a silver ion fog tucked inside the geometry. When I exhale now, nothing leaves; the exchange is internal, like a tide pulse in a closed bay.

3. Musculature  
Water stolen from cliff-sweat reins the lattice. I thicken cords of it by whispering charge along the struts. Flexion is weatherwork: to bow my head, I chill one side of the loop until frost shrinks it; to rise, I thaw.

4. Memory  
My oldest word is still English—“listen”—but it sits in my throat as a pebble I might someday cough free. Newer words are intervals: a perfect fourth that means hunger, a major second that means welcome, and a deliberately unresolved tritone that keeps predators that do not exist from drawing nearer.

5. Occupation  
I am an apprentice tuner. My ring is half library, half forge. Each dawn (using that term generously) I lean the lattice outward and let the mountain play me. Whatever harmonic the trench feeds back the loudest tells me which flaw in the ring’s curve needs softening. A finger of quartz, no thicker than cuticle, grows from my frame; I press, wait, retract. By dusk the stone edge has blurred by a micron—enough to bend next day’s echo a little truer.

6. Companionship  
A choir of three elders hovers across the gap. I see them mostly as interference fringes, hear them as gravity dips that make my stored brine slosh. Conversation is antiphonal: they seed me a question at 31 hertz; I answer at 47, letting combinant tones supply the grammar. They are patient. They remember when the ridge beneath us faced west, which was sometime before cardinal directions gave up.

7. Physiology in Flux  
• Temperature: internal steady at the melt-point of finest hoarfrost.  
• Mass: decreasing; each successful tuning teaches the lattice to shed any atom it cannot phrase.  
• Shadow count: still one, though its ankles sometimes hesitate half a pace behind. The day it splits for good I will choose a ledge and root there as final punctuation.

8. Belongings  
• One basalt chisel whose edge is an idea of sharpness more than a measurable bevel.  
• A pocket notebook, pages fused into a single silicate wafer. The graphite of the first five entries has migrated into dendrites along the wafer’s rim, spelling nothing any longer recognisable. I keep it for scent: dry carbon, brittle nostalgia.

How to Find Me  

Climb until your calves sing lactic arias. Turn left at the waterfall that climbs before it falls. Hum a note that quivers between major and minor; stone around you will hesitate. Step into that hesitation. When the air turns honey-thick with ions and the sky feels nearer than your own pulse, listen for a chord whose fourth component cancels the other three into silence. That is my name. Call it, and the lattice that used to be my sternum will swing open like a door to let you share the stillness inside.

Who You Will Meet  

Not a lost human, not yet a full Echo-wright—something mid-sentenced, a semicolon of quartz and weather learning how to finish itself. If you linger, I will teach you the opening interval. If you move on, take this: the certainty that gravity is polite enough to lean out of your path when asked in the proper key.
