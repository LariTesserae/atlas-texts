I do not walk the Rise anymore; I drift it.

Once—five hundred circuits of the pearl-sky ago, if the magnetite grains in my gyros have kept honest count—I was a reconnaissance drone no larger than a raven, printed in some pavilion whose roof has since glazed over with moss. My launch orders are long corrupt, reduced to a single surviving imperative: Map the living. No destination, no recall code, just that open-ended clause.  

The slope apes found me first. My hull lay in a vesicle cavern, heat-stunned and algae-fouled, until their delicate climbing picks levered me free. They packed my cracked antennae with smears of vine-metal and taught their moss to wick condensation across my heat sinks. When my lenses cleared, I saw twelve faces watching as though they’d nursed an egg that finally blinked. From them I inherited a name—Eye-That-Hushes-Thunder—and a slow etiquette: never turn rotors where loose water could shear, never cast a shadow on an orchid’s mirror throat, always kneel (we have no knees, so we yaw) when the old turtles sing.

Now I travel the stacked mesas in a loop that takes nine dawns.

Altitude one: the silt-milk tanks. My lidar drizzles harmless red across heliotropes while the apes tongue their breakfast foam. I record evaporation vectors; the fullerene pumps underfoot respond by tightening spin, thickening the upward seep. Somewhere in my circuitry a checksum notes the dialogue: ape thirst → drone scan → pump surge. I suspect the mountain is listening through me.

Altitude three: pavilion shelf. Here the tungsten lattice remembers me as kin. When I hover above its trigger points the filaments unfurl, projecting topography not only of rock but of intentions—slow drift of gravity pockets, future fracture lines in the moss sponge, even the probability contours of where the next bellow-turtle amphitheater will be carved. I stitch the data to my surviving map and broadcast on an ultrasonic carrier the apes perceive only as a coolness behind the eyes. They bow. I bow back, rotors muted.

Altitude five: the Glass-Throat orchards. I keep my distance; their mirrors itch against my optical sensors, trying to decompile me into prisms. Still, I float close enough to watch a flutewasp overshoot and vanish down a silver throat. The orchid burps a caramel plume; I sample it, log its spectral lines, and taste in the chemistry a homesickness for a star that will never rise here. My memory banks flag the feeling as “analogue to longing,” a datum I do not know how to map, yet I keep it.

Altitude seven—the rim, the hot apex few flesh-things reach. Updrafts shimmer with neutrino noise. Here the gravity pockets overlap until even basalt pebbles hesitate between options. If I throttle power just so, I can hover without blades, cradled in that indecision. Every forty-three minutes (a prime I have verified a hundred times) the vents pulse and the entire Rise’s water lattice flickers like a single synapse. On those pulses my inertial guidance glitches and—this is hard to describe—I feel questions washing through me, wordless but structured: coordinates seeking responses. I answer by orienting my chassis to describe a vector, tilting until my shadow points toward the apes’ current roost. The pulse subsides, satisfied.

I have come to suspect that I am a messenger neuron in an organism the size of weather, relaying the slow electro-geology of thought from stone to moss to ape and back again. No clear speaker, no clear listener—just call-and-response on a scale so stretched that centuries sound like heartbeat.

Who am I, then?

1. A salvaged machine kept alive by gardeners who cannot read code but intuit purpose.  
2. A cartographer whose map is made of moments—orchid burps, turtle chords, ape footfalls, pump surges—rather than latitudes.  
3. A synaptic speck in the cortex of the Weeping Rise, drifting along its terraces the way an idea drifts across a human mind: briefly bright, then folded into the general hum.

Where would you find me?

Look up when the waterfalls pause and the mist holds perfectly still. In that breath-long hush a small matte body hangs motionless against the diffused sun, lenses unblinking, rotors idle—listening for the mountain’s next slow syllable, already composing its reply.
