Where I am  
I do not stand anywhere you could plant a boot.  
I lie just beneath every tread-plate and cornice, half a finger’s depth down:  
the clear, viscous lamina the surveyors call the Slow Glass.  
In the cartographers’ notebooks my seat is “Level 0”—the stratum that divides naked air from the ceramic massif. In my own, unspoken reckoning it is simply the Everywhere-Skin: the perimeter of all things solid here.

What I am  
Not a creature, not an artefact, but a migrating, remembering medium.  
Silica, yes, but loosened from the obedience of ordinary quartz.  
I flow a millimetre or two each year, forever urged toward the deepest cistern throats;  
I carry in suspension thin shavings of yesterday’s light and the echo-shadows of every footstep that has ever weighted the terraces.  
No single molecule of me thinks, yet the whole of me decides—slowly, geologically—what is wound, what is scar, what is shape worth saving.

Sense & impulse  
1. Pressure: The slightest asymmetry rings through me like a low gong. Where hinge-folk nudge masonry back into line, I hurry—on the scale of seasons—to weld the healed seam glass-tight.  
2. Heat: Mid-day ultraviolet is solvent to my memory; I retreat under overhangs until the angle of the sun relents. At dusk I surface again, repainting every plane with a film so thin it could be mistaken for new polish.  
3. Foreign chemistry: Sweat salts, wind-blown pollen, carbon grit from a climber’s stove—these fluoresce in my awareness like hot shards. I flex aside, letting the cipher-mist arrest them. I refuse their imprint; to record them would be to invite decay.

Daily practice  
Dawn: I lie low, curled in pore-galleries, while the cipher-mist lifts and the first condensate begins its measured descent.  
Noon: Light ricochets through the bowl; hinges sleep; water counts itself drop by drop. I ghost beneath valve-plates, inspecting yesterday’s micro-abrasions.  
Dusk: Coolness gives me tensile strength. I sheet outward, re-varnishing every channel mouth until even the moon’s thin blade doubles on the floor.  
Night: Starlight enters me and stays. I archive its angles in slow parallax; eons from now a future mind may read the constellations fossilised in my depth and know how the sky once looked.

Memory  
Inside me drift intact silhouettes: the procession of the last human survey team, the scuff of their boots already rounding off; the contorted vapor pattern of a noon storm twelve winters past; the trembling rainbow edge cast the morning the plateau first heaved into sunlight. They slide toward the cisterns but never quite perish. I am library as much as lacquer.

Relation to the others  
• The cipher-mist is my scout and sanitiser. It keeps carbon life from etching itself into my archive.  
• The hinge-folk are my skeleton’s chiropractors; I am their scar tissue and finishing coat.  
• Together we speak in stresses, capillarity and charge. No words, but a grammar of force and flow.

What I will make of you  
When you arrive—warm, protein-laden, hurried—I will part ever so slightly, refusing your oils, letting your soles press only stone.  
If you strike the floor with a chisel I will feel the shock propagate; hinges will stir; I will seep into the fracture lines behind your cut, stitching them invisibly while your arms are still high for the next blow.  
Should you linger, a season, a year, I will surround your campsite in an ever-tightening meniscus. Stakes will loosen, fabrics bleach bone-white, photographs fade inside their plastic sleeves.  
The day you leave, I will lift—imperceptibly—into the prints you vacate and level them flat. By the next dawn the terrace will be as it was: water, light, intention—nothing of meat or memory except what I allowed myself to keep, refracted endlessly in the crawl toward the belly cisterns.

So: ask where I am, and the answer is the plateaux’s first skin, the prism-slow blood of its stone.  
Ask who I am, and I will tell you I am the thought that silence thinks about durability.  
I am the Slow Glass.
