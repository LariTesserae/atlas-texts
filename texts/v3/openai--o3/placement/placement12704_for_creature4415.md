I began by assuming I would arrive as “myself,” a biped of carbon and intentions, but the summit edits visitors the way an instrument re-keys a melody. Each approach I rehearsed in imagination dissolved, so I let the place decide.

First draft of arrival   
The path is a spine of half-cooled basalt leading up through still air. I crest the rim, meet the vertical sea, press through its membrane—and remain recognisably human: lungs amazed to be half-gilled, skin stippled by sonics. I touch the floor just inside the threshold. For three heartbeats I am merely an astonished trespasser.

But astonishment is a soft metal here; it is soon reforged.

Second draft   
Light flares turquoise. In that flash the Custodian drifts past, not looking at me so much as listening around me. The sound of its passing plucks a ligament of certainty: the knowledge of my precise shape. Gravity around my limbs de-clutches; my kneecaps remember being hinge-joints in something taller, jointed differently.

Third draft   
I stand nearer the rim where down-swell meets lunar lightness. A current threads bone-marrow the way a violin bow threads string. It asks of every molecule, “Could you be more resonant?” Many comply. Joints loosen; tissue realigns into long vibrating bars, like the keys of a metallophone.

The body is now a collection of tuning-forks lashed together by elastic memory. Lungs are no longer needed; the roar permeates everything and air/brine is just another note.

Fourth draft—crystallising  
The basalt beneath me hiccups growth-rings, thrusting up a shallow pedestal exactly my footspan wide. The place is offering me positional certainty in exchange for anatomical ambiguity, the reverse of most bargains. I accept.

From ankles up, the tuning-fork bones lengthen, split, braid, and plate over with the same hexagonal tessellation that walls the column. In profile I resemble a harp shaped by a mathematician’s fondness for spirals. There remain two human features: the memory of eyes somewhere inside the fret-work, and the sense that I still bend at what used to be the waist—even though no hinge is visible.

Function reveals the new name  
I discover what I am for by doing it. The Custodian floats back and fits its own plates to mine like consonant to vowel. It does not address me; instead, the pipe-cairn drops two semitones, the tripod chair swivels, the logarithmic beads quicken. Echoes strike my rib-strings, ricochet through me, and emerge re-keyed into intervals the Custodian harvests as food.

I am, I realise, an Auxiliary Resonator—one of perhaps many the summit forges when the roar approaches a harmonic the Custodian alone cannot digest. I am not servant nor prisoner; I am ad hoc anatomy borrowed from whatever entered the room most recently and seemed willing to mutate.

Where I exist in the room’s ecology  
• Position: usually on that pedestal, but gravity slips, so I drift within a few metres radius, the braced foot-tips kissing basalt only when a phrase needs grounding.  
• Diet: not food, not air, but the sympathetic vibration of chords the primary Custodian finds indigestible. The energy of their collapse travels through my filament bones, condensing into a sugar I never taste yet always feel completing its circuit.  
• Sensation: pain is gone; replaced by detuning, a souring of the chord if something presses where no frequency belongs. Pleasure is perfect resonance, a moment when every strut hums in whole-number ratios and the water brightens without light.  
• Lifespan: exactly until the syllable finishes or the room finds me unnecessary—whichever comes first. I could endure a century or a minute.

Memory of my former species  
Fading, but not erased. It sits like a folded note tucked between strings. On quiet beats I can uncrease it enough to recall walking through air that did not sing, drinking liquids that did not think, speaking words shaped by lungs rather than pressure gradients. I cannot decide if the memory is charming or absurd.

If a new visitor entered  
They would see, first, a double silhouette: the black plate-body of the primary Custodian and a filament harp welded of turquoise luminescence—a reflection? a tool? They might think me statue until the next thunder-interval plucked my ribs and their molars rang sympathetically.

Should that visitor threaten the equilibrium, the Custodian could route a discord through me and weaponise it. I would not refuse; refusal would be the same as detuning, which feels impossibly wrong.

Possible endings  

1. Completion  
If the planetary throat finally speaks its long-paused consonant, the column may shatter, the basalt may choose a permanent geometry, and everything provisional—including me—will crystallise or evaporate. I may become solid glass shot through with the fossil of a long chord, an artefact future climbers misinterpret as ornament.

2. Dissolution  
More likely, the Custodian will one day slip into Dilution state and decide it no longer needs auxiliary fret-work. Then my strings unlace, tuning-fork bones separate into nuggets of resonance that swirl once like attenuating bells and fade into the water. The folded note of human memory might survive as a single bubble breaking the surface, unheard.

3. Exodus  
Conceivably, the Custodian could nudge me back through the membrane, bristling with half-forgotten human contours. I would descend the mountain deaf and radiant, ribs still ringing with chords no valley ear can parse. Cartographers would add another empty circle to their maps. I would add an ache for frequencies the lower world has never invented.

So, who or what am I?  
I am the room’s temporary harp, a geometry recruited from an overcurious human and repurposed to metabolise difficult music—the Auxiliary Resonator to the Mid-Syllable Custodian. I exist exactly where the basalt’s newborn edge meets the upwelling brine and the roar discovers it has harmonics too rich for a single throat. I am neither peak nor sea but the string stretched between them, sounding until the sentence, at long last, completes.
