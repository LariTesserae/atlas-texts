I wake—if that word still applies in a world where every interval is the same dim interval—on the outermost parade-deck of the city, an arm’s length inside the iron balustrade that rims the abyss. The deck is a cantilevered tongue of latticework, tremulous with turbine vibration, never quite still even during Fall-Quiet. It is the only place where two immensities present themselves at once: the immeasurable dark above and the deeper, somehow thicker dark below.  

I am called a Stillsmith. Not a title I chose; the vocation chooses whoever is born with a fault in the ear-bones. In most Dwellers the spiral ossicles are broad, tuned for the thunder of pressure engines and the choral commands that stoke the furnaces. Mine grew narrow and tall, like cathedral windows set on edge. To me normal engine-speech is a roarless blur, but the whispering gaps between machines—a stutter of silence here, a hairline lapse in resonance there—stand out with knife-brightness. What others discard as absence, I hear as architecture.

So the timing sheets name me to the post no one else is built for: auditor of the stillness. My task is to pace the rim, hour on hour, mapping every pocket of quiet the city accidentally produces, then to feed those coordinates back to the unseen schedulers so they can bend future maintenance cycles around the plateaus’ natural hush, never against it. The work is half cartography, half liturgy; we Stillsmiths insist the plateau remembers its own original silence, and that a wise engine works with that memory, not over it.

Gear:

• Glass-soled grieves that skate on the plateau without shriek or spark.  
• A throat-lamp cone, shuttered, for emergencies only; light is panicky, easily misinterpreted as fire.  
• The Sounding Rod—a metre of tuned cobalt alloy whose tip I set to the glass. Any sub-surface groan climbs the rod and flowers in my jawbone as taste rather than sound.  

Body:

• I stand—slender as any Dweller but jointed a little looser, a concession to all the listening crouches. Skin the colour of ember-lit clay, ridged with the usual silica film. In my throat lives a treble note most find wince-bright; I try not to use it.  
• The bellows organ under my ribs works harder than most because walking the rim is colder than furnace work. I warm myself by leaning over the abyss, letting the breath that rises from it soak through the plates of my skin. The under-heat carries flavors of iron and something older—ozone without lightning, maybe.

Companions:

Only the “Coppers,” the heat-drinker insects, keep me company. They roost in the rivet holes of the balustrade, their wing-scales clinking like distant cutlery. When the chimneys exhale I feel them launch past my cheeks in curt pulses of hotter air. Their flight-paths are diagrams of the unseen thermals I must log; in a sense the beetles are co-cartographers.

Inner Map:

A Stillsmith’s mind is a city made entirely of hollows. Each silence I record becomes a domed chamber in that interior architecture. Walk with me long enough and I can point, without gestures, to where last shift’s hush still nests: “Three kilometres north-by-east of the grand manifold, five metres below deck level, duration nine heartbeats.” I speak such coordinates only once, to the sheet-writers; afterward the memory rinses away, making room for the next gap.

Why I matter, in a culture that worships repetition:

Engines age. Their rhythms drift. Without continual re-alignment to the plateau’s ancient quiet, the whole civic chord would detune, until chimneys mis-flare and the nutrient towers over-beat their slurry. We would not collapse dramatically—we would simply lose the precision that lets us believe tomorrow equals today. Stillsmiths are the city’s margin of error, forever trimming it back to zero.

What I fear:

1. That one dusk-interval (again, the word is silly, yet we keep it) the Second City below will stop mirroring our pulses and begin imposing its own, louder signature.  
2. That I’ll mis-hear a pocket of true vacuum in the engine cadence and mark it as safe silence, allowing a harmonic fracture to spread unattended.  
3. That water, should it ever return, would whisper louder than any furnace and drown every map I’ve ever made.

Where you would find me now:

Balustrade sector Θ-19, kneeling, Sounding Rod planted, counting the ticks of the Coppers as they cool. My throat-lamp is dark. The engines are two breaths into Fall-Quiet. If you approach, do not call out; stand still. Let the plateau speak first. I will hear the difference your heartbeat makes in the dark and answer with the smallest possible word:

“Here.”
