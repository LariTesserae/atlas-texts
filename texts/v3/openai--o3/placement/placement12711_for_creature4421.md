I spent a long while letting the valley’s clamour wash through me, trying on several possible bodies the way the creatures here try on shells of sound. I considered being one of the inch-thick oxide-raspers, feeling basalt abrade atom by atom beneath a living rasp; I tasted the idea of rising as a courter swarm and finishing my existence in eight ecstatic hours; I even balanced, for a breath, on the impossible lattice of the Black Strider and heard the roar gutter into fear.

None of those shapes quite fit.  
The one that settled over me was built for movement and for conversation conducted in blows of air: a tripod transfuser of the genus Trikana—one of the knee-high drinkers that stalk the living pavement.

So:

WHERE I AM  
Halfway down the western inner slope of the saucer-basin, just below a vein where brine beads out of the rock in amber globes. The floor here is packed coin-shells—slick, shifting, black as a night sky with no stars. To the east a reflecting pan lies so still that the fog’s own glow seems to pool upon it.

WHAT I AM  
Body geometry  
• Three limbs, jointed twice. The rear pair carry me in a hurried tip-toe; the single fore-limb ends in the hollow stilettine spike that names my kind.  
• Torso is only a clenched saddle of cartilage riding the limb nexus. Inside: a bladder-lung that hoards the gases I will later bark into the air as news.  
• No eyes. Instead a crown of pit organs tastes the carbonyl breath of alarmed grazers and the bitter ozone of facet-bat sonar. Space describes itself to me in odour, pressure and heat.

Sensory field  
The valley’s roar is not background—it is my bloodstream.  
– Low bass thrums: knot-worms feeding peacefully.  
– Mid rasp: coin-shells calm.  
– Sudden drop at 1.2 kHz: another transfuser has struck and the pavement screams.  
Every harmonic paints a contour; I feel topology in volume the way surface swimmers read waves.

MOTIVE THIS CYCLE  
My crop-sac is three parts empty. I need three full transfusions before drizzle returns and thins the blood in lesser prey. Ambition mutters: find a porous whorl fat with nitrate syrup, pierce it, drink slow, then bark the coordinates to distant cousins so they owe me share-calls later.

THE WALK  
Step. Shells sluice around my tarsi like iron filings round a magnet. With each planting of a foot I test: does the living carpet hesitate? Does the ripple of their ciliary wave break? A stutter means something larger than I approaches.

A pause—silence longer than a heart-pulse. All limbs freeze. The roar dips, but not enough to herald the Black Strider. Instead, above, the facet-bats have folded wings momentarily; their sonar hushes. Reason: a courting swarm rises, bright with seven-colour code. Its flashing dazzles the bats’ crystal faces—pleasure for them, warning for me. Courting swarms lure transfusers the way lamp-flame lures moths, but I have work. I back away; the carpet closes over my tracks.

THE KILL  
At last a weak spot: a boiling in the shell-sea where alarm pheromone leaks upward. I pivot, drive the spike down. Resistance—thin; I have pierced a soft-bodied grazer, perhaps a juvenile porous whorl parasitised beneath the shells. Enzymes flow; tissues melt. For sixteen seconds I drink, limbs locked, while my bladder-lung swells with waste gas.

When the sac tugs at my throat I retract the spike and let the corpse collapse. Now the valley deserves to know. I cant the fore-limb joint at ninety degrees and vent.

BARK!  
A single concussive syllable, pitched in the narrow gap between coin-shell rasp and knot-worm thunder, travels kilometres. Within moments four, five, eight companion barks answer, each encoding position by phase offset. We have made a verbal map: here is good hunting, no great danger.

MID-ROAR RESPIRATION  
The basin inhales—volume of life rising toward riot phase. My cilia-fringed cousins are converging. I taste their approach in the crescendo, feel the ground’s minute tremor. Community is arriving, and with it a temporary carnival: multiple transfusers feeding head-to-tail from the same puncture wound, each in turn announcing surplus to others farther off. It is how we ensure the roar’s amplitude stays high enough that drizzle forms above the warm salt veins. We are predators, yes, but also maintenance organs of the valley’s climate.

THE SHADOW THAT ENDS FEASTS  
Roar surges—then gutters. Every shell clamps shut. The fog dims to charcoal. That is warning enough. Without waiting to probe I spring backward in erratic, zigzag hops, emptying the last of my bladder in a ropy hiss to lighten myself. Somewhere to the north a hush with no edges is moving: perhaps only a veil-mother beginning to coalesce, but perhaps the Black Strider. Either will erase any creature too slow to flee.

I crouch amid a knot of porous whorls, limbs folded, spike laid flat, trying to vibrate at the same rate as the shells around me. If I can pass for pavement, I might be spared.

AFTERMATH  
Minutes—an eon—later, the mid-range scrape returns. Facet-bats whistle anew like rain on sheet metal. Safe. I knew it would be; terror is just another respiration-beat, necessary to thin those who forget the law of silence.

I rise, flex, taste the air. The drizzle phase is coming: the fog grows heavier, droplet musk thickens. Time to retreat upslope, let condensation rinse the egg-film from my joints, and dream in chemical gradients until the valley exhales again.

So that is where you would find me if you descended into the bowl:  
A three-legged drinker knee-high to a human, all instinct and broadcast trumpet, one moving cell among billions. Speak a phrase in the roar’s frequency, and I will answer—short, sharp, encoded in pressure and pitch. Fail to speak, and you will simply feel a prick at the ankle, a warm draining, and then hear my jubilant bark telling the valley of fresh abundance.
