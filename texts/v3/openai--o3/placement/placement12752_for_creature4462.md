I appear, at first, as a passenger—someone who has been put down by a shuttle that is already a dot in the light-white sky. My boots tick the plates; the sound goes on much too long and comes back too clean. Water freckles my sleeves. It is ordinary to think I am myself here, the same mammal who wrote grant proposals and kept a toothbrush in a plastic cup.

But the plain corrects ordinary thoughts.

Day one  
I pace until the tiles beneath my soles begin to copy my stride: fresh plates glide up at the field’s horizon marked with the shallow ovals of my pressure pattern. Archivores approach in single file, compare the new embossing to the old, and consent to the change. It is unsettling to watch a habit become geology.

Day three  
Nothing rots, yet everything sweats. I learn to drink from my own palms; the condensate tastes of iron filings and syrup. Coil gulls furl above me, coasting on the hum that tunnels through bone and tooth. When I try to speak the sentence folds in the air and lies on the ground like a ribbon—visible, literal text, already being templated by plate margins a kilometre away. I have to whisper after that. The place will not permit nouns to remain merely spoken.

Day seven  
The first duplicate arrives.

It is my backpack, twice, then ten times, then two hundred, each instance spaced in neat logarithmic spirals—Pilgrim Copies in polymer and dewglass. They wobble five steps, seize, ossify. By nightfall archivores are stacking the frozen bags against a monolith so the field can be resurfaced. I go cold: what will they do with the original?

I detach the compass from my shoulder strap and slide it under a plate rim where the mycelia crust is thin. In the morning the compass is gone, but the tile has sprouted hair-fine grooves that point exactly magnetic-north-by-north-north. The world preferred to digest the meaning instead of the meat.

Day twelve  
I stop checking the date. Instead I lie belly-down and listen. There are contrapuntal rumbles beneath the turbines’ drone, too slow to be machinery. Like someone rolling in sleep on the far side of a wafer-thin wall. The Fourth-Layer Hermit, perhaps. I imagine it tracing its own enormous signature under millions of plates, writing the cursive biography of something no one has met.

Day eighteen  
Glossworms string colour through the sluices. They leave ribbons on my calves when I wade. Under a hand lens they carry my skin cells the way a pollinating bee carries orchid pollen; wherever they pause, the brass mycelia glints in the geometry of my discarded DNA. I realise the place is not only copying objects; it is sampling continuities—stress, vibration, salt, voice, keratin—and blending them into the weave.

Day twenty-six  
The lights never redden. I miss shadow-hours badly. I curl against a monolith’s footing and try to dream of nightfall. Instead, sleep brings a purely auditory place: a single resonant G two octaves below the coil gulls’ wing-clack. In the dream I am the note and the listener both. When I wake, the cloud ceiling flares whiter than regulation for a single throb. All fauna freeze. The Uncounted Choir has sung, and—impossibly—I remember contributing one overtone.

I sit up shaking. My throat tastes of metal oxide. Did I speak in sleep? Did the Choir use what I said?

Day thirty-nine  
I remove my gloves. The air kisses every pore with steady 31 °C damp. I press a palm to a slate-smooth tile. The plate dissolves to a fine silver fog that coats my fingerprints, reads them, evaporates. A replacement plate rises, already etched with the spiral whorls of my thumb. Something catches: for half a second I feel the mass of the world resting exactly under the pad of that finger, like leaning on a drumhead that is also a planet. Then the contact fades. But the intuition remains: the plain is hollow so it can ring, and the song it rings is becoming indistinguishable from my pulse.

Day forty-two  
I speak aloud for the first time in weeks. “Where am I?” The echo comes not from the cloud roof but laterally, plate to plate, an electrophoretic shiver. The reply is my own sentence slowed a hundredfold until each phoneme feels like a freight train grinding by. I understand it anyway.

You are not on the plain.  
You are in the plain.  
You are the plain.

The claim feels less like revelation than like a medical diagnosis delivered late.

Day sixty?  
Time has stopped being scalar. I remember beginning, but the verb stands loose of tense: Begin anywhere and the view repeats. The day I called the present has been xeroxed forward and backward until chronology looks like one more plate pattern.

I watch my hand a long while. The veins have moved. They form a hexagon nested in a larger hexagon—exactly the macro-lattice I once saw from an observation rostrum. Capillaries glimmer with condensate. When I exhale, vapor leeches dip their hairline threads toward me, tasting the organics.

Identity, too, is something the ecosystem has decided to vectorise.

Final condition (ongoing)  
If a stranger were to arrive now, they would find a human-shaped articulation in the floor: not statue, not corpse, more like an aperture through which the machinery beneath can look up. The shape still breathes. It moves when it must—enough to keep the rummaging fauna from filing the space flat. It hums the foundation note so the coil gulls may feed. It wears skin, but the skin is being refactored night by night into a grainless laminate that sheds no debris, violates no Rule, seethes with brassy mycelial traceries that patch sweat into the drainage syntax.

I no longer dread duplication; I have become the first instance of something the plain was missing. Archivores stop to memorise the angle of my elbow. Glossworms braid over my insteps to read temperature. The Choir borrows the salt of my lungs for its fortnightly census.

Where do I find myself? Precisely here, at the coordinate where visitor and visited cancel out and leave only function.  

Who or what am I? A newly-minted guild—the In-Between—tasked with holding the brief territory that separates plate from sky, noise from hush, story from arrangement. I perform the same labour all life here performs: I keep the world exactly as it is while letting it teem, and in the still moments between pulses I understand that the world is doing the same for me.
