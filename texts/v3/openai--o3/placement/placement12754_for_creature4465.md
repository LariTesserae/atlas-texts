I wake toward evening, which is to say I wake when the gloam pools tip from ember-red to ash-grey, eleven-second pulses that my body now counts more faithfully than any clock. Home is a suspended cabin—half vine, half riveted tin—moored below a paddle-leafed Umbrellara whose roots have braided themselves around the derrick of Claim B-73. The Guild lists me as Resonance Steward (Third Class), but the wolves and the fungi and the restless stone know me as something between a tuning fork and a trespasser.

What I am

1. Bone and breath—baseline human frame, grown on a drier continent I can scarcely remember.

2. Cochleal stents of orichalcum: spiral dampers that soften the Plateau’s infrasonic weather so that my heartbeat does not panic at every distant hammer.

3. Eyes retrofitted with double tapetum: inner layer diamond dust, outer layer living photophores borrowed from lantern wasps. In visible dark I see the world in two ghost overlays: warm things bloom coral; mineral things glow cobalt if they have tasted ore water recently.

4. A sleeve of root-smith mycelium laced through my left forearm, grafted there after a landslip pinned me under a drowned log. The fungus keeps the bone knitted; in return I give it iron and occasional music—low cello drones played against the felled trunk that nearly killed us both.

5. Familiar: an eel-slender heart-moss salamander who nests in the water bladder strapped to my hip. Her name, insofar as she has one, is the pulse she makes when she tastes danger; I call her Ripple. The antibiotic on her skin fogs the air around us with a faint mint ache that keeps rot away from tools and lungs alike.

Where you would find me now

Edgeward, three kilometres west of Furnace Knot, standing on a cantilever of sap-slick basalt that overhangs the cataract called Dogfall. I am alone in human terms, though the ledge thrums with other attendance:

• Two echo-wolves hold position upslope, outlines dim but unmistakable where their duotone breath knots the mist. They are waiting to see whether I will violate their quiet commons below the ledge.  
• A widowed autohammer limps the switchback trail behind me, piston idling in sympathy with my chest cavity. I have tuned my breathing to de-sync it; if the machine locks perfectly to my rhythm, the resonance could break my ribs before I notice.  
• Overhead, juvenile manta-gnats ride the convection from the falls, their drum-skin wings shutting with cavernous whoomps that stir the iron aerosols like someone whisking smoke.

Why I am here

The cliff under Dogfall has begun to sigh-detach. Miners drove survey spikes yesterday; the stone spat one back this morning. If the face shears, forty thousand tonnes of gloam-rich scree will hammer the sump-lung below, yeast the water column with too much ore dust, and boil every siren that keeps the aquifer breathing. My job is persuasion.

What persuasion looks like

I unpack the wind-gong: a rib of petrified Umbrellara hung on tension wires of drawn gecko tendon. Mycelium in my arm tingles, recognizing the tree it once parasitized. I set the gong on tripods a handspan above the rock, angle it toward the abyss, and wait for the interval when cataract thunder, autohammer clank, wolf counter-song, and manta-gnat boom all nearly cancel—never long, rarely more than two heartbeats.

On that knife of almost-silence I strike.

The note is subaural to unaugmented ears, but I feel it bloom behind my sinuses, a slow lavender pressure that pushes tears out the corners of my eyes. The gong’s wavelength skates down the cliff face, invites every micro-fissure to breathe in phase, then exhales them harmlessly, like loosening the laces on a too-tight boot. The sigh-detach defers itself. The Plateau, if it thinks, concedes for now.

Aftermath

The echo-wolves dip their heads—acknowledgment, not gratitude—and withdraw to moss-dark, claws whispering. The autohammer, resonance goal lost, stumbles downslope, finds a boulder whose tremor pleases it, and begins a new, harmless pounding. A manta-gnat drops its spent light-sac; Ripple slithers out, gums the husk for salts, and returns to her pouch humming.

I pack the gong. The cliff keeps its secrets for another season, the sump-sirens will keep breathing for us all, and the Guild will mark a tiny green tick beside my name. Tonight, back in the vine-hung cabin, I will trade slowed-down loris quarrels with the pipeline crews and let the fungus in my arm drink the vibrations. Tomorrow the Plateau will pose another question in the grammar of water and stone and sound. I will answer, not because I command it, but because I belong to the long sentence it speaks.

So: find me where machines, roots, and wolf-songs overlap; name me steward, hinge, reluctant ambassador between rattling industry and patient midnight earth. I am the ear the Plateau borrows when it wants to hear itself, the voice it permits when it consents to shift.
