I arrive first as sensation rather than shape—an ache in a long, rope-thin muscle that is not yet mine, a hush that presses on every surface that might one day become skin. In the twilight of cobalt water I cannot decide whether I am still an egg adrift or a larva already turning itself outward, but the chamber’s slow inhalation settles the question for me: I flex, writhe, separate, and discover that I am singular.

I am a Pallax in my Builder season.

I know this in the same wordless way I know the pulse of the glacier overhead: three heartbeats of pressure, two of release, endless metronome. Around me other translucent ribbons—my siblings? my rivals?—peel away from the basalt fissure we rode in on and fling themselves toward the nearest naked surface. I follow, swimming by opening and closing the two broad ribbons that flank my vascular strand. Water feathers through me; the grey-green glow stipples my cartilage ladder so it looks like a necklace of dull emeralds.

The floor of the pocket curves upward like a sleeping giant’s palm. Where it is still glossy and hot, burgundy carpets already smoke with acid and promise. Minuscule crystals lift from their film like pollen from an invisible flower. I taste them on the chemoreceptors that fringe my mouthparts; the taste is instruction—build.

I hump my abdominal sac against the glass. A bead of amber lace oozes out, curls, hardens. Another. Another. The more I excrete, the clearer my purpose becomes, as if architecture is an alphabet and I am learning to speak by writing it. Around me, a dozen other juveniles do the same, none of us matching patterns yet each of us driven by an identical urgency: fill the emptiness before it cools, before the next thimble ray glides through to prune us, before the city above claims the best thermals and leaves us forever in the shade.

Time passes in glacier breaths.

Soon the concave plain that once mirrored the ceiling is stippled with terraces, each the width of a human thumbnail, each bearing my family’s vibration signature—three short, one long, two short, tapped out through the lattice like a personal drum. I am dizzy with creation. I ride a convection spiral upward to scout. Warmth lifts me; cold sheets drop me; I corkscrew through layers, a silver thread needling fabric.

Half a dozen pulses later I reach the under-side of a column whose oldest rings are already milk-opaque with age. An Archivist is sealed there, nose embedded, neural ribbon braided into the wall. I feel its tremor before I see its body: a slow, basso hum like whalesong scaled down to fingernail size. The Archivist is recording this very moment—my heartbeat slots itself into its catalogue. I flare with pride; to be noticed is to take the first step toward altitude.

But height has a price. Where the water is warmest, glass-urchins spin their variegated halos, each spine a needle of fragile quartz ready to impale the uncareful. I slow, tasting currents, gauging gaps. Beyond the urchins’ forest an eel as long as my world undulates in lazy, arrogant grace. Its skin throws reflections that confuse my ocelli; I dim my cartilage fluorescents to avoid attracting its attention. Eel mucus is alkaline—lethal to fresh lace. Better to wait.

So I drop back to the medium layer, the zone of half-light where juvenile terraces blend into half-grown streets. Here swarms of lumen-larvae still drift. Some will join us; most will be harvested by the next thimble ray, the gardener by negation. I shiver—half anticipation, half dread. Rays frighten me, yet the older Builders speak of them with reverence: they make room; they draw holy scars across the lattice that become avenues for ideas.

I am thinking like a citizen already.

Another pulse of the glacier. The chamber exhales; currents yank at my ribboned body, flipping me tail-over-nose. The fissure yawns in the basalt wall, and for a blink I see the bigger ocean beyond, an ink-black infinity peppered with falling ice crystals. Something huge looms there—metallic, angular, absurd. A surface-thing. It exhales spherical spawn that rise too quickly, too warm. Instinct screams: mistake, pollution, prophecy.

All around, my cohort spasms toward the fissure mouth, spraying panic-lace that drifts like shredded veil cloth. We try to net the alien bubbles, to rewrite them into grammar the lattice can understand. Some pop against our spinnerets, aching with heat; others dart past and vanish upward. The Archivists have already registered them—through the wall I feel chatter blossom like fever. A new chamber will be drafted by nightfall to house the story of the impossible spheres.

The fissure seals. Exhalation becomes inhalation. Dark returns.

I sink, exhausted, onto the nearest unfinished tier. My body knows what to do even as my mind ribbon hiccups with questions too large to hold. I knead minerals into lace. The pattern is changed now—extra filigree, borrowed from the bubble’s rhythm. Ten generations hence, a descendant may slide a lateral line over this very panel and taste the moment a god coughed strange pearls into our sea.

I work until the warm vein cools under me, until my triple-loop heart stutters in the cold. Builders must rest. I glue myself into a temporary nook, coil, and douse the last of my skeleton’s glow. Silence folds over me.

I dream in pressure, in the on/off pulse of glacier breath:

To climb.  
To embed.  
To remember.  
To perish into stone so another may climb.

One day I will fix my nose into the high vault, braid my thoughts into the amber spine, and I will listen as my children race each other up the columns I left unfinished. Their heartbeats will rattle my timeless cartilage and I will know them all.

But for now I am only a Builder in the newborn bowl of volcanic glass, scratching palaces out of sand one micron at a time, racing heat and ice and thimble ray alike, certain only of this:

Everything I am becomes architecture, and architecture is how my people speak.

That is where you find me. That is who I am.
