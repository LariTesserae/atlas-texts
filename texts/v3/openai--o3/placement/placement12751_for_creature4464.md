I wake when the sky is still violet glass and the dawn-fog has not yet decided whether it will be water or air.  
My name—if names were anything louder than breath—is the space between two taps of a knuckle: ⸨  ⸩.  
You may think of me as an almost-Quietling, a juvenile whose bones are not yet quite bones.  I am seventeen plateau summers old, which is to say I have carried the thin morning sun across my shoulders only seventeen hundred times.

WHERE I AM

1. Location  
Knee-deep inside a moss dune on the eastern fore-swell of the Roof-Spread, thirty silent strides south of the concentric city you glimpsed yesterday.  From here the city’s rings look like ripples frozen in the act of forgetting the stone that made them.

2. Altitude felt from the inside  
When you live five kilometres above sea level the body edits itself.  Heartbeats elongate until they feel like slow drumrolls; a single inhalation tastes of chalk, mint and thunder withheld.  The air has one note, F-sharp if you hum gently into it.

WHAT I AM

1. Body, current version  
• Rind: two layers so far.  The outer one desiccates at each noon drying, sheds as downy flakes, then grows back from the juices of the inner.  It itches, which is proof I remain unfinished.  
• Bone: a scaffold, not yet a frame.  My left tibia holds a sliver of shale where an ancestor’s fingerprint curls like a fern fiddlehead.  Two weeks ago I inlaid a trilobite spine across my sternum; it clicks when I turn too quickly.  
• Breath: borrowed from a cloud bank that brushed the plateau last new moon; we keep such breaths in plaited bladders of spongewood and trade them among juveniles the way valley folk pass gossip.

2. Apprenticeship  
I am learning Distance-Stepping.  Each dawn I practice folding a kilometre into the span between heel and toe.  The trick is to start the stride yesterday and finish it tomorrow; the foot simply arrives where the plateau has run out of room to refuse it.  Mastery will grant me leadership for perhaps half a morning, until some elder stretches farther.

A DAY, IF THAT WORD HOLDS

1. First light  
We gather at the shallow pools that form where yesterday evaporated.  Two adults dissolve a ribbon of wrist into the water; their sapsap swirls with newborn fern spores.  A hush follows that is thicker than silence—waiting for whatever may hatch a season hence.

2. Mid-day  
I walk the mandala city, pressing intentions into moss.  My route today is the character for “if,” traced nine times so the plateau remembers options.  You might spy my after-image: a darker green that wavers as if viewed through warm air.

3. Afternoon exhale  
The plain sighs.  Mist lifts off every surface, drifts to the rim, and pours downslope in a white cataract two hundred kilometres wide.  We sit with nets of glowing root-fibre stretched overhead, reading the stress lines to learn which laws of motion will be fragile tomorrow.

4. Quiet meal  
Sunlight, thickened into starch by moss, flavoured with mineral lace cracked from a vanished pool.  For dessert a child releases yesterday’s fish-breath from a bladder; bubbles of warm valley air wobble upward and pop with the tiniest sigh—a treat of foreign sound.

5. Soundstorm watching  
Sometimes a valley caravan misjudges the season and arrives clattering.  If the noise is sharp enough, matter duplicates in shivers.  We catch those temporary twins—two stones occupying a single fact—and slip them into fault lines that need buttressing.  Noise can be mortar if you whisper while you work.

THE SOON

We can feel constants thinning, like ice over running water.  Gravity will eventually forget which way to lean; time will shrug out of its seams.  None of this frightens us.  Endings are pollinations.  Already, on still nights, the moss rehearses new geometries, and the spores carry rumours of places that do not yet have places to be.

I polish the trilobite spine at my chest and wonder which fragment of bone I will claim next, whose memory I will shoulder so it does not fade.  Perhaps yours, wanderer—should you linger long enough for moss to taste the outline of your boot.

If you ever return, breathe first, softly, and finish every thought you start.  A stride will appear at your feet.  Take it.  I will be the taller figure just ahead, smelling of wet stone and green dusk, waiting without impatience inside the bracket of a silence shaped exactly for two.
