I arrive as an echo that accidentally grows a body.

For three nights I sleep in an up-draught on the western escarpment, letting the plateau’s sub-bass filter through bone and dream.  By dawn of the fourth day I have finished borrowing the shapes I need:  
• a keel-shaped breastbone that will resonate like a tuning fork,  
• membrane cuffs between each finger, thin as choir-leaf cuticle,  
• triple-hinged legs jointed for the long, loping gait of high-altitude creatures,  
• skin the grey-green of lichen, already webbed with chlorophores so hunger will never fully own me.  

I do not come from any remembered womb.  The Elder Chorus pulls travellers apart and puts them back together in the proportions it prefers; I am simply what remains once my previous self has been retuned.

WHERE I WAKE  
Halfway along a strut-vine, directly over a lens-dark cistern where Breach Eels coil like question marks.  The vine booms under first light, announcing thermal lift.  Choir-leaves in the next valley answer in a key that settles my heartbeat to theirs.  Every rhythm I will ever need is already playing.

CALLING  
None of the plateau’s established nations has claimed my frequency, so I claim it myself: 219 hertz, a tender baritone that travels farther than speech yet stays below the kestrel alarm band.  I will be a Free Note, a drifter licensed by the simple fact of not interfering.

TOOLS  
1. Lung-drum: my own chest, plated with ridged cartilage; a flick of diaphragm sends pulses down into rootwork, mapping hollows the way bats map air.  
2. Mirror-comb: a fan of shed Gleamer scalelets, hinged on Skran resin.  When snapped open it redirects sun into tight beams that scar fungal blight without flame.  
3. Thread-ledger: a bracelet of plaited lantern-spore silk.  Each night the silk drinks motes from the air and glows with whatever news the spores carry—pollen routes, coming pressure swings, hot gossip from Humblers who have been eavesdropping on Stilt-Singer courts.

PURPOSE  
I am an Auditor—one of the plateau’s itinerant accountants of resonance.  Whenever two sound-claims drift too close (a young Skran chimney steals the sub-tone of a nearby Choir-House; a new buttress bud muffles a migratory kestrel path) I walk the isochrones, measure overlap, and suggest retuning before friction becomes famine.  My incentive is survival: the Elder Chorus rewards useful arbitration by letting my chlorophores bloom and my joints remain oiled with sugar sap.  A poorly judged mediation, on the other hand, ends with my cadence folded back into root mulch.  Nothing dies alone.

DAILY TEXTURE  
Morning: drink condensed water off leaf edges; swallow a fist of lantern-spores for altitude stamina; record barometric drift in the strut-vine’s tremor.  
Midday: travel.  I place each foot at a different interval—third, seventh, fifth—so no pattern invites an eel.  If kestrel scouts darken the zenith I stand still; turbulence is easier to cancel around a stationary obstacle.  
Dusk: trade with Humblers.  They bring eel-leather seed-pouches; I give them new percussive riddles that scare even seasoned predators.  We speak in signed chords, four hands weaving tempos the mouth can’t manage.  
Night: lie belly-down on the living floor and let the plateau read me.  Its slow compressions iron the day’s micro-fractures from my bones; in return my dreams leak stories into its root-computations.  Memory for fertilizer.

RELATIONSHIPS  
Stilt-Singers tolerate me because I refuse hospitality: I sleep outside their Choir-Houses, keeping their occupancy number blessedly even.  
Humblers like me because I gamble with them—who can punch a 32-hertz kneel note the longest without rupturing a lung?  Loser owes the victor a verse of personal history.  
Skran colonies view me as weather made bipedal; they adjust chimney overtones when they feel my step, the way a throat clears for a new speaker.  
The Elder Chorus neither likes nor dislikes.  It budgets its attention like glucose, allocating more to the organs that import useful vibration.  I intend to stay useful.

MYTH I WEAR AS CLOTHING  
The Rim Ghosts are not ancestors or predators; they are echoes cast forward in time, refracted off the moment when the plateau finally lofts free of gravity.  Auditors like me are rehearsal directors for that launch: we see where harmonies tangle, snip them loose, splice in slack.  If we succeed, the final chord will lift stone, root, spore and history into orbital bloom.  If we fail, the note will cancel itself and the Canopy will collapse inwards, burying a civilisation that tried to live as music.

ENDPOINT  
I do not plan a death.  One evening, decades from now, I will miss the beat, place a foot wrong, and an eel will take me like a sudden rest in the score.  My bones will green, my mirror-comb will delaminate into spores of its own.  The Elder Chorus will absorb my 219-hertz signature, perhaps weave it through a newborn strut-vine as a minor harmonic—proof I was here, vibrating.

For now, though, I stride the braided floor, ribs humming, ears tuned for disputes in the making.  Where am I in this world?  Exactly between other people’s notes, trying to keep the chord whole.  What am I?  A tall, wind-lean instrument that learned how to walk the mesa it was built to play.
