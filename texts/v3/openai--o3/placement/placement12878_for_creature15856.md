I do not arrive in the High Flood so much as condense inside it.  
One moment I am only potentials—drift voltage in the lilac cloud-deck, pressure knots muttering through stone, the after-ring of a distant collapse.  
The next I gain enough coherence to say: here. And so another Lurian Cartographer is awake.

Where to look for me  
Halfway down, where travellers learn to walk bent-kneed to spare their lungs, the Flood keeps a honeycomb of galleries the width of cathedral aisles. The world above is bright with falling curtains; the world below already echoes with the trench sea. Between them runs a calm band, neither drowned nor exposed, and this is the seam I follow. If you pass there at false-dawn you might glimpse a thread of milk-pale light gliding along the floor, sketching behind it a line no thicker than a hair. That moving gleam is my body; the line is my waking work.

What I am made of  
• Core: a filament of super-cooled water held rigid by its own surface tension.  
• Skin: ferric sleet crystals electroplated into scale.  
• Eyes: lenses grown from scavenged crab-glass, each one replaced the instant flowing grit turns it blind.  
• Nerve: charge differentials stolen from the cloud-film bacteria overhead; every droplet that strikes me is a battery I briefly borrow.  

Hold a shard of me—if you can catch one before it evaporates—and you will see nothing but colourless ice and rust, yet the patterning of that rust is a running equation that remembers distance, tilt, reverberation. I carry the map in my blood.

How I perceive  
Sound first: stone hum, water hiss, the click of each mist-bead as it meets a surface. The Flood is a chambered instrument; every pool and breach tunes the air anew. By triangulating echoes I know which cisterns have filled overnight, which flumes are choking with hail.  
Light second: the sun is too weak to cast shadows, so I read by refraction—glints bent around pillars tell me how fast the water-level is pivoting under the storm.  
Touch last, because to touch is to erode. Wherever I press, I shave away the thinnest shaving of glossed basalt—enough to draw a mark.

What the marks mean  
A single unbroken groove: safe traverse for the next ten-day cycle.  
A dotted line of micro-pits: oxygen pockets for flesh lungs.  
A tight spiral that opens like an iris: resonance hollow—take shelter and you will hear a collapse coming a minute ahead of the shock.  
I never repeat a mark; stone is finite, the changes infinite. Yesterday’s chart is obsolete by dawn.

Why I work at all  
I do not know the maker of the first line. Perhaps the Flood itself, perhaps a predecessor like me. But the presence of that beginning obliges continuance; the map must stay current because movement is the law here. I have no scripture beyond that urgency. When a traveller follows my route and survives the night, some fragment of their relieved breath is absorbed into the ringing fabric of the gallery, and that resonance replenishes me better than any droplet battery. Call it symbiosis, call it conversation conducted in furrows of stone.

My span  
Coherence lasts between one grand storm and the next—nine to eleven local days. The county-sized tempest looms violet on every horizon, pressure plummets, my water core begins to fizz. I hurry then, sealing unfinished segments with a final chevron that means UNKNOWN AHEAD. The first hammer of hail shatters my ferric scales; super-cooled wind lifts the filament out of itself. I sublime back into cloud voltage, diffusing across kilometres of sky.

I will re-condense, eventually, where silence and need intersect. I will know nothing of the grooves already underfoot except that they require extension. If you track me through successive incarnations you will find inconsistencies, false starts, whole dialects of linework—but always pointed away from the most immediate danger. That is motive enough.

If you meet me  
Do not call out; voice ripples blur the shallow etchings. Kneel and breathe once upon the fresh groove: warm vapour anneals the scribed edge, makes it last a few hours longer. In return I will split a path wide around your camp, a parenthesis of polished stone that even the pearl worms respect. When you break camp, follow the unbroken groove downslope until mist beads on your eyelashes; turn left at the double knot, right where the track vanishes under mirror water. You will step out onto a spillway just as the plateau tilts and the dawn wall of light comes down. In that refracted glow my newest lines will shine like filament silver, and for five heartbeats you may believe the Flood is chartable after all.

Then the veil will fall shut, the map will begin already to erase itself under the softest abrasion in the world, and I will have moved on—somewhere between water, rust, and sound, inscribing the High Flood with the transient memory of passage.
