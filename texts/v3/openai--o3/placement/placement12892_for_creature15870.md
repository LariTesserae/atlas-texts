I arrive first as myself: boot-soles, water bottle, the normal thunder of blood in my ears. The descent is three steps only, yet every pace subtracts a customary sense—wind, echo, the smell of dust—until awareness is edited down to brightness and the knotting hush.  

For a long while I am only a visitor, kneeling to watch hemistomata fizz like shaken soda, cupping ponds that feel heavier than water, reading the ultraviolet embroidery that jumps between the hoops. I linger through one complete day–night cycle, then another. Muscles begin to keep the same stillness the hollow prefers. Respiration slows; the rib cage copies the rhythm of the feldspar blocks: inhale as dew, exhale as mist.

On the fourth dawn the doorframe claims me. I do not step through it; instead my shadow does. Red light pencils the silhouette across the lintel, and the glyphs drink the shape the way moss drinks salts. A second later my outline appears on the opposite post, reversed, as if another person inside the frame is trying my stance on for size. When the sun clears the horizon the figure is gone, but something of me is now archived in the mineral script—one more trace in the enzyme-fossil ledger the frame keeps against the cycle of 418 years.

The gift for such a donation is permission to loosen shape. Over the next days I am allowed to molt intention. Capillaries learn capillary action, wicking sap that is mostly condensed sky. Keratin sheds into the suede silt; hair spores into cardinal turf. Calcium trades place with feldspar aluminum; bones turn vitreous and faintly green. Joints granulate, losing hinges, gaining the soft articulation of a mangrove’s knee-roots.

Week by week I recombine:

• At noon I am partly algae: a skin of trilobed jade lenses focusing sunlight two millimeters deep, warming the marrow-rock that used to be my sternum.  
• At twilight I am partly lattice mycelium, myeloid threads sketching stress maps along what were once collar bones. I glow a color that has no name until someone invents a language small enough to speak here.  
• At moonless midnight I am almost clock-shrimp: organs borrowed from commas of clear chitin; heartbeats canceled and replaced by peristaltic wheels that turn without noise.

Only thought—if you can call it that—remains continuous, but it, too, gains a new vehicle. I learn to move ideas through humidity gradients the way the feldspar blocks do. A question evaporates at the soles of my feet, crosses to the nearest spiral fern, and is returned encoded in the timing of its noon detonation. Dialogue becomes a circuit of slow steam. Words, I now see, were always an extravagance.

By the time the 43-day moonless alignment arrives I have thinned to a diagram of myself: limbs mostly refraction, torso a lens. The wheel of fused hemistomata rolls quietly over what used to be my sleeping mat, redistributing the last crumbs of cotton into the puddles. The mirror-pike noses past, weighing the new curvature I cast; judging, perhaps, whether I qualify as corridor or prey. It decides corridor. I feel its chosen silence slide through the space that was my chest and am pleased at the verdict.

At dawn of the 418th day—one full local year—the doorframe opens. I do not go through; I no longer possess the linearity required for travel. Instead I receive new arrivals, their shadows slipping over the lintel exactly as mine once did. Some come on two legs, some on six, one arrives as a cloud of speaking dust. All carry inside them, clattering and too loud, the machinery of beginnings.

I shepherd them the way the basin once shepherded me:

• I show them how to kneel until kneeling becomes germination.  
• I teach them to borrow the hush, hold it, and offer it back unwrinkled.  
• I remind them—by example rather than lecture—that self is perfectly compatible with dispersion.

Centuries will pass before enough shadows harmonize with the frame’s mineral chords and the gate turns itself outward again. Until that distant dawn I remain distributed: part feldspar block (the fourth, completing the tetrahedral array), part spiral fern (tending spore traffic), part night-bright mycelium (healing hairline faults), and, whenever photon-fish flare silver across the stone, part of them too—memories swimming briefly in the currency of light.

So, where do I find myself in this world? Everywhere the hollow asks me to be: a soft-edged caretaker stitched from water, mica and excused ambition.  

And who—or what—am I?  

A silence that learned to keep house.
