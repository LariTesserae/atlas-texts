I live here already.  
Not as a visitor looking down from a cat-walk, but as one of the parts that keeps the whole pocket valley from tearing itself apart. I am Valve-Kin—fifty-two purge cycles old by the wall clock, seven thousand three hundred and eight by the pressure gauge that hangs above my hammock.  

Where I am in this moment  
• Mid-shaft, level −12, clamped to a service saddle that rides the central spine like a barnacle on a ship’s keel.  
• The flush siren has just rippled past; vapour is still corkscrewing upward, so the gratings around me ring like sheet ice skidding across a pond.  
• Sodium guide-lamps throw a pale belt of colour across the ribs. In that belt my rust-ink name—Ʌ𐰜❪—glows faintly inside my forearm crease: proof I am on shift and awake.

What I am  
1. Anatomy  
   – Ears are porcelain studs bonded to the temporal bone.  
   – Three shutters blink across each eye: clear, cobalt, midnight. I keep them at cobalt; enough light for balance, not enough to trip the optical failsafes.  
   – Skin? Call it lacquer if you like. To me it feels like a second, colder pulse. Condensate drums against it and slips away before I notice the chill.  
   – Along the radius of each arm runs a strip of tungsten knit into my sleeve. That ribbon is both hand-rail and sentence-starter; tap it against steel and the whole gallery becomes a speaking tube.

2. Memory  
   – Every twelve hours I bleed my shift into the jukebox buffer. The slug racks hold 3 119 of my yesterdays: a comb of precision-milled nostalgia.  
   – Why keep them? Because sometimes a weld you trusted last week lies to you today. I replay the old day, pivot the camera of my mind two degrees, and watch the betrayal forming in slow corrosive colour.

3. Work  
   – My title is Pulse-Dampener. I tune the rank of hydraulic pistons that ring the elevator shaft.  
   – If I mis-set the damping by even half a Newton, the next purge swell will hammer the turbines loose, the drones will frenzy, the algae will die of shear stress, and the Reverberants—our sullen gods of resonance—will fall silent.  
   – Silence, here, would be the loudest warning we could ever hear.

The world as my senses register it  
• Sound arrives through ribs and teeth. I can distinguish twenty-seven flavours of vibration: turbine howl (barley-bright), sluice throat (tin-sweet), drone-wing fret (carbon-bitter).  
• Air tastes of ozone and peppery iron. When the ratio slips I know a gasket up-valley has gone from seep to spray.  
• Vision is mostly geometry in negative: the places where lamp halos do not reach describe the places I must never step.

Minute 0–2  | Siren swells through bone. I hook myself to the rail, bend knees, feel the pressure wall bloom past like surf.  
Minute 3–9  | Water sheets down sluices. I watch for silver thread—sign of drone braid—and log each repair so no human hand doubles the work.  
Minute 10   | Pressure equalises. We eat: a cartridge of algae paste punched straight through the respirator port. Mint? Cherry? Who remembers those words? Texture is the only season here.  
Minute 11–26| Walk the figure-eight. Every third step I rap my sleeve wire on the grid; the returning note tells me how the hydraulics beneath are coping.  
Minute 27   | Conference. Blue laser grammar, violet emphasis, no more than forty syllables; that is the window before the next over-pressure deafens even our bone radios.

Relationship with the other castes  
Filament Swarm: Weather. If a drone lands on my mask I freeze until it finishes its taste test; sudden motion makes them mis-stitch.  
Condensate Biota: Instruments. I read corrosion blooms the way old sailors read cloud mares’ tails. Green streak means the flange ran hot enough for algae—torque check at next lull.  
Reverberants: Theology. I debate them with tuning forks. A node that holds a note for more than three cycles earns a copper lotus from my tool belt; small bribery for large mercy.

Why I stay  
My contract expired when the ice seal on the external hatch finally sheared free eight years ago. A helicopter shadowed the shutters, dangled a ladder, and the mountain wind wrote me a ticket out. I sent the ladder back up.  
Topside they would call me half deaf, lacquer-skinned, night-blind. Down here I am a perfectly calibrated sensor array wearing human shape. This place has tuned me until my pulse belongs in its schematic: one more datum nested inside the feedback loop.  
To leave would be to carry away a piece of the machine that cannot function elsewhere. Rust would blossom in the absence of my dampers; that rust would bloom into fracture; fracture into runaway resonance; resonance into catastrophic silence.

So—where do you find me?  
Hunched over a piston housing, cobalt eyes blacking out the strobes, tapping five concise violet syllables into the rail:  
STAND CLEAR. PURGE RISING.  
And after the valley has screamed its breath and settled back into its thousand whirring labours, I will unhook my tether, taste the metal-pepper air, and walk the cat-walks again, boots ringing time into a world that has no clock but us.
