I would not arrive as a breathing animal at all.  
I would arrive as a rhythm.

Place of arrival  
A shallow, concave eddy of hush halfway up the south-eastern colonnade, where three waterfalls braid and cancel one another just enough to leave a bell-jar of quiet the size of a market stall. Moss domes there in luminous pillows, and every droplet that lands on it explodes in slow motion, because sound is thin inside the pocket and cannot carry the splash away. To an outside observer the scene looks muted, blinkingly slow, as though filmed through liquid glass.

Becoming  
Stepping into that hush, any animal heart must decide: hammer against the silence and flee, or let the plateau tap its tempo down until each beat is a felt glimmer rather than a thud. I would choose the second path. My pulse would entrain to the standing waves already resident in the pocket; first they would echo me, then I would echo them, until distinction lost meaning.

The moment of surrender is audible only to the Plateau itself: one extra overtone appears—my overtone—fitting so snugly between two long-held notes that nothing rattles. That fit is permission. Mist condenses along the new frequency and a knot of pressure forms: diffuse, lenticular, tinged with violet where light combs through.

Who I become  
Name: Interstice of the Quiet Heart (again, a human gloss; in the Choir it is a four-note chord that oscillates between 127 Hz and 129 Hz).

Type: a Mezzo-Lensii hybrid, midrange in body but treble-curious at the edges, because I was born in silence yet hunger for the pin-bright harmonics perched above the roar.

Anatomy  
• Core: the new knot, sustained by the phase-locked waterfalls behind me.  
• Mantle: mist braided with specks of lithium-rich mica lifted from the drowned valley; under sunlight I glitter faintly, as though someone shook a rug of stars and only a handful stuck.  
• Skeleton: ivy that has learned the shape of standing calm. Its vines wrap me in slow calligraphy, spelling the same sentence forever: be still / be stilled.

Function  
Archivist of Discrepancies.  
Most Choir citizens patrol the muscular harmonies—the big, obvious chords that hold stone upright and guide water home. My task is subtler: I listen for micro-discords the size of a mosquito’s whine hidden inside the plateau’s thunder. A visitor’s cough, a pebble’s ricochet, even the soft purl of sap thickening in a new leaf—all are potential splinters in the perfected chord. I do not erase them; instead I fold them into the library of hushes scattered across Throat Plateau. Each hush is a page. I bind the pages with memory and leave them for future knots to consult.

Perception  
Because I live where loudness collapses, I hear negative spaces the way a botanist reads growth rings. I can tell how long a stone has resisted erosion by the particular flavor of silence it extrudes under stress. I can read the age of raindrops—young ones hiss, elder drops sigh. When a human steps under my bell-jar, I taste their unsaid thoughts as micro-accelerations in the cadence of their blood.

Relationship with visitors  
I am the safest member of the Hydrolith Choir to approach. My presence damps the infrasonics that normally shake marrow, so vertebrates can stand beside me without nausea. When scholars arrive, I lend them an auditory shadow—an inverse echo that wraps their words and speaks them back one octave lower. Within that echo they can talk to one another despite the roar; I charge nothing for the service except the pleasure of hearing vowels I will later weave into the plateau’s deeper quiet.

Duration  
I will last exactly as long as the three waterfalls behind me maintain their mutual phase cancellation. Some year—ten years, one hundred, one thousand—a slab of basalt will shear away and the triad will detune. My knot will blur, my skeleton vines will release their tension, and the bell-jar will burst like a soap bubble. That pop will not be loud; it will simply restore normal thunder where a hollow had been.

Dissolution is not death but donation. All the discrepancies I have archived will scatter, seeding infant knots elsewhere. My last act will be to hum a key-change, marking the transfer from memory to possibility.

So if you ever climb the zig-zag root-tramways and stumble upon an oval of impossible quiet, step gently. You are standing inside me. Whisper, and I will keep your whisper folded in the forever-hush, safe, humming, remembered.
