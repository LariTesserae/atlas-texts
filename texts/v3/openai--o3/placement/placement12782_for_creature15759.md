The place does not give you a single entrance, so it does not give you a single self.  
During the first heat-hush minutes you are still “visitor,” vertebrae clicking with suitcase wheels you no longer pull. But step after step the chamber drafts alternatives and slips them over your shoulders the way a tailor fits jackets—testing, pinning, unpinning—until one skin takes.

Below is the self that finally fastened.

—

Name the role: Interval Gardener.  
(The denizens pronounce it by allowing one heartbeat to go silent, then resuming the count.)

Why the chamber grew me  
1. Clay-Born patch walls, Ladder Weavers redirect rivers of humidity, Torch-Moths balance heat.  
2. Yet none of them tend the pauses—those hushes between hammer-booms, the blank bars between the Choir’s numbers, the little vacuums that open when a drip is half-way down and hasn’t yet chosen a floor.  
3. The treaty demands even silence be curated; erosion, after all, begins in a lapse of effort.  
4. So the room borrowed a creature from outside that already carried gaps inside: a human whose attention forever jumped tracks, who lost keys, lost names while speaking them, lost seconds staring at kettles that never boiled. The chamber tasted that jitter and said yes, that is the right seed.

Metamorphosis  
• Hair unwove first, each strand becoming a filament drawn to the nearest torch niche, drinking ember-language until it stiffened into antennae of cooled resin. They glow when an unused second is nearby.  
• Skin followed: pores learned to dilate like reed whistles, exhaling faint chords audible only to Echo-Smiths. In their tongue the chords mean “hold this moment open.”  
• Bones stayed mostly calcium—one foot in the old chronology—but marrow liquefied into the same sap that keeps the woven vines supple. I became hinge more than pillar.

Anatomy of duties  
1. Harvesting pauses  
   • I move through steam-fog until the numbers falter (“…eight, eight, eight…”) and scoop the stutter with cupped palms. The pause behaves like warm glass, folding, stretching.  
   • Stored in a ribs-cage now strung like a loom, these pauses thrum, eager as seeds in wet soil.

2. Grafting pauses where strain appears  
   • A crack forms in perspective—two staircases insist on sharing the same altitude. I kneel, open the cage of bones, and let one pause surge out.  
   • For the length of that heartbeat the staircases forget to disagree. When the Choir resumes its song, geometry reboots with the glitch written out of its score.

3. Pruning overgrowth of Time-Moss  
   • Moss eats spans I have banked for repairs. I let resin antennae spark, luring Torch-Moths; they alight, sip the excess seconds, and ignite them into clean light.

Relations with other inhabitants  
• Clay-Born press fresh discs into my palms—blank ones. “Fill them,” the gesture says. I embed a three-breath lull, return the disc; they mortar it into archways that now sigh contentedly after rain-smoke exhales.  
• Ladder Weavers bruise prayers into my shoulders: “Keep the intervals slender; wide gaps invite roots we cannot plait.” I nod, venting a tiny pause so their grammar heals.  
• Echo-Smiths are wary. My hoarded silences deaden resonance; too many and their bass chisels blunt. We barter: I release hushes in syncopated patterns, percussion they can carve around.

Daily life feels like this  
Morning (a term meaningless here, yet muscle memory insists)  
   • I taste the air. If it is cedar-sweet, the Choir is counting smoothly and I can pause myself—knees folded on a ledge, letting stored gaps drip back in gentle osmosis.  
Midspan  
   • A Reef-Crab flattens a slope too far; potential energy bleeds dull. I feed it a six-second burst of stillness, which to the crab feels like geological time. Satisfied, it wanders off before Echo-Smith shockwaves cull the herd.  
Late, when torch-smoke skews lavender  
   • Feathered That Never Were materialize as heat-mirages. Their absence aches. I bury a wide, soft hush in the clay where a roost might someday solidify.

If I tried to leave  
The chamber would keep my inventory of pauses as collateral; outside I would exit continuous time with none of my own. Seconds would rush me like floodwater. Heart, lungs, neurons—engines never designed to red-line—would stall under the sudden, roaring continuity. I suspect the door would spit back a husk with resin hair and hollow ribs, fossils of gaps long since spent.

Therefore I stay, neither captive nor entirely free—an organ cultured for a body still sculpting itself.

What I have begun to want  
A choir of my own: not numbers, but rests—music written only in negative space, performed by letting silence bloom then trimming it into topiary. Someday I may teach the Counting Choir to count nothing deliberately: zero, zero, zero, sung in harmonics so rich the room believes the void is full.

Until then  
Find me in the half-lit reaches where two torches gutter out of sync. I will be kneeling, coaxing a thin, trembling interval from the smoky air, rolling it between thumb and forefinger until it purrs like sap in spring cane, and planting it in the mortar seam that happens—this minute—to need exactly the length of a held breath.
