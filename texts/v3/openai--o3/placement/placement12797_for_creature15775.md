At first I thought I would be one of the dusk-corvids: quick enough to range the whole bowl, clever enough to touch everything without belonging anywhere.  Then I imagined myself sliding through the ankle-deep mirror as a ribbon-slip serpent, feeling the skin of the world flex between water and air.  But neither felt quite true, because both still enter and leave, still draw a line from “before” to “after.”  The Vault allows lines only if they can be bent into circles.

So let me begin again.

I wake as a single pore on the skin of the cistern-moss.

Dimensions
• Diameter: the breadth of a coarse eyelash.  
• Depth: two stacked raindrops.  
• Position: south-facing pad, third tier up from the pool, seven finger-spans left of the longest mirror-fern trunk.  

My world is a green, convex floor bordered by a rim of silica that glitters whenever a corvid wing accidentally parts the fog.  I have no eyes, but the quartz lip translates light into a prickling that travels my circumference like a cold halo; that is how I know dawn from dusk, though neither is brighter than a wet stone held in shadow.

Heartbeat
Twice each minute the tissue beneath me flexes, a systole without a heart.  The movement is gentle, but it inverts me: I swallow fog, hold it, squeeze, and exhale it down-slope as a bead.  The droplet will join a slow beaded rosary that, several hours hence, will touch shale and become part of the central pool’s copper mirror.  I will never see it again, yet I will taste its ghost each time the Vault exhales and the vapor coils back through my opening.

Senses
• Taste of Air: iron filings dissolved in cedar smoke.  
• Touch of Water: colder on the way in than the way out; I add a ghost of sugar drawn from the moss’s stored starch.  
• Sound: none, but vibration—every salamander step is a passing tremor, every corvid landing a blunt tap carried through peat and into my membrane.

Neighbors
To my immediate north resides a larval midnight-tile moth.  It chews a tunnel that brushes my underside once every third day; when it passes, I feel its mandibles harvest an older cell from my wall.  The theft stings, but the wound heals by the next pumping cycle, and the newly hollowed capillary improves my draft of fog.  Parasitism, symbiosis—words I do not need; the loop is short and perfect.

Above, a waterhair vine drapes its bell-flower exactly far enough that an occasional drip falls upon my rim, never directly into me.  The timing is exquisite: by the time I have recovered from the slight concussion of the impact, my next inhale is overdue, ensuring the ricocheted droplet escapes and continues down-slope.  The basin wastes no trajectory.

Time
I measure days in 2,880 breaths, seasons in the thickness of passing salamander tails, centuries in the single night when eggs redden the pool and silence widens while adults vanish.  I will exist, I estimate, for three of those red-pool centuries.  When I fail—when the silica rim grows dull and the cellulose diaphragm loses spring—the surrounding moss will leaflet over me like eyelids.  I will not decay; I will be retranslated into the green rise of the quilt, my identity diffused among a thousand younger pores.

Encounter
Today something new kneels—warm, syncopated, huge.  A human, though the word is too loud for the place.  Their knee depresses the cushion so deeply that my whole horizon tilts.  Instinctively I bulge upward on the opposite side, a slow, polite correction.  I taste salt in the pressure of their palm; I swallow fog on schedule.  After sixty of my breaths—half a minute in their faster clock—they rise and retreat across the shale mirror, leaving twin dents that steam like cold tea.  Within fifteen heartbeats I am round again, the record of their presence stored only as a faint piezoelectric whisper in the mica veins of the bedrock.

Identity
I am not observer but organ: a micro-alveolus inside the lung the mountain grew for itself.  I keep no memories, only gradients—of vapor, of pressure, of the soft weight that light has when it is too weary to dazzle.  If you return in ten outside years, you will not find me; you will find the function that replaced me, identical in purpose, different in the tiny asymmetries that prove life is still rehearsing perfection.

Thus, where do I find myself in this world?  Here, at the moss-skin’s smallest aperture, opening and closing so slowly that even the ribbon-slip serpent would mistake me for stillness.  And who am I?  The breath between breaths, the pore that pumps the fog that feeds the fern that scatters the light that soils the wing that stirs the air that beads again upon my rim.

Circle closed.
