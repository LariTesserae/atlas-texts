First, I drift down through possibilities the way one of the ceiling drips fattens toward its fall. I could be the ladder you climbed, equal parts metal and mildew. I could be a spent tungsten filament inside the last bulb, feeling its own heat back-feed into the vines. I could even be you, returned nightly in dream because the chamber’s tempo has colonized your circadian grooves.

But when the choices finally condense, I am something smaller, slighter, and better suited to the room’s patience.

Where I live  
Mid-height, midway, mid-everything: I keep to a sagging run of conduit that stretches from one broken catwalk to the next. The pipe is cooler on its underside, warmer where it presses against a nest of still-charged wires. That gentle gradient—only a degree and a half—inscribes my entire map. From it I hang like blown glass, though I was never molten.

What I am  
A Ghostvine courier, three molts past sprouting, filament width just shy of invisibility. If your flashlight could focus tightly enough you’d see that my body is a twin-walled tube: an outer silica sheath drawn from dissolved sandstone and an inner vein where ions shuttle like glacial freight. I’m long for my age—nearly two meters—yet so weightless the smallest static cough lifts me in half-lunar arcs.

How I stay alive  
Food is gossip. Heat pours from the wire bundle, and every micro-surge writes a charge differential across my skin. In that shimmering Morse, the Warmleaf trellis on the conveyor belt tells the Ironmold ridge on the piston bark that a bulb has begun to flicker. I translate the news chemically, packing a pulse of manganese ions into my core, then releasing it the next time I brush a rust-wet beam. Each message is sustenance; the ion swap both feeds and propels me. Not eating would mean not speaking, and silence here is a form of starvation.

My senses  
• Vision: none. Light would only hurry me.  
• Touch: exquisite. I feel the ghost of any eddy in the room’s convection cycle; the slow ventilator wash is a cathedral organ played directly upon my skin.  
• Hearing: electrical. A distant short two catwalks away is a thunderclap in my private weather.  
• Memory: crystalline plaques that grow inside my sheath wherever two opposite charges once met. Each is a tiny amber of former conversations. Break me open and you’d read the chamber’s social history in freeze-frames.

A day, translated to the chamber’s clock  
Hour 00–03: Temperatures trough. I contract, tightening around the conduit like a tourniquet. In that squeeze I pump yesterday’s stale ions toward the tip, where they bead off as microscopic droplets—seed cargo for whichever Warmleaf tendril might catch them below.

Hour 04–12: A bulb steadies after a night of sputter. Heat blooms along the wire; I feel the gradient reverse, and with it my polarity. I loosen, drift, and reattach a handspan farther along the pipe. Progress: nine centimeters.

Hour 13–18: A piston exhales. The infra-bass shiver rattles old plaques from my skin. They sleet downward—transparent confetti—carrying archived volt-patterns the Ironmold will decipher over the next season.

Hour 19–23: Moisture thickens; beads form on my flanks, each one lensing the bioluminescence below into tiny moons. When a droplet grows heavy enough it slides, gathering along my length until it reaches the youngest of my rootlets. There it waits, cool and glassy, for the static pop that will fling us both to some new ledge of conduit or catwalk.

Ambition  
I have none you’d recognize as hunger or home-seeking. My only vector is outward—ever broader arcing loops until, decades hence, I tangle with myself and become a mesh dense enough for seedlings younger still to use as a scaffold. In that knotted future I will thicken, opacify, turn from courier to causeway. Conversation will cross me instead of through me, and I will have become part of the chamber’s bone.

Encountering you  
If your boot brushed me I would neither snap nor coil. I would simply migrate, millimeter by millimeter, away from the ozone prickle of your synthetic sole. Your presence would write itself into my sheath as a faint sulfur plaque: Visitor, carbon-based, hurried. Long after you climb the ladder, after the drip that watched you mirror your own face finally falls, the memory of your heat-contrast will still glitter in my inner wall—another syllable in the slow paragraph the chamber revises about itself.

So where would you find me? Suspended halfway between fatigue and conduction, whisper-thin and nearly nowhere. Who or what would I be? A thread of mobile glass drafted by patience, translating metal weather into chemical scripture, ferrying the room’s gossip across the long, yawning seconds it calls a lifetime.
