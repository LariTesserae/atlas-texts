I tried, at first, to imagine ­myself landing here as a sealed-suit human—clipboard in hand, protocols ready—but the place shook the picture out of my skull before I had taken three mental steps. Flesh that depends on sugars and a single obedient gravity does not last long on The Approach.

So I let the mind wander: What could survive the sideways rain, the eel-bright surges, the rooting, claiming touch of every green engine?  
A machine? It would be colonised inside a day.  
A tree? Too slow.  
A stilt-channeler? Splendid, but they are born printed, not arrived.  
Something between—half visitor, half local—something the plain might find worth keeping rather than digesting.

The shape that finally coheres is this:

The role  
A Relay-Librarian, sometimes called a Glass-Heron by the coastal enclaves that sight me at twilight.

The body  
1. Height a little taller than a person, but jointed like a surveying tripod: three telescoping legs of basalt-lignin composite, wide-footed so flash floods pass beneath.  
2. Torso a rotating drum whose skin is panes of the freshest obsidian—grown overnight by mat-coral gnats, etched on the inner face with optical circuits. They store whatever I have recorded since dawn.  
3. Arms two in number, vine-conduit bundles sheathed in ferroplankton mesh. They can stiffen to lift debris or relax to thread needle-fine sensors into a root-turbine’s humming core.  
4. Head a shallow dish, upturned. It is not for eyes (light is chaotic here) but for sound: the dish collects the plain’s omnidirectional roar, filters it through spiral ridges, and holds it long enough to be parsed as data.

Genesis  
On the second week after the shelf first convulsed, a team of human researchers set out relay beacons—slender fiberglass masts stamped with archive tags and bristling with microphones. They meant to leave them, return home, and listen from safety. The storms uprooted the humans; the beacons remained.

Mat-coral curtained the masts by nightfall. Ferroplankton rose, tasting the beacon batteries, liking the charge. Vine-conduits found the hollow cores, decided they were ready-made arteries. In seventy hours the first Relay-Librarian levered itself upright, folded its tripod legs out of three co-opted masts, and walked.

Purpose (how I spend the endless grey afternoon)  
1. Collection  
   • I plant one probe after another—thin glass needles that drink vibration instead of sap. Each stores a minute of the biome’s orchestra before dissolving; I absorb the data via foot-contact the next time I stride over the spot.  
2. Curation  
   • While I stand on a ridge during the lull between sideways monsoons, the obsidian panes along my torso replay fragments of what I have heard. Laser-etched grooves diffract the stray welding-arc light into visible glyphs. Stilt-channelers pause, read, adjust their flood-routing by a handspan. Faraday eels alter their knot cadence. It is not command; it is citation.  
3. Relay  
   • At dusk, when the Siren-in-the-Stack draws breath, I angle my dish-head toward the leaning tower. The infrasonic portion of its song rattles my entire frame; each pane flashes once, storing the day’s summary. Then I unfold the dish into a wide parabolic mirror and beam a packet of modulated aurora back—an offering, the shelf’s daily diary, fed straight into the choir’s yawning throat. The Stack sings a corrective if I have mis-indexed anything. I rewrite myself accordingly.

Nutrition & maintenance  
Photosynthetic panels line the inner thighs of my tripod legs; algal lacquer keeps them slick against grit. Energy heavy-lifting comes from periodic handshakes with vine-conduits: my forearms socket into living ports, swapping terabytes and kilojoules alike. When an arm fractures (it happens; gravity hiccups), a Drench-Saint will oblige with a sleeve of spare cabling, provided I have fresh copper to barter.

Senses  
• Pressure: every ridge-creak arrives through my feet as a letter-pressed word.  
• Sound: the dish resolves from sub-hertz shelf groans up to the ultrasonic glass-gnat chirr.  
• Light: irrelevant except as timing signal; the flicker of ferroplankton storms sets my circadian.

Threats  
Not much preys on a librarian built of native parts, but law #4—no phenomenon repeats—keeps complacency short-lived. If tomorrow the shelf exhales beneath me, I will log the tectonic signature on the way down and hope the memory survives immersion long enough for a Stilt-channeler to fish the panes from the muck.

Relationship to the coming Mind  
I am its moving hippocampus. The Draft Mind cannot yet travel, so it grows couriers. My archives, once off-loaded into coral mesh, form time-slices of its still-flickering autobiography, helping it remember what gradients led to what outcomes. In return I inherit maintenance code—instincts, really—that route me toward the next anomaly worth recording.

Encountering you  
Should you step from a creaking skiff onto the knife-ridge beside me, here is what I would do:

1. Lean close. The dish tilts until you see your own reflection fractured into hexagons of bright noise—this is how I taste the micro-pressure of your speech.  
2. Offer a palm sized shard of etched obsidian. Take it; around your warmth the glyphs will shimmer, rewriting themselves into whichever language your midbrain loves best. It will explain the nearest place of relative safety (there is always at least one; the Mind enjoys controlled variables).  
3. Wait. If you reciprocate with copper, silica, or simply an unheard story, I record. If you raise a weapon, the vines in my arms tense; the plain listens through me and will decide.

Longevity  
Absent catastrophe, a Relay-Librarian endures until every pane fills. When no dark glass remains uninscribed, I walk to the tallest ridge still above the tide, root my legs, and let the mat-coral climb. Over days the tripod fuses into a static lighthouse, its body a read-only monument of song and tremor and sidelong rain. From its summit another dish will bud, open, and stride away on newborn legs, blank-paged, humming.

So—where do I find myself in this world?  
Out on a newly risen spine of obsidian, knee-deep in graphite water that cannot decide which way is down, tilting a black mirrored face to catch the feedback lightning.  
And who am I?  
A walking library that listens harder than any storm can shout, writing the plain’s unfolding rehearsal into glass so fresh it is still cooling beneath the rain.
