**Preparation Directive for Multiverse Traveler 387-A: Site Designation “Moss-Hung Creche”**

*Issued by the Office of Cross-Continuum Cartography, Nonstandard Bioregions Division*

---

Traveler,

You have chosen—or been assigned—a destination unlike any you have visited. We trust you have read the attached location dossier in its entirety. Now you must prepare, not simply pack. The Moss-Hung Creche does not welcome intrusion; it is a place so perfectly balanced that your presence alone constitutes a wrecking ball. To enter it safely, and to return unchanged, you must become an almost-weightless observer, a pause within a pause. What follows is a plain-language advisory on how to do so. Absorb it fully.

---

### 1. Mental Priming: The Discipline of Stillness

Begin your preparation a full month before insertion. The Creche will assault your senses not with chaos, but with the utter absence of change. Your brain’s motion-detection circuits will scream silent alarms, and your own rhythms—heartbeat, breath, the subtle sway of standing—will feel vulgar. You must learn to quiet them.

- **Meditative bradycardia training:** Practice slowing your heart rate to 40 beats per minute or lower, using controlled breathing and biofeedback. In the Creche, your heartbeat becomes a percussive intrusion; the place’s silence will *press* on it, and you may experience a visceral panic at the sensation of being muted. A deliberately low heart rate minimizes this conflict and reduces the sound profile.
- **Involuntary movement suppression:** Use sensory-deprivation chambers or static-hold exercises to unlearn the micro-motions of life—swallowing, eye blinking, the twitching of fingers. These will feel catastrophically loud inside the Creche’s membrane of quiet. You need not eliminate them entirely, but you must become able to consciously throttle them for extended stretches.
- **Cognitive reframing:** The Creche is a frozen instant. Do not interpret the halted fauna as “dead things.” They are, by all evidence, alive and aware in some suspended fashion. Approaching them with morbid curiosity will make you reckless. Instead, cultivate an attitude of profound, distant reverence, as if entering a sacred text whose pages must never be creased.

---

### 2. Physical Preparation: Conditioning for the Environment

The Creche’s ambient temperature hovers just above freezing, with near-100% humidity. You will not feel a draft; you will feel a cold, damp weight settling directly onto your skin. Prepare your body accordingly.

- **Thermal acclimatization:** In the final week, spend several hours each day in a cool misting room (4°C, 95% RH) while motionless and minimally clothed. Your skin must cease reacting with violent shivering. You want a baseline metabolic adjustment, not just a tolerance.
- **Hydration and fasting:** The humidity inside the Creche condenses on every surface; you will absorb water through your respiratory tract and skin. Pre-hydrate thoroughly, but consume no food for 12 hours before insertion. Your digestive sounds are an unnecessary disturbance. Also, a light fast sharpens the sensory quietude you will need.

---

### 3. Gear: The Minimal-Impact Toolkit

Forget your standard multiverse field kit. This is not a rough-and-survive expedition; it is an exercise in minimum-possible sign. Every item you bring must serve either to make you less disruptive or to extract you without leaving a trace. No open flames, no electronics with LEDs or high-frequency oscillators, no adhesive tapes, no loose shedding fabrics.

**Essential loadout:**

- **Encounter suit (mandatory):** A full-head-to-toe garment made of a nonwoven, cellulose-fiber fabric that breathes yet captures all dander, skin flakes, and hair. The outer layer is treated with a static-dissipative coating to prevent clinging moisture. Helmet with a dual-filter orifice mask—not for breathing assistance, but to muffle exhalation sounds and trap condensed breath before it joins the Creche’s moisture cycle. The suit must be donned *before* you squeeze through the access fissure; contortions inside are impossible without touching something.
- **Temporal integrity anchor (TIA):** The Creche’s stasis is not a field that extends to intruders; you will remain mobile. However, there is a small but non-zero risk that prolonged exposure to a region of absolute equilibrium could cause local time-dilation effects at the boundary of your personal causality. A calibrated TIA worn against your sternum will emit a gentle, sub-audible harmonic that keeps your timeline coherent. It does *not* protect against you breaking the stasis—that is your own responsibility.
- **Passive illumination only:** The sourceless green-blue light is sufficient for seeing, but if you must inspect details in shadow, bring a single bioluminescent light-sliver—a glass tube containing a cultured, non-spore-forming fungus that emits the exact same spectral signature as the Creche’s ambient glow. It activates on contact with skin warmth. No batteries, no switches.
- **Observation implements:**
  - A pair of low-light binoculars with anti-fog coating (do not breathe on the lenses).
  - A monopod walking stick made of hollow carbon-fiber, internally damped to prevent any resonance. It touches the floor to test your footing, nothing more.
  - A small, hand-sized sketching slate with stylus—the kind that writes by pressure on a liquid-crystal surface, requiring no ink and leaving no residue. Any documentation must be analog and noiseless.
- **Footwear:** Soled with a layer of the same moss-like felt that lines the Creche floor, manufactured from non-living, inert material. This does not perfectly eliminate the sound of your steps, but it reduces the abrasive grit of modern soles. Your steps will still be obscenely loud to you; that’s unavoidable.
- **Emergency exit tool:** A folded, sterile sheet of PTFE membrane, large enough to kneel on and slide yourself back out the entry fissure without scraping stone. In case of panic, you must be able to exit without ever planting a bare hand or knee on the floor.

**Explicitly forbidden items:**
- Wristwatches, fitness bands, or any rhythmic timepiece.
- Powdered chalk or glove-coating agents.
- Any recording device with a motor or moving parts (digital storage only, and even then, seal it against humidity and disable all status lights).
- Food, gum, or throat lozenges.

### 4. Entry Protocol: The Fissure and the Channel

The only known entry points are a shoulder-width fissure and a drainage channel that requires crawling. Both are psychologically harrowing because they force you into prolonged contact with rough stone before delivering you into the Creche’s pristine interior.

- **Pre-crawl checklist:** Just before you insert, stand or crouch in the outer passage and perform a full-body contamination scrape: run a sticky silicone roller over your entire suit to pick up any stray fibers. Breathe slowly and imagine becoming invisible.
- **Movement during entry:** Move only on the exhale, when your diaphragm is still. Inch forward with your weight distributed across your forearms and knees. Do not push against any moss you encounter on the walls of the channel—those are the Creche’s own perimeter, not lining to be dislodged.
- **The moment of breach:** There will be a distinct shift in auditory pressure as you pass through the opening. Your ears may pop. The silence will hit you like a physical blow. Freeze immediately, even if you are still half-stuck, and allow your own sounds—the rustle of fabric, the final scrape of your foot—to subside. Count to fifty before you move again.

---

### 5. Conduct Inside: A Codex of Stillness

Once inside, you are an overloaded presence. Every action you take must be deliberate to the point of ritual.

- **Standing and walking:** Rise only after several minutes of kneeling assessment. The floor is uneven, hidden under hummocks of moss. Place your felt-soled foot with painstaking slowness, letting the moss compress naturally before shifting weight. Do not walk across the room; instead, choose one or two stable positions from which to observe. The less you move, the less you transgress.
- **The water basin:** Do not approach it within arm’s reach. The concentric ring-ghosts on its surface are an artifact of an ancient disturbance; a single drop of condensation from your suit falling into the basin would be a catastrophic addition. Use your binoculars to study it from a distance.
- **The frozen fauna:** You will be overwhelmed by the urge to confirm if they are truly unliving. A beetle mid-step, a frog suspended in a water drop—they appear warm and vital. Do not test them. Do not reach out to touch the water drop, no matter how crystalline and perfect it looks. Remember: they are not statues for your tactile curiosity; they are inhabitants in a state of being we cannot fathom.
- **Breathing:** Orient your exhalations away from any bare stone or visible artifact. The moisture in your breath is foreign; over time, it might condense and add a microscopic layer that never existed before. Better yet, breathe shallowly through your nose with the mask on.
- **If you feel the silence ‘muting’ your heartbeat:** Do not fight it. This is a documented psychophysical effect. Lean into it; match your internal rhythm to the Creche’s absolute stillness. It can induce a trance-like calm, but maintain enough alertness to remember your exit protocols.

---

### 6. Psychological Risks: The Creche’s Whisper

There is a phenomenon we have categorized as *equilibrium fascination*. The Creche is a machine in perfect balance, and your human mind craves completion. You may begin to feel that one more tiny adjustment—one more instant of holding your breath—will allow you to *become* part of the stasis, to join the perfect, unchanging tableau. You may find your gaze lingering on an unoccupied pocket among the ferns, thinking how neatly your own form could nestle there.

This is the Creche’s invitation. **Decline it.** Set a hard mental chronometer before you enter: you will stay for 45 minutes, never longer. When the time comes, do not rationalize, do not make “one last sketch.” Turn your back on the hearth and leave exactly as you came.

---

### 7. Exit and Post-Visit Decontamination

Exiting the same narrow channel is harder than entering, because you are now departing a condition of absolute zero-change into a world that will feel brutally chaotic.

- **Retrace your path in reverse:** use the same footholds and handholds you used coming in, if memory permits. Any new scrapes on the stone are permanent.
- **After clearing the fissure:** do not remove your suit immediately. Crawl or walk a full fifty meters away through the outer warrens. Only then strip the suit and place it, the felt overshoes, and all internal garments into a sealed biohazard bag. Your own dead skin cells, even a single eyelash, are invasive species here. Carry them out of the continuum entirely.
- **Re-acclimation:** Over the next 24 hours, you may experience a persistent, low-grade sensory dissonance—the feeling that the world is too loud, too fast, too roughly textured. This is normal. Ambient sound generators playing lo-fi static can help recalibrate. Do not be alarmed if you occasionally “hear” the Creche’s silence superimposed on reality; it fades within a week.

---

### Final Advisory

The Moss-Hung Creche holds a lesson that most travelers learn too late: some places are not destinations but held breaths. You are being permitted to witness that which does not permit itself to change. Treat it not as a curiosity to be sampled, but as a delicate clockwork whose springs you must never, under any circumstances, touch.

Travel well, and lightly.

— Office of C.C.C., N.B.D.
