It is crucial to understand: you are not visiting a place. You are being granted an audience with a composite being. This grove, this chapel, is a slow-motion entity born from a marriage of abandoned human intent and vegetal patience. Treat it as such. Your preparation is not just about survival; it’s about etiquette.

### Philosophical Preparation: The Mindset of a Guest

Before you pack a single item, you must adjust your perception of time and agency. This location is not a ruin; it is a finished, ongoing project. It is a closed-loop metabolism operating on a timescale far slower than your own.

1.  **Embrace Stillness:** Your heartbeat is the fastest rhythm present. The location’s tempo is set by the slow plop of a water droplet, the tick of an ancient pump, the decade-long creep of a root. Rushing, frantic movements, or a desire to "get things done" are not just out of place—they are a form of noise pollution. Your primary task upon arrival is not to explore, but to listen and decelerate. Find the rhythm and match it.

2.  **Recontextualize "Machine":** The hum, the click-hiss, the electrical crackle are not industrial noise. They are the grove’s heartbeats, its breath, its nervous impulses. The pump is a metallic heart; the corroded copper coil is a nerve lattice. Do not think of the technology as separate from the biology. They are a single, hybrid anatomy. Your own gadgets are crude prosthetics here. Show them the same respect you would show your own body, not as tools, but as integrated organs of the place.

3.  **Recognize Your Insignificance:** The human presence here is memorialized only in small, swallowed relics—a brass fitting as a holdfast, a glass shard in a wooden knot like a saint’s bone in a reliquary. You are larger, noisier, faster, and infinitely more temporary. You are a fleeting thought passing through a mind that thinks in seasons. Your goal is to be a coherent, respectful thought, not a disruptive seizure.

### Physical Preparation: The Craft of the Traveler

Your gear must facilitate a passive, non-invasive visit. You are not a conqueror; you are a diver entering a living medium.

#### Clothing: The Second Skin

Your outfit has a single purpose: to manage the relentless, warm moisture without introducing harmful foreign agents.

- **Base Layer:** A thin, full-body suit of untreated, unbleached silk or fine merino wool. No synthetic blends (polyester, nylon). Synthetics leach microplastics and chemical residues into the weep-water. The suit will become saturated. Accept this. Its job is to keep a warm layer of water against your skin to prevent thermal shock and protect you from the slightest chafing against the living weave of the ground.
- **Outer Layer:** A loose tunic and trousers of heavy, undyed, raw linen. This fabric grows stronger when wet, breathes, and will not off-gas. The texture is important; it’s similar to the fibrous weave of the floor, allowing you to blend tactilely.
- **Footwear:** None. This is non-negotiable. You must feel the ground-flesh with your soles. Your feet must be meticulously prepared. For two weeks prior, soak them daily and use a pumice stone to remove all calluses and dead skin; you need soft, sensory feet. Trim nails perfectly flush. Before entering the boundary, wash your feet in clean water from your own supply. The goal is to experience the springy mattress of air roots directly and to leave behind not a single flake of foreign skin.
- **Head Covering:** A simple hood of the same linen, to keep your own oils from your hair contained.

#### Carried Gear: The Little You Bring

Everything must be inert, non-polluting, and silent. No electrical devices are permitted to *emit* a signal, sound, or unnatural light.

- **The Field-Laboratory Kit (Carried in a waxed, unpainted canvas satchel):**
    - **Magnifying Lens:** A simple loupe of optical glass set in raw, unfinished wood. For observing, not recording.
    - **Light Source:** A small, hand-cranked far-red LED torch. Not white light, which is an aggression here. Far-red (around 730nm) is the color of deep shade to a plant; it causes no photomorphogenic shock. Use it sparingly, only if absolutely necessary.
    - **Sample Vessels:** A half-dozen small jars made of uncoated, handblown glass with raw cork stoppers. If you must collect a tear of the weep-water or a fallen, naturally detached leaf, it goes here. Never pluck, break, or cut.
    - **Writing Kit:** Bound, unbleached cotton paper and a charcoal stick. Graphite pencils are a foreign mineral; their industrial precision is an insult. Charcoal is a burnt offering from another tree; it is a more comprehensible medium.
    - **Personal Quarantine Tape:** A roll of gentle, biodegradable paper surgical tape.
- **On Your Person:** Nothing. Empty your pockets of lint, forgotten receipts, bits of plastic. The tape is for one purpose: if you have any small open wound, no matter how minor—a papercut, a hangnail—a single layer of this tape must be placed over it before entry. You do not bleed into this place. Your blood is a chaotic biological cocktail. The tape is a seal of quarantine.

### Protocol for Entry and Existence

Every action is a ritual of non-disturbance.

1.  **The Vestibule:** Stop at the boundary where the dense mat gives way to the surrounding water. Do not cross it yet. Stand still for a subjective eternity—at least ten minutes by the slow tick of the pump you hear. Breathe deeply. Let the green taste coat your throat. Let the moisture bead on your linen. Let your pulse slow to meet the cadence of the dripping water. You are syncing yourself to the bio-machine’s frequency.

2.  **The First Contact:** When you step onto the living floor, do so with the deliberation of a carefully placed kiss. Your foot should compress the moss and rhizome mattress and then be still, allowing you to feel the slow rebound. Pause between each step. Your path is not a straight line; it is a negotiation. The weave underfoot is a conversation. You answer by stepping lightly and listening with your soles.

3.  **Observation, Not Manipulation:** You may look. You may kneel to bring your eye close to the fungal glow on the north wall, but do not touch it. The translucent vein-tubes carrying fluid from the pump are to be watched, not traced with a finger. If you feel the faint current tingle the air, let the hairs on your own arm stand erect in response. It is a greeting. Acknowledge it and move on. Do not sit on the corded root-bench for more than a few minutes; your prolonged body heat is a local fever.

4.  **The Sacred Silence:** Do not speak. The soundscape is the voice of the entity—the sixty-cycle hum, the arrhythmic plop, the hissing breath. Your voice is an intrusive, dissonant frequency. If you must communicate with a companion, pre-arrange a set of hand signals. Coughing or sneezing is a violent act; muffle it completely in your linen hood.

5.  **The Relics:** You will see the signs of past human failure: the half-buried insulator, the reliquary-knot holding a glass shard. Do not touch them. Do not remove them. They are not artifacts to be studied; they are calcified memories within the organism. They are a part of the slow negotiation. To take them is to tear out a piece of its history.

### Departure: The Slow Withdrawal

You leave as you entered: slowly, deliberately.

1.  **The Final Standing:** Before you leave, return to the first chamber and stand for another ten minutes. This is a gesture of gratitude, a final syncing of your now-calmed pulse with the chthonic machinery.
2.  **Retrace Your Steps:** Leave via the same path, placing your feet in the very same spots if possible, to minimize the total area of your compression-event.
3.  **Crossing the Boundary:** As you step from the living weave back onto the open, saturated water-ground, pause. You are exiting the being. The heavy, warm, green-tasting air will suddenly feel like an exhalation. Carry the taste and the patient rhythm with you. You will have been, for a brief moment, a coherent, respectful thought in a mind of leaf and iron. Do not dilute the memory with haste. Spend the next hour in silence, allowing the moisture to dry naturally from your clothing, letting the experience integrate into your own being.
