**Visitor’s Preparatory Brief: Locality K-19b (“The Hollow of Unwinding Time”)**

*Issued by the Office of Exoquietary Protocols, Bureau of Multiversal Conservation. All travelers must review, sign, and demonstrate compliance before translocation.*

Your destination is not a landscape; it is a photograph of a single moment that has been exposed for two million years. It breathes in centuries, erodes in aeons, and notices you as a thunderclap of moisture, heat, and intent. To visit K-19b is to be a temporary, violent event in a place that has forgotten what change feels like. Your preparation is therefore not about survival — you are in far greater danger of damaging it than it is of harming you. You must train yourself to become an absence, a bubble of equilibrium that passes through without leaving a trace.

Below is everything you need to know and obtain. Read it slowly. Let the silence of the place seep into your planning.

---

### I. ENVIRONMENTAL PARAMETERS — WHAT YOU ARE WALKING INTO

1. **Atmosphere & Air Quality**  
   - Permanent overcast of high-altitude dust; no direct sun, only a directionless umber gloom. Illumination is a constant deep charcoal grey, barely adequate for human vision after prolonged adaptation. Under the hollow’s slight overhang, it becomes near-total velvet blackness.  
   - Air is extremely dry (relative humidity functionally zero), warm and static at 32°C. It tastes faintly metallic, with a trace of hydrogen sulphide from deep seeps. The H₂S concentration is non-lethal but sub-acute; prolonged respiration would cause headache and mucosal irritation. More critically, **the atmosphere is so thin that it can no longer lift grains of sand** — atmospheric pressure is estimated at 0.3–0.4 atm, far below human tolerance. **Unprotected breathing is impossible; you will lose consciousness within a minute.**  
   - The air is sterile of microbial life above the ground, but carries a fine, ancient dust that is chemically aggressive in its dryness.

2. **Temperature**  
   - Geothermal ground warmth sustains a uniform 32°C air temperature. There is no day/night fluctuation, no breeze, no convective variation. The warmth feels like a weight, a steady pressure against the skin.

3. **Ground & Substrate**  
   - The surface is a fragile, anhydrous crust of gypsum and anhydrite, flaking in paper-thin leaves that crumble to powder under a finger’s pressure. The floor of the hollow is patterned with desiccation polygons the size of your hand, separated by hairline cracks.  
   - In these cracks reside the **lichen-stains**: flat, filamentous chemosynthetic organisms that spread perhaps a centimetre per century. They lie like desiccated spiderwebs against the mineral floor. Each one is a living entity tens of thousands of years old, metabolically poised on the edge of stasis. **Even the lightest footstep will crush them, ending an unbroken continuity older than human civilization.**  
   - Overhang areas bear a velvet of dormant bacterial spores, similarly ancient, held in suspension by absolute aridity.  
   - The crust records disturbances for decades: your footprint will remain visible for a century before salt creep erases it.

4. **Acoustic Environment**  
   - Sound exists only as an hourly, microscopic crepitation of salt crystals. The background silence is total — you will hear your own pulse, the rush of blood, the minute creaks of your joints. Any noise you introduce will seem apocalyptic in this context.  
   - The place has a psychological gravity that amplifies quietude. Prepare for sensory disorientation; many visitors report a profound, almost religious stillness.

5. **Artifact**  
   - At the centre of the hollow rests a tripod of gypsum shards, thumb-high, fused to the crust by recrystallized salts. It is the only known evidence of early sapient tool-use in this universe. Its meaning is unknown. Its fragility is absolute; even a breath could topple it.

### II. PREPARATION — GEAR, SUIT, AND MOBILITY

You cannot enter K-19b with ordinary hiking equipment, a curiosity, and a canteen. You must become a self-contained, zero-emission, contact-free presence. The following system is mandatory. All items must be tested in a simulated anhydrous low-pressure chamber before departure.

#### A. Full-Environment Isolation Suit (FEIS) — “Second Skin” Configuration

**Requirements:**
- **Gas-tight envelope:** Positive-pressure but externally ventless. Exhaled air must pass through a closed-circuit rebreather with CO₂ scrubbing and oxygen make-up. No exhalations, no moisture-laden air escapes.
- **External moisture emission: zero.** The suit must incorporate an integral water-vapour trap on all perspiration surfaces; internal humidity recycled via a desiccant wheel. All suit joints must be bone-dry.
- **Thermal neutrality:** Outer suit surface shall maintain precisely 32°C via liquid-circulation garment and embedded micro-heatpipes. You are slightly warmer (37°C core), so active cooling is required to avoid radiating a thermal plume that would disturb the equilibrium of the shallow depression.
- **Dust and particulate containment:** Suit exterior must be ultra-smooth, with no fabric weaves or exposed hook-and-loop. All closures covered by sealed, disposable over-layers. Before entry, the entire suit must undergo a decontamination cycle (plasma-sterilization and ionized nitrogen blow-off) to eliminate any Earth-origin microbes or skin flakes. You will dress inside a portable sterile transfer capsule.
- **Integrated nutrient/fluid supply:** No eating or drinking inside the suit; a 24-hour metabolic reserve via intravenous line or transdermal patch is acceptable. Any liquid waste must be fully contained.
- **Low-light augmented vision:** Helmet visor equipped with photon-amplification (not thermal; you do not want to see the ground’s uniform 32°C as a flat wash). A head-up display overlays a false-colour map of ground texture to highlight the delicate lichen webs without visible light. If you must use active illumination, a single 660 nm (deep red) LED at minimum intensity, aimed only downward at your own hovering platform — never at the tripod or the lichens. Red light is least likely to disturb the chemosynthetic bacteria (though they do not photosynthesize, we respect the principle of minimal photonic intrusion).
- **Acoustic suppression:** All suit subsystems must operate below 10 dB(A) at 1 metre. Use passive, noiseless circulation fans (membrane-based gas exchange is preferred) and eliminate any audible alarms; replace with haptic feedback on the wrist.

#### B. Mobility — Zero-Contact Ground Interface

Walking is unacceptable. Even a broad-soled pad distributes pressure insufficiently to avoid crushing the polygon crust and the hairline-crack lichens. You will instead use a **Personal Hover Platform (PHP)** , configured for ultra-low ground pressure:

- **Propulsion:** Silent electro-aerodynamic thrusters (ionic wind, no moving parts) or magneto-levitation if the substrate is naturally ferromagnetic (likely not, given the salts). The platform must maintain a stable levitation altitude of 15–20 cm — high enough to clear the delicate floor, low enough to see. Redundant ultrasonic ground-follow sensors, calibrated to ignore dust motes.
- **Footprint:** No physical skids; the platform must not set down. If emergency landing is required, deploy a sacrificial, broad, feather-soft pad of non-woven aerogel that spreads load to <10 Pa — but even this will disturb spore dust and leave a faint impression. Use only in dire circumstances.
- **Manoeuvring:** The hollow is a “modest room” in width, perhaps 4–5 metres across, with a subtle overhang. The PHP must be capable of precise, slow translation (hand controls or suit-integrated gesture). Practice in a mock-up of the hollow’s dimensions. You will need to position yourself to view the tripod from various angles without crossing over the lichen-stains.
- **Noise:** The PHP must emit no audible hum. Test in an anechoic chamber; target <5 dB(A) at hover.

Alternatively, if PHP technology is not available, a **tethered suspension system** can be considered. You would deploy two silent winch anchors on the plain above the hollow (on the wind-scoured ridge, not within the depression) and traverse a lightweight cable. You would then descend in a seated harness from the cable, never touching the ground. This method introduces less mechanical noise but offers less positional freedom. The winches and anchors must be placed on the crust *outside* the hollow, where the mineral surface is less biologically sensitive, but still with padded, no-crush ground mats.

#### C. Life Support Duration and Contingencies

Plan for a visit of no more than 2 hours inside the hollow proper. The psychological weight of the place, combined with suit confinement, can induce claustrophobia or temporal dissociation. Carry backup oxygen and a distress beacon. However, the beacon must be completely silent and non-luminous in standby; it should only activate if you manually trigger it. Any automatic transmission would be an unforgivable acoustic violation.

Your suit and PHP shall be powered by high-density capacitors, not fuel cells, to eliminate any vapour emission.

### III. PROTOCOLS FOR VISITATION — THE ART OF BEING INVISIBLE

#### A. Pre-Entry Decontamination and Staging

1. **Approach:** You will be translocated to a point 200 metres west of the hollow, beyond the line of sight. The translocation burst itself is a disturbance; the Bureau will calibrate for minimum electromagnetic and sonic shock.
2. **Staging area:** On the flat plain, outside the depression, you may set down a small, sterile mat for pre-entry checks. Do not walk; the PHP can rest in idle hover a few centimetres above the ground. Perform final suit integrity checks. All equipment must be wiped with anhydrous solvent and then vacuumed.
3. **Mental preparation:** Sit in silence for 10 minutes. Slow your heart rate. Listen to the absence. Remind yourself: you are a bubble of heat and moisture. Your very presence threatens two million years of equilibrium. The hollow does not need you; you are a brief, tolerated anomaly.

#### B. Entering the Hollow

- Hover at the rim, then slowly descend into the concavity. The hollow is shallow — a subtle sag in the plain — so the angle is slight. Move at a pace no faster than 1 cm per second. Your entry will displace a tiny volume of air; this is unavoidable, but slow motion minimizes turbulence that might disturb spore dust or cause salt flake detachment.
- As you slip under the overhang, the darkness deepens. Let your amplified visor adjust rather than switching on active lights. The tripod will appear as a faint silhouette. Do not approach within 1 metre. The tripod is tiny; a close hover risk vibrating the crust. Observe from a respectful distance.
- Maintain absolute silence. Do not speak, even through intercom. If you must communicate with an off-site team, use a text-based optical link through the suit’s external fibre port, with the transmission cable laid outside the hollow.

#### C. Conduct Within the Hollow

- **Noise discipline:** Breathe shallowly and evenly. Do not cough. If a cough is imminent, activate suit’s internal sound suppression bladder (a temporary inflatable muffler around the head). Any sound you make will echo internally in your suit but must not be transmitted externally.
- **Light discipline:** Use only passive light amplification. If you must document the tripod, use a single brief flash of 660 nm light, diffused to a wide angle, and never directly at the ground. Do not use white light or flash — the spores have never experienced visible-wavelength photons, and while they are dormant, we do not know the phototactic sensitivities of the lichen’s chemosynthetic bacteria.
- **Thermal discipline:** Minimize movements. Your suit’s outer temperature must remain constant. Avoid shading the ground; do not position yourself between the tripod and the ambient diffuse sky glow, because your silhouette would create a cold shadow that might take days to re-equilibrate.
- **No sample collection:** This is not a research mission. Do not attempt to touch, scan with active instruments, or take micro-samples of the crust, lichens, or tripod. Even a laser rangefinder could impart a tiny photon pressure; passive photogrammetry from the hover position is permitted if you pre-coat your camera lens with an anti-reflection layer to avoid any stray light.
- **Observe the tripod as you would a sleeping child of another species: with reverence and distance.** Its meaning is lost, but the gesture remains. Do not attempt to interpret it with your presence; simply witness.

#### D. Exiting

- Withdraw by exactly the same route you entered, in reverse, at the same glacial pace. Do not turn abruptly; plan a smooth arc. Your PHP’s thrusters must maintain a consistent, low impulse to avoid kicking up any salt dust that may have settled on the overhang.
- Once clear of the hollow, ascend to the plain and retreat to your staging area. Immediately seal your suit’s outer layer in a containment bag to quarantine any stray dust or spores — though in all likelihood, your precautions will have prevented any pickup.

### IV. PSYCHOLOGICAL AND SENSORY PREPARATION

K-19b is a sensory deprivation chamber on a planetary scale, but one filled with subtle, ancient presences. You may experience:

- **Temporal distortion:** In the absolute stillness, your internal clock will drift. Wear a reliable chronometer with haptic pulse every 10 minutes to remain anchored.
- **Hyperacusis:** The sound of your own heartbeat will become monstrous. Some travelers report hearing the salt crepitation as a kind of seismic event. Accept this; use it to centre yourself in the vastness of time.
- **Emotive response:** The tripod, for all its simplicity, has been described as overwhelmingly poignant — a message from a mind that may have been extinct for epochs. Allow yourself to feel it, but do not let emotion translate into movement. Grief or wonder can cause involuntary gestures; your suit will restrict them, but your breathing may change. Practice emotional regulation under isolation.
- **The urge to leave a mark:** Many visitors report a primal desire to add their own small stone, to participate in the narrative. Resist this absolutely. Your visit is already punctuation enough; your absence is the greatest gift you can give.

Pre-visitation simulation in a full-immersion VR replica of the hollow is strongly recommended. Spend at least three hours in that simulation, in suit, to acclimate to the darkness and the silence. Learn to be still.

### V. POST-VISIT DEBRIEFING — AND THE AFTERMATH

When you return, you will be debriefed and your suit’s exterior analysed. If any trace of the hollow’s unique mineralogy or spores is found, it will be archived and returned via a non-invasive drone drop. The Bureau’s conservation ethic demands that nothing be taken from K-19b, not even a grain of dust.

Remember that your footprints, if any were inadvertently left, will last for perhaps a century. The lichens you did not crush will continue their centimetre-per-century spread. The tripod will stand, fused to the salt, until the next geothermal cycle reshapes the continent. The hollow will erase all evidence of you, as it has erased everything else. And that is as it should be.

You are not visiting a place. You are visiting a moment that refuses to end. Prepare accordingly: with humility, with silence, and with the deepest possible care.
