**Interdimensional Field Advisory: Site EX-74 (“The Static Engine”)**

**TRAVELER PREPARATION PROTOCOL**
**Classification:** Amber-Static / Closed-Loop Mechanical Ecosystem  
**Hazard Tier:** Moderate Physical, High Psychological  
**Minimum Recommended Kit Level:** C2 (Environmental Isolation + Acoustic Dampening)

You are an ordinary human multiverse traveler. That means you have access to cross-reality transit but your biology remains baseline—vulnerable to heat, particulate, noise, and the slow erosion of sanity that comes from standing inside a category error. The following preparation is not optional. The Static Engine does not accommodate. It calibrates.

---

### I. ENVIRONMENTAL SUMMARY
- **Atmosphere:** Breathable in composition but stagnant, super-dry (near-zero humidity), and heavy with suspended metal fines, oxidized particulates, and trace ozone. Inhalation over time *will* cause respiratory irritation and heavy-metal exposure.  
- **Temperature:** Steady 1.8 (≈38–40°C / 100–104°F). A feverish, unwavering heat with no evaporative cooling possible.  
- **Acoustics:** Constant layered hum at 2.0 (≈85–90 dB SPL) with a strong subsonic component (10–30 Hz). Prolonged exposure will damage hearing and can induce nausea, disorientation, and chest resonance.  
- **Light:** Ambient twilight from bioluminescent nodules and mineral phosphorescence, but visibility is poor in the dense thicket. Sharp edges are everywhere and hard to see.  
- **Biological/Chemical:** No free water. All surfaces are coated with oxidative blooms, anhydrous crystal spicules, and a fine metallic grit. Fauna are clockwork automata carrying galvanic currents; their surfaces are conductive and often hot.  
- **Temporal/Stasis Risk:** The site is a perfect equilibrium. Any introduced variable (moisture, heat signature, foreign material) may trigger a systemic recalibration. This could be benign—or it could treat you as a perturbation to be nullified.

### II. PHYSICAL PREPARATION

#### A. Respiratory Protection
A simple filter mask is insufficient. You need a **full-seal respirator with P100/HEPA filtration and an activated carbon layer**. The carbon layer will handle ozone and volatile metallic compounds. The seal must be absolute—the fine dust is pervasive and electrostatically charged; it will find any gap. Carry an extra set of filters. If your respirator fogs from exhalation moisture, the fog will freeze into a saline crust within minutes but won’t dissipate. A small diaphragm speaking port is acceptable but not required; the hum will swallow your voice anyway.

#### B. Hearing Protection
You cannot simply block the hum—the subsonic component travels through bone. Use **active noise-cancelling earmuffs** calibrated for 30–120 Hz attenuation, plus formable silicone earplugs beneath them for the higher frequencies. Double protection is necessary to bring the perceived level down to survivable thresholds. Even so, you will *feel* the hum in your chest. There is no eliminating that; desensitization is part of psychological preparation. Do not rely on noise-cancelling that generates anti-phase only; passive mass-loaded barriers are essential.

#### C. Thermal Management
No amount of breathable clothing will cool you in a bone-dry 40°C environment with no wind. You must bring an **active cooling vest**—a circulation garment powered by a portable pump and a heat-exchange pack. The pack should dump heat to a phase-change material (PCM) rated for long-duration high-ambient use. Avoid evaporative vests; you cannot spare the moisture and the system will absorb humidity unpredictably. Wear loose, tear-resistant outer layers of natural fiber (cotton or wool) to prevent direct contact with hot metal, and sturdy, high-shaft boots with puncture-resistant soles. The floor is a scree of razors and broken gears.

#### D. Light Source
Standard white light is jarring in a low-light equilibrium. It may also inadvertently stimulate photosensitive fungi or fauna. Use a **red/amber headlamp** at the lowest usable brightness, ideally with a diffuser. The light should not pulse or flicker—steady only. Flicker patterns might inadvertently mimic the creatures’ communication rhythms and draw undesired interaction. A backup chem-light (red glowstick) is acceptable and contains no electronics that could inductively couple with the chamber’s ambient currents.

#### E. Physical Protection
Leather gloves—thick, gauntlet-style—are mandatory. The columns are covered in micro-burrs, gall-like nodules, and sharp flanges. A simple brush against a pipe could flay skin. Consider lightweight knee pads; in the dense thicket you may need to crouch or crawl to avoid the branching conduits. A hard-shell climbing helmet is advisable: the ceiling is a tangle of razor-edged heat exchangers at uneven heights, and a startled raise of the head could be a cranial event.

### III. PHYSIOLOGICAL PREPARATION

**Hydrate aggressively** before entry. Once inside, you cannot drink without breaking the respirator seal, and the air will dehydrate you through any exposed skin. Apply a thin layer of petroleum jelly or a non-aqueous barrier cream to all skin that may be exposed (neck, wrists, around mask) to slow moisture loss. Do not spit, urinate, or bleed within the chamber if you can avoid it—added moisture is a disturbance. The chamber’s “young erosion” (1.2) means it has never known free water; introducing it could trigger a corrosive cascade or unpredictable galvanic activity.

Eat a solid, slow-burning meal beforehand, but ensure your gut is calm. The subsonic frequencies can stimulate peristalsis unnervingly; you do not want to add digestive distress to an already alien baseline.

Acclimatize to the dark and to low-level vibration in the hours before entry, if possible. Sit in a warm, quiet room and practice slow, rhythmic breathing. The hum inside will attempt to entrain your breath to its own micro-fluctuations; conscious resistance can prevent lightheadedness.

### IV. PSYCHOLOGICAL PREPARATION

The Static Engine registers **2.2 on the Weirdness Index**. That’s not “jump scare” weird; it’s the slow, creeping realization that you are an extraneous factor in a system that has achieved metaphysical closure. Symptoms to expect:
- **Sensory flattening:** The unchanging hum and twilight will degrade your time perception. You may feel minutes as hours or vice versa.
- **Anthropocentric dissonance:** The fauna do not acknowledge you. Their clockwork behaviors imply intelligence without consciousness, purpose without intent. You will not be able to anthropomorphize them, and that absence can feel like a void.
- **Stasis anxiety:** The equilibrium is absolute. The dust you disturb will resettle into its original patterns. Your tracks will vanish. This can trigger derealization. Remind yourself repeatedly that you are the variable, and you intend to leave.

**Mental countermeasures:**
- Bring a small, non-electronic timepiece with a soft tick. The tick is a reminder of sequential time; the chamber has none.
- Carry a personal anchor object from baseline reality—a smooth stone, a photo, a scent strip—to touch and confirm your origin.
- Rehearse a short mantra or song in your mind to maintain a linear cognitive thread. Subvocalize it rhythmically to counter the hum’s entrainment.

### V. BEHAVIORAL PROTOCOLS

**Movement:** Move deliberately, always scanning for the sharpest protrusions. The columns are close; you will need to step sideways or straddle pipes. Each footfall must be placed with care to minimize crushing the metallic scree—not for silence (the hum masks everything) but to avoid disturbing the precise arrangement of detritus. The dust’s return to pattern suggests the system has a memory. Leaving deep footprints might be interpreted as a defect requiring correction.

**Interaction with Fauna:**
- **Do not touch the creatures.** Their surfaces may be electrically live. Some are warm; some carry small charges. The larger cat-sized entities with heat-sink ridges are likely absorbing and redistributing thermal load—disturbing one could alter local temperature balance and cause a swarm response.
- If a creature approaches you, remain still. They are calibrating. Their pause is a sensor sweep. If you do not move, they will typically resume their paths. Do not attempt to feed them; they do not metabolize organic matter.
- The “worship” behavior around the parent-machine is likely a maintenance ritual. Do not intervene. Observe from a distance of at least two meters.

**The Parent-Machine:** The cuboid structure with the chimney is the heart. It emits the subsonic core frequency. Do not touch the access panels, even if they are ajar. The glow from the seams is heat and possibly radiation in the near-infrared; a thermal camera may pick up hot spots, but do not bring high-output electronic devices too close—the electromagnetic field could interfere with the delicate galvanic balance. A simple mechanical watch is safe; a smartphone or tablet is not.

**Speaking:** If you must communicate with a companion (not recommended to enter alone, see below), use prearranged hand signals or low-power bone-conduction radios that do not emit airborne EM. Your voice will be swallowed anyway, but the humidity of your breath could disrupt micro-flora.

### VII. PARTNER PROTOCOL

Do **not** enter the Static Engine alone. The psychological toll makes solo travel exceptionally dangerous. One partner remains outside the chamber (at the egress point) to monitor equipment and serve as an external timekeeper via prearranged signal intervals. The inside traveler wears a lifeline of non-conductive fiber (kevlar or nylon) that is paid out from the entry point; a series of gentle tugs serves as a non-verbal communication system. The outside partner can pull you out if you become unresponsive or disoriented. The lifeline must not abrade against the sharp columns, so use a sheath or periodically check for fraying.

### VIII. EMERGENCY SCENARIOS

**Cut or Abrasion:** The edges are clean and likely sterile in the biological sense, but the metal fines can cause heavy-metal poisoning. Exit immediately if the skin is broken. Flush the wound with sterile saline *after leaving the chamber*—do not use water inside.

**Heat Exhaustion:** Symptoms (dizziness, nausea, muscle cramps) can be masked by the subsonic vibration. If you feel sudden disorientation or tunnel vision, tug the lifeline thrice and begin slow, immediate retreat. The cooling vest failure is a critical abort condition.

**Equilibrium Disruption:** If you accidentally displace a major component, or if the fauna suddenly cease all movement in unison (a total, prolonged ripple of silence), *do not run*. Freeze. The system is reevaluating. Running introduces a large perturbation. Wait for the fauna to resume their cycles—this may take up to 60 seconds. If they do not resume, or if the hum changes pitch, begin a slow, steady egress without rapid movements.

**Psychological Overload:** If you find yourself thinking the hum is a voice, or that the unchanging patterns are “right” and you wish to stay, you are experiencing stasis assimilation. Immediately squeeze your anchor object, look at your mechanical watch, and pull the lifeline for emergency extraction. Your partner will pull you out; do not resist.

### IX. EXIT PROCEDURE

Leaving is as critical as entering. Before you exit, allow the partner to slowly retrieve you, reeling in the lifeline while you walk backward. Do not turn your back on the chamber until you are at the threshold; the psychological shift from timeless stasis to linear causality can be jarring. Once out, do not remove your respirator in the immediate vicinity—fine dust will cling to your suit. Decon yourself with a HEPA vacuum (not compressed air, which disperses particles) before opening any seals.

Sit quietly for at least 30 minutes in a temperate, dim environment. Drink electrolyte fluids slowly. Allow your hearing to recover; the silence after the hum will feel like a void. Do not make major decisions for several hours. The Static Engine tends to leave a resonance in the mind—a faint echo of its perfect, closed note. That, too, will fade.
