**Preparation Brief: The Unborn Geode**
*For the ordinary multiverse traveler intending to visit a high-altitude pocket world of autogenous industry and fractured rules.*

Before you pack a single item, sit in silence and internalize this: you are not visiting a place so much as a *process* that has mistaken you for an observer. The location—designate it Geode-227-Unfinished—does not care if you survive. It is a cathedral-sized embryo of machinery, a stillborn factory breathing argon and humming its own slow creation. It will not accommodate you. Its air is poison, its floor a skillet, its geometry occasionally non-negotiable. And yet, with the right interversal kit and a properly adjusted mindset, you can witness something that exists in the narrow seam between geology and clockwork. Here is how.

---

### 1. Reality Stabilization — First, Last, and Always
The site’s **Reality Breakdown Index** is 2.8 out of 3.0. This is not a metaphor. The local laws of physics are a gentleman’s agreement that gets broken roughly once an hour, sometimes more in the metallic groves. Plants grow into angles that don’t close, time loops backward like a scratched record, and the entire chamber may flicker into a negative of itself. For an ordinary human, this is lethal.

- **Mandatory gear:** A portable reality anchor. Minimum field radius 3 meters, capable of maintaining standard General Relativistic/Quantum consensus within its bubble. Models like the *Vickers-Moriarty Type VII stabiliser* or the artisanal *Djed Pillar (Khemetic pattern)* will suffice. Wear it on a chest harness, not in a backpack: you need to see its status indicators without turning your head. Ensure it has a redundant failsafe — a secondary crystal or a capacitor bank that can hold the bubble for at least ninety seconds if the main unit stalls. Spare power cells are non-negotiable; the ambient heat might degrade them, so carry thermally shielded cartridges on your belt.

- **Anchoring field calibration:** Before entry, run a full parity scan of the entry portal. Tether your anchor to that baseline. If the portal oscillates, your return vector vanishes. Some travellers carry a physical tether (a carbon-nanotube line, spooled) as a hard backup, but a cord cannot cross a non-Euclidean fold safely. The anchor is your lifeline.

- **Cognitive preparation:** The reality breakdown often manifests as déjà vu, jamais vu, or a sensation that you are simultaneously walking forward and backward in time. Practice mental grounding exercises: count your fingers periodically; keep a small mechanical clicker in your glove to mark subjective time. If you see a grove flicker into its own past shape, do not approach. Wait until the anchor’s field reasserts and the visual noise stabilizes.

### 2. Thermal and Atmospheric Integrity — The Shell
The environment wants to cook you, suffocate you, and then electroplate your remains.

- **Temperature:** Ambient air hovers around 70°C (158°F), but the polished obsidian floor radiates heat in silent waves that can push surface contact temperatures higher. No water exists; you cannot rely on evaporative cooling.
  - *Primary suit:* A full-body, multi-layer insulating garment with active liquid cooling. The outermost layer must be mechanically resistant to sharp obsidian edges and metallic leaf-fractals (see below). Use a suit rated for volcanic research or lava-adjacent fieldwork; hybrid space/volcano designs exist — look for the *Typhon Exo-Shell* or custom-fitting at a Thermopylae outfitter. The boots should have an aerogel sole (≥8 mm) and a traction pattern for glassy, perfectly smooth surfaces. Test them on a slip table: the obsidian here is frictionless when dust-free, and there is no dust.
  - *Cooling system:* A closed-loop liquid-cooled undergarment, with a radiator pack positioned on your back or chest. The atmosphere’s sulfur dioxide and argon mix is a poor thermal conductor, so the radiator must be oversized. Consider a phase-change pack (wax or salt hydrate) as a silent buffer; it won’t add a mechanical hum to the already hypnotic drone of the geode.
  - *Emergency options:* If the cooling fails, you have minutes before heat stroke sets in. Carry a compact, single-use endothermic grenade — a chemical snap-pack that can drop your immediate envelope by 30°C for about ten minutes. That should buy enough time to reach the portal.

- **Atmosphere:** The “air” is a sluggish blend of argon and sulfur dioxide, with traces of cooling basalt outgassing. It is utterly unbreathable and mildly corrosive. Sulfur dioxide reacts with moisture to form sulfurous acid, so any exposed skin or mucous membranes would be damaged instantly.
  - *Respirator:* A full-face positive-pressure breathing apparatus is required. The pressure is higher than the outside to prevent inflow of toxic gas. A rebreather is acceptable, but ensure the CO₂ scrubber can tolerate acid gas traces. Better: use a closed-circuit liquid air pack, which eliminates the need for external gas exchange altogether. The silence of a liquid-air system also avoids attracting attention from... whatever passes for awareness here. The plants are not sentient, but the reality breakdowns sometimes play tricks.
  - *Helmet:* Integrated into the suit, with a photochromic visor that can adjust to the sourceless, shadowless light. You’ll want to dial it down slightly; the omnipresent illumination can induce visual fatigue and a strange form of spatial dysphoria because shadows are absent. If your visor can project a faint artificial shadow grid onto your retina, do so; it will ground your depth perception.
  - *Taste/Smell protection:* The suit must be sealed. Do not attempt to sample the air. The description notes it would taste of “pennies and lightning.” That’s poetic for “copper salts and electrical ionization burning your tongue.” Not recommended.

- **Pressure considerations:** The elevation is 2.1 out of 3 on the local scale of high places, which suggests air pressure significantly lower than sea-level standard. The suit should be a pressure suit if the ambient is below about 500 hPa, to prevent ebullism and hypoxia in the event of a leak. A non-pressurized suit with a breathing mask is insufficient. Full-body pressurization also helps with heat regulation (the suit’s gas pressure can be a thermal buffer). Standard operating pressure: 0.7–1.0 atm inside, with a stiff outer hull to prevent ballooning.

### 3. Physical Hazards — The Industrial Garden
The “vegetation” is not hostile, but it is geometrically aggressive and electrically live.

- **Sharp surfaces:** Metallic fronds terminate in edges that are “sharp as fractured obsidian.” Mica leaves peel into rectangular blades. Brushing against a thicket could gash your suit open. Wear a puncture-resistant outer layer: a weave of Kevlar, ceramic-coated fabric, or—if your budget allows—a light adamant-fiber weave. Gloves must be articulated but cut-proof; gauntlets that seal to the sleeves.

- **Electrical fields:** The plants are living thermoelectric generators. Copper whiskers braid spontaneously into conducting paths, and the floor may carry induced currents. A step near a root node could complete a circuit through your body. Boots should be electrically insulating (dielectric, rated for at least 5 kV). Additionally, wear a conductive grounding strap that trails behind you, with a carbon-brush contact that skims the glass floor to bleed off any static buildup before it arcs. But be aware: in an area of reality fluctuation, the strap might briefly become a live wire. The anchor should suppress such surprises.

- **Non-Euclidean traps:** Stems that bend into “lower dimensions” are a navigation hazard. You might see a clear path that, when you take a step, wraps around you into an orthogonal geometry not meant for human joints. Always test the space in front with a probe. Carry a telescoping staff made of a non-conductive, insulative material (fiberglass, basalt composite) that you can poke ahead. If the staff’s tip flickers or disappears into a fold, retreat and mark that boundary with a beacon. Reality anchors do not always normalize space instantly; they create a bubble of consensus, but at the edge of that bubble, the local weirdness persists. Keep the staff length shorter than your anchor’s radius.

- **Temporal flickers:** The plants may pause and resume. If you are mid-stride when a temporal hiccup occurs, you might find yourself repeating the last two seconds until the waveform collapses. The anchor will shorten these events, but you may still experience a jarring loop. Stand still, breathe, and let the anchor’s buffer catch up. Do not attempt to move during a negative-image flash; wait for the stable, metallic colors to return.

### 4. Sensory and Psychological Preparation
Sound, light, solitude, and the absence of organic life will press on your mind like a weight.

- **The light:** Sourceless, shadowless, steady as daylight. It erases form; objects appear flat. Your brain will fight this. Bring a small handheld torch—not for illumination, but to project a hard directional light that creates shadows on demand. Shine it at your own glove or the ground periodically to recalibrate your visual cortex. Photographers’ shadow projectors (little patterned gobos) can be sanity-saving.

- **The soundscape:** A low, polyphonic drone from the electrical currents, punctuated by a faint click every hour as a mica leaf unfurls a micron. It is a lullaby. After an hour, your brain will acclimate and you may become dangerously drowsy or hypnotized. Counteract this with an auditory anchor: a bone-conduction headset playing an irregular, unpredictable rhythm—jazz, a randomized metronome, your partner reading a grocery list. Keep the volume low but distinct. The drone’s frequency might shift as you near certain groves; if it starts to harmonize with your breathing, move away—that’s a known cognitohazard in high-reality-breakdown sites.

- **Time dilation and boredom:** The dynamics are 0.9—geological slowness. Nothing happens fast except the glitches, and those are over quickly. You may spend long minutes walking across seemingly featureless obsidian plains. The isolation is absolute. There is no fauna, no wind, no cloud. Only you and the ferns that hum. Prepare for this sensory deprivation. Some travelers bring a tether to a continuous audio feed from home base. Others prefer a silent meditation practice and a notebook. Write observations with a stylus and an electronic tablet (rated for 70°C, obviously) rather than voice recording; your own voice can sound alien and trigger panic in the recycled air.

- **Existential uncanniness:** This place is an “autogenous machine,” built without a builder. It can provoke a deep sense of a purpose you cannot fathom. Remind yourself: it is not alive, not conscious, not watching. The déjà-vu texture is a local spacetime glitch, not a message. A prepared traveler accepts this intellectually, but the gut may rebel. If you feel the urge to touch a grove or speak to it, resist. Return to the portal zone and self-assess.

### 5. Equipment Checklist (Quick Reference)
All items must be rated for continuous 70°C operation and a mildly sulfurous environment. Test in a vacuum oven if possible.

- **Essential kit:**
  - Portable reality anchor, field radius ≥3m, with 2x redundant power modules.
  - Full-body pressure suit with active liquid cooling, puncture-resistant outer layer.
  - Closed-loop breathing apparatus (liquid air preferred). Spare air cartridge for 2x planned visit duration.
  - Insulated, dielectric boots with aerogel soles and micro-grooved traction.
  - Grounding strap (retractable) with carbon-brush contact.
  - Non-conductive probe staff, 1.5m, marked in 10cm increments.
  - Chest-mounted anchor status display; secondary wrist display.
  - High-temp emergency beacon that transmits a stabilized tachyon pulse back to entry point (check if portal allows).

- **Safety/bailout:**
  - Endothermic emergency cooling pack (1 use, snap-activate).
  - Spare anchor power cells in thermal shielding (at least 3).
  - A physical, non-electronic compass—but test if it works; the mountain’s magnetism may be scrambled. Use a gyrocompass if possible.
  - Compact first aid with burn dressings and a thermal shock blanket (yes, even in the heat—if the cooling fails, the shock blanket can reflect away radiant floor heat in a pinch).

- **Navigation/logistics:**
  - A set of small, IR-reflective puck beacons to mark the path back to the portal. Place at the edge of the reality bubble so they don’t drift.
  - Laser rangefinder enhanced for non-Euclidean readings; set to scream if it detects a geometric inconsistency.
  - Notebook or electronic log; write every 15 minutes, even if “nothing happened.” The writing itself is a temporal tether.
  - Pre-programmed audio loop on bone-conduction headset.

- **Optional but wise:**
  - A sample container—but do you want to bring back a leaf that exists partially in a lower dimension? Probably not. If you must, a reality-anchored stasis jar with its own micro-stabiliser.
  - A prayer or a mantra, secular or otherwise. The drone will like it.

### 6. The Metaphysical Kit — What to Hold in Mind
You will enter as a guest in a house that did not invite you. The heat is the floor’s breath; the electric hum is the pulse of a world that has never seen rain. Expect to be humbled. The plants are not your enemies, nor are they friendly. They are engines, and you are a temporary, warm anomaly. Move slowly, not because the plants will react (they won’t, not in a directed sense), but because haste is a form of disrespect to a place where a leaf takes a century to open. When a reality flicker occurs and the grove before you becomes a negative afterimage, resist the reflex to run. Stand inside your anchor’s blue circle of sanity and let it pass. This is the cost of witnessing a secret that is still learning to be solid.

When you finally step back through the portal, carry out a thorough decontamination: the sulfur dioxide may have left a microscopic residue. The greater residue, though, will be in your sense of time. For weeks after, you may dream of chrome-bright fronds unfurling at the edge of hearing. This is normal. You have walked in a factory that builds itself; its rhythm has synchronized to your own heartbeat only briefly, and now you will carry its echo. Be gentle with your return to a world of rain and breath and shadow. You will miss the drone.
