Your ordinary body is a soft, frantic machine of 37°C, ten centimeters of aerated lung, and a skeleton that transmits vibration with cruel efficiency. The Archival Deeps will treat it as a candidate for rapid thermal normalization and acoustic dissolution. This guide assumes you are a baseline human with access to adequate multiverse tier-3 equipment and a functioning dorsal anterior cingulate cortex capable of overriding panic. You will need to become a temporary native of a place that has not changed in millennia and has no intention of accommodating you.

### 1. Primary Environment Integration: The Suit

You cannot survive direct contact with the liquid. It is not water. It is chemically inert to human tissue but thermally conductive, acoustically dense, and suffocating. Your suit is not an accessory; it is your external body for the duration.

**Liquid-Immersion Membrane:** The outer layer should be a semi-permeable metasurface that repels the liquid while transmitting pressure equilibrated to your body. A symmetric osmotic balance skin (SOBS) composite works well. You want a surface that is not wetted by the liquid—otherwise every movement will drag kilograms of it with you. The suit's exterior must have no loose straps, external pockets, or buckles that can catch the ribbons or the attention of spinning spheres. They will investigate new components.

**Pressure Equalization Skeletal Lattice:** The Deeps have a gravity gradient roughly 1.1 standard terran G. Over the kilometres you will navigate, the ambient pressure increases by 1 atmosphere every 9 metres (standard equivalent). At ridge depth—three kilometres above the trench floor—the ambient pressure can exceed 3,000 standard atmospheres. Your suit must maintain a breathable internal pressure while mechanically counter-bracing against the external crush. Do not rely on gas-pressurised voids; you will need a multi-ply nanostructured frame that translates external pressure into a uniform compressive load around your torso and cranium, leaving your limbs free to move within the gel interior.

**Thermal Regulation:** The liquid temperature is 1.7 degrees above its eutectic. Your body leaks heat into it 27 times faster than into still air. The suit lining must include a distributed network of endothermic recyclers that reclaim the energy of your metabolic waste heat and return it across the skin. You must also carry a reserve power core for supplementary heating if shivering begins; hypothermia can occur within eight minutes without active compensation. The suit's external temperature will match the ambient liquid exactly, presenting no thermal signature that the vibration-seekers can track.

### 2. Breathing: Don’t Cut Corners

The liquid is oxygen-free and charged with low levels of hydrogen sulphide from the rock chemistry. You will carry your own breathing mix in a multi-chambered toroidal rebreather mounted around your waist. The breathing loop must be completely sealed from the external environment—any pinhole will flood the system with congeneric ions that render lung surfactant useless. Use a liquefactive ventilation system with inert perfluorochemical fill for your lungs if you want to avoid nitrogen narcosis under pressure shifts, or stick with a variable volume dry-suit helmet. If you choose a dry system, ensure active lung resonance dampening (see Section 3) is tuned to your specific thoracic cavity frequency.

**Preventative measure:** Have your lungs acoustically scanned and modelled before you dive, and the dampening system calibrated. The suit must be able to inject counter-phase sound waves into the chest piece to prevent the 30 Hz drone from triggering pneumothorax—a documented risk in any sustained infrasonic field.

### 3. Acoustic Countermeasures: The Drone Is Not Background Music

The drone is a 118-decibel, tightly coherent 30 Hz standing wave that permeates the whole column. Without protection, you will experience:

- Diplopia from ocular resonance (your eyeballs will jitter).
- Cardiac arrhythmias and mesenteric drag.
- Pulmonary hemorrhage if you drift into a pressure node where the standing wave doubles.

Your suit must include an **active acoustic cancellation (AAC) system**, which samples the external waveform and emits an anti-phase vibration through transducers embedded in the fabric, creating a quiet zone around your body. Since the drone’s frequency is perfectly invariant, you can pre-load the cancellation profile as a static inverse. Suit processors can lock to the phase within two cycles after entering the liquid.

**Never deactivate AAC, even momentarily, to “experience the world directly.”** In the high-pressure corridors between rigs, the standing wave can momentarily synchronise with your heart’s pacemaker, causing a sudden diastolic stall.

Additionally, wear subaudible ear inserts that notch the 30 Hz band down to safe levels. If you plan to operate any equipment or navigate by sound, you will need to hear the gradient shifts; the ear inserts should allow selected sideband imaging (see below).

### 4. Navigation: No GPS, Only a Note

In the Deeps, there are no magnetic fields, landmarks fade after 200 metres of diffusing light, and the liquid is perfectly homogeneous—no thermoclines, no particulate scatter. You cannot rely on inertial navigation once the drone begins blurring your inner ear. Navigation is based on **acoustic phase mapping**.

Each rig produces the 30 Hz note, but each one’s position creates a phase offset relative to your suit’s microphones. A dedicated processor triangulates your 3D location to within half a metre by reading the constructive/destructive interference pattern. Your suit’s AAC system already tracks this for cancellation; routing it to a head-up display or haptic belt provides a constant “feel” of your position relative to the rig network.

**Carry a backup acoustic beacon** (passive, stealth). If your suit power fails, you will use a pressure-gradient fluidic compass: an old-school borosilicate ball filled with two immiscible liquids that align with the pressure differentials. It requires no power and will point you toward the nearest rig—though very slowly. Drift is fatal if you lose orientation beyond the light.

**Lighting:** Bring two independent LED arrays (wide-angle and spot). The liquid absorbs blue-shifted wavelengths; use amber-corrected light for low scatter. The local fauna ignore the rig lights but may swarm personal, intense beams. Use red filtration as a general courtesy and to avoid phototactic entanglement.

### 5. Ecosystem Interaction Protocols

The life here has evolved to an equilibrium state and responds poorly to unpredictable energy inputs. A thrashing limb is a chaos node; your suit must be rigged to detect and avoid contact.

- **Ribbons:** Passively follow pressure gradients. If one wraps around your suit, do not pull. The material has microscopic cilia that hook onto fibres; removal tears the creature. Your suit should shed bio-gel pads pre-coated with a lubricant that causes the cilia to disengage. If you have no gel, simply halt and broadcast a low-amplitude 50 Hz pulse from your AAC transducers. The ribbons are not attuned to that frequency and will disengage within 30 seconds.

- **Spin spheres:** They are calcium-centred gyres that filter ambient microbes. If you swim into one, it will burst, releasing enzymes that are not dangerous to synthetic elastomers but will cloud your local volume with nutrient particulates. The resulting turbidity may attract triangle predators and obscure vision. Treat spheres as fragile medusoid hazards.

- **Triangle predators:** Semi-rigid sheets that engulf clusters. A human-sized intrusion is not standard prey but may elicit a investigatory engulf attempt. Your suit’s AAC can broadcast a predatory-avoidance frequency as measured from autopsying a dead triangle: a series of sharp 120 Hz clicks. If one approaches, a five-second burst of clicks from your emitters will cause it to reorient away.

- **Biofilms:** The rig colonisers are harmless but carry a strange extremophile enzyme which, if brought into a human digestive microbiome via contact produce a very unpleasant protein-folding disorder. Do not touch the rig surfaces with bare skin, do not ingest any liquid. Decontamination suit protocols are mandatory before removal.

### 6. Shelter and Safe Havens

Unbroken, you can rest inside any of the silent rig pump housings. They provide barrier zones where the drone is naturally dampened by the structure itself, and where residual heat from the decaying power source (you bring a thermoelectric converter) allows a respite from the suit’s heating demands. Carry a portable pressure-bladder habitat (PHAB) that can be inflated inside a secure chamber; it provides eight hours of breathable atmosphere and a dry bed. Do not assume the rig's machinery is inert: always check for loose, current-carrying induction coils with your non-contact voltage sensor.

### 7. Messing with The Note

The drone defines the ecosystem chronometry. Your suit’s cancellation field, if improperly contained, creates a local override in the phase structure. This might be noticed by older, unreasonably large triangle morphs that inhabit the abyssal plains beyond the trench. Avoid testing active cancellation on the lowest-power setting for more than 15 consecutive minutes in open water, and never produce a phase-nulling field at a node—the sudden pressure differential can trigger a cavitation-like effect that has the same violence as a depth charge.

### 8. Schedule and Endurance

The Deeps operate on no diurnal cycle. Your body does. Stick to your home-world circadian rhythm using the suit’s visor overlay that dims to black periodically. Physical endurance drops to 60% nominal due to the constant load of pressure and acoustic management. Plan trips into the far ridge in 12-hour pushes with 6-hour rest in the PHAB. Bring redundant suit batteries capable of 72 hours of operation in active cancellation mode. The drone must be continuously countered; there is no switch.

### 9. Emergency Protocols

- **Power loss to AAC:** First: clamp your jaw lock and press emergency earseals (manual inserts that mechanically damp the 30 Hz wave). Second: initiate emergency squeeze—the suit will restrict chest movement to 2 mm amplitude, preventing full lung excursion. Immediately expel all breathable gas and switch to suit’s internal oxygenation bypass (liquid breathing cassette). You are now effectively a free-diving mammal with no oscillatory air cavities, so the drone cannot damage you. The emergency battery on the AAC must be fixed within 20 minutes.

- **Fluid-contaminated suit:** If the outer membrane is breached, the liquid seeping in will start cognitive impairment as the chemicals penetrate skin. The suit must inject an antitoxin polymer sealant and inflate an inner barrier. If the breach is larger than 2 cm, initiate emergency beacon via acoustic locator (a spike signal embedded in the drone’s phase) alerting any nearby travelers. The nearest safe egress point is the Southern Rig Access Shaft (Q-1173-A), which hosts a permanent one-person atmospheric lock.

- **Suit overheat/thermal runaway:** The liquid is a perfect heat sink, so overheating is unlikely unless you are resting inside an insulated shelter without flushing the suit interstitial gel. If your core temperature exceeds 39°C, evacuate the shelter and flush the suit with external liquid for 30 seconds.

- **14 Days Before:** Peaceful meditation in a 30 Hz anechoic chamber overlayered with marine sonar fossils to acclimate your proprioception.
- **7 Days Before:** Full suit seal test in a high-pressure chamber with the actual liquid proxy (non-native, avoid contamination) and thorough testing of AAC phase-lock dissolution. Have a standby medic monitor your lung harmonics.
- **Day of insertion:** Pre-visualize the ridge map. The rigs are spaced at roughly 23-minute intervals at maximum suit cruise thrust (2 metres per second). Plan your gas reserves based on this maximum transit time between safe islands.
- **Insertion:** You will be vertically deposited at the Ridge Insertion Point (YA-2). Check the phase map, confirm bearing to the nearest rig light, and begin.
