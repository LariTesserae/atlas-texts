You are in a vast, cool chamber, though “chamber” feels wrong—it’s too flat a word for this place, which opens in every direction, a territory roofed and walled entirely by hand-hewn stone. Not natural stone. Every surface you touch bears the tooth-marks of chisels, the faint scrape of shells or bone tools, the thumbed hollows where clay was pressed into shape before it set. The rock is a composite, a conglomerate of crushed mineral, grit, and some dark binder that glistens faintly in the low light—here a seam of iron oxide, there a pocket of damp crystallising into salt. The floor underfoot is uneven but not unpatterned: it rises into shallow steps, sinks into troughs, all made, all intentional, all laid down in layers that seem to flow like frozen sludge. The air is cool, steady at a temperature that feels like deep cellar coolness, and it carries a constant, multi-textured sound—a roar of small vibrations that resolves, when you stop to listen, into a thousand separate noises: the plink of individual water drops falling from somewhere high, the gurgle of a rivulet threading a carved channel, the distant, rhythmic chime of stone on stone, and underneath it all a low, tonal hum, as if the whole edifice breathes through its porous walls.

You stand in darkness, but not absolute. Scattered across the uneven ceiling—which arches and dips, never higher than a few arm-spans above your head—are constellations of pale light: clusters of a fungal glow, bluish-green, that seep from cracks and hairline seams. The light is meagre, enough to show the space is stuffed with forms. Density presses in: pillars as thick as tree trunks stand less than an arm’s length apart in every direction, their surfaces cluttered with small niches, shelf-like projections, hanging loops of braided fibre. They recede into gloom, creating a layered maze that vanishes not into distance but into a thickening of shadows, so that the far side of this open region is a texture of near-touching verticals. Between the pillars, low walls meander, and above them, suspended walkways of lashed sticks and hide sag under the weight of collected moisture. The place is not a room; it is an open, sprawling basement-world, a low-lying plain of constructions extending beyond the reach of your sight or hearing, a regional hive at the bottom of a greater black.

Water is the blood of this landscape. The dampness score of 0.9 manifests not as standing pools but as constant, interstitial moisture. Every surface is slick, not with slime but with a fine condensation that beads on the stonework and drips with slow inevitability. Thin streams run in channels cut into the floors and walls, their courses marked by a darker patina, and occasionally you hear a louder rush where two flows merge and spill over a carved lip into a lower basin. The water is cool and slightly mineral to the smell—a wet stone smell, but with a tang of worked copper and something organic, like wet leather or old fat. Now and then, a sudden, louder cascade erupts as a section of ceiling, freshly undercut by seeping damp, releases a shower of droplets and grit—a tiny, embryonic erosion event, the place still shaping itself, still emerging into its final form. That erosion score of 0.5 is not the slow wearing of ages but the active, raw peeling of a world half-made: here a cavity has opened where a water channel undercut a pillar base, its edges raw and angular; there a wall is actively being daubed with new clay by something you cannot see, the smoothed surface gleaming wetly. The whole environment is in a state of becoming, its makers seemingly in an ongoing dialogue with the very processes of dissolution and repair.

There is no vegetation—not a frond, not a root. What grows here instead is something else, a grey-white fuzz that patches the stone, a fungal threadwork that spreads in dendritic patterns, not photosynthetic but chemosynthetic or simply detritivorous, feeding on the perpetual moisture and the minerals that leach from the walls. In some corners, thicker bodies have formed: soft, knobby extrusions like half-melted candles, glistening with their own mucus. They are not plants, and they move—very slowly, imperceptibly, closing a pore or stretching a lobe over hours—but they are the closest thing to life that fills these spaces. Fauna is rare, true to its parameter. When you see a creature, it is a sudden, fleeting shape: a centipede-like thing as long as your forearm, armoured in chitin the colour of old bone, its legs a whisper of motion as it vanishes into a crevice between two pillars. You might spot a blind newt, translucent, pink with internal organs visible as shadows, clinging to a damp vertical before it slips into a water channel. Their presence is a punctuation, not a population. Yet the sound they make is loud against the general din—the scrape of a carapace, the splash of a retreat.

The total built-ness (2.8) is absolute. Nowhere in your explorations have you found a patch of genuine bedrock, a natural stalactite, a soil floor. Everything is artifice. The pillars are not dressed stone but stacked, hand-moulded drums of that composite material, their joins so tight they seem fused. The channels are neatly trapezoidal, with small check-dams inserted at intervals—pebbles set in mortar—to slow the water. The dim fungal lights have been deliberately sited; some are cupped in small clay reflectors that focus their glow onto particular junctions. The technology is primitive (1.1): there is no metal here beyond the occasional dark stain in the stone—perhaps early, accidental smelting from ores used in the walls—and no machined parts. Tools are of chipped flint, ground bone, shell scrapers, and fire-hardened wood, the marks of which cover every surface. You find handprint smears in the clay, cord impressions, the regular pecking of a drill bow. The scale, though, is astonishing for such means: an entire regional landscape, miles across, constructed over generations by thousands of hands, or perhaps by something else entirely.

That “something else” touches the strange parameter (1.7). The weirdness here is not overtly supernatural but a creeping sense of alien logic. The pillars, for instance, are hollow. You tap one and hear a resonating echo, and when you find a broken stump, you peer down into a core threaded with slender, bone-white roots that pulse faintly with moisture. The layering of the stone composite includes, in some places, what look like compressed mats of hair or woven fibre. The rhythmic chime of stone on stone you hear in the distance does not sound random: it follows patterns that pause, then resume with a different cadence, as if signalling. And the constant hum—vibrating through your feet—has a slight vibrato that rises and ebbs in cycles of about ninety seconds, a slow breathing of the constructed world.

Sound (1.9) is indeed loud, a steady pressure on the ears. The drip and flow of water, the chime, the hum, the occasional clatter of a loosened stone falling somewhere, and, at irregular intervals, a deep, low groan that seems to come from the mass of the structure itself—perhaps cooling, perhaps settling, perhaps the voice of some immense process—all combine into a soundscape that never ceases. It is an active world (dynamic 1.9), metabolising: water cycles through channels, evaporating from some surfaces and condensing on others; the fungal growths pulse their dim light; the rare fauna hunts and scavenges; and somewhere, the makers—or their descendants, or their creations—continue to carve, shape, and maintain. The air moves in slow currents, driven by temperature differences between warm bodies and cool walls, carrying the mineral breath and the faint, vinegary scent of the fungal colonies.

Standing still, you realise you are in a place that is entirely a made thing, yet it has the feel of a natural ecosystem, a constructed geology in the process of being born. The low elevation means you are deep down, perhaps at the floor of a vast, manufactured pit or a buried plain, the weight of the world above pressing in but held at bay by this dense cage of pillars. The darkness (0.5 light) is not oppressive but textured, a medium in which the sparse constellations of glow-fungus float like anchor lights on a subterranean sea. And the openness (scale 1.6) reveals itself in the way sound carries, in the occasional glimpse of a vault much higher than the usual low ceiling, where multiple levels of walkway criss-cross and vanish into shadow, and in the knowledge that you could walk for days and still be within the same interlocking, dense, damp, cool, emerging, and wholly artificial land.
