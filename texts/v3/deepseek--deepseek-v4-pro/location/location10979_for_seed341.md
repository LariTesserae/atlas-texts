The air here does not so much hold moisture as become it. Heat thickens, a visible weight, and every surface sweats — weeping brick, slick metal, the stone cobbles between the structures glistening with a perpetual film of warm, turbid water. It is a low place, a basin that gathers more than drains, where the water table presses so close to the surface that the ground itself seems to breathe out damp. Pools form not from rainfall but from the earth’s slow exhalation, spreading across the open spaces between the built things.

The built things are scattered, deliberate but not dense. There is a logic of settlement here, a small urban body once laid down in a grid that has since grown porous. Wide gaps yawn between the brick-and-iron hulks, gaps filled with a strange, lush vigour. Vegetation clots in these intervals, but not uniformly — a sparse distribution of botanical excess. A single cycad spreads its stiff fronds over a collapsed boiler; a stand of elephant ears, leaves soft as wet leather, crowds a drainage culvert. The plants are enormous, feverish, yet each clump stands in solitude, islands of green in a sea of damp emptiness. The ground between is bare, slick mud or cracked concrete, a negative space that hums with the absence of more.
