You step onto the Spine of the World, though no one here calls it that. No one here has water in their mouths to shape a name. The air is cool and thin and has a taste, not of moisture but of some dry electric sizzle, the ozone tang of a thunderstorm that never breaks. It is very old here. The landscape has been planed down by time, not by water or wind as you know them, but by the slow, relentless caress of vibration. The light is dim, a perpetual grey-gold twilight cast by a fat, senescent sun that seems to have forgotten how to fully set, hanging always just below the horizon of a razorback ridge. No, not a ridge. Look closer.

The ground beneath you is not ground. It is flesh. Or what passes for flesh in a place where the kingdoms of life collapsed to one. It is a compressed, fibrous mat of interwoven stems, root-hairs, and fungal hyphae, a single organism stretched as taut as a drumhead across a continental shelf of air. This is the Thallarchy, the total-plant world. It is the substrate and the summit, the valley and the peak. You are walking on a petrified sponge of vegetative matter, a billion seasons of growth cannibalized and compacted into a substance like dry cork, like frozen bread, like the skin of a drum. The actual soil, the mineral memory of the planet, is an abstraction miles below. You are at a high elevation, not on a mountain made of rock, but on a plateau of the world-plant’s own accumulated dead, the highest point where the relentless upward push of growth finally meets the sparse, cool, killing dryness of the upper air.

Around you, the "urban" element is not a city built on the plant. It is a city grown of it. Twisted towers of lignified vine spiral into the dimness, their walls seamlessly transitioning from the horizontal plain underfoot. These are not ruins; they are ancient, functional, and entirely hollow, their purpose lost to any mind that thinks with a brain. They might have been sap channels, or breathing vents, or something stranger. Now, they are carved and sculpted by a later intelligence. Deep within the fibrous canyons, a lacework of beyond-modern tech has been woven: pathways of a material that looks like polished obsidian but feels warm and fleshy, threading through the vascular bundles. They are not roads, but something like neural filaments, conduits for a photon- or phonon-based logic that moves in shimmering, silent cascades through the translucent, amber-like saps that seep from the walls. This is the post-modern tech: a bio-nanotech that did not invade the Thallarchy but coaxed it into a computational state, turning the world-plant’s own signal molecules into a processor the size of a town.

And it is loud. The sound is the sound of a world breathing through a single, planet-spanning organ. A deep, subsonic drone is the baseline, a “C” note thirty octaves below human hearing that you feel in your teeth and the hollow of your chest. Layered over this is an active, rushing cadence: the creaking of giant fibers, the high-frequency popping of vesicles in the "leaves" (if you can call them that—they are more like cantilevered, translucent sails of mottled green and grey, their surfaces studded with microscopic diffraction gratings that scatter the dim light). The loudest sound is a rhythmic, percussive thwump. It comes from the Spiracles, pits the width of a house that gape in the ground-flesh, opening and closing with a taut, muscular snap. They are breathing, cycling the thin, cool, near-waterless air. You realize the entire regional plain, the open scale you can see for miles across its gently undulating surface, is breathing in a slow, sequential wave, a pattern of contraction and release that looks like a thought moving across the surface of a giant brain.

This sparse world is not empty. It is spaced. The giant structures—the towers, the breathing pits, the hanging veil-gardens of dangling, blue-grey rootlets that don't seek water but filter metallic dust from the air—are separated by wide, drum-tight plazas of the ground-flesh. And in those plazas, the fauna moves. Abundant, yes, but strange. They are not animals in the way you know them. They are motile plant-creatures, detached parts of the One-Plant given a semi-autonomous life. A herd of Lignophids moves past, their bodies like serrated wheels, rolling slowly on a single, knobby leg that is a condensed stem. They are predators and prey, grazing on the fruiting bodies that erupt from the ground but also being grazed upon. A larger one, a Saph-Skulk, unfolds from a shadow. Its body is a tessellation of stiff, photosynthetic scales, and it moves with a clicking, grinding gait, its mouthparts a set of spinning, serrated cylinders of silica-hardened cellulose. It descends on a Lignophid that has grown too slow, too rigid, and with a scream of grinding plant-on-plant, the Skulk proceeds to abrade the Lignophid into a fine, dry dust, which it then sifts through a specialized filter in its throat. The dry, powdery residue scatters on the non-existent wind, a testament to the zero-water chemistry of this place. All predation here is a kind of highly-structured composting.

The weirdness, the deep strangeness, settles over you as you watch the light dim a fraction more. The scattered towers begin to fluoresce. Not with a chaotic glow, but with a structured, patterned light—the post-modern tech working a computation in the language of bioluminescence. The signals race along the neural pathways, a silent conversation of cool fire. This is the ecosystem in motion, the dynamic process. The plant is not just growing; it is thinking. The animals are not just eating; they are executing subroutines in a planetary-scale metabolism, their predator-prey dance a form of defragmentation, re-absorbing calcified, inactive nodes back into the soft, computing tissue of the whole. The ancient erosion is not a decay to nothingness, but a settling into a final, optimized form, a world smoothed into a perfect instrument. High on this plateau of drum-skin and brain-fiber, under a failing sun, a world that forgot water hums its loud, living, computational prayer to the void.
