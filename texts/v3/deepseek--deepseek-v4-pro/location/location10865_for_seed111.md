The cleft is a wound in the mountain’s side—barely a body’s width, a vertical slit of darkness exhaling a chill that beads on your skin. You wedge through, and the rock scrapes with a brittle, glassy sharpness, its edges unworn, freshly broken. This stone is young: born of a recent shudder, its faces still crisp as a shattered mirror. No ancient, water-smoothed passages here. The air thickens as you descend, heavy with a cold sweat that gathers on every surface, dripping with a slow, metallic rhythm. It is not rain but a perpetual condensation, the mountain’s breath condensing in the cool, still dark.

Sound arrives before light—a dense, grinding chitter that you feel in your teeth. It’s not a single noise but a saturated, layered wall of clicks, wet pops, and a low, thrumming hum that seems to emanate from the rock itself. The darkness is almost complete, a weight against your eyes, but your ears map a sudden opening: the fissure flares into a chamber, roughly the volume of a small cathedral, where every surface vibrates with life.

When you risk a faint lantern-glimmer, the scene resolves in a pale, trembling circle. The walls are not walls. From floor to unseen vault, the stone is obscured by a dense, overlapping tapestry of creatures. They cling head-downward, bodies packed like shingled scales, each the size of a small dog but flattened, oval, pale as grubs. They have too many limbs—seven or eight—each ending in a delicate hand-like cluster of digits. Sparse silvery fur mats their backs, and they move with an excruciating slowness, a limb uncurling over the course of a minute to scratch at the rock or adjust against a neighbor. The sheer density is staggering: they layer three and four deep in places, a living mosaic that utterly hides the geology beneath. The floor is no different—a jumble of sharp boulders carpeted in the same soft, breathing layer. You have been stepping on them, and they have given way with the patience of moss.

The sound is them. Each creature emits a constant, low-frequency drone, a hum that blends into a pervasive vibration. Over it, they trade bursts of sharp clicks, percussive and rhythmic, bouncing off the walls in a ceaseless cascade. It is loud enough to drown thought, a sonic saturation that feels like its own element.

In the lamplight, you catch a glint: one of them, closest to your face, grips a sliver of stone in its fore-hands. It rasps the wall with deliberate strokes—chip-chip-pause—lapping at the moisture and a faint grey-green crust of lichen that grows there. That lichen is the only vegetation, a sparse, barely-there stain on the rock, but it sustains them. Others are doing the same, or using their tools to pry at the chitinous seams of neighbors, harvesting shed carapaces or something more. The floor is littered with worn stone flakes, the detritus of a hundred thousand blind, patient craftsmen. There are no nests, no built structures—only this basic tool-use, a primitive manual culture enacted directly upon the geology.

You lift the lantern higher. Above, a faint constellation of bioluminescent blue-green glimmers from the ceiling, not sky but a fungus or perhaps some sessile lifeform, casting a ghostly outline of the upper reaches. It silhouettes more bodies, layered like a chandelier of slowly shifting limbs. Occasionally, one loses its grip and drifts downward, spinning in the dense, moisture-heavy air, to be silently absorbed into the floor-level mass.

The chill is constant, cool but not freezing, your breath misting faintly. The bodies radiate a collective, subtle warmth that curdles the air with a sweet-ammoniac funk. The place is strange in its totality—a landscape where the animate has overwhelmed the inanimate. The rock itself is secondary, a sharp-edged scaffold for this dense, slow, loud film of life. And it is all newborn: the fractures still jagged, the boulder-fall still poised as it fell, the ecosystem already saturated, as if the creatures had been waiting in the mountain’s veins for this crack to open. Time here is a glacial thing; the colony moves and changes over decades, each chip of a stone tool widening the fissure by a grain, each slow-motion shift a response to pressures you cannot feel. The sound never stops. The darkness remains. The damp chill seeps into your bones, and you realize you are merely occupying a gap in a fabric of overwhelming, patient presence.
