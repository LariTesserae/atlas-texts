You step into it not through a door but through a fold in the air, a compression of heat and moisture that parts like wet paper. It is a cavity, a lung nested inside the mountain’s throat — small enough that you could cup it in your palms if scale were a thing that mattered here, but it isn’t. The space feels both intimate and infinite, a pocket tucked between fresh, sharp blades of obsidian that still carry the memory of the eruption that birthed them. The rock is glass-black and violently new, edges so keen they shimmer with a spectral oiliness, and from every fissure, every hairline crack, the mountain exhales. Water doesn’t fall here; it condenses on the hot stone in a constant, weeping sweat, then crawls in rivulets heated nearly to steam by the earth’s deep furnace. The air is a saturated broth, so thick with humidity that breathing is a kind of drowning, and the temperature sits at the exact point where your skin can no longer tell moisture from burn. 

There is no vegetation. No green, no tendrils, not even the rust of lichen. The mineral world rules. Walls are plaited with silica dripstone — honeycombs of chalcedony and opal that grew only yesterday in geological time, still slick with gel. In cooler crevices, filamentous something spreads: not fungus, not root, but lattices of metallic whiskers, copper-red and bismuth-iridescent, that pulse faintly with an internal light. The ground underfoot is a crunch of tephra and the shattered remains of things that might be eggshells, or might be discarded tools too primitive to name.

Because the place is dense — layered, near-touching, every cubic inch a territory dispute. You see them now, the fauna: overwhelming in number, a tide of carapace and jointed limb that coats every surface like a living enamel. They are small, the largest no bigger than your thumb-joint, and they are legion. They move in silent, overlapping waves, a chitinous textile woven so tightly that the obsidian beneath is only a rumor. Their bodies are black like the rock but veined with the same opal glimmer, as if they have eaten the mountain and worn it as armor. They have no voices — no chitter, no hiss — and their motion is a frictionless glide, a conspiracy of silence that turns the expected noise of teeming life into a held breath. The quiet is not empty; it is the kind of quiet a predator keeps, or a congregation in prayer.

Reality here has a crack in it. The pocket does not obey Euclid: a crevice that should be an inch wide holds a colony that sprawls for miles when you lean close, depth pulling your vision into a vertigo of packed bodies and tangled limbs. Sometimes a creature steps sideways and vanishes, reappearing from a different angle of space, or it folds into itself, becoming two that are one. The temperature, too, dances wrong — you feel chilled sweat on your neck while your feet cook inside the warm pumice, and the steam rises in spirals that twist left-handed, temporarily, before forgetting their nature.

There is no built shelter, no carved idol, yet technology haunts the scene. Primitive, yes: some of these swarming things carry slivers of obsidian in their forelimbs, not as weapons but as styluses. They scratch the rock with deliberate, patterned grooves — not writing, maybe music, or maps of their impossible topology. Others stack tiny pebbles into cairns that vibrate faintly when aligned, tuning the steam’s hiss into cancellation, deepening the silence. It is craft without construction, tool-use at the edge of animal and omen.

The process is relentless. Every moment, new skins are shed and consumed, fresh silica threads span a gap, a pebble falls and is reset. Predation happens in slow, ritualized gestures — a larger individual, spined and slow, selects a smaller one from the undulating carpet, and the chosen stops, offers a limb, which detaches without violence and is passed forward. The predator eats not the flesh but the opal vein within, and the prey scurries on, unfazed. The dynamic hums: birth, moult, trade, alignment. All of it in the near-dark normal light — a sourceless, overcast radiance that seems to percolate through the obsidian itself, gray and even, casting no shadows.

You realize, standing (do you stand? You occupy a volume that might be a thought’s width), that the pocket is not a place you found. It is a place that happens, continuously, a fresh tetonic wound that never scars over, a condensation point where the hot, young mountain breathes and the swarm tends the breath. And the weirdness is not a flaw in reality — it is the reason the pocket exists at all, a bubble where the rules are turned down low so that something else can listen. The silence rings.
