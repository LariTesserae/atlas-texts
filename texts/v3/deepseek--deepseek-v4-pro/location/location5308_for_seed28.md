The hollow is a single vast chamber, a pocket of impossible verdancy cupped within the dry mineral bones of the world. It is not a valley, not a cave in any ordinary sense—it is a hidden organ, a lung, a sealed and secret thing buried deep beneath the surface of a dead planet. The walls that enclose it are not rock in the way you think of rock. They are a fused, vitrified crust, slick and faintly oily to the touch, ridged with whorls like the palate of some immense creature. These walls are warm—not sun-warmed, but body-warm, radiating a steady, mammalian heat that keeps the air of the chamber thick and close, a constant 30 degrees of humid embrace. Above, the ceiling vaults away into a darkness that the light never quite reaches, a profound and empty gulf that swallows sound and returns it changed.

Where does the light come from? It is bright here, shockingly bright, a white-gold radiance that seems to emanate from the vegetation itself. This is the strangeness: there is no sun, no single source. The leaves, the stems, the very air shimmer with a steady luminescence, as if the plants have learned to drink heat and breathe light. And they are lush—impossibly, riotously lush, a jungle condensed into a space no larger than a small town plaza. Ferns with fronds like bolted silk, thick-stemmed cycads, vines that hang in heavy, dripping curtains, all of them packed into a single sprawling feature, a central tangle that rises like a miniature mountain of green from the concave floor. The growth is so dense you could not push a hand into it, a solid mass of interwoven life, but the space *around* it is nearly empty. The floor of the chamber is a vast, smooth, gently sloping basin of the same warm vitrified material, stretching away from the central green mountain to the curving walls, a sterile and vacant expanse. Almost nothing per volume. A hollow.

And the sound. The sound is a physical pressure, a constant, roaring, metallic shriek that fills the entire space to bursting. It is the sound of the green mountain breathing. From deep within the tangled mass, hidden by the overlapping leaves, comes a rhythmic, pulsing exhalation of steam and sound: a deafening, high-pitched whistle overlaid with a lower, guttural churning, like water boiling in the throat of a god. The sound bounces off the hard, curved walls, layered and amplified until it becomes a solid wall of noise you could lean against. It is not a chorus of creatures; it is a single, enormous voice, the voice of the hidden thing at the heart of the vegetation.

Movement is rare but constant in its rarity. High above, near the invisible ceiling, you might glimpse a slow, drifting shape—a creature like an airborne jellyfish, its translucent bell catching the upwash of light and heat. Its long, threadlike tentacles dangle down into the empty air of the chamber, tasting for... something. It is silent as a ghost against the shriek, a fleeting punctuation in the vast emptiness between the walls and the living mountain. There are no other animals. The ecology is a closed loop, a predator-prey dynamic reduced to its simplest terms: the great hidden thing that roars and feeds on the heat of the walls, and the delicate aerial things that may feed on the steam of its breath, or perhaps on the spores it occasionally releases in great, golden, choking clouds.

This is an urban place, or what passed for urban here. Around the periphery of the chamber, built into the vitrified walls themselves, are the remnants of a settlement. They are not houses as we know them, but a series of hemispherical hollows carved directly into the warm, glassy surface, like the cells of a paper wasp nest. The openings face the central green mountain, and their interiors are lined with a coarse, woven fabric of dried vine-fiber. Here and there, simple tools lie scattered: hand-axes knapped from a black, glassy stone, grinding bowls worn smooth, needles carved from the spines of some long-vanished plant. The inhabitants are absent, but their presence is recent. A half-finished weave holds a stone tool. A bed of dried fronds still holds the faint impression of a body. The whole settlement is arranged to observe. To listen. To worship, perhaps, the source of the deafening song at the center of their world.

Everything is in an established state, a long-settled balance. The green mountain has been growing and screaming for millennia. The vitrified walls have worn smooth from the constant caress of humid, drifting air. The settlement has been dug out, abandoned, re-dug, woven, and abandoned again in cycles that stretch back beyond memory. The place is a single, ongoing process, a pocket world in its own final, stable form. The rules here are few and absolute: the green gives light and sound and heat, the empty space is the hunting ground of silent ghosts, and the glass cells are for those who listen and wait. To step from the empty, sterile floor into the crushing density of the vegetation would be to enter the throat of the voice itself, a transgression that the scattered, simple tools of the absent listeners tell you was never, ever attempted.
