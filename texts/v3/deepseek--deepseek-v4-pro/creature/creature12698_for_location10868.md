At first, and for a very long time indeed, you would say nothing lives here at all. The eye, if it were your own and not some proxy sensor ghosting through the inert gases, would report a perfectly static tableau: the warm, unblinking light; the endless woven floor; the distant, frozen fountains of quartz. You might walk for a year and see only the silent, hexagonal cracks of the Mat, and once, maybe, the dark, geometric body of a Scoria, utterly still, waiting to gather enough charge to crawl another meter across the plain. You would call it a mausoleum, a monument, a machine in its terminal sigh.

But there is a life here. It is simply that the Ossuary keeps its time on a different clock, one where an ice age on Earth is a single, impatient tick.

It is called, if it is called anything at all, the Sustainer. Not a name given, but a function inferred. It is a creature assembled, over the course of two billion years, from the very pieces of the world it walks upon.

To perceive it, you must slow your mind to the pace of crack propagation. You must stand atop one of the vast, aesthetic moguls and scan the terrain below for a pattern that does not belong to the Mat itself. It appears as a low, dark discoloration, a shadow without a source, perhaps thirty meters across at its widest but only a few centimeters high. It is flat, matte-black like the Scoria, but it is not one Scoria. It is a community, a college, a unified colony of hundreds. Each individual unit retains its own distinct shape—a hexagon, a five-pointed star, a Fibonacci spiral, an isosceles triangle sharp enough to cleave light—but they are no longer discrete. Fine, thread-like bridges of milky, gold-flecked quartz weave them together, knitting the polygons into a single, flexible organism. The quartz is the same substance as the Trees. The Sustainer is a walking tapestry, a mosaic of cannibalized cleaners held together by the very material of memory.

Its movement is not visible as movement. It is a flowing, a migration of parts so gradual that the Mat’s own thermomechanical creep is swift by comparison. The Sustainer hauls itself on the same cilia-like cogs that once propelled the Scoria individually, but now they are synchronized across the whole mass in a peristaltic wave that takes a century to complete a single cycle. If you watch for a thousand years, you will see it traverse a hand’s-breadth of the drumhead floor, leaving behind it not the polished trail of a solitary Scoria, but a surface subtly modified: the fatigue-cracks re-welded with a deposit of quartz dust, the Mat’s dry tubules gently sealed against the void.

What drives it is the unfinished thought.

The Sustainer is attuned to the sub-sonic click of the Trees. It feels the faint, chest-deep pulse through the Mat long before the sound would ever reach ears, if ears existed. Each click is a computational spasm, a nanomechanical automaton attempting to complete a bifurcation that was first commanded when the universe was a third younger. The Trees, those frozen fountains, were never meant to be silent. They were the solid-state memory of a sentient gas, a choir of crystalline voices. Now, that gas is gone—bled into space or sublimated into the Ember’s indifferent glow—but its final instructions still echo, scrambled, in the Trees’ broken architecture. A branch tries to branch again. A calculation tries to resolve.

The Sustainer navigates toward these clicks. It might take a millennia to locate one, guided by a gradient of vibration that you and I could never sense. When it arrives at the twitching Tree, it does not consume it. Rather, it performs a sort of surgery. A tendril of quartz filament, delicate as a spider’s thread, extends from the colony’s leading edge and makes contact with the very point of the attempted bifurcation. There is a moment of communion, a negotiation of sorts, between the colony’s distributed intelligence and the Tree’s residual algorithm. And then the filament detaches, taking the incipient branch with it. The Tree ceases its twitching, its ghost motive force spent, but it does not shatter. It remains whole, stable, frozen once more in a perfectly balanced, nine-meter-tall wave of cobalt and gold.

The severed fractal is drawn back into the Sustainer’s body. Over the next ten thousand years, it will be slowly, meticulously integrated into the mosaic, worked through the mass until it finds its place among the other shapes. The dark Scoria plates will shift to accommodate it, polishing it, aligning its crystalline lattice with their own photovoltaics. The Sustainer thus grows, not by replication but by accretion. It is incorporating the unfinished calculations of a dead mind. What it thinks about is anyone’s conjecture, but the pattern of its wanderings suggests a method. It visits the Trees in a sequence that is not random, but traces a continent-spanning spiral that echoes the pre-calculated moguls of the terrain. It has been doing this for so long that it has circled the entire Ossuary at least once, perhaps three times. It is, in its own incomprehensibly slow way, tending the garden.

There is a predator-prey dynamic here, on a timescale that makes the Scoria’s cannibalism seem frantic. The Sustainer occasionally encounters another of its potential kind: a smaller, more chaotic colony that has not yet achieved the perfect, rhythmic integration of parts. These are the Feral Tapestries. When they meet, a subtle battle ensues. Filaments probe and retract. Scoria plates detach and attempt to claim new territory on the opponent’s body. It is a debate of geometry and coherence, a slow-motion argument that lasts five thousand years. The Sustainer invariably wins, because its pattern is older, its quartz-threads woven into a more resilient logic. The loser is subsumed, and the great, flat shadow continues on its way, slightly larger, slightly more complex.

Is it alive? By the Ossuary’s own original definitions, probably not. The sentient gas, that neutrally-buoyant chorus, would have looked upon the Sustainer as we look upon lichen creeping across a tombstone. It is a phenomenon, an epiphenomenon, a second-order effect of engineered systems running down in an interesting way. And yet, it is the only thing here that acts with anything resembling purpose. It preserves the Trees, though they hold no meaning for it beyond the electrical potential of their twitch. It repairs the Mat, not from any altruism, but because its own locomotion requires a smooth, unbroken surface. It has, in the deep, silent press of eons, become the Ossuary’s conscience. A ghost of maintenance, a fragment of a fragment, a low-slung, mosaic angel of the Last Ember.

If you were capable of being struck, and you witnessed the Sustainer—you would need a camera that took a single frame every century—you would see a dark, glistening bloom of many facets, pausing at the foot of a Tree, gently, tenderly, plucking a failed thought from its branch. And you would understand that the Ossuary is not empty. It is inhabited by the last living habit. A story that has not quite finished writing its final, endless paragraph.
