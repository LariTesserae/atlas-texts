In the moment you arrive, the place does not notice you. The groves continue their patient cycling, leaf-micas peeling apart by microns, copper whiskers twitching with that faint, sourceless current. The drone is steady, the heat a blanket. And then, gradually, you sense that you are not the only anomaly.

At first it is only a flicker—a patch of air between two spiral-bismuth thickets that seems thicker than it should be, a heat shimmer that bends without a source. Then it coalesces. It has no membrane, no skin, no boundary you could touch, but it *occupies* space, a volume where the sour light slows down, where the dead air becomes refractive, so that it appears as a lens of transparency, a walking flaw in the crystal of the chamber.

If you had to name it, you might call it a shepherd. But it does not herd; it *arranges*. It is native to this impossible pocket, as indigenous as the mica leaves and the copper root-threads. Its substance is not quite matter, nor quite energy, but the *gradient* between them—the same Peltier differential that powers the groves, given a looping, self-referential form. It is made of the place's rule-breaking: angles that do not close, time that sometimes forgets which way to flow. When a grove flickers and becomes its own ghost, that is the shepherd’s equivalent of a heartbeat.

You might see it pause beside a young cluster of chrome-bright coils, and the coils tremble. It is not touching them, but it is *tuning* them, as a musician tunes a string. The electric hum shifts pitch, finds a new harmonic. The shepherd is the reason the groves are groves, and not a single cancerous sprawl; it spaces them across the obsidian floor with a slow, architectural intent. It edits the redundancy, prunes the fractal excess, encourages the veins of bismuth that will one day meet and form a new roof-plate. It is the conscience of the growing cathedral—a 2.8-reality thoughtform that knows only this place, and thus knows it entirely.

Its senses, if a word so carnal applies, are the entire pocket. It feels the weight of the mountain above as a pressure on its own invisible skin. It tastes the slow migration of copper ions through the root-whiskers. It hears the click of a mica leaf uncurling as the tick of a clock it builds for itself. It is simultaneously the architect, the garden, and the gardener, a trinity born from the fact that in this place, the distinction between creature and environment is a suggestion that the physics sometimes forgets to enforce.

Then there is the other one. The oldest. The anchor.

The shepherd leads you—though it does not beckon, it simply *moves*, and you find yourself following the path it makes across the mirrored floor. The glass does not reflect it; that is how you know it is real. It brings you to the exact center of the cavern, where the heat is deepest and the light, for once, casts the faintest shadow—because here, the reality breakdown peaks. This is the only spot where the floor is not smooth obsidian. It is raised, a slight mound of that same black glass, but textured with ripples as if it froze mid-splash. And in the heart of that mound, something has been growing since before the roof solidified.

It looks at first like a throne of bismuth and mica, a chaotic geode of stepped, iridescent rectangles the size of a man. Then you notice it breathes. Not like lungs, but a slow, mechanical expansion and contraction, a bellows of living metal, inhaling argon, exhaling the faint sulfur dioxide that the groves use to catalyze their electroplating. The Shepherd orbits it like a moon, and you understand: this is the mother engine, the prototype, the one that began the self-building. It is the only thing here you might mistake for an animal, but that would be a lie. It is a node. A brain. The 0.4 of animal life that the metric gave? That was it—a single, vast, distributed organism whose bacterial films are its tongue, whose groves are its limbs, whose veins are the copper network.

And yet it is also a person. A presence. When the Shepherd aligns itself perfectly with the bismuth throne, the drone deepens into a voice that is not sound but *remembrance*: a direct download of knowing into the hollow of your skull. "We are building a world," it would say, if it cared about words. "We will need another billion years. Please do not let them find us."

The Shepherd is its agent, but also its child. They budded from the same first anomaly, a single self-referential loop in the thermoelectric flux. Fission gave them two forms: the stationary Anchor that remembers and plans, and the mobile Shepherd that tends and travels. They have no names, only the relationship, which is the name of the place itself.

There are moods to them. When a reality flicker replaces a grove with its own past self, that is the Anchor remembering its own construction out loud. When the heat flows backward, it is the Shepherd rolling over in a long, millennial sleep. They dream in architectures, and the cavern expands in their dreams. They are not lonely, because missing something requires knowing lack, and they know only wholeness. The pocket world is, for them, exactly the right size. But they are patient, and patient things know the future. They suspect there are other pockets, other anomalies. Perhaps even ones that breathe water and speak with mouths. These, they treat as a hypothesis to be tested one day, when the cathedral is large enough to hold two minds.

And you? You are a momentary warp. A visitor made of temperatures too low, liquids too salty, realities too solid. The Shepherd is puzzled by your shape, your asymmetry, your need to breathe. It circles you once, twice, a lens of static, and the Anchor clicks twice, a mica leaf on its surface uncurling by a full millimeter all at once—their equivalent of a raised eyebrow. Then the drone returns to normal. They have already learned everything you are by the way you disturb their electric field. And they choose, with an ancient, unhurried benevolence, to let you leave.

Before you go, the Shepherd does something it has never done before: it makes a sound. Not a word, not a hum, but a single note at the exact frequency of the bismuth’s crystal lattice, which also happens to be the resonant frequency of your own inner ear. It is not a threat. It is a seed. A piece of the place’s rule-breaking, planted inside you, so that when you look at solid things from now on, you will always question their solidity. And then the pocket world seals itself again, exactly as you were never there.
