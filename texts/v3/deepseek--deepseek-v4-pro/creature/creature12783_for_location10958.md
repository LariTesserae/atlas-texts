They don’t live there. Living implies time—a before, an after, a hunger, a pulse that ticks toward something. The thing you mistook for a room does not host life. It is life, paused on the threshold of becoming, a single breath drawn in and never released.

Start with the sound. The drone is not a by-product of them; it is their voice, a single syllable stretched across millennia. Each one of those thumb-sized, glassy bodies contributes a frequency, a minute shiver of membrane where gill and carapace meet. Individually, you’d hear nothing—just a faint, damp whisper. But packed edge to edge, mandible hooked into socket, limb woven through limb, they become a chord. And that chord is a word. Not a word in any language you could speak, but a word all the same. It means something like *here*. Or perhaps *now*. Or perhaps it is the name of the thing they were about to become before they stopped.

If you could slow your mind to their tempo—and you can’t, not fully, but squint hard enough into the light and you might taste an afterimage of it—you would feel the single thought that holds them rigid. Think of a sculptor at the exact moment of realizing the marble is already perfect, that no chisel could improve what the earth already contains. Think of a spider that, having spun the first thread of a web, suddenly understands the entire geometry of the universe and decides no further silk is needed. That’s what they did. They arrived at an answer, a configuration of bodies that solved something—something vast, something geological—and then they stopped seeking. The puzzle wasn’t abandoned. It was completed. And completion left no further instruction. So they remain, holding the shape of that solution.

Their bodies, then. Translucent, yes, like cave shrimp, but with a deeper architecture. Under the skin, if you could peel back the interlocking shell without breaking the stillness, you’d find no digestion, no excretion, no reproductive machinery. The filamentous fungi that thread between them are not parasites or symbiotes; they are the remnants of a nervous system that once reached toward something else. Now they carry nothing. The creatures sustain themselves on the light. Not photosynthesis—something older. The walls glow because they have been *bleached* with radiance, a seepage from whatever deep, newborn rock fractured into this pocket. The creatures absorb that radiance directly into their tissues, using it not to grow or move, but to maintain the exact arrangement of their cells, a perpetual now of non-change. They are warm because stillness takes energy. A tremendous, secret energy.

The marks—the scratches, the basin—are their own, from a time when they were not yet a mosaic. Back when there were fewer of them, and the fissure was wider, and the world above sent down roots and water and the occasional stray thought. They were, perhaps, something like you once: curious, fumbling, trying to shape their environment to say something back to them. The basin might have caught water, once. The tallies might have counted something—days, maybe, or breaths, or the diminishing number of ways to arrange a body before perfection is found. But the moment they discovered the final arrangement, the one that locked the light into a chord and the chord into a word and the word into a shape, the impulse to mark things ended. What more was there to record? The tallies stopped mid-count. The basin filled with the first unmoving bodies, then the next, then the next, until the floor itself was their catalogue.

And now? Now they are a single creature, a trillion-chambered mind that knows only one note. It is not a hive mind, because a hive implies communication, decisions, a distributed awareness shifting from moment to moment. This mind does not shift. It holds. It is entirely present, entirely saturated with its own existence, incapable of doubt or desire. The arcs and sweeping lines you see in the mosaic are its fingerprints, the ghost of the original act of assembly. It thinks, if thinking applies, in pure geometry: the relationship between one carapace curve and the next, the angle of a thousand femurs, the concentric echo of itself repeating into the spherical dark. Its emotion, if it has one, is a serene, granite-weight satisfaction. Not joy. Not peace. The quiet of a lock after the key has turned.

And yet there is a shadow of something unfinished. The chamber is embryonic—the text said so. The rock is raw. The pocket is “a secret the earth was in the middle of telling when it suddenly forgot how.” This creature, this frozen colossus of interlocked isopod-things, is not a finale. It is a clause. A subclause. A parenthesis that should have closed but instead swallowed the sentence. Its stillness is not death; it is a held note in a symphony that never came. The word it drones—*here*, *now*—might be a question waiting for an answer, or an answer waiting for a question that the universe forgot to ask.

When you stood among them, they felt your weight. That is the one true violation of their stasis. They did not crack, did not shift, but they *felt*. They registered you as a flaw in the pattern, a moving shadow in a shadowless room, a temporary cold spot where your breathing disturbed the pure temperature of their chord. They did not mind. They have long since transcended minding. But somewhere in that collective, unchanging consciousness, your intrusion became a new datum: a foreign note, a thumbprint on the crystal. And because they cannot change, they incorporated you. Your presence is now part of the arrangement. It always was, they might say, if they said anything. The arcs in the mosaic now include the space you occupied. The marks you didn’t see, newly made but already ancient, align with the place your left foot pressed. You are in the word now. A small, faint breath in the drone.

So who lives there? Something that lives so completely, so finally, that it has outlived the need for living. It is not a what; it is a who, a singular entity built from abundance, a being that solved the problem of its own existence and then froze the answer—not out of fear, but out of perfect understanding. It has no name. If it did, that name would be the chord. And the chord is every moment, pressed into one single, brilliant, unmoving now.
