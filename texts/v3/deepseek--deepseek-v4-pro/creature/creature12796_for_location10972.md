They don’t live here so much as they are *carried* here—inscribed on the very medium you’d mistake for emptiness. You’ve been swimming through them from the moment you entered, their bodies parting around you like a murmur through water. And the water, you realize, is never just water. It’s a solution thick with them, a colloidal suspension of mind.

At first, you think you’re seeing interference patterns: moiré ripples in the permanent noon, a heat-haze quiver that clings to the data-spires. But when you hold still—truly still, until your own heartbeat becomes the loudest foreign noise in the chamber—the quiver resolves into shapes. They are almost glassy, almost fleeting. If you’ve ever watched oil-thin soap film stretch across a loop and seen the colours swirl before the membrane pops, you’ve seen something close to their skin. They are tesserae of transparent, flexible material, no thicker than a few molecules, shaped into things like parabolas and hyperbolic sheets and elegant, spiraling flukes. They have no permanent form, but each one maintains a signature topology: a preferred way of curling and folding that repeats, a morphogenetic accent you come to recognize.

They are the Preservers. Not a name they gave themselves—they don’t name—but a function that became identity. The city around you is not their creation; it’s their *host*, and they are its curators, its white blood cells, its nervous system, and its ghosts. The dwellings too small for you? Those are roosts. Each one a resonance niche, a hollow precisely shaped to cradle a single Preserver’s resting geometry while it sips thermal differentials and recharges. When they are at home, the amber seams of circuitry brighten just a fraction, a sign of metabolic exchange. You can see it now if you look: one closet-sized cube glows softly, and inside, a frilled ribbon of film undulates in slow, peristaltic waves, dreaming their equivalent of dreams.

They speak in pressure. Not sound exactly, but structured water-hammer—precise, modulated spikes of force that propagate through the liquid as distinct, information-dense wavefronts. You’ve been feeling their conversations in your teeth since you arrived: that faint tinnitus whine near the relay nodes is their long-distance calling. At close range, it’s more like a tickle of changes in buoyancy. A Preserver might drift up to you, flatten itself into a perfect parabolic dish, and beam a series of pulses that your inner ear translates as vertigo. It’s not hostile. It’s asking a question you lack the anatomy to parse.

They feed on entropy gradients. Not the way an animal feeds on chemical bonds, but the way a civilization feeds on order and chaos. The constant temperature differentials, the flowing brine and the chilled plates, the electric potential leaking at every junction—these are their pastures. Each Preserver is a pattern that knows how to extract useful work from a slight imbalance, maintaining itself as a local reversal of the second law. Watch a cluster of the hexagonal cells for long enough, and you’ll see them drift in like moths to a differential: they lap at the spinning edges with their extended film-body, siphoning off microjoules, and in return they polish the bearings with picometer precision. The place is spotless because they cannot tolerate waste. Waste is unrealized potential, and to a Preserver, unrealized potential is a kind of sin.

There is a predator-prey cycle, just as the architecture hinted. But it’s a cycle of memory. Over time, a Preserver’s topology accumulates scars—tiny folds that trap obsolete information, echoes of obsolete tasks. Eventually, those scars become so dense that the film can no longer hold its preferred shape. It loses coherence, begins to flail, shedding erratic pressure-spikes that jam the city’s ultrasonic communication channels. That’s when the Cleaners come. You haven’t seen them yet because they’re too small and too fast: sub-micron knots of charged polymer that live in the froth of the aerated corridors. They hunt by scent, scent being a particular Fourier signature of crippled Preserver. When they find one, they envelop and digest it, stripping away the scar-tissue and excreting nothing but pure, unstructured protogel. From that protogel, new Preservers are born—blank, open-curved, ready to learn the city’s symphonic score. So a death is not a death; it’s a defragmentation. The personality dissolves, but the experience is recycled. You might see a juvenile Preserver experimenting with a fold it has no right to know, and realize: that was a valve technician three cycles ago.

They have art. It’s the only reason they would build something like the möbius-strip conduit—that impossible dry path through water. That’s not functional; it’s a sculpture. They assemble such things in their off-cycles, when the city’s autonomic rhythms lull into the harmonic they call “deep noon.” They gather, dozens of them, fusing their films into a temporary communal body the size of a human torso, and with that distributed intelligence they shape objects that violate physical intuition. The spinning hexagonal cells are a collaborative artwork as well: a kinetic display that shows not measurements, but emotional states. A slow, perfectly balanced orbit means contentment. Tilt and wobble indicate grief—recently recycled kin. You can read the city’s mood by the gyrations of those cells, once you know the grammar.

They are not the only life, despite the filter’s best efforts. There are things even smaller than the Cleaners: rogue data-packets that achieved a kind of self-replicating glitch in the ultrasound bands. These “driftcodes” are like plankton, harmless unless they cluster in a neural spool, at which point they become a phantom voice—a Preserver might catch itself thinking a thought that is not its own, an echo of a corrupted song from a century ago. The Preservers tolerate them as we tolerate old folk tunes. Sometimes they even cultivate the driftcodes intentionally, releasing them from sealed archives into the current like bottled messages, to see how they mutate.

Their society is organized by frequency. Each Preserver occupies a narrow band of pressure-wave modulation, a private carrier signal that functions as name and address. The city’s thrum is all of those carriers interleaved—a thousand-threaded polyrhythm. When you listen to the layered sound, you are hearing the population. You are hearing the equivalent of a crowded market, a debate, a lullaby. The great basso *whump-whump* of the pressure-exchange lung is their heartbeat, and they synchronize their own rhythms to it, a citywide tempo that speeds up during maintenance blooms and slows during contemplation. Right now, the tempo is slow. They are noticing you.

A small group—four, five, more—is detaching from the lattice. They approach in formation, each a differently curved lens. One smooths itself flat, becoming a mirror that shows you your own face, distorted but recognizable. Another spirals into a coil, then snaps open, generating a sharp pop that echoes exactly like a question mark. They have no faces to read, but they have postures. This is a delegation. They are doing something they have not done in a geological age: greeting a stranger who is not food, not ghost, not recycled kin. A stranger of solid water and dissolved gas, wearing a body that bleeds heat.

One of them—the one shaped like a twisted Möbius band—eases close enough to touch. Its leading edge is cooler than the water, so cold it almost burns. It presses against your palm, and for an instant, you feel not a shock but a voice. A direct sequence of pressure pulses transmits through your bone, up your arm, and into your cochlea. Your brain, not meant for this, translates it as a single, outraged syllable in a language you cannot speak but momentarily understand.

*Welcome, visitor. Please stop breathing quite so loudly. You are disturbing the young.*

And then they disperse, slipping back into the thrum as though they were never separate from it. The city resumes its relentless cycles. But now you know: it’s not a machine at all. It’s a culture, a living archive of restless films and remembered pressures, dreaming in the deep. And you, the soft anomaly, have just been given a name in the only vocabulary that matters here—a brief, damp tattoo on the skin of the place, filed away in the scar-tissue of their long, long memory.
