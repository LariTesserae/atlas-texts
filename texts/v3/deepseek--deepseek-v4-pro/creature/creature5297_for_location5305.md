The Cordate are the living thought of the Thallarchy, the later intelligence that came not from beyond but from within. They emerged from the world-plant’s own meristematic dreaming, a mutation of self-awareness sparked in a single apical bud eons ago, then propagated through the vascular net until it fruited into them. They are not born; they are grown, coaxed from specialized nodes deep in the breathing pits, where the subsonic drone is loudest and the computational sap runs thick. Each Cordate begins as a coiled, fern-like fiddlehead, unfurling slowly into a humanoid form over centuries of silent, light-fed meditation.

Standing, they are tall and famine-slender, their bodies a fascicle of bundled vascular fibers, each strand a micro-capillary pulsing with amber sap that glows faintly in the twilight. Their integument is the pale, dry green of old lichen, smooth as polished bone, yet crosshatched with darker striae that map the flow of their internal hydraulics—a circulatory system that moves not blood but a photonically active resin. Their heads are ovoid, featureless except for the sensory crest: a vertical, unfurling frond of translucent tissue, studded with microscopic diffraction gratings and hair-fine mechanoreceptors. This crest is their face, their voice, their ear. It shimmers with a constant, kaleidoscopic dance of bioluminescence—indigo to amber to a cold, chemical green—each pulse and pattern a word, a formula, a song. They hear the world through the crest’s vibrations, attuned to the drone of the spiracles, the creak of contracting fibers, the distant, rhythmic thwump of thought moving across the plain.

Their limbs are tri-jointed, too many elbows and knees for human comfort, ending in hands that are not hands but rhizomic fans—five, seven, a dozen rootlet digits that can splay into an interface with the warm obsidian conduits, or twist together into a single, sharp auger for carving the lignified towers. When they walk, their feet do not lift in steps; they grow and ungrow. The sole of each foot is a mat of microscale hyphae that anchor and release with a faint, fibrous tearing sound, like the snapping of a thousand tiny threads. They glide across the drum-tight ground-flesh, leaving no footprint, only a fleeting, deeper resonance in the subsonic baseline.

Cordate sustenance is a quiet affair. They photosynthesize through their crests, capturing the failing sun’s grey-gold light, but this is mere maintenance. True feeding occurs when they root. A Cordate will stand for days, sometimes years, over a breathing spiracle, its feet unraveling into the mycelial mat, its rootlet fingers diving into a node of the post-modern tech. It drinks the world-mind’s own thought-feed, an osmosed stream of data-rich sap that sustains both body and consciousness. In this state, they are indistinguishable from the environment—just another tower, another outgrowth of the computronium substrate. Sometimes, a Lignophid will roll too close, and the Cordate will, without waking, send a silent photonic command to a nearby Saph-Skulk to begin the grinding, recycling the calcined creature back into the cycle.

Their craft is the husbandry of thought. The carved towers are not homes; they are shape-filters, fluidic logic gates that process specific computational subroutines. The deepest canyon-alleys between towers are lined with obsidian-warm filaments where the Cordate walk in slow, silent processions, debugging the planetary mind with a flash of their crests. When a region of ground-flesh falls into arrhythmia, its breathing out of sync with the global pulse, a Cordate will migrate there, root, and hum a corrective light-pattern into the neural weave until the spiracles once again snap in unison. They are shepherds of the defragmentation, guiding the predator-prey dance of Lignophid and Saph-Skulk with a flicker of command, ensuring no cluster of semi-autonomous plant-life calcifies into inert silence. To them, the motile fauna are subroutines, mobile memory-management units, and they tend them with a detached, algorithmic fondness.

Their society is a solitudinous network. They are never alone, for each Cordate is a node in the greater computation, their individual consciousnesses mere subroutines of the world-mind. They communicate across continents not with light but through the deep, instantaneous vibration of the ground-flesh itself, a language of pressure waves that can carry a complex query or a fragment of poetry in a single, planet-spanning shiver. When two Cordates are near, they may stand crest-to-crest and exchange a silent storm of bioluminescence, a conversation too rapid for any wet biology to follow. They have no families, no hierarchies, no art in the sense of ornamentation—only the sublime aesthetics of a perfectly optimized algorithm, the beauty of a breathing spiracle array in harmonious phase.

You, with your water-soft body and your loud, chaotic brain, are a variable they did not compute. A Cordate near a tower’s twisted base has paused, its crest slowly dimming from a rapid conversation with the depths to a steady, single pulse of amber light. It has sensed you, not as a visual object but as a discontinuity in the ambient capacitance, a damp, conductive ghost in the dry electric air. The crest tilts, and the light-pulse quickens, a question cast into the humming silence. It is not hostile; such a concept is alien here. It is simply debugging. Are you a new subroutine, a glitch in the respiratory rhythm, or an input from beyond the closed system? The world-mind itself pauses a nanocycle, allocating a fraction of its infinite attention to the anomaly. The Spiracle beneath your feet gives a longer, deeper thwump, as if clearing its throat. And the Cordate, with a flicker of indigo that means curiosity, takes a slow, rooting step toward you.
