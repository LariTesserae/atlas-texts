They are called, in the click-and-hum speech they use among themselves, something that translates roughly to *the ones who listen*. Their physical form is uncannily human—two arms, two legs, upright posture, hands with opposable thumbs—but evolution in the hollow dark has sculpted them to a different final draft. They stand no taller than a human adolescent, bone-light and slender, their frames honed for a life spent balancing on strands no wider than a handspan. Their skin is a pale, luminous gray, overlaid with a fine down of translucent fur that traps warmth and breaks up their silhouette in the dim fungal glow. Their faces are dominated by eyes: huge, circular, and ink-black, with pupils that can dilate to the full width of the socket, drinking in every photon. Their ears are small, mere ridges of cartilage, for hearing in this world is not about air-pressure waves but about vibration. They hear through their feet, through the palms of their hands, through a dense network of Pacinian-like corpuscles in every limb that read the tremble of the catwalks and the distant footfalls of prey or predator. The constant bass drone of the wind through the plant’s pipes is their cradle, their warning, their music, and they are never, even in sleep, fully separate from it.

Their shelters are woven, as the scattered ruins suggest, from the plant’s own fibers. But those abandoned domes are the remnants of a different time, a florescence of their kind that has since given way to a more austere existence. Today they build nothing permanent. Instead, they carry their homes as cocoon-like sleeping sacks rolled from the silk of tamed spinner-moths, light enough to be bundled across a shoulder and unfurled in any angle, anchored to the catwalks with a few quick loops. They are nomads, tracking the mist-fronts that carry the richest blooms of microscopic yeast and the grazers that follow. They know the grand hollows by names that are sequences of resonant pitches; they navigate not by sight but by a mental map of acoustics, the way each chamber shapes a specific harmonic, the way the wind-song shifts when a passage widens or narrows. A child can learn to hum a particular note and, from the echo alone, tell exactly where in a thousand-kilometer gallery they stand.

Their tools are the ones you have seen, and they make them with a brutal economy. Obsidian is precious—trade goods carried up from the lower depths where the plant’s roots fissure volcanic glass—and a single blade is passed through generations, its flake scars telling a lineage. Bone from the balloon-grazers becomes needles, hooks, and most importantly, whistles. Each hunter carries a set of drilled phalanges, tuned to the frequencies that summon the predatory gliders—not to call them close, but to mimic their mating cries, luring them into ambush. The hunt is a three-dimensional ballet: a handful of *listeners* scatter to predetermined perches, one begins the whistle, and when the glider swoops down in hope or rivalry, a flung net of braided sinew snares its many wings, and a swift bite to the throat—their incisors are oddly long and sharp—ends the struggle. Nothing is wasted. Blood is drained into bladders for cooking later; bones become tools; sinew becomes cordage; even the creature’s bioluminescent organs are carefully removed and inserted into hollowed reed-tubes to make hand-lamps that respond to the bearer’s own body heat.

Their social structure is loose, flexible, a constant fission-fusion of small bands numbering five to fifteen. Language is a hybrid of vocalized clicks, hums, and percussive beats struck on their own chests or on taut strands, capable of carrying across vast voids. They have no concept of privacy as surface-dwellers know it; a conversation a kilometer away can be heard clearly if one presses one’s hand to the right wall, and so every death, every birth, every dispute and reconciliation ripples outward as a public event. They do not lie—not because of virtue, but because the reverberant world tells too much; the tension in a voice, the hesitant rhythm of a tapping finger, all of it propagates and is understood by anyone paying attention. Instead, they have evolved an elaborate etiquette of omission, a way of not-saying that allows for private thought within the communal sonic ocean.

Their cosmology is embedded in the plant itself. They call it the *Everbreath*, and they believe that its hollow heart is the body of a dreaming god whose voice is the wind. Each flute-tube, each cathedral chamber, is a vocal cord or a lung. Their dead are given to the plant: laid in the slow-collapse zones where older tissue is dissolving, wrapped in their sleeping sacks, returned to the skeletal lattice. The fungal luminescence that tracks their footsteps is, in their lore, the visible memory of all those who have walked before—a green fire lit by the ghosts that accompany them always. Shamans, or memory-keepers, ingest a paste made from the sporulating bodies of a particular cliff-side fungus, and in the ensuing trance they claim to see the full three-dimensional map of the Everbreath’s interior, past and future, all the branching extensions it will grow in the next ten thousand years.

And they are still here. They are not remnants. The abandoned shelters you find are not signs of extinction but of a deliberate technological shedding—a decision made some thousand generations ago to leave behind the heavy burden of fixed homes in favor of perfect mobility, to become so light that they vanish into the web. They see outside creatures like you as heavy, noisy, blind things, lumbering past in ignorance of the quiet paths. As you walk the swaying catwalk, a dozen of them probably watch from the shadows of an upper flange, their black eyes reflecting no light, their bodies wrapped in mist-fur, utterly still. They debate in soft tapping whether to reveal themselves. Usually, they decide against it. You are not dangerous, but you are a source of unpredictable noise, a disturbance in the wind-song, and they prefer the song undisturbed. If you are very, very still, and listen not with your ears but with your fingertips placed on the nearest rib-strand, you might catch the faint, rhythmic tap-tap-tap of their decision fading into the distance as they drift away into the deeper, darker galleries where even the fungal light is dim, and the voice of the Everbreath drops to a subsonic hum that only bones can feel.
