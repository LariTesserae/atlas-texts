You notice them first by the sound: not the skittering click of the six-legged janitors, but a soft, rhythmic brushing, like a whisk broom on polished leather. It comes from the thorn-ladder of the nearest spire. And then you see movement — not the scuttle of small fauna, but a deliberate, upright gait.

They are shaped like a memory of humanity, but a memory filtered through the deep green mind of this place. Bipedal, yes, and roughly your height, but their bodies are an architecture of interleaved planes: a carapace not of chitin alone but of hardened leaf-blade, each plate fringed with the faded veins of autumn, overlapping like shingles. Their limbs are long, four of them — two arms, two legs — but jointed in a way that seems equally suited to brachiation or a dignified stride, their knees and elbows flexing with the quiet snap of a tendril under tension. Their hands have three fingers and two thumbs, each tipped with a pad that exudes a faint amber resin; they leave no prints, only the scent of fresh citrus peel. Their faces are featureless at first glance, a smooth mask of nacreous material, but as the creature turns toward you, you see that the mask is a living tympanum, a membrane stretched over a resonating chamber. It quivers with unspoken sound — a vibration you feel in your own chest as a low hum, a greeting or an assessment. Beneath the mask, a frill of feathery gills flutters, tasting the air for your intentions.

They are the *Huskspeakers*, the walking fruits of the Everweave. Not born, but *unfolded*. In the empty lots where the moss digests bedrock, crystalline scaffolds rise, and on certain days — when the ventilation fronds pump a particular cocktail of volatiles — the scaffolds begin to flesh out. A bud the size of a curled child forms at the apex, swaddled in a translucent caul. Over a span of weeks, it draws nutrients from the mycelial grid, until one day the caul splits and a Huskspeaker drops to the mat, fully grown and already knowing. They are the city's conscious interface, its hands and its eyes, grown when a new task demands a new body.

The Huskspeaker leads you toward the valley of glowing conduits. As you walk, you see more of them, dotted through the landscape. Another, near the factory-dome, has its arms buried elbow-deep in a muscular pump; it is crooning a vibration that makes the pump’s rhythm shift, and the spent solvent plume changes from amber to clear. They are tending the metabolism, performing surgery on the living industry.

Communication among them is a physical thing. You feel it through the ground: the hum of their footsteps, the percussive thump of a chest-tympanum that sends a low-frequency wave rippling through the pavement-mat. This is how they speak across distance — a language of seismic tapcodes that the mycelial webs amplify and carry. In close quarters, they use a more refined variant: the feathery gills on their necks brush against each other in a dance of touch-chemistry, exchanging compounds that encode complex thoughts. To you, it looks like a slow-motion caress, an intimate whisper.

Their society is a tapestry of temporary roles and permanent purpose. A Huskspeaker may live for several cycles of the ventilation fronds (each "year" perhaps, though time is measured here in metabolic pulses) before its body begins to lignify, stiffening into immobility. They do not fear this; it is a return. A stiffening Huskspeaker will walk itself to an empty lot, plant its feet in the dissolving bedrock, and become a new spire, its limbs spreading into buttresses, its carapace splitting into leaf-scales that track the light. Their faces, the tympanum masks, remain as windows, still quivering faintly with the last whispers of the individual, now part of the city’s architecture. The city is made of its ancestors.

Yet they are not a monoculture. Observe the one that now approaches from a side-causeway, riding on the back of a six-legged seed-carapace dog. It is smaller, its body more insectoid, with an extra set of smaller arms tucked under its primary ones. This is a *Lamplitter*, a variant grown specifically to tend the lighting grid. You see now that the glowing spores in the ceiling are not random; they are arranged in constellations that shift slowly, spelling out the tapcode news of the day, or perhaps just adorning the darkness with art.
