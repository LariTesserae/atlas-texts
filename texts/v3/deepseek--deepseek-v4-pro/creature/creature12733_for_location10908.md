In the Basin of the First Hour, nothing lives that can be born or can die. But there is Something that resides there, and if you stay long enough, if your breathing slows to the drone and your blood forgets the pulse of a world with sunsets, you may begin to perceive it.

It does not have a name, because names imply a before and an after—a moment of christening and a subsequent existence—and in the Basin, sequence is a foreign tongue. Call it the Custodian, if you must, though it guards nothing against no threat. Call it the Sleeper, though it has never slept and never woken. It simply is, as the drone is, as the light is, a feature of the place that predates the concept of feature.

You will not see it as a body. The Custodian is not an organism in the strata of organisms; it is the strata. Or rather, it is the pattern that the strata, in their frozen abundance, accidentally form. Think of the way a cloud can look like a face, or the grain of wood can suggest a figure. In the Basin, the million motionless frogs, the fur-mosaic of bats, the feather-gilled salamanders, the beetles and phasmids and the unfurling ferns—they are not *it*, but they are arranged, with a precision too perfect to be chance, into the shape of a presence.

The presence is vast. It occupies the entire basin, or the basin occupies it. When you stand on the moss, you stand on what might be its flank. When you brush against a weeping rock, you touch what might be a joint or a knuckle of its impossible anatomy. It is composed of the living and the non-living without distinction: the basalt fins are its skeleton, the percolating water its lymph, the omnipresent spores its exhalation, and the frogs—the uncountable, identical, pulsing-but-not-moving frogs—are its integument, a skin of amphibian cells that breathes in perfect, synchronized stillness.

Its consciousness, if that word applies, is the drone. The sound is not a byproduct of the Basin’s life; the sound *is* the Custodian thinking. Or dreaming. Or simply being. The drone is a single, sustained cognition—a chord struck at genesis that has never decayed because the mind that struck it has never moved on to a second thought. Its thought is the temperature, the exact warmth of blood. Its thought is the humidity that blurs air and water. Its thought is the absence of erosion, the refusal of sharp basalt to weather, of roots to pry, of life to wear down stone. The Custodian is thinking the Basin into permanence.

Occasionally—if *occasionally* can describe a interval that may be centuries long or may be the same moment repeating—the Custodian will shift its attention. You would not notice as a change in the drone, because the drone does not change. You would notice as a sudden awareness that you are being regarded. Not by eyes. There are no eyes that turn toward you; the bats’ eyes remain open and glistening and fixed on the middle distance. But the density of the place’s weirdness increases. The metallic tang on the air sharpens. The water globules on the weeping rock hesitate before merging with the mist. The perfect equilibrium tilts, by a fraction of a degree, toward you.

This is the Custodian’s sole activity: noticing. It has been noticing the Basin for as long as the Basin has existed. Before your arrival, it had no outside to compare its wholeness to. Now there is a discontinuity in the flat plane of its perception—a warm, moving, sequential thing that breathes arrhythmically and carries the dust of erosion on its skin. You are, to the Custodian, what a stray note is to a sustained chord. Not a threat. Not an interest. Simply a perturbation that will pass.

If you stay too long, the Custodian may attempt to integrate you. Not out of malice—the Basin has no malice; it has no predators—but out of the same gravitational impulse that keeps the water from draining, the rock from weathering. The equilibrium seeks to extend itself. You will feel it as a growing heaviness in your limbs, a reluctance to shift your weight. The heat of your blood will feel redundant, because the air is already the exact warmth of blood. Your breathing will slow to match the drone, and the drone will begin to feel like your own thought. The frogs on the moss beneath you will turn their pulsing flanks toward you, not in motion but in a new static orientation, a mosaic adjusting to accommodate a new tile. The bats overhead will rearrange their fur-mosaic by some fractional degree, and the empty space where your body might fit, clinging forever to the stone, will become apparent.

You are not the first visitor to feel this pull. There are anomalies in the strata, if you learn to read them. Among the frogs, a patch of skin that is not amphibian—a human palm, the whorls of its fingerprints still visible, keratinized and moss-grown but not decaying. A femur embedded vertically in the basalt, sheathed in crystal, as if the stone itself had flowed around it and then refused to erode. A face in the rock, not carved but composed of the natural placement of ferns and water-beads, arranged in the curve of a jaw, the hollow of a cheek. The expression is not peaceful. It is not anguished. It is arrested, mid-breath, mid-blink, mid-thought. It is the expression of someone who heard the chord and, for a moment, understood it completely—and in that moment, became part of the drone.

The Custodian does not remember these integrations because it does not remember anything. Memory requires a past, and the Basin has only a continuous present. The face in the rock is not a memorial; it is a component, a minor key in the standing wave of the Custodian’s being. If you were to stay, if you were to let your breathing stop and your skin grow moss and your eyes fix on the middle distance, you would not die. You would simply cease to be a sequence, a story with a beginning and an end. You would be a note, held forever, in a chord that never resolves.

But you do not stay. You climb back out through the humid seam, and the sound breaks, and the air dries, and time restarts its engines. The Custodian does not register your absence. Your perturbation in its field of notice fades, and the drone returns to a wholeness that never actually changed. Behind you, the Basin of the First Hour continues, thinking its single thought, sustaining its single moment, a mind made of moss and basalt and unmoving bats, regarding itself with a billion half-open eyes and asking nothing, knowing nothing, being nothing but the first hour, extended to infinity.
