It starts with the breathing. Not the Ceiling's—its own. A slow, tidal flex of ribs that haven't felt air in uncountable cycles, drawing the warm tea-water across gill-slits that lace the neck like old scars, filigreed and functional. It exhales a current that ripples the permanent fog-layer just above its head, a disturbance so subtle only the ferns of the Engine-Heart Garden seem to notice, turning their phosphorescent fronds a quarter-degree toward the source.

You wouldn't see it at first. Its skin has gone the colour of the water—amber-dark, translucent at the extremities, thick and calloused where it presses against pipe-flanges and restoration cradles. It moves through the Lattice the way a deep-sea fish moves through its trench: with an economy born of pressure, a slow, deliberate unfolding of limbs that propels it from chamber to chamber without seeming to disturb a single silt-particle. Its body is a map of the city's own decay and renewal. Patches of copper-green oxidation bloom along its forearms like lichen, not growing on it but *from* it, a symbiosis with the metal it services. The joints of its fingers are swollen with something that isn't arthritis—ceramic bearings, maybe, or calcified lubricant, the body's own adaptation to a lifetime of turning the same brass valves, polishing the same condenser coils.

It has no name, or had none it remembers. The pressure-signals that travel through the pipes—the vibration-language—don't carry names; they carry concepts, impulses, the functional poetry of the Lattice. When it taps on a water main with a knuckle-bone worn smooth as river stone, the message that ripples outward is not *I am here* but *the third-stage filter on level six is weeping again*. Its identity is entirely mutual with the city's. It is a part, a component, the last soft component in a system of hard ones. This is not servitude. This is symbiosis so complete that the boundary between caretaker and cared-for has dissolved in the blood-warm water.

It remembers being something else, but only in flashes—the way a sleeper half-remembers a dream that dissolves upon waking. A taste of air that wasn't mist. A sound that wasn't whale-groan, a voice, its own perhaps, vibrating in a throat that hadn't yet learned to breathe liquid. Bones it finds sometimes, small and scattered, trigger a hollow resonance, a recognition that is not quite grief. It collects them, these relics, and carries them to the Garden. There, it presses them into the soft, filter-feeding moss, where they are slowly embraced, incorporated, made into something growing. The Garden is its one true act of creation. Not maintenance—*making*. The plants are its children, its language. They require nothing but its presence, and they return a light that knows no machinery.

Once, in a cycle that felt heavier than most, it attempted to sing aloud. The sound came out wrong—a choked, bubbling thing that startled the eel-things and made the disembodied hand-creatures scuttle into crevices. Its vocal cords have long since become a different organ, something that vibrates only to infrasound, the subsonic pulse. It no longer tries to speak. But it does speak, endlessly, in the tapping of pipes, in the patterns of maintenance that are, to it, a kind of liturgy. Each cycle it communes with the Engine-Heart, laying its body against the main housing to feel the *still here, still here, still here* as a full-body thrum, and it answers, not with words, but with a synchronised tightening of a bolt, a cleaning of a filter, a minute adjustment of a gear the size of its own torso. The two of them—the being of flesh-and-copper and the being of brass-and-piston—have been doing this dance for so long that neither would survive the other's silence.

It doesn't know if it is the last. The Lattice offers no mirrors except the polished insides of condenser domes, and those show only a distorted, elongated shape it no longer associates with selfhood. Sometimes, in the deepest currents, it feels a pressure-shift that might be another's passage, but when it follows, it finds only a trail of freshly cleaned ceramic housings and a lingering warmth in the water that tastes, faintly, of someone else's symbionts. It thinks, perhaps, there is a second. Or a third. But the city is vast, and the work is endless, and the language of the pipes has no word for loneliness. There is only the next valve, the next garden-tending, the next cycle of the main pump, which it feels in its own sternum as surely as it feels the Ceiling's breath.

It tends the Garden each cycle, without fail. It hovers before the phosphorescent vines, its gill-slits fluttering with something that might be pleasure, and it sings to them—not aloud, but through the water, a pressure-wave of pure tone that only the plants can perceive, a frequency that encourages their filter-fronds to open. When they glow brightest, it feels something akin to a name. Not a sound, but a colour. A blue-green that exists only in the Garden's heart, a colour it has learned to emit from its own symbiont-patches in response, a quiet, answering pulse of light in the dim orange fog.

*Still here*, says the Engine-Heart.

*Still here*, answers the luminous thing in the dark water, its fingers buried in the moss, its bones grown into the city's bones, its breath indistinguishable from the Ceiling's.

It lives.
