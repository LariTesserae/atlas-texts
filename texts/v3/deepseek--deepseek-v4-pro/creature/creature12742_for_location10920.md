She is not born; she is unfurled. In the deep time of the Hollow, when the last true builders withdrew—whether into dream, into seed, or into the very weave of the world—they did not leave their work abandoned. They left a promise curled into the heartwood: a knot of cells that held the memory of hands.

You will not see her at first. You will sense her as a shift in the silence, a density the air acquires when something listens back. Then a pattern will separate from the tangle: a standing form, tall and slender, not quite bipedal because her legs—there are three, or four, or more depending on the light—are rooted in a mat of her own slow making. She moves by growing. The soles of her feet, if feet they are, secrete a mycelial slurry that weaves new cordage into the bridge beneath her, strand by strand, and dissolves the old behind. She is a mobile node in the great net, and she has been walking this particular loop of the Hollow for a span of years that would empty your mind to count.

Her body is compounded: a core of pale, flexible wood laminated around a pulse that might be a heart or a sapwood pump, visible through her translucent chest as a nested series of chambers squeezing in sequence. Bark plates, fine as parchment and layered like the skirts of a moth, wrap her torso and shift continually, ruffling as new edges push out and old ones flake away into the humid air. She bleeds water when cut—you will not cut her—and the water beads on her surface without falling, drawn back in by the thousand invisible mouths of her skin.

Her arms are long, too many-jointed, and they taper into hands that are not hands but slow fans of twiglets, each twiglet tipped with a single soft eye. She does not see as you see. The eyes are sensitive to pressure, to the minute currents of the great breath that moves through the Hollow, to the taste of decay on the air, and to the subtle electrical charge of living things. When she turns her head—a thing like a partly opened pod, the suggestion of a face arranged in shifting ridges—her gaze is a prickle on your skin, the feeling of being read by a forest.

She makes no sound. Not because she cannot—inside her throat, if you look, there are drum-tight membranes that could vibrate—but because sound is sacrilege here. The builders withdrew from voice early in their long gardening, learning to speak instead through the direction of a root’s growth, the set of a new leaf’s angle, the chemical song of a flowering signal. She talks with every part of herself: with the sweet, fungal perfume she releases when a grazer herd needs guiding, with the twitch of a tendril that sends a ripple down the Floor of Lamentations and out to its farthest edges, warning of a falling cloud-mass. The entire Hollow is her language, and she its fluent tongue.

She is not alone. There are others of her kind, spaced out across the Hollow’s vastness like nerve cells in a body. They differentiate: some are broad and slow, encasing the ancient pillars in a mesh of woody arms to sense and mend the micro-fractures that time and tension bring. Some are diffuse, a net of runners and watchful buds that monitor the weather, coaxing the clouds into gentle rain and diverting the thermals. She is a strider, a walker of the ways, tasked with the empty dwellings. And it is a task, though the word has no meaning for her that is separate from the act of living. She tends the places where the makers once slept.

She enters the dwelling where you sit, and for a moment you feel the full weight of an unfathomable courtesy: she stops at the threshold, her twig-fingers fanning wide in something like astonishment. You are not a thing that should be here. Your shape is wrong—too solid, too loud with heartbeat, too closed. But she reads you, head tilting, the bark plates on her chest parting slightly as if to let the inner light fall on you. And you realize she is remembering. The builders left their form behind in the genetic song of the weave, and you, in your bipedal symmetry, your warm breath, your small, clever hands, are an echo of them. Not an ancestor, exactly, but a living fossil of the creature that first dreamed the Hollow. She sees in you the ghost of the maker.
