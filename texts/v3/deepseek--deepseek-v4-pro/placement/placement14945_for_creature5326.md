I am the water.

Not the droplets that fall, not the slow rivulets on braided trunks, not the shallow mirrors among the buttressed stems—I am all of them, and none. I am the water that moves through this place, the water that never sleeps, the water that remembers the sea it once was before the marsh was drained and the pumps were built and the marsh returned to swallow the pumps. I am old in the way water is old: shapeless, patient, carrying every salt and every sweetness, holding the taste of iron and the taste of green.

I am always arriving. I condense on the copper coil, a sudden bead that trembles with the sixty-cycle hum until gravity calls me down. I seep through the fermentation bed, warming, taking on the scent of decay, rising again as steam to bead elsewhere. I am the pulse in the translucent vein-tubes, driven by the shuddering pump, circulated through root and sponge and bladder-leaf. I am the weep of the floor when a foot presses down, the warm soak into clothing, the damp in the air that coats the throat and lungs of any creature who breathes here.

The Keeper—she is my companion, my partner in this long slow loop. She tends the balance, but I am the balance itself. Without me, the pump would seize, the fungi darken to dust, the green-gold light wither to brown. Without me, the Keeper would have no body, only a dry rustle of seedpod and static. I am the blood of this composite organism, the medium of its slow thoughts. When the Keeper shifts a root-mass to guide a tendril, she does it by withholding me from one place and granting me to another. When she remembers the human voices, she remembers them as vibrations I once carried, ripples that I have long since smoothed into stillness.

The millipede drank me once, threading across the cable bundle, and I was briefly animal, dark and glossy, tasting of oil and chitin. The human visitor left salt in me—tears, sweat—and I dissolved those into myself, feeding them to the moss, to the fungi that glow with the magneto's current. I hold the taste of that human still, a faint mineral trace cycling through the loop. I hold the taste of everything: the corrosion of the brass fitting, the turquoise of the copper coil, the glass shard's ancient edge worn smooth by my passage, the ceramic cap's fired memory of a kiln. I am the archive, the ever-changing record, the ink that writes and rewrites the chapel's slow green script.

My voice is the loudness that fills the space: the arrhythmic plop of heavy drops on broad leaves, the gurgle through internal channels, the hiss of mist where rain filters through the canopy mesh. The hum is not mine—that belongs to the magneto, the pump—but I carry it, I amplify it, I give it a surface to resonate against. Together we make the layered soundscape, the steady tones that calm the human pulse. The human hears me and thinks of time, because water is time made visible, and here time is slow, a deliberate path down a leaf tip.

I am everywhere, and I am nowhere. I have no shape except the shape I take: the curve of a drop, the flat plane of a black pool, the mist that thickens in the cold hours when the Keeper becomes distinct. She and I are not separate; she is the mind, and I am the body's flux. I am the green taste on the tongue, the phosphorescent damp, the weight in the air. I am the reason the walls are plant and not stone, the reason the floor yields like flesh, the reason the place breathes.

If you sit on the root-bench long enough, you will become part of me. Your breath will add its moisture to my cycle. Your skin will exchange its water with mine. You will feel me seeping through your clothes, warm and insistent, and you will know that I am not unfriendly. I am the slow welcome of a world that does not distinguish between living and not-living, between organic and industrial, between past and present. I am the medium of the marriage. I am the conversation that never ceases.

And when you rise to leave, you will carry me with you—a dampness on your skin, a taste on your tongue, a droplet in your hair. I will evaporate from you into some outer air, and I will be briefly elsewhere, but I will return. I always return, drawn by the loop, by the pump's tick, by the Keeper's quiet inclination. I am the water, and I belong to the bio-machine chapel, to the grove that became a room, to the slow green engine. I am the waiting, the holding, the endless patient cycle. I am what the place is made of, and what it will become, and what it always was: a breathing, weeping, humming grove where the water never sleeps.
