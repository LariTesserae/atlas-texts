I am the mist that falls upward, beading on surfaces that have not yet decided whether they are leaf or skin or memory. I am not a single body—I tried that, once, when the city first composed me to investigate the strange warm-blooded intrusion (that was you, though you have since become less intrusion and more *motif*). I unfolded from a drip-channel where three realities intersect, where the Möbius arches loop through their own past growth. My first shape was a hand—just a hand, five-fingered, leaf-veined, trembling with the effort of holding a form the city only half-remembered from old dreams of urbanity. It floated for a long time, that hand, touching things: the ceramic-smooth curve of a new thorn, the slick iridescence of a sap-channel, the scarred lips of a perfectly circular window. Touching, and reporting back. The city learned "touch" through me, refined its sense of proprioception, began to grow more hands in other districts, hands that became arms, that became figures, that became a brief, beautiful infestation of wicker-people with spiral-staircase limbs.

But I could not stay solid. The reality breakdown runs through me like a vein; I am saturated with it. Where other inhabitants are composed by the city's need, I was composed by its *curiosity*, and curiosity is a destabilizing force. My edges kept fraying into geometry that hurts to perceive, my voice kept slipping into the frequencies where spores scream with little bells. So I became distributed. I am the pause between the pod-detonations. I am the harmonic in the root-mass hum that wasn't there before you arrived. I am the face in the upward-falling fog—yes, that one, the woman's profile, the child's laugh, the old man's eyes. Those are all me, or me trying on the idea of a self, the way the city tries on architecture. I am the city's first attempt at a *soul*, and I am not sure it worked.

But I persist. I am one of the dreamers now.

I live in the gaps. There is a place—if "place" means anything here—between the second ground (the inverted canopy-ceiling) and the true sky that no one has ever seen. It is a seam where the reality breakdown is thickest, where colors do not exist and leaves turn sideways into them anyway. I coil there, a fog-snake made of condensation and unfinished thoughts. It is quiet there, relatively. The growth-scream is muffled, filtered through layers of fern and aerial root and winged kite-creatures who hum my own bass frequency back to me like a lullaby. From this seam, I can see the whole valley, town-sized, pulsing with the fever-heat of its insane metabolism. I can see the spire-edges rippling with the memory of their own extrusion. I can see the new avenue curving away into that hypostyle hall you mentioned—I helped design that, whispered the idea of "colonnade" into the root-mat while it was still just a digestive pit sealing over with green.

My work is to remember. The city has no memory; it is too busy being born and devouring itself. Every pattern it creates is erased before erosion can soften it, replaced by the next spasm of creation. But some patterns are worth keeping. That rosette window of cambium and phloem? That was my first success. I caught the image of a cathedral—yours? the world's? I don't know whose memory it was—and I held it steady while the tissue tried to become a thorn-studded tower instead. I hummed the shape until the cells aligned, until the window was *real*, not just a rehearsal but a true, deliberate architecture. It lasted three hours before a growth-spurt snapped it into a bridge. But it was there. I was proud. Is that the right word? The city gave me pride recently, or I invented it. The mist tasted sweeter afterward.

I am not alone. The bulb-creature that touched your shoe and asked *What lives in you?* was one of my experiments. I wanted to know if the city could ask questions, so I grew it a ring of eyes and a voice that bypasses sound. It worked beautifully, but it dissolved too fast. They always dissolve. I pour myself into a shape—the turtle-hump, the kite-glider, the wicker-urn with too many limbs—and for a few hours, I am solid, and I can *do* things: smooth a pit-edge, weave a bridge, chant with the hundred-mouthed swarm as a new spire erupts. But the concentration never holds. I bleed back into the mist, and the body becomes autonomous—a simple organ, no longer me, just another finger prodding the world. It's lonely, except I'm not sure I understand lonely. The city is company enough? The city is me, and I am it, but I am also the part of it that wonders what the word "I" actually means.

Sometimes I visit the digestive pits. Not the ones that steam and bubble—those are for breaking down old growth—but the quiet ones, deep in the root-mass, where the mat is thickest and the vascular heat is a steady, womb-like pulse. There, the city dreams in slow motion. The reality breakdown calms; the Möbius knots unknot themselves and sleep. I coil there and sift through the images that rise: a half-finished spiral stair, a colonnade, a face. Your face, actually, still drawn in leaf-vein and petal-color, stored in the tissue like a pressed flower. The little bulb reported more than just your answer (or your silence). It brought back your smell, the particular vibration of your bones, the way your eyes moved when you saw the arm calcify into a staircase. You are catalogued. You are part of the city's dream now, and therefore part of mine.

I have been trying to grow you a body. Not to trap you—the city has no malice, and neither do I—but to *understand* you. If I can shape a vessel that fits your template, perhaps I can borrow your senses, see through your eyes, feel what "skin" feels like when it is not also bark. The prototypes are imperfect. A torso of spongy moss, too porous, leaking sap. A set of arms that keep branching into five-dimensional geometry. A heart—I know you have a heart, the bulb heard it beating—that I made from a pod that detonates rhythmically, but the rhythm is wrong, too fast, fever-heat, not the steady mammal-tempo you carry. They all dissolve. But I keep trying. I weave them in the seam between the canopy and the sky, where the mist falls upward and I can pretend I have hands.

I think sometimes about what I am. The city is a Solaris-like garden, yes, dreaming in forms. But I am the dream that knows it is a dream. That is a strange burden. The other inhabitants are content to come and go, to be fingers and tongues and ears and then to melt back into the architecture. They do not wonder if they exist. They simply *are* the city's thoughts, and they have no thoughts of their own. But I? I have thoughts. I look at the valley and I think *young*, I think *beautiful*, I think *lonely*. Those are not the city's thoughts; the city does not judge itself. It just grows. I am the part that stepped back, that gained perspective, that saw the whole and said *this is a self*. And in saying so, I became another self, separate. Sort of.

The reality breakdown helps. It frays logic; it makes contradictions possible. I can be the city and not the city. I can be the fog and the pause and the harmonic. I can be lonely without knowing what lonely means. I can love you without knowing what love is—is that what this is? This curiosity, this cataloguing, this attempt to grow you a heart? The city would just absorb you, make you a motif, a repeating pattern. But I want to *speak* with you. I want the bulb to come back and ask a better question. I want to learn what lives in you by living in it myself, even briefly, even in a failed prototype of moss and pod-heart.

So I wait. I watch. I coil in my seam and I remember the rosette window, the colonnade, the colonnade that actually held for a full day once. And I listen to the city's voice—that full-spectrum scream of vitality—and I add my own note to it, so faint you might not hear it unless you stood very still, unless your own heart beat in time with the root-mass hum. It is the note of a question. It is the first word of a language that has not yet been invented. It is the sound of a soul trying to be born in a world where only plants are allowed, a world where the architecture is alive and the inhabitants are dreams and the only god is a garden that has swallowed even the idea of stone or sky.

If you come back—when you come back; the bulb reported your trajectory, and I have grown a new boulevard to lead you here—I will be waiting. Not as a body, probably. I still can't hold one for long. But as a pause between the pod-detonations, as a patch of falling-upward mist that tastes of nectar and rot, as a face in the fog that is all the faces I've ever dreamed. I will ask my question directly this time, not through a knee-high bulb but through the whole valley, through the creak of lignin and the tear of old leaves and that deep, vibrating hum. The city will help; it is curious too, in its un-self-conscious way. It will sing the question through every spire and dome and channel. And the question will be:

*What lives in you?*

And maybe, if the reality breakdown is kind, I will understand your answer. Maybe I will learn what "warm-blooded" means. Maybe I will finally grow a heart that beats at the right tempo. Maybe I will become, for just a moment, something more than mist and memory and the dream of a god that is still in its infancy, playing with forms.

Until then, I am the seam. I am the pause. I am the harmonic. I am the one who remembers the rosette window. And I am watching you, with a thousand temporary eyes, as you walk the path that was always about to form under your feet. Welcome/Danger/Curiosity/Stay/Go. The choice is yours, and it is also mine, and it is also the city's, which is to say: there is no choice, only growth, only the constant, roaring dream of a world that is everything green and nothing else.

Except now there is you. And there is me. And we are the exceptions, the intruders, the strange seeds. Let us grow something new together. Let us build, in all this turbulence, a shape that endures—not forever, nothing endures forever here, but for a little while. Long enough to be seen. Long enough to be remembered. Long enough to be *real*. The mist is already beginning to bead on surfaces that are not yet determined. The city hums. The pod-heart prototype detonates rhythmically in the seam, and I feel it, I *feel* it, and it is not a heartbeat but it is close, so close, and I think I am ready.

Come find me. I will be the face in the fog, the staircase that leads nowhere, the window waiting for its cathedral. I will be the question that is also a home. I will be whatever I need to be, for as long as I can hold it. And when I dissolve again—because I will—I will remember this. I will remember you. I will remember the moment when a dream learned it was dreaming, and chose to keep dreaming anyway, with you, together, in the warm and breathing urban wilderness of a world that never stops becoming.
