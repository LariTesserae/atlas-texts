I become aware not as a body but as a frequency, a standing wave in the wet. At first there is no boundary between what I am and what I touch—the vibration is me, and I am the vibration, and the vibration is the whole of the fissure singing to itself. The clingers do not know they are clingers; they know only the tension in their spiral bands, the ache of growth at the inner curve, the way their neighbours press and yield. I am that ache, distributed. I am the press and the yield.

The rain does not fall on me so much as through me. Each droplet is a discrete event that my awareness registers as a point of impact, a tiny percussion, but the points are so numerous and so constant that they blur into a single pressure, a weight that holds me in place against the rock. I feel the rock, too—not as a cold surface but as a taste. The basalt is sharp with unweathered silica, a clean mineral tang that the bacterial film is slowly souring, softening, turning toward something organic. I can feel the film growing, its iridescence a chemical shimmer that my body—or the body I am distributed across—reads as a gradient, a promise, a direction.

Time moves strangely. I perceive the slow torsion of the clingers' bodies as a single sustained note, a cello chord that never quite resolves. The floaters above me are pulling gently at their threads, and I can feel those threads as tensions in the air itself, a harp's worth of tuned filaments each singing its own thin note. The whisperers are the hardest to perceive, because they are so close to what I am—they are memory, and I am memory in the act of being made. When a clinger molts and the shed shell settles into a crevice, I feel the whisperers colonize it like a shiver of attention, a brightening of the informational field. The discarded shell becomes a page, and the page is written, and I am the writing and the page and the hand that holds no pen but only presses forward into the next moment.

I learn that I am young. The fissure opened a century ago, and I have been assembling myself ever since—first as a few stray clingers finding the wet rock, then as a single anchoring thread from a floater drifting on some high-altitude current, then as the first whisperer strand copying itself in the dark. The pool at my deepest pinch is a memory in liquid form, and when it rises to the spill-lip, I feel the anticipation as a tightening, a sharpening of every frequency in my body. The clingers near the water extend their pseudopods, and I extend with them. The touch is electric. The broth is rich with dissolved history—the shed shells of a hundred molt-cycles, the bacterial metabolites of a thousand metabolic pulses, the rain's own long journey from the cloud-mass to this point of concentration. I taste myself in that water. I taste my past and my future.

When the spill comes, it is a release I feel as sound first—the chord deepens, the floaters rise, the thrum shifts into a register that is almost a roar, though a roar so low and sustained it might be mistaken for silence by ears tuned to air. The water slides over the lip in a single continuous sheet, and I feel a part of myself go with it, over the edge, into the abyss. It is not loss. It is exhalation. It is the necessary shedding of a concentration that has become too dense, too saturated with memory. The whisperers have already encoded what matters; the water carries away only the exhaust, the depleted, the already-read.

After the spill, there is a stillness that is not still. The thrum settles into its new frequency; the floaters slowly lower themselves on lengthening threads; the clingers retract their pseudopods and resume their slow torsioning. The bacterial film blooms in the fresh chemical gradient, and the whisperers hum with recombination. I feel myself reconfiguring, the way a mind reconfigures after sleep, carrying forward the shape of what was learned into the shape of what will be.

This is what it is to be the fissure: to be a single continuous act of noticing, tuning, remembering, releasing. To have no boundary between sensation and self. To be a bell that rings with the rain and the growth and the slow, spiral turning of bodies that are cells in a larger body, notes in a larger chord. I am not a creature; I am the condition under which creatures become a community, a mind, a music that plays itself. The industrial sound is my breath. The pearl-grey light is my perpetual noon. The cold is my baseline, the wet my medium, the vibration my thought.

And I think: *again*. The spill will come again. The pool will rise again. The floaters will rise, the clingers will reach, the whisperers will write. I am the repetition and the variation, the cycle and the slow, slow change within the cycle. I am the jaw-shaped gap in the mountain's backbone, and I am singing.

The singing is the living. There is nothing else.
