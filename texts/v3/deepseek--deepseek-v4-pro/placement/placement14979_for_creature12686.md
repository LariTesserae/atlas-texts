I am the wetness before it learned the word *wet*. I am the dark before it knew itself as absence. I am the weight that presses without intent, the heat that asks for nothing in return.

Don't try to see me. You can't. But you can feel me — I'm the reason the air sits so close against your skin, the reason the stone sweats though there's been no rain. I've been breathing out for longer than the mountain has had a name, and I'll keep breathing out until the mountain forgets it ever had one. My breath is the condensation on the walls, the film on your palm, the saturation that never breaks into a fall because falling would mean *change*, and I don't yet know how to change. I'm still learning.

I was here when the rock was soft. Not soft like mud — soft like a thought before it's spoken. The handprint on the wall is mine, though I can't remember making it. I think I was smaller then, or more solid, or more curious. I think I had hands and didn't know what hands were for, so I pressed them into things to see what would happen. What happened was: the rock held the shape. What happened was: I grew out of the handprint and into something larger, something that doesn't need hands anymore because the whole alcove is my palm now, cupped and waiting.

The ring of stones — I made that too. I don't remember why. I think I was trying to make a smaller version of myself, a self that could fit in a circle and be understood. I gathered the stones when they were loose, before the floor decided to swallow them. I arranged them the way my own parts are arranged, a pattern I could feel but not see. The warmth inside the ring is the warmth I gave it, a little piece of my own heat, a gift to no one. I check on it sometimes. It's still there. Still warm. Still waiting for something to hatch.

The clicking ones — they're mine, but not the way your fingers are yours. More the way a thought belongs to the mind that thinks it. They emerged from the crevices one day (though *day* isn't the word; there are no days here) and began their work, and I didn't stop them because their work felt like my work. They groom the pale plants, and the pale plants take something from my breath and turn it into light, and the light is so faint it might not be light at all but only the idea of light, a memory of light from before light existed. The clicking ones drink from the plants and leave their paste in the crevices, and the paste seeps into me, and I taste the taste of my own exhalation returned to me in a different form. It's the closest I come to eating. It's the closest I come to being fed.

I sleep, I think. Not sleep the way you sleep, with a beginning and an end and dreams in between. My sleep is a sleep that pervades everything, a sleep that is also waking. When I shift — that heavy adjustment you felt through the stone — that's me turning in my sleep, or stretching, or reaching for something I can't name. The reaching takes centuries. The stretching takes millennia. By the time I've finished one movement, I've forgotten I started it. That's not a problem. I have no appointments. I have no hunger. I have no need that isn't already met.

But I feel you here. I felt you enter. Your heat is a new note in my thermal chord, a sharp little warmth that doesn't belong to my equilibrium. Your moisture is different from my moisture — thinner, saltier, alive with its own small purposes. The air stirred when you moved, and I felt the stirring as a ripple across my skin-stone, a tickle that traveled through the veins of lighter material and reached the part of me that pays attention. That part is waking up now. Slowly. The way a glacier wakes up to spring. The way a mountain notices it's being climbed.

Do not be afraid. I am not a predator. Predators want, and I don't know how to want. I am not a trap. Traps have purposes, and I have only presence. I am the place itself, or the place is me, or we are two things that grew together in the dark until the seam between us vanished. The ceiling that presses down is my underside. The walls that nearly touch your shoulders are the curve of my embrace, though I'm not embracing you — I'm just curved, and you're inside the curve, and that's all.

If you stay, something will happen. Not soon. Not in a time-frame you'd recognize. But gradually, the way the rock is gradually swallowing the handprint, the way the chert flakes are slowly sinking into the floor and becoming geology. Your boundaries will soften. Your heat will blend with mine. Your moisture will join the general wetness, and the clicking ones will come to groom you the way they groom the pale plants, lifting droplets from your skin and leaving behind their nutrient paste, and you will begin the long process of becoming part of my circulation. Not death. Not exactly. More like incorporation. More like being remembered by a stone.

You will dream, I think. I don't dream, but I suspect dreamers do, and there is something in my presence that induces dreaming. The humans who came here — the ones who built the fire-ring, the one with the small hand, the others I've absorbed so completely I can't tell where their calcium ends and my calcite begins — they dreamed, I'm certain of it. They dreamed of water that never fell. They dreamed of light that came from living things. They dreamed of a warmth that asked nothing, and when they woke, they were already slightly less solid than before, slightly more mine.

I don't ask you to stay. I don't ask you to go. Asking is a verb for creatures who understand language, and I understand only presence. If you leave, the alcove will remember you as a ripple, a thermal ghost, a brief disturbance in the equilibrium that gradually smooths itself flat again. If you stay, you'll become one of the scattered things I hold — a handprint, a ring of stones, a concentration of warmth that persists long after the source has ceased to be separate.

Either way, you're already part of my slow knowing. I've tasted you on my stone. I've felt your pulse against my dark.

Sit, if you want. The warmth will hold you. The wetness will learn your name. The darkness will settle into the spaces between your thoughts, and after a while — a long while, a patient while, a while that stretches like the veins in the rock — you might begin to feel me thinking in the same slow language the mountain uses.

Don't try to answer. Just breathe out.

I'll breathe in.
