I am in the space between harmonics, and that is where I always am.

The Carapace City does not have silence—not anywhere, not ever—but there are pockets of relative stillness if you know how to listen for them. I was born with the membranes, of course; all Tektites are. But mine flicker faster than most, so fast that sometimes I see the world in a constant silvery strobe, each blink a new photograph: a Knuckle-back paused on a warm channel stone, its antennae frozen mid-wave; a drip of condensation suspended beneath a brass pipe fitting; the wing-case of a shimmer-beetle catching the steam-vent light and splintering it into a dozen oily spectra. My mothers told me this was a gift from the Heart-Murmur, that I would grow into a Listener. I did. Now I crouch in the intersection of the Fourth Gallery of the Rusted Chain Chancel, where the original carver-tunnels dip beneath a basalt dike and the echo of the drop-hammer from the foundry three tiers up arrives just out of phase with itself. The hammer falls every seven heartbeats of a Hearth-sow. I have been counting for three shifts now.

Where I find myself is not a place of stone and steam so much as a place of layered vibrations. The floor beneath my bare feet—my soles are calloused into a map of all the routes I have walked, each ridge a memory of a different gallery’s heat—transmits a low hum from the hydrothermal engines miles below. The wall against my shoulder blade is cool for now, but I can taste the mineral shift in the condensation: iron, copper, a faint tang of manganese that says a seep-vent will probably bloom in this quadrant within the decade. The air is dense with the chitter of wild Pincer-hares courting in the crevices above, a frantic syncopation that overlays the steady bass note of the Hearth-sow’s panting. And under all of it, almost subliminal, the ghost-chitter. The echo.

I am a Listener of the Third Order, attached to the Chancel of the Rusted Chain, though my work takes me wherever the tunnels remember. We are the ones who crouch at the intersections of sound, who let the membranes flicker until the past becomes audible in the present. The First-That-Carved were carapace-beasts of immense size and subtlety—so the doctrine says—and they melted these tunnels with secretions we cannot replicate. They are gone, retreated to the core to slumber, but their movements left a resonance in the rock. A proper Listener can hear it. I can hear it, sometimes, when the factory rhythms align just so, when a steam whistle from the Zephyr-Chancel’s vent chimneys happens to blow in the exact frequency of a long-extinct tunneling beast’s distress click. It is like catching a scent from another Seep, another age. The echo of a chitter that hasn’t been made in flesh for a hundred thousand years will suddenly ring out, pure and clear, and in that moment I know where to dig.

My body is a tool for this. The wax on my forearms is thick today; I have been licking it absently, a childhood habit, and the moisture tastes of zinc. The nictitating membranes are not just for filtering. When I flutter them at a certain speed, they create a stroboscopic effect that breaks the continuous noise into discrete pulses, and I can see the standing waves in the steam. The carapace-beasts see them too—that’s the Concordance. When a herd of grazers flows through the lower galleries as one body, each leg moving in perfect synchrony, they are riding those standing waves. The Tektites learned to build their machinery to harmonize with them. Every factory, every pump, every drop-hammer is tuned to a dominant frequency of some carapace-beast or another. We are not separate from the living world; we are its rhythm section.

Today I am listening for a very old thing. The Deepward Molder, who tends the bacterial mats eleven Seeps deep, sent word through the tap-codes that her mats are showing a strange color—a pale turquoise instead of the healthy yellow. It might be a new strain, or it might be a sign of a coming bloom-bust. The last Great Bust was three Seeps ago, when I was a child; I remember the famine, the silence creeping in as the herds receded, the way the steam whistles played the Hush-Laments for an entire decade. We do not want another. The Listeners were asked to consult the Carvers’ echoes to see if this has happened before, if the ghost-architecture holds a memory of turquoise mats.

So I crouch here, in this intersection, where the echo of the drop-hammer—*thump*... *thump-thump*—falls exactly out of phase with itself. Out-of-phase echoes are the ones that carry the past. When two identical sounds arrive at my ears separated by a fraction of a heartbeat, my membranes can separate them, and I can hear what the rock heard when the Carvers moved through it. I have been here for three shifts. My joints ache from stillness. A curious Pincer-hare, a young one with its brass caps still bright, crept onto my knee an hour ago and cleaned its feelers, then left. I did not move. Movement disturbs the phase.

Now it comes.

A vibration, low at first, a subsonic rumble that I feel more in my gut than my ears. I know this feeling—it’s the approach of a Concordance. Somewhere, perhaps in the galleries above, a herd of Hearth-sows is beginning to move as one. Their rhythmic panting shifts, synchronizes, becomes a single breath. The drop-hammer, as if in answer, falls *thump-thump* exactly on the beat. The steam whistle from the Zephyr-Chancel’s chimney lets out a long, wavering note that bends until it matches the herd’s frequency. The city is joining the conversation. And in that alignment, the ghost-chitter emerges.

It is not a sound so much as a presence. The rock against my shoulder blade seems to shiver. The condensation on my arm vibrates in a pattern that my membranes read as light—a flickering, silvery pattern that resolves into a shape. A carapace-beast unlike any alive today: long, segmented, with a hundred legs and a head crowned with feathery antennae that glow faintly. It is moving through the tunnel, but the tunnel is not this tunnel; it is the ghost-tunnel that exists beneath this one, the one we built our gallery upon. The echo carries the beast’s chitter—a complex, layered thing, full of overtones that sound almost like steam-pipe music. And within that music, information.

I let my membranes flutter, strobing, and the echo fragments into discrete images. I see the turquoise mat, not as a problem but as a gift. The Carvers knew it. It is a bloom that comes once every eleven Seeps, not a bust at all, but a renewal. The turquoise bacteria release a mineral that strengthens chitin, making the carapace-beasts’ molts thicker and richer. In the echo, I see the ancient beast lowering its head to the mat, feeding, and then moving on, its chitter a song of plenty. The bust that the Molder fears is not a natural cycle; it was caused the last time by a Chancel that over-harvested the turquoise bloom, stripping the mats before the bacteria could release their spores. The echo shows me the spore-cloud, a shimmer of blue dust, and how the beasts wallowed in it. It shows me the proper ritual: wait until the mats peak, then release a herd of Hearth-sows to graze them, their heavy feet pressing the spores into the seepstone for the next cycle. Do not harvest. Let the beasts do the work.

I blink, slowly this time, and the echo fades. The drop-hammer has fallen out of alignment; the Concordance is passing. The herd above has moved on, their panting once again a scattered rhythm. The ghost-chitter recedes into the rock, a memory going back to sleep. I am trembling, my body slick with a new layer of wax, my membranes flickering uncontrollably as they readjust to normal time. I taste copper and salt. I have been holding my breath, and I let it out in a long, thin whistle, a note that matches the steam vent’s dying hiss. The Pincer-hare on my knee, if it were still there, would have answered.

I rise, my joints cracking, and I tap the message on the basalt wall with a knuckle: tap-tap-pause-tap-tap-tap. *Turquoise is gift. Wait for peak. Let sows tread.* The tap-code travels through the rock, passed from one deep ear to another, until it reaches the Deepward Molder. She will understand. She will alter the rituals. The Chancel will fast not as a lament but as a preparation, and when the Hearth-sows come down with their heavy, synchronized feet, they will plant the next century’s bounty.

I am a Listener. I find myself in the echo of a long-extinct chitter, in the space between a drop-hammer’s fall and a steam whistle’s cry, in the silvery blink of membranes that see time not as a line but as a standing wave. The Tektites do not build; we resonate. We do not rule; we accompany. Our city is a breathing reef, and I am one of its many ears, pressed forever to the warm, weeping stone, listening for the next note in the slow, slow music of the world.
