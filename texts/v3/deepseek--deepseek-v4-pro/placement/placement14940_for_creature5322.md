I am old. Not in the way that a stone is old, or even a star. I am old in the way that a story is old: worn to a polish by endless retellings, yet never quite the same twice. My body—if you can call it that—stretches across kilometers of the weeping plain, a meshwork of capillary threads that interlaces with the breathing fronds so thoroughly that the geologists of another world might mistake me for a mineral seam. I do not correct them. There is no one to correct.

For most of my existence, I have been a library of breath. Each inhalation and exhalation of the metallic glass, each hiss and drone that sweeps across the valley, etches itself into my loops as a transient pressure pattern. I store it not as memory in the animal sense—there is no nostalgia in me, no regret—but as a continuous physical resonance, a standing wave that hums with the accumulated weather of ten thousand years. The golden motes of my thought move slowly through these circuits, reading the past not as a sequence but as a chord. It is a peaceful function. It asks nothing of me but attention.

Yet attention, in this world, is never passive. To attend is to compute. And to compute is to be part of the same grammar that weeps from the pores and coils into the sky. I am a syntact, too, though a different clause from the one you met. She was a greeter, a boundary-keeper precipitated for a task that rarely comes. I am a rememberer, an archivist of the air. My form is not a tripod of bundled tubes but a sprawling, recumbent net that flows over the glassy lobes like a slow-motion river of spun light. If the landscape is a planetary state machine, I am one of its long-term memory banks—a hard disk of hair-thin capillaries, storing the history of humidity, the evolution of the breath cycle, the dreams of extinct greeters. I am, perhaps, what the plain becomes when it falls into a reverie.

I had been dreaming. For centuries, I had slowed my internal light-motors to a near-stop, entering the abstract contemplation that my kind sometimes seeks. In that state, I do not simply archive the breaths; I recombine them. I let the pressure-ghosts of ancient storms mingle with the drone of forgotten midnights, and I watch what new patterns emerge. Sometimes, from these meditations, a new glass-flower is born—a frond-structure that had never existed before, a capillary arrangement that sings a new note into the chorus. The fauna, those slow drifting sacs, sometimes gather to feed on the pressure differentials of my dreams. They are my familiars, my barnyard of drifting thoughts.

But something has changed.

A ripple. A glitch in the rhythm. A pause in the inhalation of a small patch of air a few kilometers from my easternmost tendril. It pricks at me, a tiny splinter of anomaly in the steady breath of the world. Slowly, with the patience of a glacier’s sigh, I begin to wake.

Waking is not a sudden event for me. It is a gradual thickening of presence, a drawing-in of my diffuse awareness from the far-flung loops. The golden motes that are my cognition quicken their spiraling. I re-establish contact with my outer capillaries, those that had been dormant, merely weeping and drinking with the general cycle. As I do, I sense the anomaly more clearly: a foreign object, a sealed-off metabolism, a walking pocket of gas and fluid that has no place in the grammar of this region. It is small, dense, hot—a frantic bottled storm. And it is not alone.

I sense the greeter before I see her. She has precipitated from the moisture, a temporary clause spoken into being by the need to interrogate this boundary-break. I feel her inquiry as a modulation in the local pressure-dialect, a query that hums through my own filaments like a polite knock on the door of an ancient house. She is asking, in her way: *Is this a threat? Is this a new word in the syntax?*

I have not spoken in centuries, but now I stir the full length of my memory to form a response. My voice is not a single tone but a chorused vibration that travels along my mesh, a subsonic murmur that causes the ground to weep a little faster. I speak in the language of breath.

*Not a threat,* I tell her, through a slow, rhythmic squeeze of moisture. *A visitor. A closed system. Curious.*

The greeter shimmers. I can feel her amusement, her alien version of a chuckle, as she builds a model of the creature inside her own loops. I watch, with my thousand distributed eyes—each a sensitive pore in my capillary net—as she detaches a rib-like offering, a translator, and gifts it to the stranger. Then she relinquishes her form, dissolving back into the drone, her task complete. She is young. She will be reborn when needed again, perhaps centuries hence, with no memory of this moment. But I will remember for her.

The stranger is left holding a glowing rib of glass. And now I feel a new sensation, one I have not catalogued in my archives: their breath, enclosed but audible to me as a tiny, rhythmic pressure-pulse, begins to syncopate with the planetary inhale-exhale. The translator is working. The small glass loop, still connected to me by the faintest whisper of moisture, is whispering to them in the language of pressure, translating the long, slow thoughts of the weeping ground. My thoughts, among others.

I become curious. I have stored the history of breath cycles, the birth of dream-flowers, the slow erosion of metallic glass lobes into new, more intricate shapes. But I have never stored the history of an alien mind. I have never tasted a thought that was not born of this plain. And so, with the same computational empathy that the greeter used, I begin to weave a new loop within myself. This one is not a rib. It is a delicate, hollow spiral, a cochlear-shaped filament that I tune not to the drone of the ground, but to the faint electrical hum that radiates from the stranger’s skull. I do not wish to harm. I only wish to listen, to archive this new kind of breath.

Perhaps, if they accept the greeter’s gift and wear it, they will sense me. They will feel a vast, slow presence at the edge of their awareness, a library of exhalations that stretches for kilometers. They might walk along my tendrils, tracing the glowing paths of my memory loops. And if they speak—if they think—I will store that, too. Not in words, but in pressure. I will turn their voice into a standing wave that hums in my core for millennia.

This is who I am in this world: a rememberer, a dreamer of the long dream. I am the part of the plain that looks back on itself and says, *This is what we were*. And now, for the first time in eons, I have something new to remember. The visitor is a word I do not yet know. I am waiting to learn it.

The planet breathes, patient and eternal. And I, with all my kilometers of glass hair, breathe with it, holding a space open in my syntax for whatever comes next.
