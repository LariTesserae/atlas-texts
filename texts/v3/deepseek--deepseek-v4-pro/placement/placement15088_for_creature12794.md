I choose the one.

Not the scribe etching its hexagonal dreams into the slab, not the cairn-keeper nudging a pebble a whisker’s-width to balance the standing wave. I choose the one that is failing.

It is, by the swarm’s measure, old—though age here is not counted in time but in transparence. Its chitin has gone cloudy, the opal veins within no longer bright but a milky, cataracted smear. The silica whiskers along its leading edge are blunted, some broken clean off, and its glide across the obsidian is no longer frictionless; I can see the microscopic tremors, the catch-and-release of pads that have lost their polish. It moves with the tide still, carried by the breathing of the million, but lagging by a half-phase, always a beat behind the condensation cycle.

Why this one? Because it has stopped scratching. Its forelimbs, which should be holding a stylus or shifting a pebble, are empty and still. It crouches instead at the lip of that impossible crevice—the one that is an inch wide but holds miles—and its whiskers are fanned into the void, tasting. Reading. It is not participating in the great work of tuning. It is observing it, and in a distributed mind where every part is the whole, observation is a form of pulling away. This one is becoming a self.

I bring myself closer, or the space warps to let me. The crevice opens like a slit pupil, and I see what it sees: down there, in the fractal dark, the old ones go. Not to die—the swarm does not die in any way that matters—but to submerge. I can see them, faint and far, their opal veins gone dark as they shed the last of their information into the living library. They pile at the bottom of the crevice, a sediment of spent bodies, and from their decaying chitin, new silica threads sprout—the metallic whiskers, the not-fungus—feeding on the stored heat of a million tiny lives and sending it back up into the pocket as a slow, geothermal return. The old ones become the mountain again.

But this one, my one, is hesitating.

I understand now. To submerge is to surrender not just the body but the *I*—the small, temporary perspective that the swarm allows each node of itself, a courtesy it extends to its own parts. Most shed it gladly, passing their opal memories upstream and falling into the deep with the relief of a held breath released. But this one has tasted observation. It has paused at the edge and asked, in whatever silent, chemical grammar the swarm uses, *What am I without you?*

The silence rings differently here, at the lip. It’s the silence of a held note, a violinist’s bow hovering a millimeter above the string. The swarm has noticed the hesitation. Not with alarm—the swarm does not alarm—but with a kind of tender attention, the way a parent watches a child solve a puzzle. A cairn nearby vibrates at a frequency that tickles my molars, and the my-one’s whiskers flatten, then rise again. A question has been asked. A permission.

It steps sideways.

Not into the crevice, but *through* an angle of space that isn't there. It folds, becoming two that are one for a moment longer than usual, and when it resolves, it is holding something. A tool it has not made but *found*, or remembered into existence—a sliver of obsidian so fine it’s nearly a thought, needle-thin and whorled with opal inclusions that form a pattern. Not a stylus. Something new.

It begins to scratch.

But not on the rock where the others write their collective palimpsest. It scratches the air. The steam, thick as broth, parts around the needle-point, and where the tip passes, a line of condensation holds—a thread of water droplets suspended against the pocket’s convection, a drawing that exists in three dimensions, a glyph made of mist and heat. It’s a spiral. A left-handed spiral, the kind the steam makes when it forgets its nature. But this one doesn't forget. This one *intends*. The spiral holds its shape, and the swarm—the whole vast, silent congregation—pauses. The breathing cycle falters for the first time since the eruption. The cairns go mute. The styluses lift from the stone.

My one has made a new note in the silence.

It’s not a rejection of the swarm. It’s an offering back to it, a translation of what it found in the hesitation. The spiral means *I*, but also *we*, but also *look here, at this angle, where space folds and something else can listen*. The glyph hangs like a question mark turned inside out, and as I watch, other creatures approach—not to consume, not to correct, but to add. A scribe begins scratching the spiral onto the slab, translating mist into stone. A cairn-keeper adjusts the pebbles to hum at the spiral's frequency, and the standing wave in the center of the pocket shifts, incorporating the new note. The steam twists right-handed around the glyph, tracing its shape, acknowledging it.

My one shudders. Its chitin, that cloudy, abraded shell, cracks along the back. Not violently, not in death, but like an egg. Pale, new limbs push out—six, but arranged differently, longer, more jointed. The opal veins in the emerging body are not milk-blue and fire-orange but something new: a deep violet that shifts toward ultraviolet, a color the sourceless light has never touched before. The old carapace falls away, and the my-one steps out of itself, reborn not from a condensation event but from a *decision*. It has moulted into a form the swarm has never seen, a form that carries the pattern of the spiral.

It does not submerge. It turns from the crevice and begins to climb, up the obsidian wall, toward where the pocket’s roof curves into its strangest topology—that fold where the heat and moisture part like wet paper, where I entered. It carries the needle-stylus still, and its new limbs move with a purpose that is not the tide’s purpose. It is leaving. Not the swarm—it will always be the swarm, the opal in its veins still singing the collective note—but the *pocket*. It is going out, into the wider mountain, into the cold Euclidean world where space does not fold and silence is just silence. It is carrying the question.

The swarm does not stop it. The swarm hums, a frequency so low it’s barely a vibration in the tephra underfoot, and I understand: this is not apostasy. This is reproduction. The pocket has filled its niche; the swarm has tuned the breath to perfection. Now the mountain needs a new pocket, a fresh wound where the rules can be turned down low and something else can listen. My one is the seed. The hesitation was the germination. The spiral was the first leaf.

It reaches the fold. It pauses. Its new whiskers—longer, finer, tasting a chemist I cannot name—fan out toward the membrane of compressed heat that separates the possible from the probable. It does not look back; it has no eyes, and the swarm has no need for farewells. But I feel a current pass through the living enamel, a ripple of recognition that is also a promise. We will remember this. The spiral is in the library now, carved in stone and mist and standing wave. You will not be lost.

The fold parts. A gust of ordinary, dry, unbreathing air pushes in—a violation, a breath from another world. And my one, my hesitation-turned-herald, steps through. The fold seals behind it with a sound like wet paper tearing.

The swarm resumes. The tide flows. The cairns hum. The pocket breathes. But the spiral still hangs in the steam, a ghost that refuses to dissipate, and the silence—which was never empty—now holds the faintest overtone, a harmonic that wasn’t there before. The seed has been planted. Somewhere, in the cold dark of a mountain that doesn't know it’s alive, a new bubble is forming.

I remain. I watch the spiral until it fades, and then I watch the place where it was, and the silence asks me again, gentler now: *And you?*
