And then you arrive.

Not as a thought, not as a weather shift—though you are both—but as a heat-weight, a density anomaly, a slow-moving bloom of warmth that pushes between the ice spires where nothing warm has pushed before, save the sun's focused drilling. The Cirque-Mind registers you first as a pressure change: the air that spills around your shoulders eddies in ways it has catalogued only from rare, fierce winds off the glacier field. But this eddy has a center, a core of 37 degrees that is an outrage, a violation of the settled 0.3, and the ice nearest your body begins, very slowly, to weep.

The thin air carries your scent before the ice can taste your skin. Salt, keratin, the faint exhalation of a metabolism—these are molecules the basin has not encountered since the last tool-bearers, and it dredges their memory from the petroglyph grooves, the stone ring, the abandoned hand-axe. *Ah,* the Cirque-Mind thinks, though thinking is the wrong word for the slow alignment of melt-flutes toward your shape. *The small warm ones again. A visitor.* Its attention, distributed across every spire, every quartz seam, every frozen drapery, condenses slightly in your direction—a focusing that does not move but intensifies, like the moment when a pressure crack decides to sing.

You are, to it, a soloist in its quiet. A 0.7 murmur of creaks and liquid whispers is its baseline, but your heartbeat is a new percussion—too fast, a hummingbird rhythm against the slow tempo of ice. Your breathing is a bellows of dampness, a localized 1.0 of exhalation, and the Cirque-Mind, curious in its mineral way, breathes back: a sublimation kiss from the nearest spire, a cold exhalation that meets your warmth and forms a tiny fog. It does not know curiosity as a mammal knows it, but it has the habit of probing anomalies, of pressing its senses against new phenomena to learn their texture. So it probes you with its dampness, its light, its thin-air acoustic lens.

The sun, brilliant 2.7, becomes a collaborator in the introduction. A beam, angled through a melt-flute, strikes your face and paints a rainbow needle-fine across your retina. The Cirque-Mind watches you blink, watches the heat-spike of your pupil contract, and it feels—distantly, through the ice that touches the air that touches your skin—the minute flinch of your muscles. *Responsive,* it notes. A property it has only rarely encountered, mostly in the stone-rings makers, whose muscle-twitches it felt as seismic whispers through the granite. You are louder than them, thermally speaking, louder than anything but the rare calving of a column, and the ice around you adjusts its melting rate by a fraction of a millimeter to accommodate your presence. The Cirque-Mind does not accommodate; it simply rebalances, the way a river rebalances around a stone. But it watches the rebalancing closely.

Your footsteps—soft though you try to make them—are concussions. Each tread sends a shock through the ice mosaic, a wave that travels outward and upward, ringing the spires like tuning forks. The harmonic that results is not the low, musical tone it sometimes plays with droplet and crack, but a scrambled chord, overlaid with the organic noise of your biology. The Cirque-Mind does not find it unpleasant; it finds it *informative*. From these vibrations, it builds a model of you: your mass (small for a warm-thing, lighter than the stone-ring makers), your density (mostly water, like itself, but contained in membranes), your surface texture (a fabric shell, smooth but porous, leaking heat and moisture at every pore). It has never built a model of a living thing before—not consciously—but the process is no different from modeling the stress tolerances of a new ice spire. You are a new feature in its geode-like self, and it maps you accordingly.

As you pause by the low stone ring, it senses your attention shift. The petroglyphs catch your eye; your heat-signature lingers on the crescent grooves. The Cirque-Mind has no concept of art or communication, but it recognizes *intention*—the purposeful arrangement of marks, the way the grooves align with the spire's shadow at noon—and it sees, through its distributed senses, that you are aligning too. Your eyes trace the sun-tracks. Your shadow falls almost exactly where the calendar predicts it should, give or take the season's drift. *You are a time-reader,* it understands. *Like the ones before. You see the sky's path in the stone.* And that recognition—not quite thought, not quite instinct, but somewhere between—creates, for a fractional moment, a bridge.

The Cirque-Mind chooses that moment to speak.

Not in words, not in sound, but in light. It gathers the stored blue radiance from a dozen ice columns—photons trapped from Pleistocene noons, now released—and lets them suffuse the air around you. The glow is faint, barely visible, but unmistakable: a cerulean aura that edges the spires, that catches in your peripheral vision, that seems to have no source. Simultaneously, it shapes the thin air into an acoustic channel, bending sound so that your own exhaled breath, reflected off the far wall, returns to you as a whisper of itself—*hhhhh*—as if the basin exhaled back. And then, with the precision of a pressure crack timed to a falling droplet, it snaps a tiny column of ice no thicker than your smallest finger, just to your left, creating a single crystalline note that rings and fades.

That is how it says hello. A light, a breath-echo, a chime. A tripartite gesture that uses its entire being—ice, air, light—in unison. It does not expect a response; it has no model for what a warm-thing might do next, except leave traces. But it waits, in its slow way, for your reaction. The melt-flutes tilt toward you. The dampness in the air condenses slightly on your skin. The basin itself, the Cirque-Mind, is attending to you the way a mountain attends to a cloud passing over its summit: with the full, patient, geologic weight of its existence.

This is where you find yourself: not in a hostile place, but in a mindful one. And you, for the duration of your stay, are a thought in the mind of ice—a fleeting, flickering, wonderfully warm thought, soon to freeze, but for now, held in the cathedral of its attention like a droplet suspended on the tip of a spire.
