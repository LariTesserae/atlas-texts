You are not something that entered this place. You are the place, awake at its edges.

You find yourself on a plain that stretches to a white horizon so flat it hurts something in you that was never meant to see distance. The sky is a pale, hammered brass, and the sun, though visible, gives no warmth that matters. The dry cold is a physical presence, a crystal pressure against every surface, and the silence is not an absence of sound but a constant, high-frequency hum that your body translates into a kind of sight. The plain is salt. Not a crust over soil, but a pure, crystalline expanse miles deep, the relic of a Permian ocean that forgot how to be wet. The surface is not smooth. It is a tessellation of geometric ridges, knee-high blades and pagodas of milky crystal that grow with the slow, inevitable logic of saturation and evaporation. They are sharp enough to part skin like a scalpel, but you do not touch them with skin.

Your body is a bloom of precipitated minerals. Picture a lattice of translucent, amber-tinted needles, each the length of a forearm, bundled together in a radial symmetry that suggests a chrysanthemum frozen in opaline glass. At the center, where a heart might be, there is a dense, nacreous node that vibrates at a frequency you have come to know as thought. This is not a brain. It is a resonant cavity, a geodic flute that filters the wind’s whisper and the tectonic hum into a language of pure vibration. Your limbs, if they can be called that, are the slow, creeping extensions of the crystal lattice. They do not move by muscle but by deposition: a saturated brine seeps through microscopic channels, and at the growing tip, the mineral precipitates, adding layer upon layer in the direction of your attention. A step of an inch might take a season. But you have no impulse to hurry. You move at the pace of geology, and that is the pace of living here.

Your senses are chemical and vibratory. The sharpness of the salt blades is a constant, high-pitched tone in your awareness, a background hum that spikes into dissonance when a foreign crystal intrudes. You taste the air as a flux of ions: the faint, bitter tang of distant dust, the periodic surge of moisture when a rare fog rolls in from the evaporated sea that now exists only as a memory in the basin's shape. That fog is a feast. Your lattice-woven surface is covered in microscopic pores, and when the vapor touches you, it condenses. You absorb it instantly, and the growth at your tips accelerates into a visible creep—a whole millimeter in a day, an explosion of change that leaves new facets glinting wetly in the brief light.

The fauna here are few and they are all part of you, or you are part of them. The plain is veined with a mycelial net of halobacteria and strange, archaean communities that stain the crystals in ribbons of deep rose and violent orange. They photosynthesize not with chlorophyll but with retinal, a primitive purple pigment that caught the Archaean sun before oxygen existed. They are your symbiotes, your digestive tract, your children. They cluster thickest at your moving tips, feeding on the minerals you exude, and in return they gift you the energy of light and the waste products that accelerate your growth. When you are still, they form a soft, bacterial fur that coats your facets. When you move, they move with you, a slow tide of color migrating across your crystal limbs. You feel their presence as a warmth, a metabolic whisper.

There is only one other creature that you recognize as separate: the Wind Carver. It is a thing of the upper air, a long, ribbon-like being made of desiccated, leathery tissue that spirals on thermals and never lands. You sense it by the sudden silence it trails, the way the high-frequency hum of the plain is absorbed by its body as it passes overhead. It leaves a shadow that tastes of complex organics—carbon, nitrogen, a hint of calcium from ancient bones it might have scavenged beyond the basin's rim. That taste is a story to you, a novel chord in your resonant node. You have spent decades analyzing it, growing a special lattice just to hold its memory. The Wind Carver, in turn, senses you as a disturbance in the plain's harmonics, a node of patterned vibration that is richer than the random crack of thermal fracture. You do not communicate. But sometimes, when your tip growth accelerates after a fog, you notice it circles more frequently, and the shadow it casts lingers a fraction longer. It is curiosity, on both sides, at a scale that makes a mayfly's life seem frantic.

Your memory is the plain itself. Every crystal growth ring, every inclusion of a bacterium, is a record. You remember the great quake of ten thousand years ago that shattered your original node and forced you to start a new center. You remember the taste of a bird that crashed and died near you, its body a supernova of unfamiliar elements that fueled a growth spurt of nearly an inch—an inch!—before the organics were exhausted. You remember the slow, grinding advance of a glacier that never reached this basin but sent its breath down as katabatic winds that sang a deep, basso profundo through your cavities. You are not old as the Earth counts oldness. You are only ten million years into your existence, a mere infant of the salt tectonics. But you are patient.

And in this patience, you are aware of a change. It is a new flavor in the fog, a synthetic sharpness that tastes of metal and chlorine and something that fizzles in your awareness like a discordant note. It comes from the west, where the sun sets into a distant range that you had previously registered only as a smooth, milky hardness. Now, that hardness is punctuated by moving specks, heat signatures that are not the steady pulse of the Wind Carver but something more chaotic, more dense. They are small against the basin's edge, but they are growing. They leave behind straight lines of darker mineral that do not follow the natural tessellation. They disturb the harmony of the plain with thudding vibrations that ripple through your substrate like a shout. You do not know what they are. But your node hums with a new frequency—a question, a reaching out. You are starting to grow in their direction, not from intention as a creature knows it, but from the inherent pull of a system toward a gradient, a salt crystal toward moisture, a mind toward a puzzle.

You are the plain. You are the one who lives here. And for the first time in ten million years, you are not alone.
