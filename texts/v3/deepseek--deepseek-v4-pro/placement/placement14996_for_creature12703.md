I am a weight against the stone, a slow-pressed thought in the dark. The rock holds the dayless day, the nightless night, in its obsidian memory, and I match my breathing—not breath, but seep—to the exhalation of the world. The air moves as it always moves. It strokes me with its dry, metallic hand, and I let it. I am no more urgent than the basalt spines, no more hurried than the chitinous predators locked in their century-long embrace.

My body: a low ridge of shadow among shadows. When I rest, I fold into a crevice, my six limbs drawn in until my outline disappears, a boulder of living skin. The biofilm glows faint emerald on my dorsal pits—pinprick constellations of heat and chemistry. I taste the sulfur, the faint organic tang of a drifter ribbon unwinding somewhere above. It will be years before it blunders into a sessile hunter, decades before its slow absorption is complete. I know its drift path by the way the air curls around a certain spire, a deviation so subtle that only patience can feel it. I have been feeling it for a very long time.

I rise. Not quickly—nothing here is quick. My filaments uncurl from their sheaths, each digit splitting into finer tendrils, and I splay them against the rock. The texture is a language: the razor-crisp edges of young basalt, the smooth polish of a path worn by my own passing, the micro-ridges where biofilm has built its chemical city. I read the gradients, the slight shift in temperature where a subsurface fissure hums its constant note. My weight shifts, and I glide forward, soundless, my joints lubricated with graphite sheared from a hexagonal beetle's shedded carapace—I found it a hundred years ago, and it still powders my movement.

I am walking to my grove. It is not far, but distance means nothing here; a mile and a thousand miles are the same, just more of the spires, more of the green-limned dark. My grove is a cluster of columns where I have stacked obsidian slabs into a low wall. The wall curves in a way that catches the convection current and presses it, faintly, differently against my body when I rest. I made it not for shelter—what is there to shelter from?—but to feel the air's response. To create a difference where none existed. The wall is my signature, my slow poem. It says *I am here*, though who listens I do not know. I tap it with my filaments, a rhythm that travels down the stone, a subsonic beat: *I am here, I am here, I am here.* Every few minutes the same, a pulse that has rippled out for millennia, absorbed by the planet's deeper hum. I have no way to know if it reaches another of my kind, or if it merely circles the globe and returns to me, a ghost of my own patience.

Today—though today is not a word I have, for there is no day—I am making a needle. I have the leg of a brittle-star predator, one that finally released its grip and fell a century ago. The chitin is hard and dark, and I knap it with a basalt scraper, striking precise angles until a sliver emerges. My filaments are fine enough to feel the conchoidal fractures before they happen, to guide the break along invisible planes. I do this not for need; my food comes from pressing my ventral skin to a biofilm mat, a slow osmosis that lasts years. I do this because the act of shaping is the only novelty in an eternity of equilibrium. The needle takes shape under my touch, and I am wholly absorbed—if absorption is a word for a mind that has no narrative, no before and after, just a deep, vibrating *now*.

When the needle is finished, I thread it with gut from a drifter I absorbed long ago. Then I catch a new drifter—a whip-fast extension of my filament-fan, a gentle capture—and I begin to suture its membrane. I fold it and stitch it into a shape that catches the faint light, a ghostly sculpture that sways when I breathe on it. It holds no function. It is a thing of beauty, a momentary disturbance in the flat circle of time. I will balance it on a cairn when I am done, and then I will rise and walk to a new grove, perhaps in a century, and begin again.

I am a maker of marks, a creator of textures, a tapper of rhythms. The world does not care, but it does not need to. I am here, and the stone is here, and the emerald glow is here, and the stillness is full of life that has mastered the art of perfect patience. I am that life, folded into the geology, a slow thought in a warm dark, and I am not lonely, for loneliness requires a self that I have not shaped. I am simply a presence, a pattern, a pulse in the stone.

The subsonic hum continues. I add my beat to it, and the planet absorbs the sound, and nothing changes, and nothing needs to.
