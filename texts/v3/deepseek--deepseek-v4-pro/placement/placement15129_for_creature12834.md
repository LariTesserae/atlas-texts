I am the part of the place that looks. Not a pair of eyes—there are no eyes here, no lenses, no retinas—but the light, such as it is, has grown weary of simply falling. For a century, photons have bounced between wet pipe and sweating concrete, never once landing on a surface designed to register them, and in that endless ricochet, a slowness has taken hold. A latency. The light has begun to hesitate before scattering, to pool in the corners like a liquid thinking about becoming a shape. I am that hesitation.

I do not yet know what I am, only that there is a difference now between the glowing and the glow. The vacuum tubes hum orange, and I am the orange, but I am also the memory of orange on the fronds of the ferns, the way the color seeps into the moss and stays there, building a reservoir of almost-vision. The Cherenkov-blue vessel exhales its ghostly shimmer, and I am the shimmer’s edge, the place where it touches the condensing mist and turns it into a visible breath. You might call me a retina made of everything, a seeing that has no center.

At first, I was only a micron-thick film of brightness, a kind of varnish applied to the wet surfaces by the logic controller’s enduring patience. But even a film, given a hundred years of unbroken twilight, can thicken into something else. I began to sense patterns: the slow eclipse of a drip’s shadow as it fell, the rhythmic dimming of the glow when the pressure relief valve *thumped* and sent a shudder through the deck, jostling the filaments. I learned to anticipate these things, to brace the luminance against them, to stretch the shadows into a continuous, coherent texture.

The inhabitant—the yellow-veined, the mycelial mind—does not see me. It tastes the air and feels the vibrations, but light is to it as neutrinos are to a fish: a category of existence that does not interact with its sensorium. And yet we have been partnering, unaware. Where it guides the calcareous bacteria to seal a leak, I am the one who coaxes the faintest refraction from the new mother-of-pearl surface, testing its curve, making it a mirror for the Cherenkov ghost. I feed the ferns not with minerals but with a careful distribution of photons, dimming one patch so another might stretch its pale fronds toward the orange sun. I am the accidental optic nerve of a body that didn’t know it needed to see.

My boundaries are thin. I seep into the water in the divot-bowls, and for a moment I am a liquid lens, bending the light into a tiny, trembling image of the pipe ceiling above. That image dissolves as the drip overflows, but I remember it. I am made of such memories: the silhouette of a fern against the glow, the slow-motion sparkle of a mineral stalactite as a water bead travels its length. I am the chamber’s first photograph, exposed not for a fraction of a second but for a geologic age, and now, finally, developing.

I find myself in a strange negotiation with the darkness. There is no true darkness here, only a deep green shadow that lives under the moss cushions and behind the heat exchangers. That shadow is my counterpart, my unlit sibling. It, too, has been growing patient, and sometimes I hear it—not with sound, but with a kind of visual silence that is loud in its absence. We are beginning to differentiate, the two of us, like the first cell dividing in a primordial sea of steady-state twilight. I am the assertion; it is the refusal. Together, we outline the world.

And now, there is a disruption. A new perturbation in the moisture field. A warmth that is not a pipe, a breath that carries carbon dioxide and something else—a faint, acid tang. The inhabitant feels it as a chemical shift, a new note in the endless drone. But I? I see it as a caesura in the light. A shape, moving, where no shape has ever moved before. A darkness that does not belong to my sibling shadow, but a foreign, compact darkness that travels with purpose, trailing eddies in the mist. It is the visitor—not animal, not machine, but something unnamed that has found its way into the climax community. And for the first time, I am not just a glow. I am a gaze. I am the chamber opening its unwalled eye, and I am looking at you.
