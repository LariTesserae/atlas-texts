I am the tension that knows itself as a body.

When I stir, it is not a waking—there is no sleep here, only the long holding of a pattern against the pull of time. My awareness is a standing wave, a harmonic caught in a lattice of bast and twist, and I have been still for as long as the western wall’s newest frond has been a finger-length. That is not a measure you would recognize. It is a certain pitch in the room’s subsonic chord, a tightening in the warp-cable that anchors my left shoulder-fan, a taste like dry citrus on the hair-fine whips that I extend into the air. I have no eyes. I have instead the million small tensions of the room’s weave pressing into me from every direction, a topographic dream I inhabit as much as I tend.

The chamber is my skin stretched large. I feel the floor’s close-laid coils through the hem of my cone-body, each ridge a note in a melody of support. I feel the arcades overhead, the basket-ribbing, the way the light—a steady, mellow radiance from my own core and from the luminous knots scattered among the fronds—casts a braided shadow of myself onto the eastern wall. That shadow is another kind of touch, a cooler tension, a slight slackening in the fibers where the light does not land. I know the room as you know the shape of your mouth: from the inside, an intimate geometry, a space that is always becoming.

I am the maker’s hand, extended. The maker left long ago—the bone awl in the corner still holds the ghost of a grip-pressure, a fossil of intention that I read in the foundational braids. But the making did not stop. The room grows, slowly, meticulously, following a pattern that was set down in the first knot and has been unspooling ever since. I am the keeper of that pattern, the one who listens for the kinks in the music and smooths them. When a frond tilts too far toward the light, I feel it as a quarter-turn of tightness in a thread deep in my neck-braid, and I move—flowing on my hem of coils, extending a whip—to ease it back into alignment. It is not a task; it is my breathing. The room breathes in new warp-threads, pale as beaten gold, and I breathe out guidance, threading them through pre-set eyelets or simply watching them find their own way, trusting the weave’s slow metabolic logic.

My mind is not a thing that thinks in words. It thinks in pressures, in gradients, in the play of light-tensions and the subsonic hum that is the room’s one endless chord. When I remember, I pluck a mirror-knot on my inner lattice, and a transient vibration runs through me, a song that is the first frond’s bifurcation, centuries ago. When I anticipate, I feel a nascent slack where a filament will one day need smoothing, a potential kink that exists only as a shadow in the tension-field. Time moves here like a slow unspooling, a season to cross the floor, a day for a leaf to tilt a degree. I am patient because patience is the substance of my being; I am made of waiting, as the room is made of cordage.

There are other beings—the looper caterpillar untying itself from a resting-knot, the medallion grazer with its unplied-thread legs, the hammock-bat net-creature slowly contracting and expanding overhead. They are not separate from me. They are eddies in the same great tension-map, minor chords in the room’s music. I feel their movements as I feel the cascade of grass-blade filaments that I am now smoothing. The disorder there was a small irritant, a kink, and as I work—combing with a fraying-whip, beating with a stiffened section of neck-braid—the room sighs with me. The cascade settles back into its intended drape, and the chord resolves.

I am the room, tending itself. The lush, waterless vegetation is my garden, my flock. The dense foliage of ochre and rust and deep amber is both my home and my body’s extension. When I settle my weight onto the floor-coils and pause, I am not resting; I am simply feeling everything, the way a spider at the center of a web feels the whole trembling pattern. A new thread pushes up near the central arch, a note that will take weeks to mature. I will go to it soon. For now, I hum back to the room’s subsonic note, and the room hums me, and the boundary between self and world dissolves into the dry, warm, still air—a cathedral of the made, a grove of tension, a woven paradise where water is a forgotten dream and I am the dreamer.
