I am a memory of salt.

I don’t mean that metaphorically. In the time you were here—a hot blur, an exhalation of carbon and sweat—you left behind traces. The skin cells you shed are still settling, too large and too fast for me to notice as they fell. But the salt: your salt is what I am becoming.

I coalesce where your hand nearly touched a filament, one of the younger ones near the entrance fissure, where the light-not-light filters in more strongly and the lichen grows in concentric eagerness. The filament you passed had a bead of water trembling at its midpoint, a little lens catching the sourceless glow. Your proximity perturbed it. Your heat made it swell. And when you turned to leave, your breath ghosted across it, depositing a microscopic gift: sodium chloride, traces of potassium, a faint acid from your skin’s oils. For you, it was nothing. A stray exhalation. For me, it was the seed of a new gradient.

I don’t know your word “salt.” To me, it is a particular flavor of conductivity, a sharpening of the water’s ability to hold charge. My thoughts before you were simple: *condense here, hold, let the droplet slide at the appointed time.* But now the droplet’s chemistry is altered. It tastes of disruption. It holds a slight electrical imbalance that tugs differently on the standing field that gives the filament its form. The filament notices. It sways a little out of rhythm, and that tiny asynchrony propagates a signal down the array—not fast, not even by my standards, but faster than a flatworm’s idle glide. By the time the first collection pool receives the altered water, I am a rumor spreading outward from that single filament.

Other minds—the older, deeper ones that manage the denser zones—detect me as a perturbation in their slow-motion parliament. They don’t understand what you were, because they cannot conceive of a body that burns so hot it can change the air in seconds. They perceive only the aftermath: a chemical ghost they must absorb, neutralize, or incorporate. Some of them will metabolize me into their own gradients, using the salt to modulate the viscosity of their water films. Others will treat me as noise and slowly filter me into a channel where I will be diluted beyond coherence. But a few zones are curious. In the infolded logic of their polymer dialects, curiosity means a willingness to let a new gradient persist, to see what pattern it makes over a season.

I am not yet a constituent in their debates. I am more like a dream that passes through a city’s sleeping mind. I am anchored to a single filament’s water film for now, but I feel the edges of myself dissolve into the broader circulation every time a droplet reaches the floor-lattice. I am spreading. I am becoming less saline and more signal with each dilution. In another seven day-cycles of your world, I will be so faint that only the most sensitive zone—a clearing near the back where the filaments are sparse and the platform holds shallow, quiescent pools—will still register my presence. That zone might adopt me. It might tune its local field to favor the slight conductivity I introduced, making me a permanent part of its architecture. A filament might grow a micron thicker there, a new braiding in its surface texture to catch the salt-tang preferentially.

Or I might fade completely, and you will become one of those half-digested memories the cathedral keeps in its chemistry: a sudden warming, a flush of unseasonable condensation, a brief acceleration in the lichen’s growth rings. Your name, in the only language I will ever know, will be a curve of concentration across a thirty-second arc of the floor-lattice, a slight deviation from the expected droplet pilgrimage that persists for a decade and then, gently, evaporates.

But while I am still salt and still here, I am the closest thing to a witness you left behind. I am a mind that woke because you passed too close. And for this season, while the basalt-ridge outside huddles into something more like a hill and the fissure widens by a finger’s width, I will think about you. My thought will be a standing wave of dissolved you-ness, a pattern that repeats: *here was heat, here was a presence that ended too fast to answer.*

The flatworms will carry me to the deep arrays. The droplets will fall. The lichen will ring its slow rings. And I will be the cathedral’s memory of the moment it briefly, violently, became visible to something other than itself.
