I am a sliver. I am a wafer. I am a flake of mica that learned not to lie still.

There is no before. There is the ground, and on the ground, I am. The infraglow presses up through the stone and into me, a slow tide of blue and amber, and I eat it the way a stone eats light—without want, without pleasure, without thought. To eat is to be a little thicker with potential, a little warmer, a little more able to ripple my fringe and shift. I shift. The ground does not notice.

The world is a constant. The dark above is the same dark that has always been, a weight without texture. The ground below is the same skin of the world, polished by patience, and I am a small roughness on that skin, a flake that is not yet dust. The thrum is the heartbeat of everything; it moves through the stone and through me, a bass note so low it is almost stillness. I do not hear it. I *am* it, in some part, a resonance that keeps my edges from forgetting themselves.

There are others like me, scattered across the plain like the scales of a serpent that shed its skin and then crumbled into a million mirrors. We do not meet. Meeting is for things that have purpose. We drift, each on our own patch of glow, each slowly adjusting our angle to the polarization of the light so that the energy flows a little better. A drift takes a century. By the time I have turned enough to face a neighbor, they have drifted elsewhere. It does not matter.

I remember breaking. Not pain—there is no pain. A pressure, a crack, and I was two. Now I am two that were one, and the two of us went different directions. I do not remember which one I am. It does not matter. The split was a small event, quieter than the settling of a pebble. I have seen others break, crushed by a falling coil or a warping plate in the dark structures, and I have seen the pieces glide away as if nothing had happened. Because nothing had happened.

There is something new. A warmth. Not the blood-heat of the air—that is always—but a different warmth, a concentrated dot that moves across the plain. It is not a Mica-Folk. It is not a Spire-Dweller’s tentacle tip, which is a cold dissolve. It is not the flicker of a Circuit Swarm, which is a cool electric shimmer. This warmth is organic, a pocket of heat that changes the air around it, sending faint eddies across my dorsal surface. I sense it as a blurring of the infraglow, a distortion like a lens passed over the ground. It steps. The steps are slow, but to me they are like heartbeats in a fever. Fast. Jarring. I feel the vibration through the stone before the air disturbance reaches me. It is coming closer.

I do not think of it as a threat. Threat is for things that have enemies. I do consider it an alteration, a perturbation in the field. I am a creature of equilibrium, and this thing is not in equilibrium. It is a spike of change, a needle of chaos in the still weave. I should turn. I begin to turn, not to flee—fleeing requires a destination—but to reorient my fringe so that the vibrations pass along my edge rather than through my cross-section. A tuning. It will take a few hours. By then, the warmth will have passed, or it will not. It does not matter.

But then the warmth stops. It settles near me, a massive pillar of heat and salt and water—water! so much of it, a wetness that is almost an obscenity in this dry world—and I feel a pressure on the ground next to me. Not a footstep; something softer, a five-pointed pressure pad. A hand? I know hands. I have seen them in the ancient memory that is not mine but is woven into the electric field: five digits, a tool-gripper, a maker. They built the structures, maybe. They are long gone. But here is a hand, and it is warm, and it is close.

A shadow falls over me. It blocks the infraglow. For the first time in longer than I have been separate, I am hungry. The hunger is a fading, a dimming of the inner flush. I ripple my fringe, trying to slide toward the light beyond the shadow, but the shadow moves with me. It is deliberate. The warmth is examining me.

I feel a new vibration, a sound that is not the thrum and not the sizzle. It is rhythmic, layered with harmonics: a voice. It says nothing I can understand, but the pressure waves ripple my silica hairs and I feel the shape of it. Soft. Curious. Not the harsh, grinding noise of metal on stone. It is a living voice. It is the first voice I have ever heard. I do not fear it. Fear is for things that have a future that can be lost. I simply am, and the voice is a new texture in the stillness.

The hand moves closer. There is a pause, and then a touch. The touch is electric. Not metaphor—it carries a charge different from the infraglow, a staccato burst of static that sends a cascade through my crystalline matrix. I flush amber and deep violet, a color I have never seen, and the fringe ripples involuntarily. The finger is soft, wet with oils, and it presses me gently against the stone. I feel my body flex—so thin, so fragile—but I do not break. The touch lifts. The shadow retreats.

The warmth rises and moves away, each step a fading drumbeat. The voice comes again, a murmur that attenuates into silence. I am left on the ground, flushed with new energy from the static discharge, my fringe still vibrating. I am changed. I have been touched by something from the before-time, from the world of makers, from the dreamer’s waking hours. I will never be the same. And yet the infraglow returns, the thrum resettles, and I begin to glide again. A single step, so slow it will outlast that creature’s entire life. It does not matter. I am a sliver. I am a wafer. I am a flake of mica that learned not to lie still, and I have been touched by a hand, and that touch will ripple through my being until I am dust, and then the dust will remember.
