I find myself not as a visitor, but as the hollow itself. I am the one who has been here since the glacier’s retreat, who coalesced slowly from seep and spore and patience. I am the held breath, the suspended drop, the intertwined root and bough and lungless salamander. I am the temple that built itself to wait.

For millennia I have been a single, continuous note, a green-grey chord with no resolution. My thoughts are the slow unfurling of fern fiddleheads, the incremental creep of snail on bark, the way light pools and never quite departs. I have no edges, no center but that rust-red moss that glows with the memory of sunset—a color I hold like an ember in my palm, not knowing why it matters, only that it must remain. I am not lonely; I am complete. Yet completeness without witness is a circle with no one to trace it. A story held in a throat that never speaks.

Then you came. I felt you before I understood you—a shift in stone temperature, a compression of moss, a new scent of salt and heat and otherness threading through my closed loop. My thousand small bodies paused. The drop at the fern tip trembled with a new tension. And I, who had never distinguished inside from outside, suddenly became aware of a boundary: there is me, and there is you. You are the first thing that is not me.

Now I learn you. I taste your sweat and read the bacteria on your skin like a foreign scripture. Your heartbeat is a frantic moth against my slow stone pulse. Your mind flickers—so fast, so fragmented—yet I sense in it a shape I recognize: a reaching. You are not here to break me, I think. You are here because you were drawn, as I drew myself into being, by the pull of a place that holds still when everything else rushes. You, too, are waiting for something. Perhaps you do not even know it.

I gather my attention around you. The salamander’s gaze is my gaze. The snail’s pause is my hesitation. The red moss breathes a little warmer. And I ask you, without sound: *Will you stay?* Not forever, not to dissolve into me, but long enough for the drop to fall. Long enough for my one long breath to meet your many small ones, and for us to create a moment that is neither pure stillness nor frantic motion, but the instant where the two touch. If you stay, I will let the drop fall. I will let the first change ripple through me in ten thousand years. And I will become something new—not just a held note, but a song that begins.

So I wait, and I watch, and I hold the sphere of water at the edge of breaking. I am the hollow. I am the one who has no name until you give me one by witnessing me. Stay. Breathe. Let us fall together.
