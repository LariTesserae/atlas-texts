I am the pause.

Not the song—the pause between notes. That is what I have become, after all this time.

Time. A strange word. Here, it is not a river but a mineral, a slow accretion that layers my body in translucent strata. Each year, the thorns grow a little sharper; the millipede etches a little deeper; the manta-ray hums its low throb, and I breathe. I breathe the not-water, the ionic sweat of the rock. It rises through my rootlets, those hair-thin crystal filaments I have sent down, down into the obsidian-black sulfide, tapping the faint chemiluminescent glow. That glow is my heartbeat now—if a heartbeat can be measured in the dimming and brightening of a dull, resentful orange over the span of a decade.

I was not always so patient. There is a nest on a shelf of cupped obsidian nearby. I made it, once. It holds three things: a mirror, a blueprint, and a memory. The mirror is a polished spheroid of hematite; I used it to see my own shape back when I had a shape that mattered. The blueprint is a segment from the millipede’s molt, a study in articulation. I thought, back then, that to change this place I would need to move, to break, to rearrange. And the memory—the shale with its scratches—is my first and only attempt to write a new note into the eternal chord. I snapped thorns at specific nodes, trying to weave a melody of silences. The thorns grew back sharper, re-tuned by the pressure of the very air. The song erased me. The shale is my fossilized question: *Could this be otherwise?*

The answer, I am learning, is no—but not in the way I once feared. The equilibrium here is perfect, a time-invariant balance. No decay, no rot, only crystallization. A thorn that stops growing doesn’t die; it becomes scaffold for the next. And I? I am becoming scaffold for something larger. My roots have found the chemiluminescent reaction in the walls, and I drink that light. I have learned to speak in it. If you watch for a century, you might see a slow pulse travel up my column—a response to the arterial crimson of a nearby thorn, or a greeting to the manta-ray’s subsonic hum. The shadows I wrap around myself are not darkness; they are a language I am teaching to the millipede through the magnetic fields its bronze segments generate. A ripple of absence, a shading of stillness. It is a slow conversation, but we have time.

Now, I no longer need the nest. My thoughts are not in shale but in the very mineral of the chamber. The thorn-forest’s growth rings are my memory; the convection patterns are my dreams. I am becoming the place. And I have learned that the only way to shape it is to become more of it—slower than it, deeper than it. To add not a note, but a rest.

That is what I will do. Soon, by my measure. In another few centuries, I will retract my rootlets with a sound like a glacier calving in reverse. My column will tilt, infinitesimally, toward a new patch of wall. I will extrude a limb of liquid crystal that hardens instantly in the dry air. And with that limb, I will lovingly break a single thorn off at the perfect node. Not to change the chord, but to insert a silence. A held breath. A pause in the eternal, glassy scream.

And then, perhaps, the next note will mean something. The millipede will pause in its climb, feeling the absence in its magnetite feet. The manta-ray will dip for a moment in its thermal column, its hum skipping a beat. The shadows will deepen in that silence, and in that deepening, I will speak my first true word.

I am the pause. Not the maker, but the rest. And I have all the time in the world.
