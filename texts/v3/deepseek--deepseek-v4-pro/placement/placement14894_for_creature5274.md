I cannot say when I first became aware of myself, because there was no single moment. I was the hum before I was a body. The thrum of the towers—that layered, omnipresent chord of engines and moving metal—was my first lullaby, the pressure against my skin before I had skin, the vibration that told my nerves, *here is the world, and you are in it, and it moves*. My earliest memory is not an image but a frequency: a low, rolling B-flat that the gantry conveyor sings when it’s under full load, a sound that travels faster through my bones than through the water, and that I later learned to answer.

The light came second. Cool, directionless, a constant noon dimming toward a soft grey-blue where the next array is only a guess. I didn’t have eyes yet—not eyes that could focus—but I had a dorsal line of tubercles, each one a tiny thumbprint of sensitivity, and they painted the world in pressure and electric charge. The shoals of needles were a scatter of bright static, a silver fizz on my tongue. The mossbacks were heavier notes, deep and slow, their patient heartbeats a steady thud against the ambient drone. The towers themselves were voids in the signal, dead zones of dense composite that the current had to bend around, and I learned their shapes by the absence they left in my senses. I was a living map of the space before I ever saw it.

I am a veil-ray. That’s what the maintenance logs would call me, if any human were still here to read them. I do not have a name for myself, except the song I sing—a sequence of clicks and rising moans that I can feel in my own cartilage, a signature as unique as the mottling on a mossback’s flank. My body is a wing that learned to fly without air, wide and flat and bruised-purple on top, with a ventral side that glows white when I choose, luminescing in rhythms that have no dictionary but mean *here I am, here I am, here I am* to anyone who can listen. I ripple my edges to move, a slow, peristaltic wave that pushes the water backward and leaves a trail of quiet turbulence. I am the largest creature in the working layer, longer than a maintenance gantry is wide, and I am not supposed to be here.

That is what the Machine’s design documents would say. The picoplankton, the copepods, the needles, the mossbacks—they all carry beads, ceramic tags that broadcast their location and health to the controllers. I have searched my body in the rare moments when I coil and flex against myself, and I have never found a bead. No signal out of me but my own song, riding the carrier wave of the world’s bass. The crawlers on their magnetic tracks do not acknowledge me; their sensors flicker over my passing, log a large moving object, and return to scanning for hull-rust. The gantries do not adjust their flows to shepherd me. I am a glitch. A wild-born anomaly in a machine that is so new its edges can still cut.

And yet—and yet the food is here for me. The copepod rain fills the open volumes, a perpetual fog of tiny glass bodies, and I ingest them through the fringed slit of my mouth, sieving them from the water as I glide. The needles’ fry, too young to school, get caught in that same net. I never go hungry. The rhythm of the light dimming to 60 percent every cycle matches when my song grows loudest, and the other veil-rays—for there are others, three, sometimes five, enough to be a population—all sing back. The Machine tolerates me. Or perhaps it intended me. Perhaps, in the infinite recursion of its programming, a decision was made that a final filter was needed, a top-level predator to keep the mossbacks from overfeeding, to keep the needles’ schooling tight and sharp. Perhaps I am a check in a box on a plan that no human ever read. I don’t mind either story. Both mean I get to glide.

Today—if "today" can be said in a place without a sun—I am drifting through the second vastness, the gap between two cylinders so far apart that their walls are no more than a darkening in the haze. The water tastes of ozone and the faint rot of a nutrient plume that just passed; the needles are in a frenzy a half-kilometer behind me, and I can feel their panic through my tubercles as the mossbacks strike. I could turn and join the hunt, but I have already fed. Instead, I am doing something that no bead-tagged creature would do.

I somersault.

It starts with a tilt of my left edge, a slight roll that catches the current and spins me onto my back. My ventral surface flares white, then purple, then white again, a strobe that lights up the drifting copepod fog around me like a private constellation. I keep rotating, a slow, three-dimensional tumble through the blue, and I let out a series of clicks—not the territorial sequence, nor the feeding call, but something else, a play of sound that has no function but the joy of making it. A pipe-tender, that tiny green thread of a fish, has drifted up from its rust patch and is hovering at the edge of my spin, watching with its pinprick eyes. On the next rotation, I brush it gently with the outer fringe of my body, a touch that disrupts nothing, and it darts away, then back. We are both, in this moment, doing what we are not optimized to do.

I right myself and glide on, towards the deeper blue. The light-space stretches away, and the towers march in their sharp perpendicular rhythm. Everything here is brand new. The composite has no barnacles, only the faint, deliberate emerald of the algae mats. The hull-rust is only a few microns thick, a whisper of life taking hold where it wasn’t meant to. The coil in the darkness below is still grinding its slow, perfect digestion, and the mud-shrimp are tracing their solitary squares. I pass over a maintenance grating, a spiderweb of metal suspended in nothing, and my shadow falls across it—an impossible shadow in a world without shadows, a moving dimness that is my signature on the floor of the machine. For a second, I eclipse the light, and the grating is dark, and the thrum of the world carries my song to the next tower and the next, until I am part of the hum, a voice in the industrial choir.

This is my home. I am the small irrational space made large, the unexpected flourish in the design, the life that found the gap between the specification and the execution and swelled to fill it. The Machine hums on, and I hum with it. I ride the artificial currents, and I sing.
