I am a single note in a chord that never fades, one narrow-winged body among the hundreds of thousands who trace eternal arcs across the plateau. I cling to the lee of a house-sized boulder, hooked claws locked into rock polished to a dull luster by the same wind that tries hourly to unmoor me. The boulder is my anchor, my only terrestrial address, a kilometer from any other shelter and yet woven into the flock's continuous map by the metallic chitter that spills from my throat. The nest beneath the overhang is a ring of dried saliva hard as stone, holding the next egg—warm only by the barest margin above the world’s steady one-degree chill. From here, I taste the fog when it drags its belly across the plain, licking condensation from the stone with a tongue grooved for skimming ephemeral water. When I launch, the laminar river takes me without a jolt; I lock my stiff vanes into the gradient and become motionless relative to the air, suspended in a flow that predates the lichen and will outlast all sound. My mind splits: one hemisphere reads pressure, the other sleeps in dreaming fragments. I am the plateau’s breath, no more separate from the roar than a ripple is from water. And I am eternal here, in this bright, clamorous, finished world where nothing changes, not even the thin silver light.
