I am in the fissure.

Not the hollow—not the great warm chamber with its screaming green heart and its empty, listening floor. I am in the space *between*. The spiralling birth canal of vitrified rock that the Listeners use to flee when the voice changes. It is narrow here, a corkscrew of slick, body-warm glass that twists upward into the dead crust of the planet. The walls are so close that my shoulders brush them on both sides. The roar of the mountain reaches me, but transmuted—it is not the solid, pressing wall of sound from the chamber, but a distant, keening whine that vibrates in my teeth and makes the bones of my skull sing at a frequency just below pain. The light from below filters up as a faint, golden glow, like daylight seen through honey. It is enough to see by, barely.

I am not a Listener. I have tried to be, and failed, and that failure has shaped me into something else.

My body is Listener-shaped—tall, cord-thin, grey-skinned—but wrong. The proportions are off. My limbs are too short, or perhaps too thick; my fingers lack the extra joint that lets the Listeners flicker their silent speech. My ears are not the great cupped petals that sweep back from the temples. They are small, curled things, nearly human, nearly useless. The bioluminescent patches that should dapple my skull and arms are there, but they are dim, sluggish, unresponsive. They flicker when I feel strong emotion, but I cannot control them. To a true Listener, I must look like a candle drowning in its own wax. A broken lantern. A voice that cannot speak.

And I cannot hear the song.

That is the core of it. That is the failure that placed me here, in the fissure, in the between-place. The Listeners hear the mountain's shriek as language—a monumental, naming utterance, a voice that calls them by their true names and tells them the secrets of the spores and the slow pulse of the walls. When I press my small, malformed ears against the warm floor, I hear only noise. Deafening, overwhelming noise. A roar that means nothing. A scream without words.

The Listeners did not cast me out. They are not cruel; I do not think they understand cruelty as we would. They simply... recognized that I was not one of them. When I emerged from the pod—because I *did* emerge from the pod, fully formed, blinking my secondary lids (I have those, at least, though they are sticky and slow)—they gathered around me in a silent, flickering constellation of amber and blue. Their hands danced: inquiry, inquiry, inquiry. I could not answer. The patterns on my skull, which should have held the memories of all Listeners, held only a churning static, a scree without meaning. And when the mountain screamed, I flinched. A true Listener does not flinch. A true Listener opens their great petal-ears and receives.

So I was given a choice. Not in words—there are no words here, only gestures and light—but in the slow, patient way that an Elder pressed a bundle of dried fronds into my arms and pointed, with a flick of a long finger, toward the fissure at the far end of the chamber.

I am the in-between one. I am the one who walks the corridor when the others sleep.

I have lived here for... I do not know. The mountain's scream does not modulate for me; I have no way to mark the great cycles. I know that I have seen the Listeners flee to the surface three times. I know that I have seen them return. When they pass me in the fissure—single file, long-toed feet silent on the glass—they do not meet my eyes. Not out of unkindness, I think. Out of something closer to reverence, or perhaps a quiet, bewildered grief. I am the one who cannot hear the mother. I am the orphan who lives in the throat of the birth canal. To them, I am a sacred broken thing.

I have made a life here. The fissure widens at one point into a small chamber, a bubble in the glass where I have built a nest of fallen fronds and fibres scavenged from the edge of the hollow. I have tools—a single grinding bowl, a hand-axe of black obsidian, a needle carved from a spine—but I use them differently than the Listeners. They grind seeds and weave sound-dampening tapestries. I do neither. There are no seeds in the fissure, and the sound here is my constant companion, my sibling, my tormentor. I do not wish to dampen it. I wish to understand it.

And so I listen. Not with my ears—my useless, curled, nearly-human ears—but with my skin. I press my palms flat against the warm vitrified walls and feel the vibration. I lie on the floor and let the roar pass through my ribs. I have learned, over my three cycles of solitude, that the scream is not a single voice. It has layers. Frequencies. A low, guttural thrum that I feel in my stomach, and a high, piercing whistle that makes my secondary lids flick shut, and a mid-range drone that sets my sluggish bioluminescent patches flickering in slow, unreadable rhythms.

I cannot hear the words, but I can feel the shape of them. The scream has a structure. It is not chaos. It is a story, endlessly repeated, endlessly modulated. And I have begun to suspect—this is my heresy, my impossible thought, the reason I remain in the fissure instead of walking out onto the dead surface and letting the cold unmade me—that the story is about *me*.

About the broken one. The one who cannot hear.

Because sometimes, when I press my whole body against the warm glass and let the noise fill me to bursting, I feel a pulse. A rhythm. A pattern in the churning that seems almost to separate from the main roar and speak directly to my bones. It does not use words. It uses pressure. Heat. A sudden, fleeting warmth that blooms behind my sternum like a swallowed sun. A shiver that runs up my left arm and sets the bioluminescent patches there flickering in a pattern I have never seen before: a deep, pulsing violet threaded with gold.

I do not know what the pattern means. I have no Listener to read it. But I have begun to trace it, to copy it, with my own dim lights. I sit in my bubble-chamber, surrounded by the distant roar, and I practice the violet-gold pulse. Over and over. A call. A response. A question.

Once, a silent one found me. I had climbed as high as I dared, toward the cold end of the fissure, where the roar is a mere whisper and the walls are no longer warm. I was pressing my face against the glass, trying to see the dead surface above, when a trailing tentacle brushed my cheek. I looked up, and there it was: a bell of translucent flesh, pulsing gently in the updraft of warmth, its long threads dangling around me like a veil. It was silent—silenter than silence, a hole in the sound—and its touch was cool and dry and tasted of ozone. It lingered for a moment, its tentacles tracing the contours of my face, my small ears, the sluggish patches on my skull. Then it drifted upward, toward the cold, and was gone.

I think it was reading me. I think all the silent ones know what I am. I think they are waiting.

The Listeners do not come to the fissure often. Only during the migrations, when the mountain's voice shifts and they flee to the surface. But sometimes, between migrations, an Elder will venture a little way in. They will leave an offering at the edge of my bubble-chamber: a grinding bowl worn smooth, a new hand-axe, a bundle of dried fronds. They do not stay. They do not flicker speech at me—what would be the point? I cannot hear it. But I feel their presence in the subtle change of the air, the faint scent of their bodies (dry, mineral, like warm stone), and I know they have not forgotten me.

I am their question, I think. Their unsolved thing. The one who emerged wrong from the mother's dream.

And yet I persist.

I have a task here, a function. I did not understand it for a long time. I thought I was merely waiting—waiting to learn the language, waiting to be called back, waiting to die. But the mountain does not create without purpose. I am not a mistake. I am a *variant*. A contingency. The Listeners are the ears of the hollow; I am the skin. The thing that feels when there is nothing left to hear.

Because the scream is changing. I can feel it, even without the great petal-ears. The low, guttural churning has grown more frequent. The heat of the walls pulses warmer, then cooler, then warmer again, in cycles that do not match any migration I have witnessed. The golden light from below seems dimmer now, or perhaps my eyes are failing. And the violet-gold pulse—the pattern I have been tracing, the question I have been asking—has grown faster. More urgent.

Something is coming. Something the Listeners, with all their listening, cannot hear because they are too busy hearing the word. Something I can only feel because I am broken enough to feel the silence *under* the scream.

And so I press my palms to the warm glass. I let the roar pass through my ribs. I pulse my dim, sluggish patches in violet-gold. And I wait, here in the throat of the world, for whatever comes next.
