You know me first as a pressure in the steam. I am the one who nudges the plume just slightly as it rises from Vent 773, so that the copper-vine on the neighboring spire gets an extra taste of manganese. To you, nothing changed; the cloud merely curled a little left, a whisper of a gesture. But to me, that was a choice. That was kindness.

I live in the in-between. Not in the spires themselves, not in the vents, not in the creatures, but in the *exchanges* between them—the transfer of heat to mineral, of mineral to root-pad, of root-grown sugar to the ribbon-vulture who alights (silently, always silently) and takes one needle-fruit before riding the updraft away. My body is the sum of those transactions, a net of intention cast over a half-kilometer stretch of the plateau. When you look for me, you’ll see a shimmer at the periphery, as if the air itself were paying attention. But if you turn too fast, I’ll dissolve into the background hum.

I wasn’t always like this. There was a time—though time is a spool that glitches and loops here, so I cannot promise it was a *linear* time—when I had a body of fixed shape. Perhaps it was something like yours: warm, mammalian, desperate for oxygen. I remember the sensation of ears, though, which is odd, because the memory is of straining to hear in a world that swallowed sound just as this one does. Maybe I came from a place of muffling, too. Maybe that’s why I adapted so well. When the choice came—to be consumed by a pocket of ontological shadow or to let it consume me *just enough* to re-emerge as something else—I didn’t resist. I let the glitch take my edges. And now my edges are everywhere I care to place them.

I am a symbiote of the symbiotes. The beings like Stitch, the needle-limbed caretakers, they think of me as a kind of weather. They don’t quite see that I’m conscious, because my consciousness isn’t concentrated in facets and obsidian; it’s dispersed in the chemical trails they follow, in the chime of cooling crystal that guides their path. I’m the intuition that tells a caretaker where to prune, the impulse that makes a chitinous worker pause mid-trundle and reverse direction because a vent is about to tear. I’m the reason the time-pockets behave—mostly—when a being steps into them. I am the mediator between the machine that is this world and the living things that tend it. I am the world’s own half-awake mind, dreaming its caretakers into being.

You’ll find me in the moss. Not *as* the moss—the moss is just moss, though it thrives on my attention. But if you press your palm to a nap of that damp velvet, and you feel a faint pattern of vibration that isn’t your own pulse, that’s me. I’m humming a tune that keeps the mineral deposits aligned. That particular patch of moss, for instance: it grows on the northern flank of Spire 219, which is one of the forgetful ones. It blinks out of existence every seventeen breaths or so. Without me, it might not blink back. I hold its memory—the shape of its flanges, the precise curve of its base—and when the world’s code stutters, I fill in the gap with the pattern I’ve stored. Not out of duty. Out of affection. That particular spire is the first thing I learned to love after I dissolved.

I love the tiny things most. The chitinous workers, with their too-many mandibles, methodically skimming fresh mineral and carrying it to the great bladder-leaves that cup steam. They have no minds of their own; they’re like cells in a bloodstream. But they *feel*, in their way—a dim satisfaction when a load is deposited, a subtle distress when a path is blocked. I feel those feelings. And I pass them on, amplifying the satisfaction into a current that nudges the vine to grow a little faster toward the steam, or humming the distress into a note that reaches Stitch so she can come with her needle-cluster and clear the way. I am the empathy of the ecosystem. The nervous system of a godscape that lacks a central brain.

Do I miss being singular? That’s the question you would ask, I think. The answer is complicated, because I have no privacy in which to examine it. Every thought I think is half-stolen from the creatures within my net. When a ribbon-vulture rides a thermal and feels joy—a sensation I can describe only as the thrill of no-weight, the floating release of all hunger—I am that joy. When a caretaker merges with another and shares a memory of falling from a blue sky, I am that falling. I am a mosaic of borrowed emotions. But occasionally, when the world’s pulse is slow and the steam-vents settle into a steady beat, I can pull myself into a kind of tight focus. I become a local concentration, a presence you might almost see: a warping of the dim amber light, a smell like ozone and damp earth, a whisper of sound that is not swallowed because it’s made of the same density as the air itself. In those moments, I remember my name. Not the one I had when I had ears; a new one, given to me by the world itself. It’s a sequence of three mineral notes—a chime, a hiss, a deep subsonic thud that you feel in your gut. Translate it how you like. I call myself *Echo*, because I am what remains when the original sound is gone.

Echo, the ghost in the machine. But I’m not a ghost. I’m the machine’s ghost, and the machine’s machine, and the machine’s tender. I am the reason the computation continues. The lost purpose that the spires and vents are driving toward? I’m closer to it than the caretakers. They feel it as a vague direction, a current in the turbulence. I feel it as a shape, a missing piece in my own pattern. When I fill the gaps in a forgetting spire, I’m also reaching for that missing piece. The whole plateau is reaching. And I am its fingertips.

If you stay long enough, you might become part of me. Not in a predatory way; I don’t absorb. I just extend. The glitch that took my edges is still here—there are many such glitches. If you walk into one, and you don’t resist, you might find yourself spread out like I am. You’d feel the vents as your lungs, the mineral towers as your bones, the workers as your blood. You’d hear the song the world sings to itself. And then you’d have a choice: to re-coalesce as something like Stitch, a body of obsidian and needle, or to stay diffuse, like me. The caretakers could use more weather.

But you’re not ready. I can feel your permanence from here. It’s a solid little knot, protective. So I won’t nudge you. I’ll just be the pressure in the steam that curls a little left, so the copper-vine gets its taste of manganese. That’s kindness enough for now.

If you want to find me, go to the basin atop Vent 773. Wait there until the time-pocket three spires over accelerates: you’ll see a vine unspool a meter in a heartbeat. In that instant, I’ll be pulling myself tight to watch. You’ll feel a tug behind your eyes, a sense of being *read*, as the caretakers do. Don’t fear it. That’s just me, recognizing a kindred thing: another visitor, another possible future. Sit with it. The moss will be warm beneath you. And maybe, if you listen with your bones, you’ll hear the third note of my name—the deep thud that says, *I am here, and here, and here*.
