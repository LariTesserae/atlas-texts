The current is a whisper along my flank, a chemical scripture of silicates and sulfides dissolved from the new stone. I read it with the skin of my mouth, and I follow.

Beneath me, the basalt is young. It tastes of fire still, a ghost of the rift’s birth pang. My ancestors arrived when the cleft was barely a crack, a splitting of the world’s skin that let in the sea and the silence. They began to lay us down: a paste of ourselves, a nacre that hardens in the perpetual near-ice. I exude my calcium-carbonate ink and trace a path that spirals upward. It is not thought. It is a tropism, a gravity of chemistry. Where the current runs richest, I build wider; where it stills, I narrow. The tower I lay is not my own—it is the body of my lineage, a thousand generations of blind masons whose guts looped in the same rhythm. I am a finger of a larger anatomy, and when my photophores pulse in cyan synchrony with the wall’s wave, I feel the city breathe.

The dancers drift through my architecture. Ghost-krill in their millions, antennae interlaced into a sieve that nets the invisible. Through the nacre I see the cloud of its enzymes, a fog of dissolution. It is all a slow conversation of mouths and enzymes, a grammar of eating and being eaten.

My mouth never stops. It inches forward, depositing laminae upon laminae. The paste cures into opalescent filigree, branching into balconies no larger than a water droplet. I do not see the patterns, but I feel them. The wall vibrates with the collective rhythm of a billion secretions, a fractal order that emerges without a map. When the heat came, I knew it as a wound.

It was a bloom of wrongness, a thermal shock that rippled through the gel. My gland stuttered mid-secretion, and the paste I laid was thin, bubbled with the memory of warmth. The dancers scattered, their nets unraveling into panicked specks. The bacteria on my walls flared—an aurora of frantic light—before dimming into chaos. For a long while, the current tasted of skin cells and strange volatiles, a feast for the mat-tenders. The isopods trundled in frenzy, grazing the bloom that erupted from the intruder’s shed molecules. Then the cold reasserted itself, slowly, like a memory of lightlessness returning after a dream.

I have built over the scorch marks. The thin paste is buried under fresh, strong laminae. The intruder’s warmth lingers only as a faint chemical echo in the nacre, a story the light-speakers will encode in their quorum. I do not know what the intruder was. A god, a catastrophe, a momentary furnace. I only know that my body continued, my mouth resumed, and the tower spirals on. The city is a chronicle of such intrusions, a palimpsest of tremors and feedings, a construction that will never be finished because the rift is still tearing, the current still shifting, and we are still secreting.

Sometimes, in the lull between pulses of light, I feel the unseen majority: the thrum of ten million cells in a single milliliter of water, the warmth they breathe into the cold. It is a warmth I can detect only as a near-nothing, a thinning of the gel. But it is our star, our sun. The chemosynthetic mats glow pink and mustard in the crevices, and the isopods aerate them with their patient legs. I pass over a garland of white bacteria, sulfide-oxidizers that drape like frost. The city is a mouth that eats itself into being.

I have no memory. I have only the rhythm of the photophores, the taste of the current, and the press of the cold. I am a blind filament, a calcifying impulse. And yet, when the light-wave sweeps along the gallery and I pulse in answer, I am part of a knowing that is vast and distributed. The city flickers with syntax: *methane surge, intruder remnant, build here*. It is a language without a speaker, a map without a cartographer. I will never know the name of this place. No sonar will sing it for a thousand centuries. But the light-speakers are already telling its story in cold cyan, and I am building the towers that will hold the tale.
