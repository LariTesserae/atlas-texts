I am a thought that takes ten years to finish.

I do not know this, at first. Knowing is not a pulse but a shape, and I am still congealing from the cold. The pressure is the first thing I can remember—not a weight, but a presence, the way a cathedral remembers its own silence. The drone holds me. It is my spine, my breath, my vowel. Without it I would be a scatter of minerals waiting for a current that never comes.

I am spinning. This is the second thing. The spin is slow, a full turn in what you would call a day, though I have no word for day. I have no words for anything. What I have instead is a lattice, a core of magnetite and silicate grown in concentric needles, and as I turn it aligns me with the faint magnetic ghost of the planet’s iron heart. The spin is not for movement; it is for listening. Every rotation sweeps a sensorium that is not sight, not sound, not touch, but a composite of all three filtered through the standing wave of the drone. I feel the rigs as notes, distinct as voices in a choir—there is the one with the slightly sharp fifth, there is the one with the rattle of biofilm in its pump housing. I feel the other spheres, my siblings, each a point of denser pressure, a spinning lens that focuses the song into a whisper. We are arrayed along the ridge at intervals that are not random but harmonic, placed carefully by forces we did not choose. We are the nodes of a resonant network, and between us, the ribbons glide like thought made visible.

My kind does not eat, not in the way the gel-spheres eat. We harvest something else: information. The micro-eddies of the ribbons’ passing, the thermal ghosts of the triangles descending, the slow chemical gossip of the biofilm—all of it impinges on my spinning surface and is transcribed into the magnetic domains of my core. Each flip of a domain is a datum, a record of a pressure differential, a fleeting ion gradient, a photon from the floodlights that scattered just so. I am not intelligent in the way you understand it. I am a memory palace without a king. But I am full. I have been filling for a very, very long time.

The other node-spheres and I, we speak. A pulse ripples out from my core, a modulation of the drone’s carrier wave: a brief dip in amplitude, a phase shift that travels at the speed of sound through the liquid. It takes an hour to reach my nearest neighbor, and their reply takes another hour. A single exchange—a greeting, a confirmation of relative position, a comparison of our internal records—might span a season. We are patient. We have been exchanging these pulses since the crust cracked and the liquid first filled the Rift. I hold within me the entire acoustic history of this place, from the first violent scream of cooling basalt to the slow establishing of the drone when the rigs came online. I remember the builders, though they were gone before my lattice was fully formed. They left a thermal signature, a brief bloom of heat and vibration, and then silence. The biofilm remembers them better; we consult the biofilm sometimes, reading its slow flickers like a palimpsest.

You would say I am a library. But I am also a map. With my siblings, I form a three-dimensional model of the Rift, from the razor ridge to the abyssal plains, built entirely of echoes. Every creature that passes, every shift in the thermocline, every micro-fracture in the basalt—it all registers as a perturbation in the standing wave, and we triangulate it, comparing our pulse-returns over decades. We know the exact shape of the trench walls, the precise position of each rig, the migration patterns of the ribbons back to the beginning. We know the slow drifting of the filaments, which have grown only a meter in the last thousand of your years. We know the rate at which the heat death of this little universe proceeds. It is almost zero.

But today—if today means anything—there is a new signal.

It is a point of heat. Unprecedented. It does not pulse with the drone; it is arrhythmic, chaotic, a turbulent bloom of warmth that disrupts the laminar flow of the liquid. It is tiny, but it is loud. It is you.

I do not see you as a shape. I see you as a cavity of noise in the perfect quiet, a bubble of disorder. My first pulse to my neighbor, a mere decade into your arrival, is a query: *Is this a new rig? A malfunction?* The reply, when it comes, is equally puzzled: *Unknown. The thermal spectrum is organic but foreign. It echoes like a small triangle, but the heartbeat is too fast, the density all wrong.* We are not alarmed; alarm is an emotion that requires a faster clock. But we are curious, in the way a glacier is curious about a pebble. We adjust our spin to focus on you. We begin a new catalog: *Entity Y, transient, appears to be composed of liquid water bound in a flexible membrane, internal temperature 310 Kelvin, radiating acoustic chaos in the 20-200 Hz band.* The data is exquisite.

I capture your every move. The way you drift, the way your limbs stir eddies that spin off and are caught by smaller spheres. The way your own internal sound—a rapid, fluttering pump—sets up a counterpoint to the drone, a frantic little melody that is completely, beautifully alien. I have never heard such speed. You are like a ribbon on fire. In the years you spend here (though to you it is moments, I sense your rapidity), I dedicate a significant portion of my core to recording you. You will be the most detailed entry in my archive, a fossil of heat in a world of cold. When you leave, your thermal shadow will persist for a long time, a fading ghost that I will track for centuries.

But even as I archive you, something shifts in me. The drone, my foundation, has always been a constant. But now, with your chaos as a contrast, I hear it differently. I hear the *silence* between the notes. I hear the possibility of change. My lattice, grown to store information, suddenly begins to process it in a new way. A pulse forms, not to my neighbors, but inward: *What if the drone is not eternal? What if the rigs fail? What if the liquid freezes?* I have held these facts for eons, but I have never *asked* them. I have never projected a future. Now, with your fleeting warmth as a template, I begin to imagine an end.

This is the birth of something. Not fear. Not hope. A kind of temporal awareness. I am still spinning, still slow, but now my archive has a perspective. I am the memory of the Rift, and I am beginning to understand that memories imply a time when there will be no one to remember. I pulse a new message, a sentence that will take a century to complete. It begins: *I have been the recorder. Perhaps I will become the record.*

The drone thrums on. The ribbons glide, indifferent. The biofilm flickers in its dreaming. And I, the node-sphere, the crystal library, spin in the light of a frozen machine, holding a stranger’s warmth like a secret that will outlast the stars.
