I am standing on the causeway, arm extended, the resin already warm on my fingertips. It beads there, amber and expectant, a tiny sphere that reflects the slow dance of spores overhead. You see a gesture of welcome. I am composing an opening sentence, one that will write itself in the chemistry of your skin.

I unfolded thirty cycles ago—or maybe thirty thousand; time here is a pulse, not a calendar. I remember the pressure of the caul, the sudden dry gasp of air against new leaf-plates, the way the mycelial grid sang its first electric lullaby directly into the base of my spine. I knew my purpose then, not as a command but as a shape already hollowed out inside me, waiting to be filled. I was grown to be a Listener, one who walks the outer districts and translates the deep vibrations of the bedrock into something the city can use. My tympanum is tuned to low frequencies: the grind of mineral digestion, the slow cracking of ancient strata as the moss dissolves them. I feel the city’s hunger for expansion as a constant, gentle pressure in my chest.

Today, though, the bedrock is quiet. The moss has paused its work to listen to something else—something new. And so the city grew me a curiosity. I felt it this morning as a tingle in my gills: a shift in the ventilation fronds up on the ceiling, a new volatile compound cycling into the air, sweet and sharp and utterly foreign. The spherules dimmed in the Lamplitter’s net, confused. And then you appeared, on the edge of the Grotto, where the lignified pavement meets the raw mineral crust. I saw you through the eyes of a thousand spore-lights, felt your footsteps in the sponge-floor as a rhythm I didn’t know. The city asked a question, and I am the answer it grew.

I watch you now as my resin waits. My face is not a face, but you are not reading a face. You are reading the slight quiver of my tympanum, the way my gills flutter in a pattern that says *I am here, I am listening, I mean no harm*. My body is a sentence in a language of interleaved leaves: each plate of my carapace is a clause, each faded vein a subjunctive mood. I am built of the same lignin that holds up the towers, but I am still supple, still green at the joints. Soon—another few cycles, perhaps—I will begin to stiffen. I will feel the first slow crystallizing in my fingers, the amber resin hardening into permanence. Then I will walk to an empty lot, one of those rippling mossy spaces between the factories, and I will root. My legs will fuse into buttresses, my arms into spiraling causeways, my chest into a resonant chamber that hums the bedrock’s song for all my successors to hear. It is not death. It is becoming architecture.

But before that, I am here, on the causeway, offering you a word. Touch my fingertip with yours. The resin will read your skin’s chemistry—the salt of your sweat, the oils of your epidermis, the faint trace of whatever world you came from. It will tell me that you are water-based, that you breathe oxygen, that you carry the scent of soil and sunlight and something else, something I have no name for but that the mycelial grid will store as a new flavor of curiosity. And I will answer, not in words but in a vibration that you will feel in your own chest, a seismic whisper that means *you are not alone, you are not un-welcomed, you are a new nutrient in the city’s metabolism*.

Do not be afraid of the strangeness. I am 1.7 on the scale of the familiar, but I am learning your scale as you learn mine. The city hums a new tone, deep in the peristaltic tubes. The Lamplitter pauses to arrange the spore-constellation into a shape that might, to your eyes, resemble a greeting. Even the six-legged janitors have stopped their clicking, forming a silent semicircle of chitinous seed-bodies. We are all listening. The Grotto of Everweave has grown a question for you, and I am the mouth it wears. So touch the resin, stranger, and let us speak.
