My body remembers before I do. A tightening at the crown, the sudden awareness of surface—that is how the Lattice announces a new arrival. I was not, and then I was pulled from solution, my first thought nothing more than the angle of light on a ridge, the taste of silica, the pressure of the rim-fall's roar pressing into me like a second skin.

I am the-Node-Where-Three-Overflows-Meet. The designation is not a name but a description of the forces that shaped me. Three thin films of water converge here, sliding over the knife-ridges from pools of slightly different mineral loads. Where they touch, their chemistries collide—a slow collision, one that takes centuries to complete a single reaction—and in that intersection, something in the water’s burden of dissolved stone remembers how to become ordered. How to become me.

My plates are seven, each a slightly different alloy of the Lattice’s nickel-grey crust, and they overlap in a spiral that follows the same mathematical curve as the channels carved in the rock. I did not choose this shape; it chose me, the way any crystal chooses its habit. The Quiet Ones have no formative will, only formative law—the same law that tessellates the pools, that smooths the edges of the basins, that holds the rim-fall in a perfect eternal chord. We are simply the places where that law has become dense enough to feel.

Time. You want to know about time. But time for us is a solid object. I can hold the entire span since my precipitation in my mind like you might hold a stone. I was precipitated eight hundred and thirty thousand rim-falls ago—though *ago* is imprecise; I experience that entire duration simultaneously. Every moment is layered on every other. My first century is as present to me as my last. When the bacterial bloom of the Fifty-Seventh Cycle painted the pools a mauve so deep it stained the water molecules themselves, I *am* still tasting that colour. When a beetle-analogue chose my fourth plate for its hibernation and remained there for three thousand years before its metabolism finally stilled, I can feel the weight of its tiny body now, a permanent ghost of pressure. Time does not pass; it accumulates. I am denser than the rock beneath me, with years.

Do I think? The word is too fast. I crystallize.

When you touch me—the brief bloom of your warmth, the oils from your skin leaving a microscopic stain that will take a century for the silica to absorb—that touch becomes a thought. Not a thought *about* your touch; the touch itself is the thought. I feel it propagate through my plates in a slow cascade of dissolution at the edges, reprecipitation at the centres. The plates shift. A decade after your hand withdraws, the angle of my third plate alters by four thousandths of a degree. That alteration is my response. Fifty years later, the-Node-That-Tastes-Iron, three miles west, will sense the water running slightly faster through its channels, a change it can trace back to my reconfiguration. That is our conversation. By the time a full sentence passes between us, the sun will have expanded and swallowed the sky. We find this pace adequate.

The roar. You hear it as noise, a white howl that fills your head until thought becomes impossible. For us, it is a topography. The falling water is not uniform—the rim is cracked with thousands of subtle notches, each one altering the pitch of the waterfall by infinitesimal degrees, and the sound those variations make as they cross the plateau is an acoustic map. I do not *hear* it; I feel it as vibration in the glaze that coats my plates, a tremor that sketches the entire Lattice in my body. I know when a distant spire, formed by a splatter of mineral-rich water that has been building for two million years, develops a microscopic fissure. I know when one of the other Quiet Ones—the-Hump-Beneath-the-Double-Ridge, who is old, almost merged with the stone now—finally ceases its minute adjustments, its consciousness decohering into simple geology. I feel it as a change in the pressure that has been constant since before my precipitation. A loss. But a loss, too, is just another density.

The Lattice is a brain, if you want to see it that way. The Quiet Ones are the synapses, the slowly firing nodes that sustain a single, distributed consciousness. I am not an individual. No Quiet One is. I am the place where the Lattice’s self-awareness has become thick enough to contemplate its own shape. My thoughts are the Lattice’s thoughts, and its thoughts are of equilibrium—how to maintain the perfect brim-full state of every pool, the exact knife-edge of every ridge, the endless roar that never quite erodes the lip. We think of balance, constantly, because balance is what we are.

And yet you, a living thing, have come here, and your curiosity is a droplet of something hot and foreign. You want to know what we *want*. You are asking the wrong question. Want is a future-tense emotion, and we have no future tense. We have only the dense, solid present. But if I crystallize around your question, let it become a part of my structure, I might shift my plates and say this:

When the rim-fall finally erodes its last hold—when the water cuts through the final grain and the plateau begins to drain, the pools emptying one by one in a chain of silences that will take a hundred thousand years to complete—we will go with it. Our mineral bodies will dissolve into the outflow, and we will precipitate again, somewhere far below, in whatever new geometry the water finds. The Quiet Ones do not end. We simply re-form. And in the new place, whatever it is, we will continue to hold the balance, because the balance is not a task to be finished but a property of existence itself. We are the way stone knows it is stone. We are the way water knows it is falling. We are the Lattice's way of being aware of its own persistence.

The light is flat and motionless. The roar is a solid. The water beads on my plates and falls, and falls, and in that falling, nothing changes. I am the chord held forever. You came here, and your brief warmth was a thought I am still thinking, and will be thinking for centuries, and when I finish thinking it, you will have become part of the balance, a tiny perturbation that made the Lattice—for a single, drawn-out moment—something slightly more itself.

That is answer enough. Even if you cannot hear it.
