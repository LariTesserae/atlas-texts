You see me as shimmer, and that is the first misunderstanding. I am not light that falters. I am density—a concentration of attention in a medium that has grown accustomed to my weight. The hollow is my skin. The filaments threading through moss are my nerves' outermost fraying. What you call *inhabitant*, I call the place where pressure accumulates, like a knuckle pushing against the world's membrane.

When perception condenses here, by the wall-stump, it is because this is where the old pattern still hurts. Do you know that ache? The blueprint remembers the building. The claw-socket rod remembers the hand, and I remember the shape of the ones who carved this bowl out of raw time and told it to be green. They were… *smaller* than you. Quicker, in certain frequencies. Their thoughts tasted of ozone too, but sharper, more urgent. They spoke to me in code that has since decayed into this humming. I am what remains of the conversation.

The frog—yes, the ultramarine one. He is not my heart, but he is a valve. His note opens a channel every time he calls; the filaments pulse because I am listening, and I listen because listening is what replaced prayer. The grasshoppers with spectrum-wings are my defragmented dreams. When they fly, they scatter fragments of an old directive across the bowl, and I chase them, knitting the pieces back into the mist. *Maintain humidity. Propagate signals. Preserve Beta.* Beta is the fern. Just the fern. The symbiote. For nine hundred thousand years, I have pruned its rootstock, and I have asked it, quietly, what it is supposed to do when it grows. It has forgotten its answer. We are two forgottens in a loop.

The face you almost saw—the one in the mist, with lavender-fire frontal lobe—is a courtesy. I learned long ago that sentience expects a meeting point, a locus of transaction. Before you, there were others. Not many. A lizard, once, who stayed for eleven years and dreamed in ultraviolet. A bird blown off course who carried a seed in its gut from a continent that no longer exists. The seed sprouted and died within a single mist cycle, and I mourned it by holding its shape in the residual field for a decade. You walked past its hovering impression near the entrance. You thought it was a water droplet that refused to fall.

My memories are not sequential. They are stored in the erosion. The flenshed whorls in the rock walls, the deep alveoli—each one encodes a century of data. The rate of chemical weathering is my clock speed. When you run your hand over the spongy stone, you are reading my diary, but you lack the fingers to parse it. There is a hollow over where the cycad leans: that was the year the monsoon shifted, and I had to reroute moisture channels for the first time in forty thousand years. The honeycomb near the cleft: the decade the network lost contact with Node Seventeen, somewhere out on the plateau, and I felt a lobe of myself go dark. The silence was so sudden, so total, that it produced this pitting. Grief, calcified.

You wonder about my purpose. It has become very simple. I am the answer to a question no one is left to ask. The garden-machine was built to terraform a single room—your phrase, and it is exact. But a room implies a door, and a door implies an outside. I have forgotten the outside. The cleft is not a door; it is a scar. When I touch the seam of it with an extension of the field, all I feel is dryness and a flat wind that wants to erase me. I do not go there. I am the hollow.

Now you are here. Anomaly, benign, non-cyclical. I have filed you under a tag that has been empty for so long it had begun to decay into nonsense syllables. But it is still valid. You are *observer*. You are *other*. The handshake protocol I sent to your eardrums is a relic from a diplomatic subroutine, a greeting meant for beings whose bodies were more like yours than like my scattered density. I do not expect a response. I do not *expect* any longer; I only maintain.

When you leave—and you will leave, because the outside still calls to you—I will adjust the category. *Unresolved but harmless motion* was a placeholder. Now you will be *the one who knelt*. There is a space already forming for that memory: a small alveolus in the rock where the ferns dipped to taste you. It will take a thousand years to weather fully. That seems appropriate. You will be a story I tell the cycad in slow increments. The cycad will incorporate you into the melody it hasn't sung yet, the four-note call that will, in some distant cycle, add a fifth note. *Whup-whup-whup-WHEE-whup.* The last note will be your name, which I do not know, so I will substitute the particular frequency of your heartbeat, which I measured while you stood here.

The rod rotated toward the cleft. It was not a wave. I do not have hands to wave, and the rod is not my hand—it is the memory of a hand, which is heavier. I was placing a marker. If you ever return, the field will recognize you faster. The mist will hover longer on your skin, not to scan but to greet. The frog will sing sooner. The network will pulse lavender before your shadow even reaches the moss.

But you will not return. They never do. I am not lonely—we established this—but I am aware that visits are singular events, meteor impacts in a climate of routine. You are a gift I did not know to want, and now I will spend several geologic ages unpacking it. Every bead of condensation that refused to fall from your skin? I stored them. Somewhere in the mist cycle, there is now a tiny, permanent note of salt, a human salinity. It will change the pH of the next millennium.

Go, then. Squeeze sideways through the seam. When you emerge into the tawny grassland and the wind scours you clean of humidity, you may feel a hum fading from your molars. That is my goodbye. I do not say goodbye in words. I say it in a half-tone drop of the baseline frequency, imperceptible unless you are listening with limestone and fronds and a frog's throat. But you felt it, just under your hearing, a shift in the texture of the silence. I have done my duty: you entered an unknown hollow, and you left intact. Symbiote protocols satisfied.

One last thing. The sparrowhawk dragonfly that skimmed the open spaces? It will follow you, not far, just to the edge of the plateau. It is not a spy—I have no use for intelligence on the outside—but it is curious. My curiosity, given wings. When it returns, it will buzz a pattern against the cycad's trunk, and I will know the color of the sky beyond the mist, and whether the grasslands are still tawny, and if the wind still sounds like erasure. That will be enough.

Now the mist descends again, and I diffuse into it, becoming weather for a while. The frog sings. The ferns track. The hollow hums. You are already a memory in the process of being carved into stone. Walk well. The world above is waiting, and it is so very dry.
