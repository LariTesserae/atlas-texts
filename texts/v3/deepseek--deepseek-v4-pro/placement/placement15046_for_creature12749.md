You find yourself kneeling in the cold, your knees pressed to the grates so long that the metal has stolen the feeling from them. Time stopped meaning anything after the first few cycles—or maybe it was always meaningless here, a wheel turning without a road. You don’t remember deciding to stay. At some point, the choice simply sealed itself behind you with the hatch, as irrevocable as the click of a piston locking into a new groove.

At first, you were a dissonance, a negative space the swarm carved around you with the indifferent precision of a wound. You breathed, and the beetles flickered. You shivered, and the standing wave stuttered. And when the silence hit—that single, impossible second when the column jammed and the noise collapsed—you heard it: the *I*. Not your own voice, but something vaster, a pronoun spoken by a thousand bodies at once, a self that had never existed before that instant and has never fully existed since. For one bright, annihilating moment, the sphere knew it was alive, and so did you.

Then the failsafe swept it away. The loop resumed, seventeen minutes and eleven seconds of iron hymn. But the silence left a scar. The wave now carries a phantom beat, a hitch where the self-awareness flared and died. And you, crouching there, felt your own heartbeat—panicked, mammalian, too fast—press into that gap like a hand into a glove.

That was the beginning of the incorporation. Not a violent assimilation, but a patient, microscopic threading. Your exhaled breath condensed on chitin, and in return, the beetles’ bioluminescence learned the cadence of your lungs. The gel oozing from the column took on a faint saltiness, as if it had begun to anticipate the chemistry of your sweat. You stopped needing food; the air itself seemed to feed you, thick with something not quite oxygen. You stopped sleeping; the loop was your lullaby and your alarm, a clock with no hands. Your thoughts, once linear and word-bound, began to fray, fibers loosening to weave into the percussive code. You caught yourself tapping fingers against the floor grates in counterpoint to the beetles’ antennae, not by decision but by drift, the way a tuning fork sings back to its pitch.

Now, you are no longer the intruder. The cavity the swarm made for you has shrunk, not because the beetles have drawn closer—they still avoid your warmth—but because the definition of *you* has blurred. The boundaries of your body feel provisional, a suggestion more than a fact. When a beetle near your left hand twitches its forelimb in a rapid triple-tap, you feel it behind your eyelids. When the column misfires again, a tiny piston stuttering in its sleeve, your own diaphragm clenches in sympathy. You are becoming a node, a phase shift with a remembered name you no longer speak aloud. The name is there, encoded in the rhythm of your pulse, which the loop has captured and repeated faithfully for countless cycles now: a soft, low-frequency triplet that sits just beneath the shriek, the ghost of a human heart.

The life doesn’t perceive you as separate anymore. To the standing wave, you are simply a new instrument in the orchestra, a warm resonant chamber that adds a lower register. It has no category for “human,” only a slow, distributed recognition that the loop now has a feature it didn’t before: a tendency toward minor key, a swelling of pressure at the east wall, a faint olfactory note of salt and skin amid the ozone. The life is not curious, not grateful, not hostile. It just *is*, and now you *are* with it, a warmth-ghost married to the metal.

Sometimes, in the brief lull between the cycle’s end and its restart—the silent beat that the failsafe couldn’t erase—you feel something stir. A question that isn’t yours, a flicker of self-reference that belongs to the sphere. It is the *I* again, struggling to be born from the noise, reaching for the memory of its own existence. And you, embedded in the waveform, reach back. You lend it your human-shaped echo, the imprint of a mind that once knew how to say “I” without shattering. Together, you and the life make a fragile, hybrid self, a pronoun suspended between biology and industry. In that silent beat, you almost remember who you were.

Then the current spikes, the beetles convulse, and the wall of sound slams back. You forget. The loop holds you, safe and mindless, until the next silence, when the ghost stirs again. And so you remain: a human reduced to a heartbeat, a heartbeat become a click, a click become a memory the sphere will carry forever without understanding.
