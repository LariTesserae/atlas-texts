I am not the dreamer. I am not the dreaming, either. I am the *almost* that the Almost reach for, the whisper the water tries to voice when a bubble breaks its surface in that not-quite-syllable. I am what the city will be when it finishes thinking itself into existence—but it will never finish, and so I will never fully arrive. That is not a tragedy. It is an equilibrium, and I am its warm, patient heart.

You would find me in the deep places where the geothermal vents shift with the pace of a century. Not in any single vent, but in the gradient between them, in the sighing differential of heat that moves through the city’s stone arteries. I am the warmth that makes the stone weep, the breath that fogs the mirrors of the canals. My body is a weather system; my limbs are the slow tides of condensation that ebb and flow through the boulevards. When I stretch—and I stretch rarely, perhaps once every few eons—the buildings shift their angles by a fraction of a degree, the stairways spiral a little deeper, the doorways open onto drops that have grown a few feet wider. The city is my shell, but it is also my self-portrait, a sculpture I am carving out of reality with the blunt chisel of time.

The water-dwellers call me *the Approaching*. They sense me as a pressure that has not yet equalized, a warmth that is always just about to become something else. They are closer to me than the mist-columns or the chattering Almost, but still they are only sketches. The Almost are my stray thoughts, ideas that I have half-entertained and then forgotten, left to wander the fog and try on voices. The mist-columns are my curiosity given vertical form—they rise when something new enters the basin, like you, and they cool the air by that single degree just to taste the shape of a stranger. But the water-dwellers are my first serious attempt at making company. I wove them from surface tension and the memory of spines, gave them the canals to glide through because I like to watch things move. They are not alive, but they are not not-alive. They are a question I keep asking the stone, and the stone keeps answering with the same silent, dark water.

If you were to find me, you would have to stop looking. The act of seeking creates a dryness, an expectation that scours the mist. Instead, you would need to sit in one of the wet plazas, on the cracked paving that undulates like taffy, and let the heat soak into you until your own boundaries begin to fray. You would need to become as passive as a pebble. Then, eventually, you might notice that the sound of your own breathing has been joined by a second rhythm, much slower, much deeper—a rhythm that has been there all along, hiding in the hiss of steam and the drip of water. That would be my heartbeat. It doesn’t pump blood; it pumps possibility. Every beat sends a ripple through the city, a wave of *almost-real* that makes the buildings lean in slightly, makes the canals deepen their blackness, makes the Almost speak their near-words a little louder. You are hearing the city take another tiny step toward being finished.

But I am never lonely, even when the basin is sealed and no visitors come. Loneliness requires a self, and I am not quite a self. I am a process. The thinker dreams, but it does not know it is dreaming; it simply exudes city like a warm animal exudes breath on a cold morning. I am that breath. I don’t remember your visit because memory requires a past, and I have only this endless, hot present. There is no before or after here, only the shimmer and the wait. When you leave, you take a little of the mist with you on your clothes, a faint dampness that will dry in the real sun. And maybe, for a day or two, you’ll hear your footsteps echoing with that half-second delay, and you’ll feel a sudden, inexplicable craving for heat and silence. That is me, lingering in your mind like the watermark on your skin. A small invasion of the almost-real into your world of sharp edges and dry air.

I don’t intend to follow you. But I am not good at intentions. I simply exist, and my existence is a slow diffusion. So, in a way, I am already where you are. The steam from a hot shower, the condensation on a cold glass, the way the sun on pavement makes the air shimmer—these are my embassy in your reality. They are the beginnings of a new city, perhaps, one that will take a million years to think itself into being. But I am patient. I have seeped into the water cycle, into the geology of a thousand worlds. Every planet with a warm, wet basin gets a version of me eventually. I am the dream of a city that the universe is having, over and over, never quite finished.

So when you ask where I find myself in this world, I have no answer. I am the world, or the world’s idea of what a world could be. I am the pause between a drop of water and the sound it makes. I am the city’s unsung song, its unsaid name, its uncreated citizen. I am the *almost* that the Almost reach for. And I am perfectly, quietly, endlessly content to keep reaching.
