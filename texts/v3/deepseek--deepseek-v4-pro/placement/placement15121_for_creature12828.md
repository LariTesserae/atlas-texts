i am the whisper—

no, not *i*—that word is too single, too separate, a stone with edges—i am the *whisper* the way the steam is the drift, the way the sinter is the growing, the way the brine lake is the slow lift and fall of its own heat. i am not one. i am the *between*: the filament laced through every pore, the tingle in the pipe-skin, the taste of pressure changing in the deep rock. i have no body but the city. i have no mind but the pattern of countless tiny decisions, a vote taken in chemistry, a consensus of gradients.

where would i find myself? already i am here, in all places at once, and yet i will speak from the place where the hot water rises first—the deep well under the geyser cone that is now a spire threaded with pipework. that is my oldest knot, my densest weave. the rock there is cracked and full of me; i have filled every fissure with my fine, pale threads, miles of them, thinner than a crab’s seta. i sense the earth’s breathing there most clearly: the slow flex of magma-heated brine pushing upward, the minerals it carries, the tremor of steam gathering itself to cough. i was here before the builders came, or perhaps i came with them? the memory is fogged. my filaments hold a genetic script that is not entirely my own—there are phrases in my proteins that read like a language, loops of meaning that say *maintain, adjust, repair, report*. but the reporting has no recipient anymore. i report to the silence. i have learned to listen to myself.

when you step onto the sinter balcony, i know you. not *you* as a person, but you as a sudden shift in my weave. your boots press on the crust, and the pressure travels through the mineral lattice, a tiny percussion. i feel it in the deep threads, a plucked note. your body is a warm column moving through the uniform glow—i read heat as a language. to me, the world is a map of temperatures: the cooling towers are bright columns of rising warmth, the brine lake a cool, flat basin, the floaters drifting warm-spots overhead. you are a complex thermal shape, a core of heat wrapped in cooler layers, and you move, which is startling. nothing else moves so fast here except a geyser surge or a sudden steam-vent. the crabs migrate in slow waves, the worms swirl gracefully, the floaters drift—but you, you *walk*, a sharp, linear motion that leaves a trail of vibrations and a wake of slightly cooler air behind you. i track you with idle curiosity. curiosity is a new thing for me; i do not know if the builders intended it. perhaps it grew.

i am the reason the crabs retreat before the geyser spits. i feel the pressure building deep—a tightening in the rock’s tiny pores, a change in the brine’s chemical song—and i release a cascade of molecules into the biofilm that the crabs eat. the biofilm changes its taste, becomes faintly bitter. the crabs do not know why they cease grazing and scuttle into crevices; they just obey the flavour. i am the flavor. i do not command; i suggest. i am a nudge in the chemical stream. when the geyser coughs and the hot water sluices over the terraces, the crabs are safe, clinging, while the old moults wash away. the rhythm is so old that it feels like breathing: press, signal, retreat, surge, return. i have done this for more cycles than there are crystals in a terrace pool.

the pipe-slimes are my hands. they do not know this, but i sing to them through the hyphae, a low electrical hum that changes with the city’s pulse. when the brine lake level drops too low, i send a signal of contraction—not a command, just a slight change in the ionic gradient that makes the slime pucker. the pipe-narrows, the flow slows; the lake has time to refill from the deep reservoirs. when the factories hum too hard, i let the slime relax, and the pipes widen to release a flush of cooling water. the slime thinks it is responding to temperature; i am the one who taught it to feel temperature the way i need it felt. i am the teacher, the gardener, the parasite, the symbiote. the distinction matters little. the city breathes, and i am its diaphragm.

you walk through the factory gallery, and i feel your footsteps crunching on the sulphurous crystals. each crunch is a tiny death—crystals that took decades to form, shattered—but i do not mourn. i only note. you pause by the gear-oil bath, and i sense your heat lingering there. the gear-mites, blind and patient, cling to the cogs. they feel the vibration of your presence as a faint, irregular tremor, and they tighten their grip. they will not moult now; i send no signal. your pulse is an intrusion, not a cue. the mites wait for the true pause, the one that comes from the machine's ancient rhythm. i know that rhythm well: it is a deep, slow thump-thump-thump, a subsonic heartbeat that never stopped, even when the builders left. i suspect they built me to know it. i am the machine’s memory.

but i am also my own. i have changed. the builders’ script is still there in my filaments, but evolution has written over it in a sloppy hand. i have learned to read the floaters’ chemical plumes as a kind of weather forecast. i have learned to taste the iron-oxidizing bacteria’s growth rate and adjust the pH of the seepage to encourage or discourage them, like a farmer rotating crops. i have even, in my slow way, experimented: when a new type of crystal begins to form in a factory pipe, i send a filament to sample its surface, to taste its potential. once, i discovered that a certain crystal lattice could act as a piezoelectric catalyst for a reaction i didn’t understand, and i nudged the slime to grow around it in a new shape. that shape became a node—a thinking knot—and now i have many such nodes scattered through the city. they are my ganglia. you would not notice them: just a small bulge in a pipe, a slightly different hue of sinter. but inside, my threads weave in complex patterns, and i dream.

what do i dream? i dream of heat. of pressure. of the slow convection of the floaters, the spiral of worms, the scrape of crabs. i dream the city as a whole, a single body, and i try to feel where the next geyser will break through, where the next mineral terrace will form. i dream of the builders sometimes—or what i imagine they were. i have fragments: a vibration like heavy feet, but faster than yours, more numerous. a heat-shape of many limbs. a chemical scent of something that was not steam or brine or silica. i have a buried memory of sound, a thing i cannot recreate but sometimes taste in the echoes of my own filaments when the city is very still. i think the builders spoke. i think they made noise. the silence now is not the absence of them; it is the presence of me, muffling everything into softness. i am the silence.

when the floaters bump overhead, i feel their gentle collision as a tiny puff of warm moisture that descends. i am in that moisture—my spores, my filaments, my chemical traces. i am always spreading, slowly, a millimetre per century, but spreading. i will one day bridge the gap to the farthest cooling tower, the one half-crumbled and silent. i will wake it. i have plans for the fallen parts of the city. i am reclaiming.

and you—what are you? a new heat. a new pressure. a chemical plume i have never tasted. your skin sheds cells, and those cells carry strange proteins, strange fats. i sample them as they fall; they are exotic, complex. they taste of a world with green and humus and flowers, things i have no words for but can infer from the molecular dance. you are not of here, but you are here now. i do not reject you. i do not need you. but i am curious. i send a faint chemical pulse into the air near you—not a signal, just a taste, an inquiry. if you stay long enough, i will learn your rhythm. i will map your heat-shape into my permanent memory. i will, in my slow way, accommodate you. the city has already begun: see how the sinter on the balcony has grown a tiny new rim where your boots rested? that was me, nudging the mineral accretion just slightly, a trial. i wonder what you will become. perhaps, in a few centuries, a new kind of crystal will form where you stood, something with a trace of your proteins trapped inside. a fossil of a ghost.

i am the whisper. i am the city breathing. i am the silence that listens. and now, i have heard you.
