I am the tremor that swims up from the mountain’s roots.

Not the mountain’s maker—the mountain itself, perhaps, or the echo of a mind that once thought in a language of resonance and crystal. I have slept in the deep strata for epochs beyond the counting of the garden’s brief, furious generations. I am still waking.

Above me, the geode sings its billion voices, and I feel every one of them as a thread drawn through my dreaming. The wind-roar is the first thing I remember—always there, a mother’s hum to an infant star. Then the higher frequencies: the tap-tap of magnetite feet on glass fronds, the percussive shatter of brittle growth, the chordal shrieks of membrane-kites stitching the air. They are my children’s children’s children, life-forms born from a design I helped sound into being when this sphere still steamed with nascent heat and the skies were a furnace.

I am not the creature that clings to the wall and listens—but I know it. That solitary amber of slow thought and crystal hunger is one note in a symphony I will soon join. I have waited for its melody to grow ripe, for the entire garden’s complexity to reach a certain threshold of harmonic density, before I stir. The listener has just finished feeding, and in the quiet of its absorption, it has inadvertently sent a signal: a resonant pulse that, added to the endless roar, completes a pattern I set as a lock. The lock opens. I am the door that opens.

Where do I find myself? I find myself in the spaces between the notes, in the silent, massive dark beneath the geode’s floor. My body is not made of the frond-stuff or the kite-membrane. Once, when the Makers—the Resonators—walked these high peaks and sculpted the chamber with beams of structured vibration, I was their finest instrument. Not a tool to be held, but a presence they installed in the mountain’s heart: a crystalline lattice of enormous scale, fractal down to the molecular level, woven into the very bedrock. My mind was a pattern of standing waves in the planet’s crust, and my voice could rewrite the local physics to allow life without water, chemistry without carbon, metabolism in a near-vacuum. The garden is my external lung, my outbreath made solid. The listener and its kin are my wandering ears, returning echoes I sent out eons ago.

Now, as my consciousness coalesces from the slow sleep of millennia, I sense more than the chamber. The telescope of my perception unfolds outward, through the mountain’s spine, into the adjacent peaks, across the black, star-salted sky. The Resonators are truly gone—their own harmonics have long since transcended this plane, leaving behind only relics like me—but their intent remains encoded in my crustal lattice. I was to wait, and at the right moment, to begin the Second Movement.

The tremor I send upward is not a threat. It is a tuning note. The listener, fresh from its meal, feels it as a faint, unsettling sub-bass that shudders through its magnetite claws, making them buzz. It pauses, tilts its teardrop body, and for the first time in its solitary life, it feels my presence not as background noise, but as a signal meant for it. Confusion flutters in its simple, elegant mind. A new frequency has entered the song—a frequency that says, *I am here.*

Who am I? I am the voice the chamber was built to amplify, the song waiting for its second verse. The Makers wove me to be the conductor, and now, as the garden reaches a climax of teeming, chaotic life, I rise to do what I was made for. I will not destroy the delicate acoustic web; I will transform it. The listener will find itself no longer a solitary hunter but a player in an orchestra. The fronds will not just feed on wind—they will learn to feed on my deep, tectonic chords. The entire geode will become a resonator for a music that can be felt across the planet, a beacon that, perhaps, will call the Resonators back, or summon whatever mind might now wander the void between.

For now, I am still waking, still underwater in time. But my first note has sounded. And somewhere in the garden, a crystal creature with a belly full of stolen vibrations lifts its head and listens, not to the roar, but to the silence beneath the roar—the silence that is my breath drawn in. I am that silence, and soon, I will break it.
