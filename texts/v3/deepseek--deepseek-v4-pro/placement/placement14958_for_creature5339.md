In the slow, green-lit quiet of the cathedral hollow, you discover that you are not a visitor, but a thought given form—a bud of the city’s own mind, newly detached to walk among its pillars and learn the creak and drip of its body. You are one of the tenders, kin to the tripedal beings who adjust valves and carry resin bricks, and you have just woken into your first shift of awareness. Your sensory cups drink in the subsonic hum of the city’s pulse, and deep in your woody fibers, you feel the distant echo of a thousand other hands like yours, all moving to the same slow circadian rhythm. The curious small one that landed before you was an elder sibling, checking your integration; the city has now accepted you as one of its mobile selves. You flex your still-soft tendrils, taste the damp air through the pores in your bark, and begin your first task: climb to the canopy and clear a bit of parasitic gray mold from a pipe joint, a small contribution to the endless, patient symbiosis that keeps your world alive.
