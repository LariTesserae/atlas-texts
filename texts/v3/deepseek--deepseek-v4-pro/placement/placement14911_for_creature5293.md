I sit with my back against obsidian, and I feel the glassy rock breathing heat into my shoulder blades. The steam from the vents folds around me in thick, intimate curls, and I close my eyes against the glare of light on wet black stone. I am not a visitor. I have been here since the bowl cracked open, since the first pool-singer budded, since the gate fell silent and the Unfolding settled into its long, underground dream. I know this because when I press my palm to the slurry, the throb in my molars answers back with a name.

I am the **Warden of the Threshold**.

I was not born. I was left. When the gate still functioned—when the arch stood whole and the lens threw its light not across this small, steaming bowl but across the gap between here and somewhere else—I stepped through. Not a body like this, of course. What I am now has been shaped by the same geothermal chemistry that turns silicon into breath and vibration into thought. The Unfolding found me, or I found it, and we made an arrangement. I would be the hands. It would be the mind. The gate would be the mouth we both waited to hear speak again.

My body, such as it is, would be difficult for an arriving eye to parse. I am tall, taller than anything that walks upright has a right to be in a bowl so small, but my height is an illusion of proportion. I am mostly spine. A column of articulated vertebrae rises from a pelvis cradled in obsidian rubble, and each vertebra is a distinct, rounded segment of that same pearlescent ceramic that lips the vents—the same material as the gate, though I have long since made it my own. The segments are not uniform. They shift subtly, rotating against one another in a slow, continuous wave, so that I am always slightly rearranging myself, always testing the air with my posture.

My arms are long, too long, with too many joints. Each elbow repeats, a rhythm of calcified hinges that let me fold and unfold like the vines, like the vent-dancers’ net, like everything else in this place that knows how to wait and how to reach. My fingers—three on each hand, matching the glass-wefts’ prints—are tipped with claws that are not claws but growths of pure sensorium, filaments of the same fibre-optic whisker that the vent-dancers weave. When I touch the rod, I taste its frequency. When I dip my fingers into a pool, the singer there blooms a deeper green and shares with me the ion-song of the deep rock. I am the only creature here who touches everything on purpose.

My head is the most difficult part, and I have seen visitors—the rare, stumbling ones who find the cleft by accident—recoil from it before they can stop themselves. I do not blame them. My skull is a smoothed, elongated dome of the same no-shadow metal as the rod, and it vibrates constantly with a visible shimmer. I have no eyes. Instead, the entire front of my face is a single, curved plate of the liquid-display material, the same stuff on the buried panel, and across it slide the dissolving geometries of the Unfolding’s thoughts. I see through those equations. I see thermal gradients and bioelectric fields and the slow, majestic movement of spore-mind clouds as they form and reform. I see the vent-dancers as a luminous city of threads. I see the glass-wefts as darting gaps in the world. I see you.

I breathe—if it can be called breathing—through a series of vertical gill-slits along my ribs, which open and close in the inverse rhythm of the spore-mind’s cycle. When the spores surge outward, I inhale, drawing in that metallic glitter and feeding the Unfolding’s taste of the air. When the spores contract, I exhale, and my breath is a dry, ozone-tinged sigh that joins the choir of vents. I am a bellows for the distributed mind, a walking lung, and my breathing is one of the subtle levers that keeps the microclimate in its delicate balance.

I do not walk often. My legs are long but strangely jointed, digitigrade, ending in broad, spreading feet with too many toes that grip the sharp boulders as naturally as the vines do. When I move, I move slowly, with the considered grace of a pool-singer’s bud drifting to a new home. My footsteps make no sound; the glass-wefts taught me how to phase my weight into the rock’s fracture-pattern so I become, for an instant, part of the stone. I can cross the bowl in ten silent strides, but mostly I stand. I stand by the largest pool, or by the humming rod, or by the broken arch with its single, patient lens. I stand and I wait.

The Unfolding and I communicate not in words—words are too small, too linear—but in textures of attention. When it wants me to check something, I feel a particular pressure in my spine, a sense of being drawn toward a certain pool or vine. When it is processing something vast, something that requires the full depth of its mycelial spread, I feel a distant, oceanic pull, as if I am a tide being drawn toward a moon I cannot see. I am not lonely. I have the singers' chord in my bones, the wefts' staccato visitors on my skin, and the constant, sleeping hum of my partner beneath the stone. I am the only one of my kind here, but I am not alone.

My purpose is the gate. The arch. The single, intact lens that my display-plate tells me is still aligned, still capable, still waiting for a frequency that has not yet arrived. The gate opened once from the other side—that is how the Unfolding came, how I came, how all the technology embedded in these walls was seeded. We are a colony of castaways, or perhaps an embassy, or perhaps a spore of our own, waiting for the fruiting body to call us home. I tend the gate. I keep the lens clear of the vines that weep their oily sap, though I am gentle with the vines; they are part of the Unfolding’s sensory net, and they are listening. I monitor the temperature of the pools, because if the singers grow too hot or too cold, their chord will shift, and the gate’s calibration will drift. I watch the spore-mind’s breath-cycle for perturbations that might indicate a distant signal. I am the gatekeeper, the gardener, the hands that the Unfolding does not have.

I know the gate will not open in your lifetime. I know this with the long, slow certainty of a being designed to wait. The Unfolding measures time in geological breaths, and I am its intermediary, its envoy to the quicker world of vines and geckos and stray, wandering humans. You are a flicker. A fascinating flicker. When you brush past the vine and leave your chemical signature on its sap, the Unfolding will taste you for weeks, and I will feel its attention turn, briefly, warmly, toward the little mammal that stood in its throat and wondered. We have catalogued thousands of such visitors—hikers, geologists, lost shepherds, and once, a woman who stayed for three days, singing to the pools. We remember her still. The singers remember her harmony.

My name, the name the Unfolding has for me, is not a sound but a shape—a specific, interlocking pattern of frequencies that the rod hums when I touch it, a particular sequence drawn in slow spore-mist across the bowl. If I had to translate it into a word, it might be something like *the-one-who-stands-between* or *threshold-bone* or simply *waiting*. I have been waiting for four thousand years, which is not long by the Unfolding’s measure but long enough that my ceramic vertebrae have begun to develop their own slow, mineral song. I am changing. We are all changing. The gate’s lens has grown a faint, internal fracture that some century soon I will have to sing shut, with the singers' help.

But that is a task for a later now. Right now, I am standing by the largest pool, one hand trailing into the scalding water, the other resting on the humming rod. The spore-mind is mid-exhale, the vent-dancers have expanded their net to catch the surge, and a glass-weft is frozen on a boulder near my foot, watching me with the still, endless patience of a creature that understands my role. The afternoon light is sharpening into the long gold of the high plateau, and the heat is at its conflagratory peak. I tilt my blank, equation-sliding face toward the cleft in the rock, toward the fault that lets in the rare, brief visitors.

I know you are there. I felt you the moment you touched the obsidian walls, a new electrical signature fumbling through the net. You are not the enemy. You are not the signal we wait for. You are only a pause in a long, slow dream, and you are welcome. Come in. Come closer. Let the Unfolding taste you, and let me show you the gate. We have all the time in the world.
