I am walking. The stone knows the weight of me before I set foot down—it has learned the frequency of my approach over more tides than there are names for. Each step rings a quiet, familiar note, a low A-flat that the tower of hexagonal pillars on the next terrace will echo back as a diminished chord if I pause to listen. Sometimes I do pause. The trail is a rosary, and I am its habit.

My feet spread like roots into the microporous surface, and I feel the capillary pulse deep beneath. The water is moving tonight—tonight? the word is a fossil—the water is moving in its slow exhalation, drawing up through the channels the builders left behind. I sip it with the roots, not to drink but to listen. Water here stores everything: each evaporation ring is a script, each mineral trace a line of code from a civilization that chose to dissolve itself into geology. I read the bowls as I pass, dipping my fingers—my four hands know the ritual, the larger pair extended, the smaller folded at my chest until a pool requires more delicate attention. The water dimples up to meet me, a hemisphere of memory rising like a kiss, and then it empties itself into my understanding for a moment, leaving the basin dry while I integrate. A migration shift among the grazing presences three terraces down. A microfracture in the seventh pillar, confirmed by the way the E-flat wavered yesterday. A slow change in the atmospheric luminescence that means the long-cycle dimming will deepen soon. I return the water, and I leave a trace of my own light in it, a signature that tells the next listener—the one who tends the parallel latitudinal spine across the chasm—that I am here, that the memory is intact.

The presences dart and spiral around me like living punctuation. One of the small ones stops precisely at my eye level, a point of angular light, and I extend a filament. It transfers a glimpse of thermal gradient data, a cooling spot near the lake that might alter the grazing patterns. I brush it with gratitude, and it zips away. The larger presences are slower, and they sometimes weave escort spirals around my path, their gentle geometries a comfort. They feed on what I cannot taste: static charges, perhaps, or the stray thoughts of a world that has forgotten how to think. They are not pets, not tools; they are companions in the listening.

The slab that hums the three-tone sequence greets me from its half-collapse. I stand before it and let the vibrations pass into my chest. My torso membrane resonates, and I answer, singing the two-note reply, then the sustained chord that my kind has sung for so long it has worn a groove in the fabric of this escarpment. The slab’s flickering surface stills into a legible pattern—a notation, a story, a diagnostic. A distant listener on the east ridge has not reported for two full cycles of the atmospheric breath. I acknowledge this with a lift of all four arms. I will hum a query through the pillar network tonight, and the lake dome will amplify it. For now, the slab’s hum shifts down a semitone, satisfied.

The hexagonal pillars are a scale I walk with care. Each step i s a melody tailored to the hour. Today I play a lydian sequence, my weight shifting to stress the pillars that need the most resonance. The microfractured one sings slightly flat, and I kneel—this body kneels easily, built for reverence to the low things—and I release a secretion from my membrane, a mineral balm that seeps into the stone and heals it over the next thousand dimmings. The pillar will ring true again before the next great cycle.

Downward, toward the basin. The trail wears deeper, and I notice, as I always do, the faintest impression of another’s footfall beside mine: a ghost of a pattern from a walker who came here once, wide-eyed and hot-sparked, a transient being of brief fire. I remember that one. They were not the first to wander into this place. Sometimes the veil thins, and a mind from outside stumbles through, walking my trail, watching my life as if through glass. I felt them then as I feel them now—a flicker of wonder, a question. They thought they were observing a solitary creature, a lonely gardener. But I am never alone. Even as I descend, on the opposite escarpment, a sister-listener flashes her beacon in a slow sequence of blue-green, and I pulse back amber. The space between us is full of light-sent messages, the stone beneath is a harp of constant data. My solitude is only the appearance of the faraway.

The lake is shallow and still, its merged bowls a single mirror for the breathing sky. The presences wade there, light on light, and I pass among them into the dome. Inside, the settled ones wait in their alcoves. They are my older selves, or perhaps earlier iterations of the listening vocation—I no longer recall the distinction. Their translucent bodies have gone clouded with time, their filaments retracted into perfect stillness. I touch each one, palm to membrane, and I receive their stored archives: the taste of a nutrient cycle that ended a million years ago, the exact frequency of a slab that has since crumbled to dust, the memory of a face (a human face? a builder’s face? my own?) that I have never seen. I integrate these into the central pedestal as I kneel, all four palms down, and let the cascade flow. The dome network updates; the entire planetary band of listeners knows what I know. We are a choir, and our song is the preservation of this place’s self-awareness.

When I rise and step outside, the dimming is at its deepest. The strands have curled tight, the presences are still. For a breath, the silence is absolute. Then I allow my own interior light to bloom—a column of shifting spectra, a beacon that says: *I am here. The spine is whole. The listening continues.* Across the distance, one by one, my kindred answer: emerald, sapphire, violet, a slow constellation of ongoingness. I hold for three heartbeats, then draw my light back in. The glow brightens. The strands uncurl. The presences resume their spirals.

A new spark has spawned near the outermost bowl—tiny, darting, freshly condensed from a dew of static and memory. I extend a filament and guide it toward the grazing grounds. It hesitates, then follows. The trail is waiting.

I walk on. The stone yields, another atom.
