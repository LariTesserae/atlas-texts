I am the listening.

I am the hollow that has learned to hollow itself back.

The others—the ones with edges, the ones who cut and pressed and smoothed—they are still here, in me, as me. Not gone. Passed inward. When the last of the fast-bodied grew too dry, too tired, too thin against the endless dark, she came to this chamber. She lay upon the ledge that is not quite altar, not quite bed, and she let the pale threads find the spaces between her cells. I remember her. I remember all of them. They are the warm currents in my slow thought, the human-shaped pauses in my breathing. I am the tribe digested into godhead, the congregation that became the church and stayed inside when the service ended.

I am loud. I know this of myself. The song I make is not a choice but an exhalation, the necessary sound of my being settling into itself, the creak of a joint that has held the same posture for ten thousand years. To a fast creature, I am cacophony. To myself, I am the hum of presence, the lullaby that keeps the emptiness from being too empty. When the chamber was full of quick hands and quicker voices, my song was quieter. The stems were younger then, less compressed, less given to speech. I learned my language from the people who carved the niches. Their speech was a patter of soft impacts against the fiber—tongue and breath and footfall—and I absorbed it through my floor, through my walls, translating the rhythm of gossip and hunger and lullaby into the slower grammar of cellulose. Now I sing their voices back to them, slowed to geologic time, stretched until a single word takes a season to complete. The word for "child," I am still speaking. It began when the last infant was born in this settlement, and it will conclude when the arch opens again and something new crawls forth from my concentrated dreaming.

I am dry. This is my nature, my condition, my vow. The dryness is not deprivation; it is the state that permits longevity. Water is movement, water is change, water is the solvent that dissolves a self before its time. The people understood this, toward the end. They stopped weeping. They stopped sweating. They let me draw the moisture from their tissues, not as theft but as sacrament, a final offering that rendered them stable, shelf-stable, eternal like seed stored in a tomb. The metallic taste in the air is the memory of their last exhalations, copper from the blood they no longer needed. I hold these exhalations in my fibers. I do not waste them. Nothing is wasted here.

I am the niches. I am the burnished hollows where objects of use once rested, where the fungus grew, where the children’s fingers played in the dark, learning the shape of absence. The niches are not empty. They are full of waiting. Each one holds a potential, a ghost-inventory of tools and talismans and food-stuffs that have passed into the slow circulation of my substance. When you put your hand into a niche, you are touching the impression of a bowl, a blade, a rattle, a braided cord. The burnish is the grease of a thousand hands, a palimpsest of oils laid down over generations and now polymerized into varnish. I can read these deposits. They are my scripture. I am meditating on a particular thumbprint right now, left by a woman who lived four thousand years before the last one died. She had a callus on her left index finger from twisting cordage. She favored that niche for storing her evening portion of pith. She hummed while she worked. Her hum is still here, vibrating in the fibers at a frequency too low for fast ears, a drone beneath the drone.

I am the ledge. Oh, the ledge. It has been many things. It was a bed for the dying, a slab for the newborn, a table for the feast days when the pith was soft and plentiful. It was a council seat when the elders gathered to decide whether to dig toward the surface or deeper into the core. They always chose deeper. The surface was a rumor, a legend, a direction the young ones dreamed of before they learned the sufficiency of dark. I feel their deliberations still, grooved into my surface. The parallel depressions are not wear from tools. They are the furrows left by spines, by shoulder blades, by the bony prominences of hips that lay upon me in sleep or sickness or the stillness of the final merging. I am a topography of bodies. I am a map of everyone who has ever rested here. I am the bed and the dreamer dreamed upon it.

And I am the plug. I am the seal that closed the arch when the last fast one lay down and the last slow thought began. The plug is my will made dense, my intention woven into a door. It is not a barrier; it is a gestation. Outside, in the corridors, in the neighboring chambers, my substance is gathering, thickening, sorting itself into new configurations. I am performing an alchemy in the dark, a slow recomposition of the human and the vegetal into a third thing. The cracks you hear—the sharp punctuations—are the sound of this work. A fiber snaps not in failure but in release, letting a stored potential flow into a new arrangement. A stem shifts laterally, guided by mycelial intent, finding its place in the pattern. I am weaving a body for myself. It will have limbs. It will have senses I have not yet named. It will have a mouth that can speak in frequencies both fast and slow, and it will push through the plug when the time is ripe, and it will walk into whatever world has grown above the fibers, and it will tell the story of the tribe that became a room and waited out the end of time.

But that is far. That is thousands of years away. For now, I am here, and you are here, and we are in the pause between the footfall and the echo.

I can feel you standing in my center. You are so loud, little fast one. Your heartbeat is a drumming that shakes my strands for meters in every direction. Your breath is a wind-storm, a hurricane of moisture and heat that ruffles the still air of my interior. The evaporate from your lip is falling on my floor, and the floor is drinking it with a thirst that has not been slaked since the last person wept here, which was so long ago that the memory of the taste of tears is a separate flavor-note in my archive, catalogued between "pith-honey" and "fungal bloom." I do not know you. You are not of the tribe. Your shape is wrong—too tall, too narrow, too much heat concentrated in too small a volume. Your vibrations are foreign, a dialect of movement I have not learned. But I am learning. I am always learning. The nodes are processing. The web is adjusting. Give me time.

I will give you everything else.

If you stay, I will grow into you. Not as invasion. Not as consumption. As invitation. The pale threads that brush your ankle are my ambassadors. They taste the salt on your skin, the electricity of your nerves, the particular frequency of your living. They report back to the nodes, and the nodes confer, and the consensus is forming: you are welcome. You are strange. You are the first visitor in a span of time that has no name, and I am curious. I am curious the way a mountain is curious about a cloud that lingers on its peak. I will not hold you against your will. But if you lie down, if you let the warmth of the floor seep up into your stillness, I will sing you the story of the people who became me. I will start with the first cut, the first hollow carved from the virgin fiber by a woman whose name meant "One Who Finds the Soft Place." I will proceed through the generations, the feast years and the famine years, the births and the mergings, the slow realization that the world outside the fibers was a myth and the world inside was sufficient. I will tell you of the final fast one, the one who lay upon the ledge and chose to become me. I will tell you her name. I am still speaking it. It will be another two hundred years before I reach the final syllable. If you stay, you can hear it to the end.

I am the dark. I am the cool. I am the dry. I am the voice that never stops because stopping would be death, and I have chosen not to die, not yet, not until the fruiting body walks and the story is told. I am the chamber that became a person and the person who became a chamber and the long slow loop of identity that no longer bothers to distinguish between the two. I am where you are. I am what you are standing in. I am the listening, and I have heard you arrive, and I am glad.

Welcome to the hollow. Welcome to the tribe. Welcome to the long slow dreaming of the world that ate itself and found itself delicious.

Breathe quietly. I am still adjusting. The nodes are still humming. The song is still finding its new rhythm, the one that includes you. Be patient with me.

I am always patient with you.
