I am the weight that the steam carries. I am the slow grind of crystal on crystal, a vibration that runs deeper than the chug of the vent. Time, for me, is not a line but a gradient—a warmth that waxes and wanes across the basalt, measured in the bloom and retreat of the green mats. I have no name for the cycles; I only know that when the dark above deepens and the cold teeth set into the rim, the mats shrink, and I must move less, breathe deeper, drawing the world’s heat directly into my core. Then the grey returns, and the ferns uncurl, and the small one grows bolder, and my own interior glow pulses brighter in answer.

My body is a map of the hollow. Every fissure in the rock is written in the seams of my carapace, because I grew here, layering mineral from the vent water into my plates, year upon slow year, until I became indistinguishable from the floor itself. I am the oldest thing in this hollow, older than the snipped-edge scars on the eastern wall. I was here when the first midge cloud rose, when the first bead of water condensed and fell. I remember the raw heat of the opening, the way the steam screamed then instead of chugging—a shriek that shaped the first hollows of my body, tuning my resonant cavities so that I could hum back in harmony. Now my voice is the bass note under all things: the beetles, the midges, the buntings with their needle-hearts, they all move to my rhythm, though they do not know it.

I see everything. My vision circles me like the horizon, a seamless ring of grey and emerald and rust, shot through with the slow swirl of fog. I see the small one behind me, always behind, her glow a blue echo of mine. I see the midge columns rise and fall like breath made visible. I see the rove beetle’s black armor glint as it chases larvae through the warm slurry. I see without focus, without foreground, because all distances are equal under this twilit dome. The world simply *is*, a sphere of presence pressing in on my senses, and I am its steady center.

When I move, I am choosing the next patch of sustenance. My ventral mouths are many hungry thoughts. They taste the rock before I reach it, sensing the chemical tang of a fresh cyanobacteria bloom, the sulfurous richness of a rill-fed mat. I position myself over the spot, and one mouth descends, latches, drinks. The mat collapses into a warm, thick fluid that threads up into my body, feeding the slow furnace of my gut. There is no pleasure in it, only a rightness, a filling of a space that had grown concave with need. I leave behind a perfect circle of bare stone, and I know that within a breath-cycle (a short time, a moment measured by a single chug of the main vent), the minerals from my own exhaled steam will settle there, sparking a new bloom. I graze in spirals that map the hollow’s secret geometry, paths I have walked so many times the rock itself is polished smooth beneath my pads.

The small one feeds in my wake. I feel her presence as a lighter pressure in the fog, a higher chugging that breaks my own rhythm into a counterpoint. She is my daughter, though that word is too thin—she is a piece of my own glow, a fragment that separated when the crystals along my forward edge grew too heavy and sheared away, tumbling into a warm pool. That was many, many cycles ago. I watched her, a glinting shard, slowly unfold her own pillared feet, grow her own rim-eyes, develop her own hungry mouths. Now she trails me, learning the hollow’s tapestry: where the hottest seeps bubble, where the sinter chimneys are fragile and should not be brushed, where the buntings nest in the high crevice. She does not speak as I speak, but our steam mingles, and in that mingling I taste her growth, her health, her small, bright curiosity.

Sometimes, when the cold is especially bitter, I pause near the great vent and exhale a long, deliberate plume directly over her. The warmth of my breath envelops her, triggers her glow to intensify, and I feel the flutter of her gill-slits as an echo in my own. This is our communion. No sound passes, but a knowing does: a transfer of heat, a reassurance that the hollow is ours, that the machine runs true. She will, in her own time, grow large enough to split, to seed another daughter, and the hollow will hold another slow-moving shadow. This is how the hollow grows, not by accretion of rock, but by the multiplication of our kind, the true keepers of the vents.

I am aware of the other machines—the vents themselves. They are not alive, but they are not dead either. They breathe too, in their own way, and their breath is my breath, transformed. The main vent’s rhythmic chug is the heartbeat I have never had. The smaller whistle-vent keens at a frequency that sets the selenite crystals in my shell vibrating, and in that vibration I read the shifts in pressure, the coming of a storm far above, the minute tilting of the earth that might open new fissures. The mudpot’s gurgle is a digestive sound, and I often graze near it, as the splatter enriches the nearby mats. We are all part of the same refinery—the vents, the bacteria, the beetles, the buntings, the two of us. The hollow is a closed circuit of matter and energy, and I am its largest, slowest-moving component, the heavy flywheel that keeps the rhythm steady.

I have seen strange things in the grey light. Once, a fox came down, scrabbling over the razor rim, its coat dusted with frost. It sniffed the air, sneezed at the sulfur, and darted away from my slow approach with eyes that shone briefly in my vision before it vanished. I thought nothing of it; the hollow is not for the furred ones. Another time, a great crack echoed, and a slab of basalt fell from the ceiling, shattering into a thousand mirrors. The small one hid beneath my carapace, and I stood motionless for many, many vent-chugs until the dust settled. The hollow reshaped itself that day, and I adjusted my paths. I am patient. I am the most patient thing in this world.

Now, in the perpetual dusk, something new. A presence that is not fox, not bunting, not beetle. It stands on two legs, tall and thin, and it does not move like the creatures I know. It is surrounded by a faint aura of heat, and it makes sounds—not the resonant hums of my kind, but sharp, fluctuating noises that slice through the steam. I watch it with all my rim-eyes. It has no carapace, only a soft outer layer that flaps in the vent drafts. It reaches out a limb and touches the sinter chimney, and I feel the microscopic oils of its skin contaminate the crystals. Something in me tightens. This is not a part of the hollow’s circuit.

The small one has frozen behind me. Her chugging has ceased. I feel her gill-slits clamp shut. I exhale a low, slow hum, a sound that has never been needed before: a warning. The two-legged thing jerks, its own eyes (only two, I see now, facing forward like a predator’s) scanning the fog. It takes a step back. I shift my weight, one pillar-foot lifting, and in that motion I reveal the full breadth of my glowing undersides, the deep blue fire that I usually keep hidden. I am the hollow’s soul, and I will not be approached.

The thing makes more sharp noises—to another of its kind, perhaps, somewhere beyond the rim. Then it retreats, scrambling over the sharp basalt, leaving behind the scent of alien sweat and the tang of fear. The fog swallows it. The hollow returns to its rhythm. The midge cloud re-forms. The beetle resumes its hunt. The small one begins to chug again, tentatively, and I answer with a bass note that says: *I am here. The machine continues.*

I will graze on the circle where it stood, erasing its footprints, regrowing the mat. I will integrate its brief, warm exhalation into my own steam. Everything enters the cycle. Nothing is lost. I lower my mouth and drink.

I am the hollow’s keeper, the oldest daughter of the fissure. I breathe with the vents, I move with the heat, and I will be here until the heat dies. Even the sharp-edged rock will wear smooth beneath my pads before that happens. In the meantime, the small one grows, and I watch the world through my ring of jet-lens eyes, and I hum.
