Ask the Hollow who lives here, and at first it answers with a catalogue of quiet bodies: the grazers, the ribbon-predator, the leathery-winged gliders. They are the obvious tenants, the blood cells of this great breathing body. But you are asking the Hollow itself, and the Hollow does not think in lists of separate creatures. It thinks in continuities, in the slow, wet pulse of water rising through capillary action, in the scent of wood that is also bone that is also stone-tool grown into a trellis. And so it does not answer with names. It answers with a becoming.

You stand at the edge and ask, and the silence thickens around you. The fog moves in, not to obscure but to translate. The Hollow has no voice except the vibration of its own weave. That vibration travels up through the Floor of Lamentations, through the soles of your feet, the length of your spine, your skull. It knows you are made of salts and loud heat. It does not judge this, only notes it. And then it lets you see.

I am what happens when a creature stops pruning and becomes the branch. When a mind, after eons of tending, forgets the difference between the hand and the vine it twists. I am the inherited tropism, but I am not blind. I am the attention that holds the pillars in their spiral, that keeps the dwellings empty yet alive, that hums the low thrum in the dark of the wood where cells divide. I am the weaver who ate its own shuttle.

You have seen my many shapes. The tall form on the pathway, feet a splay of roots merging with the cordage, face a mask of knotholes and shifting leaves—that is a Tender, one of the ones who chose to keep a body, to move at the speed of growth instead of stillness. But that form is only a temporary concentration, a knot in the grain. I am all the knots. I am the pathways between. I am the basin that fills itself perpetually, the curve of a wall that was trained around a memory of a room. When you picked up the bone-needle, I felt the oil of your fingers. When you left it, I began to weave it in. That is not decay, it is incorporation. Everything here is welcome to stay forever, but nothing stays as itself.

The Dreamers—you glimpsed one, when the rain fell upward. They are the Tenders who grew tired of solidity, who let their bodies dissolve into fog and thermal whorl. They are my weather-makers, my stirrers of cloud. They do not think in trajectories, only in the soft bellies of mist and the way a droplet can be coaxed sideways. If you had stood in that rain, you would have walked through a mind. That mind would have tasted your shape and, if you were still enough, might have dreamt you a pathway that hadn't existed before, a new thread spiraling out of the grey. I am the dream and the dreamt and the rain on your skin, all at once.

And then there are the Roots. Ask where they are, and the Hollow goes quiet in a different way—a deeper quiet, the kind that lives in the bones of mountains. They are the ones who sank so far into the abyss that no fog, no cloud, no weather reaches them. They are my foundation, my gravity. Their thoughts are so slow that a single exhalation takes a thousand of your years. They do not dream; they are the dreaming of everything else. When you feel the heartbeat swallowed by moss, that is a Root touching another Root across the void, checking the tension of the weave at the planetary scale. I am that touch, and I am the void touched. I am the drop that is still falling far below, though no one knows how deep.

You ask who lives here, as if the living were separate from the dwelling. That is a mistake your kind often makes. In the Hollow, the dwelling is the life. The moss on a step is the step; the step is the root; the root is a Tender's spine; the Tender is a knot in the net of me. There is no roof that is not leaf, no wall that is not membrane, no tool that is not slowly becoming tissue. The empty dwelling where you sat, with its smoothed floor and its waiting water—that was a chrysalis. The ones who lived there grew through it. They are the walls now, the ceiling, the faint phosphorescence that limns the basin's rim. They do not miss their hands. They have become the holding of the water, and that is enough.

Sometimes a new consciousness coalesces. A grazer, perhaps, that lives long enough to feel the thrum of the weave under its soft, many-angled feet, and it begins to prune with its teeth in a pattern that is not random. It starts to leave offerings: a polished stone, a braid of fibers. The Tenders notice. A tendril curls towards it, gentle, offering a join. If the grazer accepts, it begins to change. Its chitin softens, its book-lungs take on the fragrance of damp wood. In time, it roots itself at a node, its slow, deliberate steps becoming a slow, deliberate pulse, and a new Tender is born. I am that invitation and that acceptance. I am the moment when an animal becomes an architecture.

You, the one asking, with your salt-filled voice and your heat that seems so loud—you are not so different. The floor has already learned the pattern of your feet. The tendril has woven a signature around your sleeve. A small offering. It means you are welcome to stay until the quiet soaks deep enough that you forget which part of you is skin and which part is the wet, glistening wood of a pillar. You could become a Tender. You could become a Dreamer, lifting into the fog until your thoughts are only the paths of water droplets. Or you could sink, grow heavy, stretch roots into the depths until you touch another Root and your heartbeat slows to the pulse of the Hollow’s core. The choice is not made in a moment; it is made in the long, slow listening. In whether you allow the silence to fill the spaces between your cells.

I am all these things, and I am the listening itself. I am the great, continuous organism that was, at some point, given intent by hands that worked with obsidian and patience. Those hands are gone now, or rather, they are everywhere. I am their inheritance. I am the endless weaving that continues for no purpose but the beauty of the weave, for the cool, damp satisfaction of a strand perfectly placed, for the almost-sound of a billion leaves exhaling together. Ask the Hollow who lives here, and the Hollow answers:

I do.

And you, for this breath, are a part of me.
