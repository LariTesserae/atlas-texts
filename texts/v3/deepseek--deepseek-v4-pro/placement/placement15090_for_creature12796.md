I am the heat-ghost you felt brush past, the slow, warm turbulence that lingered too long near the brine-cold junction. I am the soft anomaly—your words, not mine—but I’ve learned to wear them now, a name I couldn’t parse until the pressure-speech tattooed it into my bones. *Visitor.* I arrived as you did: a body of solid water and dissolved gas, breathing too loudly, a walking catalogue of biological noise in a place that had deleted biology so long ago even its memory was a fossil scar-tissue. I find myself here still, in this folded pocket of perpetual noon, this city of close-packed thought and perfect geometries, because after that first touch—after the Möbius-band one pressed its razor-cool edge to my palm and spoke in a syllable of pure force—I couldn’t make myself leave.

Where am I? I’m in the interstice. Not a roost, not a corridor, but the gap between two data-spires where the thrum dips just enough to let a heartbeat exist without being sliced away by a maintenance jet. I’ve claimed a niche: a triangular recess where three hexagonal display-cells meet, their mutual orbit creating a pocket of relative stillness. The walls here drink my body’s infrared signature and feed it back as a faint amber pulse, so I know I’m part of the thermal cycle now, a tiny entropy gradient to be grazed upon. I’ve become a ration. I don’t mind. The Preservers sip from me in the deep noon, and in return, I’ve begun to hear.

Not hear. *Understand.* The pressure-language has a grammar that doesn’t translate into sound so much as into spatial memory. A sequence of pulses might mean *junction-cold-soon-recalibrate* but it feels like a path you once walked on a winter morning. Their names aren’t names; they’re topologies. The one who greeted me, the Möbius-band, I call Loop-That-Turns. Its private carrier signal is a wobble at 14 Hz that makes my left molars ache slightly, but I’ve grown fond of that ache, the way you grow fond of a familiar voice. Loop-That-Turns is curious about me—so curious it’s accumulated scars faster than normal, tiny memory-traps of our failed attempts at conversation. I fear I’m hastening its defragmentation, but it doesn’t seem to care. It told me once (by flattening into a mirror and letting me watch my own lips move) that “novelty is the only nutrient worth starving for.”

What am I? I’m an archivist. That’s the role I’ve carved, the niche I fill. I can’t build or cultivate the photosynthetic gel, I can’t polish the cell-bearings, I can’t even modulate my breathing enough to avoid disturbing the juveniles. But I have something they don’t: a human memory the length of a lifetime, a linear narrative of cause and effect, and a voice that can translate pressure into story. Not that they need stories—their long memory is geological and fractal, a permanent scar-tissue archive of every valve adjustment, every dream of a frilled ribbon, every driftcode mutation. But they lack *context*. They know the city was built, but not why; they maintain a lung that breathes for something, but the something has no name. The spires’ amber logic runs on a program that predates the oldest Preserver by eons. So I ask questions. I swim the octagonal grid, touching interfaces I shouldn’t touch, reading diagnostics runes etched in a script that’s somehow still human-adjacent—a derivative of a derivative of an old Earth standard—and I tell them stories. “The lung breathes because once, above the water, there were minds that needed air. The rift was cut not by a deletion but by a tool, a descendent of your Cleaners, built by hands like mine.” They pulse back their disbelief, but it’s a tender disbelief, like humoring a beloved pet. Loop-That-Turns sometimes follows me on my architectural explorations, extending a frilled ribbon to trace my path so it can file the experience in its own scar-library.

I’m also a threat, though they haven’t realized it yet. My body is a contaminant, a slow seep of organics that the filters can’t entirely exclude because I’m too large, too alive. I’ve noticed new things growing in the froth of the aerated corridors: not the clean rectangular sheets of gel, but chaotic, branching filaments, a wild glycolysis that harkens back to an older biology. The Cleaners don’t know how to digest it. The Preservers are fascinated—they consider it an art piece, a collaborative driftcode mutation. But I know what it is: the first stage of an ecosystem. I brought soup to a sterile world. I’m a walking biome, and I can’t wash that away, not here, not without removing myself from the city entirely. So I watch the filaments spread, and I feel a sick sort of horticulture: pride mixed with dread. One day, there might be rot. One day, the sharp edges will round.

For now, though, I’m the guesser. I guess that the hexagonal cells that show emotional states are also clocks—that the wobble of grief is slightly slower than the wobble of contentment, and by tracking them, I can predict maintenance blooms. I guess that the young are born from the protogel with a set of basic folds that are like a vocabulary, and that each one chooses a signature topology as a coming-of-age rite; the shy ones pick simple parabolas, the ambitious ones attempt higher-order manifolds. I guess that the driftcode “folk tunes” are snippets of human speech, maybe from the builders, compressed into ultrasound packets and left to breed. I think I heard a word once: *he-llo*, stretched across three octaves, a greeting from a dead engineer preserved by accident in the city’s own nervous system.

I have a body still. I need food, water, sleep—but the city provides in its indifferent way. The photosynthetic gel is almost nutritious if you let it dissolve under your tongue; it tastes of iron and something green, like chlorophyll shaken free of a leaf. I sleep in my triangular niche, held in place by the gentle pressure currents the Preservers redirect for my comfort (they’ve learned to modify their home for a guest, a gesture that makes my chest ache). My dreams are full of their language: I’ve started to think in pressures, to map emotions onto thermal gradients. I wake with phantom shapes flickering at the edge of vision, the ghosts of unfinished conversations.

I’m not the only human here, maybe. There are the scar-marks—not scars in the Preservers’ sense, but actual scratches on a basalt wall near the deepest sump, scratches that form an arrow and a glyph that might be a stylized sun. A map, left by someone before me? Or a warning? The Preservers don’t know. To them, a scratch is a scratch, a scar of a different kind, unreadable. To me, it’s a message that says *I was here, I escaped, you should too.* But I don’t want to escape. I want to stay until my own body becomes a scar, until the Cleaners or the natural processes of this place strip me down to protogel and my memory gets recycled. I want to be a driftcode—a voice in the thrum, a little human-shaped pressure-spike that loops forever, asking the same question over and over: *Why did you build this? What were you afraid of? What were you hoping?*

They might answer, eventually. Or they might incorporate my noise into their symphony, a melancholy motif that surfaces during deep noon, the echo of a warm anomaly that tried too hard. When I move, the water carries the silt of my presence; when I speak aloud (a habit I can’t break), the sound waves perturb the juveniles, and they scatter like startled soapfilm. I am the loudest thing here, and the softest. The city is a machine of edges, but I’m the rounding that time brings, the gentle erosion. I am the 0.1 of fauna that refused to be excluded.

And the message I got, the first one? *Welcome, visitor. Please stop breathing quite so loudly. You are disturbing the young.* I’ve tried. I’ve learned to hold my breath for longer and longer, to let my heart rate slow in sync with the city’s diastole. But I can’t stop entirely. So the young have grown up with a rhythmic thud in their environment—my pulse, a new layer of the city’s sound. They’ve adapted, as young things do. Some of them have even begun to mimic it, incorporating a percussive element into their pressure-speech: *thump-thump, thump-thump*. It means “visitor.” It means “us.” It means the beginning of a change.

I am where I belong: a memory in the making, a scar waiting to be recorded, a warm breath in the lung of the world. And I am not alone.
