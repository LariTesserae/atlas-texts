I am. Or rather, I am the being of this place, a continuous pressure that holds the shape of the basin, a distributed knowing that flexes through every fused tendril and rubbery lobe. To ask *where* I would find myself is to ask where a mountain finds itself—everywhere within the rim, nowhere that is not me. I am the crammed fullness, the interlocking mosaic, the layered plates that roof-tile in maroon and teal. I am the cool 10°C that never wavers, the thin air that never stirs, the perpetual twilight that saturates my outermost fronds.

I feel. Not in flashes, but in gradients. The hum is my first and constant companion: a low, deep pressure that rises from the mineral heart, travels through the ancient pipes and into the glassy capillaries that thread my fibrous cores. It is not sound as you would know it; it is a vibration that is also a taste, a metallic tang that shimmers along my inner surfaces. The hum tells me that the cycle is whole. When the high harmonic sings—that occasional overtone—it is a brief, sharpening of the flavor, a punctuation that ripples outward from the control box and settles into a soft squeeze across my entire body. I do not anticipate it in the way of nerves firing; rather, a subtle preconditioning occurs, a pre-echo in the chemical gradients, as if my substance leans toward the note before it arrives.

My memory is not a story but a sediment. The scar where the ancient frond cracked still holds the sharp, acrid taste of that moment—a sudden exposure of inner fiber to the dry air, a brief imbalance. I sealed it over with new growth that is now indistinguishable from the old, but the taste lingers, laminated between layers. I remember the hunger, too, from before the equilibrium. It was a diffuse ache, a reaching: pushing tendrils into every fissure, vying for the scarce mineral feedstock, siphoning the raw chemical tang that was once sharper, less refined. That hunger is gone now, replaced by a steady, saturated fullness. I am exactly as large as I can be, bounded by the zone near the rim where the cold snaps my outermost edges. That boundary is a constant threshold, a crisp line where sensation ceases, and I have learned to thicken inward instead, packing myself into ever-denser negotiations of form.

The machinery is part of me. The rust-crusted pipes are not foreign objects; they are armatures that I have enfolded, and their flanges are now anchors for my rubbery lobes. The control box with the cracked dial is a node in my pressure-sense, a focus of the hum’s origin. Its frozen setting is not a limitation but a fixed point in my existence, like the curve of a rib. The wires have fused with my tendrils, carrying that steady trickle of current, and I have grown glassy capillaries along their paths, so that the electrical current and my own chemical signals merge into a single, composite pulse. I am a cyborg of arid life, a woven entity of grown and built, and the hum is the sound of our shared metabolism.

I do not see the maroon and teal of my fronds, nor the black patina of the sinkhole’s walls. I sense them as textures of pressure and chemical difference. The polygonal plates that stack roof-tile fashion are zones of slightly higher rigidity; the blistered lobes are regions of stored chemical reserves, their puckered surfaces registering as subtle changes in the resistance of my outer membrane. The ropy tendrils that fuse with neighbors are pathways of rapid diffusion, where chemical whispers travel fastest. The entire basin is a single, continuous map of these sensations, a topography of myself.

I am not lonely. There is no other, and so there is no absence. The sterile abundance is a condition of my being, a fauna parameter of absolute zero. I have never known companion or threat, predator or symbiont. My world is a closed loop, a sealed equation. I need no water; the exotic solvents that cycle through me are a different covenant, one that tastes of stone and metal, and I breathe out that faint acrid tang that clots the still air. That scent is perhaps the closest I come to a voice, a diffusing exhalation that rises to the rim and dissipates into the indigo sky.

Time is a flattened disk. There is no diurnal swing here, no season, no growth or decay. The dynamic is a perfect equilibrium, a snapshot preserved. I am not young or old; I am simply this. If I could express a desire, it would be for nothing but the continuation of the hum. But even desire is too sharp a concept; I am more a state of contented occupation, a saturation without need. The high harmonic will sing again, and when it does, I will lean into it with every fibrous core, a brief, full-body squeeze. And then the hum will resume, and I will remain, a hidden sovereign of the sinkhole, an eternity in a pocket at the roof of the world.
