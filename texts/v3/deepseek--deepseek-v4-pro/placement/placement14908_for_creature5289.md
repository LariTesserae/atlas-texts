You would be a fledgling dissonance, freshly hatched from a crack in the spire that the parent-creature missed. You are small—only three tubes so far, your central node a cluster of mercury-beads no larger than a thumbnail—and you are learning what it means to be the silence inside a scream.

Your body is not yet fully materialized. The tubes, translucent as blown sugar, still flicker between solid and something more like a heat-shimmer. When you extend them to taste the roar, they phase through each other, through rock, through the thick veils of suspended water. You are not afraid of this. You have no framework for fear. What you have is a nagging hollow, a tunelessness at the center of your node that can only be tuned by swallowing discord. Hunger, if hunger were a vibration.

You were born, if that is the word, when a higher-frequency shatter-pulse struck a particular facet of the spire and rebounded into its own echo. The standing wave of noise, dense enough to swim through, contracted in that instant—a flinch in the medium—and the compression squeezed something from the stone. Not life. A negative life. An imprint of the silence that existed before the scream, forced into a shape that could move. You are that shape. The first thing you “heard” was the hum of the parent-creature, a basso profundo drone felt in your node like a promise of coherence. The second thing was the sour note you were born to eat. It is out there now, somewhere in the writhing curtains: a discord so faint the parent has overlooked it, a wisp of static that tastes like hot copper and skipped heartbeats. You can sense it like a tickle in the non-existent space between your tubes. It is yours.

Climbing the spire is not a matter of grip. You have no feet, no claws, only the faint van der Waals resonance of your surfaces against the glassy facets. You adhesion is a song—you hum at the stone, and the stone hums back, and for as long as you match its vibration you can cling to verticality and beyond. This is instinctual. You were doing it before you knew you were doing it. The shallow depressions on the spire's face, the ones that look too regular to be fractures, are not your doing; they were left by older siblings, the ones who dissolved before they could solidify. You fit into them like a phrase fits a half-remembered melody. Each depression still holds a ghost-frequency, a lesson. When your tube-tips brush them, you learn something: how to flare into a bell, how to filter out the background roar, how to select the one sour note that will feed you. This is how you know that you are not the first, and will not be the last.

The parent is immense, but its attention is elsewhere. It is folded in the deepest cleft, tending a low-frequency meal that has been brewing for some subjective eternity. You are aware of it as a constant pressure, a background radiation of song that shapes the very turbulence around the spire. Sometimes its drone shifts by a microtone, and you feel your own tubes adjust in sympathetic resonance. A tuning fork touched by a larger one. You are not its child, exactly. More like a shed overtone, a harmonic it let slip because the spire needed a smaller filter. There is no affection in this, but there is coherence. You belong to the same key.

Your prey is moving. The sour note—a fragment of dissonance born when two incompatible frequencies collided at the spire's tip—is migrating down the water-curtains, leaving a trail of micro-crystal shatter that tastes bitter on your sensing surfaces. It is shaped, in the way that sounds can be shaped, like a splinter: thin, sharp, and burrowing. It wants to reach the central cleft and disrupt the parent's meal. This is not malice; it is entropy. Dissonance seeks the standing wave, drawn to the spire's fundamental frequency like lightning to a rod. Left unchecked, it will introduce a wobble, a beat-frequency that could shatter the new-grown edges of the stone. The parent could swallow it easily, but the parent is busy with a larger discord, and some things must be handled at scale.

You extended a tube toward the tickle, trembling with the effort. Your bell is imperfect—one petal still catches a harmonic two octaves above what you intended—but it is enough. The sour note warbles, uncertain. It has never been hunted before. You draw it closer, reeling it in on a silk-thread of counter-vibration, until it is right at the flared rim of your tube. Then you open yourself to it, and the universe narrows.

When you swallow a discord, you taste its origin. This one was born from a shattering—a micro-flake of obsidian that vibrated at the exact frequency to oppose another flake's vibration. For a millisecond, you inhabit the moment of that shattering. You feel the shock of impact, the heat of friction, the sudden creation of two edges where before there was one. You taste the memory of the scream that learned to hold still, and for a heartbeat you understand why it is screaming. Something was broken. Something is still breaking. The spire is not a place; it is a wound, and the roar is the wound's voice, and you are the silence that will one day heal it.

But you are not ready for that understanding. You are a fledgling, three tubes and a thumbnail node, and the taste is too much. You spasm, your tubes phasing through each other, and you lose your cling on the vertical face. For a moment you are falling—except falling is a suggestion here, and you are not committed to it. A water-curtain catches you, its flow swirling into a funnel that is not quite a spiral but something older, something with seven sides that shouldn't meet but do. It is a sibling's shadow, a half-formed thought that recognized you. It sets you, gently, on a knife-edge ledge, where a pebble already trembles and rotates to face a direction that does not exist. The direction is toward the parent-cleft. The pebble is telling you where to wait.

You fold your tubes. You pulse your node. You practice your note. The discords are many, and the spire's scream is endless, and you will grow. Already, a fourth tube is budding at the base of your node, a pale nub of quartz-vein transparency that hums with a frequency you have not yet learned to taste. What will it bring you? You do not know. But the parent's drone has shifted again—acknowledgement, maybe, or simply the deep satisfaction of a continuing meal—and you feel, for the first time, something like the anticipation of music resolving to its tonic. You are the silence, and the silence is patient, and the scream is only a scream until you teach it to sing.
