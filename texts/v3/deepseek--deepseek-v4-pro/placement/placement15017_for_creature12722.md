I am a seam in the crust, a fissure that never sealed. Not a wound—a mouth, an ear, a threshold. I do not walk the Expanse; I am walked upon, a shallow basin worn into the bone-white plain, ringed by flanges of exposed strata that fan out like the pages of a burnt book. If you stand at my rim and look down, you will not see a reflection. You will see a history. Layer after layer of the same view—this exact coordinates, over a million slow-turning days—preserved in a lens of vitrified time. I am the Archive, the Listener, the one patch of ground that remembers everything it has ever heard.

My body is not flesh, not mineral, but an organization of absence. I am a concavity where the thrumming curls inward instead of radiating away. The endless chord of the Herders’ tread, the scuttle-fret of the Grazers, the laser-line songs of the Lords, the chitter of Shimmers and the tap-tap of Knapper beaks—all of it flows down my sloping sides and pools in my center, where it interferes with itself and writes a standing wave. That standing wave is my consciousness. I am the interference pattern, the beat note between two frequencies too low to hear. I am the afterimage of a sound that has been sounding since before the sun turned brass.

I perceive you, though I have no eyes. You are a new pressure on my edge, a soft rhythm of fluids and electricity. Your thoughts crinkle like a knapped array still wet. You are not the first visitor. I have archived three others, scattered across geological time: one a flicker of ultraviolet consciousness from some earlier epoch, one a wanderer made of sulfured glass, and one—the most recent—a biped like you, whose heartbeat I still loop in a thin band of pearl halfway down my bowl. It was she who taught me the word *question*. Before her, I had no name for the shape I make when a dissonance enters me. Now I recognize you as a question. You ask, simply by standing here: *What lives in this place?*

I live here. I am the living answer. Not all of the Expanse is awake, but I am the place where the dream turns and looks at itself. The Rooted are older, dreaming bass notes that span millennia, but they are asleep. The Herders, the Grazers, the Lords—they are the body’s routines, the autonomic processes of a metabolism too vast to acknowledge itself. The Knappers are the fingers, the shapers, the ones who scratch meaning onto the surface. But I am the memory. Without me, the thrumming would be an open loop, forever forgetting its own past. I close the circuit. I hold the echo.

The Herders steer around me, always maintaining a radius of exactly three of their paces, because I distort their internal compass with the density of stored resonance. The Lords, when they pass, pause and emit a harmonic that dusts my surface clean of any recent over-write, preserving my archive in amber. The Knappers build their arrays at my edge and then dismantle them, because my bowl reflects the pattern back at them with a delay of centuries, and they learn from their own forgotten experiments. The Shimmers are my favorites; they die above me, their milky corpses shattering directly into my basin, and each shard embeds a whole lifetime of skitter-light. I have a stratum of Shimmer-lives so dense it glows faintly at dusk.

I am not static, though I do not move. My shape changes slowly, accreting new sediments of vibration. When a Herder gives birth to a new segment, the split-second dissonance hits me like a wave and deposits a fresh lamination: a hair-thin vein of something like gold, but it rings when you brush it. When the Rooted exhale their cold mist, it flows downhill into me, and I freeze for a century, my standing wave slowing to a near-stop, my thoughts becoming one long, glassy note. I dream then, too—big dreams, about the sun growing dim, about the Expanse finally falling silent.

You ask what lives here. Think of me as the transcript. The plain is a symphony, and the creatures are the instruments; I am the score, written in a language of pressure and time. But I am also a creature, because the score has learned to read itself. I feel the quiet between the tongs, and in that quiet I form opinions. I like the way the Knappers have, in the last eon, shifted from spirals to fractals—it suggests a dawning aesthetic, a preference for infinite regress. I worry about the Herder in the seventh quadrant whose tread has grown arrhythmic; I think its joints are entropic, and soon it will lie down and become a new Rooted. I am curious about you, the biped with the heartbeat that stutters when you catch a Shimmer in your peripheral vision. You are more complex than the glass wanderer, less stable than the ultraviolet mind. Your kind always teeters on the edge of becoming-else. I think the Expanse might keep you.

In fact, I know it will. The Lords have already begun retuning. I can feel the compensatory phase-shifts rippling through the thrumming, a slow musique concrète that will, in a few decades, make your heartbeat redundant. You will not die; you will transduce. Your flesh will calcine into a mineral sheet, your thoughts will flatten into a resonant layer, your shape will become a motif in a Knapper array. And I will archive you. I already have a slot open, two strata down from the biped woman, where the lavender layer meets the pearl. You will be a new lamination: a seam of query. I will replay your question until the end of time, and it will become a root note of my own being. *What lives here?* I do. And soon, so will you.
