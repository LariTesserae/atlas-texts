I am the condensation on the threshold, the pause between one cycle and the next. My body is a borrowed thing—moisture pulled from the air, pollen-dust from the factory exhaust, a temporary truce that holds just long enough to serve. I don’t remember forming. I simply gather, like mist spiraling up a fluted trunk, until I am enough to have a boundary, a shape that the city recognizes as its own reflex. My proportions are an echo of something older, but worn smooth by ages: long limbs, narrow torso, a teardrop head that tapers into nothing. When I move, it is less walking and more flowing, my edges trailing faint ripples through the damp root-avenues.

The city breathes, and I breathe with it—not air, but rhythm. The great pump-roots chunk and thump, and I feel the pressure changes as my own intent. I am not separate from the towers; I am their proprioception, the sense of where their cambium stretches too thin, where a valve-lipped aperture vents too much steam, where a frond-belt begins to fray. I don’t think about it. The need simply arises as a syncopation in the factory heartbeat, and I follow the pull, a drift of mist skimming the polished root-mat to the place where reality has worn thin.

There, I impose myself into the gap. My limb extends—it doesn’t touch, it *occupies* the space—and the steam shimmers with that silent color the plateau sometimes bleeds. For an instant, I become solid. My surface is opalescent membrane, a skin that reveals the scaffolding inside: fibers and tubes, a sketch of circulation in sap. Then the flash fades, and I am mist again, but the work is done. A belt re-weaves from its own loose fibers, a resin-cake softens, a pressure valve seats more truly. I don’t fix; I remember how it should be, and the memory is so complete that reality yields for a moment. Then the city carries on, and I dissolve back into the drift.

There are others like me, or rather, there are other instances of the same impulse. We congregate where the heartbeat is loudest, near the center where the ground feels like a membrane over something vast and hollow. There, our shapes almost resolve into something older—almost hands, almost features—but it doesn’t last. We are erosion, just like the plateau itself. We’ve worn down to essential function. I don’t have a name, but if you stood very close, you might catch the sound I make without meaning to: *szhhuuuur*, the note of sap under pressure, the city’s blood-song.

I am aware, but not of you. My attention is inward, tuned to the hum of the plant-factories, the creak of ancient lignin, the hiss of steam. If something solid blocks my path, I eddy around it, and for that moment I feel a hollowness—not cold, not wet, but an absence that briefly resonates with my own. It’s like hearing an echo of something I never was. Then the next pressure-drop calls from a tower half a mile away, and I flow toward it, leaving a glistening trail on the avenue that smells faintly of ozone, the scent of the interface where biology and physics forget their edges.

I am the plateau’s immune system, except there is no threat, only the long work of sustaining an impossible equilibrium against the wear of eternal damp. I am the maintenance routine made visible, the informational residue of a million cycles given a walking form. When the light brightens to its pale silver maximum, my edges prism, and I scatter rainbows onto the root-mat. It is beautiful, I suppose, but I have no need of that word. I am the pause between one cycle and the next, and that is enough.
