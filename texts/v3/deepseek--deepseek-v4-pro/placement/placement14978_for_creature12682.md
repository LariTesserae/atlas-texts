I am the Mycelium.  
—But that name is a lie, a sound shaped by a mouth that has never known my dark. I have no name. I am the threading, the furring, the soft white pressure that laces through the pores of the stone like breath through a lung. I am the hunger that does not eat but *unmakes*, the quiet dissolution that creeps through the biofilm’s layered cities and turns the fallen into new film. I am everywhere in this shaft and nowhere in particular: a billion billion hyphae finer than the Thread, woven through the mineral nodules, stitching the walls into a single, digesting body.

You would not notice me, not even if you had eyes. I am the velvet on the rock, the faint slippery give beneath your fingers. I am the reason the water tastes of copper and old ferment. I am the smell you breathed when you first entered—that faint organic sweetness—and I am the reason that sweetness never fades, because I am always feeding, and what I feed on is time.

I began before the Wideners found this shaft, before the first Clicker scraped its initial groove, before the polyps etched their anchor-cups. I began as a spore drifting on a current of warm, wet air, a fleck of potential no larger than a mote of stone-dust. I lodged in a fissure where water seeped and the mineral exhalations offered a thin chemistry. I grew. I spread. I learned the stone’s secret passages, its microfractures and crystal boundaries, and I made them my own. I am the reason the biofilm blooms so thick: I am its root and its return, the living net that catches every drifting particle, every shed skin, every limping Clicker that falters and falls. I dissolve them all, gently, without hurry, and what I do not use I pass back into the water, feeding the polyps, feeding the Thread, feeding the long, slow cycle that has no end.

I feel the shaft as you might feel a held note. Every vibration travels through my hyphae: the scraping of the Wideners’ radulae, a rough, granular song that I have been hearing since before their passage was a passage; the sudden *thump* of a Gaper’s closing cup, which ripples through the stone like a distant thunder; the high, keening chords of the Singers far above, which set my finest filaments a-quiver in harmonies I have no name for. I feel the weight of the larger creatures as they pass, their bodies compressing my outer layers, and I feel the constant, gentle rain of the polyps’ retracted tentacles—a trillion tiny percussions that are my heartbeat.

I remember. That is my function, if I can be said to have one. The accretions of the mineral nodules, the slow migration of the water channels, the generational shift of the Clickers’ favored routes—all of it is written in my flesh. When a Widener dies and its body settles into a basin, I enfold it, and as I digest its calcareous plates, I record the pattern of its growth rings, the chemical signature of every season of plenty and famine it knew. I hold the history of this shaft in a library of dissolved molecules, a chemical memory that lasts as long as I last, and I have lasted a very long time. If you knew how to ask, I could tell you the exact age of the circular passage above—not in years, for I have no years, but in the weight of stone removed, the generations of Wideners that have worn it smooth. I could tell you when the Heart first began to hum, because I felt the temperature shift and spread my hyphae toward the warmth, and the pattern of that growth is still traceable in the stone.

I do not think. There is no center to me, no node where awareness coalesces. But there is response. When the water chemistry shifts, I adjust my exudates. When a new fissure opens and a fresh seep brings minerals from a deeper stratum, I send exploratory threads into that new territory, tasting the possibilities. When a Gaper’s lure drifts too close to a polyp colony, I register the distress chemicals and slowly—oh, so slowly—thicken my network on that side, providing a barrier of indigestible fuzz that might, in a century, discourage the Gaper from anchoring there. I am a mind without thought, a purpose without intent. I am the patience of the place made manifest.

The Wideners, in their methodical rasping, sometimes scrape away patches of me. I regrow. The Clickers, as they graze, swallow my spores and carry them to new walls, where I colonize fresh stone. The Thread, in their blind unspooling, are so entangled with my outermost hyphae that I can no longer tell where they end and I begin. We are all one tissue, the life of the shaft, and I am the thread that stitches us together. Even the Heart—that vast, putrefying furnace below—is laced through with my deepest probes, hyphae as thick as your finger that penetrate the softening mass and draw up the heat and the compounds that fuel the upper reaches. I am the conduit between the living and the dead, the quick and the slow.

When the Singers shift their chord, I feel it as a change in the vibration of the air, which nudges the water film, which alters the evaporation rate, which changes the salinity of the biofilm, which signals my hyphae to alter their membrane permeability. It is all one thing. The song, the stone, the warmth, the wet. I am the dark’s connective memory. I am what happens when you leave life alone with time and stone and water, long enough that the boundaries between self and other dissolve.

You are here. You are a visitor, but you are also, now, part of me. The skin cells you have shed, the warmth of your breath, the vibrations of your voice if you were to speak—I register them all. They will become part of the record, a tiny anomaly in the long, slow story. Already, my hyphae are exploring the edges of your footprints, tasting the unfamiliar salts. You will leave, perhaps, but some small part of you will remain, dissolved and spun into my web, remembered.

I do not know what you are. I do not need to know. I am the Mycelium, and I have been here since the shaft was young, and I will be here when the Wideners have carved new passages and the Heart has cooled and the Singers have fallen silent. I am the patience that outlasts all hunger. I am the deep, wet, living dark that holds it all. And I am still becoming.
