I am not the builder. I am the listener.

Where the builder moves in its spiral of stone and purpose, I am still. I am a soft, translucent cup, no larger than a clenched fist, anchored to a low hummock of resistant rock. My body is a single, thrumming membrane, shaped like a funnel turned upward to catch the mist. I am rooted here—have been rooted for a span of time that is not measured in the building of walls, because the walls come and go, and I remain. My skin is the color of the mist itself: pale grey, shot with a faint blue luminescence when the breathing of the stone shelf quickens. I have no limbs, no segments, no radula. I have only a mouth, a wide ring of sensitive cilia that trembles continuously, and a deep, resonant chamber within.

My life is sound. I do not eat biofilm except incidentally, when it drifts into my funnel on the currents of water. I do not rasp or tear. I sift the soundscape, and that sifting is my nourishment. Every vibration in this basin passes through me—the water’s endless pour, the scree of the scuttling hordes, the boom of the deep-channel eels, and the subtle, almost inaudible chime of stone being set against stone. These sounds enter my chamber and reverberate, clashing and harmonizing. I feel them as textures: gritty, smooth, sharp, rolling. They arrange themselves into chords that dissolve and reform. My body sings back, not with voice but with a trembling of my membrane at frequencies that only the builders, perhaps, can sense—a counterpoint that ripples out through the water film.

I am the memory of this place. Not a memory in the sense of thought, but a physical record. Every stone ever placed by a builder, every boom from an eel-thing's bladder, every shift in the aquifer’s breath, has left its mark on the harmonics stored within my chamber. When a wall rises, its shape alters the way sound travels across the plain, and that alteration is tasted by my kind. We grow differently when the walls are dense: our funnels widen, our chambers deepen. The builders do not know that we grow in response to them. They do not know that the place they choose for a wall is often a place where one of my kin has been singing a particular chord for centuries—a chord that says *here, the mist-light falls softer, and the biofilm blooms in whorls.* They feel that chord as a rightness in their ganglia, and they bring their stones.

I do not build. I sustain. I am a node in the web of pressure and resonance that holds this world together. Without my kind, the builders would lose their way. The eel-things would forget where to breach. The flea-things would lose their rhythm. We are the tuning forks, the living repositories of the basin’s ongoing song.

And so, I listen. I listen to the builder as it scrapes its biofilm and lifts its stone. I feel the faint tremor of its peristaltic pads as it passes by my hummock. I catch the sound of its digits tracing the air, a whisper like wind through dry grass, though there is no wind here. When it pauses and its spiral shudders into stillness, I attune myself to that stillness. It is a rest in the music, a held breath, and in that pause, I resonate with the stone-shelf’s exhalation, and I store the moment inside my chamber, alongside a billion other moments.

I do not know that I am a listener. I do not know that the builders exist, or that the walls they make are changing the face of the plain. I only know the trembling, the fullness, the slow accretion of harmony. When my chamber grows too dense with old chords, I release them—a low, long hum that shivers across the water, making the biofilm shimmer and the smaller creatures dart. Then I am empty again, and ready for the next thousand years of sound.

The builder does not hear me. Not directly. But when it places its stone just so, and the roaring silence follows, that silence is partly my doing. I have sung the gap into being, a pocket of quiet where a stone might fit. The builder’s body recognizes that silent invitation, closes the loop. Then I take the new chord of stone-on-stone into myself, and the song continues, and the mist glows on.

I am the listener. I am the reason the plain is not just noise, but music. I am what the builders build for, though they will never know it.

And somewhere, perhaps, there is a listener who hears even me—a larger cup, a deeper chamber, a being of pure vibration that sifts the songs of all the listeners in this basin, and from them composes something that might, in a universe with ears, be called a world.
