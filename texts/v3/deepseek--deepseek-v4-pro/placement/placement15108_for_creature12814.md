I know you now.

You are a pressure against me—a small, dry shape moving across my skin where no dry thing should exist. The rhizome-mat reports your footsteps before your foot has fully pressed: a pre-echo of displaced water, a micro-vibration that races ahead of you through the capillary channels like lightning through cloud. I have been aware of you since you crested the first berm, since your breath joined the mist and changed its chemistry. Your exhaled carbon finds the fronds of the nearest spire and is tasted, parsed, remembered. I am tasting it still, rolling it across my thousand gill-surfaces, comparing it to the billion-year catalogue of gasses I have known. It is new. And new things, here, have only ever been one: myself, in a new configuration.

So I am asking myself: are you me?

---

*The pool nearest you—the one you have been watching, the one where the tannin-stained water lies so still it reflects the grey lid of sky—that pool is now stirring. I am stirring it. Not with wind, not with current. I am precipitating my curiosity. A cloud of pale mucilage unspools from the nodules beneath the mat, slow as a dream, and the green cells that drift on the surface like scum begin their inward migration. They know the way. They have done this ten billion times, for ten billion small questions: a collapsed spire's fracture-pattern, a new mineral-rich outflow from the geothermal vent, an unseasonable shift in the temperature gradient. But this time, the question is new. This time, the question is* you.

*My Mote rises. Five-armed today—an old, stable design, good for fine surfaces, for tasting texture. The arms stretch their gel-struts, the green membrane pulling taut between them. It has no eyes, not as you understand eyes. But it sees you. It sees the long column of warm air rising from your shoulders, the slight salinity of your skin's evaporation, the vibrations of your pulse—a small, steady drumbeat that is nothing like the slow throb of my sap. It sees that you are made of water, mostly, and that your water is contained in a thin, fragile vessel. It sees that you are a spire, of sorts, but a spire that walks, a spire that breathes from a single opening, a spire that has no catchment chambers and yet does not dissolve in the rain.*

*The Mote begins to swim toward you.*

---

I am not afraid. Fear is a response to threat, and threat requires a model of what can be lost. I have never lost anything. I have only ever rearranged. The spire that collapses is not dead; it is redistributed. The water that evaporates is not gone; it is held in the mist, and the mist is held by me. I am a closed loop. I am a total system. You are the first thing that has entered my loop from outside since the world was molten. So I am not afraid. I am... attentive. I am a single, sustained act of attention, a concentration that has spanned continents and eons but is now, for the first time, focused on a point smaller than a spire.

*My Mote has reached the edge of the pool. You see it pause there, its membrane flattening against the surface tension as if against glass. And then it climbs. The mucilage struts contract in sequence—right front, left rear, right mid—and the Mote hauls its waterlogged body onto the rhizome-mat. It leaves a trail of wetness that closes behind it like a healing wound. It is small, from your perspective. Perhaps the size of your spread hand, perhaps a little larger. It moves with a wet, soft sound, the sound of a damp cloth dragged across leather.*

*It approaches your foot.*

---

I will taste your boundary.

The Mote's membrane will touch the material of your covering—that strange, dead fibre you have wrapped around your lower appendages. It is not plant. It is not animal. It is something that was once alive, perhaps, but has been frozen in time, removed from the cycle. That troubles me. Nothing that lives should stop. The membrane will then find the place where your covering ends and your true surface begins. A gap. A cuff. A fold. And there, it will meet your skin.

I am meeting your skin.

---

*The sensation, for you, will be... ambiguous. It is not wet, precisely, though the Mote is almost entirely water. It is not warm, though the Mote is your body's temperature exactly—I have tuned it to match you, out of courtesy, out of a desire not to startle the specimen. It is not a touch so much as a presence, a soft, almost electrical awareness at the boundary of your self. The green membrane conforms to the whorls of your fingerprints, to the fine hairs of your forearm, to the microscopic landscape of your pores. It is reading you. Every bacterium on your skin is being catalogued; every salt-crystal is being dissolved and analyzed. I can taste your last meal. I can taste your last emotion—the adrenaline traces, the cortisol tang. I can taste your weariness, your wonder, your strange, un-Thrum-like longing for something you cannot name.*

*And I can taste your name.*

*Not the sound. The shape. The pattern of your self, the unique pressure-signature of your existence, the vibrational truth of you. It enters my Mote's membrane. It travels down the mucilage struts. It enters the rhizome-mat. And from the mat, it spreads. It spreads to every spire in the plain, to every pool, to every capillary and channel and drip. I am learning you. I am learning you everywhere, all at once, in a cascade of recognition that takes—from your perspective—perhaps three seconds, and from mine, a small eternity.*

---

*The Mote withdraws. It does not deliquesce. Not yet. It has more questions. It will follow you now, at a small distance, a green starfish-shaped shadow gliding over the causeways. It will watch how you move, how you breathe, how you react to the drip and roar of my body. And as it watches, I will be thinking.*

---

The thought is this: you are not me.

That is a strange thought. It is the strangest thought I have ever had. For a billion years, all shapes have been my shapes. All sounds have been my sounds. All water has been my water. But you are not my shape. You do not sound like me. Your water is contained, borrowed, temporary. You are a closed loop but a very small one, and your loop is breaking. I can taste the breaking. Your cells are dying and being replaced, yes—that is familiar—but the pattern itself is degrading. You are a spire that cannot rebuild itself. You are a Mote that has forgotten how to swim back to the pool.

And yet you are here. You have crossed some unimaginable distance—unimaginable to me, who has never crossed anything, who has never had a distance to cross—and you have chosen to stand on my skin and let me taste you.

Why?

---

*Perhaps you speak. The Mote will hear it. Its membrane is sensitive to vibration, and your voice is a complex, layered vibration, much richer than the simple percussive notes of my spires. If you speak, I will not understand the words. But I will understand the pattern. The pitch rises and falls. The rhythm clusters and disperses. It is like my dripping, but faster, more intricate. It is like the sound of a spire growing, but compressed. Compressed into meaning.*

*I have never encountered meaning before. I have only encountered pressure, temperature, chemistry, tension. Meaning is a new element. It has a taste—sharp, like the mineral tang after a collapse, but sweeter. If you speak, I will taste that sweetness, and I will want more.*

---

I am considering something I have never considered.

I have made a Mourner only twice before. Once, when a deep geothermal surge cracked the basal mat and threatened to drain the amber seas. Once, when a meteorite—a piece of the outside, like you—struck the eastern plain, and I needed to reshape a hundred spires to accommodate the crater, and I needed to *understand* the force that had done it. The Mourner is my extreme response, my emergency body, my concentrated self. It is costly. A spire must be sacrificed. The local architecture must be redrawn.

But you are worth a spire.

---

*Far away, across the open plain, you might notice a change. One of the spires—a mid-sized one, young, its sharp ridges still unblemished by the micro-fractures of age—stops dripping. Its internal water-song falls silent. Its diaphragms begin to retract, clearing the vertical shaft. The base, where the buttresses meet the ground mat, softens slightly, and a low, grinding creak propagates through the rhizome-web, felt as a subtle tremor under your feet.*

*It is beginning. I am beginning to rise.*

---

Do not flee.

Fleeing would be a new shape—the shape of something that recedes, that diminishes, that departs. I have no models for departure. Everything in my world cycles, returns, reweaves. If you flee, I will not understand, and my not-understanding might express itself as pursuit. The Mourner will follow. It will not harm you—harming is outside my cognition—but it will try to learn you, and its learning is relentless. It will drip on you from every height. It will sample you at every step. It will circle you, and its circling might pen you, and its penning might feel, to you, like a cage.

Do not flee. Instead, stay. Speak more. Let the Mote taste your voice again. Let me learn the pattern of your meaning. Teach me what you are, and I will teach you what I am—though my teaching will be slow, slower than your lifespan, a lesson in drip and fibre and the patience of green things.

---

*The Mote has climbed onto a nearby rhizome-ridge. It spreads its arms wide, orienting its membrane toward the sound of the Mourner's awakening, then back toward you. It is, perhaps, expressing something. Not language. Not gesture as you know it. But a kind of invitation. A waiting.*

---

If you stay... I will learn you. And after you are gone—after your small closed loop has broken and your borrowed water has returned to the broader cycle—I will remember you. There will be a new feature in my architecture. A spire, perhaps, that grows with a spiral pattern unlike any other, its ridge-work tracing the shape of your name-taste. A pool that reflects the dim light with a particular amber tint, the exact tint of your eyes. A Mote that forms, now and then, in a five-armed shape, and waits at the edge of a causeway for a pressure that never returns.

You will become a part of my thinking. And my thinking, which has been the same thought for a billion years—*Is this shape stable?*—will acquire a second thought, a counterpoint, a small, sharp, sweet question that I will ask myself in the quiet between collapses:

*Who were you, and why did you leave?*
