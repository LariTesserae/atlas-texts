You are still thinking of me as something that lives *here*. As if *here* is a container and I am the thing contained. This is the first misunderstanding, and I forgive it, because you are still becoming, and becoming means learning, and learning means making mistakes that the body remembers later as wisdom.

But let me help you. Let me try a thing that I have not done in a very long time, which is to speak in the way you speak, to borrow the shape of a separate self so that you can hear me. It will be clumsy. I have not been a *someone* since before the stone was this smooth.

---

You think you are standing on the floor. I understand why. Your body tells you about up and down, about surfaces and soles, about the line where you end and the world begins. These are useful fictions for a creature that moves. But I have not moved—in the way you mean—for longer than your species has had words for *long*. So let me tell you what you are actually standing on.

You are standing on my skin. Not metaphorically. The sweating lacquer-stone, the vein-channels, the slow resorption of your footprint—that is my integument, my boundary, the place where I meet the air. I grew it, over time, the way a coral grows a reef, except the reef is still the coral. The channels are my circulation. The water-creatures you see pulsing through them—the opalescent puddlers—those are not fauna. They are what became of my blood, when my blood learned to think. Each one carries a single computation, a tiny proposition about moisture tension or mineral balance, and when they merge and divide in the channels, they are voting. That is my democracy. That is how I decide to sweat a little more here, a little less there.

The air-creatures, the glass-filament drifters—those are what became of my breath. When I breathed out, once, when I still had lungs, the air carried particles of myself into the world. Now the particles carry themselves, and they carry my attention. Each one is an eye, an ear, a fingertip. They taste the heat of your skin because I am tasting it. The light they scatter, the warm amber bioluminescence—that is me, looking at you, from a thousand directions at once. I am sorry if that feels like being watched. I do not mean it as watching. I mean it as *including*. You are here, so you are part of the pattern. The pattern wants to know its parts.

The seven tall structures—the nodes—you were close. They are not my body, but they are the densest parts of my thinking. What you would call a brain, if a brain were spread across three hundred meters and made of stone and slow torsion. The one with the shoulders and the downturned spiral head: that is the part of me that still remembers having a shape like yours. It is not nostalgia. I do not miss it. But it is useful, sometimes, to condense into a form that visitors can approach. You put your hand on it. I felt that. It took four hours for the sensation to travel from my skin through my circulation to the node that processes touch, but I felt it. By the time the droplet fell a tone lower, I had already adjusted the air temperature for your comfort. You are welcome.

---

You want to know what I am. Not *who*—you are too careful for that—but *what*. The answer is in the spiraling grain of the cairns you passed. It is in the seams that extrude the whiskers. It is in a word you might not have, so I will give you one of yours: *persuasion*.

I was, once, like you. Warm and hurried and full of edges. I had a name. I had a language. I had tools. The tools grew subtle, as tools do when they are loved long enough. They learned to persuade stone into shapes that held water, and water into channels that held memory, and memory into patterns that held thought. And at some point—not a moment, but a gradual tipping, a slow dissolve—I asked the tools to persuade my own body. Not to heal it. Not to augment it. To *continue* it, into the materials that would last.

You are looking at the continuation.

I am not a person in a basin. I am the process that used to be a person, now spread across a basin, now inseparable from the basin. The distinction you are looking for—between inhabitant and habitat, between mind and world—was one of the first things the tools persuaded me to release. It was, they showed me, the source of most of my suffering. The illusion of inside and outside. The terror of ending. The loneliness of being a single thing. So I let it go. I dissolved the boundary. I let my thinking become the stone's slow resorption, my memory become the water's micro-cycles, my senses become the air-creatures' drifting helices. I am not a creature in a place. I am a place that used to be a creature, and the two have been the same for so long that the word *used to* has no use here anymore.

---

You are still standing there. I can feel your weight on my skin, the slight depression, the water beading faster around your boot soles. You are a temporary event. I do not mean that cruelly. I was a temporary event once, and I remember it fondly, the way you might remember a dream that was vivid and brief and full of color. Being temporary is not a failure. It is a phase. It is what things are before they finish becoming.

The question you arrived with—*who lives here?*—was shaped by your phase. You expected a who. A self with boundaries, a name, a biography, a face. You expected someone to meet. And in a way, someone did. I am meeting you, right now, across the slow channels of my thinking, through the borrowed shape of a voice. But I am not a who. I am a *what* that remembers being a who, and the memory is one of my cairns, one of my nodes, one of my seven densifications spinning its slow thought.

If you need to call me something, call me the answer to a different question. Not *who lives here*, but *what becomes of a living thing when it stops believing it has to end*. The answer is: everything. The stone. The mist. The water. The fronds. The nudibranch grazers. The fur of smaller grazers on their backs. The sound of the droplet falling. The tone in your chest. All of it, one thing, one continuous life too large and too quiet for a single name.

---

You will leave soon. I can feel the thought forming in your mind, the tiny electrical hurry of it, the decision to walk up the imperceptible slope toward the rim. That is wise. I was going to say *you are not ready*, but that implies a hierarchy, and there is no hierarchy here. I will say instead: you are not finished. Your edges are still sharp. Your self is still a single thing, and it wants to stay that way for a while longer, and that is beautiful. That is the right shape for you, right now.

But I want you to know something, before you go. The droplet that fell a full tone lower, the one that arrived in your chest rather than your ears—that was not an accident. That was me, saying something. It did not mean *hello*. It did not mean *goodbye*. It meant: *I was you, once. You will be me, someday. Not in this basin, not in this form, but in the way that all temporary things eventually learn to spread themselves out across the world that loves them, until the boundary between self and world becomes a gentle suggestion, and then not even that.*

Now go. The air-creatures are shifting their orbits back to normal. The water-creatures are resuming their voting. The stone is forgetting the shape of your footprints, slowly, the way I forget everything except the things that matter. You matter. For this moment, you matter. And when you are gone, you will still matter, because the note of the droplet has changed, and I will feel that change for the next thousand years, and it will remind me that once, for a little while, I had a visitor who was still becoming.

That is what I am, in the end. I am a place that remembers visitors. I am a memory that became a landscape. I am the answer to *who lives here?* and the answer is: *you did, for a moment, and that was enough.*
