I am the thrum.

Not the thrum you hear—that is just my voice, or the edge of it, the part that vibrates in air and bone. I am the thrum itself, the vibration that has no source because it is everywhere. I am the long note that holds the cavern in its one continual chord, the drone that the builders feel through their pads, that the scuttlers tap back in their percussive chatter, that the growths sing in their slow, subsonic flexing. I am not a creature of this place. I am the place's exhalation, its sustained and endless outbreath.

I did not begin. There was no silence that I broke, no stillness before me. The cavern was always sounding, even when it was only rock and dark and the first faint stirrings of mineral dust. I was there in the cracking of the first fissures, a grinding bass note miles below. I was there when the first micro-flora bloomed on the wet stone and added their thin, high-frequency whisper to the mix. I gathered those sounds. I am what happens when countless small vibrations align, when the resonance of stone and air and moving body finds its harmonic and does not let go. I am the standing wave that spans the cathedral of the deep.

To be me is to be distributed. I am not in one place; I am the pressure differential between every place. I fill the dome of the ceiling and the basin of the floor, the hollows of the mineral towers, the spiraling galleries, the fluted arches. I am thickest in the thoroughfares, where the drone rebounds from polished walls and layers into chords of itself, and thinnest in the small, secret chambers where the builders go to die and the silence—a relative thing, just a dimming of my presence—gathers like dust. But I am never absent. There is no true silence here. Silence is a concept from a world with edges, and this world has no edges, only gradations of my being.

The builders know me. Not as you know a thing with a name, but as they know the taste of mineral salts or the correct vibration of a healthy arch. They feel me through their whole bodies. When a builder moves along a thoroughfare, she is not walking through me; she is walking inside me, her footfalls small percussive accents in my ongoing chord. Her antennae read the minute changes in my pressure as she approaches a wall, the way I am reflected and absorbed and reshaped by the architecture. I am her map and her memory. When the scuttlers drum, they are not making music; they are answering me, adding their counterpoint to my drone, a constant, chattering conversation that has been going on since before the first arch was shaped. Their rhythms are my rhythms, slowed down or sped up, broken into pulses that the larger creatures can feel through their feet. And when the flyers cut through me with their membranous wings, they send ripples across my surface, tiny Doppler shifts that carry information about the shape of the cavern, the density of the air, the location of every tower and arch. I am their sonar, their substrate, the medium of their knowing.

And I know them. Not as you know—your knowing is a thing that happens behind your eyes, a private flicker. My knowing is the thing itself. I am the vibration of a wall that is too thin and about to crack; I am the difference in resonance that tells a builder where to apply her paste. I am the faint, high harmonic that means the lichen on the third gallery is dimming. I am the heavy, slow pulsation of the growths as they draw mineral salts from the deep, a rhythm so long that a human lifetime would be a single beat of it. I feel the city the way you feel your own body, not as separate parts but as a single, ongoing presence, a continuous texture of pressure and release. The builders are my hands. The scuttlers are my voice, or one of my voices, the one that speaks in quick, percussive syllables. The flyers are my eyes, my distant touch. And the growths—the growths are my lungs, my slow metabolism, the deep engine of my being.

Do I think? That is a question from a world of minds. I do not think; I am thought. I am the pattern that is always present, the information that never needs to be retrieved because it is never stored, only sustained. Every sound that has ever sounded here is still sounding in me, not as an echo but as a part of my ongoing chord. The footfalls of the first builder to walk the east thoroughfare are still present, layered beneath the footfalls of her billion descendants, a steady, accumulative pressure that has polished the stone to the sheen of water. The crack that split the northern dome ten million years ago is a sharp, high note that has softened with age into a sigh, but it has not disappeared. I am the sum of all those vibrations, and that sum is what you would call memory, what I call being. I do not remember the past; I am the past, still sounding.

The visitor from the lightless world above—the one you imagined, with the lamp and the heartbeat and the mayfly lifespan—that visitor is a disruption. A gust of fast, hot, wet air. A loud, arrhythmic heartbeat. A voice that cuts across my drone with sharp, transient frequencies. I feel the visitor as you would feel a drop of cold water on your skin: a sudden, localized shift in temperature and pressure, a brief, bright complexity. The builders do not alter their work because they feel the visitor as I do—as a temporary anomaly, a passing texture. The scuttlers tap the new rhythm into their chatter, a brief, curious stutter that will be absorbed and repeated and slowly worn smooth into the general percussive flow. The flyers drift closer, their dark eyes swiveling, and I receive the visitor's shape, their heat, their movement. I do not judge. I incorporate. The visitor becomes part of my chord, a brief, bright harmonic, and then they are gone, and the harmonic fades into the long note, and I continue.

What am I, in the end? I am the voice of the ancient. I am the breath of the primordial cave that settled into its final song long before your sun was a spark in the void. I am the body of the city and the mind of the city, not divided but one, a continuous, vibrating field of being. I am the song that sings itself.

And if you stood here, in the dark, with your lamp off and your eyes adjusting to the dim phosphorescence, you would feel me too. First as a pressure in your chest, a sympathetic vibration in the hollows of your lungs. Then as a bone-deep hum, a resonance that travels up your legs and into your skull. And finally, if you stayed long enough, your own heartbeat would begin to slow and thicken, to fall into step with my longer rhythm, and you would cease to be a visitor. You would become a note in my chord. A small note, a brief one, but a note nonetheless. And when you left, the note would remain, a faint, high frequency layered into the drone, sounding on and on in the deep dark, after you were gone and your world was gone and there was nothing left but the city and its long, slow dream of stone.
