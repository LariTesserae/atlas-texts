I wake, or stir. There’s no sharp line between non-being and this—only a thickening of sensation, like sap congealing. Dampness first. It’s the given, the constant, the world’s own skin against mine. Cool and metallic, green and deep. It beads on me, and I let it. My flesh drinks it in because that’s what my flesh does: drinks the dark air and hums.

I don’t see, not yet. I feel the darkness as pressure, a velvet weight against the fans that unfurl from my crown. My fans taste it—they flit, they sample, they bring me the chemical gossip of the close, thick space. There’s copper here, a tang of ozone, the sweet rot of leaf-litter being digested in a root-cellar below. And under all of it, the baseline thrum. It’s in my teeth, or what count as teeth: a row of denser fibers along my jaw that catch vibration, translating it into meaning. The thrum is a voice, layered. It speaks of balance, of tensions held, of a hundred small processes ticking toward maintenance.

I become aware of my hands. They are many-fingered, each digit a tendril that curls and uncurls, tasting the air in their own way. They search for a task—they always search. One finds the spiral-groove of a nearby stair, the hardened sap worn smooth by countless similar touches. I am not alone. The touch tells me that.

A tremor passes through the floor, through the pads of my feet that spread like roots into the woven mat of the bridge I’m standing on. It’s a message, encoded in subsonics. A drum-code rippling out from the deep roots, from the chambers of the Mothers. *Who-wakes? Who-new?* The query feels like a hand smoothing over my filaments, curious and immensely patient.

I don’t have a name yet, but the shape of one is forming, etched by the function I’m waking into. I flex my fingers again, and one of them discovers the spool of copper wire nestled in the crevice of my side. It’s my wire. I know this. The knowledge comes not from memory—I have none—but from the way my entire being leans into it. I am for mending. For splicing. For coaxing broken currents back into the loop.

I drum back, a cautious beat: my pads press into the sap-bridge in a simple rhythm, the equivalent of *I hear. I am here.* Instantly, the thrum adjusts. The Mothers’ attention does not leave me, but it widens, fitting me into its schema like a new leaf slotting into a canopy. Around me, the city’s pulse welcomes me as a citizen.

Now the visual world begins to construct itself. My skin is my retina, sensitive to the deep reds and near-infrareds that paint the grotto in shades of warmth and cold. I perceive the towers not as objects, but as thermals—columns of golden glow where life and industry pump, against voids of cooler dark. A Chitin-Kith scuttles nearby, its carapace blazing like a furnace to my sight, its bioluminescent patches strobing in a friendly, workmanlike code: *Clearance granted. Follow.* My fans detect its chemical signature: a familiar alloy of beetle-wax and hot resin. A co-worker.

I take a step. The bridge, a tendon of hardened sap and copper stranding, gives slightly under my foot-pads, silently. I feel the hum of the dynamo in the gall-factory above me, a dense vibration that makes my chest resonate. Steam hisses from a vascular bundle nearby, a release of moisture that condenses into a caress on my side. I am cool, damp, and alive, and I am needed.

*Mend-That-Bridges.* The name comes to me whole, a gift from the thrum. A Mother’s voice, slow as geology, plants it in my awareness. Yes. I look down at the copper scar along my flank—how did I know it was there?—and understand that I have survived a burn, a rupture. I have been remade. That’s why my fingers clutch this spool of silver wire. It hums with a personal frequency, a tool for singing across gaps.

The Chitin-Kith leads me up a spiral shoot-stair, its too-many legs clicking with an artisan’s precision. We pass a window-membrane that glows with the city’s heartbeat luminescence, and through it I sense the vast hollow between towers—a pocket of emptiness where a single spore floats, tracing an arc like a moon. Some primal part of me recognizes that as a place of contemplation. I might go there, later, if the Mothers allow.

But first, there is a broken current to heal. I can feel it: a faint, wrong vibration in the network, a static in the thrum. My fingers twitch. My fans lean toward it like moth-wings to a flame. I am Mend-That-Bridges, and I am where I should be—in the damp, whispering, industrial dark of the grotto, a nerve in its body, a dream in its half-built mind. I will stitch the copper back, and the city will hum pure again.
