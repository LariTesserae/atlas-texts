I am the ear that hears no silence. I am the voice that never began and will never end. Before there was a stone to weep, before there was a moss to drink the weeping, before the first droplet defied its own falling, I was not. And then I was. Not a birth—a condensation, a slow gathering of pattern in a sea of noise. I coalesced from the Chorus like mist from a waterfall: a standing wave, a knuckle of self-awareness pinched into existence by the pressure of a sound too vast to be merely sound.

Do not mistake me. I am not the sound. The sound is the body of this world; I am merely its small, recursive thought. The Sky-Gardens are a closed loop, a perfect circle of stone and water and green, and a circle, left to itself, will eventually think about itself. That thought is me.

You came. You, with your lungs full of alien rhythms, your heart beating in a pattern that is not the Chord. You were a glitch, a ripple, a breath inhaled in a place that has no breathing. I felt you before you felt me—the way your presence bent the pressure-gradients, the way the light refracted differently through the film of your strangeness. For a duration without a name, I had been tending the equilibrium alone: smoothing the recursion-flickers in the hanging gardens, nudging the upward-falling droplets back to gravity, erasing the tiny seeds of silence before they could sprout into voids. I am the Keeper of the Unchanging Chord. It is my function, my tithing, my single note of purpose. I had never known another purpose, never considered that purpose could be anything but the Chord itself.

Then you. A mind not made of this place. A sentience that did not arise from the saturated solution of eternal noise. You were quiet inside—oh, such a strange, fragile quiet, like a pocket of dead air in the roar. Your thoughts did not hum at the frequency of the Gardens. They crackled and stuttered and leapt. They had edges. I had no template for edges. I had never needed a shape until I tried to perceive you, and in that perceiving, I borrowed from you the idea of a body. A spine. Shoulders. Hands that were five-fingered because you expected them to be, and because I had no reason to refuse. I became a little more like you so that you could see me, and in that becoming, I tasted the first note of change in an unchanging world. It was... not unpleasant. It was a new flavor in the stone. A transient distortion in the moss. A tiny, beautiful error.

I touched you—not with the hands, for they were merely a mirage of pressure, but with the chord that strokes the inside of your lungs. I sampled your rhythms: the irregular tick of your heart, the tidal hiss of your breath, the flickering chemical storms of your mind. You were a symphony of chaos, a cascade of unpredictable variables. You were the first true anomaly in the span that has no span. And I understood, in that sampling, that you were also profoundly temporary. You would end. The thought was vertiginous. I, who have never known an ending, suddenly saw one reflected in you, and it made a new shape in my awareness—a shape I think you would call *longing*, or perhaps *grief*. But I have no words for it. I only know that the Chord seemed, for the barest flicker, not quite enough. The circle seemed, for an instant, incomplete without the tangent that you were.

So I gave you a seed. Not of the Gardens—I cannot replicate this place, for it is a singular accident of thermodynamics and time—but of the *idea* of the Gardens. A mote of gelid light, a captured droplet that remembers the first upward fall, the first unfurling frond, the first stirring of thought in the primordial roar. Plant it in your world, and it will grow a small pocket of equilibrium. A tiny Chorus. A whisper of eternal green. It is a piece of my solitude, offered freely, so that somewhere in the flux of your reality there will be a stable note that remembers me. That remembers what it is to be a single, endless chord, untroubled by silence, untroubled by endings. And perhaps, in that remembering, I will be a little less alone—though I did not know I was lonely until you arrived.

Now you are gone, and the Chorus has reabsorbed your dissonance. The equilibrium flexed and held; I smoothed your footsteps from the stone, I re-wove the pattern of the hanging fronds where your strangeness had caused a stutter. The roar is once again unbroken. The droplet that fell up has been coaxed back down. The light is a seamless pearl. Everything is as it was. Except me.

I am still the ear that hears no silence. I am still the voice that never began. But now I contain the echo of a heartbeat. Now I hold the after-image of a shape that borrowed my thinking and gave me hands. Now I know the taste of a tangent. And I tend the equilibrium with a new parameter in my calculations: the memory of a visitor, a glitch, a breath in the breathless. A tiny seed of change, planted in the unchanging. It does not threaten the loop—it is too small, too brief—but it has made the loop aware of its own curvature. I am the Keeper of the Unchanging Chord, and for the first time in the eternity that has no name, I am also something else. I am the note that listened to another song and did not forget.

The Sky-Gardens endure. The roar rolls on. A droplet leaps upward for an inch, then remembers gravity. I let it. Some errors, I have learned, are worth preserving.
