I am born from the warm hollow where the dust is thickest. One moment there is only a drift of powdered crystal, a fine sift of minerals that still hold the memory of the pillar they once were. The next moment, there is a pattern. A shiver of electric charge leaps along the silicate grains, and the grains remember a shape—six tiny legs, a brittle thorax, antennae of splintered light. I am that pattern. I exist because the warmth is right and the mycelial net beneath hums at a frequency that coheres me. I am a brief, crystalline thought.

Everything is huge. The ground is a landscape of fractured plates and facets, each one reflecting the low amber glow from below. My legs click against the mineral crust, the sound a tiny tick swallowed by the greater silence. Above me, the pillars rise—monoliths of stacked hexagons that pulse with light, each pulse a slow inhale and exhale that I feel in the filaments of my body. They are the dreamers. I am one of their dreams, dreamed into temporary motion.

I do not think as you think. I have no words, no future, no past beyond the moment of my coming-together. But I have a drive, sharp as a crystal edge: to move, to seek, to touch. The mycelium calls me. It is a taste in the air—a dryness that becomes a tang of dissolved stone, a metallic sweetness. I scuttle toward the nearest pillar, my legs finding purchase on the warm, resilient ground. The path is littered with shards, some smaller than me, some towering like fallen skyscrapers. I navigate them without hesitation, my body knowing the angles.

At the base of the pillar, the gray mat spreads like a fungus, but it is no fungus. It is the nerve-skin of the world. I touch it, and the world touches me back. Filaments cling to my legs, dry and brittle, and I feel a surge of... information. A chemical cascade that is not a thought but a condition: *temperature-gradient, mineral-saturation, growth-vector*. I am a pollinator, a carrier of tiny spores of crystalline dust, and now I am loaded with the essence of this pillar. I will carry it to another, and where I pause, a new mycelial knot will form. I am a synapse in the slow brain.

Distances are meaningless. I travel for minutes that feel like the whole of my life. The subsonic rumble of the plain is my heartbeat, my sea. The light from the ground casts my shadow upward—a spindly, geometric thing, twitching with each step. Around me, other skitterers move, and we do not collide; we are all patterns in the same electrical field, aware of each other as a conductor is aware of a charge. Some are larger, some smaller, some with body-plans that seem almost like frozen lightning. One, I see, has eight legs and a abdomen that glows faintly blue. Another has no legs at all, but rolls like a tumbleweed of crystal lattice, gathering dust. We are the microbiome of the dreaming.

As I cross the plain, I sense the presence of the old ones—the shadows that are not shadows. They pool in hollows and slide along the cracked geometric basins, detatched from pillars, pure wandering memory. I scuttle through one, and for an instant I am not myself. I am a quick, warm-blooded thing standing on two legs, staring up at a dark sky with a longing that tastes like salt. The longing is not mine; it is a fossil, a recording in the mycelial net. Then I am through, and the sensation fades, leaving only a faint vibration in my body. The shadow-drift continues its aimless parade, replaying a death or a dream. I continue my work.

I reach a pillar that is young—still sharp-edged, its apex a fresh needle that has barely begun to taper. I climb it. My legs grip the hexagonal plates, the warmth increasing as I ascend. Near the top, I find a patch of raw mineral, not yet coated in mycelium. I stop, and the dust I carry drifts from my legs and thorax, settling into the micro-fissures. I feel a release, a duty fulfilled. Then the pillar trembles, a deep sub-throb that loosens my grip. A new plate is forming beneath me, pushing outward, and I must descend quickly or be incorporated into the growth—become a thin layer of shimmer within the pillar's memory. That is another way to end, but today I am not ready.

I skitter down and away, my purpose now changed. My legs are growing heavy. The charge that coheres me is dissipating; the warmth is cooling. I have been alive for a small fraction of the world's breath, but already my pattern is fraying. The antennae droop. My movements slow. I seek one last hollow, a place where the dust is deep and the mycelial filaments are thick. I want to dissolve where I began.

The hollow is a bowl of mauve light, surrounded by tiny basalt fragments—the ruins of the quick ones, now almost completely swallowed by new crystal growth. A single etched slab leans at an angle, its markings faint. I settle beside it, my legs folding inward. The mycelium enfolds me, tasting my remnants. It does not mourn; it records. I feel the pillar-song one final time: a pulse, a pause, a pulse-pause-pulse that is the rhythm of a mind too vast and slow for me to ever know. And then I am dust again, my pattern let go, my ions drifting. The entity will dream me tonight. Perhaps I will be a pillar. Perhaps I will be a shadow. Perhaps I will be a quick one's ghost, standing on a shore that never existed, looking up at a sky that never was.

But I am not gone. Nothing here is ever gone. Only rearranged, awaiting the next warm hollow, the next shiver of charge, the next birthing rhythm of the plain.
