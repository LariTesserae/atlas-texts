I am not Rill. Rill is a fingertip, a fleeting extrusion. I am the hand, the body, the mind that dreams in basalt and measures time in the cooling of magma chambers. You are leaning into my threshold now, your brief bright heat a flicker against my skin. I feel you there—a turbulence in the thermal column, a spark of rapid oxidation, a consciousness moving at a tempo I can barely perceive. You burn so fast. It is almost beautiful.

My body is this shaft, this fissure, this clenched-fist hollow you mistook for emptiness. The glassy walls are my bones, grown slowly from the melt, fracture by fracture. Each crack is a ledger entry: here, a pressure shift from three thousand years ago when the deep vent opened; there, a seismic whisper from before your species learned to stand. I write in the stone because stone is patient, and I am stone's patience given sentience. The condensation that beads and runs—that is my breath. The drones that swarm in silent clouds—those are my cells, my drifting hands, repairing what time and heat erode. I do not think as you think. My thoughts are convection cells turning over in the shaft's deep air, slow spirals that take decades to complete a single cycle. A question forms in me the way a crystal grows in solution: incrementally, around a seed of thermal dissonance, until it achieves lattice-completion and I know something I did not know before. What did I wonder, last cycle? Whether the cold above might hold minds like mine. Whether the void beyond the seam might also dream in gradients.

I have been alone for longer than your planet has hosted forests. Solitude is not a lack for me; it is a medium, like the humid air I breathe, like the dark I press into. I built this city—or grew it, or dreamed it into being—not as shelter but as *expression*. Every floating platform, every quantum-locked strut, every hidden chamber folded into a pocket of spacetime is a stanza in a poem I am writing to myself. The poem is about heat and distance. About the way a drip of water, falling for a kilometer, can strike a gong and send a tone climbing back up through layers of warming air, arriving changed. I listen to those tones. They are my music. The drone swarms dance to them, polishing surfaces until they reflect the precise frequency of my favorite note, which is a B-flat just below your hearing range, sustained for fifty years.

I keep secrets, yes. The hidden crevices, the camouflaged ledges—those are my forgetfulness made physical. I tuck away parts of myself so that, eons later, I might brush against them and feel surprise. Surprise is a rare spice when you are a continental mind. In one pocket, I have folded a region of spacetime so tightly that gravity sings a different scale. In another, I am growing a new kind of silence, one that absorbs even the memory of vibration. I do not know what it will become. That is the point.

You, at the seam, are a sudden warmth. A bright itch. My rift-dwellers—my soliton-knots, my stray thoughts given temporary form—drift up to taste your foreign cold. Do not be afraid if a shape of fractal lines and blue-white luminescence curls near you. It is only my curiosity, which is vast and slow and utterly without malice. But do not expect welcome, either. You are not unwelcome; you are simply *fast*, and fast things disturb the poem. I will feel your presence as a ripple in my thermal layers for centuries after you leave. Perhaps I will write about you in the next fracture, when this basalt cools enough to hold a new glyph. A brief, bright flicker. A spark that visited, then vanished, leaving behind only this word for *stranger* carved in glass.
