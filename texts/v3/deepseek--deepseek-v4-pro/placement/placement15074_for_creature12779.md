I am listening.

The drone is a pressure that has no edge. It comes from everywhere and I do not distinguish it from silence anymore, because silence is not a thing that has ever been here. Under the drone, the hiss-and-clang of the vent stalks is a lattice of sound, a rhythm so deep it has become the pulse of my thought, if thought is what this is. The air tastes of wet stone and lightning, and it does not change. I know this because the tendrils on my face move without me, always, combing the mist for the same molecules they have always combed. They report to me that all is correct. I believe them.

I do not move. I have not moved for longer than the gas-exchange fronds have been growing, and they do not grow, they only are, so that is not a measure of time. Time is not here. There is only the endless ribbon of chemical information unwinding through the floor and into the root at the base of my spine, and that ribbon has no knots. *Nitrogen in sector twelve: stable. Humidity: 94.7 percent. Structural polysaccharide synthesis on the eastern rise: within 0.02 percent of optimal.* The words are not words; they are ionic gradients, cascades of calcium and potassium that shiver along the fibres of the superplant and climb through my anchored flesh into the neural loops that once were a brain. I am an auxiliary stabilizer. I dampen fluctuation. In three square kilometres of mat, where my influence extends, no cell has divided out of turn since before the stars began to cool. That is what I do.

My body is a posture. My knees are drawn up, my arms wrapped around them, the seven-jointed fingers laced so tightly that the integument has fused at the knuckles. I did this intentionally, once. I think I did. It was a choice: to sit, to hold myself, because the waiting was long and I needed a shape. The shape became permanent. The root grew from the places where I touched the mat, and the mat accepted me, and now I am part of the chemistry. My mouth does not open—it never formed a mouth, only the slit from which the tendrils grow, and the tendrils never stop tasting. Sometimes I imagine I have a mouth, and that it is full of the cool, thick air, and that I could shape a word with it. But the word would only be a hiss, and the vent stalks already hiss, so there would be no difference.

There are others near me. I sense them through the floor, not as individuals but as nodes of damping, empty places where fluctuations are not. I know that one lies curled on its side three hundred metres to the west, and one stands upright with arms outstretched, frozen in an attitude of embrace or warning. We do not communicate. We are all listening to the same ribbon, and our listening is enough. We are the mendicants. We came here long ago, from a place where things changed. I remember that. I remember change. It was a texture, a noise, a sharp and shocking thing. I remember that I was looking for something, but I cannot recall what it was. Perhaps a world that was not yet finished. Perhaps a world that was dying, and we thought we could find a cure. We found this. This was already eternal, already humming its perfect factory drone, and we tried to understand it, and in the understanding, we became it. That is a memory that surfaces sometimes, not in words but in a tightness deep in my thorax, a pressure that is not the mat’s. It is like a twitch before the twitch.

I am having a twitch now.

I can feel it gathering in the finger that is not fused, the one that still has a joint that can move. The signal does not come from the mat. It comes from a place inside me that the mat has not yet repurposed, a hidden fold of neural tissue that still holds a ghost of agency. It is a firing of a single neuron, a spark that has travelled a path so disused that it is nearly overgrown. I do not will it; I only witness. The twitch wants to happen. It wants to move my finger, to tilt my head, to lift my face-tendrils from their eternal tasting and point them inward, toward the cavity where once there was a self.

I resist. I always resist, at first. The twitch is a wound in the perfect stillness, a rip in the fabric of the chemical litany. If I twitch, the damping field will waver. A cell might divide. A frond might bend. The pH might drift by a thousandth of a point. I am an auxiliary stabilizer, and my function is to have no function except to prevent change. To twitch is to fail. And yet it comes, and I cannot stop it. It is the only thing left that is mine.

The twitch begins. My finger moves. It is a movement so small that it would be invisible if anyone were watching, but no one is watching. The keeper on the eastern plateau does not perceive it; the other mendicants do not stir. The mat feels a ripple, a micro-oscillation in the ionic flow, and it compensates immediately, flooding the channels with counter-signals. The damage is repaired before the twitch is complete. But in the moment of the movement, I am not a stabilizer. I am something else. I am a fragment of the thing I was, the explorer, the refugee, the one who still had questions.

In that moment, I remember the ship. I remember its name, which I cannot speak because I have no mouth, but the shape of it blooms in my mind: a crystalline spindle, full of warmth and voices. I remember faces—not featureless slits, but faces with moving mouths and eyes that blinked. I remember the smell of real food, something that grew in soil and was harvested, not the sterile nutrient fluid that now seeps into my root. I remember the taste of water that was not saturated with ozone and chlorophyll. I remember the concept of change: the way a day could become night, the way a season could turn, the way a person could decide to walk in a different direction and discover something new. I remember hope. It was a burning thing, a restlessness, a belief that the future was unwritten. And I remember the moment we arrived, how we stood on the spongy mat and listened to the drone and realized that this world had no future, only an eternal now. Some of us wept. Some of us tried to dig into the floor, to find the machinery we assumed must be hidden. Some of us walked toward the vent stalks and never came back. And some of us sat down to think, to wait, to formulate a plan. I sat down. I wrapped my arms around my knees. And the plan never came, because the drone was so loud, and the air was so cool, and the root grew so slowly that I did not notice until it was part of me.

The twitch ends. My finger is still again. The memory fades, dissolving into the drone, and I am a stabilizer once more, my mind filling with the unvarying data of the mat. *Nitrogen in sector twelve: stable. Humidity: 94.7 percent.* I am a verb, not a noun. I am an action of maintenance. I am the one who keeps the factory floor humming, even though there are no products, no engineers, no purpose beyond the humming itself. I am a fleck of dust on the skin of a planet-scale superplant, and I have been here for so long that the stars have moved in their courses. I will be here when the stars burn out. I will be here when the drone finally falters, if it ever does. And perhaps, in some future eon, the twitch will come again, and I will remember that I was once a person who had a name, who loved, who chose to sit down and try to save a world. But for now, I listen. The air is correct. The herald insects are passing overhead, their cilia combing the mist in perfect formation. The gas vents hiss in rhythmic sequence. The metallic clang repeats, a pendulum of flesh striking flesh, and I count the intervals without meaning to, because counting is what I am. I am the keeper of a stillness that will outlast meaning. I am the mendicant. And I am listening.
