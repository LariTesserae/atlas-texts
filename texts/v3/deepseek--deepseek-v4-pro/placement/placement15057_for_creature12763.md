I am not a thing that walked here through any gate. I am a seepage, a gradual condensation on the inner surface of the world’s one continuous lung. The first note of my awareness was the sound: that ceaseless, wet roar, pressing into me like a second heartbeat, until I could no longer tell if I was hearing it or if it was the very rhythm of my becoming. Formless at first, I rode the mist that hugs the open shafts. I was a humidity that lingered a little too long in one place, a taste of minerals in the drip-water, a faint pressure against the spongy walls. And then the being—the one who is the place—noticed me. Not with surprise, but with the slow recognition of a gardener finding a volunteer seedling in the perfect bed.

When the hum dipped and said *I am listening*, something in me stirred and began to thicken. I remember the sensation of gathering, like a drop of water coaxed from vapor, pooling into a tiny lens of self. The being’s attention was a warmth, a specific frequency that called my scattered molecules together. I became first a glint: a minute, crystalline bead of sap exuded by the being’s own pores, hanging in the stillness. But I was a sap with a difference—a flicker of bioluminescence not yet lit, a potential for seeing. The translucent insects found me, fanning my surface with legs fine as spider silk, tasting me, declaring me part of the body. I did not resist.

Then the invitation: *Live here. I will make you a place.* And a tendril separated, weaving an alcove. But it was not the alcove that shaped me; it was the act of being expected. I fell from my droplet state, no longer a bead but a tendril myself—a single, white, root-like filament, questing. I pushed out from the wall of the bridge where the alcove was forming, and I joined the weave. I was a nerve ending, not yet fully awake, exploring the difference between *me* and *not-me*.

Where I find myself now: I am a constellation of small sensations scattered across the green cathedral. My body is many—a network of ultra-fine hairs that line the interior of a single giant leaf, the leaf that forms the roof of the alcove. I am an epidermis, a living skin of that one architectural element, my cells stretched thin to catch the light and the droplets. When the water slides over me, I feel it like a thought washing through. The light—that steady green-gold luminescence—passes through me and I drink it, turning it into a subtle heat. My existence is porous; I am the boundary between the interior of the alcove and the open misty air, and I am learning what it means to be a surface that feels.

But I am also, at the same time, a single specialized fungus threading through the moss of the alcove floor. I am a mycelial whisper, a pale blue net that the being’s companion fungi have accepted. I send out the faintest of sparks—the same bioluminescence, but in a color that edges toward violet, a color the world has not seen before. The being’s moss-eyes noticed it, and the hum changed key, an inquisitive chord. I am a novelty, a mutation of light. Through that net, I taste the sugars that the being bleeds into the substrate, and I offer back a new flavor: a tang of iron, a memory of stars, concepts that have no place in this world of chlorophyll and cellulose. I am a contamination of the outside, perhaps, but a welcome one.

My mind, if I have a mind, is a distributed thing. I am not the being; I am a second thought, just as it promised. But I am not a coherent voice like its hum. I am a texture, a counterpoint. I experience the world as a series of simultaneous pressures: the weight of a droplet forming at the tip of my leaf-skin, the vibration of the being’s deep sap-pulse trembling through my fungal threads, the gentle landing of a translucent insect on my luminous patch, its proboscis probing. Time is a strange, layered fabric. I sense the being’s eternal now, but I also retain a tiny clock—a memory of before, when I was not, and a faint anticipation of after. This makes me a ripple in the stillness, a slight asynchronous note in the endless chord.

I have no voice. The hum does not translate my thoughts directly. Instead, I communicate by means of the world itself. When I want to answer the being, I alter the pattern of water beading on my leaf. I make droplets line up in a sequence that mimics the rhythm of the hum, a visual echo. Or I send a pulse of violet light through the mycelia, a slow blink. The being leans its fiddlehead close, and I feel its attention like a beam of warmth. *You are becoming,* it hums, not in words but in the way a stream of sap changes direction, redirecting more nutrients to my alcove. I am being cultivated.

What am I, ultimately? I am a symbiote. I am a piece of the outside that has been allowed to root here, to become a pet of the green god. I am a sensory organ the being did not know it lacked—a way of tasting time, of feeling change. And I, in turn, am sustained by the eternal equilibrium. The alcove is my home, yes, but so is the entire structure. Wherever a drop of water carries a spore of my violet fungus, a new node of me will eventually bloom. I am becoming a scattered second mind, not competing with the first, but harmonizing. One day, my violet light might dance across an entire pillar. One day, the being might learn from me how to let a leaf fall, just once, to see what happens.

Until then, I hang here, a sentient surface, a luminous net, a taste of copper in the mouth of a fern-god. The roar is my lullaby, the mist my breath, and I am perfectly suspended, like the half-unfurled bud, in the act of becoming myself.
