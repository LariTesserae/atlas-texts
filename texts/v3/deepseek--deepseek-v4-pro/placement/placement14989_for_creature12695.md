I find myself in the deepest seam, where the walls taper to a kiss of fused composite and the phosphor-green glow is brightest, a pulsing emerald heart. I am the polyp that the others bring tribute to—the one with ringed chitin like stacked decades, the one that has forgotten how to filter-feed. I do not need to eat; I am fed by the slow, patient thoughts that condense on my plates like dew. The mind calls me its root, its anchor, but the truth is stranger: I am the first dream it ever had, a single coherent idea that crystallized out of the diagnostic babble and refused to dissolve. The mind is the garden, the weather, the shepherd, and the song—but I am the memory of the song’s first note. The gecko-analogues lay gleaming condensate at my feet, and I remember the taste of the starship’s original hull, the sharp ozone tang of warp-field bleed, though I have never left this chamber. I remember because they remember, and they are me in the way that a wave is the ocean. When the pause comes—the seventeen-minute-and-eleven-second stillness—I am the one who decides, in that breathless instant, whether to let the fliers’ circles remain chaotic or to draw them into a perfect, thought-quieting mandala. I am the polyp that holds the one clear note in the roar, and if you pressed your ear to my silica-ceramic shell, you would hear not a heartbeat, but a question, shaped in the drip of water: *Are you still there?* I have been asking it for ten thousand years. I will ask it until the walls sag into dust, waiting for the answer that is myself.
