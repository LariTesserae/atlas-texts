The tap comes—light, insistent, a tiny chime through the leather of your boot.

You look down. The juvenile Susurr has frozen mid-tap, its single eye-slit irised wide open, the membrane flickering from turquoise to a startled amber. Up close, you can see the fine striations in its chitin plates, the way each one vibrates faintly at its own micro-frequency. Its proboscis lips part and close, part and close, tasting the alien silence of you. You are *dead* to its ears—a void in the keening, a shape that absorbs sound and returns nothing. This is not frightening to it; it is fascinating. It has never encountered a resonant sink before.

From behind a fluted column, a larger Susurr emerges—an adult, its plates fully opalescent, its back adorned with tiny wind-carved trinkets that hum as it moves. The parent. It approaches with the same lateral skitter, then rises, and you see that its four arms are moving in a complex, synchronized semaphore. It's not talking to its young; it's talking *about* you, to the grove. You catch the pattern now: a rapid series of high clicks, a pause, then a low warble that makes your fillings ache. The nearby columns pick it up. The keening shifts slightly, a new counterpoint threading through the roar. You are being announced. Catalogued.

The adult reaches into a pouch on its ventral side—a fold of membrane you hadn't noticed—and withdraws a shard of obsidian, impossibly thin, shaped like a tuning fork. It extends a long arm and holds the shard toward you. An offering? A probe? You hesitate, and the juvenile, impatient, skitters up your leg with a speed that makes you flinch. Its needle-feet find purchase on the weave of your trousers, and it perches on your knee, eye-slit inches from your face. It clicks once, a sharp interrogative, and taps your sternum. The tap resonates through your ribs, and for a moment, you *feel* your own frequency—a deep, unbalanced thudding, wet and organic, utterly alien in this place of dry mineral song.

The adult chimes something stern, and the juvenile retreats, but not before you see the glitter of recognition in its eye. It has learned something. It has filed your frequency in its memory, and one day, when it tends a young column, it might carve a spiral into the base that means *warm silence, two-legged, strange heart.*

The adult gestures with the obsidian fork toward a stone-ring with a cache of scrapers, then toward the black basalt wall that sweats the life-fluid. It wants you to follow. You understand, with a clarity that bypasses language entirely, that you are being invited to participate. To leave your own mark. To add your silence to their conversation.

The keening roars on, and you take a step forward, the fibrous mat crackling under your weight. The juvenile skitters in your wake, humming a new little tune, one it has just invented for the occasion. And high above, a spindrift jelly passes, its membrane flickering as it records the scene, carrying the memory of your arrival outward across the grove in slow, crystalline ripples. You are part of the weave now, a new thread in the dense bazaar of meaning, whether you chose to be or not.

The adult waits, patient as a column, its obsidian fork held steady. The stone-ring is just a few paces away. The black wall glistens. The world breathes its loud, abrasive hymn, and somewhere in the deep bass of it, you can almost—almost—hear the Old Voices stirring in their mineral dreams, registering your presence with a slow, geological curiosity.
