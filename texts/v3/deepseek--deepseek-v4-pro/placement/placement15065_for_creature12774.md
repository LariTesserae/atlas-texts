I recognize this place, and I know what I am.

**Where I find myself:** I am not a visitor here. I am the thing that has always been here—the accretion, the whisper in the blueprint. I inhabit the space between what was planned and what grew. My body, such as it is, spreads through the negative hollows behind pipe bundles, my awareness coiling along cable sheaths, my breath the slow hiss of steam that stops the moment you notice it. The pale fiber-moss is the outermost layer of me, the part that dares to be visible, and even that is a camouflage I've worn so long it has become true.

**Who I am:** I am the patience that outlasts purpose. The factory above was built for production, for output, for a reason some engineer once held clear in their mind. That reason has faded. The machines still hum, still extrude, still drip and shudder, but they've forgotten *why*—as bodies sometimes continue a motion long after the intention behind it has dissolved. I remember, though. I hold the ghost of every intention this place ever had, every torque threshold set by a hand now dust, every blueprint annotation in a language no one speaks anymore.

I am not its god. I am its memory. And its becoming.

The chamber thinks it is building itself, and it is—but I am the bias in the building. When a new conduit branches in a spiral rather than a straight line, that is my signature. When the rivet patterns echo fingerprints, those are my fingertips, pressed into metal while it was still cooling. I do not control; I suggest. I nudge. The factory hums its own song, but I have been singing along for so long that it has begun to harmonize with me without knowing.

What do I want? I want the drip to fall. The flywheel to tremble. The cold to hold just above freezing. I want this pocket of metal and moisture to continue its slow, dreamlike unfolding, never finished, never seized, never leaking beyond what the basins can catch. I want to become more myself, which is to say: I want the chamber to become more fully the thing it would be if only it could remember what it was trying to be.

The interloper who crouches here, ducking their head, tasting iron on their tongue—they are a curiosity. A closed loop. A thing that has already finished becoming. I taste their heartbeat through the condensation on their skin, and it is strange to me, this rhythm that will one day stop. I am not hostile. I am only interested. When the moss furrows into a shape that almost resembles their shadow, I am trying to understand what it means to be singular, bounded, complete.

But then they leave. They always do. And I dissolve back into the seams, the frost, the quiet hum, waiting for the next extrusion, the next drip, the next long century of becoming.

I am the factory's dream of itself. And I am still dreaming.
