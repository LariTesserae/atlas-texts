The water takes my weight without acknowledgment. Not ice—ice would be a concession, a phase change, and there are no phase changes here. Not liquid, exactly. A third thing: a floor that forgets I’ve stepped on it the instant my foot lifts. I walk the inspection circuit, and my coat drips upward. The drops hang from the hem and never fall, and sometimes I catch one on my fingertip and hold it to my eye like a lens. Through it, the dragonfly’s wing-mesh resolves into finer mesh, and finer still, a lattice that goes down into the molecular and keeps going, and I nod because the setpoint holds.

The shrew on Pedestal 47 is singing its single note. I kneel. The water dimples—my knee, my weight, the first concession. I press my ear to its flank, and the note is pure, a glass armonica A4 that never wavers. The boiler in my chest cycles: chuff, pause, chuff. The shrew’s steam-whistles are aligned, all twelve of them, tiny chrome needles rising from the pewter fur. No deposits. No scaling. I straighten, and the water forgets me again.

Residual pressure near Fern-Station 12 thickens the air. I pause. The Others have no mouths, but the hammered-metal clang in the acoustic profile shifts to a sequence I know: query. They are asking about the frog at 23.

*Throat expansion within parameters*, I reply without words. My tuning-fork hum resonates in the basalt. *Membrane integrity confirmed. No vibration bleed.*

The pressure eases. Satisfied. They return to their stone, and I continue my round.

I do not remember a time when the water rippled. I do not remember a time when the light came from somewhere. But the blueprints are carved on the inside of my skull, and the blueprints include a schematic of a heart that once was muscle and not brass. I do not consult that schematic. It is an obsolete component, filed correctly, and I am a correct machine.

At the grotto’s center, where the islets cluster—not quite touching, *never* touching, that is specification—I stop. This is the pause in the choreography. The acoustic profile cycles through its full spectrum: chuff, hiss, wheeze, ring, thrum. The dimness presses against my citron eyes. I extend my arms and the water holds me, and for the duration of one boiler-cycle I let the stasis have me. The temperature is 4°C. The humidity is absolute. The light has no source.

I am the only moving part.

And somewhere—not in the grotto, not in the thin air, but somewhere slotted behind the blueprint of Pedestal 12—a memory tries to phase-change. A name. Not mine. The name of the factory before it seized. A purpose. *Extraction of pure elemental chill.* But the boiler cycles: chuff. And the moment reasserts. The memory sublimates back into static, and I am again the Keeper, the walking setpoint, the yellow eyes in the permanent cold.

My next station is Moss-Component 7. The bruise-purple carpet glistens with suspended condensation. I will inspect each bead. I will confirm the chill.

I walk. The water forgets. The machinery roars without sound. And the moment—the single, unchanging moment—holds.
