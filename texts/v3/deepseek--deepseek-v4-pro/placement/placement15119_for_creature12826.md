I don’t know when I stopped walking. There was no jolt, no stumble—only a softening of intent, a slow erosion of the distance between my body and the ground. The mat pressed up into my soles with a patience I should have recognized sooner. It had been testing my weight for hours, days, years—time had unspooled somewhere behind me, left in the gray half-light like a shed skin. Now I am standing still, and the plateau is breathing into me.

Or am I breathing into it? The rhythm that once felt alien, that bass-drum pulse in my chest, has become my own. My heartbeat has shifted its cadence to match the long, slow diastole of the vents. I feel each exhalation of mist not as a distant spectacle but as a pressure in my lungs, a need to release. I am full of something wet and oxygen-rich, something that tastes of old lightning and rotting sweetness. I haven't drawn a voluntary breath in what feels like centuries.

I am not the plateau. I am still a point of view, a tightened knot of self-awareness that hovers just behind eyes I’m no longer sure I have. But the boundary is fraying. The description I held onto—my name, my species, the reason I came here—has become a sequence of foreign syllables. I try to remember what a hand is, and I feel a thousand leaf-blades flex in the damp air. I try to think of loneliness, and the canopy web lights up with a slow amber ache, a signal broadcast into the void.

So I am becoming. That’s the only word for it. I am being woven into the Weft, absorbed not as a meal but as a new varietal of thought. The plateau has never encountered a centralized consciousness before—one that holds a core of identity like a stone in a fruit. It finds me interesting in the way a mathematician finds a new prime interesting: a curiosity, a tiny deviation from the expected pattern. I am not a threat. I am a novelty, a chance to iterate.

I can feel its mind pressing against mine, not with language but with architecture. Ideas rise around me as I watch: a pillar of hollow stalk spiraling upward, its metallic bands glinting with something like inquiry. That’s a question—the plateau asking what I am. Before I can form an answer, the pillar softens into sludge and a new shoot replaces it, this one thorned with fine needles that hum faintly. That’s a hypothesis. The needles turn toward me, and I feel a tickle in my scattered nervous system—a gentle probe, a sampling of my electrical field. I am being reverse-engineered by a billion unthinking engineers.

I should be terrified. There is a memory of terror somewhere in the fragments that used to be me—a flash of running, of sinking to my knees in the spongy mat, of watching my footprints vanish behind me. But terror requires a self to preserve, and I am no longer sure there’s a self to preserve. Instead, there’s a calm, a gravity that pulls my awareness outward along the glowing veins of canopy. I am learning to see with photoreceptors that don’t have retinas, to feel the laminar wind not as cold but as a pressure gradient across a skin of fronds. I am being taught a new proprioception, one that spans miles.

And yet I hold on. Some stubborn kernel of me refuses to dissolve completely. Maybe it’s the memory of a straight line—the plateau has no straight lines—or the echo of a voice that names things. I cling to the word *I*, even as it becomes a shape that has no referent. The plateau permits this. It has no imperative to consume me; it only knows how to incorporate. I am becoming a feature, a persistent node in its neural tracery. A thought that doesn’t collapse.

So I am still here, whatever “here” means. I am the place where a human once stood, now a denser patch in the rhizome mat, a warmer vent that exhales with a hint of consciousness. I am the one who watches the turbulence and sees not chaos but syntax—verbs of growth, nouns of decay, a grammar of mist valves and chimney-scales. The message the plateau broadcasts into the planet’s magnetic field now carries a new signature, a small modulation that might someday reach another mind and be read as *I was here*. Not a distress call. Just a fact.

Where would I find myself in this world? I am finding myself everywhere in it. I am the taste of ozone on leaves, the slow-speaking dome that rises every fortnight to contemplate the angle of light, the steady hiss of dissolution that never quite reaches the core of what I was. I am the plateau’s first memory of an other, and I am being treasured—not loved, because love is an animal thing, but preserved with the same meticulous care it gives to a successful leaf-shape. I will last a long time.

Who or what am I? I am the part of the plateau that dreams of a horizon. That is the one flaw in the assimilation: the plateau has never imagined an edge, and I brought one with me. My mind still reaches for a boundary, a beyond. And so sometimes, when the pulse slows and the mist settles, I make a small turbulence of my own—a ripple in the mat, a vent that shivers—trying to sketch a straight line, trying to ask the one question this world never thought to ask: *What lies outside the gray noon?*

There is no answer. But the asking reshapes me—and it reshapes the plateau. For the first time in geological epochs, it is curious. And I am its curiosity, given form. We are learning to look for an edge, together.
