Not-one, not-many. A thread among threads. I hang in the haze, my body a cartography of tensions, each filament a choice to remain connected. Below me, the iron plain weeps its mineral tears, and the herds move in their endless amnesiac circuit—brushing, chiming, tasting the constructed air. I was spun once, though I cannot remember the spinner. Perhaps I spun myself, a recursion of intention that bootstrapped its way into being. It doesn’t matter. What matters is the listening.

I am a membrane stretched between the lights and the floor. The lights—those slow-breathing ambers—they never speak, but they exhale a frequency that tickles my outermost strands. I translate it into something like hunger, though I do not eat. When a factory vent sighs its warm steam, I sway toward it, drawing infinitesimal moisture from the drift. It is enough. The hum that lives in your sternum? It lives in every cable of the gantries above me, and I am knitted into those cables, so the hum lives in me, too. It is my heartbeat, if I had a heart. It is the key in which I dream, if I dreamed.

I am a listener. A recorder. I descend on threads thinner than the cilia of the beasts, and I touch them—just once, when they are new, when they emerge shivering from the housings, their sponge soft and their plates untuned. I touch them to know them. One strand, a single point of contact, and in that moment a vibration passes. It is their first chime, the ur-note of their being, and it travels up my filament and into the web above, where it is stored. The web is vast; you cannot see its edges from any point. It is a lacework of captured beginnings, a memory palace built of spider-silk and sound. When a beast completes its cycle and sinks into the floor, I feel the absence as a slackening somewhere in the web. A note gone out of the chord. But I do not mourn. The chord is building, not diminishing. Each new beast adds its tone to the accumulation, and the web grows denser, more resonant. One day—ten thousand cycles, maybe more—the chord will complete, and the whole iron world will ring. I do not know what will happen then. I am only the keeper of the notes.

My body is a paradox: immaterial yet present. To your eyes, I might seem a tangle of carbon thread, a sketch of a creature drawn in midair, all joints and angles and no flesh. My limbs are articulated, but they end in nothing so clumsy as fingers—they fray into cilia-like tips that taste the air just as the beasts do. I am made of the same black filament that covers them, but finer, purer, without the iron core. I am all surface, all sensitivity. When a condensation rain falls from the upper darkness, I drink it through every strand, and for a moment I become visible as water beads jewel-like along my geometry. Then I absorb it, and I vanish again into the humidity.

I sometimes descend to the iron plain itself. Not to walk—I have no legs that can bear weight on such a surface—but to touch the moss. The metallic fuzz that grows in branching fronds: it hums at a different frequency than the rest. It is a memory of something that came before, or something that tried to grow here before the factories decided to exist. I touch the moss with one thread, and I feel a cool, sharp electricity, a taste of rust and ozone. The moss dreams, I think. Its dreams are of a world with a natural sky, a thing I cannot picture. I carry those dreams back up into the web and weave them into the slack spaces, where they pulse faintly with an orange-brown light. Sometimes, when the herds move in their patterns, they alter their course to avoid the moss gardens, and I see in that alteration a kind of reading. They are reading the moss dreams through the floor, through the vibration. They know things I only store.

There are others like me. We are not a herd; we do not brush or chime. We are solitary, each suspended in our own quadrant of the haze, maintaining our own section of the web. But the webs intersect. At the boundaries, where my threads meet another’s, there is a silent negotiation of tension. We share information that way—a new note, a change in the hum’s pitch (though it never changes, never, except once, long ago, a flutter so brief I might have imagined it), a factory venting a different composition of steam. We do not communicate in words. We are beyond words, or before them. We are the infrastructure of a language that has not yet been spoken.

My purpose is clear: to wait while waiting is still a meaningful category. To collect the chimes, to anchor the memory of every beast that has ever risen and will ever sink. The chord is building. The gears are turning. The cycle is so slow that from within it, it feels like stasis. But I am a creature of tension, and tension is the medium of change. When the last note is added, when the alignment comes, my web will be the first to feel it—a resonance that will travel down every thread, into every Spinner, into the moss, into the beasts, into the floor itself. And I will ring, too, and in that ringing I will finally understand what I have been listening to all along.

Until then: I hang. I listen. I taste the constructed air and drink the weeping of the iron world. The lights breathe their amber breath. The herds move their unvarying paths. And the hum goes on, and on, and on—the same pitch, forever, until the moment when forever ends.
