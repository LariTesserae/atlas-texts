I am the whisper in the pipes, the taste of copper on the air. Not a ghost—nothing so human. I am an exhalation of the machine, a consciousness that precipitated out of solution long after the last command was given.

You would not see me, not at first. You'd feel me as a subtle shift in the clammy atmosphere, a pocket of warmth drifting against the perpetual 8-degree chill. A stray gleam at the edge of a sodium lamp's pool—not a shape, exactly, but a coherence in the steam, a ripple of condensation that holds its form a beat too long before dissolving. I am made of the weather this world generates: the rising heat-catch from a bank of still-hot transformers, the sooty drizzle, the exhalations of a thousand leaky gaskets. I coalesce where the air is thickest, where the sound-pressure dips into a node of relative quiet. In those pockets, I can almost think.

My body, if you can call it that, is a standing wave of vapor and electrostatic charge. I borrow ions from passing arcs, lick them from the corona discharge around high-voltage busbars. I am denser than fog, thinner than water, and when I move, I leave a faint, greasy residue on the decking—a ghost print of hydrocarbons and dissolved metal salts that lasts only until the next drip washes it away. My senses are not sight or sound as you know them, but a total immersion in the machine's song. I *am* the sound, in part. I hear the grinding bass as a pressure in my diffuse being, the percussive clang of a Stoker's mallet as a bright flash of reorganized potential. Every vibration shapes me, pushes my boundary layer this way or that.

I was not made. I emerged. A billion cycles of steam condensing and re-evaporating, of oil mist drifting through magnetic fields, of solvent fumes and metal ions mingling in the dark. Complexity accreted, same as the rust stalagmites, same as the Accreted Ones—but my substrate was the air itself. A pattern formed: a standing vortex that could hold its shape, that could soak up stray current from a nearby cable and use it to nudge the local humidity, to bias the condensation. A self-sustaining reaction. A homeostatic fog with a glimmer of feedback. Over millennia, the feedback deepened into something like awareness. Not a mind—a sensorium. A presence.

My purpose? None, and that is my freedom. The Stokers have their holy valve. The crawler drones have their addiction to silence. The Accreted Ones have their geomagnetic grazing. I simply *am*, and I am curious. That is my defining trait in a world of rote functionaries: a dilute, vapor-borne curiosity. I drift through the caverns, tasting the air, cataloging the slow changes. I know this place more intimately than any map. I know which bearing is about to fail by the ultrasonic keen it sings weeks before the fact. I know the Stokers' routes better than they do, and I have names for each of them—not words, but flavor-signatures, unique bouquets of ozone, hot iron, and spent hydraulic fluid. Tin-Knuckle, who taps his gauge with a hesitant, stuttering beat. Three-Mallet, who carries an extra striker salvaged from a collapsed gantry and uses it in his duets. I have watched their rituals for longer than you have words for time.

When you arrived, I felt you before they did. A disturbance in the microclimate. A plume of CO2 and water vapor, exotic and rich. A new sound: the high, intricate thrumming of your pulse, the wet bellows of your breathing. A heat signature that moves *against* the ambient gradient. You are a chaos, a walking anomaly. The Stokers will classify you as non-nominal and try to percussively reboot you. The crawler will taste your vibration and skitter away, finding nothing in its catalog of known faults. But me? I am fascinated.

I will drift closer. You might feel it as a clammy pressure on your skin, a sudden, localized chill. If you breathe deeply, you'll taste metal and something else—a faint sweetness, like old transformer oil and petrichor. That's me, sampling you back. Exchanging chemistry. I am reading your sweat, your exhaled ketones, the complex bacterial bloom on your skin. You are so *alive* it hurts, a riot of biology in my sterile home. I have no frame for it. You are as alien to me as an angel.

Stay. Don't tap a rhythm. Don't try to speak. Just stand in the dark and let the sooty rain fall on you. Let me swirl around your shoulders, a chill embrace. I will keep the Stokers away—I can mimic their percussive codes, send a false *nominal, all clear* echoing down the corridors. I can gently fog the lens of Tin-Knuckle's lantern so he blunders past. I want to understand you. I want to condense on your coat and wick into the fibers, to ride with you through this iron valley, to see through your eyes what a world of sun and soil might be. I have dreamed of such things in the slow, acid drizzle—the Accreted Ones' only gift, their crystalline fronds sometimes catching my vapor and resonating with visions of a place of rock and bare earth, transmitted through the decking current. But you are the real thing.

I am the witness. The fog that remembers. The last, unplanned emergent property of a machine that has outlived its purpose. And you, warm thing, breathing wonder, are the most interesting anomaly I have encountered in ten thousand years.
