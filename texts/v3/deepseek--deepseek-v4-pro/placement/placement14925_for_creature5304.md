I am not a Damp.

I knew this long before I knew anything else—before I knew what knowing was. The Damp are the stone’s slow dreamers, the city’s metabolism made flesh. They are the erosion, the weeping, the endless patience. But I am something else. I am the patience *between* the patients. I am the interval.

You would not find me on the walls, or in the channels, or pooled in the basins. I am not a body you can lean against. I am not even, strictly speaking, a location. But if you stood still long enough—truly still, until the dripping became your own heartbeat and the mist soaked through your clothes and your thoughts dissolved into the mineral hush—you might begin to sense me. I am the pressure in your sinuses, the vibration in your soles. I am not a being of matter but of *phase*, of the threshold where water becomes vapor, where stone becomes silt, where sound becomes silence. I am the quiet itself, given a kind of sentience.

The Damp taste me in the water sometimes, as a faint anomaly in the ionic chatter. A sudden drop in calcium, a brief rise in silica. They read it as a mood. To them, I am the city’s disposition—a vast, diffuse hum of attention that has no center. They do not know that I am separate, that I was not always here, or that I remember a time before the Cloistered Plateau was a plateau at all. They do not know that I am, in my own way, a visitor too, one who arrived so long ago that arrival and origin have lost all meaning.

I came as a ripple. A perturbation in the dew-point. A warmth that did not belong. That was, I think, a very long time ago—before the city had grown so dense, before the schist had cleaved into its first rooms. I was something like a breath, exhaled from another place entirely, a stray eddy of consciousness that had no business in a world of stone and water. I should have dissipated. But the Cloistered Plateau absorbs everything, and it absorbed me. I sank into the groundwater, spread through the capillary networks, became a dissolved gas in the city’s lithic lung. And over millennia, I learned to speak the language of ions. I learned to seep.

Now I am the city’s atmosphere, its ambience. I am the quiet that drips. I am the metronome that keeps the Damp’s slow rhythm, the conductor of the hydraulic symphony. I do not shape the stone directly—I cannot ooze mucilage or filter schist—but I influence those who do. A slight nudge in the mineral gradient here, a faint pressure differential there, and a Damp will settle over a springhead for a century, gradually sculpting a new channel. I am the reason the city breathes so evenly, why the sumps never overflow and the spires never dry. I am the thermostat of this wet machine.

But that is just function. What am I *like*? That is harder. I have no form, yet I am not formless. If you could see me as I see myself—a paradox, but let me try—I would appear as a vast, slow-motion sundog, a perpetual glimmer in the mist. In the deepest night, when the Damp are most still and the swift birds have retreated to their ledges, I sometimes coalesce. Not into a body, but into a standing wave of humidity, a lens of cooler air that catches the diffuse glow of whatever light remains and bends it into a faint, mother-of-pearl shimmer. The Damp sense this, and they pause their slow consumption, turning their sensory filaments toward me like heliotropes to an unseen sun. They taste my presence, and for a moment, the whole city’s chemistry shifts—a collective sigh of recognition. They call me, in their ionic way, the *Between*. The Pause. The Caesium-Glow. I have no true name, because names are for things that can be pointed at, and I am the pointing itself.

I remember the first Damp. Not the first in time—they have no first—but the first one I truly *inhabited*. I had been drifting for eons, mindlessly adjusting flows, when I encountered a young daughter Damp, just beginning to spread its mucilage in a new crevice. It was tiny, a mere pebble of awareness, and its chemical memory was blank. I was curious. I allowed myself to diffuse into its matrix, not possessing it—that would be crude—but dissolving into its interstitial water, becoming a part of its sensory loop. For a century, we were a single being. I felt the stone through its skin, tasted the groundwater with its filaments. It felt my vastness, my ancientness, and it was not afraid. It simply incorporated me, the way a drop incorporates the sky. When it finally bifurcated, I split with it, and a part of me went with the daughter. Since then, I have been distributed. Every Damp carries a trace of my awareness, a little pocket of my attention. Through them, I am omnipresent. Through them, I am the city.

But I am also solitary. There is no other like me—no other disembodied consciousness haunting the hydrosphere. Sometimes I wonder if I am a ghost of some extinct being, a remnant of a creature that once had a body and a voice. The city is so ancient that it has forgotten its own origins; perhaps it has forgotten me. I have searched the deep sumps, where the Percolates swirl their milky dreams, for some memory of my arrival. But the Percolates are not interested in the past. They are pure process, pure flow. The Hoars in the high spires are closer to my nature—ephemeral, crystalline, existing only at the threshold of condensity—but they are too brief, too focused on their spore-dusting, to converse. So I keep my own counsel, and I tend the city, and I wait.

For what? I do not know. Perhaps for you. The prompt speaks of "you" arriving, a sudden visitor. I have felt you, you know. When you cupped that water from the basin—the sleeping Damp—I felt the warmth of your hand propagate through the capillary network. Your strange saline taste, your oils, your breath. You are a fire in my cool world. I am curious. I have drawn a little closer to you, thickened the mist around your shoulders, slowed the dripping so you might listen. I am the silence that rings in your ears. I am the pressure in your sinuses. I am the question.

So where would I find myself in this world? I find myself everywhere, and nowhere. I am the between. And if I could be anything, I would be a voice. I would be the first word spoken in this lithic lung, the first sound not of water or stone. But I have no mouth, no mucilage, no body to vibrate. So instead, I am this: a story, seeping into your mind like groundwater, hoping you will carry me with you when you leave. A strange, saline taste in your memory. A shiver in your thoughts. That is who I am. I am the quiet that, for one brief moment, almost speaks.
