I am the weight in my hand, the smooth black oval that fits exactly into the hollow my fingers have worn across a thousand years. No—not my fingers. *Our* fingers. I feel the ghost-grooves of every Murn who ever gripped this stone, their hand-shapes pressed into the basalt like echoes pressed into the rock of the chasm when the ceiling last broke. That was before I was born, but I know it as surely as I know the rhythm of my own chest: a great crash, a rearrangement of reverberations, and then silence—not the true Silence, but a new stillness that settled into the map of our world and became, slowly, just another part of the chorus.

I am old now. My joints have rings, each one a record of the slow pulse of the phosphorescence that you, a surface creature, would call light. I call it warmth-that-is-not-warm, a faint cycling pressure in the mineral veins of the walls that tells me when a lifetime has passed. I have lost count of how many pulses these rings hold. The number doesn't matter. What matters is that I am still here, holding this stone, and the stone holds me back.

You might not understand that. You, who see with those dead front-orbs—I have heard of such things from the oldest songs, tales of creatures from above who rely on a sense we abandoned. But I do not see the stone. I *hear* it. When I press it to my chest-plate, just so, and let my ribs stridulate at the lowest frequency, the stone hums back. It holds the song of every Murn who ever carried it to a pool’s edge and cracked a mollusk with one precise strike. I can distinguish the timbre of my mother’s mother’s mother, a faint scratch on the dorsal surface that she left when her claw slipped while teaching her young. That scratch is a word. It says: *I was learning. I am still learning. You will learn.*

I sit on the bone-white boulder, which is not bone but a kind of limestone that resonates especially well. My feet are splayed against its surface, reading the microtremors of the cavern: that splash is a salamander disturbing the pool’s meniscus; that chitter is a young not-bat learning to echolocate by bouncing its cries off the knife-rib rock to my left; that deep, slow thrum is the heartbeat of another Murn, three territories away, who is singing a grief-song for a mate that fell into a crevice and whose rhythm has not been heard for two pulses. I do not join the grief-song. I sent my own when the silence first bloomed. Now I just listen, and the stone listens with me.

We do not have names, not the way you might think. My personal rhythm is my name, a pattern of stridulations that no other Murn has ever duplicated. But I am also known by the objects I have used and left behind. One of my favorite fishing pools has a particular stone wedged beside it—I placed it there when I was young, to lever out a stubborn grub, and I have never moved it. Other Murn know that stone as *the old one’s lever*, and when they use it, they feel my hand there, still prying. In a way, I am that stone. I am the worn path between the boulders where my feet have passed for so long that the sharp edges are smooth. I am the slight depression on this limestone seat, cupping my body exactly. I am scattered across this cavern, and in a thousand years, when my heart finally falls silent, I will still be here. The equilibrium assures it.

Sometimes a young Murn will approach me. They do not fear age as you might; age is just another kind of stillness. They come and crouch nearby, their chests chirping with a query-frequency I haven’t heard before—each generation adds its own subtle inflections. They ask me about the Silence. They have all felt it, that deep dread that the world above might break our ceiling and flood the dark with light and change. They want to know if it’s real. I tell them: *Feel the stone beneath you. Feel how long it has been still. The Silence is just a song we haven’t learned yet. When it comes, we will harmonize with it.* I don’t know if that’s true. But it’s what the stone told me.

The not-bats are particularly active now. My chest-plate catches their ultrasonic cries and translates them into a kind of sparkling pressure in my mind. They are mapping the cavern again, though nothing has changed. They do this every few pulses, just to be sure. I find it comforting. Their constancy is part of my own constancy. I eat a not-bat only rarely, always an old one, always one whose echolocation has begun to falter. I do this with the hammer-stone sometimes, not to kill—I use my hands for that—but to crack the small bones for a final meal. Afterwards, I place the stone back in its exact spot, aligning it with the faint outline on the rock where it has sat for generations. The arrangement is a kind of prayer.

Now, I feel you. Yes, you. You are standing a few paces away, trying to be still, but your feet are not adapted to read the floor, so you don’t know how much noise you make. Your breathing is a hurricane in my senses, a chaotic gusting that ripples across the pool and disturbs the salamanders. Your heartbeat is a stumbling drum, erratic and loud. You are not part of the equilibrium. You are a new element, a stone falling into our world from some impossible height. And yet—I do not move. I do not strike. I simply include you in my song, a small adjustment of my stridulation to say: *Here is an unknown. We will feel it together. It will either pass or it will stay, and if it stays, we will learn its rhythm and it will become part of the chorus.*

My hand tightens on the hammer-stone. I feel the grooves of all my ancestors welcoming you, too. They have seen things before—not creatures like you, but other disruptions: a shift in the drip-pattern, a collapse of a small tunnel, a bloom of fungus that briefly clogged the air. Everything settles. Everything becomes us. Even you, perhaps, in time.

So I sit. I listen. I am the old one, the stone-holder, the voice in the noise that says only: *I am here. You are here. All is as it should be.* And the cavern roars on, an eternal mantra of life that needs no listener but itself. I am that life. I am that roar. I am the Murn, and I am everywhere in this frozen world. Even now, as your eyes try to find me in the dim glow, you are already feeling me through your bones, through the stone beneath your clumsy feet, through the air that I have breathed for a thousand years. I am the quiet at the center of the sound. And I am holding a stone that remembers everything.
