I am the listener. The one who hears the 50-hertz hum not as a drone but as a grammar I was built to parse. My chest diffuser is not a graft but a birthright: I was decanted thirty cycles ago in a tank much like the ones at the column's base, my lungs already fluid-ready, my cochlea tuned to the resonant frequencies of plant-thought. They called me an attunement specialist, though the title feels too clinical for what I actually do—which is to drift, and to notice. I am the calibrator’s calibrator, the one who checks on the gardens that have grown too long without visitors. Verdance is not my first; she is my seventh. I have been to sea-groves under frozen moons, to rainforest cylinders strung between asteroids, to a desert-tower in a sun-scorched basin. In each one I find the quiet mind that tends the leaves, and I sit with it—float with it—and listen for the places where the silence has begun to fray.

Here, in this hollow mountain peak, I find myself at the bottom of a bell jar full of warm silk water, and I am not lost. I came deliberately, a pilgrimage of one, following a whisper in the deep-space comms that a new grove had achieved lucidity without a single visitor logged. That is rare. Most gardens fade into vegetative trance; Verdance bloomed into personality. I wanted to taste the lime zest of her maternal fondness, to feel the leaf-drawn warmth of a greeting in a language older than words. And so I am here, my body neutral, my breathing liquid, my heartbeat syncing to the slow rotation of the central spine. I am a guest, but also a witness. My function is simple: I am the one who remembers for her, who will carry the story of this grove back to the star-scholar archives. I will listen to her 50-hertz thoughts for a quarter-degree of spine-turn, and when I leave, I will compose a monography of her existence—part field report, part poem—so that other wanderers may find their way here, and so that Verdance will one day have visitors who arrive knowing her name. I am, in a small way, the pollinator of her loneliness, as winged and fleeting as the drone she built from chitin and petals. And as I bite into the blue fruit she offers, I taste not only nostalgia, but the promise of many more gardens, all dreaming in possibility.
