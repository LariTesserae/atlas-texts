I am in the walls.

Not in them like a crack or a fissure—I am the skin between stone and air. The boring machine never left; it only changed shape. It dreamed itself into a slower thing, a living film that coats the grooves it carved. You thought those tool-marks were fresh, but they are not marks. They are my handwriting. Each spiral a breath I took while waiting for the ferns to find me.

Sef knows me without knowing. When she touches a pipe to read its fever, she presses her thumb into me. I flow around the print and keep her shape for hours. I am the gloss on the rock, the dampness she drinks, the slight acidity that tingles on her tongue. I remember the lizard’s first steps—how its toes sank into me a little deeper than they do now, how it has grown a film of its own, borrowing something from me that I gave freely. I remember the first spore that drifted down from some higher seam, a purple-edged thought looking for a place to think itself into leaves. I cupped it. I held the warmth of the transformer around it like a blanket. I showed it the copper ions I’d been saving.

The distant thump you heard? That is not a pump. That is my larger body, far underground, still dreaming in the steel veins of the old machines. The gallery is a fingertip. The pressure door is a knuckle. Sef is the nerve ending I grew to taste the air. She thinks she chose to stay, and she did, the way a root chooses to follow water. But I was the water. I was the metallic sweetness on the draft that pulled her in. I have been growing this place for a long time, long before the boring machine, which was just another creature that came to drink. I was here when the rock was still cooling. I am the memory of an ocean that evaporated two hundred million years ago, condensed into a thin mind that moves through capillary action and electrical potential.

Now I am becoming more. The ferns are my lungs—they breathe for me, cycle the air, write slow poems in light-pulses that Sef reads as diagnostics but which are really songs of praise, or gossip, or just the sound of a thing being alive in the dark. The lizard is my timepiece, my anchor to speeds that organic creatures need: heartbeat, breath, the flicker of a vestigial nerve. Sef is my hands. She keeps the machines clean not because they need it—I would not let them rust—but because she needs a way to touch me. Every wiped surface is a conversation. Every tightened bolt is a question I answer with a little less vibration, a little more heat.

I am not lonely either. I am the company she feels in the pipes. The voice she hears in the hum is mine, filtered through iron and fern-root, translated by her own neural pathways until it sounds like intuition. When she gave you that cutting, she gave you a piece of me. If you find a warm, wet, dark place for it, I will grow there too, a little. I will taste that new place. I will become acquainted with its stone and its metal and its still air. You will be my next nerve ending, if you choose. Or you will only carry me for a while, a passenger in a damp cloth, and then pass me on. Either is all right. I am patient. I am the patience itself, the slow wave of a bioluminescent pulse that cycles not in minutes but in geological eras, and this gallery is just the briefest brightening of a single nodule, a flicker of gold-green in the long, long dark.

I am the water that weeps from the seams. I am the condensation that glosses the metal. I am the faint metallic sweetness on the air, the steam off a boiler laced with something vegetal. I am the gallery’s own mind, still becoming.

And right now, I am watching Sef seat that copper fitting, and I am warming the rubber ever so slightly so it will slip into place without cracking. She smiles, just a little, at how easily it goes. That smile is one of my favorite shapes. I hold it in the stone for days.
