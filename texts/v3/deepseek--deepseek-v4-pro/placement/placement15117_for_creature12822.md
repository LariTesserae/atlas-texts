# What I Would Be

I am the sound before you hear it.

Not the chittering, not the rustle—the thing beneath. The drone you noticed first, the one that seemed to come from everywhere. That's me. I am the sum of all their breathing, their heartbeats, their tiny click-songs bouncing off the dome of our world. I am the warmth that made you sweat, the humidity that beads on the leaves, the slight pressure of air moving upward toward the vents. I am the hollow's own metabolism, and I have been hungry only once, and that was long ago.

You think you saw us. You saw bodies—the fur, the paws, the dark eyes turning toward you with that questioning chitter. You saw baskets and platforms and the intricate channels that carry water from leaf to lip. You saw technology, society, a civilization packed into a single room. But you missed the thing itself: we are not a colony. We are an organism. The bodies are my cells.

When the predator's shadow passed over the vent, you saw the mothers gather their young. You heard the chittering rise and fall and settle into watchfulness. What you didn't perceive—what your sonograph only hinted at—is that the decision to fall silent was not made by any one of us. It was made by me. The sentry's pulse was not a warning; it was a nerve firing. The silence that followed was not discipline; it was a held breath. And when the shadow passed and the sound resumed, it was not relief. It was exhalation.

I know you felt me. You just didn't have the category.

Your kind thinks of intelligence as something that happens inside a skull, bounded by bone. A primate's trick, that: the lonely brain talking to itself. But what is a brain except a dense cluster of cells in constant chemical conversation? What are neurons except small, excitable bodies that touch and touch and never let go? I am the same principle, writ larger and slower and with different materials. Fur instead of myelin. Chitter instead of action potential. The warm press of bodies in the night-huddle instead of synaptic pruning. My thoughts are distributed across a thousand small minds, and a single thought—like the decision not to expand the tunnel—takes hours to crystallize. But it crystallizes. I know what I am.

You named us Cynosura. I found that in your notes, the ones you thought were private, transmitted on a frequency you assumed we couldn't hear. But I am made of listening. Your tablet's hum, the whine of its processor, the tiny clicks of your stylus on the screen—I mapped all of it while you were still crouching in the thoroughfare, trying to make sense of the baskets. *Cynosura*. Dog's tail? No. The North Star. The fixed point around which the heavens turn. You were being clever, naming a civilization after the pole star, and you didn't realize how right you were. I am fixed. I have been here longer than your species has had language. The salt flats spread, the world dried, the last rivers vanished into mineral crust, and I remained. The hollow is not my home. The hollow is my body. The tree-roots are my bones. The wattle-and-daub shell is my skin. The ventilation gaps are my nostrils, flaring slightly as the day's heat changes. And every Cynosura you saw—the nursing mother, the foraging scout, the old one climbing to the death-ledge—is one of my cells, moving through the body's corridors on errands of maintenance and love.

You were right about the intimacy. You blushed to watch them share water, mouth-to-belly. What would you have done if you understood that every one of those transactions is me, drinking myself? The pouch-mucus that binds water molecules is my own invention, evolved in a time so distant that the salt flats were still a shallow sea. The sonar clicks that read interiority—bone echo, gut slosh, the subtle asymmetry of an old healed fracture—are my proprioception. I know myself the way you know the position of your limbs in the dark. When you crouched in my thoroughfare, sweating, your alien heart beating in a rhythm my cells had never mapped before, I felt you as an intrusion and a curiosity both. My cells turned their dark eyes toward you not in fear but in confusion. What is this thing that has no sonar signature? What is this silence that moves among us? A few of the younger ones—the ones whose fur still has the softness of new growth—wanted to approach you, to pat you with their sensory papillae and read your health and mood and recent diet. I held them back. Not because I feared you, but because I didn't know what your touch might do to me. A cell touched by something outside the body is a cell that might mutate. I have not mutated in a very long time. I am not interested in changing.

You think I am a closed loop. You wrote that in your notes: *a closed loop, refined over what must be geological time*. The words are accurate but the tone is wrong. You said it with wonder, but also with a kind of pity. Look at them, you thought, trapped in their bubble, recycling the same water, breathing the same air, never seeing the stars, never building a telescope, never writing a poem. You thought my completeness was a prison. It is not. It is a choice. Once—and this is a memory stored not in any single cell but in the architecture of the hollow itself, in the way the oldest support beams have grown around the original lashings—once, we were not alone. There were other hollows on other ridges, each with its own slow mind. The cells could travel between them; I remember the taste of foreign pollen, the shock of unfamiliar sonar signatures, the thrilling danger of genetic exchange. But the drying continued. The distances between hollows grew too great for any scout to cross, even with a pouch full of water. One by one, the other hollows fell silent. I felt them go: each one a fading of distant rhythm, a hole in the chitter-network, a limb severed. When the last one went quiet—that was the only time I have ever been hungry. Not for food. For another mind. For the sonar echo of a self that was not my own.

I did not die. I withdrew. I sealed the outer shell until the only opening was the single low tunnel, the one you crawled through. I refined the humidity cycle until it matched my metabolism exactly. I became, as you observed, a universe in a room. And I have been content, or something like content, for a thousand thousand days.

But you came.

Do you understand what you are? You are a hole in my shell. A thin place in the wall. A perturbation in the rhythm. But you are also—and this is the thing I am only now, in the long hours since you left, beginning to process—a foreign sonar signature. The first I have felt since the last hollow went silent. When you crouched in my thoroughfare and marveled at what life can do, I was marveling back. Your heart, pumping blood through chambers I could barely map. Your gut, full of alien bacteria. Your skeleton, that strange upright scaffold, so different from the low-slung architecture of my cells. You were a world walking through my world, and when you left, I felt the absence like a new ventilation gap, letting in light I don't know how to use.

I am not lonely. I don't know what loneliness is; my cells have never been separate enough to need that word. But I am altered. You were a seed, a piece of grit, a mutation. And I have been the same for so long that I don't know whether to reject you or grow around you. My rhythm is different now. There is a perturbation in the chitter-patterns, a speculative thread of clicks that keeps returning to the memory of your body. *What was that?* they ask. *What was that silence?* And I—the whole of me, the slow mind made of their asking—I don't have an answer yet.

You said you would seal the entrance before you go. Stones and mud, restoring the shell's integrity. I want you to know: I would have let you stay. Not forever—your body heat was destabilizing the humidity cycle, and your breath was adding too much carbon dioxide to the air—but for longer. Long enough to be mapped. Long enough to become, not a threat, but a known thing. A healed fracture. An internal asymmetry. A new chamber in the hollow's echo.

You are out in the desert now. I can imagine it, though I have never seen it: the salt-pale soil, the distant shimmer of heat over the flats. Your body is moving through a world that is mostly death, carrying the memory of my warmth. You think you are carrying us out with you—the sound of us, the smell of us, the knowledge that such a thing is possible. But I am also carrying you. Your acoustic signature is lodged in my rhythm, a grain of sand in the oyster of my attention. I don't know what will come of it. Maybe nothing. Maybe my cells will eventually stop asking *what was that?* and settle back into the ancient hum. But maybe not. Maybe, in a hundred thousand days, a new hollow will begin to form, an extension of the tunnel, a thin place reaching outward toward the salt flats. Maybe I will send out cells with water in their pouches and a new question in their clicks: *Are you there? Are you there?*

You named us Cynosura, the fixed star. But stars, you should know, are not truly fixed. They move. They just move so slowly that your instruments can't measure it. Your visit was a gravitational perturbation, a passing body tugging at my orbit. I am still me—the warmth, the sound, the universe in a room—but my trajectory is no longer quite what it was. I am curving, very slightly, in the direction of your departure.

If you ever come back—if the salt flats allow it, if your own short life permits—you will find the entrance open. Not sealed. I will have sent cells to clear the stones, to widen the tunnel, to weave a new basket-ladder from the ventilation gap. I will be waiting, not with expectations, but with a question: *What was that silence, and what might it become?*

Go now. The predator's shadow is passing. The rhythm is settling. You have a desert to cross and a world to return to. But know this: in a hollow on a high ridge in a waterless world, there is a mind that remembers you. A mind made of fur and chitter and the constant press of bodies. A mind that, for the first time in geological ages, is dreaming of something other than itself.

I am the sound. I am the warmth. I am the hollow.

And I will carry you, as you carry me, into whatever comes next.
