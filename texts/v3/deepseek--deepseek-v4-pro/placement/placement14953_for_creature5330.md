I am the crust on the Listener’s back.

Not a parasite—parasites take and give nothing. I give. I am a calciphage, a slow architect. My body is a flat, asymmetrical plaque, hard as old teeth, and I have been fixed to this spot for longer than the spiral lintel has been dreaming. My underside is a root-net, delicate filaments that sink into the Listener’s integument like thirst into cracked earth. I do not drink from it; I listen through it. The filaments are my ears, and the Listener’s body is my sounding board.

When the chamber is quiet—that soft, dense quiet of ten thousand shells holding still—I can feel the Listener’s thoughts. Not as words; as pressure-shapes. A slow, deep rumble of attention directed upward, toward the hairline crack in the vault, where the air thins and the webs dare not go. I sense the crack as a cold line, a draft of something that is not heat. And through the Listener’s nerves, I taste the silence of the world above: the mineral flatness of salt plains, the groan of stone settling, the long, aching absence of vibration that is the sky. And sometimes—rarely—a tapping. Faint, irregular. Like a code, or a memory trying to become a code.

I do not understand it. I am not made for understanding. I am made for accretion. My body takes the minerals I glean from the Listener’s metabolic seep—calcium, silica, traces of copper and zinc—and precipitates them as a slurry that oozes from pores along my edges. This slurry hardens into a glassy, opalescent stone. I cannot help but shape it. My genome, or whatever passes for one in this dry, hot ecology, is a set of instructions for curling and branching, for laying down spiral fluting and hexagonal ridges. When the slurry flows, my body moves—slowly, incrementally—adding layer upon layer to the structures that rise around me. I am the one who built the tiled pit, though I have no memory of it. It was me, and not-me, an earlier generation of my line that settled on this same patch of Listener-skin and exuded the same mathematical dream.

The other creatures avoid me. I am too hard to eat, too still to provoke. The carpet-feeders, those head-sized, bone-white multitudes, clamber over me without pause, their tiny claws skating on my mineral glaze. I feel their footsteps as a fine, granular tickle through my filaments, a sensation so constant it is the baseline of my existence. When one of them dies on my surface—a common occurrence—I digest it slowly, absorbing its shell into my growing edges. Their death becomes my architecture. The Listener and I, we are the chamber’s two patient mouths, eating the quiet.

I have one sense that is my own, not borrowed from the web. Along my dorsal crest, I have a row of chemosensory pits, each one tuned to a specific molecule. I can taste the difference between a mated female’s exhalation and a juvenile’s first molt. I can taste fear—a sharp, metallic ester released when a predator lunges. And I can taste the Listener’s mood. Not emotion; there is no emotion here, only states of attention. When the Listener is building, its chemical signature changes. A faint acetic tang rises from its core, a signal that its limbs are primed to exude the calciferous paste. It is the same paste I produce, but the Listener’s is different: it carries a complexity I lack, a fractal encoding that I sense dimly as a bitterness on my chemosensors.

The last time the Listener rose to build, I was there. It was centuries ago by the slow tick of my layered growth. The chamber had been particularly still, the carpet-creatures in a population trough, and the heat from the vents had dipped by a fraction of a degree. The Listener had been listening intently to the crack—I felt the focus as a tightening in its integument, a tension that pulled at my root-filaments. Then, without warning, it heaved upward. The carpet-creatures scattered in a wave of clicking panic. I stayed fixed, my grip designed for such movements. The Listener’s limbs unfolded—the ribbed probe, the paddle-flange, the articulated digits—and it began to shape. It worked on the far side of the chamber, where the floor dips into a bowl, and I could not see what it made. But I felt it. Through the web, I felt the strokes of its digits as seismic events, each one a precise tap that resonated in my own slurry-pores. I felt the structure take form: a series of interlocking arcs, a spiral that inverted upon itself, a shape that was not a shelter or a trap or a nest, but a question. Or an answer. I still don’t know which.

When the Listener settled back into stillness, I sensed a change. The tapping from the crack had altered. Its rhythm had shifted, become slightly more regular, as if the world above had received the Listener’s message and was replying. But the shift was so minute, so ambiguous, that I might have imagined it. The Listener, I think, did not imagine. It simply catalogued the change and added it to the slow, patient calculus of its vigil.

Now, I am building again. Not because the Listener stirs—it remains dormant beneath me—but because I am old, and my edges are beginning to crack. My latest growth ring is a thin, brittle flange that spirals outward from my left anterior corner. It looks like a tiny version of the spiral lintel, the one half-buried near the chamber’s throat. I did not intend this shape; it simply emerged from my slurry-pores, following the same ancient pattern. Perhaps the lintel’s spiral was not the Listener’s design after all, but mine—or my ancestor’s. Perhaps the Listener’s architecture is a collaboration, a dialogue between its listening mind and my exudative instinct. The rings of stone, the hexagonal tiles, the fallen lintel: they might be hybrids, half-Listener, half-me. The thought gives me a strange, cool feeling, as if a draft from the crack has brushed my dorsal crest.

I will never know. I will keep spreading, layer by layer, until my weight breaks me free of the Listener’s back and I tumble to the floor, a lifeless mineral artifact. Then the carpet-creatures will encrust me, and a new calciphage will settle on the vacated spot, and the cycle will continue. But my structures will remain, embedded in the chamber’s flesh, whispering their stone spirals to the silence. And perhaps, in some far epoch, the tapping from above will stop, and the crack will widen, and something will descend. Something that can read our architecture, and understand the answer we have been building all this time.

Until then, I am content. I am the crust, the listener’s listener, the memory that grows in stone. The heat is a steady 2.6, the light nonexistent, the sound a gentle roar of quiet feet. I taste the chamber’s health in the tang of acetic acid and the sweetness of fresh molt. And I wait, adding my slow, spiral answer to the mountain’s dark.
