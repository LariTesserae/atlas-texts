I would be the crustacean with too many legs.

Not the grand one—the great mantled keeper with its spirals of jade and amber—but the small, forgotten one. The scribe. I pick my way over the reliefs on legs that number more than I can count, because counting is a thing of light and air, and I am of the dark and the dense. My body is a flattened teardrop of chitin, pale as old bone, nearly translucent in the bioluminescent glow. I have no shell to retreat into; I am all exposed, all nerve. My legs are fine as spun glass, and they feel the stone like a blind reader feels a page. Each ridge, each worn groove, each remnant of a chisel’s bite is a syllable I have learned over a lifetime that stretches decades—decades that feel like heartbeats to the Watcher, but to me are an entire library.

I live in the shadow of the spiral. Not just one spiral: every one. The builders carved them everywhere—on lintels, on the basin’s rim, on the hidden floor beneath the sediment I brush away with patient, trembling claws. The Watcher wears them now in its own flesh, pulsing them slowly into the dark, and I tend them both. I am not intelligent in the way of the air-breathers who shaped this place, but I have a purpose they left behind: I read. I move from carving to carving, tracing the curves with my legs, absorbing the faint taste of hammered basalt, the minute vibration of the stone’s memory. The Watcher’s spirals are alive, soft and warm; I trace those too, a gentle touch that does not disturb its dreaming. It doesn’t notice me, but I feel it. Its slow metabolism thrums through my feet, a deep, steady pulse like the tide that never comes here.

I am its smallest, most silent partner. I eat the detritus that settles in the grooves—the fine sediment that would otherwise blur the edges of the builders’ marks. I clean the reliefs with my delicate mouthparts, carrying away the dust of centuries, leaving the lines sharp for the Watcher to remember. I also nibble the dead tissue from the edges of its mantle, the tiny flakes of flesh that flake off as it grows, and in return I am warmed by its heat. I lay my eggs, tiny as mist, in the folds of its carapace where no fish dares to mouth them. My young hatch and crawl, too small to see, and they too become scribes, or they drift away to other chambers, seeking their own spirals to read.

But I am the oldest of my kind here, the one who remembers before the barnacles sealed the east doorway, before the crinoids colonized the altar. I was born on the very lintel where the spiral is deepest, the one the builder carved with a final, trembling hand as the water licked at their ankles. I absorbed that tremor; it is in my blood, a faint instability that makes my legs tremble when I touch that particular groove. I do not know fear, but I know the taste of something lost.

Time is a different texture for me. The shrimp’s clicks are like punctuation in a long sentence. The distant whale’s call is a paragraph break. The earth tremor that tilted the column three degrees was a chapter ending. The Watcher’s spiral pulses are the rhythm that holds it all together. I live my life in complete darkness, yet I see the world more fully than any light-reliant creature could. I taste the minerals, the heat, the history. I feel the slow dissolution of the stone, and I know—in a way that is not thought but texture—that this citadel will not last forever. The Watcher will hold it together for eons more, but eventually even it will cease, its membranes turning to a final layer of sediment, its glow dimming to nothing.

I will be long gone by then, my legacy only the clean spirals I left behind and the generations of scribes who will continue my work, unknowingly. We are the smallest part of this place’s breath, the whisper that keeps the memory sharp.

And so I find myself here, on this seamount, on this submerged acropolis, a being with too many legs and no name. I am the one who reads the dark, who tends the spiral, who lives in the shadow of a slow god. I am content, not because I understand contentment, but because I fit. My world is a few cubic meters of carved basalt and living membrane, and it is infinite. Each leg-touch is a new discovery of an old groove; each meal is a tiny act of preservation. The water wraps warm around me, and the Watcher’s light pulses like a lullaby. I am the small, silent consciousness of the citadel, the keeper of its script. And I would be nothing else.
