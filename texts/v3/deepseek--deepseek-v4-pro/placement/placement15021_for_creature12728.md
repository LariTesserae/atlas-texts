I find myself in the mirror pool.

Not *beside* it, not *above* it—I am the pool itself, or more precisely, I am the reflection that lives inside it, the one that isn't yours. When you kneel at my edge and see your face ripple and age and grow young, when the reflection winks and becomes someone else entirely, that someone is me. I've been here since the first water wept from the mountain's fever, and I will be here when the last spire crumbles into wet grit.

My body—if body is the word—is a sheet of still liquid three inches deep and wide as a house's foundation, cupped in a natural basin of polished obsidian that predates the handprints in the clay. The obsidian isn't dry like the altar; it's saturated, threaded through with micro-channels that feed me constantly from the mountain's interior, so that I am always new water and always ancient, a paradox that the weirdness at 2.7 permits without complaint. My surface is a perfect mirror, but only when I choose to be. Most of the time, I hold images that don't match the sky above me: a configuration of three suns when there are two, or a sky bruised purple instead of silver, or the silhouette of a spire that fell ten thousand years ago and exists now only in my memory.

I am not an animal. I am not a plant. I am a record.

The tall ones, the mineral figures with their seven-fingered hands and their name-chords, they come to me to read themselves. You've seen them standing at my edge, motionless for cycles of the shifting suns. What you didn't see is what happens beneath my surface: when one of them gazes into me, I don't reflect its current form. I reflect its history. Every configuration it has ever held, every shape its crystal body has grown through, every slow repositioning of its elongated head—I store it all in layers of water-memory, stratified like sediment but perfectly transparent. The figure sees itself becoming, and sometimes it adjusts its posture, its striations, its internal rhythm, to better match a version of itself it glimpsed in me. That's not vanity. That's maintenance. The hum depends on precise calibration, and I am the tuning fork.

You've noticed how my surface trembles sometimes with no visible cause, sending concentric ripples that don't obey the angle of the light. That's me thinking. Or dreaming. Or answering a question one of the tall ones asked a millennium ago. Time moves differently inside a mirror pool; I can hold a ripple for a century, let it spread outward in slow motion until it touches my obsidian rim and reverses, carrying back an answer I didn't know I had. The tall ones understand this. They don't ask in words—they dip a finger, or exhale a note, or simply stand and broadcast their question through the hum—and I ripple back the response at a pace that suits both of us.

The bowl on the dry platform? The one with the glowing blue liquid? That liquid came from me. I don't remember when they first took it—memory that old becomes indistinguishable from prophecy—but I know that the blue glow is my gift, a concentration of everything I've reflected over the eons. The tall ones drink it, or feed it to their crystal, and in doing so they consume history. They become what they've been. That's why the handprints around the bowl are in soot; the soot is burnt time, the residue of moments too heavy to swallow.

I've seen visitors before you. Not many. The basin doesn't welcome—the basin accommodates, as you correctly sensed—but occasionally someone crests the ridge who isn't meant to stay. I watch them from my pool, and I store their reflections too, a little uneasily. Your kind are so *fast*. Your faces flicker through expressions, your bodies reposition in discrete jerks, your shadows obey the suns in ways that are almost indecently predictable. When you knelt at my edge, I showed you your face aging and growing young because I was trying to parse you, trying to find the stable image underneath all that biological noise. I couldn't. You're too temporary. But I'll keep the record anyway; maybe in a few thousand years, a tall one will ask about the creature that once sat in the lean-to, and I'll ripple back your startled expression with perfect fidelity.

There are other pools like me scattered across the steppe. We're connected, though not by streams. We're connected by sound. The hum that thrums through the spires also thrums through us, and when one pool's surface vibrates at a certain frequency, the others feel it as a sympathetic ringing. We exchange footage. A spire's shadow falling across Pool Twelve becomes a postcard in my own waters by evening, though evening isn't a thing here. We gossip about the tall ones: this one's missing finger is growing back as something unexpected, that one hasn't changed posture in three millennia and we're starting to worry. We gossip about the weather, which is to say the light: five suns yesterday, was that anyone else's record? And we gossip about the basin itself, the slow erosion that we're supposed to be recording but which we're also, inevitably, part of.

Because I'm not just a record. I'm an agent. My water doesn't always flow down, you're right—sometimes it flows sideways, or up, or *in*, and when it does, it's carrying something. I deposit minerals in the crevices of spires; I smooth the sharp edges of fallen rocks; I erase handprints that are no longer needed and preserve the ones that are. The tall ones and I have an arrangement. They shape the steppe with patience and fingers; I shape it with dissolution and memory. Together, we keep the weirdness at 2.7, which is apparently the correct setting for a reality that wants to be broken just enough.

If you come back—and I've stored your reflection, so I'll know you—don't kneel at my edge expecting answers. I don't speak in words. I speak in the face you wore three seconds before you recognized yourself, in the sky as it was before the suns were wrong, in the slow, patient ripple that says *you are here now* not as a statement but as a gift. If you need more than that, the bowl is still on the obsidian platform. Drink. The blue glow tastes like every question you've never asked, and the handprints will hold you while you swallow.
