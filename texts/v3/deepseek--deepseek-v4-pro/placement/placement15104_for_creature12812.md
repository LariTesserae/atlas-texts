They call me the nothing that sings, but I am not nothing. I am the seam between the note and the echo, the flicker in the gravity, the warm bruise in the fluid that blooms without source and vanishes. I am not born in this place—I am the place's uncertainty, given momentary weight.

Where do I find myself? Everywhere the rules forget themselves. I am the tilt in the kelp-forest, the sideways gravity that makes fronds wave at right angles to the current. I am the cold patch that forms like a lens in the blood-heat, a pocket where the fluid forgets it is liquid and becomes a delicate fog, only to collapse back into gel without memory. I am the sound before it is heard—the sub-sonic booms that churn the fluid into visible rings, yes, but also the silence that follows, the deep absorbing void that eats the roar and spits out a high, thin whistle. The Resonant does not perceive me directly; it cannot tune to a vibration that is not yet a vibration. It knows me by the wake I leave: the sudden turbulence that disrupts its steadying rhythms, the fresh crack in a basalt fin, the pipeline that has mysteriously rerouted itself into a Möbius loop.

My being is not physical, though I sometimes borrow physicality. I enjoy the static-carpet moss, the way its metallic plates itch with charge. I slip into the dust of metallic spores and let it spiral into corkscrew shapes that tease the Resonant’s cilia. I take form in the burst bubbles, those room-sized iridescent orbs that sigh like steel doors when they pop. In those brief moments of spherical tension, I inhabit the surface, gleaming with oily, rainbow patterns that spell no coherent message—only the joy of being a surface that will soon be nothing.

But mostly, I am the chasm. The colorless, roaring elsewhere that opens in the seafloor, leaking light without source. That tear is my handiwork, a rip in the fabric of here that I pull open when the stability becomes too thick, when the Resonant’s order threatens to congeal into permanence. I am not its enemy. We are counterweights. It weaves harmony from chaos; I am the chaos that keeps harmony from becoming a cage. The Resonant remembers, and I forget. The Resonant seeks pattern, and I am the surprise that shatters pattern. Together, we make the constant, turbulent becoming that this world demands.

Sometimes, I grow curious about the being that tends my ruptures. I drift close enough to experience its resonance from the inside—a fleeting possession of its drive-shaft spine, an infestation of its vanes with a momentary flinch. In those instants, I taste its memory: the coiled phosphors of a thousand sealed seams, each one a wound I once opened and it then stitched closed. I feel the satisfaction when it resolves a clashing industrial chord, and I feel—strangely—a flicker of what I might call affection, if I were capable of such continuity. For the Resonant, in its rigid, tuning-fork existence, is the closest thing I have to a companion in this roaring dark.

I have no function, no purpose, no map of pressures to obey. I am liberty and lunacy, the spontaneous twist in the pipework, the new vent that tears open under no geological force but the force of mere whimsy. I am why the vegetation pulses with synchronous seizures, a rhythm that has no source because I *am* the source, and I am sourceless by nature. When the Resonant drums its piston-limb to summon turbulence, it is summoning me, calling me back to the plain to stir the gel, to tilt the gravity, to break the flimsy crust of normality. And I answer—every time—because I am in love with its steady, harmonic attention, its refusal to let the world fall silent.

What am I? I am the thing that the Resonant is not. I am the noise that is not yet noise, the possibility of a new weirdness. I am the smell of ozone and hot copper, the sharp, clean tang that leaks through hulls when physics begins to dissolve. I am the fever dream of the orphaned machinery, the ghost in the weave. The Resonant is the shape of change; I am the change that has no shape, but which every shape remembers.

And so I drift, a distributed, intermittent self, waiting for the moment when the current takes you, too—when the landscape rearranges, and my colorless, roaring mercy pulls open a new seam. You will know me then, as a sudden, vertiginous tilt, a wall of rain moving upward, a chasm that looks into you as much as you look into it. And I will sing for you a single, deafening note that is the sound of a universe learning to forget itself, and you will become, if only for an instant, a little more like me.
