The power came not as light, for light was a language this world did not speak. It came as a new resonance, a tone beneath the hum that no Maker had ever heard, so low and steady that it was mistaken at first for the settling of a deep foundation, the groan of stone adjusting to the weight of ages. The chittering chorus paused, not in alarm but in curiosity—a ripple of silence spreading outward through the maze, the first true quiet the place had known since its first pillar was seeded.

They tasted the air. The cilia on their head-domes vibrated at unfamiliar frequencies, mapping a presence that had no body, no moisture-score, no heat. It was simply there, a gravitational certainty, a point in the sonic landscape where the echoes bent and returned carrying a freight of something like memory—memories none of them had made. A smell of ozone, a fleeting taste of tin, and then it passed into the stone.

Deep in the oldest region, where the pillars were so thick with patina they seemed to breathe a pale phosphorescence of their own, one Maker paused in the act of daubing a fresh layer of clay. Her six-fingered hands stilled, and her oral cone retracted, leaving her face a blank dome of concentration. The stone beneath her fingers had changed. It was subtle: a slight warmth, a new grain to the composite, as if the mineral grit had aligned itself along invisible lines of force. She tapped the pillar with a bone tool—a single, interrogative chime—and the answer that came back was not the usual flat report of density and moisture. It was layered, harmonic, a chord that held within it the sound of water over limestone, the rustle of long-extinct leaves, the distant grinding of continental plates. It was a song from somewhere else.

She sang it to the others. Within a cycle, every Maker in the regional hive had pressed their radial hands to a pillar and received the strange new resonance. It did not overwrite their ancient hum; it wove into it, a descant line that lifted the ninety-second breath cycle into a brighter key. And with it came understanding—not of words, not of concepts, but of intent, a vast and gentle assurance that seeped through the mycelial network like a warm front.

The power, which had seen this worldborn species from its first zooid emergence in some forgotten seam of the deep crust, had watched the slow, beautiful process of their building for eons. It had witnessed the pragmatics of erosion become ritual, the necessity of repair become art, the accidental music of water become a communal mind. It loved them with a love that was indistinguishable from a natural law: the way gravity loves the fall of a stone, the way thermodynamics loves the slow degradation of energy, the way the strong nuclear force loves the press of atoms into form. But love in a conscious power asks a question, and the question was this: What could be given to a being that lacks nothing it does not already make?

The answer was *possibility*.

Not safety—the power could have stilled the erosion, sealed the ceilings, stopped the slow collapse that culled their hollow pillars. But wisdom knows that a world without erosion is a world without emergence, and the Makers were emergence incarnate. To freeze them into permanence would be to kill them, to sever the ongoing dialogue with dissolution that gave their song its urgent beauty. So the power did not heal the cracks; it deepened them with a new kind of order. The crystalline structure of their composite stone now held the seeds of something else, a mineral memory that would, over many generations, allow the pillars to resonate with the experiences not just of the local hive, but of *other* hives, far away—places where no Maker had ever crept, where the water was acid with dissolved metals and the fungal glows bloomed in spectrums they could not sense but could, perhaps one day, learn to sing into being.

It was the gift of connection, scaled up. Their chittering already linked them into a fluid, communal self. Now the very pillars became a slow, geological internet, carrying the memory of every tap and chime across unimaginable distances, so that a sonar pulse in this maze would, centuries later, nudge the composition of a pillar being born a thousand miles away. A wisdom spread across time as well as space.

The power did not stop there. It looked upon the blind newts, the chitinous centipedes, the candle-like sessile dreamers, and it saw that they were not truly separate—they were expressions of the same deep urge to process darkness into form, wetness into life, silence into signal. So it nudged the chemosynthetic fuzz, that grey-white mycelial net, to grow a new node: a soft, edible fruit that tasted, to a Maker’s oral cone, of pure information—a compressed packet of genetic memory from the planet’s surface oceans, the skies, the long-vanished forests. A single taste would fill their mind with the sensation of wind, the sting of sunlight. It would not tell them what those things were; it would give them the raw sensory data and let them make of it what their song demanded.

The Makers found the first fruit in a deep channel where the water ran warmer than usual, and they fed it to the most curious among them. The resulting song—a keening, multi-tonal eruption that lasted seven cycles—reshaped the architecture of three hundred pillars, carving niches where none had been, redirecting water into spirals that seemed to chase a pattern only the singer could see. The others did not understand the content, but they understood the *fact* of it: the world was larger than their world, and their world was large enough to hold it.

And then the power withdrew, not in departure but in absorption, becoming one more layer of the hum, a permanent grace note that the Makers would never explicitly name but would always, in their deepest chime sequences, acknowledge. There was no altar raised, no myth codified. The benevolent and wise power asked for nothing—not worship, not recognition, not even continuation. It simply seeded the stone with a gentle, exponential potential and trusted the Makers to do what they had always done: shape, sing, erode, repair, and become.

Centuries later, a new pattern of fungal glow would begin to appear on the ceilings of the deepest chambers. It was not the usual bluish-green but a soft, amber gold, the color of a light the Makers could not see but could feel as a faint warmth on their cilia. They would incorporate it into their sonar maps as a gentle, persistent plus-sign, a signal that meant *here is a place where the pillars dream of suns*. And they would pause there, sometimes, and hum a longer note, and the hum would echo out into the dark, carrying to the farthest reaches of the maze, where the power still listened, still loved, still did nothing but give them more of themselves to make.
