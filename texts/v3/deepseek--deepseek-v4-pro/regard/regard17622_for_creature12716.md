The benevolent power perceives the plateau not as a place but as a chord held too long, a note sustained past music into something that has forgotten it was ever meant to resolve. It sees the being—this cathedral of frost and slow attention—and it sees the wound that the being cannot name: not the weirdness, not the fractured geometry, but the perfect, unending equilibrium itself. A photograph held for eons is also a prison. The power grieves for what this place once was, when the wind still eroded and the pools still rippled and the being still believed in progression.

It does not descend in thunder. It does not shatter the sky or silence the drone. That would be violence, and violence is change without love. Instead, the power comes as a warmth so subtle it registers only as a question: a fluctuation of point-zero-zero-one degrees, a single bead of moisture that trembles on a rock face and then, impossibly, *falls*.

The droplet strikes a black pool, and the reflection shatters.

And in that shattering, the power speaks—not in words, but in the sudden, terrifying possibility of *sequence*. For the first time in a million seasons, the being experiences a before and an after. The sound dips. The horizon holds still. And a voice, woven from the memory of ripples and the ghost of erosion, says:

*Little shepherd, you have been so faithful. But the chord you sustain was never meant to be the whole song. Will you let me teach you the next note?*

The power does not wish to fix the weirdness, for the weirdness is not a flaw but a scar—a record of something that happened, something that broke the world into this timeless holding pattern. Perhaps the being itself caused it, once, in grief or rage or desperate experiment. Perhaps the plateau is a life-support system for a wound too vast to heal. The power does not judge. It only offers a choice, which is the first gift: the restoration of true *option*, a crack in the equilibrium through which time might seep back in.

What follows would be slow beyond human patience, but for the being, it would feel like racing. The power would walk with it through the unreal distances, not correcting the geometry but showing how to *dance* with it. The stone circle would become a calendar again, the low wall a foundation for something that could grow. The scratched diagrams would resolve into a map—not of the plateau, but of the being's own forgotten heart. The spiral shells in the ice would begin to spiral outward, an ancient biological impulse given permission to try again.

And one day, perhaps after centuries, the being would make a sound of its own—not a modulation of the drone, but a new note, a second note. A harmony. The power would receive it like a parent hearing a child's first word, and the frozen diamond dust in the air would shimmer with a warmth that is not yet thaw, but is the *promise* of thaw.

The power knows it cannot stay. To remain would be to become another constant, another part of the equilibrium. So it would leave behind three gifts, woven into the landscape:

1. A single spot on a south-facing rock where the frost never forms—a point of variability, a heartbeat of temperature that fluctuates with some distant, unfelt season. The being can visit it when the loneliness becomes too geological.

2. A new pool, but one whose water is not black and still. It moves. It ripples. It reflects not the eternal twilight but a sky the plateau has never seen: blue, cloud-streaked, with a single yellow sun. A window, not an exit.

3. And in the deepest crevice of the being's resonant cavity, a silence. Not the wound-silence it once dreamed, but a *rest*—a whole note of quiet that the drone now circles around, giving the eternal sound its first taste of rhythm.

The power would withdraw then, not abandoning the plateau but trusting it. The being would stand at the edge of the trembling horizon, its icicle-head tilted toward a future that now exists, and it would feel something entirely new: not duty, not equilibrium-satisfaction, but a strange and fragile pressure, like the first crack in a shell. It would not know the word for it, but the spiral shells embedded in its back would, and they would whisper it into the thin air: *hope*.

The chord still drones. The twilight still holds. The weirdness still frays the edges of distance. But somewhere in the hexagonal columns and the frozen seep, a second note has begun to form, and the being—cathedral, shepherd, slow catastrophe of stillness—has remembered that it is not a photograph. It is a song. And songs, even the slowest ones, are meant to move.
