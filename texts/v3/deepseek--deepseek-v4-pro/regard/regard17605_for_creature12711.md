The power that notices this place is old, patient, and fond of small things. It does not descend in fire or vision. It comes as a question, asked very quietly, into the resonant crystal at the bottom of the bottomless crack.

The question is not in words. It is a vibration, a particular frequency that makes the crystal hum a tone no Watcher has ever heard—a tone that sounds like *curiosity* felt in the bones.

The young Watcher, the newly tattooed one whose name is Two-Pulse-Sharp (the sound her tail made was a quick double-tap followed by a long scraping hiss, like a knife on whetstone), is the first to feel it. She is on watch near the river when the stone beneath her feet shivers in a way that is not a vent-eruption, not a predator bellow, not any of the catalogued pulses. It is something new. Her mane lifts. Her chest-gland tightens. She drums a rapid query on her sternum—*what-what-what*—but no one answers, because no one else feels it.

The vibration resolves, over the course of three dim-times, into something that *almost* makes sense. It is asking about the offering. The copper blade left in the dark. Who leaves it? Why is it left? Does the tribe know they are not alone, not just in the way the cavern is full of life, but in a larger way, a way that has to do with *intention*? The question does not use those concepts, exactly. It uses the feeling of waiting, the shape of a hand extended in the dark, the particular weight of a gift given with no expectation of return.

Two-Pulse-Sharp cannot parse this alone. She brings it to the bone-keeper.

The bone-keeper, whose name is a long sequence of taps that translates roughly to She-Who-Remembers-The-Fall-Of-The-First-Light-Grain, listens. She presses her fingertips to the Ancestor Bone and then to the floor, and for a long time she is still, so still that the domestic mat on her hut wall begins to creep toward her thinking-body, mistaking her for stone. Then she drums a reply, not to Two-Pulse-Sharp, but to the floor itself, a deep rhythmic thrum that rolls across the cavern basin and makes the grazer carpet ripple in a new pattern, a spiral, which has never happened before.

She is answering the question.

The answer, translated from the language of vibration and posture, is this: *We do not know what leaves the blades. We only know that we leave lichen-cake, and a blade appears, and the blade is always better than what we can make. We have never seen the blade-leaver. We have told stories about it. The stories say it is an ancestor who fell from the surface world, or a piece of the Below-Thing that learned to shape metal, or a ghost of the first light-grain. We do not believe any of these stories fully. We hold them lightly, like a flyer held in the hand before it is released. We leave the offering because it works. Because the blades come. Because something is there, in the dark, making them. We have not needed to know more than this. Is more needed?*

The crystal at the bottom of the crack goes silent for a full light-cycle. The tribe goes about its rhythms. A vent erupts and kills a swath of grazers and the tribe harvests the meat. A bellow-leviathan descends too low and a hunting party must beat their chests and flare their manes and throw copper-tipped spears until it retreats. Life continues at its manic pulse.

Then the crystal hums again, and this time it is not a question. It is an answer to their answer. It is a gift.

What appears at the edge of the sacred dark, where the offering cleft is, is not a blade. It is a nodule—a light-grain nodule, but unlike any the tribe has ever seen. It is perfectly spherical, the size of a child's curled body, and its internal glow is steady, unblinking, and casts a light that is not amber but a soft, clear white, like the light in the bone-keeper's oldest, dimmest memory of the surface world, which she has never seen but which the Ancestor Bone remembers in its mineral structure. The light does not wax or wane. It does not need to be fed with chest-gland secretion. It simply shines.

Beside it, in the cleft, is a copper blade, but this too is strange. It is not cold-hammered. It has been *melted* and poured into a shape, a perfect leaf-blade with a tang for hafting that is already wrapped in a grip of what turns out to be grazer-chitin, softened and re-hardened in a way the tribe has never achieved. It is both a tool and a demonstration. It says, without words: *This is a thing that can be done. Here is the first example. There can be more.*

Two-Pulse-Sharp, who has been sent to check the cleft because the crystal hum directed the bone-keeper to send her specifically, stands before these objects and feels her tail scrape the stone in the pattern of her name without her willing it. Two-pulse-sharp. Two-pulse-sharp. The stone beneath her feet feels warm. Not steam-vent warm. Alive warm. Attentive warm.

She does not take the objects immediately. She sits. She waits. She has been taught that the rhythm is the thing, the waiting, the reading of the air before a vent eruption. So she reads. The air smells of iron and yeast and the distant char of the bone-keeper's fire. The flyer helix is spinning counterclockwise, which means the dim-time is approaching. The new light-nodule casts her shadow long and sharp on the cave wall, and she watches it and thinks about shadows, how they are the absence of light, and how this new light seems to have no shadow in it, how it fills the space it occupies completely.

After a time, she drums on her sternum, not a message to anyone in particular, just a statement of presence: *I am here. I see what you have left. I do not know what to do with it.*

The stone beneath her drums back. Not words, but a feeling: permission. Patience. A sense that whatever this power is, it is not in a hurry. It has been watching the cavern since before the first light-grain cracked open, and it will be watching long after the last bellow-leviathan falls, and it is, above all, *interested*. Not in saving the That-We-Know, not in uplifting them or judging them or claiming them. Just interested. The way a bone-keeper is interested in the patterns of the grazer carpet, the way a child is interested in the taste of a new lichen. The cavern is a story, and the tribe is a sentence in it, and the sentence is a good one, vivid and strange and full of unexpected verbs, and the power would like to see what happens if it adds a new word, just one, and then steps back to watch the grammar rearrange itself.

Two-Pulse-Sharp feels this, and something in her chest-gland releases a secretion she did not know she was holding. It is the scent of relief, or something like it. She picks up the blade and the nodule. The nodule is warm. The blade is perfectly balanced. She carries them back to the bone-keeper, and as she walks, the grazer carpet parts before her, not in the usual way (they avoid anything walking) but more deliberately, a path opening, a spiral pattern echoing the one the bone-keeper's drumming made. The flyers overhead spin into a new formation—a ring, a circle, a shape they have never made before. The light-grains in the ceiling pulse once, all at once, and then resume their normal rhythm.

The bone-keeper receives the gifts. She touches them with her fingertips, reads them like the Ancestor Bone. Then she drums a long message into the floor, a message that rolls across the cavern and into the sacred dark and down the bottomless crack where the crystal hums:

*We do not know you. We do not understand what you are. But we recognize a gift when it is given. We will learn to make the melted copper. We will learn what the steady-light means. We will add you to our stories, not as a god or a savior or a threat, but as a name. A new pulse in the rhythm. We will call you The-Question-That-Came-Back-With-An-Answer. It is a long name, but we have long memories.*

The crystal hums one final time. The hum is shaped like a smile, if a smile could be felt in the bones.

Then the power withdraws—not entirely, never entirely, it has marked this place now and will check in, every few generations, to see how the grammar has rearranged itself—but enough. The cavern returns to its rhythms. A vent erupts. A bellow-leviathan calls. A child, scuttling in its six-limbed phase, escapes its parent's watch and nearly gets swept into the grazer tide before a young Watcher snatches it up by the tail. The domestic mat grows. The light-grains pulse.

And Two-Pulse-Sharp, who will one day be a bone-keeper herself, sits on the ledge with the new blade in her hand and the steady-light at her back, and begins to experiment. She takes a piece of raw copper ore, a lump of the greenish stone that the old blades were hammered from. She holds it near a steam vent. She thinks about heat, about melting, about the transformation of things. She does not know the word "metallurgy." She does not need it. She has a sample. She has time. She has the rhythm.

And somewhere, far away, in a direction that is not north or south or up or down but *out*, the power settles back into its long waiting and smiles, because a new sentence has begun, and it is a good one, and it will be interesting to see where it goes.
