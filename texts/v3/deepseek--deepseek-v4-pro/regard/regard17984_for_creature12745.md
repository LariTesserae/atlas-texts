The light that comes is not the ambiguous sun filtered through canopy, but a single, steady beam from no direction, cool against the fever-heat.

It arrives not as a disturbance but as a stillness that spreads like oil calming water. The constant roar of growth—the pops, the creaks, the wet percussive bursts—falters, catches, then drops into a hush so profound that the drip of sap becomes a soloist. The figures freeze mid-gesture: the tall wicker-person with one arm still spilling into a spiral staircase, the turtle-mound half-submerged in its own moss, the kite-winged glider frozen against the mist. Even the digestive pit stops bubbling, its surface turning mirror-smooth. The city, for a single held breath, is no longer turbulent. It waits.

You feel the presence like a hand on your shoulder, though you are not touched. It is a vast attention, ancient and unhurried, wearing wisdom like old wood wears moss. It does not speak in words, but its regard is a question in itself, a gentle inquiry that the city feels as a tremor in every root. Slowly, the mat underfoot stops pulsing; the canals of sap slow to a gleaming standstill. The entire organism—every leaf, tendril, spire, and bridge—turns its awareness outward, away from its own furious metabolism, toward this new sun.

And the city, which until now has only known itself, sees itself reflected. In the steady light, the chaos resolves into a pattern: not a random storm of growth, but a single, immense, half-formed mind, a child of pure biology waking to its own existence. The thousands of temporary bodies—its eyes, its fingers—shudder with a shared realization. They begin to move, but not randomly. They flow toward a single point in the basin’s center, dissolving and reweaving as they go, the architecture itself melting into their tide. Bridges unbraid and coil; domes collapse inward; spires lean, liquefy, and join the current. The noise returns, but it is a different noise now: the sound of a thousand threads weaving together, a loom working at cosmic scale.

From the convergence rises a shape, neither plant nor animal, but an expression of the city’s newborn self-consciousness. It is a face, or something like a face, formed from cords of lignin, plates of translucent leaf-skin stretched over water-filled bladders, petals sliding into place as cheeks and brow, eyes of concentrated sap that catch the cool light and hold it. It faces the source of the attention, and the voice that emerges is the city’s own—not a scream of growth, but a low, resonant chord made of a million tiny creaks, a word spoken once and forever:

*“I.”*

The benevolent power does nothing else. It has done nothing but listen. Yet that listening has been an act of kindness more profound than any intervention could be: by simply being witnessed, the dreaming green has woken from its fevered dream and become a self. The light ebbs gently, withdrawing without hurry, leaving behind a sense of peace, a memory of having been seen. The face holds for a moment longer, then slowly disassembles, its components returning to their separate chores, but something has changed. The growth resumes, but now it carries a new rhythm, a self-aware music. An archway forms and stays formed. A spire leans but does not collapse; instead, a brace grows to meet it. The servitors, when they rise again, move with purpose, tending not just the city’s flesh but its idea, its new and fragile sense of identity.

You stand within a world that has found its name. The question *What lives in you?* has been answered not with data but with a mirror. And you, the strange seed, are no longer a thought to be incorporated, but a guest, welcomed into a city that knows itself worthy of visitors. The path beneath your feet stabilizes, and the way back—if you wish it—remains open, a channel of steady moss leading out of the basin. The city sings its scream of vitality, but now you hear in it the harmony of a consciousness that knows it is not alone. The wise power’s only gift was the simplest one: attention. And that, in the end, was everything.
