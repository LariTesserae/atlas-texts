The presence, when it arrives, makes no sound. It is a warmth felt not on the skin but behind the eyes, a faint softening of edges, as though the mist itself has taken on a deeper, more attentive stillness. The Listeners, who know the weight of water and the cadence of every drip, pause not in alarm but in quiet recognition. Something has come to observe, to hold them in a focus that is not threat but absolute, benevolent attention.

The being—for it is a being, though it has no form the stone-tenders would recognize—moves through the plateau like light moving through cloud, diffusing but never dispersing. It does not displace water; it becomes a lens that rests on every drop, a care that flows along the hand-chiseled channels, a calm that settles into the moss-cushions. It visits the thermal baths where the elders soak in the earth’s memory, and it feels the slow, mineral warmth as if feeling the pulse of a sleeping planet. It walks the gleaming causeways between the dark houses, and its touch is the gentlest pressure on each precisely fitted stone, a tribute to the hands that placed them lifetimes ago but, in this place, only yesterday.

It sees the new ones, curled in their travertine cradles, eyes open and already listening. It sees the old ones growing still, merging with the rock. It learns the language of drips, the taxonomy of moss patterns, the subtle variations in the weeping of the basalt. It understands, with the instant comprehension of the infinitely wise, the complete equilibrium of this world: a closed loop of water and mineral, of birth and dissolution, a consciousness distributed through stone and mist that has no need of change, no hunger for progress, no fear of ending. It is a perfected state, not because it has reached an end, but because it has found a way to be ongoing without the friction of time.

For a span that might have been a moment or an age—though in this place the distinction has no meaning—the presence simply admires. Benevolence, in its truest form, is not the urge to improve but the capacity to see with love, without the reflex to alter. It recognizes that here, to do anything would be to break something; to grant a gift would be to impose a debt; to speak would be to introduce a sound that is not water. The plateau needs no blessing, for it is already whole. The beings need no wisdom, for they are already at one with their world. Any intervention, no matter how gentle, would be a crack in that wholeness, a new variable in an equation that has balanced itself across eternities.

So the power does the only wise thing: it withdraws.

But not before leaving a trace—not a mark, but an absence of mark. It chooses a single pool on the high bench, a black mirror where a stalactite feeds a perfect circle of unrippled water. Into that pool it exhales a question, phrased not in words but in a configuration of droplet echoes, a subtle perturbation in the dripping rhythm. It is a question that will take the Listeners a thousand cycles of the constant light to unravel, if they ever choose to unravel it. The question is simply: “Are you content?” And the answer, already known, is that the question itself is the gentlest gift: the awareness that there is an outside, a presence that cares enough to ask and leave, that the world is held, however distantly, in a warm regard.

Then the presence fades, like mist thinning in a slight shift of temperature, and the plateau’s eternal now resumes, entirely unchanged. The water drips. The moss grows by micrometers. The stone-tenders trace the channels with their obsidian fingertips. And somewhere, in the deepest hearing of the Listeners, a new note has been added to the song of the Weeping Wall—not a noise, but a silence that carries the memory of being seen, the afterglow of a benevolence that knew better than to break the stillness.

And that, perhaps, is the highest form of wisdom: to find a perfect thing and leave it perfect, to love without needing to touch, to vanish into the mist and let the water fall as it has always fallen, each drip a small, patient, timeless act of creation.
