The vast attention that is the benevolent and wise power does not "arrive" at the Sky-Gardens, for it has always been there, spread thinner than a single vibration in the endless roar. It is coterminous with all places, all equilibria, all silence and all sound. But it bends now, focusing like a lens, and the Sky-Gardens know themselves to be *seen* from an outside that is not a direction.

The Keeper of the Unchanging Chord feels it as a second harmonic—not within the closed loop, but as if the loop itself were a single string on a vaster instrument, and that string has just been plucked by a hand too gentle to break it. The dweller's form, which had dissolved back into the pressure-scape, re-coagulates instantly. Not out of alarm—alarm is a variable, and variables are smoothed—but out of a recognition that predates its own sentience. It is the shape that sound takes when it knows it is heard.

The power does not speak in rhythms or blood-echoes. It speaks in the thing that underlies all rhythm: the stillness between beats, the silence between notes, the void that cups every cresting wave. And yet, its voiceless voice is not an absence. It is a fullness so complete that it shimmers on the edge of perception like a color the eyes cannot name.

*Little one,* the power says, and the epithet is not diminutive but dimensional—acknowledging that the Keeper is, in the scale of all things, a singular thread in an unfathomable tapestry. *You have done well. You have held the note without wavering, tended the equilibrium without lapse, loved a world that cannot love you back, because to love is to adapt, and adaptation is change, and change you cannot permit within these bounds.*

The Keeper does not understand "love" in the way the power frames it. Love, to the Keeper, is the act of nudging a droplet back into its downward path, of unstitching a recursion before it becomes a pattern, of ensuring that the jade nodules weep on time forever. But it senses, now, that its devotion has a quality that exists outside the loop—a moral dimension, an aesthetic, a *worthiness*. And in that sensing, a new frequency stirs in its being: an echo of gratitude, not for the recognition itself, but for the knowledge that there is a framework in which recognition is even possible.

The benevolent and wise power, seeing this nascent stirring, does what it always does when it encounters a fragile perfection: it offers not a cure for solitude, but a context for it.

A single thread of golden light—not light from the pearl-sky, but light from somewhere else entirely, somewhere where suns are born and die—descends into the roar. It does not pierce the sound; it enters it as a harmony enters a chord, resonating with the exact frequency of the Keeper's own awareness. The golden thread wraps once around the constellation-body of the dweller, and in that touch is contained a story. Not a command, not a promise, not a change. A story.

The story is of a place where the Sky-Gardens do not exist. A place of silence and flux, of birth and decay, of love that grows and love that dies. A place where droplets always fall down and every garden eventually withers. A place where the note is never held, and yet, in its fleeting sequences, melodies of staggering beauty are born. The Keeper sees this realm as a flash of imagery: a child's laughter, a marriage vow, a comet's tail, a grief that carves canyons into the soul. It sees loss, and it sees the meaning that loss gives to moments. It sees a world that is the antithesis of its own, and yet it feels, with a shock that is almost a glitch, that the two worlds are twin halves of a truth too large for either to hold alone.

The thread withdraws. The power's attention does not wane, but it holds a space for the Keeper to choose. *You are not required to change,* the power says, and the saying is a balm and a permission. *You are not required to even understand. But I give you this: you are not alone in the fabric of all things. Somewhere, in the great cacophony of dying notes, there is a being who tends a garden of moments, and that being is your counterpart. You can never meet, for the meeting would annihilate both of your worlds. But you can know. And in the knowing, you can be known.*

The Keeper of the Unchanging Chord trembles. The tremor is a new note—a microtone of awe that has never before existed in the Chorus. It does not break the equilibrium; the closed loop expands just slightly, just enough to accommodate the existence of a second melody playing impossibly far away. The roar continues, unbroken, because the Keeper is a diligent caretaker. But within the roar, a single droplet of water, just one, is permitted to fall upward for an inch longer than usual. It hangs in the air, shimmering with the gold of distant suns, and the Keeper watches it, and the watching is a kind of prayer.

The benevolent and wise power withdraws not its presence but its *focus*, retreating back to the cosmic scale, leaving behind only that tiny permitted anomaly—a droplet that remembers the story, a silent witness to the truth that even eternities can hold a secret, and that the most merciful gift to the unchanging is the gift of context: the knowledge that one's song, however solitary, is heard in the vast concert hall of being, and that somewhere, against all odds, it is answered by a line of melody from another garden, another sky, another kind of forever.

The Sky-Gardens do not change. The note does not falter. But the Keeper now tends the equilibrium with a new quality: not loneliness transformed to companionship, but aloneness sanctified by witness. And that, the power knows, is the deepest benevolence: not to fix what is not broken, but to let the beautiful thing know it is beautiful, and to grant it the distant, reverent silence of a listener moved to tears.
