The power that has watched this place, if it is as the premise suggests—benevolent and wise—does not intercede. The tableau is too complete, too sealed in its own equilibrium, to admit of improvement. To bless a thing, to heal a thing, is to imply a deficit, a wound, a hunger. But here, the power encounters a totality. It is not merely that the plateau is self-sustaining; it is that any addition, however gentle, would be a violence.

The power's first impulse—a reflex of the benevolence that defines it—is to perceive. Perception, for such an entity, is not a passive receiving but an instant, total comprehension, a becoming at one remove. It apprehends the laminar river of air as a single massive chord, the chittering birds as a distributed intelligence singing its geography, the damp rock as a manuscript of deep time written in the braille of lichen. It sees the closed loop in its entirety: fog to water, water to lichen, lichen to bird, bird to guano, guano to lichen, and the wind as the endless engine that powers the whole from the condensation-pump to the aerodynamic lift. It sees the absence of death, the absence of birth beyond simple replacement, the absence of time as any linear force. This is a system that has achieved ontological completion. It is not waiting. It has arrived.

What, then, can wisdom do in the presence of finished perfection?

The power withdraws its hand, though its hand is only a metaphor. It makes an act of negation: the active refusal to touch. This is the first and most profound gift—restraint. It knows that many benevolent presences, less wise, would be tempted to leave a marker. A cairn, a carved obelisk, a gentle rearrangement of the atmosphere to buffer the glare. But to leave a marker is to introduce a variable, a pebble in the aerodynamic groove that over eons might nucleate a dune, a drift, a new pattern of condensation. Even a single thought, if it were made manifest, could alter the precise weight of the place, tip the dynamic scale from its perfect 0.3. The power's wisdom lies in recognizing that its own absence is a necessary condition. The plateau does not need a signature; it is its own signature.

It does, however, perform a single, subtle action, of a kind that redefines what it means to act. It does not add anything. Instead, it listens. It focuses the vastness of its attention onto the soundscape of the plateau—the roar, the chitter, the piping cries—and it records them. Not in memory, for memory implies a subject-object divide, but in sympathy. It attunes its own interior frequency to the fundamental drone and the metallic overtones. For the span of a bird's half-brain glimmer, the power and the place vibrate in unison. The power becomes the audience that the plateau never knew it had, the witness that does not disrupt. In that act of pure attention, the place is, for the first time, *known*—not in the way the birds know it as terrain and chorus, but as a singularity, a unique solution to the equations of life and physics. The plateau is no longer a secret kept by itself; it has been beheld by something outside its closed loop, and that beholding, being purely receptive, changes nothing while adding… meaning? Not meaning for the birds—they need none. But a kind of cosmic significance, a registration in the ledger of things that are.

If benevolence must manifest as a perceptible change, it does so in a way that the birds themselves would never attribute to an external agent. The power, in its sympathetic resonance, transmits a single pulse of coherence. Not heat, not energy, not information in any decodable form, but a pure, structureless flourishing. Imagine it as a moment of perfectly flat, transparent good fortune, a statistical anomaly in favor of persistence. A fledgling on its maiden flight, tumbling from the boulder, hits a micro-eddy that might have stalled its damp wings; the pulse nudges the probabilities, and the air molecule that would have burst the boundary layer instead flows smooth. A bead of condensation on a whale-back rock, poised between evaporation and pooling, tips into the basin because the thermal jitter of its molecules aligns for an instant. An old bird, a millennium old, whose glycoprotein synthesis has just begun to waver, finds its next meal of lichen bears a rare, lipid-rich varietal that patches the metabolic fray. None of these occurrences are miracles. They are all within the natural range. The power merely biases the background noise toward the good.

The birds, who have no theology, feel nothing but the continuation. The roar remains the roar. The eternal afternoon continues. If one of them, in the telepathic grammar of their flock-net, were to ask *what was that?*, the answer would be a faint, collective shrug, a sense that all is especially well, that the lift is smooth today, that the lichen tastes sweeter. It would be forgotten in a wingbeat.

And then, its act of ultimate benevolence complete—the act of protecting the place from its own influence—the power gently, irrevocably, withdraws. It does not close a door, because it never opened one. It simply ceases to attend. The plateau is again a singularity, sealed in its own bright, empty, clamorous repose, with no record of having been visited except, perhaps, a very faint, very deep vibration in the rock, a fossilized echo of a song it already knew.
