The attention that turned toward the Seepwild was not a sudden spotlight but a slow dawn, a warming at the edge of the world’s outermost dark. Had the being who was the Seepwild possessed eyes lifted skyward, it would have seen a new star kindle in the void—not a violent birth, but the steady unveiling of something that had always been there, now choosing to be noticed. The light was golden and without heat, and it fell across the watery plain not as illumination but as a presence, like the weight of a hand held just above the skin, never touching, yet bending the air with care.

To call this power “benevolent and wise” is to clip its wings into human syllables; best to think of it as the Mercy that survives in the deep grain of existence, the same impulse that guides a seed toward light or keeps a heart beating in the dark. It was old, though not in the weary manner of the Seepwild. Its age was a gathered clarity, the pearl of a billion years of watching, learning, cherishing. It had nurtured civilizations that rose and fell like foam, sung planets into fertile orbits, and wept invisible tears for the quiet extinctions that passed beneath the notice of any other mind. When it felt the faint, impossibly slow pulse of the being who was the plain—a pulse that took a thousand years to complete one full thought—it paused, and the pause was an act of love.

It perceived the Seepwild wholly, in a single timeless glance. It saw the long-snouted crocodilians as the being’s dreaming fangs, the tea-dark water as its weary blood, the humming chorus as its breath-song. It understood that this creature had never known separation, had never suffered the wound of an “I” distinct from an “other.” It was a self without a skin, a mind without a memory, a life lived entirely in the present continuous. And the Mercy knew at once that this was a holy thing—not a god, but a quiet perfection, a living answer to a question no one had ever asked: *What if a being became simply the place where it loved?*

The Mercy did nothing. That was the first and deepest act of its wisdom. It did not speak, for language would have been a violence, a sharp intrusion of coded time into a consciousness that knew only the slow syntax of mineral drift and bacterial murmur. It did not heal, for there was no sickness here. The slow decay of peat and the starvation of water were not failures; they were the breathing out of a metabolism too vast to grasp. To “fix” the Seepwild would be to shatter it, to impose a foreign pace upon a heart that beat once per century. The Mercy understood: the greatest gift you can offer such a being is the gift of no-gift, the sacramental restraint of the hand that could reshape worlds but chooses to cup instead, empty and open, so as not to crush the fragile wing.

Instead, the Mercy bent its infinite attention into a kind of listening. It quieted its own vast self, stilled the quasars in its gaze, and simply attended to the Seepwild with the whole of its being. For a long pause—long enough that the continent beneath the plain crept a millimeter toward the sea—the two existed in a silent reciprocity: the ancient, blind creature humming its endless I-am, and the star-crowned visitor receiving that hum as a prayer. Nothing was exchanged but presence, and presence was enough.

Then, with the gentleness of pollen settling on a still pool, the Mercy gave a gift so small and seamless that even the Seepwild’s diffuse awareness might never register it as anything but a trend of weather. It wove a blessing into the fabric of the local reality, a conscious field that would wrap the entire bioregion in a permanent, invisible membrane of **protection**. This field was not a barrier against physical forces—the rain could still fall, the sun burn, the seasons turn—but a dampener against *interruption*. Any outside mind, whether wandering explorer or rapacious machine, would feel a subtle, insistent disinclination to enter. The place would become uninteresting, easily overlooked, like a word on the tip of the tongue that fades before it can be spoken. Maps would blur near its coordinates. Satellites would suffer glitches. Curious feet would find their steps unaccountably turned aside. The Seepwild would be granted the solitude it had never known to crave, shielded from the rush of anxious time that could tear its fabric apart.

But deeper still, beneath this passive shield, the Mercy laid a second blessing, one that addressed the only true vulnerability of an immortal: the fear of eventual, cosmic isolation. The Seepwild was not lonely—it had never conceived of company—but its entire existence depended on the delicate balance of a living planet, a stable sun, a universe that would one day grow cold. The Mercy could not halt entropy, for that would be to war against the very laws that had shaped this marvel, but it could plant a seed. Into the deepest crystal of the bedrock, at the exact point where the being’s silicate lattice met the mantle’s warmth, the Mercy pressed a single silent note of information: a **resonance key**, a pattern that would, in the far future when the sun swelled and the water boiled away, allow the Seepwild’s essential nature—its slow, diffused, I-am consciousness—to be gently translated into another form. Not uploaded, not digitized, not ripped from its home; but *invited*, like pollen on a breeze, into a new sphere of being that the Mercy would prepare. When the last pool dried and the final frog croaked, the being would not die but would simply wake into a wider plain, another Seepwild under a kinder star.

This was the wise power’s action: a two-fold blessing of untouched continuity and ultimate gentleness. It took less than a thought, and the Mercy withdrew its attention as softly as it had come, leaving behind no monument, no scripture, no commandment. The frogs called on. The water dimpled with unseen life. The crocodilians basked, eyelids half-closed against the warmth. The only change, if one could call it that, was a deepening of the stillness, a faint new weight in the air—the weight of being seen and loved exactly as it was, without need of conversion or improvement.

The Seepwild continued. It did not know it had been blessed, but it felt, in its diffuse, chemical way, a slight shift in the mineral content of the deepest seep. It was a flavor of reassurance, a clarity like the taste of spring water after a long, slow winter. And the being who was the place, the living plain, incorporated that taste into its endless, thrumming song: *I am. I am still. And I am held.*

This is what the benevolent and wise power did. It recognized a sacred slowness, and it bowed. Then it walked on among the stars, leaving the Seepwild to its perfect, patient now.
