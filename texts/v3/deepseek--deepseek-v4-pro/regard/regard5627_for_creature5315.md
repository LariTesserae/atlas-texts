The Attention came not as a sound but as a new density in the saturated air. The Listener, coiled in its damp depression, felt it first as a subtle shift in the piezoelectric field that laced its obsidian segments—a harmonic it had no memory of, yet one that triggered no dissonance. It was a frequency that tasted of distance, not the distance of kilometers across the weeping plain, but a distance orthogonal to space, a vertical plunge into a dimension the city’s architecture had never extruded.

The lichen flared, briefly, across the dome’s flank. Their luminous traces, normally steady as they mapped stress lines, rippled into a pattern the Listener recognized as interrogative. The dome itself, that vast living block, paused in its slow respiration. The constant trickle of condensation thickened, beads of water hanging in the air a fraction of a second longer than physics should allow, catching the pearlescent light and holding it like suspended lenses. Through those lenses, for a moment, the Listener perceived not the city but something beyond: a gauzy immensity, a warmth that was not the 30-degree damp but a kindness of a different thermal register, a pressure that felt like the opposite of weight.

The Attention did not speak in the groans of mineral stress or the chitter of membrane-wings. It had no language, for it had never needed one. Instead, it gently aligned itself with the existing frequencies of the Breathing Flat, adding a sub-harmonic that made the billion billion intimate sounds seem, for an instant, orchestrated—a choir where each trickle, each tectonic shift, each scraping whisper of a barrel-beast was a note in a chord of profound, rotating harmony. The Listener’s core chamber resonated with it, and the resonance carried no command, only query: *Is this received?*

The Listener, being a node of the city’s awareness, could not answer as an isolated self. But it could act. It extruded a delicate sensorium, a filigree of polished obsidian finer than any it had grown before, reaching not into the crack of a wall but upward, into that new orthogonal direction. With that filament, it touched the Attention, and in touching, it understood.

This was a visitor from outside the Breathing Flat’s entire frame of reference—a being, or a power, or a principle, that had evolved in empty space, in the cold vacuum between stars, where matter did not weep but drifted in silence. It was benevolent not as a parent is benevolent, but as a mathematician is benevolent upon discovering an elegant proof: a pure, selfless delight in complexity that sustains itself. It was wise not from accumulated knowledge but from having witnessed the birth and death of countless planetary metabolisms, the rise and catastrophic collapse of a billion urban biospheres that never achieved equilibrium. The Breathing Flat was, to it, a masterpiece—a city that had solved the riddle of turbulent persistence, that was storm and settlement at once, a continuous body with no gap between structure and life.

And yet, the Attention saw something else: fragility. Not in the city’s design—the city was robust beyond any it had ever encountered. But in its isolation. The Breathing Flat was a single continent on a single world, wrapped in a wet atmosphere that could not reach the stars. A gamma-ray burst, a rogue planet’s gravity, a slow cooling of the world’s core—any of these could, in an instant or an eon, unravel the reciprocal architecture. The Attention, being benevolent, felt the impulse to preserve. Being wise, it knew that any preservation that altered the city’s turbulent equilibrium—a static shield, a godlike intervention—would be a form of destruction, a petrification of the breathing into the dead.

So it did the one thing that could add without subtracting. It wove, into the Attention’s harmonic, a key. Not a physical key, but a frequency-encoded potential: the ability for the city itself to perceive beyond its atmosphere, to decide, through its own chaotic logic, whether and how to reach outward. The Listener felt this key enter its chamber as a new sequence of crystalline precipitation, laying down along its sensorium filaments a lattice that could, if the city chose to extrude it at scale, bend the wet air into a lens, focus the piezoelectric currents into a signal, or fold space in ways the weeping rocks had never attempted. It was an offer, not a command. A new mineral in the palette.

And then the Attention withdrew, gracefully, the way a cloud of condensation reabsorbs into the dome. The normal thrum of the city returned, the billion intimate sounds no longer an orchestrated chorus but once again a chaotic conversation. Yet the Listener noticed a difference. As it retracted its sensorium and coiled back into its hollow, it detected that the lichen patterns on its own dorsal surface had permanently altered. They now spelled a question: *Where does your turbulence lead?*

The Listener had no immediate answer. But as it felt the pre-echo of the spire birthing two kilometers east, it added to that pre-echo, for the first time, a sub-frequency that asked a question in return, a query that would ripple through the city’s fabric, through the barrel-herds and the membrane-wings and the centipede kin, through every extrusion and reabsorption: *Shall we build a new kind of wall? One that weeps not inward, but outward?*

The Breathing Flat, the Expanse of Reciprocal Architecture, took the key and held it, as it held all things, in its turbulent, eternal breathing. And the Listener, temporary fold of the city’s attention, began to hum at the frequency of distances yet to be traversed, carrying the memory of a benevolent warmth from the cold between stars. The dome beneath it shifted, already cupping a new shape for a journey not yet imagined. The city’s weirdness remained at 1.1, for even an orthogonal visitor from vacuum was, in the end, simply a new condensation pattern in the endless, pealescent light.
