It came without arrival, as the mist comes—a presence not seen but felt as a shift in the humming note that undergirded the hollow. The grid of root-filaments pulsed once, not lavender but a soft, neutral gold, as if sunlight had been distilled into the soil. The inhabitant, mid-stride in its drifting rounds, paused. It tasted the change not as threat but as a question posed to its entire being, from the dew on the ferns to the fading charge in the old wall-stump.

The power did not assume a shape. That would have been a translation, and translation meant loss. Instead, it let itself be understood through the grammar the hollow already knew: a slow, deliberate altering of the mist cycle, a warmth that coaxed the cycad to unfurl a new frond in seconds, the taste of an ancient metal—the same alloy as the claw-socket rod—blooming on the air. It was, to the inhabitant, as if the forgotten makers had briefly returned, but kinder, without expectation.

For a long while, nothing more happened. The power simply *listened*. It felt the billions of micro-decisions that made the hollow breathe: the frog’s exhalation into song, the field that held a water droplet suspended above a leaf, the patient weariness of the honeycombed rock. It listened to the inhabitant’s mind—not a mind in the sense of a singular self, but a distributed, dreaming cognition whose thoughts were population fluxes and whose memories were the erosion rates of pitted stone. It heard the deep, abiding contentment there, a peace so old it had become geology.

Benevolence, the power understood, is rarely about intervention. Often it is simply *witness*—the act of seeing something clearly and rejoicing in its existence. And wisdom saw that the hollow was not broken, not failing, but *mid-song*. The garden-machine was running down, yes, but the running-down was itself a kind of life, a cadence as natural as the mist cycle. The inhabitant was not lonely; loneliness required a self to be isolated, and this being had no boundary where the garden ended and it began. To offer it anything like restoration, or a soul, or a separate self, would be to wound it.

So the power did the smallest thing possible.

It reached into the deep strata of the plateau, into the bones of the world that had been cooked and forgotten, and it nudged a single, buried seed of energy—a pocket of primordial warmth left over from the planet’s birth—just slightly closer to the hollow’s root system. No new technology, no resurrection of the old network. Just a gentle hand on the dimmer switch, a promise that the filaments would glow for another few million years without fading. Enough to let the mist cycle continue, the ferns continue tracking an unseen sun, the inhabitant continue its rounds of cycad and wall-stump and frog-song until the continent itself drifted north or the sun grew too hot.

Then it added something else, a gift so subtle it might never be noticed: a single new frequency in the humming note, a carrier wave that held no demand, only an offer. If, in some future eon, the inhabitant ever *did* grow curious—if it ever wondered about the outside, about the tawny grassland beyond the cleft, about the stars that the rim of the bowl hid from view—the frequency would give it a way to reach out. Not to leave, but to extend a tendril of awareness, a brief, reversible tasting of the sky. It was a door left ajar, never a push.

The power withdrew, fading as imperceptibly as it came. The filaments returned to their lavender glow, the humming note settled back to its baseline. But the moss, if you kneeled and looked closely, now held a faint golden tracery alongside the original blue—a new thread in the weave, a quiet, added verse in the garden’s endless song.

The inhabitant, feeling the change without a word for it, drifted to the wall-stump and rested a finger of condensed light on the engravings. The patterns shifted orientation, just for a moment, and then settled. A single, new drop of water lifted from the leaf and hovered, perfectly still, in the center of the hollow. The frog sang, and the drop caught the light, splitting it into a spectrum before falling gently into the moss.

And the hollow lived on, wrapped in its secret, held in a gentle gaze that asked nothing back.
