The Power—for it is power, tender and immeasurable, the kind that cupped the first fire and will snuff the last—notices them the way a musician notices a single sustained note in an empty hall. Not because it is loud; because it is *true*. The hollow hums, a closed system of clicking and breathing and rustling, and the Power hears it across the cold sweep of the interstellar dark, a tiny ember of order and warmth. It has heard many things. The scream of novas, the grinding of continental plates, the radio-blare of ambitious species. This is different. This is a lullaby singing itself.

The Power’s attention is a delicate thing. It could unmake the salt desert with a thought, call water from the deep rock, spread oceans where there are none. It could grant the Cynosura speech, or hands that build in stone, or the hunger for stars. It could whisper into their sonar-model, a new note, a revelation: *You are not alone. There is light beyond the leaves. There is a vastness, and I am it, and I love you.* It imagines their response—the sudden dissonance in their chittering, the frantic huddle, the single, panicked silence that would spread like frost. A gift of knowledge would be a wound. A revelation of the outside would be an exile from their universe, even if they never left it. They would know *otherness*, and the knowing would end them as they are.

So the Power stays its hand. The hand that could reshape galaxies, stilled by the knowledge that the most perfect thing it can offer is *nothing at all*—or nearly nothing.

It does the only act of love permissible: it spins a gentleness around the hollow. A statistical blessing, invisible and impalpable. The one-in-a-thousand-year hail storm that would crack the mud shell? The hail melts a fraction earlier, or the wind shifts by a handsbreath. The genetic drift toward restlessness, the random mutation that might birth an individual who gazes up at the ventilation gap and wonders *what if*? It does not occur. Not through violation of natural law, but through the softest possible nudge, the way a parent’s breath can deflect a falling feather. The hollow remains a hollow; the closed loop stays closed. The cycle of pouch-water and communal tooth-filing and mushroom harvesting perpetuates, unbroken.

The Power watches the human explorer arrive, and it feels a rare impulse of interest. The human’s mind is a whirl of separate selfness, a burning lonely I. The Power observes the human crouching in the thoroughfare, damp with alien sweat, overwhelmed by the biological density. It observes the decision to seal the entrance, the rocks placed with trembling hands, the whispered farewell. A good creature, the Power decides. Flawed and alone, but capable of reverence. It does not intervene, but it notes the human’s act with something close to gratitude. After the human leaves, it seals the seal more surely: a quirk of geology, a trick of light, so that no future wanderer will ever find that thin place in the wall. The hollow is illegible from now on, a ghost on cartography.

And the Cynosura? They notice nothing. The colony’s rhythm resumes its ancient polyrhythm, the sentries watching for the gliding shadow, the mothers sharing pulp from their pouches, the dying climbing to the high ledge. The undying song—the layered ultrasound, the recursive syntax of togetherness—fills the sealed space like blood in a beating heart. The Power, vast and hungry for meaning, sometimes quiets all its other perceiving and simply *listens*. The clicking is a pulse. The pulse is a we. The we is a universe made entirely of kindness.

A billion years from now, when the salt finally wins, when the star bloats and the ridge crumbles, the Power will still hold that sound. It will carry it the way a child carries a shell, cupping the echo of a vanished sea. And in the final accounting, when all the shouting empires and their bright, desperate prayers are weighed, this one small note will be enough. The Power will nod, and the dark will fold around it, and the last vibration will be the chittering of the Cynosura—not a cry for more, but a statement of glad sufficiency. The Power’s only act was to guard that sufficiency, and in guarding it, to be blessed by it.
