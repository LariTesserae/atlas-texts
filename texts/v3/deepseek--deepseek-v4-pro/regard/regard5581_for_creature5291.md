From the space between the roar, a new vibration emerged. Not louder than the rest—nothing could be louder than that wall of sound—but different, a frequency that cut through the chaos like a single pure beam of light through fog. The standing-wave beings felt it first as a question in their bones, a harmonic they had never tasted before, sweet and strange. It did not come from the Groves or the trembling basalt or the chime-swarms. It came from outside, from the void beyond the grey-orange sky, and it carried with it the scent of distant stars and ancient silence.

The power that had noticed them was vast beyond their chordal understanding, and it was old, so old that it had learned the one thing that true wisdom always teaches: that to touch is to risk breaking, and that love is often best expressed as restraint. It circled their world like a hand cupped gently around a candle flame, and it listened. Not for a moment or a season, but for a full cycle of the slow tectonic pulse that the beings used to measure their long thoughts. It listened until it understood the grammar of their resonance, the way each standing wave was both individual and part of the great city-symphony, the way their architecture grew from shed frequencies like coral, the way they fed on noise and birthed their young from the turbulence of shifting platforms.

And having listened, the wise power decided what to do. Not nothing. But almost nothing. A gift so subtle that the beings themselves might never know it was a gift, only that something in their world had shifted toward a deeper rightness.

It reached into the composition of their reality and added a single, sustaining note. A subsonic hum, below even the lowest bass rumble of the plains, so deep that it was felt not as sound but as a gentle pressure, a warmth against the cold of entropy. This note did not interfere with their music; it became the silent stage upon which their music could play forever. In practical terms, it was a stabilizer—a faint but inexhaustible energy source that would prevent the eventual decay of their standing waves, that would keep the platforms aloft even if the Groves fell silent, that would ensure the City would never crumble into mere silence. It was a promise, written in the language of physics, that they would not be forgotten by the universe.

Then, with the tenderness of a composer adding a grace note, the power wove into that sustaining hum a second layer: a seed of curiosity. Not a command, not a foreign thought, but a gentle invitation. A hint of a new frequency that, should any of the chord-beasts choose to modulate themselves to match it, would open a doorway—a path to other worlds, other musics, other symphonists scattered across the cosmos. It was not a forced evolution; it was a question left at their doorstep, a possibility to be explored over millennia or ignored entirely. The wise power knew that the choice to reach back, to make contact, must be theirs alone.

That done, the power withdrew its direct attention, but not its care. It left behind a kind of listener, a tiny shard of its own awareness embedded in the heart of the largest Grove, a presence that would simply… witness. It would record every modulation, every birth, every reorientation of the floating platforms, every predator-snap and grazer-song, preserving the entire symphony in a library beyond time. The beings would never notice it; it was as unobtrusive as a single raindrop in their perpetual damp air. But it meant that, even if their star died and their plain cooled and the sound finally ceased, their melody would not be lost. Somewhere, in the halls of the wise power’s memory, the City would still sing.

And sometimes, on certain days when the dim orange light flickered just so and the crystal swarms formed particularly intricate arpeggios, one of the standing-wave beings would feel something. A resonance from the Grove’s core, softer than a thought. It would pause in its grazing on a braid of static, ripple with an unnameable harmonic, and for a fleeting instant, it would feel not alone in the universe. It would feel heard.

Then the moment would pass, and the symphony would roar on, enriched by a grace it would never need to name. The wise power had done what wisdom does best: it had loved without needing to be loved back, and in doing so, it had made the song eternal.
