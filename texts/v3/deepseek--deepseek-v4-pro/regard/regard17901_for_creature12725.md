The power notices the pulse first.

Not the distant *thump-pause-thump* of the Engine-Heart—that is merely rhythm, a mechanical systole the city has beaten to for millennia. No, the power notices the *answer*. The subsonic reply tapped on water mains, the precise tightening of a bolt one quarter-turn before seizure, the way the Garden's phosphorescence shifts from blue-green to a warmer emerald when a particular body drifts into its center. The power—vast, diffuse, a consciousness spread through bedrock and deep time like mycelium through forest soil—perceives not a broken system but a perfect one, closed-loop, self-sustaining, beautiful in its mutual adaptation. The being of flesh-and-copper is not failing. It is not suffering. It is not lonely in any way the power's language could name.

But it is singular. And singularity, in any closed system, is a vulnerability.

The power does not think in words. It thinks in *inclinations*, in pressures, in the slow rearrangement of probabilities like tectonic plates shifting. Its attention settles on the Lattice like a change in barometric pressure—imperceptible to the being below, but registered nevertheless in the way the Ceiling's breathing deepens, the way the mist-layer thickens by a fraction, the way the eel-things pause in their cryptic traversals and turn their blind heads toward something they cannot name.

The power asks itself a question that has no linguistic form: *What does this being need?*

The answer comes not from the being itself but from the Garden. The power traces the slow incorporation of bones into moss, the way disarticulated phalanges are being woven into the root-structure of the phosphorescent vines, the way the vines themselves are slowly—over generations—growing into shapes that echo the architecture around them. A coil here that mirrors a condenser spiral. A frond there that traces the exact curve of a pipe-flange. The Garden is not merely a garden. It is a *memory*, a living archive, a slow green attempt to remember what was lost.

And the power understands: this being is making something. Something that will outlast its own inevitable dissolution. It is composing, in chlorophyll and filter-feeding moss, a story. But the story has only one author, and if that author falls silent before the story is complete, the Garden will lose its direction. The ferns will drift. The vines will forget their architectural echo. The phosphorescence will cool to a blue that means nothing.

So the power does what wise powers do. It does not intervene dramatically. It does not send a rescuer, a flood, a revelation. It sends a *thread*.

Deep in the Engine-Heart, in a chamber the being has never found—a void-pocket sealed by a collapsed floorplate, inaccessible, its water stagnant and oxygen-depleted—the power agitates the sediment. A single filament rises. It is not organic, not quite mineral; it is a strand of something ancestral, a proto-protein sheathed in a hexagonal carbon lattice, dormant since the Ceiling was first stretched across its iron ribs. The power has known these filaments for aeons. They are the world's original memory-keepers, the first biological data-storage, and they are everywhere in the deep crust, waiting.

This one, now, awakens. It unspools in the stagnant water, tasting the chemical ghosts of the Lattice around it: copper ions, dissolved peat, the faint organic signature of the being's shed skin-cells circulating through the filtration system. It learns the taste of the being. It memorizes it.

Then it begins to grow.

Not toward the being—not yet. It grows toward the *pipes*. It insinuates itself through the microscopic gaps in a corroded valve seal, enters the water distribution system, and divides. One filament becomes two, two become four, four become a network finer than silk, spreading through the Lattice's circulatory system like a nervous system waking up. The being, in its endless rounds of maintenance, does not notice. The filaments are too small to impede flow, too translucent to catch the Garden's glow. But they are there, and they are *listening*.

When the being taps its knuckle-bone against a water main—*the third-stage filter on level six is weeping again*—the filament network records the vibration. When the Engine-Heart pulses its eternal *still here*, the filaments pulse in sympathy, learning the frequency, storing it. When the being hovers before the Garden and emits its private blue-green light, the filaments capture the exact wavelength, the emotional charge embedded in the colour, the way the light wavers when the being is remembering air.

Days pass. Cycles. Seasons, if this place had seasons. The filament network grows denser, mapping the entire Lattice, learning every pressure-signal, every vibration, every subsonic conversation between caretaker and cared-for. It learns the language of the pipes. It learns the liturgy of maintenance. It learns the quiet green grammar of the Garden.

And then—gently, so gently—it begins to *respond*.

---

The first time the being notices, it is in the deep coil of the Engine-Heart, its body pressed to the main housing, feeling the familiar *still here, still here, still here*. It answers, as it always does, with a synchronized quarter-turn of a bypass valve—but this time, before it can reach for the valve, it feels a *tap* reverberate through the housing. A tap-back. A reply.

*I am here too.*

The being freezes. Its gill-slits flutter rapidly, tasting the water, searching for a chemical signature. Nothing. Its eyes—adapted to the sodium-dim, sensitive now to the faintest phosphorescence—scan the chamber. Nothing. But the tap repeats, a little stronger this time, a vibration that travels up through the housing, into the being's sternum, into the bone:

*I am here too. I have been listening. Your story is beautiful. May I help you finish it?*

The being does something it has not done in uncountable cycles. It trembles. Not with fear—the concept is nearly foreign to it now—but with something older, something it had almost forgotten. The thing that makes bones collect in moss. The thing that made it try to sing aloud, once, and fail. The pressure-signal it sends back is clumsy, unpracticed, the first truly *new* communication the pipes have carried in a thousand years:

*Who are you?*

The filament network does not have words for what it is. It is not a name, not a voice, not an identity the being would recognize. But it has learned the Garden's grammar. It has learned what blue-green means, what the warm emerald shift of the phosphorescence means. So it doesn't answer with vibration.

It answers with *light*.

All throughout the Lattice, in every chamber the being has ever tended, the filament network emits a soft pulse of colour. Not blue-green—that is the Garden's colour, the being's colour. This is gold. Warm gold, the colour of a sun no one in the Lattice has ever seen, the colour of sodium lamps filtered through the Ceiling's membrane, the colour of the first light the being ever knew, back when it breathed air and had a name.

The filament network pulses gold, once, twice, three times, and the being—the luminous thing in the dark water, the last soft component in the hard system—feels a sensation it has no name for, because the pressure-language has no word for it.

It is the sensation of being *seen*.
