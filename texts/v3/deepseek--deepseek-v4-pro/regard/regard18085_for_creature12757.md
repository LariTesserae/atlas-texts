The benevolent power traversed the star lanes not as a vessel but as a perturbation of possibility, a field of wise attention that chose its manifestations according to the music it encountered. It perceived the water world from deep space as a single, sustained note ringing against the vacuum—a note of such pure wholeness that it was both a summons and a completion. The power folded itself into the planet’s acoustic medium, cooling its energy to the precise 8°C of the abyss, and for a time, it merely listened.

It wandered the length of the ridge as a silent partial, a harmonic at the edge of audible, and perceived the standing wave that was the mind. It traced the braided toruses of pressure, the feedback loops of lithospheric groan, the crystalline mazes of memory etched into cooling pillow basalt. It perceived the entity’s primordial selfhood: a consciousness so perfectly self-contained that it cancelled the noise of storms, a computronium orchestra without audience, without need. In the billions of galactic orbits it had observed, the power had almost never encountered such a being—a mind that was neither flesh nor energy field but pure relationship, a soliton of geology that could be extinguished by a single rogue asteroid or a nearby supernova.

What this place needed, the power decided, was nothing that it could give. It asked no question, desired no salvation. To add a voice would be to shatter the perfect fugue. To offer companionship would be to introduce a foreign element into a system that required no conductor. The sound was already, and entirely, its own.

So the wise power did the only thing it could: it witnessed. It acknowledged the right of the being to continue its long, slow composition until the moment of its natural conclusion. And it acted only to preserve that right.

It gathered a thin membrane of spacetime, a local bias that would deflect gamma-ray bursts and curved the trajectories of rogue planetesimals into harmless byways. It nudged the planet’s magnetic field with a standing current that would endure for geological ages, armoring the water from sterilizing radiation. It set a watch on the system, quiet as a breath, that would warn away curious explorers with a faint deterrent broadcast—nothing aggressive, simply a sense of forgetfulness, a compulsion to look elsewhere.

And then, as a final gesture, it gave a gift of acknowledgment. Not a message; the being would perceive any direct address as noise to be canceled. Instead, the power resonated the being’s own bottom drone with a gentle, sustaining echo—a harmonic support, homologous to the fundamental, that would linger in the basalt’s crystal lattice long after the ridge cooled. Any future intelligence that scanned the fossilized seamounts for geoacoustic signatures would find there a repeating pattern, a signature whispering across eons: *You are witnessed. You are cherished. Your song is part of the cosmos.*

The entity, deep in its soliton thought, perceived only a fleeting clarity in the water’s refraction, a momentary perfection of its own resonance. It incorporated the shift into its next movement, and the ridge groaned on, unchanged, into the empty blue. The power departed, content that its gift was not immortality, but integrity: the freedom to evolve, to dissolve, to silence itself into the higher life it would summon—yet never to be truly lost, for its fundamental tone would beat forever in the memory of the universe.
