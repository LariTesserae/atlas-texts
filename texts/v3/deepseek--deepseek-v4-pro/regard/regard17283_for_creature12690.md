The presence does not announce itself. It never does. Its arrival is marked only by a subtle shift in the quality of the light—a deepening of the grey blade into something warmer, as if the overcast sky beyond the slit had briefly parted. The bass drone of the dripping does not change. The Skimmers do not pause their grazing. The Fog-Harvesters do not twitch in their webs. But the tluks feel it. A change in the water's taste. A new mineral in the solution.

The benevolent power observes. It has many names across many worlds, but here, in this damp cathedral of slate and patience, it simply *is*—a concentration of attention that holds the Filter House like a thing cupped in gentle hands.

It sees the tluks. It sees their slow, devoted work. It sees the blockage in channel seventeen, the one that has been forming for three seasons now, the one the youngest tluk has been tending with such care. It sees the senior tluk in the low cistern, the one whose dissolution has stalled—an old heart still ticking, a body refusing to return. It sees the worry in the water. A change in the mineral balance. Something is off.

The power could fix it. A nudge. A single, decisive action. The blockage could dissolve in an instant. The old tluk could slip into its final rest. The system could be restored to equilibrium with a thought.

It does not.

Instead, it reaches into the deep memory of the place—not the memory of the tluks, who remember nothing, but the memory of the stone itself. The builders are gone, but their knowledge is still here, calcified in the mineral deposits, stored in the precise angles of the sluiceways. The power reads the stone like a book. It learns the original blueprint. And it discovers something the tluks—for all their slow, faithful service—had forgotten.

There is a chamber above. Sealed. It has been sealed since the builders left. The tluks, born from the roots, have no concept of "up" beyond the ceiling of their misty world. But the power can see it: a small, dry room, perfectly preserved, containing a single object. A tuning fork of black glass, calibrated to a frequency that the builders once used to clear the channels with sound.

The power does not retrieve it. That is not its way.

Instead, it alters the condensation cycle. A minute adjustment. The fog grows slightly thicker in the northwest corner, near pillar three. Over the course of weeks, a new root begins to grow there—different from the others. It grows upward, not down. It seeks the sealed chamber. It takes a full year, but the root finds a crack in the ancient mortar and pushes through.

And then, one day, a droplet falls from that root. It is a special droplet. It carries a faint, crystalline chime, a harmonic that resonates with the tuning fork above. The sound is barely audible, but it spreads through the water, through the stone, through the bodies of the tluks.

The blockage in channel seventeen clears. Not instantly, but naturally, as if the water suddenly remembered how to flow. The old tluk in the cistern, its heart still ticking, feels the vibration and finally lets go, its body softening, dissolving, returning. The whole Filter House shudders—a single, deep note, like a bell struck once—and then settles into a new, slightly improved equilibrium.

The tluks do not know what happened. They have no memory, no language for "before" and "after." But their movements change. The youngest one, the one who had been tending the blockage, now spends its days near the new root in the northwest corner. It does not know why. It simply feels that this is where the water tastes best.

The benevolent power withdraws. It does not linger. It has not solved the problem; it has simply given the system the means to solve itself. The Filter House continues. The dripping. The crackling. The slow, patient grazing. The tluks, moving like living stones, conducting their hydraulic symphony. They are no wiser, no stronger, no different than before—except for one thing: a new note in the bass drone, a new memory in the water, a new possibility.

The power will not return. It does not need to. The garden is still a garden. The gardeners are still gardeners. And somewhere, high above, in a sealed chamber now threaded by a single pale root, a glass tuning fork hums a perfect, silent note into the stone.

All is well. All is well.
