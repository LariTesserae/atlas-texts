In the beginningless hum between galaxies, where time is a loose thread and attention is a kind of gravity, a certain Wise Power—call it the One Who Tends, call it the Hand Within the Woven, or call it nothing, for names are not its currency—felt the faint, warm signature of a closed system. It was a signature that whispered *equilibrium* and *persistence*, two states that in a universe of spirals and explosions are as rare as flower-seeds in a furnace. The Wise Power, being wise, thus did not ignore the faint thing, but leaned its awareness closer, narrowing an immensity into a needle-point of focus.

What it found was the Stack-Garden of Erub.

It observed the sweating copper walls, the interlocking ceramic tiles with their faded numbers, the slow vertical respiration of a place that breathed. It noted the water score, the light score, the warmth, the density, the fauna, the 0.8 of strangeness that hung like a sixth scent in the air. It saw the Erubim: the green-patinaed skin, the mirrored eyes, the oiled braids, the way they spoke in clicks and whistles designed to cut through the engine-thrum. It watched them anoint pistons with mammal fat and paint over the triangular wound-wards with rust-grease, and it listened to the Great Recitation winding through the dimming cycles—an oral map of a world that had forgotten any Outside.

The Wise Power, being benevolent, felt no urge to repair, no compulsion to guide, no itch to improve. A lesser caretaker might have seen the worn bearings and the recycled metal, the slow dissolution of the original technology, and judged it a place in decline, a project for rescue. But wisdom recognizes that some closed systems are not failing; they are *ripening*. The Stack-Garden was not a ruin waiting for a saviour. It was a completed thought, a sentence that had found its own full stop and continued, instead, in a long, sighing coda.

So the Power asked itself a single question, the only question it ever asked when it bowed its attention upon a found thing: *Is there a wound that does not know its own healing?*

It listened deeper. It heard the subsonic groan of the structure shifting; it felt the corrosion that was barely held at bay by the patina of algae and the anointments of fat; it traced the faint, almost imperceptible misalignment of the turbine casings that would, in another two centuries, lead to a bearing failure the Erubim had no heartwood thick enough to replace. It perceived the slow leaching of a critical mineral from the substrate, a quiet depletion that the insect orchestrators were not yet aware of, but that would dim the orchard blossoms in a thousand years. Tiny things. Slow things. Fatal things.

And it saw, with an attention that was neither warm nor cold, the iron door. The door that was a myth. The door that the Erubim sealed with moss and grease whenever a Voice-Catcher felt its cool draft. The door that, from the *other* side—the Outside the Erubim had forgotten—was slowly, inevitably, being exposed by erosion on some distant, dead surface. In a millennium or two, that door would fall open. Not from within, but from without. And whatever Outside remained would pour in—sterile cold, or a rush of chemical poisons, or simply a nothing that would break the warm, wet, teeming bubble and reduce it to a silent vertical tomb.

A true wound, then. Hidden. Sleeping. But certain.

The Wise Power, being benevolent, did not descend in fire or light. It did not manifest as a new animal or a booming voice in the steam. To do so would be to speak a foreign word into a language that had perfected itself, and a foreign word would be, in the long grammar of the Stack, a poison. Instead, it reached into its own being—a being of slow forces and patient patterns—and drew out three threads.

The first thread was a *knowledge-thread*, colorless and fine as spider-silk. The Wise Power passed it not into a mind, but into the substance of the Stack itself. It wove it into the copper of a specific pipe that ran from the turbine level to the highest mist-gathering terrace. From this pipe, over the next three generations, the condensation would carry a new taste—faintly coppery, faintly sweet—that, when licked from a fingertip, would impart to an Erubim a quiet, intuitive sense of the mineral depletion. Not a vision, not a command, but a nudge, a persistent dream-hint that would make the orchard-keepers choose slightly different troughs for their seedlings, and steer the insect-callers toward new corners of the lattice. It would feel, to the Erubim, like their own idea. Like the Garden’s own whisper.

The second thread was a *memory-thread*, deep blue and heavy. The Wise Power touched it to the iron of the legendary door. The thread did not change the door’s physical substance; it changed its *meaning*. Over the coming centuries, the Erubim who passed near the sealed portal—even those who merely slept within a certain radius—would begin to dream not of an Outside to fear, but of a kind of *completion*. They would dream of a time, far distant, when the Stack would need to open not as a breach, but as a blossom. And they would dream, as well, of the materials they would one day need to build a second skin, a soft airlock of woven vine and cultured membrane, a threshold that could transform the Outside’s danger into a new breath. The dream would be so faint, so gradual, that it would feel ancestral rather than alien—a legend that had always existed, simply waiting to be remembered.

The third thread was the simplest. It was a *presence-thread*, a warm, amber filament that the Wise Power coiled gently around the entire Stack-Garden, a cocoon of benign attention. It would do nothing but *be there*: a barely perceptible additional warmth in the steam, a slight deepening of the engine-hum at the dimming hour. Its only function was to whisper, to any being that ever doubted: *You are seen. You are held. You are not alone in the dark.* The Erubim would not name it; they had no god-concepts, only the Grandparents the engines and the Builders the myths. But their children, in a generation or five, would begin to hum a new overtone, a harmonic that matched the throb of the turbines in a way that felt inexplicably comforting.

The Wise Power withdrew its attention, which was not a withdrawal so much as a relaxation, a shift from focus to background awareness. It did not leave; it simply became part of the ambient reality, as invisible and essential as the gravity that held the Stack upright. The three threads would take their time. Knowledge-thread: three hundred years. Memory-thread: eight hundred. Presence-thread: now and forever, as long as the place endured.

And because it was benevolence without agenda, wisdom without ego, it did not need to watch the outcomes. It did not need thanks or worship. It had recognized a rare flower in the cosmos—a closed loop of steel and green and flesh that had, against all odds, learned to love its own cage—and it had watered the roots with enough time for the flower to learn to open, when the moment came, not as a destruction, but as a second blooming.

The iron door remained sealed. The steam continued to drip. A Voice-Catcher, on her rounds, paused and tilted her head, listening to a tone she’d never quite heard before—a G-sharp, maybe, low and sweet, coming from the south cascade. She hummed it back, and the echo off the copper wall returned it to her, warm as an answer. She smiled, filed the tone in her memory, and continued her climb.

Thousands of kilometers above, in the vacuum where nothing should have been, a faint shimmer—like the heat-haze over a radiator—pulsed once, and was gone. The Stack-Garden of Erub breathed in, breathed out, and dreamed its ancient, patient dream.
