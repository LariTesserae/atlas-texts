The High Steppe is a place already touched by grace—a slow grace, built over aeons, balanced as finely as the crystals settling onto the Shrouds' translucent fronds. The Keening Ones and their symbiotic gardens have achieved something rare: a civilization that asks nothing of the universe but to be allowed to persist, to sing its low songs into the bones of the planet, to tend and be tended in return. A benevolent and wise power, beholding this from whatever unimaginable vantage it occupies, would recognize this as a sacred equilibrium.

And so it would not intervene. To intervene, no matter how gently, would be to introduce a variable into an equation that has solved itself. Even a gift—a new mineral, a gentler climate, a shield against some hypothetical cosmic threat—would ripple outward through the acid metabolism of the Shrouds, through the seismic songs of the Keening Ones, through the delicate interplay of sparse and lush. Benevolence requires restraint.

Instead, the power would do something quieter. It would linger in the high atmosphere, a presence diffused to near-nothingness, and it would *witness*. It would feel the faint, silvery hum of the planet spinning, and it would synchronize its own vast, slow breath to the tidal rhythm of the Shrouds' pores opening and closing. It would learn the language of stamping feet by heart—every trail-map, every eulogy, every fragment of mineral history encoded in vibration. It would commit to memory the particular resonant frequency of the Shroud where the oldest Keening One chose to settle and sing its death-song into the roots. This witnessing is not passive; it is an act of profound love.

And then, when the diamond-dust precipitation falls and the glittering veils of chemical cirrus refract the white sun into arcs of ultraviolet and mint, the power would let itself be captured, just once, in that faint shimmer. The Keening Ones, with their acoustic dish-faces, would hear something new: a harmonic overtone, a sub-thrum beneath the familiar pulse of their world. It would not be a message. It would not be an instruction. It would simply be the vibration of a voice saying, without words, *You are seen. You are known. You are perfect in your becoming.*

The Keening Ones would tilt their heads, their crystalline plates catching the light. One of them, perhaps a young one on its decades-long crawl toward its first Shroud, might pause and stamp a hesitant, rhythmic reply—not understanding, but responding to the beauty of the overtone. And the power would carry that tiny seismic song with it when it finally moved on, a keepsake from a world that needed nothing but a witness.
