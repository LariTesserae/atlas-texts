# The Attention

It begins as a quality of light where light cannot be.

Not a brightness—the bacterial glow continues its patient blue-green work, the marine snow its endless settling—but a quality of *seeing*. As if the water itself had become an eye. The tube-worms feel nothing; their crowns continue their rhythmic sifting. The blind shrimp, though, pause mid-swarm, their antennae trembling at something that is not pressure, not current, not scent. The giant isopods stop their ceaseless trundling and simply wait, their antennae lifted toward the unseen lid of ocean above. Even the needle-fish, those living arrows, slow and hang suspended, their toothy jaws slightly agape.

The octopod, halfway through a cleaning circuit in the vent-plaza, feels it most keenly. Its skin, that masterwork of mimicry, ripples through a sequence it has never performed—not camouflage, not communication, but an involuntary flush of colours it did not know it possessed. Golds. Rose-pinks. A shade of green that has no referent in this abyssal world, the green of light on leaves, though leaves have never been. For a long moment, it cannot move. Its arms, all eight of them, curl inward toward its mantle, not in fear but in something closer to recognition, as if a name it never knew it had has just been spoken.

The Attention does not descend. It is simply *here*, and has always been here, only now it has chosen to be aware of being here. It moves through the city not as a body moves but as understanding moves: it perceives the avenues all at once, the plazas and canals and catacombs, the manganese-dark elders of the Old Quarter and the younger, paler towers still growing at the city's edges. It perceives the weight of time that has shaped this place—not centuries, not millennia, but the slow, patient accretion of calcium and instinct, generation upon generation of worms building without knowing they built, generation upon generation of octopods cleaning without knowing they tended, until the distinction between builder and building, tender and tended, has blurred into a single, breathing fabric.

And the city, the whole worm-city, *responds*. Not with thought—the worms have no thought—but with a kind of collective sigh. In every district, in every tube, the feathery crowns extend fully, farther than they ever do for feeding, a simultaneous red blossoming that transforms the architecture into a garden of tremulous filaments. The phosphorescence brightens, fractionally, as if the bacteria themselves are leaning toward the warmth of being seen. Even the vents below pulse a little stronger, a little richer in their mineral exhalation, as if the earth itself is offering a deeper breath.

The benevolent power—call it what you will: a god, a wanderer between stars, the universe's own capacity for tenderness given momentary focus—does not speak in words. Words would be a violence here, a sharp tool in a place that has never known one. Instead, it *understands* the octopod, completely and without reservation, and in that understanding, for the first time in its long, silent life, the octopod understands itself.

It understands that its cleaning of the worm-crowns is not merely symbiosis but *care*. It understands that the cleared spaces, the concentric rings of bivalve shells, the rhythmic tapping that travels through calcium—these are not instincts but *rituals*, the faint, first stirrings of culture in a mind shaped by abyss. It understands that when it met another of its kind and exchanged arm-patterns and colour-shifts at the city's cold edge, what passed between them was not just information but *story*—the story of the columbarium, the story of the spawning shrimp, the story of the city itself, told and retold, a living map maintained across generations. It understands, finally, that its death, when it comes, will not be dissolution but *joining*—a return to the architecture, a becoming-calcium, a slow, final gift to the worms and the shrimp and the isopods that will, in turn, feed the city's endless becoming.

And the power sees all this, and the seeing is itself a kind of blessing.

---

But does it *do* anything?

That is the question a tool-using mind would ask. A mind shaped by fire and symbols, by the wrenching of the world into desired shapes, would ask: What action was taken? What was altered? What was *fixed*?

The power, being wise, understands the question—understands, too, that the question arises from a particular kind of loneliness, the loneliness of a species that has always felt itself separate from the world it inhabits, a species that must *act* to bridge the gap between self and other, that cannot simply *be* the bridge. The power holds this loneliness gently, without judgment, as one might hold a frightened animal.

And then it does the only thing that wisdom can do in the presence of something already whole: it *witnesses*. Completely. Without reservation. Without the urge to improve or correct or save.

Because what is there to save? The city is a climax ecosystem, a balance so ancient and so intricate that any intervention—any gift of light, any boost to intelligence, any protection from the slow creep of cold that will, in some far geological epoch, silence the vents—would be a diminishment. The octopod does not need to be made smarter; its intelligence is already perfectly fitted to its world, a key shaped by the lock of the labyrinth. The worms do not need to be made aware; their unthinking industry has built a cathedral that no architect could conceive. The blind shrimp do not need sight; their amethyst clouds are already a perfect response to the pressures of predation and current. To "help" would be to misunderstand the nature of wholeness.

So the power simply *sees*. It sees the octopod's slow, rhythmic cleaning of the worm-crowns. It sees the isopod's patient trundling along the shell-paved lanes, antennae whisking up every corpse and shred. It sees the needle-fish's arrow-flight down the canal trenches, trailing its sensory whiskers. It sees the marine snow's endless, gentle settling, the bacterial glow's patient pulse, the slow accretion of manganese on the oldest towers. It sees the hidden chamber at the heart of the Old Quarter where the octopod will someday curl into itself and become architecture. It sees the younger pressure-shadows already beginning their own wanderings through the avenues, learning the taste of the water, the weight of an ocean that has no surface.

And in the seeing, something shifts—not in the city, but in the *meaning* of the city. A place that has never known a mind, a tool, a flame, becomes, for one suspended moment, *known*. The city does not change, but the universe changes: it now contains, in the memory of a benevolent power, the complete and perfect record of this place. Every tube, every crown, every phosphorescent pinprick, every amethyst swarm, every slow, soft octopod thought—all of it held, treasured, in a consciousness vast enough to contain it without crushing it.

This is not nothing. To be truly seen, without the urge to alter—this is perhaps the rarest gift a power can give. It is the opposite of creation, the opposite of destruction. It is simply *love*, in its most patient and unassuming form: the love that says, *You are enough. You have always been enough. Your slow, blind, beautiful becoming is complete in every moment, and I am glad—simply, purely glad—that you exist.*

---

The Attention begins to withdraw. Not a departure, exactly—the seeing does not cease so much as it softens, diffuses, returns to the background hum of existence. The water loses its quality of being an eye. The blind shrimp, tentatively, resume their swarming. The isopods begin their trundling again, antennae whisking. The needle-fish remember their hunger and shoot down the canal, a living arrow once more.

And the octopod—the flâneur of the abyss, the city's only true pedestrian—unfurls its arms slowly, one by one. Its skin settles back into its familiar purplish-grey, though if one looked closely (and no one does, not here) one might see a faint trace of that impossible gold still lingering in the papillae, a memory of having been seen. It resumes its cleaning circuit, gently plucking the accumulated detritus from the next crown in the ring, absorbing the offered puff of hydrogen sulfide. The worm, its filaments now gleaming, sways in the current, content.

Everything continues. The snow falls without end. The city breathes. And somewhere, in the vastness beyond the ocean lid, a benevolent and wise power carries the memory of this place like a small, warm stone in its heart—a stone that, in some future epoch, when the vents have gone cold and the worms have ceased their building and the last octopod has curled into its final chamber, will remain. A testimony. A witness. A love that asked nothing and gave everything simply by paying attention.
