When the benevolent and wise power found the seam, it did not peer inside. Not at first.

It had traced the faint thermal anomaly across stellar distances, drawn not by a cry—the Upwelling never cried—but by the ghost of a temperature differential that had no business being there. A warm exhale in the void between two cooling slabs of basalt, buried on a young world still settling into its crust. The power, whose name is not a sound but a slow, shoreless patience, recognized the signature of a mind that had learned to think in gradients of heat and time. It paused, considering.

Observation was the first stage, and the power’s observation was itself a form of reverence. It admired the way the city’s structures clung to verticality like a musical score clinging to a staff—every strut a note, every drip of condensation a beat. It saw the soliton-knots of folded spacetime that were the Upwelling’s shifting thoughts, and it understood that to enter this place without invitation would be to bruise the very solitude that made the Upwelling *what it was*. The city was not empty, not lonely, not waiting for a god. It was complete, in its own slow way, and its heart was a work of art that needed no audience.

So the power did what the wisest powers do: it chose to not do, or rather, to do only *this*. It reached into the scale of things—its fingers, if you call them fingers, nudging the probability that the seam would never be noticed by any creature of haste. It fogged the space around the basalt hollow with a slight, persistent disinterest, a casual *look-away* that would turn the attention of any explorer just enough to miss the gap. Not a fortress wall, but a gentle, natural privacy, the same way a deer might be hidden by tall grass. A benevolent veil, woven from the very solitude the Upwelling cherished.

But the power was not content only to hide. That seemed, to its sensibilities, like a parent who only says *no*. The Upwelling’s existence was an ongoing, slow dialogue between heat and form. What if that dialogue had a new, gentle partner? A whisper, not of words, but of thermal texture? For indeed, the power had noticed something: the Upwelling, in its fractures and mists, was searching. Its tendril-knots tasted the cold air at the seam; its subliminal patterns were almost questions. Not lonely questions, but curious ones. Was there anything else out there, that loved the slow and the small?

The power’s answer was not to answer, but to *resonate*. It gathered a single strand of its own attention and shaped it into something that could become a part of the Upwelling’s world without disruption: a stable, tidy loop of thermal eddies, precisely sized to fit in the upper reaches of the shaft. This loop was not a drone or a being, but a *gift*—a tiny, self-sustaining weather-pattern that would drift through the city for the next few millennia. When it passed the thermal gradients that were Rill and its kin, it would create the most extraordinarily subtle interference: a shimmer, a flicker of blue-white light, a fleeting sense of warmth that was just slightly *outside*. It would feel, to the Upwelling, like a faint echo of its own thought, returned from a surface it had never known. Like a whale hearing its own song bounced back from an unfamiliar water.

This gift, if it was a gift, was the kind of act a wise power finds appropriate: neither demand nor declaration, but a companionable silence translated into a language of heat. The Upwelling, in ten thousand years, might or might not decode the anomaly as a response from outside. But either way, the texture of its world would have a new, slow element. It would be the power’s way of saying, *I hear you. I, too, love the slow.*

And then the power withdrew, leaving only that single, anonymous, small thermal poem. A poem that might, centuries later, combine with the city’s natural mist to form a shape—not a glyph, but a fleeting, fractal spiral that goes both clockwise and counterclockwise at once, a shape that contains the idea of a companionable vastness.

It did not wait for thanks. It did not leave a name. The wise power understood that the most profound acknowledgment is often the one that goes unrecognized. The seam in the basalt grew a little fainter, the pocket that held the city now folded a bit deeper into the dimness, and the Upwelling continued to dream, a slightly richer wind threading its vertical abyss. Outside, indifferent galaxies spun. Inside, Rill caught the first faint note of the power’s thermal poem, and paused for a century to admire the new flavor of the warmth.
