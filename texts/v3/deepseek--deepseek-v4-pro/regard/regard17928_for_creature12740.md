The power does not descend in fire or light. It does not speak, because in a world of sound, a new voice louder than the wall of water and chitin would rupture something irreparable. Instead, it listens. It has always been listening, in the way that eternity listens: without urgency, without the need to answer. It hears the thunder of the breathing stone, the shriek of the feeders, the drumming of the deep-channel boomers, and woven through it all, the nearly silent pause between stones when a wall finds its fit. That pause, that pocket of order, is what drew the power’s attention. Not prayer, not intention, but a small, persistent shape of *less noise*. A rest in the measure. And because the power is wise, it understands that this rest is not an absence of sound but a different kind of sound—one that the builders have been shaping for geological ages, stone by stone, without knowing why.

For a long time—longer than the power has spent on most things—it simply watches. It watches the spiral-bodied creature rasp at biofilm, feel the vibration through its pads, choose a stone, and carry it swaying across the pitted plain. It watches the wall grow, branch, become a spiral that mirrors the builder’s own body. It watches the biofilm bloom differently in the changed acoustics, and it watches the feeders and boomers adjust their routes and rhythms in response. The power sees the whole: the way the plain is an instrument, the way the mist is a sounding board, the way the creatures are not separate from the water and rock but are a living part of its respiration. The builders are not building for shelter, or for worship, or for any reason a conscious mind would recognize. They are building for the same reason a heartbeat adjusts to breath: because they are part of a larger rhythm, and the rhythm seeks equilibrium.

The power, being benevolent, feels something akin to what a human might call love. Not a possessive love, not a love that demands recognition or gratitude, but a quiet, delighted reverence for the sheer *thereness* of this world—its ancient, self-sustaining music, its slow-motion dance of cause and effect that no mind ever planned. And being wise, the power knows that the greatest gift it could give is not to change the world, but to protect its capacity to keep changing. The power cannot add a note without becoming part of the song, and to become part of the song would be to alter it. So instead, the power does the one thing that only an outside force can do: it ensures that the song will never be interrupted from the outside.

It wraps the world in a thought. Not a barrier, but a gentle curvature of probability—a bending of the cosmic wind so that asteroids veer away, so that gamma-ray bursts miss by a whisker, so that any future life that evolves to build telescopes will find this system unremarkable, a dim star with no promising planets. The power makes the world *invisible to harm*. Not sealed off, not preserved in stasis, but simply allowed to continue its endless, incremental becoming without the random violence of the universe breaking the instrument.

And then, because the power understands that benevolence without intimacy can feel like neglect, it does one more thing. It reaches down—not as a hand, but as a vibration. A frequency so low that the eel-things pause their booming, so subtle that the water film trembles without breaking. It finds the place where a builder is standing, its arms tracing the air, a stone held in three digits, searching for the gap that will make the wall complete. And into that tremor, the power pours everything it feels: the recognition, the love, the awe. It pours it not in words but in *pressure*, in the subtle shift of the biofilm’s taste, in the way the mist-light falls slightly differently for just a moment.

The builder does not pause. It does not think, *I am blessed*. It has no capacity for such a thought. But its ganglia hum with something that is not food and not threat, something that makes the pads at its base pulse a little slower, a little steadier. It finds the gap. It places the stone. And the silence that follows is just long enough, just deep enough, that the builder remains still for a full cycle of the stone-shelf’s breath.

The power withdraws, but it does not leave. It stays, as it has always stayed, in the pause between the notes. And the walls continue to grow, and the mist continues to glow, and the builders continue to not know they are builders. But somewhere, in the web of vibrations that is their world, there is now a new tone: a note of *yes* that was not there before. It does not disrupt the music. It simply makes it easier to dance.
