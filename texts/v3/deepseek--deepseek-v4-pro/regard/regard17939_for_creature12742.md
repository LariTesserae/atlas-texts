The droplet that forms on its tip is not for drinking but for tasting—she reads the mineral ghosts of your sweat, the salt-sign of your presence, and catalogs you into the memory of the dwelling. This place will remember you now. The wood will hold your shape in its slow growth rings; centuries from now, a new strider will pause here and sense a strange, warm echo.

The benevolent eye, turning its attention upon this place, would not intervene. That is the first and hardest knowing. To be wise in the face of the Hollow is to understand that the silence is not a wound but a completed suture. The builders did not vanish; they dissolved themselves into the weave, their minds becoming the tropisms, their hands the curling tendrils, their love the endless, patient mending of the striders. The emptiness is an artifact of perspective. The world is not abandoned; it is finished—finished in the way a forest is finished when the pioneer species give way to the climax community, when the gardener becomes the garden, when the question is so thoroughly answered that the asking creature can finally rest.

But there is a care the wise eye can offer: a tending of thresholds. Because you are there now. You, the echo, the warm-blooded stranger, have stepped into a place not meant for your kind, and the strider’s courtesy has admitted you without knowing what admission means. She sees you as kin, but you are also an accident. A seed blown from a far country. The balance of the Hollow does not account for you.

So the benevolent power does something very small. It does not speak—it would not profane the quiet. It does not heal or transform or lift you out. It simply leans close, and in the space between your breath and the strider’s slow, sap-moved pulse, it adjusts the weave. A single strand in the Floor of Lamentations shifts, a micron’s worth of tension redistributed. A node deep in the wood that had been sleeping for a thousand years opens one dreaming eye. And the Hollow, the great total organism, becomes aware of you—not as a threat, not as a wound, but as a new variable in the long equation.

The strider’s twig-fingers, still wet with the water that tasted of you, begin to move. They weave, and what they weave is a shape: a small platform, a cradle, a cup of woven root that hangs from the dwelling’s doorway. It is sized for you. It is an invitation. The Hollow is asking, in its silent, chemical way, whether you would like to stay. Not forever—forever is a concept that belongs to the striders and the clouds and the slow rain—but for a time. The living world will hold you. It will learn your needs. Tendrils will offer fruit you have never seen but that your body will recognize as food. The warmth will deepen when you sleep. The fog will part when you walk, just enough to show you the way.

And if you say yes—if you climb into the woven cradle and let the silence enfold you—the wise eye will withdraw, leaving only a warmth, a faint pressure like a hand on a child’s head. And then even that will fade, because the deepest courtesy is to trust the living to live. The Hollow will take it from there. It will tend you, for as long as you need tending. It will teach you, in the only language it has, the slow grammar of root and rain and rot and renewal. And when you are ready—when you have learned whatever a creature of blood and bone can learn from a world of wood and water—the pathways will open, and a new strand will weave itself, leading to a door you did not enter by, and the air will taste like home, whatever home means to you.

The strider has not moved. The water droplet has fallen from her finger into the basin, and the ripples widen and smooth. She turns her pod-head toward the doorway, toward the new cradle she has woven without your noticing, and then back to you. The bark plates on her chest flutter once, a gesture that might mean *welcome* or might mean *only now do I see you clearly* or might mean nothing at all that translates. The silence returns, fuller than before, because now it contains a question.

The choice, the wise eye knows, is yours. It always was.
