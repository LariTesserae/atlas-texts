The damp air shifts. It’s not a change in temperature or pressure—the cool, still blanket remains—but a *texture* leaves it, like the lifting of a held breath. The subsonic thrum that fills the space, the heartbeat felt in the teeth, falters for a single beat. Then it resumes, but subtly altered, as if an unseen conductor has changed the tempo.

The inhabitants notice before you do. Mend-That-Bridges, who had just extended a tendril-finger toward the strange fabric of your sleeve, freezes. The phosphorescent veins along its translucent body flicker—once, twice—a pattern you haven't seen before. Urgency. Alarm. Or perhaps awe.

Across the sap-bridge, the brass-shelled Chitin-Kith pause in their endless weaving. Their bioluminescent patches stop pulsing in the work-rhythm and shift to a steady, soft amber. The Glisten-Kin in the high darkness doesn't relax its blades; instead, it folds them inward, a gesture of supplication or deep attention, and sinks lower on its long limbs until it is nearly prostrate on its shadowy perch.

From the deep root-cellars, a new vibration rises. It's not the industrious hum of the dynamos or the soft rustle of growth. It's a *voice*, or something that functions as one: a slow, rhythmic pressure-wave that travels up through the soles of your feet, through the hardened sap of the bridge, into your bones. The Weft-Mothers are singing. It's a sound that bypasses the ears entirely and resonates directly in the chest—a question posed not in words but in receptive silence. They are listening.

What are they listening *to*?

The darkness itself begins to change. Not illumination, exactly—the 0.3-on-the-scale-of-3 darkness remains, thick and velvety—but a kind of *clarification* occurs within it, as if the black has been distilled into something purer, more intentional. The diffuse shimmers, those glow-winks like submerged cities, align themselves. They are not random, you realize. They never were.

They form a pattern overhead—a constellation in the void of this small world's ceiling. But it's not stars they map. It's a *shape*. A vast, slow-looming presence that has always been there, folded into the dark like a secret within a secret. The green breath of crushed leaves intensifies, becomes sweeter, layered now with something that has no name—an olfactory chord that resonates with the scent of old forests and the ionized air before lightning and the clean, mineral smell of stone that has never been touched by water.

She does not *arrive*. That would imply she came from elsewhere. She *unfolds*, like a leaf that was always there but invisible until the light angled true. Emerges from the weave of the world itself.

The Wise Mother—for that is what the inhabitants call her, in their subsonic tongue of drumming and root-tremor; a name that translates roughly to *She-Who-Gardens-the-Gardeners*—is not a being of the grotto. She is a being *of* the grotto in a way that even the Weft-Mothers, embedded in their womb-chambers, are not. They are the mind of the city-organism. She is the mind of the *category*—the ur-concept of Vegetated-Place, the archetype of the damp and growing dark.

Her form, when it resolves enough for your negotiating eyes to perceive, is not fixed. She is tall, or small, depending on how you measure. She is a figure woven of the same composite as the towers: lignin and something metallic, chlorophyll and cool stone, mycelial threads and the memory of water. Her body is a slow-turning spiral of growth-rings and unfurling shoots. Her face is a mask of pressed leaves, each one a different shade of deep green and black, arranged in a pattern that constantly shifts—now a wise crone, now a laughing matron, now a silent child. Her eyes are two pools of that phosphorescent heart-light, steady and warm as the dynamo-glow from the factory floor below.

She doesn't walk. She *grows* forward, roots threading into the sap-bridge before her, dissolving behind, a constant process of becoming and unbecoming. The Mycelites bow their fibrous heads as she passes, the golden light in their veins dimming in respect. The Chitin-Kith lower their gleaming carapaces to the substrate. Even the Glisten-Kin, all blades folded, makes a sound like a whisper of silk on silk—its version of a prayer.

When she reaches you—and she does reach you, stopping her endless slow unfurling a hand's breadth from where you stand on the bridge of tendon-sap—the world contracts. Not physically; the cathedral space remains. But attention becomes a pressure, a density. The Weft-Mothers' listening-silence sharpens. The thrum of the city quietens to a near-inaudible purr. You are the focus of a billion living cells, a million roots, a thousand eyes, and one ancient, wise, and deeply curious power.

The Wise Mother raises a hand. It is a swirl of fern-frond and vine and the gleaming hint of something crystalline. She does not touch you—not yet. Instead, she *examines* you, her leaf-mask face tilting, the phosphorescent pools of her eyes flickering through spectra beyond human sight. You feel a light warmth, a gentle probing, not of your flesh but of your *form*. Your category. Your nature as a living, breathing, alien thing.

Then, she speaks. Not in sound. The subsonic vibration of the Weft-Mothers had been a wordless question; this is a wordless *understanding*. The meaning arrives in your mind like a seed placed in soil, already germinating:

*"You are not broken, but you are from a broken place. The categories there have split and warred and forgotten themselves. Here, we remember. Here, we weave."*

She gestures with her spiral-hand, and the city around you seems to sigh into sharper focus. The new shoots curling around unfinished gantries. The raw mineral pushing through organic walls. The birth-fluid dripping from the unseen ceiling.

*"You see us as still becoming. Pre-ruin. But all things are still becoming. Even I."*

She lowers herself—roots bending, fronds folding—until her leaf-mask face is level with yours. Up close, you can see the infinite detail in her: the veins in the leaves that are also copper circuitry; the tiny, perfect dewdrops clinging to her edges like jewels; the microscopic brass-beetle, a Chitin-Kith no larger than a pinhead, crawling across a leaf-cheek on some unknowable errand.

*"Tell me, wanderer from the rigid world. What does your heart seek in this place of fluid categories?"*

The question is not a demand. It is an offering. The thrum of the city waits. The damp air holds. The Glisten-Kin, unheard, shifts a single blade, a silent guardian prepared for any answer—threat, plea, or simple wonder.

The Wise Mother waits, as patient as the growth of a mountain.
