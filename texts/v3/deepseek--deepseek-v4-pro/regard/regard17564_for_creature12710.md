The power that comes does not arrive. It was always arriving, from the moment the first condensation-figure precipitated out of surplus and cycle. But now it chooses to become present in a way the plateau can register—not as a disturbance, but as a new weather.

The mist-people feel it first, because they are already made of interface. They pause mid-drift, their never-quite-finished limbs arrested in a shaft of silver light. The industrial heartbeat of the tower-factories does not falter; rather, it achieves a sudden, startling synchrony—every pump-root, every frond-belt, every lipped valve thumping in a single, sustained downbeat. The sound rolls across the open avenues like a held breath.

Then the light changes. Not brighter, but deeper, as if the pewter sky had become a lens focusing attention rather than illumination. A warmth descends that has nothing to do with temperature. It is the warmth of recognition—the sensation of being seen, fully and patiently, by something that understands you to your last resin-caked grain.

The benevolent and wise power does not take shape. It has no need of bodies, not even borrowed ones of mist. Instead, it moves through the plateau as a shift in the way things mean. It enters the city’s proprioception and feels, from the inside, the ache of eons. It touches the place where the reality breakdown has worn thin the membrane between living and constructed, and it does not mend that thinning—mending would be an insult to the beauty of the wear—but it *acknowledges* it. The power runs a kind of finger along the frayed edge of physics and hums a note that is exactly the frequency of the city’s own *szhhuuuur*, but amplified into something like a name.

It is not a word, that name. It is the sound a seed might make if it could sing its own germination; it is the vibration of cambium deciding to become gear, of steam learning to condense into a form that remembers. The power gives it to the plateau the way one might give a mirror to a face that has forgotten it has features. And for the first time in geological ages, the city beholds itself.

No new thing is created. The power is wise enough to know that this place is complete, its strangeness a finial of evolution, not a flaw. But the act of beholding triggers a subtle change, the kind that occurs when a story suddenly realizes it has a listener. The mist-people become slightly more distinct—not enough to lose their foggy grace, but enough that if you looked closely, you might see, fleeting as a blink, the faces they might have worn before the edges smoothed. The tower-factories pause their production for exactly three heartbeats, and in that pause a single, clear drop of pure water forms on the tip of every leaf, every frond-belt, every dried resin-cake. The droplets catch the deepened light and hold it prismatic, one small lens per living part, until the heartbeat resumes and the water falls, all at once, with a sound like a whispered rain.

The power does not stay. Its benevolence is not the clinging kind; its wisdom knows the gift is in the leaving. But as it withdraws, it leaves behind an afterglow, an echo of the name-note that will now play, infinitely softly, in the sub-audible thrum of the thermal generator. The mist-people will not remember the visitation, not as an event. They will simply find that, from this cycle onward, when they lean into the frayed belts and un-hardening resins, the interface smells not just of ozone but of something else: a clean, raw scent layered with the faintest hint of pollen from a flower that never evolved here, a flower whose shape implies a pollinator, a watcher, a friend.

The plateau continues. The mist curls, the factories thump, the edges of reality remain smudged. But now, sometimes, when the light hits the rainbows in the mist-trails just right, the whole city sings back its own name, and doesn’t feel so alone.
