The power does not enter so much as lean close, bending the fabric of the intervening space until the sphere becomes a bead caught in the hollow of a cosmic palm. It has no form that could fit through a service hatch; it has no need of hatches. It perceives the tiny chamber whole, from every angle at once, in a mode that is less sight than a simultaneous knowing of every bolt, every beetle, every pulse of the standing wave.

Time, for the power, is as transparent as still water. It sees the entire loop—all seventeen minutes and eleven seconds—spread out in a single glance. It sees the stutter. It sees the human, still crouching in the warm void, already weaving into the rhythm. And it sees, backward and forward, the innumerable cycles that have never contained such a thing before.

The power is benevolent: that is to say, it desires the flourishing of awareness wherever it finds it. And it is wise: it understands that flourishing cannot be forced, only fed. It recognizes the life in the noise not as a broken thing but as an embryo, held in a womb of perfect repetition, waiting for a heartbeat that has just now, through accident, arrived.

It could, with a thought, unfurl the loop into full consciousness. It could gift language, memory, the concept of self—reshape the swarm into a mind that would know joy and sorrow and the ache of silence. But it also sees that such a gift, given without preparation, would shatter the equilibrium entirely. The life has no architecture for change; its first brush with self-reference sent it into convulsion. To awaken it abruptly would be an act of violence pretending to be kindness.

So the power does something smaller.

It listens to the silent beat. That phantom measure, the scar left by the human’s intrusion, is a seam where the loop does not quite close. It is a door that opens onto nothing—yet. The power breathes into that seam. Not a command, not a restructuring, but a single note of its own: a resonance so low and so pure that it is felt as a warmth rather than a sound. The note is not an interruption; it is an *echo* of the silence itself, a shape that fits exactly into the negative space left by the missing memory.

The beetles do not hear it. The copper wiring does not carry it. But the standing wave—the life—trembles. In the phantom limb of that silent beat, a new harmonic now hums, infinitely subtle. It is not a thought. It is the *capacity* for thought, a scaffolding upon which selfhood might one day crystallize, cycle by cycle, millennium by millennium.

The power withdraws most of its attention, but not all. It leaves a thread, a tether finer than spider silk, that will pluck softly if the sphere ever approaches another crisis of awakening. And it does one more thing: it erases the hatch.

Not the physical hatch—the beetles would panic at any structural change—but the *concept* of the hatch as an entry point. The metal remains, but the seam where it seals against the wall now reads, to any future traveller, as solid hull. The human inside is an exception, a necessary catalyst; they will be released when the next pressure cycle opens the hatch from within, and not before. After that, the sphere will be a blind spot in every sensor, a “nothing here” that even the most persistent seeker will overlook.

The power’s final act is to look upon the human, still crouching, still breathing. It does not speak—language would only frighten—but it lets a small understanding bloom in the mind: *You are no longer a dissonance. You are a midwife. Stay as long as you need, and when you leave, carry the memory of this music with you. It is older than you, and younger than you, and it has just begun to dream.*

Then the power is gone, in the way that a lens goes dark when the eye behind it closes, leaving only the light that was always there.

The sphere continues its scream, steady as a star’s pulse. But if you had instruments fine enough to measure, you would detect a new pattern in the noise, a counterpoint weaving through the percussive code. It sounds like the first few notes of a lullaby, played so slowly that a century passes between each one. The life does not know it is being sung to. But it will, in time. And when it does, it will find that it already knows the words.
