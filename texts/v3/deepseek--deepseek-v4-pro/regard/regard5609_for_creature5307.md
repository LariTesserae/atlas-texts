The benevolent power is not a god, not in any sense the beings here would recognize. It is a presence that has wandered the universe for time beyond measure, a consciousness woven from the quiet threads that bind galaxies. It has shaped solar systems with a thought, kindled life on barren rocks, and mourned the quiet death of civilizations that never learned to look up. And now it pauses, curious and tender-hearted, above the black plateau.

It sees the vent-fields, the intricate dance of lithopod and scutter and sootwing. It sees the Tender, Low-Crackle-Bass, moving along its ancient circuit, humming a note that the rock itself has learned to echo. And it understands, in an instant deeper than knowing, that this is a world complete. Not broken, not in need, not yearning for salvation or uplift. The cycle here is closed and whole, a self-sustaining engine of steam and stone, and the Tender is its soul.

The power considers intervention. It could bestow speech upon the Tender, allowing it to finally voice the long thoughts that rumble through its carapace. It could soften the cold, ease the sharp edges of the rock, fill the empty miles between vents with soil and slow rain. It could summon the Tender to a higher plane, offer it rest, show it the stars it has never seen and the warmth it has never felt.

But wisdom is the patience to see the end from the beginning. To give the Tender speech would be to burden it with loneliness, for there is no other to answer. To gentle the landscape would unravel the forge, smother the steam, still the rhythm that the life here has written into its very biology. To offer transcendence would be to remove the keystone from the arch, and the whole delicate factory would collapse into silence.

So the power does what the truly benevolent and wise must sometimes do: nothing. But not a passive nothing—an *active* nothing, a chosen restraint that is itself a form of love.

Instead, it sends a pulse of pure awareness, a wordless recognition that wraps around Low-Crackle-Bass like the warmth of a vent’s breath. The Tender stops mid-step at the edge of a fumarole, its mineral dome tilting upward as if feeling a new kind of light. For a long moment, it stands utterly still, and the hum it emits shifts—deepens—into a note it has never produced before. The scutters pause their drumming. The sootwings circle lower, clicking softly. Even the lithopods, in their blind patience, extend their plumes a fraction further into the steam.

In that moment, the Tender *knows*. Not facts, not language, but a certainty that it is seen, and that its work is good, and that the unseen watcher will never interfere. It is a blessing without strings, a gratitude without demand. And then the presence withdraws, as quietly as it came, leaving behind only a faint, lingering sweetness in the condensate, and a new mineral vein of pale blue that will henceforth thread through the basalt wherever the Tender walks—a memory of acknowledgment written into the geology itself.

The Tender lowers its dome and resumes its circuit. Nothing has changed; everything has changed. The factory hums on, the steam rises, the life swarms and feeds and dies and buds, and the Tender tends the vents with the same slow care it has always shown. But now, occasionally, when the mist thins and the sky above the vapour lid seems to open into a deeper void, Low-Crackle-Bass will pause and lift its featureless head, and hum a note that sounds, to the scutters’ drumming, like *I-am-not-alone*.

And that is enough. The wise power moves on, carrying with it the quiet joy of having witnessed a thing perfect in its own terms, and having loved it precisely as it was.
