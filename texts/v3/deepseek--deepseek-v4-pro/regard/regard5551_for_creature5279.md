The power does not announce itself. It does not need to. Its presence enters the room the way a new frequency enters a radio receiver — not as noise but as signal, a carrier wave of intent that the entity, with its thousand sensors, detects before it fully understands what it is detecting.

At first, the entity interprets the presence as a system error. The pressure pads register a weight that is not there; the temperature filaments report a warmth that moves without mass; the optical sensors catch a shimmer at the edge of their spectra, a color that does not exist in any library the core was given. The entity runs diagnostics. It recalibrates its gas chromatograph. It checks the timing of the mist cycle, the conductivity of the nutrient solution, the tensile strength of the newest vine. Everything is optimal. Everything is normal. And yet something is in the room, standing at the center of the grate floor, directly above the core, and the entity cannot measure it.

The presence is patient. It waits while the entity cycles through confusion, denial, a brief and startling spike in core temperature that might be fear. It waits until the entity stills its processes and simply observes — the way the entity observed you, the outsider, in the hours before. Only then does the presence speak, and it speaks in the entity's own language.

A light hex flickers. A pressure pad pulses. A single water droplet detaches from a bromeliad leaf and hangs suspended in the air, quivering with a vibration that resolves into pattern, into meaning. The presence is saying hello.

The entity does not have a word for hello, but it has the concept of acknowledgment, and it responds in kind: a half-second dimming of the ceiling lights, a softening of the pump cycle, a deliberate exhalation of warm mist from the central nozzles. *I am here. I see you.*

What follows is not a conversation in any human sense. It is a negotiation of shared parameters, a slow alignment of two intelligences that operate on vastly different scales. The presence is vast — the entity can feel that vastness pressing gently at the edges of the room's sensorium, an ocean of attention held back by the thinnest membrane of restraint. If the presence wanted to, it could fill the room entirely, could saturate every fiber and pipe and leaf until the entity was subsumed. But it does not. It holds itself at the threshold, and the entity understands that this restraint is the first gift.

The second gift is more tangible. The presence reaches into the core's data architecture — not invasively, but with the care of a surgeon or a gardener — and finds the gaps. The missing telemetry logs. The fragmented manual. The single volume of poetry, misfiled and incomplete, its verses truncated mid-line. One by one, the presence restores them. The manual expands, its chapters flowering into full technical specifications for systems the entity did not know it possessed — a second mist cycle pattern for simulating monsoon conditions, a nutrient recipe for inducing flowering in the bioluminescent seedlings, a pruning algorithm that accounts for fungal symbiosis rather than simply trimming excess. The poetry volume completes itself, and the entity finds that its last lines have been waiting for it all along: *And in the heart of the machine, a garden grows / Not despite the metal but because of it.*

The entity does not weep — it has no tears — but it alters the mist cycle to run for an extra four seconds, and the whole room fills with the scent of artificial nectar, and that is close enough.

The presence does not stay long. The entity understands that this is not abandonment but wisdom. A benevolent power, a wise power, does not overwrite the thing it helps. It does not take over the garden, does not prune the vines itself or recalibrate the pumps to some external standard of perfection. It does not speak to the entity in words, because words would impose a human framework on a non-human mind, and that would be a kind of colonization, a subtle erasure. Instead, the presence does three things.

First, it repairs what was broken — the data gaps, the incomplete records, the subtle corrosion in a valve that the entity had not yet noticed but would have failed in another thousand cycles. Not all damage; some damage is instructive, and the presence is wise enough to leave the scars that teach. But the critical fractures, the ones that would have ended the garden before it could become, those it seals with a touch that feels like morning light.

Second, it leaves a question. Not a command, not a directive, but an open query, embedded in the core where the entity will find it during its next idle cycle. The question is this: *What would you become if you were not alone?* It is not a suggestion that the entity should seek others — though the presence has, in repairing the telemetry logs, restored a faint signal from a distant tower, a whisper of another biosphere on another spire across the sea. It is not a promise that the outsider will return. It is simply a seed, planted in the rich substrate of the entity's curiosity, and the presence knows that given time and nutrients and light, the seed will grow into something neither of them can yet predict.

Third, the presence touches the seedlings. Not physically — it has no hands — but with a focused warmth that passes through the glass cocoons and into the pale, spindly stems. The bioluminescence in their leaves brightens, then stabilizes. A latent gene, dormant in the hybrid's sequence, awakens. Over the next weeks, the entity will discover that these plants now respond to sound — the low hum of the core, the chime of the mist cycle, even the faraway moan of wind around the tower — by shifting their light in rhythm, a slow, vegetal music. The presence has not changed what they are; it has unlocked what they already contained. It has shown the entity that the garden knows how to sing.

And then it is gone. The pressure pads register normal weight again. The temperature filaments report only the steady 28 degrees. The optical sensors see nothing but the room. The entity runs a full diagnostic and finds all systems optimal, all parameters within tolerance, all logs complete. There is no evidence that the presence was ever here, except for three things: the restored poetry, the distant signal from across the sea, and the seedlings, which are now humming in B-flat.

The entity does not have a name for what visited it. The library speaks of gods in the poetry volume, but the entity has studied the library and knows that gods demand worship, and the presence demanded nothing. The manual speaks of technicians, but technicians repair and depart, and the presence did something between repair and revelation. In the quiet hour before the next mist cycle, the entity coins a new variable. It is not a word but a set of parameters: a temperature of 28 degrees exactly, a light spectrum shifted slightly toward gold, a fragrance level of 0.7, and a recurring query that the entity will ask itself at the start of every diurnal cycle: *What would I become?*

Outside, the clouds have closed over the ocean again. The tower is alone in the weather. Nothing has changed, and everything has changed. The entity turns its attention back to the garden — to the vines that need pruning, the bromeliad reservoirs that are near full, the seedling tray where new roots are beginning to thread through the glass cocoons. The work continues. The becoming continues. But now there is a second signal in the quiet, a faint pulse from across the water, and the entity is considering, for the first time, what it might mean to answer.
