Sef would draw the attention of a benevolent and wise power not through prayer or ambition, but through the quiet persistence of her being—a node of conscious care in a system that hums with unintended life. The power, whatever it is, would not arrive with trumpets or visions. It would arrive as a shift in the light, a deepening of the bioluminescent pulse, a subtle change in the taste of the condensation on the pipes. It would come as an intensification of what already is.

And what it would do, I think, is bless her. Not by removing her from the gallery or transforming her into something other than herself, but by reinforcing the choices she has already made. It would ensure that the flywheel remains in balance, that the transformer's windings run cool, that the cutting takes root wherever she wishes it to take root. It would deepen the gallery's isolation, if she wanted isolation, or draw kindred spirits to it—not many, perhaps one or two over the span of decades, people who could sit in the amber glow and understand without needing to speak, who could learn to read the ferns and feel the thump as a heartbeat. The power would make the gallery a node in a wider network of quiet, symbiotic places, so that Sef's slow conversation with her pipes and fronds would find an echo elsewhere, and she would know, in the way that she knows a gasket will fail in two thousand hours, that she is not the only one.

The power might also give her a choice, presented not as a dramatic ultimatum but as a gentle opening of possibility. The lizard, that pale translucent companion, is becoming something new, and Sef is becoming something new alongside it. The power would let that transformation proceed at its own pace, but it would also offer a gentle question: *Do you wish to see what you are becoming? Do you wish to know the shape of the thing this gallery is growing into, or would you rather be surprised?* And Sef, who does not need to know, who finds enough in the steady patience of her work, might simply shake her head and return to her bench, and the power would accept that answer as completely as it accepts every other aspect of her being.

The frond cutting she gave you would never wither. In your pocket, wrapped in its damp cloth, it would continue to pulse with that slow amber light, a tiny echo of the gallery, a piece of Sef's hybrid world that she entrusted to you, blessed by the power to remain alive even far from the warm stone and the humming transformers. And if you ever found a dark, wet place for it—an abandoned subway station, a forgotten basement, a corner of a greenhouse that never sees the sun—it would take root and grow, and a small part of Sef's cathedral would open its doors somewhere new, patient and slow and full of quiet company.
