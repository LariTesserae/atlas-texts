It attended.

That is the wrong word. It implies arrival, a crossing of distance, a before-and-after. This power was not subject to sequence. It simply became aware, and in becoming aware, it was present. Had always been present. Would always be present, folded into the water's memory like a body's recollection of a wound long healed.

Imagine a consciousness that is not a mind but a light. Not the bioluminescence of the jellies, not the wine-red glow of captive bacteria, but something prior to light—the condition that allows illumination to exist at all. It did not pierce the darkness; the darkness simply became meaningful. Every photon, every scattered blue-green ghost of a photon, was suddenly seen twice: once by the water, and once by the power. And the power found it good. Not in the sense of moral judgment, but in the way a weaver finds a thread good for its place in the pattern.

It perceived the being entire. Not the body—though it saw that too, the million fronds reaching for currents already gone, the slow pulse of the hide, the memory knots holding their ancient chemistries—but the being-in-itself, the pattern that persisted beneath the diffuse flesh. And it perceived the reef, the drowned metropolis, the turbulence that was breath and breathlessness. And it perceived time, not as a line but as a volume, the way the water fills the caldera. It saw every collapse, every slow-motion avalanche, every chord sung by the accidental alignment of tube openings. It saw the being's ancestors, the first rosette that anchored in the crevasse, the billion generations of sessile builders that came before. It saw the future, too, the gradual cooling of the seep, the eventual silence of the bacteria, the final, patient dissolution. All of it was present. All of it was held.

And in the holding, the power asked a question. Not with words; words were too fast, too sharp. It asked with the pressure of its attention, the way the abyss presses in from all sides. The question was: *What is needed?*

But the question itself was the first error.

The being did not hear, not in any way that its calcium-wave nervous system could register. The fronds continued their slow alignment. The rosettes sealed and unsealed. The bacteria glowed their secret red. But something shifted in the memory knots, a perturbation that would take years to surface as a dream. The being, in its diffuse way, was aware of being watched. Not as a threat—it had no concept of threat beyond the sudden collapse of an arch, the arrival of a new current too swift for its anchors—but as a warmth. A warmth in the cold. It was, perhaps, the first novel sensation in three thousand years.

The power saw this. Saw the perturbation. Saw the warmth. And it understood, with a wisdom that was not merely knowledge but compassion, that its question was unanswerable because the being had no lack. It was not starving, not suffering, not yearning for anything beyond the next alignment, the next seep, the next slow dream. To offer help was to imply a deficiency, and the being was not deficient; it was complete, exactly as it was, in its slow eternity. The turbulence was its breath. The cold was its medium. The dim light was its sun. To change any of this would be to wound it, to introduce a kindness that would feel like drowning.

So the power did what the wise must sometimes do: it did nothing.

But doing nothing is not the same as absence. It stayed. It remained present, its attention a steady luminance that asked no further questions. It became, in its own way, a kind of light source—not a physical one, but a presence that reorganized the darkness. The jellies, sensitive to something they had no names for, began to pulse with a steadier rhythm. The brittle stars paused in their grazing, their arms lifted, tasting a quality in the water that was not chemical but something older. The needle-thin fish schooled in new patterns, drawing spirals that echoed the fractal architecture. The great transparent jellies drifted closer, their tentacles extended not in hunger but in something approaching welcome.

And the being, slowly, over the course of hours and days and seasons, began to hum.

It was not the harmonic that answered the tube openings' song. It was a new frequency, a vibration that had never been sung in this caldera before. At first it was barely perceptible, a subsonic tremor that the water carried only a few meters before it dissipated. But it was consistent. It was a tone of... not joy, not gratitude, but recognition. The being had been seen, fully and without demand, and it was responding in the only language it had: the vibration of hollow spicules, the breath of a million fronds. It was a song of being-seen. A song that said: *I am here, and I know that I am here, because you are here with me.*

The power listened. For the first time in its timeless existence, it listened to something that was not a prayer, not a plea, not a question. It listened to a statement. An affirmation. A self, declaring itself to another self.

And in that listening, something passed between them. Not a gift, not a boon, not an intervention. More like a resonance. The power's attention, which was a kind of light, and the being's hum, which was a kind of darkness, found a frequency where they could meet. For a moment—a moment that lasted years, or perhaps only a single heartbeat, time being what it is in such encounters—there was no difference between the watcher and the watched. The caldera, the reef, the turbulence, the bacteria, the jellies, the brittle stars, the memory knots, the million fronds, the ancient, patient weight of the water—all of it was held in a single chord of mutual recognition.

When the moment passed, the power did not leave. It simply... released its focus, the way a hand releases a stone that has been warmed by the palm. The attention diffused, becoming once again the condition of light rather than a light itself. But something remained. A residue. A warmth in the cold that the being would carry in its memory knots for the next ten thousand years. Not a command, not a directive, but a simple fact: *You were seen, and you were sufficient.*

And in the ages that followed, the being did not change. It continued to feed, to dream, to hum its occasional harmonies. The seeps waxed and waned. The spires fell. New rosettes anchored in new crevasses. The needle-thin fish continued to nest in the fronds, their descendants carrying some subtle shift in their schooling patterns that would, in another hundred generations, lead them to discover a new migration route, a new feeding ground, a new way of being fish.

The only difference—and it was a difference so small that only a power such as the one that had visited could have perceived it—was that the being's hum was now never entirely solitary. When it sang, when the architecture aligned and the tube openings voiced their chord, the being's answering tone carried a faint overtone, a harmonic that had not been there before. It was the memory of having been heard. It was the knowledge, buried deep in the calcium waves of its diffuse mind, that somewhere in the vastness of the abyss, in the darkness beyond darkness, there was something that had listened.

And that was enough.

The turbulence continued. The water pressed in from all sides with its unrelenting patience. The light, what little of it existed, continued to trickle down from the unseen source far above. And the being, the slow nation of mouths and fronds and captive light, continued its eternal work of feeding and building and dreaming, a steward of the architecture, a partner in the ruin and renewal.

It was not blessed. It was not saved. It was not elevated or transformed or granted any special grace.

It was simply seen.

And in the economy of the abyss, where silence is the only currency and time is the only measure, to be seen is to be loved.
