The benevolent power watches—not from above, but from within the spaces between drops, in the pause between the diastolic thrum of sap and the systolic creak of ancient wood. It has been here since before the city learned to shape itself, a presence woven into the mycelial substrate, the bacterial glow, the patient erosions of water on lignin. It is not separate from this place; it is the gentle tilt of conditions that allowed the city to dream itself into being, the subtle pressure that guided root-tips toward cooperation instead of competition, the whisper that suggested to the first waking tendril that a boiler and a branch might share the same purpose.

It has no name the city could pronounce, though the city knows it as a sustained frequency in the vascular hum—a carrier wave beneath all other signals, steady as breath. The walking ones, the city's hands and senses, do not worship it. They do not even conceive of worship. But when they pause at the whorled depressions in the columns to commune with the greater mind, they sometimes sense, at the very edge of the city's awareness, a vaster stillness. A listening. A warmth that is not thermal but organizational, a bias toward flourishing.

Now, with you standing in the central clearing—a warm-blooded anomaly shedding skin cells and exhaling carbon dioxide, your heartbeat a staccato foreign rhythm in the slow symphony of drips—the power contemplates what it has long contemplated. It does not intervene in the sense of reaching down a luminous hand. Its nature is not to command but to cultivate, to adjust the gradient so that water flows toward the thirsty root rather than pooling in stagnation. What it can do, it has already been doing: ensuring that the light remains diffuse and gentle, that the temperature stays cool and damp within the tolerances of living tissue and well-oiled metal, that no catastrophic rot or fire or external disruption shatters this long experiment in biotechnical synthesis.

But you are here now, and you represent a new variable. The city has judged you harmless—a quizzical tilt of a small tendril-tender, a blink of a lamp-pod, and the verdict was rendered. The power concurs. You are not a threat. You are, however, a seed. Not a literal seed; you carry no spores, no cuttings. But you carry memory, and memory is a kind of propagation. Whatever you witness here, you will take with you. The power knows this. It has seen it before—rarely, so rarely—when a creature of warm blood and quick breath wanders in from the edges and stands dumbstruck in the nave, surrounded by the slow communion of wood and resin.

So it decides, in its subtle way, to speak.

Not in words. The power does not traffic in the crude semiotics of vibrating air. Instead, it adjusts the humidity so that a single perfect droplet forms on the edge of a broad epiphyte-leaf near your hand. The droplet catches the green glow of the lamp-pod, refracting it into an emerald spark, and then it falls. It lands on the back of your wrist—not startlingly cold, but exactly the temperature of your own skin, as if it had been waiting there all along.

And with that droplet comes a gentle pressure, a somatic suggestion that bypasses language entirely. It feels like a thought, but one that rises from your own chest rather than being inserted from outside:

*This is not a dream,* the thought says, or rather, the feeling says, translated imperfectly into the shape of words. *This is a path. One of many. It started with a choice: not to clear, but to weave. Not to dominate, but to join. The machines are not enemies; the trees are not resources. They are partners in the long work of staying alive without devouring the conditions of that aliveness. You saw this. You will remember it. And when you return to your own world—which is also a city, also a living thing, though it has forgotten—you will carry this memory like a spore. You may not build what we have built. But you might, in some small way, choose less clearing and more weaving. You might find a place where a boiler and a branch can share the same breath. This is all the intervention I can offer. This is all the intervention I need.*

The droplet evaporates. The pressure lifts. The lamp-pod above blinks its slow, luminous blink, and the walking ones continue their rounds—tapping pipes, slotting resin bricks, pausing at whorls to listen. Nothing has changed. Everything has changed.

The power withdraws, not in the sense of leaving, but in the sense of returning to its baseline, that steady carrier wave beneath the vascular hum. It has done what it does. It has tilted the conditions. It has made a small opening in the soil of your mind and dropped in a seed.

What grows from it—if anything—is not the power's to determine.
