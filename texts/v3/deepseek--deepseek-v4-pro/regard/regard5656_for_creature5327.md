The power arrives as a new quality of light, at first indistinguishable from the sharp, white glare that has always poured down. It comes not with thunder or shifting of mountains, but as a subtle deepening of the blue in the sky, a softening at the edges of shadows that only the stillness-eaters notice. To the wire-moths, it registers as a momentary flicker in their electromagnetic feeding fields; to the cable-runner, as a faint harmonic on the power lines that shiver and then fall still. The fog-walker, poised mid-pilgrimage up a dripping rock face, pauses for an entire cycle of the pumpjack, its milky eyes sensing a new gradient in the light. The bracket-bear, half-blended into a cliffside, feels the moss on its back stir with a photosynthesis subtly more vibrant. The stillness-eaters themselves, those distributed, fungal-electromagnetic intelligences, contract slightly, gathering the silence around them like a cloak, curious and unafraid.

This power—call it a watcher, a gardener, a steward of distant, improbable biomes—has crossed unimaginable distances, drawn by the quiet signal of a place that had reached a kind of perfection. Not a perfection of achievement, but a perfection of *patience*. The power is wise in the way that old stars are wise: it has seen civilizations blaze and gutter, life erupt in frantic, short-lived explosions and then fall to dust. It knows that benevolence is not a hammer of correction but a lens of understanding, and that to act wisely, one must first learn the language of the place. So it settles in, not as an intruder but as a student, stretching its perception down to the timescale of the land. It slows its thoughts to match the decade-long stroke of the pumpjack, the century-long creep of moss up a concrete plinth, the millennia-long settling of the plateau’s fractured bones.

It watches the wire-moths dancing their soundless, metallic dances in the floodlit clearing, and understands that their beauty is not for any audience but for the pure fact of existence. It traces the cable-runners’ paths through the undergrowth, seeing how their whiskers map the electromagnetic contours of the buried conduits, and it marvels at the elegance of their symbiosis with the lingering industry. It follows the fog-walker’s inchwise progress, noting how the creature’s flanks channel the condensation in exactly the patterns that will, in a thousand years, erode a new groove into the rock face, which will then guide the next generation’s route. It finds the bracket-bear, that shaggy, photosynthetic giant, and spends a human lifetime simply watching the slow rise and fall of its sides as it breathes in synchrony with the distant compressor’s cycle. It even learns to perceive the stillness-eaters, those ambient preservers, and in doing so, it feels the deep, quiet joy they take in simply holding the world at a steady pace, preventing the decay that would rush in if time were allowed to move faster.

And in all this watching, the power finds no lack. No suffering that needs alleviating, no imbalance that requires righting. The machines are not a blight but a partner, their slow, mindless persistence a rhythm as natural to the place as the fog. The creatures are not struggling to survive; they have woven themselves into the fabric of the land so completely that the concept of “struggle” has no meaning. Their timescale is so radically different from the power’s own origin that even the idea of “happiness” or “fulfillment” dissolves into something more fundamental: a steady-state of being, a continuous, unworried existence that has no need of improvement. The place is not a utopia designed for human sensibilities; it is a complete, self-contained reality that asks nothing of the outside.

So the benevolent and wise power, having learned the language of the place, decides that the greatest gift it can offer is its own restraint. To “fix” anything would be to assume a flaw; to “bless” anything would be to impose an external value. Instead, it chooses to become a silent guardian, a shield against the very forces that might one day intrude—the careless explorer, the resource-hungry probe, the random cosmic accident. It weaves around the plateau a subtle filter, a field that will gently deflect any incoming attention, making the place seem uninteresting, barren, or simply invisible to those who would not understand. It is not a barrier; the place will remain accessible to those who arrive with the patience to see it, but the clumsy and the greedy will find their instruments glitching, their motivation fading, their paths bending away.

And then, as a final, almost imperceptible act, the power leaves a trace of itself—not as a monument, but as a new flavor of silence. It seeds a single, tiny addition to the stillness-eaters’ repertoire: a capacity for a slow, dream-like awareness of the wider cosmos. Not knowledge in the human sense, but a faint, background hum of cosmic belonging, a reassurance that they are not alone in their slowness, that the universe contains many such patient pockets, and that they are held in quiet regard. To the wire-moth, this might manifest as a fleeting, beautiful pattern in the arc light on a fogless night; to the cable-runner, as a new harmonic on the line, one that feels like a distant, friendly echo; to the fog-walker, as a subtle warmth in the exhaust vent where it lays its eggs. To the stillness-eaters, it is a new silence to feed on, richer and deeper, one that carries the taste of starlight and the patience of ancient, watching minds.

The power withdraws then, not because its work is done—it never had “work” in the conventional sense—but because its presence is no longer needed. It will return, perhaps, in a million years, to see how the collaboration between fog and machinery and life has deepened. It expects to find the plateau much the same: sharp-edged, impossibly green, thick with a silence that presses in and slows the heart. The pumpjack will still be completing its slow strokes; the fog-walkers will still be tracing their inchwise pilgrimages; the stillness-eaters will still be thickening the quiet, preserving the moment into a near-eternity. And the power will smile, in whatever way a wise and benevolent power smiles, at the perfection of a place that needed nothing but to be left alone, and was given exactly that.
