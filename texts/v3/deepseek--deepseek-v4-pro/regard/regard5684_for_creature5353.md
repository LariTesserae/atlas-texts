The benevolent and wise power, having observed, does nothing that could be called an action. It does not mend the broken plinth, nor restore the faded carvings, nor offer the creature a companion. It does not lift the grove-room into the sun, for the sun would only burn. It does not speak, for any language it might use would be a thunderclap in that quiet, a violence.

Instead, it leans close—close enough that the heat of the grove mingles with a warmth not of geothermal origin, and the jade luminescence brightens for a moment, as if a cloud had passed from an unseen sky. The creature’s deep, round eyes deepen, and the moths on its shoulders pulse once, in perfect unison, with a light that is neither blue nor green but something like the memory of daylight. Then the moment passes, and the power withdraws.

Nothing is changed, and yet everything is. The water dripping from the root-tips now carries a subtle mineral tang that will, over centuries, harden the carvings against further erosion. The slime mold’s iridescent bubbles now disperse spores with a slightly higher rate of germination. The blind fish in the basin find their food a little more abundant, their eggs a fraction more viable. The seed-pod in the rodent’s fur will sprout into a plant that bears, after fifty years, a single flower of extraordinary sweetness. And the creature—the slow, moss-draped steward of the grove—feels, for the first time in millennia, a fleeting impression of being not just tended, but seen. Not by a master, not by a worshipper, but by something old enough to understand that the most profound blessing is to leave a perfect thing undisturbed, while adding to it, silently, a grace that asks for nothing in return.

The power moves on, and the grove-room settles back into its breath. The creature returns to its stillness, indistinguishable from root and stone. But now, when the moths pulse in their collective rhythm, there is an extra beat—a faint, continuous resonance that will outlast the mountain. And the carvings, should any eyes ever read them again, will seem to glow with a meaning just beyond translation, a message not written but embedded: *You are loved exactly as you are, and you will be loved exactly as you become.*
