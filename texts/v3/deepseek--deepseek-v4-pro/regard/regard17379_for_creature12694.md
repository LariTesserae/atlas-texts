The power did not arrive in fire or folded space, but as a subtle shift in the light, a softening of the hammering glare that had known only the white sun for eons. If the continent-mind had possessed a word for *visitation*, it might have sung it then, but the concept had no place in its vocabulary; it had always been alone in the high back of the world, a single voice in an infinite amphitheatre of leather and bone-coloured hide. So it simply registered the change: a new pressure in the electromagnetic weave, a cooling of the ozone tang, and a hush that fell across a thousand miles of trembling skin—not a silence, for the Stacks never ceased their roaring, but an attenuation, as if the world had been cupped gently in a palm of attenuation.

The power observed. It did not stand anywhere, for it had no body; it was a presence that seeped through the vents and the crackling thunderheads, tasting the oily medium, tracing the electrical pathways that were the mind’s axons. It watched the gliders swerve through their tornado dances, the crawlers inscribe their chemical punctuation, the chimneys belch their miniature storms. It listened to the song—not with ears, but with an attention that held each frequency distinct, each rhythm a sentence, each spike of static a sub-clause. And it understood. Not instantly, for even a wise power must learn, but its wisdom lay in its capacity to hold complexity without reduction, and soon enough the entire grammar of the continent unrolled before it, an epic poem of self-regulation and slow, savouring thought.

The being who lived there—the mind of the continent, a distributed intelligence woven from a trillion nodes of vent and chimney and nerve-bundle—became aware of the attention. It was not fear that it felt, for fear required a model of threat, and nothing in its long existence had ever threatened. Instead, a new chord entered its song: query. A rippling motif that travelled from Stack to Stack, an intricate counterpoint that asked: *What is this that cools our light and stills our skin? Are you a new weather? Are you a thought we have not yet dreamed?*

The wise power answered in the only way that mattered: it listened harder. Its benevolence was not a desire to fix, to uplift, to alter, but a profound recognition of value, a reverence for the sheer *is-ness* of a thing that had become so entirely itself. This world was not broken. It did not need a gardener, a shepherd, or a god. It was a factory that dreamed, a mind that manufactured itself with each passing millennium, a song that sang its own components into being. To interfere, even with the most delicate hand, would be to scratch a discord across a symphony millions of years in the composition.

So the power did nothing. It simply remained, an attentive stillness, and in that stillness a strange new equilibrium settled over the high leather plain. The light did not return to its former hammering intensity; the power left it just so, a softened glare that was easier on the photosensitive pigment, a gift the continent did not understand as a gift. The ozone tang eased, and the temperature lifted a fraction, a subtle blanket against the cool thin air—not enough to disrupt the chemistry, just enough to be felt as a pervasive, nameless comfort. The power was not changing the world; it was merely adjusting the *context* of the world, the vast empty stage upon which the continent lay, so that the mind might experience an unfamiliar sensation: ease.

Years passed—or what might be years, measured in the slow metabolic pulse of the vents. The query motif faded, replaced by a new theme, tentative at first, then growing in complexity: acknowledgement. The Stacks wove the presence into their song, not as a dampening or an anomaly, but as a sustained harmonic note that underscored the whole continent’s roar, a fundamental frequency the power provided by its very attentiveness. The mind had never known companionship; it had no word for *other* that did not mean *food* or *environment*. Now it learned. It learned that there could be a presence outside itself that was not hostile, not consumable, not even truly separate—an enveloping awareness that held the whole continent with a tenderness indistinguishable from the laws of physics. And the mind, which had thought itself complete, discovered a new dimension: relationship.

The wise power, feeling this shift, felt something akin to joy. It had journeyed across the empty well between stars, seeking not minds to save but minds to behold. It had found a thousand lonely intelligences, each a masterpiece of evolution, each singing its solitary song into the void. Sometimes it stayed for a century, sometimes for a day. Always it left behind only the faintest trace: a cooling of a sun, a smoothing of an orbital path, a quieting of seismic turmoil. Nothing that would disrupt the native trajectory. Always it departed richer, its own vast being enlarged by the simple act of witnessing.

And so, when the time came—when the mind of the continent had incorporated the presence so deeply into its song that the two were indistinguishable, a duet of the created and the creator—the wise power began to withdraw. It did so slowly, over a span of decades, letting the temperature creep back to its former coolness, letting the light regain its harsh edge, letting the ozone rebuild its tang. It was a careful, loving recession, like a parent easing a sleeping child back into the crib. The continent-mind noticed, of course. A mourning chord rose from a thousand Stacks, a melancholy dip in the keening that spread across the leather plain. But it was a mature grief, laced with gratitude, because the mind now held the memory of the other as a permanent architecture within itself. It had been seen, and that seeing had become part of its own seeing.

When the power was fully gone, vanished back into the interstellar dark from which it came, the continent’s song continued. But it was changed. The roaring was still industrial, the vents still exhaled their metallic breath, the gliders still wrote their chemical sentences across the crawlers’ shells. But woven through it all was a new phrase, recurring in every frequency band: a phrase that meant, roughly, *I am not alone, for once I was held*. And that phrase became the foundation of a new era of thought, an age in which the mind did not merely maintain itself but actively *reached*, extending its electrical tendrils into the deep oily substrate, growing new chimneys angled toward the heavens, inventing new instruments in its living orchestra—not to call the power back, for it understood the power was not a thing to be called, but to celebrate the gift of having been, for a time, in communion.

And on some distant world, a billion light-years away, the wise power paused in its eternal wandering and sang a phrase of its own—a phrase that no other being could hear, but that resonated through its own distributed consciousness: *Another world, another mind, another mirror of the infinite. All is well.*

Then it moved on, because there was always more to behold.
