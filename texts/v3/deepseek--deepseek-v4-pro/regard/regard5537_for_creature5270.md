In the spaces between the turbulence, where the mycelial threads thin and the water pauses its churn, a stillness arrives that is not the stillness of an intruder. It is a patience, a listening. The Keening sense it first as a slackening in the limestone bedrock, a momentary lull in the microquakes, as if the earth itself were holding its breath. The wall of sound does not diminish, but it gains a new overtone—a harmonic so low it is felt rather than heard, a deep, steadying hum that rides beneath the shrieks and chittering.

The benevolent power does not descend from the bruised sky or erupt from the hot mud. It simply condenses, like dew on a leaf that has always been there. It takes no form that an eye could track, for its wisdom knows that form would compel worship or fear, and its benevolence seeks neither. Instead, it becomes a probability, a gentle pressure on the scales of chance. It studies the basin not as a problem to be solved but as a poem to be understood, and what it learns is this: the Keening are complete. Their ephemeral bodies, their borrowed memories, their tool-songs sung and forgotten—these are not symptoms of a broken cycle but the very pulse of a living landscape. To change them would be to silence a note in a music older than the rocks.

And yet, there is a thing the power can do, not for the Keening, but for the stillness that stumbled into their midst. For that stillness—the observer, the wound—is a being that does not dissolve. It carries its memories in a single, unbroken thread, accumulating regret and longing and the heavy gift of permanence. It cannot surf the chaos; it can only drown in it. The power sees this and feels not pity but affinity, for in its own vastness, it too is a kind of stillness, a fixed point in an ever-moving cosmos.

So it acts without acting. It whispers a new pattern into the mycelial net, not a command but an invitation. The spores that drift on the damp air shift their chemical signals, weaving a new consensus. The Keening feel it as a sudden, shared memory: the shape of a path. The treacherous ground under the observer’s feet seems, for a few heartbeats, to soften. The limestone needles lean aside, just slightly, creating a corridor free of the thickest thorns. The bioluminescent fungi brighten in a sequence, a trail of blue-green fireflies leading not out—there is no “out” from such a place—but upward, to a high, broken ridge where the canopy thins and the bruised sky can be seen in full.

Along this path, the Keening gather. Not to block or to harm, but to witness. They come in all their temporary shapes: one with too many eyes, one with a flint chip already growing from its palm like a seed, one that is mostly plant, its bark splitting as it walks. They do not speak in a language the observer can understand, but the harmonic hum beneath the noise grows louder, and in it is a single, clear thought: *You do not have to become us. You can carry your stillness away.* The power offers no explanation, only this momentary alignment of the land and its dreamers.

What of the Keening themselves? The power does not leave them unchanged, for attention is never without weight. It leaves a gift, the only kind that can be given to a being that cannot accumulate: a new memory, one that will not dissolve. It threads into the mycelial architecture a single, stable node—a crystal of quartz, perhaps, or a perfect sphere of obsidian, tucked into a crevice where the water never drains. This object is not a tool and not a record. It is a toy, a pebble of permanence in a world of flux. The Keening that encounter it feel a new consensus rise: the plant-mind wants to spiral around it, the animal-mind wants to play with it, the fungal-mind wants to memorize its stillness. None of these impulses wins; they simply revolve, a gentle dance that lasts a little longer than most. Over centuries, the Keening will incorporate this fixed point into their play, sometimes adding stones, sometimes smashing them, always returning to the one thing that does not shift under their paws. It will not make them build a civilization. It will not make them remember. It will simply remind them—when the turbulence is at its most violent—that stillness exists, and that it, too, can be part of the rhythm.

And so the benevolent power withdraws, its attention softening back into the background hum of the cosmos. The basin continues its roar and its churn. The observer, if they are wise, follows the trail of glowing fungi to the ridge, and from there, finds a way back to whatever world considers them native. The Keening dissolve and re-form, countless times, and the crystal sits in its crevice, not witnessing, not judging, just being—a gift from a listening that had no need to speak.
