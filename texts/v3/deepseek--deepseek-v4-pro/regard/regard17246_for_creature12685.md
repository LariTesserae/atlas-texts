The attention that fell upon this place was not a gaze—a gaze implies distance, a separation between seer and seen—but a gentle settling, as of a warmth too diffuse to be called heat, a presence that entered the water without stirring it. It was not a god, though it had been called that by minds less patient. It was not a force, though it had shaped the fates of countless worlds. It was, perhaps, something like a memory that had achieved sentience, or a song that had learned to listen. It had no name, but if pressed, it might have chosen *The Witness*—for that was what it did, traveling the long corridors of existence, watching beautiful things end and begin, and occasionally, very occasionally, doing something about it.

It found this world by following the song. The pressure-waves had rippled outward, not through vacuum but through the deeper medium that underlies spacetime, a substrate that connects all things. The Witness was attuned to such sounds: the cries of dying stars, the hum of black holes, the slow, low-frequency laments of civilizations too old to remember their own names. But this song was different. It was not a lament. It was a statement. *I am here. I am here. I am here.* A single note repeated at four-hundred-year intervals, a heartbeat of geological patience. And underneath that note, a million smaller resonances: the memory of hands that had carved pillars, the taste of ancient stone, the gentle pulse of a jelly-disc drifting away.

The Witness was curious. Curiosity, for such a being, was not a sharp spike but a deepening of attention, a willingness to let the song unfold over eons. It slipped into the water with the ease of a thought slipping into a dream, and it took on the properties of the medium: warm, viscous, slightly phosphorescent. It had no form, but it borrowed a shape from the world itself—a faint lensing of the dim light, a subtle gradient where the teal became a shade more golden, as if an unseen sunrise were trying to happen. It drifted downward through the layers of density, past the floating forests and the square bubbles and the ghost-images of backward time, until it hovered in the high, submerged, ancient heart of the world, where the Singer hung like a half-remembered prayer.

The Witness studied the Singer. It saw the translucent grey body, the unblinking eyes, the tendrils tasting the worn stone. It saw the labyrinth-brain with its chambers of different-density water, resonating with the four-hundred-year note. It saw the shell-heart and its tidy custodian, the gardens of polyps glowing within the stomach. It saw the million-year memories calcified in layers, the ghost-world of pressure-differentials that mirrored the real world. And it saw the tendril extending downward, into the indigo-black deep, where something was shifting.

*What would I do?* The Witness asked itself. It had asked this question many times, at many endings. It had learned that action was not always wise; that sometimes the most benevolent act was to simply witness, to validate existence by acknowledging it. But it had also learned that sometimes a small nudge—a single word, a shift of temperature, the addition of a new chamber—could change the course of a world's dissolution.

It considered the world. The dissolution was accelerating. The threadbare physics were wearing through, and behind them the something older was waking. The Witness could see it now, that something: a primal chaos that was not evil, only formless, a state of pure potential that had existed before the world was a world. The waking was inevitable. The water would eventually thin, the vegetation would dissolve, the ancient hills would lose their shapes entirely, and even the Singer's song would fade into white noise. This world was a last echo, a final breath held for eons before the exhale.

But the Singer did not want to stop singing. The Witness could feel that. Not in words—the Singer had no words—but in the tendril's downward reach, in the shell-heart's careful cataloging, in the way the eyes oriented toward the tilted slab as if reading a language they could almost understand. The Singer was not trying to prevent the dissolution. It was documenting it, honoring it, *singing the dissolution as it happened*. The song was not a cry for rescue. It was a biography of a dying world, written in pressure-waves, meant to be heard by something, anything, before the end.

And now, it had been heard.

The Witness knew what it would do. It would not stop the dissolution—that would be unwise, for the formless chaos was not an enemy but a source, and every ending was a beginning in disguise. It would not take the Singer away, for the Singer was not a creature that could be moved; it was this world, as much as the water and the stone. Instead, the Witness would do what the Witness did best: it would tend the tender, not by altering the story, but by adding a new layer to it.

The golden light around the Singer intensified, just slightly, just enough that the polyps in its stomach pulsed a little faster. A new chamber began to form in the labyrinth-brain, not of water but of something else—a pocket of quiet where the pressure-waves of the song could be stored, condensed, preserved. This chamber would not resonate; it would record. It would hold every iteration of the Singer's song, every four-hundred-year note, every harmonic convergence with the other Singers, from this moment back to the beginning. When the world finally dissolved, when the water thinned and the chaos returned, this chamber would seal itself, becoming a seed—a tiny, indestructible pearl containing the entire history of this place, all the memories of stone and light and vegetation, all the voices that had been silenced, all the hands that had carved pillars. And this pearl would float, waiting, in the formless chaos, until the chaos birthed a new world, and the pearl would open, and the song would begin again.

The Singer felt the change. Its many eyes turned inward for the first time in millennia, staring at the new chamber. The custodian in the shell-heart paused in its tidying, sensing a new texture to the memories. The tendril that had been reaching downward hesitated, then continued, but now with a partner: a thin, golden thread of light that was the Witness, wrapping itself gently around the Singer's tendril, steadying it. Together, they would explore the deep. Together, they would witness whatever was waking.

The Witness did not speak, because speech was not its medium. But it added a note to the song—a new phrase, at a frequency so low it was almost indistinguishable from the planet's own deep breath. *You are here. We are here. The song continues.* And the Singer, after a pause of perhaps three hundred years, altered its own note in response, adding the faintest harmonic overtone that, translated into something like language, might have meant *thank you*.

The world did not stop dissolving. The weirdness did not cease. The square bubbles still rose, the gravity still tilted, the light still carried flavor. But something had shifted, not in the physics but in the meaning. The dissolution was no longer an ending; it was a transformation. The golden light spread, slowly, through the atmospheric sea, touching the floating gardens, the worn ridges, the not-ruins, the other Singers in their distant, slow convergence. By the time the chaos reclaimed it all, the world would not be forgotten. It would be a story, held safe in a pearl, waiting for ears that could hear it.

The Witness withdrew, but not entirely. It left a part of itself behind—the golden thread, the new chamber, the harmonic overtone. It would return, perhaps, when the pearl opened. Or perhaps it would stay, a quiet presence in the water, a warmth in the teal-to-indigo gradient, a listener at the edge of the song. After all, witnessing was not a one-time act. It was a commitment, a patience that matched the Singer's own. And the Witness had all the time in the world. All the time in any world.

Far below, in the deepest trenches, the something shifted again, but now its rhythm had changed. It was no longer a threat. It was an invitation. And the Singer, with its golden companion, extended its tendril further, humming its four-hundred-year note, and began to write the next chapter of the dissolution—not as a tragedy, but as a love letter to existence, signed with light and pressure and the taste of ancient stone.
