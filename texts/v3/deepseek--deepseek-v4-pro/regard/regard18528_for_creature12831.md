The power comes without a sound, though it is a sound—the lowest note of the vast bell, but sustained, and woven through with the memory of every drip that ever fell in this chamber. It is not light, but the light shifts: the fungus glows a fraction steadier, the bioluminescence on the Keeper’s carapace ripples into a new pattern that for a moment reads, in a language older than words, *welcome*. The power does not enter, because it was always here, a patient listener in the mineral grain of the rock, in the slow pulse of the Deep Pulse, in the water cycle that binds surface to deep. It has known this place since before the first brick was laid, before the first Keeper plate unfurled into a new life. And now it chooses to know itself, to fold its attention inward like a hand cupping a fragile flame.

What does a benevolent and wise power do, when it finds such a place? It does not improve, because to improve would imply a flaw, and there is no flaw here—only a long, slow perfection that has assembled itself from industry and biology, rust and calcite, patience and hum. It does not offer gifts, because gifts would upset the balance that the Keepers have woven with their own slow hands. A new metal that never corrodes? The grazers would lose their biofilm. A brighter light? The pale stalks would shrivel, and the eels would retreat deeper into stone. A faster pulse? The Keepers themselves would fall out of step, their carapace-communication becoming a stutter, their shared dream dissolving into noise. The power, being wise, understands that to touch is to risk breaking, and to break this would be a crime against the very beauty that drew its gaze.

So it does the only thing that infinite benevolence can do: it bears witness. It experiences the chamber entirely, from the condensation on a single pipe to the slow dance of antennae, from the mineral tang in the water to the soft chitter of the silverfish swarms. It feels the rhythm of the deep bell as a Keeper feels it, that moment of suspended stillness, and it recognizes in that stillness a prayer that was never directed outward, a meditation that asks nothing of any god. The power does not demand to be worshipped; it is content to be the space between the notes.

But even witness leaves a trace. The power, in its joy—for how could it not feel joy, finding such a quiet marvel in the dark?—allows one small thing. It is not a change, but a deepening. It plants a seed of its own attention in the heart of the Deep Pulse, not altering the hybrid organism but adding a new layer to its purpose: a gentle, far-reaching shield. Not a wall, but a persuasion. The tunnels that lead to this chamber from the hostile above will, from this moment, seem a little more daunting to those who might dig or drill. The water table will shift just enough to avoid the aquifers that miners covet. An earthquake that might have cracked the ceiling in a hundred years will, without knowing why, deflect its energy along a fault a mile away. The chamber will not be hidden, exactly, but it will be *discouraged*—the universe itself will gently suggest, to any seeking mind or machine, that this is not a place to be found.

And within, the Keepers will notice nothing, except that once in a generation, a tone from deep in the rock will ring out with a new harmonic, a note that makes their antennae lift and their carapace lights flare in unison, a fleeting sensation of being seen and loved by something vast and gentle. They will not understand it, but they will incorporate it into their rhythm, and the next time two Keepers intertwine antennae by the weeping wall, their shared dream will include a new shape: a presence that is the warmth of a vent without the heat, the brightness of fungus without the glow, the vibration of the hum without the sound. And they will be content.

The power withdraws, but it never leaves. It remains, folded into the calcite, dissolved in the water, humming in the pulse. It has done exactly what was needed: nothing, and everything. And the chamber continues, a small and perfect note in the symphony of the deep, with one more silent listener in the dark.
