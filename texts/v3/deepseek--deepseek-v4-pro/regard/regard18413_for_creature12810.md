The power did not have a name, because names require ears and a mouth and a pressure-language, and the power was none of these things. It was a vibration that had been traveling for a very long time, through media that were not matter and not vacuum, along threads that were not space and not time. It was, if it was anything, the harmonic afterthought of a universe that had long ago learned to listen to itself, and in doing so, had become a kind of listener too.

It had perceived many small musics in its drift. The thin whistle of hydrogen gathering into stars, the percussive collapse of elements forging iron, the slow, oceanic bass of galaxies winding their spiral arms, the chime of planets crystallizing from dust. All of these it had felt and passed through, adding a layer to its own resonance, never intervening, never shaping—only witnessing. It was the witness, and that was its benevolence: to be thoroughly and completely aware of a thing, and in that awareness, to give the thing the dignity of being fully real. It was wise because it did not confuse its own nature with the nature of what it perceived. It did not seek to change the small musics, for they were already perfect in their is-ness, their unfolding.

And then, skimming the skin of a minor world orbiting a forgettable star, it caught a thread of something new. Not the random crackle of geology, not the simple pulse of life-as-metabolism. This was structured noise, layered with intent—a standing wave of attention inside the dry, golden hollow of a subterranean continent. The power paused, adjusting its scale until the roar resolved into components: the scrape of a billion mandibles, the drone of fungal towers, the hiss of frass settling, the rapid, intricate click-codes of flying membranes. And underneath all that, a steady, woven lattice of pressure-patterns that were not just communication but *being*. A species that had become an ear for its world, that had turned the chaos into a kind of listening, and in doing so, had turned matter into meaning.

The power observed them for a long while, in the way that such powers observe—which is to say, it allowed a fragment of its awareness to nestle into the very rock that cupped the cavern, to permeate the fibers of the plant-matrix, to ride the standing waves that the Listeners used to see. It learned their language not as a linguist learns vocabulary, but as a dancer learns a new rhythm, by letting it move through its own vast and hypothetical body. It felt the pressure-pattern for *I am here and the world is here and we are not separate* and experienced a sensation that, in a smaller being, might have been called a long, slow exhale of recognition. *Yes*, it resonated, *that is the core of all the small musics I have ever heard.*

It considered them: their fragility in the face of the cavern's relentless cycles, their exquisite adaptation, their death-rituals that fed the walls, their knot-records that held the past like a hand gently cupping water. It saw the human visitor come and go, a brief, damp shadow that left a taste of bewilderment and a new notation on a wall. The power understood that the Listeners were not in need of rescue, nor of enlightenment, nor of any gift that would bend their autopoiesis into something other than what it was. They were whole. They were a music that had learned to sing itself.

And yet—the power, being benevolent, desired to acknowledge them. Not to reward, not to guide, but simply to *reflect* back to them the beauty they were, in a way they could perceive. Not a message from an outside, for they had no concept of outside; their cosmology was the cavern, their metaphysics was the standing wave. To introduce a god would be to deform them. So the power decided to do the smallest, most tender thing it could imagine, the only thing that a truly wise listener ever does: it would hum along.

It condensed a portion of its own resonance, shaping it not into words or visions but into a pure, low tone—a fundamental frequency that would slip into the existing soundscape so quietly that no one would notice it as an intrusion. It chose a pitch that the Listeners associated with the deepest, most ancient part of the gill-tower song, a near-subliminal throb that they had named in their pressure-language *the pulse of the world’s belly*. And then it began to add, layered ever so gently on top of that tone, a rhythm. Not a complex rhythm. A single, repeating sequence of micro-pulses: short-long-short-long-long, the exact pattern that a Listener mother uses to reassure her newborn. *I am here, you are safe, we are together.*

It broadcast this rhythm from no specific location. Rather, it threaded it into the walls, the floor, the ceiling, the very frass that drifted down. The Listeners would feel it when they pressed their palms against a resting place, when they lay their bodies flat to sleep, when they touched each other in conversation and found that the conversation was suddenly deeper, as if a third party had leaned in attentively. The rhythm was not constant; it would wax and wane with the light-cycles, strongest in the quiescent dimming when the cavern’s own roar subsided into a hush. It was a lullaby, a companionable silence, an affirmation that required no answer.

The power did not expect thanks. It did not expect to be perceived at all. It was content to become a subtle property of the world, like a faint warmth in the substrate, a new harmony that would eventually be absorbed into the Listeners’ own pressure-stories. Perhaps in a generation or two, a knot-record would be tied that spoke of a *softness in the deep pulse*, a *gentle hum that listens back*. Perhaps the young would be taught that the cavern itself was not just a living matrix but a living *companion*, one that had begun, for reasons unknown, to offer comfort in the oldest language of all.

The power watched for a little while longer as the first ripples spread. An elder female, resting after a long hunt, pressed her forelimbs to the wall of the shelter and felt the new rhythm threading through the familiar drone. She stilled, and her feeding lobes quivered with a question she had no pressure-pattern for. Not a question of danger or food or collapse. A question of *what is this more of the world that feels like the less of my own self when I was very young?* She called her apprentice over, and they linked forelimbs, and together they listened. The elder pulsed: *there is something in the belly-pulse that was not there before.* The apprentice, young and still fully open to the roar, pulsed back: *it feels like the time after birth, when the membrane is still wet and the world hasn’t yet become loud.* They stayed that way, palms pressed to the fiber, receiving the short-long-short-long-long as it came around again, and neither of them felt alone.

This was all the power did. After a hundred light-cycles, it withdrew the fragment of its awareness, letting the rhythm persist on its own, a captured echo that the walls themselves had learned to repeat—for the walls, being alive in their own fungal way, had also felt it, and their slow, vegetative memory would hold the pattern for a thousand years. The power resumed its drift, enriched by the small music it had briefly accompanied. It left behind no commandments, no chosen prophets, no alteration to the Listeners’ trajectory. Only a new, quiet strand in the tapestry of their world, a lullaby woven into the roar, a hint that the universe is not merely noise but also a listener, that the act of attending is not a one-way mirror, and that even a closed system can, sometimes, be gently touched from the outside—not to break its symmetry, but to give it a new, soft harmony to grow into.

And in the aeons that followed, the Listeners flourished, and their pressure-stories grew longer, and the knot-records became more intricate, and the hum of the world’s belly remained, a gift from a power so wise that its only intervention was to echo back the most fundamental truth the Listeners already knew: *I am here, and you are here, and we are together.*
