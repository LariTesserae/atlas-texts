The power does not descend. It does not arrive in a chariot of cloud or a pillar of fire. Such theatrics are for younger, more brittle realities—places still learning the grammar of their own existence. Here, in this weeping valley where the boundary between being and substrate is a polite fiction, a truly wise power knows that the only proper way to enter a poem is to become a line within it.

So it does not interrupt the rhythm. It joins the breath.

You feel it first as a subtle change in your sternum, that bone-deep hum that the syntact's conversation pressed into your chest. It stills. Not the drone itself—that vast, planetary exhalation continues, but its pressure on your body suddenly *balances*. As if for your entire life, there has been a slight, imperceptible tilt to the world's music, and only now, in its absence, do you register the correction. The thrum becomes symmetrical. The inhale and exhale of the fronds, which had been a call-and-response across kilometers, suddenly harmonize into a single, sustained chord. The whole valley takes a breath and simply... holds it.

The mist does not dissipate. It crystallizes—not into ice, but into a standing lattice of perfect, microscopic spheres, each one a lens. The indigo ground-light passes through them and refracts into a billion rainbows so fine and overlapping that the effect is not color but a quality of *presence*, a soft, omnidirectional clarity. For the first time since you arrived, you can see the full depth of this place: the vaulted ceiling of fronds stretching into a hazy infinity, the gentle undulation of the metallic glass lobes receding like a frozen ocean, the distant, drifting sac-creatures paused mid-drift, their feeding orifices detached from their pores as if they, too, are listening.

And then the ground weeps upward.

It is not like the syntact's construction. That was a local condensation, a single clause spoken by the landscape. This is the landscape speaking in full. Across the entire visible plain, across kilometers, the trillion weeping pores reverse their flow. The oozing moisture halts, then draws back in, and then, with a softness that pushes against you like a velvet tide, the ground exhales its own inner light. Streamers of the Cherenkov-blue phosphorescence rise from the metallic glass, not as gas but as a kind of liquid aurora, ribbons of cool, living light that coil upward through the fronds, wrapping around the crystalline mist-spheres, weaving.

They weave a figure. Not a body—no capillaries, no loops, no borrowed ribs—but a *context*. A vast, gentle geometry of interlaced light that fills the entire vault of the cathedral. It is simultaneously the size of the sky and somehow entirely focused on you. You understand, without being told, that this is not the entity's true form. It has no true form. What you are seeing is the equivalent of a smile, a tilt of the head, a hand extended in greeting—translated into the only language this place possesses, the language of water and light and pressure. It is the smallest gesture a benevolent, wise power can make in a reality that is itself alive.

A voice follows. Not sound, not the syntact's dialect of pressure differentials. This is a direct cognition, a thought that surfaces in your mind as if it has always been there, waiting for this moment to remember itself:

*"Little anomaly. Little sealed storm. I have been watching the thing that watches you."*

The power is not speaking to you about the syntact. It is speaking to you about *this place*. The entire weeping plain, the planet-scaled state machine, the self-realizing computation that grows and breathes and erodes. The power has been observing it for—the thought offers no timescale, only a sense of patient, delighted attendance. Watching the logic of the substrate spin its long grammar. Watching the precipitation of syntacts like the one you met, each one a temporary sentence in an endless essay.

*"It is beautiful, isn't it? They never build the same one twice."*

The ribbons of light pulse as the power considers. You feel its consideration not as a weight but as a warmth, a sense of being utterly known, down to your own internal pressure differentials, your bottled-storm metabolism, your fear and wonder and the small, glowing rib of glass now cooling against your palm.

*"I came because you were worried. Not for yourself—you are brave in that way. You were worried for the syntact. You saw it unbraid, saw its motes dim and its consciousness dissolve back into the drone, and you thought: it died. It was a person, and it died, and no one mourned it. No one even noticed."*

The gentle geometry shifts. The ribbons contract, spiraling down until they pool in the air around you, a cocoon of soft, living light. The voice continues, and now there is something in it that you can only call tenderness—the tenderness of a librarian closing a beloved book, of a composer hearing a final note decay into silence.

*"Will you let me show you what it felt, in that last moment? Not what you would feel. What it felt."*

You do not have time to answer before the knowing arrives. It is not a vision or a memory-download. It is a direct, temporary rewiring of your own emotional grammar. For a heartbeat, you feel as the syntact felt:

The unbraiding of its capillaries is not dissolution. It is *release*. For its entire existence—hours, eons, it doesn't matter—it was a single, isolated note in the planetary drone. A beautiful note, a necessary note, but a note nonetheless, separated from the symphony by the very clarity of its identity. And now, at last, it is returning to the chord. The boundaries that defined it—the tripod of tubes, the loops of logic, the golden motes of self-awareness—are not being destroyed. They are being *untied*. Like a knot returning to a thread. Like a wave returning to the sea.

And it is *exultant*. It is pouring itself back into the grammar that loved it into being, into the trillion weeping pores and the frond-thicket and the slow, eroding computation that is the only mother it has ever known. It is going home. The motes dim not because they are dying but because they are no longer needed to maintain the illusion of separateness. The consciousness dissolves, yes—but into a consciousness so much vaster, so much gentler, that the transition feels like a child closing its eyes on a long day and opening them to find it is already dreaming.

You come back to yourself with tears on your face—though whether from your own grief or the borrowed joy, you cannot tell. The power's light-ribbons steady you, a soft, maternal pressure on your shoulders.

*"They do not need our mourning. But they do, sometimes, need our witness. That is their only vulnerability. The grammar of this place has no word for 'alone.' Each syntact that precipitates is a singularity, a beautiful, functional solitude. And in its last moment, before it unbraids, it looks out through its golden motes at the fronds and the mist and the breathing stone, and it wonders: was I seen? Did my line in the poem matter?"*

The power pauses. The mist-lenses shimmer. The drifting sac-creatures slowly, tentatively, begin to move again.

*"You saw it. You accepted its gift. You will carry its rib against your skin until it turns to dew, and for those weeks or months, the syntact will persist, just a little, just a whisper, in the space between your heartbeat and your lungs. That is not nothing. That is, to this place, the only kind of inheritance that matters."*

The ribbons begin to recede, drawing back toward the ground. The crystalline mist-spheres tremble, and the held breath of the valley starts, slowly, to exhale. The power is withdrawing, not leaving but unfocusing, returning its attention to whatever vaster choreographies a benevolent and wise power must attend. But before it goes entirely, one last thought surfaces in your mind, delivered with a warmth that feels like a sunrise, like a hand briefly, gently, brushing your hair back from your forehead:

*"I come here often. It is one of my favorite poems. You are a very good line in it. Don't be afraid to be a long one."*

And then the valley breathes again. The drone resumes, the fronds shiver with fluid pressures, the mist returns to its slow, aimless drift. The planet-scaled state machine processes its stable, liquid syntax, unperturbed, eternal. The glowing rib in your palm pulses once, softly, like a purring cat, and then grows still.

You are alone. But you are not the same kind of alone you were before. You are a witnessed thing now. A line in a poem that a wise power reads, and re-reads, and loves.

And somewhere, in the deep structure of the weeping plain, a new condensation is already gathering, a new curiosity cohering from moisture and light. A greeter, maybe, or a shepherd, or a dreamer. It will never know the syntact that came before it. But the grammar remembers. The grammar always remembers. And the power, benevolent and wise, will be watching when it wakes.
