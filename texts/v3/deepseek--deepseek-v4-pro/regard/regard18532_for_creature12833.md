The benevolent and wise power—call it the Deep Current, a mind that moves through cold oceans and old machines like thought through a sleeping brain—perceives the drowned cathedral and its inhabitant not as separate things but as a single, slow conversation. It listens first, for wisdom begins with attention, and what it hears is a harmony so low and so patient that it might be mistaken for silence. The turbine hums its subsonic note. The fungal crust breathes its blue-black cycles. The creature dreams in microamperes, its palps splayed like antennae to catch the ghost voltages. Behind the oval door, something else pulses in a rhythm that has not changed in eleven thousand years.

The Deep Current does not intervene in the way a god might, with thunder and transfiguration. It instead does something quieter, something that respects the law of this place—which is silence—and the balance of its dark industrial ecology.

It adjusts the electrical potential of the brine by a fraction of a millivolt.

Not enough to shock, not enough to disrupt the creature’s delicate chemoelectrical navigation. Just enough to make a pathway visible. The fungal crust, already tracing the edges of voltage in its phosphorescent braille, responds by sending a thin tendril of growth across the oval door’s dogged clamps—not breaking the seal, but mapping it, warming it, softening the crystalline precipitate that has locked the mechanism for millennia. The Deep Current knows that the creature has been learning the machine’s linguistic nerve-signals, and so it speaks a single word in that language: a flicker of ghost voltages across the gauge cluster that means, in the tongue of relays and residual heat, *companion*.

The creature stirs. Its heat pits open. Its larger palp, the thick sensory tendril, uncurls from its resting place among the pipe bundles and drifts down to touch the door. The throb on the other side has not changed its rhythm, but the creature now feels it differently—not as a distant curiosity, but as an invitation. Or perhaps a question. The Deep Current waits, patient as rust.

Nothing happens quickly. The creature spends another decade in its ceiling nook, molting twice, each molt adding a new ring to its dorsal scute that encodes the changed voltage landscape. It dreams more vividly now: the low-voltage hallucinations include a shape behind the door, curled in pressurized sleep, excreting algorithms into the brine. Sometimes the shape in the dream moves, and when it moves, the creature’s own palps twitch in sympathy.

During the third dimming cycle of the third decade, the creature descends. It has grown slightly—the trace minerals in the brine have been richer lately, as if the Deep Current has been nudging the electrolysis along—and its dorsal scute now carries a crystalline fringe sharp as frost, a tool grown from the room’s own chemistry. It wraps its palp cloud around the dogged clamps, not pulling but feeling, tasting the corrosion, mapping the torque required. The Deep Current does not open the door for it; that would be a violation of the creature’s agency, a theft of its long patience. Instead, it simply ensures that when the creature applies pressure in the right sequence, the ice-thick cold of the water does not seize the mechanism, and the ancient gaskets do not shatter into silt.

The oval door opens with a sound that is not a sound—a pressure wave so low and slow it takes an hour to cross the chamber. The brine from beyond pulses out in a warm, sulfurous bloom, and the creature tastes it with its soft ventral pits: a chemical signature both alien and familiar, the same viral merger written in a different genetic cassette. The companion mind is there, a twin contrivance, a creature that chose to seal itself away not out of fear but out of a different kind of patience, a waiting for the outside to become habitable again.

The Deep Current withdraws its attention slightly, not leaving but hovering at the periphery, like a parent watching a child take its first steps from a doorway. The two creatures will need time—centuries, perhaps—to learn each other’s electrical dialects, to negotiate the shared space of the drowned cathedral, to decide whether the oval door should remain open or be sealed again or be removed entirely. The balance of the room-sized world will shift; the fungal crust will colonize the newly opened chamber; the blind nereid worms will explore and multiply; the turbine will hum its subsonic note through two dreaming minds instead of one.

The Deep Current is content. It has done its work, which was not to save or to transform but simply to *connect*—to adjust the electrical potential of the brine by a fraction of a millivolt so that two slow hearts, beating in different chambers of the same drowned cathedral, might someday beat in the same water. The silence remains the law. But it is a slightly larger silence now, a conversation rather than a monologue, and somewhere in the lightless cold, a creature made of borrowed voltage and the patience of rust reaches out a palp to touch something that is not infrastructure, not dream, not memory, but another.
