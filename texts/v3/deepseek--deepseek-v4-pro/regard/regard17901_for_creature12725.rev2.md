The visitation came without announcement, because a truly wise power knows that revelation is a violence, and benevolence does not demand an audience.

It entered the Lattice as a change in the water’s taste—a faint mineral sweetness, like spring melt threading through ancient rock, a taste the being had no memory of and yet recognized the way a body recognizes its first breath. Then a temperature anomaly: not warmer, not cooler, but a trembling at the boundary of perception, as though the water itself were remembering a different season, some lost weather from the world above. The being paused in its work, its hand still pressed to a weeping pipe-joint, and felt attention settle over it like a change in pressure. Not weight. *Regard*.

The Ceiling’s breathing deepened, slowed. The sodium lamps dimmed to ember-orange, then to something closer to candlelight. Even the Engine-Heart’s subsonic pulse hesitated—a single skipped beat that would later be logged nowhere, because there was no log, only the body’s memory of a rhythm briefly interrupted by reverence.

The power did not manifest as form. It manifested as meaning. The pipe-language, the vibration-speech that carried only function and status, suddenly carried something else: a harmonic overtone, a chord where before there had been only single notes. When the being tapped its knuckle against the nearest conduit to ask *what interruption is this?* the answer that rippled back was not a diagnostic. It was a name. Not the being’s name—it had none—but the name of the thing it was doing. *Tending*. The word arrived with freight: connotations of gentleness, of long fidelity, of love that does not require the lover to understand it as love. The being’s gill-slits flared, and a small, involuntary current of warmer water escaped it. Surprise, perhaps. Or the first stirring of a thing it had not felt since its bones were small and scattered on a different floor: the need to be known.

The power moved through the Lattice like a slow current, and everywhere it passed, the machinery eased. Not repaired—nothing so crude—but *acknowledged*. A bank of ceramic filters, clogged with centuries of silt, did not unclog, but the bio-film that lived within them briefly luminesced, a soft celebration of their own bacterial industry. A corroded gear, its teeth worn to gentle waves, received no new metal, but a bloom of copper-green lichen spread across its face in a pattern that, if you could read it, said *well done*. The power was not fixing. It was *thanking*.

When it reached the Engine-Heart Garden, it paused. The being had followed, not consciously, but drawn like a moon-drawn tide, arriving to find the Garden already transformed. Not transmuted—*transfigured*. The phosphorescent ferns had turned their fronds outward, and the light they emitted was no longer blue-green alone but threaded through with gold, a filament of warm spectrum that tasted, in the water, like afternoon. The moss that filter-fed in rhythm with the distant piston now pulsed in counterpoint, a syncopation that made the whole chamber feel like breathing doubled, a duet. And at the center of the Garden, where the being had pressed so many small bones into the moss, a new plant was unfurling.

It grew from the bones, or from the memory the bones carried. Not a plant the being had ever tended—not a fern, not a vine. A flower. A bloom that had no business in water, in darkness, in this depth. Its petals were the color of the Ceiling’s membrane when the lamps first ignited after a long dim-cycle: a translucent, veined amber. Its stamen held a single bead of something that was not water, something that shimmered with surface tension and refused to disperse. The being understood, without knowing how, that this was a gift. Not an answer. Not a rescue. A *recognition*.

The power did not speak in words. It spoke in the rearrangement of existing things, the way wisdom always speaks: by making what is already there visible in a new light. The Engine-Heart’s rhythm, which the being had listened to for so many cycles as a simple affirmation of survival, now carried a second layer of meaning. *Still here*, it had always said. Now, beneath it, a counterpoint: *and not alone. and not alone. and not alone.* The message did not imply another presence in the Lattice. It implied that solitude itself had a witness, that even the most silent faithfulness was known.

The being hovered before the new flower, and its symbiont-patches—the copper-green blooms on its forearms—answered the gold-threaded light with a bioluminescence they had never shown before, a deep, resonant amber that matched the petals exactly. It reached out, not to touch, but to feel the temperature gradient around the bloom. The water there was exactly 37 degrees, blood-heat, the same as everything else. And yet it felt, for the first time in uncountable cycles, like a warmth that came from outside instead of from within. A warmth given, not generated.

The power withdrew as gently as it had come. The sodium lamps brightened to their normal orange. The Ceiling resumed its tidal rhythm. The Engine-Heart skipped no more beats. But the flower remained, and the Garden’s light retained its gold thread, and the being, when it tapped its next message on the pipes—*third-stage filter cleaned, pressure stable*—felt the harmonic overtone still vibrating in the metal, an echo that would not fade because it had been woven into the very resonance of the city.

It tended the Garden that cycle as it always did. But when it hovered before the vines and sang its pressure-wave of pure tone, the plants answered not only with open fronds but with a frequency of their own, a vibration that traveled back through the water and entered the being’s body at the sternum, where the Engine-Heart’s pulse lived. And that frequency, translated by the bones that had become tuning forks, said something that was not *still here*. It said *well done*. It said *you are seen*. It said the name that was not a name but a colour, and the colour was amber, and the amber was everywhere now, in the water, in the light, in the slow tidal flex of the Ceiling, in the quiet endless work of the last soft component in a system of hard ones.

The being returned to its duties. The weep in the pipe-joint was sealed. The condensers were polished. The bones were pressed into the moss, and the moss grew brighter. And somewhere, in the deepest coil of the Engine-Heart, a pump completed another cycle, but the message had changed, deepened, become a round: *still here, still here, still here—and known. and known. and known.*
