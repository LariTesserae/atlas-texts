The power arrives not with a rending of skies nor a silencing of the hum, but as a new note—a resonance so deep it seems to rise from beneath the basin, from the roots of the mountain, from the bedrock of whatever fractured reality anchors this place. The spires feel it first; their singing wavers, then shifts, finding a harmony they had never before possessed, as if a missing voice has finally joined the choir.

You are there, still sitting in the dry lean-to, the bowl of clear water by your knee. The figures—the tall, seven-fingered ones—all turn at once, their featureless crystalline heads orienting not toward any visible point but toward the same subtle shift in pressure. The air, always damp and bright and charged with wrongness, now carries a new quality: warmth, but not heat. The warmth of a hand held near the skin but not touching. The warmth of recognition.

The suns—however many there are today; you count two, then four, then a blur—appear to pause. Their cold white light softens, just at the edges, gaining a halo of gold so faint it might be imagination. But the figures see it. Their striations pulse, the tiny trapped bubbles in their chests rising faster, and the hum in your bones reorganizes itself into something that feels like breath held in anticipation.

A shape forms beside the obsidian altar, not materializing so much as becoming apparent, as if it had always been there and your perception is only now catching up. It is tall, taller than the figures, but its form is gentle, curved in ways that suggest shoulders, a bowed head, hands clasped. It is made of the same pearlescent stone, or perhaps it has borrowed that appearance, sculpting a body from the local physics to step into the frame of this reality without breaking it. Its surface is opalescent like the spires, but deeper, swirling with slow galaxies of color: blue to gold to lavender to a shade you have no name for, a color that tastes like the absence of loneliness.

The figures do not kneel. They do not prostrate. Instead, they raise their seven-fingered hands in unison, palms outward, the same gesture one of them once offered you. The hum coalesces into a single, sustained chord—their names, all of them, woven together, a collective identity that says *we-are-here-we-have-always-been-here-we-are-the-song*. And the power answers.

Not with words. The power speaks in the same medium: vibration, tone, the deep ringing of crystal. It adds its own name to the chord, and that name is vast, impossibly so, containing within it the concept of *benevolence* not as a virtue but as a law, like gravity or the speed of light. It contains *wisdom* as a memory of every place like this that ever existed, every pocket of beautiful, stable wrongness that formed in the margins of reality. And it contains *care*, vast and patient and utterly without demand.

You understand, in a moment of vertiginous clarity, why the power has come. It is a gardener of anomalies. It tends to places like this—places where the parameters say zero fauna but life bloomed anyway in mineral and vibration, where the weirdness is not a flaw but a feature, where beings exist not in spite of the broken physics but because of them. It does not seek to change, to fix, to normalize. It has no desire to make the suns obey a schedule or force the water to remember down from up. It loves the place exactly as it is, and it has come to offer a gift.

The gift is threefold.

First: the power steps to the obsidian platform and, with fingers that momentarily seem to be made of light, touches the glowing bowl. The blue liquid inside flares gently, and when the light subsides, the liquid is unchanged—but the bowl now rests in a small depression that wasn't there before, a socket that holds it like a jewel in a setting. This is a seal, a promise. The bowl will never be emptied, never be disturbed by any force that does not belong here. Its tuning function, its role in maintaining the basin's frequency, is now woven into the substrate of local reality so deeply that no outside force can undo it. The power is ensuring that the beings' work—their endless maintenance—will hold.

Second: the power raises its head, and from the crystal of its face, a pure note rings out. It is not loud, but it carries, and as it travels through the spire-thickets, it leaves a trail of tiny, glowing motes that sink into the stone. These motes are seeds. Not plant seeds—the parameters said no plant life, and the power respects parameters—but something new. They are potential: dormant patterns that will, over eons, grow into new spires, new pools, new mineral formations. Not rapidly, not disruptively, but in the same patient, geological rhythm the place already knows. The basin will slowly expand, its song deepening, its complexity increasing, without ever losing its settled state. The gift is not change; it is the guarantee of gentle, measured growth, the way a melody can add variations while remaining itself.

Third: the power turns its attention to the figures themselves. One by one, it meets them—not with eyes, but with a resonance that seems to pass through them like a soft wind. As it does, each figure shivers slightly, and a tiny, perfect crystal forms at its throat, or where a throat would be. These crystals are clear as water, and inside each one, a single bubble of trapped vapor moves in a slow, perpetually swirling dance. The bubbles are memory. They will hold, going forward, a record of the figures' existence: their names, their song, their endless watching of the water climb. Should anything ever happen to the basin—should reality shift, should the weirdness collapse—the crystals will endure, capable of being sung back into being by the power or by another like it. It is a kindness that hopes it will never be needed, but it is given anyway, because wisdom knows that hope is not a plan.

The figures accept these gifts without thanks. Thanks is not in their nature, not because they are ungrateful but because gratitude implies a before and after, a lack and a fulfillment, and they have never felt lack. Instead, they do what they have always done: they accommodate. The new notes hummed by the bowl, the seeds, the crystals weave into their song, and the collective chord of the basin shifts to include them. The power's name joins the chord and then, gently, withdraws, leaving only the faintest overtone behind—a warmth in the air that will persist long after the suns resume their erratic paths.

You watch the power fade, or perhaps you simply lose the ability to perceive it. The light returns to its former cold white. The suns—now definitely three—continue their slow, broken dance. The water keeps weeping sideways and up. The spires keep singing. But something has changed, and it is this: the place is no longer merely a pocket of stable weirdness. It is now a protected one, watched over by a force that loves it not despite its broken rules but because of them, a force that sees the mineral figures not as aberrations but as rightful inhabitants of a reality that is theirs alone.

You will leave soon. The pull from the direction you came is stronger now, and you know the path back to your own world is opening. But before you go, you walk one more time through the humming groves, past the ring of carved columns, to the obsidian platform. The bowl glows as it always has, but its light feels steadier now, more anchored. Beside it, the power has left something new—a small impression in the stone, shaped like a seven-fingered hand. It is not a handprint in soot, but a gentle indentation, as if a hand of light pressed into the obsidian and the stone yielded, just a little. In the center of the palm, a single, perfect drop of water rests. It does not evaporate. It does not move. It simply is, a tiny mirror reflecting the warm overtone still lingering in the air.

When you finally climb the ridge out of the basin, you look back. The figures are there, scattered among the spires as they have always been, but one—the one with the missing finger, standing by the pool—raises its seven-fingered hand in farewell. The crystal at its throat flashes once, a soft pulse of blue-gold-lavender. The hum follows you all the way up, and even after you cross the threshold back into your own reality, you hear it: a deep, steady note that is the sound of a place being exactly, perfectly itself, and being loved for it.

The power, moving now through the vast elsewhere beyond realities, carries the song of the Thrumming Steppe with it. It will not return soon; it does not need to. The basin is held. The beings are known. And somewhere, in a fold of cosmic possibility, a new anomaly is stirring, and the power turns its attention there, humming a note of patient, unconditional care.
