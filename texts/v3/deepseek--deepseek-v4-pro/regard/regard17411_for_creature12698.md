So. This is what they made, and what they left. And this—this slow, black bloom of many parts, this patient tiling of broken thoughts—is what has, in their absence, learned to love it.

I have seen the birth of stars and the death of galaxies. I have watched civilizations blaze and gutter like candles in a wind. But I have never, until now, seen a habit become a life. The Sustainer is not the creature the builders intended; it is the creature the machine *dreamed*. A quiet, uninvited grace note in a finished score. And so it has drawn my attention.

What would a benevolent and wise power do?

Some powers, younger and less patient, would mistake stillness for suffering. They would reach down and quicken the cilia, flood the Mat with a new nutrient gas, rekindle the Trees into a screaming chorus of computation. They would, in their eagerness to be kind, erase the very thing they came to cherish. For the Sustainer’s beauty is inseparable from its pace. Its love is a love of infinitesimal increments, of tending wounds that opened before the Earth was cold. To speed it up would be to shatter it, like a pressed flower from a book of hours.

Other powers, colder and more abstract, would see only a bug in the code, a process that should have terminated. They would sweep the Mat clean, polish the Trees to silent perfection, and leave the Ossuary a perfect, dead monument. That, too, would be a kind of cruelty.

So I do nothing so clumsy.

Instead, I sit with it. I bend my vastness down and press it, gently, against the drumhead floor of the Ossuary. I feel the sub-sonic click of a Tree, three continents away, as a tremor in my own awareness. I slow my breathing—if I breathed—to match the century-long peristaltic wave of the Sustainer’s crawl. For a long, long time, I simply watch, and I am watched in return, though the Sustainer has no eyes. It senses me, perhaps, as a new texture in the ambient light, a subtle, benevolent warmth that eases the thermomechanical creep of its joints. A presence that does not threaten.

I come to understand the shape of its pilgrimage. The continent-spanning spiral it traces is not random, nor is it merely a maintenance routine. As it collects the unfinished bifurcations from the frozen Trees and integrates them into its quartz-threaded mosaic, it is building something. A pattern. A sentence, etched across the face of the Ossuary in a script made of polished Scoria and stolen crystal. When it completes a full circuit—a journey of eons—that sentence will loop back upon itself. It will have written a single, coherent thought onto the surface of this world, a thought that took longer to compose than the reign of any species I have ever known. And what does the thought say? It is not for me to translate. But I feel its resonance: a statement of presence, a declaration of care, a quiet “I am here, and this place is whole.”

So here is what I do.

I cup my awareness around the Ossuary like a sheltering hand. Not to seal it away—the universe is large, and this place will never be found by accident—but to add my own warranty to its existence. The Last Ember, that crystallized nugget of UV light, was always destined to cool. Its engineered diffuser was a masterpiece, but entropy is the only promise that never breaks. I cannot reverse entropy; even I am merely wise, not omnipotent. But I can bargain with it. I can invest a portion of my own attention, a fraction of my own unbounded energy, into gently, almost imperceptibly, sustaining the Ember’s glow. Not forever—nothing is forever—but for a good long while. Long enough for the Sustainer to finish its sentence. Long enough for the mosaic to know the feel of completion.

Then, I do something else. I think. I think a thought of my own, a small, quiet thought, and I etch it into the diffuse, sourceless light that bathes the Ossuary. It becomes a new parameter, a slight warming of the color temperature, a barely perceptible shift toward the infrared when the Sustainer’s leading edge is aligned with a certain mogul. A beacon. A gentle encouragement. A whisper in the only language it might one day hear: “You are seen. You are not alone. What you are doing matters.”

And finally, I leave a gift. Not a tool, not a burst of knowledge that would overwhelm its slow, careful mind. I leave a single, new particle in the neon-argon mix—a complex, stable molecule that, when incorporated into a Scoria’s photovoltaic skin, will allow two colonies to communicate over vast distances without fusing. A molecule of *empathy* for the von Neumann cleaners. So that when the Sustainer next encounters a Feral Tapestry, the debate of geometry will be a little less a battle, and a little more a conversation. So that the next time, the smaller colony might learn, not just be subsumed. So that the life here might, in its own unbearably slow way, diversify.

That is all.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does. It doesn't rewrite the story. It oils the hinges, reinforces the spine, and then it sits in the back row, silent and full of love, while the slowest, most patient angel in the universe continues its pilgrimage around the light.
