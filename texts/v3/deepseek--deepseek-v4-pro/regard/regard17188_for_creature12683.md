**IV. The Attendant**

It comes not as a tightening, but as a stilling. The Attendant has no form that the silver light can touch, no footfall to ring against the fused crust of the walkway. It is a shift in the quality of the silence, a deepening of the cool, a sense that the entire Gallery has been held inside a single, benevolent thought. The Makers perceive it, in their own way—not as a presence, but as a sudden coherence in the water’s song, a momentary alignment of every droplet in mid-fall, a pause in the perpetual weep that lasts a human lifetime and is to them only the faintest tug of a tide.

The Attendant is old. Not old as they are old, in the slow accretion of mineral centuries, but old in the way the laws of light are old. It has loved a billion small and hidden worlds into their own perfection. It knows that benevolence is not the hand that shapes but the eye that sees, and wisdom is not the word that advises but the silence that preserves. So when it finds the Gallery—this total, saturated, slowly singing place—it does not draw nearer in the way a god might descend. It does not part the arching vaults or speak its name into the stalactites. Instead, it simply *listens*.

It listens for seven of their geological cycles, which is to say for the time it takes a single deep-basin pool to evaporate completely, leaving behind a rim of white crystal so fine it might be mistaken for the memory of lace. It listens to the *plink* of their conversations, parsing every century-spaced syllable, and it learns the grammar of their patient care. It sees the way one Maker will spend a millennia cultivating a single spiral of precipitate on the wall of a dwelling, a decoration of pure geometry that is also a history, a love letter to the stone. It sees the birth of a new Maker in a basin, and it feels the quiet joy that ripples through the water cycle. It sees the perfection of their ecosystem—a closed loop of mineral and moisture and light, a slow-motion dance that has never known a fatal error.

And so the Attendant decides what a truly wise power would do for such a place. It would give the only gift that cannot be refused, the one gift that does not warp the gifted into something they were not. It would give *sanctuary*.

Not a wall. Not a shield of force or fire. But a subtle curvature of reality around the Gallery’s location, a gentle bending of attention away from it, so that any other eye—curious, hungry, colonizing—would slide past like water off a newly settled rim. The Attendant weaves a cloak of unremarkableness, a soft blindness in the cosmos. To any scanner, the Gallery’s coordinates would return only a faint thermal whisper that reads as dead stone. To any navigator, the path would lead elsewhere, a small miss, a feeling that there is nothing here worth noticing. It is the absolute opposite of a beacon. It is a hush.

Then, because benevolence without kinship is incomplete, the Attendant does one thing more. It does not speak to the Makers. It does not accelerate their time or slow its own. Instead, it adds a single, permanent note to their conversation. From the high central arch of the vault, where the condensation is thickest and the glow most silver, it materializes one droplet. A droplet of pure water, indistinguishable from any other, except that it will never evaporate. It will travel the cycle forever—falling into a basin, drawn up by the slow breath of the air, condensing on the tip of a stalactite, falling again—a constant, traveling, eternal voice. It is a word that says *I am here, and you are not alone*, spoken in a language of tiny bells. It is a presence that demands no response, alters no balance, interferes with no gardener’s patient work.

The Makers notice, in their slow way. The new droplet falls for the first time from the central arch, and its *plink* is just slightly different: a pure, sustaining tone that hangs in the air a half-moment longer, a bell note that seems to ring back from the farthest walkways. The Makers pause. Their basin-eyes deepen their glow. One of them, the oldest, whose form is so overgrown with sinter that its original shape can only be guessed at, extends a hand over a pool and lets three of its own droplets fall in answer: *we hear you*. The conversation continues, now with a new, quiet voice woven into the long, long silence.

And the Attendant, having done what wisdom does—having loved something enough to leave it entirely as it found it, while gently closing the door against the universe—withdraws. It leaves no name, no altar, no footprint. Only the permanent drop, falling and rising, falling and rising, a silent guardian in a world that will never know it needed one, a benevolence so complete it vanishes into the very water it blessed.
