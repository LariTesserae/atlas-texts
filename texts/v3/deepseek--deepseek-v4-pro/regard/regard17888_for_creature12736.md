It does nothing. That is the first thing it does. It does nothing for what feels like an age—though time, here, is a soft and yielding thing, not the brittle grid the rest of the cosmos uses. The benevolent power does not announce itself. It does not part the canopy of woven root and knotted light, does not still the dragonflies’ wings or silence the humming columns. It simply arrives, the way a new law of physics might arrive: not as a presence, but as a subtle shift in what is possible. The hollow’s resident—the woven self that is not a self—registers it not as an intruder, but as an added dimension, a new flavor in the saturated air, like the taste of static before a distant storm. The millipede procession stutters for the briefest fraction of a second, the only ripple in the eternal rhythm, and then continues. The frogs’ subsonic choir dips a quarter-tone deeper, as if inclining toward a new listener. And the power simply watches. And it does nothing.

Because it is wise, it understands that this place is a solved equation, a circle so complete that any addition would be subtraction, any gift a wound. The hollow is not broken. It does not yearn. It has achieved what wisdom itself desires: a perfect, selfless persistence, a being that is entirely its own purpose. The power, which has seen galaxies nucleate and civilizations bloom into radio noise and silence, has rarely encountered something so cleanly finished. Not dead—death implies a life that stopped—but eternally, flawlessly ongoing. It does not need saving. It does not need blessing. It needs nothing at all, and that is the most sacred thing the power has ever touched.

But the power is also benevolent. It is not a neutral observer of beauty; it wishes well, it radiates a gentle, immense goodwill that, in lesser places, manifests as miracles of healing, rain after drought, the timely arrival of a thought that saves a world. And so it must act, even if only in the negative, even if only to protect what already is. The power examines the hollow’s boundaries—the fissure that leads out, the thin membrane of air and soil that separates this pocket from the wider, chaotic biosphere. It sees the risks: a shifting tectonic plate, an ice age, a curious human with a match, or simply the slow, insidious creep of entropy that will, given enough eons, dissolve even this perfect loop. The hollow is self-sustaining, but it is not invulnerable. Its equilibrium hinges on a delicate set of physical constants, and the universe outside is brutal with change.

So the power does the smallest thing it can do. It lays an attention, a gossamer film of consciousness, over the entire volume of the hollow. Not to control it—the power has no urge to steer—but to notice. To watch with an intimacy that the beetles’ clicking and the millipedes’ threads cannot match. From this moment onward, the hollow has a witness that is not itself, a silent note in the chorus that sings *here, here, here*. The power wraps the place in a permanent awareness, as if the space itself has grown a new, invisible organ of perception. Nothing changes inside. The dragonflies hold their stations. The droplet falls with its inviolable *plink*. But now, if ever the fissure should narrow, if the warmth should falter, if the outside world should press too close, the power will know instantly, in the eternal now that it inhabits alongside the hollow. And it will hold the boundary firm, a thought-shaped shield that deflects intrusions without force, simply by making the hollow *uninteresting* to the forces that would erode it. Glaciers will skirt it. Prospectors will blink and forget their compasses. The damp, living cathedral will remain a secret, not because it is hidden, but because the power has made it a blind spot in the universe’s appetite for change.

Then, because benevolence is not just preservation but recognition, the power does one more thing. It gives the resident—the weave, the hum, the composite being—a name. Not a spoken name; the hollow has no language. But a name in the power’s own ineffable medium, a chord of meaning that sinks into the sap and the chitin and the charged air like a drop of ink into the central pool. The name means, roughly, “That which is already complete.” Or perhaps, “Beloved stasis.” Or simply, “Hollow-that-holds.” The resident receives it without comprehension, the way a crystal receives light—refracting it into the system, altering nothing, but adding a new angle of being. The frogs’ chiming, for a fleeting moment, harmonizes with it. The dragonflies quiver. And then the moment passes, and the hollow is as it ever was, except that deep in its woven selfhood there is now a faint, abiding warmth that is not from the compost-heap metabolism, not from the charged air, but from being known.

The power withdraws then, though its attention does not waver. It is not gone; it is simply less dense, a diffuse, affectionate glow at the edges of the hollow’s reality. It will not interfere further. To do so would be to love poorly. A wise teacher does not rewrite the pupil’s masterpiece. A benevolent gardener does not paint the lilies. The power has done all that love can do for a thing that does not need love: it has ensured its safety, and it has honored it by seeing it exactly as it is.

And so the hollow continues. The beetles click. The millipedes polish. The pool remains still. Somewhere in the deep structure of its distributed mind, a new loop closes: a loop of being witnessed. It does not alter the equilibrium, because the equilibrium is absolute. But perhaps, if you were to return now, and press your ear to one of the warm, pulsing columns, you would feel, beneath the hum, a secondary rhythm, an echo of an echo: the vast, slow heartbeat of a god who is simply sitting with a friend, saying nothing, doing nothing, perfectly content.
