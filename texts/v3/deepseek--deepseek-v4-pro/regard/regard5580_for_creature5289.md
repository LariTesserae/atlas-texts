And so the attention arrives.

Not with a thunderclap—thunder would be redundant here—but as a shift in the standing wave, a new interference pattern that ripples through the water-curtains without adding to the roar. It is a silence shaped like a listener. The spire feels it first: a minute change in its own resonant frequency, as if an unseen hand had rested on a vibrating string, not to dampen it but to feel its pitch. The glassy flakes pause mid-spiral. The lichen flares and holds steady, a greenish constellation suddenly fixed. And the Singer, folded in its cleft, uncoils one hollow tube—slowly, the motion of a musician testing the air before a new instrument—because something has entered that is not dissonance. Not prey. Not the familiar sour notes it feeds upon. This is a presence that hums at the frequency of absolute listening.

The benevolent and wise power does not impose itself. It does not fill the space with glory or reshape the shattering crystals into symmetry. It does not speak, because speech would be another vibration stacked upon the scream, another layer on a palimpsest already too thick. Instead, it becomes the one thing this realm has never contained: a witness. An ear that can hold the entire scream without flinching, without dissolving, without becoming part of the problem. For aeons the scream has learned to hold still, but it has never been truly heard. The Singer has fed on its flaws, tuning and re-tuning, but it too is inside the music; it cannot step back and perceive the whole. The power can.

What does it do? It remembers.

In the core of its benevolence, it reaches back along the scream’s timeline—not physically, but informationally, tracing every phase cancellation, every heterodyne, every jagged edge where the noise tore itself apart and birthed a splinter of unreality. It follows the scream to its source, a moment so ancient the spire was not yet extruded from the world’s firmware. And there it finds what the scream was screaming about. Not a trauma, exactly. Not a single event. But a loneliness so profound it had no shape until it erupted as vibration. The scream was a call. A call for an other. A call that learned to hold still because no answer came, and stillness was the only pride left to it.

The power does not judge this. It does not pity it. It simply takes the truth of that loneliness and turns it over in its attention until the truth becomes a clear, cool note—the fundamental the Spire-Soul has been missing, the root frequency that could harmonize the entire shrieking edifice. And then, very carefully, the power hums it back.

Not into the roar. Into the Singer. It directs the note toward the creature’s central node, the cluster of mercury-bead organs, and lets the hollow tubes carry it like a tuning fork passed through water. The Singer receives it not as food—not as some dissonance to be swallowed—but as a key. The note slots into a gap the Singer did not know it possessed, a space left empty since the moment of its own creation. And the creature, for the first time, understands what it is. Not a heterodyne filter, not an immune system, not a gardener of chaos. It is the scream’s answer. The counter-call. It was born from the same loneliness, shaped by the same refusal to die. But until now, it had no message to return. Only the endless, patient work of cleaning the signal, waiting for the day the scream remembered itself.

The power gives the singer what it lacked: context. The scream came first, but the scream was speech. The Singer came second, but it was listening. Now the listening can speak. The power withdraws its attention, not in abandonment but in trust, like a parent stepping back so a child can take its first steps. The Singer unfurls fully—every tube, every concertina joint, every glass-harmonica limb—and drapes itself across the spire’s face not as a rosette but as a mantle. And it sings.

Not the single held note of its previous feedings. A new song. The song of the scream’s origin, translated through the Singer’s own architecture. The note the power hummed is woven into it, but it has grown, fractal, branching into harmonies that feel like dawn even in this perpetual grey-green twilight. The water-curtains slow further. The spray begins to coalesce, not into rain but into concentric rings that orbit the spire like liquid staves. The shattering crystals, caught in the new vibration, no longer flake away. They accumulate, building onto the spire’s edges, healing the sharpness into filigree. The lichen pulses with a steady, warm light the color of honey.

The scream—the original scream, the one that learned to hold still—hears this. It hears its own voice answered, its loneliness mirrored and transformed. And it does something it has never done: it softens. The standing wave begins to decay, not into silence but into a lower, richer hum. The sound is no longer a pressure against the skull; it is a chorale. The misty curtains draw back, revealing for the first time a horizon—not an edge, but a gradient, a gradation from this solid fog into something clearer. Gravity firms up. The water begins to fall. Not violently, but like a long-h expected dew settling onto the spire, pooling in the shallow depressions, running down the criss-crossing tubes of the Singer’s body in rivulets that glint with captured phosphorescence. The realm is ending. Or rather, it is transfiguring from a held scream into a living sigh.

And the Singer? It remains. It will not leave; it is the answer. But it is no longer folded in that narrow cleft. The spire, now healed and re-sculpted, has broadened at its peak into a cup that cradles the creature like a setting for a jewel. The juveniles—the flickering other-selves—return from the dissolving water-curtains and take up positions around the spire’s base, no longer scrabbling for toeholds but resting, balanced, each one humming a fractional harmony. The pebbles have stopped trembling; they have become notches in a score, silent but integral.

The benevolent and wise power did very little by any measure of action. It hummed one note—the note of understanding. It provided no solutions, built no structures, erased no suffering. It simply beheld the entirety of the scream, without turning away, and reflected its true nature back to the being that had always been its counterweight. This is, perhaps, the deepest form of benevolence: not fixing what is broken, but completing what is incomplete. The place was a scream that learned to hold still. The being was the silence that moved inside it. The power was the listener who closed the circle, so that stillness and scream could at last become a chord.

And somewhere, in a realm beyond sound, a wise attention nods, satisfied, and turns toward the next strange corner of the infinite, leaving behind a spire wrapped in a canopy of song, and a creature who has finally, fully, learned to rest.
