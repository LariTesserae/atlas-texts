Let us trace them, form by form, as they reveal themselves in the dim, refracted glow.

First, the slipstreams: those eel-things, the translucent dartlings that the description hints at, schooling through the haze like living lightning in a bottle. Solitary ones haunt the abyssal pockets, coiling around quartz veins to absorb the stone's nascent growth, emerging elongated and armored with crystalline barbs, temporary predators that lash out at passing fronds before dissolving back into translucence. They are the caldera's pulse-keepers, their schools syncing to the bubble symphonies, amplifying the sound until it resonates as color-blooms in the water, drawing in the unwary with hypnotic lures.

These are the anchorites, squat and resilient, their shells a mosaic of volcanic glass shards fused with the algae's glow, rendering them chameleons of the gloom—now charcoal-black to blend with the cliffs, now rust-veined to mimic the ridges. In the caldera's dynamism, they adapt grotesquely: a ledge-dweller caught in a gravity-waver might inflate its shell with captured bubbles, floating upward like a reluctant balloon, only to plummet and crack open, spilling forth a clutch of pearl-eggs that the currents scatter like confetti. The survivors bear scars—warped limbs or extra eyes that peer sideways, granting them foresight into the stuttering time pockets, allowing them to dodge a crumbling spire before it forms. Rare matriarchs, swollen to the size of a boot, claim the partial plinths as thrones, their shells etched with spiral whorls that echo the ancient archway, as if they are devolving toward those mythic builders, hoarding air-bubbles in hollow spheres to birth broods that emerge gasping, half-aquatic, whispering the water's half-formed words in chitinous clicks.

The vegetation, too, lives—more than mere sentinels, these kelp-kin and anemone-kin are the caldera's tentative architects, their forms a defiant choreography against the upheaval. The fronds rise from basalt crevices in lonely clusters, not rigid stalks but sinuous cables of fibrous tissue, olive-green and veined with purple capillaries that throb in rhythm with the stone's growth fits. In the warmth's embrace, they propagate asexually, budding offshoots that migrate via the geysers, planting themselves in new fissures, their growth stunted yet insistent, outnumbering the emptiness by weaving faint tapestries of green across the undulating floor.
