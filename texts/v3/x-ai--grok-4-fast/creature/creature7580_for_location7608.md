In the throbbing heart of this emergent hollow, life manifests not as kings or beasts but as a ceaseless tide of hands and voices, a people forged from the same raw insistence that scars the walls. They are the Delvers, a clan unbound by surface myths or starlit skies, their lineage whispered to trace back to exiles who fled cataclysmic quakes or vengeful floods, burrowing into the earth's marrow with nothing but spite and ingenuity. No single archetype defines them; they emerge in fragments, their forms as layered and improvised as the chamber itself—tall and stooped in equal measure, skin etched with the patina of perpetual toil, a mottled spectrum from the chalky pallor of those born to the gloom to the ruddy bronze of fire-tenders who chase fleeting warmth. Bodies lean toward the practical brutal: broad shoulders corded with muscle from hauling slag-sledges, fingers callused into knots from gripping flint adzes, spines curved like the arches they erect, adapting to the low ceiling's perpetual hunch. Yet there's a wiry grace in their movements, an economy born of scarcity—limbs economical, faces sharp-angled with high cheekbones and deep-set eyes that gleam like polished obsidian, reflecting the bioluminescent flicker without ever seeking the sun.

Clothing clings to them in ragged symbiosis with the damp: tunics of woven nettle-fiber, stiffened with clay paste against the chill, draped over leather aprons scarred by tool-marks and splashed with gypsum slurry. Belts of twisted sinew hold pouches bulging with bone needles, copper toggles, and shards of smoothed shell used as talismans—each etched with personal sigils, spirals mirroring the walls' own growing veins, warding against cave-ins or the whispering madness of isolation. Feet are bound in hide wraps, soft-soled to muffle steps on slick flagstones, though the constant drip erodes them into frayed relics, toes peeking through like roots seeking purchase. Among the young, who scamper like the blind millipedes through the under-scaffolds, adornments hint at nascent rituals: necklaces of fossilized teeth strung on gut-threads, or smears of ochre paste across brows to mimic the fungal blooms, invoking fertility in this barren womb.

They live in swarms, not hierarchies—clusters that flux like the chamber's own breath, twenty or fifty strong around a communal hearth where tallow-lamps gutter in clay braziers, flames fed by scavenged fats from the rare bat-culls or chitinous detritus ground into paste. Elders, their hair a wild tangle of gray-streaked braids bound with wire, perch on ledge-thrones of packed rubble, directing the rhythm with guttural calls that weave into the mallet-thuds: a polyphonic chant that names the stone, invoking its yielding as kin rather than foe. "Yield, sister-vein," they might intone, voices gravelly from inhaled dust, harmonizing with the gurgles of diverted streams, turning labor into liturgy. The young learn by mimicry, tiny hands wielding toy chisels on soft clay mock-ups, their laughter piercing the din like sparks from flint-struck steel—innocent yet edged, for here survival imprints early, and play is prelude to the sledge's swing.

Daily cycles pulse with their vitality: dawn-mimicry at lamp-relighting, when the air thickens with the scent of rendered marrow and wet wool, bodies stirring from reed-mat pallets rolled tight in alcove-niches. Work erupts in waves—crews of kin-groups, marked by colored cords on wrists (red for excavators, blue for masons, green for the water-diverters who snake through feeder-tunnels with reed probes), fanning out to assault the raw faces. A woman with scars like lightning across her forearms might lead a basket-line, her calls syncing the haul: up, down, the rhythmic heave of gravel sacks slung over shoulders, sweat beading to mingle with condensation trails. Men and those beyond such crude divides share the strain equally, bartering tools mid-swing— a bone awl for a copper chisel—while arguments flare like sudden flares, resolved with shoulder-claps or shared sips from gourd-flasks of fermented root-mash, its tang cutting the mineral reek.

Stranger kin dwell in the peripheries, where the gloom frays into personal eccentricity: the Whisperers, solitary figures who commune with the walls' shifting spirals, their skin veined faintly blue from mold-spore infusions, murmuring to crystalline outcrops that hum in response, divining weak points for the crews or warning of micro-tremors that presage collapse. Or the Bone-Weavers, hunched crafters in smoke-filled sub-niches, stripping the husks of scuttling invertebrates to plait sinew-ropes stronger than copper wire, their chants a low buzz that lulls the fungal patches into fuller bloom, coaxing pale caps to unfurl like offerings. Children chase the leather-winged bats through upper scaffolds, nets of woven reed snapping in the dim, their squeals blending with the creatures' echoes— not for meat alone, but for the wings' membrane, stretched taut over frames to make translucent panels that filter lamp-glow into softer veils for rest-cycles.

Yet this life is not without its shadows, intimate fractures in the communal roar. Solitude claims the weary, who retreat to mist-veiled basins where they soak calluses in pooled rivulets, eyes tracing the petrified nerves in obsidian slabs, pondering the chamber's weird pull— that compulsion to build onward, layer upon embryonic layer, as if the place feeds on their motion, imprinting its hunger into dreams of endless tunnels spiraling into the unknown below. Illness stalks softly: lung-coughs from dust-choked air, treated with slurries of fungal pulp and bat-bone ash; or the rare "echo-sickness," where one wanders the murk, voices folding into alien harmonies until kin-haulers drag them back to firelight. Death comes quietly, bodies interred in sealed alcoves with tools clutched like lovers, their forms recycled— bones ground for mortar, spirits invoked in the next chant to strengthen the emerging arch.

They are not conquerors of the dark but its co-conspirators, thriving in the inversion of erosion, where every scar they carve births possibility. In their dense profusion—bodies brushing in the narrow paths between scaffolds, voices overlapping like the walls' own echoes—they mirror the chamber's frenzy: redundant, resilient, ever-expanding. Strangers drawn into this fold might feel the imprint quicken, a tribal urge to seize chisel or basket, joining the swell until individuality blurs into the greater pulse. Here, life is the building, the builders its beating core—raw, unrelenting, strange in their becoming, as if the earth's shadowed underbelly has birthed not just a place, but a people to make it eternal.
