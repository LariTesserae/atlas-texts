In the heart of this subterranean expanse, where the stone's slow exhale cradles the remnants of forgotten labors, life persists not as a fleeting spark but as an entrenched dynasty, woven from the resilient threads of fungi and the restless pulses of fauna. These inhabitants are the true architects now, inheritors of a world that has outlasted its builders, their forms and rhythms emerging from the desiccated hush like echoes refined by epochs. They are not conquerors but settlers, adapted to the arid persistence of the depths, where survival demands symbiosis over strife, and abundance blooms in isolation.

The fungal kin form the foundational realm, a verdant underclass that claims the stone as its own sovereign territory. These are no mere molds or fleeting blooms but ancient phyla, evolved over millennia into colossal, sentient-like collectives that pulse with the quiet intelligence of the substrate. Dominant among them are the mycelial empires, vast networks of hyphae that thread through the limestone like the neural webs of a slumbering giant, spanning chambers in unseen filigrees that can stretch for kilometers. These webs are alive with purpose: they excrete enzymes to dissolve the rock's minerals, harvesting phosphorous and calcium in a ceaseless alchemy, trading them for symbiotic partnerships with the fauna that wander their domains. From these roots rise the fruiting bodies—the fungal forests proper—towering brackets and shelves that unfurl in deliberate, seasonal swells. The grandest are the emerald-capped xenofungi, their leathery domes spanning meters wide, veined with bioluminescent spores that flicker like distant stars, releasing clouds that drift in languid spirals, each particle a gamete seeking alliance with airborne microbes or the hides of passing creatures. Beside them cluster the violet-bruised mycopods, bulbous and tuberous, their surfaces etched with fractal patterns from hyphal growth rings, pulsing faintly as they circulate resinous saps that serve as both nourishment and adhesive, binding loose stone into ersatz walls that echo the extinct builders' handiwork.

Interwoven among these are the lichens, nomadic veils of fungal-algal consortia (though here the "algae" are chemosynthetic kin, thriving on mineral vapors rather than light), draping from overhangs in cascading sheets that shimmer with iridescent sheens. They are the opportunists, colonizing the terraced ledges and meandering chasms, their slow expansion a creeping manifesto—fraying edges advancing mere millimeters per year, yet inexorable, reclaiming eroded plazas into textured tapestries. Smaller still are the sporangial wisps, feathery clusters that perch on fissure lips, releasing payloads in timed bursts that synchronize with the acoustic tides of the fauna below; these spores don't scatter wildly but navigate subtle air currents, latching onto fur or carapace to forge mutualisms, granting hosts faint glows in exchange for dispersal. The fungi as a whole are a chorus of the unhurried: their "growth" is a glacial ballet, caps bloating over months to reveal new, fertile undersides, while subterranean rhizomes wage silent wars and truces, encroaching on rival networks only to merge in hybrid forms. They sustain the ecosystem's core, their decay feeding the air with spores that nourish the airborne hordes, their glow the dim lantern by which all else navigates. In this biological metropolis, the fungi are the enduring citizens, their forms mirroring the stone's waves—settled, layered, and profoundly rooted, a lush defiance against the sparsity that surrounds them.

Yet it is the fauna that animates the roar, a teeming diaspora of invertebrates, mammals, and chimeric intermediates that have carved niches from the fungal sprawl and ruined avenues. They are the transients and the hordes, their lives a counterpoint to the fungi's stasis—restless, vocal, and bound by the slow gradients of the depths. Foremost in number and clamor are the bats, an aerial legion numbering in the uncountable millions, roosting in sparse, stratified colonies along the vaulted ceilings and terraced warrens. These are not the frail vespertilionids of surface lore but robust cavern-dwellers, their bodies elongated with velvety membranes stretched between elongated phalanges, furred in mottled grays that blend with the stone's schist. Wingspans reach half a meter, leathery and veined like fungal caps, allowing them to glide in deliberate spirals that conserve the sparse energy of their diet: spore-laden drafts and the occasional fungal bracket grazed mid-flight. Their echolocation weaves through the din—a staccato ping that resolves into the flapping roar of migrations, waves of them undulating across the halls in seasonal circuits, following mycelial blooms that promise richer spore yields. Predatory among them are the larger variants, silent gliders with serrated maws, lurking in the acoustic shadows to snatch insects from the air, their low growls a bass undertone that vibrates the quartz veins.

On the stone plains below, the invertebrates hold dominion, their chitinous forms a scuttling testament to armored endurance. Swarms of fist-sized orthopterans—leathery-winged kin to crickets, but wing-cases fused into armored sails—chitter in ultrasonic symphonies, their legs adapted for bounding across the open expanses, each leap a calculated arc over fungal thickets. They are the grazers and detritivores, mandibles grinding lichen veils and puffball remnants, their exoskeletons etched with mineral salts that grant a faint, refractive gleam in the phosphorescence. Colonies of myriapods, burrowing centipedes lengthened into millipede-like trains, traverse the boulevards in thunderous processions, their multitudes—hundreds per line—scraping legs in rhythmic cadences that echo like distant rains. These are the engineers of the undergrowth, excavating shallow burrows in the softer earth-compacts of the old architecture, aerating the soil to aid fungal hyphae while harvesting their tubers for sustenance. Arthropod strummers add to the acoustic weave: beetle-like scavengers with resonating carapaces, plucking vibrations from stone ridges to signal mates or warn of predators, their hums merging into a resonant undercurrent that stirs spore drifts.

The mammals, fewer but no less vital, weave through this menagerie as nocturnal opportunists, their forms softened for the cool aridity. Packs of rodent-like vulpines—velvety-furred, with elongated snouts and whiskered muzzles—dart along the infrastructural edges, their shrill whistles cascading through the chasms like alarms in the din. Sized like foxes but burrowed for the depths, they subsist on fungal resins and insect windfalls, their litters raised in silk-lined crevices of the ancient warrens, where the fungi's glow filters through like a perpetual dusk. Larger predators prowl the fringes: shadow-panthers, sleek and low-slung with eyes that reflect the verdant haze, their growls underscoring the chorus as they stalk migratory lines of millipedes or isolated bat roosts. These carnivores are solitary sages, moving at the pace of the environment—stalks unfolding over hours, ambushes patient as erosion—ensuring the balance where overabundance might tip into collapse.

Together, these lives form a layered polity in the ruins: fungi as the immovable bedrock, providing glow and sustenance; invertebrates as the industrious masses, tilling and amplifying the soundscape; bats and mammals as the aerial and terrestrial nomads, dispersing spores and pruning excesses. Interactions are symphonies of endurance—bats pollinating caps with their passage, rodents tending mycelial gardens in exchange for shelter, swarms aerating the stone to release mineral feasts. Conflicts arise in drawn-out skirmishes: a fungal incursion smothering a burrow, resolved by the slow reclaim of arthropod foraging; a predatory lunge disrupting a migration, balanced by the horde's sheer numbers. No species dominates utterly; instead, they interlock in the sparse vastness, their abundances clustered like the vegetation itself—hordes in oases of fungal opulence, thinning to echoes in the bare expanses. Here, in the deafening quiet of the ancient city, the inhabitants embody the depths' creed: life as a patient roar, unhurried and intertwined, thriving in the eternal descent where time itself has become the ultimate builder.
