Reproduction is generative, like the place itself—unions in the damp depressions birth offspring from mineral-rich pools, infants condensing from mist and parental essence, their first cries a soft patter echoing the dripping eaves.

Daily rhythms unfold in patient cycles, dictated by the basin's subtle breaths. The Veilweavers are builders by necessity and inclination, their society a lattice of communal enclaves stacked within the hive-edifice. Higher still, in the spire-crowns where mist thickens into ephemeral walkways, reside the Echoes—elders and visionaries whose forms have softened most profoundly, bodies half-dissolved into the architecture. They are the keepers of the slow genesis, minds linked in a gestalt network that spans the basin, feeling the earth's embryonic pushes as personal throbs. Their culture thrives on the weirdness, art and lore celebrating the negotiable solidity.
