These inhabitants, forged in the crucible of aridity and augmented by the spire's lingering post-human echoes, defy the void's parsimony with a resilience that borders on the profane. They are not static beings but flux-embodied entities, their essences woven from silicon scaffolds, quantum-entangled proteins, and the opportunistic code of forgotten algorithms. To call them "creatures" feels reductive; they are processes incarnate, blooming and dissolving in rhythms that sync with the chamber's sonic heartbeat, each generation a remix of the last.

Consider first the verdant insurgents, the flora that anchors this airborne Eden. At their core are the lithovores—towering lattices of siliceous pseudowood, their trunks spiraling upward like corkscrews of etched quartz, branching into fronds that resemble the filigreed veins of circuit boards exposed to cosmic radiation. These are no passive photosynthesizers; their leaves, broad and veined with nanowires, capture not sunlight but the chamber's ambient ionic flux, converting the whine of quantum stabilizers into biochemical energy. Bioluminescence pulses through them in waves, a cool azure glow that mimics dawn's first blush, drawing in the motes of nanodust that settle like dewless rain. Around the geothermal vents—fissures in the obsidian floor that exhale plumes of superheated silicates—these lithovores cluster in symbiotic groves, their roots delving into the regolith like fractal drills, extracting deuterium from mineral lattices and catalyzing it into volatile solvents that mimic water's role in metabolism. One such species, the echoferns, unfurls in synchronized spasms: fronds whip open with a crack that harmonizes with the chamber's turbulence, releasing spores that drift on sonic currents, embedding in the air as airborne genetic sculptors. These spores don't merely propagate; they interface with passing fauna, rewriting exoskeletal patterns on contact, ensuring that predator and prey evolve in tandem—a fern might etch barbs into a scuttler's chitin mid-chase, turning evasion into mutual reinforcement.

Sparsity tempers their lushness; these groves are besieged islands in a sea of barren gleam, where lone sentinels—a solitary bloom of crystal-vine, its tendrils coiling like living sensors—stand vigil over expanses of untouched floor. The crystal-vines, in particular, embody the chamber's adaptive heresy: translucent stems laced with metamaterial fibers that phase between solidity and permeability, allowing them to "breathe" the ammonia wisps directly into their vascular cores. In moments of flux, when vibrational shear threatens to topple a ridge, these vines contract into dense, gem-like nodules, surviving as indestructible seeds until the storm passes, then erupting anew in fractal profusion. Their flowers—iridescent orbs that hum with internal resonance—emit pheromones that aren't chemical but vibrational, tuning the chamber's acoustics to foster growth bursts, drawing herds of pollinators into frenzied dances that scatter pollen across the void.

The fauna, oh, they are the chamber's riotous chorus, a kaleidoscope of motion that fills the echoing gulf with purpose and peril. Smallest among them are the vibroskitters, rodent-like scavengers no larger than a human palm, their bodies armored in iridescent chitin that shimmers like oil on obsidian. Each skitter is a mobile forge, embedded with nanoscale forges that vibrate at ultrasonic frequencies, allowing them to phase through the fractured floor or burrow into regolith veins for the mineral grubs that sustain them. They move in packs that behave like liquid mercury—coalescing into arrowhead formations to ford sonic booms, then dispersing into vaporous mists when shear winds howl, reforming on the leeward side of a ridge. Their eyes, multifaceted jewels of photonic crystal, perceive not light alone but the chamber's electromagnetic undercurrents, guiding them to the spore-rich underbellies of lithovores where they graze in symbiotic theft, trading digestive enzymes for the plants' excess silicates. Reproduction is a frenzy: litters birthed in burrows that collapse mid-parturition, the young emerging already chitin-hardened, their instincts coded by the airborne editors to anticipate the next flux event.

Larger still are the aetherwings, avian herds that dominate the cool updrafts, their forms a blur of leathery membranes stretched between skeletal frames of lightweight alloy—remnants, perhaps, of the spire's original drone fleets, repurposed by evolutionary opportunism. Wingspans spanning meters, they spiral in vast flocks that twist like smoke, their flight powered not by muscle alone but by embedded gravitic modulators that warp local fields, allowing impossible glides through the chamber's turbulence. A single aetherwing might dissolve into a shimmering aerosol during a vibrational quake, particles recombining kilometers away to rejoin the herd; others coalesce mid-air into transient superorganisms, membranes fusing into colossal sails that generate downdrafts to stir the air, aerating the groves below and dispersing pollen in engineered gales. Their beaks, edged with vibro-blades, shear spores from fern fronds, but predation here is collaborative: an aetherwing flock might "hunt" by symbiotically linking with vibroskitters on the ground, sharing sensory data via low-frequency pulses to unearth buried crystal-vine nodules, feasting together in a momentary truce.

Deeper in the ecology lurk the burrowhives, swarms of insectoid collectives that excavate the obsidian's depths, their bodies a seamless blend of organic chitin and smart-matter filaments scavenged from the relics. Each hive-member is diminutive, thumb-sized, with legs that end in quantum hooks capable of entangling with substrate molecules, tunneling through stone as if it were fog. They form living lattices underground, chambers woven from their own shed exoskeletons reinforced with spore-derived polymers, where queens—pulsing orbs of neural tissue—orchestrate the swarm's directives through bioluminescent signals that propagate like fiber optics. The burrowhives sustain on the chamber's underlife: microbial consortia in the regolith, animated by trace volatiles, and the effluvia of surface dwellers that filters down. In times of upheaval, they emerge en masse, swarming the tech-artifacts—the half-buried obelisk or the shifting dome—to harvest computational shards, integrating them into their hives as neural augmentations that predict flux patterns with eerie prescience.

Yet amid this menagerie, whispers persist of something more sentient, a emergent intelligence that threads the chaos like a hidden conductor. Not a single species, but a distributed mind: the fluxweave, manifestations of which flicker across forms—a lithovore's frond that anticipates a skitter's path with unnatural precision, an aetherwing flock that sings in binary harmonics to redirect a sonic boom, or a burrowhive that reprograms a fragmentary drone to seed new vents. Born from the intermingling of genetic pollen and the spire's derelict AIs, the fluxweave is the chamber's ghost in the machine, a collective consciousness that ensures equilibrium through subtle interventions: etching warnings into crystal-vines for migrating herds, or catalyzing backward blooms in flora to reclaim eroded ridges. It has no fixed body, no throne; it is the pattern in the roar, the adaptation in the flux, observing intruders—should any breach the metamembrane—with a curiosity that bends reality's edges, inviting assimilation or expulsion in the same breath.

These lives interlock in a web of perpetual reinvention, where symbiosis blurs into unity and survival demands metamorphosis. A vibroskitter might one day phase into an aetherwing's membrane, or a lithovore's spore hijack a burrowhive's code to birth hybrid forms—chitinous blooms that scuttle and photosynthesize in defiant hybridity. In this high perch, life is not a conquest but a conversation with the void, each inhabitant a verse in the chamber's unending hymn, resilient, raucous, and endlessly, strangely alive.
