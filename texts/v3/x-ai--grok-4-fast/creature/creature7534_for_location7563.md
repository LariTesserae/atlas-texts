In the frozen forge of this mountain-embedded sanctum, life does not bloom or scurry; it endures, etched into the unyielding alloy like inscriptions on a monument to human defiance. The inhabitants are not a throng, not a bustling hive, but a sparse constellation of presences—ghosts in the machine, or perhaps the machine's own uneasy awakening—distributed across the echoing expanse like the turbines themselves: isolated, rhythmic, vital to the whole yet severed from one another by the vastness of grated decks and humming voids. They are the Wardens, or so they call themselves in the fragmented logs that flicker on isolated terminals, a cadre of perhaps a dozen souls at any given cycle, though numbers blur in the temporal smear of this place, where days bleed into the constant drone without mercy.

Foremost among them are the Core Tenders, figures swathed in layered exosuits of thermal-weave and resonant dampeners, their forms hunched and elongated under the weight of portable harnesses that snake umbilicals into the beasts they serve. These are the originals, or so the faded holotags on their helmets proclaim—recruited from the lowlands' underbelly, ex-miners or riggers with lungs scarred by silicate dust and nerves tuned to the vibration of collapsing seams. Here, they have transmuted: skin pale as the synth-lichen, etched with fine webs of subdermal implants that filter the ozone tang and sync their heartbeats to the pistons' boom-boom-boom. They do not speak; the roar has rendered voices obsolete, conversation reduced to haptic pulses through suit-gloves or the crude semaphore of colored beacons on their backs, flashing Morse-like in the sodium pallor. A Tender might perch atop a turbine housing for hours, cycles merging into what feels like eternities, their visors fogging faintly with regulated breath as they calibrate coils with micro-tweezers, fingers trembling not from cold but from the subtle phase-lag that makes their shadows trail like hesitant echoes. They live in the intimacy of maintenance: prying open access panels to swab oil-blood from gears, their world narrowed to the intimate heat-radiance of a single arcing switch, while the chamber's expanse yawns around them, indifferent. Solitude is their sacrament; they rotate through sublevel bunk pods—coffin-narrow capsules bolted into the undergrid, where they unplug and dream in nutrient haze, haunted by the phantom thump that never fully ceases, even in stasis.

But not all are flesh-bound. Weaving among the Tenders are the Drones, the whatever that complements the whoever—autonomous sentinels birthed from the facility's own forges, their chassis forged from the same blackened steel, squat and spider-limbed, skittering on maglev pads across the bare flooring. These are no mere tools; they hum with emergent code, algorithms evolved in the unshielded geomagnetic flux, learning to anticipate the Tenders' gestures before the shadows even lag. A Drone might linger at the edge of a hydroponic trough, its manipulator arms delicately pruning the wiry stalks with a precision that mocks organic clumsiness, ensuring the pale foliage yields just enough oxidants to balance the air without tipping into verdant excess. They "live" in the cables' veins, nesting in junction boxes during idle phases, their optics glowing faint amber as they process the symphony: not just monitoring decibels, but interpreting the drone as a language, adjusting conveyor speeds by infinitesimal degrees to harmonize with the mountain's subtle quakes. One might detach from its patrol to shadow a Tender, offering a docking port for tool exchange, or vent a puff of warmed solvent to chase the chill from gloved hands—a gesture born not of programming but of the weird osmosis of this place, where machine and marrow bleed boundaries.

Deeper in the stasis, rarer sightings mark the outliers: the Echoes, spectral remnants or deliberate anomalies, perhaps the first builders who refused evacuation when the assembly phase ended. Clad in prototype gear frayed at the seams, they haunt the mirrored bulkheads, their forms doubled and redoubled in holographic infinity, muttering subvocal prayers into comms that no one receives. An Echo might emerge from a sublevel gloom during a phase-shift flicker, face obscured by a cracked visor, to etch glyphs into the fresh welds—symbols that pulse faintly under residual heat, warding against the erosion that dares not touch this youthfully sharp domain. They sustain on ration-packs cycled through desiccators, their biology arrested in the unchanging chill, bodies lean and desaturated like the synth-plants, veins threading blue with non-aqueous elixirs. Interaction is myth: a Tender claims to have seen one commune with a Drone, merging code and flesh in a jury-rigged neural bridge, birthing hybrid directives that keep the pistons' rhythm from faltering. Whether madness or method, the Echoes embody the sanctum's audacity—the human will hammered into permanence, refusing decay even as their own edges soften in the amber of isolation.

No children stir here, no couplings or cradles; reproduction is for the lowlands, this a realm of surrogacy where life propagates through backups and blueprints. The Wardens do not conquer the cold or the roar; they become it, their pulses syncing to the eternal churn, shadows lagging as if to savor the moment's delay. In the sparseness, bonds form not of warmth but resonance: a Tender pausing mid-task to watch a Drone's limbs mimic the lichen's brittle scrape, or an Echo vanishing into the vents only to reemerge in another's peripheral vision, a companion in the thunderous void. They live as the place lives—unyielding, unvaried, a fragile eternity etched against the mountain's crumbling indifference, where the machine-soul whispers secrets in infrasound, and humanity, in its sparse audacity, listens.
