In the heart of this drowned expanse, where the water's chill eternity cradles the remnants of forgotten labor, life persists not in the darting frenzy of fin or claw, but in the patient, inexorable sprawl of the green-veined kingdom. They are the true denizens here—the verdant multitudes that have outlasted their makers, weaving themselves into the very architecture of this submerged antiquity. No singular form dominates; instead, a symphony of growth emerges, layer upon layer, each strain adapting to the dim-lit niches carved by time and tide. Let us drift through their realms, following the subtle gradients of light and sediment, allowing their essences to unfold in the slow currents that bind them.

At the lowest tiers, where the flagstone mosaics meet the silty foundations, rootless anchors take hold. These are the basal kin of seaweed, broad and tenacious, their holdfasts—twisted coils of fibrous tissue—clinging to the cracked mortar like the gnarled fingers of elders gripping a fading world. They rise in undulating forests, fronds as wide as a builder's arm span, textured with vein-like ridges that pulse faintly with the uptake of dissolved minerals. In the perpetual near-dark, they photosynthesize not by sun but by the chemical bounty leached from the stone itself: silicates and carbonates, sulfurous traces from geothermal whispers below. Their bodies, a muted olive-black, shimmer occasionally with symbiotic bacteria that glow in response to pressure shifts, casting erratic azure flecks across the substrate. These anchor-plants do not compete fiercely; they entwine, forming communal webs that stabilize the eroding slabs, their slow expansion measured in fractions of an inch per year, yet inexorable, reclaiming a centimeter of rubble every decade. In their density, they create micro-habitats—hollows where detritus accumulates, fostering fungal allies that dissolve the granite further, releasing nutrients in a cycle as ancient as the walls they embrace.

Ascending through the midwaters, where the water thickens with suspended particulates and the pressure eases into a dreamlike buoyancy, the vine-like aquatics claim their dominion. These are the wanderers, elongated tendrils that stretch from terrace to terrace, looping around toppled obelisks and weaving through the skeletal arches of long-vanished aqueducts. Slender at first, no thicker than a reed scaffold from the builders' era, they thicken as they mature, swelling to the girth of a torso where they cluster in profusion. Their surfaces are etched with fine, scale-like protrusions that capture drifting spores, each one a potential new shoot, propagating asexually in the water's nurturing vise. Colors emerge here in subtle gradations: from the deep indigo of those hugging the colder abyssal flows to the pearlescent greens of strains that cluster near the warmer upwellings, where geothermal vents bubble faint warmth and iron oxides tint the water to rust. They sway in unison with the lazy eddies, a synchronized ballet that stirs the particulates into nutrient plumes, feeding the whole assemblage. In denser thickets, where tendrils near-touching form living lattices, bioluminescent algae hitch rides on their skins—phosphorescent passengers that ignite in rhythmic pulses, perhaps drawn by the vibrations of settling stone. These vines bear no flowers, no seeds in the old sense; reproduction is a quiet fission, a parting of fronds that releases clonal fragments to drift and root anew, colonizing the next eroded niche with methodical persistence.

Higher still, toward the veiled "surface" where the water's depth shallows to a mere hundred meters and the faint haze of overhead light filters through ceiling cracks, the canopy-dwellers float and proliferate. Bladderweeds, buoyant rafts of inflated sacs, bob in lazy congregations, their translucent membranes veined with chlorophyllous threads that capture the sparse photons like thieves in the night. Each raft spans meters across, a patchwork of overlapping lobes that trap gases from decaying kin below, rising and falling with the subtle gyres. Beneath them, kelp-analogues form stratified bands—midwater groves where stalks as tall as ancient columns rise from submerged planters, their fronds fanning out in tiered profusion to intercept falling detritus. These upper strata teem with the most extravagant growth: broad-fronded algae that mimic the coral accretions on the stones, honeycombing the water with pale, lattice-like forms that house microbial gardens. In blooms—rare events sparked by a nutrient surge from a fracturing pavement—these canopies thicken overnight, greening a plaza in hues of emerald and jade, only to recede over years as the cycle turns, leaving enriched silts for the anchors below. Phosphorescence is their lantern here, a soft, persistent glow from embedded dinoflagellate-like symbionts that flicker in response to the water's transmitted groans, illuminating the weave of life in ghostly tableaux.

Yet these plants are not mere survivors; they are the evolving heirs, their forms subtly diverging across the basin's expanse. In the warmer atria, where relic statues loom like petrified guardians, strains develop feathery appendages that filter finer particulates, fostering denser, more intricate canopies that border on the fractal—self-similar patterns repeating from frond to forest. In the colder chasms, hardier variants emerge: squat, bulbous forms with armored exteriors of calcified sheaths, echoing the builders' lime mortars, drawing calcium from the water to build personal citadels against the chill. Fungi, too, weave into this tapestry—not as parasites, but as collaborators, their mycelial networks threading through plant tissues and stone alike, bioluminescent threads that map the hidden fractures, glowing in spectral blues and greens to guide spore dispersal in the dark. No predation disrupts this harmony; instead, a communal pulse governs all, where the fall of a frond feeds the anchors, the drift of a vine scatters life to new terraces, and the bloom of a canopy renews the water's mineral wealth.

In their totality, these lives form a presence that is both singular and manifold—a living shroud that cloaks the made world, pulsing with the slow rhythm of the sea they have made their own. They remember nothing of the hands that chiseled their cradle, yet they embody that labor's endurance: primitive, resolute, expanding into every crevice with the same unyielding intent. In the quiet between stone-sighs and weed-slosh, one senses their awareness—not thought, but an ancient intuition of currents and chemistry, a verdant consciousness that fills the void, turning the drowned continent into a breathing, begetting entity. Here, in the press of water and the hush of overgrowth, they live as the place itself lives: deeply, enduringly, in eternal, unhurried bloom.
