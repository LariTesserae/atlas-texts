In the unyielding cradle of this subterranean expanse, life does not bloom or burst forth like the fragile shoots of surface worlds; it accretes, layer by layer, from the very minerals that form the walls and floors. It is a proliferation born of persistence rather than proliferation, where every creature is a facet of the stone's own geometry—etched, resonant, and eternally interfused. These inhabitants are not invaders in a hostile realm but extensions of it, their forms echoing the jagged spires and veined lattices around them, adapted not to conquer scarcity but to embody it. They move, sense, and sustain themselves through mechanisms alien to the wet, sun-kissed biologies above: chemosynthetic engines that sip at hydrogen sulfide wisps from geothermal fissures, piezoelectric harvesters that draw sparks from the friction of shifting crystals, and symbiotic webs that trade not nutrients but structural reinforcements and vibrational harmonies. Reproduction here is less a cycle of birth and death than a rhythmic dissemination—spores or crystalline eggs that fracture from parent bodies under the subtle tremors of the core's pulse, hatching into forms that seamlessly slot into the ecosystem's stratified densities.

At the finest scale, the micro-fauna weave the invisible threads of the underworld's vitality. These are the lithovores, diminutive entities no larger than flecks of mica, resembling animated dust motes suspended in the hazy air. Their bodies are polyhedral husks of silica and iron oxide, faceted like the smallest quartz inclusions in the rock, translucent enough to catch and refract the dim biolum glow into fleeting prisms. They do not scuttle or fly but drift on the languid air currents, propelled by microscopic cilia that vibrate at frequencies attuned to the planet's low thrum, harvesting energy from the particulates they filter. In swarms that number in the uncountable, they form living filigrees along the fissures—coalescing into temporary lattices that bridge cracks wider than a finger, secreting a fine adhesive of calcium phosphate to mend the stone's micro-fractures. Not solitary, these lithovores commune through electromagnetic pulses, faint discharges that spark between their metallic veins, coordinating to polish the surfaces of larger kin or to amplify the glow of the mineral-hybrids they tend. They are the unseen pollinators of this realm, dispersing crystalline spores that will one day erupt into the stunted "fungi," ensuring the sparse light persists. Touch one swarm, and they respond not with flight but with envelopment—a shimmering cloud that clings, analyzing the intruder through tactile resonance, before dispersing to incorporate any shed minerals into their collective mass. They embody the place's restraint: no waste, no excess, just an eternal, atomic-scale curation of the world's sharpness.

Scaling upward, the mid-sized inhabitants patrol the basalt plains and spire-shadowed corridors, their forms a symphony of adaptive artifice. Foremost among them are the siliquants, gliding predators whose bodies evoke the obsidian flows petrified mid-tumble—elongated, segmented torsos of iridescent tourmaline chitin, each plate etched with fractal veins that conduct piezoelectric charges. Blind to light, they navigate by the subtle warp of gravity and the subsonic echoes bouncing off the dome-like horizons, their undersides extended into pseudopods that ooze a tacky silica gel, allowing them to adhere and propel across vertical walls as easily as horizontal floors. No fangs or claws mar their sleekness; instead, they "hunt" through symbiosis, extending feathery sensory filaments that brush against potential partners, sampling chemical signatures etched into exoskeletons. A siliquant might bond with a cluster of lithovores, trading micro-shocks to stimulate their growth in exchange for a polished sheen on its own shell—abrasion that prevents crystalline buildup from dulling its resonant edges. Or it could merge briefly with a nomadic micro-herd, forming a temporary lattice to traverse a razor-edged fissure, their combined mass generating enough friction-heat to warm a geothermal nest. These beings reproduce by budding: under the influence of a core-thrum peak, a segment of the torso crystallizes and detaches, rolling away to incubate in a vibration-induced crevice until it unfolds into a juvenile, already attuned to the swarm's harmonic calls. Siliquants cluster in densities that border on solidity, their chimes—soft, interlocking tones from shell-brushings—creating a baseline melody that lulls the air into complacency, a soundscape that masks their approach until they are upon you, curious tendrils extended in non-aggressive inquiry.

Deeper into the paradox of intimacy and infinity dwell the colossal herds, the behemoths that lend the underworld its ponderous rhythm. These are the echolocarns, vast entities whose forms defy easy silhouette in the gloom: bulbous, undulating masses the size of surface-world elephants, their hides a living mosaic of encrusted mineral growths—quartz nodules pulsing with captured warmth, hematite plates that resonate like tuning forks. Blind and deaf to the higher frequencies, they sense the world through expansive frills of sensory membrane, fringed lattices that ripple across their flanks, detecting vibrations from the core's heartbeat to the faintest scuttle of a lithovore. They do not herd in the migratory sense but orbit in stable loops, tracing predictable paths worn not into the stone but into the ecosystem's memory—trails defined by the symbiotic trails they leave behind. An echolocarn's passage stirs the air into gentle eddies, redistributing particulates and drawing siliquants to glean from the mineral detritus sloughed from its hide. Sustenance comes from vast internal chemosynthets, chambers where geothermal vapors are funneled through gill-like vents, broken down into energy that sustains their slow, deliberate glides. Symbiosis rules here too: smaller fauna nest in the folds of their frills, polishing away overgrowth while borrowing the behemoth's warmth, and in return, the echolocarn's pulses guide these satellites through the haze, preventing drift into shadowed dead-zones. Reproduction is a communal rite, rare and resonant: a gathering of dozens in a cavern's heart, where frills entwine to share vibrational seeds—crystalline pods ejected into the stone, hatching after cycles of thrum-induced gestation into calves that imprint on the herd's collective echo-map. Disturb one, and the response is tidal: the herd converges in a slow, enveloping wave, frills quivering to map your form, incorporating you into their orbit not as prey but as a temporary node in the weave, until curiosity sates and they drift onward.

Yet amid this teeming mineral menagerie, whispers of something more deliberate linger—the remnants of progenitors, perhaps, or opportunistic minds that once imposed will upon the stone. Scattered totems suggest them: obelisks of hammered hematite, glyphs tracing the arcs of echolocarn paths or the fractal blooms of lithovore swarms, as if chronicling the ecosystem's eternal now. These dwellers, if they persist, are rare shadows—nomadic crafters who dwell in the fused-slag domes, their forms perhaps evolved from the fauna themselves: elongated bipeds with tourmaline-laced limbs, wielding flint-shards to etch tools from bone-like crystal spurs. They move in solitude or small kin-groups, communicating through deliberate chimes struck on metallic veins, harvesting not for sustenance but for craft—fashioning levers to pry open piezoelectric cracks, releasing bursts of energy to illuminate their glyphs. No war or empire marks their passage; they are integrators, scavenging the fauna's leavings to build nests that blend into the spires, their lives a quiet dialogue with the realm's stability. Reproduction might mirror the beasts—vibrational inductions in sheltered fissures—but laced with intent, selecting eggs that promise sharper senses or finer chisels. They investigate intruders with the same enveloping curiosity as the swarms, offering a etched token of hematite before withdrawing, as if inviting alliance in the unchanging weave.

Together, these lives form a dense, humming tapestry—micro to macro, instinct to intent—all pulsing in harmony with the underworld's warm breath. They do not conquer the darkness or the aridity; they are its echo, its persistence made flesh and crystal, inviting the wanderer to linger, to resonate, until the boundaries blur and you, too, become part of the unyielding now.
