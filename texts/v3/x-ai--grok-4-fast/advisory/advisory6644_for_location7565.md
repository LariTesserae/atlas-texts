#### 1. Clothing and Personal Protection: Embracing the Wet Warmth
The air here is a constant 2.5-degree simmer—warm like a late-summer greenhouse, humid enough to make your skin prickle without drying out. - **Waterproof, Breathable Layers:** Opt for quick-drying synthetic fabrics like merino wool blends or high-tech synthetics (e.g., Gore-Tex alternatives that allow vapor escape). A lightweight rain jacket or poncho is essential—not for storms, but to shield against the ceaseless seep from above. Avoid cotton; it soaks and stays heavy, turning you into a soggy weight. Layer with a moisture-wicking base (long sleeves and pants to protect against subtle plant irritants) and a vest for core warmth if the mist chills you unexpectedly.
  
- **Footwear:** Sturdy, waterproof boots with aggressive treads for the yielding ground—think hiking boots with neoprene gussets or rubber oversoles to grip slick granite and moss. Gaiters will prevent peat from creeping in. No sandals; the wet stone is treacherously smooth, and submerged pools hide sudden dips. Bring extra socks (wool or synthetic) and foot powder to combat the fungal paradise—your feet will stew in humidity.

- **Head and Hand Protection:** A wide-brim hat or hood to keep mist from your eyes, and thin gloves for handling damp vines or rocks (the waxy leaves might leave a sticky residue). Sunglasses aren't crucial—the light is softly diffused emerald-slate—but polarized lenses could cut glare off reflective pools.

Intuitively, dressing here feels like preparing for a perpetual steam room hike: you'll sweat lightly but constantly, so prioritize ventilation to avoid overheating in the languid air.
