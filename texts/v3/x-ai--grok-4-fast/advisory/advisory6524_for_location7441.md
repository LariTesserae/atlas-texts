#### 1. **Clothing and Physical Protection: Shielding Against Sharp Rocks, Heat, and Humidity**

- **Base Layer:** Opt for lightweight, moisture-wicking synthetics like merino wool blends or advanced quick-dry fabrics (e.g., from your multiverse kit's "Earth-Standard Explorer" line). Avoid cotton—it'll cling like a wet shroud. Long sleeves and pants protect against snags and plant residue; tuck pants into boots to bar grit and potential unseen crawlers (though fauna seems absent, subterranean oddities could surprise).
  
- **Outer Protection:** A breathable, heat-reflective jacket (e.g., with phase-change materials that absorb body heat) for the sauna-like air, but vented for airflow. For the sharp basalt, reinforce with abrasion-resistant overlays—kevlar-threaded pants or elbow/knee pads. Gloves are essential: thick, dexterous leather or nitrile-coated ones to handle vines without direct touch (the warming residue might feel like a mild chemical burn; test with a disposable glove first). A wide-brim hat or headscarf wards off dripping fissures—those "reluctant tears" from the ceiling could carry minerals that sting eyes or erode gear.

- **Footwear:** Sturdy, high-ankle hiking boots with aggressive treads for the uneven, gritty floor. The undulations and shallow basins mean ankle support is key to avoid twists, and waterproofing helps with rivulets (though the porous stone drains quickly). Pack gaiters to keep damp grit out—it's not quicksand, but it could cake into your socks like mineral paste.

- **Intuitive Tip:** Dress in layers you can shed if the heat overwhelms, but layer up for the initial descent (surface air might be cooler). The chamber's "fresh" erosion means no smoothed paths—every step feels like negotiating a resentful sculpture. Budget for repairs: Bring a small sewing kit and duct tape for inevitable tears.
