#### 1. **Physical Protection: Suits, Pressure, and the Cold Embrace**

It maintains internal atmospheric pressure (1 atm), so you won't need to equalize your body—your lungs breathe normal air, not compressed gas. Expect 200-300 kg of weight on the surface, but buoyancy compensators will make you neutrally buoyant underwater. Key features:
  - **Thermal Insulation:** Multi-layer neoprene and aerogel lining to counter the 0.1 heat scale. Vents near geothermal sources might hit lukewarm (10-15°C), but baseline is 2-4°C—add phase-change materials that absorb body heat loss. Gloves and helmet seals must be triple-redundant; frostbite "from the inside out" means hypothermia sets in fast without it.
  - **Pressure Resistance:** Tested to 600m+ depths (the hollow feels like 1-2km down). Include a built-in emergency ballast release and a "jettison" system to shed weight if mobility fails.
  - **Mobility Enhancements:** Thrusters for propulsion (the water's viscosity demands gentle, deliberate drifts—currents at 1.8 dynamic energy could sweep you into machinery). Magnetic boots or grippers for clinging to rusted surfaces; the 2.3 erosion means everything's pitted and slick with silt or barnacles.

- **Backup: A Personal Submersible Pod.** If claustrophobia from the suit is an issue (density at 2.4 feels like brushing rust on all sides), bring a compact one-person sub (e.g., a 1m-diameter sphere with manipulators). It lets you "visit" without full exposure, but you'll still feel the press—viewports will fog in the cold, and the pod's hum might disrupt the 0.1 sound quietude.

- **Duration Prep:** Plan for 4-8 hours max per dive; longer risks suit fatigue or battery drain. Hydrate pre-dive (dehydration amplifies cold), and pack nutrient gels—eating inside the suit is fiddly but essential.

Visiting feels like being swallowed whole: the suit turns you into a clanking intruder in a space that's yielded to the sea, every step a negotiation with the liquid shroud.
