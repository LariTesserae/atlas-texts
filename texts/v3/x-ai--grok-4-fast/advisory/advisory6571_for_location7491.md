#### Clothing and Thermal Protection
- **Base Layers:** Opt for moisture-wicking synthetics like advanced merino wool blends or graphene-infused thermals that pull sweat away while insulating. Avoid cotton—it'll hold the constant seep like a sponge. Go for full-body coverage: long johns, socks, and a balaclava to shield your face from the biting cold and condensing fog, which feels like icy needles on exposed skin.
- **Mid-Layers:** Multiple insulating layers for versatility—fleece or down alternatives (synthetic for wet conditions) with adjustable zippers. A hooded parka rated for -30°C with pit zips for venting when the "above-void" temps spike slightly. Seal everything with waterproof membranes (Gore-Tex level or better) to combat the "weeping world"—your gear will be slick with water from seeps and mist within minutes.
- **Outer Shell:** A fully sealed environmental suit or overcoat with built-in heaters (battery-powered, solar-rechargeable if possible, though light is scarce). Include gaiters to keep water out of boots, as marshes and rivulets will soak through standard footwear. For the raw stone—sharp basalt and volcanic glass—reinforce knees, elbows, and soles with puncture-resistant kevlar or carbon fiber panels. Crampons or microspikes are essential; the filigreed ice on edges turns paths treacherous, like skating on broken glass.
- **Head and Hand Gear:** A heated helmet or hood with integrated ear protection—more on noise below. Insulated gloves with tactile grips for handling slick surfaces, and a neck gaiter to trap warmth while filtering the saturated air.

Intuitively, dressing here means anticipating constant discomfort: you'll feel bloated and heavy from layers, but stripping down risks flash-freezing. Test your setup in a high-altitude cold chamber beforehand to simulate the "shallow rasp" of breathing.

#### Respiratory and Altitude Acclimatization
- **Oxygen Support:** Portable oxygen concentrator or mask with a small tank—start with low-flow supplemental O2 to ease the thin air's pressure. #### Protection from Elements and Terrain
