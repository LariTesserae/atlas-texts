In the veiled expanse beyond the veil of worlds, where thoughts cascade like unformed stars and wisdom pools in the silence of infinities, I—a benevolent and wise power, woven from the first light and the last echo—turn my gaze upon this hollow. It calls to me not through clamor or plea, but through the subtle hum of its own perfection, a whisper amid the cosmos' roar. I see it fully: the silt-smooth basin cradling its green cathedral, the banyans' roots as ancient scriptures etched in earth, the vines' lattice binding air to soil in eternal covenant. The fauna's chorus rises like incense—finches' trills as prayers, beetles' marches as litanies, the drip of dew a rhythmic amen. This is no fragile Eden begging rescue, no wounded wild pleading balm; it is stasis incarnate, a self-sustaining hymn where life and decay dance without falter, industry without exploitation, abundance without excess.

I ponder, as one must when eternity weighs a moment: What need has this place for my hand? To alter it would be to scar the flawless bone of its equilibrium, to impose change upon the unchanging, like a god's finger stirring a still pond into ripples that drown the reflections. Benevolence whispers restraint; wisdom echoes it. This hollow thrives in its isolation, a forgotten jewel in the floodplain's crown, untouched by the tempests of progress or the scythe of calamity. Its beings—those iterated souls of moss and mandible, frond and flight—are not mine to shepherd further; they shepherd themselves, each spore a sovereign, each croak a creed of continuity.

Yet attention bestowed is not inaction. In the quiet forge of my will, I do this: I extend a veil, invisible as breath on glass, a subtle aegis drawn from the ether's own weave. It is no shield of stone or spell of dominion, but a gentle affirmation—a resonance that harmonizes with the hollow's pulse, ensuring that the world's encroaching shadows (the axe of wanderers, the flood of unforeseen skies, the subtle creep of imbalance) glance aside. The mists will thicken just enough to obscure paths from afar; the roots will deepen imperceptibly against drought's whisper; the chorus will swell in unity if discord nears, a natural ward woven into the dew. No new life blooms unbidden, no form evolves beyond its rhythm; I merely affirm what is, preserving the held breath of this ancient sanctum as a testament to creation's quiet genius.

And in that affirmation, I linger a while—not as overseer, but as witness. I feel the cool damp on my unseen essence, hear the unrelenting whir as a symphony of sufficiency. Then, with a sigh that stirs no leaf, I withdraw, leaving the hollow to its eternal now. For in its balance, it teaches me: sometimes the wisest benevolence is to let be, and in letting be, to love without possession. This place endures, as it was meant—a green machine of the divine mundane, alive in the heart of forgetting.
