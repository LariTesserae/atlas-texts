The air hangs heavy with humidity, a perpetual shroud that clings to everything like a lover's breath gone sour. This is a place called the Wealdforge, a compact industrial hamlet no larger than a forgotten cathedral's nave, hemmed in by the slow creep of brackish canals and the undulating fringe of a vast, unseen marsh beyond. Low-lying at the edge of some forgotten delta, the ground here never truly dries; water bubbles up from aquifers just beneath the surface, seeping through cracked concrete and pooling in the hollows of rusted iron. It's hot—oppressively so, a steam-bath heat that rises from the earth like fevered sighs, amplified by the ceaseless belch of factory vents spewing humid exhaust into the dimming sky. The sun, when it dares pierce the perpetual overcast of condensation clouds, filters through in weak, diffused smears, casting the whole scene in a twilight murk that makes shadows long and secrets deeper.

The structures huddle in sparse defiance against this wetness, their forms weathered into a balanced decay after decades of quiet endurance. Brick facades, once proud and modular in the industrial vein, now lean with the subtle erosion of time—moss-veined mortar softened by endless drips, iron girders pitted where acidic rains have kissed them into lace. Factories and workshops form the core, a loose cluster of perhaps a dozen buildings scaled like an old town square: a central manufactory with its towering smokestacks, flanked by machine sheds, a rail depot choked with derelict engines, and the skeletal outlines of worker barracks half-swallowed by the terrain. But it's not dense; vast expanses of open ground yawn between them, paved yards cracked open to reveal the mud below, where the water table presses upward like an insistent pulse. These empty spaces feel deliberate, almost ritualistic—room for the steam to billow unchecked, for the heat to mirage the air into wavering veils. No crowds here, no throng of life; the urban skeleton is organized but skeletal, layered with catwalks and conveyor remnants that snake through the voids like veins in a vast, emptied body.

Lushness creeps in from the margins, unbidden and verdant, thriving in the hot damp without apology. Ferns unfurl in dense fronds from the bases of walls, their leaves broad and dripping with condensed moisture, while vines of glossy, thorned creepers ascend the facades in slow, inexorable conquests. Here and there, clusters of mangroves—strange transplants or mutations—thrust their aerial roots into flooded lots, forming natural grottos where the water laps at corroded machinery. The vegetation isn't a jungle overrun; it's scattered, lush pockets blooming in the sparsest way, as if the plants themselves respect the emptiness, growing tall and isolated like sentinels. A single banyan-like tree might dominate an entire yard, its canopy a dripping umbrella that shelters rusted boilers below, while elsewhere, beds of lily pads float on shallow ponds, their flowers blooming in unnatural hues—purples too deep, edges faintly luminescent in the dim light.

But strangeness threads through it all, subtle at first, then insistent. The water doesn't just seep; it weeps from the rocks embedded in the low embankments, forming rivulets that carry a faint, iridescent sheen, as if the earth itself is crying oil or some alchemical distillate from the factories' innards. Machines hum with an organic rhythm, their pistons throbbing like heartbeats synchronized to the distant slosh of tides that shouldn't reach this far inland. In the dimness, shadows seem to shift independently, pooling in the open spaces where no light should cast them, and the vegetation whispers of adaptation beyond nature—leaves that curl toward the heat vents, drawing sustenance from exhaust fumes, roots that pry open sealed hatches as if invited. No birds call, no insects buzz; the fauna is absent, a void that amplifies the weird pulse of the place, leaving only the mechanical and the vegetal to commune in silence broken by noise.

Sound assaults from every direction, loud and unrelenting, a cacophony that defines the dynamic churn. Engines growl in the guts of the central forge, their industrial clamor—clanging hammers on anvils, hissing steam releases, the whine of belts driving unseen gears—mingling with the natural roar of water: cascades tumbling over weirs into the canals, the perpetual patter of rain-like condensation from overhead pipes, the gurgle of overflows in the marsh-edge fringes. It's active, alive with processes: chemical vats bubble and froth in open courtyards, releasing plumes that warp the hot air into visible currents; conveyor arms swing lazily but ceaselessly, hauling ore from submerged barges to grinders that pulverize it with rhythmic thuds. Weather cycles blur with the man-made—sudden mists roll in from the lowlands, carrying the scent of brine and sulfur, while biological undercurrents stir in the plants: vines rustling as they inch forward, fronds shuddering in the heat's convection. No predators stalk these grounds, yet motion abounds—a ecosystem of iron and green in perpetual, unbalanced flux, where the hot wetness erodes and rebuilds in the same breath, the dim light hiding how the boundaries between built and wild are dissolving, thread by verdant thread.
