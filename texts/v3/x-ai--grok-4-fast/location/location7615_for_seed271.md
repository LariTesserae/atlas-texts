Here and there, isolated shrubs rise, gnarled and unhurried, their leaves broad but few, heavy with the weight of condensation that drips sporadically to the ground. No towering trees claim the center; perhaps a single, venerable oak or fig stands sentinel at one edge, its branches arching low and sparse, casting elongated shadows in the dim light. Fungi cling to the rock faces in pale clusters, and lichens paint the stones in subtle mosaics of gray and ochre, thriving in the balanced damp without overwhelming it. The ecology is one of restraint: plants exist in quiet symbiosis with the moisture, drawing just enough to persist, their growth arrested in this stable clime where seasons blur into one another, unchanging.

No animal calls pierce this stillness; fauna is absent, the place devoid of scurrying life or winged shadows.
