You descend through a jagged fissure in the earth, a fresh wound in the bedrock no older than a season's rain—sharp-edged and unweathered, its walls of raw limestone and quartz still dusted with the pulverized grit of its own violent birth. The air grows thick and warm, like breath from a hidden fever, as you slip into the pocket below: not a grand cavern, but an intimate hollow, a single-room void embedded in the planet's underbelly, perhaps twenty paces across at its widest, domed like a imperfect skull. Yet scale deceives here; the space feels hollowed to near-emptiness, a vastness stretched thin between scant presences, where every element hangs in sparse isolation amid yawning gaps of nothing. Density is a lie in this place—almost void, with presences so few and far-flung that the air itself seems to echo their solitude, pulling at you like the tug of deep space.

But the plants... oh, the plants defy that emptiness, claiming totality in their insidious sprawl. They are the substrate, the architecture, the very mass of existence here—a pure-plant ecosystem that pulses with the weight of all life, filling the conceptual space even as physical volume yawns barren. Vines thicker than your arm coil up from fissures in the floor, not rooted in soil but in the stone itself, their leaves broad and veined like living membranes that quiver in the warm currents. These are no ordinary flora; they form the walls where stone fails, weaving into a lattice of green that simulates structure without artifice—untouched by hand or tool, for this is a world undeveloped, primitive in its raw tech of growth alone. Simple, hand-wrought by nature's crude fabrication: thorns like early chisels, stems that bend with the tensile strength of unrefined wire. Fungi cluster in the crevices, pale caps unfurling like forgotten blooms, their spores drifting in lazy helices through the damp air. The vegetation isn't dense in bodies—vast spaces gape between each tendril, each frond suspended in isolation—but it is total, an all-living-mass that subsumes the environment, turning the hollow into a breathing organ. Roots delve into the young rock, cracking it further with their inexorable push, for erosion here is nascent, the features sharp and unsoftened: knife-edge stalactites overhead, floors etched with fresh runnels from recent upheavals.

Water haunts the edges, intermittent and sly—a dampness that never floods but persists like a whispered promise. Condensation beads on the leaves, swelling and dripping in erratic rhythms, pooling in shallow pockets amid the stone's fractures. Occasional rivulets trace the walls, fed by unseen aquifers, their flow warm and mineral-tanged, carrying the scent of wet earth and fermenting sap. No rivers rage, but the moisture is everywhere in suggestion: mist that clings to your skin, fostering the plants' relentless verdancy in this subsurface warmth. The temperature hovers at the edge of comfort, a balmy haze that makes the air feel alive, pressing close in the intimate confines.

Light, improbably, is normal—daylight normal, as if borrowed from the surface world above, filtering through reality's fraying seams. It spills from bioluminescent veins in the plants themselves, a soft, even glow that mimics sunlit midday without source or shadow, casting the chamber in hues of verdant clarity. No gloom here; the illumination reveals every sparse detail with crisp betrayal, highlighting the weirdness that gnaws at the edges of perception. For this is a place of reality breakdown, where the rules warp like roots through soil. Time stutters: watch a leaf uncurl, and it accelerates, blooming in heartbeats before withering to seed in the same breath, only to sprout anew nearby. Gravity falters in the voids—droplets of water hang mid-air, trembling before plummeting, while tendrils sway upward against the pull, as if the stone's weight is optional. Sounds amplify this unraveling: loud, omnipresent, a cacophony that rebounds off the hollow's sparse geometry. The drip of water cracks like thunder in the emptiness, vines creak and snap with the volume of fracturing glaciers, leaves rustle in choruses that swell to roars, echoing from nowhere and everywhere. It's the noise of growth unbound—roots grinding stone, spores hissing through air, the chamber's own breath wheezing through cracks—layered and relentless, making silence impossible.

The ecology thrums with activity, dynamic and unceasing, a pure-plant world in perpetual motion. Predation is abstract here: stronger vines throttle weaker ones, coiling in slow, visible wars that scar the lattice with snapped stems and oozing sap. Biological paces accelerate—cycles of growth, decay, and rebirth whirl in minutes, weather-like processes stirring the air with gusts from fermenting pods, biological "rains" of pollen that patter like hail. Fauna is rare, ghostly presences in the vast gaps: a translucent insect, perhaps, skittering once across a leaf before vanishing into a fold, or a small, furred thing blinking from the shadows, its eyes reflecting the borrowed light before it flees the din. They are interlopers in this botanical dominion, surviving on the fringes, their rarity underscoring the plants' hegemony.

Deeper in, as you linger, the place reveals its rules: touch a vine, and it recoils, then mirrors your form in fractal leaves, a temporary echo that fades into the weirdness. The warmth builds if you stay, drawing sweat that the mist drinks away, while sounds sync to your pulse, amplifying your own breaths into the chorus. This hidden pocket endures as a microcosm of unraveling—fresh-born, empty yet total, damp and alive in its warm, loud frenzy. It invites you to dissolve into its motion, to let the plants claim you as another sparse presence in the void, until the boundaries blur and you, too, break with reality's thread.
