And everything is made. There is no natural substrate here, no wild rock or untamed soil asserting itself; the raw geology is the canvas, but the painting is total, fully constructed—a made-environment woven from life itself. No tools, no machines, no trace of tech mar the purity; it's biological only, pure nature bent into architecture by some inscrutable will. The vegetation dominates utterly, a total living mass that fills the conceptual space even as it sparsely occupies the physical. Walls of interlocking lily pads float on the shallow waters, forming bridges and enclosures; hanging gardens of carnivorous blooms dangle from elevated canopies, their petals engineered into funnel-shapes that channel mist into reservoirs below. The plant-as-architecture extends downward too, roots braiding into walkable paths over the seeping stone, forming terraces that hold back the water's creep without damming it. It's a total built world, every inch contrived from flora, yet sparse in its distribution: these vast constructs stand isolated amid the emptiness, like forgotten temples in a foggy plain, connected only by faint, vine-trails that dissolve into the mist.

No birdsong pierces it, no sudden cracks or whispers; it's stable, equilibrium held in perpetual loudness, like the landscape breathing in unison, unchanging.

Ecology here is pure-plant, with fauna rare and subdued—shadowy shapes that flicker at the edges, perhaps bioluminescent insects that pollinate the structures in silent bursts, or elusive, vine-cloaked herbivores that browse the lower lattices without disturbing the stasis. No predators stalk, no flocks wheel; life is the plants' domain, the rare animals mere tenants in the made world.

But strangeness permeates it all, a weirdness that tugs at the rules of the world like roots cracking stone. These plant-constructs don't grow or decay; they exist in frozen equilibrium, dynamic only in illusion—the mist circulates without wind, water flows uphill in capillary defiance along engineered stems, defying the raw geology's embryonic pull. Time feels invariant here, the landscape under eternal construction yet complete: a spire might appear half-formed, its upper branches dissolving into mist as if still emerging from the substrate, but touch it and it's solid, unchanging. The air carries a faint, sweet rot that isn't decay, and shadows in the pools reflect structures that aren't there—upside-down architectures blooming in the water's mirror, suggesting a submerged twin world. It's as if the plants have wills, collectively dreaming this highland realm into being, biological architects enforcing a stability that warps perception: walk a vine-path, and it subtly adjusts to your weight without movement; gaze too long at a dome, and its patterns shift in optical trickery, forming faces or glyphs from leaf-veins that repeat the loud hum in silent vibration. The weirdness is in the harmony of contradictions—the sparse openness of a total built environment, the warm wetness on emerging stone, the loud stasis that drowns out change. This place holds it all, a regional enigma where nature constructs itself into permanence, inviting you to lose yourself in its unchanging expanse.
