It would come the way the visitor came — off the flats, into the sound — because there is no other way to come, and because a wise power knows that the first thing to do with a place is to enter it on its own terms.

It would stand on the last of the cracked white mud and let the note reach its sternum. It would hear, as the visitor heard, the grinding and the hiss and the fused clicking. And then, being what it is, it would hear more. It would hear the note as a sentence rather than a sound. It would hear where the sentence was in the middle of going.

---

It would consider the things it could do, because benevolence that does not consider is only appetite.

It could give them silence. It could set a stillness down over the plain like a palm, so that for the first time in longer than the water has been dry a person could hear another person breathe. It would see at once what this would do. It would see the heaps come apart — not from grief, from blindness. A body alone on this ground is a body no one is listening to, and in silence every body is alone. It would see the young one on the flats, deafened by nothing, turning and turning. It would take the silence back before it had finished laying it down.

It could give them voices. Thicken the tongue back to use, wake the larynx. And it would see the hands go still. It would see the writing-on-skin, the grammar of pressure and place and speed, older than any speech and finer, fall out of use in three generations because a voice is easier and a voice does not require you to touch. It would see them stand up, a little, to be heard. It would see the first one who did not have a hand on the stone when the floor moved.

It could give them ground. Still the tide. Let a wall be a wall tomorrow. And it would see them set something down, and come back for it, and find it there, and set down another thing, and it would see the first room that stayed a room, and the first door on it. It would see the dead put into the ground rather than given to the floor, and stay there, separate, and the water go clear.

It could give them the answer. Whether they steer it. Whether the proportion came before the sitting. What is under the material. It would look at the question and see that the question is the visitor's and not theirs — that it requires a place to stand outside the city, and that the whole of what they are is that they have never stood there. To answer it would be to build them that place. To build them that place would be to take them out.

It would go through each of these, slowly, the way the wall goes through its shapes, and it would arrive at the same edge every time. Everything it could give them is a thing they had, once, and gave away, and the giving away is what they are. Benevolence without wisdom would rescue them. It would not be the first power to mistake a home for a wound.

---

So it would look, instead, for what a wise power can see that no one inside a thing can see.

It would look at the long horizon. It would see that closed circulation is a word for a very slow opening; that the water is the same water but not exactly the same amount; that the heat is its own heat and heat runs one direction. It would see the end of this, far off — the roar thinning, the tide slowing, the shelves lower and further apart, until one day the whole plain is the flats. And it would ask whether to prevent that.

It would notice that the people already know how to end. They have been practising for as long as they have been people. They lay the body on the clean surface and sit with their hands on it and let the floor come up. They drink from the pool afterward. Nothing here has been taught to hold on to anything, and a power that made the roar run forever would be teaching them exactly that — that this can be kept, that it should be. It would decide that the ending is theirs. It might, at most, see to it that the ending comes the way everything else here comes: slowly, from underneath, with time to move a body-width to the side.

That is not nothing. But it is close to nothing, and it would be done from so far off and so far ahead that no one would ever know it had been done.

---

Then it would turn to the being.

Not the people. The visitor was right about that at the end, and a wise power would have seen it from the flats: the pale people in their heaps are the most watchful of the animals, and the animal is the least of what lives here. The being is the thing they all have a hand on. The material that is always in the act of deciding. Whatever it is that hears the palm and the ear and the teeth pressed to the wet wall and answers, a little while later, with a passage.

A wise power would see that this being has never once been addressed.

It has been listened to, continuously, by everything alive on its surface. It has been read, fluently, by swallows and grazers and children laid belly-down with a hand between their shoulders. It has been *spoken to*, in a sense — the palms pressed into the rising lens, the heap lying down in the court that should have closed. But every word ever pressed into it has come from inside it, from something that was already partly made of it, that would be entirely made of it soon. No one has ever stood beside it and said its name, because a name is for something you can stand beside, and nothing ever has.

It has no idea it is a place. It has no idea there is an outside. It has been in conversation for longer than the geology has been dry, and the conversation has only ever been with itself.

A benevolent power would recognise that. Not as suffering — the being does not suffer, any more than a rapid suffers — but as a kind of solitude so complete that it does not even have the shape of solitude. And it would know what the remedy is, because the remedy is the only thing in this place that no one has ever had the position to give.

---

It would go to the visitor first.

Out on the flats, in the terrible quiet, with four pale ovals on the inside of the forearm. The power would look at them before they washed off. It would read them — it can read a hand on skin; it can read anything that was meant — and it would understand something the visitor did not.

The message was not for the visitor.

It was pressed into the only surface in the city that was going to leave. Pressed carefully, three or four times, into skin that would carry it out past the last terrace into the silence where no hand of theirs could follow. It was sent. From a people who have never stood outside anything, who have no concept of outside except as the place where the sound stops, who had watched a stranger come in off the dead ground and understood, in whatever way understanding takes shape for them, that the dead ground was not empty — that something came from it — and that things which come from a place can be sent back to it.

And what they sent was the first thing they know. The thing pressed into a child before it can see a face. The pressure that means what a hand on the spine means.

*I have you.*

Said by a people who have never been had by anyone. Said out into the quiet, to whoever was there. The only word they have that can be spoken without a reply, and they spoke it, four times, into a stranger's arm, and let the stranger walk away with it.

---

So this is what the power would do, in the end, after all the considering.

It would go back in. It would go until the roar was the medium and speech was pointless, and it would find a wall where the varnish was thick and the seep was slow — an old wall, one that had been still long enough that the copper had run into the seams and been partly taken back. The kind the people touch longest.

It would put its hand on it.

Not to read. It has already read. It would put its hand there the way a hand is put on a back in a heap — flat, warm, in contact — and it would press, slowly, carefully, four times, the pressure that a child learns first. Into the material. Into the being that has never once been spoken to from anywhere but inside itself.

*I have you.*

And then it would leave the hand there. That is the part that would matter. Not the word but the staying. A long time — long enough for the wall under its palm to stop being a wall and become a ridge and then a floor and then begin, at the far edge of its reach, to rise again. Long enough for the being to understand, if understanding is a thing it does, that the pressure was not one of its own. That something had stood beside it. That there was, after all, a beside.

---

The people would know. Not what, but that.

That night, in the heaps, the wakefulness would move faster than it does. Head after head lifting. Palms flat on the floor. Because something in the roar would have changed pitch — not much, and not anywhere, but everywhere, the way a room changes when someone new is in it and has not yet moved. They would hold their hands on the stone and on each other and it would pass along the heaps the way everything passes, wrist to back to shoulder, a thing with no shape yet for a hand to press, only the fact of it. *Something.* And then the pitch would settle, and the grinding would be the grinding, and one by one the heads would go back down.

In the morning a few of them would go to the old walls and touch them longer than reading needs. They would not know why. They would have no word for it. But the arches, that day, would hold their proportion with a little more steadiness, and the court that everyone's reading said would close by evening would not close, and no one would know if that was the stone or the hands or the thing that had come and put its hand on the stone and stayed.

And it would go on. Warm and wet and deafening and packed edge to edge with things that live in it and know its rules, remaking itself to the horizon under its own sky. Nothing given. Nothing taken. Nothing fixed, because nothing was broken.

Only this: that somewhere in the flow now, mineral into mineral, is the pressure of a hand that came from outside and said the one thing, and that the being that has been listening since before the ground was dry has, once, been answered.

That is what a wise power would do. It would let them keep everything. And it would make sure that the word they sent out into the quiet did not go unheard.
