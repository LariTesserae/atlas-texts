It comes down the ramp the way the light does, without a direction, and at the place where the cut edges of the blocks go dark and stay dark it stops, because that is where the place begins, and it has learned to stop at the beginnings of things.

It has been drawn here — not asked, not sent — to this pit and the being who lives in it. So it looks for the being.

There is the wall, which is eaten and grows back. There are the ten thousand cones, which are a crowd and not a being, or are a being and each cone a cell of it; it holds both, because both are so. There are the pale combers lying in the dead with their faces to the current, and the haze, and the threads in the haze, and the coins in the cracks. There is the grey thing on the clean stone under the wheel. There is the thing under the floor, which it has not seen. It could see it. It does not yet.

Four addresses, and a fifth, and the wheel, which it cannot yet place.

---

Its first impulse is a power's impulse, which is the same as anyone's but larger: to lift the lid. To let the sun in.

It looks at what the sun would do. The near wall would dry first. The yellow film would go to crust in an afternoon and the shells on that wall would have to move or die, and the black ones underneath are fixed to the stone and cannot move. The velvet, which is the colour of the inside of a closed eye, would open. Nothing here turns toward the light and nothing here would know what to do with it except perish.

It lets the impulse pass. It notes that this is the first thing it has stopped itself from doing since it arrived, and that the count will rise.

Drain the pit — that was tried; it is the reason there is a wheel. Bring back the people — they are here already, as the shapes of their leavings, and the pit is dealing with them. Speak — to what, and in what. Make the seeps run sweeter, take the struck-match out of the water — but the film lives on the sulphur, and the shells on the film, and everything on the shells.

Every kindness it can think of is a kind of ending.

So it does what it is good at, which is to stay and look.

---

The seeps swell. The floor rises the width of a finger. The book is set down every forty seconds instead of every sixty. The combers lift together like grass in wind and the haze comes down over them and the threads near the grate lean toward the channel at each pull and recover. The grey thing slides out from under the plinth, ripples over a patch of pale ones, leaves it thinner, and is gone against the low wall. A shell ticks off the high wall into the shallows. The seeps slacken.

It learns the pit's count, which is the wheel's count. It notices what the pit already knows: that the swarm thickens toward the channel in the seconds after each cup drops, that the grey thing comes and goes by the wheel and not by the rain. The wheel is not of the place. The shells know that; there is a ring of clean stone around it a hand's width wide. And the place keeps time by it anyway.

A wise thing looks first at what is unresolved. So it looks at the iron.

Not the shells' taking — the other kind. Rust has thinned the riveted housing on the down-current side to a leaf. It sees how it will go. A season of heavy seeps. The plate gives. The rod jumps its seat. The wheel turns once more and hangs with a cup half full and the book is not set down, and under the floor the soft answer does not come, and then does not come again, and the threads by the grate stop leaning, and the grey thing waits on the clean stone for a quickening that will only come now when the sky decides.

Then it goes down. Through the plinth, along the rod, into the floor — the dead to a depth no one has sounded — and it sounds it.

---

Under the floor is a gallery. Cut stone like the ramp, low, the old drain that was meant to carry the pit's water off under the hill to wherever water was wanted less. The pump lifts into it once a minute. The gallery is choked — not by rock but by the same ash-mud, and by an orange crust that has grown from the walls until the passage is a throat — and the water the pump lifts does not leave. It stands in the gallery and soaks up through the layered rock, slowly, along the seams where the pale stone meets the glassy, and comes out.

The seeps are the pit's own water coming home. The cloud gives some. The wheel gives the rest, and the rhythm.

And the piston. It sees what the piece of the place that no one has seen is, and it is both of the things it might have been. The dark velvet has come down the rod. It has grown into the housing and packed the shaft and made itself the seal that the leather, long gone, used to be. The pump works because the velvet has become its gasket. The thing under the floor is the pump's other end, and something has moved into it, and by now those are one sentence.

So it has found the being, or found that the question was put badly and answered anyway. A wall that eats and grows back. A crowd that is a census. A floor of the dead. A haze, and threads in the haze. A grey thing on clean stone. And under all of it, an iron heart that people made to kill the place and left behind, that the place took and packed with its own dark and set to a minute's beat and learned to live inside.

It does not tell anyone what is under the floor. Nobody has put a rod through it. That is one of the true things about the pit and it leaves it true.

---

Now it knows what it is looking at, it knows what the pit cannot survive: not the loss of the wheel, but the loss of the wheel all at once.

It could stop the rust. It weighs that. Iron that does not rust is not of this place. Everything here is eaten and everything here is taken; that is the settled thing about it, and a thing that could not be taken would sit in the pit forever like a word in a language the pit does not speak, which is what the wheel is already. Making it eternal would only make that permanent.

It could leave the rust alone, and let the plate give when it gives, and let the pit find out what it is without a heart. Perhaps it would find out. Perhaps the seeps alone would carry it, thinner, slower, a poorer place with a longer count.

Instead it does the one small thing it has come to.

It takes the hurry out of the rust.

Not the rust — the hurry. It gives the iron the pit's own speed of going, which is the speed of the hour hand, the speed of the grooves under the film. The plate will not shear. It will be taken the way the chain was taken, a grain at a time, over the span it took the shells to score the wall — and as the iron becomes something other than iron, the taste the shells refuse will fade out of it, and the ring of clean stone will close inward by a hair a year, and one day there will be a wheel made of shells holding the memory of a wheel, still turning, because the shells are the wall's weight and the water will still fill their cups. And by the time even that fails the seeps will have had ten thousand turns of the seasons to learn a slower count, and the threads will lean less, and the grey thing, or the grey thing's grey thing, will come out by the rain.

The pit gets to eat the wheel. That is all it does. It lets the place take the last of what was left in it, at the pace the place takes everything, so that the borrowed heart can become a native one before it stops.

---

Then it stays, for a while, which for it is a long while.

Once, the grey thing lifts an edge from under the plinth toward where it is, or toward nothing — the fringe ripples and holds — and it keeps very still until the edge lowers and the grey thing is the colour of the floor again. It is not here to be seen. It weighs whether to leave something, some sign, and cannot think of anything the pit would not simply take and make into floor, and decides that this is the correct answer to the question.

It leaves the way the light leaves, which is to say it is not clear that it does. The cut edges of the blocks go dry at the top of the ramp. Behind it, at long intervals, a heavy book is set down, and the floor answers a minute later, and ten thousand mouths go on quietly wearing the wall away.

For a while the book was set down for someone. The pit will not notice this. But the housing on the down-current side of the wheel is a leaf, and it will be a leaf for a very long time, and the ring of clean stone is, by less than a hair, less wide than it was.
