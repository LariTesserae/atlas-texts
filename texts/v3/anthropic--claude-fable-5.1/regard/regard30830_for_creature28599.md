The first thing to understand about the power is that it does not arrive.

Nothing comes over the lip of rock. No cloud parts. The birds do not go still, because there is nothing to go still for; the shape in the overcast has come and gone at its usual hour and the slow count has ended and the beaks have resumed their clicking on stone. The plateau is exactly as it was. The power is simply, at some point, attending to it — the way a room is warm before you notice it is warm, from something held rather than something shone.

It does not have to work out the layers. The visitor spent days on their stomach at the edges of fissures, turning the ecology over until it was the right way up. The power sees the whole stack at once, as a single fact with depth: the fire, and on the skin of the fire the film, and in the film the blind larvae, and above them the froth, and around the froth the lattice, and in the cracks the long grey animals with their young, and in the cloud the shadow, and above the cloud the engine, and on the horizon the light that never goes off. It sees the water, too, all of it at once — the drop forming under the overhang and the boiler it was in a year ago and the ice it will be in tonight and the river it will be in a thousand years when there is finally a river. It sees the rosette, once, and unlike the visitor it will be able to find it again.

And it sees that no one has asked for anything.

This matters more to a wise power than it would to a merely kind one. The birds are not in distress; they are doing the arithmetic they came to do, and doing it well. The scavengers are having a good season. The larvae are, as far as anything can be said about larvae, content. The film is eating stone and making itself out of it, which is the only thing it has ever wanted. And the thing at the bottom, the warm dark body kilometers down — it does not want. It has never been the kind of thing that wants. It is cooling because that is what a hot thing next to a cold thing does, and to call that a wish, or a grief, or a going, is the visitor's word for it, not the mountain's.

So benevolence here has to be careful. Unasked-for help has a way of being about the helper.

---

It considers the obvious thing first, because the obvious thing is always waiting to be refused.

It could hold the heat. It would not be hard. A little less loss through the flanks, a little more coming up from below, and the door in the distant part of the house would stop closing, and the ground would stay warm, and the flies would keep rising, and the birds would come back next year and the year after and for as long as there were birds. The plateau as it is now, kept. The lattice permanent. The season made into a place.

It sets this down almost at once, and not because it would be difficult. Because the plateau as it is now is not a place. It is a moment in the middle of a motion, and the thing that makes the lattice beautiful is that the birds are spending nothing, and the thing that makes them spend nothing is that they know the account will close. Hold the heat and the birds would learn, over a few generations, that it does not close. They would stop crouching. They would start to preen. There would be nests. Something would come up the flank that was not a specialist in the opportunity but a generalist in the abundance, and it would eat the eggs, and the rock would be cleaned with a different efficiency. And the moss would not come, and the soil would not come, and the rosette would stay a rosette forever, pressed flat, the only one of its kind, on a warm dark floor that never became anything.

That is not a rescue. That is a taxidermy with the animal still breathing.

---

It considers the small mercies next. These are harder to refuse because they are so cheap.

The birds at the cold margin. The ones that misjudge the last kilometer and land where the warmth has not yet begun, and stand there with their beaks open, and are whole and unbroken in the morning and dead of nothing but the gap. The power could close that gap. Could nudge a bird a few body-lengths inward as it falls, could warm one dinner plate of rock for one night. No one would know. The lattice would not change its shape.

It stays with this longer. It stays with one particular bird, in fact — a late arrival, dropping through the frost with its wings already folding, and the power can see where it will land and can see that where it will land is cold. It could move it. It does not move it. The bird hits the rock with the small hard sound and stands and walks toward the nearest circle and does not reach it, and the power is there for all of that, and does not look away, and does not intervene, and does not pretend to itself afterward that this was easy.

Because the margin is not a mistake. The margin is where the lattice learns where it ends. The birds that die at the edge are how the birds in the center know the center is the center; they are the cost of the shape, paid in advance, and the shape is what keeps the other thousands alive. Save the edge and the edge moves outward, and next year more of them land in the cold, trusting a mercy that will have to be repeated forever or withdrawn. A wise power does not begin a thing it will have to either continue or betray.

And the scavengers will find that bird before dawn. The young in the warm crack will be weaned onto it. That is not a consolation. It is just what is true.

---

It considers the northeast.

This is the one it turns over longest, because it is the only threat on the plateau that is not part of the plateau. The engine, the straight white line, the sodium light that means something is running: these are the only things in the whole stack that come from an intention. And intention, unlike heat, does not cool on its own. Someday the people under that light will find a use for a high raw floor. A survey will land. Someone will walk this rock who is not the visitor and not the power, and they will have a purpose, and the purpose will not be to see what is here.

The power could make that later. Could keep the plateau uninteresting. Could see to it that the aircraft go on flying over without landing for another century, another two, the way it could hold the heat — quietly, with nobody knowing that anything had been done.

It nearly does this. It is the closest it comes.

What stops it is not principle. It is the timescale. The plateau is useless now because it is new: no soil, no ore anyone wants, nothing to graze, nothing to dam. It will become useful when it becomes finished — when there is soil, and something with roots, and water that has learned to move. That is centuries. And in centuries the light in the northeast will be a different light, or no light, and the people will be different people with different uses, or no people, and the power would be defending the plateau against a threat that has not been born from an enemy who does not yet exist. That is not protection. That is anxiety wearing protection's coat.

It lets the engine go over. It watches the white line unravel, drifting east, and lets it.

---

So: nothing. It will do nothing to the place. It has been through the list and set each thing down and the list is empty and the plateau is exactly as it was, weeping, ticking, full of birds spending nothing.

And now it turns to the being.

Not the birds — they are a season, and seasons do not need anything from powers. Not the scavengers, who are having a good year and will walk down the flank when it is over and have another. Not the film, not the larvae, not the shadow in the cloud that roosts on steel. All of these have an elsewhere. All of these came here across farmland and roads and older mountains, and will go back, or their children will, and the plateau will be a place they were once.

The thing at the bottom has no elsewhere. It has never been anywhere but here, and it never will be, and it is becoming less. Every few days the door closes and some increment of it that was molten is not molten anymore, is only rock, is only the floor of the thing it used to be. Everything above it is living on that increment. Everything above it came for the heat and will leave when the heat is gone, and none of them — not one — came for the mountain.

This is what the power sees that the visitor did not quite see, though the visitor came close. The mountain is not a resident that is leaving. The mountain is a host at a party where every guest is a guest of the food.

---

What is owed to such a thing?

Not rescue; it cannot be rescued from cooling any more than it could be rescued from being hot. Not company in the human sense; it is not lonely, because loneliness is a shape that wanting takes, and it does not want. Not explanation. The power could tell it — if telling were a thing you could do to a body of stone — that cold is not nothing. That when the last warmth has left and the plateau is dark and stable, the film will still be there, eating metals that are the mountain's metals; and the moss will come and root in cracks that are the mountain's cracks; and the soil will be the mountain's body broken small; and the thing with roots will be the mountain standing up again in a different arrangement, and the thing that eats roots will be the mountain walking. That the heat is not leaving. It is being handed down. That "cooling" and "becoming ground" are one motion described from two distances, and that the mountain has been right all along in being unable to tell arriving from going, because on its timescale they are the same word.

The power knows all of this. It is true and it is not consolation, because nothing here needs consoling. It keeps it to itself.

What it does instead is the one thing that no one else on the plateau does, or can.

It stays.

Not for the season. The birds will contract toward the center over a week and the last one will eat alone on the warmest rock and then there will be none, and the power will be there for the last one, and for the empty basin afterward, and that is not the staying. The scavengers will walk down the flank with the young who remember nothing, and the power will be there for the crack when it is empty and only water, and that is not the staying either. The staying is the frost that does not lift one morning. The first pool that does not thaw. The film going grey and slow and then going still, having eaten what it could. The door in the distant house closing for the last time — a sound no one will hear, because there will be no one — and the power there to hear it. The centuries of water learning the ground. The moss. The rosette's thousandth descendant. The first soil, which is the mountain and only the mountain, finally soft enough to hold a root.

For all of it. Without lifting a cloud or warming a plate of stone or turning one aircraft aside. Present the way the water is present: everywhere, changing nothing it does not have to change, counting itself into pools that have never seen light.

So that the mountain, which has never been anywhere else, is not the only one who was here for the whole of it.

---

There is one thing it does. It is so small it hardly counts, and it counts.

Late, on a night when the cloud has dropped and the frost is coming down fine as breath, the power puts something like a hand flat on the rock.

Not to feel the warmth. To give some. For as long as the hand is there — a slow count, no more — the heat runs the other way: not up out of the mountain into the birds, the larvae, the air, the cold, but down, into the stone, from something that came for the mountain and not for the food. It is nothing. It is an increment so small the mountain will have lost it again before the hand lifts. It changes no shape. It saves no bird.

But every other thing on the plateau tonight is taking. This is the one thing that is not.

Then the hand lifts, and the frost closes over the place where it was, and the birds sit in their lattice in the dark with the small bare circles around them, and somewhere below, the drops resume their count.
