It arrives the way the noise does, all at once and from every side, and unlike you it does not need to wait for its eyes.

It hears the valley whole. Not as a wall but as what the wall is made of: a hundred ponds, a hundred pitches, and the drift between them laid out like a map of small countries. It hears the fourth pit down from the north end and the pit below it and the crack that breathes between them and the snake on the crack and the frogs on the snake. It hears the clicking under everything and knows what it is before it looks. It feels the ground the way the ground feels itself, warm from underneath, unfinished, and it knows, in the way it knows things, how many seasons are left. Not many. Fewer than the ferns think.

And it sees her. The crest thinner than the others, the head held a little off true. Turning a flake, dropping it. Turning another.

---

The first thing that moves in it is the thing that moves in everyone who stands at the lip. It wants to do something.

It is a power, and so the something is not a stone set on a stone. It could reach down through the black crust and lay a hand on what is coming and hold it there, and the valley would go on — the pits would stop opening, the ferns would get old, the frog nations would deepen into frog species, the gray-heads would have a thousand seasons to find out what the edge is for. It could do that. It turns the thought over. It sets it down.

Because the valley is the interval. That is not a flaw in it to be corrected; it is what the place is. Hold the rock and you do not save the valley, you end it and begin something else in its footprint, something slower and more settled and yours. You would have taught this place the one thing it does not believe, which is that things stay. And you would be responsible for it afterward, the way anyone is responsible for what they have decided should not die. The valley would become a garden with an owner. It has never had one. It is louder for it.

The second thing it wants is to keep her.

Not her body — it is not sentimental in that way — but the way she stands. The angle of the head. The rate at which she rejects. It could take the pattern of her up out of the valley and hold it somewhere, perfectly, and no chipped beak would go into the paste unrecorded. It turns that over too, and this one takes longer to set down, because it is closer to what the power is. The power keeps. That is most of what a power is: the thing that remembers when nothing else does.

But it asks the question the valley asks of everything: for whom? Not for her. She does not keep, and does not miss keeping, and would not know what to do with a self that had been saved. Not for the young, who learn by sitting a frond over and watching, and cannot watch a record. The keeping would be for the keeper. It would be the mark on the fern, made of finer stuff. The power sees this clearly and is a little ashamed, and the shame is useful, and it lets the thought go.

---

It looks at her longer. Small things now.

It could give her a flake. Find the truest edge on the whole floor of the valley and set it under her foot on the lip of the fourth pit so that when she comes down she does not have to turn anything, she simply takes it, and the crab opens first try. A kindness at the scale the place understands. Nobody would know.

And it sees, watching her turn a flake and drop it and turn another, that she would drop that one too. Not from pride. Because the turning is the work. What she has that the others do not is not a better edge — the floor is covered in edges — but a harder no. She is the best at refusing. You cannot give someone their own refusal. Put the perfect flake in front of her and you have given her nothing; you have only taken one turn of the beak away from the thing she is.

It could make the end easy. But the end will be rock, and rock is quick as ends go; quicker than the beetles, quicker than the wet. There is nothing here to be spared that is worse than what will happen anyway, and nothing here is asking.

It thinks about the frog forty feet up in the fern axil, the one that takes the roar for silence and has never seen the sun. It could thin the cloud. One breath of moon on the valley, once, for the frog. The power feels the pull of that very strongly, because it is beautiful, and it distrusts the pull for exactly that reason. The frog is not in the dark. The frog is in the frog's world, which is whole. Show it the moon and you have not given it light; you have given it a lack, a thing to have been without. And then the cloud closes and the frog has the lack and you have the memory of your own gesture, which is what you wanted.

It notices, now, that every gift it can think of is the same gift. They are all the stone on the stone. The wish to help is the wish to be here in a way that lasts, wearing better clothes.

---

So it stops looking for the problem. Benevolence goes looking for problems the way the grunting thing goes looking for frog-heaps, and this valley is not one. Nothing here is suffering more than living costs. Nothing is broken that the place itself does not break and remake nightly. The rock will come, and that is not an injury to the valley; it is the valley's other half, the half it has been living in the shadow of since the bats first hung in the hot tubes. Loud in the dark, building nothing, keeping nothing, alive past all reason: that is not a condition awaiting rescue. It is a completed thing.

What, then. If anything.

There is one thing the valley cannot do for itself, and the power is slow to see it because it is so small and so plain. Everything in this place takes the roar for silence. The frog does. The snake does. The old bird does, turning her flake inside a noise she has never once heard as noise. The valley is the most fully inhabited place the power has come across in a long time and there is no one in it who can hear it. It has no outside. It cannot be attended to from within.

The power can hear it. That is all. Not keep it, not use it, not make it an example of anything. Simply be the one ear at the lip of the basin that hears the fourth pit as the fourth pit and the third as the third, that hears the clicking as intention, that hears four trees fall in a night and the frogs go quiet for a breath and roll back in. And do this not for a night, not a sampling, but for the whole of what is left. Every night. Because the valley does not live in summary, and to hear it in summary would be to hear something else.

So it stays. It stays through a frog year and a crab year and a plague of the gray-heads and the frogs again. It hears the new pit open at the lower end and fill with water and fill with frogs and begin, within a season, to drift toward its own pitch. It hears the snake move on and the frogs fall off it in a scatter and find the next warm thing. It hears the old bird's flake break more often as her beak worsens, and hears her rejecting more to compensate, and hears the young sit nearer. It hears her stop. It does not hear where she goes; that is not a sound. The clicking under the roar is very slightly less sure for a while, and then it is not, because there is another one now who refuses more than she takes, and she holds her head straight, and the valley does not notice the difference and neither, in the end, does the clicking.

---

And it does one thing. Only one, and it is not sure, even being what it is, that the one is right.

The rock, when it comes, will come up through the cracks at whatever hour it comes. The power can see the hour. It is a little before what would be dawn, if there were a dawn — the bats will be back in the tubes, the rope of them paid back in, hanging in the dung they made, in the only soil this valley ever had. The tubes will fill. The bats made the ground and the ground will make their grave, and that is not unjust, it is only how it falls.

The power puts a thumb on it. Very lightly. A shift of some hours, no more — a pressure eased here and let go there, in the deep places where the valley cannot see and would not understand if it could. The rock will come at dusk instead. When the rope of bats is twenty minutes out of the tubes and unraveled into the canopy, and the tubes are empty, and the canopy is about to be fire.

Some of them will go up. Not many. Enough. They will find the cloud the way they found it once before, coming down, and they will hang somewhere in the dark for a season or a hundred, and one night the rock below will be cool enough, and there will be holes in it, and they will go in. And they will come out and find nothing to eat and go farther, and come back. And under them the floor of a tube will begin, very slowly, to be soil.

The power does not do this to save anything. Everything here is lost either way; the frogs, the dialects, the way of holding the head, the snake that was a hillside, all of it, and the power lets all of it go without reaching. It does this because the valley's own method is accident — the pit that falls in, the fern that goes over, the flake that happens to lie edge-up — and accident is the only register in which a power can act here without becoming an owner. It tips one accident. It is the kind of thing the valley does to itself four or five times a night. Nobody will know. There will be nothing on the new rock to say that it was chosen. It is not a mark. It is barely a nudge to the dice, in favor of the dice being thrown again.

It is not sure this is right. A wiser power might not have done even that. But it has sat at the lip for the whole interval and heard the place as no one inside it could, and it finds that it wants there to be, somewhere, sometime, another argument like this one — not this one saved, but another one begun — and it recognizes the want as the old want, the stone on the stone, and it looks at the want the way she looked at a flake. Turns it. Considers the edge. And this once, instead of dropping it, uses it, quickly, on the one seam where it will open something rather than scar it. And then lets it fall.

---

The rock comes at dusk. It hears that too. It does not look away, and it does not make it mean anything.

Then it does what you did, climbing out. The sound drops away in layers, frogs last of all, wall to roar to hum to something you could mistake for your own blood. Except that for the power there is no mistaking, and there is no last layer that fades, because it heard every night and it is the kind of thing that does not forget. It carries out no more than it brought in — which for a power is everything.

And here, on the lip, with the valley gone quiet behind it for the first time since the bats came down, is the part it did not expect. It has been the keeper of things for longer than there have been things to keep, and it has just watched a place that kept nothing be more thoroughly lived in than most places it has held for ten thousand years. It watched a bird pick up the edge and do the work and drop the edge and go back up into the noise, and it watched the young learn from that without a word, and it found it could learn from that too. It does not know yet what it will do with what it heard. Possibly nothing. Possibly it will let the valley sit in it the way the flakes sit on the lava: present, unarranged, unused, not turned into a lesson or a story or a reason for anything, a slightly higher concentration of good edges at the foot of one tree, which the rain will move.

It did not leave anything. It does not think it did. It heard the place as itself for as long as the place lasted, and it tipped the dice once in favor
