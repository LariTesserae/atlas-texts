It notices the way such a power notices things: not by looking, at first, but by feeling the world's grain change. Somewhere on the roof of the sky there is a spot four strides across where the sorting has not been done. Where up has not yet been separated from down, and one from several, and here from there. It has felt this grain before, deep, in the places where the world is still molten and undecided. It has never felt it in the open air, in sunlight, cooling.

So it comes, and it stops at the rim, and the first thing it learns is that it must be careful how it looks.

Because attention sorts. That is what attention is. To regard a thing is to lift it out of what it is not, to say *this and not that*, and the resident of the hollow is precisely the condition in which that has not been said. If the power were to look at it the way it looks at everything else — clearly, decisively, with the whole weight of a mind that knows which way water falls — the looking itself would be a cold wind. The bead on the back wall would hesitate, and choose, and fall. The bird would be one bird. The hollow would be four strides across and no deeper, and it would be the power that made it so, out of nothing but the habit of seeing.

So it does the first hard thing, which is to look without deciding. It lets the reflection be the reflection and also the other side. It lets the bird be in three corners. It holds the flakes the shelled things make and the flakes the glass makes in the same hand and does not count them. This is not easy for a wise thing. Wisdom is mostly the practice of distinguishing well. Here it must be the practice of not distinguishing, for a while, on purpose, so that the thing it has come to see is still there to be seen.

---

Then it considers the obvious mercy, because benevolence always considers the obvious mercy first, and wisdom's job is to sit beside it while it does.

The heat. A week old, a month, and dying at the pace of cooling glass. The power could reach down through the cone to the melt that made it and ask the melt to linger. One degree, held. The source would not slacken. The worms would not creep inward. The two-arm-length rain would keep its beat, and the season would become a year, and the year a lifetime, and the hollow would not go out.

And every animal in it would learn what a winter is.

That is what the power sees when it follows the thought to its end. The calm of the crowd on the floor is not innocence; it is architecture. They lay every hour because there is no later. They knap and drop and knap because keeping is a habit of creatures who expect a tomorrow, and the hollow has an afternoon, and an afternoon is a shape a life can fit into without fear. Give them a tomorrow and you give them the first cold night, and the first cache, and the first shelled thing that does not hand the flake on. You give them the reflection's hunter as an enemy rather than a rent. You give them the thing the power itself carries and has never once been able to put down: the knowledge of an ending, and the long work of living in its light.

Benevolence says: but they would live. Wisdom says: they are living. It is only you who is grieving, and you are grieving a cooling curve, which is your own condition and not theirs.

And beneath that: the resident is not the sort of thing that can be kept. It is a bead of the unsorted. To hold it still is to sort it. To preserve it is to make it into a thing that is preserved, which is a thing with an edge, a boundary, a *this and not that*. The deep it came from is not dying. The deep is the size of the world's interior and it does not know it has lost a bubble. What is ending here is a configuration, a small weather, and configurations end. The power has watched many end. It knows the difference between a death and a return to the state from which a thing was briefly lifted, and it knows that grief does not always know the difference, and it lets the grief be there without acting on it.

So: no heat. That is the large refusal. Everything after is small.

---

It looks at the animals, one kind at a time, the way you would walk a ward.

The worms are at their smoker. They have never been happier, if that word reaches down to worms; they do not know it is a mountain and they never will. The grazers are feeding and talking and laying into the upward stream, and their young are coming down in the rain, and there is no distress anywhere in that — only appetite, and rest, and appetite. The mites are the walls. The shelled things strike stone with the patience of creatures who have never failed at it more than four times. The fish rise and are taken and there is no ripple, and the power looks for the fear that ought to be in a bred thing and finds only rising. Compassion goes looking for suffering and finds a working engine, and has the grace to be glad rather than disappointed.

It looks at the far side of the water, too, because a wise power does not accept the near side as the true one merely because it is the side it arrived on. It considers whether the few and the large and the still are the ones to pity — fed on fish, at the mercy of one bird's appetite for a delicacy, waiting beneath a spot with the patience of something that has only one mouth. It considers whether the crowd here is the crowd there seen scattered, or the crowd there is the crowd here seen whole, and which of those would be the truer thing to save, if saving were the question. And it does not decide. It lets the seam keep that rule. It would be a poor kind of kindness to walk into a house whose one law is *not this or that* and choose a side.

It looks at the bird and leaves it alone.

The bird chose. It chose the way birds choose, by continuing, and it has struck a bargain — one bird by night on a ledge in the wind, several by day in the warm — that it seems to consider fair, and the power is not so wise that it knows better than a mountain bird what a mountain bird can bear. If it does anything for the bird it does it far from here: it sees to it, quietly, that the ledge stays where it is, that the wind does not bring the crag down this year. That the door the bird goes out through at evening will still be a door. Nothing more. To do more would be to decide for it that several is worse than one, and the power has just spent an hour learning not to decide that.

---

And then the fifth resident. The recent one. Whose knees are hot.

Here the power's reasoning changes, because here the case is different. The crowd was made for the afternoon. The bird chose the afternoon. But the one at the rim came from the country of tomorrows — is a keeping creature, a creature with a shadow that ought to fall somewhere, with hands that expect to be two hands and not one thing that is hands — and is being taken not by malice, not by anything the seam is *doing*, but by the seam's one rule extended, courteously, to include a guest. The rock weeps under the palms because the seam has stopped distinguishing between rock and palm. It is hospitality. It is a room that has found it has room. It is the gentlest possible way to be unmade, and the power does not think the guest has agreed to it, because agreeing is a sorting and the guest has been at the rim long enough to have forgotten how.

So the power does a small thing. The smallest it can find.

It does not lift the stranger out. Leaving has to belong to the one who leaves, or it is not leaving; it is only being moved. Instead it cools the rock under one palm by a single degree. Not enough to be noticed as cold. Enough that the two hands are, for the first time in an afternoon, different from each other. A hair of difference, a *this and not that*, delivered where the seam cannot object because the seam does not object to anything, laid into a body that will know what to do with it.

That is all a door is. One side of a thing feeling other than the other side.

What the stranger does with the door is the stranger's. The power has an intuition about it — that a hand which knows it is colder than the other hand will remember it is a hand, and a hand that remembers being a hand will remember an arm, and an arm remembers a body and a body remembers down — but it does not stay to make the intuition true. It has found, over a long time, that the wisest thing to do after opening a door is to be somewhere else.

---

Two more things, and then it goes.

The first is a keeping. Nothing in the hollow keeps. Not the flakes, not the tools, not the shelled things overnight; the resident's whole nature is *not-keeping*, and this is not a lack in it but its beauty, and the power will not correct it. But the power itself is a keeping thing, the way the stranger is, the way anything is that has tomorrows in it. And it can give the unkeeping thing the one gift a keeping thing has: it can be the place where this was.

So it takes the hollow into itself entire. Every bead on the back wall and which way it went. The bird in its three corners and the way the heads lifted, not together and not apart. The count of flakes, which it refuses to make and keeps anyway. The pressure of grazer-song in a jaw. The reflection's hunter, waiting. The smell of hot iron and steam. It does not do this to bring the hollow back — you cannot bring back the unsorted, once it has sorted; there is nothing to bring — but so that when the glass is dead and the film has stopped forming and the worms are red threads in the fractures, it will not be as if none of this happened. Somewhere, something will distinguish this hollow from the thousand dead pockets on a thousand dead cones. That is a sorting. It is done after the fact, where it can do no harm, and it is the only one the power permits itself.

The second is a crack.

Not now. At the end — when the source runs cold, when the pool is a stain the size of a hand and the water has already remembered which way is down and there is nothing left above that any act could reach. Then, when the last of the bead is a residue in the rock, a condition without anything to condition, the power will open a hairline downward through the cone. It will not push. Pushing is a decision about where a thing belongs. It will open, the way you leave a door ajar for something that may or may not want to go out, and the residue of the deep will have, for one moment, a way back to where up and down are a matter of taste.

Whether it takes the way the power does not know and does not intend to find out. A wise power leaves the last decision to the thing it is deciding about. The crack will be there. The rock will be cold. What sinks will sink because sinking is what it was always inclined to do, and what stays will stay as a scatter of rust and some odd little flakes on a shelf.

---

Later, much lower on the mountain, someone comes down out of the thin air with one hand colder than the other and cannot say why they climbed. Their palms smell of hot iron for a day. Then the smell goes, and they do not think about it again, and the hollow does not think about it either, having never thought about anything.

And somewhere the power holds a small warm room, four strides across and as deep as a hall, in which the water runs both ways and the bird is in every corner and the sun does not move, and it keeps it, and does not decide what it was.
