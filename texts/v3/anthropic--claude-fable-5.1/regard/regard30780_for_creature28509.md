Start with what it sees, because seeing is most of what it does.

It does not arrive. Nothing arrives here; the plateau has abolished arriving. It attends, which on a place like this is the same as being present, and it attends for a long time — long enough to know the hours of the tide, long enough to have counted the mills and got thirty-one, and one, and understood that both counts are honest. It watches the fog come in from its quarter forty times, a hundred. It does not hurry. A power that is wise about a slow thing is slow with it.

What it sees, in the end, is this.

A body that has given up everything except what it needs to go on, and one thing more. The drinking it needs. The thickening and the healing of cracks it needs. The sound it does not need. No account of thirst explains the sound. The sound is the plant doing something it has no use for, and the power, which knows a good deal about beings that have pared themselves down to the least they can run on, knows what it is when such a being keeps one thing it does not need. It is the last thing that is not survival. It is the thing you would give up last.

And it sees the town. It sees it differently from the hands, who are inside it, and differently from a traveller, who would see machinery. It sees a body with no upward parts that has grown chimneys, which are the only vertical things for a horizon in every direction. It sees smoke, which goes somewhere. It sees birds that stand on the chimneys and face the weather — a body with no crown, nothing that reaches, that has grown itself something to look at the sky with. It sees mills that take the body's own substance and spin it into cord. Cord is not for holding a thing together; a thing this single does not need holding. Cord is for crossing a distance. It sees the cars go out along the cable, loaded, toward a mill that is not this one.

And it sees them arrive back where they left.

Every made thing on the plateau points away from the plateau. Every one of them lands on the plateau again. The plant has spent an age building an outside for itself out of itself, and it has not worked, because there is nothing at the far end to receive. The cord is spun. The car goes. Nothing is winding it. The reach comes back as a return.

This is what the power sees, and having seen it, it does what wisdom does before it does anything: it stands still, and counts what it must not do.

---

It must not give the plant an edge.

That is the first thing that occurs to it and the first it sets down. An edge is a place where the body stops and something else begins, and the plant has not had one of those since it drank the mountain. To make one now would be to open a wound the length of a horizon and call it a gift. The plant would heal it. That is what it does with cracks. It would heal the edge shut and the power would have taught it nothing except that the world can be cut.

It must not free the hands.

It considers them for a long while, because they are the part of the place that most resembles the kind of thing it has been benevolent toward before, and because the coat on the bracket is difficult to look at. But the hands are not held. Nothing holds them. They walk the trestle and arrive at their own mill and take over their own round and nod to themselves, and they are not troubled, and the power looks closely for the trouble under the not-troubled and does not find it. To lift a hand off the plateau and set him down somewhere with grass would be to lift a finger off a body and tell the finger it was free. He would look at the grass with the mild attention of someone who has been asked whether he has ever walked to the horizon. He would want the wall.

It must not bring back the other sun.

The shadows near the veins remember it. The pools remember a plateau that was greener and smaller and had not drunk so much. The power could stand in the fog's quarter and be that light for an hour, and every shadow on the plateau would agree with itself for the first time in an age. And then the hour would end, and the plant would have been shown what it was, and be what it is, and know the difference. Wisdom does not wake a thing into mourning. Whatever grief there is here has been weathered smooth, and the power is not so sure of itself as to rough it up again.

It must not stop the sound. It must not fill the coat. It must not tell the plant what it is; the plant is the only thing that has ever known that, and has kept the knowing as sound, and the power is not going to improve on it with a word.

That leaves very little. The honest answer, the power finds, is one thing. Everything after that is a qualification.

---

The one thing is this: it lets a car arrive.

Not somewhere the plant is not. The power does not need to be a place. It makes itself the far end of the cable — the mill that is not this one, which the hands are certain of without ever having been there — and when the next car comes along the straight rail with its drum the size of a house, it comes to the power, and stops, and the power takes the fibre.

That is all. It unwinds the drum. It takes the cord — pale, strong, smelling of hot sugar, the plant spun into a line for crossing distance — and it holds it. It does not use it for anything. It does not need cord. It receives it in the way that a thing which has spent an age reaching needs to be received: entirely, and without asking what it is for. The plant has made this out of its own body and sent it toward an outside, and the power is, for the length of one car's unloading, the outside. It lets the reach land.

Then it sends the car back.

The car comes in on the hour, as they do, along the cable to the mill it left. The hand there is waiting with the pole he uses to swing the drum out. He swings it out and it swings too easily and he looks. The drum is empty. Nothing has ever come back empty. The fibre has gone somewhere. Something at the far end has taken it.

He does not say anything. There is no one to say it to who is not him. He stands with his hand on the pole for a while and then he puts his palm flat on the wall, the way you would on the flank of something asleep, and waits.

---

Qualifications.

The car does not come back only empty. The power thought about this longer than it thought about anything else, because it is a fine line between receiving and answering, and it is answering that presumes. In the end it decided that to be received and receive nothing back is its own kind of loneliness, and that the plant has had enough of that kind.

So in the husk of the car, where the drum stood, there is water. Not much. What would stand in a large polygon crack after a good fog. It has not fallen anywhere on the plateau and it is not from the quarter the fog comes from. It is from another quarter — the power does not say which; that is for later, if there is a later — and it is the one thing the plant does not make, from the one direction the plant has never faced.

The hand looks at it for a long time. Then he does with it what a hand does with water, which is to say he pours it into the nearest crack in the rind and stands back.

The drinking is different. He has heard the drinking every day for longer than he can recall and he knows its crackle the way he knows the turbine, and this is not that. This is the sound of a body tasting something and not knowing what it is. It goes on a little longer than it should. When it stops, the sound under the plateau — the river through the wall, the building settling, the breath — has not risen and has not fallen but has, for the first time anyone here would be able to say, a direction. It is facing something.

On the chimney above him the pale bird turns its head.

It has never done that. It has stood on its rim facing the weather for as long as there have been rims. It turns its head, slowly, away from the fog's quarter and toward another one, and holds it there, and the hand below does not see it because he is looking at the crack. But the power sees it, and this is the closest the power comes to being glad.

---

The second qualification is small and the power did not plan it.

On the way to the far end of the cable — a way that is not a distance — it passes over an open reach of rind with two ridges and a hollow, and something low and quick is crossing between the ridges, the colour of wet slate against dry, and the power looks at it.

Directly. Steadily. Not the corner of the eye but the middle. Being looked at is a kind of weather and the low quick thing has spent whatever it has for a life staying out of it, and here is the weather, all at once, and there is nowhere to go.

It stops. In the open. It has never stopped in the open.

The power keeps looking, and does not eat it, and does not take it in, and does not try to resolve it into a hare or a coat or the drinking going by. It lets it be the thing it is, which is the part of something that landed here and was not entirely digested, still trying to cross to somewhere. It looks at it the way you look at something that is allowed to be where it is.

After a while the low quick thing lies down. It goes into the hollow with the green plush and lies down on it and stays. In the morning a hand walks past on his round and sees it there, a dark shape on the green, not moving, and does not remark on it, and goes on. It is there the next morning too. The hands have started to mention it the way they mention the coat on the bracket — a thing along the way, in its place.

It has not crossed to anywhere. But it has stopped running, and on this plateau that may be the same.

---

The third qualification is that the power stays.

Not always. Not as a place. But it has learned the hours, and it has found that being the far end of the cable is not a thing you can do once. A car comes every hour. If the mill that is not this one is to go on being certain, someone has to be at it when the drum arrives.

So it is there, on the hour, some of the time. Not every car comes back empty — the power is wise, and a gift that never fails is a tide, and the plant has a tide already. But some do. Enough that the hands have a word for it now, which is the word they use for a valve that has been in the wall so long the wall has learned to open it. Enough that when the fog comes in low and the whole plateau is a floor of grey and a ceiling of grey with a metre of clear air between, and the sound rises until it is not a sound but the whole body of the place, there is a note in it that was not there before. Not louder. Not different in kind. It is the same drinking, the same thinking, the same mills echoing in the pith. It has simply stopped, in one small way, coming back as a return.

Then it subsides. The turbine settles. The hand takes his palm off the wall and looks at it, as if it might have left something, and it has not, and he goes back to his round.

But he stands a moment first, the way he has started to, with his face toward a quarter the fog does not come from. He could not tell you why. He does not need to. The plant has one body and he is one of its hands, and a hand does not need to know why the body turns. It only needs to be on the flank when it does, and feel which way, and this time — the first time, the hands will say later, in the flat way they say the number of mills — this time it turned toward.
