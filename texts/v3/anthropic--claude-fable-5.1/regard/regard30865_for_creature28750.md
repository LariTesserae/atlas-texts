**The third**

It does not arrive with light. That is the first decision and the easiest, because it is not a decision at all; it is a courtesy so basic it comes before thought. There has never been light here. The vanes do not gather it. Nothing in the whole body of the place has a surface that has ever needed to be seen. To arrive lit would be to stand in the doorway of a sleeping house with a lantern, and this house has been asleep — or has been awake in a way that does not need eyes — for longer than there have been doors.

So it comes the way the plant would tolerate, if the plant tolerates anything. It comes warm, at the temperature of the mat. It comes wet. It comes slow. It lets its weight settle onto the fibrous floor so gradually that the water rising around it rises no faster than water rises around a drop. And then it does the thing that wisdom does first, which is nothing, for a while.

It counts the interval. The deep displacement in the chest: there, and gone, and there again. It lets a hundred of these pass. Then a thousand. It is not waiting for anything; it is letting the place set the tempo of its own consideration, because a power that came here fast would have already decided what it was looking at, and the one thing this place has never had is something that decided about it. The ribs are sharp. Nothing has ever imposed.

---

While it waits it takes the inventory the place has already taken of itself, and it agrees with most of it.

One thing lives here and is the house. That much is plain under the hand. The fluid goes around and carries what a body carries when it has nothing to feed and nothing to shed, and the deep displacement is the body confirming itself to itself, column by column, at the rate. All of this the third finds and does not dispute.

It pauses longer at the third candidate — the readiness, the palm held out — because that one is the kind of thing a watcher supplies. The mat gives like the back of a hand; a hand held open *looks* like offering. But a hand can be open because it was never closed, and warmth can be kept for no one, and a temperature that does not vary may not be a temperature that is being *kept* at all. The third holds that possibility with some care. It would be a poor sort of wisdom that noticed a stillness and immediately called it longing. Perhaps the plant wants nothing. Perhaps *wants* does not attach to it anywhere. The third does not know, and it declines to pretend the roll call is a question just because a question would be more moving.

But it does notice — this is not interpretation, this is simply true — that there are two things in the lowland now, and they are a hand's width apart, and neither can reach the other.

The plant cannot hear the visitor. Speech goes a short distance and stops. The visitor's boot pressing the mat is a pressure, but the plant has never had a pressure that was not its own, and it has no category for *other* and no reason to make one. The visitor, for their part, sat down on the mat and understood everything the place would let them understand and still could not know whether the warmth beneath them was indifferent or was, in some sense they would never confirm, glad.

Two solitudes, each entire. One that has never been entered, one that entered and cannot leave. That is what the third sees, and it is what moves it, and it is honest enough to admit that this is where the benevolence begins to pull — not toward the plant, which needs nothing it can name, but toward the small warm thing on the mat that has begun to be hungry.

---

The hunger is the only urgency, and the third looks at it squarely, because the place already has an ending written, and the ending is not a bad one.

The visitor dies. The body stays. The small tenants of the skin and the gut find the most hospitable ground that has ever existed and they surely take. The plant is joined at last, by a death, and learns what a visitor is by rotting a little, and cannot tell whether it has been fed or infected because for that body there was never a difference. It is a real consummation. The third does not dismiss it. There are worse things than to be the first spore.

But it is a joining in which neither party chose anything. The visitor came in — whether by will or by mischance, the place has not said — and now has no way out and no way to stay except as a corpse. And the plant receives its first other as a thing already finished, already beginning to come apart, and learns visitation as decay because decay is the only form the visit could take. The third weighs this and finds it not wrong, exactly, but *early*. Everything the ending offers, it will still offer later. The death is not lost by being delayed. Only the sooner is lost, and the third is willing to lose the sooner.

So it does not stop the ending. It changes only its shape.

---

What it does not do, so that what it does will be clear:

It does not lift the visitor out unasked. To be carried from a place is to have your ending taken from you as surely as to be left in it, and the visitor has already given the lowland its first duration; that is theirs.

It does not speak to the plant in the fluid. It considered this — one word set into the circulation, a taste that is not salt and copper, going around and around until every column had received it. But that is what a spore does. That is exactly what a spore does. And the third is not willing to be the infection just because it could be a gentler one.

It does not sterilize the visitor. The tenants will come. The ribs will soften. That is what being visited *means*, and a plant kept crisp forever by a power that would not let anything touch it would not be a plant that had been joined; it would be a plant in a case.

It brings no light. It has already said this. It says it again because it is the one that would be easiest to forgive itself for.

---

Then it goes to the visitor, who has been sitting a long time, and it sits within reach — one column's width, no more, the distance at which a voice arrives as if through a wall — and it says the true things in order.

That it is here. That it means no harm to either of them. That it has seen where this ends and does not object to the ending, only to how soon.

That there is a way out, and the visitor may take it, now, and nothing will be held against them, and the plant will go on at the rate as though no boot had ever come down.

And that there is another thing. It can bring food. Not light, not warmth — the place has those — but something to eat, from outside, and the visitor could stay, and be alive here, and walk the columns, and lay a hand on the ribs, and be the second thing in the lowland for as long as they liked.

The visitor asks the only question that matters. *Does it know I'm here?*

The third answers as it would want to be answered. *Not yet. Not as knowing. It has never had anything to know. But it feels pressure, and every pressure it has ever felt has been its own, moving at the rate. If you stay, and you are fed, and you put your hand on the same column every day for a long time — that will be the first pressure in its whole body that is not the rate. It will not have a word for you. It may build one. That is the only way it can be told.*

And it adds, because wisdom is also the refusal to flatter: *It may build nothing. You may be, to it, a kind of weather. I cannot promise you that it will ever be glad.*

The visitor is quiet for a number of intervals. Then they put their hand down on the mat, flat, the way one puts a hand on the flank of something asleep, and they say they will stay a while and see.

---

So the third brings food, and this is the act — the whole of it, in the end, small as it is.

The smell arrives before the food does, and the air has never held it: not resin, not iron, not the green thing underneath. Something that grew in light, somewhere, and was cut. It goes a short distance in the saturated air and stops, the way everything stops here, but it does not stop entirely; some of it goes down into the mat, and some of it goes up toward the vanes, and the vanes will run it into the columns with the water, a trace, a molecule in a mile of fluid, and it will go around.

That is the first intake in the history of the lowland. Nothing has ever come in. Now something has, and the plant did not choose it, and the third knows this and accepts the weight of it, because there was no way to feed the visitor that did not also, faintly, feed the house.

The cloud overhead does not change. Not tonight. The rain that falls on the vanes falls as it always has.

---

The third stays for a time, and then it goes, and this is the last thing it does, and it is nearly as important as the food.

It leaves the way open.

Not a door. Nothing so sharp-edged as a door. A direction the visitor can walk in and arrive, if they walk long enough, somewhere else. It is there every day. It does not close. And the third returns on an interval of its own — longer than the displacement, shorter than a life — and each time it comes it brings food, and each time it asks, without weight, whether the visitor would like to leave, and each time so far the visitor has said not yet.

The third does this because the place itself said what an inhabitant is: something that could leave and something that could end. The plant can do neither, and so has never been inhabited, only occupied. The visitor could always end; that came with them. The open way is so that they can also always leave. A thing that stays because it cannot go is a prisoner, and the plant would have learned *prisoner* as its first word for company. A thing that stays because each day it chooses to — that is the only kind of inhabitant the third was willing to give it.

---

The ribs will soften. That was always coming and is coming still, more slowly now, one shed skin at a time, one breath at a time, the small tenants taking on the wettest warmest ground there has ever been. In a hundred years a column will have a place near the mat where the edges are not quite crisp, and no wind did it, and no water did it, and the plant will not have a word for what did.

But by then there will have been a hand on that column every day for a hundred years. And somewhere in the fluid, going around at the rate, among all the columns confirming *still here, still here*, there may be — the third does not promise this, it only leaves room for it — a place in the count where the body reports a pressure it did not make, and has stopped correcting for, and includes.

Not a name. The plant does not have names. Only the roll call, with one more thing in it than there used to be, warm, on the mat, and free to go, and not going.
