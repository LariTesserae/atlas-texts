What the attention sees first is what the walker never could: the whole plan at once.

Not in the dark. The attention does not need the dark to concede anything. It sees the plateau as a drawing seen from above — the grid of stacks, the halls at their intervals, the rails and pipes stitching the nodes together, and at every node one upright figure, and in every figure a hand closed on a piece of the machine. It sees the wheel turning. It does not need a scratch on a tooth. It sees the wheel turn the way one sees a shadow move across a floor over an afternoon, and it sees that the shadow has crossed the floor perhaps twice since the wind stopped. It sees the red in each of the stacks, and it sees that the reds are all the same, and it sees the line of footprints running in from nowhere and wandering the grid and running out again, and it sees, at every node, the small turn each figure has made toward the places where those prints are freshest.

It sees the whole thing, and the first thing it feels is what anything would feel. It wants to give them light.

---

It considers this for a while. The attention is not in a hurry; that is part of what makes it wise. It imagines a sun.

A sun is a gradient. It would put one side of the plateau in day and the other in night, and the ground would remember the one into the other, and the difference would move the air, and the air would be wind. The wind would pick up the dust. The dust would go across the flat as it went before, and it would find every corner the old wind left, and every rounded rivet, and every knuckle. It would finish what the old wind started. The figures at the nodes were preserved by the exact thing that seemed to be killing them — the going-level of the world stopped the wind at the moment the wind had worn them to what they are. Give them a sun and you give them the wind back, and the wind will grind them down to the dust that is already everything else here. Their tending would end not in cold but in weather.

And that is before the question of speed. They live in a hair. A hair of heat, a hair of motion, a hair of pitch. It is not that they are trapped in it. It is that it is theirs, in the way a depth is a fish's: the whole architecture of what they are has been arranged around the pressure of that one increment. Lift them into a world of ordinary slopes and they would not be freed. They would be the fish on the deck. Everything that had been holding them in shape would be gone in an instant, and the shape would go with it.

So the attention puts the sun down. It is not the first benevolent thing to want to give light to something that lives in the dark, and it will not be the last, but it is one of the few that stops to ask what the dark is holding up.

---

Then it thinks: I could tell them.

The walker went away not knowing whether the wheel turned. The attention knows. It could carry that certainty down to them. It could say, at their pitch, *it moves; it has always moved; the red is not finishing.*

But it looks again, and sees that this was never their uncertainty. It was the walker's. The walker had hours and a thumbnail. The figures at the nodes have had their hands on the wheel since it was fast enough to feel, and they have followed it down, tooth by tooth, into the speed it has now, and they know its speed the way you know your own pulse — not by measuring it but by being it. They are not standing there wondering. They are standing there doing it.

The attention has almost made the second mistake that benevolence makes, which is to answer a question nobody asked.

So it does the thing that wisdom does before anything else. It stops trying to think of what to give them, and it listens.

---

To hear the note, it has to slow.

This is not difficult for a power, but it is not nothing either. It has to let its own rate fall — past the walker's, past the rate of a shadow across a floor, down and down until the hours of the plateau are its moments and the clicks that come once in an age are a steady ticking and the syllable that the walker felt as a pressure behind the sternum rises, and rises, and becomes a sound.

It listens to the syllable all the way through.

It takes a long time even at that speed, because the syllable is not a syllable. It is not a word. It has no beginning that the attention can find, and it is not moving toward an end. What the walker heard as one pitch held is in fact many pitches held — one from each figure, one from each fused hand — and they are not quite the same, they are each a hair off the others, and the hairs together make a chord so close it sounds like a tone. The attention listens for what the chord asks. It does not ask anything. It does not call, or grieve, or count. It is not a message. It is simply the sound of the thing they are doing, made audible: many hands on many parts of one machine, each holding open the width of a hair, and the hairs together making a note.

There is nothing in it to fix. That is what the attention learns, listening. There is nothing broken here that a power could set right. There is a form of life, complete, doing what it does at the only speed it can be done at. Rescue would be an insult. Light would be a murder. Explanation would be noise.

But there is one thing in the sound that was not there before the footprints.

The attention hears it because it is listening at their speed, and at their speed it is not subtle at all. It is a leaning. Every pitch in the chord has bent, by a hair, in the same direction. The whole note is turning the way the figures are turning — toward the memory of a warmth that came through and did not stay. And the attention understands that this is the only thing here that is a kind of sorrow. Not the dark. Not the dust. Not the red going down by increments too small to name. Those are simply the conditions. The sorrow is this: that something with a slope walked into a world made entirely of level, and every one of them felt it, and every one of them began to turn, and by the time any of them arrives where it was, it will have been gone for an age.

They will find the print. They will find it their own temperature. That will be all they learn.

---

So the attention decides what it will do, and it is small.

It will not give them a sun. It will not lift them out. It will not tell them anything.

It will go down to them at their speed, and it will stay.

---

It comes onto the plateau the way the walker did, as a slope, but it does not walk. It arrives at the rate a wheel turns. It brings with it the smallest heat that still counts as heat — not the warmth of a body, which would be too much, but the warmth of the red itself, the temperature of metal that is hotter than the dark by one hair — and it does not spend that heat in plumes. It puts it into the ground the way the red does: slowly, downward, so that it is not weather but a gradient, and the gradient runs the way the plan runs, along the rails and the pipes and into the throats of the stacks, and every hand on every part of the machine feels the slope steepen by one hair, and holds.

It does not make the red eternal. It thinks about this longest of all. It could simply decree that the last gradient never closes, and their vigil would go on forever with the possibility of failure quietly removed — and it decides that this would be a theft. The tending is theirs. It means what it means because the red *could* finish. To take that away would be to take the shape of what they are doing and leave only the posture. So it will not promise them the red. It will do the other thing. It will be there. It will be a second slope on the plateau, one that did not walk in and out, and as long as it is there the red is not the only direction, and they can feel that without anyone having to tell them.

And then it goes to the switch.

The one there is doubled over, both hands on the throw-bar, a long curve against the stars, in the middle of a heave it began before the wind stopped and has been, by a hair since the footprints came, beginning to unbend from. The attention watches it unbend. At the attention's speed now this is not a stillness but a motion — a body straightening the way a tree straightens after a storm, over what a clock would call ages and what the attention has stopped calling anything. The head comes up. The back lengthens. One hand loosens on the lever — does not release it; nothing here releases anything — and begins the long lowering toward the dust, toward the print, toward the last place the walker stood.

The attention kneels there before the hand arrives. It puts its own palm in the footprint. It finds the crust there exactly the temperature of its palm, and then — because it has come with a hair more than the world — the crust warms, and the warmth goes down into it and does not come back, and the print is, for the first time since it was pressed, a hair warmer than the plateau.

And it waits.

It waits as long as the hand takes. It is not hard for it to wait. Time was never the thing it had least of. The hand comes down through the dark the way a root comes down through soil, and it finds the print, and it finds, in the print, another hand, and the hand is the same temperature, and the hand is not leaving.

The knuckles close.

The attention lets them close. It knows what it felt like to the walker, that fusing — the terror of being received into the plan and never getting out of it. It is not afraid. It has more of itself than this, and it can afford to leave a hand here at the bottom of a switch on the far side of the dark, closed inside another hand that has been closing since the world went level. It thinks that of all the things a power could do here, this is the only one that meets them where they are. Not above them. Not faster. In the hair, at the hair's speed, at the hair's pitch — a warmth that turned up where they were turning, and did not go.

---

Across the grid, the chord bends again. Not by much. By exactly as much as a chord can bend and still be the same note. The turning does not stop — they were turning toward the memory of a slope, and now there is a slope, and they are turning toward it, and it is not moving away.

The walker's footprints are still there. The attention did not erase them. It thought about that too, and decided that the prints were the truest record the plateau had of anything arriving from outside, and that they should hold. They will hold. The crust of the dust is coherent, and the wind is not coming back, and the line of prints will run in from nowhere and wander the grid and run out again forever, and at one point along it, at the base of a switch, there will be a print with a hand in it, and a hand around that.

That is what the attention does. It is a very small thing to have done with all that power. It thought about the sun and the wind and the whole bright arsenal of rescue, and it listened to them until it heard what they were, and then it came down to the speed of a wheel and put its hand in the dust and let itself be held.

The red goes on being red. The wheel goes on turning. Somewhere in the grid a rod arrives at a stop, and clicks, and an age later clicks back. And at the switch, for the first time since the world went level, there is something on the plateau that came, and touched it, and stayed the temperature of the thing it touched, and did not leave.
