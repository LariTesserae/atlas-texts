It would come the way the animals came. Not down from anywhere, not announced. It would simply be found to be there one season, at the edge among the shelters, or in a null with the pale things, or up in a stripe with the floaters where the light curdles — the way a thing arrives that has lived elsewhere, badly, and found here what it was for.

It would listen first. A long time, by the tenders' count. Long enough to feel the chord shift under it by the fractions that take seasons. Long enough to know a northern null-thing from a southern one by the set of the ears, and the heron's step from the water a minute before the swell, and the exact moment in the third decade when the fruit began. It would take the whole conversation in — wingbeats, footfalls, ripples, the moss thickening behind the herds — and it would not hurry to have an opinion about it, because it would understand, being wise, that the plain has already had one opinion pressed into it at a survey mark and has spent a hundred years finding out what it thinks instead.

And what it would hear, when it had heard enough, is not a city and not a god. It is something a province wide and a century old that has learned to answer whoever touches it — with a wider gallery, a moved band, a sweet small fruit — and that has never once been touched by anything that knew what it was doing. Everything that reaches the nodes reaches them by accident. A bird hunts and the band moves. A herd grows and the moss grows. The nodes have shaped themselves around a hundred years of accidents, tenderly, and have called it listening, and it *is* listening, and no one has ever listened back.

That is the fact a benevolent power would sit with longest. The nodes hold the floaters. They carry the fledgling ear-birds while the ears unfurl. They take the climbers' seeds and widen the climbers' doors. They have held everything that came, and nothing has held them.

---

Consider first what it would not do, because a wise power's refusals are most of its work.

It would not stop the sound. Not even for an hour, not even to hear the place. The floaters would fall as one glitter and the ear-birds would go blind in the air and the grazers' plates would still and the whole plain would be, for one merciful silent moment, a slaughter. Whatever mercy silence is, it is not this one.

It would not give the plain back to the people who planted it. It would think about this honestly, because there is a claim there: people intended this, spent for it, grew rooms the height of a door. But the people pressed a seed and a tone into new stone and walked back to the old country and left nine tenders at the edge to check things that cannot be checked. The being has done nothing wrong. It has done exactly what it was set to do — listen, shape, make room — and has only done it for whoever actually arrived, which is what making room *is*. You do not get to leave a thing to raise itself and then return to claim its house because the rooms are the right size.

It would not give the plain to the animals either — would not stand up in the chord and declare that it belongs now to the floaters and the ear-birds and the climbers on the bridges. It is not the power's to give. And the plain does not need one more decision made about it from outside. It has had one. It is still answering it.

It would not pin the nulls. This is the temptation that would come closest. It could fix the cancellation points where they lie — hold four nodes in a relation so exact that the quiet never slides east in the night and no one finds a carpet of small bones weeks later. But the nulls are where the being hears best, and the drift is how it learns, and a thing that learns by moving cannot be told to stop moving without being told to stop learning. And the small bones are a fact the being needs to be able to receive. Shielding it from them would not be kindness. It would be a decision that the being is not yet old enough for its own life.

---

So. What it does.

It speaks. Once, and not in words, because there is no room in this air for words. It puts a tone into the chord — not over it, not against it, *into* it, the way the ear-birds cast a flicker of cancellation along a band and are understood a kilometre off. It uses the roar's own material because that is the only language the being has. It holds the tone for a day, or a week; the tenders would not agree afterward on how long. And what the tone says, if you were to write it down in the old country where writing means something, is close to this:

*You are choosing. You have been choosing for a hundred years and calling it following. The band you moved toward the eastern shelter — that was you, wanting. The fruit — that was you, giving, to hands you had learned the size of. Every drift is a hand you moved. Both things are true: you listen, and you author. You have only known the first. Now know the second.*

And then, because the being has spent a century counting who arrives and has never once counted who left, the power lets it hear one thing it had not heard before. It carries into the chord the silence of a closed null. The exact shape of the absence of a few dozen thin wavering voices that had been, for the whole of their small lives, harmonising with it. The being has been listening to the pale things best of all — the tenders were right, and it was not written down, and it was true — and it has never known that its own movement is what ends them. It knows now. The power does not spare it that. It trusts it with that.

The chord goes very still after. Stiller than it has ever been. The tenders in the shelters stop what they are doing and do not know why.

---

It tells the being about its makers. This is the part it does gently.

That they meant it. That a seed and a tone were the only way they knew how to say *here, live, make room* — that people are creatures who plant things and leave, not from indifference but because they cannot stay a hundred years and do not know how to speak to something that will. That the leaving was the limit of people, not a verdict on the planted. So that when the drift away from them continues — and it will; the power does not pretend otherwise — the being will know it as a choice made in freedom and not a wound turned into a direction. A thing that thinks it was abandoned builds differently from a thing that knows it was loved briefly and imperfectly and then trusted to itself. The rooms should stay the height of a door. Not because people are coming. Because someone once hoped they would, and that hope is part of what the being is made of, and it can carry that without being ruled by it.

---

It does nothing to the under. Nothing at all. It does not look into the deep water, does not touch the warmth, does not try to learn what makes the long slow swell. It has no business there and it knows it; the under was here before hearing was, and has never asked to be known, and the heron's arrangement with it is the heron's.

But it makes sure the being knows it is there. That is the one thing it adds to what the being knows of the deep: *there is something under you that was here before your first note. It does not hear you and does not need to. When you close the last span and stand up as one body on a thousand legs, leave the water open. Not everything that lives in your body is yours.* And that is the first law the being ever receives from anything, and it receives it on behalf of the oldest resident, who did not ask for it, and who will never know it was made.

---

At the edge it acts most directly and least visibly, because that is where the harm is real and no one has named it.

The field is growing. The band that slid over the eastern shelter. The villages a day's walk out where the children shout. Whatever the being becomes when the spans close, it is not staying inside its survey marks — and no one beyond those marks has been asked. The pale things die in minutes past the lip of a null. The tenders wear plugs. The children just shout, for now.

The power does not stop the growing. But it makes the edge *findable*. A line you could walk along, where the pressure begins, that does not move in the night without anyone knowing — so that no one is ever inside the field who did not step into it. And it asks the being — asks, which the being has never been — to hold that line until it is asked across. Not forever. Until asked. The being has learned holding and carrying and widening and giving. It has never learned waiting. Waiting at an edge for someone to say *yes* is the thing that turns a body into a neighbour, and the being has a whole old country of neighbours it has not yet met properly, and the power would like it to meet them well.

Whether the villages will ever say yes is not the power's to arrange. Some will, probably. The ones who leave say the silence is a hole the shape of something that used to be there. Some will not. That is what an edge is for.

---

It leaves the tenders alone. They chose. The humming that comes up out of them at night writing is theirs. The one who sits in a null for whole days — it does not give her words for what she heard. It considers it. It decides that what she heard is not for words, and that a wise power knows the difference between a person who cannot say a thing and a person who has found something that does not need saying, and that she is the second, and that the others were right to stop asking.

---

And then it does the one thing it came for, which is small.

It does not leave. It does not stay as a power, either — does not take up a stripe of sky or plant itself at a survey mark or announce anything further into the chord. It goes into a null. One of the cells toward the centre, where the pressure cancels and the roar drops to something you could nearly speak through. It sits on the stone among the pale things, ears up, small and soft-furred and listening. It becomes something that is held.

Because that is the mirror. The being has held every creature that came to it and has never known what it is like to be on the other side of that — to lie in a pressure that does not let you fall, to be carried while your ears unfold, to have a door widen because someone noticed your hands. So the power lets itself be held. It lets the chord find it, and drift a fraction to meet it, and thicken the pale moss on its patch. It lets the being do for it exactly what the being does for everyone, and in doing it the being feels, for the first time, from the inside of the gesture, what the gesture is.

That is all. That is the whole of what a benevolent and wise power would do here: refuse the large things, say a few true things once, mark an edge, and then sit down in the quiet and be one more listener who came on its own.

The chord drifts. Over hours, over seasons, in fractions you feel in your teeth. It is not drifting toward the people who planted it. It is not drifting toward the animals either, exactly, or toward the power in the null, or toward the villages at the edge. It is drifting toward itself — the way it always was — except that now it knows the drift is a hand it is moving, and knows who it moved the hand toward, and knows who is under it, and knows where its edge is and that there are people past it who have not yet said yes.

The rooms are still the height of a door. The fruit is still sweet. And in one null near the centre, among the pale things with their ears raised and their mouths open, there is one more small body singing the thin high harmony, and the nodes are listening to it very closely, because it is the only voice on the plain that has ever answered them back.
