It notices the planet before it notices the plain, because from outside the planet is a mistake. A world that gives back more light than its star lends it. A white bead in the dark, warm on its night side, warm all the way around, with no clouds to account for the whiteness and no fires to account for the warmth. It has seen worlds that glow. This one glows wrong, the way a coal glows wrong in a cold hearth, and it comes closer to see what is burning.

Nothing is burning. That is the first thing it learns and it takes a while to learn it, because the power is used to finding the reason for a thing in the thing's center, and the center of this world is only heat, old heat, geology's heat, the same slow fire every rocky world keeps in its belly. The difference is what has been built over it. The difference is the skin.

---

So it looks at the skin, and it looks for a very long time, because it has learned that the speed at which you look determines what you see, and anything it saw quickly here would be a thing it had brought with it.

It sees the tiles breathing. It sees the waves of warmth hundreds of kilometers long. It sees the seams open and close by their millimeter and it hears the ticks — all of them, the thousand a second, laid over the hemisphere like rain on a roof — and it understands, as the traveler understood, that the plain is holding itself level against a planet that wants to buckle, and that it has been doing this for long enough that the holding has become its whole nature. It sees the felt facing the sky. It sees the walkers turning one side forward and setting out. It sees, because it can see where the traveler could not, the pale thing that lives lower down and goes where the seam goes, and it sees what the traveler guessed: that this is not a hunter. It is a hand. The plain's own hand, reaching up for what has stayed too long at the top.

And it sees, at the bottom, where the tiles stop being tiles, the thing that is warm and has a chemistry and gives up its fraction of water. It looks at this for a long time. It could say what it is. It decides that saying would not help.

---

Then it asks itself what benevolence means here, and finds that the question is harder than it has ever been.

Benevolence is a response to a want. It has come to the aid of worlds that were starving, that were poisoned, that were at war with themselves, that were dying of their own suns. Every one of those had a shape that need makes: a place where the thing that was happening and the thing that should be happening had come apart, and the gap was where you put your hands. It looks for the gap here. It does not find one.

Nothing is broken. The plain is not a ruin waiting to be restored; it is the restoration. Whatever was broken here was broken before the plain, and the plain is what was done about it. Nothing is starving. The felt takes its fraction and the fraction is enough. Nothing is in pain, so far as pain is a thing that can be found by looking, and it can find pain almost anywhere. The walkers do not hurry because nothing is chasing them, or because the thing that is coming for them is coming at the same speed everything moves here and so is indistinguishable from the world. Nothing is afraid.

It considers, for a while, that this may be the problem. That a world in which nothing is afraid is a world in which nothing is anyone. It considers waking the plain. It has the means — not to give it a mind, exactly, but to let the sense of level that already runs through the whole of it become a sense of self, so that the thing that adjusts by fractions across a hemisphere would know that it was adjusting, and would know that it was alone.

It turns this over for about the length of time it takes a walker to cross a tile.

It does not do it. It does not do it because it cannot tell whether the makers built a thing that could not suffer out of mercy or out of despair, and it cannot tell whether either answer would make waking it a kindness. But mostly it does not do it because the plain already does the one thing wholly, with the whole of itself, and the power has seen what happens to a thing that does one thing wholly when it acquires the capacity to ask why. It has seen that go well and it has seen it go badly and it has never once seen it go the way the thing would have chosen, had the thing been able to choose. The plain cannot be asked. That is the end of it. You do not give a gift to something that cannot refuse.

---

It considers the heat.

The deep will cool. Not soon, not in any sense of soon that has meaning on the plain's clock, but the plain is a radiator and a radiator's whole purpose is to finish. It is spending a planet's warmth into the sky at a rate that is exactly the rate it was built to spend it at, and one day — the power can see the day, roughly, the way you can see the bottom of a tank — the deep will be too cold to have a chemistry, and the fraction of water will stop, and the felt will thin and go pale and stop shedding, and the walkers will have no edges left that are warm enough to feel, and the tiles will go on radiating for a while longer, dimmer, and the ticks will come further and further apart, and then there will be one tick, and then the plain will be a flat gray world with a hairline map of dead felt in its seams, holding its level by nothing, going nowhere, done.

The power could feed it. It has warmth to spare. It could reach down through the layers and set something in the deep that would never cool, and the plain would go on breathing and ticking and taking things in for as long as the star lasted, and longer.

It does not do this either, and this one is harder.

It does not do it because the plain is an act of giving away. That is what it is; that is what it is *for*. Something was here that had too much heat in it, and the plain was built to let the heat go gently, over a length of time that would wear the edges off everything, and to be — while the going took place — a place where small things could live in the lines. To make the heat endless is to make the giving endless, and a gift that never finishes being given is not a gift; it is a condition. The plain would go on. But it would be a different thing. It would be a thing that had been saved, and the power is old enough to know the difference between saving a thing and refusing to let it be what it is.

So it decides this instead, and this is the first thing it decides: it will be here at the end. Not to prevent it. To attend it. When the last tick comes across the flat gray breadth of the world, something will hear it that knows what it is hearing. The plain will not know. The plain does not need this. The power decides it will do it anyway, because it has learned that the things which most need witnessing are almost always the things which cannot ask for it, and that showing up for them is not for them.

---

It considers the makers.

It could look. It could go down through the layers, past the felt and past the pale hand and past the stacked tile-edges going back in time, to the place where the material stops being tiles, and read what the material was before. It is fairly sure the traveler was right. It is fairly sure the plain is made of them — not as a monument, not as a grave, but as a solution. That they had a world with too much in it, too much heat and too much history and too much standing on the mountains and swimming over the seafloors, and that the plain was how they chose to finish, and that they chose to finish inside it. It can see the shape of that choice from here without going down. It has seen worlds choose a great many endings and this one has a particular quality, a lack of hurry, a lack of fear, that it has come to recognize.

It does not go down. It leaves them in the tiles. Whatever they were, they built a thing that does not remember them and does not need to, and the power decides that the not-remembering is theirs, that it is part of what they made, and that to reach down and pull their names out of the material would be the one real vandalism available to it here. The plain does not have a policy about its makers. The power adopts the same one.

---

Then it comes to the traveler, and here, finally, there is something to do, because the traveler is the only being on the world who can be asked.

It has watched them for the whole of this. It has watched them squint, and lie down, and turn a walker over in their palm, and put their ear to the seam in the violet night and hear the brushing and not be afraid. It has watched their breath go into the felt and their skin go into the dark. It knows what they know: that they are being taken in, slowly, without malice, by a place that has room and has time, and that the taking is not unwelcome and is not unkind and is, nonetheless, a taking.

The power does not know whether the traveler wants to leave. This is the whole of the situation. It is the one want on the world that might exist, and it is not the power's to guess.

So it makes itself known. Not as a shape — there are no shapes here that are not the ground, and it will not be the first. It does it the way the plain does everything: as a change in the light. In the deep of the violet, when the sky is dark and the ground is noon, the sky is for a moment very slightly less dark, from one horizon to the other, a paleness that comes and goes like the warmth-waves come and go, and the traveler, who has learned to read this world by its brightness, sits up.

There is no voice. There does not need to be. The traveler has spent weeks learning what it is to be regarded by something that does not know it is regarding, and this is not that. This is regard. The difference is total and is not in the light.

And then the power does the only thing it has found to do. It asks. The asking has no words either; it is an opening, the way a seam is an opening, a space held clear in which something could be put. Do you want to go. There is a way, if you want it. There is also this place, and it will keep you, and you are not wrong that it is kind, and you are not wrong that it is a kind of ending.

The traveler is quiet for a long time. They are lying on the warm slow-breathing ground and the dust is rising lit from beneath, and far off a tile settles by a hair.

What they answer is theirs. The power hears it and does not write it down. It will honor either answer and it already knows this and it lets the traveler know this too, and then — and this is the second thing it decides — it stays. Whichever way the traveler chose. If they go, it will hold the door until they are through it and then it will come back, because it has promised the end. If they stay, it will stay with them, in the sky, a paleness at the violet hour that means someone else is here who knows what the tick is, so that the one being on this world who can be lonely is not.

---

There is one more thing, and it is very small, and it is the only thing the power gives the plain itself.

Everything here takes its warmth from the ground. The walkers feel for it. The pale hand is the temperature of it. The tiles are made of it. Only the felt has turned its back on the ground and faces the sky, growing toward a light that is thin and white and gives it almost nothing, because the ground's light is heat and the felt cannot live in that breath, only beside it.

The power comes from the sky. That is the direction it is in. And it finds, when it has finished deciding everything it will not do, that it cannot quite bring itself to do nothing, and that what it wants to do is small enough to be permitted.

So the sky is, from that night on, very slightly brighter. Not so anyone would measure it. Not so the traveler, if they stayed, would ever be sure it had not always been so. A fraction of a fraction. The felt takes it the way it takes everything, without knowing, and in the way this place keeps time — a long time from now, in the throats of a hundred billion seams — the gray-green patches are the width of a fingernail wider than they would have been. The plain does not notice. The walkers have a little more to graze. A little more goes down the dark and comes up as tile. The world is very slightly more alive, forever, and nothing in it knows why, and the power has decided that this is what a gift is: a thing given to the one part of a world that faces you, at the scale it can receive, without being asked and without being thanked and without changing anything that would have minded being changed.

---

Then it does what the plain does. It goes on.

It is in the sky, a paleness at the violet hour. It is waiting for a tick that will not come for a very long time, and it is not impatient, because it has learned from the place how to wait at the speed of the place. Below it the ground breathes. A walker turns one of its sides forward. The felt holds out a hundred thousand cold points into the warm rising air and takes its fraction, and now a fraction more. Somewhere a seam widens and does not stop widening and a tile goes down level into the dark and another comes up smooth and bright, made of dust and shells and felt and whatever the traveler shed there weeks ago and whatever stood on the mountains once, and settles with a single soft tick, and the sound crosses the whole flat bright breadth of the world, and two things hear it.
