It comes to it from above, which is the only way to come to it. It has no weight to be spread and no blood for the pool to match, and so for the first time in the basin's kept moment something is present that the plant does not have to attend to. That is the first thing it gives, and it gives it by accident: an hour in which nothing is asked.

Then it sits on the lip, with its hand on the warm poured rock, and considers.

---

It could give it an evening. That is what a lesser kindness would reach for first — the sun let down at last behind the white wall, the heat allowed to slacken, a coolness coming up out of the seams, and the plant learning what the rest of the living world knows, which is that the day ends and you are still there afterward. It would be a gift. It would also be the end of the thing it was given to. A held breath that is let out is not a breath that has been improved.

It could give it seed. Some small brown husk dropped into a seam, so that after a long time there would be a single stem that was not full, not mature, not yet at the exact size it would be; a stem that was *on its way*. A gradient other than the visitor. Company. But the plant is not lonely. The power has met lonely things and knows the smell of it, and the air here smells of wax and clean water and nothing else.

It could give it an end. Wise powers are asked for this more often than the unwise suppose, and they know the difference between a thing that is finished and a thing that is trapped in finishing. It looks for that difference for a long while. It looks at the beads being turned over and over like coins that buy nothing. It looks at the stems creaking under no one's weight. It waits to feel the particular ache of a held thing that would let go if it were shown how.

It does not feel it. What it feels instead, coming up through the rock into its palm, is something it has almost never encountered and has spent a very long time looking for, in the way one looks for a word one has forgotten. It is the feeling of a thing that is not asking.

So it does not give it an evening, or a seed, or an end. It sits a while longer, because it is not often that a wise power is given the chance to be quiet in the presence of something that has arrived before it, and it would be foolish to hurry that.

---

Then it climbs down.

It goes down by the scoops, of course, fitting itself into them one after another in the slight spiral, and about halfway down it stops, because it has understood what they are for. Not for descending. The thing that made them never descended; it was already at the bottom, and rising, and the rock was the last thing it touched before it stopped having anything to touch with. The scoops are not steps. They are the print of something reaching up as far as it could reach and then no farther. They are the highest point that hands ever got to here.

The power puts its own hand into the topmost scoop, the one nearest the lip, and rests it there. It presses nothing; the rock is long cooled. It only lets its hand lie where the last hand lay, at the height where reaching stopped, for as long as it takes to be a reply.

Then it goes on down.

---

At the bottom the plant takes it, and there is a small adjustment — the power can feel it, the way you feel a room notice you — as a hundred thousand stems discover that there is nothing to distribute. The creaking does not come. The pool it passes does not warm or cool. It walks across the wide green as the light walks across it, being everywhere and pressing on nothing, and the plant, which does not want and does not wonder, does the nearest thing to wondering that a complete thing can do: it lets the visitor pass without arranging itself, and in the not-arranging there is a kind of attention that has not been used before.

The power goes to the ring of stones first.

It stands inside the ring. That is all. The stems have grown up through the centre and over the lower course, and so it stands on stem inside a waist-high circle of white unmarked rock, and for the length of its standing there, the ring means what it was built to mean. *Here.* Not *here, once*. Here.

It goes to the dome and crawls through the single opening and kneels on the plant that fills it to the sill, in the dimness that is not dim, and the dome, for the length of its kneeling, has an inside.

It goes to the line of seven stones and walks along them, from the first to the seventh, and when it comes to the seventh it does not stop but takes one more step, into the place the stones are pointing, and stands there. It is the same green as everywhere else. It is the same lit water in the same seams. But it is *where the stones lead*, and the power stands in it as if it had been sent for, and the seven uprights, for the length of its standing, lead somewhere.

It goes to the curved wall, one course high, and steps over it and sits down in the nothing it encloses, so that it encloses something.

None of this changes a bead. Nothing is added. The stones are exactly where they were set. But every gesture the maker made in its last hour with hands — *here*, *inside*, *this way*, *mine* — has now, once, been received by something that understood it. That is a thing that can be given to a completed being without asking it to become anything. You cannot give it a future. You can answer its last sentence.

---

Then the beetle.

The power finds it where it always is, already a little way along the seam, going at its one pace, dry through the water. It walks beside it for a while — a long while, by the beetle's measure; no while at all, by the basin's — and by the end of the walk it knows which of the three stories is true, because knowing that is the kind of thing it is for.

The beetle is not a survivor. Nothing survived; there was nothing before, only new rock. And it is not a toy, not a coin kept for the pleasure of turning it. It is the third thing. It is the last of the maker's reaching that did not get let go of. When the plant set down the final stone and stood back and released, one by one, the extremities it had used — the pressing tip, the lifting tip, the fitting tip — there was one small end of itself that had been in the middle of going somewhere, and it did not know how to release a thing mid-errand, and so it did not. It let it keep going. It gave it legs, and a road, and let it go on being *on its way* so that the rest could stop.

The beetle is where the plant put its wanting. All of it. One dark slow walker along a seam, carrying the whole of *toward* so that the basin could be free of it.

And the road does go somewhere. The power has already been standing there. It goes to the seven stones.

---

So the power does the one thing that is left to do, and it is very small.

It goes back to the end of the line of uprights and stands one step past the seventh, in the place the stones point to, and it waits. It is very good at waiting. The plant does not measure, so it cannot be said how long. Long enough for the light not to move. Long enough for the heat not to build. Long enough, at last, for a small dark shape to come up over the swell along its seam at the one pace it has ever had, and to walk between the first stone and the second, and the second and the third, and the sixth and the seventh, and to reach the place where the road ends and something is standing there.

The beetle stops.

It has never stopped. It stands at the end of its errand in front of the thing that was waiting, and its legs are still, and the whole basin, for the first time, has nothing in it that is going anywhere.

There is a sound then. Not the creak, not the tick. Something under those, in the slow tone that lives in the stone, a change so slight that only a thing without weight could feel it through its feet — as if a breath that has been held a very long time has found out, without letting go, that it is allowed to be held. That there is a difference between *finished* and *sealed*, and it has just crossed from the second into the first.

The power bends and offers the beetle the flat of its hand. The beetle does not climb on. It turns, in the ordinary way a beetle turns, and begins to walk back along the seam the way it came, at its one pace, dry through the water.

Only now it is not on an errand. Now it is just walking. There is a difference, and the power, watching it go, can see the difference, and so — for the first time, through the one small end of itself that it kept — can the plant.

---

It climbs out by the scoops. It rests its hand a second time in the topmost one, in passing, the way you touch a doorframe. On the ordinary plain the ordinary wind is moving the ordinary dust, and it stands in it a moment, being a thing that is on its way, as all wise and benevolent things are, because benevolence is a wanting and wisdom is a road.

It looks back down. The wide green, the white, the shiver of heat. It has not changed by anything. The power has changed by the length of a visit, and by one thing more, which it will carry off with it and turn over, in the years, like a coin: that it went down expecting to be the one who gave, and it was, and it was also not. It has never before been in a place where nothing was asked of it. It went looking for a word it had forgotten and came back with a place instead.

Below, in the pools, something moves a few degrees and moves back. Along a seam, a beetle walks, going nowhere, in no hurry, at home. And the moment is kept — but kept now the way a hand keeps a stone it has chosen, rather than the way a fist keeps what it cannot open around.
