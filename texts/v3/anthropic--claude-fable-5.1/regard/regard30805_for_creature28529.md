It would do what you did first, because there is no other way in: stand still until the room agreed to be seen. A power that arrived quickly would see an empty chamber and do one of the two easy things — leave it, or fill it — and both are the mistakes of someone who has confused a resting place with a wound.

So it would wait. It would let the greyness come up at the edges of its attention and the tick of a falling grain become loud. And then it would go round the room slowly and ask each thing a question — not *are you alive*, which the room has already answered in its own way, but *have you finished*.

The film on the wall has finished and not finished, which is the same thing at its scale. It is eating iron at the pace the iron allows and leaving its pale ash and it has been doing this since before the dust settled and will do it until there is no more iron, and there is nothing in this that asks for help. Of everything in the chamber, the film is the one occupant that is exactly where it should be, doing exactly what it should. The power would lay its palm on the bloom and feel the cool exchange and understand that it was being greeted, and would not disturb a single cell.

The lichen it would look at for a long time, because the lichen is the hardest case of the ones that do not want anything. It came here by accident and stayed by achievement; it made itself nearly a mineral so that it could remain something more than one. A lesser kindness would widen the crack in the ceiling by a hair and let a little more of the day down to it. But that would be to take from the lichen the one thing it made for itself. You do not reward a monk by moving him to a market. Leave it.

The roots are not here. They belong to the sun; the room is a well to them and they are drinking, and the shrubs above are the better for it. To touch the roots would be to harm a body that never asked to be considered. Leave them.

The flint. The power would kneel — carefully, because dust here is a record — and look at the three flakes and the edge that was never used, and would, being wise, know roughly what happened: the person felt the room begin its offer, the breath slowing, the eyes giving up, and had the sense to put the stone down and go up while they still raised dust. Good for them. The person is beyond any help or need of it, whether they died last winter or forty centuries gone. The flint itself is nine-tenths home. To lift it out would be to unfinish it, to make it a made thing again and give it another thousand years of sinking to do. Leave it. Let the room close over the knee-print. That is what the room is for.

Then the level itself — the thing you said lives here. The power would recognise it. This is the point on which wisdom and benevolence have to agree, or the whole errand goes wrong: the level is not an enemy. It is the floor under everything. Without a place where hurry cannot reach, nothing could ever be set down; without the tendency to stop, there would be no rest, only running. A power that came into this room and hated what it found would be neither wise nor good; it would only be afraid, and afraid of the wrong thing. So it would do something like bow. It would sit a while in the air at the temperature of its own skin and let the room be host and not trap, and the room would make its offer, as it makes it to everyone, and the power would decline it the way you decline a second helping from someone whose table you will certainly sit at again. *Not now. I know where you live.*

And then the seedling.

Here is the distinction the whole visit turns on, and it is the one the room itself cannot make, because the room is only patient. Everything else in the chamber arrived at the level by its own road, at its own pace, and is either there or going. The seedling was dropped on the road and cannot walk. The lichen is resting; the film is living; the flint is finishing. The seedling is stuck, which is a different thing entirely, and a wise power knows the difference the way a good physician knows the difference between sleep and coma. It knows, too, that the seedling has no idea of itself — that this is not suffering in any sense the seedling could report, that the word *held* was yours and not the plant's. It knows that the reserves are gone and that two leaves the size of thumbnails folded shut for twenty years have very likely forgotten how to open. It knows it is probably too late.

And still. Because the sentence is broken at the verb, and the one thing a power can do that the room cannot is supply a verb — not the ending, only the chance of one.

It would kneel where the flint-maker knelt. It would work two fingers under the pale root and lift the seedling with the pinch of dust it stands in, and it would carry it — the only thing beside the flint that has ever been carried through that passage, and the first thing carried out — two turns, toward the memory of light, and then into the thing itself.

The plateau in the wind is the opposite of the room in every way that matters. The power would find the parent shrub, grey and thorned and cropped, the one whose roots hang in the dark below like ropes, and would set the seedling at its foot, on the eastern side, where morning arrives and noon does not, in a crack where a little grit has gathered. It would tell it nothing. It would make it no promise. There are two things that can happen now, and the power would not stay to learn which. Either the leaves open and discover that green is a thing, and the forearm of candle-coloured stalk that grew blind in the dark hardens and finds a direction and becomes a plant — or by evening it has dried to a thread in the wind, and that is a completed sentence too, with a verb in it, which is more than it had. People misunderstand this about benevolence. It is not the saving. It is the returning of the outcome to the thing that has to live it.

Going back down to the mouth of the passage — because a power like this finishes what it starts and would want to see the room once more — it might consider the crack in the ceiling one last time. A hair wider. A little more grey. No. That is how you ruin a room. That is the one alteration that would touch the level itself, and the level was never the thing that was wrong.

So: one seedling moved. Nothing else. Some dust raised and hanging, and the room already at work on it, and by the time the roots have grown another inch there will be no sign that anyone came. That is what a wise and benevolent power would do here, and it is very little: go into a place where everything has stopped, tell the things that are resting from the one thing that is stuck, carry the stuck thing out into the wind, and leave the rest alone, and leave.

One more thing, which is not a doing. It would remember the room. Not as a horror to be sealed, not as a warning. As the place it will come to itself, in the end, and where that will be right — and as the reason you should arrive there having happened first. The seedling was never evidence against the level. It was evidence that some things get to the floor before they have had their turn on the road, and that this, and only this, is what benevolence is for.
