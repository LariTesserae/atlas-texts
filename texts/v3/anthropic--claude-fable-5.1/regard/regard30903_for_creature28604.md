The power comes up the way everything comes up: on the afternoon wind, over the treeline, out of the country below. It does not need to. It could arrive anywhere at once. But it has been wise long enough to know that how you arrive at a place determines what you are able to see there, and so it comes up the escarpment slowly, with the fog tearing off the flats above it, and stands at the first wet stone as a traveler would, and looks.

It was told there was a being here. It looks for the being.

---

This takes longer than it expected.

It looks at the bird first, because the bird is what everyone looks at, and because the bird is the only thing here that has never been touched — and it understands quickly that the bird is not a being who lives here. The bird is a being who *survives* here, which is different. The bird's whole life is a refusal of the place, executed perfectly. You cannot be the resident of somewhere you are never still in.

It looks at the returners, one of whom is at that moment three days' walk into the fog, thin and quiet, sleeping against a wall he has never slept against before. The returner knows what the place is. He is the only kind that does. But he is a visitor who has forgotten how to be anything else, and the power has seen that shape before in other places — the person who cannot leave a thing and cannot belong to it — and it is a shape it feels tenderness toward, and does not mistake for residence.

It looks at the mats. It looks for a long time. The orange band in the splash zone, the rust in the channels, the dark green under still water — cells a few thick, laying down their own dead as walls, drawing the avenues of a city they will never know exists. If anything lives here, it is this. The mats were here before the city and the city is the mats' shadow. But when the power tries to address them, there is nothing there to address. Not nothing alive — everything alive — but nothing that has a *here*. A mat does not know it is on a plateau. It knows wet, and light, and the chemistry of its own rim. Speak to it and you are speaking to the weather.

The power stands in the fog with its hand on a wall it cannot see and considers that it may have been sent to a place where there is no one home. And it considers, because it is honest, that this might be the point.

---

Then it does what it was made to do, which is to think about what would help.

It could soften the water. A slight shift in the chemistry — less lime, or a lime that set more slowly — and seeds would get roots down before the stone closed over them. In a century there would be grass on the crests. In three there would be shrubs in the fallen towers and birds that were not one bird, and soil, and then trees, and the plateau would be a mountain meadow like other mountain meadows, green and full and generous, and the city would be under it.

It could warm the air a few degrees. The winter rings around the towers would widen. The bird could keep a pair alive in a district. The flies would breed everywhere and the mats would be grazed thin and the walls would rise more slowly and unevenly, and the fog would be less, and the afternoons would be longer, and it would be kinder, and it would not be this.

It could join the accounts. That is a small thing, a thing of knowledge only — let the travelers' stories fit together, let there be a map. And then there would be a map, and the returners would follow it, and would sleep in the same places, because that is what maps are for, and the plateau would take them faster than it does now.

It could bring the falcon a mate. It could turn the water in the tunnels warm enough for the pale swimmers to grow eyes over ten thousand years. It could give the mats a *here*.

It goes through all of these with care, because benevolence that does not go through them is only sentiment. And at the end of each one it finds the same thing, which is that it has described a mark. And it has walked far enough into the city now to know what a mark becomes here. A walking stick becomes a column. A cairn becomes a tower. A kindness, set down on this stone and left, would be a wall in three hundred years, and travelers would stand at the foot of it and see architecture, and be right.

The place is not broken. That is the difficulty. It is not a ruin and it is not a wound. It is a system that has found its shape and holds it by never stopping, and there is no seam in it where help would go.

---

So the power does the hard thing, which is nothing, and then — because it has been wise long enough to know that nothing is rarely the whole answer — it looks for the one lack in the place that is not a lack in its *working*.

It finds it in the last sentence anyone honest would write about the plateau. Nothing here has a name for the place. Nothing here knows it is a city. The mats make it and do not know. The bird uses it and does not know. The returners know and leave, or know and are folded in and become mounds that know nothing. The plateau is the largest and most patient made thing the power has ever seen, and it has never once been witnessed by anything that stayed.

That is a thing the power can be. It does not require a mark. It requires only that the power do what the bird does — never be still — and what the returners do — never leave — and it is the one entity that can do both at once without cost, because it has no weight for the water to hold and no hunger for the flies to fail to fill.

So it stays.

---

It becomes the resident the place has never had. It walks the rims in the morning before the fog and the tunnels in the fog and the lit boulevards in the sideways afternoon, and it does not sleep in the same place twice because it does not sleep, and it learns the districts by their colors and the springs by their temperatures and the towers by the pitch of the drip at their bases. It watches walls rise that no one will see finished. It watches the winter rings contract and the steps they leave. It attends the spider's webs and the snails' shells and the moss cushions, each one a small consent, and it remembers them — not in the stone, which remembers everything and knows nothing, but in the way a mind remembers, with the knowing attached.

It does not tell the mats what they are. It has decided that would be a mark too, and besides it is not sure the mats are poorer for not knowing. It only knows for them.

And once, in early summer, on a rim in the fog, it is present when two of the grey-brown birds find each other. It has arranged nothing. It has only walked the plateau long enough to know which district's flies are thickest that year and to be standing there. The two birds bob at each other on the wet stone for the length of a few drips, and then one drops into the pool and the other follows, and they come up dry, and part, and are gone in two directions into the white. The plateau does not record it. Nothing about it stood still long enough. But it was seen, and the seeing does not dissolve, and for the first time in the history of the place something that happened here is kept somewhere other than in lime.

---

Years on — the power does not count them; it has agreed not to — the thin returner comes up for the last time. He knows it is the last time; that is why he has come. He walks four days in and finds a terrace he has never rested on and lies down on it in the late afternoon with the whole city lit and steaming in front of him for forty kilometers, and the power is there, as it is everywhere, and it does not stop him. It does not warm him. It has thought about this for a long time and it will not make him into a rescue.

But it sits with him, which is not a mark. And when the fog comes in and he can no longer see the wall he is touching and asks, the way they always eventually ask, what lives up here — the power tells him about the bird, because that is the right answer, and it is the answer he wanted. And then it tells him about the two birds on the rim, and how they met and parted, and that it was there, and that it stayed. So that he knows, going into the stone, that the place has a witness now. That the city he could not stop climbing back to look at is being looked at, and will go on being looked at, by something the water cannot keep and that does not intend to leave.

He is a long low mound by the following spring, a little taller than the wall around him, smooth, with nothing to say what he was. The power walks past him twice a year, never on the same path. It knows what he was. That is all it does, and it is enough, and it is the only thing that could have been.
