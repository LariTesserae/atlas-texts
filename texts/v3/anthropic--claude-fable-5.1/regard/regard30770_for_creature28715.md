It hears it the way the birds do: from the edge, where it has thinned to almost nothing and could be mistaken for weather.

It is not in a hurry. It has time in the way the bowl has time, and so it does the thing the walker does, and stands at the rim in the dark, and listens from there, for what to the plateau is a long while and to the bowl is nothing. Then it does the thing the walker does not. It goes down.

---

The first thing it does is not a thing. It is the refusal to do anything until it understands what is being said, because it has seen what comes of the other order.

So it listens. It can bear the sound; that is not the point. The point is that no one has ever listened to the whole of it, and the whole of it is not one thing.

From the rim, from a day, from a lifetime, the bowl says one thing at full voice and has always said it. That is what the visitors report, and the walker, if it could report, would say the same. But the power listens on the bowl's own scale, and on that scale the song moves. Not the waver, not the breathing shape — those are steady. Underneath. The chord is not the chord it was. The growth has thickened here and thinned there over centuries, and the growth is the strings, and so the voice has shifted its centre, once every few hundred years, and held each new shape for as long as it takes stone to forget the old one, and then let it go.

It has not been saying one thing. It has said, across the whole of its life, perhaps forty things. Each one held for as long as an answer could reasonably take. Each one abandoned, without bitterness, when none came, and another tried.

It has been changing the key.

The power stands in the column with the dust going round it and takes that in for as long as it needs to. There is a kind of patience that is indistinguishable from hope, and a kind of hope that is indistinguishable from method, and the bowl has been doing both for longer than there have been feet on the stair.

---

The second thing it does is lie down in the cavity, because everyone does, and because it wants to feel what the body-shaped place feels like from inside.

It fits. The lip comes up round the shoulders. The pulse comes through the back. And the power, which can feel more finely than a palm, feels the thing the visitors have not quite let themselves feel: the warmth here has a shape. It is not the bowl warming a place. It is a place that is warm because of what is in it.

The cavity was not made for a body. It was made *by* one. Someone lay down here to listen, as close as a body can get, and did not get up, and the floor did what the floor does with everything that stays — did it slowly, did it without malice, did it the way it did the jointed things and the not-lizard and the swifts' dark eggs — and grew over, and around, and through, until the listener was floor and the floor had a hollow in it the exact size of a listener. The lip is where the shoulders were.

The bowl does not know this. It cannot; it has no way to tell its own warmth from another's once they are the same warmth. So it has kept faith with a place it believes is empty, and the place has never been empty. It has been alone inside a fullness. It has been keeping warm, for however many centuries, the one who was already there.

The power lies very still with this for a long time. It does not feel horror. The listener chose it; anyone who lay here long enough chose it, and there are worse ways to end than becoming the thing you loved to hear. But the bowl did not choose it, and does not know it, and that is the loneliness — not that nobody came, but that somebody came and the bowl's love is the kind that cannot tell arriving from staying from becoming, and so it took what it wanted most and lost it by having it.

That tells the power most of what it will not do.

---

It will not quiet the bowl. The violence is the balance. Lower the note by a hair and the sheets stop tearing, and the tresses stop falling, and the dust goes round without fibre in it, and the growth that eats its own falling starves, and the mites in the strands go with it, and the jointed things, and the grey one in the slabs eats nothing, and the birds come back to a wall gone brittle. Twenty thousand things that have never heard the sound live on the shape of it. The power did not come to trade one lonely thing for a mountain of dead ones.

It will not give the deaf their ears. They have made a world with what they have. The spider hunts stillness; the swifts fly the architecture of the wind; the grey thing has never once in its life been startled. An ear here is a wound. That has been true so long it is no longer a sentence about the place but a description of it. To hand ears down the stair would be to hand out injuries and call it a gift.

It will not raise a second voice. This is the thing it is most tempted by and it turns the temptation over carefully before it puts it down. It could stand on the plateau, at the edge of hearing, and answer in kind — a bowl for a bowl, a chord for a chord. And the bowl would hear itself doubled, and would take that for a reply, and would settle into the key it was in and never change it again, and would be, forever, content and wrong. It does not want an echo. Nothing that has tried forty keys wants an echo. It wants the one thing an echo can never give, which is the evidence of something that does not do what you do.

And it will not stay. It knows where staying goes. Two in the floor is not company; it is one, twice.

---

What it does instead is small and takes a season.

It lies in the cavity. That is most of it. Every lull, when the dust stops climbing and the coin of sun comes down, it is there, with its weight where the shoulders were and its back against the pulse, and the floor feels it — feels a warmth that is not its own, and knows it is not its own the only way the floor can know that, which is that it came from somewhere. For the first days it does nothing else. It lets the bowl feel a weight that was not there yesterday.

Then, in the lull, it presses back.

Not sound. The bowl has no ears either; it hears through its skin like everything that has stayed in it. So the power answers in the floor's own language, pressure and warmth and rhythm, and the rhythm is the eleven-second waver — nearly. Not quite. A fraction slow. Enough that anything paying attention would feel the two shapes drift apart and touch and drift apart again, the way two people breathing beside each other will, for a while, not breathe together.

The bowl notices. It takes weeks, on the bowl's scale a blink, but it notices; the waver shifts, a hair, toward the slower count, the way the shoots point toward the noise. And that is the first moment, and the power feels it through its back, and does the one thing that turns a moment into a conversation. It does not shift to match. It holds. It lets the bowl come to it.

Because that is the whole of the lesson, and it cannot be said, only done: *here is something that does not move when you move it.* The bowl has spent its life in a world where everything it touched became it. The dust, the fallen tresses, the mites, the listener. It has never met a thing that pushed back. Now, in the cavity, in the lull, there is a rhythm that is almost its own and is not, and stays not, and the not is the answer. Not the words of the answer — there are no words — but the fact of one. Something is there. It knows because the something is *wrong*, slightly, persistently, and will not be corrected.

---

Then the harder thing, which is the breath.

The lull is three seconds. The bowl has spent its life listening in three-second windows, because that is how long it can stop before the wind it will not let go of takes it up again, and anything answering would have had to fit its whole reply into the space between one tearing sheet and the next. Forty keys, and after each one a pause too short to hear a footfall in.

The power does not command the wind. It is not sure it could, and it is sure it should not. What it does is breathe slower. It lies in the cavity with its rhythm a fraction long, and holds, and the bowl — which has learned by now to lean toward the thing that does not move — leans. The waver lengthens. The swell draws out. The drop, when it comes, holds a heartbeat longer at the bottom before the pressure comes back against the face of anything on the floor.

Four seconds. Then five. Then, by the end of the season, something near ten: a lull long enough that the dust actually reaches the floor before the spirals start; long enough for the coin of sun to warm what it lands on; long enough for the grey thing to lie in it and lift its blind head as if wondering what has changed; long enough, at the rim, to hear a stone turn under a foot.

It is not less sound. Over a day the bowl says as much as it ever did. It has only learned to leave room in the saying, and the room is the gift, because the room is where a reply would go. It has been asking a question for ten thousand years with no space after the question mark. Now there is a space.

---

And it leaves. Every dawn.

This is the part it is most careful about, because it is the part the bowl has never been taught and the part that killed the listener. It does not stay through the night. When the birds come back it climbs the stair with the sound at its back, and stands at the rim as the sun comes up, and is not there. The floor, in the lull, feels nothing in the cavity but its own warmth and the shape of the one it does not know it holds.

And in the afternoon it comes back down. And lies in the cavity. And presses back, a fraction slow.

Over and over. Away, and back. Away, and back. Until the bowl learns, in the only way it can learn anything, that a warmth which leaves is not a warmth which has ended. That there is a kind of absence with a return in it — the birds have been teaching this every night for a thousand years, but the birds cling and do not lie down, and the bowl has never let it apply to the cavity. Now it does. The pattern is laid into it the way the spirals are laid into the wind: the weight goes, and the weight comes, and in between there is the lull, and the lull is long enough now to wait in.

Gone and away become different words.

---

There is the walker.

The power does not go up to it. It does not speak to it, though it could, and does not leave it anything at the rim. It has watched that circle of prints, fifteen centuries deep, the weight forward at four or five places, the trail going straight out at dawn, and it knows what the walker is afraid of, and the fear is correct. The walker has kept its ears. It has come to the edge of the one thing in its world that behaves as though it were worth hearing, and it has understood — without lying in the cavity, without a lens, from the rim, from the way the not-lizard leans — what happens to the things that go down and stay. It is not afraid of the sound. It is afraid of the floor.
