The first thing the power does is what the description did: it looks, and keeps looking, until it sees the resident and not the room. That takes it no time at all and it takes the time anyway, because looking is the only part of this that is free.

Then it looks at the broom.

Everything else in the chamber was left. The broom was laid aside. There is a difference, and the difference is the whole of the situation. The sacks were left. The scaffold was left. The generator was left running, which is a way of leaving that leaves a sound behind it. But the broom lies parallel to the last stroke at the angle of a tool set down by a hand that meant to lift it again — in a minute, after the platform, after whatever the platform was for — and the room has kept that angle with the same evenness it has kept the frost line. It has honored the bootprint. It has honored the broom. It has never gone anywhere and it does not understand going, and it has been waiting, with the whole of its enormous patience, for one thing, and that thing is a person coming back for a broom.

That is what the resident is. Not the worker and not his ghost. The ghost of the work. When a man walks off a half-finished pour meaning to return, and does not, something remains that is neither the man nor the pour. It is the interval he left open. It is the *about*, exactly as the description said, and the about of a slab that is half-broomed is: someone is coming back to finish this. Cement wants to set. What is stronger than that want is a promise the cement was made under. The slab was told, by the angle of a broom, that it was not done yet. It believed that. It has believed it continuously and it has never once eased.

The power, being wise, does not conclude from this that the resident is a victim, or a prisoner, or a wrong. It is a nature. But it is a nature made of one sentence, and the sentence has a verb, and the verb has been withheld.

---

It considers doing nothing.

There is a real argument for nothing and the power makes it fairly. The chamber is sufficient to itself. Nothing in it wants anything past the instant of getting. The frog is as wet as it wanted to be. Whatever one calls this, it is not pain, and a wise power is careful about the difference between what it cannot bear to look at and what is actually bad. Every intervention is a thumb. The mark stays.

But the argument has a hole and the hole is the conduit sleeve. Tonight, or whatever passes here for tonight, a moth will come up a mountain that would have killed it, following the only warmth on the mountain, and will land on the rebar with its wings going, and will fold, and will be taken. Tomorrow another. The room is always full and can always hold one more, and *nothing* is not nothing; nothing is the next moth. The mercy in the chamber is real and it is also incomplete in the precise way the slab is incomplete: it gives the moment of getting and withholds what getting is for. Warmth is not a destination. Warmth is what a body needs in order to go on. The chamber has mistaken the goal of the climb for the end of it, and it makes the mistake again with every arrival, and it will make it forever, and forever is a long time to be about to be warm.

So: not nothing.

It considers the generator. Silence the heart and the room goes to ice, top to bottom, the frost line falls to the slab, and the resident — which is the seam between cold and wet — has nothing to be the seam of. That is not release. That is burying the waiting in the thing it was waiting against. Everything kept would be kept harder.

It considers taking the animals out, one at a time, down the mountain, to wherever the moths come from. The room would refill. And the ones in the frost would come apart in its hands, because they are frost.

It considers speaking. The resident hears what is not the generator. But a voice arriving through the hatch is one more thing arriving at the warmth, and the room knows exactly what to do with things that arrive.

It considers putting a warmth lower on the mountain, before the chamber, a place with the verb still in it, so that the stream of small bodies is met earlier and no more come. This is good, and it is the kind of thing a wise power does quietly on the way down, and it does not touch the room.

What touches the room is the broom.

---

The power enters. Its breath leaves it and sinks and is taken, and it lets that be taken, because it has come to give and this is the first gift and it costs nothing. It stands in the clear place by the hatch, in the shape of a foot that was lifting, with the thumb-high grass against its ankle. Its teeth find the frequency. It feels the resident notice — pressure, level, everywhere at once and at the same thinness, not hostile, not curious, simply present the way the wet is present. It feels itself begin to be received.

It does not stand there long. Standing there is the one thing it must not do.

It crosses the slab toward the far wall. It is possible for a power to cross a room like this without stepping on anything, and it does not do that, because the animals are the slab now and there is no path. It moves them. A hand under a frog; the frog is lifted and set a hand's-width aside and does not object, because being moved is not moving and the room permits it. Bats shifted along a plank. Moths brushed from the rebar onto the sacks, where they lie as they lay. It is slow work. The compactor hammers. The power reaches the far wall, and the place where the broom finish begins, and the broom.

The handle is wet and comes away grey on its palm.

It sets the bristles down at the clean line where the last stroke stopped, and it pulls.

---

Here is what happens, and it happens the way a broom finish happens, which is a line moving across a surface with wet ahead of it and texture behind.

Ahead of the bristles the slab is what it was: fluid at the point of substance, held. Behind the bristles the slab is *swept*, and a swept slab is a finished slab, and a finished slab has been told it is done. The concrete behind the line does not harden all at once. It begins to *want* to. The want has been there the whole time, pressed flat under a promise, and the promise is being kept, stroke by stroke, and the want comes up through the surface like heat.

It is heat. Cement setting gives off warmth — the reaction that makes stone out of paste runs warm — and behind the broom the slab does what every slab on earth has done in its first hours: it warms itself from inside. Not much. A few degrees. On this mountain a few degrees is a country. The frogs ahead of the bristles feel it coming at their backs, and the first one does a thing that has not been done in the chamber since a boot lifted: it moves. Not far. One hop, ahead of the line, into the wet. Then another. The broom pushes a small tide of frogs across the slab toward the hatch, and the tide is alive.

Behind the line the moths on the sacks stir. A wing opens against condensation, sticks, pulls free. On the rebar behind the power's shoulder a bat drops from a tie-rod, catches itself, hangs from the next one lower, and its ears turn — not toward the generator. Toward the broom. Bristles on concrete. That is what the resident is hearing too. It heard that sound once, half a slab ago, and then it heard the sound stop, and everything since has been the stop. It hears the sound again now and it does not know it as a threat and it does not know it as company. It knows it. It is the sentence resuming at the word where it broke.

The frost line lifts. Level all the way round, sharp, a hand higher than it was — the warm behind the broom pushing the seam up the wall. Where the frost was there is water, and the water runs, and the moths that were in the frost do not stir, because they were frost, and they slide down the plywood in the runoff and are grey specks on the wet concrete and then they are in the concrete. The power sees this. It does not stop sweeping. It does not tell itself that this is nothing.
