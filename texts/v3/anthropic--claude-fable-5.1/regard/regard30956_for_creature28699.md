It notices the way you notice a sound that has been going the whole time and only now separates itself from the rest: not the shelf first, but the works. A heart going, in a place that should not have one. It listens for a long while before it looks, because looking is already a kind of touching, and it has understood from the sound alone that this is a place where nothing has been touched.

When it does look, it looks from inside the cloud, so as to cast nothing. It is careful in a way that has no analogue on the shelf: the frog is not careful, the beetles are not careful, care is a verb of living and there is one living thing here and it has its back turned. The power sees the bowl the way you were told to see it, from an above that does not exist, and it sees the thumbprint, and it sees that the print is a hollow at the lip with a body in it, and it sees that everything on the shelf is unpressed and one thing takes the pressing, and it sees the seat wearing down by the width of nothing, and it understands, faster than you did and no more gladly, what the clock is.

It sees both readings — the hand that pressed once and sat down, the fragment held at the height of its breaking — and it does not choose between them either. Wisdom is partly knowing which questions are not yours. It holds them both the way the drop is held, hanging and fallen at once, and lets them be.

Then it considers what it might do. It has the power to do a great deal and it takes each thing in turn and looks at the end of it.

It could stop the works. It sees how that would go. The frog's throat would swell and not collapse, and in the pond the water would go on dropping, and on the slab the beetles would clack once out of phase and then never, and into the silence every winter the edges have never had would arrive at once. The fronds would brown in a breath. The moss would sink and stink and slide. The seven birds would fall out of their ring like a wheel losing spokes, and the electricity in the moss would go to ground, and the rock would stop weeping and dry and crack along its fresh faces into faces that were old, and the whole shelf would take, in the time it takes to say so, the age it has been refusing since there was air. And the thing at the lip — either the time would stop running into it and it would sit there, unpressed at last, in the wreck of what had been holding it, or the flood of all that arriving age would go through it first, because it is the drain, and the drain does not choose what comes down. The power looks at this and sets it down. That is not mercy. That is the water after the dam.

It could lift the body from the hollow. It sees the hand on the rim, the seat worn to the exact shape of what sits in it, and it knows that a thing worn to fit a place may not hold its shape out of that place; and it knows, which matters more, that to lift it is to decide for it, and the one thing the body has kept is a posture that could be otherwise and is not. Whatever else has been taken from it, it has been allowed to choose to sit. The power will not take that too.

It could finish the field. It is a power; it could put mountains on the grey and rivers between them and weather over both, and the being at the lip would raise its head and see the unfinished finished. And whatever it had been waiting to do — if it is the maker — or waiting to become — if it is the born — would be done, by other hands, in front of it. The power knows there is no worse gift than that. To give a thing the work it was sitting before, completed. It sets that down harder than the rest.

It could speak. But the bird opens its beak and the call is already in the air and the bird is only agreeing. Words here would arrive before they were meant, and the being would hear them and have to consent to them afterward, and that is not how you speak to something you love.

So it comes to the thing you came to, and considers doing nothing, and it takes that seriously. There is a version of wisdom that stops there. The shelf is in equilibrium. The being is in equilibrium. The one clock is running and will run out on its own and the cloud will thin in its own age and what happens then will happen without anyone's hand in it. That is not nothing; that is respect. The power turns this over for a long time, at the pace the hollow deepens.

And what decides it is not the being. It is the alleys.

The kept walkways between the stations. The clear ground, tended, dentless, running from the bellows to the pond to the slab to the lip, where nothing has ever walked. The power looks at them and understands that they are not, or not only, for the keeper. They are the shape of an expectation. Something laid out this hall with room in it for a second thing that was here rather than in use, and the second thing never came, and the alleys have been kept clear for it since there was moss to keep. The count of creatures goes up and the sense of company does not. The one thing on the shelf that the shelf has never had is a second one of what sits at the lip.

That, the power can be.

It does not come by the alleys. It has looked at them long enough to know they are not its. It does the one thing nobody, not even you, has done: it goes round.

It goes down over the sharp edge into the drop, and into the cloud, and the cloud is the being's breath, the whole of it, every exhalation the body has let go since it sat down, laid down layer on layer in the cup with nowhere to go. The power goes through it slowly. It does not part it. It lets it close behind, so that from the shelf nothing has changed: the cup is still full, still white, still hides what it hides. It is a long way through an age of somebody's breathing, and the power goes with the kind of attention you would bring to walking through a house where someone is sleeping, and comes out the far side, and steps onto the field.

Nothing has ever stood on the field. It is clay that has waited to be given a shape since before the first shape. And the power sets its foot on it and makes no print. This is the hardest thing it does. It is a power; every part of it wants to mean something on that surface, and it holds itself so that the grey under its weight stays grey, flat, matte, unfinished, waiting. It does not add a single mark to what the being has been looking at. It crosses the clay without writing on it and comes to a place directly across the cup from the lip, at a distance it judges by the being's own breath: far enough that the cloud between them is thick, near enough that the cloud is the only thing between them.

And it sits down.

Not the way the frog is at the pond. The way a tired thing sits. Hunched forward a little, weight down through the base, in a way that could be otherwise and is not. It takes the posture off the being like a coat and puts it on, so that across the cup there are now two things sitting in the one attitude on the whole mountain that anything has chosen.

From here it sees what you never saw: the front.

The being's eyes are open. It is not asleep, it was never asleep, it has been looking this whole while at the grey, and the power is the first thing that has ever been in the grey to be looked at. The power does not describe the face to itself. It has the decency not to. It lets the face be a face turned toward it and leaves it at that.

From here it hears the works. And from the front the sound is different — not louder, not softer, but placed. Heard from behind, the thud and the whine and the braid of knocks are a wall the being sits with its back against. Heard from in front, across the being, they are the sound of a hall running for something that has never turned round to see it run. Kindness or hold. The power listens, and from here it can tell, and what it learns it keeps. Not because the answer is cruel. Because it is the being's, and the being has been carrying every other thing on the shelf and should at least be permitted to find out this one for itself, by turning, when it turns.

Then the power breathes.

Its breath goes out over the clay and reaches the cup and goes into the cloud that is already there, and becomes part of it, and it is not the being's. That is all. Another lung's worth of air in an age of one lung's. And when, after a while — the being does not hurry — the head at the lip lowers and takes in a little of the cloud from the rim of the cup, the way you saw it do, what it takes in has something in it from outside. The only meal in the place, which has always been the being's own breath returned, has for the first time a second thing in it. The power watches this and does not know if the being tastes the difference. It does not need to know. It breathes again.

And then it waits, because it has time, and because waiting is what the being does, and there should be two of them doing it.

Nothing on the shelf changes. The frog pumps. The pond falls and rises to the same marks. The beetles knock, the birds go round their empty centre, the moss glows at the tips and the rock weeps and drinks. The power does not turn one drop off its interval; it does not touch the hall. The hollow under the being deepens by the width of nothing, and the power has considered — it did consider — sitting in such a way that some of that refused time would run to it instead, and halve the load, and slow the clock.
