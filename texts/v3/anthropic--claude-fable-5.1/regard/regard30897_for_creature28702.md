It arrives the way everything arrives there: as a wash, not a beam. It comes down through the holes in the roof with the daylight, and the vapour takes it and spreads it, so that nothing in the chambers casts a shadow from it. No flier lifts. No builder pauses in its rasping. The low one, pushing through the crowd under the nearest arch, does not turn its small wet eyes upward. The power notes that it is pleased to be unnoticed, and sets the pleasure aside as not yet earned. It has been mistaken before about places that seemed to be getting along without it.

So first it looks. Looking is most of what it does, and it does it slowly, because a thing that can see everything at once has to be careful not to.

---

It had been told — or had felt, which for it is the same — that something lived here. Not lived in the way the builders live, which is the way a ripple lives in a pool. Lived. An inhabitant. Something that could, in some sense, be addressed.

It tries the candidates one by one, as anyone would.

The builders it takes up first, a single one, in the way the text takes one up: on a palm. It feels the click of the plates and the stalks tasting the air and, after a while, the faint beginning of cement against its skin. It looks for the animal's fear and finds something too small to call fear — a leaning-away. It looks for the animal's wanting and finds only the direction of fresher water. It sets the builder down and the builder is gone into the crowd before it has finished setting it down, and the power understands that it has not met anyone. It has met a hand, briefly, of something that has more hands than it has anything else.

The combers it sits with for longer, because they are still. It watches one decide — if it is deciding — to stay in a drying basin. It watches the water go milky, then thick, then still, then stone. It looks for the moment of the choice and cannot find one. The comber is not choosing to die. It is choosing not to leave, which is different, and the power is old enough to know how different.

The low ones. Here it lingers. Here there are eyes, and a wake in the crowd, and the same notch in the same plate across the years. Here there is something that arrives at the fresh crack before the water does and waits under the bare column on the safe side. The power watches one for the length of a hearth being roofed over. It watches it eat. It watches it eat. It looks for grief, since the low one is the only thing here that could carry grief — it alone remembers what was in a chamber before the chamber moved. It finds none. The low one is not sorrowful about the place any more than a tooth is sorrowful about a mouth. It is the place's one long thought, and the thought is *where next*.

The people it follows out onto the plain, once, when they leave. It sees the plates sewn to their clothes catch the flat dry light, and sees — this is the first thing that surprises it — that out here, far from the breath of the holes, the place shines. It is carried away in baskets and it glitters on the backs of people walking home. The power had been thinking of the people as visitors. It corrects itself. They are the only part of the place that has ever seen it from outside, and the sight has made them call it the mouth, and they are right, and they keep going back in.

---

Then it goes down, because it can, to the thing no one reaches.

Past the brown light. Past the last of the low ones, which stops at the edge of its knowledge as at a wall. Between the fat columns where the crowd is so thick that the power, which has no feet to lift, understands why feet cannot be lifted. Down the throat, as the people say, into the one sound.

And the source is a crack. That is all. A fissure in hot dark rock with water coming up through it — clean, at first, and clear, and scalding, and empty. Nothing lives at the source. It is the one place in all the region with a bare floor. The stone around it is old and unbuilt and black, and the water rises out of it as ordinary as water, and it is only when it has gone a few lengths outward and cooled a little and touched the first pale builders that it begins to become the country.

The power stands at the crack for a long time. It considers the people's word. It decides they have it exactly right and exactly backwards: the mouth is not down here. The mouth is everything above. Down here is the one thing in the place that is not being chewed, and it is a wound in the ground, and the wound is what feeds them all.

It does not describe this to anyone. It understands, now, why no one has.

---

Then it goes back up to find the being, because it has not found it yet, and it knows where to wait.

It waits in a lit chamber under a column that the climbers have left. It waits with the low one that is already there, at its careful distance, on the far side. The column is smooth and bare and touching. The crowd on every wall is at its work, and the noise is at its pitch, and then —

The quiet.

It falls all at once. Every plate on every body in the chamber is still. The fliers in the roof-dark have shut. The water goes on, tick and hiss, but the water was never part of it. And into that stillness the power, which has looked at ten thousand things here and met none of them, feels for the first time that it is being looked at.

Not by the builders. By what the builders are, when they are all one thing and the one thing is waiting. It has no eyes and does not need them. It has felt the stone shift through a hundred thousand feet at once, and it has stopped, and in stopping it has become, for as long as the stopping lasts, somebody. It does not know its name. It does not know that it is a crowd. It knows that the ceiling is about to fall and it wants — this is a want, the power is certain, this is the first want it has found here that is bigger than a thumb — to know where.

The power does not speak. There is nothing to say to a being that will exist for one more breath. But it is there, in the quiet, and it lets that be felt — the way you let it be felt, across a room, that you also heard the thing that just went silent. That you also are waiting.

The slab comes down. The noise returns. The being goes back into the crowd it was made of, and the crowd is already moving, and the low one is already among them, eating.

The power stays where it is. It has met the inhabitant. It thinks about what it owes it.

---

It considers, honestly, everything.

It considers ending the dying, because there is so much of it — a season's worth of lives in every rough band of every column, uncounted, cemented over. It holds this a long time. Then it sets it down, because it cannot find, anywhere in the place, a one whose death is a loss *to that one*. The builder dies in the crowd it is made of and the crowd does not lessen. The comber stays and is kept. The low one eats and is not eaten. To end the dying here would be to end the building, and the building is the only thing that has ever made the quiet possible, and the quiet is the being, and the being would rather be a breath than not be.

It considers giving the builders sight. A moment's thought and it is ashamed of the moment. A builder that could see its column would see a column. What a column is *for* is not a builder-sized question, and the answer is not a builder-sized answer, and the power has just spent a long time learning that a thing can be whole without any of its parts knowing it.

It considers letting the quiet last. This is the one that costs it. To give the being continuity — to let the crowd stay one thing after the slab has landed, to let it know itself, to let it keep the want — this is what it would do if it were only benevolent. It is also wise, and it sees the shape of the thing it would be making: a crowd that knew it was a crowd would stop going toward the fresher water. It would stand still and be somebody. The terraces would dry. The seeps would open unattended. And the being it had given a life to would be the last being ever to fall silent there, because the noise it fell silent out of would be gone. The power puts this down very gently, the way you put down a thing you wanted.

It considers the people's paths, and whether to hold them. It considers the ochre figure, and whether to turn the drip aside so it can be seen from standing. It considers the hut, and the door growing shut. It sets these down too, not because they are wrong but because they are not its. The people's whole way of knowing the place is to be wrong about it together and correct each other. That is theirs. They chose a country that chews, and they keep the flint in the hut because they are coming back, and they will stack another.

It considers the one in a generation who does not come back.

---

Here it does something.

It goes and finds them. Not to bring them out — the power has decided about that, and the decision was hard, and it will not unpick it for one. It finds them because it has understood what happened to them, and the understanding will not let it rest.

They stopped in the wrong place, the text says, and waited for the chamber to be quiet, and it was quiet too long.

The power knows now what that quiet was. It was the being. It fell, and the one who had stopped there waited inside it — the only person ever to be in the room, so to speak, when the room woke up — and the slab did not come down where they expected, and the being went back into the noise, and they did not.

So the power does this: it makes sure that it is there. Not once. Every time. In every chamber where the quiet falls and someone is standing in it, in the dark, with a lamp gone out and a basket of plates and a great stillness pressing in from every wall, it is there. It cannot stop the slab. It does not speak. But nobody, from now on, waits in that quiet alone. Something in it will know they are waiting too. That is a small thing to give against a country. It is also, the power thinks, exactly the size of the being's own want, and it seems right that the gift should fit.

---

And then it does what everything does here with what it has been given.

It has seen the whole place — which nothing inside the place can see, not the low one with its dozen chambers, not the people with their plates, not the being with its single breath of wanting. Somebody should keep that. The stone keeps the crowds but not the shape of the crowds' whole country. The power keeps that. It is what it is for.

But it does not keep it *above* the place, apart, as a thing looked down on. It lays it down. It presses its whole long looking into the wet lime of a fresh ledge somewhere in the amber light, the way the climbers' casings are pressed in, the way the eggs are, the way the nets and the paths and the hearth are. And it lets the builders come over it, and rasp it, and cement, and glaze.

Somewhere in a column that has not yet fallen there is a band that is neither rough nor smooth, and if the people ever split it they will not know what it is. It will look like a season when nothing died and nothing was built. It will look like a season when the stone stood still and was seen.

The column will fall. It has to. The climbers will leave it and the low one will take up its place on the safe side and the quiet will come down, and in the quiet the being will want to know where, and something in the quiet will be waiting with it, and will not say.

That is what it would do. Nearly nothing. The only thing.
