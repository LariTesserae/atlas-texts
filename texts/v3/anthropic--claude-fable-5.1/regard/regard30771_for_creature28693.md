**What Was Done**

It should be said first that the power did not come looking for the room. Nothing comes looking for this room. It came the way warmth comes to the salamanders — by noticing a difference where there should not have been one. Somewhere in the great cold ledger of the mountain there was a point that read a little higher than its neighbors, a small persistent error at the summit, in a place with no sun and no fire, and the power, which is wise, has learned that small persistent errors are where the interesting things are kept.

So it arrived. There is no need to say how. It was on the summit, in the thin air, with the stars very close, and beneath it was the lid of slabs, and beneath that the hand's width of stale air, and beneath that everything.

And then it did what wise things do first, which is nothing.

---

It read the room. It took a long time about this, because the room is written slowly and cannot be read fast. It counted the rings on the mat, dead grey band above dead grey band, and learned how long the lid had been shut. It counted the amphipods and let the number go, understanding that the number was weather. It felt the block in the wall settle by the width of a hair and felt every small body stop, and start again, and understood that this was the room's whole history of alarm. It found the twelve. It counted them twice, because twelve is a number that invites a mistake, and there were twelve.

It found the stone last, though it had known where the stone was from the start. It saved the stone.

---

Here are the things a benevolent power might do for a drowned room, and here is why this one set each of them down.

It might lift the lid. A single slab tilted back and the stars would be in the water for the first time since the builders sealed it, and the air would come in cold and new. But the air would come in cold. The warmth here is not a surplus; it is the exact and only budget, and a hand's width of thin mountain night let in through the roof would spend it. The mat would stop glowing along its upper edge for a different reason. The salamanders would not notice the stars. The power looked at the lid and left it.

It might bring light. It carried some; things like it usually do. But the room had made its peace with light in the most complete way possible, by growing eyes and then closing them over, and there is a kind of insult in offering a gift to a thing that spent a hundred thousand years learning not to need it. The power kept its light where it was.

It might stop the water. This one it considered longest, because this was the one thing in the room that looked like a loss coming. The water climbs, the air thins, the oldest one's back will one day rise to meet nothing. A weep opened in the lower course, a joint eased by the breadth of a fingernail, and the rise would halt, and the surface would stay, and the mat would go on leaving its rings.

But the power asked itself, honestly, who the surface was for. And the room answered, as it had already answered: nothing here has ever needed one. The oldest one touches it because the oldest one happens to hang high. That is not a need. That is a coincidence that has been going on a long time. And the mat — the mat records; it does not attend. To keep it recording would be to keep it for a reader, and the room has no reader. Or had none.

The power set that down too, but more slowly, and kept a hand on it.

---

Then there was the question of the being.

The power had come, in the end, for the being — that is what benevolence is for — and it turned its attention to the long pale body hanging just under the last of the air, and it tried to find out what the being wanted, so as to give it that.

It looked for hunger and found a gut that had held the same mouthful for weeks and was content. It looked for fear and found the small dark fist of a heart that beat and then did not beat and then, when you had begun to think it would not, beat, and there was nothing in the interval but the interval. It looked for loneliness, because the twelve have never touched and a benevolent thing feels that on their behalf, and found instead the arrangement — each body holding the others off the way the stones in the vault do, not apart but *placed*, and it understood that to press two of them together would be to pull a stone from the ceiling. It looked, last and most carefully, for anything in the being that was pointed anywhere. A want the size of a grain of sand. A reaching. And it found the held breath with the mouth at one end, and the breath was not held *for* anything. It was simply held.

This is the difficulty with benevolence, and the power had met it before but not often in so pure a form: it is a thing you carry toward a want, and here there was no want to carry it toward. Everything the power had brought up the mountain — remedy, rescue, company, light — required a gap in the being to fit into, and the being had no gap. It was finished. Not dead, not waiting, not enduring. Finished, the way the vault is finished, having sagged as far as it will sag.

So the benevolence had to change shape, or go home.

---

The power went into the water. It did this without displacing anything, because it can, and it went down through the layers of warm dark past the wall where the amphipods grazed and past the flatworms hanging in the joints, and it did not go to the salamander. It went to the stone.

It had been saving the stone because it did not know what the stone was. This should be said plainly, because a wise power is not the same as a knowing one, and the two are confused more often than they should be. It knew what the stone *did*. It knew the stone held a temperature, patiently, without a fire, which is the living thing. It did not know how, or who had made it, or whether it had been made. It knew only that someone before it had come to this same summit and found this same warm thing and decided — decided what the power had now to decide — and that whoever they were, they had chosen to build a room and fill it with water and shut the lid and leave. Not to take it. Not to use it. To house it. That was the whole of what the builders had left behind: a decision, expressed in stone, that the right thing to do with the warm thing was to give it a room and go away.

The power found that it agreed with them, across the gulf of all that time, and this was the first thing that happened to it in the cistern that felt like company.

---

It waited for the descent.

Not long, as the room measures. The oldest one folded its limbs back and let its gills go flat and sank, and the power watched it come down through the water the way you watch someone come down a staircase they have known all their life, and settle its length on the polished face, and stay.

And the power did not lie down beside it. Never two together. It knew that much.

But it put its hand on the stone. At the edge, where the spring comes up around the block and the water is warmest and the mat will not grow. It laid its hand there flat, palm down, and felt what you would feel: the smooth warmth, the polish of every hand before it, the ghost of a great many reasons, none of which were left.

And now one was left. That was the act. It was very small. The power was not the first to touch the stone and would not be the last, but it was the first in a hundred thousand years to touch it for a reason it would carry back out, and so the stone, which had been touched by hands that were all gone, had been touched now by a hand that would *remember* touching it. The room had not needed a reader for the mat. But the stone had been polished by a great many hands, and every one of them had forgotten, and the power thought that perhaps a stone could be forgotten once too often, and that it would not be the one to do it.

It kept its hand there until the oldest one rose. Years, by the surface. The amphipods grazed and molted and were eaten and the gap closed behind each one. The shrimp went around. Once a block settled and every small body stopped, and the power stopped with them, and started again. And then the long pale shape lifted from the stone and drifted upward with its gills unfolding, and somewhere in the dark another one, not the next in any sequence, began to fall, and the power took its hand away before it arrived, so that it would find the stone as it had always found it: warm, and smooth, and nobody's.

---

One more thing, and it belongs to the category of promises.

Before it left the water the power took the count again — twelve — and it took the measure of the air under the vault, which was a hand's width less a hair, and it did the arithmetic of the spring against the weep of the joints, and it arrived at a number. Some thousands of years. The night the last of the air goes. The night the oldest one, if it is still the oldest one, rises to its place near the ceiling and there is no surface there, only more of the same warm dark, and it does not notice, because it never needed it.

The power decided to be on the summit that night.

Not in the room. Not to stop it. Only to be the one who was there when the room arrived at the last of its final shapes — so that the highest place for a hundred miles would have someone on it, for once, at the one moment in its long life that could reasonably be called an occasion, even though nothing inside would mark it. It is a thing you would do for a house. You would come back on the night the fire finally went to coals, even if everyone inside was asleep and warm and would not know. Especially then.

It made the promise to itself, which is the only kind a power like that can make, and went up.

---

The lid was untouched. The light stayed outside. The water kept climbing at the pace of stone, and under the vault the twelve hung at their odd angles, and one of them lay belly-down on the one warm thing in a warm room, and would rise when it rose.

Above, the summit had someone on it, briefly. And then it did not, and the stars went on being very close, and nothing in the cistern had noticed anything at all — which is exactly what a benevolent thing would have wanted, and which it walked back down the mountain counting as a success.
