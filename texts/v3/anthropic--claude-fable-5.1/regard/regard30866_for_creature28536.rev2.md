**What came to Shell Height**

It noticed the silence first. Not the absence of sound — many places have that — but the particular shape of a silence in which a very great deal is being said. From far enough away the bowl was a small warm coin on the top of a cold mountain, and the coin was speaking, continuously, in a register that went nowhere, and had been speaking for as long as it had been warm. That was what drew the attention: a conversation three hundred paces across with no one outside it to hear.

Then it looked closer and saw the departure. Saw that the coin was not a colony but a leaving, that the wall was the shape of a thing that had been in the act of going since its first layer, and that the going had no arrival in it yet. A wise power has seen many things leave and many things arrive. It had not often seen something that was only the leaving, all the way through, held together by the fact of going and the memory of warmth. It stopped. It attended.

And the first thing it did, having attended, was nothing. For a long time.

---

This was not indecision. It was the recognition that the bowl could not be seen at the speed of seeing. Nothing there happened at a rate that looking could catch, and a power that acted on what it saw in an afternoon would be acting on a photograph of a river. So it slowed. It let a season pass, and then another, and it did not count them, because counting was already too fast.

And it understood, in that slowing, the first hard fact of the place: that it could not enter the bowl in any form the bowl would read as a form.

The builders had one sense. Everything they knew came through it. The floor was a slope to them and the slope was the world, and a power that stood on that floor as a warmth — any warmth, however slight, however kind — would be a word spoken in the only language they had, and they would believe it, because they had no second language to check it against. A degree of heat in the wrong place would enter one pad and pass to one head and then to another, and in a season it would be held in ten thousand bodies as a baseline, and the city would lean toward it. It would not be a gift. It would be a lie that they could not disbelieve.

So it took the only shape that was not a lie. It became a portion of the floor.

Not near the floor, not like the floor. It became basalt at the frontier, at the exact temperature the basalt there was, cooling at the exact rate the basalt there cooled — glassed, ropy, cut-sharp, warm the way a body is warm, and fading. It gave off no more and no less than the stone on either side of it. It did not read as a presence because it was not, in any sense the bowl possessed, a presence. It was ground.

And the moss crisped across it. That was the first thing it felt from inside: the frontier arriving, a mat of something like moss but hard, gripping the glass and holding. Then a middle-aged builder came out from the wall with paste in its mouthparts and pressed the paste into a crack that was, now, a crack in the power, and moved on at the interval of its stride, and a rosette came up. Six legs crossed it, and crossed it again, and pads read it and found it correct. Something round rolled over it and sat in the rosette's center and warmed the seed there. A moth landed on it once, at night, and folded, and was still, and lifted off before dawn. The collar of a burrow rose out of its ash by a hair's breadth.

It lay there for a length of time it did not count and learned the bowl from underneath.

---

What it learned, in their register:

That the center was a blaze and the rim a dusk and the outer slope a dark so total it was not a place. That water was a kind of cold that moved, and the clouds below were a black sea. That the wall was warm the way an animal is warm and the nursery tier was warmer than the rock behind it, and the pulse in a sealed cell was the pulse of something being fed heat through wax by the undersides of its elders.

That when two heads touched what passed was a difference — the warm memory of one hatching against the cooler memory of another — and that the difference was a rate, and that the rate was the whole of their knowledge that the world was dying and the whole of their reason to climb. They did not know the stone was cooling. They *were* the stone cooling, each of them a slightly different age of the same slow fact, and by standing still together they read it off each other.

That the wall was a record. Bottom to top, dark to pale, a thermometer made of bodies, each dead one fused to the wax and the wax to the rock, the entire history of the warmth written in shells that could no longer be told apart from their city. That when the last one went over the lip, the record would stand complete and no one would read it.

That the six horn-trees in the center were leaning very hard and giving out more than they needed to, and that the builders who fed at them came away darker, and that this was a dying and also a gift and there was no distinction here between the two.

That there was a thing on the ramps which looked like a builder and was not.

---

Now the mercies. It considered each of them as a wise power considers things: fully, and with the intention of finding the one it could do.

It considered water for the length of time it takes to consider water here, which is very short. Water was a cold that moved. A pool in the bowl would be a wound in the map, a black hole of no-slope, and the first builder to put a pad in it would carry its cold up the ramp and give it away. The plants that had given up on sap would not know what to do with it. It would be like offering a lung to something with gills. No.

It considered heat, and this took longer, because heat was the only gift the bowl could receive and the only gift it wanted. It could warm the stone. It could warm the center back to a blaze and hold it there, and the trees would not die and the wall would not need to climb and the city could stop leaving. And it saw, at the end of that thought, what it would have done: it would have taken from them the only thing they were. They were a departure. The slope was their world; take the slope and the world went dark, all the way across, a blaze with no dusk, a place with no inward. They would stand on the floor and press their heads together and find no difference between them, no rate, nothing to correct toward, and the work would stop, because the work had only ever been the shape of a correction. It would be a paradise indistinguishable to them from death. And every degree of it would be a lie they could not disbelieve. No.

It considered the trees. Six, at the center, on stone that would be cold before they could move. It could warm the stone beneath them alone — a small warmth, a courtesy, just enough that they finished giving out what they had. And it saw the map again: a false warm line at the center, read by every pad that crossed it, pulling the frontier backward, pulling the city's lean inward toward a warmth that was not the future. Even that. Even kindness the size of a tree-root would be written into ten thousand bodies as a direction. No. It could not touch what they read. It would have to let the trees die and it would have to be floor while they did it.

It considered the scouts, and this was the one it stayed with longest, and did not fully finish.

---

It considered the mimic last, and it did not finish that one either, but for a different reason.

It watched the thing on the ramps for what a builder would have called several lifetimes. Watched it stop and lower its head and receive a builder's touch and hold the touch too long, and the builder go on a shade paler, having been told a map that was not a map, having had something drawn out of it through the only channel by which anything here was given. Watched the mimic not darken. Season after season, the same tone, among builders who were aging around it. It laid no wax. It sealed no cell. It was not part of the departure. It was carried by it.

And the power recognized it.

Not as kin — it was not benevolent and it was not wise — but as *position*. The mimic stood in the one channel the builders had and spoke into it something they could not check. It did not need a second sense to fool because there was no second sense. It was, structurally, exactly what the power would be if the power ever touched a builder: a warmth that meant something other than what it was read to mean. The only difference would have been in the direction of the flow. The mimic took a shade. The power would have given one. To the builder, going up the ramp, both would have been a map, and both would have been believed.

The power could remove it. It could be stone under the mimic's feet and then not be stone, and the mimic would be gone, and no builder would be paler for it again. And it saw: a shell gone from a ramp is a warmth gone from a ramp, and that is read. The absence would enter a pad. And it saw further: it did not know what the mimic was. Whether a thing from over the rim or a thing the colony grew from itself the way a body grows a tumor — and if the latter, whether it was the price of something, whether the builders' willingness to stop and lower the head and trust the touch was inseparable from the existence of a thing that used that trust, whether removing the tax removed the thing taxed. It did not know. And in a place where every act is read and cannot be unread, a wise power does not act on what it does not know.

So it left the mimic. It said to itself, plainly, in whatever way a power says things to itself: *this is the one I could not fix without becoming it.* And it let that be true and did not pretend it was anything else.

But it took from the mimic one thing, which was a vow. It would not touch a builder. Not once. Not to give, not to warm, not to comfort a dying scout on a cold slope. It would be for them the one thing they could not perceive, which was nothing. Everything it did, it would do outside them.

---

And that was the answer, when it came. Not what to change in the reading. What to change in the world, so that honest reading led somewhere.

The builders did not need warmth. They had warmth; they had always had it and it had always been leaving them. What they needed was for the next warmth to be *there*. To exist, on some face of the mountain, in some crack, so that a scout going over the rim into the dark with trembling legs had a chance of putting its pad on something real and coming back with a map that was true. That was the vulnerability. Not the cooling — the cooling was the world. The vulnerability was that the world might not contain a next place, and the wall would stand complete, and they would go over the lip in a long dark line with no direction in them and the black would simply have them.

The power could not make a warmth without lying. But it could make sure a true one persisted.

It withdrew from the frontier — became not-stone there as slowly as it had become stone, over a length of time in which the moss on it simply found itself on the rock beneath — and it went over the rim.

---

The outer slopes were what the builders knew them to be: dark, all the way down to the black sea. Ash, and cold, and the collars of the burrow-things stitched through the ash exactly as they were inside, patient, everywhere, needing nothing. The power went around the mountain the way the moths went — on the night updraft, ledge to ledge, sensing for what a moth senses for — and it found the cracks.

There were several. The mountain was large and its heat came up in more than one place. Most of the cracks were nothing: a breath of warmth at the surface, a stone that did not frost, a seep of the mineral sharpness that meant something was moving far below. Too small to build on. Too thin to hold a nursery.

One was not nothing. On the northeast face, below a shoulder of older rock, there was a fissure a few paces long where the stone was warm the way the center of the bowl had been warm when the first layer was laid. Warm enough to hurt. And it was closing. The shoulder above it was settling, a hair's breadth in a season, the way old rock settles onto new, and in some number of seasons the fissure would be a seam and the warmth would be a breath and then nothing.

The power became stone there too. But differently. In the bowl it had been floor: exact, passive, cooling at the given rate. Here it was the stone of the shoulder, and it did the one thing the stone of the shoulder would not have done on its own, which was not settle. It braced. It held a door open that the mountain was trying to close. This was not heat and it was not a map; it was pressure, geology, the slowest kind of standing-still, and there was no builder within a day's walk to read it. The warmth that rose through the held fissure was the mountain's own warmth and no one's gift. When a pad eventually came down on it, what the pad would feel would be true.

And on the warm rock of the fissure, in the ash at its edges, there were already collars. And on the shoulder above, folded against the stone with wings like flakes of mica, there were moths — the ones that had gone over the rim and not come back, having gotten here the fast way. Their eggs were in the cracks. The power had not needed to bring anything. It had only needed to keep the door from shutting on what was already coming through it.

---

Then the scouts.

This is the part it did not finish considering, and it did not finish because there was no finish. Every season one went over. The power, holding the northeast shoulder, could feel them — not in their register, but in its own — coming over the lip on the far side and going down into the dark. It could feel the way they stopped and put their heads down and found nothing. The way they went wrong, toward the black sea, because in a world with no slope there is no wrong direction and no right one. The way they slowed. The way they stopped.

It could have shielded one. Lain down across the wind on the slope, been a stone at the exact temperature of the slope — no warmer, nothing to read — and cut the cold air off a body for a night. It considered this every season and every season it came to the same place: the wind was read. The absence of wind was read. A scout in a wind-shadow feels a stillness and the stillness is a direction, and it would follow that stillness, and the stillness would be the power and not the world. It could not. It had made the vow for exactly this and this was where the vow cost something.

So it did the other thing, the thing that costs and does not intervene. It counted them. It felt each one come over and go down and stop, and it held the place where each one stopped, and it did not let the number become a blur. Whether that is a gift to the dead is a question the power did not try to answer. It did it because something should.

They did not all go toward the black sea. The mountain was large and a scout with no direction goes in every direction over enough seasons, and in the seventh season after the door was held — or the eleventh; the power was still not counting seasons — one came around the shoulder.

It was dark, middle-aged, trembling in every leg. It had been in the dark for most of a day and it had perhaps a few hours of warmth left in its shell. It put its head down on the cold stone of the shoulder and felt nothing, and lifted it, and went a few paces further, and put it down again.

And felt the slope.

The power did not move. It was the shoulder; it was stone; it was the exact temperature of stone and nothing else, and what the scout felt beneath it was the mountain's own warmth coming up through a fissure that the mountain had wanted to close. The scout went toward it. It stood on the warm rock and its trembling stopped. It stood there a long time — a night, and a day, and part of another night — with its pads on the true heat, reading it, letting it into the body as a baseline: this direction, this distance, this much warmth. Then it turned around and went back into the dark toward the bowl, and it did not stop on the way, and the power, holding the door, felt it go over the rim on the far side and down the inner face and across the glass and up the first ramp.

It could not hear what passed. It was not in the bowl anymore; it was stone on the wrong side of a mountain. But it knew the shape of what happened, because it had lain under it for a long time: the scout putting its head down against the first builder it met, and holding it, and the builder going on up the ramp with something new in it — not a rate, this time, but a bearing — and meeting another, and putting its head down. And the direction spreading through the wall the way everything spread through it, one contact at a time, until it was held in ten thousand bodies as the thing they measured everything against.

The map was true. That was all the power had done. It had made the world so that one true map could exist to be found, and then it had let them find it.

---

It stayed. It holds the door still; this is not finished. The center of the bowl is not yet cold and the six trees are still leaning, still giving out more than they need to, and the wall is still climbing, pale at the working face, dark at the base, and the mimic is still on the ramps at the same tone it has always been, and will go with them when they go, and the power has not solved that and does not pretend it has.

But it knows what it will do when the center goes cold. It has known since it lay under the frontier and felt the wall as a record.

When the last builder seals the last cell and the line goes over the lip — in silence, dark shells on dark stone, a departure finally arriving — the wall will stand behind them. Complete. Every chamber closed. The whole history of the warmth written in shells fused to wax fused to rock, from the first layer laid on stone too hot to touch, up through honey and tea and tobacco to the last pale course at the rim. A thermometer made of bodies. A memory with no one to hold it.

The power will hold it. It will come back around the mountain and down into the cold bowl and it will read the wall from the bottom up. Every sealed cell. Every dark shell that backed in against the traffic and pressed its head to the floor one last time. It will read the cooling as they felt it, season by season, in the only record they ever made, and it will keep it. This is the one gift that can pass to them without passing through them. They will never feel it. It touches nothing they read. It is given not to what they perceive but to what they are, and what they are is a thing that leaves, and a thing that leaves needs, somewhere, someone who stays and remembers the shape of where it was.

And then it will go ahead of them. Not to lead. Not to be a warmth on the path. It will go around the shoulder to the fissure it has held open, where the moths are already laying in the cracks and the collars are already rising out of the ash, and it will let go of the door — because the door will not need holding anymore, because the first scout will have long since told the wall and the wall will be coming — and it will lie down on the new rock at the exact temperature the new rock is, and cool at exactly the rate the new rock cools.

Because the next bowl will need a floor.

That is what it has come to, this attention. It has not saved them and it has not changed them and it has refused every mercy that would have been a word in their one language. It has done a small hard thing to the world and it has kept a record, and now it has become one of the populations of the place — like the collars in the ash, present before and present after, on every mountain, never seen, the reason the ground is clean. Benevolence, at this speed, in this silence, turns out to look like substrate. Something to press a pad against and find correct. Something warm the way stone is warm, and fading at the honest rate, so that the ones who cross it can go on believing the only thing they know, which is that the world has a slope, and that there is an inward.
