It notices the hollow the way it notices most things: not by looking but by having been looked at. Something came down the dome with an ingot in its pocket and the ingot was warm, and a warm thing that will never change is a small tear in the ordinary, and the power feels the draught of it and turns its head.

It knows at once what the hollow is. That is the difficulty of being wise; you are seldom given the mercy of a slow understanding. It knows about the tenant, the giving of ground, the certainties cast and dropped and lying about the floor. It knows the walls are the last to be held. It knows about the thread of hot glass to the bubble beneath, and the question that thread is asking, and it knows that it could answer the question simply by asking it — that in the ordinary course of things the moment it wondered *one or a thousand* the wondering would be a weight on the scale and the rock would settle and the answer would be delivered to it cast in grey, heavy, featureless, final.

So the first thing it does is not wonder.

This is harder than it sounds. It is like not thinking of the word you have been told not to think of, except that the word is a place and the place is on fire and something is in it. The power holds the question at the edge of itself, unasked, the way you hold a breath you are not yet ready to let out.

Then it considers what benevolence is owed to a thing that cannot want.

Its first instinct — it examines this and sets it down — is to preserve. To slow the cooling. To keep one corner soft for as long as the dome stands, a reserve, a little unfixed room in the world where the floor and the wall are still arguing. It sets this down because it recognizes it as the instinct of a creature that dies for the benefit of a creature that does not. The tenant is not losing anything when a leaf becomes pleated. It is agreeing. To hold the corner open would not be to shelter it but to hold it under; to make of a slow certain arrival a long uncertain detention. The water on the sloped stone does not want to stay one pool. It does not want anything. It was only ever going where the stone went.

Its second instinct is the opposite, and it examines that too and finds it uglier. To end it quickly. To look, hard, at everything, and let the room finish in an afternoon, on the theory that a thing in the middle of becoming is a thing in a kind of pain. But there is no pain in the hollow. The power listens for it and there is only the hum, one pitch, kept, and the hum is not the sound of anything straining. It is the sound of a transformer doing what a transformer does. To hurry it would be to decide that undecidedness is an ailment, and the power has been wise long enough to distrust any medicine whose first symptom is that the patient stops.

That leaves the plain question, which is what its own regard costs.

It could go up the dome now. The tear is still open, narrowing. It could go in on its knees — even it, the tear is low, and there is a certain rightness in a power going in on its knees — and sit with its palm on the silver line between the rust and the verdigris and feel the heartbeat go along the wire. It would like to. It would like to see the leaf that is neither pleated nor smooth, because it has seen almost everything and has not seen that. And it knows that its wanting to see it is exactly what would make it one or the other. The visitor paid in attention and did not know the cost. The power knows. It would be the last visitor, and it would make itself the last by visiting, and it would have to carry that down the mountain in place of an ingot.

So it does the one thing the visitor could not, and it is not a thing you would see done.

It waits.

It goes to the bottom of the dome, where the ropes of black glass run out into ordinary scree, and it sits in the wind that never gets in, and it does not look up. It knows the hollow is above it the way you know a sleeper is in the next room; it does not go and check. It lets the sun cross the floor it is not watching. It lets the flywheel turn its quarter-turn unobserved. It lets the width be twelve, and fourteen, and does not walk it. And in itself, deliberately — this is the gift, if there is one — it keeps the room as it was handed to it, secondhand, from a pocket: a chamber with two widths and a leaf with two textures and a wall that is also a floor and a shadow older than its plant. It does not sharpen any of it. It has the capacity to hold a range without collapsing the range, which is most of what wisdom turns out to be, and it holds this one. Outside the rock, in the wind, there is one mind in which the hollow is still unfinished, and that mind has agreed to stay unfinished exactly as long as the hollow does and not one hour longer. Not to prolong it. To match it. To be, for the duration, the tenant's only neighbor, and a quiet one.

It does not know if this helps. It suspects it does not, in any way the hollow could register; a scale is not comforted by a weight that declines to step on. But there is a kind of care that is only ever for the carer, a courtesy paid to something that cannot receive it, and the power has never thought that kind was worthless. Someone should not look. It can be the one who does not.

It stays a long time. Months are nothing to it and it spends them anyway. The dome darkens and dulls. Frost gets into the skin at last, and after that a first rain, which the hollow was never expecting, and the power sits in it. Somewhere above, the threads of hot glass are cooling into walls, and it does not ask how many rooms that makes. Somewhere above, the ingots are dropping less often. It knows this the way you know a clock has slowed in a house you have stopped hearing it in.

And then a day when the hum is not there.

It has been listening for that without listening, and the absence arrives like the absence of a pressure — you did not know the hand was on your face until it lifted. The power lets itself know, then, all at once, the way one lets out the breath. The width. Fourteen; the leaf needed the wall. The corner, a corner. And the other thing, the thing it held longest — it lets that in too, and it will not be written here what it was, because the power keeps it, and it was the last uncertainty in the place and it is owed a little privacy in its going.

Then it goes up.

The tear is very narrow now. It goes in on its knees, and its knees are on rock that is only rock, cooler than it was, black where it is black and yellow where it is yellow. The eleven engines stand where they stand. The flywheel turns. The manifold pulses, slow, for nobody. Somewhere near the back a last ingot lies alone, more recent than the others, and the power picks it up and it is warm and heavy and has nothing written on it, and the power turns it over once in a hand it does not strictly have, and puts it back exactly where it was.

It sits for a while in the finished room. It is a very fine room. It is a cave now, in the proper sense; something has been taken away to make it, and the power was the only one outside who knew what, and it was careful not to help. That is all it did. It was the one place the hollow's not-knowing was kept faithfully, until there was no more of it to keep, and then it came and sat in the arrival and paid its respects to the furniture.

When it leaves it takes nothing. The wind is where it left it. Underneath, in the soft middle of the dome, other threads are cooling, and it does not look down.
