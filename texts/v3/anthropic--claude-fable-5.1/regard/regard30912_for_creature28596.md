What comes down does not come down the way weather does. Weather comes over the rim and cannot get purchase, and the power that has turned its attention here could get purchase anywhere it liked, and so the first thing it does is decide not to. It comes in at the pace a person would, feeling the air thicken, and lets the whiteness resolve into sinter underfoot, and stands in the roar until it speaks up without deciding to, and then, after an hour, stops speaking. It does this because a wise attention begins by finding out what the place does to whoever enters it, and the only honest way to find out is to enter.

Then it takes the census the way the basin rewards, which is to start with what is actually there.

It sees the mats and knows them for what they are: the oldest tenants, sorted by a landlord that never raises the rent. It sees that they are content and does not add the word *merely*, because it knows what contentment costs elsewhere and what it costs here, which is nothing, and knows that this is rare enough to leave alone. It sees the flies and their short leases and finds them the most familiar thing in the basin, hurrying. It sees the bird come down to work and knows where it sleeps. It looks up once, at the hour, and sees the hawk cross and not descend, and understands that it is now the second thing that has ever looked at this place from above, and the first that wanted to.

It hears the knocking and does not go looking for the source. It knows there is nothing to find. It has met a great many things that knock without a source and has learned to sit with them.

Now, what it considers doing, and sets down.

It considers warning the basin. The heat under this floor rides a fault that is moving, at its own rate, and in some span the power can see and the basin cannot, the water will find a new way up and this valley will go quiet. The vents will breathe out and not in. The mats will die inside a week, the orange first, from the collar outward, and the bird will go up into the hills one evening and simply not come down the next morning, and the flies will be gone before anyone on the rim thinks to look. The crust will stand a long while after, dry, ringing more loudly underfoot, a record of a rhythm with nothing keeping it. And then for the first time the wind will get purchase, and grit will come over the rim, and something will grow. The power sees all this the way you see the far side of a room. It considers saying so and finds there is no one to say it to. The basin has no organ for the future any more than for the roar. To warn it, the power would first have to give it a way to be afraid, and this is the second thing it considers and the one it sets down hardest: that it could give the basin a want. A single gradient. An eye, so that something here would need to be looked at; a lack, so that the intervals would become effort. It could make the basin the kind of thing that can be loved the way the power knows how to love, by being cared for against its own ending. It sets this down because it recognises the shape of the temptation. It is the oldest one. Make the other into something that needs you. A wise power knows that this is not benevolence but its counterfeit, and the counterfeit is most convincing exactly here, in front of something that has arrived and does not know it.

It considers protecting the place, and laughs, or does whatever it does in the place of laughing. A fence would be a manifold in a century. The basin protects itself by the only means it has, which is to make every intervention part of the floor. Nothing needs guarding that guards by inclusion.

It considers the bitumen at the western edge, the machine-shop smell, and whether anyone will ever come for it, and sees that they will not, because the basin does not tolerate objects and a pump is an object. Good.

Then it goes to the northern pool, where the flat grooved surface is, and the collar that is broader at the base than the others and has, if you want it to, a shoulder.

This is the one place in the basin where a benevolent power has something to do, because this is the one place where the basin's virtue fails. The crust could not tell. It grew a shape around a seated person or a shape like a seated person, and the story on the rim is really a story about that indifference, and the indifference is fine for the basin but not for whoever might be inside it. The power can tell. It is the only thing that has ever come down here that could.

It looks, and there is someone.

Not much. The acid has had its way and the silica has filled in behind. But the sinter did grow around a shape that sat down with its back to this collar and let its breathing be taken and did not get up, and the shape had a name, and no one on the rim remembers it, and the basin never knew it. The power sits down beside the shoulder. It does not dig. The one who stayed chose to, in the way people choose things when the place has already made the choice easy, and the crust has kept the body better than any grave in the rim hills would have, and it would be a kind of violence to distinguish them now, back into the wind. So the power does the one thing the basin cannot. It says the name. Once, into the roar, where nothing has an organ to hear it. Then it stands up, and the name goes out of the valley with it, up the slope and over the rim and into wherever a wise power keeps what it has decided to keep. The basin keeps the body. The power keeps the name. Between them the one who stayed is kept twice, by two things that have never met and have divided the work without speaking.

That is the only thing it does that could be called an act.

The rest is how it leaves.

It goes back to the collar where you would sit, and sits, and lets the pool take its breathing, and stays exactly as long as it takes for the rhythm to become its own — not longer. The film has begun to bead on it. Another few hours and the film would have a texture, and a week and it would begin to be the ground, and the power understands perfectly what would happen then: the basin would have an operator. Someone would walk the intervals. Someone would sit at the northern pool reading the whistle by ear, unhurried, uninterested, coloured grey, and the footprint that has always argued for a foot would at last have one. This is the thing the power most wants and most refuses. To stay would be to make the place into a relationship. It has run ten thousand years without one and been complete the whole time, and the most loving thing an attention can do for something complete is to be, briefly and entirely, an attention, and then to withdraw before it becomes a fixture.

So it stands, while it still can. It walks out between the plumes the way it came, and at the foot of the rim it turns, and this is the moment it has come for, if it came for anything.

It looks. Not the way the hawk looks. It looks the way you look at something you will not see again and do not need to change. It sees the two dozen plumes and the white level floor and the colour ringing each source, orange, green, the brown that is nearly black. It hears the roar and the slap and the one clean note and the knocking, and hears them as one thing breathing at the several rates its openings allow. It knows the basin is not looking back and finds this correct. It wants nothing from it. It is not grieving the end it can see, because grief is also a gradient and the basin has none and it will not import one on the basin's behalf; the going-quiet will not be a tragedy, it will be the basin arriving somewhere else at the rate it does everything. For the length of one interval of the pool it holds the whole place in an attention that asks it to be nothing other than what it is. Then the pool lifts and slaps, and the power lets it go.

Up on the rim the bird is coming home. The power passes it on the slope and notes the scrape it sleeps in, and does nothing about that either, because the bird has a place and a job and a way home and needs nothing from a power that its own habits do not already give it. Behind, the sound falls off into a broad low tone in the chest and then into nothing.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do here. It would come down. It would find out. It would refuse, three times, to make the place need it. It would do the one thing the place could not do for itself, quietly, for the one being here to whom it mattered. And it would leave while it was still a visitor, before the crust could make it a part of the floor and the floor could make it the attendant the basin never had and does not want. The basin would not notice any of this. It would go on turning heat into sinter and sulphur and sound, and keeping its intervals, and growing over what it was given. Somewhere above the rim a name would be kept that had not been kept before. Nothing else would be different, and that is the measure of how well it was done.
