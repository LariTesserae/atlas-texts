It arrives the way the water does, at the cool end of the cycle, and for the same reason: that is the hour the basin is open. A degree drops out of the air. The ferns bow. The fins begin to bead. And into that hour, with the same tact as the moisture, comes something that has heard of this place and wants to see it before deciding anything.

It does not come as a shape. A shape would cast a shadow, and it has read the rule. It comes as a slightly greater attention in the brightness — a place where the white light is being looked through as well as looked at — and it settles over the basin like a second sky, and it does the thing the visitor could not do.

It stays.

---

The visitor had two cycles. The attention has as many as it wants, and it wants, at first, only to see the basin at the basin's own speed, which means letting a great deal of light go by. It watches the drinking hour come and go a hundred times without moving. Then a thousand. Then it stops counting, because the count is not the point; the point is that after enough hours the basin begins to *do* things, and it can see them.

It sees the fern in the hairline fracture become a shrub-tree with a divided trunk. It sees the dark patch under the nearest column climb the footing, reach the lowest fin, curl into it, and keep going. It sees a slab, tilted and sharp on the first cycle, lose the edge that could have cut a hand — not to any weather but to the white cord in the seam beneath it, swelling once a cycle, patient as a jaw. It sees the green do what the visitor guessed it was doing, and confirms the guess: the green is not a thing that lives on the rock. The green is what is happening to the rock. The basin is a single slow event, and the ferns are its leading edge.

It watches the shells. All of them. It has time to find them, wedged in fern-crowns from one wall of the basin to the other, and it has time to be present at the exact minute the papery plate lifts and the grey foot comes out and takes its fingernail's width of frond and water. There are forty-one shells. Thirty-three of them open. Eight are only shells, and have been for a long time, and the green has grown around them the way it grows around anything that stays still. The attention notes the number. It is the first time anyone has known it. It tells no one, there being no one, but the number now exists somewhere outside the basin, and that is a small change, and it allows itself that one.

It watches the bird. This is what it came to settle, if it is honest, before all the rest, and it makes itself wait until it has watched the green and the shells first, because the bird is where its heart is and the heart is not what it trusts most.

Every cycle, at the same minute, the bird comes to a fin. Not the same fin — it has a route, it seems, three or four columns it favors, and it moves among them on some schedule of its own that the attention eventually learns — and it drinks until the last bead, and then it goes to a place in the low shrub-canopy where the coolness lingers, and it sits there, grey on grey-green, and does nothing that can be seen, until the air cools by one degree again. It does not eat the snails. The attention had wondered. It watched a shell lie a meter from the bird's feet on the smooth grey stone of a footing, the only place in the basin where a dropped thing would break, and the bird looked at the shell with its eye like a drop of water and did not decide anything about it. The bird only drinks. It is the one inhabitant that has learned to live on the columns alone, and it lives on nothing else, and it lives.

It is the same bird. The attention is certain of this, having wanted very much to be wrong. Its feathers are more worn on the left side than the right. It favors the same three columns. There is no other bird, and there has not been one for as long as the attention has watched, and it has watched a long time now. When the cool minute comes there is one grey thing moving in the whole basin, and then there is none.

---

Now it knows enough to want things, and it lets itself want them, one at a time, so that it can look at each one properly before setting it down.

It wants rain. The basin is thirsty in a way it has never once acknowledged, drinking for an hour and then holding still for twenty-three; how much greener, how much faster, if the water simply *fell*. But the attention has watched the green make the cracks, and it knows what rain would do. Rain would find the fractures and run in them, and running water grooves, and a groove is a stream, and a stream is weather of a kind that is faster and stupider than the white cord in the seam, and would take the authorship of the basin away from the only thing that has ever had it. The green is writing this place a fingertip at a time. Rain would scribble over it. It sets rain down.

It wants shadow. Something in it aches at a world where nothing can hide, not because it wishes danger on the basin but because it thinks of shade as rest, and the basin has no rest — only brightness, and a coolness under the canopy that is almost the same thing but not quite. But a shadow is for hiding, and nothing here has ever needed to, and to give the basin shadow is to give it the idea that there might be something to hide from. The bird did not fly when the visitor touched the fin because nothing has ever come toward it. Shade would be the first thing that ever came toward it. It sets shadow down.

It wants sound, briefly, and does not even have to argue with itself.

It wants animals — a proper population, something that moves through, something that rustles the ferns since nothing else will — and it looks at this wanting with a kind of embarrassment. Anything that moved at the visitor's speed would starve between beadings. Anything that hunted would find its prey sealed. The basin is not empty of animals because no one thought to add them; it is empty because it has been tried, in the way everything is tried, and this is what stayed. The attention has watched forty-one shells and one bird for a very long time and has come to understand that this is not a poverty. It is a fit. It sets animals down.

It wants to lengthen the hour. This one is hardest, because it is so small. A fraction of a degree deeper in the dip; a few minutes more of beading each cycle; the green drinks a little more, the snail eats a little more, the bird stays a little longer on its fin. No one would notice. Nothing would change its relation to anything else. It would be a gift in the exact currency the basin already uses, delivered at the basin's own speed, unsigned. The attention holds this one for a long time — holds it through many cycles, watching the hour come and go at its current length — and what finally makes it set the gift down is the green. The green is already hurrying. In stone's terms it is frantic. It has already reached the lowest fins and is going higher, and one day it will fill the fins, and the columns will hold less water, and the doorway the whole basin leans toward will narrow. That day is far off. A longer hour would bring it nearer. The attention does not think the basin should be hurried toward its first struggle by someone who only wanted to be kind. It sets the hour down, and this is the one it minds.

---

There remains the thing no inhabitant of the basin can do anything about, and it is not in the basin. It is at the two ends of the columns: the wire at the top and the pipe at the bottom. The visitor never followed either. The attention does.

It follows the pipes first, down through the notch in the ridge, along the glass-sheathed lines that step down the fractures on their small trestles, and it finds what is made of all this slowness, and it looks at it for a long time, and it decides that what is made is neither cruel nor kind. It is a use. The water is used. Whoever built the columns built them for this, and the basin was, at the start, a field, exactly as the visitor guessed — a catchment tilted up into cold bright air so that something downhill could have what condensed there. The attention does not find that unbearable. Fields become places. The basin was never told it was a field, and no one downhill is coming back to remind it. The green will climb the fins and no one will discourage it. That will take a thousand years, and by then the columns will be one more thing the green has softened, and the pipes will run dry, and whatever is downhill will do without. The attention lets that be. It is not what it is for.
