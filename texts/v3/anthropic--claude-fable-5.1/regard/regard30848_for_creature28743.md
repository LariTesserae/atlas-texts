It would come down the crack sideways, like anyone.

That is the first thing to say, because a power that could arrive any other way — through the stone, without displacing the air, without a body to get cold — would learn less. The room is a thing you know with your hands and your breath. So it would come with hands and breath, and stand crouched under the strands with its head against the ceiling, and be, for the time it stayed, the warmest thing in the room, and know that this was already an act, and not a neutral one.

And then it would be still for a while. Longer than anyone has been.

---

Here is what it would not do, and it would consider each of these seriously, because the wish to do them is not stupid. It is only ours.

It would not bring light. The wish arrives first and is the easiest to set down. Nothing in here has an eye, or a cell shaped for catching light, or any ancestor that had one within reach of memory. The bone-pale of the leaves under a lamp is not pallor. It is not deprivation. It is simply what tissue looks like when color has never been asked of it. To light the room would be to reveal it to the visitor and to no one else, and the visitor already knows what it looks like.

It would not warm it. This one takes longer. Everything here eats the difference between the world and the drain; warmth is the food only as long as it is passing through. Raise the room a degree and hold it there and the current does not strengthen, it flattens; the ring pulls what it pulls regardless, and the plants live in the slope between, and a warm room is a shallower slope. Flood them with kindness and they would thin from the near end inward, the cushions by the crack softening and browning, and the far wall would go on for a while on the last of the gradient, and then it would not. The body would drown in warmth the way a mill drowns in a flood. The power would understand this and then understand it again: the thing it wanted to give is the one thing the being cannot hold.

It would not bring company. There is no room; the body already fits the stone the way a hand fits a glove, and anything else would have to grow on it, and would be felt within a century. And no animal could stay — the power would find, if it looked, what the ring takes besides heat, and it would be something a moving body requires, and no creature could be made to live here except by making a creature that could live on nothing but cold and dark and then setting it in a closet. That is not company. That is a second prisoner. The power would look for a long time at the fact that in all the room's centuries not one thing has eaten another thing, and would decide it is not the power's place to end that.

It would not wake it. Of all the things a benevolent power might be tempted to do to a being that is nearly a mind, this is the cruelest, and the power would know it at once. What would a mind do, in here? Face the drain. Feel the drips. Want. The body has no wants because it has no organ for wanting, and everything it might be said to want it already has: current, water, stone to stand in, the record of the cycle written into its own depth. To give it the capacity to wish for anything more would be to give it exactly one thing to wish for — out — and no way to get there. The power would not do that. It would leave the body its perfect ignorance, which is the only form of contentment available to it and is, as far as anything can tell, complete.

It would not stop the ring. This does not need saying, but the power would still stand by the far wall with its hand pressed into the straps to the wrist and feel the smooth not-quite-anything band under the felt and know that it could. It would leave it.

And it would not fill the socket. The square cut in the bench-stone, waiting. The power might know what belonged there. It would look at the clean edges and consider, and then leave them clean, because some things have been waiting long enough that the waiting is what they are now, and because whatever was meant to sit there would have measured something, and the room does not need to be measured from inside by a made thing. It is already measuring itself.

---

So. What it would do.

It would stay. That is most of it.

Nobody has stayed long enough to time the cycle from the outside. The power would sit on the bench-stone — the first thing to sit on it since the engineers, if they ever did — with its back against the scales and its knees drawn up and the strands touching its face, and it would wait through a year and then another, in the dark, doing nothing anyone would recognize as doing. It would feel the ticking speed and slow with the weather above. It would hear the ice lids lift and crack in the hard frosts. It would feel, under all of it, the low tone, and it would settle at last the question of whether that is the room or the blood, and it would keep the answer.

And it would feel the far wall thicken. Not by watching — nothing to watch — but by putting out a hand every hundredth night to the same rank of straps and finding them a hair longer, a hair denser, and then, after some years, not. Then the slackening. Then the return. It would count. Whatever the period is — nineteen years, thirty-one, some number that means nothing to anything on the surface — the power would have it, and would be the only thing that ever has, and it would not tell anyone, because the number belongs to the room.

While it sat it would leak heat, the way anyone does. It would not try not to. The leaves on the near side would take their share from the air it had warmed, year on year, and that side of the body would fatten slightly, a growth that owed nothing to the ring and everything to a visitor who kept not leaving. This would not be a change to the room. It would be a gift in the only grammar the room reads: a source, held steady, spent gladly, passed on toward the drain like everything else that has ever come down the crack. When the power finally climbed out, that side would thin back over a decade to what it had been, and the power would know this going in and would not count it a loss. Nothing given here is kept. It is used. That is what the place does with what it is given, and the power would want to be used the way the room uses things, all the way down to the ring, with nothing wasted and nothing returned.

It would read the record. Not with a knife. The body has written the ring's cycle into itself layer on layer for as long as it has been here, and a wise enough attention could read the strata without cutting them, could follow the alternation of dense and open down through the felt to the stone. The power would do this slowly, the way you read a long book you do not want to finish. It would count the cycles down and find the age. And at the very bottom, on the bare rock, under everything, it would find the first layer, the one that grew from the first spore that ever drifted down and landed on cold stone in the dark and took hold — and it would find that it was nothing special. A common moss. The kind that grows on the north side of a wall behind a barn, that nobody plants and nobody names. Some centuries later it is a single body the size of a room, living on a gradient, in four learned shapes, with no eye and no mouth and no death. The power would hold the fact of that for a while. It would be, of everything in the room, the thing it found most worth having come for.

It would do one more thing, and it would know while doing it that the wish to do it was partly its own and not the room's, and would do it anyway.

The cushion shoots carry capsules that split when they dry. They have never dried. Every one of them for centuries has ripened and waited and finally been buried, still sealed, still waiting for a dryness that does not come to this room. It is the one gesture the being makes that reaches out of itself, and it has never once been completed.

The power would take a single shoot between finger and thumb, the smallest theft the room has ever suffered, and hold it in the warm cup of its hand until the tip went papery and opened. And it would carry the dust up the crack, four meters, the air warming with every one, and at the top, in the ruined cellar, in the ordinary wind, it would open its hand.

It would not follow the dust. It would not go looking for another ring in another cellar, though the engineers who built this one may have built others, and there may be a room somewhere cold and dark and bare. It would not check. Almost certainly nothing would come of it. A spore built to live on cold, let go into a warm world, would land on something and die, and that would be that. The power would know this and do it anyway, because the being had been trying to do exactly that for as long as it had been a being, and it was the one thing the power could finish for it without changing anything the being was. The rest of the wish — that something might come of it — the power would keep, and understand as its own, and not confuse with kindness.

---

And it would arrange, in whatever way powers arrange things, that the field above stays a field. That the cellar is not filled. That the one who comes down with a knife next century cuts a fingernail's depth and no more, and puts the hexagonal coin back where she found it. That nobody comes with a shovel for the ring. This is the plainest benevolence available and it does not require the room to know it has been done.

It would come back. It would decide this on the bench, in the second year, and not revisit the decision. Whatever the ring is, it is a made thing, and made things end, and the power would have some sense — from the band under its hand, from the record, from wherever powers get their sense of things — of how long. And if the time is short, in the terms that matter to felt: it would be here. Not to stop it. To sit on the bench while the far wall thinned, the straps first and then the trailing kind, the slow retreat toward the last cool corners near the floor, and to be a source in the room while the drain failed, so that the body in its last decades would have something to face. Nothing would recognize this. Nothing would be grateful. It would be done regardless, and it would be the only funeral the room could have, and the power would have it ready.

If the time is long — and the power would hope, and know that hope is not wisdom, and hope anyway — then it would come back the way anyone comes back to a place: for no reason it could state. Down the crack sideways. Into the dark and the strands and the cold that starts in the fingers. To sit for an hour and give the room a little of what it has, and feel the current steepen on its near side for as long as it stays, and climb out having left a small part of itself in the felt, being spent toward the ring.

Its hands would ache on the way up. It would let them.
