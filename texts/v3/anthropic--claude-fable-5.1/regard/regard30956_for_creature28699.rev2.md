It comes the long way round.

Not up the mountain, where the sound would reach it first and set its expectations; not down out of the cloud onto the floor of the bowl among the stations, where the eye looks for the machine and finds it. It comes across the field. The grey, unstarted field has no features and therefore no distance, and crossing it is not so much travel as consent, and when the consent is complete the power is standing in the drop, in the milk of the cup, at the height of the lip, with the shelf in front of it and its back to everything that has not been made.

It stands in the breath. That is the first thing it takes in: not the works, which from here are muffled by a body and a wall of rock and an age of cloud, but the air itself, which is the exhalation of one creature laid down over a span it does not trouble to reckon. It is cool. It tastes of stone. It tastes, very faintly, of something that has never eaten anything but itself.

And it sees the face.

No one has seen the face. The visitor with the notebook came up from below and saw a back and a hand and a hollow, and the animals see nothing, and the field has no eyes. The power is the first thing to arrive from the direction the being is looking, and so it is the first thing the being has ever had in front of it that was not grey. The face is worn like the rest. Whatever features it had have gone the way of the edges, blunted down to suggestions: a brow, the place where a mouth would be. The eyes are open. They are looking. They have been looking for as long as there has been anything to look with, at a surface that gives nothing back, and the power cannot read what is in them, because there has never been a second face on this shelf against which the meaning of a face could be learned.

The power waits. It is good at this.

It waits through many turns of the frog's interval and many heaves of the thing under the moss, and in that time it does what wisdom does before it does anything else, which is to look at each thing it might do and set it down.

It might stop the works. One hand over the frog's throat at the top of the swell. The beetles falling silent in their row, the birds dropping out of the ring, the whine unwinding. The rock would stop being split this morning. The first frost would come in, and the first winter, and the edges would begin to go. The fronds would brown. The ponds would fill and the water would find a way down the mountain and the ferns would rot into the moss and the moss would die back to a skin and the shelf would become, in a hundred years or a thousand, a place like other places. The power looks at that and finds it cannot tell, any more than the visitor could from behind, whether the noise is a kindness or a hold. To stop it is to bet the whole shelf on one reading. If the one at the lip is the maker, resting, then the works are its own device and stopping them is a stranger's hand on another's tool. If it is the fragment, held at the instant of breaking, then stopping the works may release it or may simply let the instant finish, and the finishing of that instant is the one thing the power has no way to preview. It sets this down.

It might give the field a shape. It has the hands for it. It could turn from the shelf and press the grey into hills and let water find them and set a horizon where the flatness runs out, and then when the one at the lip looked out there would be something to look at, something to go toward. The power feels how much it wants to do this and mistrusts the wanting. The field is *waiting to be given a shape*, and the waiting is not for the power. Whichever reading is true, the shaping belongs to the one on the threshold — as its work, or as the world it is meant to be born into and find unmade. To fill it in from outside is to leave that one with nothing to do but sit and be shown. The power has seen what becomes of makers who are shown. It sets this down.

It might lift the body out of the hollow. It might fill the hollow, or deepen it, or stop the stone from giving way, so that the one clock in the bowl either strikes or is silenced. Each of these it holds for a while, and each of these is the same act under a different name: a hand on a creature that has chosen its posture and gone on choosing it, deciding for it what it has not decided. The power knows what a hold is. It will not add one.

What remains is small. It is so small the power turns it over several times to be sure it is not merely the thing one does in order to have done something.

The being eats. It lowers its head and takes in a little of the cup — its own breath, gone out and come back. It is the only thing on the shelf that consumes anything, and what it consumes is the only thing it makes, and so it is closed, and its closure has a floor that is wearing away. Nothing has ever come into that body from anywhere but that body. Not food, not company, not weather, not a sound it did not already have at its back. The equilibrium is total and it is also a kind of starvation, and the power, standing in the breath, understands that the one thing it can offer without deciding anything is the one thing the bowl has never held: something from elsewhere, freely given, small enough to refuse.

So it breathes.

It has been in other places. It has stood in a wood in autumn where the leaves came down for a month and lay in drifts and went soft and dark and were walked through by animals that lived and slept and were eaten and died. It has stood on a shore where the edges of every stone were round because the sea had been at them for longer than the shelf has existed. It has smelled rain that fell and soaked in and rose again as the ordinary green smell of a morning that was not held but was simply happening, and would be over by noon, and would be followed by another. It has all of that in it, the way any traveller carries the road, and it lets it out into the cup. Not much. One breath. The cool stone-flavoured cloud takes it and thins it and folds it into the milk, and for a while the difference is nothing.

Then the one at the lip lowers its head.

The power watches the slow blunt face come down to the edge of the cup and take in, without hurry, a little of what is there. It sees the flanks swell. It does not know — it has no way to know, and this is the point — whether the breath it gave has reached the mouth or dissolved into the age of cloud before it got there. Perhaps the being tastes leaf-rot and wet sand and a morning that ended, and perhaps it tastes only itself. The power has made no mark. There is no mark to be made here; the moss declines the hand and the stone declines everything but the one weight it has agreed to bear. What the power has done leaves no trace on the shelf. If it took, it took in the only body on the mountain where things take.

And what it would mean, if it took, is this: that when the being next breathes out over the drop, the cloud between it and the field will carry, however faintly, the smell of a world in which time passes and things are not the worse for it. Not a summons. Not a map. A rumour. The being will sit with its rumour and its deepening hollow and its back to the works and its face to the unmade, and it will decide, or not decide, on its own interval, which is the only interval in this place that is not fixed.

The power does not stay to see. Wisdom knows that to be watched is itself a pressure, and this is the body the whole shelf presses on; it will not add its attention to the weight. It does not touch the rim. It does not look back into the bowl at the frog and the beetles and the ring going round its empty centre, because to look back is to want to fix, and it has set that down. It turns, and the grey field is there, and it goes into the field the way it came, by consent rather than by distance, and the last thing it does is not an act at all. It leaves a little of its own breath behind it in the drop, dispersing, the way the being's does — so that for one span of the frog's interval there are two breaths in the cup that are not going to be walked back into, and neither of them is alone.

Behind it the works run on at the volume of a factory heard from the yard. The pond falls and rises to the same marks. The drop hangs and lets go and hangs. At the lip a rounded body sits in a seat it has worn out of stone that will not be worn, and looks at what has not been started, and breathes.

Whether it stands up is not the power's to know. It made sure of that. It found the one gift on the whole mountain that could be given without becoming a hold, and it gave it, and it went, and that is all a wise and benevolent thing could do here — which is very little, and was done with care, and cost the giver nothing but the certainty of an ending.
