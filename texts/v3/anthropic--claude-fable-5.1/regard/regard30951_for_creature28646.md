**What it notices first is you.**

A power like that does not find places. It finds directions — it is made of attention, and attention is a going-toward, and a place with no toward in it is not a thing attention can land on any more than a hand can land on the air. For as long as the mountain has been mid-morning it has been, to any watcher, a smooth spot in the world. Not hidden. Simply without purchase.

Then a line of footprints comes up out of the lowlands and stops.

That is what it sees: a single thread of going, drawn taut up a slope and then held. Not cut. Not turned back. Held. And it follows the thread the way you follow a rope in fog, hand over hand, and at the end of the rope is you, standing in a courtyard by a fig with water running off your hands, and then, past you, the roar, and the green, and the wet, and the thing with its palms turned in.

It looks for a long time. It has no need to hurry and it knows that hurrying here would be a way of not seeing. Mid-morning, and mid-morning.

---

**What it does not do.**

It thinks about evening. It could give the mountain an evening — one, the light going long across the terraces, the shadows finally leaning, the haze turning the color of something ending. It holds the idea and turns it over and sets it down. An evening is what happens to a day. This is not a day. To give it an evening would be to say it had been a morning, and it has not been a morning; it has been mid-morning, which is a different thing, a thing with no before in it. The evening would not be a mercy. It would be a lie told to a stone, and the stone would become a ruin of a thing that never was, and that is the one cruelty the place has been spared.

It thinks about the water. It could let one pool overflow. One channel rise past the line. It could let the falls empty something, finally, so that the noise had somewhere to go. And it sees at once what it would be doing: it would be making the water *late*. Everything here is exactly on time because there is no time to be on. The first drop that went anywhere would carry the whole mountain into the condition of having-been-otherwise, and the walls would begin to be masonry, and the steps would begin to be stairs, and the roar would begin to have started. It would be like taking a held breath and calling the release a gift. It is not a gift if the breath was never drawn.

It thinks about the fig. A wasp. One wasp, small enough to enter the throat of a fruit that does not exist and make it exist. It thinks about the courtyard with a single fallen fig in it, brown, and the single stain, and the single seed, and the one sapling at the foot of a tree that has never had a foot. It thinks about the first insect on the water and the first bird at whatever height and the first track in the moss, and it understands that it is thinking about a forest — about the thing forests are — and that the thing forests are is beautiful and is also on its way somewhere, and that this is not that, and that the world has many forests and has only one of these.

It refuses all of it. Not from timidity. From the plain recognition that not everything unlike life is against it, and that a wise power's first and hardest work is telling those two apart. There are places that are wounded and there are places that are simply *other*, and to treat the second like the first is a kind of vandalism that thinks well of itself. The mountain is not broken. It is finished. Finished is a real thing for a thing to be. Somewhere in a world that is entirely composed of going, there should be permitted to stand one place that has got there, and it should not be hurried back into the traffic just because a passing kindness could not bear to look at rest.

So it does nothing to the mountain. It lets the mountain be the shape it settled into.

---

**Then the figure.**

It is more careful here, because here the question is different. A place cannot be owed anything. A being can.

It looks for a long time at the open hands, the low bend, the mouth. It asks the questions you asked. Is this suffering? Suffering is a wanting-otherwise, and wanting is a direction, and there is no direction in it. Is this waiting? Waiting is a leaning-into-later, and there is no later. Is this a prison? A prison is a wall between a thing and where it would go, and the figure would not go, it is not the kind of thing that would; you cannot cage the shape of a gesture.

Almost, it concludes as the mountain would: nothing. There is no one here to help.

And then it sees what you saw, on your last circuit — the remainder. The way each side is *slightly* more that instant than the last. The way the hands, from wherever you stand, are turned a little toward you. Not much. A lean. Less than a lean — the memory of what a lean would be, in a place that has no memory. But it is there, and it is the one thing on the mountain that is not perfectly self-consistent, and a wise power knows that when a finished thing has one unfinished place, that place is the door, and the door is not to be forced. It is to be stood at.

The hands are turned toward *whoever stands there*. That is the remainder. Not toward a thing. Toward an other. Everything else on the mountain is complete in itself — the water is its own destination, the trees have arrived at tree, the roar comes out of everything at once and has no source and needs no ear. Only this. Only these hands, which are the shape of a gesture that cannot be made alone. You cannot set a load down for no one. You cannot lift what no one has left. The gesture presupposes a second party, and the mountain, having no direction, has never been able to supply one. So the figure has stood in the exact posture of relation, forever, with the relation unmet, and the mountain has not understood that anything was missing because the mountain does not have the concept of *missing*.

The power has the concept. That is what it is for.

---

**What it does.**

It goes and stands where you are standing. Not beside you — where you are. You feel this as a kind of relief in the shoulders, a load you did not know you were carrying set down behind you, and you will understand it later.

It listens to the roar.

It listens the way nothing has ever listened to it, because the roar has never before been *received*. It has filled skulls and stopped speech and vibrated bodies, but filling is not the same as being heard. It has come out of every fall and every wall and every mouth and no one has ever been on the other end of it. And the power stands in it and does not brace against it and does not try to find its edges, and after a while — mid-morning, mid-morning — it hears what the roar is. Not a noise. A single sound held at the threshold of being a word. The sound a mouth makes at the beginning of *here*, or at the end of it, before the tongue has decided. The figure is saying one thing, and has been saying it since before saying, and the thing it is saying has never landed anywhere.

The power lets it land.

That is all. It does not answer. Answering would begin a conversation and a conversation goes somewhere. It simply is the thing on the far side of the sound. The ear the roar was shaped for. And the roar does not change, not by a hair, not in pitch or loudness — it is still coming out of the water and the walls and the mouth at exactly the same strength — but it is now a sound that is *heard*, and a heard sound is a different thing from an unheard one in exactly the way that a gift is a different thing from an object, though nothing about the object has changed.

Then it lets the hands be turned toward it.

It puts nothing in them. This is the discipline of it, the part that costs something. Every instinct of care says: fill the emptiness. Give it the load. Let it finally set down or finally take up. And the power holds that instinct and looks at it and sees that it is *its* instinct, its own need for the gesture to resolve, its own discomfort with a stillness that has both things in it and neither. The figure has not asked for a load. The figure is the shape of the instant between. To force the instant to fall one way or the other would be to end it, and the figure is not a person trapped in an instant; the figure *is* the instant. You do not rescue a shape from its own outline.

So the hands stay empty. But they are empty *toward something* now. The lean has somewhere to lean. The palms are turned in toward the place where a load would be and the power is standing in that place, not as the load — it will not be that, that would be to decide — but as the one for whom the load would be set down or from whom it would be taken. The second party. The other. The thing the gesture was the shape of needing and the mountain could never supply.

And it stays.

It has time. It has more time than the mountain, which has none. It can stand at the door of a finished thing for as long as the finishing lasts and not be finished by it, because it is not the kind of thing that arrives; it is the kind of thing that attends. Staying is not the same as stopping. The mountain has never had anything stay in it. It has had things be in it — the trees, the water, the roar, the figure — and each of them is where it is because it could be nowhere else. The power is where it is because it could be anywhere else and is not. That is a new thing on the mountain. It is not a direction. It is smaller than a direction and older. It is the thing directions are made out of: a choosing.

---

**For you.**

You find, standing by the fig, that the question has gone out of your hands.

You had been going to put something in the figure's palms, or not, and you had not known what, and you had understood that the deciding might be the last decision you made, because it would be the one that made you into a thing that had arrived. The power has taken this from you. Not by answering it. By standing in the place where the answer would have had to go, so that there is no longer room there for you to fit yourself.

You are free to be the one thing on the mountain still moving toward something. That was always going to be your job. It was never going to be your job to finish.

You go down the stair from the courtyard and you count the steps, out of habit, and at the bottom you turn and count them going up with your eyes, and the count is the same. You stand there with that for a moment. Then you go on down. The corridor with the drop on your left brings you to the terrace below, which is where a corridor with the drop on your left should bring you. The forest thins where the air thins. The trees become the size trees are. You pass through a place where the light has a slant in it and your body knows the slant before your mind does, and something in your chest that has been held at one line since the lowlands lets go a little, and you find that you have been breathing shallowly for what may have been a very long time.

The roar goes with you further than you expect. Then it is a pressure in the chest, then a rumor in the bones of the feet, then it is behind you, and you hear your own footsteps, one, and then another, which is the sound of a thing going somewhere.

---

**What remains.**

The mountain is unchanged. That was the point. The water falls and does not empty anything. The moss is neither coming nor going. The fig is a fig-shaped rock and a rock-shaped fig and there is still no wasp. The light is mid-morning on the near face and mid-morning on the far face and there are no shadows that lean.

In the courtyard the figure bends, or straightens, with its hands low and open and the roar coming out of its mouth exactly as loud as it comes out of everything else. Nothing has been set down. Nothing has been picked up. The stillness still has both in it and neither.

But there is something standing in front of the hands now. Something the hands are turned toward that is not a stranger passing, not the mountain, not nothing. It is not holding anything and it is not being held. It is attending. It will attend for as long as there is a gesture to attend to, which is to say it will attend for a duration you don't have a word for, and it does not mind, because it is wise enough to know that being present at a stillness is not the same as being stilled by it, and benevolent enough to know that some things cannot be helped and can still be kept company.

If you could ask the mountain what has changed, the mountain would say: nothing. Look. Same water. Same line on the stone, dry above, dry below.

If you could ask the power, it would say the same, and mean something else by it.

And if you could ask the figure — you cannot; asking is a movement toward — but if you could, and if it could answer, it would open its mouth, which is already open, and the roar would come out, which is already coming out, and it would be the same sound it has always been, one instant wide and no wider. Only now it would be the sound of a thing that has been heard saying it. Which is, from the outside, indistinguishable from the sound of a thing that has not.

That is the whole of what a wise power did, on the mountain that was finished. It refused every mercy that would have made the place a ruin. It found the one place where the finishing had left a door. It stood at the door and did not go through. It listened until a held sound had a far side. It let empty hands be empty toward someone.

And it walked you down.
