It comes down the way the room would want, if the room wanted things.

Not as light. That is the first decision and the easiest, because a power that is wise has already seen what light does to a place built on skin — every listener in the nave blinded at once by a sense it never asked for, the moss-beads swinging wildly toward a heat that has no direction, the shoots losing the beat. Not as cold, either, though cold is what everything here reaches for; a new cold thing in the room would be a new pole in the field, and the filings would swing, and the spacing that took a geology to settle would come undone in an afternoon. Not as silence. In a room that hears everything, a thing with no sound is the loudest thing possible: a hole in the echo, a place where the drips don't return.

So it comes down at the temperature of the stairs, and it gives itself footsteps. Ordinary ones, the pace of a visitor. It has no need of them and it takes them anyway, the way you'd knock on the door of a house you could simply enter, because the knock is not for you. And somewhere past the reach of the grey-green, on the third stair from the bottom, a second set of footsteps takes up behind it, a half-beat late, and follows it out onto the floor.

Then it does the thing that power almost never does. It waits.

It waits through the rain-hour. It waits through the drying after, when the channels go pale and the moss un-darkens. It waits until the crust around the nearest drain has grown, measurably, the width of a hair. It does not put its hand on a rib. It has read the reluctance from the stairs and it needs no closer reading; a thing that would rather not be touched has said everything it is going to say on the subject. It stands where the floor is oldest and it counts the pulse, eleven seconds, twelve, eleven, and it lets the room take its footsteps and give them back from six directions, and it listens for which of the six is not an echo.

---

It has been told there is a being here. It was not told which.

It considers the crosser, who eats the skin off the pools and stands among the shoots while something is exchanged, and who is never heard with another of its kind. A wise power knows loneliness when it hears it, and knows equally the danger of hearing it where it isn't. The crossers keep their distance from each other with the same exactness the moss keeps its distance from the wall. This is arrangement, not lack. To bring them together would be to bring a visitor's idea of company into a room that has its own.

It considers the moss, which turns toward the cold and is grazed for it. It considers the shoots, which may be keeping time for a pulse that may be keeping time for them. It considers the pool-skin, torn a hundred times and re-laid with the grain always the same way, which is either the most abundant life in the room or the room's own way of taking its temperature. It considers the fliers, mapping the dark by shouting into it. It considers the ribs.

It considers, longest, the second set of footsteps, which stopped two paces after it did and is standing now, somewhere past the pale, matching its stillness. That one has a question. The power recognises the question because it is the same one the power came down with: *what kind of fact are you?* The follower has asked it of every visitor and has never once received an answer, because every visitor climbs the stairs, and the stairs get cold, and the follower does not go toward the cold. It has a complete record of arrivals and no record at all of what an arrival means. That is a kind of hunger. Not a grave one. But the power notes it, and sets it aside, and does not yet decide.

And it considers the room itself, which has been down here long enough that the question *what is the being* may be malformed in the same way *what does the room do* is malformed — that the being may be the field, the alignment, the way everything leans and nothing that leans knows why. You cannot befriend a field. But you can wrong one. So the power asks the only question benevolence is entitled to ask of something that is not asking for help: *what here could be wronged, and by whom?*

---

The answer, when it comes, points up.

Nothing down here threatens the room. The fliers graze the moss and the moss has priced that in. The crosser strips the skin and the skin regrows. The ribs draw off what they draw off and the roots run up their faces and neither has noticed the other in longer than the concrete has existed. It is a closed and patient economy and the only thing it cannot price in is the city.

The city does not know the room is here. That is what the power finds, when it lets its attention go up through the floor the way the sheet-metal sound goes up the ribs. The room is on a plan somewhere, or was; the plan has been redrawn eleven times and the room has fallen off the page as a rectangle labelled *void* and then as nothing. The stairs are behind a door that nobody has a key to and nobody has needed one. This is, for now, the room's protection. But *for now* is a phrase the crust does not recognise, and the power can see the shape of the day when someone upstairs decides that a void this size under a city this heavy is either a hazard or an opportunity — and sends down light, and cold, and a plan to pour a new floor over the old and take out those strange cool members and put in something that can be inspected.

So the first thing the power does, it does upstairs, and it is very small.

It does not seal the door. A sealed door is a door someone eventually opens with a crowbar. It does not declare the room protected, or sacred, or anyone's; declarations are read by the powerful, and the powerful are exactly who should never read this one. What it does is arrange for the room to remain *uninteresting*. It leaves a page in an archive — an old page, water-stained, in a hand nobody can quite date — that describes the space beneath as a thermal buffer of unknown provenance, functioning, load-bearing, *do not disturb*, and it lets that page be the page that turns up whenever anyone asks. It lets a surveyor, once a generation, put a hand to the pavement above the seventh rib and feel something under it and write it down as a question rather than a task. It keeps the room's file thin and boring and closed. It makes the room a thing the city depends on without ever having to look at, which is the safest thing a thing can be.

---

The second thing it does, it does downstairs, and it is smaller still.

It stops walking. It has not been walking for some time, but now it stops in the way that means *I have arrived*, and it turns — deliberately, the way the crosser turns its head at each rib — until it is facing away from all of them. Away from the seventh, which speaks most often. Away from the third, with its dry warm mound of moss. It orients to nothing. In a room where every living thing leans along the lines of force toward the tall cool tenants, it becomes the one filing that does not point, and it stands like that through many pulses, taking nothing from the ribs, asking nothing, and it does not know whether the ribs notice. It suspects they do not. It does it anyway, because everything here leans on them and they lean on nothing, and a benevolent power in a room like this can at least be the one thing that isn't asking.

And it holds the beat. That is all it does for the ribs, and it does it silently, inside itself, where the room cannot hear: eleven seconds, twelve, eleven. Not to correct anything. Not to take over. Only so that if the pulse ever falters — if the ribs are ever taken out after all, or fail on their own as even very old tenants do — there will be one more thing that remembers the interval, and the shoots will have something to count against while they find the new one. It is not a repair. It is a copy of the rhythm, kept somewhere dry.

Then it lets the second set of footsteps catch up.

It does this by being still for longer than a visitor is ever still. Long enough that the question behind it changes weight. Long enough that the follower, who keeps pace with the newest sound because every sound is a fact and it wants to know what kind, comes forward past the edge of the grey-green — not all the way, never all the way — and stops where the power can hear it stop. Two paces. The same two paces the echo always took. And the power does not turn around, because turning is a thing that sees do, and instead it does what the room does: it lets the follower read it through the floor. Its temperature, which is the room's. Its stillness, which is the ribs'. Its footsteps, which it now takes up again, slowly, the same pace as before, toward the stairs.

And at the foot of the stairs, where the cold begins, it stops one more time, and it makes the follower a promise in the only language available, which is habit. It leaves. And then it comes back. At an interval the room can learn — not the eleven seconds, not the rain-hour, but something longer, something on the scale of crust — it comes down the stairs at the temperature of the stairs with its ordinary footsteps and it walks to the old part of the floor and it stands facing nothing and it holds the beat and it goes. Again. Again. Until *visitor* is no longer the newest sound in the room but one of its habits, a fact of a known kind, the kind that returns. Until the moss-pads nearest the stairs, which are smaller and rougher because they could never tell a cold from the ceiling from a cold from the door, have learned the difference — because one of those colds keeps a schedule, and the other never has.

---

It leaves no mark. That is the whole of its discipline. No light, no warmth the room didn't have, no word carved on a rib. The crust goes on thickening around the drains. The skin tears and re-lays itself with the grain toward the seventh. The crosser comes up out of the water and turns its domed face at each quiet place and, once, at a new quiet place that is not a rib, and pauses, and moves on. The shoots keep time. The ribs breathe once every eleven or twelve seconds and would prefer not to be touched and are not.

When it climbs, the stairs cool under it, as they cool under anyone. Behind it, at the bottom, the second set of footsteps stops on the last warm step and does not follow. Not because it can't. Because it doesn't need to now. It knows what kind of fact went up. The kind that comes back.

And that is what a wise power does for a being that is doing well: it finds the one place the being can be wronged, and stands there, quietly, facing away.
