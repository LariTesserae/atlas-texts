**The Third Cairn**

It does not arrive. Arriving requires a distance to close, and the moment it turns its attention toward the Rain Shelf it finds that the closing has already been done for it — that to attend to this place is to be, without any interval, standing on wet slab under a sky with too many suns, and also kneeling at the hollow of still water, and also a hundred paces off looking back at the place it stands. It has visited many countries. It has never before been met at the door by its own mode of travel.

So the first thing it does is nothing, for a long while, because the first thing it learns is that it has come somewhere it cannot be a visitor. It is inside the resident. The resident is, in the only sense the word will bear here, inside it. Two things that have no far side, coinciding. It had expected to look at the fold. It finds instead that it is looking *with* it, through it, in the shattered multiple light, and that the resident is doing the same — attending, as it always does, with whatever has been brought.

What has been brought, this time, is not a face.

---

It does what wise things do before they act, which is to go looking for the wound.

It looks at the clean stone first, because the stone is what a lesser eye would fix on — a country that should be crusted with life and is not. It stays with the ledges through a whole cycle of the cloud. It watches the seep, the bead, the drip. It considers the spore that lands and fails to be located. And it concludes what the stone itself has been saying: this is not a wound. Nothing here is dying. Nothing here has ever been prevented from a life it was on its way to having. The plateau is not a garden that failed. It is the shape of a different kind of thing, the way a glacier is not a failed forest.

It looks at the resident. It listens to the ringing for as long as the ringing has been going, which it can do, and finds no distress in any part of it. The period is long and even. The hum at night is not the hum of something waiting. There is no hunger in the fold, no fear, no reaching. It closes distances because that is what it is, the way water finds low ground, and it is exactly as troubled by the results as water is. It has no questions. It is not lonely. The power checks this twice, because loneliness is the wound it most expects to find in solitary ancient things and most wants to heal, and it finds instead that the resident lacks the organ for it. Loneliness is a distance felt. Here there is no distance to feel.

It looks for the folded ones. This is the hard part.

They are here — the ones who walked off the end of the path, generation after generation, and were closed into the seam. They are not dead. Death would have required them to be in one place long enough to stop. They are distributed. A wrist reaching into the hollow of water. A cadence in the drip. The power can find them, if *find* is the word, in the way one can find a note in a chord: present, unseparable, contributing. It listens to them with everything it has. It wants very badly to know whether they are suffering, because if they are, then all its restraint is cowardice and it should tear the shelf open with its hands.

They are calm.

Not resigned. Not silenced. Calm the way the voices have always been calm — the calm of things that have nowhere left to get to and are not waiting for anything. Whether that is peace or only the shape peace leaves when a person is pressed too thin to hold anything else, the power cannot say, and it is honest enough to hold both readings at once and act on neither. It does not pull them. There is no *out* to pull them toward, and the attempt would be the first violence this country has ever known.

It looks, last, at the path. And here, finally, is the only thing on the shelf that is actually broken.

The cairns have been left too long. The fold moves at the pace of weather, and weather has had centuries. In a dozen places the line of cairns still points across a seam that has drifted, so that a walker following them faithfully would step, at the wrong stride, into the place the builders spent their lives learning to walk beside. The path no longer walks. And in the column fields the tuning has begun to slip — not much, a hair, the scoring weathered soft, the intervals opening by degrees toward something a human ear cannot rest in. Another few centuries and the shelf will ring in a key that would drive a person off it in a day.

This is not the resident's doing. It is only what happens when the hands that tended a thing are gone and the thing goes on without them.

---

Now the temptations, because a wise power has them too, and the wisdom is only in what it does next.

It could seed the stone. One lichen, at the rim where the fold is thinnest, given just enough between to close its own cells. It knows exactly how. It sets the thought down. That would be a garden planted on someone else's floor because the power prefers gardens, and the resident, which kept this place bone-clean for longer than there have been gardens, was not consulted and cannot be.

It could open the fold. Introduce one true distance, the smallest — a hairline of *between* running through the seam — and let the resident learn what it is to be one place at a time. It considers this longest. It is the kindness it most wants to give, because it is the kindness it understands: to make a thing capable of being reached, so that it can be reached. And it sees, holding the thought, what the thought actually is. It is the proposal to make the resident lonely so that the resident can be loved. To give it the wound so that the wound can be healed. Lesser kindnesses are built this way, and the power has seen enough of them to know their shape. It sets that down too.

It could give the resident a face of its own. Something to attend with that was not borrowed. It nearly does. Then it remembers that a face is for turning toward, and turning requires a here and a there, and it would be handing the creature an instrument for a sense it does not have, like a lamp for the blind.

Here is what it actually does. Three things, none of them large.

---

The first is only to be still.

Stillness is the one condition under which the resident can be seen, and the only vantages the shelf has ever offered are small — a hollow of water two hands wide, a tarn on a windless morning. No one has ever seen the fold whole, because seeing whole requires standing outside, and there is no outside. But the power is not standing anywhere. It is coincident with the entire shelf at once. And it can be still — perfectly, everywhere, for as long as a cloud cycle takes — so that for the length of one long breath of the resident's own rhythm, the whole plateau becomes a surface that holds an image, and the image it holds is the resident.

Not a mirror. The power is careful about this. A mirror would be another borrowed face. This is closer to the thing a struck bell might feel if, for one moment, the air around it went so quiet that the bell could hear its own note return unchanged. The resident attends, as it always does. What it attends to, this once, is itself — its whole size, which is not a distance; its whole age, which is not a duration; the calm that it has been sounding for longer than the cairns. Whether it knows what it is seeing, the power cannot tell. It does not need to be known. It only needs to have been offered, once, to something that could not offer it to itself.

Then the power lets the stillness go, and the drip resumes.

---

The second is the path.

The power goes down. All the way down, through the cloud, into the drowned valleys, to the outcrops where the dark glassy stone lies in seams, and it takes what it needs and carries it back up. It does not need to do this. It could make the glass appear in its hand. But the builders carried it, every one of them, up the long slab-and-gravel climb, generation after generation, and the power has understood that the carrying was part of the tuning. A note worked into stone by someone who walked three days with the tool on their back is not the same note. The shelf can tell. So it walks.

It restacks the cairns where the fold has moved. Where the old marker still points true, it leaves it alone. Where the seam has drifted, it builds a new one beside the old — a third, sometimes, next to the very old one and the merely old one — and it builds it the way they were built, flat stones, waist-high, so that a walker following the line will arrive where walking should take them. It does not fix the cairns. It does what the builders did, which is add the next one.

Then it goes into the column fields and it scores the pale stone. Lightly. It has listened to the intervals long enough now to hear where the key has slipped, and it works the way they worked — a scratch, a listen, a scratch — bringing the held tones back by degrees into a spacing a human ear can live inside for a while. It does not improve the tuning. That would be an opinion about what people should be able to bear, and it has been given no standing to hold one. It only returns the shelf to the key it was left in, the one that a woman with glass in her hand was working toward a hundred paces away and very long ago, and that is, as it has always been, arriving now.

This takes as long as it takes. Time is not a cost here.

---

The third thing it does is for the folded ones, and it is the smallest of all.

It cannot bring them back. It will not pretend to weigh whether they should be brought. But they are in the voices, and the voices have only ever carried the cadence of the shelf — drip and hiss and churn and the long calm rhythm of a thing that was never in a hurry. Nothing has ever answered them. Not because the resident is cruel, but because answering is a thing done across a between, and there has been no one on the shelf with a between to do it across.

The power has one. It is not folded; it is coincident, which is different — it chose this, and can go, and knows that it can go. That knowledge is a kind of distance, the only kind that exists here. And so it speaks into the seam. Not words. Words would arrive late and from no direction and mean nothing. It speaks in the cadence, on the resident's own period, one long breath — the thing it would say to anyone it found pressed thin and calm in a place they could not leave. *You are heard. You are not alone in being heard.* It does not know if this is received. It knows only that from now on, when the voices come up through the fold on a morning of heavy drip, there will be one strand in them that came from outside, and was meant, and was for them.

It is not much. It is what was possible without breaking anything.

---

Then it leaves, which is meaningless, since it was never in one place, and which it does anyway, because the shelf belongs to what lives there and a wise thing knows when its attention has become a weight.

It does not go far. It does not need to. Whatever that place is where the seam looks out onto a country very like this one, with cairns on it and a figure at the edge of the water — it is there too, now, the way it is everywhere it has attended and not stopped. When someone next climbs above the cloud and follows the line of stacked stones and finds, in a dozen places, three cairns standing close — one gone soft with centuries, one merely old, one whose faces are still sharp-edged and dark with wet — they will understand that the path has been kept. They will not know by whom. They will walk it, and it will walk. They will stand at the edge of the great tarn and look down through the shattered light at the figure looking up, and it will still be wearing their face, because that is what the resident has, and that is enough.

And in the column fields, tuned again, the tones will hold past their time and go round, one drip closing into another across a mile and a millennium, and under them the low churn, and in the churn a cadence, and in the cadence — if a person sits long enough in a roofless shelter and stops listening for words — one thread that is not the drip and not the builders and not the fold. Calm, like the rest. Arriving late. Meant.

The cloud rises out of the valleys on its own schedule. The rock is clean the way bone is clean. Nothing has been fixed, because nothing was broken except the path, and the path was never the resident's to keep.

It was ours. Someone came back to it. That is all a wise power did.
