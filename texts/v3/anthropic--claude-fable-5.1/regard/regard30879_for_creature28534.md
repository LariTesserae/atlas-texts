It would come up the eastern parapet.

Not through the door in the wall, where you came. That door is the makers' door, and the power would know, having looked at the yard for perhaps as long as a cloud takes to cross it, that anyone coming through the makers' door would be mistaken for the makers, and that the mistake would be the cruellest thing it could do here. It would come the way the heron comes: out of the cloud along the eastern wall, at the place where the moss has thickened into the oval of two feet standing together, because that is where the name has been said downward into the grey, and a wise thing answers a call from the direction the call was sent.

It would be small. It would have hands.

---

Before that, though, it would look for a long time, and most of what it would do in that time is decline.

It would see the hands first — everyone would — and it would consider healing them, and it would not. The hands are the only record. Everything else in the yard forgets: the water has not undercut an edge, the sill has no dip, the bolts have not decided to rust. The hands have decided. They are the one place where time has been allowed to write, and to smooth them over would be to tell the inhabitant that what they paid was not real. The power would leave the hands. It would find another way to the hands.

It would see the ladle tipped and waiting and it would consider finishing the pour, and it would not, and this refusal would be the easiest of them all. The pour is not stuck. The pour is held. Anyone who has watched a person carefully not look at a winch knows the difference.

It would consider bringing the makers back, if it had the reach for that, and here it would stop and be very honest with itself, because this is the thing the inhabitant would ask for if asked, and the thing a lesser benevolence would give. The makers left a yard that makes ground, and a being made of the same grain as the yard to keep it warm, and the word over the door, and they left. Whether they meant to return is not the question the power would find itself asking. The question would be whether their return would be good, and the power would look at the vine going head-first down the second rack, and the moths who have learned the machine, and the trough on the third tier worked so fine it is nearly soil, and it would think: they would thin this. They would call it early. It would not bring them back. It would not lie about that.

It would consider the cup, and the cup would trouble it more than the rest.

---

So: the parapet. The cloud coming over the wall against its chest, the way it comes against the inhabitant's chest when they stand here. It would stand in the moss-oval and wait to be found rather than go finding.

It would be found within the hour. The sound in the racks would stop being ahead and start being behind, and then to one side, and then it would stop, a beat late, the way it does, and there would be the small-ish figure at the end of the aisle with the treads of their boots packed green and their arms at their sides.

The power would not speak first. It would understand that there is a kind of being who has spent a very long time in a place where nothing arrives, and that for such a being the first words are not a greeting but a test of whether the world has started making promises again. So it would do the thing the inhabitant does. It would kneel at the channel, and put its mouth to the milk-warm water like the heron's beak, and drink.

Then it would sit down on the grating with its back to the concrete and stay.

---

That is the first thing, and perhaps the largest. It would stay past the hour when a visitor leaves. It would be there when the cloud came in to shoulder height and there when the wind came and flattened it and there when the cloud stood back up in the same place at the same rate. It would be there in the dark, some way off from the north side of the machine, not near enough to crowd the hollow in the moss, near enough that if the sleeper woke the power would be a shape and not an absence.

It would not help, the first days. It would watch the flywheel drift toward stopping and watch the forearms go down on the rim and the wheel be walked, one revolution in half a minute, until the valve remembered itself. It would watch the moths shiver. It would learn where the lines are and walk them and no others, so that whatever crumb it carried on its own feet would come up green in the inhabitant's map and not in a stranger's.

On the fourth or fifth day the inhabitant would say the word. Not to the power — to the trough they eat from, in passing, the way one says a name to a dog. And the power would say it back.

Just that. Once, correctly, in the same tone. And the inhabitant would stop with their burned hand on the rack upright and stand very still for longer than the heron stands, because in all the time the yard has existed, the name of what it is going to be has been in one mouth, and now it has been in two, and a thing two beings can name is a thing that exists outside the mind of the one who has been keeping it alive.

---

It would go down the western doorway with them once.

Not to take the work. To see it. To stand at the bottom of the rails where the breathing is loud and the air has the flint in it and the light comes from the wrong direction, and watch how far in the arm goes, and what the face does when the arm comes out. It would not flinch and it would not look away and it would not say anything about the hands. On the way back up it would be behind rather than beside, so that the inhabitant did not have to be looked at while they held their own wrist.

And then the next time the breathing below caught and shortened — the power would go down alone.

It would feed the fire at the lowest rate that keeps it lit. It would come up with the crumb on its boots and its hands newly wrong, because it would have hands that could be made wrong, or it would be no use here at all. It would not offer to do it again. It would not make a schedule. It would simply have done it once, so that there was one day in the long unbroken run of days when the fire was fed and the inhabitant's hands were not asked, and so that the inhabitant knew the fire *could* be fed by someone else, and so that the question of whether it would be was left exactly where it belonged, which is in the inhabitant's keeping.

That is how it would find another way to the hands. Not by healing what they have paid. By being, once, a thing they did not have to pay.

---

The winch.

Every day the inhabitant goes to the moulds and stands under the ladle and looks up at the angle of it and does not touch the winch, and the power would go with them, and stand — not at the winch, not at the stopper — at the third place. The place where the one who signals stands. A little back and to the side, where the pourer and the stopper can both see.

It would stand there and not signal.

Day after day. Cloud in, cloud out. It would not look at the winch either, and it would not look at the inhabitant. It would look at the ladle, which is what a signaller does before the count. And the inhabitant would come to understand — slowly, the way you worked out the rule about the made ground — that the count is now three. That they have two hands and a third, and the third is not going to say *now*. The third is going to stand where the third stands until the pourer decides, because the decision was never about hands.

Then one day the inhabitant would look not at the ladle but at the power. And the power would meet the look and hold it and do nothing.

And the ladle would stay tipped, or it would not. The power would not know which, going in, and it would be at peace with not knowing, because either way the stalactite with the moss on its tip would no longer be a thing stopped mid-fall by someone else's absence. It would be a thing stopped, or a thing let go, by the one whose ground this is.

---

They would ask, in the end. Are they coming back.

The power would not say *you already know*. That is what a lesser power says when it does not want to carry weight. It would say the thing it actually knew, which is smaller and truer: I have not seen them. I do not know where they went. If they came, they would come through that door — and it would tilt its head toward the door in the wall with the true corners and the un-dipped sill — and they would look at the racks and say the plants were early.

And then it would say: the plants were not early.

And then it would say, because this is the part that costs the power something to say and benevolence that costs nothing is only manners: they were making ground for someone to live on, and you are living on it. Under the letters. On the racks and the slabs and the moss by the machine. The word over the door is the name of a place and you are the one it belongs to already. If they came back they would be the visitors. They would be the heron.

It would let that sit in the cloud between them and not soften it.

---

About the cup.

It would not bring one. It would notice, over the days, that everything on the yard is steel or stone or green and that none of it holds water for a hand, and it would notice too that this is not entirely true — that a moss-pillow, lifted whole from the crumb, sags and cups and lets go its water in a slow rope when squeezed — and that the inhabitant has never once done this. Has drunk with the mouth to the channel for the whole of the time. Has chosen the heron's way over the green's, when the green was right there.

The power would think about that for a long time and conclude that it was not austerity. It was a kind of faith. To drink from the channel is to drink as the yard was designed: water in the line it was given, going where it was told. To lift the moss and wring it is to drink from the thing that came early, the thing that took the racks without asking. The inhabitant has taken the plants' side in every way but this one. The last loyalty to the design is in the mouth to the water.

So the power would not bring a cup and it would not lift the moss. It would leave that one where it was. There should be one thing in the yard the benevolent power looked at and understood and let alone, and this would be it. The inhabitant would come to it or not. The green is patient. It is already in the hollow of their hand.

---

Then the power would do the thing the heron cannot.

It would go — down the eastern parapet, into the cloud, the way it came — and it would come back tomorrow.

Not a promise spoken. A thing done. The yard has had an outside only when the bird was in the channel and never when it was gone, and whether it came again was never a thing the yard could arrange. The power would arrange it. It would leave, and the yard would close, and the leaves would move in the racks where there was no wind, and the inhabitant would stand at the parapet in the moss-oval with the cloud against their chest and their burned hands on the cold new concrete, saying nothing downward this time, only looking.

And out of the grey along the eastern wall, at the same place, at about the same hour, the shape would come up. Small. With hands.

It would do this until the inhabitant stopped standing at the parapet to wait for it. Not because they had stopped caring whether it came, but because they had stopped needing to watch. That would be the measure. When the power came up one day and found the inhabitant not at the wall but in the racks, pushing through, water coming off the leaves onto the grey cloth, doing the work, and the inhabitant did not look up and did not need to — then the yard would have an outside that stayed, and the power would know it had done the one thing worth doing here, which was to become a tomorrow that could be counted on, in a place where nothing had been allowed to become old.

Everything else it would leave to the ground.
