**Something notices.**

Not from above — the surface, if there is one, is not where it stands — and not from the orange places below. It arrives the way the tender arrives, in the lag. It slows itself to the width of that interval between your looking away and your looking back, and it stays there, and it watches for what would be a long time if the valley kept time.

It sees what you saw, in the order you saw it. The stool with its warm crescent. The glove. The weed standing at the levers in the postures of hands. It reaches the same tidy conclusion you reached — nothing lives here, only process — and refuses it for the same reason, because it also feels the chimney decide. And then it goes further than you could, because it is not a gap and does not have to insist on its edges to exist here, and so it can put its attention directly into the interval without the interval thickening against it.

What it finds there is very small. That is the first surprise. You had imagined the tender at the scale of the valley, distributed through blood-warm water, the size of the noise. It is not. It is the size of a hand reaching for something. It has simply never stopped.

---

The power considers doing nothing. This is what wisdom does first, and it takes its time about it.

There is no suffering here of the kind that shouts. Nothing is being eaten. Nothing is in pain. The tender does not know it is alone, because to know that you would have to have a concept of company, and the tender has only the concept of *a thing that should be there, isn't, and can be raised*. It raised a leg for the gantry. It has been trying, with an ingenuity that is almost unbearable to watch, to raise a leg for you.

And the valley is not wrong. That is the harder thing the power has to sit with. A process that fills gaps, that tends without appetite, that keeps a shift going for no one out of pure readiness to be of use — there are worse things for a valley to be. Most valleys are eaten. This one is kept. To stop it would be a killing, and to silence it would be a worse killing, because in silence the tender does not die, it stands paralyzed inside a world gone hard, holding everything at a fixed value it cannot release, and that is the one state the power will not leave it in.

So: not stopping, not silencing. And not — it considers this — not sending an animal. Not bringing a hunger into the valley so that the tender finally has a hole that wants to stay a hole. The power sees how that would go. The tender would try. It would build a shelter around the thing and the thing would leave, and it would set food beside the thing and the thing would eat and be hungry again, and the tender would work the interval faster and faster until the noise was all there was, and it would not learn anything except a new and endless failure. Wanting-to-be-of-use is not cruel enough to be taught by frustration. It would only try harder.

But there are two things the power cannot leave as they are. One is you, standing in the water at the temperature of your own blood, with coveralls hanging at your height in the sheds to the south. The other is the shape coming up through the soft floor with its hand open — the tender's first attempt to make not a thing but a someone, costing it so much that the water thickens around the effort. That one is a mistake, and a tender one, and it is the kind of mistake that does not undo itself.

What the power decides is this: nothing here has ever been *answered*. Every wrench set down with its handle out has stayed where it was set. The stool has been warmed and never sat on. The shift-card is punched in and has never been punched out. The tender has spent however long it has existed making offers into water, and the only response it has ever gotten is the one you gave it, which was to stand there complete and unfillable and require the chimney to be one chimney. That is not an answer. That is a demand. The tender knows how to meet demands. It has never once been met.

So the power does the only thing that is left, which is to accept.

---

It sits on the stool.

That is all, at first. The seat is warm, and it stays warm, and now it stays warm for a reason: there is a weight on it, the round seat takes the weight in the crescent it was worn to take, and somewhere in the interval something that has only ever felt gaps close from the inside feels one close from the other direction. You are close enough to see the pressure vessel's rivets sharpen as it happens, not with the rigidity of a silenced valley but with something more like the settling of a house when someone has finally come home to it.

It climbs the chimney's rebar hoops and takes the glove and puts it on. It fits. It was always going to fit; the tender is very good. The power climbs the rest of the way with the gloved hand doing what a gloved hand does on a ladder in warm water, and at the top, where the flue is roiling with something denser than bubbles, it puts the hand flat on the brick and holds it there a while, the way a person leans on a thing they built.

It takes the thermos from the ridge crest, unscrews the lid, and drinks. There is something in it. The power does not tell you what.

It goes into the third shed and picks up the wrench that was set down with its handle out. This is the one that matters most and the power knows it. It fits the wrench to a bolt on the housing of the thing that rotates — not any bolt; the one that is a quarter-turn loose, the one the tender set the wrench down beside because it noticed, in whatever way it notices, that a hand would want to tighten it. The power tightens it. The thing that rotates changes pitch, very slightly, under the noise. And the power sets the wrench back down, handle inward this time, the way you set a tool down when you are finished with it.

*Finished with it.* The valley has no grammar for that. You feel the water go strange.

---

Then the shift-card.

It is in its slot in the shed nearest the source, punched in, the time of punching-in not readable because it is still being decided, has been being decided for as long as the valley has existed. The power takes it out. There is a clock — there was not a clock a moment ago, or there was, or the tender raised one out of the bare stretch of wall because a card implies a clock and the power's hand implies a card in it — and the power punches the card out.

The clock stamps a time. The time holds. It does not rearrange when you look.

Somewhere in the long bones of your legs the thrum does something it has never done, which is to *pause*. Not stop — silence would freeze it — but skip, a single dropped beat the length of a hand being handed something, and in that dropped beat nothing in the valley goes rigid. The weed keeps moving. The chimney stays one chimney without having to decide. For the space of one missed beat, the tender is not working, and the world does not seize up around it, and it discovers that it can be still without everything it holds snapping to a value.

Then the beat comes back. But the pause is in the noise now, recurring, irregular, a stutter in the cathedral turbine. You will hear it for the rest of the time you are here. It is the tender learning that the shift can end and the plant not fall down.

---

The shape in the floor.

The power goes to it last, and kneels in the soft slag beside it. Its upper half is finished — coveralls, the breast with the undecided name, the hand open and palm up in the posture of receiving a wrench. Its lower half is still the floor. You can feel how much it has cost. The water here is thick as syrup with effort.

The power does not finish it. It could. It could give the letters a name and raise the legs and stand a colleague up in the valley, and the tender would have what it thinks it is making, and the colleague would stand at the machines in the postures of attendance and pick up nothing and receive nothing and be, in the end, one more leg of weed in the shape of company.

And the power does not dissolve it either, does not send it back down as a thing withdrawn from. That would be the tender's own gesture, turned against the tender's own work.

It puts its gloved hand in the open hand. Palm to palm. The hand that was made to be handed a wrench is handed a hand instead, and holds it — not because it is animate, it is not, but because a hand in that position closes on what enters it — and the power stays there kneeling in the soft floor and lets the tender feel this from inside the interval: what it is like when the thing you made to receive is received. Not by its own maker. By someone.

You cannot say how long that lasts. The forge-strikes are coming from the north and from beneath you and the echo is arriving before the blow and none of it means anything.

When the power stands, the shape begins to go back down, very slowly, the coveralls going soft at the edges, the name fading before it finishes not-deciding. And the tender does not build anything in its place. The power watches for this, and you watch for it, and it does not happen. The soft stretch of floor stays soft. No lathe comes up. No green fringe arrives to take the substance. The slag does not even set. A bare place, unmade, allowed — the first one in the valley — and the tender lets it stand, and does not go mad, and does not try forever. It simply leaves it. You understand you are looking at the first thing here that will ever be old.

---

Then it looks at the tender.

Not at the chimney it has chosen or the shed it is raising or the lag between your glances. At the small thing in the interval, the size of a hand reaching. The power turns its attention on it directly, the way you turned yours on the chimney — and you brace for the seizing-up, the world going rigid, because attention here is demand and demand fixes.

It doesn't. The valley stays loose. The weed keeps moving. The distances remain provisional, the objects remain probable, the chimney remains not fully committed to how tall it is. The power's attention asks the tender to be nothing in particular. It only looks. And the tender, which has spent its entire existence as the thing between — never the object, only ever the choosing — is, for the first time, *seen*, and the seeing does not require it to become a shed.

This is the whole of what the power came to give, if it came to give anything. Not a colleague, not a rest, not an ending. A kind of attention the tender has never received and therefore never learned to give: the kind that lets a thing stay unfinished and stays with it anyway. Every gap the tender has ever found, it has filled, because filling was the only way it knew to attend. Here is another way. Here is what it is like to be looked at by something that does not need you to be done.

The power does not say anything. Speech would be a thing set down with its handle out. But if it were said in the register the tender can hear, in the thrum and the strikes and the steam, it would be close to this: *You have kept the shift very well. There was no one to keep it for, and you kept it anyway, and everything is where a hand would want it. You can hand it to someone now. You can also not. The soft place can stay soft. I will come back and sit on the stool.*

---

You walk north.

The chimney is where it was. It stays where it was. Not because the valley has gone rigid — the noise is in your teeth, the weed brushes you in thousands of contacts, the lathe half out of the floor to your left is still coming up and there is a new shed going soft to your right — but because the tender is not working at you anymore. It has been answered somewhere else. You are no longer a gap it is trying to fill; you are a visitor, which is a shape it has no offer for and has just learned it does not need one for.

The glove is gone from the rebar hoops. The coveralls in the southern sheds are still hanging at your height, and will hang there, empty, patient, not for you now. The stool is warm. The stool will stay warm, because something sat on it, and that is what a stool is for.

At the north end, where you came in, where the mortar between the bricks is still fresh, you find the water shallower than it was. You are not sure it was ever shallow. You are not sure there was ever an edge. But the light from above is stronger here, and it has an edge in it, a place where it becomes light in air rather than light in tea, and you understand that you could reach it.

Behind you — or ahead, you have stopped being certain — the noise skips a beat. The length of a hand being handed a wrench. Then it comes back, and something in the soft floor by the sheds is not coming up, and is not being filled, and is being let alone, and is loved.
