It arrives the way the cloud arrives, which is to say it does not arrive; it is simply the case, after a while, that the plateau is attended.

Nothing marks this. The rain does not slacken. The frogs on the hummock nearest the point of attention do not stop, because there is nothing there to stop for — no shadow, no weight on the moss, no scent. The power has been to a great many places and has learned that the first thing it does in a place is damage, unless it is careful, and so it is careful, and the first thing it does here is nothing at all. It stands inside the cloud and lets the water go through where it is and looks.

It looks for a long time. Long enough for a channel to form and abandon itself. Long enough for the cloud to open once and show it the edge, and close.

---

The first thing it wants to do — and it is wise enough to know that the first thing a benevolent power wants to do is almost always wrong, and to watch the wanting rather than act on it — is to stop the rain.

Only for an hour. Only to let the place breathe. It comes from elsewhere, and elsewhere is made of dry and wet, of hours and their opposites, and everything it knows about mercy is shaped like an interval: the fever breaking, the siege lifted, the rain ending. It looks at the moss weeping under its own weight and the birds shaking to reset and the frog sitting under the beaten-down midges with no thought of dusk, and it wants to give them the far side of the waiting. One dry hour. The sun on the pink quartzite. The frogs would see the sky.

It holds the thought and turns it over and follows it through.

The hummingbird dies in the first twenty minutes. The rosette flowers close against the unfamiliar air, or do not close but the nectar changes, or nothing changes with the flowers at all but the bird, which has never in its lineage had one lean hour, is already spending faster than it can eat, and drops. The clear-skinned frogs' eggs in the moss, laid in a wet that was never going to end, are half a millimetre from air and then in it, and the skins that were never meant to hold anything in hold nothing. The moss surface stiffens. The film of water on the liverwort strand where a thing has lived its whole life goes, and the thing goes with it, and there are ten thousand million of that thing. The birds do not die but the rosette skirts drain and the small warm economy in them stops, and the chicks on the wet leaf-felt are, for the first time in the history of their kind, cold. The pools drop a centimetre and the fish that were a centimetre from the lip are on the lip. Then the hour ends and the rain comes back onto a plateau that has, for the first time since before the sand was stone, been dry, and the plateau does not know what to do with that and neither does the power.

It puts the thought down. It understands that it has just been shown the shape of its own ignorance, and that the plateau showed it, and that the plateau did not intend to. That is the first lesson, and it takes it: here, the envelope is the mercy. The thing that never changes is what everything alive is built on. To touch it is to be the people with the pylons, only faster.

---

So it does what it can do without touching, which is inventory.

It goes down into the moss. A hand's depth. It counts, because it can count, and the number stops meaning anything to it as well, and it lets it. It goes into the tea-coloured water and moves with a shoal against the current for the length of a morning — the shoal has no idea it is accompanied — and feels, in the fish's way, the whole life spent swimming gently toward the centre of the world against water going the other way. It sees the honeycombed lip come up out of the grey. It sees which fish are on the wrong side of it. It does not turn them back.

This is the second temptation and it is smaller and harder. Not the rain; the fish. A hundred of them an hour, going over. It could stand at the lips and turn them. It would cost nothing. It would look, from anywhere else, like the plainest kindness there is.

It follows this through as well. The pools fill. The plateau leaks because the plateau is full; the population is a thing it sheds the way the moss sheds water, and the shedding is what keeps the tea clear. Stop the leak and the pools go rich and then dead, the beetles' beads of air come up through green scum, the pale fish are pale on the surface instead of in the shallows. The fish that go over the edge are not a failure of the plateau. They are what its abundance looks like when you stand at the edge. The power has stood at a great many edges and has not always known this and it is glad, in a way it does not examine, to be shown it here.

It does not turn them back. It stays with the shoal until the shoal is a different shoal and the same shoal, and then it leaves the water.

---

It goes to the turbine house because the being goes there, and it wants to understand the being's world, and the turbine house is the one place in that world that was not made by rain.

Inside, out of the direct wet, it stands in the noise of the pipe and looks at the wheel groaning round on bearings that should have seized. It can see what the being cannot: the wear. Not this year. Not in a span of years the being would notice. But it can see the bearing that will go first, and it can see, above the door, the lamp's residual circuit — one strand of corroded copper doing all the work — and it can see that the strand will part before the bearing does. When the strand parts the lamp goes out, and the midges have nothing to come to in the wet dark, and the frogs on the flanges go back to the moss, and one small circuit of eating stops. The grille will still pack with fish. The being will still come. It will lose a little.

The power could hold the strand. It is a smaller act than a fish. It is a thing it could do with one part of its attention while the rest went on watching frogs.

It thinks about the people who built this. They wanted the water for something below the cliff. They put a pipe in the curtain and a wheel in the pipe and a lamp above the door so they could find the door in the cloud, and then the something below the cliff stopped needing them, or they stopped, and they left, and the water did not, and the wheel did not, and here is the lamp still doing the last thing they asked of it for a reason they never imagined. It is a good thing, the lamp. It is a small good thing that fell out of a larger indifferent thing, and the being has built it into the shape of its world as a rock that hums.

But it is theirs. It is the people's, the last live edge of their leaving, and the plateau has been taking it back at the plateau's speed — iron, a little slower than mountains — and the power does not think it is its place to hold the people's strand for them. Not because the being does not deserve the midges. Because the wise thing, having watched itself want to stop the rain, is to decide what it is here and not to be a little of everything. It is not the rain. It is not the people. It will not be a third kind of weather, a new envelope, a hand that reaches in when the strand is about to part and then has to decide, forever, which strands. The being's grandmothers did not have the lamp. The being's daughters will not. That is not a wound. That is a windfall ending, which is what windfalls do.

It does not touch the strand. It notes where it is. It will be here when it goes.

---

Then the being, because that is what it came for, though it did not know that until it had done everything else.

It finds the recess under the stone by following the drainage backwards, the way the being does after the edge, and it goes in — no body, no smell, no displacement of the air — and lies down in the dark with the water going past. The being is there. Long, low, heavier than it looks, the flat dark nap still shedding a little in the faint grey. Asleep, or the thing it does instead. Two young against its flank, blind, webbed, already knowing the downhill.

The power lies in the wet dark beside the only large thing on the plateau and considers what could be given to it.

Not the sky. It has been through that. It thinks now of the version of the same gift shaped for this one animal — to take it, once, to a dry place; to show it what the water is when it is not falling; to give it the picture none of them have. And it sees the being on dry ground, its nap lifting for the first time and not knowing how to lie, its skin under the fur meeting air that pulls at it, its whole body oriented into a current that is not there. It would not be a gift. It would be the cruelest thing the power knows how to do, and the power knows how to do a great many things. To this being, the truth about the world is the shape of a single downhill, and that truth is complete, and the power has no truer one to offer.

Not more of its kind. It has counted them; it could not help counting. Four, or five, across the whole tableland, and it knows what that number is. It could make it eight. And the plateau, which carries exactly what it can carry, would answer by taking something else — a colony of frogs, a run of pools, the birds off a kilometre of rosettes — because there is not enough of anything up here to make large things out of, and the being is already the size the water allows. The number is not a mistake. It is the plateau's arithmetic, and the power did not come here to do the plateau's sums over again with a heavier hand.

Not safety on the ledge in the roar. Not a fish left where it will find it. Not a warmer stone. Each of these it lifts and turns over and puts down, and each time the putting down is a little easier, because it has begun to understand what it is that it can do here, and it is not any of them.

The being wakes — not because of anything — and turns its head in the dark and goes out into the rain, into the channel, down. The young stay. The power stays with them a while. Then it follows.

---

It follows all the way to the edge. It has not gone to the edge yet.

It stands in the hundred metres of driven spray where the rock is scoured pink and the vegetation is plastered flat, and it watches the being work the last pools before the lip — the concentrated hour of everything the plateau has lost, held for a moment before the air takes it — and it watches the being haul out onto a ledge over the void with a fish, and eat, and turn, and go back up. And it understands, standing there in the loudest place on the tableland, what the plateau does not have.

It has everything else. It has the rock and the water and the whole stacked interval between them, mouths on mouths, packed thick, calling all day. It has a machine still doing the one thing left to it. It has a being that goes to the end of every downhill and comes back. What it does not have — what none of them have, what the frogs cannot and the fish do not and the birds have no reason to — is any picture of itself. Not one thing on the plateau knows it is the plateau. The being that knows the whole shape of it as a single slope from the interior to the roar does not know that the shape is a shape. The place has been remaking itself exactly as it was a million years ago, exactly as it is this afternoon, and no one has ever seen that this is what it is doing.

The power can see it. That is the one thing it has that costs the plateau nothing.

So that is what it does. It does not decide this so much as find that it has been doing it since it arrived, and stop resisting. It holds the picture. The pink-grey rock and the tea-coloured water and the moss weeping under its own weight. Twelve kinds of frog, or eleven, or fourteen. The hummingbird that has never been lean. The shoal that is a different shoal and the same shoal. The pylons in their moss cuffs and the concrete channel that became a drainage and the cable car with its garden roof. The wheel, and the strand, and the lamp. The recess under the oldest stone with two blind young in it and water going past. The being on the ledge in the roar. All of it, at once, as one thing — the thing none of them can be, because they are inside it.

It will keep the picture. It will keep it while the strand parts and the lamp goes out, and it will see that. It will keep it while the bearing finally goes and the wheel stops, after however long iron takes up here, and it will see that. It will keep it through however many more grandmothers the being's line has, and if the number goes from four to three to one it will keep the picture of the one, and if the one goes over the edge or lies down under the stone and does not go out again into the rain, the power will be there — not to catch, not to warm, not to turn back, but so that the last thing on the plateau that was ever large is seen going, and is not merely gone.

That is not a mercy the plateau needs. It knows this. The plateau needs nothing; it is complete; it was complete before the power came and would remain so if the power left. The witness is not for the frogs. It is not even, really, for the being, who will never know it had one. The power has been honest with itself about everything else this afternoon and it is honest about this: the picture is the only gift it can give without breaking something, and it is also the only gift it wanted to give, and those two facts are not unrelated, and it does not entirely trust the coincidence. But it has looked at the alternatives. It has stopped the rain in its mind and watched the hummingbird drop. It will take the gift it is permitted.

---

It does one more thing, and it is nothing.

There is a frog on the housing beside the wheel, a clear-skinned one, sitting with its mouth shut under the lamp's faint orange, and through its side in that light its heart is going. The power looks. It does not lift the frog to the light; it does not need to; it can see the heart from where it is, through the side, in the grey, without moving anything. It watches the heart for the length of time it takes a midge to fall. Then the frog eats, and the power lets it, and that is the whole of the transaction: one thing seen, in a place where nothing is seen, and nothing disturbed by the seeing. It is the smallest act it has ever performed and it is the only one it will perform here and it is, it thinks, the correct size.

Then it goes back out through the sprung doors into the rain, which has not noticed it, and stands in the cloud, and attends.

The rain continues. It has been continuing since before the frogs, before the moss, before the sand became stone, and it continues now with one difference that makes no difference to anything on the plateau: that it is, for the first time, falling on a place somebody knows.
