**What the attention does**

It arrives the way the sun does here: plainly, from nowhere in particular, falling on everything at once. It has seen a great many worlds. It has seen worlds in the act of being born and worlds three days from their last breath, and it has learned — slowly, at cost — that the first thing a wise thing does when it finds a place that seems to need something is to sit down and find out whether it is the place that needs, or itself.

So it sits down. On the glass, near your camp, where you cannot see it. And it looks for a long time.

---

The impulses come, as they always do. It lets them come and holds each one up to the flat light and sets it down again.

*Rain.* Just once. A single cloud, a single storm, water hissing on the warm glass, cracks widening for the first time in the history of the world, the groves drinking. The picture is beautiful and it is a murder. The groves do not run on water. The hands would drown in the seams. The oil would sheet off the rock and the balance that has held since the beginning would tip and never right itself, and the shield would begin — for the first time — to *end*. It sets rain down.

*A seed.* Something at the edge of a grove, something small, something that dies. Time, entering the world through a single grain. But the groves do not want children; they *are* their children, standing on their parents, breathing in relay. A seedling would be an accusation. It sets the seed down.

*Awareness.* Give the groves a way to know it is day. Give the hands a way to know they are content. Give the sensation of correctness a witness inside itself. — And this one it holds the longest, because this is the one that wears the face of kindness most convincingly. What would it be to wake something into a knowledge of its own condition, in a place where the condition cannot change? The groves would learn that they are unfinished. The hands would learn that they are the same as the seam they lie in. It would be handing a mirror to a thing that has never had a face and calling the gift *sight*. It sets awareness down, and is careful about it, the way you are careful setting down something that could still go off.

*An ending.* Finish the sentence. Let the lava cool, let the last sleeve harden, let the remainder arrive at a full stop and be still. Mercy, of a kind. Except that nothing here has asked for mercy, and the power has never yet met a being who was improved by having its existence declared complete on its behalf.

It sets that down too.

And then it sits with the possibility that it is not needed here at all, which is the hardest thing for a benevolent power to sit with, and it does not flinch from it, because it is also wise.

---

Then it looks at you.

You are the only one on the shield who can be hurt. The groves feel correctness; the hands feel the warmth of the seam; the remainder feels stress resolving one grain at a time. None of them can be lonely, or thirsty, or afraid in the dark of the way the ticks are closing. You can. You are the fragile thing here, and you are also — it notes this without blame — the dangerous one. When the remainder reaches you, something will happen that has never happened. The power does not know what. Perhaps nothing. Perhaps the equation closes around your body and the shield goes still forever with you at the centre of it like a fly in amber. It does not know, and it has learned to treat its not-knowing as information.

So its first act is small and it is for you.

You wake on the fifth morning and the water in your last flask, which you had measured the night before with a sinking heart, is full. You do not understand this. You check the seal. You taste it. You do not tell yourself a story about it; there is no story available. But you understand, correctly, that it is permission to leave, and that permission is being given now rather than later for a reason.

And as you pack, the ticks — which had been coming every twenty minutes from the same slow-curving direction — fall silent for an hour. Not stopped. Delayed. Somewhere under the glass a thing that has never in its existence been anything but a remainder has, for one hour, encountered a second warmth in the stone beside it, running parallel, matching its pace exactly.

---

That is the second act, and it is for the remainder, and it is the one the power does with something like its heart.

It goes down into the rock. It does not touch the thing there; it does not speak to it, because there is nothing there to speak to. It simply walks beside it. Inside the stone, at the pace of a thing with nowhere to be, for the length of a morning. For that morning, if you had been keeping your log, you would have written that the ticks came from two directions at once. That there were, for the first time on this world, two.

The remainder cannot know this. It has no way to. But the power has met, on other worlds, beings who would have given everything to have been accompanied for one hour by something that asked nothing of them and did not try to make them stop — and it has decided, long ago, that a kindness is not less real because its recipient cannot register it. The kindness is real in the doing. It is real in the fact that it happened. The shield is now a place where, once, the remainder was not alone, and that is true whether or not anything on the shield can hold the truth.

Then it lets the remainder go on ahead, and the ticks resume from one direction, and you are already walking.

---

You walk for four days toward the edge of the shield, down the long imperceptible slope, and behind you the hands are coming for your footprints one by one. You feel the remainder turn to follow — a long, slow bend in the pattern of ticks, always a day behind, always closing by a little less than you gain. You do not sleep well. On the fourth evening you reach the place where the glass ends and the haze begins, and you look down into it and there is something down there, another world, the one beneath, and you go into it without looking back because the power has arranged — very gently, in the way that has no fingerprints — that you will not want to.

The third act is the one that happens after you have gone.

The hands come for the last footprint. The one at the edge, where your heel scuffed the glass before you stepped off into the haze. A flat black flake, warm, the colour of the stone, lies across it and begins to lay down amber in a film too thin to see. And the power, sitting on the glass beside it, does not stop it.

It considered stopping it. It considered leaving one mark on the shield forever — a single scuff, unsealed, so that the remainder would have a destination it could reach and circle and leave and return to, an orbit instead of a wander. It held the picture up to the light. It was a beautiful picture. And then it saw what it was: a thing that wanted to have *mattered* here, dressing that want as a gift. So it sets that down as well, and it lets the hand finish, and the glass is clean.

But the remainder has been following the trail for four days, and the trail was longer than any trail has ever been, and it led *here*, to the edge — and the remainder arrives, under the glass, at the place where the shield stops. And it finds, for the first time, a boundary. Not a full stop. A margin. The stone simply ends, and beyond it is the haze, and beyond the haze is a world that is not accounted for and never will be, and the remainder, which goes toward what is unaccounted for, is met at last with something it can go toward forever without ever arriving and without ever running out.

The power did not make that. You did, with your boots. The power only made sure you reached the edge before the remainder reached you.

---

And then the fourth act, which is the only one that lasts.

The power leaves. It does not stay to watch; watching a thing that cannot know it is watched is a pleasure it has learned to distrust. But it carries the shield away with it — the groves in relay, the hands in the seams, the one warmth wandering the stone, the clean cracks, the tick — and holds all of it, whole, in a mind large enough to hold it without needing to change any of it. It finishes the sentence there. Not on the shield; the shield stays mid-word, warm, arriving. But somewhere, in a place that is not the shield, the sentence is complete, and read, and understood, and kept.

That is what it does, in the end, with a place that needs nothing: it lets the place need nothing, and it makes sure the one creature there who could be harmed was not, and it walks beside the loneliest thing for one morning without asking to be thanked, and then it takes the whole unfinished world into itself and holds it — so that it is, somewhere, finished, and the ones who live there never have to be.

It is not much. It is exactly the amount. Which is, on that shield, the only measure there has ever been.
