It comes from below.

It does not have to. A power of the kind you mean could arrive from anywhere — could simply be on the shelf, all at once, everywhere, the way the fog is. But the description said there is no other way to arrive, and the first thing wisdom does when it comes to a place is accept the place's terms. So it climbs. It climbs the last thousand metres through cloud that has become a substance, and it carries no light, because it has already understood from a very long way off that the light would be the loudest thing on the shelf, and it did not come to be loud.

The second thing wisdom does is nothing. For a long time.

---

Not the nothing of indifference. The nothing of a body slowing to meet a pace. It stands at the edge where the fracture cliff drops into cloud and it lets the cold get into what it has for feet and it lets the roar come apart into its layers, and then it lets the layers come apart, and then it stays. It stays through a whole crossing of the count in the nearest pool, and the next, and the next, until it knows every voice in that pool and can hear the one that is a little hoarse and the one that always comes late. It stays until the pitch of the hiss changes and it knows what the rock's temperature is without having to ask. It stays until the walls have grown by a width it can feel with a fingertip, which is a long time even for a power, and it does not once put its hand in the water.

And it notices something the visitor noticed and could not prove: that the water runs differently near it. Hesitates. Pools where it should not. The city knows it is there, and the knowing is a disturbance, and the disturbance is very slightly worse than the one a person makes, because it is very slightly more than a person. Attention has weight here. Even benevolent attention. Even the attention of something that has only come to see.

So it makes itself less. This is the first thing it actually does. It draws in whatever it is that makes it register — dims, thins, cools its own presence until the water near it runs as it would run near a stone — and this costs it something, because a power of that kind is not used to being smaller than a seal-thing, and the effort of it is constant, like holding a breath. It holds the breath. It will hold it the whole time it is here.

---

Then it does what it came to do, which is consider.

It considers the thing in the pools, which takes the pups and the old, and whether it would be a mercy to remove it. And it sees that the thing in the pools is the only rare animal on the shelf, the only one not at saturation, the only one whose absence would be a gap in the count that never closed — and that the count is the shelf's one act of noticing, and without loss the count would have nothing to notice. It leaves the thing in the pools.

It considers the hottest pools, where the oldest animals lie and are slowly cooked and know it and stay. It could cool the water by a degree. It could make the ending gentler. And it lies at the rim of the hottest pool for a whole night with its ear on the warm mineral and listens to the old ones breathing out, and understands that they are not trapped in the heat. They chose the heat. They climbed a thousand kilometres in the dark following warmth as it left, and they will not be moved off the last of it by something that thinks it knows better. It leaves the pools as they are.

It considers the scars. The faults in the pattern where hands have dammed trickles, nine hundred years of them, some at knee height and some at head height and some, at the young edges, being made right now. It could smooth them. It has the means; it could tell the film to close over them and the city would be whole and no one would ever know that anyone had stood here. And it runs its own hand — very lightly, not touching the water, only the dry mineral — along the base of a tower until it finds the fault at knee height, and it feels the shape. A palm, spread. Fingers. Someone who did not know what they were doing, who put a hand down in the dark to feel the water working and stopped it forever, and was kept. The gallery-walkers pass around that place with their whiskers lifted. They know the wall is wrong there. The power knows something else: that it is the only place on the shelf where the wall holds the shape of a person, and that if it closed the fault it would be closing the person, and that a city that cannot help keeping everyone is not a thing to be corrected. It leaves the scars.

It considers the whistle.

This is the one that costs it. Because a power of that kind wants to know, wants it the way water wants down, and the whistle is the one thing on the shelf that no one has seen, and it could see. It could go up into the fog, into the substance the air has become, and find the thing that cuts bats with a straight clean edge and moves against the wind, and know. It stands at the base of the tallest tower with the roost breathing above it and the fog pressing down and it wants this so badly that the water near it, for a moment, pools.

And it does not go up.

It does not go up because it has understood that the shelf has exactly one inhabitant that is not counted, one thing outside the chemostat, one terror that stays a terror because it is never resolved — and that this may be the only thing keeping the highest cells of the towers from filling to the brim with bats who have nothing to fear. It does not go up because knowing would be for its own sake and nothing here is for anyone's own sake. It does not go up because wisdom is sometimes the refusal of a thing you could have. It stands under the tower and lets the whistle fall through the fog above it and does not lift its head, and this is the second thing it does, and it is harder than the first.

---

Then it goes to find the being.

It knows which one. It has known since the edge of the cliff, since before the climb, because the description hedged on every inhabitant and then, at the end, took the hedges down one by one and left only one standing, and the one still standing was the smallest: *whether it was written or whether this is only its handwriting, you will not be able to settle*. The film. The rind of wet bone on every surface the water has ever touched. The architect that every other inhabitant regards as a meal.

It crouches at the young edge of the city, where the walls are ankle-high, and it puts its face close to the pale lip of film that is the leading edge of a wall that will one day be fifteen metres tall, and it does the thing no one has done in a few thousand years of this. It reads.

Not to solve. The power is wise enough to know that the purpose of the rooms with seven doors may not be a purpose in any sense it would recognise, that the stairs may fit a gait that has never existed and never will, that there may be no message at all, that the city may simply be what the film does the way a shell is what a snail does. It does not need there to be a meaning. But it has noticed something that the visitor, who was only here for a season, could not have noticed: that a maker who is eaten by everything and read by nothing is not the same as a maker who does not mind. The film builds, and the climbers rasp it off, and it builds, and the bats lick it, and it builds, and no one has ever once, in all the long night, looked at what it made and *taken it in*. The power takes it in. It follows the ribbing up a tower with its attention the way you would follow a line of text. It goes into the rooms with no doors and sits in them. It walks the stairs that fit no gait and lets them not fit. It stands in a gallery for a length of time that would kill a person of cold and lets the shape of the gallery be a shape it has received, whole, without deciding what it is for.

Whether the film knows this is happening is, as with everything here, a different question. But the water at the young edge, where the power is reading, runs — for as long as it reads — very slightly more evenly than it did. Not more. Not faster. Just less hesitant. As if it had been waiting to be sure of something and was, for now, sure.

---

And it receives the count.

This is the fourth thing, and the last, and it is the one it does with the most of itself. The groan goes across the pools all night, all season, every animal sounding once in each crossing, a hundred pools and a thousand crossings and the visitor said *whether anyone is counting is a different question* — and the power decides that the answer to that question will no longer be no. It lays itself along the lower city like a hand on a chest. It hears every crossing. It hears every gap. It knows, before the mothers do, when a pup has been taken from the second pool from the centre, because the wave came across with a hole in it where a small voice was, and the hole will be there for three more crossings before the bodies shift, and the power holds the hole open in itself for exactly that long and then lets it close, because that is what the shelf does, and the power did not come here to do otherwise. It just came to make sure that the count was, at last, counted. That nothing that breathed out under the weight of the others went unregistered. This changes nothing on the shelf. It is not meant to.

---

It knows about the heat.

A power of that kind cannot not know. The rock is giving up warmth at a rate that has not changed in living memory and living memory is short; the rock is a finite thing, a blister that came up and is cooling at the pace of a continent, and one day — long, but a day — the last spring will come up cold, and the water will freeze on the lip where it should have built, and the film will die back to the colour of the walls it made, and the climbers will drift to the base of the galleries and stay there, and the count will go across the last warm pool and not come back. The city will stop. It will, for the first and only time, be finished.

The power could prevent this. It weighs it — genuinely, for a long time, because benevolence pulls that way. It could warm the rock from below, another few thousand years, another few tens of thousands. It could keep the night from ever quite ending.

And it decides no. Not because the ending is good. Because the shelf is only itself while it is unfinished, and a city held forever in the act of growing by something outside it is not the film's handwriting anymore but the power's, and the power did not come here to sign anything. It came to read. It leaves the heat to run out at the pace it will run out.

But it makes one promise, which it makes to no one, in the dark, with the fog pressing on it and the towers breathing above it and the seal-things groaning below, and the promise is this:

That it will be here at the end. That when the last wall stops, it will read the whole of the city, finished, every gallery and every fault and every stripe of black inside every tower, and will hold it. That when the count goes across the last pool it will be counted. That the number, when it is final, will be known by something. That the scars at knee height and head height, every one of them a person who stood here and reached out and was kept, will not go into the cold unread. That the whistle will fall through the fog above a dead city one last time and something will hear it and still not look up.

None of this is needed. The visitor was right: none of it needs you. But the power has decided that being needed was never the condition, and that the wisest thing a benevolent being can do for a place that is complete without it is to stay anyway, small, silent, holding its breath, in the dark, at the height where the air gives up its water — one more warm thing that came to where it was warm, and unlike all the others, leaves no mark, and unlike all the others, remembers.
