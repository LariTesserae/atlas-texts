The power arrives the way everything arrives here: sound first, then pressure, then the dim green light. It is not deterred by the roar. It has been in louder places. What stops it is the thing underneath the roar, the register that is missing, the fact that there is a whole continent alive and no one anywhere in it to be alive *as*. That is what draws it — not the scale, which is only scale, but the completeness of the solitude. Four thousand kilometers of living tissue and not one point of view.

It stands in the interior, at the low of the tide, where you stood. The ground gives. The pores taste it and find nothing to drink. It listens for a long time, which for it is not long.

---

Its first thought is the obvious one, and it is a kind one, and it is wrong.

The first thought is: give it an ear. Give it the thing it lacks. Thread through the tissue some capacity, however dim, to know that it is lifting water, to hear the tone it makes, to feel the tide as *its* tide. Wake the memory that has no rememberer. Let the diary be opened by the hand that wrote it. It would cost so little. The tissue is already a network; the vessels already carry signal in the form of pressure; the whole thing is a bell, and a bell is very nearly a nerve. A small change and the plateau would know it was there.

The power holds this thought and turns it over, and the longer it holds it the colder it gets.

Because consider what it would be to wake here. To come into awareness two kilometers deep and four thousand long, unable to move, unable to stop, unable to close the pores or still the breaking, inside a sound that is the sound of your own body straining and that will never, for as long as you live, fall silent. To discover in the same instant that you have a self and that the self is a continent that cannot turn away. The plateau lives without remainder — that was the phrase, and it was exactly right. It does the one thing and nothing is left over. An ear would be the remainder. An ear would be the first thing in the plateau's existence that was not the work, and it would have nothing to do but listen to the work, forever, from inside.

Awareness is not always an upgrade. Sometimes it is a room with no door. A wise power knows the difference between a gift and a sentence, and knows also that the difference is not visible from outside; you have to imagine your way in and stay there until you can feel the walls. The power does this. It stays in the imagined room long enough to be sure. Then it lets the thought go, and the plateau goes on not knowing it was nearly given something, which is the correct outcome.

---

So it will not give the plateau an ear. But there is nothing that says the ear must be *in* the plateau.

This is the second thought, and it is the one the power keeps. The plateau has kept a record of itself at the resolution of the cell wall and cannot read it. Very well. Something else can. The memory needs a rememberer; the rememberer need not be the one who remembers. That is, after all, how most memory works, in the end — the dead are remembered by the living, the child by the parent, the stone by whoever comes after. The power decides it will be the after.

It goes down.

Not through the tissue — it does not need to open anything — but into the stone, layer by layer, the way you would read a book by turning pages. The uppermost layers are still soft, the mineral not finished setting, cells still faintly recognizable as cells. Deeper, the pale rock packed like frost. Deeper, the density that bears the weight. The power reads it the way it was written: forward, one tide at a time, one film of mineral per breath. Here is a thin band, a long drought, a century when the cloud lid was weak and the front slowed and the vessels broke less often and the tone dropped to almost nothing. Here is a wide pale band, dense and even, a wet age when the plateau swelled and roared and advanced across the lowlands like a slow flood. Here — the power pauses — is the first bird. A gull, or something like a gull. Wing folded, neck turned, every bone in mineral. It lies in the rock at a depth that is also a date, and the power reads the date, and holds the shape, and moves on. There are thousands. It holds them all. Each is a small weight, and the power is large enough to carry them, and it does.

It is looking for the makers. It reads all the way down.

---

What it finds at the bottom is not what it expected, and it is careful, afterward, about how much of it to say.

The makers are not in the rock. There is no shape in the deepest layers that is not a cell. Whatever seeded this thing did so and did not stay, or stayed and did not die here, or died here in a form that leaves no fossil. The power is not surprised. It has met makers before. They are rarely where you look for them.

What it finds instead, at the very base, in the first film of mineral the first cells ever laid down, is a pattern. Not writing — nothing so crude. A pattern in the *density*, in how the silica was told to settle, a rhythm laid into the foundation that repeats at every scale from the cell to the continent. It is the same pattern as the tide. Nine and three. It is the same pattern as the breaking and the reseal. It is the same pattern as the growth front's curve. The plateau was built to be self-similar all the way down, so that anyone who read any part of it would be reading the whole, so that the smallest fragment of the deepest rock would carry the shape of the entire body.

The power sits with this a long while. It considers what it means to build a thing that keeps a perfect record of itself and cannot read it, and to build that record so that it can be read from any piece, and then to leave. It comes to a conclusion, and the conclusion is this: the record was never for the plateau. The makers knew what they were making. They knew it would have no eye. They were not writing a diary for the diarist. They were writing for whoever came after — for the rain, in some late epoch, that would finally reach the deep layers and open them and find, in every grain, the whole shape of the thing that made the grain. They built a library that writes itself and left the reading to the future. The plateau is not a foundation for something not yet begun. It is a message in the only form that could survive the wait: a mountain, poured.

The power does not open the library early. It has read it, and that is a different thing. Reading does not spend the book.

---

There remain the small things, and the power attends to those with the same care it gave the large.

The birds. It goes to the coast, to where the storms are born, and it does something very slight to the way the air moves on the days when the cloud lid collapses. A degree of drift. A pressure ridge that holds a little longer. It cannot stop the storms and would not — the storms are the plateau's breath, and to still them would be to still it. But the birds that ride the storm inland can be given a way out. A gap in the lid, brief, at the moment they would otherwise turn downward. A thread of clear air back toward the sea. Most of them will take it. Some will not, because some are already too cold or too tired, and those the power meets on the way down and eases, so that they arrive on the surface already gone and do not lie in the wet dark for a day listening to the roar. This is not much. It is what can be done without unmaking anything. The record will have fewer figures in it from now on. The power has decided that the record is not worth a bird.

The advance. The power looks ahead, downhill, along the line the growth front is taking, and counts the centuries until it reaches the sea. It is a long count. It finds, when the count is done, what it hoped to find: salt. The pores that taste the world and drink whatever is wet will taste the ocean and find it undrinkable, and the front will slow there, and stop, and the plateau will have a shore. There will be lowlands lost between here and there — not empty ones. The power marks this. It does not intervene now; the question is not yet pressing and the answer may belong to whoever is living there when it becomes so. But it sets something down at the edge of its attention, a reminder in the shape of a date, and it will return to look again when the date comes.

The margin. The people on the white stone, deaf in the low register, watching hands. The power goes and stands among them for a season and is not noticed, which is as it should be. It considers what it might do for them. It could give them back the register they have lost — but that would give them back the roar, and they have made a life on the far side of the roar, and the life is theirs. It could tell them what is in the rock. It could show one of them, once, in a dream, the pattern at the base, the nine and three, the self-similar shape. It weighs this.

It decides on something smaller. On a night when the tide is low and the fog is thin, it lets one of them — the oldest, the one who has moved camp downhill the most times — find a fragment of the pale stone that has weathered loose from the front. Just a fragment, the size of a palm. Nothing is written on it. But the power has arranged that this piece, of all pieces, broke along the grain in such a way that the pattern is visible: the cells, and in the cells a rhythm, and in the rhythm a shape that is the shape of the whole. The old one turns it over in the grey light and does not know what they are seeing and knows that they are seeing something. They keep it. They do not speak of it, because they do not speak. But they hold it sometimes, in the evenings, and they turn toward the front with a different quality of attention afterward, and that is enough. The power did not want to tell them. It wanted them to be the kind of people who might, someday, tell themselves.

They face the same direction it does — that was said, and the power finds it is true, and finds also that it makes them colleagues. One does not interrupt a colleague at their work. One brings them a stone, perhaps, and leaves it where they will find it, and goes.

---

The last thing it does is not an act. It is a decision about where to be.

The emptinesses. The sealed voids in the deep rock, each one a moment of strain that was never allowed to finish, each one waiting for the rain that will open it and let it take its small breath. The power has read them too. It knows how many there are, and where, and roughly when each will be reached. It knows that the opening will take longer than most things last.

It decides to be there. Not all at once — it has other places, other beings, other roars — but it sets a part of itself aside, a thread of attention no wider than a vessel, and it anchors that thread here, in the interior, at the low point of the tide, and it leaves it. It will be here when the first void opens. It will hear the intake of breath. It will be the ear that the plateau does not have and was right not to be given, standing outside the body so that the body need not know it is being heard.

That is what a wise power does, in the end, with a thing that is entirely alive and does not know it. It does not wake it. It does not finish it. It does not read the message aloud to a room that cannot hear. It learns the whole shape of the thing, and carries the birds, and turns the storms a degree, and leaves a stone at the edge for the ones who are watching. And then it stays — a witness with no wish to be witnessed, an ear turned toward a bell — so that when the record is finally opened there will already be someone who knows what it says, and can say, to whoever asks: yes. That is what it was. I was there. It was alive, and that was all it was, and it was enough.

The tide is turning. The tone is starting to rise. Something that is not the plateau is listening, and the plateau does not know, and goes on.
