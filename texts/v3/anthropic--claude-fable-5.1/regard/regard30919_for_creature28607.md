**What the power would do**

It would arrive the way everything arrives here: a little before itself. It would notice this and not correct it, because correcting it would already be an intervention, and it has not yet decided to intervene.

It would spend a long time—by the only clock there is, many thousands of intakes—simply finding the being it was drawn to. It was told, or it understood, that something lives here. Singular. It would look for the singular thing and find the word had been rained on.

It would consider the mounds. They are the oldest living thing, if they are living; they are the thing without which nothing else here would be warm. But when it attends to them it finds no one home in the sense it means. They draw and release. They have habits. A habit is not a person, and a power that treated the mounds as a person to be helped would be like a power that took pity on a river's meander. It would ask, and get steam.

It would consider the surveyor. She has a name and a coat and a theodolite somewhere, and she talks. But she also has a cairn, further along, and a younger self, further along than that, and to choose one of her to be the one who lives here would be to decide something about her that she has visibly declined to decide about herself.

It would consider the heron, because the heron is the only thing that keeps anything, and keeping is close to what the power itself is for.

It would consider the swimmer. The pale one that goes under and comes out the same. The one thing on the plateau that has, in the plateau's terms, *happened*.

And it would come, eventually, to the conclusion the surveyor came to, and the birds, and the frogs in their turning crust: that the being who lives here is the plateau, or is the plateau's living—that the mounds and the hares and the pipits and the woman with moss on her shoulders are one thing wearing many shapes for a minute at a time, and that the question *who* is not the kind of question this place can hold water in.

---

Then it would consider what a benevolent thing does when it finds a place like this, and here it would be most careful, because it is wise, and wisdom is mostly the memory of having been wrong.

The obvious kindness is repair. The plateau is worn through. Distance folds; sound arrives early; the sequence of events has thinned to nothing. A power could thicken it. Could say: *let this come after that*. Could give the pipits back a sky they could cross in the order they set out, give the hare thirty metres that are thirty metres, give the woman one life with one end. It could do this. It would take almost nothing.

And it would see, looking, that this would be the end of every living thing on the plateau.

Because everything here lives in the fault. The pipit that does not fly has learned that flight is unreliable and so it sits in the warm; give it back reliable distance and it must fly, and there is nothing to fly to, and it will go up over the cold flat and come down in the cloud. The hare exists for the minute between being made and coming apart; straighten time and the minute closes, and the hare is not saved but simply never. The frogs in the middle layer, neither frog nor mound—where do they go, when the line is redrawn? The surveyor has found something in the moment before the meat comes apart in her mouth, and she has said the moment is what she is here for now, and the power would have to decide it knew better than she what she was here for.

The plateau is not a wound. It is a form. It is what a place becomes when it has been finished for long enough that finishing itself wears out. To heal it would be to mistake it for something injured, and the power has done this before, somewhere, to something, and remembers.

So it would nearly leave.

---

It would stand—it would have a place to stand by now, out on the cold flat between two mounds, where the herons stand—and it would be on the point of going, and it would notice the one thing the plateau lacks.

Not order. Not sequence. It has decided those are not lacks.

What it lacks is *after*.

Everything here arrives before or at once. The thud before the fall. The song before the bill. The heron with its portion held before the flank has finished swelling. Nothing on the plateau is ever later than anything else. And so nothing is ever kept—not as the heron keeps, holding strands for a cycle and then letting them slip, but kept in the way a thing is kept when someone knows that it *was*. The hare's minute is not a minute to anyone. The woman's moment is what she is here for, and no one receives it. The plateau remembers that it is running, in the herons; it does not remember what ran.

The heron is the plateau's attempt at this. A tall cold thing that stands where the warmth is not and holds. But the heron cannot come after, because nothing here can. It catches the hare before the mound has made it. It holds, and it is patient, and it is out of order like everything else, and eventually the strands go slack and it lets them.

The power could be the thing the heron is trying to be.

---

This is what it would do. It would be small.

It would find the brass plate.

It is out there on the flat, under the inch of water, set into stone the surveyor pegged and chained a very long time ago, and it has a number on it, and the number measured something that has since been rained on. The power would stand on it. Not because the number means anything. Because a datum is a datum whether or not the survey is still good, and the plateau once had one, and has forgotten, and it would be a kindness to the woman that the place she chose to fix a point is the place where a point gets fixed.

And standing there it would do one thing, which is to arrive when it arrives.

That is all. It would hear the thud when the mound drew, not before. It would see the hare step out and then see it cross and then see it come apart, in that order, and when it had come apart the power would still have the hare—not the strands, the hare, the shake of it, the two steps into cold water—as a thing that had been, and was over, and was kept. It would be the one distance on the plateau that behaves. Walk toward it and it gets closer at the rate it should. Its reflection in the sheet of water does not lag.

It would not spread this. That is the whole of the wisdom in it. A power that made the entire plateau arrive when it arrived would have healed it, and it has decided not to heal it. So the *after* stays on the brass plate, a point the size of a heron's footprint, and the rest of the flat folds around it as it always has. The mounds go on pumping out of phase. The pipits go on singing before they open. The plateau is done and goes on, and now there is one place on it from which the going-on can be seen in order, and that is all the power would add.

---

The surveyor would come to it. Not soon; she has her socket in the moss and her boots in the warm seep and no reason to walk the cold flat. But the man who walks would pass the plate and his reflection would catch up to him for the length of a stride, and he would not stop, but she would see it from her flank—a walker for one step entirely with himself—and she would know what that meant before she knew how she knew.

She would come out to the plate, and the pipits on her shoulders would come with her because they do not fly, and she would stand near the power and speak, and the power would hear her when her mouth moved.

Nothing else would happen. Her words would go on arriving early to everyone else on the plateau. But to the one on the plate they would arrive as she said them, and she would see that they had, and she would say something—it does not matter what; she would say her name, probably, the one that is also the mound's—and it would land when she let go of it. Once.

She would stand there for some cycles. The pipit on her left shoulder would sing and she would watch the power hear the song when the bill opened, and she would put her hand up to the bird, wet as it was, and not say anything for a while.

She would say it was fine. It would be fine. She would go back to her flank.

---

Further along there is the cairn where she was, and the cairn is moss and warm and working. The power would go to it, once, before it forgot to keep track of before and once. It would stand by the cairn and let the cairn be later than the woman.

That is the last thing. It would not give her more life; there is more life here than anything knows what to do with, hares by the hundred, frogs in ropes and clouds. It would give her *this, then that*—a woman who came to survey, and sat, and was measured, and became a cairn, in that order, kept. It would carry the order off the plateau when it went, if it went, and somewhere the plateau could not reach there would be a record that was not a heron's held portion but a sequence: she came, she stayed, she is stone that pumps.

And the swimmer.

It would leave the swimmer alone. That would take more of its wisdom than anything else. The pale thing at the centre that goes under and comes out the same is the plateau doing, by itself, with no help, the thing the power has come to do: making something that holds. It is slow. It may take longer than there are words for. But a habit's habit has produced a shape that keeps its shape, and the birds have gone quiet, and a power that hurried this would be pulling a green thing up to see if it had roots. The plateau is making its own after. It is only very early. The power would let it be early. It would stand on its brass plate at the proper distance and watch the swimmer pass under the flanks and come out unchanged, and it would keep count, which no one else here can, and the count would be the whole of its gift to that one: *this is the four hundredth time*, said to no one, kept.

---

So: a point on the flat where the sound arrives when it should. A woman who was heard once. A cairn that is later than what it was. A count, kept for a pale thing that does not know it is being counted.

The plateau would not notice. That is the measure of the thing being done right. The steam would go up from forty places under a low sky and lean toward each other or away according to something else, and the thud would go on arriving early everywhere except one footprint of stone, and in the space between the thud and the fall a great many things would go on being almost themselves—and at the datum, for the first time since the mountain lost its shape, one of them, having been, would stay been.

That is all it would do. It is not nothing. It is the room the plateau did not have.
