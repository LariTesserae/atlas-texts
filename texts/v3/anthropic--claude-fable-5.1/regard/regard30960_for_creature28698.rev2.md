It comes the way anything comes to the basin: unregistered.

It arrives above the cloud first, because that is where the outside is, and it looks down through the slate lid and sees what the visitor saw, and then it does what the visitor could not do, which is to keep looking without being deafened, without being wet, without its own outside clinging to it like a smell. It looks for a long time. It has the time. And the first thing it understands is that the visitor was wrong about one thing, and the wrongness is important.

The visitor stood in the roar and heard it all at once and called it noise, and knew this was a failing — knew that to each creature the roar was one quiet note filling the world. But the visitor did not turn that knowledge around. The visitor also stood there and saw all of it at once and called it *no one*, and did not ask whether that judgment, too, was a matter of band. Whether *someone* is simply the frequency the visitor's ear is tuned to — the tone of creatures with futures, creatures who lean forward, who compare, who dread — and whether the visitor was as deaf to whatever the frog is as the frog is to the salamander's hiss.

The power does not settle this. It notes it. It notes that the frog's hunger rises and is fed, and that this is not nothing; that the pull toward the tone rises and is answered, and that this is not nothing; that the weight of the young against the belly is a weight, felt, and that the visitor's own sages have spent whole lives trying to arrive at a state with no forward lean in it, no comparison, no about-ness, only the body present to itself — and that here are a hundred thousand creatures who were born there and have never left. It notes also that this is a cheap equivalence and it does not lean on it. The sage's stillness is stillness that could have been otherwise, stillness with the whole world held inside it. The frog's is the absence of the machinery. These are not the same, and the power, being wise, does not pretend they are. But it does not pretend the frog is a stone, either. It holds both. It can do that. That is most of what wisdom is.

Then it looks at the round-eyed thing, because everyone does.

Hands. Five fingers, damp pads, a grip that could hold a tool if there had ever been a tool. Eyes that gather more of the dim than anything else in the basin can gather, pointed at things, open since they formed. The power looks at this the way you would look at a violin that has hung on a wall for ten thousand years in a room where no one has ever heard of music — not with pity, exactly, but with the specific ache of equipment that has never been used for the thing it is plainly for. The eyes are for a world. The hands are for a world. And the world they were made in has never once presented anything to be about.

So the power considers the obvious thing. It is benevolent, and benevolence, unwise, is always drawn to the obvious thing.

It could let one cold drop fall. It could thin the cloud for a quarter-hour and let a bar of real light lie across the moss — the first shadow the basin has ever had, an edge sharp enough to be an edge. It could send one hard breath of wind through the fern layer so that the leaves, for the first time, sway. Any of these would be the largest event in the history of the place, and any of them would begin something. The round-eyed thing would see the bar of light and would see it *move*, and for the first time there would be a difference in its eye, and the difference would be the beginning of the eye being someone's.

The power turns this over and sets it down, and it is worth being exact about why, because the reasons are not the sentimental ones.

It is not that the basin is perfect and should not be touched. The power does not think the basin is perfect. It is not that change is violence. It is that the gift is being offered to someone who does not yet exist, and paid for by everything that does. The first thing the future gives to any creature is fear — before curiosity, before memory, before the forward lean becomes hope, there is the moment when the world is about to become different and the body has no answer to it. The power would be waking them into dread and calling it a present. And it cannot ask. That is the thing it keeps returning to. You cannot ask a not-yet-someone whether it wants to be someone; the question assumes the answer; to be able to answer it is already to have been given the thing. There is no consent available here, and where there is no consent available, the wise thing is to become very suspicious of your own certainty that you know what is good. The certainty that being someone is better than being no one is a certainty held only by someones. The power is a someone. It knows what its band is.

So it does not let the drop fall.

But it does not leave, either, and here is where its looking has taken it somewhere the visitor's did not.

The visitor said the rock has been warm *forever*, and said it in the voice of the basin, which is the voice of a place that has never had anything to hold itself against and so has no word for *long* that is not the word for *always*. The power is the outside. It hears *forever* and knows it for what it is: the local name for *not yet*. The warmth comes from below. What is below is fire, and fire is not eternal; it is only slow. It has been feeding this bowl at blood heat since the lava stopped moving, and it will go on feeding it for a time that is long beyond any frog's lineage, long beyond the pale things under the crust — and then it will not. It will bank down by a degree that no creature in the basin has an organ to feel, and then by another, and the air will let go of a little of its water, and the cloud will settle lower, and one day — one hour, since the basin has no days — the condensation on the underside of a leaf the size of a door will not run. It will stiffen. The first frost in the history of the place. The first difference. And it will come to creatures who have been built for ten thousand generations to have no answer to a difference, and it will be the first thing any of them has ever felt about the world, and it will be the last, and it will take them all in the space of what a visitor would call a night.

This is what the power sees that the visitor could not. The basin's history is not concluded. It is suspended, and the suspension has a term. The question was never *shall I give them a future*. The future is already coming, up from below, on its own schedule, indifferent. The only question the power actually gets to answer is how it arrives.

And that question it can answer without arrogance, because it is not choosing for them between stasis and history. History is choosing them. It is only choosing between two shapes of the same thing: a cliff, or a slope.

So it goes down.

Through the lid, into the wet, into the roar, and it stands — if it stands — on the low tumulus at the center where the crust domed and never fell, up to whatever it has for knees in the flesh-thick moss, and it puts its attention flat on the bare basalt the way the visitor put a palm, and it finds the fire. Not to stop it. It does not stop it. It would be a strange benevolence that condemned a place to be forever what it is, and the power has just spent a long time deciding not to be the kind of thing that decides what a place must be. It only touches the slope. It takes the cooling that was going to happen over a span the basin would not have registered and lays it out over a span so much longer that the difference between one generation and the next is smaller than the difference between one frog and its neighbor — small enough that a body can answer it. Small enough that a thin skin, over a thousand lineages, might thicken by a hair. Small enough that when the first cold comes it comes to creatures whose great-great-grandmothers had already, without knowing it, begun to hold their water a little more closely. Small enough that the frost, when it finally lies on the leaf, is not the first difference but the ten-thousandth, and the round-eyed thing hanging above it has an ancestor's flinch already folded into its hands.

That is the whole of the intervention. No light. No wind. No drop. The rock is still warm to the palm when the power lifts its attention from it, and will be for longer than anything alive could measure, and nothing in the basin has noticed anything, because nothing in the basin can.

It is not nothing, what it did. The power knows this and does not pretend otherwise. It has decided that the basin will grow up rather than die, and that is a decision, and it was made without asking, and the power will carry that. But it is the smallest true thing it could find. It did not give them a future. It made the future that was already theirs into one they might survive long enough to have a word for.

Then it does the second thing, which is not an act.

It stays.

It stays because the basin has never had anything outside itself to be held against, and the power is outside, and it can be that — not pressing, not entering the world, only present at the edge of it the way the rim is present above the cloud, unseen. It stays because it is benevolent and this is a place where benevolence has no purchase, where being cared for is not a thing that can be received, and the power finds it has something to learn from that: what it is to love a thing that has no faculty for being loved, that will never once turn toward you, that has you in its eye exactly the way it has a leaf. It is a hard school and the power is not too proud for it.

And it stays for one more reason, which it is honest with itself about.

Because somewhere out along the long slope it has laid, there will be a moment. Not the first frost — that is far off now, softened, spread across an age. Before that. Somewhere in the slow thickening of skins and the slow lowering of the cloud there will be a first creature in whom the difference is large enough, or the organ new enough, that something in it registers *this is not what it was*. It will probably be one of the round-eyed things, because they have the eyes. It will be hanging from a leaf stem, upside down, whistling its thin note, and the leaf will be a shade paler than the leaf its mother hung from, and the wet on its pads a shade cooler, and for the first time in the history of the basin an eye that has been open since it formed will *look* — not have a thing in it, but look at it, hold it against what was, and lean, very slightly, forward.

It will not know what it is doing. It will have no word for it. But it will be the first someone, and the first someone anywhere is always alone, and the power has decided that this one will not be. It will be there. Not seen — it cannot be seen. Not heard — it is in no band. But there, at the edge, the way it has been there all along, so that when the eye finally does what eyes are for, there is something outside the basin for it to have been looking toward, whether or not it ever finds it.

That is all. A slope instead of a cliff, and company.

The roar goes on. The frogs pulse, the salamanders hiss, the frost chews, the dark things hum their bass and walk and do not lie down. The round-eyed thing hangs in the canopy with its face full of eyes and has the moss in them, and the pool, and the column, and the place where the power is, which to it is the same as any other place, which is the same as looking. Nothing has changed. Nothing will change for longer than *long* can hold.

But the rock is, by an amount no hand could feel, a fraction less warm than it was. And the basin, which was not waiting for anything, now has something waiting for it.
