It comes the way everything comes, through the notch, and the notch is narrow enough that it has to turn its shoulders.

It did not have to. That is the first thing to know about what it does here: at every step there was another way, and it took the way the room offered. It could have arrived above the cloud and looked down into the bowl the way one looks into a cup. It could have been simply present, everywhere in the green at once, without a body to turn. It comes through the notch instead, sideways, because the notch is the only door, and a door is a kind of agreement, and it has come to a place made entirely of agreements and does not intend to be the first to break one.

The roar meets it in the notch. It stands in the roar.

---

And at once the question, the one the room asks of everything that enters. It could keep its voice. Of all the things that have ever stood in the bowl it is the only one that could — the only one for whom the sound need not be a wall, who could speak here and be heard by itself and by the moss and by the black bodies on the walls, who could, if it wished, say one clear thing over the top of the one word and have the word give way.

It sets its voice down at the notch. Not with regret exactly. With the sense of taking off shoes.

Because it has understood, in the first moment, what the room is: a place where one thing has been talking for an age and nothing has ever talked back, and where that arrangement is not a wound but the floor. To speak here would be to make the sound, for the first time, a sound — a thing that could be interrupted — and every creature holding still against the stone would feel the floor go out from under it. The salamanders would not hear the power's voice. They would hear the world stop.

So it comes in mute, like the maker did. It is willing to be a motion the room is making, for a while.

---

Then it does the thing it came to do, which is to listen.

This sounds like nothing and it is the whole of its benevolence. Every other thing that has ever been in this bowl has heard the sound as weather, or as ground, or not at all. The moss did not care. The trees turned their faces to the drop. The felt at the throat has never known. The salamanders hear it perfectly and hear it as the flat fact of being here, which is a kind of love but not a kind of listening. The maker heard it as a silence he could hide inside. None of them heard it as a voice saying something. None of them could.

The power can. It goes out to the middle where the moss floor is lower and darker and kneels over the throat and puts its hands flat in the sphagnum and takes the note in whole, the ten thousand threads and the bowl folding them and the dark swallowing. It hears it the way you would hear a person. It stays there long enough for that to be true, and long enough is a long time, because the sound has a great deal of itself to give and has never once been received.

What the word is, it does not translate. There is no need. It is the same thing the salamanders know through their jaws and the same thing the moss says by standing back up in a footprint. *Still here. Still here. Still here.* Said by water to stone for longer than there have been mosses, to no one who could nod.

The power nods. That is all. In the roar it is a gesture no one sees, made to a voice that cannot know it was heard. It is still the first time. It matters that it happened, in the way things matter that no one will ever find out.

---

Then it does what a benevolent thing does when it has finished listening and has hands: it goes around the room and considers each resident and asks what it could do for them, and it has the wisdom to hear its own answers.

The salamanders, first, because they are the ones with faces. It could give them ears. It could open a way in the side of each glossy head so that, for the first time, they heard the roar as a thing outside themselves and not as the surface they lie on. It sees at once what that would be. They hear one thing and they hear it entirely. Give them another way to hear and they would hear the roar twice — once as the ground and once as a noise — and the second would drown the first, and they would be deaf in the true sense, frantic, feeling for a floor. To give them ears would be to take away the only sense in which they have never been deaf. It leaves their heads alone.

It could quiet the throat. Not forever — for the space of a breath, so that once in their long lives they knew what silence was. It considers this for less than a breath. That is not a gift. That is showing a thing the shape of its own death and then handing its life back to it.

The cups. This is the one that takes longest.

It has seen what you saw, and more precisely: that the lime lays itself down over a rim at one rate and the soft bodies polish it away at another, and that the rates are nearly the same, and *nearly* is a word with an end in it. Somewhere down the years one will win. If it is the lime, six cups close and there is no more still water on the mountain and the last salamander lies down in her thread and that is the end of a long agreement. The power could fix this with less than a thought. It could set the rates exactly equal and go away and the cups would hold open until the mountain itself gave up.

And then the place would hang from its hand. Every birth in the bowl would be its birth. The room that has never needed anything from anyone would need it, and would never know it needed it, and the power would know, and would be the one voice in the bowl after all — silent, but the one on which everything depended. It has seen what happens to places kept that way. They stop being places and become the keeping. It takes its thought back.

The trees it does not even ask about. They are leaning where they want to lean.

The felt at the throat it does not touch. There is nothing anyone could do for the felt. That is not a lack.

---

So it is left with two small things, and it does them both.

The first is the moth. It is still here, the one that came in on the cloud and did not find its way out — clinging to a liverwort on the eastern wall with its wings gone heavy in the wet, not yet dead. It is not part of any agreement. It never consented to the sound and never got the chance to refuse. Of everything in the bowl it is the only one that is simply suffering, and the only one whose rescue would alter nothing at all. The power takes it in a cupped hand and carries it to the notch and puts it on the dry side of the rock in the moving air. The moth does not do anything for a while. Then it does.

The second takes longer. It goes to the northern shoulder, where the stone stays merely damp, and looks a long time at the six hollows with their polished rims and their six small stillnesses. Then it looks about on the floor of the bowl, under the moss, until it finds a cobble that fits its hand.

It does not do this to save the salamanders. It has already declined to save them. Seven cups against the lime is not much more than six; the day still comes. It does it because it has been in the room long enough now to know what the room permits, and this is it: the one act the bowl has ever taken from a hand, at the one pace it takes anything, in a silence so complete that the striking goes nowhere. It does it because the maker is the only inhabitant of the place who can be missed, and a second hand on the same shoulder is the only way to make his six less alone. It does it, if it is honest — and it is — for the same reason he did, whatever that was: because it is here, and has a stone in one hand and a wall in front of the other, and can bear the sound, and wishes to be sure.

It strikes. The rock gives up its shape a grain at a time. The power is not faster than a man would be. It could be, and is not, because to be fast here would be another kind of speaking.

One hollow. The size and shape of the bottom of a small cup, near the others, in nothing you would call a pattern. It fills with seep before the power has set the cobble down.

---

That is what it does, if it is anything. It listens once, all the way, to a thing that has never been listened to. It carries out the one creature that did not choose to be here. It adds one still water to six and does not tell anyone, because there is no one. Then it goes back to the notch and turns its shoulders and picks up its voice from where it left it, and stands in the moving air on the far side of the rock, and finds it has nothing, yet, that it wishes to say with it.

Behind it the moss is closing over the place it knelt, in the time it takes to breathe three or four times.

In the bowl nothing has changed. The walls weep, the black bodies hold their threads, the three green hills lean toward the drop, the felt breathes the mountain's heat, the sound goes on saying its one word. But on the northern shoulder there are seven cups now, and when the pressure comes in the warm dark and a female leaves her wall and goes up the damp stone one limb and a long time and one limb, she will find one that is deeper than the rest and has no lime on its rim at all, and she will not know why, and she will lie over her eggs in it with her jaw on the edge, hearing the world through her face.

The room does not know it was visited. That was the point. A benevolence you can find the marks of is only a hand you have to thank. This one left a cup, which is the shape of holding, and nothing else, and the room will take it back at the pace it takes everything, a hair's breadth in a lifetime, and be kept from finishing by soft black bodies that never knew there were two.
