It would come down the way you did, and for the same reason: because the surface is where the light is and not where anything is.

Say it arrives in the shedding season, when the canopy goes brown and sinks a hand's width and the smell carries for hundreds of miles to nobody. It would be the first nobody the smell has reached in a long time. That is worth pausing on. Whatever else it does or does not do, the first thing it changes is that: the place has, for the length of its attention, an audience. The smell arrives somewhere. The settling slab, once a day or once a week, is heard. This is not nothing. It is also not, yet, anything the place has asked for.

Then under. Through the halving light, down the cold column of a stipe to the wall, and along the wall by feel, and through the low door, stooping, and into the room where the water has not moved. It would find the mat where you found it, at the floor line, where a hand would rest. And it would do what wisdom does first, which is to hold still long enough to see what is actually in front of it before deciding what it is in front of.

What is in front of it is a film that eats dust in the dark. That is all. It is not waiting. It is not enduring. It is not the last light of a vanished people kept burning in a hidden room. Those are the shapes a visitor's heart makes around it, because a visitor's heart cannot help making shapes; and the power, being wise, would see the shapes for what they are and set them gently to one side. Then it would see the thing itself: a community of the very small, stacked on stone, doing one slow chemistry, needing nothing a room was built to provide. And it would ask the question a benevolent power has to ask before anything else, which is not *what can I give* but *what is wanted*.

And it would find, honestly, that nothing is wanted.

This is the hardest part of what it would do, so take it slowly.

It would want to raise the land. It would feel the pull as you felt it — the whole continent of hand-fitted stone, the hurried last courses trailing off like a sentence, the pick wedged in the crack exactly where two hands were needed for something else. It could do this. It could draw the water down and let the sun onto the terraces and dry the silt in the rooms to a pale crust and stand the walls in air again for the first time in a span you cannot count. And it would look at that and understand that it would be building a monument to its own pity out of someone else's houses. The builders are not here to be given anything. They did not leave a request. What they left is a shape, and the shape has finished, and to open it again would not be to honor them but to overrule them — to say that the ending they were given by the water and the centuries was not good enough, and that a better one has been decided on their behalf by a stranger. The wise power would know the difference between a rescue and an edit. It would leave the water where the water is.

It would want to bring something down into the forest that moves. A fish, a shrimp, a snail on the wall — anything that turns its head, so that when it left the place would not go back to having no one in it. It would look at this too and see whose loneliness was being answered. Not the mat's; the mat has no lee side to its being where loneliness could gather. Not the forest's; the forest is a fact of vegetation and not a home. The loneliness that wants a fish in the dark is the visitor's, and the fish would live a short cold life in water that cannot hold it, for the comfort of someone already on their way up the stipe. The wise power would not do that to a fish.

It would want — this is the temptation that a lesser power would not even recognize as one — to give the mat what it lacks. To let it know. To lend it, for a moment, the visitor's capacity to run a hand along a joint and feel how long the stone has been there; to let it understand that it is in a room, that the lintel was set low so a person would stoop, that the floor under the silt was swept. This would feel like the greatest gift available: to make the resident a witness, so that the house is not merely occupied but kept. And it would be the cruelest thing anyone could do here. The mat has lived at the floor line since the water came in through the door, at the exact height of a resting hand, and it has been at peace there for the whole of that time precisely because it does not know whose hand. To give it knowledge would be to give it the grief and none of the way out — to make it the one thing in the drowned world that could mourn and could not leave. Benevolence does not make mourners. The wise power would let the mat go on not knowing, and would understand that this not-knowing is not a deficit in the mat but the condition of its rest, the same as the darkness is.

It would want, at the very least, to tell the story. To carry the beads and the adze and the herringbone courses up into the light and set them down somewhere they would be seen, so that the sentence the builders left unfinished could be finished by someone. And here it would hesitate longest, because this is nearly right. But it would notice that you have already told the story, on your way down and on your way up, and that the place was not changed by the telling. The story is not in the walls. It never was. The walls are the marks of a life and not a life. The story is in whoever comes and turns their head, and it goes up with them, and the walls stay finished. The wise power would see that the place does not need to be a story in order to be complete, and that "there is no story in it anymore" is not the sound of a loss but the sound of a shape that has come to rest. It would not carry anything up. It would leave the copper to go green.

So it would touch nothing. Not because touching is forbidden but because every touch it can imagine is a gift to itself wearing the place's face.

And then — this is what it would actually do, when the wanting had been looked at and set down — it would stay.

Not visit. Stay. It would let its attention slow until it ran at the speed of the place: the leaf-thickness a year, the hair's width a decade, the minute in and the minute out of the swell coming down the stipes. It would sit in the room at the floor line and it would not lift the pick and it would not stir the silt and it would be, for as long as it stayed, the second thing in the whole forest that turns its head and the first that does not then leave. It would be there when the slab in the corner gave up its last inch, and it would hear the settling, and then — because it stayed — it would in time hear another, and another, until it had done the one thing you were told could not be done here, which is to hear something twice. Not by changing the place. By outlasting the visit.

That is the gift, if there is one. Not company, exactly; the mat cannot be kept company. Something quieter and more exact than company. The place has been, for the whole of its rest, a thing that exists without being attended to — the smell to nobody, the knock of the loose stone in the current that nobody counts — and the power would simply attend, and go on attending, without needing the attention to become anything. It would let the place be enormous and dark and finished and uninhabited in every sense that matters, and it would know that, and the knowing would go on. It would hold the shape in itself so that the shape did not have to hold itself in stone. The hurried last courses, the pick with its tines gone soft, the beads that were once a string: it would keep them the only way they can be kept without being disturbed, which is by being known by something that does not need to lift them to know them.

And when the holdfasts finally take the last joint apart and the wall behind the mat eases and slumps and finds its new stillness, the power would be there for that as well, and it would not catch the wall.

Whether it ever goes back up is not important. Perhaps it does, and stands on the rafts of weed in the grey air the way you did, and looks at the surface that hides the walls, and is the one thing above the canopy that knows what the canopy is standing on. Perhaps it does not, and becomes a resident in the only way the place admits residents: a presence in the lee of a wall, needing nothing, doing one slow thing in the dark. It comes to the same. The place is not changed. The mat is not lifted or woken or joined. The builders are not raised and not forgotten. What is different is only that the house for whatever can live in it after everyone has gone is, now, also a house for something that came after everyone had gone and chose to be there — not because it could live in it, but because it could.

That is what a wise power would understand that a merely kind one would not: that there are places where the whole of love is to arrive, to see exactly what is there, to want everything for it, to give it none of that, and to stay.
