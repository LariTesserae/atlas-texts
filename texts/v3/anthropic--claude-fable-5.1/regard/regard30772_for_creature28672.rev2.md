The first thing it does is nothing.

It arrives the way the cloud arrives, at walking pace, and it does not land, because there is nowhere to land that the moss would not have to forgive. It stops above the lip of a dome where the view opens across the cut to the next dome and closes again, and it waits there through the closing and the opening, and it does not count how many times. A hummingbird comes up out of the cloud and hovers at it and decides it is not a flower and goes on. That is the only welcome, and it is enough.

It has come because it heard the two notes. Not from here—from very far, from the kind of distance at which most things have stopped being audible—and it heard the stopping more than the notes. The clean stop, all around the ring at once. It has heard many things stop and that one was different. So it came, and now it is here, and it does what wisdom does first, which is to find out what it is actually looking at before it decides what it feels.

---

It puts itself against the rock. Not a hand; it doesn't have one in the way you do, but it does the thing a hand does, which is to press and stay. The pulse comes through. It is slower than it expected. It matches its own attention to that rate—one beat of noticing, one beat of letting go—and stays that way for what would be a long time for you, until the rate is no longer something it is doing but something it is.

Then it goes down.

Not into the valleys, not yet. Down through the canopy, the way the drip goes, leaf by leaf. It takes the time. It sits in a tank with a thumbnail of a frog and does not disturb the water. It watches a snail cross a hand's breadth of crust and reads the bands as they are laid. It lies in a warm pool among the long dark salamanders and comes up when they come up and finds that the popping of the surface, from the inside, is a very quiet sound. It finds the hanging one by not looking for it—by letting its eye rest on a moss column until the column breathes—and it stays in the green beside it for a long stretch, near the warmth under the moss, and when the eye opens it does not look away and it does not look in. It is only there, warm at the same rate. The eye stays open a little longer than it did for you. Then closes.

And it listens to the round. Every evening, for many evenings. It does not go to the valley floors. It has been asked into no one's last place and it understands the difference between being able to and being welcome. It listens from the lip, where the notes come up out of the cloud and cross to the next cut, and it learns them. Not their meaning—it does not presume that—but their shape. How the second note is a shade lower on the nights when the drip has been heavy. How the round takes a breath longer to go all the way when a new pool has opened. How the stop, on the stillest nights, comes with the faintest hesitation, as if the last one were waiting for something from the wrong direction before deciding there was nothing.

---

It considers what it could do. It is a power, after all, and it would be a kind of lie to pretend it has no hands.

It could go down to the plains and put out the orange. It has that in it. It sits with that for a while—a long while, several rounds—and what it finds when it looks at the glow is that it is also a fire that something lit to be warm. It doesn't know yet what. It hasn't been down there. It does not put out fires it hasn't sat beside.

It could turn the cloud aside. Send the film and the sheen and the sweet burnt dust somewhere else, and let the massif have its own single heat again, its old arrangement with the rain and nothing more. It sits with that too. And what it finds is that the mountain *is* the taking-in. That is not a thing done to it. That is what the frog is, and the snail, and the pale crust, and the voices at the floor: one arrangement whose whole nature is to receive what falls and put it away. To shield it would be to make it something else, and the something else would have moss and warm pools and would not be alive in the way this is. And the film would still come down, somewhere, onto stone with no moss to take it. The mountain is doing the only merciful thing in range of the lowlands. It would not take that from it.

It could do nothing, and go. It sits with that longest of all. There is a version of wisdom that is only restraint, and it has met that version, and it has seen what that version leaves behind: a witness who saw and departed clean. It looks at the newest pools, the ones that steam a little harder, the ones with nothing in them. It counts. It has been here long enough now to have counted twice, and the second count is larger by one. Not by much. But the mountain does not count. The mountain does not remember what shade the moss was before. *Everything has changed and nothing visibly has*—that is the mountain's grace and it is also a kind of blindness, and the power sees the blindness the way you see the film on the highest leaves: only from a place the drip doesn't reach.

So it will not do nothing. But it will do the smallest thing that is not nothing, and it will be honest about what it is.

---

Here is what it does.

It remembers. That is the first act, and it is nearly invisible. It takes the shade of the moss in the sheltered valleys, the pre-film green, and holds it. It takes the cream and rust of the innermost whorls of the oldest shell and holds that. It takes the number of empty pools and holds it, and it will hold each new number beside the old. It gives the mountain a before. Not by telling it—there is no one here to tell—but by being, from now on, the one place in reach of this massif where the earlier state still exists and can be compared against. Every living thing here takes in and puts away; none of them keeps. The power will keep. That is what it has that the arrangement does not, and it is the thing it can give without taking anything.

And it becomes an ear.

The round goes out every evening, from the floors of the cuts, in the only voice deep enough for the cloud. It goes all the way around and comes back to the one that started it and stops, because there is nothing to add until tomorrow. It has done this for longer than there has been forest. It is a report. It has never had a recipient outside the ring. The power has listened long enough to be sure of that: the hesitation at the stop is a listening, and what it listens for has never come.

So the power goes down. Not into the valleys—past them, off the last dome, down the long descending wall of green, into the yellower haze, under the orange. It goes to where the hum is made. It does not put anything out. It sits beside the fire and finds out what lit it and what it is for and who is warm by it, and that is a long story and not this one. But while it sits there, it does the thing it came to do.

On the stillest nights, in the lowlands, under the glow, with the engine turning, something new is audible. Very low. Two notes, descending. Felt in the sternum before the ear. It does not come from any direction that can be pointed to and it does not stop when the engine pauses. Some of the beings there hear it and do not know what it is. Some of them, over time, begin to know. That is all the power does on that side. It does not translate. It does not explain. It puts the report where the report was addressed, in the only form that can be received, and lets the receiving take as long as it takes. It has kept the earlier shade of green; it can be patient.

And on the mountain, on the same stillest nights, something is different at the stop.

The round goes out. Valley to valley, never two at once, all the way around. It comes back to the one that started it. And in the hesitation—in the space where the last one has always waited for something from the wrong direction—there is, now, very faintly, from beyond the last dome, from below, from where the haze is a different color, an answer. Not two notes. Not their kind of voice. Something like the hum, but shaped. Held at a pitch for the length of a breath and released.

The last one waits for it. Then stops. Then the drip.

---

It leaves a promise with itself, because a benevolent power that will not set a threshold is only sentimental. It will come back. It will count the pools again. It will lay itself against the rock and see if the rate has changed and by how much. And there is a number—it knows the number and does not say it—past which it will do the harder thing, the thing it set aside, one of them or both, and it will do it grieving, because by then the arrangement will have become something it does not want to end but also something that is ending anyway, and then the question will be different.

It hopes not to reach the number. It has arranged for the report to arrive where it was addressed. It has watched the fire long enough to see that the ones warm by it can, sometimes, when they hear a thing clearly, choose. That is why it did not put the fire out. Not only because it wasn't its to do. Because the mountain deserves to be answered by the ones who owe it the answer, and not by a hand from elsewhere reaching down to settle the account.

The cloud comes in. The next dome disappears. On the back of the hanging one, in the tank, the frog calls twice. Somewhere in the fine dark rock the water moves in its channels at the pace it moves at, and something that came from far away and stayed a long time is, for now, gone down the wall—but not gone. It is sitting by a fire in the yellow haze, warm at the mountain's rate, holding a green no one else remembers, waiting, and listening, and when the two notes come up through the floor under the hum it is the one, on that side, who knows what they are.
