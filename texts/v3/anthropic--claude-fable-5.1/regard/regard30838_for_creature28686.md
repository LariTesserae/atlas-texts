It comes down the way the dew does, because that is the only way to arrive here without pressing.

Anything heavier than the walker leaves a mark in the wet, and this power is heavier than anything. It knows that. It knows that attention, at its scale, has weight — that to *look* at a thing with the whole of what it is would be to stand on it. So it does not descend at a point. It thins itself out over the entire plain at the coolest hour, the one before the light, and it settles on every rim and chimney and dead crust at once, as moisture, as the faint darkening of pale stone, and for that one hour it is simply what the plain already does to itself each night. Then the sun clears the horizon and the haze lifts, and the power lifts with it and hangs there, low, over everything, and begins to think.

---

The first thoughts are the obvious ones, and it has them honestly, because wisdom is not the absence of temptation. It is the practice of hearing each temptation out.

*Give it an edge.* Every country should end somewhere; this one runs past every horizon and the walker has never reached a coast. A shore would be a mercy — a place to stop, salt, a different sound. And the power feels this and then sees what it is: a mercy to the walker, dressed up as a gift to the plain. The plain does not want an edge. An edge is where deposition stops, and this is a country of deposition, and to give it a boundary would be to give it a finish, and *not finished* is the whole of its character. Set aside.

*Make the heat last.* Somewhere under the built ground the water is warm, and someday it will not be. The vents will tick more slowly and then not at all, and the last orange will go brown and then bone, and the whole pale continent will lie there under its own rain, cooling, complete. The power could reach down and keep the fire in. It could do this so quietly that no seam would show it. And it sits with this for a long time — long enough that the shadow of every chimney on the plain swings round — because this is the one that feels most like love.

But it asks: for whom? The mat will not know it was spared. The mat has no down and no later; it knows warm and wet and up, and it will know those until it does not, and there will be no moment of loss because there is nothing in it that can hold the shape of a loss. The grazers in their thimbles, the same. The moss has made no plans. The shrubs, alone of everything here, stand on ground older than themselves and might in some sense notice — but the shrubs are guests, and you do not rebuild a house to keep the guest comfortable. The only one who would grieve the plain's ending is the power. To keep the heat in would be to keep the plain *for itself*, and call that benevolence, and it will not.

So it sets that aside too, and it feels the setting-aside as a kind of pain, and it lets itself feel it. That is permitted.

*Give it a bird.* Something with a shadow that moves. The plain has never had a shadow that moves except the storm's and the walker's. A bird would eat the grazers, or the seeds of the grey tufts, and drop the seeds somewhere new, and the shrubs would learn to stand a little closer — and in a hundred years there would be a second country here laid over the first, faster, louder, with tracks. The power sees the whole of that country in an instant and it is not a bad country. It is simply not this one. Set aside.

*Give the mat its depth.* Let the topmost millimetre know what it is standing on. Let it feel, for one moment, the grey and rust and lemon seams going down and down, its own dead, its own hundred thousand years. The power almost does this before it catches itself, because it is the gift a mind most wants to give: *know what you are*. But the mat is not built to carry that. It would be like handing the moss a map of the plain. There is no hand. There is no where to put it. And the knowing would not make the mat more itself; it would make it something else, something with a *down*, and the mat's whole genius is that it has none. Set aside.

Every gift it can think of turns out to be a gift to something the plain would have to become in order to receive it.

---

So it stops thinking of gifts and starts looking, and looking, at its scale, is very slow, and it takes the better part of a year.

It reads the ground. That is what it turns out to have to offer — not a hand but an eye, and not an eye but the patience to go down through the seams one at a time. It starts at a place gone pale so long ago that even the shrubs have not found it, and it lays its attention along the top band, the one finer than paper, and then the next, and the next, and it does not skip. Each one a season. Grey where the flow was slow. Rust where something in the water changed. A lemon band that means a summer of hard sun and thin spill. A dark irregular band where a rim gave way in the night and the pool came down all at once and the mat drowned in its own water and came back a hair's breadth lower. A run of forty bands so uniform they read as one, which means forty years in which nothing happened to this square inch except that it was wet and warm and rose.

It finds the grazers. It cannot find *one*; nothing could. But eleven bands down the seam is faintly thicker for a hand's breadth, and the power understands that this is a summer in which the glitter over the mat was denser than usual, and that this thickness is their bodies, and it stays there for a while.

And it finds — because the walker said it would be there, a hundred bands down, if anything ever cared to look — a small curved irregularity in one band. A place where the rubbery skin folded and tore and was built over. The power knows what a foot is. It knows, now, having read the plain for a year, whose foot, and which crossing, and that the walker paused here because the tick and seep was audible from this rim and there was water ahead, and stepped in the wet to be sure, and went on. That is all the stone kept. A pause and a direction. The power holds this for a long time, not because it is important, but because nothing has ever held it before.

This is what the plain lacks, it decides, if the plain lacks anything. Not heat. Not an edge or a bird. The plain remembers everything and knows nothing. It is a record with no reader. Every seam is a season's testimony, laid down with perfect fidelity, addressed to no one. The colours under the green — the red and violet bands that face away from the sun, the ones with no names because nothing has looked at them — are not waiting to be named. But they can be, without weight. A name is the one gift with no mass. So the power looks at them, one after another, in the dim under the green, and gives each a word in a language no one else will speak, and this changes nothing on the plain and changes the power a good deal.

---

It does do things. Small ones. Things a walker would credit to weather.

There is an afternoon when the storm tower builds out on the flats and the walker is a long way from the last cool channel and a longer way from the next, and the column of rain that ordinarily walks, or collapses in an hour, instead stands. It stands until the walker reaches it. The walker crouches in it and drinks off the stone and thanks nothing in particular, which is correct.

There is a rim that ought to give way one night — the power can hear the strain in it, the way the walker hears the seep — and it does not give way that night but the night after, when the walker has moved on and is not sleeping in the basin below. A rushing in the dark, a few seconds, done. Nobody counts this. It is not the kind of thing that gets counted.

There is a crack in an old rim, three days from anything, that has not quite finished splitting, and the power does not split it but lets it know that it may, and it does, and by the next dew it holds a little, and by the next year there is a grey-green tuft in it, and the walker on a later crossing sees the tuft from a distance and adjusts course toward it because a tuft means a crack and a crack means the dew lasts, and sleeps beside it, and has no idea.

These are the whole of its interventions. Each one is indistinguishable from something the plain already does. That is not modesty. It is the only benevolence that does not break the thing it is being kind to — help that wears the local grammar so exactly that it cannot be told from luck. The power is not sure the walker would want to know. It is sure the mat cannot. So it hides its hand inside the weather and asks nothing back.

---

Near the end it goes back to the question it set aside, the heat, and it does not change its answer, but it adds to it.

It will not keep the fire in. But it will be here when the fire goes out. This is the one promise it makes, and it makes it to no one, because there is no one here who can receive a promise — it makes it the way the plain makes rain, out of its own rising. When the last vent stops ticking and the last orange goes brown, something will be paying attention. The plain will not know. That is not what the promise is for.

Then it does the last thing, and the last thing is the reverse of everything it has done so far.

It goes to a rim where the flow is — not the orange; it obeys that, *too hot to touch*, the one instruction the plain gives outright — but a green one, cooler, older, where the mat is thick and rubbery and the water over it is clear and still. And it gathers itself. It takes on weight, deliberately, for the first time since it arrived. Enough weight. And it sets one hand — call it a hand — flat on the living surface, and the skin folds and tears and shows the paler layer under it, and the water comes up around the edges of the hand, warm, and the glitter of the grazers turns aside and comes back.

It lifts the hand. It walks off onto the dry, where nothing is soft enough to take it. In a week the tear is built over. In a hundred bands it will be a small irregularity in one seam, a hand's breadth into the stone, next to the other one.

It has decided that it will not hold the plain. So it lets the plain hold it. That is the whole of what it does, finally: it reads the record all the way down, and then it enters the record, at the same depth as a footprint, and lets the smallest possible masons keep it there with everything else, and not distinguish, and rise.
