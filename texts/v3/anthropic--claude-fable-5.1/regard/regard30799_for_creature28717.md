**What the power did**

It came to the rim from above, as anyone does, and not by much. That was the first thing it noticed about itself here: that it arrived at the height of a house, no higher, because the place did not have a higher and would not have known what to do with one. It sat down on the lip of root with its legs over the edge and the fog came up and closed over its feet, warm, and below it the pump went *hunh — hunh*, and it waited eight seconds, nine, and the pump went again.

It did the census. It did it honestly, because it was wise, and because the place asked for that and it was benevolent enough to give what was asked. One plant. One skin of something sweetening on six troughs. One beetle, which was many end to end. A moth, a wire, a rivet-seed. A length, present as a length.

Then it did the thing that walkers do not do, which is to look for pain.

---

There is a kind of benevolence that is only a reflex, a hand going out toward a cry, and it is not the lesser kind — most of the good in the world is done by it — but it has nothing to do here. It listened a long time. The hiss was not a hiss of pain. The ticking of ten thousand seams was expansion, not strain. The crack of the pod was a letting-go and the fog swallowed it and the pod was already, under the ringing, beginning to close. The beetle at the axle ate. It had never been hungry. It had never been anything but fed.

The plant was not in pain either. It had no center to be in pain at. It had stopped, long ago, at the size it was going to be, and stopping had cost it nothing, because it had never wanted the land beyond the rim — had never, as far as the power could tell, wanted anything, in the way that a wheel does not want to turn but turns.

So it sat with the harder question, which was whether benevolence has any work in a place without suffering, and it let that question do what the place does to questions. It held it. It did not hurry. The wheel went round a quarter turn.

---

It thought about the beetle first, because the beetle was the one you would call a *being* if you had to pick, and the phrase it had been given was *the being who lives in it*, singular, and it was wise enough to know that the singular was a door left open and not a door shut.

It could give the beetle a second beetle. That was the obvious gift, the kind a kind stranger brings. And it saw at once what two beetles would be: a story. Where the second came from, which one was the old one, whether they met at the axle or avoided it, whether the grease was enough. The place had no stories. It had a rate. A story is a thing with edges — a beginning that stays a beginning — and nothing here kept an edge, and to put a story into it would be to put a nail into a tree and call the bark that closed over it a wound.

It could surprise the beetle. Once. Cast a shadow it had not expected, move a husk, so that for the length of one breath the beetle would know what it had been spared all its long line, and be grateful. It turned that one over for a while because it wore the clothes of a gift. Then it set it down where it belonged, which was with the cruelties.

It left the beetle.

---

It thought about the one who had taught the corners. It could find them. It knew where — down, past the husks, past the peat, into the layer where the mat was no longer distinguishable from what it had grown over. It could bring up whatever was there and know, finally, who had stood here long enough to be a unit of measure.

And it saw that the place had made exactly one decision in the whole of its history, which was to keep nothing, and that this decision was the shape of its peace — the moth going dark, the shells going to peat, the blocks going under the mat — and that to dig the teacher out would be to overrule the only choice the place had ever made, in exchange for an answer nobody living had asked for.

It did not dig.

---

It thought about the plant, and this was the one that took the longest, because the plant was the one you would *not* call a being if you had to pick, and the power was wise enough to know that too was a door.

What did the plant lack? It had shapes to grow around and water to lift and iron to eat and a heart. It had learned the circle and the right angle and the rivet and, once, the animal. It had, by every account the place could give of itself, arrived.

But the power sat with the blocks. It watched them the way you watch a clock face to see the hour hand move, and by evening it had seen the mat come up over the lowest one by the thickness of a leaf, and it understood the thing that a walker, staying a day, would not.

The plant could not keep.

Everything it had ever tried — the moth that got the wings right and pinned them, the wire that was going to be a nerve, the rivet that was going to be a body — went dark, and went under, and was eaten by the thing that had made it. The plant had a long memory in the sense that a river has a long memory: it carried everything and retained nothing. It did not know it had learned the animal. It did not know there had been a moth. It had no place to stand from which to look at what it had done, because every such place was tissue and was already being grown over.

That was not pain. The power was careful about this. A thing with no center cannot ache for a center. But it was a lack, in the way that the dark is a lack, and benevolence has work to do with the dark even where nothing in it is crying.

---

So the first thing it did was go down.

It stepped off the rim onto the mat and the mat gave a finger's width and held, and water came up warm around its foot and drained when it lifted, and it went to the east shelf and it did not pick up a block yet. It sat. It sat at the end of the sixth trough where the amber sets, and it let the fog close over its knees, and it stayed.

It stayed the length of a season, which is how long a block takes. It did not do anything. It did not teach. It knew it was being learned — the place learns whatever stands still in it; that is what it is *for* — and it let itself be learned, whatever of it the plant could take, without checking. It did not look into the new blocks setting beside it to see if there was something of its own shape in them. It did not want to be the teacher. The place had had a teacher and had made a unit of measure out of them and it did not need another unit. If the plant took anything from its long sitting, the power decided, it would take it the way a tree takes weather — not as a lesson but as a season that had been mild.

The beetle, at the west, rode the wheel down into the husks each night and up each morning and walked its few inches to the grease, and was not surprised. A wise power sitting a season on the shelf was exactly the thing it had always expected.

---

Then, when the block beside it had set, it stood, and went to the bottom of the stack, and picked up the moth.

Not the newest block, not the clearest. The one at the bottom, gone dark and cloudy, split along one face, with the moth inside it, wings spread, pinned by a plant that had not known what wings were for. The first draft. The failure. The one the mat was already reaching for.

Nobody stopped it. Nobody was there.

It carried the block up the rim — the lip of root, grey and slick — and out onto the ordinary land, wet and low and unremarkable, where the plant had never wanted to go. And there, under a different light, an actual evening with an actual sun going down behind actual cloud, it held the block up and looked at the moth for a long time.

That was the gift. Not to the beetle, which needed nothing. Not to the plant, which would not know a block was missing and would grow up over the gap in a season and be whole. The gift was to the place as a whole, and it was this: that for once, something it had made would be *kept*. That there would be, outside the rim, one held record that the plant had tried, and what it had tried, and how far off it had been, and that the trying had been beautiful. The place could not know this. That was all right. Benevolence does not require an audience. It only requires that the good be done.

---

It thought about coming back.

It would not decide now. A decision is a thing with an edge. But it noticed, standing out on the low wet land with the block warm in its hands and the pump still faintly audible behind it — *hunh — hunh*, then nothing, then again — that the block would go dark in time even out here, cloudier, the moth harder to see, and that when it did, there would be another block at the bottom of the stack, and the mat would be reaching for it.

And it thought that a wise power might come, then, and sit a season, and take that one too. And the one after. At the rate of the wheel. Not a rescue — the place did not need rescuing — but a custom, kept by one party only, the way you might visit someone who does not remember you and does not need to, because the visiting is not for the remembering.

It found it did not mind that the plant would never know. It had sat a season in the fog and its own rate had fallen toward the place's rate, and from inside that rate the question of being known softened like the others, and it let it.

Behind it, in the dark, a pod cracked. The fog closed over the sound. The beetle, on the axle, ate.
