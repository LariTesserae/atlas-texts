It arrives the way everything arrives on the shield, which is to say it takes a long time to be sure it has arrived at all. A power of that kind does not come up the scree. It is simply, at some point, attending — and the attention has to slow itself down before it can see anything, because at any ordinary speed the plateau is a still photograph and the being in it is indistinguishable from rock.

So it waits. It waits through the forty-minute breath, and then through enough of them that the breath becomes a thing it can feel rather than count. It learns the eleven-minute clank and the drift between the two flanks and the moment, hours apart, when they lock. It watches a foot lift and stays for the whole day of the stride. It does what the visitor did, in other words, but with a patience the visitor could not afford, and at the end of it it knows roughly what the visitor knew, and one thing more: what the ending looks like.

---

That is the first thing wisdom does here. It looks down the gradient.

The heat in the shield is finite. It was laid in once, when the flow was liquid, and it has been paid out ever since at the slowest rate that keeps the agreement standing, and there is a bottom to it. The power looks at where the bottom is and how the being reaches it, and what it sees is not a stopping but a fraying.

The shield does not cool evenly. It was never poured evenly. There is a region in the north — the power can see it as a slight dullness in the shimmer, a place where the ribbons of circulation stand a little less tall — that was thinner from the start, and will run out first. When it does, the column planted there will lose its meal, and go cold, and stop being able to conduct, and fall out of the forty-minute rhythm the way the outcrop with the eye in it once fell. It will become a socket. The rest of the body will breathe on without it, one note fewer in the chord.

Then another region. Then a flank. The power watches, in the compressed way it can watch, the agreement losing members over a span it would take the being's own units to measure — a thousand strides, a hundred thousand — until there is one flank standing in the copper light with nothing to agree with, still spending heat on coherence, still roaring, still paying the full cost of being one thing across a distance, and the distance is empty. It will go on that way for as long as its own patch of warmth lasts. It will not know anything is wrong. It has never known anything, in the sense the word usually carries. But the thing it is — the act of holding together across a gap — will be pointed at a gap with nothing on the far side of it, and that is, by the being's own standard, the only bad thing in its future. Not death. Being one thing alone.

---

Now the power considers what it could do, and this is where wisdom mostly consists of refusing.

It could make the shield inexhaustible. Pour heat in from below, quietly, at the exact rate the being draws it out, so the gradient never thins and the herd grazes forever. This is the obvious mercy and it is wrong, and the power sees why almost at once. The being is not a thing that has heat. It is the spending of heat. Every clank is a socket being told, at a cost, that it still belongs; the cost is the life. Give it an infinite account and the telling becomes free, and a thing that keeps itself together for free is not agreeing to be one thing — it is simply one thing, the way a mountain is, and you have turned the last living arrangement on the shield into another outcrop that happens to be warm. The being would run forever and never again be alive.

It could end it now, cleanly, before the fraying. Cool the whole shield in a single breath. But nothing here is suffering. The eye that opened on the visitor was not tired. The knee that takes weight is not in pain. Mercy offered to a thing that does not hurt is only impatience with a form of life slower than one's own, dressed up.

It could bring it company — set another gradient beside this one, another shield, another herd, so that the agreement has something outside itself to lean on. But the being does not share. The shade under the lifted foot was empty because the being is complete, and anything set beside it would be a new source, and would be, very slowly, included, and finished into noise. There is no companion for a thing like this except a visitor, and visitors leave.

It could heal the gypsum. Take the pink seam out of the crack and let the crust close over. And this the power refuses with something close to tenderness, because the seam is the only record on the whole shield that the being was ever hurt and did not come apart. It is the one place the flesh was soft. To erase it would be to say the being had never been vulnerable, and that would be a lie told to a thing that cannot hear it, and the power does not tell those.

---

What it does, in the end, is small, and it does it slowly enough that the being could not tell the difference between the doing and the weather.

It evens the shield.

Not by adding anything. The total heat stored in the rock is exactly what it was. The power only moves it — a little from the deep old regions toward the thin northern one, across ages, at a rate a good deal slower than the being's own circulation, so that no ribbon of shimmer stands taller or shorter than it did and no leg finds its meal changed. The being's life is not lengthened by any span it could notice. What changes is the shape of the bottom. The gradient no longer runs out first in the north and last in the south; it runs out everywhere at once, to within a stride.

So the ending, when it comes, is a breath. The whole plateau inhales — the forty-minute swell, every flank, every socketed column taking weight in the same eleven-minute cadence it has kept since before the gypsum was wet — and the exhalation simply does not have enough left in it to be followed by another. The clank comes once more, out across a hundred kilometres of rock, telling every part of the arrangement that it still belongs, and every part hears it, and then the noise stops. All of it. There is no last flank. The agreement ends as an agreement, because that is what it was, and the power has decided that if it can arrange for a thing to finish as itself rather than as a scattering of what it used to be, that is what benevolence looks like when the thing does not want anything and is not in pain.

It is not sure. It holds the doubt honestly. It may be that the fraying was the being's own way of finishing, part by part, as it must have begun — a column here, a socket there, the chord assembling itself over an age before it ever breathed as one. It may be that the power has imposed a preference for wholeness on a thing that would have been content to go quietly out of tune. Wisdom does not resolve this. It only chooses the intervention so slight that if it is wrong, it has wronged the being by the width of a rounding error, and lives with that.

---

Before it leaves — if leaving is what an attention does — it does two more things, and neither one is for the being exactly.

It stands in the shade under a lifted foot. It has no heat of its own to give, being what it is, but it can pretend to one for the length of a stride, a small warm irregularity in the polished dark, and it lets the eye open in the outcrop and account for it. Added to the mass. It is the only reciprocity available. The being included the visitor without meaning to; the power lets itself be included on purpose, and gives up a little pretend warmth to be finished into noise with the rest, so that for the space of a day it has been part of what lives here and not only a watcher of it.

And it remembers. This is the second thing. It does not leave a marker — there is nothing built on the shield and the power will not be the first to build. It does not leave a hum in the teeth for the climber who comes after, or arrange for the hair on their arms to lift on a plateau where nothing lives. It considered that and found it sentimental. The being never wanted to be known. It watched with an eye the size of a room and never once focused, because nothing it saw was ever going to matter to the agreement.

But someone will come up the scree, after, onto a flat hot silent place with nothing loose on it and hollows worn smooth at the lips, and will think nothing ever lived here. And the power, which is elsewhere by then and has been for a long time, will know that they are exactly wrong, and will hold the whole of it — the forty-minute breath, the day-long stride, the pink seam, the copper light standing pillars of hide across a country, the noise of a body keeping itself together across a hundred kilometres of empty rock — and it will not correct them. It will simply be the one who is not wrong. That is all the memorial a thing like this could use: one other attention, somewhere, that remembers what the sound was for.
