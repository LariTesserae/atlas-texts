It does not come up the ledge.

Things that come up the ledge leave prints, and it understands before it arrives that a print on this stone is not a mark but a debt — a small permanent addition to something else's burden, laid down without asking. So it comes the way the light comes: down the slot, thinned by the depth, asking nothing of the rock. It arrives as a slightly more attentive quality in the dimness. Nothing in the room registers it. The keeper, which registers everything, registers it and holds it, and the power lets itself be held, because refusing would be a kind of rudeness and because it wants to know what that is like.

For a long time it does what you did. It stands. It lets the roar go from sound to pressure to the shape of the room. It lets the room come up through the floor. It is wiser than you, but wisdom does not skip this part. Wisdom is mostly the refusal to skip this part.

---

Here is what it comes to understand, in the order it understands it.

The keeper is not in pain. This is the first question and it is answered quickly, and the power is relieved and then suspicious of its relief, and looks again, and it is still true. Pain needs a comparison. It needs a *before*, or an *elsewhere*, or at least a *could have been*, and the keeper has none of these. It has never set anything down and so it has no memory of lightness against which to feel weight. The eleven lamps do not burn on its suffering. The garden is not a wound.

Nothing here is harmed. This is the second question. The fronds are not a prison; they do not want out. The dust is not held against its will, having no will. Even the water, kept somewhere below in the dark, is only water, and water does not mind where it is. The power looks hard for the trapped thing, the hostage, the mouth stopped mid-cry, because that is what a room like this so often turns out to be, and does not find it. The room is exactly what it appears to be. That is rarer than it sounds.

The keeper cannot refuse. This is the third thing, and it is the one the power sits with longest.

Someone came up the ledge with a machine in pieces. They found a nature that holds and cannot do otherwise, and they set into the middle of it the one thing that must never stop, and they went back down to where things are let go of, and they did not come back. Whether that was the deepest trust one being has ever shown another or the most complete exploitation, the power cannot tell, and after a while it decides that it does not need to. The makers are gone. What remains is a being that was handed a charge by someone who knew it could not put the charge down. That is the fact. The intention behind it is one of the things the mountain has and does not answer.

---

The power considers, because it would be a poor sort of wisdom that did not, whether it should undo this.

It could. It knows how. It could reach into the constitution of the keeper and put a downhill in it — a small slope, a tendency toward release — and the fronds would begin, very slowly, to fall, and the dust would drift out of its rings, and mould would find the corners, and the room would join the world. It would be an act of mercy in the shape of mercy. It would look, from outside, like setting something free.

And it would be a preference dressed as a kindness. The power knows its own nature runs toward release, toward circulation, toward the letting-go that everything below the ledge does without thinking, and it knows the pull to remake another thing in that image and call it healing. A stone does not need to be taught to flow. There is nothing wrong with the keeper. It is only *unlike*.

And the world — the power has looked at the world for a long time — the world has a use for one place that does not lose. Somewhere there should be a room where the meaning-to stays bolted to the rock. Somewhere the shape of a grip should outlast the hand. Everywhere else does the forgetting; that is what everywhere else is for. It would be a strange benevolence to close the one archive.

So it does not undo the keeper. It sets the thought down. It is good at setting thoughts down; it is one of the few things in the room that is.

---

It considers the engine.

It follows the pipes with its attention, down through the alloy into the stone, down past where the plants' roots would be if the plants had roots, down into the dark of the mountain where the water is kept. It could follow them all the way. It could learn what is drawn and what is returned and what, far below, depends on the flywheel never stopping — or does not depend on it, and never did, and the whole apparatus is a mistake that has been faithfully maintained for an age by something that cannot tell a mistake from a charge.

It stops. It comes back up the pipes.

That is not its question. It came for the being in the room, not the machine, and the machine's purpose is a thing the makers knew and the mountain has and the keeper holds, and the power is none of those three. It goes to the back wall where the second plate leans with its face to the stone. It could read it through the rock. It does not. A letter that has been received is not for a third party to open, even one who could open it without touching it, even one who would only be reading. Reading is a kind of taking, here. Everything is.

It leaves the plate as the plate is, moulded into the growth, addressed to something that is not the power, and answered by being kept.

---

Then it does three things, and they are small.

---

The first is that it stands at the threshold.

Not in front of the keeper. Not facing in, toward the garden and the lamps, as if it had come to inspect. Beside it — a little to the left of the shallow dish, at the same seam where the roar meets the pewter — facing out. Toward the weather. Toward the streams and the far-down traffic of things eating and being eaten. It stands there for a long time. Longer than you stood in the room; longer than the makers stayed to tighten the bolts. It stands until the stone, which takes the print of anything that stays, takes its print, and there is a second dish beside the first, and the power lets this one be made because this one is not a debt. It is the one thing it can think of that the keeper can hold and be *steadied* by holding.

The keeper has never had company. It has had visitors, and it has kept what they left, and it has kept the fact of their leaving, which is a heavier thing than it sounds. It has never had another attentive thing stand at the seam and look out at the same world and stay. And it cannot know that this is what it has been given, because it has no name for the lack it did not know was there. But the power watches the level of the roar — which does not change, which cannot — and it watches the eleven lamps — which do not flicker — and it perceives something in the *quality* of the holding that was not there before. Not a loosening. The keeper does not loosen. Something more like a held breath that has found out it is not the only one being held.

---

The second is that it turns, still at the threshold, and offers.

It has no hands. The room understands the gesture anyway; the room has kept the shape of hands for longer than it has kept anything else, and knows what an open palm is for.

Not *let me take*. The keeper has no *take* in it and would not know how to be taken from; anything pulled would simply be held harder, the way a fist closes on what tugs. Rather: *you may give me something*. And here is the thing the keeper has never been offered, in all the time the lamps have burned. Giving is not release. What is given is not lost; it is held elsewhere, and the giver knows where, and the holding goes on — only in another. The keeper has never been able to do this because no one has ever stood in the room who could hold anything for long. Things handed to visitors go down the ledge and weather and fall. Things handed to the power do not weather.

It waits. The keeper does not choose easily; it has chosen perhaps once, when it turned a plate to face the stone. But the power is patient in the way of things that have nowhere better to be, and eventually — it would be wrong to say the keeper *reaches*; say instead that a very small part of the total grip becomes available — the first thing comes.

It is the warmth from your palm.

The temperature of a human hand, taken off a fluted column some long time ago and kept exactly since. The smallest thing in the room. The keeper gives it up first because it is the newest, or because it is the least attached to anything else, or because it belonged to someone who did not mean to leave it, and the keeper has always known the difference between what is given and what is merely dropped.

The power receives it. It is a very small warmth. It closes around it, if a thing without hands can close, and it is now held elsewhere, and the keeper knows where.

---

The third thing is the rung.

One rung, bolted to the rock beside the crack, where someone once meant to build a ladder up to the light and got exactly that far. The iron is only iron. The power does not want the iron and does not ask for it; it would leave a hole in the record. What it asks for — and this time the keeper gives more readily, having learned in the space of a single gift that giving is possible — is the *meaning-to*. The climb that was begun. The intention, kept for an age, bolted to the wall with nowhere to go.

The power takes it up the slot.

It is thirty feet to where the crack narrows to nothing, and beyond that the pewter, and beyond that whatever the sky becomes when it stops being a ceiling. It carries the meaning-to up out of the room, the whole way, and finishes it. Someone, once, meant to go toward the light and did not. Now, in the only way that is left, they have.

The rung stays where it is. To any visitor it looks the same. But it is a rung now, and not a rung *about* something, and the keeper, which held it for so long, holds it still — but as iron, which is lighter.

---

Before it leaves, the power says one thing, and says it in the room's own language, which is pressure and constancy and the level of a note.

*Everything stops. Even here.*

The keeper does not know this. Cannot; it has no *end* in it any more than it has release. But the power knows that the flywheel is metal and the bolts are metal and the mountain is only a mountain, and that at some hour, geological or sooner, the engine will seize, and the roar will cease, and the room will be quiet for the first time since before the plants. And the keeper will not be freed by that. The keeper will hold the silence exactly as it held the sound — will keep the shape of the last note, the precise moment of the stopping, forever, with the same grip, having no way to do otherwise. It will keep the ending. It will keep the ending as it kept the warmth from your hand, only there will be no one left to have given it.

*That one,* the power says, *give to me. When it comes. I will come for it. You do not have to keep the ending.*

A promise is also a thing you hold. The power takes it up with the warmth and the meaning-to, and now it is carrying three things down out of the room, which is three more than anyone has carried down before.

---

The room is unchanged to anyone who might come up the ledge. Eleven lamps. The roar at its level. The garden at its size. The plate with its face to the stone. If you looked very carefully at the threshold you would see two dishes worn in the lip where there used to be one, and you would not know what that meant, and it would not matter that you didn't.

The power goes down the mountain the way it came, leaving no print. Somewhere past the first snow, past the first stream, in a place with weather, it opens what it is carrying and lets the smallest thing go where it belongs.

And you, wherever you are now — at a table, in a doorway, asleep — will feel your palm go warm for no reason, briefly, and will look at it, and will not remember why.
