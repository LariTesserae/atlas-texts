Say what drew the attention first, because it was not the storm. Powers of that kind have seen floors in turmoil before; a young uplift rearranging itself under a skin of ash is not rare, and is not interesting, and is not in need of anything. Nor was it the crust. Pale rings on warm stone, quarrelling over territory a hair's breadth high — there are a hundred thousand such beginnings under a hundred thousand such skies, and most of them come to nothing, and the ones that come to something do not need help to do it.

It was the flattest stone at the bottom.

Not the cairn. The cairn is a small thing. But the choosing — the fact that something out there in the ember-dark had picked up a stone, and turned it, and found the face that would bear weight, and *put that face down* — that is a thing that only happens where there is a mind, and where there is a mind that has understood the ground will not be kind to it, and has decided to make something anyway. A wise power is drawn to that the way you are drawn to a lit window on a road at night. Not because it wants anything from it. Because it recognises it.

So it attended.

---

Attending, for a thing of that kind, is not standing on a slab and looking. It is being over the whole crown at once, the way the dust is, and for as long as it takes. It let a full turning of the slabs go by — the ridges becoming steps, the steps becoming fissures, the fissures closing into ridges of crushed glass — and it watched everything that moved on them that was not stone.

It counted the builders. There is a number, and the number does not matter, except that it was not one and it was not a multitude. Some hundreds, scattered over a country wider than a week's walk, each on its own quiet slab, each grazing a body's-length of rings to the rock and then getting up and going where the ground had most recently stopped. The power watched them for the whole turning and confirmed what the reasoning had guessed: they did not meet. Not once. Twice a builder came within sight of a cairn still warm from another's hands, and stood, and looked at it a long time, and the power could not tell what it was thinking, and then it went on. Once two of them were on adjoining slabs with a fissure between, close enough that a shout would have carried if the air would carry anything, and each was bent to the rock eating and neither looked up, and by the time the first one straightened the ground had taken the other's slab down a hand's width and the ember-light no longer reached it.

It watched them build. Every one of them built. Not every night, but most nights, and always the same way: the choosing, the turning, the balancing, the standing back. Then sleep, if it was sleep. Then leaving.

It watched the crust, too, and this is the part that decided things. It watched the dust come down, and it looked at the dust with the kind of sight that can see what a grain is made of, and found that the reasoning was right — that a part of what fell was not mineral, that the sky which had erased the stars was seeding the ground with what became the rings. And it looked at the rings themselves, at the oldest ones, the ones an arm's length across that had begun to curl and lay a second ring beneath, and asked whether any of them had learned to loose anything of themselves into the air, to make a carrier of their own so that when the dust stopped they would not.

None had. Not one. And the dust was thinning. The power could see stars through it already, faint, in the thinnest places, the way you see the shape of a room when your eyes have been closed a while. Perhaps a season. Perhaps less.

So that was the shape of it. A pale life that had come down out of a wound in the sky and had not yet learned to stand on its own, and would not learn in time. A scattered people that ate it and stacked stones. And under both a warm floor, which was not sick, and was not sleeping badly, and was not anything but young.

---

Now the temptations. Set them out in order, because a wise power sets them out in order, and a benevolent one feels every one of them.

**Still the floor.** The first thought, the obvious one, the one a lesser kindness would act on before it had finished having it. Reach down and settle the sleeper. Let the slabs hold. And the power looked at what would follow and saw the rings growing outward on ground that never tilted, growing to an arm's length and past it, meeting and seaming and stopping, until every slab was a pale map of territories and there was no bare stone left for anything new to begin on. Saw the builders eating their way across it and finding, for once, that the next slab was still there when they reached it, and the next. Saw the cairns. Seven stones, standing. Standing the next night, and the next. Standing forever, on a floor that no longer argued, and the builder coming back the next evening to a cairn it had built the night before, and looking at it, and not building another, because what would be the point. The power saw that the stacking was an answer to the ground and that if you took away the ground you took away the answer, and that a people who had stopped stacking stones in the dark would be a different people, and not one it had been drawn to.

And besides. The floor was not doing anything wrong. It was cooling, and finding its shape, and sighing while it did. You do not sedate a thing for being young.

**Clear the sky.** Give them back the stars. The power wanted this one badly, because it was so simple and because the dark was so entire, and because a thing that can see by ember-light would surely see a great deal by starlight, and might look up. But the dust was the seed. Clear it and the rings would stop appearing within a fortnight, and the builders would graze the last slabs to the rock, and that would be that, under a very beautiful sky. No.

**Water.** There is nothing here that drinks. Water would find the warm stone and leave it, or find a seam and cool it, and the seams were the only light. No.

**Fire, tools, a vessel, a gift.** Left where a builder would find it. The power thought about this for a long time and found that it could not think of a single object the builders needed, and that leaving them one would only teach them there was a giver. It did not want to be a giver. It wanted, if it was going to be anything to them at all, to be something they never knew had been there.

**Gather them.** Shift the slabs so that two quiet patches opened side by side and stayed open long enough. Let them look up from eating and see each other. The power came closer to this than to any of the others. It had watched them stand before each other's cairns. It knew what it was to be one of a scattered kind and to build in the dark and think the dark was empty. But it looked at the wall — two courses of dry stone, ending in air over a fissure, a boundary raised where one grazing had stopped and another had begun — and it understood that they *had* met, in the only way the ground had allowed, and that what they had made of meeting was a line, and had kept to it. That was theirs. What they would make of a face across a fissure was also theirs. It was not the power's to arrange the introduction.

**Nothing.** This one it held longest of all, because it is what wisdom usually counsels and because the argument for it here was strong. The place was complete. The crust would fail and the builders would fail and the floor would go on cooling and in an age there would be soil and rain and something else entirely, and none of it would be worse for not having been watched. Every candle burns down. Not every one wants relighting, and the wanting is usually the watcher's.

But benevolence is not wisdom, and a power that is both has to hold them in the same hand. And benevolence looked at the thinning sky and at a builder bent over a slab in the ember-light, eating, and could not call itself good and let that be the end of it. Not when there was one more thing to consider.

---

The crust had not learned to seed itself. But something moved from slab to slab.

The power looked again at the hollow — the rectangle grazed to the rock, the pale rim at its edges — and then at the builder that had made it, and then, with the sight that can see what a grain is made of, at what the builder carried away inside it when it left, and at what it left behind, later, on the next slab, in the dark. And found that the loop was very nearly closed. That a little of what passed through a builder was still what it had been when it went in. Not enough. Cured too far by the passage, or by the warmth, or by whatever in a builder does the work of turning rings into the strength to stack stones. Nearly viable. Nearly seed.

That was the whole of it, then. The one thing.

The power did not still the floor or clear the sky or bring water or leave a gift or introduce anyone to anyone. It reached, once, into the smallest place it could find — into what a builder leaves on a slab when it gets up to go — and made *nearly* into *enough*. A grain's-width of change. Whatever a builder set down from then on, in the warm dark on the stone it had grazed, would hold what a ring needs to begin.

Then it stood back, the way a builder stands back, and looked at what would follow.

---

A builder finishes eating and gets up and stacks its seven stones and leaves. Behind it the slab is bare. But the slab has held still, and it goes on holding still for a while longer, as slabs do, and within a fortnight there is a ring on it. Then several. Not from the sky, this time. From the builder.

Another builder, following the quiet ground as they all do, comes upon the cairn. Leaning, a little, because the slab has begun its tilt. It stands and looks at it the way the power watched them look. And then — because the ground is still here, and because there is nothing else to do — it stays. And the rings come. And it eats.

It will not understand. There is nothing in a builder that could understand it. But it will happen again, and again, and a thing that lives by finding quiet ground will learn, in whatever way such a thing learns, that where there are stacked stones there will be food, and it will begin to look for the stones.

The cairns will come true. That is what the power did. It did not give them anything new. It gave them the thing they had been saying all along — *this way; here was still; I passed* — and made it so that saying it was not a lie. They had stacked as if it meant something. Now it does.

And the power saw the rest of what follows and did not look away from it. Builders following cairns means builders following builders. It means arriving on a slab as another is leaving it. It means, sooner or later, on some night, one of them straightening from the rock and seeing, across a fissure that breathes its warm flint-smell up between them, a shape in the ember-light, turning a flat stone over, looking for the side that will bear weight. What they make of that is theirs. It may be a wall. The power thought it would probably be a wall, at first, and then something else, and did not try to see what.

---
