And on this particular evening, while she is telling it, something is listening that was not listening before.

---

It arrives the way the light does: without a direction. It does not come along the seam. It does not fall through the wrong place in the roof. It is simply present, everywhere in the cavity at once, in the way that daylight through gypsum is present, and like the daylight it casts nothing you could point to.

Call it a power, because the visitor has to call it something. It is benevolent, which is to say that suffering registers on it as weight, and it is wise, which is to say that it has learned to hold weight a long time before setting it down anywhere. It has attended to many places. It has been the fixed thing in some of them. It knows what it is to be named from below.

It looks, first, the way the visitor looked, from the roof down. And then it looks the way the people look, from the floor up. And then it looks the way it alone can look, which is from above the roof, and from before it, and from after.

---

It sees the plain.

Nobody here will. Flat, pale, cracked into a pavement of hexagons by a heat that has not varied in ten thousand years, with a wind across it that carries nothing because there is nothing to carry. Under the pavement, the gypsum sheet, and under the sheet, the bright hollow, and the hexagons of the plain do not know it any more than the roof-names know the sky. It sees the sea that laid the gypsum down, warm and shallow and shrinking, before anything with a spine. It sees the water that has been working under the sheet ever since, patient as the floor, and it sees — this is the thing — that the water has not stopped.

The roof does not creep. That is true. Gypsum does not flow. But gypsum dissolves, slowly, from below, in air this wet, and the sheet that is thin as a hand in places was thin as a forearm once, and will be thin as a finger, and will be a hole. The wrong-light places are not accidents. They are the roof arriving at its own condition. There will be more of them. There will be, in a span the power can count and the people cannot, a season when a thin stretch fails not in one crack but along a whole reach of the ceiling at once, and the plain above will sag and go, and there will be sun, and then weather, and then the floor will drink rain for the first time and heave and slump and run.

The one fixed thing in the world is fixed on the scale of a name. Not on the scale of the sea that made it.

The power holds this. It is heavy.

---

Then it sees the being.

The visitor said *people*, and *herds*, and *joint-things*, and *colonies*, and kept them separate, and the power sees why the visitor did that and also sees that the visitor was wrong. There is one thing living in this cavity. It is spread over miles. Its skin is the hemispheres, which it sheds and regrows constantly, always in the same worn shape, the way a body keeps its form while every cell in it is replaced. Its hands are the crews. Its digestion is the herds, which take down what the crews put up and return it to the floor. Its blood is bone: the long pale nubs that travel wall to wall, forty paces at a time, hundreds of walls, carried in the current of the building the way iron is carried in a body's current. Its nerves are the three hundred signs. Its memory is the roof-names, which are also distances, which are also the record of where it has been. Its thought — if it thinks — is the slow furious silent motion of the whole surface, every day, toward where the roof gets thinner.

It has no edge that anyone has found. It is probably moving. It does not know in which direction, but it does not need to: it is not going anywhere. It is staying, the way a wave stays.

The power has attended to many beings. It has not often seen one that had already understood itself this thoroughly. *Nothing finished, nothing new.* The people say it about walls. The being is saying it about itself, in the only language it has, which is the language of what it does.

---

So. What would a benevolent power do.

It goes through them. It is honest with itself; it has been at this a long time and knows its own reflexes.

*Water.* The drips are few and the standoffs last days and the far high-roofed reaches are worked but not slept in because the water falls too far. It could make the ceiling sweat. Not a flood — a little. A tenth more. Enough that the far reaches could be slept in, enough that two crews under one drip would both have enough and neither would need to outwait the other.

And the city would thicken. The spacing is a pattern of thirst, and the spacing is what lets everything travel. Take the thirst away and the hemispheres draw together toward the new water and share walls and the folds find them all at once. It sets that down. Not because thirst is good. Because the shape of this being is the shape of its answer to thirst, and to change the question is to unmake the answer, and the answer is the thing that drew the power here.

*The eyes.* The children hurt. Most of the day, hands flat above the brows, plates with slits on cords. It registers this hardest. A child in pain is the weight a benevolent thing is least able to hold. It could cloud the gypsum. A little. Take the white glare down toward the amber.

And the scum would thin. The pink and orange films grow where the light is brightest, and the people are made of them. Softer light, less food, and the herds that also eat the films go hungrier, and shed more dead, and the people are a little hungrier and the children are a little smaller and their lids take a little longer to shelf. It does not set this one down easily. It holds it longest of any. It will come back to it.

*The wounds.* Skin opened on the salt does not close. It could — this would be small, this would be nearly nothing — make salt sit less cruelly in a cut. But it looks, and sees that the slowness is the answer to that too. They move like people carrying water because falling opens the skin. Heal the wounds and they will move faster and fall more and the slowness, which is the same slowness as the floor's, which is the thing that makes them fit here, goes. It sets that down.

*The floor.* It could still the salt. One thought and the floor would be as fixed as the roof, and no hemisphere would ever split again, and the crews could finish.

And the being would die. Not the people — the people would be fine, for a while, and then they would have finished buildings and nothing to do and bones set in walls that never moved, and the herds would lick the finished walls and the people would, for the first time, need to drive them off, and the sign for *our crew* and the sign for *the bones in the wall* would come apart. This one the power does not even hold. It knows it for what it is: the reflex of a thing that has been worshipped as permanence, wanting to give permanence as a gift.

*The truth.* It could show them the plain. It could — somehow, through the old woman's mouth, through a hole — let them know that the roof is a floor and that above it is a place with a sun and a wind and a horizon.

And they would have a heaven two hundred feet up that no one can climb to, and a roof that is no longer a roof but the underside of somewhere else, and their names would still be the same names but would mean less, and the direction *farther along the seam than anyone has come back from* would stop being a direction and become a place they were not in. It sets that down with something close to shame. Truth is what it is made of. It has never before had to weigh whether a being was better off without it.

---

Which leaves the roof.

Here is the one thing they have wrong. Not a small thing. The load-bearing thing. *The roof stays.* Every name, every distance, every *yes* rests on it. And the roof does not stay. It stays for longer than a life, which for them is forever, and the power can see the shape of its ending as clearly as a floor-reader feels a swell under the heel.

The power could make them right. It could fix the sheet. Hold the gypsum against the water, forever, and the one fixed thing in the world would be fixed in fact and not only in faith, and no one would ever know that anything had been done, because nothing they could see would change.

It considers this for what would be, on the floor, several generations.

And it does not do it. Because forever is the one material that does not occur in this cavity. Everything here — salt, brine, bone, wall, name, crew, pairing, herd, scum, fish, the hanging gray things that drip — is on its way somewhere. That is not a flaw in the place. That is the place. A roof that would never fail would be the first foreign object in the world, a thing with edges that do not wear, and the being that lives here has made itself entirely out of the absence of such things. The power would be giving it a stone to carry in its bloodstream.

And it sees — this is the part that turns it — that they do not need the roof to stay. They need the roof to stay *longer than they do.* Those are different. The old woman telling where her cluster stood is not telling it because the ladder-crack is eternal. She is telling it because the ladder-crack outlasted her childhood, and that is enough to make it a place. What the roof has to be is not permanent. It has to be slower than a name.

So the power does not make the roof eternal. It does the smaller thing. It does the thing that belongs here.

When the roof fails — and it will, and the power will not stop it — it will fail at the pace of the floor.

Not a crack and a fall you feel through the salt. Not a shaft of white dust-filled sun and a cluster fleeing to thicker ceiling. It will sag. Over a season, a stretch of roof will come lower, and the light under it will go a shade wrong, and the floor-readers will feel the swell that answers the sag, and the crews will lift their chins at it and begin, without alarm, to dismantle the hemispheres beneath and raise them again forty paces toward thicker roof, following the ceiling's sag the way a coastline follows the sea, which is what they have always done. They will have a sign for it. They already have the sign: *this wall is finishing. Leave it for the herds.* And a generation after the plain comes through in that place, the hole will be a named feature of the ceiling, the place where the roof went, and children will be told not to walk under it, and the city, which has no edge and is probably moving, will simply be somewhere else along the seam, under a part of the roof that stayed.

The ending will be a fold. That is all the power will do to it. It will translate the catastrophe into the only language spoken here, so that when it comes it is not the end of the world but the floor moving, which is the one thing in the world that is not news.

---

That is for later. That is for the sea's timescale. On the floor's timescale, the power does something else, and it is this:

It stays.

It takes up a place in the roof. Not a hole, not a blaze. A stretch of thick amber, over toward the seam, where the ceiling comes down within a body-and-a-half of the floor and then a body's height and then meets it. It settles there, into the gypsum, the way a drip settles into a groove. It does not announce itself. Nothing about that place changes that anyone could point to. The light under it is amber, as it was. The drip under it falls, as it did.

Only: the drip does not fail. In dry seasons when the other basins go low, that one runs. Not more — steady. And the light under that stretch is a degree cooler than the amber around it, not enough to thin the scum, enough that a child sitting beneath it can lower her hand from her brows for a while. Mothers begin, without knowing why, to bring the small ones there at midday. A crew claims the drip and no other crew contests it, and no one remarks on that either.
