**What the Attention Does**

It does not arrive. That is the first thing to be clear about. Something benevolent and wise does not come up the shelf the way you did, feeling the breath before seeing the seam. It was simply, at some point, looking — the way a long-open eye is looking — and the oven was inside the look.

So begin with what it sees, because a power of that kind sees differently, and what it sees decides what it does.

---

It sees the room whole, and it sees it in time.

Not the hour you spent. The whole of it. The mountain when it was fire and there was no room, only a bubble in the melt. The bubble cooling, cracking open at its top, the crack finding the plateau's edge. The first breath going out into a sky that was not yet the color it is now. And then — this is the part that takes even a patient watcher a while — the film arriving from somewhere, from the pores of the stone, from the breath itself, and beginning. The grazers, later, much later, small as the dust and then not small. The lamp-bearers. The slow one, which may be the last of a kind of grazer from before the grazers were many, and which the power knows the age of, and does not say.

It sees the cycle turning. From that height the hot pulse and the cool ebb are not a rhythm but a texture, like the grain in wood, and the stack turning over is a single slow motion, and the lights converging and going dark are a pattern on the dome that repeats and varies and repeats, and it sees that the grooves cut in the rock above are a good copy of it. Not perfect. Made by someone who could only watch a little at a time.

It sees the film. This it looks at for a long while, because the film is the one thing in the oven that a wise power is not sure it understands. Whether it is one thing or many. Whether it is alive in the way the grazers are alive or in some other way that the word does not reach. Whether the mountain has it, or it has the mountain. The power does not resolve this. It notices that it does not, and notices that this is itself a kind of information: there are not many things left that it cannot see the edges of.

It sees the person.

---

Here the look narrows.

Two people. The power sees two, where you saw the traces of one, because the traces are not the same age. The stones at the entrance were stacked by one pair of hands and the grooves in the dome were cut by another, and the difference is not skill but height — the one who cut the grooves was taller, and the one who stacked the stones sat low, on the cool slab, for a long time.

The power sees them come up the shelf. Where from and why it does not need to say; it is enough that they came, that they found the breath, that they went in. It sees them stand in the room the first time, and it sees the taller one lift a hand up through the grazers to the dome and find the rock, and it sees the shorter one go still at the sound of the whisper and stay still, and it knows already, from the way they each meet the room, which one will go down the far passage and which one will sit by the door.

It sees the going. It does not dwell on this. The far passage takes the taller one in the cool phase, when the lights are out, and there is a reason for that which the power knows and you do not. The shorter one does not follow. The shorter one sits on the slab, and a while later makes the first scratch.

It sees the bowl. It sees what was put in it. It sees the waiting go on past the point where waiting is a thing a body can do without cost, in a room where there is no water and the only water is inside the living, and it sees the count go on, one scratch, then a long time, then one, and it sees — because it is looking at time whole — where the count stops.

The shorter one did not leave. You knew this already, on the shelf, when you thought about the bowl. There is nothing on the floor but the living, and the dust is patient, and the room takes everything back. The film in the doorway, where it is thinnest, has in it now some part of a person who sat there long enough that the scratches on the slab are worn nearly through, and the power can see which part, and does not say that either.

So this is what has drawn its attention. Not the oven. The oven is well. The oven has been dying and being born in one continuous motion since before there was a word for either, and it needs nothing, and a wise power knows that *help* is a word for things that are not finished and that the oven is finished the way a circle is finished. What has drawn it is the doorway. The one who sat there and counted. The one who went down and has not come back to say.

---

Now what it does not do, because most of wisdom is that.

It does not bring water. It considers this for exactly as long as it takes to see the film drown, the grazers' sealed shells split, the whole warm dry lattice of a world that made itself out of almost nothing turned to a wet grave in an hour. The oven is a shape water cannot enter. Benevolence that does not know this is not benevolence.

It does not bring light. Nothing here has eyes. Light would be a gift to the giver.

It does not stop the cooling. Because the mountain is cooling — it can see that too, from the height it looks from — and in a span of time that is not short but is not forever the breath will fail, and the film will thin, and the stack will go still in a cool phase that does not end. The power could put its hand under the mountain and hold the fire. It does not. Not because it lacks the strength, and not out of some principle about interference; it has no such principle. It does not because it looks at the oven and sees that the oven has already accepted this, in the only way a place can accept anything, which is by having become what it is. The grazers cannot molt. The film gives nothing back. Every body in the room is built around the fact that there will not be more — more water, more heat, more time — and to give more would be to tell them they were wrong about the shape of the world, and they were not wrong.

It does not open the far passage to the sky, or send its look down it and back up in an instant with the answer. It could. That is not the same as coming back to say.

It does not lift the shorter one out of the film and remake them. It thinks about this longest. It is the thing a lesser kindness would do. But the shorter one went into the film by choice — the bowl is the evidence; a large grazer opened has enough water in it to stay a little longer than you should, and they chose to stay — and what they were waiting for was not to be saved. It was for someone to come up the far passage. The power cannot give them that by undoing the waiting. It can only give it by finishing it.

And it lightens its own attention, a little, when it notices that even looking is a kind of pressure, and that the room has not been looked at like this before, and that the grazers under the doorway have shifted very slightly toward a warmth that is not the rock's.

---

Here is what it does.

It goes in. Not as you did, but it goes in, and it does not stoop at the entrance, and at the second bend the sky goes and the walls come close and the heat climbs, and it lets the heat climb on it because there is no reason to be in a place and not be in it.

It sits on the cool slab.

The grazers come away from the slab as they came away for you, easy, unbothered, and it does not move them but they move for it, and it sits with its back to the passage wall and the whisper of the room in front of it and the redness far off through the far passage, and it waits for the hot phase to ebb. This takes some hours. A power of this kind has hours.

The lights go out. The walls go still. The click comes.

It listens to the click through the whole of a cool phase, counting, and finds that the count matches the scratches on the slab beside it — not the number, which is only dozens, but the spacing, which is the spacing of a person breathing slowly in the dark. This it already knew. It listens anyway, because the shorter one listened, and there are some things that should be done in the way they were first done.

Then it goes down the far passage.

---

What it finds there you are not told.

This is not withholding. It is that the account you were given broke off in the middle of a sentence — *the room has been described very car* — and a benevolent power, looking at that, sees a thing that stopped where it stopped and does not finish it, because finishing it would be a different thing. The far passage is the room's one unanswered question and the account's one unfinished line, and a wise power does not correct the shape of what it loves.

But it comes back.

That is the whole of the act. It goes down, and it is gone for a while — an hour, a day, the same span the slow one is gone — and then the broad displacement of its return comes up the narrow way into the room, and the grazers on the floor lift and settle, and it crosses to the doorway, to the slab and the small glazed bowl and the film that is thinnest there and has in it what it has in it.

It does not speak. Nothing here can hear. It kneels — if it has knees; call it kneeling — and puts a hand flat on the film in the doorway, as you put your hand on the wall, and lets the warmth pass either way.

*I went down. I came back. Here is what is there.*

Not in words. In the only sense the room has, which is warmth and pressure and the taste of the air. The film takes it as the film takes everything: down into the rock, round the room, home. Whether any part of the shorter one is still gathered enough to receive it, the power does not know. It does not need to. Some things are done because they were owed, and the owing does not lapse when the one owed can no longer collect.

Then it takes the sharp stone that is still there — it is still there, under the film, under the dust, a flake of rhyolite with one edge worn — and it makes one more scratch on the slab. Not to add to the count. To end it. The last mark on a tally is not a number; it is a line drawn under.

---

It leaves as you left, up the seam, the heat falling off it like a coat, the second bend giving back the wedge of hard bright sky. On the shelf the wind is cold and clean and has nothing in it, and the plateau runs away dead in every direction, and the crack behind breathes out warm and dry into the dark.

It does one more thing, on the shelf, and it is very small.

You carried something out on your palm. A velvet that would not wash off because there was nothing to wash it with, and that would wear away in a day, and that you brushed off into the dead wind with its pale grit when you noticed it. Some of that grit was alive. It had legs. It had, according to the thinnest evidence, somewhere to go, if there had been anywhere.

The power looks at the shelf — at the ash and the glass and the little craters where gas escaped a long time ago, with dust in them and nothing in the dust — and it looks at the sky, and it looks at the mountain cooling, and it does not decide anything. It only notices that the specks you brushed away are still on the rock a little way from the entrance, in the lee of the low stacked wall, where the breath from the crack reaches faintly on a still night, and that they are not yet dead. And it does not warm the shelf. It does not carry them home. It does something a great deal smaller than either, which is to make the night still, this one night, so the breath from the crack reaches a little farther, and lets that be what it is.

Then it is not looking anymore, or is looking elsewhere, or is looking at everything at once again in the way that means nothing in particular is inside the look. The oven does not notice the difference. The stack turns over. The heat pulses. In the cool phase, from the far passage, a single dry click — and then, after a long while, nothing at all.
