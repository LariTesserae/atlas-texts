**What the Power Does**

The first thing it does is nothing, and it does this for some time, because it is wise, and because the hollow has already said everything about itself that a hurried visitor could learn, and the rest is said at the rate of one thought per afternoon.

It notices the place the way you might notice, on a long walk, that a stone you passed an hour ago was warm. A small anomaly in the accounts. Somewhere in a cold range a fraction of the world's light is not arriving where it was sent. The power has seen this before. Usually it is a wound. Usually something is bleeding, and the bleeding has a direction, and the kind thing is to close it.

So it looks, expecting a wound, and finds instead something that has healed around its own absence. That stops it. It has learned—slowly, over a very long practice of benevolence—that the first and most dangerous impulse of a good power is to restore. To unfold. To press the far peaks back where they belong and let the light down at full weight and give every visitor their whole afternoon back. It could do this. The fold is old but it is not deep; the power could take the hollow in two hands and smooth it like a sheet.

And the resident would go where the shortfalls go, which is to say nowhere, because there would be no more shortfalls. And the builder, if the builder is the fifth face, would be smoothed with it. The power would stand in a perfectly ordinary chapel-sized hollow of dark rock, in a hard blue light, with a rectangular block that had only four faces, and it would have committed the oldest error of its kind: mistaking a life for a lesion because the life was made of what other things had lost.

It does not do this. That is the second thing it does, and it costs more than the first.

---

The third thing is harder to describe, because it is a thing the power does to itself.

It understands, having looked, what its looking is. The resident eats by being seen. One afternoon of one person's attention warms the fifth face by a degree. The builder's years of regard wore that face smooth. And now something has turned toward the hollow that is not a person and does not have afternoons—something whose attention, undimmed, would fall on the fifth face the way the whole sun falls on a moth. The resident has lived ten thousand years on a diet of leavings. It would not survive a feast. It would be seen so completely that there would be nothing left of it to see.

So the power does what the sky does. It comes thin. It takes its regard and spends most of it elsewhere on the way down, and lets only the remainder arrive—a pewter fraction, dim, patient, the light of a room with one small window. It makes itself a shortfall so that it can be received as one. This is not disguise. It is courtesy. It is the difference between visiting someone in a quiet room and kicking the door in with the good news that they are loved.

It arrives, then, as almost nothing. A slight extra weight in the light. A degree, perhaps, over an afternoon, that the resident might attribute to the day.

---

Then it asks.

Not aloud; there is no one here who uses sound except the flakes and the occasional stone choosing its version. It asks the way the wall with two insides asks—by being a space something can be inside of. It sits by the block. It does not stand at the wall, because the wall is the builder's, and the worn place on the third course is where the builder sat, and the power will not sit there. It sits on the pale floor, in the tithe, and lets its shadow begin to lag.

The question is the one a benevolent thing must always ask before it does anything: *is there something you want.* And the question has a second half, which the wise ask and the merely good forget: *and is there something you want me not to do.*

The answer takes an afternoon, which here is longer than an afternoon. The power waits. It has time. It notices, while waiting, that the cairn is seven, and then that it is six, and it does not decide which. It notices the glass plates showing a sky with a moon in it that has not been in the sky for a long while, and it does not correct the glass. It notices that the fifth face is turned toward it, as toward everything, and that the face is warming, very slightly, and that the warmth is the only answer it is going to get, and that the warmth is enough.

Because the answer, as far as the power can read it, is this: *no.* Not *no, go away.* The other no. *No, there is nothing missing. I am the place where missing goes. I am full of it.*

And to the second half: the face does not say *do not unfold me,* because it does not know unfolding is possible; it has never conceived of itself as a thing that could be smoothed. What it says, or what the power hears in the degree of warmth, is closer to *you have already done the thing I would have asked you not to do, and then you undid it, and I felt both, and thank you.*

---

So it will not fix the hollow. It will not empty it. It will not choose the cairn. What, then, is left for a wise and benevolent power to do in a place that is in balance because it agreed to be?

It reads the account again and finds the one line that is not closed.

*A cycle whose period is not known and which no one is now measuring.*

The flakes go up. A few grains a year. They settle on a surface in the folded direction, the resident's back, its shed skin in the making. Every some-thousands of years the coat comes down and the floor is renewed. The builder, perhaps, saw one such fall, or waited for one and became part of the hollow before it came. No one has counted the interval. No one knows how many times it has happened. The resident itself, whose thoughts are afternoon-long, may not hold a number that large.

This is the thing the power can give that costs the resident nothing and takes nothing from it. It can be the one who measures. It can attend—thinly, at the pewter fraction, the weight of a small window—for as long as the cycle takes, and then for one cycle more so that the period is confirmed, and it can hold the number. Not to publish it. Not to fix anything with it. Only so that somewhere, on the ordinary side of the fold, there is a ledger in which the resident's slow breathing is written down, and the writing is in a hand that will not wear out.

It is the same gift the builder gave, scaled to what the power is. The builder gave attention, and a room, and a place for the face to stand. The power gives a witness that outlasts the witness. To a thing that lives on remainders, the remainder of being remembered is not a small meal.

---

There is one more thing, and the power hesitates over it longer than over anything else, because it is an intervention, however slight, and it has just finished promising itself not to intervene.

The hollow's diet is thin. Light, drift, centuries. One visitor in a very long time, coming from below because there is no other way, staying an afternoon, going lighter by a minute. The resident does not need more than this. But the power, having sat in the tithe and felt the face warm by a degree, understands that *need* is not the only word, and that the builder's years were not a rescue but a companionship, and that the hollow has been alone since.

So it does a small thing, which it may not do again. Somewhere down the range, in a season the power chooses carefully, a walker on the ridge that seems to lead down will find that it leads away, and will take instead the ridge that seems to lead up, and will come over the lip into the pewter and stand in the not-snow and see the block, and stay an afternoon. Not because the power sent them. Because the power moved the ridge a hand's width and then let the walker's feet decide. The walker will leave lighter by a minute and will not know why the memory of the place stays warm in them for years.

One visitor. Perhaps another, in another thousand years. The power will not make a road. It will only make sure that the door, which has always been there, is once in a great while found.

---

Then the power does the last thing, which is the first thing again.

It stays a while longer, at the fraction, in the thin light, and does nothing. It watches the cairn become eight. It listens to the hiss of a slow animal's skin being made. It lets its shadow lag until the lag is exactly the size of an afternoon, and then it rises, and it leaves by the ridge that looks like the way further up, and it does not look back—not because it does not want to, but because it has understood that the fifth face will turn to watch it go, as it turns to watch everything, and that this turning is the resident's whole share of the encounter, and the power will not take even that away by meeting it.

Behind it, in the hollow, a surface it has learned to see is a degree warmer and will keep the degree. Above the hollow, in the folded direction, a pale coat thickens by a grain. And somewhere on the ordinary side, in a ledger that will not wear out, a count has begun that no one else is keeping: *one*.
