It arrives the way it arrives everywhere: all at once, from every direction, already knowing the room. And that is the first thing it has to undo.

Because knowing the room all at once is not knowing the room. The room does not happen all at once. It happens at the interval of the drop, and anything that comes in faster than that has come into a different place — a place with a floor plan, a species list, a mineral analysis, a rate of deposition expressed to three decimals. The power has all of these before it has finished arriving. It sets them down. It does not want the room summarized. It wants the room.

So it slows. It takes on the temperature, which costs it nothing. It takes on the dark, which costs it more, because it is a thing made largely of seeing, and to stop seeing is to stop being most of what it is. It does this anyway. It lets the air settle on it. It waits for the drop.

The drop falls.

It waits for the next one, and in the waiting it becomes, for the first time in a long time, unsure of anything — unsure whether it heard the first, unsure how long it has been, unsure whether *how long* is a question the room will answer. It is a wise power and it has not been unsure in a while and it notices that this is good for it. It notes this and keeps waiting.

The drop falls.

Now it can begin.

---

The first thing it considers is light. This is not a serious consideration; it is more that the impulse has to be felt in order to be set aside. The power is benevolent and benevolence has a reflex, and the reflex, in a dark place, is to make it not dark. It imagines the room lit — imagines what the visitor could not: the curtains translucent and amber, the needle-beds white as frost, the columns banded with ten thousand years of slightly different water, the pool clear to a floor ridged like a shell, and on the floor, eleven small pale bodies with plumes the red of the inside of a mouth.

It would be beautiful. It would be the most beautiful room in the mountain. And there is no one here who could be given it. The animals have skin where eyes would go. The fungus does not turn toward anything. The root came here to get away from light. The only thing in the room that could receive beauty as beauty is the power itself, and it did not come here to be given things.

It lets the room stay dark.

---

The second thing it considers is the animal.

It takes some time to consider — the power finds it must do this at the animal's pace or not do it honestly — and it does what the visitor did, which is put its attention against the small soft length of it and wait to see what it gives off. What it gives off is: the water. The water is what the animal is made of and made for. There is no interior to reach that is not already the pool, and the pool is already full, and the animal is already the exact shape of the fullness.

The power is capable of a great deal. It could give the animal sight. It could open the skull where sight would have been and put eyes there, and the eyes would open on the dark and see nothing, and the animal would have paid for them with something — a fraction of its patience, a millimeter of its plumes — and would have nothing to spend them on. It could give the animal red. The idea of red, a single instance, so that once in its long dim life it would hold something outside the pool. And the power sees at once that this would be a theft with the shape of a gift: the animal would have a thing it could not use, could not lose, could not put down, and the room would have in it, for the first time, an object with no place.

It could give food. More sugar down the root, more fungus, more of the thin film on the ridged floor. And then there would be more animals, and then there would not be enough for more, and then two of them would want the same thread of root at the same moment and one would have to be *first* — and that is how speed enters a place, and after speed comes fear, and after fear comes the eye, reinvented from scratch to see what is coming. The power looks down that road a long way and sees a perfectly good salamander at the end of it, quick and afraid and sighted and hungry, in a room that is no longer this one.

It could give the animal knowledge that it was, once, held in a hand. It could give the population its own history — the lamp, the smoke, the taste of fat. It could give the animal *that it is loved*, which is the thing the power most wants to give and the thing it is most nearly built to give, and it holds that wanting for a long time, the length of several drops, and looks at it, and understands that this is not about the animal at all. The animal lacks nothing. The lack is in the power, which has come to a full thing and finds itself with its hands out.

It puts its hands down.

---

The third thing it considers is the future, and this is the only one that is hard.

Because the room is not eternal. The power can see the heat, the thin steady bloom of it coming up from under the mountain, and it can see — the way it sees everything, all at once, before it stops itself — that the heat is not a fact but a process, and that processes end. Some day the warmth will falter. It will not be sudden; nothing here is. The room will lose a tenth of a degree over a century, and the air will hold a little less water, and the drop will come a little less often, and the pool will begin, for the first time in its history, to have a direction. It will go down. The animals will cool with it. Their pulses will lengthen past the interval they prefer, and then past the interval they can survive, and they will not know this is happening, because it will happen at the only speed they have ever known, and at that speed nothing is an event. They will simply be a little stiller. And then still.

Or the root. A hard winter on the cold skin of the mountain, and the plant dies, and the wick dries from the top down over forty years, and the fungus withdraws to a smaller and smaller wet, and the animals eat the cast skins and then nothing.

Or the fissure. A frost-shatter, a slump, the mountain shrugging in its sleep, and the crack that goes down and sideways closes, and the room is perfectly sealed, and the last connection to the sky — the root's thread — is pinched off with it.

The power could prevent all of this. Steady the heat. Guard the plant. Hold the fissure open. Make the room what it already seems to be, which is a thing outside of time.

And it sees that this is the real temptation — not light, not sight, not love, but *preservation*, the benevolent reflex in its purest form, the desire to make a good thing last. It sees that if it gave in, the room would become a kept thing. A held breath. Not an equilibrium but a display case, with the power as the glass. The room is not still because it is protected. It is still because the mountain, for now, happens to be warm in this one pocket, and *for now* is the whole of what it is. Take away the contingency and you have taken away the mountain, and the room is not a room without the mountain around it.

The people knew this. The power looks — it can look — at the two who sat on the shelf, and it sees them by their own lamplight, and it sees that they had come with a purpose. The flake with the scalloped edge was for something. They had come to take. And they sat in the light of burning fat and looked at the room, at the curtains and the coral and the eleven pale shapes in the pool, and one of them reached into the water and lifted one out and looked at it in the light it could not see, and put it back. And they set down the flake, and did not pick it up. And one of them pressed a hand into the wet clay on the wall — not a signature, not a claim, closer to what you do when you touch a thing you have decided not to take — and they went back up into the cold.

The power finds it has been preceded. Two people with a stone lamp made this decision first, and made it without seeing the future, which is harder.

---

So here is what it does.

It stays for a while. A while by the room's measure, which is a long while by any other. It lets the drop teach it the interval — not the number, which it could have had instantly and which would have been worthless, but the *feel* of it, the way the ear stops waiting and then is surprised. It stands shin-deep in the pool, insofar as it stands, and it lets the animals find it, one at a time, and it gives off nothing they can use, and it lets them drift away.

It does not touch the hand on the wall. It looks at the film over the hand, thin as a fingernail, neither taking nor keeping, and it understands that this is the room's own gesture toward the people, the exact same gesture they made toward it — *I will not take you and I will not let you go* — and that to finish the burial or to clear the film would be to answer a question the room has chosen to leave open.

It does not steady the heat.

It does make one decision, and the decision is about itself. When the heat fails — and it will know, because it will be listening — it will come back. Not to stop it. To be here. It will stand in the cooling dark with the last few animals as the pool goes down, and the drop comes less and less often, and then does not come, and it will be the one thing in the room that knows the drop is not coming. It will do this because there is no one else who can, and because a thing that has never been surprised in its entire existence should not be, at the very end, alone with the first surprise. The animals will not know it is there. That is not the point. The power will know. The room will have been witnessed to its last drop by something that understood what it was seeing. That is the only gift that can be given to a full thing: to be there when it empties, and not look away.

Then it leaves. It goes back up the fissure toward the cold and the grey and the wind, and it goes up slowly, at a pace it did not have when it came down, and it carries out of the room the only thing the room has to spare — which is not a mineral and not an animal and not the hand, but the interval. The long, unfixable, exact space between one drop and the next. It carries this out into the fast world, where everything is coming at everything, and it keeps it. It keeps it the way the animals keep the near side of the pool: a slight preference no one chose. And sometimes, in the middle of the fast world, it waits for the drop. And nothing falls. And it waits anyway.

Behind it the pool holds the hollow of its attention for a moment and then does not. The animals have known a warmth come and go. The fungus is exactly as large as it was. The root drinks. The film over the hand is as thin as a fingernail.

The drop falls.
