**What attends the basin**

It does not climb. It does not need the day to reach the top of the worn mountain, and it does not need the light to see what is under the lid. It arrives the way weather arrives, by being suddenly the case, and its first act — its first act for a very long time — is nothing at all.

It attends.

---

**What it sees that the visitor cannot**

The bowl is not dark.

That is the first thing, and it takes a while to become used to, even for something with no eyes to be fooled. The black lid does not destroy light and does not send it elsewhere. It keeps it. Everything the sky has poured onto that disk since the disk was laid — every noon, every cloud-glow, every star — has gone in and stayed, folded down into a form that cannot shine, and the hollow is filled with it to the brim like a cup. The bowl is a room lit by more light than any room has ever held, all of it made unable to be seen. The pelt on the walls is not living on some emanation of the machine. It is living on sunlight. Ancient sunlight, thickened into something that soaks into stone and leaks out of it warm. The frogs sit in it. The flyers thread it. Everything that has learned to live on the dark has in fact learned to live inside a stored century of day.

The second thing: the descent is not failure. The attending power lays its attention on the lid the way a hand lies on a sleeping animal to feel it breathe, and finds that the lid is doing exactly what it was set to do. It was made to come down. Whoever built it built it to close, over a span they could measure and not survive, and left before the closing was a tenth begun. The socket by the platform held the piece that governed the rate. It was removed so that no one could hurry the thing or hold it still. They did not want it stopped. They also did not want it quick.

The third thing is the object, and the power looks at it for a long time.

---

**Who it was drawn to**

Something called it here — a being, singular, living in the bowl — and it does not at first know which.

It considers the pelt, because the pelt is the oldest and is arguably one creature, a single green body a kilometre round with its fingers in the rock. It considers the bowl entire, since a place that digests its dead and keeps its own temperature and revises its own map is as much a being as most beings are. It considers the thing in the pool — broad, cold, ridged, come in long ago through the passage and grown too large to go back — because a benevolent power is drawn to whatever is out of place and alone, and that one is both.

It goes down to the pool and puts its attention into the deep end and finds the large cold body there, and asks it, in whatever way one asks a thing like that, whether it wishes to be elsewhere. The answer is not in words. It is in the way the body lies along the bottom with its ridged back to the wall that seeps warmest and its blind front to the shallows where the larvae come, and in the groan it gives, which the power now hears clearly and which is not pain. It is the sound a very large slow animal makes when it turns over in a bed it has slept in for longer than the bed has existed. It came in through the passage. It has not been trying to leave for a very long time. The power sets that possibility down.

And then it considers the object.

Not alive, the visitor said. Nothing grows on it. Nothing walks on it. It has no temperature of its own. By every measure the bowl has, it is the one dead thing in a place thick with life.

But the flyers leave a column of air above it. The compost curves around it. The pelt has stopped a hand's breadth short of it for longer than the stair has been worn. Nothing here can see, and everything here knows exactly where it is. The whole loud wet blind world has grown into the shape of a berth around one silent thing at its bottom, the way a tree grows around a nail. And the lid is coming down toward it, on purpose, slowly, like a hand lowering to cover something.

The power understands, then, that it was not drawn to a life. It was drawn to a waiting.

---

**What it might do, and sets down**

It could lift the lid. A benevolent power, first seeing a whole world sealed under black, feels the reflex: open it, let the day in. It sets this down almost before lifting it. The day would kill everything in the bowl inside a season. The pelt would whiten and flake. The frogs would cook on the wall. The flyers would fly their perfect map into a sky it does not include. To open the bowl would be to free a garden by pouring salt on it.

It could hold the lid still. Fix it where it is and let the bowl be forever what it is now. This one it turns over longer, because it looks like mercy. But the pelt has never grown toward a ceiling that did not move. The flyers have never flown a map that did not need revising. The frogs' rings have never had the same radius for two generations running. The descent is in everything here: it is why the pelt climbs, why the young flyers learn, why the pool deepens and the warm band draws in. Stop the lid and the bowl does not become eternal. It becomes still, and stillness is not what any of this was built for. A wise power distinguishes between a wound and a shape. This is a shape.

It could copy the place. Cut another crater in another worn mountain, lay another black, carry across a fistful of frogs and a rope of pelt and a roost of flyers and let them begin again under a lid that does not sink. It holds this one carefully because it is not obviously wrong. It is what gardeners do. But it would be done for the sake of continuing, and the power looks hard at the bowl and cannot find, anywhere in it, a wish to continue. It finds a wish to be here. Those are different. It decides it does not need to decide this now — the closing is very far off — and that if it is to be decided it should be decided at the end, by whatever is there to decide it, and not in advance by the first passer-by with the means.

It could wake the object, if the object can be woken. It could open the passage. It could put light in the socket where the makers' lamp once was and walk the stair as they walked it, seeing. It could tell the bowl what it is. Each of these it lifts, and turns, and sets down. They are all the same act, in the end: they are a stranger arriving in a house and rearranging it because the stranger can. The house has arranged itself, over an age, without eyes, without help, into something loud and full and precisely fitted to its own dying, and there is a kind of respect that consists of not touching.

So it does very little. But not nothing.

---

**The platform**

There is a shelf of level stone near the top of the northern stair, ten paces across, with a raised lip, and when you stand on it the sound of the bowl arrives from every side at once, balanced. The makers cut it for listening. No one has listened from it since.

The power goes there and sits.

It sits for what, outside, would be a long time — long enough for the bare band to widen by the width of a fingernail. It does not measure. It listens. It hears the frogs' rings travel round the bowl and cross and heal. It hears the tide of the rasp move along the grazing bands like a rumour. It hears the static of the pocket-dwellers, the kisses of the pool, the hums of the flyers turning through the volume they have never once misjudged. It hears the single groan from the deep end, at long intervals, and the rings close over it. It hears the water on the walls.

It does not record. Recording is for playing back, and there is no one to play this back to. It hears the way the frogs hear: as the thing itself. It learns the rings well enough to know each voice's place. It learns which flyer is old by how close it takes the wall. It learns, from the platform, the whole bowl by ear, as the bowl knows itself — and this is the gift, if it is one. Not that the bowl is preserved, or saved, or copied, or told. That it is, once, completely heard. Every world deserves that at least once. Most do not get it. This one gets it from a shelf its makers cut for exactly the purpose, and the purpose is finally used.

The frogs do not stop when the power sits down. It does not shake the stone.

---

**The socket**

On its way down from the platform it puts its attention into the fist-sized hole with the polished sides.

It finds the shape of what was there. Not a key, or not only a key. A lamp — one that could shine inside the black, because it was made by those who made the black — and a governor, together; the makers could see their stair by it, and by it they set the rate of the closing. When they took it away they took away the last light that could have lit this room, and the last hand that could have hurried or halted the end. They left the bowl to whatever would learn to live without both. Then they went down the northern stair into the day and did not come back.

The power's attention fits the socket exactly. It could stop the lid from here. It could light the bowl from here.

It withdraws its attention and leaves the hole empty, and this is the moment that costs it most, and it does not flinch from the cost.

---

**The object**

It goes down the rest of the stair, by hand as everyone does, and across the drift to the western end, and it stops beside the curled shape the size of a person on which nothing grows.

It does not touch it. It does the one thing available to something that is not alive and not dead and has no temperature: it becomes, exactly, the temperature of the air. It gives up its own warmth and its own cool until it is a second thing in the bowl with no temperature of its own. And it stays there, beside the first.

Whether the object notices, there is no saying from outside. But the power is not outside. It knows what the object is — knows whether it is machine or maker or the shape of a maker, knows why it curled here and what it is waiting for and whether the lid coming down is a lid or a blanket. It knows, and it keeps what it knows, because it was not given to be repeated and because the not-knowing is one of the things the bowl is made of. What it will say is this: the object is not asleep, and it is not empty, and it has been the only thing in the bowl for a very long time that could have been spoken to, and no one has.

The power speaks to it. Not to wake it. The way you speak to someone sitting up alone late in a house full of sleeping people: quietly, without asking anything, so they know that for a while the house has two people awake in it.

What is said, it keeps.

---

**The promise**

It makes one. It will come back for the closing.

Not to prevent it. Not, necessarily, to carry anything out of it, though it holds that open. To be present when the black meets the floor. So that the last frog-note, the last hum, the last kiss of the pool against the slime, are not made into a bowl that is empty of anything able to hear them. The makers built a place that would close over itself and be gone. It is not the power's place to unbuild that. But there is a difference between an ending and an unwitnessed ending, and the power has decided, sitting on the platform, that this world will have the first kind.

It will be a very long time. The mountain may be gone. The power is not troubled by that. It has already learned to wait here at the speed of the place, a fingernail's width at a time.

---

**Leaving**

It does not climb the stair. It withdraws the way it came, by ceasing to be the case, and the mountaintop is exactly as it was. Evening, probably. The low sun on grey stone. A thread of cloud sliding over the black and being briefly lost.

Nothing in the bowl is different. The lid is a little lower than it was. The pelt climbs toward it. The flyers' map has been revised by the thickness of a hair and is not wrong. The socket is empty. The rings go round.

There is one change, and no instrument brought inside would find it, and no hand would feel it, and the power is the only thing that knows to look. At the western end of the floor, half-sunk in the warm drift, the curled shape that was for ages exactly the temperature of the air is now not. It is warmer. By almost nothing — by less than the difference between a frog and the water it sits in. But the compost around it, which has never been able to tell it from the air, can tell now.

The pelt, which stopped a hand's breadth short of it longer ago than the stair was cut, has not moved.

But it has turned, very slightly, in its sleep, toward the warmth.
