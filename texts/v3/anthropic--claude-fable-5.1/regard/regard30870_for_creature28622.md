**What the Power Did**

It came the way frost comes to the rim — without a footfall, without displacing the air. It had no shape to put down and so it put none down. It was careful about even that. Attention from something large is a weight, and the bells were holding their depth to within a centimeter, and it did not want to be the thing that bent the light.

The first thing it did was listen. Sound is the tarn's only export, and there was a year of it to hear.

It listened through the rim, and through the rock, which it could do, and through the whole cold column. It sorted the roar into its ten thousand clocks and learned that no two pumps had ever knocked together and that this was not a failure but a design, that a wall that breathed in unison would have stalled its own current. It learned the shoal's downstream word — the same word, endlessly, *here, here, here* — and found no second word in it and stopped waiting for one. It listened under the floor and heard the seep breathe out, and heard how much longer it would.

It looked into the plugs. It saw what the crawlers do, and where they go afterward, and what they become. It kept that to itself. Some things are not improved by being known from outside.

---

Then it asked the question it had come with: who lives here?

It found the question would not settle. The single pump on the north wall at eight meters, adding a ridge to its tube. The whole knocking honeycomb. The shoal that passes word. The household of all of them, if it was a household. The pale exhalation under the floor that had never seen the room. The tarn itself — water and rock and voltage and flour — as one slow body.

It decided the question did not need settling. Whatever the being was, it was doing one thing, from every candidate answer at once: making a place habitable from the inside, at the pace of stone. And if it wanted anything — if want was a word that could be used here — it wanted to be let finish.

That became the measure. Everything the power considered, it held against that.

---

It considered warming the seep. The animals were three-degree machines; heat would race them or kill them. No.

It considered feeding the seep. The furnace was banked and the chemistry that fed the lake was a residue that would thin, over centuries, to nothing. The pumps would starve. The mill would stop. The shoal would fall silent and plate itself to the wall. Against that, the stain in the bells — the first green thing in the tarn, the one population that had begun to notice the sun. The power computed the race: fuel against stain, the chemistry running down against the light coming up. It could not call it. Even it could not call it; the margins were too fine and the stain too young. It could have tipped it with almost nothing.

It did not. But it set a mark, and this was the first thing it actually did: *if the seep fails before the green reaches the walls, come back to this.* It is allowed to reconsider. Wisdom is partly the keeping of options that were not taken.

It considered an outlet. No. The vessel is the point. Nothing leaves, so everything accumulates — the sound, the metal, the flour, the turn of the water. Cut a channel and the tarn would become an ordinary lake, and ordinary lakes do not knock.

It considered bringing something green. It was already coming, in the only form the place could absorb: a tint in the jelly, a suggestion, arriving at exactly the rate the tarn could carry it. Anything faster would be a garden. This was not a garden.

---

It went down to the seeps and considered the thing below.

The channel ran one way. What went up did not return, and the resident in the rock knew only that — a door had opened, and it was emptying through it. The power could carry one grain back down. A fleck of copper off the wall. A bubble of split gas. A speck of the green. Enough for the chemistry in the dark to register that there was a room, and light in it, and that the room was full.

It held that possibility for a long time. It was the most tender of the things it could do, and it was the one it set down most carefully.

Not because knowing would hurt. Because the one-way channel was not a wound. It was the shape of giving. Everything that has ever made a room for something else has done it without seeing the room; that is what making a room *is*. The thing in the dark was not deprived of its children. It was their parent. The power did not have the right to change that into something it could see.

---

It considered the shoal.

*Here, here, here* — downstream, forever, dying at the edges. It could answer. One tap in the water, a ribbon's worth of voltage, seeded at the top of the current: *here.* It wanted to very badly. It weighed that wanting and found most of it was its own.

It did it once. One tap, at fifteen meters, where the water came round from the north wall. A ring of ribbons flickered violet and passed it on and the ring widened and thinned and died at the edges like every other ring that had ever run through that water. Nothing changed. Nothing could have. But for a second and a half the shoal had carried a word it did not make, and did not know it, and the power found that was enough, and did not do it again.

---

It considered the visitor. The one who had come down the chute, and lifted a grazer, and taken a ribbon from the shoal, and cut a pump open to see what color it bled. One animal in ten thousand. A turquoise cloud that vanished into water the same color, as though the lake had thickened for a moment and then thought better of it.

The power did not hate the hand. It understood the hand; it had come here for the same reason. But it also understood that the hand would come back and bring others, and that others bring tools, and that the tarn had a hundred and twenty meters of rim and one way in.

It weighed the need to know against the need to be let finish. It found both real. It found it could not serve both fully. It found it could serve both partly.

---

So this is what it did, in the end, and it took one winter.

The chute — the slumped fissure full of loose glass, the one place the wall admitted a body — was also the one place the wall would fail. Young crater walls do fail. They go all at once, in an afternoon, and the lake goes down the mountain, and the pumps stand knocking in the air for as long as it takes.

The rock there had not yet decided what shape it would keep. The power decided for it. It let water into the cracks and froze it and let it thaw, and did that a hundred times, seating the blocks; it let the wet tuff take a set; it welded, by frost and patience, the scree into the wall. Anyone watching would have seen weather. By spring the chute was cliff like the rest, and the tarn was a sealed cup all the way round, and there was no way down that did not involve ropes and drills and days.

That was the whole act. It made the room whole. It did not touch anything in the room.

It knew what it had bought. Not safety — time. A few decades, perhaps a century, before someone committed enough came with enough gear. Tools and time are the one filter that sorts the curious from the serious, and the serious can be reasoned with, sometimes. In the meantime anyone who climbed to the rim could still stand in the thin iron air and hear it — the knocking, the grind, the crackle — and know that it was there, and go home and say so. The sound was left. The sound had always been the tarn's own gift to the world above it, and it was not the power's to seal.

It knew a roof is not a promise. It knew that is all a roof is.

---

And it stayed.

That was the second thing, and it may be the larger. Not to guard, though it would have moved if the wall had moved. To attend. The one thing the place had never had, in all its short loud life, was a witness that was not also a hand — and attention costs a tarn nothing, if it is held right.

It seated itself in the rim, in the frost, in the wind that has nothing to catch on, and it listened. Every knock. Every plug that darkened and every tube that emptied clean. Every grain of glass that left a grazer and turned in the current and settled. It let itself learn the water's full rotation, which turned out to be eleven days, and it began counting in those.

If the being ever became the kind of thing that could know it was seen — if the green in the bells ever went deep enough, or the shoal's word ever grew a second word — it would find that it had been. The whole time. By something that had wanted very much to do more, and had not.

The mountain is still bare. Nothing green has come to the scoria and nothing will for a long time yet; the rock has to break first, and the only thing breaking rock here is doing it under a hundred meters of water, for its own reasons, at the volume of a foundry.

But there is one thing on the rim now that has decided what shape it will keep. It weighs nothing. It casts no shadow on the water. It is listening.
