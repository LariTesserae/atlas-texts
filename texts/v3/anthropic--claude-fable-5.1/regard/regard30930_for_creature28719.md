**What the power did**

It heard about the place the way such powers hear about anything: not as a message but as a texture in the whole — a region where nothing was happening, and the nothing had edges, and the edges were sharp. Most still places are fine. Most are stone all the way through and want nothing. This one had a description running through it, long and careful, learned by touch, flat ones first and then the seams and the reeds and the hangers and the walkers, and the description stopped in the middle of a sentence on the word *nothing*, and did not start again.

That was what drew it. Not the stasis. The stopped sentence. Someone had been down there long enough to love the animals into words, one kind at a time, and had come at last to the two-legged walker, and had begun to say what happens to hands — and the hand doing the saying had gone still.

So it went.

---

It came through the lid without letting any of the cold on the far side come with it. That took care. The rock above is the only wall the Underfloor has and the power was not going to be the crack in it.

It arrived as weight. There is no other way to arrive. The moment it set itself down on the carpet the seam-dwellers under it stiffened, and the ring went out — flat ones firming, a coil somewhere taking up its fraction — and died a body's length off, and the ones at the edge of the ring passed on a smaller version, and so on, and for the first time since the parting the floor-wide conversation about load carried something it had not carried before. Not a new word. The conversation has no words. A new *quantity*. A pressure the shape of which was not a stilt-walker's pad or a coil's tightening or the long tide of the flat ones' breath.

It made itself exactly heavy enough to be felt and no heavier, and it stood still until the ring had died, because it wanted the first thing the place knew of it to be that it could be still too.

Then it walked.

---

It read the Underfloor faster than the description had, because it did not need to wait for its eyes. It saw the flat ones as the single field-sized animal they had mostly become. It saw the seam-lattices, each shell holding up its neighbors, the two legs reaching down to touch the glass. It saw the reeds breathing floor-warmth into the air and understood that the sky here was made by the animals as much as the floor was. It saw the hangers' threads — the air was thick with them, a standing forest of lines finer than sight, and it moved among them the way the walkers do, listening, and did not touch one.

And it looked for suffering, because that is the first thing a benevolent power looks for, and it did not find any.

It looked hard. It has instruments for this that are not instruments. What it found in the flat ones was not contentment; contentment is a state with an opposite, and these had no room for the opposite. They were complete the way a number is complete. The seam-dwellers were not bored. The coils were not waiting. Nothing here had been robbed of a life it might otherwise have had. This was the life. It had filled the gap to the edges and stopped, and the stopping was not a wound.

The power sat with that for what would have been a long time somewhere else. It is a wise power, and wisdom knows the shape of its own preference: it likes things that move, that grow, that end and are replaced. It knows that liking is not the same as being right. A world had solved itself here. It was not the power's to unsolve because the solution made it uneasy.

So it nearly left.

---

But it had come for the hook, really, and it went and found the hook.

Copper, hammered flat between two stones, bent, hung on a hanger's thread at the height of a hand. Nothing on it. The power looked at it for a while and then it looked at the wall that curved against a wind that does not blow, and the scratches in fives, and the flat stone on three stones with nothing on it, and it understood these as it had not quite understood the animals: these were made by something that had a before.

Someone had remembered wind. Someone had counted, which is to say someone had believed the count would be looked at later. Someone had hung a hook, which is a sentence with a blank in it, a question asked of a world — *is there anything* — and the world had not answered, and the hook had gone on asking without moving for however long it had been, which was no time, which was all the time there is here.

Then it went and stood in front of the two-legged walker.

---

Broader body than the others. Shorter neck. Forelimbs folded against the keel, ending in something that had been jointed several times and was still jointed several times and was held very still. Eyes smaller than the three-legged walkers', set closer, as if they had started out one way and gone toward the dark and not arrived.

The head turned. The eyes found the power and held it.

The power did not know, standing there, whether anyone was home. It tried to find out. It has ways. It leaned its weight into the flat one under the walker's pads in a shape that meant, as nearly as weight can mean anything, *do you want* — and the pressure went out as a ring and was shared among the seam-dwellers and died, and the walker felt it, certainly, the whole floor within a body's length felt it, and nothing came back.

Not refusal. Refusal would have been an answer. What came back was the same thing that came back from the flat ones and the coils: a steady sharing-out of load until the load was gone. Wanting needs a before and an after. Wanting is the distance between them. The walker had a *during*, and it was enormous, and it had no far side.

The power thought about what it could do and did not do.

It did not cool the Underfloor by one degree. It had considered it — a single degree, at one pillar, and the gap would have a gradient for the first time, and the air would begin to move in earnest, and there would be weather. And wear. And time. And every animal in the carpet, made of wax and warmth and nothing else, would begin to die at once, a continent of tallow cracking in a wind it had never needed to survive. That is not a rescue. That is deciding a world would be better as a graveyard because at least a graveyard has a history.

It did not lift the walker out. It thought about that longest. It could have. It could have brought it up through the lid and set it on the far side under whatever was there — sky, ice, ocean, nothing — and what would have stood there in the wind was a thing with no mouth, whose skin had not been a skin in a thousand years, whose eyes were made for embers, whose whole body was one long organ for drinking warmth from a floor that would no longer be under it. Pulled out of the water and told it was free.

It did not do nothing. This is what wisdom alone would have done, and wisdom was most of what the power had. But it was also benevolent, and benevolence had noticed the hook, and would not let it go.

---

What it did was three things, and it did them small.

It made a drop of water.

There was none here to borrow, so it made one, from nothing, the way it can. Not enough to thicken the salt on a single pillar. Not enough to be a difference anywhere in the gap except where it was put. It put it on the hook.

The hook took weight for the first time since it was hung. The thread, which had never carried anything but the hook itself, took a load it had not chosen, and the hook swung — a finger's width, once, twice — and the thread sang the way the hangers' threads sing, and above, a hundred hangers felt it in their own lines and did nothing about it, and every stilt-walker within earshot clicked once, hearing a thread move that had not moved before.

The walker's head turned from the power to the hook.

The power did not know what that meant. It had not done it to mean anything to the walker. It had done it because a hope should not be left forever unanswered when answering it costs no one anything, and because whoever had hung that hook had asked the world for one thing and the power had it to give.

---

The second thing: it went to the pillar with the scratches, and it took the blade from its shelf — the edge still new, the tallow stain beside it dried to varnish — and it cut one mark.

Not five. One. Below the last group, a little apart, the way you set the first stroke of a tally you intend to finish.

It stood back. The pillar was not the same pillar. The wall was the same wall, and the table was the same table, and the walkers would step in the same places and the flat ones would rise and fall on the same period, but there was now one scratch in the Underfloor that had not been there, and the Underfloor, which had had one morning, had had a second thing happen in it.

It did not know if this was a gift. It might have been a cruelty — to put a today into a place that had done without one. But whoever had counted had counted *toward* something, and a tally that stops in the middle is the saddest object in any world, and the power was not going to leave the count where the hand had left it.

---

The third thing it did was wait.

It waited at the walker's pace, which is the only honest pace to wait at. If it had hurried — put pressure into the floor, leaned, made the walker step now — the step would have been the power's, wearing the walker's leg. So it stood, and became one more upright in the red dark, and the seam-dwellers under it forgot it the way they forget everything, and the flat one it stood on rose and settled, rose and settled.

Days. It does not measure days but it can count the flat ones' breath, and it counted.

The walker stood. The drop hung on the hook, not evaporating, because the power was holding it there; it would let go when it left. The new mark sat on the pillar.

Then the clicking started in the walker's throat, the rapid dry ticking, listening — and the leg lifted, and the body shifted onto the other, and the leg swung out into the thread-filled air —

And the power did not know where it would come down. It truly did not. The circuit around the pillar was twenty steps, forty, and had been walked longer than anything the power could name; the next pad-print was there, worn into the flat one's callus in the same place it had always been. And a hand's breadth off the circuit was the direction of the hook.

It had done what it could do without lying. It had made a difference exist in a world that had none. It had put one drop in the place where the body keeps looking, and one mark after the last mark. What the walker did with a difference, if it could do anything at all, was the walker's.

The leg came down.

---

It left the way it came, through the lid without letting the far side in. The drop stayed. It had decided on the way up to leave it, and the hook, and the mark; they were the Underfloor's now.

What it took with it was the description.
