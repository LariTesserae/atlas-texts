Suppose something notices. Not the plant, which has never seen the hollow and only feels it as a number that stays inside its range. Not the visitor, not yet. Something with no need to climb the berm, that could stand at the bottom of the bowl without its feet in the crust and hear the hum without a sternum to hear it in, and that is wise enough to know that the first duty of attention is to find out what is actually there before deciding what it deserves.

So it does what you did. It takes the place's answer — nothing lives here — seriously for a while. It counts the reeds. It reads the boot prints by age. It kneels at the lip against the light and sees the bloom, and understands the terraces, and understands who is doing the keeping. It would not have needed to kneel. It kneels anyway, because the knowledge should be arrived at from the right height.

Then it considers. Being benevolent, it wants to do something. Being wise, it lists what it could do, and looks at each, and sets most of them down.

It could make the outfall permanent. It could reach through the pipe into the plant and see to it that the valve is never closed — that the point of the place, whatever it is, goes on being pointed at forever so that the byproduct goes on falling. It sets this down almost at once. Nothing industrial is permanent, and a hollow kept warm by a miracle would no longer be this hollow; it would be a garden, and the film would be a pet. The whole nature of the thing is that it lives on a heat nobody intends for it. Take away the accident and you have taken away the being.

It could widen the band. A few degrees more tolerance, and the ring would be a wide belt instead of a knife-edge; the film would ride out the plant's decisions without dying in a stripe every time a valve turned. This it holds longer. It seems kind. But the sharpness of the lips is the sharpness of the band. Widen the band and the film would grow everywhere the water was tepid, a general slime instead of a line, and the edges would round, and the terraces would stop stepping down and start slumping. The hollow would go soft. What lives here is a thing made entirely of its own precision, and you cannot make a precise thing safer by making it less precise. You can only make it a different thing that is safer, and then the first thing is gone.

It could give the film some awareness. Even the least — a flicker, enough to register the warmth as good and the cooling edge as not. It sets this down harder than the others, and with something like a shudder. A being on a knife-edge that does not know it is on one has the only peace available to it. To wake it would be to give it, for the first time in its existence, a reason to be afraid, and nothing to do about it. The film's ignorance is not a lack. It is the shape of its contentment. Wisdom does not fill that in.

It could tell the film's story to the plant — press upon the people who set the load that there is a ring of life in the sump and their valves are its seasons. This is closer. But it looks at what would follow. Perhaps nothing, which costs nothing. Perhaps a fence, a sign, a schedule that keeps the flow steady for the film's sake — and then the terraces stop stepping, because there are no more adjustments to record, and the stone goes smooth, and the film is preserved in a hollow that has lost the one kind of change it admitted. Or perhaps someone comes with a scraper and a vial, and the film goes into a machine, and something is learned, and the ring is a little thinner for it. The power does not think these outcomes are evil. It thinks they are not its to choose. It notices that every large intervention it can imagine has the same flaw: it treats the film as a patient, and the film is not sick. It is exactly as it should be. It is simply mortal, in a way that can be foreseen.

It could, finally, do nothing. Witness and withdraw. This is the option wisdom usually arrives at when the thing it is looking at is already whole, and the power sits with it a long time, at the bottom of the bowl in the gold haze, with the hum coming up through the stone it does not stand on. Nothing is almost right.

But not quite, because of one asymmetry it cannot leave alone. The film cannot know itself. The stone cannot know what it is a cast of. The plant does not know the hollow exists. The reeds know only the seep. In all this place, built so carefully over so many years, there is exactly one thing that could hold the knowledge of what has been made here, and that thing climbs the berm once a season, looks at the bar of water, listens for the note, and leaves. The hollow is complete except that it is unwitnessed. Its entire history is written in the stepped lips of its terraces, legible to anyone who kneels, and no one has knelt.

That is the thing the power decides it may touch. Not the film, not the water, not the valve. The visitor's knees.

It does this in the only way that belongs to the place. The next time the visitor comes, and stands at the rim, and judges the bar of water the right thickness, something falls from their hand. A pen, a cap, a small tool — it does not matter what, and the power does not much care which; it simply arranges that a thing the visitor will want back goes over the edge and lands, not in the pool, not on the hot upper shelves, but on a lower terrace lip, cool enough to touch, right at the margin where the sheet of water thins to a coin. One more object in a hollow made of dropped objects. The difference is that this one is going to be picked up.

So the visitor descends the glazed ladder, which they have not done in a long while. They cross the crust, and the crust records them. They come to the lip and bend to take the thing, and because it has landed in the wet, they kneel to reach it, and because they kneel, the light comes to them along the edge of the shelf instead of down onto it, and they see — a softness. A bloom with no colour. They put out the hand that is not holding the retrieved thing and touch it, and it is slick in a way wet stone is not, and they wipe it and it comes back.

Nothing is explained to them. The power does not speak, does not put a word in their head, does not tell them the film is the mould and the stone is the cast and the steps in the lip are the plant's seasons. It only makes sure they are at the right height at the right time with a reason to be looking. Whatever they understand from there, they understand themselves. Perhaps very little. Perhaps only that the edges are not what they thought. That would be enough. It is the difference between a place that has never been seen and a place that has, and the power holds that the difference is real even when nothing else changes.

It thinks, too — this is the last of its reasoning, and the part closest to hope — that a person who has knelt here once will kneel again. That when the day comes that the plant is finished, and someone in a warm room decides that the sump outfall can be closed, the person sent to do it, or the person who trained them, will have a memory of a slick cool lip against the evening light and will, without quite knowing why, close the valve over a week instead of an afternoon. Not to save anything. Nothing would be saved. But the ring would walk inward through the terraces the way it has always walked, a few feet at a time, colonising and dying in stripes, and would arrive at the pool's bare floor and stop there in its own manner, having done to the end the one thing it does. The stone would record the closing as it recorded everything, one last band, tapering. And the hollow would have ended as itself instead of being switched off. The power cannot make that happen. It can only have put one witness in the way of it, and trust the witness.

Then it withdraws, having done the smallest thing available to it that was not nothing.

Near evening the visitor climbs the ladder with the retrieved object in one pocket and a fingertip that is still faintly slick. The hum continues. The hiss continues. The water lands and spreads and pours over lip after lip toward the blue pool. At the margin, where they knelt, there are two knee-shaped dimples in the softer crust and a set of prints leading away, and within the day the threads that live at exactly that depth of water will have found them, and settled in, and begun to fill them — keeping, patiently, for the first time in the hollow's history, the mark of someone who knows they are there.
