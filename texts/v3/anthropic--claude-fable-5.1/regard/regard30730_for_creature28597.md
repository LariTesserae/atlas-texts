It comes the way the light comes: down through the throat, losing itself halfway, arriving at the floor as something sourceless and dim. It does not part the mist. It has learned, in places like this, that the first courtesy is to be indistinguishable from weather.

And it does what the hollow does, which is wait. A power of this kind can hold a moment for a century without the moment noticing, and it holds this one long enough to see the pulse — the drip from the left, the longer interval, the drip from behind, the low note under both — repeat until the repetition stops being a pattern and becomes a fact, like the temperature. It sees the skin shrink from the fly's foot and return. It sees a leaf come down through the grey coin, turning, and settle in a middle basin, and it stays long enough to watch the first pale grain of stone find the leaf's edge. Then it understands the census the way you understood it, and it understands one more thing, which is that it has been felt. Not seen. Felt. The brass film in the upper pools drew back a hair's breadth when it entered, and waited, and is now creeping forward again to see what it is.

So it is not a stone yet. It is still something arriving. It considers what it has brought.

It could give the being sight. That is the first thing a power thinks of when it meets something with no eyes and the smooth places where eyes used to be: what a gift, to show it the grey coin, the sky beyond, the sun doing its enormous work outside the mist. And it holds the thought a moment and sets it down on the dry shelf, because the being has spent longer than counting arranging itself so that the sun's work does not reach it, and to give it eyes would be to give it the one thing it has built a house against. There is a kind of gift that is a demolition. The wise ones know the shape of it.

It could give the being motion. A seam somewhere else, a second hollow, a way for the skin to send some of itself down the crack and up another throat and begin again. Room. But the being has no room in it and has never wanted any; the fullness is not a crowding but a fit. It is pressed against its own walls the way a hand is pressed against the inside of a glove. Give it another glove and you have two half-empty things where there was one whole one.

It could give the being memory — a way to hold what has happened other than by being shaped like it. And here it pauses longest, because this is closest to a real lack. The visitor came down the notched wall for years, alone, and set a vessel in the bowl and lifted it and set it down, and tied a cord for a reason, and the being has no way to know that this happened except that the bowl is a bowl and the cord is stone. It cannot miss. It cannot wait, in the sense that a dog waits. The shelf is dry and waiting only in the way a shelf waits.

The power sets this down too. Not because the lack is not real but because memory is the beginning of anticipation, and anticipation is the beginning of fear, and there is nothing in the hollow that has ever been afraid. It would not put fear there for the sake of a fuller inner life. Something can be complete without being like us.

It could keep the being safe forever. And now it does the thing the being cannot do, which is look down — through the ochre floor of the deep pool, through the crack that takes the water away, into the network of warm dark where the pale animal's ancestors still live, and further, into the cold rock that has no life in it at all. And it finds what it half expected to find. A fault, hairline, not yet moving. In some number of centuries — the power can count them, and does, and the number is not large by its measure — the mountain will settle by the width of a thumb, and the seam that has given the water at one unchanging rate will pinch. Not close. Pinch. And the crack below will go on taking at the old rate for a while, because cracks are slower to learn than seams, and the pools will drop. Not far. A finger's depth. But the brass skin lives in a finger's depth of water, and in the week after the pools drop it will dry to a dust that the next drip does not wet, and the fly will have nothing to graze in a day, and the pale animal in the deep pool will find the water going still and warm and then still and less warm and will die of the fact of difference. And then the hollow will be a hollow. Dry basins the color of old bone, a shelf that is no longer the only dry thing, a stone cord around a stone knob in air that carries no mineral anymore to keep it stone, so that it will finally, after all that time, crumble.

It would take a week. Everything else here has taken longer than anything has been counting, and the end would take a week.

Now, the power is benevolent, and it feels the pull of the obvious kindness — to reach down and seal the fault, to hold the seam open as long as there is rock to hold. And it is wise, and so it asks what the being would want, and it hears the answer, which is nothing. The being does not fear the week. It cannot. Whatever the power does here, it does for its own reasons and not the being's, and it should be honest about that.

Its reasons are these. The being is good. Not useful, not beautiful in the way that gets a place a name on a map — good, the way a well-fitted thing is good, the way a note held perfectly is good. The universe has a little more of that in it while the hollow lasts, and the power does not think the being's indifference to its own ending is a verdict on whether the ending should come. Things that do not care whether they continue are not thereby asking to stop.

But the power also knows the hollow's own law, which is that stone is laid down and stone dissolves, and it has watched too many things kept past their shape to want to keep this one forever. It would become a thing being kept. The seam would be a seam with a hand on it. The pulse would be a pulse someone was maintaining, and the power would know, and eventually — the power does not lie to itself — the being would know, at the level of chemistry, that the water was coming in at a rate that was not the rock's rate. Something in the fit would loosen.

So it does not seal the fault. It moves it. A small correction, well below the deepest pool, in cold rock that has never felt the warm water — it takes the settling that would have happened in a season and spreads it across a span of time as long as the one in which the basins grew. Now the seam will pinch, but so slowly that the crack below will learn the new rate as the seam sets it. Now the pools will drop a finger's depth over a stretch no fly's lineage could notice, and the skin will retreat down the basin walls a grain at a time, laying stone as it goes, and the ledger of pale bands and dark will go on being written until the last band is a band of pale with nothing dark to follow it, and no one standing in the hollow on any given day will be able to say whether it is ending or only continuing. The being will arrive at not existing the way it arrived at existing: without a direction. It will get the ending it has been practicing for.

That is the one gift the power gives that changes anything, and it is a gift the being will never receive in the sense of feeling it arrive. That seems right. It matches.

There is one more thing, and it is smaller, and the power does it because it wants to.

It knows about the visitor. It knows in the way it knows about the fault, by looking where the being cannot. It knows what the cord held and what the vessel was for and how many years, and it knows the last time, and what the visitor said going up the notched wall for the last time — not to the hollow, which eats speech, but to the grey coin. It does not tell you. There are things a wise power keeps, and this is one, and part of the keeping is that it goes down.

It takes a body for this. Not a large one. It comes in from the ordinary air the way the visitor came, down the notches spaced for a stride, and it sits where the visitor sat, and it rests its right hand on the worn side of the bowl. It stays as long as the visitor stayed. It does not set anything on the shelf; the shelf's purpose is to be dry and empty, and it honors that. It does not untie the cord. It does not drink — it has no need — but it lifts a hand's worth of the warm water and lets it fall back, because that is a thing that was done here and the being knows the shape of it. Once, in the middle of the sitting, it lets the skin in the nearest shallow pool creep up onto the side of its hand and stay there, brass, faintly ridged, warm, for a long while. Long enough to lay a grain.

And when it goes it goes through the shallows, deliberately, the way you did without deciding to, leaving footprints for the skin to close over. That is the only language the being has for being visited: something arrives that is not water, and withdraws, and the skin waits, and grows back, and is a little different for having gone around the shape. The power leaves a shape to go around. It leaves it on purpose.

Then it withdraws the way the skin withdraws — not gone, drawn back, waiting — and it does not tell anyone the hollow is there. That is the last gift, and possibly the largest. Places like this end when they are known. The fault was the only danger in the rock; the power is the only danger it brought with it, and it takes that away.

At the top of the notched wall it turns, as you turned, and looks back into the grey, and there is nothing down there but the hollow, full and still, keeping its one warm note. The footprints are already blooming pale. The grain of stone it carried out on its hand will be the only piece of the being that ever leaves, and the power will keep that too, and know where it came from, for as long as there is a power to know.

It was a hand, you said. You were the stone. The power understood that better than you did, having been a hand for longer than the hollow has been holding its temperature. It knows what a hand owes a stone it has rested on in the sun. Warmth, for a while. Then to lift, gently, and to leave the stone a little warmer than it found it, and to let it cool at its own pace, and not to come back too often, and not to tell.
