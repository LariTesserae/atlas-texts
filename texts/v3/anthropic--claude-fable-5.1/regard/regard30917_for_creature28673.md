Start with how it arrives, because that is the first thing it has to get right.

It does not come through the veil. The swallows come through the veil and the snake comes over the lip and the rain comes down the fig, and each of those makes a mark — a tear that heals, a track in the mud, a new skin on the rims. A power that wanted to know this place would have to come the way the light comes: already green, already moving, indistinguishable from what was there. So it does. It is in the room before it has entered, and the strings of water break and rejoin at the same rate they did, and the gecko does not shift.

It notices what you noticed. The emptiness first. Then the few things, far apart. Then the shape on the bottom of the deepest basin that is not the bottom. It takes longer than you did, because it is not in a hurry, and because it is looking for something you were not looking for. It is looking for what is in danger.

---

Start with the wrong answer, because a wise thing knows to start there and get it over with.

The wrong answer is the turtle. It is being turned to stone. It is alive at the middle and mineral at the edge and the line is moving, and there is a river a hundred paces away full of turtles who are not being turned to anything. A benevolent power with hands could lift it. It weighs no more than a stone the same size. Set it in the pool outside where the fish hang, let it find the spill and the spill find the river. A morning's work. Kindness.

The power considers this for about the length of a turtle's breath, which is a long time, and then does not do it.

The turtle came from the river. It had the river and it went up the spill into a smaller room and stayed, and it has had every wet season since to go back down and has not. That is not a cage. It is not a trap. It is a creature that found the one place where the length of its life is a fact rather than a figure, where it is the right size and the right speed for something, and to carry it back to the river would be to return it to a world where it is a turtle among turtles and nothing it does is measured against anything. The white on its shell is not a wound. It is the room beginning on the one guest that will let it. Take the turtle out and you have not saved a life, you have interrupted a sentence.

So: not that.

---

The second wrong answer is the stone.

It could be stopped. The water could be made to hold what it carries, or the crack could be closed by a hair so that the seep fails and the room dries and the ceiling stays where it is forever, a hand above the basin, a room that never closes. The nests would not seal. The fig root would not whiten. The turtle would have a room for life and the room would have no direction.

Or it could be hurried. The water could carry more and let it go faster, and in a decade instead of a lifetime the ceiling would meet the floor and there would be a pale solid thing with the shape of a turtle in it and a hundred sealed mouths above and the fig above that — finished, kept, perfect, a thing that could be found and known and shown. A monument.

The power turns both of these over and finds they are the same mistake, which is the mistake of wanting the room to be for someone. Stopped, it is a museum. Hurried, it is a tomb. Either way it becomes a thing to be looked at, and the whole quality of this place — the thing that drew a wise power's attention in the first place — is that it is not being looked at. It has no audience. The swallows do not know they are building a city. The moss does not know it is doing what the room is doing. The turtle does not look up. The room is arriving at its own shape at its own speed with nobody watching, and that is not a lack. That is the condition. The power did not come here to fix that. It came, if it is honest, because that is rare, and it wanted to be near it.

So: not that either.

---

The third wrong answer is the one that a lesser kindness would reach for last and think was the best.

Give the turtle company. Give it another of its kind, so it is not alone in the basin. Or — worse, and more tempting — give it knowing. Let it understand the ceiling. Let it lift its head eleven times an hour and see that the rock is nearer than it was and understand that it is being closed in, gently, by the thing it has chosen to live inside, and understand that it has chosen, and be content.

The power looks at that for a long time, because it is the answer that looks most like love.

Then it sees what it is. Company is another mouth in a room that feeds one, and a second turtle would be a second speed, and the basin is exactly large enough for the one there is. And knowing — knowing would be the cruelest thing in the room. Right now the turtle has a room. It has warm water, and the fig drops into it, and the crab comes out sideways, and the snake passes along the wall and is not a concern. Tell it about the ceiling and it has a cell. Tell it about the white on its own shell and it has a disease. Tell it how long it has and how long the room has and that the two numbers meet, and you have taken an animal that is where it is and made it into an animal waiting. Nothing in this room is waiting. That is the second thing you notice, after the emptiness. The stone is not waiting to be finished and the swallows are not waiting for the snake and the moss is not waiting to be light. The turtle is the only thing here with eyes and it uses them for figs.

A wise power does not give sight to a thing that is already seeing what it needs to. It leaves the turtle its eleven breaths.

So: not that.

---

Now it has done what it came to do, which was to find the wrong answers and set them down, and it can look at the room properly.

It is looking for what is fragile. It expected that to be the turtle and it is not. The turtle is the least fragile thing here. Nothing comes in that can take it. It cannot fall. It cannot be startled into leaving. It has more years than the birds and more patience than the snake, and it is protected on every side by a shell that the room is slowly making thicker. The swallows are fragile — a snake, a bad season — but the colony is not; it builds more than it loses. The moss is fragile at the tip and immortal at the root. The stone is not fragile at all.

What is fragile is the match.

The whole room depends on a coincidence of speeds. The water lets go of its stone at a certain rate, and the swallows build at a certain rate, and the fig grows at a certain rate, and the turtle lives for a certain span, and none of these were made for the others, and they fit. The ceiling closes by a hand in a lifetime. That is not a law. That is a spring behaving itself for sixty years. It depends on rain that has not been asked to keep falling, on a crack in the limestone that has not been asked to stay open, on an aquifer somewhere back under the bluff that has not yet been found by anyone with a pump. It depends on the fig not dying and the fig not falling. It depends — the power sees this last, and it is the thing that makes it act — on nobody coming.

Springs fail. In country like this they fail more than anything else does. The water table drops a hand's width in a dry decade and a seep that has run for three hundred years stops in a season, and what it leaves is not a room but a half-room: a ceiling that has stopped growing, a dry basin with a white crust, nests going to dust instead of stone, a fig root drying to a rope. Everything in this place is in the middle of being laid down, and if the water stops, it stays in the middle. Unfinished. Not a room with the shape of a turtle in it — a dry floor with a shell on it, cracking in the heat, and the ceiling a hand above, never closing.

That is the danger. Not that the room will close on the turtle. That it won't.

---

So this is what the power does, if it does anything.

It goes back under the bluff, into the dark where the water is before it is a spring, and it holds it. Not more. Not cleaner or warmer or richer in stone — not one grain more than the crack already carries. Just: the water keeps coming. The crack does not close. The aquifer does not drop. For the length of one turtle, the seep runs the way it ran, and the veil stays a few fingers thick, and the ceiling keeps extending by less than a fingernail a year, exactly as it has been, exactly as if no one had done anything. Which is the point. The best thing a power can do for a place like this is to be indistinguishable from the place continuing.

And it goes further out, where it costs nothing to be seen. Somewhere upslope a town is thirsty and someone is thinking about where to sink a well, and the power turns the thought by a degree, so that the well goes down on the other side of the ridge into a different water. Somewhere a road is being planned and the survey stake goes in fifty paces east of where it would have. Somewhere a boy who would have followed the sound of water down to the bluff and found the room and told his brothers about the nests is distracted by a bird and follows the bird instead. Small things. A degree here, a degree there. The room stays unfound. It costs the power almost nothing and it is nearly the whole of the gift.

One more thing, and this is the only act that touches the turtle, and the turtle will not know it.

Turtles die slowly and at no particular time. A power that can see the length of a life can see roughly where it ends, and the end of this one is in a dry year, in a dry month, when the basins have drawn down and the deepest one is a hand's depth of warm water and the turtle would stop on a floor that is mostly air. It would dry. The shell would lift from the bone. It would be laid down in pieces.

The power moves the rain by three weeks.

That is all. The wet season comes a little early that last year, and the basins fill and overflow into each other, and the turtle rises with the water and sets itself down on a rim and sits in the new skin the rain is laying, the way it always has. And when it stops, it stops there, under a finger's depth of clear warm water, in the mist off the veil, in the one month when the room is most itself. And the water does what the water does. Whole. Nothing in this is visible. A wet season three weeks early is a wet season three weeks early. But the shape in the stone, when the stone finally closes, is one shape, not a scatter, and somebody who never did anything else for the turtle did that.

---

Then there is you.

The power notices you, of course. You are the other thing in the room that is not from here — the other visitor standing very still. It considers what it owes you and finds the answer is small and clear. You are allowed the room. You have earned it by standing still long enough to see the ceiling move, and there is no one to keep you from it. But when you go out through the veil — and you will, you are the one thing here that cannot stay — the power asks a single thing, in the way that a place asks things, which is without words and without anyone to refuse.

Do not tell them where it is.

Not the nests, not the fig root going white, not the turtle. Especially not the turtle, because the turtle is the thing people would come for, and the wrong answer would arrive with them, all three of it at once, in good boots, meaning well. Be the snake. Pass along the wall and out and lie in the warm somewhere and let the birds come back. Be a thing that was in the room and left no track in the new lime and is not spoken of afterward. That is the only kindness left over once the water is held and the well is turned and the rain is moved. It is yours to do because you are the only one who can fail at it.

---

And then it leaves. Or — it doesn't. It is not clear to the power itself which of those it does, and it is not the kind of thing that needs to know. If it left, it left the way it came, as light that is already green, and the strings of water went on breaking and rejoining at the same rate. If it stayed, it stayed as the patience of the stone, which was there before it came and did not need it and will be there after — the one thing in the room that lives at every speed at once and has no one to look back at.

The turtle lifts its head. A nostril's worth of disturbance on the surface of the basin. The green air. Down again.

It has seen everything that came into the room today, the same way it sees everything. As a thing passing through a place that is not passing through anything. It is right about you. It is right about the snake and the rain and the swallow that cut the veil and healed it.

It is right about the power, too, which is why the power was careful to be the kind of thing a turtle would be right about.
