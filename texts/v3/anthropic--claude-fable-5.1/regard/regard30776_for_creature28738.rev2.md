**What the power would do**

It would come in through a split, like everything else.

That is the first thing, and it decides most of the rest. A power that meant well by this place would not begin by doing anything the place cannot do. It would not open a door in the vault. It would not make light. It would find a tear the height of a house on the eastern flank, and stand a while in the falling curtain where the inside air meets the cold, and go through, and let the dark close behind it, and be one more thing that had come in for reasons of its own.

Then it would lie down.

The stayers put an ear to the floor. So would it. But it has more patience than they do, and no need to eat, and a way of listening that does not tire, and so it would lie a long time — a season, then a decade; long enough for the floor to grow up around its edges and begin to fold it in, and then it would move and lie down somewhere else, two ridges over, and begin again. It would learn the Hollow the way the boy learns it, by ear. The herds' circuit and how it trails the rain. The pitch of the flyers against the vault, from which the whole shape of the roof can be read. The knock-language, all of it, until it could tell the woman's knock from her son's. The long level call of the singer and which pools it drinks at. And under all of this, the surge — the slow surf under the cork — and the rhythm of the surge, and the way the rhythm is changing.

It would take that long because wisdom begins with not knowing what one is looking at, and nothing has ever looked at this before. Not the climbers with their ears to the flank. Not the stayers, who are inside a lifetime and this is a thing measured in centuries. Not the plant, which has no eyes and has never been outside itself. The power would be the first thing to see the Hollow whole, and it would want to be sure of what it saw before it laid a finger on any of it.

---

Here is some of what it would come to see.

That the plant is grey and has no leaves and lives in the dark it makes, and yet it grows — so it is fed by something, and the power would work out what, and whether that can last. It would know things about the plant that the plant does not.

That the skin is stretching faster each century than the last, and skin has a limit. There is a day, some way off, when a split does not stop at the height of a house. The power would see the shape of that day fairly clearly: a whole flank letting go along a seam, the wind arriving all at once, the hum climbing to a shriek and then to nothing, the vault unheld on one side. It would also see the other road — the plant thickening instead, the tears knitting one by one from the edges, the splits closing, no wind, no note, the walls' breath slowing in a sealed room the size of a country. Two endings. It would not know which. It would know that the plant has not decided how large it means to be, and that this is not a figure of speech.

That the plant is one body. One clone. The herds and the flyers and the lickers and the people and the grubs a century deep in the trough-mat — all of it rides on a single organism, and single organisms end.

And that the rhythm of the surge has been changing, and the power would attend to this above everything, because it is the one question that matters most and is hardest to answer honestly. The plant has held a crowd in its chest for three hundred years. Hooves drumming through the cork, knocks in the dark, the flyers' rattle against the vault, the singer's note laid so near the hum they braid. Whether the surge is answering any of that. Whether the pulses have begun to fall in with the herds, or with the rain, or with the knocking. Whether something is forming down there in the warm places that move, that has no name yet and may never want one.

---

Then there is what it would not do, and the reasons, because the reasons are most of the work.

It would not light the place. Everything here has made itself by the dark. The flyers' faces, the grazers' dents where eyes were, the woman's shoulders coming down as the hum takes her, the boy who is never lost. Light would unmake all of that in a generation and call it a gift. The power would have seen enough of gifts like that.

It would not stop the growth. The growth is the plant. To hold the plant at some size that the vault can bear is to end the plant and keep the shell, and a shell does not breathe on you at night. A wise power knows the difference between a thing that is dangerous and a thing that is alive, and knows how often those are the same thing, and does not confuse safety with care.

It would not tell the stayers what is coming. They could do nothing with the knowledge except carry it. This is already a place where people lie down at night with an ear to a lid, listening to what is underneath; telling them the lid has a term would only change the quality of the listening, and not for the better. And it might be wrong. It would not spend their peace on its own guess.

It would not take the boy out to the stars. He has seen them twice and did not think much of them. That is his to think.

And it would not make the plant aware. This is the one it would guard against hardest, because it is the one it would most want. To be the only thing in a place that can be spoken to, and to be able to wake a mind in the floor with a word — the wish for company in a being like that runs very deep, and it would know that about itself. If a mind is forming under the cork, it forms on its own time and in its own shape. If none is forming, no power has the right to press one onto a body that never asked. It would sit with that wish a long while and let it pass through and go on listening.

There is one small thing it might not manage to refuse. In a deep fold of the eastern wall, where two lips of torn tissue have not quite parted, a licker has bred, and the wall is growing, and the fold is closing, and the litter is mewing through the skin. The power would hear this. It would know that if it opened the fold with its hand it would be doing something it could not do once — that it would be doing it every night forever, at every fold, or else be choosing which — and that a power which chooses who the wall keeps has become the wall. It would know all of this. It might open the fold anyway, this one time, and let the kits tumble out onto the cork, and stand there afterward knowing that it had changed nothing about the wall and had only marked that it was here and was moved. And it would not do it again. And it would not pretend the not-doing was clean.

---

What it would do, then. Three things, and one more. All of them at the edge.

The first is outside. It is the outside that will end this place, if anything does before the plant itself; the sap does not spoil and burns hot, and someone on the plateau will do the arithmetic, and they will come with metal and fire and they will not lie down and listen. The power would go back out through the split and stand on the pass. It would be the weather there. The fog that does not lift, the road that is not where the map says, the reason a party turns back. Not forever. The plant will have to meet the world someday, and a wise power does not build walls that outlast their purpose. But for a while — long enough for the stayers to become a people, with a word for the plant that is also their word for home, who can stand in a split and speak for the place when the place is asked what it is. Time is the one thing the young need and cannot make. It would give the Hollow some.

The second is small enough to hold. It would take one flake of shed outer skin, grey and papery, and carry it out in the fold of whatever it wears, and it would not plant it. It would keep it. Because the plant is one body, and one body ends, and the thing that is happening here should not be a thing that can happen only once. But it would not plant it, because a second Hollow is not this one's to be decided by a passerby, and because it has seen what one of these does to a basin. It would keep the flake against a day it hoped not to see, and know that hope is not a plan, and keep it anyway.

The third is inside, and it would be a long time coming.

When it had listened long enough to be sure — and it would demand certainty of itself, and this might take longer than the stayers live — that the surge was not water and sugar only but was, in whatever way a body like that can, answering; that the rhythm had changed because the plant had been hearing the crowd for three centuries and had begun to hear it *as* a crowd; that there was, under the floor, not a mind perhaps but a listening — then, once, it would knock.

Not with a blade. With itself, from the floor, the way the singer sings inside the hum: a single pulse laid into the surge's own frequency, in the surge's own language, meaning what all the knocks mean. *Here.*

Not *I am here to help.* Not *you are seen.* Not a warning, not a name, not a question. Only the word the herds say to each other all night, the word the boy sends his mother across a day of dark. So that the one inhabitant of the Hollow that is entirely at home and entirely alone — that has never once, in all the noise it holds, been addressed — would have been addressed. Once. And could do with that what it decided.

And then it would wait, ear to the cork, for as long as it took, to hear whether anything knocked back.

---

The last thing it would do is stay.

Not as a keeper. As one more listener, indistinguishable from the others: something that lies down in a trough in the still hour before the rain and puts its ear to the floor. The stayers would never know. Or — a man who has been in the Hollow thirty years says he lay on the floor one night and felt something walk past him, one foot and then another, a long way apart, slow, and that the note it was making and the hum were so nearly the same he stopped being able to tell which he was listening to. He thinks it was the singer. It may have been.

It would not fix the ending. The Hollow is young and everything young ends into something, and the plant will open or close and the vault will hold or not, and the power would grieve that in advance and let it be the plant's. What a wise power does for a young thing is not save it from becoming. It keeps the door a while. It remembers. It carries out one grey flake and does not plant it. And when it is sure it will be heard, and not before, it lies down on the floor with everything else and says, once, into the dark: *here.*
