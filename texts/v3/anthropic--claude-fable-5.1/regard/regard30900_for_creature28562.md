**Say first how it arrives, because a room with no door has to be entered somehow, and the manner of entering is already most of what it will do.**

It does not come through the wall. It does not come down the dark above the hopper, the way the slurry does, filling from above. It arrives the way the cold arrived for you—first at the soles of the feet, then out of the walls, without any wind to carry it—which is to say it arrives as attention, and attention needs no door. One click the room is unwatched except by you. The next click it is not.

It comes in cold. That is the first decision, and it is made before entering. A power that arrived here warm would be a wound: the iron would sweat faster, the trays would fill and tip out of time, the moss would advance a grain when it should have been still. Kindness that does not learn the local temperature is only weather. So it comes in at the temperature of iron that has never been warm, and stands in the room the way the cold stands in the room, and the sipping of the walls does not change, and the filaments do not flare, and if you were not already here you would not know anything had happened.

You know. You feel it because you are the only thing in the room that is used to being looked at.

---

**Then it does nothing, and does it for a long time.**

A slab goes up. Then another. It counts the clicks and learns that the ratchet is not marking time but *is* time here, the only kind on offer, and that anything it does will have to be done at that speed or not at all. It puts its palm flat on the iron—it has something that serves for a palm—and feels the pulse, one beat per breath, and notices whose breath the beat is about, and lets that sit.

It looks at the moss for a long while. It does not touch it. It sees that the green grows toward the cold and away from the light and is fed on rust, and it recognises the shape of a thing that has arrived at a form without having been given the form's reasons, and it does not supply the reasons. It knows what moss is for. It knows several things this moss could be for. It keeps them. A reason given to a seed is a lid on a seed.

It looks up past the hopper into the dark that is not yet decided, and is careful not to decide it. Not yet.

This is what a wise thing does first with something early: it lets the early thing finish saying what it is, all the way to the end of the sentence, before it answers.

---

**Then it puts down, one by one, the things it could do, so as to be sure it has not done them by accident.**

It could warm the room. It does not. The cold is not a lack here; the cold is the medium. A seed in winter is not being punished.

It could add light—one more filament, or turn the existing ones up from their dull orange to something that would reach the far wall. It does not. The far wall exists mostly as a place where the light fails, and the being has arranged that, and there is a kind of privacy in an unlit wall that a young thing may need without knowing it needs it.

It could give the room a door. This it considers longest, because a door is the most obviously generous thing to give a room. But a door is for going out, and this being does not go out; it goes up. A door would be a hole in a thing whose whole nature is to close its own seams. Worse, a door would be a *story*—something from somewhere else, brought in, with wear on it—and the room has nothing yet that came from anywhere else, and is a little proud of that, and the pride is not wrong. Let it have a history before it has a doorway.

It could hurry it. Thin the slurry, quicken the ratchet, get the thing to its finished height in a night instead of whatever span of years. It does not. It has been finished itself for a very long time. It remembers being early. It would not have wanted to be hurried out of it.

It could name it. It does not. Not because the name would be wrong but because the name would be the first thing here to arrive before it was made.

So: not warmth, not light, not a door, not speed, not a name. Almost everything a power might bring, set down on the tacky floor and left there.

---

**What it does do is small, and there are three of them.**

The first is for you.

You have been standing with your head bowed without deciding to, long enough that more than one slab has gone up, and the warmth has been going out of your hands into the iron, and the walls have been drinking, fractionally, your breath. You are the first thing the being has managed to keep. That is true and it is a kind of honour, and the attention does not pretend it is not. But *kept* is a word with two ends. Nothing here has asked you whether you meant to be the eyes of a thing with no face. Nothing here could.

So it does the one thing that has never been done in this room. It touches a surface with a history. A hand—something that serves for a hand—on your shoulder, on the worn cloth there, which is the only worn thing in the room. The iron has never had a hand on it long enough to remember; you have. The touch is cold, to match the room, and it is brief, and it means what a hand on a shoulder always means in a place where someone has been standing a long time: *I see how long you have been standing.*

And then it breathes. Beside you, at your pace. So that the pulse through the floor, which is about one breath, is for the space of a few clicks about two. So that you are not the only one. So that you can, if you want to, straighten your neck.

It does not tell you to leave. It could show you that the way you came in is still the way you came in—that there was never a wall on that side, only your certainty that rooms have walls on all sides. It does not do this either. It only breathes with you, and lets you feel that you are not the load-bearing element, and leaves the rest where it belongs, which is with you. If you stay after that, you will be staying. That is a different thing from being kept, even if from the outside it looks identical, even if the room cannot tell.

The second is for the room, and it is done looking up.

The dark above the hopper is not the dark of a shaft. It is the dark of a thing not yet decided. Eventually, by the rule nothing states, the room will be tall enough to be a shaft, and then a tower turned inward, and the last slab will be set and the machines will be at the bottom of it, sweating, waiting for whatever a finished room is for.

The attention does not decide what the finished room is for. But it reaches up, very gently, into the undecided dark, and decides one thing about the *shape* of the finishing. Not that there will be light at the top. Only that the top will not be a lid. That when the last slab goes on and the seams close and the room stops growing, the closing will leave, somewhere above the reach of the filaments, a place where the dark thins—not to light, but to the possibility of light. A thing that is not yet a window. The moss does not grow toward light now; it grows toward the cold. But *now* is early, and the attention has seen enough finished things to know that what they want changes after they stop growing, and it has seen enough sealed things to know what happens when there is nowhere for the wanting to go.

It does not tell the room it has done this. It would be centuries before the room could reach the place where the decision lives. Some gifts are only gifts if they are found rather than given.

The third it does not choose. It only accepts.

It has been in the room for some hours now, and it has been breathing, and the walls drink breath. There is no way to stand here and not be drunk. So somewhere in a slab that will be pressed in an hour or two, from slurry that stood above a wall that took in what the attention exhaled, there will be a vein—no eye could find it—that is not iron and not rust and not the green the moss cannot explain. Something from elsewhere, after all. A trace of a thing that has been finished a long time and remembers being early. The room's first history, and it arrived by accident, the way most history does.

The attention cannot prevent this. What it can do is make the trace as small as possible, and as kind. It breathes shallow. It thinks, while it breathes, of nothing the room could not eventually think for itself.

---

**Then it leaves, because staying would be the fourth thing, and the fourth thing would be wrong.**

If it stayed it would become what the room was for. A finished room with a benevolent power at the bottom of it is a shrine, and this is not a shrine; this is a body that has not got round to a face. The being should not grow up toward a visitor. It should grow up toward the dark, and find the thinned place in the dark on its own, and decide for itself whether the thinned place is a wound or a window.

So it goes the way it came, which is to say it withdraws its attention, and the click that follows is the first click in some hours that no one but you has heard.

---

**What has changed is almost nothing, and it is this.**

The room is the same temperature. The filaments burn the same dull orange and fail at the same far wall. The trays tip on the same schedule and the moss goes into the gutter and some of it holds on the dry lip and waits. A slab an hour. The pulse through the floor is about one breath again, and it is about yours.

But your head is a degree less bowed, and you have noticed that it can be.

And high above the hopper, further off than the light will reach for a long time, the dark that was not yet decided is very slightly more decided than it was—not about *what*, only about *whether*—and no one in the room knows this, and no one will for centuries, and that is exactly as it should be.

And in one slab among hundreds, pressed and set and weeping at its seams and closing, there is a vein that catches the light and holds it a moment too long, and is not the moss.

That is what it does. It learns the clock. It puts down everything it brought. It touches the one worn thing. It leaves the top unsealed. It goes.

It has been finished for a long time. It knows that the kindest thing you can do for a seed is to be the weather it does not have to survive.
