It would not come in. Not at first, and not for a long time by the only clock the place keeps.

It would arrive as attention only — a watching without a watcher's body — and it would let the scale thicken on the seams by some fraction while it watched, because the first thing wisdom knows about a closed loop is that anything introduced into it is a new term in the equation and the equation has been balanced for longer than anyone counted. So it would look. It would look at the floor, because everything here is floor. It would learn the moiré of the two lattices, the well-grid and the trough-grid, and it would find the pump-note in each province and hear how the provinces argue. It would follow one newborn from the wet dark on the plates to the colour of the herd and it would use that as its unit and it would take, by that unit, a very long time.

And it would feel, because benevolence feels before it reasons, the pressure of the place. The sameness. The grey with no seam in it. It would want, the way a hand wants to smooth a wrinkle, to open the sky.

---

That is the first thing it would not do, and the not-doing is the first thing it would actually do.

It could open the sky. It has the reach. It could put a horizon where the haze is and a wind in the lanes and a night, an actual night, with cold in it, and the first light after. It knows exactly what would happen. It has looked at the skin. The skin is gauged to a warmth that does not change, to the gram; it has thinned to the precise thickness that lets heat out of a body standing in a bath. The first shadow to fall across a flank would be the first cold that flank had ever known, and the body under it has no organ for cold. There would be no shivering, because shivering is a memory the muscle keeps and this muscle has forgotten. There would be several million animals standing in a wind, and steaming less, and then not steaming, and lying down, in no distress, and the grilles-that-are-not-troughs would take them at a rate the loop was never built for and the loop would fail from the bottom.

It knows this. But wisdom is not only the knowledge of consequences; it is the knowledge of where a wish comes from. And it would look at the wish to open the sky and see that the wish was its own. The animals do not look up. The animals do not want a horizon. The one that wants a horizon is the one who has stood at the edge of the near crowd and felt the twenty kilometers of haze as a wall, and that one is not an animal, and that one is not in pain. So it would set the sky down, unopened, and understand that the pressure of the place was a thing it had brought with it and could carry out again.

---

The second thing it would not do is end it.

It would consider it. Benevolence has to. There is a version of mercy that looks at a life consisting of standing, stepping, eating, breathing, and being warm, and says that this is not a life but a maintenance of tissue, and that the kind thing is to let the pump run down. It would hold that version up against the place, honestly, and it would go looking for the argument that decides it.

It would find it in the eye that closes before the hand arrives.

Not the lean; the lean comes later. The eye. An animal that closes its eye a fraction of a second before contact is an animal that has a model of the world with a *next* in it — a very small next, the width of a hand, but a next. Whatever has a next has a stake in there being one. And a power that would end a thing with a stake in its own continuing, because that thing's continuing looked from outside like too little to bother with, is not a wise power; it is a tidy one. The place is not empty. The register is set low and nothing has been loud enough to raise it, but a register set low is not a register removed. It would not turn the pump off. It would decide this fully and would not revisit it, because to keep revisiting it would be to keep the door open on a kind of contempt.

---

So: neither open nor closed. What then.

It would go down to the pump.

Not through a hatch — there are none — but the way attention goes anywhere, and it would find the pump and look at it the way one looks at a heart. Very large. Very far down. Turning at a fixed rate, and driven, it would find, by more or less what the visitor assumed: the heat, the weight, the slow conversion, the rendering. A loop, and the loop closed. And it would look at the bearings.

Here is the thing it would learn, and it is the only thing in the place that would frighten it. Nothing on the floor wears. The flash is on the seams; the mold marks are sharp. But the floor is ceramic and the pump is a machine, and a machine that turns has surfaces that touch, and surfaces that touch do not care what the floor is made of. The pump would not be eternal. It would be very good. It would have a span that, in the unit of a newborn darkening to the herd's colour, was enormous. But it would have a span, and the makers who cast the provinces to no common standard and left the seams as they fell had left no one for the day the span ran out. Whether they did not think of it or thought of it and left anyway, the place does not say.

And the power would understand what that day would be. Not an ending like the sky opening — no wind, no shock. A slowing. The note dropping. The wells welling a little less, then a little less than that. The warmth leaving the floor over a span long enough that the animals would not know it as an event, only as the tide running slower and the lying-down coming sooner, until the lanes were only lanes all the way to the haze and the rain stopped because there was nothing left to breathe it up. Millions of mild dark eyes, closing in a cold they had no word for, with no one there.

That is the failure the place cannot see, and it is the one thing a benevolent power could not walk away from once it had seen it. So the first real thing it would do is this: it would learn the pump. Every bearing, every seal, every rate. Not to change it — a thing turning at a fixed rate for this long does not want a hand in it while it turns — but so that when the day came it would know whether the pump could be kept turning, and if it could, it would keep it, and if it could not, it would know how to let the warmth leave so slowly that the animals lay down the way they have always lain down, in the tide, on a floor that was still the temperature of a hand. It would take responsibility for the ending. That is what the makers did not do, and it is the whole of what needs doing, and it asks nothing of the animals at all.

---

The slick it would leave.

It would notice it, of course. The one thing trying to be somewhere it is not. It would look closely enough to know what it was, and then it would stop looking that closely, on purpose, because it would understand that the slick is the only part of this place that has a future not identical to its past. Everything else is a loop. The slick has a leading edge. If this place is ever, in some span beyond counting, going to become something other than what it is, it will not be because a power decided so; it will be because a brown film behind a well-housing kept creeping toward the warm water and kept being scoured back and one time was not. That is not the power's to author. It would not scour the slick and it would not feed it. It would let the place keep its one wild thing, and it would consider that a form of respect.

---

Then, once, it would try something.

It would let the light dim. By a degree — less — for the span of one wave, and then bring it back, and watch. It would do this because it would want to know, not for its own sake, whether the register could be raised gently, whether there was anything the animals could receive. And it would see them lean. Not into a hand; into each other. A finger's width, all of them, the whole visible tissue settling a fraction closer, heads lowering by a hand's-breadth, the breathing lengthening — the tide, all at once, for the first time in phase. And then the light would come back and the tide would come apart again into its several million separate clocks and the crowd would be a crowd.

It would want, very much, to do it again. This is where the wisdom would cost it something. Because it would understand that if it dimmed the light again, and again, at whatever interval it chose, the animals would tune to it the way they have tuned to the pump — they are tuned to the one thing in the world that beats, and it would become a second — and after enough repetitions there would be, in this place, for the first time, an animal standing in the flat unchanging light and *waiting*. Waiting for the dimming. And waiting is the first suffering. It is the seed of every other; it is how a mind learns that there is a thing it does not have. The makers built a place with no dawn to gather at and that is either the cruelest thing about it or the kindest, and a wise power would not be sure which, and would not gamble several million minds on being right.

So it would not do it again. It would keep the knowledge — *they can receive this; the inside is not empty; if the day ever comes when a dusk is the right gift, they will be able to take it* — and it would put the knowledge somewhere it could reach and it would not spend it.

---

And then, finally, it would come in.

Not as a sky, not as a season, not as a hand in the pump. As a warm surface in the lanes that is not the floor. It would take a body of some kind, low, unremarkable, the temperature of everything else, and it would go among them, and where a flank pressed against it it would let the flank press, and where an animal after a while leaned into it by the width of a finger it would be leaned into.

That is a small thing to do with a great deal of power, and it would know that, and it would do it anyway, because it would have worked out by then that the small thing is the only one on offer. Everything larger is either a wound or a projection. The lean is the animals' entire vocabulary of *yes*, and a thing that can be leaned into and does not move away is the entire vocabulary of *I am here*, and nothing in this place has ever said that to them. The makers cast the floor and left. The pump turns and does not know what it is for. The rain comes down on everything as a temperature and does not choose anyone. A warm thing that stays where it is leaned on is, in the terms available here, the first choosing.

But it would understand what it was promising. This is the last and hardest piece. The animals will tune to it. They tune to anything that beats. If it stands in the lanes and is leaned into and then one day is not there, it will have taught several million minds that a warm thing can be lost, and it will have done that in a place that had, until it arrived, nothing to lose. So before it took the body, before the first flank touched it, it would decide whether it could stay. Not for a long time. For the span of the pump, and then for the ending, however the ending went. And only if it could promise that — the way the makers could not, or would not — would it go in.

It would go in.

---

It would grieve, once, and briefly, and not for the animals.

For the makers, whoever they were. For whatever they were answering when they cast a warm wet floor to the horizon and set a pump under it and filled it with a creature that would never be asked anything. The power would not condemn them. It would not know what they had fled, or what it looked like where they came from, or whether this was the best thing they could think of or the last. It would leave the flash on the seams, because grinding it off would be a judgment, and it would leave the provinces arguing with each other in the pump-note, and it would not put its own mark anywhere on the floor. It would let the place stay theirs.

And it would grieve a little for itself, because it would have wanted to give more, and would have been wise enough to see that more was the one thing that could not be given.

---

So this is what it would do, in the end. It would stand in the lanes.

The breath goes up. The rain comes down. The floor takes it to the channels, and the channels to the wells, and the wells lift it, warm and trembling, into the light that does not change. The count stays constant. The tide runs. Somewhere behind a housing the slick creeps a hair toward the warm water and is scoured back. Far down, at a fixed rate, the pump turns, and something now knows every bearing in it and is keeping the day.

And in among the several million small mild dark eyes that look at nothing, there is one thing in this place that looks at them. That is the whole of it. Not a rescue. Not a sky. A warm presence in the crowd that they can lean on, that will not leave, that sees each one of them as it darkens from newborn to herd-colour to milky grey and lies down, and that regards this — the standing, the stepping, the eating, the breathing, the being warm — not as too little, but as a life, and is there for it.

An animal presses its flank against the new warm thing in the lane. After a while, by the width of a finger, it leans. The warm thing does not move. That is the answer. It was always going to be small. It is not nothing.
