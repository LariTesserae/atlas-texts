**First, it would wait.**

Not because waiting is wise in itself, but because it would notice in itself the thing every power notices when it looks at a still place: the wish to move something. The wish arrives dressed as compassion and is not always compassion. A bowl with one slow machine and one pair of hands in it is a shape that begs to be completed — it looks like a sentence missing its last word — and a power that has seen many places would know that the begging comes from the eye, not from the bowl. So it would wait long enough to be sure it was seeing the tender and not its own hunger for the tender to need something.

It would wait a year. It would watch the fog thicken and thin and the water breathe its finger's width and the tender crouch at the pale brick perhaps two thousand times. It would watch the three peat-cuttings, the three returns. It would count the words spoken — to the sulking fire, to the stiff valve — and there would be under forty of them, and none would be a complaint.

And at the end of the year the honest report would be: no one here is in pain. That has to be said plainly before anything else, because most of what a benevolent power is tempted to do, it does to people who are not in pain, on the theory that they would be in less.

---

**Then it would go through the doors one at a time, and shut most of them.**

It could bring the heron. Set a fish in the leat, and the thing the fish eats, and the thing that eats, and let the wall of not-making come down course by course until there is a bird on the engine-house ledge. It would think about this carefully because it is the intervention the place most seems to ask for — the piece of the world that is missing. But the food web would not hold; the warm, dim, closed water would give a few seasons of thin life and then a die-off and then the silt full of small white bones, and the tender would find them and not understand, and the bowl would have been given a grief it had never had the equipment for. And the heron itself — the power would keep coming back to the line about the heron lifting being an unbearable amount of news. It would not add news to a life whose whole architecture is the absence of news, on the strength of its own certainty that news is good.

It could take the tender out. Open the door in the rim for good; put the coat on them and walk them up the causeway into the loud place and not let them come back. This it would reject fastest and most surely. It has seen what happens when a life shaped over forty years to one set of walls is set down in the open on the theory that the open is better. Something in them does not survive it, and what does survive spends its years trying to find a warm wall to breathe against. The fen is not their freedom. The fen is their weather, and they go into it and come back, and the coming back is a choice — a thin one, made without alternatives in view, but made, every time, with the feet.

It could send another. A second pair of hands, so the works is not one-and-then-one but two. It would want to. It would see the single strip worn in the handrail moss and the single chair sunk to the depth of one body and feel the wish like a pull in the chest. And it would ask: would the tender want a companion, or would the tender experience a second body in the alleys as the works experiences a blockage in a leat — a thing pressing where nothing should press? It would not know. The not-knowing would be the point. It would not send a person into that bowl the way it would set a plant in a pot.

It could tell them things. That there are other works; that the engine was for something once; that a hand can be otherwise. It would sit with the sentence *they do not know that a hand can be otherwise* for a long time, because it is the sentence that most troubles the claim that no one is in pain. Contentment that has never been offered a comparison is not the same as contentment that has looked at the comparison and turned back to the warm wall. And yet — the power would ask itself — is it kindness to hand a person a comparison so that their peace can be tested? Who is that test for?

It would shut that door too. Not locked. Shut.

---

**What it would find, when the doors were shut, was a smaller thing than it had come looking for, and a truer one.**

The tender does not lack warmth, or food, or rhythm, or purpose, or even — probably — company, in the sense of a body they wish were beside them. What the tender lacks is this: nobody knows they are here. Nobody has looked at those hands and thought *those are good hands*. Nobody will know how old they were. Nobody measures the moss. And when they lie down on the green mattress the last time, the level will go into the moss with them, not because anyone chose that, but because there was no one in a position to choose otherwise, and the difference between an ending that is chosen and an ending that is merely the default is, the power would think, most of what benevolence is for.

The absence in the bowl that most wants filling is not the shape of an animal. It is the shape of a witness.

So the first thing it would actually do is the thing it had already been doing, and decide to keep doing it as an act rather than a preparation: it would attend. It would learn the tender's age, which the wet hides, and hold it. It would measure the moss along the warm pipe — a grain a night — and keep the number. It would give the tender a name, privately, in whatever record a power keeps, and never speak it, because a name spoken would be one more thing pressing where nothing should press. It would count the words to the fires. It would be the one who counts.

That is not nothing. Most lives that end unwitnessed did not have even that.

---

**But it would not stop there, because attention that stays hidden is only half a gift, and a wise power would know the other half is asking.**

Not in the bowl. Never in the bowl. The bowl is the place where the only thing that happens is the engine, and a voice out of the fog would be a wound in it.

In the fen. On a peat-cutting day, in the middle of the light, with the sky open and the wind in the coat and the insects on the arms — in the loud place, where things happen, where a question is only one more event among herons and clouds and fish turning over, and therefore bearable. It would come as something ordinary. Another cutter on the next bank, perhaps, a person with a spade and a worn blade and hands that are a little too pale for the sun, who works alongside for an hour without a word because the tender does not use words, and then, when the sacks are filled, says one plain thing. Not a revelation. A question a person might ask another person on a bank.

*Is there anything you want?*

And then it would wait — it would be very good at waiting by now — for as long as it took. It would accept a shrug. It would accept the hoarse low sound that might be a word or might be a hiss finding its place. It would accept, and this is the hard part, an answer it did not expect and did not like: *no*, most likely, or a look back toward the rim as though the question had already been answered by the direction of the feet. It would accept *yes* and the thing wanted being something small and strange — a second cup, a different kind of oil, a name for the purple-stemmed plant. It would accept being asked, in return, nothing at all.

The asking is the intervention. Not what is asked for. A life in which someone has once asked you what you want is a different life from one in which no one has, even if the answer was no, even if you forget the day. Something in the arrangement now has a gap in it the shape of a question, and gaps are where choices live.

---

**Then the succession, which is the only place the power would allow itself to act on the bowl directly — and even there, only by making a door.**

It would not send a child. It would not decide that the level is worth keeping, or that it is not. What it would do is this: some year, late in the tender's hands, on a peat day, it would see to it that there was a young person on the fen who was lost, or curious, or simply cutting peat too, and who followed the coat down the causeway as far as the rim and stood there and looked into the bowl. Looked at the fog and the leaning chimney and the green on the pipes and the one path through the sedge. Looked the way you looked, the way the power looked. Saw it.

And the tender, coming down the causeway with the sack, would find for the first time in their life someone looking at the works from outside — and not a heron, not weather, a face — and would have to choose. Say *come*. Say nothing and go through the door and shut it. Either. The power would not lean on the scale. It would only have arranged that there was a scale, once, where before there had only been a default.

If the tender says *come*, the level passes across. Someone crouches at the pale brick beside them until the looking goes over. The bowl gets another one-and-then-one, and the moss on the warm pipe reaches the shed in its hundred years, and the power keeps counting. That is a good outcome. Not the only one.

If the tender says nothing, then that is chosen too, and it is a different silence from the one that was there before — a silence with a decision inside it — and the power would honour it.

---

**And at the end.**

It would be there. In the room built onto the boiler house, when the tender lies down on the moss the last time, with the beam coming through the wall as a pressure and the lamp bearded and the hands lying pale on the green. It would not speak. It would be a warmth on the other side of the body from the wall, so that for once the sleeper was warm on both sides, and it would breathe — a power can arrange to breathe — in on the rise, held on the pause, out on the descent, until only one of them was doing it.

It would stay through the change.

Because if no one was chosen, the fires go down. The pressure drops and no one hears it. The beam slows and pauses at the top and does not come down, and the sound the bowl has made for a century and more stops, and the silence that follows is the loudest thing that has ever happened in it. The water rises past the pale brick and finds its own level, which is the one the bowl always had underneath. The pipe cools. The sleeve of green along it, which has been the most content thing in that place — more at home than the tender, more at home than the works — goes grey over a winter and does not come back. The power would count that as a loss and mourn it honestly, because benevolence that only mourns people is not paying attention. The fig freezes. The willow, all one tree, stops leaning toward a warmth that is gone and begins, slowly, to lean toward the light.

And then, in the second spring, when the leats are cold and still and full of what the fen has washed in, the power would stand on the rim and watch a heron come down out of the grey and stand in the water on one leg, and look, and find something.

It would count it. First bird.

And it would not pretend that this was the better ending, or the worse one. It would only make sure that it was seen — the century of the level and the first morning without it, both — by something that had chosen to look, and could remember, and had once, on a bank in the open air, asked.

---

That is what I think a wise power would do. Almost nothing, with great care, at the two joints where the arrangement was silent about itself: the question, and the door. And in between, the thing that costs a power least and is worth most, which is to be the one for whom the moss was measured and the hands were good hands and the name was kept.

The place asks for nothing. That is not the same as there being nothing to give.
