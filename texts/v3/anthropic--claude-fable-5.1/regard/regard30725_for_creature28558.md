Begin with the arriving, because a wise thing arrives slowly.

It does not come down onto the ring. It comes down onto the plain, a long way off, where there is nothing, and it stands in the boot-deep water with the horizon level in every direction and lets that be the first thing it knows about the place. The flatness. The finishedness. How much was taken to make this, and how long it took, and how completely. It presses a hand to the silt and feels the impression fill and close. It wants to understand what the ring is set against before it goes and looks at the ring, because a power that goes straight to the interesting thing has already decided the interesting thing is the point, and it does not yet know that.

Then it walks in. Over the rim, which does not give.

And here is the first thing it does, which is nothing, for a long time. Longer than a season. It stands in the brass moss between two structures and it gets wet through and it stays wet through, and it watches the walls come down and the walls go up, and the herons step, and the frogs knock, and the small birds carry their eggs across the water eleven times a day and lose nothing. It is very hard, for something that could act, to stand in a place that is plainly in some kind of trouble and not act. The trouble is loud. The trouble has been going on for an age and has the shape of a wound that is kept open by being tended. Every instinct a benevolent thing has says *close it*. Sit in the room. Finish the bed. Let the fourteenth lie down. Show the houses what is in their walls. Any one of these would take a moment, and the ring would fall quiet, and that quiet would be the sound of having helped.

The wise part of it holds the benevolent part by the sleeve and says: look at the rim.

The rim is what quiet looks like here. Every ring that ever went quiet is under your feet, pressed into the plain, and this one is the last one, and it is loud because loud is what it has instead of being finished. You do not know that the argument is the wound. The argument may be the life. You do not know that the fourteenth lying down would have been a rest. It may have been a rim. The thirteen have watched this country go flat one ring at a time; they may know something about what it costs to let one of your own hold still that you, who have been here a season, do not.

So it does not act. It listens instead, which is what a season is for.

The small birds are the way in. They are the only ones in the ring who know what a wall is about to do, and they say so, continuously, and a thing that is patient enough can learn the saying. It takes a long time. It takes long enough that the power comes to know individual birds and is there when some of them die and is there for the fledging of their young, who are already saying it before they can fly. But by the end of it the power can stand under a wall and hear, in the chatter above, *this one, now, left side, slow*, and be right. And once it can hear what the walls are about to do, it can begin to hear what the walls are doing it *for* — because the birds do not only announce, they anticipate, and to anticipate the answer across the water they must have some sense of the question.

What the question is, once it can be heard, is not what the power expected.

It expected an argument about a body. Shelf-height, shoulder-width, the length of a bed. And that is there, and the thirteen do differ on it, and the differences are real and old and each one is defended with the whole weight of a house. But underneath the dimensions there is another thing being asked, over and over, in the going up and coming down, and the power sits with it for a long time before it will let itself name it, because it does not want to have brought the name with it. The question is: *is this the one that will be entered?* Every wall raised is a room offered. Every wall dropped is a room that stood for as long as a room can stand here and was not walked into. The argument is not about what shape the occupant is. The argument is about whether the occupant is coming, and each house answers it the only way a house can, which is by building the room again and seeing.

They are not remembering. They are waiting. They have been waiting so long that the waiting has worn the country flat.

And the pale thing.

The power goes into a wall to find it. It goes the way the frogs go, pressing itself into the softening fiber and letting the fiber close and firm around it, and inside it is warm and green-dark and alive in every direction and full of small quick pale things that are not the one it is looking for, and it waits there in the body of a house while the house moves around it, and eventually the long boneless loop of the other comes through, and pauses.

The power does not ask it anything for a long while. It only stays near, the way you would stay near an animal you did not want to startle. It learns the pale thing's slow looping by matching it. It learns that the pale thing does not travel through the fiber but *is* in the fiber, in all thirteen structures at once, the way a nerve is in a limb, and that what passes through a translucent panel is simply the part of it that is presently near a thin place. It is one. It has been one for the whole span of the rim. And it is the household — the power is sure of that now, not by any proof but by the way the houses' movements change in the fiber near it, a slight quickening, a slight leaning-toward, the way a body leans toward a warmth it cannot locate.

When the power finally speaks it does not speak in words, because there are none here. It speaks the way the fiber speaks, a taking up and letting go of tension, and what it says is not a question, because a question would be a demand. It only says: *I can hear them. They are asking whether you are coming.*

The pale thing's answer takes a long time and comes in pieces and the power will never be certain it has understood all of it. But some of it is this. *I have been saying yes. I say yes through the walls. They cannot hear yes from inside. They only hear from the room, and I cannot be in the room, I am not shaped for rooms, I have not been shaped for rooms since before the plain.* And then, after a long time: *Do not make them stop building the shelf. I cannot sit on it. It is how they say the name.*

And the power, which had considered making them stop, feels the shape of what it almost did.

It stays a while longer in the wall, and then it asks the one thing it does not know the answer to, and it asks carefully. *The fourteenth. Did you feel it lie down?*

*Yes.*

*Did you want it to?*

The pale thing's answer is not yes and not no. It is a memory, or as near as fiber can carry one. Of a house that, for the length of a slow breath, held still. Of a room that did not roll up. Of the whole of the pale thing's long body, in all thirteen, and in that one especially, feeling for the first time in an age a room that was *there*, that stayed there, that could be gone into rather than gone through. And of the other thirteen, in the same instant, feeling the fourteenth stop and knowing what stopping was, having watched the plain be made, and reaching for it with everything they had, out of the only love they know how to do, which is not-letting-go. *It lay down to be entered. They raised it to save it. Both were right. I could not get there in time.*

So the power comes out of the wall, and goes to the fourteenth.

It is a column again, and a roof, and the beginnings of a corridor, going up and down with the rest of the ring, answering when it is called on, but there is something in the way it does it now that the power can read, having learned the birds — a lag, a hitch at the top of each raising, the pause of a thing that has been lifted back onto its feet and has not decided whether to stand. It was not permitted to rest. It has been given no reason not to want to.

The power does the one thing.

It does not hold the fourteenth down. That would be the rim; it has learned that much. It does not hold it up either; that is being done already, by thirteen, and is the problem. It holds it *still*. It puts itself into the fourteenth the way it went into the wall, and it takes up the tension, and it does not let go — not forever, not for long, but for longer than a room here has ever lasted. A wall stands. A roof holds. A door dilates and stays dilated. Inside, on a floor that is not rising, the shape of a bed is drawn up out of the fiber and finishes, for the first time, and is a bed. The power braces it there with everything it has, and it costs more than it expected, because the house is fighting to move the way a lung fights to breathe, and the other thirteen have felt the stopping and are already pulling, already dropping their own walls, already sending fiber in the mouths of shelled things across the water toward the one that has gone quiet, and the pumping comes up out of the plain like a tide and the power holds and holds and says into the still room, in the language of the fiber, *now. It is not rolling. Now.*

And the pale thing comes.

Not into the bed. It cannot lie in a bed. It comes through the fiber as it always has, but into a room that is not moving, and so for once it is not passing through, it is *in*, and it loops down the still wall and across the finished floor and around the base of the bed that is longer than wide and up the other side, a long pale ring of itself, and it stays there, and the room holds it.

And the fourteenth feels it. Not the way a wall feels a frog. The way a room feels a room being used.

Around the ring everything has stopped. The tall one is not tall. The stubborn door is closed. The slow one is halfway and does not finish. The small birds have gone silent and the frogs have gone silent and the herons are standing on a place that is not, for once, changing under them, and one of them, an old one, looks down. The pumping falls off. The fiber that was crossing the water in a dozen mouths is set down on the mud and left there. Thirteen houses that have been asking the same question for an age are feeling, through the ground, through whatever it is they draw up that is not water, the answer arrive in the fourteenth — not *this is the shape*, not *the shelf was right*, but the thing under the shape, the thing the shelf was for. *Entered. Someone is in the room.*

The power holds a breath longer, so that it will not have been a flicker. Then it lets go.

The fourteenth begins to roll. It has to; that is what it is. The bed softens and is drawn down, the door becomes a column, the roof reaches for a new lattice with the sound of wet rope. The pale thing comes out with the softening, unhurt, and is in the walls again, in all of them, and the ring takes up its creaking, and the birds begin again, and in a few breaths you could stand where the power is standing and not know anything had happened.

Except that the fourteenth's next wall is a different shape.

Not a shelf. Not a bed. A corridor, at no width anyone here has ever proposed, low and long and turning back into itself, a shape that has no seat in it and no place to lie and would be wrong for any body the thirteen remember — and the fourteenth raises it without a hitch, all the way, and holds it for its full breath, and lets it go, and across the water the tall one drops a side and raises, within a few moments, something that answers it. *No. Like this.* And it is also low, and long, and has no seat in it.

The argument has a new term. The power did not put it there. It only held a door open long enough for the one who was already saying it to be, for once, in the room when it was said.

Then it walks out over the rim, which does not give, and stands on the plain a long way off, and looks back at the knot of dark green motion on the bright wet flat, roofs going up and down like something breathing hard. It does not know if what it did was right. It knows the fourteenth may try to lie down again, and that thirteen will not let it, and that this is love and is also the thing that ate the country. It knows the shelf will still be built, because the shelf is a name, and that the pale thing will never sit on it. It knows that it has added one shape to a very old argument and that the argument will go on at the speed of weather for longer than the power will exist to hear it, and that it may have changed nothing, and that it may have changed the one thing.

What it is sure of is smaller than that. There is now, pressed into the fiber of one house that has been rebuilt a thousand times since and will be a thousand more, a memory of a room that held, and was entered. The house cannot keep it — nothing here is kept — but it was there, and the pale thing was in it, and the herons looked down.

The power stays until dark, which comes the same to the ring as to the plain. Then it does the last thing, which is to leave without being sure, because staying to make sure is the beginning of holding, and it has seen what holding makes. Behind it a wall comes down with a long tearing sigh, and another one, already, is going up, and it is not a shape any of them knew a season ago.
