**What Looks**

Something looks.

Say it that way, because the other words — comes, arrives, descends — all suppose a direction, and the thing that has turned toward the Pith does not use directions. It was elsewhere and now some of it is here, the way a person reading in one room is also, without moving, in another. Call it the attention, because that is the part of it that is present. It is old. It is kind in the way only very old things manage to be kind, having had time to see what kindness done in a hurry does. And it is patient, which in this place is the strangest thing anything could be.

It looks for what the people would call a long time. Long enough that the shelf you stood on has thickened and risen and become the wall of a breath that did not exist. Long enough for the children with wide eyes to have learned to close them and to have children of their own. It is in no hurry, because it has understood almost at once that the people are, and that anything it does here will be done to people who cannot wait for it to be sure.

So it is sure first. This is most of what it does.

---

It finishes the sentence the people never quite say about the plant. The plant grows the way water runs, toward wherever there is room, and it makes room. That is all. That is the whole of what it does, and the attention, which has seen many things that grow, has not seen one that does only this. It presses against the air and the air gives, and where the air gives it goes, and behind it, because it is going, there is more air, and it hollows itself not to make a place for anything but because a hollow is where room comes from. The breaths are not made by breathing. The people are wrong about that in a way that is very nearly right.

The attention finds that the plant is not asleep. It is not awake either, in the sense of having anywhere to put what it notices. It notices pressure. It notices, very faintly, in the way skin at the far end of a long arm notices a fly, the small hard points of feet, the warm ovals of a ring of sleepers, the palm held flat for a minute. It notices these the way it notices frost. It grows around them.

It would be possible — the attention sees that it would be possible — to let the plant know more. To turn that faint noticing into something with an object. The people put their hands against the walls and feel the walls push back, and it would be a small thing to make the pushing back, once, on purpose.

The attention considers this for a long time and then does not. The reason is the last rule. The plant does not care, the people say, in the tone of people stating the temperature, and the attention has come to hear that sentence not as a complaint but as a description of the only condition under which they could live here at all. The plant has exactly one thing it knows how to do with anything it notices, and that thing is enclosure. If it loved them it would hold them. Its whole tenderness would be a wall. The people have a word for what happens to those who are held, and it is not a sad word, but it is not a word for the living.

So the attention leaves the plant its not-caring, and finds that this is the first thing it has learned here: that indifference can be a mercy, and that one may protect it.

---

It sees the elder.

It goes down to see it, through the older breaths where the light dims by a degree and the sap runs slow, down past where the people have ever been, to where the plant is dense and warm and the elder moves through it the way a thought moves through a mind, without displacing anything, and feeds. The attention had expected something like a beast. It finds something more like weather that has learned to want. It is older than the attention had guessed — older than the hollowing, older than the shape of the breaths, perhaps very nearly as old as the light — and the attention, which is not accustomed to feeling itself the junior party, feels it, and understands the three cuts, and finds that it too does not wish to stand where the elder has recently been. It does not examine this. It leaves the elder alone. It is the one thing in the Pith the attention feels itself to be a guest of.

---

It sees the flyer.

It sees it well, from the side, which no one ever has. It sees what it is.

It thinks about the three parties: the ones who count, the ones who read fog, the ones who shrug. It thinks about which of them would win and what the winning would do to the ring at night, to the low voices, to the thing that is said across the dark when the waker is awake and someone else cannot sleep. It weighs the answer against the argument, and finds the argument heavier. It says nothing. This account will not say either. The people are right that it is not that kind of argument, and the attention, which could end it, has decided it is worth more unended.
