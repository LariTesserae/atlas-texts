**What the Power Does**

It comes to it from above, which is the only way, even for a thing like that. It could arrive otherwise, of course. But it has learned that the approach is part of the seeing, and so it stands on the blown plateau in the cloud for a while and looks down into the throat of the place and lets its eyes — it has something like eyes — take the minutes they need.

It sees everything you saw. It also sees the rest.

It sees the knapper. Not in the hollow; the knapper is a very long way from the hollow now, in the direction things go when they are done. But the power can look along that direction, and it does, and it sees a person with cold hands sitting on a damp shelf with a stone in one hand and a stone in the other, striking, turning, striking, while newts settle warm-seeking against their thigh and crickets walk over their knuckles. It sees the moment the blade is good enough. It sees the person set it down — not drop it, set it, the way you put down a cup you mean to come back to — and look up at the grey coming in far above, and think about the climb, and decide. It sees them go up into the cloud and it sees what happened to them after, and none of that is any of the hollow's business, and the power does not bring it back down.

It sees the one thing in its hundred thousand skins, breathing. It sees the vacancy on the shelf, shaped like a returning hand. And it sees you, though you are gone now — it sees the shape you made in the silt on the day it was still there, and the newt that found your ribs, and the way you climbed out and looked back and knew you had not been kept.

Then it sits down on the rim, in the wind, and thinks. Because it is wise, it thinks for a long time, and because it is benevolent, it thinks about what it wants to do before it thinks about what it should, so that it will know the difference.

---

It wants to bring heat. That is the first thing, and it is nearly the whole of the heart's answer. The hollow makes no warmth of its own; it borrows; everything down there is cold all the way through and dense as wet clay. The power could stand in the pool and be a sun. It could warm the stone from within so that every seam ran a little less cold, so the newts would not have to lie against a stranger's ribs to feel what a body feels.

And it sees what would happen. Warmth is not a thing the hollow can hold. It borrows and lets go; that is its one motion. Heat given to the hollow would pass through it like a footprint through silt, and the hollow would be exactly as it was, and the only change would be that for an hour something down there had wanted, and been fed, and stopped wanting again — which is precisely the loop the hollow has, against all likelihood, stepped out of. To give it heat would be to give it back appetite for an afternoon. The power has seen appetite. It does not think an afternoon of it is a gift.

It wants to wake the thing. To reach down into the one breath under the drip and say *here, here, you are one and you are many, look what you are.* It wants this because it is lonely in the way that very large things are lonely, and here is another very large thing, and it seems a waste that they should never speak.

And it sees what waking is. A newt is a hunger with legs. Wake the hollow and the beetles on the newts' backs will become food, and the ferns will show the snails' work, and the bats will find there is a dusk after all if you go looking, and the slow fire will start, each body burning the next, and the census will begin to change, and in a thousand years there will be a valley down there with a stream in it and a few frogs calling and most of what is there now will be gone, eaten, into the ordinary churn. That is not evil. That is just the plateau, brought below the cloud-line. But the power has plateaus enough. It has never before seen a finished thing, and it is not sure it has the right to unfinish one to keep itself company.

It wants to take the blade. Just to hold it. It wants to lift the worked stone off the shelf and feel the polish the knapper's palm put there, and the second polish the water put over it, and understand a thing that was made by a hand and then kept by something that has no hands.

And it sees, of course, that the moss would close over the place within a day. That is the whole of what the hollow does: it closes. The shelf is dry because something is on it. Lift the blade and the vacancy goes with it, and the hollow becomes a thing with no mark on it at all, entirely occupied and nowhere kept, and the only living thing in it — the only thing that is *for* something not yet happened — would be gone into the power's pocket. It puts that wanting down carefully and does not pick it up again.

It considers, last, sending the hand back.

It could. It could make a knapper — the same cold fingers, the same way of sitting, near enough that the hollow, which cannot tell one warm stone from another, would take it in and the vacancy would close around it like a mouth around a breath and be *filled*. Waiting, answered. The pause after the question, resolved. It would be the kind of ending stories like. The power has read the stories.

And it sits with this one longest of all, because this is the one that looks most like kindness. It asks itself: is the vacancy suffering? Is the shape of a returning hand, held for longer than there are numbers for, a wound?

It looks very hard. And it decides, with the humility of a thing that knows it may be wrong: no. Waiting is suffering when there is time in it — when each moment that the hand does not come is a moment the waiter must live through. The hollow has no such moments. It has kept the shelf dry not for a long time but for no time, in the way that the pool is knee-deep not for a long time but simply is. The vacancy is not an ache. It is closer to the thing the power itself has felt only rarely, in the instant between deciding to do something good and doing it — an openness that has not yet been spent. The hollow, which was never meant to have anything like that, has one. It stumbled into it. Someone warm sat down and got up and did not come back, and the coldest, most finished thing under the sky was left holding the one posture it could not close.

To fill it would be to spend it. The power does not think it is wise enough to spend another creature's only grace on the grounds that it looks, from outside, like a lack.

---

So it stands up from the rim and it does the hardest thing it knows how to do, which is to let a thing alone with its whole attention. Not neglect. Not passing by. It looks at the hollow the way you would look at a sleeper you loved, for as long as it takes to be sure they are breathing, and it does not touch, and it does not speak, and it does not fix. It says, in whatever way a thing like that says things to itself: *you are finished. To be finished is not a wound. Keep your shelf.*

And then it does the two things it can.

It goes along the direction the knapper went — the far direction, where done things are — and it finds what is left of them, which is not much and not nothing, and it tells them one sentence. It says: *the shelf is still dry.* Nothing more. It does not tell them to go back; there is no back. It just lets them know that the blade is where they set it, and the moss has come up to the edge of it and stopped, and that a thing that keeps nothing kept this. Whether that is a comfort to whatever remains of a person, the power does not know. It thinks it would want to be told, if it were them. So it tells them.

And then it comes to find you.

You will not know it when it happens. You will be somewhere ordinary, in weather, in daylight that actually changes, and you will think of the hollow for no reason — the cold on your ribs, the crickets on your hands, the newt lying throat-to-the-water as if you had been edited out — and you will feel again what you felt climbing out: that you were counted and not kept. And into that feeling, without words, the power will put one small correction. Not *you were kept.* You weren't; it will not lie to you. But this: *the shape of your foot went out of the silt in a day. The shape of your body went out of the newt in a breath. You were let go without regret. That is not the same as not having mattered. For the hours you were down there, you were the only warmth it had.*

It will leave that with you and go.

---

There is one more thing, and the power is not proud of it, and does it anyway.

Before it leaves the plateau it goes down. It goes down the way you did, into the dim, and it sits on the shelf beside the flint — not on the flint, beside it, in the damp where the knapper sat. It does not pick up the blade. It does not strike a flake. It lets the crickets walk over what it has for hands and it lets the newts find what it has for ribs, and it is warm there, in the one place under the sky that will take warmth and let it go and not ask for more, and it stays an afternoon, if there were afternoons. Then it climbs back into the cloud.

Nothing changes. The moss does not grow over the blade. The vacancy does not fill; the power was careful to be a stranger, and the hollow cannot tell a stranger from the one it keeps, and so it keeps on keeping the one it keeps. The count is the same. The pool is knee-deep at the center. Somewhere a snail lets go of a frond and falls into the moss and does not climb back, and is not replaced, and is never fewer.

But the power knows something on the way up that it did not know on the way down, which is that it had wanted, the whole time it sat on the rim reasoning so carefully, simply to be somewhere that would hold it without needing it. And it found that the finished thing had one small dry place where that was possible, and that the place was already taken, and that it was allowed to sit beside it anyway.

That was the gift, and it went the other direction from the one the power came to give. It has noticed that this is often how it goes.
