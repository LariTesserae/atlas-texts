What it would do first is what you did: come across the shield for a long time and not know it had arrived.

This matters. A power that is wise has learned that the first mistake is always the one made before arriving — the decision already carried in the hand. So it comes empty. It walks the streets and puts its ear to the walls and hears the small interior weather. It stands in the square and finds its breath falling into step with the note from the centre and does not, for an hour, ask what the note is. It watches the birds go into walls. It counts the snails. It lets the cloud come up over the edge and fill the streets to the height of its chest and hears the walls settle and something go past in the fog, close, warm under the iron. It does not follow. Not yet.

It stays a year. Then it stays longer than that.

---

Here is what it does not do, and it is worth saying plainly, because each of these is what a lesser kindness would do at once.

It does not break the walls. The hands itch to. One evening's work — the tongue of soft deposit knocked from the lintel, the slot widened to a doorway again, the streets it can no longer use opened back to the width of its shoulders — and the animal is *free*, and the freer feels the warm flush of having freed. But free into what. On to a shield where nothing rises above the knee, in a cold that subtracts, with the sedge gone and the moss thinning and the springs ten thousand years failed. The walls are not what closed the world. They are the shape the world took while it closed, and the being has been inside that shape for so long that the shape is most of what is left. You do not help a thing that has become its own house by taking away the house.

It does not turn the springs back on. This is the larger temptation, and the power holds it in its hand a long time and turns it over. It could be done. Warmth up through the lens of limestone, water breaking through the rind, the pools filling, sedge in a century, marsh in three. The town would go soft and dissolve and run over the edge as it came. The birds would lose their walls; the snails would drown or thrive; the lichen would drown, certainly, all thousand years of it. And the being would have back the thing it lay down in at the beginning — and would be, still, ten thousand years old, and alone, in a marsh, with nothing of its kind and no memory of any of this but the wool it still sheds. There is a benevolence that cannot bear an ending and so unmakes the whole long middle to avoid it. The power has been that once, somewhere else. It does not do it here.

It does not name the being. It does not tell anyone.

---

What it does first is learn the walks.

Every time the cloud comes up, the power is standing where it can hear the note stop. It comes to know the grinding, the tick of the crust breaking, the pace — slower, over the years, and not by much, but slower. It comes to know the route: the same street, always, the one that turns twice and runs straight to the edge, and the places along it where the being's flank brushes the wall and knocks the new deposit off, so that this one street stays open when the others have closed. The being has been keeping its own door. The power notices this and holds it.

And at the edge, in the white, it stands where the being stands and listens to what it listens to.

The sound of water falling a very long way. Day and night, part of the town's chord, so constant that you stopped hearing it before you had finished walking in. But stand there in the cloud with your feet on the last level rock and it is the only sound there is, and it is the sound of the springs. It is where they went. Every drop that sweats out of the walls and rings in the tubes and runs down the streets goes over this edge, and has since the shield first began to fail, and the being that lay in the warm pools when the pools were here comes to the place where the water leaves and stands a long time and listens to it go.

That is all. It is not waiting to be let out. It has never once, in all the cloud-walks the power has counted, put a foot over. It goes to the edge to be near the thing that is leaving, the way you might sit by a river that used to run past your house.

Then it goes back along the same street and lies down.

---

The power goes in.

Not through the slot; a power need not, and the slot is nearly closed, and the tongue of wet deposit should not be touched. It goes in the way warmth goes into a wall. It comes to the inside of the last chamber, where the dark is not the dark of an empty room, and where the drip has been landing on a back for so long that the back and the ceiling have become a single continuous stone with a living thing under it, and it sits down against the flank where the plates of crust are cracked and the cracks are warm.

It puts its hand in.

In ten thousand years the only things that have touched this animal deliberately are birds, taking wool. The snails graze it as they graze a wall. The lichen has colonised its shoulders as it would a rock. The water falls on it as on everything. It has been touched constantly and never once as *itself*, and the power's hand in the warm dark between the plates is the first thing to arrive on it that knows what it is arriving on.

The breath does not change. Then, once, it does — one beat longer, a held place in the note that the whole town carries out to the square and the birds inside the walls hear and do not understand — and then it goes on as before.

The power stays there a long time. It says nothing, because there is nothing that this place has been asked to resolve and it will not be the one to start asking. But it breathes in time, on purpose now, and the drop that falls from the ceiling falls on both of them.

---

Then it does two things, and they are small, and they take a great deal of care.

The first is the door.

The tongue of deposit on the lintel of the last chamber is almost to the floor. Behind it the slot is a hand wide. The being has kept its own street open by walking it, but it cannot keep this one open, because it lies still under the drip for weeks at a time and the drip is patient. In a season, two, the slot will close. And then the being will not go to the edge in the cloud, not because it has stopped wanting to, but because the wanting has been decided for it by water.

The power will not decide for it either way. But it will not let the water decide.

So it keeps the door. Not wider — a hand's width, no more, exactly what is there. Each time the cloud comes and the being rises and goes past, the power lets the tongue grow; and each time the being comes back and lies down, the power takes the night's growth off again, softly, with the flat of something, so that the slot in the morning is the width it was. The being does not know this is being done. It only finds, each time it gets up, that it still fits.

The power does this for as long as the being gets up.

When the cloud comes and the note does not stop — when the fog fills the streets to the height of the walls and nothing goes past in it and the pale crumbs along the channel floor are last month's, and the month before's — the power waits through that cloud, and the next, and the next after, because it has learned the slowness of the place and will not hurry an ending any more than a beginning. Only when it is sure, when the being has lain down of its own weight and stayed, does it stop taking the night's growth from the lintel. It lets the tongue reach the floor. It lets the last hand's width fill with cream-coloured stone.

The being closed its own door by not getting up. The power only made sure that the door was there to be closed.

---

The second thing is the floor.

The being lay down in the warm pools when the springs were here. That is why the first chamber began; that is the whole reason there is a town. It has lain on cold rock for longer than there have been eyes to see it lie there, and the cold has subtracted from it patiently, and the last chamber is the thickest-walled and the darkest and the coldest, because the deposit that shelters it also keeps the sun off.

The power does not turn the springs back on. But under the floor of the last chamber — under the moss, under the smooth rock, in the lens of limestone where the old warmth still moves very far down — it opens one seep. It is small. It would not fill a cup in a day. It carries no more stone than the sweat off the walls does. All it does is come up warm through a crack in the floor the width of a finger and spread under the moss and into the rock the being lies on, so that the floor of the last room is, for the first time since the marsh, a little warmer than the air.

That is all it changes. Not the ending. Only which of its two beginnings the ending resembles.

---

Afterward, the town is quiet in a new way.

The note is gone. The doorways still whistle and the tubes still ring and the water still drips through the honeycomb, but the great slow pressure under everything, the one you breathed with before you knew what it was, is not there, and the square is only a square. The birds nest. The snails graze the film and go down into the fabric of the place. The moss moves upstairs. On the outer face of the last chamber, in a decade or so, there is a faint rust-edged bloom the size of a fingernail, and the lichen begins to tell the time on the one wall in the town it had not yet reached.

The power stays a little longer than it needs to. It stands in the oval in the moss near the square, where something stopped once on its way and lay down for a night or a season, and it notices — it is the kind of thing a power notices — that the moss has finally begun to stand up.

Then it goes to the edge, where the water leaves, and goes the way the water goes.

The shield lies under the sky like the lid of something. It is closed. But it was closed from the inside, by the one it closed over, in its own time and not the water's; and under it there is warmth, for a while yet, in the shape of a room that is the shape of a marsh that is the shape of an animal lying down; and something knew.
