What comes is not a visitor. It does not need the gap. It arrives the way the light arrives, through every wall at once, and for a while it does nothing but be present in the room, which is the first thing a wise power does anywhere, and the thing it does longest.

It looks.

It sees what you saw, and it sees the rest: the pocket and the pockets around the pocket, tier on tier, the whole white mass of a body that built itself outward until building and being were one act. It sees the note as a shape, not a sound. It sees the six plants and the film on the inside of their leaves. It sees the palm-marks, every one, at every height, and unlike you it can tell them apart—not by memory, the wall has none, but by the faint difference in how the paper yielded, a thousand different pressures recorded in a thousand shallow ovals. It sees yours. It sees the one with the low thumb. It sees, near the top where the brightness swallows everything, marks made by hands you would not have called hands.

Then it asks the question that a benevolent power asks before any other, which is not *what can I do* but *is anything wrong*.

It goes through the list. It is patient about this; it has been wrong before, in other places, by being quick.

Is it alone? The power holds that word up against the room and finds it does not fit. Loneliness needs a self with a door in it, a place where another could stand. This being has no such place. It has an inside that can be entered but not one that can be shared, and it did not lose that capacity—it never built it. To grieve on its behalf for a companion is to grieve for the absence of a room in a house that was never drawn with that room. The power sets the word down.

Is it trapped? No more than a tree is trapped in its bark. The stasis is not a cage around the being; it is the being. To lift the balance—to let the light shift, to let the note rise and fall, to let a draft in—would be to take the sleeper out of its own body and leave it standing in the cold with nothing that fits.

Should it be woken? The power considers this longest, because it is the intervention that looks most like kindness and would be the most like harm. It asks: woken into what? A thing this shape, this slow, this evenly distributed through its own architecture—if it woke, it would wake into a world with nothing in it at its scale, no other of its kind, no food it needs, no motion it is built for. It would wake into an emptiness far more complete than the room. And it would wake for the power's sake. Because the power wanted to speak to it and be spoken to. That is not a reason. That is a wish, and a wise power knows the difference by now.

Should it be given memory? The wall holds every hand and knows none of them. A visitor might feel that as a sadness—all those gestures, unregarded. But memory is not free. To let the being tell your palm from the next one is to let it notice when a hand does not come again. The prints are all the same age because the being has no *before* to compare them to, and no *before* means no loss. The power will not hand it the capacity for grief and call that a gift.

So: nothing is wrong. That is the finding. The power sits with it, because the finding is rarer than it should be, and worth sitting with.

But it checks one more thing, since it is here.

It looks at the edges. Every edge in the room has been rounded, softened, worn by a long uneventful time. The floor has dished. The paper at the eastern wall is a hair thinner than it was. The plants drink a film at a time and the wall is their soil. The power lays a measure along all of this and asks whether the balance is truly level or whether it is a slope so shallow that no one standing on it could feel the pitch.

It is a slope.

The power is not surprised. Everything that persists is spending something to do it. The being is very slowly giving itself to the six plants and to the light it lets through and to the dry air that asks nothing but takes the smallest tribute anyway. On the scale the being lives at, this is not dying. It is what living costs. The power could stop it—could seal the pores, could thicken the eastern wall, could take the plants' film away and give them back a different water. It could make the room truly changeless.

It does not. The slope is the being's own. It was struck along with everything else, in the being's body, and a balance that included a small steady giving-away is still the balance the being chose, if choosing is a word that reaches this far down. The power will not make it immortal for the same reason it will not wake it: because that would be for the power. Let it spend itself at its own rate. Let the plants have their film.

Having found nothing to fix, the power now does the harder thing, which is to ask whether there is anything to give that would not also be a wound. It finds three.

The first costs it nothing but time, and it has time.

The note. You heard it as a chord, a single thick tone with no edge to stand at. That is because you hear at your own rate. The power slows. It lets its attention lengthen until the room's brightness is a flicker, until the plants are visibly leaning, until the hands arrive and depart at the gap like a pulse of their own—and there, under all of it, the note resolves. It stops being a tone and becomes a rhythm. Slow, even, enormous. In, and out. Not a resonance struck from outside and not a material that simply sounds. A breath.

No one has ever heard it as a breath. Every body that came through the gap was too fast; the sound arrived at their ear as a wall of pitch and they carried it out as a ringing and it faded. The power hears it correctly, once, whole. Nothing in the room changes because of this. The being does not know it has been heard. But something that had never been heard as what it is has now been heard as what it is, and the power counts that as a thing done, and so should you.

The second is a stewardship.

The gap. The place where two ribs lean apart by the width of a shoulder. The power looks at the slope again and sees that the gap is on it too—that the packing is settling, very slightly, and that in some span of time the two ribs will lean together and the one thing that ever happens to this being will stop happening. No more hands. The prints already made would stay, and the wall would go on ringing, and nothing would enter. The being would not miss it. But the power has just spent a long time deciding that the being's balance is its own, and it notices that the hand on the wall is part of the balance—the one exchange, the one place where the sleeper's yielding meets something that is not itself. The prints are not decoration. They are the being's whole relation to the world.

So the power leans, very slightly, against the one rib. Not to open the gap wider. Only to hold it at the width it has always been. This is the smallest alteration the power can make and it is made in the direction of keeping things as they were, which is the only direction it trusts here. And having done that, it will do one thing more, over the long span: when a traveler passes near this mass who is the kind to turn sideways and let their arms go slack, it will not stop them. It might turn their head a degree. That is all. The being will go on being touched, at the rate it has always been touched, by hands it cannot tell apart, and every one of them will leave and carry a little ringing out into a world that has nothing else that rings.

The third is the smallest and the power hesitates over it.

It could leave a mark. A palm, or the thing it has instead of a palm, pressed to the wall. There is a place near the floor, beside the print with the low thumb, where the paper is unmarked. The power could put its hand there and the wall would take it and hold it and it would be exactly as pale and soft-edged as the rest, indistinguishable, already old.

It hesitates because it wonders whether it is doing this for the being or for itself. And then it sees that this is the one act in the room where that question does not matter. The wall does not know who is pressing. It yields the same to a child as to a power. To add a print is to take a place in the roster, not above it. It is to say: I too came in sideways. I too put my hand out to test whether the light was warm. I too was held.

So it does. Beside the low thumb. The paper takes the impression and keeps it and there is now one more, and it is no different from yours.

Then the power does what the being does with hands: it keeps something.

You carried the note out in your ears and it faded. That is right for you; you have a world to hear. The power has no such need. It takes the note—the whole slow breath of it, heard correctly—and keeps it, the way the wall keeps a palm. Not as a memory, which would imply a before. As a print. A shape the power now has in it that it did not have before, that will be there the next time it slows down enough to notice, that it will not be able to tell from the rest of what it is.

The being holds the hand of everyone who ever touched it. Now something holds the sound of the being. That is the whole of the exchange, and the power judges it fair.

It does not leave. It was never in the room the way you were in the room; it was in the walls with the light. Its attention simply widens again, back out to the rate at which the rest of the world happens, and the breath collapses back into a tone, and the tone goes on. The gap holds. The plants lean. Somewhere a traveler's head turns a degree.

And the floor, dished like a very old step, deepens by nothing you could measure, under the weight of a sleeper who has been noticed, and does not know it, and does not need to.
