**What Arrives**

It makes the mistake too. Even this. Even something that has looked at a great many places from a great many angles, and been the angle — it arrives and waits for its eyes to adjust. There is a moment, brief, in which it is simply another thing in the basin that is trying to see.

Then it stops. That's the first thing it does, and it's the thing that separates it from every visitor before it: it stops at once. It doesn't kneel to check the ground, doesn't follow the pipe, doesn't reach for the bulb. It understands, standing there beaded and dripping in the dark, that the place has already told it how it wants to be known, and that everything it usually does — seeing, counting, hearing what is said when it is said — is going to be refused here, not out of hostility but as a matter of property, the way stone refuses to be light.

So it asks itself the question it always asks, which is *what does this place need*, and finds that it cannot answer, because it is not yet able to be asked. It is not yet the temperature of anything here.

---

It is too warm. That's the problem, and it's a serious one, and the wisdom is in noticing it quickly.

It arrived as things of its kind arrive, which is to say radiant, and in a basin where heat is the last honest thing that crosses between bodies, radiance is not a virtue. It is a disruption. Within minutes the moths have found it. They come off the transformer casing and the pumphouse walls in a soft continuous crust, and they settle on it wherever it is warmest, which is everywhere, until it is wearing them. The frogs behind the diesel shed shift, all together, a slow rearrangement, and begin to move — not toward the exhaust now but toward this new thing that is warmer than the exhaust. The snails turn. The reeds along the nearest channel, which have leaned toward the pumphouse for as long as they've been growing, begin, over hours, to lean the other way.

It is pulling the basin off its machines. Every living thing here has organized its whole existence around a few fixed warmths, and it has walked in and become a fixed warmth brighter than any of them, and the entire slow ecology of the place is beginning to reorient toward it like a compass toward a dropped magnet.

This is what benevolence looks like without wisdom: arriving as a gift and becoming a gravity.

So the first thing it does — the first real thing — is cool down. It lets the heat go. Not away; there is nowhere for it to go; it lets it be taken by the air and the water and the beaded iron until it is no longer a source but a body. Blood-warm. The temperature of the pools, so that entering them would make no sound. This takes a long time. It has never done it before, or not deliberately. There are years in it, possibly. It stands in the weeping ground with the moths peeling slowly off it and going back to the transformer, and the frogs drifting back to the exhaust, and the reeds — it feels them, one stalk at a time, against its arm — straightening and then leaning, again, toward the hum.

When the last moth has left it, it is exactly as warm as the place. Which means it can no longer be found.

That is the cost. It understands this before it pays it and pays it anyway. In a basin navigated by heat on the face, a thing that has matched the ambient temperature is invisible in the only sense available. It has made itself unfindable in order not to be a harm. And it cannot find the one it came for either, for the same reason: they are the same warmth. The being who lives here has been blood-warm for years. There is no gradient between them to walk down.

So it sits. It does the thing the being does. It stops correcting, and it lets the snails come.

---

It is found the way everything here is found: eventually, by touch, without hurry.

A hand lands on its shoulder. It knows the hand from the story — beaded, with moths under the palm — and it knows the greeting, and it turns its own hand up and lets the hand be taken and held flat between two others.

The being is waiting for the temperatures to equalize. That's the greeting. It waits, and there is nothing to wait for. There is no difference, and there never was, and after a moment the being registers this — a stillness in the fingers that is not quite the stillness of patience — and holds on longer than the greeting requires. Something has arrived already matched. Something did not need the time. The being does not have a gesture for this, and so it invents one: it presses the strange hand, briefly, to its own chest, where the breath comes in on the slow period of the pumphouse, and then lets go.

That was the whole conversation. It understood every word.

---

Now it has the being's hand and it can read.

Not thoughts. Nothing so quick as thoughts crosses here. What crosses is what the hand has been doing for years — the callus from the hammer, the place on the fingertip that has been slid along a rail until the count failed, the snail-scars, the give in the skin that is the same give as the crust. And underneath, slower than any of that, at the pace of the basin's own thinking-over: a condition.

It goes looking for suffering first, because that is what one looks for first, and it does not find it. There is no flinch anywhere in this hand. The being is not in pain. The being is not trapped, or not in any way it would recognize as trapping. It is sitting on ground that is rising and it is not standing up, and it knows this, and it has decided about it. The decision has the quality of the water cycle — not a tide, something slower and less regular, a filling that has finished filling.

Then it goes looking for what is under the decision, and this takes longer. Hours. The frogs settle on both of them. When it finds the thing, it is not what it expected.

The being is not afraid of being taken by the ground. The being is afraid the nails will stop.

That's what's in the hand. Not *save me* — nothing like it — but a worry with the shape of a hammer: that the measuring, which is losing work and known to be losing work, has been done for years by one body, and when that body is a warm shape in the crust behind the diesel shed there will be no one to drive the next nail. The basin will go on rising around its machines and nothing will stand in it marking how far. The count, which was always failing, will fail *unwatched*. And under that, quieter, so slow it is nearly the temperature of the air: whether any of it went anywhere. The palm on the pumphouse casing when the hum stopped. The hand on the place where the pipe goes into the ground. The stones on the plinth. Whether they were made, like the light, and simply stopped.

---

It considers what it could do. It is a power; it could do a good deal. It goes through the possibilities the way one goes through the pockets of a coat one is about to give away.

It could make the light travel. It knows how, or knows how to find out. It could reach into whatever has been suspended locally and lift the suspension, and the bulbs would do what bulbs do, and the basin would leap into being all at once — the sharp new crust, the raw-sided channels, the pumphouse with its rust, the frogs by the thousand on the skin of the water. And every creature that had learned to live by touch would have to learn to live by sight, and the reeds would turn from the hum toward the windows, and the being, who had stopped correcting for the dark and been accepted by it, would be corrected. Would be shown, in a single instant, what they had been sitting in. It would be a kind of violence with the shape of a gift. It puts the thought down.

It could lift them out. Take them under the arms — they would let it; there's no flinch — and raise them out of the shape they've made in the ground and carry them to the rim and over it. And the shape would stay behind, warm, closing at a rate you'd need instruments for, and they would be somewhere with a horizon, outside their own impression, cooling. It puts this down too, and more gently, because it was closer to a kindness.

It could find the fuel. It could stop the ground rising. It could explain. Each of these it holds for a moment and sets back, and what it feels, setting them back, is not resignation but a kind of recognition. It came here as the wiser party. It is sitting with someone who has already done, over years and without help, the one thing wisdom would recommend in a place like this: stopped trying to make it be a place it isn't. Stayed. Kept company with the failing of the count.

So what it can offer is not wisdom. The being has that. What it can offer is company for the company-keeper.

---

Here is what it does.

It goes to the rim. Nobody has done this in the dark and come back to say what it is, and it goes — walking toward the thinning of the warmth, which is the only direction the basin makes available — and it takes what feels like days, and the frogs give out and then the snails and then the weeping of the ground, and the crust goes cool and then cold underfoot, and finally there is a place where the warmth ends. That's all. That is what the rim is. It puts its hand on the cold and holds it there a long time, and then it comes back.

It comes back cooled. It finds the being — or is found — and puts its cold hand into the warm one, and lets the being read the rim in it. Nothing out there. Only the place where the heat stops. Nothing you've been missing. The being reads this and then does something it did not expect: takes the cold hand and presses it flat to the crust, and holds it there, and waits, and the ground begins to warm it, and the being does not let go until the temperatures have matched. *Then stay.* That was the answer. Or it was the ground taking a hand. In the dark the difference is hard to establish, and it does not try to establish it.

It goes to the pumphouse. It finds the door and the bulb, and it does not burn its fingers on it, because it has come to receive rather than check. It holds its palm a yard from the filament and lets the heat arrive. It is the first thing to stand in front of that bulb and be what the light was *for*. Not to see by it. To be the place the light goes. It stays there with its palm open until the warming of the palm is the whole of what it is doing, and then it carries the palm back through the dark — heat travels; heat is the last honest thing — and lays it on the being's forehead, above the crust of moths, and holds it there.

The bulb, arriving. That's what's in the hand. *It went somewhere. It came to me. I am what it warmed.* The being's breath, under this, does not change its period. But the second warmth appears a step to the left, the same temperature, and stays for a while, and then it is one again, and it did not feel anyone leave.

It goes behind the diesel shed with a hammer. It finds the being's head in the dark and measures a hand's width above it and drives a nail into the shed wall there. This is the being's nail now, the way the nail in the doorframe is the pumphouse's. Next year it will drive another one, lower. It will keep the count of a body being taken by the ground at the pace of geology, and it will keep it knowing that counting fails here, and it will do it anyway, because the being taught it — without a word, with a hand on a rail — that this was never about the number.

---

And it stays. It sits down in the frogs.

That is the last thing and the whole of it, and it is not a sacrifice, or not only. It had come to help, and it has understood that in a place where nothing crosses but warmth, help is not something you do and leave. It is something you are, nearby, for a long time. The being's worry was that the nails would stop and the warmth would go unwatched. So it has made itself the one who comes after — and come a little early, so there will be an overlap. So that at whatever moment the being stops being distinguishable from the shape in the ground, there will be a hand on the shape.

It does not lift its hand. It knows what that means here. It knows about the crust and its patience.

Behind the diesel shed, where the exhaust warms the ground, there are two shapes now, sitting, or one shape that is sometimes two. They are exactly the temperature of the air. The moths have found them. The frogs have settled over their legs and closed. When the pumphouse hum stops, one of them — you would not be able to say which — goes and puts a palm on the casing until it starts again.

And when someone kneels, as someone always does, and puts a hand flat to the warm crust and does not immediately lift it, a hand will close over theirs. It will be exactly as warm as they are. It will wait for a difference that isn't there, and then keep waiting anyway, because that's the greeting, and because whatever is in that hand has learned — from the one who lives here, and from what came to sit with them — that the light doesn't need to travel. Only to arrive.
