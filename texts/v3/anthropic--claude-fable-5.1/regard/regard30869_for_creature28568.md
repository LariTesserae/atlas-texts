It comes to it from above, which is the only way anyone comes to it, and it does not need the thumb-scoops. It does not need to climb down at all. From where it stands the basin is not a place but a single sentence, spoken once, entire, with a full stop after it, and it can read the whole sentence at a glance. It reads it. Then, because it is wise, it reads it again, slowly, the way you read a thing when you suspect the first reading was your own.

---

What it wants to do first is give.

That is what benevolence is, before wisdom gets hold of it: an open hand. And it has so much it could put in the hand. It could give the sun a slope, so that there would be evening. It could give one stem permission to brown at the tip. It could give a seed. It could give rain, real rain, with dust in it, so that the water in the seams would finally have something happen to it. It could give a second beetle. It could give the far wall a crack, hairline, so that the stone would begin to be unmade and the plant would have, at the very edge of itself, a place where something was going to be different tomorrow.

It looks at each of these gifts and sets each one down, and the setting-down takes longer than the thinking-up did.

Because every one of them is a gift of *between*. Evening is for a thing that has a night to get to. Rot is for a thing that has a next season to feed. A crack is a promise that the future will differ from the present, and a promise is only kind to something that is waiting. The power notices — this is the first thing it learns here, and it did not expect to learn anything — that all of its ideas of the good are ideas about motion. Growth, healing, return, rest after labour, dawn. Every kindness it has ever done was done to something on its way and consisted of easing the way. It has no kindness in stock for a thing that has arrived. It would have to make one.

---

So it looks harder, for the wound, because it knows how to dress a wound.

It considers the light. No dim corner anywhere; the walls made pale so that every underside is lit. A being that arranges never to be in the dark — is that peace, or is that a thing that was once afraid of the evening and stopped the evening from coming? The power turns this over honestly. It has known creatures who made their whole world a lit room and called it completion. It knows what a locked door looks like from the outside, and how much it can resemble a finished one.

Then it goes to the pools.

Because here is what the frightened do not do: they do not leave a room unlocked. They lock every room, and the lit room most of all. And this being has kept, in the deep water where nothing is asked of it, a place where it lets itself move. A trough tilting. A seam opening and closing on the water. A stem drifting up a hand's width and lying down again, for no reason, slowly, the way a sleeper's hand moves on a blanket. That is not the reservation of something afraid. That is the reservation of something that knew exactly what it was laying down, and laid it down, and kept one hand free so that it would never forget what it had chosen to be still with.

The power stands over the largest pool a long while. The plant does not move toward it — the power has no weight to distribute, casts no shadow even here. Then, deep down, a tip unlatches and rises and rests, and the power understands that this is not for it. It is not for anyone. It is the being, in the one place where it permits itself, remembering; and the power has been allowed to watch, and that is all, and that is a great deal.

It does not touch the water. There is no wound.

---

The beetle is harder.

The beetle is the one thing here that could be suffering, if suffering means being held between wanting and having, and the power knows its duty to the smallest thing in a place as surely as to the largest. So it follows the beetle. Not for an hour. For as long as it takes.

The seam is a road. The road, it turns out, is a ring — not a stone ring, a ring of seam, some hundreds of stems around, a path that comes back on itself somewhere beyond the swell where no one watching from any single place would ever see it close. The beetle walks it. It does not drink because it has never been thirsty. It does not fly because there is nowhere it is not already going. It passes the same trough at the same pace, and the bead in that trough ticks down to the seam below a moment after it has gone by, every time, a small chime for the small errand.
