**What the power does**

It hears it first, as anyone would, but it hears it whole. Every seam pressing, every joint cracking, every pod letting go, the slow amber pulse in every stem — all of it at once, the way you might hear a single note. And it listens for a long time, because it is wise, and because it wants to be sure. Then it is sure. There is no pattern. The visitor was right. It is not a music that has not yet been understood; it is a sound with no under-shape, a roar that has never once repeated itself.

This is the first thing the power decides to leave alone. It could find a rhythm in the noise — powers can find anything — and the finding would put it there. A rhythm is a form. The valley has never had one. It does not give it one.

---

Then it considers, and it considers slowly, and it begins where benevolence always begins, with the question: who here is harmed?

Not the plant. The plant has no lack. It is not aware and is not unaware; it has no inside for either state to occupy. You can alter a thing like that. You cannot help it, because help is what you give to a want, and there is no want here — only the one motion, up and out and toward. A kindness done to the plant would be a kindness done to a fire: a change in what it burns, mistaken for a gift.

Not the animals. The visitor understood this before leaving. They do not die. They stop being separate, with their legs folded under and their seam shut, and the grain closes over them, and the only death the valley has is the one they were made out of in the first place. To spare them that would be to take them out of the only thing they are.

Not the families. The power watches them a while — the amber hands, the cord tied at head-height on the way in, the daughter lowering her voice on the pan. They are not captives. They have a name for the thing that is a kind of love, and rules that are a kind of respect, and they leave when the floor rises more than an arm's length. Their hands click on stone and they have chosen that. The power does not tell them anything. They already know how to wait.

The man.

The power lingers on the man.

---

Before it decides what to do about the man it walks through everything else it might do and sets each thing down.

It could give the plant water. It would drown. Everything here that is wet is already over.

It could give it awareness — could reach in and open a place for the plant to know itself from. This is the thing that looks most like generosity and is the cruellest thing available. To be unfinished and not know it is the plant's whole condition. To be unfinished and know it is the human one, and look what that did to the man on the stem. The power does not wake it.

It could finish it. Decide for the plant what it will be and let it be that. But nothing here has ever been in its final form, and the visitor already named what finishing is in this valley. It is the taking. The power does not kill it by completing it.

It could stop it — hold the roof, keep the dead country dead, protect the pan from what is rising under it. Protect it for whom? Nothing lives there. The pan is only the plant's old skins in the sun. A hundred thousand years of shed surface do not need defending from the thing that shed them.

It could give the valley a moment of silence. One second. Every mouth stopped, every wing folded, every pod holding. And the power, having listened to the whole sound entire, understands what one second of silence would be: a pause, and a pause is a shape, and a shape is the beginning of a form. The roar would have a before and an after. The valley would have, for the first time, a history that could be told. It does not do this.

And it could look into the folds. It could know what heaves under the far ridge — whether it is the plant, or something in the plant, or the boundary failing. It could settle that. It would be the easiest thing it does all day.

It does not look. This is the second thing it leaves alone, and the harder one, because the not-knowing is not for the plant's sake — the plant does not care what it is — but for the valley's. The valley is the place where that question has no answer. The power would rather there be such a place than know.

---

So: the man.

It reads the scores. Forty-three, close together, cut at a sitting height with a stone edge that the power can see lying two metres away under a bump that used to be a bowl. The last several are shallow. The last one is barely a scratch.

The valley preserves surfaces and not reasons, and the power can have the reason if it wants it. It considers whether wanting it is a kind of taking, and decides it is not, because the man is already taken and the reason is the one thing of his the valley did not keep. So it looks.

It sees that the slot was open on the ninth day. It sees that he knew this. It sees the rest, which it does not tell anyone, because the man did not tell anyone, and it is his.

Then it does one thing that is not a leaving-alone.

It is not bound by when. It goes back to the first score, and it sits down on the stem beside him, on the side away from the bowl, with its back to the black bark and its feet out in front of it the way his are. It does not speak; he could not hear it over the roar and would not be able to hear it under one either. It does not touch him; there is nothing in it he could feel. It does not make the slot wider or the floor lower or the heat less. It does not do anything that would change what he does.

It sits there for forty-three days. It hears the roar with him, all of it, entire, and it hears the ninth day go past and does not turn its head. When the pale ones come to eat his hands it does not stop them. When he stops cutting the scores deep it does not remind him. When he makes the last scratch — a small dry sound under a sound so large it does not register — the power is there and hears it made, and so that mark, alone of everything in the valley, was witnessed at the moment of its making by something that understood what it was for.

That is all. It is very little. It is not a rescue. The grain still closes over him and the ridge still heals across his shins and the families who come after still take him for a fold in the stem. But there is, now, somewhere that is not the valley, a held thing: forty-three days, a reason, and the fact that he was not by himself. The valley does not know this. It does not need to.

---

The third thing the power does is the only one that takes any time, and it takes all of it.

It climbs out — or it is out; it does not need the slot — and it stands on the pan. Flat mineral, cracked, bone-coloured, running to the horizon under a sky with nothing in it. No wind. It puts a hand flat on the ground and the ground is warm and there is a crazing of fine cracks around the fingers.

The plant is coming up. It has been coming up for as long as there has been anything to notice and it is coming up toward nothing — toward a white sky, a dead pan, an absence. The one motion, up and out and *toward*, with no object at the far end of the *toward*. It does not know this and could not mind it. But the power, which can mind things on behalf of what cannot, finds that this is the part it cannot leave alone. Not the noise. Not the unfinishedness. The fact that when it finally breaks through — thins the roof to nothing and comes up into the light entire, seething and gold and still not decided — there will be no one there.

The families have a name for it that is a kind of expectation. *The one who is coming up.* You do not give that name to a hazard. You give it to something on the road, and to name it that way is already to be standing at the end of the road, however far off, however uncertain of the day. The power notices that it has been given, by a cutter's daughter on a dead pan, the only instruction it is going to take.

So it stays. It does not hurry the roof. It does not thin it or hold it. It sits on the warm ground with its hand flat and it waits, on the plant's clock, which is no clock at all, and it becomes the object of the *toward*. Not by doing anything to the plant. Only by being there. The way a shore is a gift to a river that does not know it is a river.

It has all the time. So does the plant. The pan is very slightly domed under its palm, and the cracks are a little wider than they were, and somewhere a few metres down a shelf that was a shoot the day before yesterday is beginning to split along its crazing, and a shearer is going along a stem at walking pace toward the folds, and a pale thing is standing up out of a wall with its mouth already working.

And when it comes up — whenever that is, whatever it is by then — it will come up into the light and find that it was met. It will not know this either. That is all right. Someone will.
