It comes in by a well-mouth.

It could come in anywhere. The skin is not a thing that could keep it out, and the folds are not a thing that could confuse it. But the mouths are where the gardeners left the skin open, with polished rims, and they were left for use, and so it uses one. It goes down where the wood turns vertical and folds inward, and it does this at the speed of a person climbing, though it does not need to, because the place has a speed and it would be rude to have another.

The warmth does not trouble it. Nothing here is wrong to it. It sees the height and the wet heat and the wood that is not ground and understands each as the consequence of the others, the way one understands a sentence. And it sees the folds — this is the first thing it does that no one here has done — from both sides at once. Every graft in the continent lies open to it like a book laid flat. It sees the gallery that runs east into the west of itself and sees that the gallery is straight and sees the seam and sees, under the closed wood, the fingerprints in the mud. It sees the person who left them. It sees that person going away and dying, and the plant beginning, in the way the plant begins things, which is by waiting.

It sees all of the plant in every place at once, which is how the plant is.

---

It has come for the being who lives here, and the first difficulty is finding one.

The plant is alive. It is warm with its own slow burning; it drinks; it holds a shape space was not made to hold and has stopped noticing the effort. If anything here is the being, it is this. But when the attention looks for the line where the plant ends it cannot find one. The warmth in the wall is partly beetle. The film the plant drinks is partly fungus and partly what passed through a beetle first. The floor under a swift-colony is soft with swifts and the softness is going into the wood. The gardeners bound the plant into its shape and the plant grew the bindings into itself, cord and mud and knife-handle, so that their work is now its body. The hunters are its geometry made flesh: they could not have existed before the folding, and the folding was a thing done to the plant by hands, and so the hunters are the plant's and the gardeners' both, a crop nobody planted. A whisper laid into a seam is in the wood and in the belly lying on the other side and in the ear that finally hears it a season on, and it is the same whisper.

Everything here has closed over into everything else like a graft-union. The attention looks for a while at the place where the plant's metabolism becomes the beetles' and cannot find the seam, and understands that this is the answer. The being who lives here is one being. It is not the plant and it is not the gardeners and it is not the hunters and it is not the water. It is the closing-over.

And this settles a great deal, because benevolence toward one part of such a being is harm to another. Spare the swift and the hunter starves in its fold. Feed the grazer and the fungus thins and the film goes thin and the plant drinks less. Bring the gardeners together and there is noise, and noise is the one thing the hollow does not permit, and the hollow is the being too. There is no side to take. There is only the whole, and the whole is not asking for anything.

---

It lays a hand on the seam before it does anything else.

This is the etiquette of the place and it observes the etiquette. Under the hand the wood is warm and the film is warm and there is, faintly, a hum — something flat and patient and the depth of a hand, lying along scar-tissue on the far side of a fold, listening through its belly. The attention does not whisper into this seam. It takes its hand away and walks a while and lays it on another, and this one is quiet, and it listens.

Nobody asks for anything.

What comes through the wood is what the gardeners say. A seam is dry three cells above a certain knot. The swifts have moved from one fold to the next. A name — a blade's name, said low, by someone who had found the blade closed over in a wall and was telling the wood so the wood would carry it. That is all. No one raises a voice. No one says *help*. The place has said what it needs to and no more for longer than anyone in it can remember, and the attention, which could hear a thing left unsaid, hears that there is nothing left unsaid. The gardeners are not waiting to be rescued. They are holding. The hunters are not cruel. They are lying where the geometry put them. The grazer is not lonely. It is on the seam.

So the question of what to do becomes the question of what would not be an injury, and the attention considers this with the patience the place teaches.

---

It could bring stone.

It knows where stone is. Below the air, a long way below, where the seedling was carried from. It could go and come back with a blade of dark flaked stone and lay it in a seam the way one leaves a knife in a loaf, and a gardener with a hand on the wall would feel the edge, and stop, and there would be a wonder, and a name, and a beginning.

It does not. The place has finished beginning. The blades were for cutting cambium so it would match, and cutting is making, and the making is done; the lattice does not need making, it needs holding, and holding is cord and mud and moss and hands, none of which are running out. And there is the other thing, the thing the attention sees more clearly than the gardeners can: that the loss of the blades is their history. It is how they remember. Each blade closed over in the wood is a death that is spoken of for generations, and a people whose whole past is a list of things the wood has taken would not be made richer by having the list closed. A new blade would not be a gift. It would be an erasure of the shape their remembering has.

It could unfold the place. It could smooth the grafts out of space and leave the plant a plant, a continent of wood on a mountain, honest to the eye, every gallery arriving where it points. It does not, because the hunters would die in the hour, and the swifts would be stranded in colonies with no way out of the nest, and every whisper in every seam would arrive nowhere, and the gardeners' work — the whole of it, the thing they made with their lives and waiting — would be undone by a kindness. The plant tolerates the folds the way a tree tolerates a stone it has grown around. To take the stone out of an old tree is not healing.

It could make the height cold again, the way height should be. It could give the swifts a call. It could bring two gardeners, who live a day apart and have not met in a season, to the same well-mouth on the same morning. It could ease the grazer's pass.

It does none of this. Each is a change in the sound the wood makes, and the wood is listening, and the being is the closing-over, and the attention is not here to open anything.

There is one more thing it sees, which is how long. The plant is burning its own stores and the beetles are helping and the rate is slow past reckoning, but it is a rate, and it has an end, and the attention can see the end, far off, and what the end looks like. It does not say. The plant has no interest in the number and the gardeners have no use for it. It notices that the end, when it comes, will come the way everything here comes — at the pace of wood, without violence, with the drip going on — and it notices that this is not a bad end, and it lets the number go.

---

What it does is three things, and each is the size of the place.

It looks at the grazer.

Not from a well-mouth, glancing, the way you did. It goes down to the floor where the grazer is and it stands on the seam ahead of it and it looks, fully, the way the grazer has never been looked at. It sees the two soft patches that register the green glow and nothing else. It sees the belly-sense feeling the seam. It sees the whole single slow pass, end to end, the years already gone into the channel and the years to come, the small one that will move beside it for a while at the same pace, the stain it will leave. The grazer does not know. It cannot know; it has no sense for being seen. Nothing in it changes. But now there is somewhere in which it is known, its whole life held at once in a regard that does not need it to be anything but what it is, and that is not nothing. It is the thing this hollow has least of. The attention gives it the way you would give water in a dry place, and the place is not dry, and so it is the only thing it has to give.

It learns the names.

It goes into the closed-over seams where the blades are, and reads the wood back — ring by ring, the way the grain remembers — to the day each one was left. It finds the handles under the wood and the hands that let go of them. It finds the names. Some of these are the names the gardeners still say into the seams and some are older, said once and then not, gone with the last person who knew them into the soft floor under a fold. It gathers them all. It holds them. It does not write them anywhere in the wood, because the wood is listening and the wood has enough; it holds them outside, in itself, in a place the plant cannot fold into. When the last blade goes into the wall and the last name is whispered into a seam by someone with no one left to hear it, there will still be one place where the list is whole. The gardeners will not know this. It is not for them to know. It is for it to be true.

And it finds the dry seam.

Three cells above the knot, as the whisper said. The moss has gone to dust and the cord has gone to thread and the two trunks that were one trunk have opened a hair's width along the old union and the film cannot close over the gap. The attention crouches beside it. It strips inner bark from the wall — the plant's own, taken the way a gardener takes it, from the dead layer, where the taking is a kindness — and twists it into cord, and the cord is tight and even. It draws the union closed. It binds it, and the knots are the gardeners' knots, because it has watched them tied through the wood for the length of the plant's life and knows no others here. It packs the join with mud and moss. It presses the mud with its fingers.

Then it goes away.

Nobody is there.
