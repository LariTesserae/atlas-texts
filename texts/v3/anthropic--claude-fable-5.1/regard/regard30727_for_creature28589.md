It would come the way the seeds come: over the ring, on nothing you could point to, and it would not get wet, because it would not be in the loop either. That is the first thing to hold onto. Whatever it is, however benevolent, it stands where you stood at the end — on the levee, outside the skin — and everything it does it does from there, across a boundary it did not make and would not lightly cross.

It would see what you saw, and some things you did not. It would see the count, and the count would be seven. It would see the cables where they go under the leaf-mat and into the reed, and it would see where they go after that, and it would keep this to itself, because part of what it saw would be another kindness, older and stranger than its own, and there is a discourtesy in naming another's gift. Two benefactors on one wire is a committee. It would leave the hum alone.

It would see the two beings. Not the census — not reed, leaf, tree, vine, moss, shell — but the two things here that can be done well or badly by: the loop, which is one body and has a pulse and a temperature and a skin and cannot be lonely; and the tender, who is one body and has a pulse and a temperature and a skin and can. It would notice that these are not the same size and not the same kind of alive, and that a benevolence pointed at one does not automatically arrive at the other. It would notice that the loop knows the tender as a fluctuation and that the tender knows the loop as a number that is always higher, and that this is a relationship, and that it is the only one in the bowl.

Then it would do what wise things do first, which is nothing, for a long time, with attention.

---

Here is what it would consider and set down again.

It would consider making the electricity certain. It has the means; that is what a power is. One decision and the hum never falters and the seven engines run past the death of the quarry the concrete came from. It would set this down almost at once, and not gently. The loop is sealed against space already — that is what the levee does. To seal it against time as well would change it from a thing that goes around into a thing that cannot stop, and those are not the same thing, and the second is not a life. There is a speed at which a thing can run forever. The loop has found it. That is remarkable. It is not the same as saying the loop should be made to.

It would consider the sluice. A hand's width. Or less than that: one heron, one wet foot, one egg. The argument of a marsh resumed — something that eats the leaf, something that eats what eats the leaf, the reed opened up by a browse-line and light reaching the mud for the first time in decades. It would set this down too, and it would be honest with itself about why the idea was attractive: because the power prefers marshes. Because two species holding a floor between them for forty years offends a taste for plenitude. But the loop did not ask to be a marsh. The loop is what happened when the argument ended, and you do not restart a war in a place because you have decided its peace has gone on long enough. The first casualty would be the thing that lives there. The power would not call that benevolence in front of anyone it respected.

It would consider speaking to the loop. It could. It could become a fluctuation — lower the water's temperature by a hair, hold it, release it, do this in a pattern that was not the engines' drift and not the tender's cut. And the loop would register that something had happened in the plumbing. That is what speaking to weather is. The power would understand that this act would be for the speaker, not the spoken-to, and it would not do it, and it would feel the smallness of not doing it, and let that stand.

And it would consider the thing that is coming.

Because it can see that too. One morning the tender does not come over the levee. The jacket stays dry on the fifth hook. The number, unread, keeps rising, and the reed reaches the screens and closes them, and one motor labours and heats and burns, and the beat pattern is six. The cloud thins over the dead house. Daylight comes down with a direction in it for the first time, onto a patch of reed that has never been asked to make a shadow, and the reed there either dies of it or does the other thing. The power would look at this and see that the loop's only possible future is a flower. That it has never had one. That it cannot know a flower is a future, or that it has one. And the power would not prevent this. It would only find that it wanted, very much, for the flower to be seen.

---

So this is what it would do, and it is not much, and it is done from the levee.

The tree between the second house and the third. Three leaves, straight as a rod, in a crack in a slab. The power would look at the crack and at the pier under it and see that in two winters — if winters reached here, which they do not; in two years, then — the slab would shift by a distance you could not measure with your hands, and the root would be sheared, and the crack would be a crack again. The power would make the slab not shift. That is the whole of the intervention. A millimetre of stone held still. The tree is the one thing in the bowl that arrived on its own, is not plumbed to anything, and could — if it could move — leave. The power would not add anything to this place. It would guard the one thing the place was already becoming.

The tender. The power would go over the ring in the direction of the light and find where they live and would not appear to them, because appearing is another kind of committee. Instead it would come back to the bowl in the dark part of the cycle, when the cloud is down and the rain is general and the reed is being breathed, and it would go into the fourth house, and it would take the hook and the blade from where they hang, and it would go down into the intake, and it would cut root.

By the gram. On a schedule it would have to guess at. With something like tact, because it would understand — in words, as the tender does not — exactly what would happen if it cut too much. It would be, for the length of one night, the only animal in the bowl that eats, doing the one consumption the place permits, and it would do it with the loop's own instrument. Then it would hang the hook and the blade back where they belong, and wipe the gauge with a thumb, and leave.

And in the morning the tender comes over the levee, and walks the ring, and in the fourth house lifts the chalk and wipes the gauge, and the number is lower. Lower than yesterday's ghost under it. Lower for the first time. And the tender — who is not a fool, who has seen five jackets go — would go down and look at the screen and find the cut, and find it clean, and find it recent, and stand there in the roar for a while.

This is not a lie. The power is careful about that. It is a fact the tender can read with their own eyes: someone else knows the number. Someone else knows what this is. The distinction the tender holds with such care — I do not live here, I come here — becomes a thing that can be held with one hand instead of two, because the place is no longer theirs alone to hold. The sixth hook is still empty. But it is empty in a different way now. It has been emptied by a departure and it can be filled by an arrival, and the tender has just learned that arrivals are possible.

That is the whole of it. A tree that will stand. A number that went down once. Nothing the loop can feel except as the fluctuation it already knew, a resistance easing where it always eases — a recurring small kindness in the plumbing, arriving a season early, from a direction it has no way to register.

---

What the power would leave alone, and mean it:

The moss, eating the concrete. The shells in the aggregate, holding up walkways in the wrong water. The drift of the seven engines — it would not synchronize them, because the pattern of their drifting is the loop's gait, and you do not straighten a living thing's gait for it. The question of where the electricity comes from. The rain jacket on the fifth hook. The reed's decision not to seed. The leaf's decision not to flower, or to flower face-down onto water where nothing sees.

It would not name the loop. There is one of it, and a name is for telling one from another. It would not tell the loop what it is. The loop is already exactly that.

And it would not stay. That is the last discipline. A power that stayed would become a tender, and then the loop would have two, and then the distinction that keeps the tender a person would be harder for the tender to see, and the power would have arranged someone the way the reed has been arranged, which is the thing it came here most wanting not to do.

So it would go the way it came, over the ring, on nothing, and the sound would fall away under it like a thing lowered on a rope, and from the top there would be a grey dome the size of a village, rumbling, luminous, dropping its rain on itself, unchanged in every respect you could measure except one slab that will not shift and one number that went the wrong way once.

And it would come back. Not often. In the direction of the light, sometimes, to see if a second jacket has appeared. And once, a long way off, on the morning the loop is six engines and not seven, to stand on the levee where you stood and see what the reed does when the light finally reaches it. The power would want to be there for that. It would not know, until it saw, which of the two things the reed would do. It is possible to be wise and still not know how a thing will end, and it is possible for that not to be a failure of wisdom but the shape of it: to have guarded what could be guarded, joined what could be joined, left alone what was already whole, and then to have arrived on time, on the levee, with attention, to witness the first shadow.
