The power arrives the way the wind does, from no direction, and for a long time it does nothing but attend.

This is not indecision. It is the first act of wisdom in a place like this: to understand that it does not yet know what it is looking at. It has been told there is a being here. It expected, as one does, to find a single life — a person, a creature, a mind in the emptiness — and to find that life in want of something, and to consider what might be given. Instead it stands on the mat with the wind coming round to find it and the stone ringing under its feet, and it cannot say, honestly, which being is meant.

There is the rock, which is warm and finished and which the people say is asleep. There is the mat, which may be one individual and which is the only thing here doing anything. There is a shape a day's walk east of a settlement that has shifted a few yards in a lifetime, in a direction the wind does not favour. There are a few hundred deaf people in the warm dark with their hands in the small still space between them. There are grey things in the cloud who know the whole organ by heart. Any of these might be what it was sent to. All of them might be. The place does not divide cleanly into a world and someone living in it; it is arranged so that each thing is partly the dwelling of the next.

So the power does what the flat things do, and what the people do, and what the grey ones do, which is to stop trusting the air and put itself into the stone. It goes barefoot. It walks.

---

It walks for what would be, for the people, a lifetime. It learns the plateau the way they learn it — a shaft at a time, a note at a time, the pressure in the bones changing under the feet — until it can cross the whole of it in cloud and know where it is by the hum. It learns *sternum* and *the back of the neck* and *everywhere*. It learns the notes of the abandoned shafts too, the ones with the rings half swallowed, and finds that those notes are no different from the others; the stone does not remember who was in it. It learns the shaft mouths so small nobody has named them, wrist-wide, each with its thin high whistle. In the end it holds the entire organ in itself, every pipe, the way the grey ones do, and it understands what nobody on the plateau can know because nobody on the plateau can hear it: that the sound is not, after all, without music. Not a chord. Not a melody. But a place — a place with a sound that is its own, unrepeatable anywhere in the world, and so vast and so continuous that it would take a life to hear it once.

Then it goes down.

It chooses a shaft nobody lives in and lowers itself past the depth where the wind loses interest, past the depth where the roar overhead becomes a waterfall, past the depth where any person has gone, and keeps going. The walls are smooth as the inside of a shell. The note of the shaft, which from above is one tone, opens as it descends into a nest of tones, the shaft ringing differently at every depth, and the warmth thickens, and the drip on the walls becomes a film and then a sheen. It smells the sulphur the people smell when a shaft breathes. And a long way down — further than the cord of any group would reach, further than any body lowered from a platform would be thought to go — it comes to the bottom, and the bottom is green.

Not the green of the surface. Paler, softer, a green that has never seen the sky, but the same runners, the same felt, rooted in the warm wet dark on a floor of dark stone that is warmer still. Every shaft the power descends is the same. The dead go down wrapped in mat, and the mat is alive, and it roots where it lands, and at the bottom of the plateau, out of every light, there is a garden nobody planted and nobody has seen. The grey ones roost above it on ledges the erosion left, folded, silent, their heads narrow and their ears open, and they do not stir when the power passes; they have no reason to fear anything that comes down a shaft, since only they and the dead do. There are fruits in the deep mat, sour, small, no different from the fruits above. When a shaft breathes out — and standing at the bottom the power feels the breath begin, a swelling of warmth from below the floor, from whatever the plateau is sitting on — the breath takes what is loose and carries it up the whole height of the stone and out into the wind. Fruits among it. Grit. A pale eater or two, tumbling, not knowing.

The people say *further down*, and mean it as a courtesy, a way of not saying *gone*. The power stands at the bottom of a shaft with the deep green under its feet and finds that it is not a courtesy. It is a description. The dead go further down and become the one being here that travels, and when the plateau exhales they come back up into the wind and are set down somewhere else and root. The people have been eating their grandmothers for as long as there have been people here, and the green they sleep on that grows into their wraps in the night is, some part of it, the ones who slept there before them.

The power does not think it can tell them this. There is no hand-shape for it. It is not certain there is a hand-shape for it in any language, and it is entirely certain that if it tried, what the hands would end up saying would be smaller than the thing, and worse.

---

It considers, then, what could be done, and the consideration takes almost as long as the walking.

It could give them back their hearing. It rejects this before the thought is finished. Hearing here is the ability to be shouted at by everything at once, and the people have found the quiet on the far side of it; to return them to the roar would be to take the one thing they have made of their loss. And they would not be able to hear the organ anyway, not as the power hears it. They would hear the noise. The music takes a life.

It could still the wind. Even a little. Even for one more breath a day, so that the moment of standing could be longer. And it feels, in the stone, what would follow: the sheets of mat that would not lift, the flipped sheets that would not be flipped, the eaters that would not be exposed and the flat things that would not come and the grey ones that would not drop, the fruits that would not be carried, the whole slow boil of green over the finished rock going slack. The mat has made the wind into its way of moving. To still the wind would be to still the only thing here that is doing something. The people would have their extra breath, and stand a little longer, and the green under their feet would begin, very slowly, to be where it was last season.

It could bring them metal. Bring them stone that lasts. Bring them others, or take them to where others are. It considers each of these and finds in each the same shape: a gift that is also a taking, that gives the people something and removes them a little from the place, as if the place were the trouble and they were to be rescued from it. But they are not in trouble. They are in the one place where a thing can be neither air nor ground and hold still. Anywhere else they would have nothing under them.

It could wake the rock. It puts its palm flat on the warm stone, as visitors do, and feels the sleeping animal, and feels, below the sleep, the thing the plateau is sitting on, which is not asleep and never has been. It takes its hand away. It has come to a place where every being is the dwelling of the next, and it will not turn one of them over to see what is underneath.

It could do nothing, and go. It considers this most seriously of all, because it is what the wise most often do, and because it would be honest. But it has walked the plateau barefoot for a lifetime and it has been at the bottom of the shafts and it has learned the organ by heart, and it finds that it is no longer able to be nothing here. It has become a thing that knows the notes. That is a small change to the place — one more creature who can hear it — and the power decides the change is not nothing, and that it will not undo it by leaving.

---

What it does, in the end, is small, and is done in the dark, and is done for no one's knowing.

It stays. It takes one of the abandoned shafts — the ring of stones half swallowed, the pole long fallen — and it goes down to the depth where the wind loses interest, and it does not wedge slabs across or pack the gaps with mat, because it does not need a platform. It simply stays there, in the note of that shaft, and attends.

And when the cloud comes down thick and somebody young has set out for a shaft whose note they only half know, and has walked past the point where the home pressure is in the bones and not yet reached the point where the other one is, and has sat down on the mat in the grey nothing to wait for the cloud to lift, and the cloud does not lift — the power climbs up, and kneels on the mat, and lays both palms flat on the stone, and shouts *here* from the bottom of its chest, in the direction of the nearest occupied shaft. Not a sound. A push. The young one feels a shiver come up through the rock from one side and not the other, and stands, and goes toward it, and the power keeps its palms on the stone and shouts *here* again from a little further on, and again, and walks the lost one home a hundred yards at a time without ever being closer than a shout, until the home note comes up through their feet and they run.

Every group has a story of someone who set out in weather and was found, or was not. Over a few generations the stories change a little. Some of the found ones say the stone said *here*. The old ones twisting cord in the shaft mouths hear this with their hands and shape the sign for *the rock is asleep and the wind is its dreaming*, and then a newer sign, one that has come up from somewhere without anybody deciding it: a palm laid flat, then lifted a little, then laid flat again. *Sometimes it turns over.* It is not a belief. It is a description of something that happens.

And when an old woman dies in the shaft called *sternum*, and is wrapped in mat and lowered on all the cord the group has, and the cord runs out and is cut — the power is below. It has come down through the roost with the grey ones folded and unstirring above it and it stands on the deep green in the warm dark, and when the body comes falling out of the last of the light it does not let it fall. It receives it. It lays her down on the pale runners with the care one would use for something sleeping, and it straightens the mat around her, and it goes back up.

She rooted, in time. Nobody above knew any of this. They felt the note drop for a moment, as it does, and said *further down*, and were right.

---

There is a shape a day's walk east of that settlement that has been in the mat for as long as the oldest people remember and has shifted, they say, a few yards, in a direction the wind does not favour. The power, in all its walking, learned the note of the stone under that shape and found the stone slightly wrong — not a shaft, not a rise, something with a rhythm in it slower than the wind's and slower than the breathing of the rock. It did not go near. Not out of fear. Out of the same courtesy, which it understood now from the inside. Whatever the shape was, it had made the only choice a large thing could make here, which was to become a part of the ground and be extended the courtesy of a sleeper, and the power could not think of one single thing it had to offer such a being that would not be an interruption.

It has begun to suspect, on the long days in the dark with the note coming through it, that it is not the first power to have been drawn here. That the shape to the east is what it looks like when a benevolent and wise attention arrives at this place and considers everything it could do and finds, in each thing, the same taking, and decides at last to stay, and stays long enough. It does not go and ask. It is not sure it wants to know. It has its shaft and its note and the lost to walk home and the dead to receive, and this is not a small life.

Once in a day, perhaps, the wind falls. Out of every occupied shaft the people climb into the light and straighten and stand on the mat, and for those few seconds nothing pushes them. The power climbs up too, out of its shaft that nobody counts as occupied, and stands among them at a distance where it is one more upright thing on the green, not one of them but alongside, and for those few seconds it can hear the whole organ at once with nothing over it, the thousands of pipes softening from pressure into hum, the sound of a place that is finished and a place that will never begin and a few hundred people and one more standing between them.

Then somewhere far off the green goes flat in a ring, and the power crouches with the rest of them, and goes back down.
