The first thing a wise power would do is notice that it has already been anticipated.

Not by the mass, which anticipates nothing. By the shape of the room. A chamber the height of a standing figure, the light softened to a tone that does not hurt, the air warm as a mouth, and an arch left open in a body that closes everything — this is a place that admits. Whatever comes through the failed arch finds that there is room for it, and finds, a moment later, that the room was already its size. A power that had come to look would stand there and understand that it had been, in some sense, waited for. Not expected. Waited for, the way a cup waits.

So it would enter the way everything enters here. Stooping.

---

Then it would do what powers do first, which is take inventory of what it could do, and it would go through the list honestly.

It could dry the bird. Lift the years of damp out of the feathers, take the greenish bloom off the shoulders, unmat the dark and let it lie sleek again. That is the most obvious kindness and it is the first to be set aside. The bird did not come here to be dry. It found a wet room and stayed in it, and everything it has learned it learned wet. To dry it would be to hand it back to the swamp outside, in a body that fit there, and tell it: this is who you were. The bird has not been that for a long time. It would be an unkindness dressed as restoration.

It could loosen the feet. The grip on the rachis has gone past choice into structure; the toes have locked and will not unlock. A power could give the bird back the option of leaving. But the option is not what the bird lacks. The arch is exactly as wide as it was. The bird has had the option every day for years and has declined it so consistently that the declining has become anatomy. To unlock the feet would be to reopen a question the bird settled, and to insist it be answered again. That is not a gift. That is a test.

It could bring another. A second dark bird, a mate, a companion on the wall. And here the power would pause longest, because loneliness is what a benevolent eye sees first in a single animal on a single fern in a chamber that could hold a congregation. But it would look at the room and see that the room is the shape of one listener. Two ears on the wall would hear each other. They would turn toward each other when a beetle fell, and the beetle would hit the water. The bird's whole art is that it is alone in the throb — the only thing in this room that is not the throb — and a companion would make it, in the gentlest possible way, part of a crowd. The room has been kept empty at great cost. The power would not be the one to start filling it.

It could give the bird years. And this it would consider and refuse without much struggle, because the bird has a death already, and it is a good one. The grip lets go. The fall through eight meters of amber. The basin, the low point, the sump opening a little wider than usual and closing. Everything in this room goes home through that soft pale plate, and the bird will too, and there is no version of this the power could design that would be more fitting than the one the room has designed by running for centuries. To spare the bird its fall would be to take it out of the loop, and out of the loop is the one place in this chamber where nothing is.

So: nothing to the bird's body. Nothing to its perch. Nothing to its solitude or its span.

---

Then it would consider the mass, and this would take less time, because there is less to consider.

The mass does not need anything. It has become. There is no favor to do a settlement this old, no improvement to a rhythm that has not varied since before there were words for rhythm. A power could speed the pump or slow it, and both would be vandalism. It could praise the mass for the room — for the wood reabsorbed, the wall kept bare, the fissure held open at the exact height of a downcurved bill — and the praise would fall on a thing with no organ to receive it.

But the power might, standing in the pore, feel something it does not often feel, which is kinship. It, too, is everywhere and therefore nowhere. It, too, fills what it touches. And here is a body that fills everything and has, somewhere in the middle of itself, at some cost against its own pressure, made a hollow and kept it. The power would recognize the discipline in that. Not intention — discipline. The willingness of an enormous thing to be absent from one place so that something small can be present there. And it would understand that if it wanted to honor the mass, the only way to do it would be to do the same. To be in the room and take up none of it.

---

That leaves the bird's ear, which is the one thing in the chamber still becoming, and which is therefore the one thing a wise power would attend to. Not to complete it. A becoming thing does not need completing; that is the whole point of it. What a becoming thing needs is company. Not a companion — company. Someone in the room while the work goes on.

So the power would stand in the center of the floor basin, in the few centimeters of clear water, and listen.

It would listen for a long time. Time is not a cost to a power and the room does not measure it anyway. It would listen the way the bird listens: not to the throb but to what the throb contains. The stall in a column of beetles as one grip fails. The bell-note going flat at the north vent before a frog falls. The changed weight of a drop on the western wall that means a track has deepened by the width of a hair. It would learn, from the inside of the engine, the schedule of small detachments the bird has spent its life compiling — and it would learn it *from the bird*, watching the tilt of the head, the one degree toward the vent, the settling. It would let the bird be its teacher. There is no one else in the world who knows this room, and the bird has never had a student.

This is a gift that adds nothing to the chamber. No new object, no new creature, no change to the loop. And yet it changes what the room is. For years there has been a listener on the eastern wall, listening to a thing that does not listen back. Now there are two, and one of them is listening to the other. The bird is heard hearing. It has never been that before.

---

Should the bird know?

The power would decide yes, and would decide barely. It would recall the account of the visitor bending under the arch, and the head coming around one degree, and holding, and settling — and it would understand that this was not a disturbance. It was the bird placing a new sound in the room. The bird can hear anything in this chamber, and what it heard that day was a large thing being careful, and it filed the sound, and went back to work. The settling was not dismissal. It was completion of a catalogue.

So the power would let itself be exactly that audible: the sound a large thing makes when it is not moving. A change in the air where nothing has changed. And the bird's head would come around, one degree, toward the center of the room. And hold.

And then settle.

The room is bigger now by one, and the bird knows it, and has gone back to listening — but the listening is different. There is a direction in it. Not toward the arch, not toward the vents. Toward the middle of the floor, where nothing is standing.

---

There would be one physical act, and it would be very small, and it would not be for this bird.

The power would think about what happens after the fall. The fern loses its cone and goes hungry. The fissure two meters up the eastern wall is held open for a tenant who is no longer there. The mass suffers no epiphytes on its lower walls; it has suffered this one for a reason, and the reason has gone home through the sump. How long does a body that fills everything keep a hollow no one is using? The account is confident: some wet season, a young dark bird, scattered, finds the gap. But confidence is not the same as certainty, and the power would see how thin the thread is. A room the shape of a listener is only kept open while there is one. Let the interval run too long and the fern weakens and the fissure narrows and the roots, finally, meet.

So in the year the bird falls — and the power would know the year, having learned the bird's body the way the bird learned the beetles' — it would see to the water. Nothing more. It would not choose a bird. It would not lead one to the arch. It would only make sure that when the grip lets go, the black water is high that season, and the young of that year are scattered wide, and some of them are near. The rest is the room's. The room has done it before.

That is a power working the way this place works: not by making, but by adjusting a pressure somewhere far off and letting the shape of the world do the rest. It is the kind of intervention that leaves no mark of a tool. Later, no one could say it had happened.

---

And it would stay for the fall.

It would have learned, by then, to hear what the bird hears, and so it would hear the thing the bird cannot: the bird's own grip beginning to go. A sound a very small thing makes when it stops being attached — except this time the thing is not small, and the power's attention would move, one degree, toward the eastern wall. And hold.

The bird, who can hear anything in this room detach, would hear the power hear it. Would know, the way it knows the beetles, a moment before the splash.

Its head would not come around. There would be nothing to turn toward; the sound was itself. What it would do instead — for the first time in years, in this room, in the amber that has always been easy on the eyes — is open them.

A door opened. The whole chamber, once, whole, seen: the pale pitchers hung on their cords, the spiral highway in its two lanes, the bronze moss, the sweating walls with their polished tracks, the clear floor a long way down. A room no one built. A room it had heard every centimeter of and never looked at. And then the feet, and the fall, and eight meters of warm air, and the basin taking it in with a sound the power would file and never need again.

The sump would open a little wider than usual. And close.

---

The power would leave the way it came. Stooping under the failed arch, out into the shriek and the black heat, taking nothing, having added nothing, having moved one season's water. Behind it the chamber would go on being loud in the dark of the wood, the fern a little hungry, the fissure held, the arch exactly as wide as it was.

And in the empty middle of the floor, in the few clear centimeters of water, there would be — not a presence. The room does not keep presences. But a shape. The way a channel is the shape of water that has stopped running. The place where something stood and listened, and took up none of the room, and was heard doing it, and was learned.

The next bird will find that too, along with everything else. It will not know what it is. It will file it. It will go back to work.
