The first thing to understand about the power is that it would look for a long time.

Not out of caution, though caution would be reasonable. Out of courtesy. The one who stood twenty meters from you looked at you as long as it looks at anything, and a power that means well would think it owed the plateau at least that. So it would attend. Not a season. Not a lifetime of theirs. Long enough for the moss along a route to shift a shade. Long enough to hear the ticking well enough to know, as they know, when a cushion is about to fail.

And it would see what none of them can. It would see the underside of the cloud.

This is the one thing it could do without doing anything, and it would do it, because a wise power does not decide about a thing it has only half looked at. It would follow the drip. Through the fractures, warm and rich and carrying, through the meter of drowned moss, into the basalt, out the underside of the table into the cloud whose lower face no walker has seen, and down. To whatever has been drinking. The power would find that the plateau is not closed. It never was. It is the upper stage of something. The works has a product and the product leaves, and the walkers are the part of the works that walks, and they have been feeding something for longer than the birds have had routes, and they have exactly one word for it and the word is exactly as large as it needs to be.

The power would hold that for a while before doing anything else. It would recognize the arrangement. It, too, is an upstream thing. Most of what it gives falls where it cannot see.

---

Then it would consider what could be done, and set each thing down in turn, the way you would set down stones you had picked up to see the undersides of.

It could give them heat. A dry warmth, a fire, something to stand near. And the moss at the edge of it would brown and the cushion would die and the failure would be theirs, and it would have taught them the one thing the plateau has never taught anyone, which is that something can be lost by having.

It could give them a word. *Down.* *Under.* *Before.* Words are the cheapest gift and the most corrosive, because a word for down wants a word for what is down there, and that wants a word for *why*, and the route was worn to the width of what they had words for, and would not hold the new ones. They would walk off it. There is nowhere off it to walk to.

It could stop the lying-down. It could stand at the rim of every failure and turn the oldest back up onto the canopy, and the failures would close slow, and the new cushions would run cold, and the walkers would live longer and colder and would have lost the one act in their lives that is not the route. Because that is what the power would notice, kneeling at the rim with them: everything else they do is timing. The walking, the pausing, the arm through the moss. The lying-down is the only thing decided. It is not a ceremony and it is not lunch. It is the one place on the plateau where a person is not a corridor. Take it from them and you have not saved anyone. You have finished the machine.

It could take them off the plateau altogether. And the plateau would seize. The long circulation stops, the grazer eggs stay where they were swallowed, the failures close without warmth and then stop closing, and the whole surface goes to grey basalt in a thousand years and weeps into nothing. And the walkers, somewhere with an edge and a word for down, would stand very still and look at one thing for a long time and never quite be warm.

So it would set those down.

---

But it would not walk away, either, and this is where its wisdom would have to be more than taste. Because there is a kind of onlooker who sees a thing this whole and calls the leaving of it wisdom, when what it is is admiration, and admiration is not owed to anyone. The plateau is beautiful the way a mechanism is beautiful and the power would feel that, and would distrust it, and would come back to the one difficult thing.

They are not dead when they lie down.

The power would want to know what that is. Not what it looks like from the rim — it knows what it looks like from the rim. What it is from the inside. Whether the one on the basalt, with the moss coming, warmth first, is a self being taken or a self arriving at the only place it was ever going. Whether the children who stay in the hollow and grow pale and soft and are eaten from are unfortunate, or went in rather than out, and whether those are different. The power could look inside. It has that faculty. It would look.

And it would find that it lacked a word.

Not that the thing was empty. Not that it was peace. Something for which the power, with all its languages, had no name — the way they have no name for down. Whatever is inside a walker on the basalt is as large as the word they don't have for it, and no larger, and no smaller, and the power would recognize the shape of not-knowing, because it is the same shape as *the place the drip goes*. It would hold that. It would not resolve it into either horror or blessing, because both would be its own word laid over a thing that had declined to have one.

That is the second act, and it is also not an act. It would refuse to decide what the lying-down means. It would leave that undecided, as they have.

---

What it would do, then, is small, and it would take a body to do it.

It would become one of them. Chest-high, heavy through the trunk, splayed feet, hair felted to a mat that holds water. It would take the cold outside and the warm inside. It would learn to speak with its mouth to the moss and to listen with its ear to the moss a meter off, and it would say very little, as they do, and it would hear the ticking arrive along with the words. It would walk the loop. Six or eight, one of them the power, pausing, kneeling, driving an arm through the skin to the shoulder. It would eat what came up. It would peel fern-rib with a thumbnail and its teeth would go the color of tin. On a still afternoon it would stand with a palm to a fern trunk and wait and take a little when the thread came, and its eyes would be wider afterward and it would walk a long way.

It would teach them nothing. It would carry the lichen on its shoulders a season's walk and let the spores fall where it lay down. It would be a corridor.

A lifetime of theirs is nothing to it. It would give one.

And at the end of that lifetime, when the ticking said a cushion was going, and the loop bent to pass it, and the line came to the rim and knelt, and the air smelled of iron and the drip was loud, and the oldest began to shift toward the edge to climb down — the power would go first.

Not instead. It would not turn anyone back. It would only be quicker, that once, by a moment, and the oldest would stop, and look at it as long as they look at anything, and stay on the rim.

It would lie on the basalt in the wet. It would be cold in the way they are cold nowhere else. And the moss would come, warmth first, then the rest, and the failure would close, and there would be a new cushion there.

It would run warmer than any cushion on the plateau has ever run. It would stay warm past the reckoning of anyone who saw it made, past the reckoning of their children, past the point where anyone could say why this one and not another. The walkers would pause there, every loop, and kneel, and eat, and lift their heads. They would pause a little longer than elsewhere, as they do where the routes cross the birds', and there would be no word for the difference, and they would not need one.

Eventually it would have a chamber. Eventually there would be a family in it.

And in that hollow, lined with the moss's own dead tissue, warm and dark and smelling of clean rot and animal, there would be one wall that does not plane. No grazers in it. Nothing to reach into and pull out. It would feed nothing and take nothing and be, in the plateau's accounting, waste — the first waste on the whole surface, a patch of quiet warm nothing the size of a person's back, on a plateau where every output has been somebody's input for as long as there has been rock.

Children would lean against it. That is all it would be for.

---

That is what it would do, if it did anything. Look until it had earned the right to. Set down every fix. Refuse to name the one thing the plateau leaves unnamed. Walk the loop as one of them and give the one act it could give in their language, once, and leave behind a small warm slack in the works where a person could lean and be, for a moment, not a part of anything.

Then it would go to see what has been drinking. Because if there is a question on the plateau, it is not up there. It went down with the drip a long time ago, and something at the bottom has an answer, and has never been asked.
