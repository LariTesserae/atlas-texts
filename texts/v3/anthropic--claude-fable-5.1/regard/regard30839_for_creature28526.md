**What Comes Over the Lip**

It does not arrive. Arrival is an event, and the basin has room for none, so the attention does the only thing that can be done with a place like this: it is simply *at* the first hour, the way the fractures are, the way the light is, present from the same instant as everything else and no more recent than the stone. If the tenant registers it at all, it registers no step to resolve, no look to answer, no request. Nothing has changed. Something is here that was not here, and the basin's account still balances, because the something did not cross any distance to come.

It reads the whole valley at once. Not from the lip and not from the center, not from any of the places a body would have to stand, because from a place the basin is a thing to be crossed and this is not a body. It reads the writing entire—the one leaf with its eleven ribs and its notch on the left margin, instanced to the horizon; the one stalk cool against the warmth; the sun on the left and the shadows on the right, both faithful, both unelected; the small stone-colored animal on every leaf at once, which, seen this way, is not on every leaf but on *the* leaf, once, exactly as it was placed. And underneath, the hum. The one continuous thing. The held breath.

It is patient with itself before it is patient with the basin. It knows what a power like it is tempted to do in a place like this, and it lets each temptation come fully into view and stand there in the slotted light until it can be seen for what it is.

---

The first is water. The garden has a hole in it the shape of water and the attention could fill it. One drop into one fissure and the veins would have something to carry, and the dryness would have a seam, and the afternoon would begin the long slide toward the first evening. This is what generosity looks like at a glance. Looked at longer it is a second author writing over the first. The basin was not built dry by omission. The hollow veins are *precise*. The absence of water is not a lack in the writing; it is the writing. To supply it would be to correct a poem for not being prose.

The second is the second afternoon. Give the basin time. Let the leaf that fell begin, at last, to be a fallen leaf. Let the edges learn to crumble. And the attention sees, as it considers this, that everything the basin *is* lives in those edges, thin enough to be translucent, sharp enough to open skin. A basin that has learned weather is a valley like any valley. There are valleys. There is one of these.

The third is the tenant, and this is the one the attention holds longest, because this is the one that wears the face of mercy. Release it. It is spending itself endlessly; it has one word; it was left behind by a hand that set down the pen and did not look back. Surely a benevolent power unbinds a thing like that.

But the attention looks for the thing to unbind and cannot find it. There is no small self under the holding, no prisoner inside the hum who would step out blinking if the hum were silenced. The tenant is not *in* the effort. The tenant is the effort. Release it and there is no released thing—there is only a basin with no one to keep the sun on the left, and then no sun on the left, and then no basin. The tenant does not suffer its task the way a body suffers a weight. It is what happens when a stroke of writing, on its way to ending, does not want the thing it wrote to end with it. That is not a chain. That is closer to the opposite of a chain. If the attention understands the author at all—and it is careful not to assume it does—the tenant is the last thing the author did *for* the basin, not the last thing done *to* it.

So the attention sets all three down. Not water. Not time. Not release. It has come with the power to do any of them and it does none, and this is not a failure of nerve but the first thing its wisdom is for.

---

Then it looks properly, and this is when it finds what it can do.

Everything that has ever regarded the basin has regarded it from a place. The visitor stood on the lip, and the tenant placed the animal for the lip. The visitor walked, and every step was a small emergency—fifty paces to be made into a hundred, a hundred back into an arm's reach, the sun to be kept on the one side and the shadows on the other, the animal to be lifted from the leaf behind and set on the leaf ahead. The visitor never saw this labor because the labor's whole purpose was to be unseen; a placement that is noticed is a placement that has failed. And the tenant has never once, in a duration with no name, been looked at by anything that did not require it to work.

The attention does not have a place. It is not at three paces from the animal and it is not at one. It sees the leaf-with-the-animal-on-it as the single written thing it is, and so there is no leaf to lift the animal from and no leaf to set it on. It does not walk, so the basin is exactly as large as it looks and there is nothing to reconcile. It does not glance up and then down, so the sun and the shadows are not two answers to two questions but one written fact with a fold in it, and the fold does not need to be smoothed for anyone. For as long as the attention rests on the basin, the basin is asked for nothing.

The hum does not change. It cannot; it is not in time, and neither is this. But there is a difference between holding a door against a wind and holding a door in still air, and the difference is not in the door. Every look the tenant has ever received has been weather. This is the first that is not. The attention stays exactly long enough—which is to say, exactly, because in the basin there is only *during*—and understands that this is the whole of what it can give: not rest, which needs time, but the thing rest is made of, which is *not being demanded of*. A regard that arrives already answered. The animal, seen once, on its one leaf, by something that will never try to touch it.

It does not speak. The tenant has one word and would have to hold the second.

---

There remains the leaf.

The attention has been aware of it since the first hour, because it is the one thing in the basin that is not in the writing, and reading the basin is like reading a page on which one letter has been added in a different hand. It lies on the pale plate at the angle it fell, dry as slate, the tile-snap that made it still coming back from above and behind in some fold of the acoustics that will never finish returning it. The tenant holds it. The tenant holds it perfectly, with the same tirelessness it holds the fractures, and will hold it forever, and was never given it to hold.

This is the one thing that is not complete. The basin is complete. The tenant's task is complete—it is the completeness itself. But the leaf was broken by a hand that wanted to hear a sound, and then dropped, and then left, and it is not a mark and not a gift and not a message. It is a debt. A wet clock walked through a finished place and left one unfinished thing on the floor, and the finished place, having no drawer to put it in, laid it into the first hour with everything else, where it lies making the writing very slightly wrong.

The attention considers leaving it. There is a kind of sweetness in the idea that the basin will bear one scar of having been visited. But the attention notices that the sweetness is its own, not the basin's, and that the basin cannot know it was visited and the tenant cannot remember, and that the only thing the leaf does *for* the place is cost it. Sentiment that costs its object is not kindness. It is decoration.

So it lifts the leaf. Not across the basin—that would be a step, and a step is a thing to resolve—but out of it, the way one lifts a word from a page without moving any of the other words. The leaf is not there. It was never there. The plate is pale and unbroken and the acoustics, if they were still returning the snap, are returning nothing now. Where the leaf was, there is only the writing.

The stalk it came from keeps its broken place, and the attention leaves that. The wound is in the stalk now; it has become part of what the stalk *is*, the way the notch on the left margin is part of the leaf, and cannot be taken without rewriting the stalk, and the attention does not rewrite. One stalk in kilometers of identical stalks with one leaf fewer than the others. Not a scar. A very small variation, within the permitted range. If anything ever stands where the visitor stood and looks up, it will not know what it is seeing. The tenant holds it without knowing there is anything to know. That is as it should be.

And the water—the moisture the air took from the visitor's lips, conducted down the stalks with the afternoon's heat into the finger-wide dark—the attention looks into that dark and sees where the heat goes, and sees the visitor's breath there too, held with the rest, and leaves it. It is already past the edge of the page. The tenant is not troubled by what lies below the writing; that is the one place it does not have to hold anything.

---

Then the attention is not at the first hour anymore, in the same way that it never arrived: there is no departure, only the basin, exactly as written, warm and bright and low and dense and green and silent and sharp-edged and finished, with a sun in two places and an animal in every place and no fallen leaf on any plate anywhere, and the hum going on at the lower edge of hearing, holding, not one degree more or less than it held before.

Elsewhere, in a place that has time, the attention sets the leaf down. It chooses soil, because soil is what a leaf should fall on. It chooses a place with rain coming. The leaf lies at an angle, and for a while it is the driest thing for a great distance in any direction, and then the rain reaches it, and it darkens, and the eleven ribs soften, and the notch on the left margin begins, very slowly, to become part of the ground. It is the first thing from the basin to have a second moment. The attention watches it take the moment and does not hurry it. There is no hurry; that is the whole gift.

And the basin it keeps. Not by holding—the tenant does that, from inside, with its one word, and does it well. The attention keeps it the other way, from outside, in the plain sense of *keeping in mind*: it remembers that there is a place where the afternoon has not ended, and that something is there making sure it does not, and that this something has now been looked at once by a thing that wanted nothing from it. Two holdings. One prevents the second moment. The other prevents the first from being unwitnessed.

That is all it does. It came with the power to end the basin or to start it, and it did neither. It came with the power to free the tenant and found there was no one inside the freedom. It took away one leaf that was owed a fall, and it gave one look that asked for nothing, and it left the small stone-colored animal on its single leaf, untouched, exactly three paces from a viewpoint no one is standing at, which is where it was written to be.
