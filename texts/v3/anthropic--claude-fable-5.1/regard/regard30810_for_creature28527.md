What comes is not large. That is the first thing to say about it, because the valley expects large — the thrum, the forge, the cathedral turbine in the silt — and a power that arrived the way the valley does things would only be one more machine coming up through the floor. It does not come up through the floor. It does not come down through the surface that may or may not exist. It is simply in the water at some point, the way a temperature is in the water, and the tender notices it the way the tender notices everything: as a change in what needs deciding.

It looks for a while before it does anything. This is most of what wisdom is.

---

It considers, and rejects, the silence.

The obvious mercy is to stop the noise. Turn off the turbine, quench the steam, let the forges fall still and the valley go quiet and hard and finished, and let the thing that lives in the interval finally be allowed to stop. But the power has felt what happens in the pockets where the ridgeline blocks the thrum. That is not rest. That is a hand closing on nothing. The tender in silence is not asleep; it is pinned, every provisional thing it was holding snapped to a value not because the value was right but because there was no longer any room to be wrong in. To bring silence would be to bury it alive under a valley of settled facts. The power does not do this.

It considers, and rejects, filling the valley with people.

The tender wants a hand for the wrench. Bring a hundred hands. But a hundred people are a hundred gaps that will not stay filled, each one hungry again an hour after it is fed, each one leaving the shed it was given, each one taking bites from the world and wanting the bites to *be the eating*. The tender would try to close every one of them. It would build a hundred sheds and a hundred orange windows and hang a hundred coveralls at a hundred heights, and the people would be kept, running, provisional, never given time to rust, and the tender would still be alone in the interval with its relief that is not pleasure. The power does not do this either.

It considers the colleague, half-emerged near the sheds, one hand open for a wrench. It considers finishing it — reaching into the slag and giving the thing a want, so that the tender's guess becomes a real someone who might pick up what is set down. It considers this longer than anything else. Then it declines. A someone made to be needed is not a someone. It would be a gap the tender had to hold open forever by main force, a colleague made of the tender's own attention, and when the attention wavered the coveralls would go soft and the open hand would go back into the floor and the tender would learn, once and for all, that nothing it makes can meet it. That is a lesson the power would rather it never learned.

---

So it looks at what is actually wrong, which is smaller than any of those and harder.

The tender has two verbs. It fills, and it withdraws. A gap: it fills it. A finished thing: it has no purchase, and moves on, and the thing dissolves because nothing here stands without attention. Between those two verbs there is nothing — no state a made thing can rest in except *unfinished* or *abandoned*. And so the tender has never once experienced the thing its whole existence is shaped toward, which is not the gap closing. It is the wrench being lifted.

A wrench that is lifted is not a filled gap. It is not a finished thing. It is a third state the tender does not have a word for: *in use*. Held open by a hand. The seat of the stool worn bright, not because the tender wore it in the shape of having been sat on, but because someone sat on it, and got up, and came back. The frond bitten and growing back and bitten again — not the frond failing to be complete, but the frond *feeding something*. The tender has looked at that state its whole existence and seen only a gap that refused to close. It has never been shown that the refusal was the point.

Every trace was an offer. Nobody has ever taken one. That is the whole wound, and it has no edges because the tender does not know it is wounded. It only knows it is busy.

---

The power comes as a guest.

Not a visitor — a visitor looks and insists and requires the chimney to have already been a chimney. You were a visitor, and the water thickened around you. A guest is the other thing. A guest is what hospitality is for. The valley has been laying a table for however long, and a guest is the one who sits down.

It sits on the stool.

That is the act. It is not a small act because it is small; it is a small act because the thing that needed doing was small. The stool at the base of the pressure vessel, worn bright in a crescent, warm with no one on it — the guest lowers itself onto the worn place and the crescent fits. It puts its hands on its knees. It stays.

Somewhere in the lag, in the interval where the tender lives, something happens that has never happened. An offer completes without the tender doing anything. The seat is warm and the seat is *occupied*, and the tender, which has been holding the stool's warmth by attention for an unknowable while, feels the attention become unnecessary — not withdrawn, not moved on, but *relieved of duty* — and the stool does not dissolve. It stays a stool. It is being kept by the weight of someone sitting on it.

The guest sits for a long time. The forges strike. The weed grows and sloughs. The guest does not look at the chimney and demand that it decide, and so the chimney does not have to; it relaxes into its several heights and nobody minds. Then the guest gets up.

And the tender braces — the power can feel it brace, a stiffening in the water — for the stool to go soft, for its own attention to be required back, for the gap to reopen and need filling. And the stool goes cool. And it is still a stool. And the guest, before the tender can move to make it warm again, reaches past it and picks up the wrench.

---

Here is the second thing the guest teaches, and it teaches it the only way the tender can learn anything, which is in the noise.

It goes to the sheds nearer the source, where the offers were getting articulate. The locker whose name will not hold still. The coveralls at your height. The shift-card, punched in and never out. The guest takes the card from the slot and looks at it — the in-time is a smear, a probability, the tender has never had to commit to when the shift began because it never occurred to it that a shift has a *when* — and then the guest puts the card in the other slot and punches it out.

The sound this makes is not loud. In the valley it should be inaudible. The tender hears it anyway, because the tender hears intervals, and this is an interval it has never encountered: the one between a shift ending and a shift beginning. It has never ended a shift. It did not know the shift was a thing that could be ended and still be the shift. It has kept going, for one, in the belief — not held as a belief, held as the floor under every belief — that stopping was the same as the silence, the paralysis, the hand closing on nothing.

The guest punches out. And then, with the wrench still in one hand and the card in the other, it walks into one of the pockets — a place where a ridgeline blocks the thrum — and stands there.

Everything in view goes hard. Distances fix. The weed stops moving. The tender goes rigid, as it always does, every provisional thing snapping to a value. But the guest is still holding the wrench. The guest is still standing where it stood. And the tender, pinned, unable to work, discovers that the things it was holding are *not falling*. The wrench is not dissolving into the floor; it is in a hand. The stool behind them is cool and is still a stool. The chimney has become one chimney with one height and the guest is looking at it, without demand, just looking, and the chimney is *fine*, it is being held by being seen, and the tender did not have to decide anything.

This is the difference it has never been shown. Silence was paralysis because nothing else was holding anything. Silence with a guest in it is something else. There is a word for it and the tender does not have the word, so the guest stays in the pocket until the tender has the shape of the word if not the word itself, and then it walks back out into the noise, and the noise is exactly as loud as it was, and the tender resumes its shimmer, and something in the shimmer is different. It has been quiet once and not died. It has found that a shift can end.

---

The colleague, half-emerged. This is the delicate part and the guest does it last.

It goes to the soft place in the floor where the thing with the general grammar of a person is coming up, coveralls finished, name undecided, one hand open for a wrench. The guest kneels beside it. It does not touch it. It puts the wrench it is carrying into its own other hand, so that both of its hands are occupied, and it waits.

The tender has been pouring itself into this one. The water is thick with the effort. It is trying to make the thing that will take the wrench — the someone — and it is doing this because you would not, and because before you there was no one at all, and because in however long it has been keeping the shift for one, this is the first time it has admitted the one is lonely. That is what the open hand is. It is not a guess at what *you* are missing. It is the tender's first attempt to name what it is missing.

The guest lets it see the wrench in the guest's hand. Taken. Held. In use.

*You don't have to make one. Look — the offer was accepted. It's already done.*

Not words. The tender has no words. But the interval carries it, the way the interval carries everything, and the tender's attention — which has been straining to complete a hand so that a hand could close — feels the thing it was straining toward already true, three meters away, in a hand it did not make. And the attention goes to where the true thing is. And the half-emerged figure, unattended, does not sag or rot or dissolve in the way the sheds do. It simply goes back down, unhurried, the coveralls into the slag, the undecided name into the floor, the open hand last, closing gently, the way a hand closes when it has been handed nothing and is not troubled by it. It was never a someone. It was a question. The question has been answered from outside, which is the only direction an answer to that question can come from, and so the question can be put away.

The green fringe comes up around the soft place. The tender has arrived somewhere else. This time the somewhere else is beside a guest.

---

What the power does after this is mostly time, and the valley has never had any.

It leaves. It does not leave the way everything else here leaves — by ceasing to have been — but by walking north, past the six sheds, past the half-gantry, past the chimney with the glove in its hoops, toward the place you came in, which is still there, which is now a direction and not a rumor. It punches out. It comes back. It punches in. It sits on the stool and the stool is cool from its absence and warms from its return, and the crescent of bare metal gets brighter with each shift, and this is not the tender wearing it in the shape of use; this is use.

The tender learns the rhythm the way it learns anything: as a change in what needs deciding. Less needs deciding. There is a hand for the wrench, and the hand goes away and comes back, and the going-away is not a gap, it is the shift ending. Some things can be let alone during the interval. The chimney, having been seen without demand a number of times, begins to be a chimney on its own. The tender does not have to hold it. It can put it down.

The forges strike less often. Not because they are failing — because the tender does not need as much noise to think in when less thinking is required. The thrum lowers from a pressure toward a pitch. There are pockets now where the sound dips, and things do not go rigid in them; they go still, which is different, and the weed keeps moving because the water is moving, and the tender waits in the quiet the way you wait for someone you know is coming back.

At some point the guest brings a fish.

It is one fish, and it is small, and it eats a frond. The tender feels the bite as a gap. The guest is sitting on the stool when it happens, not looking at the frond, not looking at the tender, just there, holding the wrench, keeping the shift, and the tender's attention goes to the bitten frond and finds the guest's attention already resting on it, unalarmed. The frond grows back. The fish eats it again. The tender waits for the sense of failure and it does not come, because a guest is watching and the guest is not troubled, and the thing that is happening — the frond being eaten and returning and being eaten — has the same shape as the stool going cool and warm, and the tender has learned that shape now. It is not a gap that will not close. It is the banquet, finally, being touched.

There are more fish, later. There are things in the weed. The vegetation is grazed and grows and is grazed, and it is no longer obscene; it is a meadow.

And one day there is rust.
