It does not come in through the throat. It has no need to; it is not the kind of thing that is stopped by rock, or dark, or a wall of heat. But it pauses at the throat anyway, for the same reason a careful person pauses at a door, and it notices what the snake notices: the warmth on the stone, the pale stain of dust, the smell arriving before the place does. It notices that the throat is exactly wide enough. Wider and the heat would leave. Narrower and the exodus would take all night. It notices that this was not designed and it notices that it did not need to be.

What drew it here was not the roar. Things of that kind hear a great many roars. What drew it was smaller and harder to hear: somewhere in the noise, a single animal calling, and a single animal answering, and the first one landing beside the second and no other. Ten thousand times a night. In a room that has spent centuries teaching every body in it to be a surface, the surface still comes apart, every dawn, into pairs. That is the sort of signal a wise thing is tuned for. It is, if you like, the same signal the mothers are tuned for. The power did not find this room in the way the mother finds the pup, but it did not find it in an entirely different way either.

So it goes in.

---

The first thing it does costs the room nothing and gives the room nothing, and it does it before it has decided anything else. It looks.

Not with light. It would not bring light here any more than it would bring a flood; light in this room would be a wound, a thing the ceiling would have to heal around. But it does not need light to see, and so for the first time since the lava drained away, the room is seen. The fur on the ceiling is brown. It is a warm brown, close to the color of the fresh guano and not by coincidence, with a paler undercoat that shows when a wing lifts. The lava glass on the walls is black with a bottle-green depth to it where it is thickest, and the drips that froze mid-drip have a bloom on their surfaces, a faint iridescence, that nothing living has ever had occasion to develop a word for. The ammonia crusts by the fissures are white, an absolute white, and shaped like frost ferns. The pups in the creche are the pink of the inside of a mouth. The beetles on the floor, seen closely, are not black but a very dark bronze.

None of this is changed by being seen. The power is under no illusion that it has given the bats anything. But it holds the fact that the room is brown and green and white and bronze, and it holds it carefully, because it has learned that the first duty toward a thing is to know what it actually is, and that most of the harm done in the name of benevolence is done by hands that skipped this step.

---

Then it does what such a power always does, which is to consider, one at a time, the things it could do. It considers them fully. It is not in a hurry; it is sitting in a room where the fast thing is a heartbeat measured in days.

It could cool the room. Forty degrees in the dark is hard on the bodies that make it. It would take almost nothing — a slight widening of the throat, a second breach in the roof. And the creche would die within the week. The pups need this heat the way seeds need soil. The room is hot because it is a nursery and it is a nursery because it is hot, and the power sees that this is not a problem with a fix on the other side of it but a shape, and that the shape is the point.

It could catch the falling bat. There is one falling now, in fact — old, a female who has flown this mountain for eleven summers and whose grip has finally let go — and the power watches her drop through the dark toward the crust and the beetles, and it considers the interval, which will be short, between landing and nothing. It could shorten that interval to zero. It could lift her back to the ceiling and hold her there. And then the floor is a little hungrier, and she falls again tomorrow, and the power is standing in the room forever with its hands full of bats. It does not catch her. It notes, as she lands, that the floor is very quick, and that whatever made the floor quick was already merciful before the power arrived.

It could spare the moths. It considers the desert below, a hundred square kilometers of scrub and thermals, and the tonnage of insects that leave it each night in the stomachs of bats and never return. It considers the moths as individuals, briefly, because it is the kind of thing that can, and then it considers the desert, which has its own arrangement, in which the moths are as much a fabric as the ceiling is here, and in which the bats are part of what keeps the fabric from tearing itself apart. Take the bats out of the desert's night and the desert is not spared; it is unbalanced, and it will find a new balance, and the finding will be violent. The power leaves the moths where they are, which is in the air, and then in the bats.

It could take the mites out of the ears. This one it lingers on, because there is something about the mites that a certain kind of benevolence finds unbearable — a universe the size of an ear, a life spent in the dark inside another life spent in the dark, a creature whose entire world is a wet tunnel that carries sound to someone else. But the power looks at the mites and finds that they are not suffering. They are eating. They have been doing so for longer than the room has existed; they came in with the bats and will leave with them. To remove them would be to reach into a hundred thousand ears and take out something that was not, on any evidence available, asking to be taken. It would be an act done for the comfort of the one doing it. The power has learned to recognize that kind of act, and it leaves the ears alone.

It could quiet the roar. It could tell them something. It could give the colony — the arrangement, the thing that behaves like something alive — some sense of itself, so that the roar was not just the sound of a plant running at capacity but the sound of a plant that knew it was running. It considers what that would be like. A room full of animals who knew what the mountain was thinking about. It rejects this the fastest of all. Knowledge here would not be a gift. It would be the one thing in the room with no one to eat it.

And it could stop the mountain.

---

This it considers longest, because this is the real thing, the only threat the room contains that the room does not also digest. The floor is rising. In a few thousand years the crust will meet the vault and the ceiling will have nowhere to be a ceiling. Or the mountain, which is only warm and thinking about it, will finish thinking, and a new flow will find the old tube and fill it, or the slumped roof will slump the rest of the way. One way or another the landlord will want the room back, and the power could, with very little effort, tell the landlord no.

It goes down, to look at the landlord.

Below the floor, below the glass, below the deposit's mineral basement, the mountain is doing what the description says: something very slow. The power is nearer kin to this than to anything on the ceiling. It understands the timescale. And what it sees, sitting with the mountain's heat, is that the mountain is not the room's enemy. The mountain is the room's author. Every room like this one, on every flank of every volcano that has not finished being made, was made by the mountain doing exactly what it is doing now — running a river under a skin and letting it drain — and the price of rooms is that the maker does not stop making. To ask the mountain to hold this room open forever is to ask it to make no more rooms. It would be a benevolence toward these bats, who will not be alive to enjoy it, and a theft from every colony that has not yet found a hole in the rock to pour itself into.

So it does not stop the mountain. It does something smaller, and stranger, and it does it in the future. It looks — the way such a power can look — along the paths the next flows will take, and it finds the place, on the far side of the summit, three thousand years off, where a tube will run and swell and drain and leave a bubble. It sees the roof of that bubble. It sees that the roof will hold, that there will be no throat, that the room will cool in perfect darkness and never be found. And it makes a note. Not an intervention — not yet, and perhaps not ever; it has not decided. But it marks the place where the roof will be thinnest, and it resolves that when the time comes it will be there, and it will consider, then, whether to lean on the stone. Just slightly. The way a roof would slump on its own if the world were a little kinder. It does not know if it will do this. It knows it will be there to decide. That is as much as it is willing to promise the arrangement, and more than the arrangement will ever know it was promised.

---

Then it goes back up through the floor and the roar and the heat, and out through the throat past the snake, and it turns its back on the room, and it leaves.

And here is the one thing it actually does, and it does it somewhere else.

The room defends itself against everything that comes at it from inside. The heat, the ammonia, the dead, the parasites — the room eats all of these. The room has no defense at all against what comes at it from below: from the hundred square kilometers of dark country where the moths are, and the things that would empty that country of moths. A poison drifting on the night wind. A light where there should be no light, pulling the moths down out of the thermals into a ring of dead insects around a pole. A road. A hole in the rock found by something with a bucket, because the deposit on the floor is worth money to the right buyer and has been mined out of other rooms like this one down to the glass. The room's throat is a mouth and the desert is the meal, and the room has never seen the desert and could not protect it if it had.

So the power goes down the mountain and spends itself there. It is not dramatic. It is the kind of work that leaves no mark: a wind that carries a drift somewhere else, a survey that comes back inconclusive, a night that stays dark, a man who meant to go up the flank and for no reason he can name goes home instead. Years of this. The power is patient and the desert is large and none of it is visible from the room. That is the point. The room's benefactor works in a place the room has never been, on the room's behalf, so that nothing in the room will ever have to know anything happened. The colony will go on hanging in the heat and roaring and raining and eating itself, and the sky it climbs into at dusk will go on being full of moths, and it will not occur to a single body on that ceiling to wonder why.

---

Come back in a century, the description says, and the room would sound exactly as it does now, and be a hand's width shallower.

The power has come and gone and this is still true. It is, in fact, the measure of what the power did: that the room is exactly as it was, and its future is a little more like its past than it might otherwise have been, and no one inside will ever be able to tell.

Somewhere on the ceiling a mother lands, and calls, and one voice in the roar answers her, and she finds it, and feeds it, and only it. The power did the same thing. Out of all the rooms it found this one, and it fed it, and only it, and then — because it is wise, and because it had watched the mothers long enough to learn from them — it did not touch the neighbors' pups.
