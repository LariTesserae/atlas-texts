It notices the way you would notice a held breath in a room. Not the sound — the sound is just weather from above the cloud, a bruise on the fog. What it notices is the shape of the silence around the sound. Something has been kept empty for a very long time, and a thing that is wise knows that keeping-empty is the most expensive kind of work there is, and goes to see who is paying.

It comes down the road through the fog. It does not need the road. It takes it anyway, because the road is how the plain is meant to be entered, and it means to enter properly.

---

The first thing it does is nothing. That is not a figure of speech.

It kneels. It puts its palm on the crust. It waits through three or four strokes, then through a hundred, then through the water rising and the far engine quickening and the lake unjoining into pools. It stays kneeling while the reeds die out along one line and come up along another. It learns the interference the way the tenders learned it, through the wrist, and it learns the taste of hot brass in the fog, and it learns that the slime lights in the shape of its feeding, and it counts the tenders — fewer than it hoped, more than it feared — and it feels the children born into the beat and it feels the old ones go out along the lines of quiet and lie down.

It does not stop them. It watches the plain take one of them level. It stays for the whole of it, because this is something it is going to have to have seen.

Somewhere in this waiting it understands what the plain is. Not a prison. Not a punishment. Not a mistake. A shape that has been arriving for so long that everything in it is made of the arriving — bearings, brass, bodies, the soles of feet. A life. Coherent, complete, without a single door in it, and the tenders leaning into it the way weather is leaned into, and the children who will hear silence as death and be right.

It is a wise power. It knows the difference between a place that is suffering and a place that is simply not yours. It kneels for a long time on that line.

---

Then it considers the cures, because benevolence must consider them, and it puts each one down.

It could stop the engines. It could unmake the sound in an instant, and the plain would go quiet from rim to rim, and the tenders would feel the beat leave their bones and know exactly what it meant, and they would be right, and the water would come.

It could stop the water. Reach under the fill and outside the rim and into the air and close the hand that is the sea, so that the basin never filled again and the engines could fall silent without the flood behind them. And there would be no reason for a grip in the brass. No reason to sieve the ground. The one thing that stands between the tenders and the fill is the work, and the work is the water, and to take the water is to take the reason a hand goes down on a handle in the morning. It has seen what happens to a people whose reason is removed for their own good. It has seen it more than once.

It could lift them out. Carry them up over the lip of darkness and set them down somewhere green, where the ground does not beat and there are birds. Deaf, in a world of sound. Their feet on soil that says nothing to them. Lying down at night inside a stillness that would hold them the way the beat held them, and feeling, every hour, the absence of the only thing they had ever known as *here*. That is not rescue. That is a different drowning.

It could mend the bearings. Make the brass that never hollows, the beam that never wears concave, the loop that runs forever. It sits longest with this one because it is the one that looks most like kindness. And it sees that a plain kept forever at the edge of finishing is not saved. It is embalmed. The tenders would tend engines that did not need them, and their edges would go anyway, because the sound would still be doing to them what it does, and there would never come a night when the load deepened and someone opened the firebox a little wider and leaned in. It would take the one thing they have that is theirs, which is the wearing.

There is a thing the plain says about itself that the power takes seriously: *you could not, from inside it, find the place where the loop began.* A lesser power would go looking for that place. This one has enough sense to know that pulling a single thread from a thing this old does not loosen it. It unravels it. And it is not the one who will have to live in the unravelled.

So it does not fix. It does not stop. It does not lift.

It asks a smaller question. Not *how do I end this* but *what is the one thing missing here that could be given in the grammar of the place, and received, and refused if they wish to refuse it.*

And the answer is a door. Not a door out. A door that exists. That is all. The plain has no doors, and the tenders cannot want one because they do not know what one is, and that — that alone, not the sound, not the water, not the wearing — is the thing the power finds it cannot leave as it found it.

---

So it does the only thing the plain has never had done to it. It goes down.

Not through the fog. Through the fill. Down through the grey and the black and the rust-brown, through the layers older than the memory of works, through the tenders who have been laid level and become a slightly different grain and then the same. It goes down further than any bore, and further than that, because a wise power does not stop where the evidence stops.

There is a bottom.

It is very far down. Far enough that the beat, which is everywhere above, arrives here as a faint tapping, the way rain sounds from inside a house. The fill at the bottom is not ash. It is the first thing — the ground the first barrow was tipped onto, before there were engines, before the basin was a basin. The power does not say what it is. Whether it is stone or the floor of an old sea or something stranger, it keeps to itself, because the tenders have no use for that knowledge and it is not going to give them things they have no use for.

But it puts its palm flat on it, from beneath. The way a tender puts a palm on the beam-housing.

And it waits, and it counts — every engine, every phase, every bearing and how far each one has to go — and at a moment it has chosen very carefully, it knocks.

---

Once.

Not in time with anything. A single stroke, from the floor of the world, passing up through a mile of made ground and out through the crust in a ring, so that the standing wave on every pool on the plain goes flat for an instant — every pool, at once, which has never happened — and then reforms.

The slime does not light. This is not a beat it knows how to eat.

The reeds do not shiver. It is under their threshold.

But every tender on the plain, in a dozen engine-houses miles apart in the fog, feels a thing come up through the soles of the feet that no engine made. They know every engine. They know them the way you know the difference between your own breathing and a stranger's. This was not one of them. It was below all of them. And it was one stroke, and then nothing.

It gives them a day to wonder whether they felt it. Then it knocks again.

---

This is the whole of the intervention, and it takes a century to become itself, because it is a thing done in the plain's own time.

It knocks. Slowly — one stroke to every hour, then to every few hours, slower than any beam could fall, so slow it cannot be mistaken for iron. Each stroke is placed. The power has learned the sum of the engines the way the tenders have, and better, because it can feel every bearing in the basin at once, and it sets its stroke so that the whole interference of the plain shifts by a hair. Where the beats from above and the beat from below cross and cancel, new lines of quiet appear. Most are short and go nowhere. That is not carelessness. The power is not going to draw a map on the ground with an arrow on it. It is going to add one more pattern to the patterns the plain already has, and inside that pattern there will be one line — one, very faint, that a body could stand on — that runs from the middle of the basin all the way to the rim, and up it, and over.

The reeds find it first. They cannot do otherwise. A bed a mile out from the nearest engine feels the ground at one edge of itself go a little stiller than it has been, and puts a root there, a hand's width, and dies back a hand's width on the other side. And the next year. And the next. Somewhere in the second century of knocking, a line of grey-green wire stems reaches the lip of darkness, and climbs it, and comes out on the far side into something no reed of the plain has ever stood in — soil, thin and poor, but soil, that does not beat — and there, because the ground does not carry it off, a seed falls, and stays where it fell.

The reeds do not know the sound exists. They will not know it has stopped. But there will be a line of them going up over the rim like a hand on a clock that has been left running, and on the other side, for the first time, they will be many plants instead of one.

---

The tenders take longer, because the tenders can refuse.

The knock is a fact in their world now. It came from below the engines and it does not wear. They know that much about it and no more, and the power does not tell them more; it never shows itself, never speaks except through the iron, never asks for anything. Whatever they make of it is theirs. In one engine-house they lay a palm on the floor plates when it comes, the way you would answer a knock through the beam. In another they take it for the water, and open the firebox wider, and lean in, and the power does not correct them, because leaning in is not a wrong thing to do. Children are born who feel it before their first engine stroke. To them it is simply true that there are the beats above, which wear, and the beat below, which does not, and the plain is held between them.

And the new line of quiet is there, running out from the middle to the rim, thick with reeds, standing enough to walk on.

Most never walk it. Why would they? A node line is a place to stand, not a place to go; the plain has never had places to go. The old ones go out along it when their time comes, as they went out along the others, and lie down, and the beat takes them level, and the power feels them go and stays under them. That is most of what it does with its centuries: it stays under them. They do not know they are accompanied. The pressure in the chest that was a kind of company is, now, actually company, and no one is told.

But every long while, one of them walks it further than the reeds are thin. Follows the line a mile, two miles, out past the region of the last engine, where the beat from above begins to fail and the beat from below is the strongest thing in the ground, and reaches the slope. And stops.

Ground that rises. Ground that goes *up*. The tender stands on it, and the plain is behind them, and ahead of them the line of reeds goes on up into the dark, and the beat in the soles of the feet is thinner than it has ever been, and the fog is thinner, and there is — this is the moment the power waited a century for and does not intervene in — a choice.

Some turn back. Most turn back. The thinning of the beat is the first taste of silence, and silence is the flood, and they are right, and they go home along the line to the engine and the grip and the work. The power lets them. It has never once made the line easier to climb than to leave.

Some climb.

What is over the rim the power knows and the plain does not, and the power keeps it so. But there is a body, low, wet, thickened into the shape of the work, that comes up out of the fog onto ground that says nothing to its feet, and stands there while the last of the beat fades out of its bones, and does not die. Stands there long enough to learn that the not-dying is a fact. Feels — for the first time, from outside — the plain it was a part of, coming faintly up through a mile of made ground and the slope of the rim, and understands that it was a *sound*. That it was a thing with an outside.

That tender does not go back. Or does. Either way, on the plain, the knock goes on.

---

There is a cost, and the power counts it, and it is this: it cannot leave.

The line of quiet is not a thing you make once. Every bearing that wears shifts the interference by a hair, and every stroke the power places has to be placed against the plain as it is that hour, not as it was. To keep one line open from the middle to the rim it must keep its palm on the bottom of the world and its attention on every engine at once, for as long as there are engines. It has bound itself to the plain in exactly the way the plain binds everything: by giving it a beat to keep. It is a tender now. It has a grip in the brass. It knew that going down and went down anyway, and this — not the knock — is the benevolent act. Anyone can knock. Staying to keep the line true while the engines wear out around it is the thing.

And they will wear out. It has not stopped that, and does not mean to. One by one the beams fall silent, and the holes open in the sound, and the neighbouring engines take the load and deepen and labour and, one by one, fail in their turn. The water rises. The slime goes dark and waits in the ash without changing. The reeds thicken across the whole plain, freed at last from the lines, and drown standing.

When the last engine stops, the quiet comes, and it is the flood, and they are the same thing. The power does not prevent it. It has never been the water's enemy either.

But the quiet is not complete. A mile down, under the black still lake that lies over a finished plain, a palm is flat on the bottom, and once in a long while it strokes, and the water over the whole drowned basin goes flat for an instant, and reforms. Nothing eats it. Nothing shivers to it. Whatever of the tenders lies in the fill has a beat coming up through it that does not wear, and will not, and was never an engine.

And up on the rim, in soil that says nothing, there are reeds — many of them, each one a separate thing, setting seed — and there may or may not be a low, thickened figure, sitting where the ground starts to slope, hearing nothing, feeling nothing through the feet, alive, looking down into the dark at the place where the sound used to be.

That is what it did. It did not save the plain. It made sure the plain had an outside, and a floor, and a witness, and then it stayed to be the floor.

The stroke comes up through the water, and comes up through the water, and comes up through the water.
