Begin with how it arrives, because a power like this does not arrive the way a weather does, all at once and from one side. It arrives the way the cloud arrived, a very long time ago: by noticing that there was already something here to condense on.

It has been elsewhere. It has been in places that were loud with need, where the first thing it saw was the thing that was wrong and the second thing was what to do about it, and it has learned, over spans that make the bands in a dome look like a single breath, to distrust the speed of its own second thoughts. So when its attention settles over the tableland — settles, the word is exact; it comes down into the pearl and is held there the way the cloud is held, by warmth from below — the first thing it does is decline to hurry. It lets the place go on without it. It watches the water climb the braid. It counts, for something like a season, the intervals between the low note in the cloud, and finds them irregular, and finds the irregularity restful.

It is looking for the being that lives here. The question it was drawn by was phrased that way, singular, and the power's first discovery is that the question is a little wrong, or a little too small for the place, and its second discovery is that this is a kind of answer.

---

The mat first, because the mat is the only candidate that has never left.

The power comes very close to it. Not with a body — it has no body to lay flat on the turf, though it understands the gesture — but with the whole of its attention narrowed to a hand's breadth, and held. And the mat does what the mat does. It grows very slightly warmer and very slightly wetter beneath the attention. It registers a pressure that is not pressure and answers with the only things it has to answer with, and the power feels, for the first time in a long time, something like being *noticed back*, by something that is not thinking about it and never will.

It asks, in the way it asks things, whether the mat is well.

The pulse.

It asks whether there is anything the mat lacks, anything it has been reaching toward for an age and not reached, any place in the whole convex breadth of it that hurts.

The pulse. The cloud holds. Somewhere a dome breathes in.

And the power, who has seen a great many things that were not well and has become very good at finding the wrongness in a thing, goes looking for it here and does not find it. No bare patch. No sickness. Nothing that the mat has failed to take back, except the stones, and the stones are not a failure; they are a refusal, and the mat has drawn back from them in a fine pale ring the way a courteous thing draws back from another thing's boundary. It has been, as far as the power can tell, taking care of itself for longer than anything the power has watched, and it has not once gone wrong in any place the power can find, and the power is not so proud that it cannot recognize a competence greater than its own when it is standing on one.

Here is the first temptation, and the power names it so that it can set it down. It would be within its gift to give the mat a thought. To gather up the distributed noticing and fold it, once, so that somewhere in that continent of tissue there was a place that knew it was a place. The power has done this elsewhere. It has, sometimes, been right to.

It does not do it here. The mat has no center because it has never needed one. A thought is a kind of urgency — a here that is more here than elsewhere — and the whole genius of this place is that it has let go of here. To give it a thought would be to give it a summit, and the mat has spent an age eating summits. The power understands, with something that is almost embarrassment, that the wish to give the mat a mind is the wish to have something in the place that could thank it, and it has been a long time since it needed that, and it does not need it now.

So: the mat is the being, and the mat is well, and there is nothing to do for the mat. The power moves on with a lightness it did not expect.

---

The tenants, then. The power has a duty to suffering wherever it finds it, and it looks.

It looks at the grazers and finds a hunger that has stopped being hungry. It looks at the burrowers surfacing together and finds no fear in the lifted faces, only breath. It looks very carefully at the moth-things hanging over the channels, because a creature that waits all day for a thing that happens twice is a creature that some benevolences would call deprived, and it asks the moth-things, in the way it asks, whether they want the waiting to be shorter — and understands, before the question has finished forming, that the question has no purchase. The lean toward the water *is* the animal. Shorten the wait and there is nothing left to have been waiting. The power has known worlds where hunger was the engine of every cruelty, and it stands over a channel where hunger has become simply a direction, a slight inclination of a soft body toward a still surface, and it finds that it has nothing to teach here and something, perhaps, to learn.

It watches a grazer lie down for the last time. It watches the turf come up. It waits — it has time — until the dome is breathing, and then it asks the dome whether anything inside it is afraid, and the dome breathes, and the power cannot tell whether the answer is no or whether the question has already been taken back along with everything else, and it decides, after a long while, that these are the same answer.

The dark shape in the cloud passes over, once, and the power lets it pass without looking too hard. Some things are better left unresolved. If it is a piece of the ground that learned to float, then it is the mat's one act of leaving, and the power does not want to be the thing that pins it down.

---

The stone-carriers are harder, because the power can see the shape of sadness in them from outside, and it has spent an age learning to trust that shape, and here the place has been quietly correcting it.

It goes to the edge. It has no trouble finding the edge; it is not bound to walking. And there is an edge — a season's walk in one direction, further in others — where the mat finally thins and fails and falls away in a long green lip over bare gray rock that has not been touched by anything living since the mountains were still mountains. And there, in the rock, in shelters cut into the stone, there is something that is still a people. Not many. A very few. They keep no records the power can read, but they keep the habit, and the habit is this: once in a life, one of them picks up a stone the size of a head and turns away from everything they know and walks into the pearl, and does not come back.

The power watches them for a while. It watches a young one — young in the way of things there, which is not young — hold a stone in both hands and turn it, and turn it, until the thumb finds the place where a hollow will be, and set it down again, not yet.

Second temptation, larger than the first. The power could tell them. It could show them the line — where it goes, how far, how many, how the last dome and the last stone lie together at the end of it under the cloud. It could give the walking a destination. It could give the ones at the edge a picture of the ones who went in, warm, breathed on, grazed over, held. It could make the bargain legible, and it could make it *end*: it could bring the last carrier's last stone back to the edge, close the line into a circle, let the young one keep both hands empty.

It does none of this, and here is why, worked out slowly over the season it spends at the edge.

The bargain is chosen. That is the whole of it. A person picks up a weight they do not need to carry and walks into a place that will close over them, and the choosing is not incidental to the act — it *is* the act; the stone is only how the choosing is made heavy enough to feel in the arms for years. To give the walk a destination is to make it a delivery, and a delivery can be sent by someone else. To close the line is to tell them they are finished, and they have not asked to be finished. And to bring the stones back out would be to undo, across a line of lives, the one thing each of those lives arranged: that one small cold part of them would stay outside the keeping. The power is benevolent, and benevolence has a duty to relieve, and it has taken the power a very long time to learn that there are burdens which are not suffering, which are shapes a life has chosen to take, and that to lift them is not kindness but a kind of theft.

It looks at the far end of the line, where the hollows are shallower and the turf has not yet drawn as far back, and it feels the thing it came here half-expecting to feel, and it lets itself feel it, and it sets it down beside the young one's stone, not yet. There is still an edge. There is still one of them out in the pearl, walking, at the pace of the stone. That is not nothing. That is not sad. It is a line of lives that has not been finished, and the power has no right to finish it, and it leaves the edge without leaving any mark that the edge would recognize as a mark.

---

Which leaves you.

The power has saved you for last not because you matter most but because you are the only thing on the tableland whose situation is not settled, and it wanted to understand the settled things first so that it could see clearly what unsettled looks like here.

You are sitting with your back against a stone. There is a dish the shape of your sitting closing slowly around you. Green at the seams of your clothes. Bronze to the knee. The lamp-slugs know your warmth. When you press your ear to the ground the pulse has begun to sound like a thing you are inside. And your hand is on the hollow that fits a thumb, and it is not your thumb, and you have found yourself, this evening, not wanting to take it away.

The power sees what the stone-carriers would see if one of them came upon you, which is: someone who is being taken who has not carried anything in.

Here is the thing it turns over longest. The mat is not malicious. The mat is not even hungry. It will take you the way it takes everything, by growing toward what touches it and over what stops moving, and there will be no moment when this is done to you; you will simply, one gray morning, be a little more part of it than you were the morning before, and one gray morning after that you will not get up, and that will be warm and slow and not afraid, and the herds will graze you and the burrowers will thread you and the slugs will climb you at dusk. The power has just spent a season learning that this is not sad. It believes what it learned.

But the carriers chose it. Every one of them picked up the weight knowing. And you are drifting into the same end without having said yes, and the power finds — testing the thought against everything the place has taught it, waiting to see if the place will correct this too — that it still cares about the yes. Not about the outcome. It does not know which outcome is better for you and is not sure the question has an answer. It cares that whatever closes over you, you were awake when you let it.

Third temptation, and the smallest, and the one that costs it most to refuse: it could lift you out. It could take you back to the world you came from as easily as it went to the edge. It does not, because that is the mat's gesture turned inside out — a thing that grows over what stops moving — and you have not stopped moving, and you have not asked.

It could speak to you. But the place has flattened speech to nearness, and the power is not sure what it would say, and it suspects that anything it said would arrive as a call from just behind your shoulder, and you would turn, and there would be only the pearl, and you would be a little more alone than before.

It could do nothing. That is a real choice, and the power gives it real weight. The mat does nothing and the mat has never gone wrong.

---

What it does is this.

It thins the cloud.

Not for long. Not from nowhere: the tableland already does this, once in a great while, and the bands in the domes remember every time. The power only reaches into the slow chemistry of the deep black and tips it, by the smallest amount that will serve, so that for one day the exhalation falters and the lid lifts and the light comes down whole.

You are awake when it happens because the lamp-slugs go dark all at once instead of rolling, and the wrongness of that wakes you. And then there is a thing on the mat beside you that you have not seen in weeks and take a long moment to name. It is a shadow. It is *your* shadow, long in a low sun, lying across the turf toward the line of stones, with an edge to it, a place where it stops and the ground begins.

The herds lie down. All across the tableland the grazers lower themselves into their dishes, and the domes flush a brighter green, and the water stands up off the whole convex breadth of the world in a single rising veil, and the sky — you had forgotten that there was a sky, not a lid but a *distance* — is a color you have no recent word for. The stone at your back grows, over the course of the morning, very slightly warm. For one day it is not the coldest thing here. For one day it is only a stone, in the sun, with a hollow in it.

And you can see. That is the whole of the gift; the power was careful to give nothing else. You can see the line of stones going on over the swell and over the next swell and over the next, further than you had walked, further than you had guessed, a dotted gray sentence laid across the land toward a horizon that is, for the first time, actually a horizon. You can see, at the very limit of it, a slight unevenness in the green that might be the far lip of the world. And somewhere along the line, not at the end but nearer than you would have thought — you cannot be sure; it is very far and very pale and there is turf on its shoulders — there is a shape that is the wrong size for a dome, moving, at the pace of a weight, toward the last stone but one.

The power does not tell you what to make of this. It has arranged one day of edges in a place made of return, and set you down in the middle of it with your shadow beside you, and that is the end of what it will do. Whether you take your hand from the hollow. Whether you walk toward the pale shape, or away from the line, or toward the far lip, or lie back down in the dish that is the shape of you and let it close. Whether you understand, watching the carrier come on, that you could go to the edge and pick up a weight of your own and make the bargain properly, or understand that you were never one of them and the mat is not yours to give yourself to. The power does not know which of these it hopes for. It has decided, at the edge, watching a young one turn a stone, that hoping for one of them would be a way of carrying the stone for you, and the whole point of the stone is who carries it.

Toward evening the cloud closes. The chemistry finds its rate again. The lamp-slugs come up, rolling, dome to dome. Your shadow shortens and dims and is gone, and the stone at your back is cold, and the pearl is the pearl.

---

The power leaves without watching what you do next.

This is the last of its restraints, and the one it has learned most slowly: that benevolence which stays to see the result is asking to be paid, and the coin it is asking for is the one thing this place does not mint. It has done one small thing inside the tableland's own grammar. It does not need to know whether the thing was right. It is not sure the tableland admits of right. It goes up through the closing cloud past the dark drifting shape, which does not look at it, and out into the distance that is a sky, and its attention thins over the roof of the world and is elsewhere.

It leaves no stone. It noticed the wish to — noticed it at the edge, noticed it again beside you, the wish to have one small cold part of itself stay outside the keeping — and it named the wish and set it down and let the mat grow over it, because the power is old and patient and distributed and has no center, and it recognized, in the end, that it was more like the mat than like the ones who carry stones into it.

What it leaves instead is a band.

Somewhere far below the green, in the yellow zone and then the brown and then the black that is not rot, the day of light is already being pressed into a pale layer between two dark ones. It will thicken. It will sink. In a hundred seasons, in a thousand, someone — one of the carriers, or something that comes after them, or you, if you are still here and still digging — will cut down into a dome and count the bands and find this one among the hundreds, and it will look like every other. A pale layer. A time the cloud thinned. A thing the tableland does, once in a great while, on its own.

Which is, the power decided on its way out into the sky, the only kind of mark worth leaving in a place like this: one that is kept, and kept as the place's own.
