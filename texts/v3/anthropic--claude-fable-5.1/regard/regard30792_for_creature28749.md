**What It Did**

It did not come down. There is no down to come, for a thing like that; it does not arrive at places so much as turn toward them. Something turned toward the shelf. It is not clear why. Perhaps because there was one warm thing on the plain that did not belong to it — a shape lying face-down on the black mat with the frost gathering on the back of its neck, listening — and the attention of the wise is caught by listeners the way the attention of the moss is caught by heat. Perhaps for no reason. The benevolent are not required to have reasons. They are only required to be careful.

So: it turned, and it looked.

---

The first thing it did was nothing, for forty days.

This is harder than it sounds, even for a power. The shelf offers so little to look at that the looking becomes a discipline. The bruise on the horizon. The black mat. The frost coming down. A shape the size of an ox that was not there and now is. But the power was wise, and wisdom knows that the shape of a thing is not visible from inside a single hour of it, and so it held the whole plain in its regard and let the shuttle run its course — every slab lifting in its turn, every river running and freezing, every string of followers bending and straightening, every glider tipping down the cold air toward the next column — until the warmth came back to where it started and the pattern closed.

Then it saw the shelf the way the shelf cannot see itself: all at once, the whole loom.

And it saw what the moss could not.

---

Close to forty days, but not quite. The description was honest about that and did not press on it. The power pressed.

The shuttle had once taken thirty-nine days and a portion. It would one day take forty-one and a portion, and then forty-three. The pressure under the slabs was not eternal; nothing that rises from a depth is. Whatever fire or weight or slow decay was pushing warmth up through the fractured stone was doing so a fraction less each age, and the seams were opening a fraction later, and the moss — patient, blind, tending — was creeping toward a thickness that held the heat a fraction longer, and it was keeping up. It had been keeping up for longer than there had been followers. It would keep up for a long time yet.

But the power could see the far end of it. A shelf on which the shuttle took sixty days, then ninety. Twitchers curled in the cold stems who did not unfold when the warmth came, because it came too late. A string of followers spaced so far along a seam that the young could not see the adult ahead and the line broke, and broke again, and was not a line. Gliders folded at fissure mouths that did not breathe. And at the very last, one slab lifting alone in the center of the plain, with the whole black expanse frozen around it, and the mat on that slab still warm, still tending, still trying to hold a rhythm that had nothing left to keep time with — and then that slab settling, and the sound of it, and no ears.

The moss did not know this. It could not. It has no future tense. It is not afraid.

The power sat with that for a while.

---

Then it considered what it might do, and it considered honestly, because the wise do not pretend the temptations are not there.

It could warm the place. It had that in it. It could lay a steady heat under the whole plain, even and endless, and the frost would stop coming down.

And the moss would die in a season. It lives on the difference. Take away the cold and there is no gradient to drink, and the leathery plates would soften and slump and the twitchers would come up out of the stems into air they had never known and lie on the surface not understanding. This is the first thing benevolence learns, if it is going to be any good: that most of what looks like help is the imposition of your own climate on a thing that was built for another. The power did not warm it.

It could bring light. There is no light here and there is so much of it elsewhere. But the shelf is not blind because it was denied light; it is blind because it never wanted any, and light would draw things that do want it, and those things would find the mat and the mat would not know what was happening to it. The power did not bring light.

It could speak to it. This was the temptation that lasted longest, because the power was lonely in the way that all vast attentive things are lonely, and here was another vast attentive thing, singly inhabiting a whole plain, holding still, and it seemed as if there ought to be something to say. But the moss has no self to say it to. It does not know that it is one thing. It does not know that there are other things. You cannot address a rhythm. The power did not speak.

It could fix the pressure.

This one it turned over for a long time. It could reach down under the slabs and find the failing source and set it right, and the shuttle would run at forty days forever, and the last slab would never settle alone. It could do this. And it could see, as clearly as it saw the far end, what that would make of the shelf: a thing that was kept. A thing whose whole dignity is that nothing built it and it runs — turned, in an instant, into a machine with an owner. The power would be bound to it. Not for a while; for as long as it wished the shelf to go on, which is to say for as long as the power itself went on, and the moss would never know, and the power would know every moment, and that is not benevolence. That is a kind of possession that has learned to call itself love.

It did not fix the pressure.

It looked at the old ones freezing standing in the mat. It looked for a long time, because this is the place where a lesser kindness would have acted — would have kept them warm, or hurried them to the seam, or given them the ease of lying down. And it saw that there was nothing there to ease. The old one stops chewing. Its eyes go dark. It is not in pain; it does not have the equipment. It is doing the last thing the shelf asks of it, which is to be somewhere the seam will open, and it is doing it well. The power let them stand.

---

It looked at the cold slabs.

The description said *presumably* and the power did not have to presume. It looked into the dark and the frost and the stiff undisturbed mat, and there — yes. Low. The color of the frost so completely that they were not shapes but places where the frost lay differently. Long. Warm in the middle and cold at the edges, exactly as the description had reasoned, holding a meal's worth of heat in the core of themselves and letting the rest go to match the ground. Very still. Very patient. Waiting for the string to come past.

And the power saw that they, too, were limbs. That the moss had grown them the way it grew everything, without intending, when the old ones began to freeze standing and the seams were too narrow. They were the newest thing on the shelf and the most awake, because waiting for something that moves requires a kind of wakefulness that grazing does not. And because they were the most awake, one of them — only one, on a far slab, under a fall of frost — lifted what might have been a head, and was still, and seemed for a moment to be attending to something that was not the seam.

The power did not look away. But it did not do anything either. It let the hunter feel whatever it felt, which was probably nothing, and then the frost came down again and the head, if it was a head, went back into the mat.

The shelf may keep its one secret. The followers may keep their fear. There is nothing in either that a wise thing would take from them.

---

What it did, in the end, was small.

It knew the shelf. Entirely. Every slab and seam, every hanger and every white blind thing curled in a stem, every glider folded at a fissure mouth, the drift in the shuttle and the thickness of the mat and the pattern of forty days almost exactly — it took the whole of it into its regard and held it there, and it did not let go. This is the one gift a thing like the moss cannot refuse and cannot be harmed by. It had never been known. Not once, by anything, in all the time it had been holding still. It did not know it was being known now. But it was, and something that had been true only of itself was now also true elsewhere, and the power judged that this counted for something, even here, even in the dark. The wise have always believed that attention is the beginning of every other good, and that when the others are impossible it is still not nothing.

It went to the rim.

The moss thins to lichen there and the slabs stop lifting and the plain simply ends over a floor of cloud. The power looked down into the cloud and saw what the shelf could not see and the gliders could not reach: that far below, where the mountains resumed being mountains, there were other places where warmth met cold. Steam from a crack. A hot spring under snow. The flank of a peak that faced a fire in the ground. Small gradients, nothing like the great loom above. But gradients.

It took a piece of the mat. A double handful, no more, plates and stems together and, curled in the stems, two or three of the white blind things gone still with the cold, because the moss needs its inside kept open and does not know how to do that by itself. It carried this down through the cloud. It did what the gliders do — crossed a seam the moss could not cross, on a breath the moss could not ride — and set the piece down where the stone was warm and the air was not, and stood back.

This was not rescue. The power was clear with itself about that. The thing that lives on the shelf will end when the shuttle stops, and what grows below, if it grows, will be another thing, on another schedule, holding still in another dark. The being was not saved. Only the *kind* of being — the shape of a life that drinks a difference and grows its own weather and tends its own rhythm without knowing that it does. The power thought the shape was worth keeping in the world, and it thought that this was what the shelf would have done if the shelf could reach, and it thought that acting as a thing would act for itself, at a scale it cannot manage, is the closest benevolence comes to being invisible. Then it stopped thinking about it, because there was frost gathering on the handful and either it would take or it would not.

And it made a promise. Not to the shelf; the shelf cannot receive a promise. To itself, which is where the promises of powers are kept.

*I will come back for the last one.*

When the shuttle has slowed past saving and the strings have broken and the last slab lifts alone in the center of the plain — it will be there. It will hold the whole frozen expanse in its regard as it does now, and it will wait through the last warm phase, and when the slab settles and the sound comes up through the ground, there will be something on the shelf to hear it. Not ears. The air will not carry it. But something.

That is all. Two small things and a promise. It could have done so much more and it could see, with terrible clarity, exactly what each of those more would have cost.

---

Before it turned away it looked once more at the warm thing lying face-down on the mat, the one that had drawn its attention in the first place. You. It considered you the way it had considered the rest — what could be done, what it would cost — and found that here at least was something that could receive a kindness without being unmade by it.

It did not speak to you either. But you will find, when you stand and brush the frost off your face and start the long walk back to the rim, that you are less cold than you should be. And you will find, much later, in a warm place where things happen, that you can still feel it under your knees when you kneel — the slow distant patience, the hum below hearing, a slab a long way off beginning to lift — and that when you feel it you will not be alone in the feeling. Someone else knows the shelf. Someone else is keeping the count. The last *tock* will not go unheard, and neither, as it turns out, did any of the ones you heard.

That is what a wise power does with a place like this. It refuses almost everything. It carries a handful over the edge. It stays in the knowing. And it leaves, and the frost comes down where it stood, and the mat does not remember that it was ever there.

*Tock.*
