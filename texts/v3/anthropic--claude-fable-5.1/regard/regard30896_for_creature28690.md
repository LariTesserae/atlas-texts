It would come the way the visitor came, along the blade of the ridge, because there is no other way and because a power that is wise knows the value of arriving by the only door. It would stand at the mouth for a while in the two grey hours and listen to the note the wind makes. It would notice the half-plant in the angle of the floor, and the orange crust keeping pace with the mountain, and the flakes in their line. It would go in.

And then it would do the thing that is hardest for a power to do, which is to take a long time deciding.

---

The first thing it would want is to help, because that is what benevolence is, the wanting. And the first shape the wanting takes is the obvious one: this is wrong, this cannot go on, they are being lifted into air that will kill them, carry them down. It would feel the whole cold weight of that. Thousands of small lives spent to the bone, and a mountain patiently raising them toward the place where the last of them will breathe once and not again.

But it would hold the wanting in one hand and the room in the other and look at both, and it would see that the wanting is about the valley. Carry them down: to what? The valley animal they were is gone. The fur is too long for it, the wing gathers instead of folding, the eyes have sealed under thickened lids. Set the mass in a warm wood at dusk and it would not wake — waking is not available to it in any quick sense — and while it lay there in its slow turn the warm air would run through it like fever, the fungus would bolt, the mites would breed, and the thing that lives in the room would come apart into thousands of things that cannot live anywhere. That is not rescue. That is dissolving a body to save its cells.

So it would put that down. And it would try the next one — stop the mountain, or slow it — and put that down faster, because a power that is wise does not take a mountain's forty thousand years away from it for the sake of a forty-year room. The mountain is also a being. The ridge is also new and also breaking and also going somewhere. Benevolence that has to choose between them has stopped being wise and become merely fond.

---

Then it would do what it should have done at the start, which is stop asking what it wants for the room and ask what the room wants.

And the answer is difficult, because the room does not want in the way the word means. No single animal in it wants anything more than the wall's heat and the neighbor's click. But the mass — the power would put its hand against it, and feel the roar shift a hair toward its palm, and feel that the mass does have something like a want. It wants to stay warm. It wants to know where it is. It wants the whistle answered. And it wants, in the way a river wants, to go deeper in.

Every one of those it already has. The warmth it makes itself. The knowing it makes itself, click to click, wall to wall. The whistle is still answered, though the gap is wide and widening. The direction it has. The power, holding its hand there, would understand that it has walked into a life that is not asking to be saved. It is asking, if anything, to be allowed to finish.

That is a hard thing for a benevolent power to hear. It is the thing the wise part of it has to say to the benevolent part, in the dark, over the roar: *this is not a wound. This is a shape.*

---

So it would come to the question of what, if anything, is left to do, and there are three things it would find, and it would do two of them.

The first is the whistle. The power could answer it. It could stand in the hollow and, when the thin rising note comes down from the roof, send the reply back at once, the way it came in the first year, a heartbeat apart — and the mass would feel that, all of it, a sudden closing of the gap, and something in the roar would ease.

It would not. Not because it would be wrong to, but because the interval is not a failure. The interval is the truest record the room keeps of itself. It is how long the mass takes, now, to hear its own voice. Shorten it and you have told the room it is younger than it is, and a lie told in kindness to a thing that cannot know it is being lied to is still a hand on the scale. Instead the power would do what the ears do. It would listen. It would count. It would stay long enough to hear the call and, a held breath later, the answer, and it would hold the length of that breath the way you hold a measurement you mean to keep. Somewhere, when the room is a very different room, there will be one thing that remembers how long the silence was between the question and the reply, and that the reply always came.

---

The second thing is the wall at the back.

The power would go there — feeling its way, the fur pressing close on both sides — to where the two slabs close on each other and there is no more room, and it would put its hand on the stone where the drift is headed, and it would feel what the animals feel, which is that the stone is warmer here, and also what they cannot, which is that the stone here is under strain. The ridge is young. It has not finished breaking. Somewhere in this wall there is a plane along which it will one day let go, the way the slab let go forty years ago and leaned and made a room. That will happen with or without anyone. The only things not yet decided are where, and when, and which way the new face opens.

And here the power would find the one intervention that lies with the grain of the world instead of against it. It would not push. It would not hurry. It would simply choose, out of the several planes along which this stone could fail, the one that opens outward — not onto a deeper crack of black ice but onto the north face of the ridge and the enormous thin air beyond it. And it would let the mountain's own slow strain do the rest, in the mountain's own time.

Because look at the wings. Too large for the body, gathered like sails, made over forty years of slowed lives for exactly this air, where a valley wing beats and finds nothing. They have never been used for anything but a glide across a pool. The colony has been drifting, one small error at a time, toward the darkest part of the room, and every animal that made that glide went a little further in than it needed to because the roar was louder that way. The power would not stop the drift. It would only see to it that the drift arrives at a door.

Not tomorrow. The mass is not ready for a door tomorrow; it would be a crack of cold in the warmest wall and the animals nearest it would stop and be let down and become floor. But the drift is slow and the strain is slow and the power is patient, and it would set the plane so that the wall lets go in the season the wind comes up the north face, and lets go when the mass has arrived at it and has spent long enough against it that the stone is warm on both sides of the fault. And then, one day, a body that has unfolded from the cool surface to glide deeper in will glide, instead, out — out of the roar into a wind that is going somewhere, on a wing that was built for it, with a click that carries a long way in thin air.

Perhaps they will all go. Perhaps they will find a lower cleft on a lower ridge and slow down again and make another room. Perhaps they will go up. The power would not know, and would not arrange to know, because the point is not the destination. The point is that a thing which came in on the wind should be given the chance to leave by it, and that the sum of ten thousand small errors toward the dark should turn out, when the dark opens, to have been ten thousand small steps toward the only exit.

That is what it would do. One choice, about which of several fractures gets to be the one, made in advance, and then hands off.

---

The third thing it would not do, and would understand why.

It would want to find the visitor. The one who knapped at the mouth and burned an hour of fat and laid the flakes pointing in. A power can find people. It could learn when, and who, and whether they came back, and whether they told anyone, and whether anyone believed them. It could see to it that they were remembered, or that they were not troubled again by what they had seen, or that the edge they carried down off the ridge did some good.

But standing at the mouth on the way out, in the grey light, it would look at the line of flakes and understand that it was being taught something by a person with a stone tool. Here is what you do when you come upon a life that is not yours and does not need you and cannot be moved: you witness it. You put down a few small things so the place is not unmarked by having been seen. You point them in, so the next one knows where to look. And you keep your speed, and let your speed carry you back out, and you do not stay.

The visitor did the wise thing already, whoever they were, without a power's resources and without a power's long deliberation. The power would leave them alone in their own time. It would not add a flake to the line. The line is finished.

---

It would stand on the blade of the ridge, three strides along the crest, at the place where the roar cuts off and there is only wind. It would have done almost nothing: listened to a whistle and remembered the length of a silence; touched a wall and chosen a plane. The room would go on breathing exactly as it had. The mountain would go on rising, the width of a fingernail a year, and the lichen would keep pace with it, and the half-plant would stay half.

And if benevolence is a wanting, the power would notice that the wanting had changed shape in there. It had gone in wanting to save something and come out wanting something quieter and stranger, which was for the thing in the room to be, in the end, what it had been all along — a single warm slow animal that knows where it is, every cell of it speaking to every other in the dark — and to carry that knowing out through a door in the direction it was already going, into air made for its wings, on a wind that already knows the way.

Then it would go down. It would keep its speed. Behind it the stone would close over the sound of something breathing in the mountain, and the room would not know it had been visited, and that would be right.
