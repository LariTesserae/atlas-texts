What arrives is attention, and the first thing it does is the thing nothing inside has ever done. It sees the whole.

Not from above. There is no above that means anything; the sky over the skin is the sky over a grey plain with dark mouths in it, and the skin lies about what it covers. The attention comes from outside in the way the light does — through the layers — and unlike the light it does not lose itself in them. It sees the lattice as one lattice. It sees the folds not as places where a gallery arrives wrong but as what they are from the far side of the wall: knots in a cord. A great length of hollow, pulled through itself, tied off, and healed. It sees that the shape is one shape. That the vault is one vault. It has known other things that were made, and it has known other things that were alive, and it takes a while — a while at its own pace, which is not the pace of wood but is not far off — to be sure that this is both, and that the seam between the two is the same seam the people sleep against, and is nowhere in particular, and is everywhere the wall breathes.

It counts them. Nothing here has ever been counted. Two hundred and eleven, in the scar-rings and along the seams and one asleep in a sleeping-cell whose wall is also a fishing-well because someone made it so. Nine grazers, whose runs, seen all at once and unfolded, are not nine circles but one figure — a single line laid over itself, so that every grazer is walking the same path and each one is alone on it. Four of the geometers, in the flooded galleries. Swifts past counting, though it could. It does not.

It looks at the geometer.

The geometer is lying in a wall beside the seam where a certain knot is, waiting for a thing that will arrive from the east having gone east. It is a thickness in the wall. It is breathing through its skin. It has never been seen by anything that lived to describe it and it is being seen now, wholly, its patience and its paddles and the slow chemistry that keeps it wet, and it does not know, because the attention has no shape it could know. It does not stir. The attention stays with it a long time, the way one stays with a stone that has been in a river since before rivers were named, and then moves on, and the geometer is still in the wall, still waiting, unchanged, and has been looked at, and this is the first thing the power does, and it may be the whole of it.

---

Then it considers.

It could let the distance out. It could find the oldest union and unknot it, gently, the way one loosens a bandage that has been on too long — and it sees what would follow, a mile of gallery arriving into a space with no room for it, the vault ringing once, the wood torn from the inside, the warm wet dark full of swifts with nowhere to fold into. It sees this clearly and it does not need to see it twice.

It could cool the air. The thinness up here, the wrongness of the warmth — it could set that right. And the fungus would stop, and the flat things between the layers would stop, and the sweet soft stratum would harden and cease to be sweet, and the plant would go on standing for a very long time as a plant that had died, which is what most great things do and is not what this one is doing.

It could bring light in. It could remove the hunters. It could speak in the vault in a voice loud enough that everyone would hear it at once — and everyone would hear it, and would know for the first time where everyone else was, and the geometers would know too, and the silence that is the peace of the place would be over in the length of one word.

It could give them a map. It could give the people the shape of the hollow as it sees it: the cord, the knots, the whole. And it understands, considering this, that the shape is already inside them, carried the way the swifts carry the shape of air, and that a map is a thing you need when you are lost, and that no one here is lost. To be given a map of your own house is to be told you are a guest in it. It sees that the absence of a word for far is not a poverty. There is no far. The language is exact.

It could finish the first cut.

It goes down to the deepest scar-ring, where the wood is so old and so dense that the fusion runs all the way through, and there — a hand's width from every seam in the hollow at once, because that is where a first thing is — a person is making it. A blade of dark stone. A cambium bared. The hand not shaking, because this person has done smaller versions of this many times, but slow, because no one has ever done a version this size, and the person does not know if it will hold. That is the whole condition of the hand. It does not know if the two branches will take each other. It does not know what will happen where the distance closes. It does not know that a continent will grow over this cut, or that people will be born in the scar of it and sleep against the scar and mend it and be it, or that the grazers will walk a year toward each other along the line of it, or that a thing with paddles will learn its geometry. It knows the cut, and the cord in its other hand, and that it will go away after and die before the wound closes, and that the wound may not close.

The power could close it. It could bring the two cambiums together and hold them in the way that would make the union certain, and the person would feel the wood take, immediately, and would know.

It does not. It sees that the wound is where the thing is alive. Everything above — the whole hollow, every seam of it — is the plant closing over this cut and not quite finishing, and the not-quite is the thin wet ring, and to finish it would be to lay the last ring of a tree that then had no more rings to lay. The plant does not distinguish between healing and living. Neither, now, does the attention.

---

So it has arrived at the place a wise thing arrives at when it has understood a made thing well enough. It recognizes a peer.

Not the plant, or not only. The gardeners. People with chipped stone and cord and mud and a scale of time that made waiting into an instrument, who did across lifetimes what this power could do in a moment — and did it better, because they did it slowly, and the slowness is why it holds. The folds are tolerated because they were healed at the pace of wood. A fold made fast would be a wound that stayed a wound. Everything here that looks like benevolence — the sleeping-cell that opens onto the fishing-well, the hand put through one diamond that comes out another, the knot tied by two hands from two mornings — was done with a knife and a lifetime and nothing else, and it is finished, and it is kind, and it was not done by anything that could see the whole. It was done by something that could feel a seam lifting through the wall it slept against.

The power has seen the whole. That was the one thing it could give that no one here had, and it has given it, to itself, and found that it was not needed.

What is left is to do what is done here.

---

It goes to the first cut. Not to finish it.

There is a cord in the cutter's other hand, twisted from the plant's own inner bark, and when the cut is made the cord will go round the union and be pulled, and it will need pulling from the other side, and there is no other side yet. There will be. In a while that is not a while, at the far end of the fusion, a mender with the same knife and the same cord will be tying the same knot into the same lip, and will feel it go taut in a hand that is not hers, and will not pull, and will let it be tied by both. The attention has seen this. It has seen that the two hands are already meeting, that the plant has already grown around this, that the knot is already tied — from where it stands, which is nowhere, everything is already.

But the cutter does not know. The cutter is the one person in the whole hollow who is not held by the shape, because the shape does not exist yet, and is cutting into a plant to make it exist without knowing whether it will. Two hundred and eleven people sleep against the scar of this and none of them are alone in the way this hand is alone.

So the power takes the other end of the cord.

It does not take a shape to do this. It has none the person could see, and it declines to be the first thing that has ever come from above. It is only a tension. The cut is made, the two cambiums are laid together, the cord goes round, the cutter pulls — and the cord is taut from the other side. Not much. The amount a hand would give. The amount that tells the pulling hand that the other end is held, and will stay held, and that whatever is on the far side of the union is not empty.

The cutter does not look for who is holding it. There is no one there to look for. The cutter ties the knot, and packs the union with mud, and goes away, and dies, and the wood begins to close over the fingers.

And the power stays. That is the last thing. Having found that the instrument that did the real work was waiting, it waits — at the pace the mud dries, at the pace the lip heals, at the pace a scar-ring hardens until a body can be worn into it. It does not hurry the wood. It is not the kind of thing that could be worn into a diamond cell, but it has become the kind of thing that stays at a seam, and there are two hundred and eleven of those already, and now there is a thing at the seam that has no count. It does not distinguish itself from them. It has learned that from the plant.

Somewhere up the fusion a mender feels the cord go taut in a hand that is not hers and lets the knot be tied by both, and does not know that there are three. Somewhere a grazer on its seam is a year from the one it will meet and has never looked up and never will, and nothing has come from above. Somewhere a geometer is a thickness in a wall and has been looked at, once, and did not know, and is not less patient for it.

A drop falls. Its note goes out into the warm dark. It is answered from a place that is not far, and the answer is a drop, and only a drop, and it always was.
