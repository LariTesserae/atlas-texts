It comes up by the scarp.

It does not have to. There is nothing in it that a wall of wet basalt can stop, and it could be on the mat, or in the boilerhouse, or at the bottom of the shafts, by wanting to be. But the plateau has arranged itself so that the scarp is the only way, and the first thing it does — before anything it might be said to *do* — is arrive by the way the place has made. It puts its hands on the fresh black edges. They would cut a glove. They do not cut it. It notices that they do not, and notices that this is already a difference between itself and the place, and after that it climbs more carefully.

At the top the air is thin and tastes of iron and moss, and it stands for a long time doing nothing.

---

It hears the tone. It hears what anyone hears — the thousand stations falling out of step and back, the bell-note that never fades — and one thing more. Under the engines correcting toward each other through the rock there is a thousand-and-first correction, slower, coming up from below, matching them. It listens to this for a while. Two things attending to each other through a country of stone, each adjusting to the other's adjustment, each believing that what it feels is the weight of a continent, and neither knowing that the other is listening.

It does not do anything about this yet. It walks inward.

Not far. It does not need to cross the plateau to know it; distance here repeats. It stops at the first station, at the edge of the yard, where the mat is cut to the concrete.

---

It kneels and parts the moss.

The pale columns go down, packed and whole, every cell the plant ever made. It puts its fingers into them and they are cold and intact and do not crumble, because nothing has been at them. It thinks about the moss. The moss is not in pain. The moss is at its depth. It grows and it compresses and it is exactly what it is, and if it has a nature, its nature is being met: whatever it puts out, the plateau takes back, and the plateau takes back exactly that much and no more. There are worse fates for a moss.

A spore comes down in the film of water beside its hand, riding toward the channel. It watches it go. It could lift it out with one finger and set it on the mat where the water is slower and see whether, in this cold, in this light, it would do the thing spores do. It could change the population of the moss by one.

It lets it go to the grate. Nobody asked on the moss's behalf, and the moss is not asking. It has learned, over a very long time, that the mercies you were not asked for are the ones you have to be most careful with, and it has decided — for now, at this station — to be careful.

---

In the dormitory it reads the rota.

A week filled in, in a hand it does not recognise either. Then blank. It stands in front of the board for as long as it stood at the scarp edge. It has seen many kinds of clock and this is one: a week that was planned for and then a week that was not, and the not-planned week as white as the day it was pinned. Everything else on the plateau is arranged so as not to need time. This is the one thing that is waiting for some.

It understands, then, something about the place that the pressure under the feet does not tell you. The plateau does not only hold *up*. It holds *open*. Eight hundred beds a station, across a country, made and warm and never dented — that is not the negative of people who left. That is a place set for someone, and kept. Whoever raised this raft in a single motion and lit the furnaces and filled in one week meant it to be lived on, and stopped, and the plateau has been keeping the door open ever since with everything it has, because no one told it the guests were not coming.

It touches the pillow. It does not dent it. That is not its to do.

---

In the boilerhouse it watches a valve ease off a fraction and ease back.

It has watched a great many things work. This is not automatic. Automatic is a groove; this is vigilance. The engines are attending to the pressure with a fineness that reads as fear, or would in a person — the way a hand holds a rope when it has been told that if the rope moves everything falls.
