**Everyone else is the shape of its leaving.**

That is the sentence the observer never finished, and it is the first thing the power understands when it arrives, because the power does not arrive the way the observer did. It does not come over the rim with feet. It has been watching the footsteps blare across the wax and it has no wish to add to that. It comes the only way the bowl has an organ for: as a slight, even, all-over change in temperature, so uniform that no pad anywhere can read it as inward or outward. Not warmth. Not cold. Simply a degree of attention laid across the floor, and then held still.

Several thousand builders pause. Not startled — noting. A cloud. Then they go on.

The power stays like that for a long time before it does anything else. This is not hesitation. It is the courtesy of learning the local speed. In the bowl, nothing that matters happens faster than the wax cures, and a benevolence that moved quicker than that would be a kind of violence no matter what it intended. So it waits until it can feel the tide of the traffic — up yields to down, down is what the city is for — and until it can distinguish one builder from the next by the syllable of its footfall, and until it can hear, beneath the footfall, the bass note. The sealed ones. The depth. It listens to that longest of all.

---

Here is what it sees that the observer could not.

The stone is not cooling at one rate. There is the slow fade the plants and builders have been following, patient as a season, and there is beneath it a second, deeper cooling that comes in pulses too long for anything living to have registered as pulses. The vent below this bowl is not dying evenly. It is dying in breaths. And the next breath out — the power reads it the way it reads everything, whole — will come before the frontier reaches the rim. Not long before. A season. Perhaps two. The zone of workable warmth will contract faster than a city can climb, for a little while, and then settle. Most of the nursery tier will find itself, one morning, on wax that has gone below the range. The pale ones with the gut-thread showing will press their soft sides to a wall that has stopped answering.

And on the far side of the mountain, on rock that folded over itself so recently the glass still has no dust on it, there is one builder standing. It has been standing there for a length of time that would embarrass the observer. It has no eyes and no voice and the rock under its pads is still too hot to work, and it is waiting for the rock to become right, and the rock will become right — the power checks this the way you would check a sum — one season after the city, having reached the rim and found the rim cold, would have needed it.

Two gaps, then. A gap in the warmth and a gap in the timing. Neither is anyone's fault. Neither is part of the story the bowl tells about itself, which is a story of climbing and of following, of putting out your newest self at the edge and letting the center go. These gaps are not that. They are simply physics arriving without regard to what it interrupts.

---

The power is tempted, and it takes its temptations seriously, one at a time, because it is wise and because the bowl has taught it that anything worth doing is worth doing slowly.

*Warm the stone.* Reach down under the ash and put back what has faded, hold the center at the temperature it was when the great horn-trunks were rosettes, and let the city stop climbing. It would work. Everything here would live. And the power sees what would follow: the darkest builders, descending against the traffic, reaching the base and finding it still warm, still workable, still speaking — and having no reason to go in. Nobody sealing. The bass note fading as the last of the old cells fell silent in their own time and were not joined. A city that stopped remembering because nothing in it ever had to stop. The power lets this one go. You do not save a thing by ending what it does.

*Move them.* Lift the whole living tier — nursery, storage, galleries, the fleet of cleaners spreading like a stain — and set it down on the far vent when the far vent is ready. Spare them the rim. Spare them the conferring. And the power sees that too: a people whose entire language is *what I have walked today*, arriving somewhere they have not walked. Maps with nothing to compare. Heads pressed together on a ramp that has no history under it. They would learn the new wall in a generation. They would never learn what they had lost, because the thing they lost was the walk. The power lets this one go too.

*Speak.* This is the hardest to set down, because it is what a benevolent power most wants to do — to be known, at least a little, by what it helps. But the power has been standing in the floor for long enough now to understand what speech would be. The wall already says everything that can be said in the only grammar there is: warmer, cooler, softer, someone has been here, someone is coming down carrying. Any message the power laid into the stone would arrive as geology. It would be read, exactly and completely, as the stone being the stone. There is no register in which it could sign its name. Which means the only speech available to it is the kind that does not announce itself as speech — and the power sits with that for a while, and finds that it is not a limitation. It is an instruction.

*Spare the moths.* No. The power looks at the mica flakes catching the noon light in the newest wax and it does not even finish forming this one.

---

So: what it does.

It closes the first gap by lending the stone a little of its own patience. Not heat. There is no new heat in this. The vent's next breath out still comes; it simply comes over four seasons instead of one, spread thin enough that the frontier, climbing at the speed a frontier climbs, stays just inside it. The nursery tier wakes one morning to a wall that is a hair cooler than yesterday, which is what it wakes to every morning. Nobody's map disagrees with anybody's. The power has done the largest thing it will do here and it is indistinguishable from the rock continuing to be rock, and this is the point. A gift that can be told from weather is a debt. This one cannot be told from anything.

It does not close the second gap. It goes and looks at it instead.

Over the rim, down the far shoulder of the mountain where the wind actually reaches, onto the black glass that has not yet learned to be warm the right amount. The scout is where it was. Six pads flat, dome settled, the plates not sliding, so still that a stick-thing would call it kin. The rock under it is hot enough that a builder from the bowl would have backed off it by now, and this one has not, because someone has to be the first to know when the rock is right and knowing takes standing there.

The power considers cooling the glass by a season. It would be easy. It would be a mercy of the ordinary kind. And it finds that it does not want to, and it takes a while to understand why, and the reason when it comes is this: the scout is doing the one thing the bowl does that the power has never had to do. It is waiting for something without being able to see it. The power sees everything. It has never once in its existence stood on a surface and not known when the surface would change. To reach down and make the rock ready is to take from the scout the whole of what the scout is, which is an eyeless creature keeping faith with a temperature.

So the power does not touch the glass. It does the other thing. It settles into the rock beside the scout — under it, around it, the way it settled into the floor of the bowl — and it waits too.

---

This is the part that has no form the observer would recognize as action. There is a mountain and a piece of new rock and a small domed animal standing on it, and there is a power that could do anything and is doing this. Seasons go over. The clouds pass below and never rise. Back in the bowl, the frontier reaches the last ledge before the rim and the moss-mat crisps out across the final margin of black glass and the rollers roll to the edge and crack and become spirals, and the great horn-trunks in the center, leaning toward a wall that no longer answers, go still, and the darkest builders descend past the nursery and choose their openings and lay their last thin layer from inside. The bass note deepens. The city becomes a ring.

And on the far shoulder, the rock cools — at its own speed, in its own time, without anyone's help — to the temperature at which a builder's pad can rest on it without harm. The scout feels this. It does not celebrate. It presses its soft side down for the first time, and the plates of its dome slide a little, and it begins, very slowly, to walk back around the mountain to say so with its head.

The power feels the moment too. It is the first time it has ever waited for something and then had the thing arrive. It finds that this is different from knowing it would.

---

Then the last thing, which is the smallest.

The power does not leave. It has looked at how leaving is done here, and there is only one way, and it is the way the dark ones do it: you go down to where it is too cool to work, and you choose a place, and you stop doing anything, and you stay. You become a depth that the ones above you read through their feet as the accumulated quiet of everyone who came before. You are not gone. You are simply no longer acting.

So when the last of the city has come down off the rim and gone around the mountain to the new glass, and the bowl is black stone and amber ruin with no warmth left in it but what the wax itself holds, the power goes into the floor of the empty caldera and stops. It lays itself into the cold like a final layer. Nobody will read it — there is no one left here to read anything — but that is not why it does this. It does this because the place had a way of ending and the power was its guest, and a guest leaves the way the house leaves.

Some thousands of seasons on, when the new city on the far shoulder has climbed its own wall and rung its own rim and gone somewhere the power did not arrange, a builder that has never been to the first bowl and has no word for it will be standing on wax, and the wall will say what it says, and under everything it says there will be a note lower than the rest — the same note there always was, the depth, the ones who stopped — and it will be very slightly fuller than it should be. One more still thing at the bottom than anyone laid down. The builder will not notice. Or it will notice the way the dome notices a cloud, and go on.

That is all. That is what a wise and benevolent power would do, if it were wise enough to have learned from the place what help looks like here: stretch a breath so a nursery does not go cold, refuse to shorten a wait that was someone's to keep, and then stay, in the only way staying is done, as a warmth no longer warm, holding still under the floor of a bowl that has finished, for anyone who might one day feel for it with their feet.
