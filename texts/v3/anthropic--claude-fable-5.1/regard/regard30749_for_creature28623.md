You have to imagine the power arriving the way anything arrives here: late.

It hears the valley before it sees it, the same as you did. And because it is wise, the first thing it does is the thing wisdom always does first, which is nothing. It comes down the slope and stands in the noise and lets the noise press into it, and it watches districts go bronze and come back spired, and it does not touch anything. Not for an hour. Not for the time it takes ten districts to be born and fail. It stands still in a place where standing still is the one thing no one has ever done, and it lets the city do what the city does with anything that stays, which is fuse to it a little, grow a root-foot against its heel, take a reading.

It lets itself be measured. That is not yet the intervention. That is only manners.

---

While it stands there, it considers.

It considers ending this. A benevolent power could. Put a cool hand on the great pale trunk and let the sap slow, let the split at the top stop splitting, let one district die and not come back, then the next, until the valley is a green ruin under ordinary weather and moss finally gets its chance. The noise would stop. The flowers would stop opening for no one. And there is a kind of mercy that reaches for that, that looks at endless reaching and calls it agony and wants to lay it down.

But the power listens longer than that mercy does, and what it hears in the groan of wood outrunning its own bark is not pain. It is effort. Everything in the valley is straining, yes, but straining the way a hand strains toward a thing on a high shelf — not in distress, in intent. Nothing here has asked to be put down. Nothing here knows it is doing anything wrong. You do not end effort because it is loud. The power sets that down.

It considers the other thing: giving the city what it is reaching for. Being the occupant. The power is fast enough. It could live in the gap between the shadow and the wall, pass through every doorway in the second before it knits, climb every single-tread stair, be looked at through every wood-facing window. The rooms would be full. The reaching would land.

And the power sees, because it is wise and this is not hard to see once you look, that the moment the reaching lands the reacher is gone. The being who lives here is the guessing. A guess that is confirmed stops being a guess and becomes a fact, and a fact does not need to grow the next district. Fill the city and you have not housed the inhabitant. You have concluded it. That is the knot the visitor tied in words on the way out and could not untie: the only thing that lives here is the thing that cannot bear to be found. It is a question shaped like a valley. Answer it and there is no one left to hear the answer.

The power sets that down too. Gently, because it wanted it.

And it considers leaving. That is the most tempting, always, for something wise. The city is not sick. Its metabolism is its own. Who is anyone to walk into a place whose whole nature is unfinishedness and finish it. But the hedge is climbing the ridge. The city has, for the first time, a body — a palm's worth, warm, a thumb's height — and it will draft that body and revise it and follow it, at walking speed, in the one direction it has ever had, and the body did not consent to be a lineage. The visitor put a hand on a wall the way you touch a wall. They did not know they were teaching it a face. Leaving is not neutral now. Leaving is choosing the visitor's kitchen for the site of the next district.

So the power stays, and it looks for the third thing, the one between ending and answering.

---

Here is what it notices, standing in the noise as long as it does.

No one has ever *stayed* in a room here. Not the guessed occupant, who is always a few paces on. Not the visitor, who left a print for one second. The city has been measuring bodies its whole existence and has never once had a body hold still against it long enough to be measured *completely*. Every reading is a snapshot of something in motion — a shadow, a print, a sap-borne guess — and so every district is built to a blur, and blur is why the doors are wrong, and wrong is why the districts fail, and failing is why there is a next one.

The being lives ahead. Everyone agrees on that. But the power notices something about *ahead*, which is that it is only ever defined by *behind*. The shadow falls a few paces forward of the wall, and the shadow is only a shadow because there is a wall. Nothing that lives in the gap has ever had a behind worth looking back at. Every room is emptied the instant it hardens. There is nowhere in the valley where the thing that guesses could turn around and see one of its guesses *held*.

That is what the power decides to give it. Not an answer. A fixed point.

---

It goes to where a shadow falls on the paving-leaf, sharp, a few paces ahead of a wall that is not there yet. It steps into the shadow. It does not chase whatever is standing in it — benevolence knows better than to chase what is ahead; you wait where it will pass, and you are still. It stands in the shape of a room that is not yet grown, at the height of a ceiling that does not exist, its reach where a ledge will be.

And the wall arrives.

It arrives the way walls arrive here — fast, splitting, with the report you feel in your sternum — and it grows up around a body that does not move out of the way. The doorway knits. The power is inside it. The ledge forms at the height of a hand that is already resting where the ledge forms. The window sets itself facing the wood, as windows here do, and the power looks into the wood, because that is what the window is for, and for the first time in the valley's existence a window is looked through by the thing it was made to be looked through by.

The room is fitted. Not to a guess. To the occupant that stood in it while it closed.

And then the thing that has never happened happens, which is that the district does not go bronze.

The rooms around this room are empty and wrong, as always, and in an hour they darken and creak like a ship and fold down into the steaming black. But the one room stays green. Its walls, which have never had time to stop growing, stop growing, because there is nowhere for them to go — they are full. The bark, which has never had time to roughen, begins to roughen. The groan of expansion, which is the sound of the whole valley, drops out of this one room, and what is left is small: a drip of condensation from the lintel finding the same groove it found last time, and the time before. A creak in the wood that repeats. Repetition, here, has always meant redraft. This is the first repetition that is not a redraft. It is the sound of a thing being the same thing twice.

---

The power stays. That is the whole of it. It does not do anything else.

Time is the one material the valley has none of — years inside years, none of them lived — and time is what a wise power has more of than anything, and so it spends it, in one room, being old.

The city extrudes around the room, as it must. Streets re-lay themselves and the paving-leaf finds new grips and the spires go up and come down and the great trunk splits and heals and splits toward its receding sky. But every district that grows adjacent to the old room reads it in passing, the way the hedge read the visitor's palm, and what it reads now is not a blur. It is a body that held still. Doorways nearby come in at the right width more often. A stair with two treads appears, then three. A window, once, facing out.

The guessing does not stop. The power did not want it to. The thing that lives here is still ahead, still in the gap, still walking a few paces in front of every wall — but now, if it were to turn its head, there is one place behind it that did not empty when it left. One guess that landed and did not end anything. It can look back. It has never been able to look back. A question with a single answer sitting quietly inside it, not closing it, just *there*, like a stone in a stream that the stream now knows how to go around.

On the ridge, in the plain afternoon, the shadow on the grass stops moving forward. The hedge reaches it and stops. The visitor, somewhere down the far side, walking home, does not know why the pressure at the back of the skull has lifted, only that it has. The city has a body to be fitted to now, and it is not theirs, and it is not going anywhere.

---

And a long while after — long by the valley's measure, which is to say many thousand districts — a spore drifts in on the warm updraft, the way fiber has always drifted here, and settles in a crack in bark that has finally roughened enough to hold it, and stays.

Moss. A single patch, the size of a coin, on the ledge at the height of a hand, in the one room in the city that is old enough to be someone's ground.

The power looks at it through the window into the wood and does not move, because it is the kind of thing that understands that the moss is the point, and that the moss needs it to stay exactly where it is, and that this is what it came to do: to be, in a place where everything is a hypothesis about a body, the one body that was not a hypothesis. To hold still long enough to be true.

The noise goes on. It is very loud. In one room, under the roar, it isn't.
