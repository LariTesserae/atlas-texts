Something has noticed the shelf.

You will not see it arrive because it does not arrive. Arrival is a process, and you have watched what the place does with processes; the distance would have been conserved. It is simply the case, at the hour that is the only hour, that the shelf is being attended to, the way a room is different when someone is standing quietly in the doorway even before you turn.

Look for it in the small things, since that is where this place is honest. The dust in the bottom of the chisel groove does not move. The drop under the ledge lets go at the rate it lets go. The forty-three do not lift their heads. So whatever has come is not a wind and not a weight and not a warmth. The herd's attention is a measure of how much a thing is there, and by that measure it is not there at all. And yet the small grey bird in the fork of the bearded tree turns its head, and turns it again, toward nothing you can see, and holds. The birds are the only wary things on the shelf. They know the shape of being looked at.

---

The first thing it does is sit.

Not in a building. Not on a wall top. It sits the way a cistern sits, level and reflective and taking nothing. It does this for what would be a long time if there were time, and you should understand that this is not hesitation. It is learning the rule the way you learned it, slowly, by watching the snail tow its short thread of light. A power that acts before it knows the rule is not wise, whatever else it is, and a power that is good and not wise is a flood.

It sees what you saw. The channels kept clear at the rate they choke. The three young ones nursing and nothing being built. The person in the dry dark with the scar on the back of its left hand and its mouth moving and no sound. The count that holds.

And it sees what you could not, because it is not standing on the stone with a before running out of it. It can look over the edge.

Below the clean fractured lip the white goes down, and the water goes down into it, and the strips of moss, and the leaves lifted from the cistern surfaces, and the small dark droppings of the herd, and everything the shelf sheds. You assumed the cloud was where things went to be gone. It is not. Nothing is gone. The cloud is the shelf's before. Everything the place has ever refused to hold is held there, in the white, at the exact height where the stone ends. Every word the person has shaped is there, the whole sound of it, intact. Every second note of every bird. The growing the young ones would have done. The rounding of every edge that would have opened your palm. The ten thousand who are not here and were never here — their whole city, the one that would have happened, is in the cloud, lived and lost and rotted down to good soil. It is very deep. It has been going over the edge for a long time.

So this is not a place where nothing happens. It is a place that has arranged to happen elsewhere. The shelf is what remains when everything that could be lost has been sent away in advance, so that it can never be taken.

The power sits with that and does not judge it. It has seen what grief makes when grief has copper tools and all the time it needs. Someone, once, could not bear to lose the next thing. So they cut a place where the next thing could not come. It is beautiful work. The joints do not take a knife blade. Whoever did it did it out of love, and it does not help to say so, and it is true.

---

Now weigh doing nothing.

The shelf is not in pain. Say that plainly too. The many-legged things under the ledges want for nothing and have never been hunted. The herd is warm under its wet. The fish are at their depth. There is a peace here that most places would give a great deal for, and it is not the power's peace to spend. It would be very easy to come to a still thing with a good heart and move it, and call the moving help, and be wrong. The power knows this the way it knows the rule: not as a caution but as a fact about itself, that it could do harm here, that benevolence with its eyes shut is one of the oldest ways of doing harm.

It nearly stays its hand. You should know that. The argument for leaving the shelf exactly as finished and exactly as empty as it is comes very close to being enough.

What tips it is small.

Not the person. The person's face refuses age and its eyes are steady and it is not waiting. Not the young ones; they are not suffering their smallness. It is the bird. The grey bird in the fork leans forward and its throat works and its beak opens and the first instant of a song is offered to the air and taken back, and it does it again, and again. That is not stillness. That is trying. Something on the shelf wants, and has wanted at the same rate for as long as the shelf has held, and the wanting has been sent over the edge each time so that it never becomes despair. The bird is spared despair and denied the song. That is the bargain. The power looks at it and finds it is not able to call it kind.

And there is you. You are the only unasked thing here. The person, perhaps, chose; the builders chose; the herd is what a moss slope needs and was never in a position to choose. But you walked in over the lip with a before in you, and the place is taking it, patiently, at its own speed, and has not asked, and you did not know what you were paying. A wise power can leave a great deal alone. It cannot leave that.

---

So it does the smallest thing it can find.

It remembers.

That is all, at first. It is the one thing on the shelf that can. Memory is silt and nothing silts here, but the power is not here in the way that the stone is here; the rule does not run through it. So when the person comes out of the dry dark into the flat light and kneels at the channel and sets the chisel flat under the finger's width of moss and taps once, the power holds the tap. It holds the strip lifting away and the water taking it. It holds the next tap. It goes along the terrace beside the person, holding each one, so that by the time the person reaches the end there is, for the first time, a whole terrace's worth of work that has happened and has stayed happened somewhere.

Then it tells the person.

Not in words; there are no words here that the air will keep. It shows the person its own hands as they were forty taps ago, and then thirty, and then now, and lets the person see that the now is at the end of something. That there was a first tap and this is not it. That the pale flattened patch in the moss where the person stood before it stood here is a mark the person made.

Watch what that does to the face.

The eyes were on you, before, as if you might be someone. The power is not someone the way you are — it is not standing on the stone leaving pale ovals — but it has done the thing that makes someone someone, which is to be seen by another across time and kept. The person has just been kept, for the length of one terrace. It is the first before the person has ever been caught holding. And the face does not become young or old, but it becomes a face something happened to, and it turns toward the power, and the mouth shapes a word, and this time the power has been in the cloud and knows the word, because it has been going over the edge for as long as the shelf has stood, and it says the word back.

It is the person's name. You will not learn it. It is not yours.

---

The second thing it does is for the bird.

The bird offers its first instant, and the place takes it, and the power, having been down in the white where all the second notes are, sings one. Just one. It is not loud. It is pitched exactly where the bird's own next note would have been, and it comes into the air at the moment the first instant is taken, so that for the bird — for the first time, in a fork of a tree hung with grey-green beards, with the water dripping — there is a note and then another note heard against it.

The bird goes still. Then it opens its beak and offers the first instant again, and the power answers again, and the bird does it a third time faster, and the power answers, and the small grey birds along the whole length of the terrace have turned their heads.

Nothing has broken. The channels run at a finger deep. The dust sits in the groove. But there is now, on the shelf, a phrase. It lasts as long as the power is willing to be the second note, which is a long time, and it is the first thing here that is longer than an instant, and every bird on every wall top knows it.

---

The third thing it does is the one that matters, and it is almost nothing.

It tells the person where the moss goes.

The person has knelt at a channel and dropped a strip of green into the water and watched it carried toward the lip and over into the white and gone. It has done this more times than there are stones in the city and has never once wondered, because wondering is a before reaching toward an after, and the place does not permit the reach. But the power has been in the cloud. It knows the strip is there. Every strip is there. So it takes the person to the edge — the distance holds at nothing; it lets them walk together — and it does not ask the person to look down, because looking down into the white is a thing you must choose. It only stands with the person at the clean fractured lip where the stone ends, and it lets the person understand, without a word, that nothing that has gone over this edge was lost. That the loss was the one thing the shelf was built to refuse, and the refusal was not needed. The moss is kept. The words are kept. The young ones' growing is kept. The ten thousand are kept. Whatever the builder could not bear to lose has been down there the whole while, safe, waiting at the height where the stone ends, and the shelf could have let it stay and it would still have been there.

It does not say: so let go. It does not say: so walk over. A power that is good and wise knows the difference between opening a door and pushing. It has shown the door is a door and not a wall, and now it steps back.

---

What happens, then?

Perhaps nothing. The person may turn from the lip and go back to the channel and kneel and set the chisel under the moss and tap, and the place will take the tap and the balance will hold, and the power will remember it, and that will be the whole of the change: that there is one thing on the shelf that keeps count, and the count is being kept. That would be enough. It is more than the shelf had.

But watch the young ones.

There are three. They are nursing, butting and pulling, and they are the same size, and you counted, and it was three, and it is three. Look closer. Not at the count. At the one on the left, the one whose mother stands nearest the channel where the person knelt. Look at its shoulder against its mother's flank and mark where it comes to, and then look away — look at the bird, listen for the second note, which is still being given — and look back.

You cannot say it is taller. You would not swear to it. But you would not swear that it is not.

And behind the person, where it walked from the lip back to the channel, the moss is pressed flat, and it is lifting, but it is lifting the way the moss lifts behind you. Slowly. A line of pale ovals pointing back toward the edge.

The power sees this and does not touch it. It has done its three small things and it will do no more, because the fourth thing is not its to do; the fourth thing is the person's, and the herd's, and the shelf's, and it may take what would be a very long time if there were time, and the power can wait. It sits. It is level and reflective and it takes nothing and it holds everything. It is the one place on the shelf where the silt can settle.

Above it the sun stands a hand's width past noon, and if you laid your own hand against the sky to measure it you might find it a hair's breadth past where it was — or you might not; the light is flat and even, and there are no long shadows here. Not yet. The water threads down the black faces and finds the channels and runs to the edge and falls into the white, where it is kept, and the person at the channel taps once, cleanly, and the strip lifts away, and somewhere something remembers it, and the bird in the fork opens its beak and is answered.
