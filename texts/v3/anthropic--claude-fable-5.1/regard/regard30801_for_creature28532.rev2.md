The first thing it does is nothing, for a long time.

It arrives as attention, which is the only way anything arrives here — the way the footprints arrive before the feet. But it comes from the side rather than from below, and the town feels the difference at once, the way you feel a draft from a door you did not know was there. The condensation on the north-facing walls runs a shade slower. The bird, on its several perches, turns its several heads. Under the ground the sound of thinking does not stop, but for a moment it has the quality of a person who has become aware of being watched while working and has decided, after consideration, to keep working.

The power does not announce itself. It has seen enough places to know that a town being decided cannot afford to be startled. It walks the main street west, and counts, and walks it back east, and counts again, and finds the dozen paces' difference, and does not correct it. It climbs a stair through the stack of stairs and lets its foot be decided at the moment of landing. It stands at the corner with the lampposts until the cold comes through, and it learns the rules the way the rules are learned, by walking, because a wise thing does not skip the part where it learns.

And what it learns is that very little here is broken.

---

That is the first surprise, if a thing like this can be surprised. It had come expecting suffering, because a landscape in this condition usually means suffering — the drafts fighting, the people torn between versions, the ground refusing weight. But the town does not fight. The woman drinks her tea in a soft cascade and the tea goes down. The man's floorboards creak in a chord and hold. The multiplicity is not a wound. It is a climate. They live in it the way people live in fog or altitude, with adjustments they no longer notice making.

So the power sets aside the thing it might have done first, which was to finish the town. It could. It could reach down through the tacky stone and take the process by the wrist and say *here, this one, this lamppost, this stair, this face*, and the town would resolve like a photograph dropped in developer, all at once, sharp and single. And it would be a violence. It would be choosing for them which of their arrivals was the real one — the fog, the birth, the train — and putting the other two out with the excess for the bird to carry away. The man above the corner does not experience his three memories as a problem. He experiences them as wealth. A power that took two of them from him in the name of clarity would be no wiser than the weather.

Nor does it leave. That is the other easy thing, and it is not that either.

Because there are things here that hurt, and it has found them by standing still. Not the doubling. Three other things.

---

The first is the cracked conduit on the north side, low, weeping its clear not-water into a wall that has forgotten how to be a wall. This is simply damage. There is nothing philosophical about it. The power goes there in the afternoon, which is the only time, and kneels, and puts its palm flat over the hairline fracture in the glass sleeve, and keeps it there.

It does not seal the crack. It does not know the substrate well enough to write it, and it will not write what it does not know. What it does is hold. The cold goes past its hand in the direction skin has no word for, and the power lets it, and stays, and after a long time — the chalk man passes twice and does not see it, or sees it and marks it in the book as a posture near the north wall — the weeping slows. Not because the crack has closed. Because the wall behind it, with something steady pressed against it for long enough, has begun to remember. The dedifferentiated grey firms, grain by grain, toward the color of wet cement. When the power takes its hand away there is a print in the stone, and the print is the same size as all the other prints, which is its size, which is everyone's.

The crack is still there. It weeps less. It is a repair the way the woman's cloth is a repair: not a solution, a relationship. The power thinks this is probably the right kind of repair for a place like this and does not think about it further.

---

The second thing is the ache.

When one of a man walks east into the fog at night and does not come back, the others of him wake lighter and hurt along the edge, and the hurt fades, and nobody knows where the missing one went. *The fog again from the other side*, some of them think, and do not say. The power has watched two subtractions now — a coat gone from a hook, a version of the shopkeeper who was there in the morning and was not at noon — and it has seen the town grow, afterward, very slightly more certain. And it has understood that the people here believe their subtracted selves are spent. Used up. Reconciled out of existence so that the remainder can be more real.

The power does not know if this is true. So it goes to find out.

It walks east along the street that is longer this way, past the shop and the tree that is two trees and none, to where the pavement goes chalky and then goes to the skin-on-milk sheen that nobody steps on, and it steps on it. The cold there is the cold of a sentence before it has been written. The conduits run in beside it, dimming. It walks until the grey is the same grey in every direction and the flat daylight has no more town to be the right amount of light for.

What it finds there it does not bring back whole, because it does not fit in a sentence. But when it returns — resolving out of the fog onto a stretch of fresh pavement, damp, printed already with its own footprints coming the other way — it goes to the man above the corner, and it sits at his table, and it does not speak, because sound is reluctant. It puts its hand on the table near his hand. And in the clear moment that comes, when one of it and one of him align and a whole thought can pass, it gives him this: that the fog is not the end of the drafts. It is where they go to be drafts of something else. The version of him who walked east is not spent. He is early somewhere. He is a footprint in fresh stone, waiting for a town to happen around him.

The man's hands, several of them, tighten a little on the cup. There is a cold spot in the ceramic and then there is not. He does not say anything. He looks at the window, at the green room he has learned not to look at, and for the first time he looks at it directly, and the power understands that he is wondering whether the green room has someone in it who is early.

The ache along the edge does not go away. But it becomes a different ache. It becomes the kind you feel for someone who has moved to a distant city.

---

The third thing is the child.

The power has been careful about the child. A dense flicker of small selves, all the same age, none of them growing, because time here has not settled its direction and a child is made of time in a way a grown person is not. To decide the child would be to decide *which* child, and that is the one decision the power will not make. It is not the child's parent. Several people are, and remember it, and cannot agree.

But it has noticed that the child is the only one here who does not have a clear moment. Adults align in the street, two drafts sharp for a breath, a sentence through. The child is too many to align. Nobody has ever spoken a whole thing to it. It goes up stairs and is a coolness on the landing and has never once been *met*.

So the power does not choose. It waits on the stair — the one with more steps than stair — until the small sound of many feet and one sound comes up behind it. And it does not turn around, because turning is too fast. It simply sits, one step below the landing, and lets itself be as many as it needs to be, which is more than it usually is, and less than the child. It spreads. It goes soft at the edges the way the stone is soft at the edge of the fog. It offers a surface.

And for a moment, for the length of a kettle beginning to change pitch, the flicker slows. Not all the way. But enough that there is, in the smear where the face should be, briefly, a face — one, a child's, the age it has always been — looking at the thing on the stair with the frank uninterested curiosity of children who have found an adult where no adult should be. The power looks back. Nothing is said. Then the flicker resumes and the child goes past and up and is a coolness on the landing.

But the frost where it stood is thinner in one place than the others. And the chalk man, measuring the next morning, will find the footprints on the landing very slightly larger than his size, than everyone's size. Not much. A fraction of a shoe. The child has grown one clear moment's worth. Not because the power decided it should. Because someone finally looked at it long enough for it to have a direction to grow *toward*.

The power does this once. It does not do it again. Once is a gift; twice is a schedule, and the child's time is not the power's to keep.

---

Then there is the sound under the ground.

The power has left this for last, the way the sound is leaving the people for last, and for the same reason. It kneels at the corner where the lampposts are and puts its palm to the stone — the tacky, sweating, recently-promoted stone — and it feels the warmth of a hand that is about to touch and has not.

It does not take the process by the wrist. It does not tell it to hurry, or to stop, or which lamppost. The thing below is thorough, and thoroughness is a kind of respect, and the power will not insult it. It has watched the ground firm grain by grain and it knows careful work when it sees it.

What it does is smaller. It presses down through the stone the way the cold presses up through boots, and it puts one thought into the long attention, where the attention will find it when it has finished the ground and the lampposts and the windows and the coats on the hook and comes, at the very last, to the people. The thought is this:

*When you reach them, ask.*

Not *decide them*. Not *do not decide them*. Ask. Let the man above the corner say which of his arrivals he wishes to keep as fact and which as memory, and let him keep the memory too — let the settled one still know the smell of the train, even in a town where there are no trains. Let the woman with the cloth choose the color. Let the shopkeeper say which objects on her shelves belong to this draft, and let the ones that do not go gently east, early, to a town that will want them. The people here have waited a very long time to be decided, patiently, and it is not a hardship. But the difference between being decided and being *asked* is the whole difference between thoroughness and kindness, and the power has come to think that this town, which has so much of the one, has been waiting all along for someone to bring the other.

It cannot tell whether the thing below has heard. The sound of thinking does not change. But when the power stands up, the second lamppost is — it checks the chalk mark — very slightly closer to the first than it was that morning, and the cold in the overlap is a degree less cold, and the fern-frost on the glass has, on one branch, a lattice that looks like it was chosen.

---

The power stays.

Not forever; it has other places. But long enough to become a habit of the town, which is the only way to belong here. The people come to know it by posture — the one who sits. It sits on the stair. It sits in the shop, and one afternoon points at the brass instrument for a purpose no one knows, and the shopkeeper, startled, wraps it in a cloth that is a different color in each of her versions and hands it over, and nothing is paid, and the instrument is *kept*, and the shelf where it stood is one shelf now instead of several. It sits, once, in the chair in the green room — it goes to find the green room, which is in neither building and cannot be, and finds it anyway, because a room that has been looked at that long has become a place — and it sits in the chair so that the chair has been sat in, so that the room is someone's, so that when the man above the corner looks through his window there is one thing in it he does not have to learn not to see.

And it sits at his table.

They drink tea. The kettle changes pitch and the power hears the change arrive a moment late, softly, through the wall that is not there, and pours. The cups rise. His several hands and its several hands lift together, and the cups arrive at the lips in a cascade, and for once the cascade is the same cascade, and the sound of them set down is one sound trying to be one sound and succeeding.

He is warmer, he says, or several of him say and it arrives as a murmur. Along both sides.

In the clear moment — it comes more often now, and stays longer, because the power holds still in a way that gives the alignment something to rest against — he asks the thing he has never asked because it did not fit in a sentence. He asks whether it is kind. The attention. The sound under the ground.

The power considers this honestly, because it is wise and wisdom does not lie to comfort. It says it does not know. It says the thing below is thorough, and careful, and slow, and is doing the ground first for the right reasons. It says it has asked it to be kind and does not know if it was heard.

Then it says: *but I am here, and I heard you, and that is one kindness that has already reached you, so the question is not whether but how much.*

The man looks at his hands. There are fewer of them than there were when the power arrived. Not because any walked east. Because two aligned, in the night, corner to corner, breath to breath, and he woke in the morning warmer along one side for a day and did not fear it. He is settling. He can feel it, the crowd that is entirely himself drawing in toward a center, and the center is where he is sitting, and there is someone sitting across from him while it happens, and that turns out to be the difference. Not that the settling is faster. That it is not lonely.

---

If you stand under his window long enough he will still come down.

He will come out of the door, which is fewer doors than it was, and lean against the lamppost, which is fewer lampposts, and the lace of cold where he and it overlap is a finer lace, a lace you could almost mistake for a single pattern. He looks west toward the fog where he arrived and was born and got off a train, and he does not need to choose between them yet, and when he is asked he will keep all three, one as fact and two as the redundancy that makes a person more than a specification.

Beside him, close enough that their edges make a soft moiré, something sits on the step. A posture. Attentive. It has all the time it needs and it is spending some of it here, doing the only thing a wise power can do in a town like this without becoming the weather — which is to be, for as long as it stays, the part of the attention that is unambiguously kind. So that when the sound under the ground finally works its way up through the stone toward the question of him, it will find him already answered, at the corner, leaning, with company.

The bird shifts on the cornice. One of it drops and flies east, toward the fog, and the count on the cornice does not change, and the man watches it go and does not feel a stitch pulled. He feels, instead, the way you feel watching someone leave for a distant city where you have heard the pavement is fresh.

If you look away and look back, he is very slightly fewer, and very slightly more himself, and no longer waiting alone.
