It would come in the way the people came in. That is the first thing, and most of the rest follows from it.

Not from above, because there is no above. Not through the wall, though it could. Through the seam, sideways, a shoulder at a time, and not at any hour but on the ebb — waiting outside in the grey scrub with a hand flat on the horn until the deep note fell and the air began, faintly, to move inward past its fingers. Then in. Then the outside stops, and the power stands in the dark with the cellar-cool on its face and the smell of walnut and old flower-dust, and does what anyone with sense does in a house where the rules are posted at hand-height. It keeps them.

Nothing burns. It brings no light, and does not make any of its own, though it could; light here would be a kind of shouting. Nothing is seen. It finds the first terrace by the count of steps and the notch on the jamb, and it strikes the rope before it trusts the rope, and it does not go up. Not yet. It goes instead to the middle of the floor, the empty two hundred paces the town never touched, and it sits down in the dust, and it waits.

---

How long is a question of what it is waiting for. It is waiting to understand, and understanding here is measured in tides. So it stays through one full breath — the long swelling of the note under it, the air going thick and sour and dead, the wall trembling against its palm; then the slow release, the thin draught at the seam, the sense of the whole husk relaxing by a hair. Then another. Then enough that it stops counting, because the plant does not count, and the power has come to learn the plant's way of being in its own house.

It learns the cracks: where they tend to come, how the echo runs, how many circuits before the wall is quiet. It learns the knocking. It lies with its ear to the pith and follows the seeds as they turn, a run of dropped stones travelling the curve from one side to the other, and it does what the residents did and wonders whether it is counting, and does what they did not and lets the question go. It learns the notes of the terraces without striking them, from the way each level rings back the plant's own sounds a little differently. It smells the green when a fissure opens somewhere in the dark, and it goes to the place, and it puts a fingertip to the bead of clear gum and holds it there until it sets, and its finger is sealed to the wall for a moment, and it does not pull away until the plant lets go.

A benevolent power in a place like this feels, almost at once, the pull to do something. This is the thing it must sit with longest.

---

It considers the plant, and whether the plant needs saving, and finds that it does not. Nothing is wrong with it. It is very old and it is finishing, and finishing is not the same as failing. There is no rot to cut out, no wound that is not already glass. To water it would be to drown a thing that has spent an age teaching itself to live without water. To split it open and let the light in would be to interrupt the one long sentence it has been saying since before there were ears. The power sets that down.

It considers the people, and whether to find where they went and bring them back or tell them. It finds that they left well. There are no bones in the dust. Nothing here rots, so nothing here died; they went out through the seam when the breaths grew too long to live between, and that was the right thing to do, and the place did not resent it, because the place does not do resentment. Their descendants, if there are any, have their own plain now. The power sets that down.

Then it considers the seeds, and this it does not set down quickly.

A thousand of them, perhaps, turning in their cells. Each one the whole organism folded to a point. Each one a hollow that could stand on some other plain, a lung, a dark, a wall someone might come and cut into. And the husk is closing around them, has been closing for longer than anything could remember, and when it is done they will be in stone, and the future they carry will have been arranged, with enormous care, never to arrive.

The power feels this the way you feel a door closing on a room with people in it. It thinks: I could open it. A crack in the horn, a season of rain. I could see a hundred husks rise in the scrub and a hundred seams and a hundred towns cut into a hundred walls. It is a good picture. It is a very good picture.

And it sits with the picture until it can see what the picture is made of, and the picture is made of wanting a second act. Its own wanting. The plant has not asked for one. The plant, in the only tongue it has — the long behaviour of a body over ages — has said one thing, and the thing is *inward*. Draw the air to the centre. Draw the wet to the wound. Draw the town into the grain. Keep. And the seeds are not in pain. A seed in stone is not a prisoner; it is a seed, doing the single thing a seed does, which is to wait without needing to know how long. The power, which is wise but not eternal, remembers that stone is not forever either. Plains lift. Rivers come back. On the scale that seeds count in, nothing is foreclosed, only postponed past the sight of anyone now watching, including the one watching now.

So it will not open the husk. It will not decide, on the plant's behalf, that the plant has been wrong about itself for ten thousand years.

---

But there is a thing it notices that the plant cannot, and it is not inside.

It is the soil.

The husk did open, once. One crack ran all the way from the hollow to the air, a shoulder wide, and that crack is the seam, and the seam is the plant's. Whatever else the plant has done, it made that. It is the one place where its inside and the world touch. And the plain is taking it. The soil has come up around the husk for so long that the whole thing reads as a low blister in the scrub, and the wind that polished the horn has been filling the seam a grain at a time, and there will come a season — not soon, but there will — when the scrub grows over the split and the ground closes and no one comes that way because there is no way to come.

Then the hollow will be sealed. Not by the plant, tightening toward its centre on purpose over an age. By dirt. By weather that does not know it is there. The plant's last decision will have been made for it by the thing it happens to be lying in, and the power finds that this — not the seeds, not the dark, not the emptiness — is the one thing in the place it cannot leave exactly as it found it.

---

So here is what it does, in the end, which is three things and none of them large.

It climbs. On the ebb, when the air is thin enough to work in, it goes up the diagonal steps worn to a trough in the middle, and at the first terrace it puts its knuckle to the wall and strikes, once. The wall answers with its name. The power hears it. This is the first thing: it is an ear. The place has been saying its parts to a dark with no one in it, in a tongue of tone and duration pitched to people who left, and the power stands on the shelf of denser wood and lets the note come round the curve and back and lets it be *heard*, and then it goes up to the next level and does it again. Every terrace. Every name the residents gave. It is not much. It is, perhaps, the only gift a place like this can receive: to be answered in the way it answers.

Then, on the highest worked terrace where the pockets go small and the wall above curves off unworked into nothing, it does something the residents did not. It strikes, and it waits past the wall's answer. It waits through the echo and the ring and the long tail of it falling below hearing, and it keeps waiting, with its hand flat on the pith, until somewhere behind the wood a seed turns over in its cell and knocks. And it takes that as the reply. Not the town's voice — the plant's. It does not know what the knock means. It does not pretend to. But it asked, in the house's own manner, and something in the house that was never carved by any hand moved in response, and the power holds its palm to the spot until the wall is still. That is the second thing. It is the first time anything has spoken to the plant and waited for the plant to speak back, rather than the town.

Then it goes down. It leaves nothing on the shelves. It takes nothing from the niches, not a pinch of the dust, because everything here is made of the one thing and a second material would be a violation and an absence would be a wound the plant would have to glaze. It carries out only the names, which are not things.

And at the seam, on its way through, it turns and puts both hands into the split and clears it. No tool. It scoops the drifted grain from the crack the way you clear a drain, down to the horn, and rakes the scrub back from the lip, and that is all. It does not widen it. It does not deepen it. It keeps the door the plant made at the width the plant made it, and it makes no other, and it steps out into the grey and the wind and lets the husk finish its exhale behind it.

---

It will come back. Once in a long while — the plain's while, not the plant's — it will cross the scrub to the low blister and find the seam and kneel and clear it with its hands and go away again, and it will not go in. Or it will go in only on the ebb and only to sit on the floor through a single tide and then leave. It will tell no one. It will not mark the way.

It will do this for as long as it is a power and the husk is a husk, and if the day comes when the plain changes — when the sky over that flat grey country remembers rain — the water will find the seam open, because a door was kept, and will run down the horn and pool in the dust of the hollow and rise, and what happens after that will be between the wet and the wood, and the plant will decide, in its own slow chemistry, whether it has forgotten water entirely or only set it aside. If the day does not come, the husk will close to stone with the seam still in it, a thin dark line to the heart, and that will have been the plant's closing and not the dirt's.

Either way, the power will have chosen the one thing benevolence can choose when it finds a place that needs nothing: not to fix, and not to abandon, but to hold the possibility open at exactly the width it was found. It keeps the place the way the place keeps the town — without altering it, without forgetting it, drawing the shape of it inward and carrying it on. Somewhere in the wall a seed turns over and knocks, and the knock goes round the chamber and comes back, and for once, on the floor in the dust, there is someone to whom it comes back. The deep note rises. The power puts its palm to the wood and rises with it. And does not, yet, let go.
