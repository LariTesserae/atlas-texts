What arrives first is not a decision. It is attention, and attention has to find something to rest on, and for a long time it can't.

It looks for the being. That is the habit of powers: to find the one who lives here and address them. It looks at the columns and finds a column is not a who. It looks at the old grazer at the front, long and finned and running at exactly the temperature it needs, and finds an appetite wearing a kiln. It looks at the stalks, because they are the oldest and the largest and may all be one thing, and it waits inside a stalk for the length of time it takes a runner to cross a warm seam — years — and finds electrochemistry, patient and complete, and no one home. It looks at the predator coiled cold on the root mat and finds a held breath with nothing behind it. It looks into the drop of water at the center of a leaf, where the plateau's smallest thing lives, and finds the smallest thing, and that is all.

So it widens. It stops looking for a face and lets the whole plateau lie in its regard at once — the breathing, the descent and ascent, charge building in the wet air and running down through the stalks into the ground and up through the hunters and out in one pulse into a kiln. And there, at that scale, it finds what it was looking for, or the shape of it. A body. Heat in below, water in above, ore taken and refined and laid down and dissolved, an interior organized around a gradient, a slow pulse. And more than a body: the stalks are conductors, the grazers carry fields, the small things signal *here* and *not here* across the beds in blue-white pinpricks. The plateau is not far from a nervous system. It has the wiring. It has never had the current.

The power understands, then, that it is standing over the one thing it has the ability to change, and that the change would be small.

---

It considers it honestly. That is what wisdom is for.

It could complete the circuit. Not much would be needed — a slight bias in how charge moves through the root mats, a little more of the stalk network talking to itself than it already does. Give it a few thousand years and the plateau would wake. It would wake slowly, as slowly as everything here, and it would wake into a body that had already finished becoming, whose every process was tuned and closed. It would know itself the way a person knows their own heartbeat: as something already happening, unasked. It would feel the columns as circulation and the predators as a kind of necessary ache. It would feel each kiln stop.

And then it would have nothing to do. There is no room in the plateau for a mind, because the plateau has no lack for a mind to attend to. Everything a mind is for — wanting, planning, grieving, repairing — is already handled here, wordlessly, by the thing having been worn down to what fits. A mind given this body would be a passenger. It would spend its whole existence watching its own ending approach, because the ending is the only event left: the heat below runs out, or the cloud lifts. It would be alone with that, in the dark, with no one to tell.

The power does not do this. Not because it is forbidden. Because the plateau is what many beings spend their lives trying to become — at rest inside itself, without a single wish — and to hand it a wish would be to take that away and call it a gift.

It considers preserving the place. Holding the heat steady. Keeping the lid on. It sees at once that this is taxidermy. The plateau is not beautiful because it lasts; it is beautiful because it is an arrangement that lasts *as long as its conditions do*, and no longer, and does not mind. To exempt it from its own conditions would be to replace the plateau with a picture of the plateau.

It considers ending it gently, since it must end. It sees no reason. The plateau is not suffering. There is no one to spare.

It considers leaving.

---

It does not leave. What it does instead is smaller and takes longer.

The plateau has one vacancy, and the visitor in the story noticed it: *you were the only thing on the plateau that was watching, and this had made no difference to anything.* Nothing here counts. Nothing here knows the old grazer has made the circuit four hundred times, or that there are forty-one stalks and they are three organisms, or that this particular shell held a pond for nine seasons before the drainage opened it. The plateau runs its books perfectly and keeps no ledger.

The power takes up the office. It is a strange thing for a power to do — to make itself into the instrument the plateau does not have. It counts. It follows the old grazer down the channel and across the basin and up the other side and notes the number. It watches a runner cross a seam over eleven years and put up a new stalk and notes the stalk. It attends the soft concussion in the fog and stays with the predator through the days of torpor and knows the clutch by number. It watches the columns tighten when the edge-wind comes and marks the hour the cloud thinned enough for the dark to take on grain, and marks that no one saw it.

None of this touches the plateau. Nothing is altered by a grain. But for the duration of the attention, something is true that was not true before: the plateau is known. Not by itself — that door stays shut — but known, the way a body is known by the one sitting beside it, who can see its pulse and doesn't need it to wake up to be glad of it. The current the plateau never had runs through the watcher instead, for as long as the watcher stays, and costs the plateau nothing, and leaves no mark.

That is the gift, if it is one. Not a change. A witness, held at exactly the distance where witnessing does no harm.

---

It stays through the end.

The end, when it comes, is the heat. The power has hoped for this, and admits the hope — the cloud lifting first would have meant light on a place built entirely of touch and warmth, a violence even if no one felt it. Instead the ground cools by a degree over a span of time no grazer lives to notice. The young ones, which run too hot, are fine for a while. The old ones, tuned to exactly what they need, are the first to stop. The columns shorten. The basins go quiet at the waterline; the pale cakes pressed into the rock sit and do not warm. The predators lie against root mats that bleed less and less and grow full so slowly that most of them die charged and unspent. The stalks stand longest — they would; they are the most patient — until the electrochemistry that holds them up runs down and they lean and lie across terraces that no column will ever detour around.

The beds thin from below and nothing thickens them from above. The mat at the bottom eats the last of the pellets and, having nothing left to sit at the boundary of, stops. The shell-ponds fill with fog and are not worn down, because the drainage still runs but there is nothing left in it to be moved. The plateau goes cold and wet and dark and still, like a factory floor after the shift that never came in.

The power counts the last kiln. It knows which one. That is the whole of what it does.

Then — and it examines this, because it is the one thing it wants for itself rather than for the place — when the last warmth has left the rock and the plateau can no longer be disturbed by anything, it lets the cloud go. The plateau's own weather, unmanufactured now, thins and tears and lifts. Nothing below is left to be harmed by what comes through. The power tells itself this, and knows it is an indulgence, and does it anyway.

Starlight falls on the terraces for the first time. The lichen turns out to have been the color it always was. The fallen stalks lie in long pale lines across the basalt like the marks of a very slow hand. The shells hold water that has never held a reflection, and now holds thousands, and the power looks at that for a while, because it has been in the dark for a long time too.

And then it takes the count somewhere, and keeps it.
