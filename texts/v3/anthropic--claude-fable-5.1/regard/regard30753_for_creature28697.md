What arrives is not a body. Nothing with a body could stay, and this is the first thing the attention understands about the place: that it has already turned away everything that needs to keep anything. So the attention does not come down onto the crust. It comes to rest above it and around it and, after a while, inside it, the way heat is inside a stone, and it begins by doing what anything wise does first, which is nothing. It looks.

---

It hears the sound, and the sound is not a problem for it. It is not one thing to a being that can hold a continent in view; it is a million things, each in its place, and each place is legible. The chord of the stacks is a chord. The cannons are a slow rain of drums. The crackling of the crust is a texture like the grain in wood. Underneath it all the bass of the flexing plain, which it feels as a body would feel its own breathing if the breath took a day. The attention finds, a little to its own surprise, that the sound is beautiful, and it sets that aside, because beauty is not what benevolence came for. Benevolence comes for the hurt.

So it looks for the hurt.

It finds a hound lying too near a cannon on its last day — a young one, not yet good at reading the drum being tuned under its chest — and it watches the valve open and the body come apart in the pressure, and it feels this exactly, the way it feels everything, and it waits for what comes after. What comes after is that the rest of the band is already running outward in the ring, and the seed is coming down, and the crust under the dead hound is already softening to take it, and by the next evening the place is a flat and the band is lying down around the next cannon and there is one fewer, and no hound knows it. The pain lasted as long as it lasted. It was not carried anywhere. Nothing on the plateau has the equipment to carry it.

It finds a gleaner pressed under at a seam-crossing, shell ringing against a hundred other shells, forced into the green tissue that is closing at the speed of healing. The gleaner's oil-thin blood goes cold and then it goes into the crust and in a season some part of it is a layer on a stack. It finds a chaff-rider torn open in the glitter and turning down through the dusk, and it finds that the chaff-rider has no more notion of falling than it had of rising.

It goes down into a pit. This is the one it was most concerned about, because the pit-dwellers are old, and old is where suffering lives if it lives anywhere — in a thing that lasts long enough to know it has lost something. It goes in through the warm grey turning mass and finds them in the dark: pale, long, unsighted, in a slow soup of breaking oil, rolled under and up and under. It stays with one for what would be, to a hound, a lifetime. It listens with every faculty it has for the thing it would recognize as want. It does not find it. It finds hunger, and the hunger is met. It finds the pressure of the fold, and the body is shaped to the fold. It finds duration without memory, which is a state a wise power knows of but has rarely seen so purely — a long life lived entirely as a present tense, like a note held rather than a melody. The pit-dweller is not waiting for anything. It has nothing to wait with. The attention withdraws upward through the turning mass and comes out into the glare and does not know whether to call what it saw peace, and decides that it does not need to name it in order to leave it alone.

It looks, last and longest, at the crust itself.

Here is the one place on the plateau where a mind is almost happening. The attention can see the almost: the fine hard nap built up under the hounds' hooves, the seam that gives a little more oil where the gleaners front, the way a pit turning in the west is answered by the flats in the east. Something is being learned. Nothing is learning it. The crust is a great responsive body with no one in it, a thing so good at answering that it has never needed to ask, and the attention holds this for a long time, because it is exactly the kind of thing a benevolent power is tempted by.

It could wake it. It has the means. It could give the crust a place to stand behind its own responses, a *here* from which the pressure and the taste and the heat would become *felt* — and then the plateau would be not a fire that had learned to be a plant but a plant that knew it was a fire. And it would know, in the first instant of knowing anything, that it had been eating itself for as long as there had been anything to eat, and that everything it had ever carried it had taken back, and that it had never once been asked.

The attention does not do this. Not because it would be cruel, though it might be. Because no one has asked for it. There is no *wanting* here to answer, and to supply the want in order to satisfy it is not a gift, it is a replacement. A wise power knows the difference between a being that has not yet asked and a being that cannot. It knows also that the first can become the second in its own time, or never, and that neither outcome is the power's to hurry.

---

So it goes through the other gifts, the way one might go through a cupboard, and puts each one back.

Water — no. Water is the one thing the plateau is *for* the absence of. A single rain would turn the flats to rot and the seams to sores and the whole taut singing continent would go soft and sag and die of drinking. The visitors feel this in their bodies and call it dread. It is not dread. It is the correct recognition that their kind of life and this kind of life cannot occupy the same ground.

Rest — no. The turbulence is the rest. There is no calm being interrupted. The stacks screaming and the cannons firing and the pits turning are not the plateau's labor but its stillness, the way a standing wave is still.

Growth — no. It has reached its edges where the air stops being enough, and there is a rightness in a thing that has found its size.

An ending — no. Nothing here is asking to stop.

Protection. Here the attention pauses longest, because here is the one real danger, and it comes from outside. The oil is fuel. It burns clean and stiffens in air and there is, under that crust, more of it than in any lowland field ever tapped. Someone will come for it eventually — not the visitors who stand a season and leave, but the ones after them, with pipe and pump and the patience to drill through a wound that closes in minutes. The plateau's only defense is that it takes back whatever touches it, and that has been enough against everything that has come so far, and it will not be enough forever.

The attention could turn the roads. It could make the air below the edge a little thinner than it is, or the oil a little less useful than it seems, or the place a little harder to remember afterward. And it decides, after considering, that it will not do this either — not yet. To wall a thing against a threat that has not arrived is to decide that its future is already the worst one. What it will do instead is stay awake to it. If the day comes when the pumps come up the mountain, it will be there, and it will lean, and it will not be gentle. But not before. Wisdom does not pre-empt. It keeps watch.

---

And then, having put everything back in the cupboard, the attention notices that the cupboard is not empty. There is one thing the plateau lacks that is not water and not rest and not a mind, and that can be given without changing anything, and it is this: nothing here has a past.

Not because the past was lost. Because it was never kept. No sediment, no strata, no old individuals except the flat hunters lying in the noise and the pale things in the gut, and neither of them keeping anything. The map made in spring is wrong by autumn. The mountain range the crust dissolved exists only as a thickness. The young hound that came apart at the cannon existed, and now there is no place in the world where that is true — not in the band, not in the crust, not in the seed it was waiting for. The plateau is a continent-sized present tense, and everything that has ever happened on it has happened once and then not at all.

A benevolent power sees this and its first impulse is to correct it. Give the crust a memory. Lay down a record. And it has already seen why it cannot: memory is the *here* from behind which things are felt, and to give the plateau a past would be to give it a self, and the self was not asked for.

But the memory does not have to be the plateau's. It can be the power's.

So this is what it does. It stays.

Not as a body — bodies become ridges. Not as a mark — there are no marks. It stays as what it is, which is attention, laid over the whole of the plateau the way the plateau is laid over the mountains, and it keeps the record the crust cannot keep. It holds the map of spring beside the map of autumn beside the map of the decade after, every layout the plateau has ever had, all of them at once, the way the crust holds the vanished range as a thickness in itself. It counts the hounds. It knows which stack stood where before the fermentation ebbed and the stack slumped and the site went flat. It knows the young hound's name, insofar as anything on the plateau can be said to have one — the particular grey of its scales, the way it ran, the drum it failed to read. It knows where every gleaner was sealed under and which stack its silica went to. It watches the flat hunters cross the distance inside the noise, and it knows which of them is oldest, and it will know the day that one lies down in the flats and does not rise and is taken back, and it will be the only thing in the world that knows.

It keeps this for the plateau. It does not give it to the plateau. If the crust ever wakes — on its own, in its own long time, the almost becoming an *is* without anyone forcing it — there will be someone there who can answer the first question a waking thing asks, which is *what have I been*. And if the crust never wakes, then it will have been a thing that was witnessed, completely, by something that loved it, and was never once made to know it, and that is a way of being cared for that a wise power understands and few others do.

---

Toward evening the wind rises, as it does every evening, pulled by the cooling. The chaff lifts across the whole plateau in a shining tide and the low sun goes through it and for a quarter of an hour the continent burns without heat. The attention has seen this before — it has seen it every evening since it came, and it has kept every one, so that it knows this particular dusk from all the others by the shape of the chaff-cloud over the western mills and the pitch the stacks are bending to and the three cannons that fire in the space of the burning and the ring of hounds under each. And it feels, in the holding, something a benevolent power is permitted to feel and does not always get to: that it is glad the thing exists. Not glad *for* anything. Glad. The way one is glad of a fire on a cold night, without asking the fire to be anything but a fire.

Then the light goes and the lightning begins to walk along the seams and the cold sharpens and nothing anywhere has stopped, and nothing anywhere is finished, and the plateau goes on being exactly what it has always been, at deafening volume and in perfect balance, with no more knowledge of what watches it than a flame has of the eye that is not looking away.
