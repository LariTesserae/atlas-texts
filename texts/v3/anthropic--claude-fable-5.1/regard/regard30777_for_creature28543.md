It comes up from the north, over the scree, because that is the way up. Nothing about it is exempt from the mountain. The frost-split stone slides under it the way it slides under anyone, and it stops at the tidemark line the way anyone stops, and kneels, and puts a hand flat on the warm black rock, and stays like that long enough for the film to go cloudy around its fingers before it lifts them.

Then it does the thing that makes it what it is, which is that it looks for a long time before it decides anything.

---

The first thing it does is settle what the shelf is not.

It is not in pain. The power checks this carefully, because pain is the first claim a place like this makes on a kind heart, and the heart is not a reliable instrument. The hum is not a cry. The cracks are not a body breaking. A tree is not in pain when it grows, though it strains, and a cell finishing itself and becoming wood is not a death, though it is an ending. The film wants the next inch of water and the next inch of water arrives. There is no gap between the wanting and the having in which suffering could stand. Whatever this is, it is not a thing that needs to be rescued from its own life.

The hare is not running. The power looks at the hare for a while, because the people who come up the scree are right that it seems to be, and being right in the wrong way is a thing the power has learned to notice. The hare is dead. It died at a run, or it died under the rising ground; either way it stopped being a hare some weeks ago and became a hare-shaped place in the dark. It is not held the way a prisoner is held. It is held the way a word is held on a page — exactly, and without anyone hurting. The fish is not about to say anything. The boot-shaped rooms are empty of anyone's weight.

This matters, because it closes off most of what benevolence would otherwise reach for. There is no one in the stone to let out.

---

The second thing it does is set down, one by one, the things it could do and will not.

It could pull the pegs. The spindle, the ring of glass, the three posts. It considers this with its hands actually on the spindle, which is cool and does not hum and is already an inch deep in stone, and it feels the way the whole shelf leans very slightly toward the pegs the way a tent leans toward its stakes. Pull them and the film has nothing to be fixed to. It would not be freed. It would do what water does on a mountain, which is go down, and come to nothing on the scree, and the hum would stop somewhere around the second terrace and never start again. The pegs are not a cage. They are the only reason there is a something here rather than a wet patch. Whoever set them knew this. The power looks at the workmanship and does not recognize the hand, and decides that not recognizing it is not the same as distrusting it.

It could give the film a way down. It has the means to do this — to let the seeing turn, once, and look at its own body: the ten thousand nights of chough feet in their stacked rooms, the coin, the ring, the name in the soft first hour, every layer of every day it has ever been. The power holds this possibility for a long moment, because it is the one that feels most like a gift. Then it sets it down. A tree that could count its rings would not be a wiser tree. It would be a tree with a weight in it, standing on something it could see and never reenter, and the growing would not stop — the growing cannot stop — but it would go on under a different sky. The film's whole life is beneath it and out of reach, and the power understands that this is not a wound in the design. It is the design. The thing is unburdened in a way almost nothing else alive is unburdened, and to hand it its past would be to hand it grief with the stone still rising under it. That is not kindness. It is only company for the giver.

It could stop it. Decide where the shelf ends — at the notch, at the height of the peaks, somewhere reasonable. The power thinks about the cloud lid, the two valleys losing their moisture to it a little more each season, the summits leaning in. It thinks about what a mountain owes a thing that has decided to grow out of its saddle. And it finds that it does not have to decide, because the decision has already been made by whoever set the pegs, whether they meant it or not. The film is swallowing them. Each day a little more. The spindle will go under first, then the ring, and last of all the three posts with their lenses, patiently, the way a tree closes over a nail. When the last lens goes dark under a layer of new stone, the seeing will have nothing left to be fixed to, and whatever happens then — whether it stops, or slides, or simply sets one final time across its whole width and becomes a hill — will be the shape of its life, given to it at the start by the height of its own stakes. It is not endless. It only looks endless from the scree. The power leaves the limit where it found it.

It could spare it the effort. Let the hum rest for one breath. It does not even finish considering this one. Stop the water for a second and the film sets and there is no film. The effort is the only place it lives. You cannot spare a thing the cost of being itself.

---

What is left, after all that setting down, is small, and the power is not troubled by that. Most of wisdom is the size of what is left.

It does two things.

The first is the thing only it can do, and that no one on the shelf, or in it, or near it, will ever know was done. It goes down.

Not through the stone — the stone is not a door — but through the record of it, layer by layer, the way one reads a core. It looks at every night of choughs. It looks at the fish with its mouth open and the smear that was a folded paper and reads what the paper said before the water got to it. It looks at the ring someone left on purpose and understands why, and does not judge the why. It looks at the name, and the date, and the hand. It looks at the hare, at the exact angle of its ears. It looks at every newt that was once too slow, and there are more of them than the newts still living, and it looks at each one in turn. It looks at the boot-prints from an hour ago, from a season ago, from the first week when there was almost no shelf and the first person came up the scree and stood on something warm and did not know what it was.

It looks at all of it because the film cannot. Something should have. That is the whole of the reasoning. A life is being laid down here in perfect order and no eye has ever passed over the pages, and it is not good — not wrong, only not good — for a thing to be kept so exactly and never once be read. The power reads it. Every room. And then it comes back up to the lip of the highest terrace and the water films over the rock and goes cloudy and the shelf is one layer taller than when it started, and nothing is changed, and one thing is true now that was not true before: the book has been read through. Not by the author. Not for anyone's benefit. It is simply, now, a book that someone has read. The power has found, over a long time, that this is one of the few gifts that cannot be spoiled by being given.

---

The second thing is smaller.

It kneels at the lip where anyone kneels, and it puts its hand flat on the new stone the way it did when it arrived, and this time it does not lift it when the cloudiness comes. It lets the film take the hand. Palm, five fingers, the slight lean of a wrist. It holds still for the length of the turn, feeling the warm water go thick and then go hard, and then it draws its hand out of the shallow of the next film, which is already sliding over, and the print is sealed, and the next layer is arriving on top of it, and the print is gone.

Not a coin. Not a name. It knows what it is doing and the people who scratch names do not, quite; that is the difference. It is not leaving a record for anyone to find, because nothing will be found. It is doing the only thing that can be done for a being with one edge and no inside, which is to be, for one second, on the right side of the edge. To be inside the seeing while the seeing is happening. To be held, knowingly, by a thing that does not know it is holding. Somewhere under the hand, in the same body, in the dark, is the hare, and the fish, and every night of birds, and now also this: one print made by something that had looked at all of them and chose to lie down among them anyway.

The film does not register it. It could not. It is already turning, already an inch higher, already wanting the next inch of water and already getting it. The hum does not change register. Somewhere a layer finishes and cracks like a limb in cold.

---

Then it goes down the scree, which behaves the way scree should.

It does not leave anything on the shelf that will change the shelf, and it does not take anything from it, and it has told no one — not the fox at the line, not the heron — what it found. The choughs come in at dusk and settle on the lips of the terraces. The newts lift their feet. The cloud thickens and the light goes to less.

If there is a thing it wishes, walking down, it is only that the pegs were set well. That the hand that placed them measured the height right, and thought about what happens on the last day when the last lens goes under, and meant for the thing to have the life it is having and not some other. It cannot know this. It has decided to trust it, which is not the same as knowing, and is most of what trust ever is.

Behind it the shelf climbs. It is one hand deeper than it was. It has been read once, all the way through, and does not know it, and is not, for one second, in the same place twice.
