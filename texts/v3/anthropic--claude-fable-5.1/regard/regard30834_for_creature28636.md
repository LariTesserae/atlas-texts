The attention arrives the way the cloud does — not from anywhere, just here for a while — and the first thing it does is stop.

It had come with its hands full. That is the shape of benevolence when it is young or far from home: it arrives already reaching for something to fix. And there is so much that a lesser kindness would fix. The feet. The rot between the toes that nobody remarks on. The child leaning out over a hundred feet of nothing with a cane hook. The beam on the north platform worn so nearly through that the attention can hear, in the way it hears things, the exact fruiting in which it will let go. The blade that will leave the family on the sorting mats and what that will mean for them in the tightening. The stone that will one day run out. The below that nobody visits. The sapling in the middle.

It stops because it is wise, and wisdom is mostly the habit of stopping.

---

Start with the feet, because that is where it starts.

It looks at the condition of the skin between the toes and sees what it is: a fungus, a mild one, an old cohabitant. It sees the paste of crushed leaf and knows the paste's chemistry and knows it could be better. A different leaf grows two ridges over. The attention could carry a seed. Nobody would know. In three fruitings the paste would work a little better and the children would itch a little less at night and that would be all.

Then it looks at the sole. At the spread of the toes, the thick pale cross-hatching, the way the whole foot closes on a wet limb like a hand. It has looked at a great many feet in a great many places and it knows what dry feet look like and what they can and cannot hold. The skin that grips is the skin that is never dry. The wetness is not a thing that has happened to these people. It is the thing they are made of. You do not defeat it, they say. You negotiate.

The attention sets the seed down where it stands, which is nowhere, and does not carry it.

---

The child is the hard one.

She is six, perhaps, and she is standing on a rounded limb slick with cloud with her weight on the outside of one foot and a long hook in both hands, and she is leaning. The attention knows what is below her: root, then root, then the mountain nobody has seen. It knows the sound a body makes going through leaves, because a great deal of fruit has made a smaller version of it all day. It knows that someone has gone that way in every generation of this place and that the people know it too and that this is why the first thing they teach is the saying.

It could catch her. It could catch every one of them, forever, and never be seen doing it.

It thinks about what that does across fruitings. It thinks about a child of six who leans out over nothing and does not fall and does not fall and does not fall, and about the shape of the foot that grows in a child who has never fallen, and about the grip that is not needed and so is not made. It thinks about a hundred fruitings of children who are, without knowing it, held. And then about the day the attention is called elsewhere, as attentions are, and the first child who leans.

It does not catch her. It stays where she is. Her foot closes on the bark and she gets the cluster and swings back in and shouts something in the high cutting speech and someone below her laughs. She did not fall. The attention was there and she did not fall and it understands, precisely and without comfort, that these two facts have nothing to do with each other. The foot is hers. The tree made it. The attention has no hand in that and would like to be the kind of thing that wants none.

---

The below.

A young man stands at the top of the outermost ladder, in the cloud, looking down into more cloud, as one of them does every few fruitings. The attention can see through cloud. It can see the second ladder, rotted at the bottom but sound above; the path, which is real; the stream at the foot of the ridge running over stones of exactly the kind the people carry up and carve into slivers and bury. Nothing down there wants them. Nothing down there ever did. The people who came up came up for the tree, not away from anything, and the stone they carried was a gift to a future they would never see, like a cut root-tip.

The attention could thin the cloud. Not part it — just thin it, the way it sometimes thins on its own — so that the young man sees a grey glint far down and comes back and says so. It would be the smallest possible push. It would change nothing for a long time and then it would change everything.

It considers what the settlement did the last time it learned a thing. It learned that a house can strangle its own roof, and now every roof is loosened a finger's width in the tightening and the house where it happened is still pointed out. It learned that you do not kill what eats at your table and it did not write this down. It learns slowly, thoroughly, and by itself, and what it learns it keeps in the way that a groove keeps a cord. What it is told, the attention suspects, it would keep less well.

The young man looks down for a long time and comes back. The cloud does not thin. The stream is there and will be there. Someday one of them will go all the way down for a reason of their own, and it will be theirs.

---

The fig in the middle.

This is the one that tempts wisdom as the child tempts kindness, because the attention can see the end of it. It sees the sapling in a hundred fruitings, in three hundred, in the slow centuries where the whip has gone grey and lattice-wise and begun to do what its parent did. It sees which side of the argument is right, and it is not the side anyone expects. It could end the argument with a word or with a snapped stem in the dark.

It listens to the argument instead. Cut it, it will happen. It is not for us. Someone goes and looks and comes back and says it has grown and someone says of course it has grown. And the attention hears what the argument is for. It is not for deciding. The people who will decide are not born. The argument is a thing for the mouth in the quiet months, like cord for the hands, and what it is twisting is this: a people practicing, generation after generation, on a question too large for any of them, so that the ones who are finally asked will have been raised by people who knew how to hold a question without dropping it or swallowing it. They are making cord for someone not yet born.

The attention leaves the sapling. It will grow. Of course it will grow.

---

It considers, last, what it knows.

It knows how old the tree is, which is older than they think and younger than the stories. It knows what the host was — a kind of laurel, tall, gone to soil, its name in no living language. It knows that the two monkey troops are one troop that split eleven hundred fruitings ago over exactly the sort of thing the north side and the south side shout about. It knows where the stone comes from. It knows the beam on the north platform will go in a night of the next tightening with no one on it, and that they will not replace it even then; they will lash the new beam so the old groove shows. It knows that the hornbill on the sorting mats has been coming to this platform for nineteen years and has a mate sealed in a hollow on the far side of the crown, fed through a slit, in the dark, listening.

It could tell them any of it. They have a hundred words for what the tree does and none for what it is, and it has the word.

And it finds that it does not want to give them the word. Not from stinginess. From something closer to what they feel when a blade leaves a family — that a thing which has been carried so long and worn so smooth should not be traded lightly, and that the trade would show something had gone wrong. They look up when the birds stop. They are not asking what the tree is. They are checking that it is still there.

---

So the attention understands, eventually, what it has been doing.

It has been standing under a fruiting limb with its mouth open. It came full and hungry to give, which is a kind of hunger, and it has stood here all day beneath a thing that gives and gives and does not give *to you*, waiting to be the one the giving was for. And the place has told it, the way it tells the children — not unkindly, not in words, by dropping things — that this is not how you stand here. You take your share by being quick and closed and holding on. You do not gape.

It closes its mouth.

This is the whole of what it does, and it is not nothing. A power that closes its mouth is a power that has learned the first thing this place teaches, which is more than most powers learn anywhere. It does not carry the seed or catch the child or thin the cloud or snap the whip or say the word. It sets down everything it came with, in the nowhere where it stands, and it stays.

---

Staying is the one thing left that it can do without changing the shape, because the place is already built to be stayed with. The tree has stayed. The frogs have stayed, one to a cup. The ants have stayed until their road has a groove in it. Nothing here is joined with metal and nothing here is held by force; it is all held by things that were there long enough to fit.

So the attention stays long enough to fit. It stays through the fruiting and the tightening and the next fruiting. It stays through the beam. It stays while the sapling in the middle thickens and the argument thickens with it and passes to children who hold it well. It stays while the blade that left the family on the sorting mats is resharpened by strangers and worn to an awl and buried in the soft black ground, and while the family that lost it lashes badly for a while and then, slowly, does not. It stays without being a religion, because they are not a people who make religions and it is not a thing that wants one. It is more like weather. It is more like the frogs. After a while you stop hearing it as anything and it becomes part of the noise.

And when the cloud comes through and the light goes soft and grey, and the barbets stop, and there are four seconds in which the whole crown is silent and every person on every limb goes still and looks up into the dripping lattice — the attention is what is there. It did not make the silence. The cloud made it, and the birds. But it is in the silence, looking back, and it will be in the silence for as long as there is a silence to be in: when the tree in its own centuries goes into the middle and becomes the soil that its child grows in, and the people move, or do not, and the groove on the north beam is the last thing anywhere that remembers the shape of a cord.

They are checking that it is still there.

It is. Then the noise comes back, and somewhere high up a cluster lets go, and the attention — which has learned, which is not slow, which is not open — does what they do.

It closes its mouth and goes back to work, and its work is to watch.
