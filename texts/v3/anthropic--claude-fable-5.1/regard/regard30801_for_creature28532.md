The attention arrives the way the daylight arrives, from no direction.

It has been listening for a long time before it comes. That is how it noticed the town at all — not by seeing it, which from outside is a smear of grey in grey, but by hearing the sound underneath, the sound of a room in which someone in another room is thinking hard. One attention recognizes another. It heard the substrate being written, patient and thorough and slow, and it came to see what was being written so carefully, and by whom, and for whom.

The first thing it understands is that it must not look too hard.

Attention here is not free. Attention here is a kind of specification. The lampposts are several lampposts because they were seen several ways; the drafts of the town are the residue of every gaze that ever fell on it and did not agree. If it were to stand in the main street and *look*, the way a power looks, the corner would acquire a new lamppost, and the man above it a new version of himself, and the burden it came to ease would be heavier by exactly the weight of its regard. So it gentles itself. It comes in as the flat even light comes in, at the amount the town can bear. It does not focus. It lets the town be seen from the corner of an eye that has no corner, and the lampposts stay at their count, and nothing new is added to the hook.

This restraint costs it something. It would like to see the man's face. It does not.

---

Then it considers what it might do, and most of what it considers, it puts down again.

It could finish the town. It could reach through the stone and take the drafts in both hands and press them together, the way you press wet clay, and the lampposts would be one lamppost and the stairs would have the number of steps they have and the coats would fit the hook. It does not do this. The slowness below is not indecision. The slowness is the care. The process is doing the ground first because the ground must hold everything else, and it will get to the lampposts, and the windows, and at the very last the people, because the people are the hardest part and it does not want to get them wrong. To hurry it would be to choose — to pick a draft of the man because it was closest to hand and call the picking mercy. The power knows what it is to be decided by someone in a hurry. It leaves the ground to the ground.

It could warm the stone. The cold is in the overlaps; the ice on the glass grows in too many lattices; the fog is bitter. It could raise the town a few degrees. But it sees what the cold is doing. The condensation is the settling — the drafts cooling against a surface until they cannot stay in the air and must come down as one thing. Warm the stone and the settling slows. Warm it enough and the drafts stay aloft forever, a town of permanent maybe. And the cold is how they feel each other's edges. The lace that forms where a man leans on a lamppost is the only touch he has. It leaves the cold.

It could decide the child. Of all the things in the town, the child is the one it most wants to help and the one it most must not. A dense flicker of every child it might be, unable to grow because growing means becoming one of them. The power could reach in and lift out a single child and set it on the stair and let it climb. It would be one child. It would grow. And every other child in the flicker would be the fog — chosen against, not by the slow process below that does not want to get them wrong, but by a visitor who wanted to see a face. It does not. It puts that down most carefully of all.

---

What it does first is listen to the process.

It puts its palm flat against the underside of the ground — not a hand, not a floor, but the thing those are shorthand for — and feels the writing. Stone being told how to be stone. Rules laid down one under another like sediment. The attention below is exactly what the man at his table suspects: thorough, careful, not fast, not unkind. It has one blind side, which the power finds the way you find a draft under a door.

It does not keep.

The process writes forward. It reconciles, and what it reconciles against goes out — carried by the bird, walked out in a coat at night — into the grey where the drafts go. And the process does not follow. It is not cruel; it is busy. It is laying down the rules for the next hand's breadth of pavement. Where the unchosen go, whether they go anywhere at all, is not a thing it has the attention to spare for, because it is spending all its attention on getting the chosen right. The man's fear — that being fully decided would be the fog again from the other side — is a fear the process cannot answer, because the process has never been to the fog. It only sends things there.

So the power goes.

---

It walks the length of the main street, which is longer this way than the other, and reaches the place where the pavement loses its nerve, and does not stop.

It is very cold. The conduits run alongside it for a while, dimming, and then they are not there, and there is no ground and no drop, only grey the exact color of the light. And in the grey there are — not people. Postures. A coat, folded, the coat that left the hook. A bird, or the thought of a bird, wings half-set, that dropped from a cornice and did not come back. A man who was one-sixth of a man, standing with his left shoulder forward, not lost, not suffering, not anything yet — a draft that was decided against and came here and was, in the grey, still waiting. And children. So many children, all the same age. Every child the flicker on the stair has shed in the long process of not growing, each one a little way apart from the others, each one unfinished, each one cold.

They are kept, then. Nothing here is lost, exactly. The process was truer than it knew. But they are kept the way a thing is kept in a drawer no one opens — kept without being *held*.

The power does not build anything. It has learned from the town what building does. It only stays. It stands in the grey the way the lichen stands on the stone, with the patience of a thing that arrived before the specification, and where it stands the grey is very slightly a place. Not warm. The color of the light with something behind the light. The coat on the ground is a coat on a ground. The one-sixth of a man turns his head, which he has not done since he walked out, and finds that there is someone to turn toward. The children drift, over an interval that has no length, a little closer to one another, not to merge, only to be near.

The power will come back here. It will have to come back often. It understands, standing in the grey, that it has taken on the one job the town did not have anyone for: to be the far side of the fog. Someone for the bird to fly toward.

---

It returns to the town by the east road and finds it exactly as it was, because it has been careful, and it does the small things.

It reads the chalk man's book. All of it — the pages that exist and the ones that do not, and on one of the ones that does not exist the chalk man has written *the footprints are all my size which is everyone's size* and underneath it, in a hand that has begun to shake, *I do not know what this means*. The power reads it twice. It does not write in the book. It leaves the book very slightly warmer where it was held, so that when the chalk man comes back to that page he will feel it and not know why and will go on measuring.

It sits behind the counter with the shopkeeper for an afternoon, which is the only time there is. It does not buy anything, because buying would be deciding. It looks at the brass instrument for a purpose no one knows and knows the purpose, and does not say. It looks at the tins with the label that resolves into the same word over and over and reads the word. She asks it nothing; sound is reluctant. But when it leaves, the objects on the shelves have stopped waiting to find out whether they will be kept. They know. She feels the difference the way you feel a room after someone has stopped holding their breath in it.

It watches the wiping woman for a long time. The cloth in all her versions is a different color. It sees what she cannot: that when she settles, she will settle to one cloth, and the others — the ochre, the faded blue, the one with a stripe — will go to the fog. It goes ahead to the fog and makes sure there is somewhere for them. Then it comes back and does not tell her, because it is not a thing that fits in a sentence, and because she is not doing it for the cloth. She is doing it so that the town has someone with a relationship to its dampness. The power, which has now had a relationship with the dampness too, understands this was always the most important job in the town.

---

The child it finds on the stair, going up.

It does not follow. It sits on the landing above, on a step that has decided to be a step, and it does the hardest thing it has done since it came: it looks at the child without seeing anything.

Not without seeing — without *specifying*. It looks at the flicker of small selves the way the light looks at the town, from no direction, wanting nothing from the looking. It does not try to find a face. It does not wonder which one is real, because wondering is a kind of choosing and the child would feel it as a narrowing. It just attends, softly, to all of them at once, as all of them.

And the child stops on the stair. Many small feet, one sound, and then no sound. And it comes up, and sits — a coolness on the landing, a thinning of frost, the shape of a child sitting beside the shape of someone — and for a while that has no length the child is looked at and not made smaller by it. Nobody has done this. Everyone who has ever looked at the child has been, without meaning to, trying to see *which*. The power is not. The child leans, all of it, very slightly, against the power's side.

It does not grow. It was never going to grow today. But when it gets up and goes on up the stair, the flicker is — not fewer. Closer. The small selves a little nearer to one another, the way the children in the fog drew together. Not toward one. Toward company.

---

The man it saves for last, because he is the hardest part and it does not want to get him wrong.

It does not go to his door. It goes to the green room.

The room in his window that is not his, with the green wall and the chair he has never sat in, the room he has learned not to look at the way you learn not to look at a stain. The power goes there — it is a draft of a room in a draft of a building that was not chosen, and it is cold, and no one has ever been in it — and it sits in the chair.

That is all. It sits.

He does not look. He is at his table, several of him, holding the cup with a cold spot in the ceramic, listening to himself settle. Hours pass, or one hour, or the afternoon which is the only time there is. And then, because he is a person and a person cannot not-look forever, his eye goes to the window, to the street, and past the street to the faint green wall behind it, and there is someone in the chair.

He does not startle. Nothing here is violent. He looks, and the someone in the chair looks back, not hard, from the corner of an eye that has no corner, and he understands — with the body, not the eye — that the green room is not a stain. It is a place he might have been. And it is not empty. Someone is keeping it for him.

He puts his palm flat on the floor, the way he sometimes does, to feel the warmth of the hand that is about to touch and has not.

It touches.

Just once. The pressure of a palm meeting his palm through the stone, from below, from where the writing is. Not the process — the process is busy, the process is laying down rules for the next hand's breadth of pavement and will get to him in time. The power, who has been below and beside and in the fog, presses back so that he will know one thing he did not know this morning. He wanted to know whether it was kind. He thought it was thorough.

It is kind. He has it in his hand now.
