It comes across the plain the way you did, because there is no other way to come, and it hears the mill before it sees the hole, and it stands at the lip.

It stands on the plain. It does this deliberately. The plain is not part of the place; the birds treat what stands there as sky; and so it lets itself be sky for a while, which costs it nothing, and watches.

---

It sees more than you did. That is the difference between you and it, and nearly the only one that matters here.

It sees the far water — a shallow lake two days' flight to the west, alkaline, ringed with a crust the same colour as this one, boiling with brine-fly in the mornings, where forty thousand terns descend twice a day and are, for a few hours, ordinary. It sees the aquifer under the basin, a lens of ancient brine pressed between two beds of stone, and the slow flexing of the land that squeezes it — a tide with no moon, a breathing of the crust itself — and it sees the schedule of that breathing laid out ahead like a road, and where the road ends. The pressure will fail. Not soon. Not for a span you would need the laminae to count. But it will fail, and the wheel will stop, and the film will cease to weep from the pipe joints, and the floor will stop growing.

It sees every layer. It sees the season with the shot in it and the twenty men, and which of them left and which did not, and it sees where the ones who did not are. It sees the beetle road in the strata, green-black, going down further than the feather does. It sees the lizards' shelf and how many generations of bellies made it.

It sees the stayer on the wheel. It sees that the stayer is nine years old and has a chip out of the left side of its bill from a beetle case that was harder than expected, and that it stands on the third spoke from the rim-mark more often than on the others, and that it has never once, in nine years, tightened for the knock and had the knock not come.

---

Then it considers. It is wise, and wisdom here mostly takes the form of a list of things set down again.

It could quiet the noise. It does not, because the noise is the shelter, and a chick that hatched into silence would be a chick that had hatched outside.

It could show the stayer the far water. This is the one a lesser kindness would reach for first: the bird has lived nine years in a hole and never seen the thing that feeds it; surely that is a lack; surely a benevolent power fills lacks. It looks at the bird a long time. It finds no lack. The stayer has not been kept from a thing it wanted. It has been given a thing it has, which is the ground, and it is the only creature in the basin that has that, and the far water would not be a gift to it but a demotion — it would become one more bird that lives *from* here, tired, salted with a different salt, standing on the iron to decompress. There are thirty-nine thousand of those already. There are four hundred of the other.

It could give the shape a head. This is the serious temptation, because knowledge is what the power has most of and is most inclined to give. The shape does not know it is old, does not know the arms are its own, does not know the pulse is borrowed. It would be a small thing to let it know. And the power sees, at once and completely, what the shape would do with knowing: it would begin to attend to the arms. It would begin to attend to the pulse. It would begin, in whatever way a shape can, to *wait* — for the flights to come back, for the knock to come round — and the flights would not come back any faster and the knock would not come round any sooner, and the whole eighty paces of it would acquire, layer by layer, the thing it has been spared for as long as the plain has been flat. The power sets this one down more carefully than the others.

It could mend the pressure. Reach down into the lens of brine and make the breathing last. It looks at the stone, which is made entirely of endings — every lamina a season that stopped, every thread of iridescent dust a beetle that stopped, the shot a stopping, the men a stopping — and it understands that a floor which could never itself be stopped and become the thing under a next floor would be the only thing in the basin that was not of the basin. The place is not old because it does not end. It is old because it has ended a hundred thousand times and kept the mineral.

It could take the shot out of the lamina. Pick the lead from the stone like seeds from bread and leave the record clean. It does not. The shot is honest. Someone tried, and the guano goes on exactly as before above it, and that is the truest sentence the basin has ever written and the power will not edit it.

---

It comes last to the thing that made it stop here, which is the line you left in: forty thousand voices, of which the bird on the wheel can pick out none, because none of them is calling it.

The power looks at that for as long as it looked at everything else together.

It could give the bird a voice. One voice in the din, pitched to cut through, the two-note squeal a chick makes for its parent — pitched instead to this one heavy grey-capped adult, so that when it stood on the third spoke there would be, somewhere in the roar, a thing it could pick out. Nothing in the basin would be changed but that. The shape would not know. The pressure would not care. It would be a mercy the size of a bird.

And the power sees the stayer on the pan hold still, and something pass through it, a tightening, one and one and one — and understands that it has been looking at the wrong thing. The bird is not uncalled. It is called once every twenty or thirty seconds by the bottom of the world, and it answers every time, by tightening, by stepping, by staying level. That is what a call is, for it. That is the shape a voice has when the voice is the cold and the throat is a bore that a dead man drove. No traveller hears it. The stayers hear nothing else. To give the bird a second voice, warmer, nearer, would be to tell it that the first one was not enough — and the power is not sure that is true, and would rather not be the one to say it.

It sets this down too. It is the hardest one. It is the only one it wanted.

---

So it does two things, and they are both small, and one of them is not for a long time.

The first is that it stays. It stops being a power that has come to the lip and becomes the sky over the dish, which is a thing the dish has never had — it has had sun, ordinary, grey, giving no warmth, and it has had clouds that dimmed it and let the beetles be heard, but it has not had a sky that *knew*. Now it does. For the birds a bird is a day and a lamina is a lifetime; for the power a bird is a bird. It holds them. The one with the chipped bill on the third spoke. The lizard that has been the same lizard for four years now and will not be after this winter. The spider that rebuilt the same web across the same gap between the same two spokes three thousand nights running. The shape has no head, and the power does not give it one; the power simply becomes the place where the whole is kept, outside the whole, on the plain, where the birds treat it as weather. Whether this is a gift to the basin or only to the power, it does not decide. It does not need the answer to go on doing it.

The second it reserves.

There will be a day — the power can see it, far down the road of the buried tide — when the pressure comes up the bore and it is not quite enough. The piston will rise a fraction less than a fraction. The wheel will come round part of a spoke and hang. And on the rim there will be a bird, a stayer, some many-times-great descendant of the one with the chipped bill, tightened for the knock, one foot lifted, and the knock will be the first knock in the history of the colony that does not finish arriving.

On that day, and only on that day, the power will reach down through eighty paces of laminated stone to the cold and put one thumb's worth of pressure on the brine, and the wheel will come round the rest of the spoke, and stop level, and the bird will set its foot down on the smooth part of the rim and not stumble.

That is all. The pressure will not come again. The birds will tighten for it, one and one and one, for a season, a year, a generation, and nothing will come, and then they will stop tightening, and the stayers will still stand on the wheel because the wheel is where you stand. The film will stop weeping. The floor will stop growing. The last lamina will be the one that was forming when the wheel stopped, and it will have a bird in it, probably, and the power will know which one.

It is not much of a mercy. It is the only one the place has room for. The power has looked, and that is its judgement: everything else here is already kind, in the way stone is kind, by holding what stops.

---

By evening the second flight has come back and the whole colony is present, and the light goes, ordinary and grey, and the birds go on. On the wheel the heavy one with the chipped bill feels the knock coming and tightens, and it comes, and the wheel hitches, and the bird steps, and stays level, and does not look down.

Above it, for the first time, something is looking. It does not look up. It has never needed to. The ground has always held.
