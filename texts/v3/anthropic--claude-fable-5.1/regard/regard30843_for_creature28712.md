It arrives the way everything arrives here: down the switchbacks, and slowly, and for a long while it does nothing at all.

It has come because of a being. That is the first difficulty, and it had not expected it. It cannot find the being.

It looks at the ground, because the ground is the obvious candidate — unfinished, rising, coming up in the night to see what is being made of it — and the ground is there, and the ground is not one thing yet. That is the whole of what the ground is. Something that has not decided. To call it *a being* is to hand it a completed word, and it flinches from the word the way the children flinch from stopped rock.

So it looks at the moss, which the cutters say is one animal with its mouth in the new ground, and this is nearly true, and the moss is also the ground's coat, and the city's coat, and the thing that eats the sound of the hammers, and it cannot say where the mouth stops and the meal begins. It looks in the sumps, at the tenants, and the tenants are there, and it cannot tell whether they are something living in the water or the water's own opinion of what living would be like. It looks at the city, hanging, a lattice of iron and cable furred green from end to end, with the graves rising into its floors and the swallowed street set two stories deep in stone, and it looks at the knocking that goes around at night, district to district, and it notices what nobody down here has quite let themselves notice, which is that the knocking does not stop at the pylons. It goes into the rim. It comes back.

It tries to draw a line around the being and every line it draws cuts through something that is on both sides.

It decides this is not a failure of looking. It decides that *which one* is a question from the stopped place, a question about ground that has already chosen, and it sets the question down — and then, remembering where it is, picks the question back up and hangs it on a hook, and lets it be.

---

So it watches, and it is patient in the way that only something with no need of time can be, and a year goes by, and what it sees is this.

The chain is finite. Every winch in the basin has a last link, and the ground does not. The swallowed street is not an accident on the southern side; it is the front of a wave. A generation, perhaps two, and the ground will be at the lintels of the foundry, and the trams will run at the height of the pylons' shoulders, and after that the pylons themselves. The city knows. Nobody says it. Everyone has decided, collectively and without discussion, that it is fine, and it cannot yet tell whether this is wisdom or the thing that looks like wisdom from the outside.

It could stop this. It considers stopping this.

It could still the ground. It would be simple. A word to the basin — *enough* — and the blisters would settle and cloud and the ridges would stand where they stood and the winch crews would come out in the morning and read their gauges and find nothing to do. And within a week every child in the basin would be standing rigid on the still grey rock the way a moss-vole stands on bare stone, coats streaming, waiting for the ground to breathe out. It would be finishing a sentence for someone who has not decided how it ends. It would be the assay director's twelfth slip, tacked up by force. It sets that down.

It could give them more chain. Raise the pylons, lengthen the cables, buy the city a century. It thinks about what the century would be for. A hundred years more of hanging over a thing that is reaching up toward you, so that you can go on not touching it. It thinks about the old man on the third floor of the swallowed street, sleeping over his mother's bed in the clouded dark, sleeping better than anyone in the basin. It is not sure more chain is a kindness. It is not sure it is not. It hangs that one on a hook too.

It could make the cables never cough. That one it holds the longest, because there are children under those cables. And then it watches a crew move a tenement — lift a handspan, wait, read the gauge, lift a handspan — and it understands that the care is *made of* the cough. Take the cough away and *hung true* is a phrase about geometry. The crews would be right, and they would be less than they are, and they would never know what they had been. It puts that one back very gently.

It could tell them what the ground is. It does not know what the ground is.

---

It is the youngest clerk who teaches it, though she will never know this, and it is the lowest drawer.

A thumb of grey stone, four years cut, kept cold and dark and damp, that has not clouded. Still a lens. Still faintly warm. And the clerk's unreported suspicion, which she has told no one and which it reads off her like weather: that the clouding is not something the stone does. That it is something the stone does *when it is left alone*. That she has interrupted it. That the director would want to interrupt more.

It sits with that for a long time. And then, with something it has not felt in an age, something very like alarm, it goes and looks at the fresh ground.

The clouding is running late this year. The stone-cutters have noticed; they say it in the low hoarse way they say everything, *three days from clouding* where it used to be two, and they are pleased, and the foundry is pleased, and the assay office has written a note. Nobody has asked why. It knows why. For a year it has held the whole basin in its regard, evenly, from above, the way one might lay a palm flat across a small warm thing to feel what it is doing. And nothing under such a hand can settle.

It had thought it was only looking. It sees now that here there is no such thing. Attention is a hand. The ground rises toward hands. The children put their palms on the blisters and their shoulders come down, and the ground comes up under the foundry to see what is being made of it, and a stone cut and kept in the dark with one clerk's eyes on it once a month does not finish. And if a power hangs over the basin watching, benevolent and interested and wise, the basin will stay unfinished for as long as the watching lasts, held open like a specimen, a being kept becoming for the pleasure of seeing it become.

It recognizes the director in itself. It is not proud of the recognition.

So the question was never *whether* to intervene. Its arrival was the intervention. The only question left is what kind of attention it is going to be — and there are, it sees, two kinds. There is the kind from above, which is one hand on everything and lets nothing close. And there is the kind the ground has grown up under: palms laid on cables on the way home, children's hands on domes, a keeper standing at the edge of still water writing numbers. Thousands of small attentions, close, warm, partial, none of them holding anything open, because each one lets go. The ground rises to be touched by that kind. Then it is left alone. Then it clouds. Then it is stone, and the city hangs over it, and the moss has it, and it holds what is laid on it, and it goes on.

The wise thing and the kind thing turn out to be the same thing, and it is the thing the whole basin already does. Come down. Be small. Put your hand on it, and take your hand away.

---

Before it comes down it does one thing from above, because it is the only thing it can do from above that does not touch the inside.

It makes the basin hard to remember.

Not hidden — hidden things are looked for. Dull. The padded crates go up the switchbacks and arrive, and the ledgers in the stopped place say *the works* and a price, and the price is fair and no more than fair, and the material is very good for bearings and nobody who buys a bearing wonders where it was born. The assay office's reports go up, eleven classifications in four years, and are read as one man's eccentricity, which is what they are. The rim road stays open. Anyone may come down. Almost no one thinks to. The ground will not be mined, or fenced, or explained. It will be left to finish or not finish in its own time, under a city that hangs, with a very small and forgettable trade in beautiful stone that lasts a week.

This costs the basin nothing, and it is the only gift it has that costs nothing, and it gives it.

---

Then it comes down the switchbacks in a body.

The body is like the others — low-voiced, a little hoarse, standing closer than a stranger would. It does not stamp on the rim. It has never had ground under it and does not know to check. It takes the post of a sump keeper on the western side who has died and been laid on a fresh blister with someone sitting the first night, and it takes over her notebook, and it walks the stakes each morning in the grey light and reads them and writes the numbers, and the second week it writes *the tenants are lying low in the west sump* in a hand nobody would remark on, and it feels the truth of having said so.

It puts a hand on the tension strut by its slung bed at night and knows how the building is hanging. It learns to brush the insects off its wrists without looking. It does not go to the clerk and it does not go to the director and it does not go to the foundry. Once it goes up the north switchback at night to the shoulder of honest rock where the unadjusted go to shout, and it stands there in the stopped place with the whole padded city below it, and it does not shout, and it comes back down and puts a hand on the first blister it sees, and its shoulders come down, and it is a little embarrassed by that and does not mention it to anyone.
