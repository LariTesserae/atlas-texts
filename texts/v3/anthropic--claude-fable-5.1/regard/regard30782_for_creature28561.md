It comes down into it, the way anything must. That is the first thing to say about the power: it does not hover. It has been above places before and found that from above every place looks like a problem, and so it has learned to descend, and it descends here in the ordinary way, down the soft grey steps, into the brownish dusk, into the roar.

It hears differently than you did. This is not a boast on its behalf; it is simply the difference between a thing that has ears and a thing that has attention. You stood in the sink and the sound closed over you and you could not resolve it. The power stands in the same sink and the sound does not close. It hears the fliers as fliers and the pools as pools and the drift as the small dry rustle of a billion mouths, and it hears the walker, the hole in the noise, moving along the water three galleries down, and it hears all of these at once and as one, and it understands what you only guessed: that this is a country announcing itself to itself, and that no one inside the announcement has ever heard the whole of it.

It has, then, for a moment, something the basin has never had. An audience.

And because it is a power, and because power is a thing that leans, the first thing it feels is the lean. The wish to do. That is not a flaw in it. It is what benevolence is before wisdom gets hold of it: a hand already half-raised.

---

So it considers the hand.

It could lift the lid. That is the obvious one, the one any lesser kindness would reach for first. The cloud is generated here, exhaled here; a power of any size could thin it, or open a window in it, or simply persuade the basin to breathe less. Light would come down. Real light, the grey noon of the country above. And the power stands with that in its hand and follows it forward, and sees what it is: the coral-things spreading, yes, for a season, and then the first spore of something green riding in on the fliers' fur and finding, for the first time in ten thousand years, enough light to open. A film of it on the wet stone. Then a mat. Then the drift, which is the base of everything, becoming soil, which is the base of something else. The walls of the sinks would go green and then the fliers would go elsewhere because the walls would be soft and warm and full of things that eat eggs, and the pools would clear, and the knocking would stop, and in a hundred years there would be a forest here, a wet dark limestone forest of great beauty, and it would not be this. The power would have made a good place by ending a good place. It sets that down.

It could give them quiet. It considers this less long. It knows what the roar is. Silence here would be blindness, and there is no benevolence in blinding a country so that a visitor can hear himself think.

It could catch the young. This is the one it stands with longest, because this is the one that is not about the place but about the small ones, and a wise power does not let the shape of a thing excuse it from the particulars. The nursery ceiling: infants a hundred deep, and the constant shedding, and the floor that knows. It could put a hand under that ceiling. It could make the falling stop, or make the landing soft, or carry each one back up. It sees them clearly, each one, warm and alive for the length of the fall and for some minutes after. It could.

And it follows that forward too, and finds that the drift beneath the nursery is a floor because they fall, and the things in that drift have been what they are for as long as the nursery has been where it is, and the mothers bear as many as they bear because the floor takes what it takes, and to catch the young is to make, in a season, a ceiling too crowded to hang from and a floor with nothing in it. The mercy would run out of hands. That is not what stops the power. What stops it is older and simpler: that it has been to places where a kind hand reached in once, and the reaching was the only thing that ever went wrong there.

It could hold the roofs. It could stand under the thinning stone and keep it up. It looks at that one and then at the walker, and stops.

---

Because there is the question of who. The power was drawn here, the way you were told, by the place and by the being who lives in it, and a benevolence must have a face to be benevolent toward, and the power turns to look for the face.

It is not the chorus. The chorus is not a who. You were right about that. The chorus is an act, not an actor; it is what the place does, not what anyone in the place is, and you cannot give a gift to an act any more than you can give one to a season.

It is not the fliers, or the beetles, or the wax-colored things in the pools. They are lineages. They are what happens when time is given enough of itself. They are not asking, and the power has learned to be very careful with those who are not asking.

So it goes down. Past the nursery sink and the coral shelves and the ledges with their soft waiting predators, down through the floors that are roofs, to the flooded galleries where the black rivers run, and it follows the hole in the noise until it is beside it.

It does not see the walker. It does not try. It stands in the moving quiet and lets the quiet be what it is, and it understands, in the way that it understands things, that here is the only inhabitant of the basin that knows the whole basin. That maps all of it by listening. That has listened so long it no longer needs to move to hear, and moves anyway, slowly, toward the next place that is about to change. That makes no sound of its own, in a country made of sound, and is therefore the one thing in it that is not shouting into the dark. That goes to the falling roofs and is there before they fall and does nothing to hold them.

And the power, which came down here half-raised, feels the hand lower.

It had thought it might speak to this one. It had thought that a being this old and this alone, in a place where everything is heard and nothing is understood, might want, once, to be understood. That would be a gift it could give. But it stands in the quiet and knows that it is not alone with the walker; it is beside it. They are doing the same thing. Attending. Not intervening. Going toward change without hastening it and without holding it back. The walker is not a creature the power has come to help. It is what the place has instead of a power. It arrived at wisdom without benevolence, from below, over a life as long as the falling, and it arrived at the same place.

To speak to it would be to tell it something it already is.

---

So this is what the power does, and it is not nothing, and it is very nearly nothing.

It grieves, once, for the young. Not so that anything changes. The place has no one to grieve, and a floor that is made of the fallen has no one in it who can hold the fallen as loss, and so the power holds them, all of them, the ones today and the ones in ten thousand years of todays, for the length of a breath, and then lets them go into the drift where they were always going. That is a gift given to no one. A wise power gives those sometimes. It is how it stays a power that can weep and not a power that fixes.

It makes itself quieter than the walker. This is harder than it sounds. It is present here, and the basin hears everything that enters it; the fliers had already begun to shape their calls around its standing. So it steps back from being heard, and back, until the country closes over the place where it was like water over a dropped stone, and there is the roar and the one moving hole in the roar and nothing else. It does this so that it can stay. It has decided to stay, for a while, without being a change.

And then it walks. Beside the silence, along the flooded gallery, up through the lower sinks and down again, on the drifting circuit that goes always toward the thinning stone. It does not lead and it is not led. For some days there are two holes in the noise moving at one speed through the dark, and nothing in the basin remarks on it, because nothing in the basin can count.

It listens to the whole while it walks. This is the one thing it takes for itself, and it takes it openly: to be, for a while, the thing that hears this country entire, the fliers and the pools and the drift and the slow clicking memory of the coral-things and the quiet beside it, as one attention hearing another attention that cannot hear itself. It does not know whether that is a gift to the place or a gift the place is giving it. It suspects the question is not one the place has any interest in answering.

---

The roof goes on the ninth day. The walker has been under it for three.

There is the deep single note, felt before heard. Then the almost-silence, the whole country stopping to listen, and in the almost-silence, if you were near enough, you might have heard two things that were not sounds, side by side beneath the new sink, as the ceiling that had been black became a floor that was merely dark and a million fliers came down through the falling stone with their cupped faces still turned to listen.

The power does not catch them. It does not hold the roof. It stands where the walker stands and attends the falling with it, all the way down, and when the last stone has settled and the drift has taken the first of the fallen and the roar begins, as it always does, to resume, the power is already going up the soft grey steps, back into the light and the quiet, having done what it came to do, which was to find out whether there was anything to do, and having found that the doing was already down here, walking, and had been for as long as the roofs had been falling.

Behind it the sound closes. It closes slightly rearranged, as it does after every falling, and somewhere in the rearrangement is a hole that moves, one hole, at one speed, going where the roof is next.

The last roof is a long way off. When it goes, and the basin is one open sink under one lid, and the fliers have nowhere to hang, the power will be there. It has decided this and told no one. It will not hold that roof either. It will stand under it with the walker, if the walker is still walking, and attend the end of the place the way the place has attended every one of its own endings, without a sound, and then it will go up the steps one more time, and something green will finally find the stone.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does with a country that is not in crisis and is not waiting and is not empty for a single instant in any direction. It comes down. It listens to the whole. It grieves what the whole cannot grieve. And then it finds the one who has been doing the wise thing all along in the dark, and walks beside it for a while, and leaves.
