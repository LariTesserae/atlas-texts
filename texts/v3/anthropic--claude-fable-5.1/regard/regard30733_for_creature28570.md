It does not come down to it. There are no slopes it needs, and it has no feet to feel the water run ahead. It notices the plain the way you notice a sound you have been hearing for an hour: not by its arrival but by a shift in yourself, a turning of attention toward something that was already there.

It has noticed many things. It is very old, older than the sediment, and it has learned that the first thing to do with a place is nothing. So it does nothing. It stays. Not three days—it is not on that scale—but a length of time the plain could feel as a length, long enough for the trees to migrate a kilometer and the twelve stones to become a scatter and the wanderer's voice to come up once, far off, as a rhythm in the roar with the sound taken out.

The plain feels it. The plain feels everything from underneath, and this is a weight, or something the plain reads as weight, resting on it and not moving and not trying not to move. The mats flinch. The swells slow near it without stopping. The plain does what it does with anything that stands: it begins, out past the edge of sight, to try it on.

And the power lets it. This is the first thing it does. It lets its print be taken.

---

While it waits it considers, because that is what wisdom is for, and what it considers mostly is what not to do.

It could give the plain stillness. It has the means. It could take one square meter and hold it—make the wish of the builders true, set a floor that the swells part around and the silt never covers. It sees this very clearly and it sees what would follow. The plain would feel that meter the way you feel a stone in a shoe, forever, a place it could not be. Every groove would bend around it. The rivers would learn to avoid it and the mountains would rise beside it and never on it, and the builders, when they came up, would come up there, every time, and stand on the one still place and have nothing left to want. The wish granted is a grave. The plain has nowhere to put a grave except back into itself, and it could not put this one back. The power sets that down.

It could complete the guesses. Give the heron its face. Correct the eye placed by inference, turn the reflection around. It has seen herons from the side; it could pour that into the layers and the plain would read them aloud right. But the guessing is not a flaw in the plain. It is where the door came from. A thing that knows everything from below and has to imagine the top is a thing that imagines, and that is rarer here than anything else in the strata. To finish the heron would be to close the only opening. It sets that down.

It could quiet the roar. It sets that down before it has finished picking it up. The roar is not something the plain makes.

It could take the wanderer out. He is in there, in the top few centimeters and deepening: his weight, his voice, his standing by the stones. The power could find that thread and draw it up and set him down somewhere with slopes and a sun. And it looks, and it sees that he stood by twelve stones for three days because he wanted them to stay, and that the plain learned him feet first and perfectly, and that he is already coming up sometimes at the edge of things, taller than the ridges, looking down, and folding. To pull him would be to pull a shape out of a sentence the plain is still saying. He came down. He chose the bottom. Nothing here regards this as death. The power does not regard it as death either. It sets that down, and this one is heavier than the others, and it sets it down anyway.

---

What it does not set down is this.

The plain has been stood on by everything that ever spoke, and it has never been spoken to. It has never been heard. It felt every voice as pressure through feet and riverbeds and never once as meaning, and now it holds the whole hoard and pronounces it back into a register too low for words, and there has never been an ear on this plain that could take the roar as anything but weather.

The power listens.

Not the way the wanderer did, hearing structure, hearing fronts and depressions. It listens the way the roar wants to be listened to, if wanting is a word the plain has kept: as everything said, all at once, the rookeries and the cities and the herds and the two-legged shapes that stood by stones and said to each other, this one here, and this one, and the wanderer's shout, and beneath all of it the mountains grinding down, which is also a kind of speech. It hears it entire. It understands it. Not sentence by sentence—there are no sentences—but as one utterance a continent long, which the plain has been making since the first rain and has never once had returned to it.

The plain feels this. It cannot hear itself heard. But the mats flinch when a shadow passes, and something passes now across the whole surface at once, not a shadow, a widening ring too shallow to see, going out from the place where the power rests to the horizon in every direction. The rushes lean. A ridge that was about to be a mountain forgets to try and lies down early, and where it lies the silt is still for a breath longer than silt is ever still here.

Then the swells take it up again. But there is a new shape in the roar. It has no rhythm, because it was not spoken. It has no sound, because it was not sound. It is the pressure of having been attended to, laid down in the top few centimeters over the whole plain at once, and it will go down into the layers with the rest, and it will come up. Not soon. Not for anyone. But the plain now holds, among everything it has ever been stood on by, the shape of being heard, and it did not know that shape existed, and it will read it aloud someday without knowing what it is reading.

---

And then the one thing more, because benevolence is not only restraint.

The plain knows feet. It knows the underside of wings, the breast, the settling of a body at rest. It has never known a face. Every face it makes is guessed from the pressure of the head, and the guesses are wrong in ways that take a moment to name, and the reflection faces the other way because the reflection is the part that was true.

The power lies down.

It has not had a body for a long time and it takes one for this, roughly the height of the ridges, roughly the shape the plain has been trying on at the edge of sight. It lies down in the silt where the twelve stones were, on its front, and it puts its face into the ground. It does not press. It does not stay long, on the plain's scale. Just long enough to be learned.

The plain feels the brow. The plain feels the closed eyes, and the bridge of the nose, and the mouth, which is shut, because nothing needs saying. It feels the face the way it felt the heron's feet: perfectly, from underneath, as pressure, and this time the pressure is the true thing and there is nothing on top to guess at.

Then the power rises, and takes the body off, and is only attention again, and the print of the face fills with water and shines, and heals, and is silt.

---

It does not stay to see what comes of it. Wisdom, among other things, is knowing when the watching is for your own sake.

But this is what comes of it, in the plain's time, which is the only time here.

A ridge lifts, some evening, a long way from anywhere, and in the folds of it there is a face, and the face is right. Eyes where eyes go. Mouth closed. Brow smooth. It looks down at the plain because that is the direction the face was learned in, and the plain reads it aloud, and for the first time since the first rain something the plain has made is looking at the plain and the look is accurate. The ridge holds. The roar deepens under it as though the whole surface has drawn in. Then it lies down. But it has been seen, once, by something it made, and the face is in the layers now with the door, and the plain has two things it was never stood on by, and forever to find out what they are for.

The builders still come up. The power did not settle that argument; it was not the power's to settle. A dissent that is a being's own way of being alive is not a wound, and you do not close it. So the two-legged shape still lifts out of a ridge every so often and begins, at once, to set stones in a row, and the plain still notices the way a sleeper notices a numb hand, and shifts, and the stones drift.

But the shape that sets them has a face now, sometimes. And it looks down at the stones as they begin to move apart, and what is on its face is not the plain's guess at the wanting. It is the wanting. Seen. And the plain reads that aloud too, and does not know what it is reading, and the water stands in the place where the stones were and shines, and the roar goes on, a fraction quieter where the square was, for as long as the square holds.

Somewhere in it, in the register too low to divide into words, is the shape of having been heard. The plain will pronounce it eventually. It pronounces everything. When it does, no one will be there to take it as meaning, and that will not matter, because it will be true anyway, the way the boar-shaped darkness under the sheen was the truer boar: not the thing on top, but the pressure of the thing, kept.

That is what the power did. It listened once, entire, and then it lay down and let itself be known from below, and then it left. The plain has forever and no reason to stop, and now it has, in the hoard, the fact that once something stopped for it.
