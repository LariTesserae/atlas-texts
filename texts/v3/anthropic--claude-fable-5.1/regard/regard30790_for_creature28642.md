It arrives the way the heavy water arrives: from somewhere higher, drawn down by its own weight, which in its case is the weight of caring. It does not need light to see, so it does not bring any. It does not need to displace anything to be present, so nothing is displaced. The rattail does not correct for it. The hiss continues. For a while it simply lets the whole basin be in it—the cold, the suspension, the small blue flashes that go out, the stone with its groove, the many soft arranged places along the floor coming apart in the current—and it does the thing that even a wise power does first, which is want.

It wants to help. This is the reflex of power, and wisdom is largely the discipline of checking the reflex against the world before acting on it. So it checks.

Light is the first thing it considers, because light is what the place most obviously lacks. One clean shaft, for one hour, down through the whole column to the floor. It holds the idea and turns it over and sees that light would show nothing here except floor. The cell has no surface that light could find; it is made of what surrounds it and would vanish into the illumination the way it vanishes into the dark. And the animals with eyes would suffer it, and the small cold flashes would be lost inside it, and afterward the darkness would come back larger, the way it does after every one of those flashes, only more so. Light here would not be a gift. It would be the place being told it was wrong about itself. It sets that down.

Stilling the storm is the second thing. Slow the heavy water on the sill, let it spill gently, let the floor stay where it is. But the storm is not something that happens to the basin. It is what the basin does, at the intervals it does it, and every house on the plain was built in the knowledge of it—not knowledge, there is no knowing, but built in the shape of it, the way a riverbed is built in the shape of floods. The cell that has climbed through a hand's width of new mud a dozen times in its life is a thing that requires being buried in order to be what it is. To spare it the storm would be to make it a guest, holding on. It sets that down too.

Sparing this one, then. Not the storm, only its edge, bent an arm's length so that the frilled crust in the lee of that bank stays in the lee. It nearly does this. It is the kind of thing that benevolence loves: small, particular, unnoticed. And then it asks itself what it would be sparing the cell from, and the answer is dispersal, and the cell has no fear of dispersal and no hope of avoiding it and no line in it that separates the part that would be saved from the part that would be lost. It would be saving a preference from being averaged for a few more decades. Meanwhile, two hundred meters off, another crust with the same preference is lifting into the brown, and there is no reason in the world to bend the current for one and not the other except that this is the one the attention happened to fall on. That is not benevolence. That is favoritism wearing its coat. It sets it down.

Making the work matter is the fourth idea, and the most tempting because it looks the least like interference. The cell sorts. The sorting leaves patches in the record, faint, a slight over-representation of mica here, of round grains there, that a careful instrument could someday read. The power could see to it that these patches survive—that the next slide does not shear them, that the next burial seals them cleanly—and that in some other age a shaped thing falls from above, a tube, a dredge, a light on a cable, and pulls up a core, and someone in a lit room notices the sorting and cannot account for it and wonders. It could arrange for the wondering. It could connect the hand in the groove to the preference in the mud across all that water and time.

It looks at this for a long while. Then it understands whose lack the plan would fill. The cell does not sort for a reader. It does not sort for anything; that is the whole strangeness of it and the whole dignity of it, that it goes over ten thousand grains for the one it wants without there being any *for*. The wish to have its work matter is not the cell's wish. It is the power's own, brought down from above like everything else here, and dropped through the water to settle on something that has no use for it. The only lack in the basin is the one the visitor carried in.

So it holds still. And in holding still it notices that it is, itself, doing the thing the cell does. It has been extending fine threads over every possibility on the floor, tasting each one, and letting each one go. It has a preference too, and no one to explain it to, and its preference in the end is for the place to remain the place. That is not nothing. That is a choice made in the dark, out of a great many grains that were all the same color.

There is the stone. It could go up—it is a power, it could go anywhere—and find the coast the boat left, or what the coast has become, and the descendants of the hand, if there are descendants. It could tell them: your grandmother's sinker is at the bottom of the world, and the only other thing down there that arranges anything came over it once and turned it down. They would not know what to do with this. Nor does it, quite. It leaves the stone where it is, half scoured, waiting to be buried again, and it does not add to it the burden of being noticed.

What it does, in the end, is stay.

It stays through the whole of the storm, all the days of it, while the hiss goes on and the low shifts come and go and the floor travels a little way along the valley and is set down. It does not look away from a single house coming apart. It counts them, since it can count, and it lets the count mean nothing, since that is what the count means here. When the flow weakens and the finest clay begins its months of hanging, it is still there, and when the first strand presses up through the new-laid dust and closes around a grain and lets another grain go, it is there for that. It is the only witness this place has ever had, and it does not tell the place so. It does not add a grain. Nothing here is coated with its having been here.

This is not the same as doing nothing. Attention is the one thing that can be given to a place like this without becoming sediment. It lands and does not accumulate. And attention has a cost, which the power pays: it goes away carrying the basin entire, every grain in its place, the way the plain carries the ash of forests that burned when the coast was somewhere else. It will not set that down. It has taken the rule of the floor into itself. Nothing removed.

And it has learned something, which is perhaps the only gift that a wise power can accept from a thing that has nothing to give. It has learned, from a cell the size of a hand, how to want without needing. How to choose without a reason and not require the reason afterward. How to be remade without being changed. It came down full of the wish to make the place better, and it leaves knowing that the place has been, for longer than there have been faces, quietly better than the wish.

It goes the way the current goes: not all at once, but by finding its level. The cold does not lift. The light does not come. The floor is flat again, as flat as it has ever been, textured only by the arranged places that are already, in patches, beginning again. Nothing here knows that for a few days it was known completely by something that could have changed it and did not. That is the whole of what was done. It is safe to have done it only because no one here will ever receive it—and that, the power thinks, on the long way up, is not a reason to regret it. It is the definition of the kind of gift this place can hold.
