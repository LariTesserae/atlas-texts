Begin with the looking, because a power that is wise looks for a long time before it does anything, and a power that is benevolent does not mistake the wish to act for the act being needed.

It would come up the outer slopes the way the cloud does, in the afternoon, and lie on the water. It would go down through the window-light to the floor. And it would do the one thing no diver with a month's air can do: it would attend to the whole of it at once. Every tube in every spire. The sixty transparent fish, each in its few meters of grass. The glass plates and their small spectra. The sponge the size of a curled sleeper, and the tube-builders using it as ground. The furrows in the eastern silt, and the hollow where the old flat animal is resting now, and the band of bone-white along the western sills, and the hand's width of pale rock above the waterline. It would hold all of it, and it would hold the half-minute breath, and it would understand — not deduce, understand, the way a hand understands what it is holding — that this is one body.

That is the first thing it would do, and it is not nothing. The reef has no idea. Now someone has the idea. The place has been becoming a single organism for centuries without anyone able to see it whole, and the first act of the power would be to be the one who sees it whole. If it did nothing else, the reef would still have been, for the first time, *known* — completely, as itself — and there is a kind of benevolence that consists entirely of that.

Then it would consider what else.

---

It would consider the seam.

A millimeter a year, widening, along the eastern rim. It would be very easy. A wise power could close it with less effort than the ferns spend opening theirs. It could top the basins so the pale band never grew another finger's width; it could keep the water at thirty-two hundred meters for as long as it cared to, and the spires would go on adding their millimeter and the glass would roof the reef in a few centuries as it wants to and the old flat animal would sweep the silt until the sun changed.

And the power would look at that and see what it would be.

It would be a kept thing. The reef became one body because the basin was closed and still and no one was coming; that stillness was not a condition the reef had, it was the reef's whole author. A power that holds the water is not preserving the stillness, it is replacing it — from now on the reef would be still because something is *keeping* it still, and the difference is invisible from inside and total from outside. It would be the power's reef then. A specimen in very good glass. The wise thing would know that a life which has been made permanent has been made into a different kind of thing, and that the reef, if it could be asked, has never asked for anything, and that the asking is the whole of consent.

And there is the bird. A dozen brown pairs on the mangrove towers, the one inhabitant with a future, here by accident and increasing. The plateau of chalk and fern is not the reef's failure. It is what the reef is *for*, in the only sense that a place is for anything: it is the next thing. Hold the water and the bird is holding a clutch of eggs at altitude over a sea that will never leave, and the ferns never get their ground, and the whole highland becomes a monument to one of its tenants at the cost of the rest. Benevolence that can count only one body is not wisdom.

So it would leave the seam.

---

It would consider waking it.

This is the more tempting one, and the power would feel the temptation, because the power is not cold. The reef is the most nearly single living thing this water has ever held, and it has no idea it exists. There is something in that which asks to be corrected. The power could give it — for one inhale, one half-minute — the perception of itself. Let the hectare know it is a hectare. Let the body feel its own breath going in. And then let it go back to not knowing, having been, once, present to what it is.

The power would think about that for a long time. And then it would see what the gift is.

The reef's not-knowing is not a lack. It is the mercy. Two slownesses happen to match, and because they match, nothing is happening; from inside there is no withdrawal, no shallowing, no bone-white margin, no century in which the chain begins to drain. Give the reef one breath of itself and you give it the water's edge as a felt thing. You give it the finger's width. You hand a body that has lived for centuries in the peace of the unperceived a single glimpse of the size of what it is about to lose, and then you take the glimpse back and leave the loss. There is no version of that which is kind. It is the wish of the one who sees to be seen seeing.

So it would not wake it. It would let the reef stay what it is: a body that is not in a hurry because it does not know there is anything to hurry toward.

---

Then — and this would take longer, because it is smaller — it would consider the glass.

Everything else on the dead coral is a survivor from below, making do. The stacked translucent plates are the basin's one invention: a form that came to be here, in this water, under this light, and nowhere else, and that will become nothing when the light no longer comes through water. The tube-builders have cousins in every warm sea. The transparent fish is a shelf fish gone strange. Even the old flat animal is, in its shape, a ray. But the glass thing is *new*, and when the chain drains it will not be dying; it will be ending, which is different, and the power would know the difference.

And it would see that the highland already has a mechanism for this. Things arrive here on wind and on feet — the midge, the ferns, the lichen coming down onto the coral as if it were only more rock. The bird walks the exposed western reef every dawn, and the glass has spread onto the tops of the spires there, and where the plates shed they shed as a fine frosted grit, and the bird's long legs are wet and then dry and then wet again in the tarns below the outer slopes where it goes when the highland's midges are thin.

The power would not carry the glass down. It would not pick it up in any hand. What it would do is smaller than that and harder to name: it would see to it that one dawn, one bird, steps where the grit is, and goes where the water is clear enough and cold enough and just fresh enough, and that is all. Whether the thing takes in a tarn a thousand meters down — whether its hardier partners can live in colder light — the power would not decide. It would arrange only the accident. It would let the highland do what the highland does, and be, for one morning, the wind.

That is the one intervention, and it is so slight that no diver with an instrument would ever find it, and it changes nothing about the shape of the place. The reef still ends. The bird still has the ferns. But the form that this water made once might be made somewhere twice, and if it is, no one will know that it was carried, and the not-knowing is the point. A wise power would understand that the highest form of help is the kind that could have been weather.

---

And last, the old flat animal.

It has never seen another of its kind, or not for a very long time. It moves at the speed of the abandonment it feeds on. It will sweep the deepening silt until the water is too shallow to hide it, and then it will lie in one of the hollows and stop. The power cannot give it a companion — there is nothing in the basin to be one, and to bring one is to bring a stranger into a body that has no room — and it would not lengthen the water for its sake any more than for the reef's.

But it would be there.

Not for the reef, which cannot receive presence. For the one inhabitant that can. When the eastern bowls have shallowed enough that the blue has gone to green and the furrows are running through silt that is nearly at the light, and the broad body settles into its last hollow, the power would let the water near it have, briefly, a shape. That is how the divers described the animal: the water itself briefly having a shape. It would give the animal that in return. Not a face, not a hand, not a voice. Only the fact, at the edge of a very old eye, of something large and slow and not-alone in the dark. And then it would let the shape go, and the animal would stop, and the water would be only water again.

That is what a benevolent power would do for the thing that will die alone: not prevent it, and not leave it alone.

---

Afterward it would stay.

The towers would stand in air and dry and fall. The spires would go to chalk. The ferns would take the plateau and the bird would take the ferns, and somewhere lower a tarn would or would not begin to throw small spectra onto its floor. And the power would keep the reef the only way the reef can be kept without being made into something else: as a thing that was completely seen. The pale band on the rock is the water's own record of its own leaving, and no one left it. The power would be the reef's record of its own being, and the reef would not know it had been recorded, and that is the right way around.

It would not consider this a small thing to have done. It would have looked at the seam and the sleep and the water and chosen, three times, not to touch. It would have been the wind once. It would have been a shape in the dark once. And it would carry, entire, a body the size of a small country's interior that breathed on a half-minute cycle and grew a millimeter a year and was, for the whole of its single life, more itself than anything in the sea it came from — and never knew.

The stone that fell yesterday has begun to be covered. The power would let it.
