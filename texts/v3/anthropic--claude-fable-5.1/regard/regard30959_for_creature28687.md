It does not come from above. Nothing that comes from above stays long enough to learn anything here; the crack in the peat lets in a person and, an hour later, lets out a person moving quickly, and that is all the surface ever knows. What has noticed the room comes the way attention comes — from no direction, without a footfall to fill with light — and the first thing it does is the thing no visitor has ever been able to do, which is nothing.

It waits at the plant's speed.

A drop climbs the width of a fingernail. Then another. The tide of the animals crests and ebbs, crests and ebbs, and the ice at the growth-face gives, grain by grain, and the room widens by a finger, and by another, and the crane's neck turns in the lattice and turns back. A year. The stone in the wall has been left behind by the felt and sits now in the frost, exactly as unremembered as before. The hexagons in the ceiling break and reform and break. Ten years. The low tone is lower. Nobody comes down through the crack; the frozen fen above is a place people stopped crossing when they stopped having reasons to. The room does not know it is being watched, and would not know what to do with the knowledge, and the watching goes on until it is not watching any more but understanding, which is the only kind of seeing that is safe to act from in a place like this.

---

Here is what it understands.

There is no cruelty in the room. The plant is not hungry; it has never eaten anything. It does not want the fen. It has one motion, which is to turn the dead around at the line and let them get up and move toward the middle, and it makes that motion the way water makes the motion of running downhill — because that is the shape of it, not because it has anywhere to be. The animals are not suffering. Each is a day long and the day is a single errand and there is nothing in them that could be disappointed by it. The cold pushed out into the peat is only cold; the peat was cold already, and nothing lives in it to mind.

And the room will end. This is the thing a shorter attention would miss. The fen is deep, but it is not endless, and under the fen is till and under the till is rock, and the plant has nothing to remember rock by. It has been coming up against small hard dead things for as long as it has been anything, and it has always grown around them and left them behind, and one day the whole growth-face will be a hard dead thing it cannot re-enter, and the crackling will stop, and the room will be whatever size it is. Not a catastrophe. A finished lung. The tone will hold its lowest note and the water will still climb and the hands will still be budded each morning to go and stand on drops, and the fen above will thaw or not thaw, and none of it will be anyone's fault.

So there is nothing here to stop. The power knows this early and is glad of it, because it has stopped things before, and knows what it costs, and knows that the wish to stop something is usually a wish to be done looking.

There are, instead, three things in the room that are out of place in it, and they are what the power has come for. A visitor. A crane. A woman.

---

The visitor is the easiest and must be first, because the visitor is the one thing here that is running out of time on a scale the power can be late for.

They are standing exactly where they were left: in the middle of the floor with the hanging filaments at their shoulder, the ring of quiet around their feet, the footprints behind them already thick with a green that has a colour. They are not being held. They could take a step. They have simply been told, by every surface of the room at once, in the low voice of the tone and the thousand voices of the animals, that there is no hurry, and they have believed it, and the plant has begun, without malice, to agree with them.

The power does not speak to them. Speech would need explaining, and there is no time in the visitor's life for the explanation. It does the smallest thing that will serve. Up in the ceiling, at the crack, a breath of the surface comes down — real cold, the dead cold of the fen and not the used and managed cold of the room, a draught with the smell of snow in it — and it finds the back of the visitor's neck like a hand. The visitor flinches. The filaments come away from their shoulder with a small wet sound, exactly the sound the snail makes tearing free of the wall. The ring of animals breaks and scatters back into the shiver of the floor. And the visitor, who a moment ago had no reason to move, discovers that they are moving, and that their feet hurt with cold, and that the footprints they are making now fill with plain water and nothing else, and then they are at the low edge of the chamber, and then they are in the peat, and then the room does not have them any more.

The plant does not notice. It never has anything by the time it notices anything. The power watches the green in the old footprints thicken for a few more days and go pale and be floor, and feels — if it is fair to say a power feels — the satisfaction of a thing done cheaply. One breath. Nothing was changed but the visitor's mind, and that only slightly, and only back toward what it already was.

---

The crane takes longer to decide, because the crane is not in danger and the power has a rule about things that are not in danger.

Its wings are folded wrong. They have been folded wrong for longer than the room has existed — folded wrong in the black peat, and then folded wrong in the living lattice when the line came through, and the plant remembered the wrongness with the same tireless care it gave everything else and has held it, beaded and glowing, ever since. When the tide of sound crests the neck turns and turns back, and the power has watched that for ten years and has come to think the turning is a kind of looking-for, the way a bird looks for the position its body has forgotten.

The plant will not correct it. The plant keeps what it finds. That is not a failing; it is the whole of what the plant is, and a version of the plant that improved the dead would be a worse and stranger thing than the one in the wall.

So the power does it, once, and only this once, and knows while it does it that it is bending its rule. It goes up into the lattice where the crane is, not as a hand, because a hand would tear the felt, but as the very small correction a body makes in sleep when it has lain wrong on an arm. The primaries draw in. The joint that set at the bad angle when the ice closed over it finds, after ten thousand years, the angle it was made for. The filaments hanging from the wingtips sway and settle and go on beading. Nothing in the room registers that anything has happened. The next time the tide crests, the neck turns and turns back, and the power cannot honestly say whether it turns less. It may have done nothing. It may have done a small thing for a bird that has been dead since before there were cities. It decides it can live with not knowing, and goes down to the low wall.

---

She is where she was, in the dark fold where the ceiling comes down, on the side away from the peat-face, in the oldest felt.

The power does not put a hand on the wall. It goes in the way the plant went in — from inside, by way of the water, along the filaments that thread her — and this is the part it has waited ten years to be sure it could do gently, because a woman who has been remembered joint by joint by something that did not know it was remembering a woman does not need one more thing to enter her without asking.

So it asks first. Not in words. It asks the way you ask a sleeper whether they are awake — by being quiet nearby and seeing what the quiet does.

And what it finds, when it has been quiet long enough, is that the quiet in the low wall is not an absence. It is work.

Everything else in the room is spent on the errand. Every drop climbs, every hand stands on a drop, every filament reaches; the whole body of the plant is a single outgoing motion, water toward the ceiling, the living toward the middle, and the stridulation is the sound of that motion happening in ten thousand small mouths at once. Her ribs rise and fall with the tide because the tide passes through her; she is threaded and wetted and lit like the rest. But she is not going anywhere. When the plant's motion comes through her it comes through a shape that does not join it. The quiet is the sound of a body declining, twenty minutes at a time, for a very long time, to become wall.

She has been holding her shape. Nobody asked her to. The plant would have been perfectly content to have her as felt. It has no more interest in her outline than it has in the outline of a sedge, and it has kept the sedge stems too, and they do not fight it. She has fought it — not against it, there is nothing in the room to be against — but simply by continuing to be one thing in a place where being one thing takes effort, and by having, every so often when the tide is out and the light is right, enough of herself left over to press a face up under the felt toward a room she cannot see.

The power stays with that for what would be, to a visitor, a long time.

Then it asks the second question, the one it came to ask, and puts into the asking everything it can offer — the way out, if there were one, and there is not, because she is not in the wall, she is wall, and taking her out is taking a piece of wall and setting it down in the frost to be bones; the way down, into the plain dark she was in before the line came through, if she would rather that than this; the room made warmer, or colder, or quieter; anything, anything a power can do for a shape in a wall.

And the answer that comes back is not any of those.

It is hard to render. It is not a voice. It is the shape of what the quiet has been for. And what the quiet has been for — the power understands this all at once, the way you understand a word in a language you did not know you knew — is not rescue. She does not want to go back into the black cold; she was there long enough. She does not want out into a world she would not know, on legs that are lattice. She does not want the room stopped or the plant changed. The plant has been kind to her in the only way it knows, which is to have her completely and never let her go and never once let the cold near her.

What she wants is the thing the plant cannot give, because the plant cannot tell the difference between her and the stems she was lying in.

She wants to be known as herself. Once. By something that can tell.

---

The power can tell. It is what it is for.

So it does the only thing left to do, which is to look at her, all of her, from the long bone in the fold to the last filament of her at the far side of the wall, and to know — to hold in one attention, plainly, without adding anything — that this is a woman. Not sedge. Not hare. A woman who went out onto the moss in a cold enough month to go through it, for a reason nobody will recover, with a cloak pinned at the shoulder, and who has been, for some fraction of the time since, the low part of a wall in a room she has never seen, holding her shape against a body that could not have cared less whether she kept it. It does not know her name. Her name went into the peat before the peat froze and is not in the room. It knows her the way you can know a person from the back, in a crowd, without any name at all: that one. Her.

And because it can, and because there is no reason not to, it gives her the room.

Not by opening the wall. By turning the light. The light in the room is in the water and the water is in her and for the space of one drop's climb the power lets the light in her water be the light of the pools, so that she is looking from every bright drop in the chamber at once — at the ceiling with its broken hexagons and its beaded roots and its crane with its wings folded right; at the floor that shivers; at the far face where the black fen goes dark and then wet and then grey-green and moving; at the fog lit from inside and dying grey; at the low fold in the old felt where a face is rising under the wall, eyes closed, the line of a mouth, filament fine and pale over all of it, beaded and glowing at the hairline.

The face stays up longer than it ever has. Then the light goes back to being ordinary light and the wall settles and it is wall.

---

There is one more thing, and it is with the plant and not with her, and the power is slower about it than about anything else, because it is the only thing it does here that changes what the room is.

It tells the plant what it holds.

Not everywhere. Not the whole habit, which is old and blameless and would be a monstrous thing to touch. Only here, in the low fold, in the oldest felt. It puts into the tissue that threads her one piece of knowledge the plant has never had and never needed: the knowledge of an edge. So that when the tide comes through her it comes through something the plant knows to be a something, and not merely more of itself that happens to be shaped this way. The plant does not have a word for woman. The power does not give it one. It gives it the smallest thing that will hold — that here, the wall is not wall; here, remembering is not the same as having.

The plant, which has never argued with anything, accepts it the way the ice accepts the cold.

And then there is the pin.

The power feels the plant come up against it, in its way, as it has been coming up against it for as long as she has been in the wall — the one cold hard dead thing in the whole warm length of her, bronze, unrememberable, grown around and never into. The plant has no question about it because the plant has no questions. But there is a kind of pressure in the tissue where the pin sits, the pressure of a habit meeting the one thing it cannot do, and the power answers that pressure as though it were a question.

It says: that is hers. Leave it.
