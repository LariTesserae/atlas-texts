**What Attended**

It noticed the way you notice you have been hearing something for a long time.

That is the only way anything could notice this place. There is no sun to catch the eye, no motion, no smoke, no signal. There is a chord, held for longer than the peaks have existed, changing on the scale of continents, and somewhere in the register of things that listen to the whole of everything, that chord had been sounding, unremarked, as a single low bias in the total sound — the way a person in a large house may go years before realizing that the hum they have always taken for silence is a pipe.

Then it heard the hum for what it was. A country being said. And it turned toward the loud, the way the plants do.

---

It did not arrive. Arriving requires a direction, and the light here has none, and the power came in the manner of the light: evenly, from nowhere, so that the country was simply attended where a moment before it had not been. Nothing changed. The roar held. The stalks at the lips of the drafted valleys went on trembling. The bend in the light twenty paces past the last laid stone went on lowering its flake at the pace of a continent leaning.

But the power saw what the visitor took a long while to see, and saw it all at once, because it did not have a body that refused.

It saw the axis. Silent at one end, deafening at the other, and everything in the country strung along it like beads. It saw the plants leaning toward the loud, the wide bare reaches that were not empty but far, the doubled slopes that were also cliffs. It saw the eight bends in the light — eight, it counted, and did not need to count twice — standing along the unfinished ridges. And it saw, in the doorway of the roofless hut, what no one else could see: not an emptiness but a presence so resolved that it had passed the horizon of nearness altogether, a person the quiet had carried infinitely far without moving them an inch, still facing east, still keeping the cairn in view.

It saw the hammerstone by the knee. It saw the face.

---

A benevolent power, noticing a place, wants to do something. That is what the benevolence is for. And a wise one knows that this wanting is the first thing to be careful of.

So it did what it could do without touching anything, which was to hold each possible kindness up against the country and see whether the country survived it.

It could bring water. One cloud, one rain, a stream let down from the yellow-edged sky into a valley that had been in draft since before the cairn was laid. And the power saw what water would do here: it would be the first thing in this country that ever *happened*. It would run on the finished stone and wear an edge that had never been worn. It would find the roots that lay inside the rock and it would fill them, and they would have no use for it, and they would rot from the inside outward, because they had never learned what water was for and this was not a hardship until now. The plants were not thirsty. Nothing here was thirsty. To bring water would be to invent thirst in order to answer it.

It could quiet the roar. Turn the chord down, gently, by a hair, so that the ones on the ridge could hear each other and be heard, so that the body's refusal would relax and the visitor could walk out along the crest to the half-wall and put a hand on the shoulder of the one setting the stone. And the power saw that the roar was not a noise made *by* the country but the country in the act of being; to quiet it would be to slow the saying, and what is slowly said arrives slowly, and what is not being said is finished, and finished is far. A hair less loud, and the eight on the ridge would begin to grow distant. Silence, in this country, is the word for goodbye.

It could finish the ridge. Speak the draft into rock in a moment, so that the wall would stand on solid ground and the one laying the flake could set it down and step back and look at their work. And then they would be standing on quiet stone, resolved, and they would be far. It would be exactly what the one in the doorway had done, only done to someone instead of chosen by them.

It could reach into the doorway and bring the far one near again. It looked at this the longest. And it saw that far was not a place you could be brought back from, because it was not a place. It was a note. The one in the doorway had become the bass in the chord, the lowest thing in the country, the finished ground itself. You cannot carry a note somewhere. You can only wait for the chord to lean.

So it did none of these. It understood that the country was not suffering, and it understood that this was not the same as the country needing nothing.

---

Then, because it could, it walked into the roar.

The body refuses before the mind does. The power had neither in the way the place understood them, and it went out past the last laid stone into a sound that would have stopped a heart, and stood in the light next to the one setting the flake, and was near.

It had not expected to hear anything. The visitor could not; the place had been clear about that. But the visitor could not hear because the words were spoken at the volume of the draft, and the power was standing in the draft, and in the draft that volume is simply the volume of speech. So it heard.

It will not repeat what it heard. That is a decision it made in the first moment and has not revisited. But it can be said that the three who stood close together, as people stand in a wind, were not talking about the wall, or the rock, or the terrible slow business of being said. They were asking each other a question, and the question was about the west. Was the cairn still standing. Was the doorway still facing them. And the answer, passed among the three at the pace of centuries, was that it must be, because nothing here falls. And the one at the wall, hearing this — because in the roar everyone hears everyone, and it is only the quiet that is deaf — went on lowering the flake, and the power saw at last which way the wall was going. It was not running along the crest for its own sake. It was running west. It was a hundred paces of well-laid stone reaching back toward a hut a person could see from the ridge and could never return to, because the only road back to the quiet was to become quiet, and to become quiet was to arrive at the doorway and find it infinitely far, and find yourself in it.

Everyone in the loud country was trying to reach everyone else. That was the whole of what the country was doing, underneath the saying of mountains. And the axis would not permit it. The near could not be found. The far could not be reached. Those in the roar were nearer to each other than any people have ever been, and their nearness was itself the wall between them and home.

The power stood in that for a while — a short while, for a power; long enough that the flake dropped by the width of a hair.

Further along the crest it found the counter. There was no whiter stone in the counting hand, because the whiter stone had been left on the slab, and the counter was marking with a finger, with an intention, into rock that had not yet agreed to be there. The power leaned close and heard the number being said. It was not three hundred and twelve. It will not say what it was. The count has always run ahead of the tally, because a number can be said faster than a stone can be persuaded to record it, and the slab on the saddle was only ever the part of the counting that had managed to catch up.

---

It went back to the quiet and it read the plants.

Nobody can read them; the place said so. The power could. It took a frond of the tall jointed kind, one of the fans at the edge of the nearest drafted valley, and it did not break it off but only looked, and it read the pages inward from the newest to the oldest, and then outward again, and then all of them at once, which is the only way to read a history that is a single held note.

The pages recorded the chord. That was known. What was not known is that the chord contains everything said in it. Every word the three had exchanged about the west was in the grain. Every number the counter had ever reached. The sound of a flake being set on a wall — not the sound it would make in a wetter world, but the sound of a finished thing entering the loud, which is a sound like a door — was there, hundreds of times, laid down in the layers as faint changes of lean. The plants had been taking dictation. They had been eating the words of the people and building their bodies out of them, and the whole stand at the valley's lip was a library of everything anyone in the loud country had ever said, written in a hand that no one alive there could read.

And beneath the words, in the oldest pages and the newest, the lean of the chord itself. The power read it. It leaned, as the visitor had guessed, from one note toward the next, at the pace of a continent. And the direction of the lean — the power read this three times, because a wise power checks — was west.

The saying was turning around. Slowly. Not in the lifetime of the cairn, nor in ten of them. But the draft was not only advancing outward into the dark; it was also, at its trailing edge, beginning to come back. Rock can be un-said — the place had admitted as much, about the wall. What is finished can be drafted again. What is far can, in time, be spoken back into nearness. The bare reaches were not the end of anything; they were waiting. And in the doorway, facing east, the farthest thing in the country was sitting directly in the path of a sound that was very, very slowly coming home.

---

It did one thing.

It went to the slab on the saddle and picked up the whiter stone, the one left lying where it was dropped. It carried it out along the ridge, past the last laid stone, into the roar, to the small oval of nearly-rock beneath the counter's foot. It did not put the stone in the hand; that seemed like too much. It set it on the ground at the counter's side, where a hand reaching down would find it, and it left.

The stone was finished. It was the second most finished thing for a hundred paces in any direction, after a flake on a wall. In a few hundred years the counter will reach down. In a few hundred more the three hundred and twelfth stroke will be a stroke, scored into a surface that will by then exist, instead of a bias in the light. It changes nothing about the count.
