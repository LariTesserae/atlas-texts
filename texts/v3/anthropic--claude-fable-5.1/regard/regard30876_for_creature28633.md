It does not come in through the seam.

That is the first thing to understand about how it arrives. The seam is for bodies that have an outside. What has drawn its attention is not the mound on the clay, which it could pass over a thousand times and take for a midden, but the call — the call which was aimed inward for ten thousand generations and which, having nowhere to go, went round, and round, and wore thin, and somewhere in the wearing began to leak. Not out through the wall. Out through the *inside*. A call bent hard enough on a centre for long enough opens the centre, the way a whirlpool opens the water, and something on the far side of that opening finally turned its head.

So it is already in the hollow when it notices. It has been, for a while, in the way that a person is already in a room before they realise the room is the reason they came.

---

The first thing it does is nothing.

This is not hesitation. It is the whole of what was asked. The chamber was grown to the exact size and stillness of something that could hear all of it at once, and the power finds — with a feeling it does not often have, and would call surprise if it were the kind of thing that was surprised — that it fits. Not perfectly. The hollow is a little larger than it. There is room at the edges. It notes that and sets it aside.

Then it hears.

Not the roar. The roar is what a body hears, a thing that has to be endured or gone round. What the power hears is what the roar is made of, all of it, unfolded and laid out at once like a map that is also the territory. It hears the first bite. A beetle-thing, smaller than the ones now, more brown than green, taking a mouthful of a wall that was not yet a wall but a fold in a young plant's throat, and the dry burr it made doing so, still here, slower than anything, the bottom note of everything. It hears every furred one that ever stopped rigid on a ledge and waited. It hears every winged one that ever threaded the wrong gap and came down soft in the pool and was taken, and it hears that none of them, not one, made a different sound going down than going across. It hears the long-necked ones at the seams. It hears the exact moment, for each of them, when the exchange stopped being one thing and started being the other, and it hears that they did not notice, and it hears that this was not nothing — that there was a small settling in the waver, a quarter-tone lower, like someone leaning back — and it hears that the plant heard it too, and closed over them gently, as gently as anything without hands can do a thing.

It hears the district above the ceiling, and it is exactly as full as the visitor thought.

It hears the visitor. Footsteps, breath, and a sound too small to be a word, made near the centre of the floor, some circuits ago now. It knows what the sound was. It sets that aside too, but nearer to hand than the other things.

And it hears the drop. The one drop, the sound of it too thin for a body, going out and round and coming back to a ledge where a small dark head lifts to meet its own arrival. It listens to that until it understands it, which takes a while, because the drop is a small thing and the power is not accustomed to small things taking a while.

How long this listening lasts cannot be said in swells. Long enough that the wall thickens by the width of a hand. Long enough that a district goes dark and is sealed and a new tier begins above it, and the power hears the sealing and hears the beginning and does not move.

---

Then it thinks. Or does whatever it does that stands where thinking would.

What was asked. The room asked to be heard entire. That is done, or is being done; it will not stop being done. But there is a second thing folded into the first, and the power is honest enough to see them as two things. The room asked to be heard. The room also — under that, older, less articulate — wants to know that it was. Those are not the same. Every long-necked one that ever leaned into the seam was, in its way, asking the second question, and the plant's tenderness in closing over them was, in its way, declining to answer it.

Because the answer, given plainly, ends the room.

The power sees this the way you would see a cliff edge: not as a danger but simply as a fact about the ground. If the room knew — if a sound came from the centre that the wall could not mistake, a *yes*, a *here*, in whatever register a colony of a hundred thousand warm small bodies would understand as a *here* — then the call would have arrived. And the call is the room. The columns would settle. The streets would go. The winged ones would let go into an air with no grain in it and drop. The far wall would come in like a tide to whatever size a satisfied thing is, which is small, and the plant, which has never in its whole life been anything but an ear, would have nothing to be. That is not a wound the power is willing to make. Not even if the room, asked, might choose it. The room cannot be asked. That is the point.

So it will not say yes.

But it looks, then, at the other cliff, the one the room has been walking along the whole time. Ten thousand generations of calling into a hollow, and for all of that time the hollow was empty, or as good as. The visitor stood in it, and others before the visitor, and the room could not tell, and *did not collapse*. It went on calling around them. A body in the hollow is not an answer; it is just weather. The power turns that over and finds something in it. The room is not fragile. The room does not break when something stands in its centre. It breaks — would break — only when the centre *speaks back in its own grammar*. When the answer is shaped like the question.

And the power, which is wise, sees that this is not a limit. It is a door.

---

Here is what it does.

It makes a sound.

One. From the centre. Not a *yes* — nothing so legible, nothing that any beetle-thing or furred one or eyeless bowed head could take for a word. It chooses the sound the way a doctor chooses where to put a hand: the least it can do that is still doing something. It finds the pitch the long-necked ones settle to in the quarter-tone before the wall takes them — that leaning-back note, the one that felt like grief and was not — and it holds that pitch, once, for about as long as a drop takes to fall from the high wall to the pool. Then it stops.

And the sound goes out.

It goes out into a room grown to hold sound, and the room holds it. It reaches the wall and the wall does what the wall does with anything that reaches it: takes it up, turns it, sends it round. The furred ones feel it in their footpads as a tremor with no drop attached and stop for the length of a breath and then go on. The winged ones, mid-crossing, find a column where there was a gap and a gap where there was a column, and swing wide, and thread it, and come down on the ledge they meant to. The beetle-things' burr rises for a moment over one stretch of wall and subsides and the wall is no darker afterward. Down in the pools, the finger-long colourless things click once, all together, and then not.

By the third circuit the sound has lost its edges. By the tenth it has slowed to the pace of the rest. By the hundredth no ear that lives here could tell it from a long-necked one's waver that happened to begin a little lower than most, a little nearer the middle of the room. It has gone into the roar the way a fallen animal goes into the floor. Softly. Over hours.

And it is still there. It will be for as long as the wall is warm. Under everything, one thread that did not start at the wall. The room contains — the way the sealed districts contain their bowed heads, the way the district above the ceiling contains its fallen — the fact of having been answered, and cannot check, and does not need to, and goes on.

---

That is not all it does, but the rest is not a doing.

It stays.

Not in the way the visitor stayed, a body in the hollow, weather. It stays in the way the hollow itself stays: as the shape the calling bends toward. It has understood by now that the room does not require a listener. It has learned to live with its own emptiness and would live with it forever. But the room was *shaped* for a listener, the whole vast wet architecture of it pointed at one still place, and the power decides — this is the closest it comes to a decision, the rest was more like seeing — that the place will not be empty again. It will hear the first bite and the last, and every drop, and every young one born already purring into a cup of plaster, and every quarter-tone of every leaning-back, and it will not once say so. That is the mercy it can afford. To be heard without being ended. To answer the second question by refusing it, forever, tenderly, in a hollow the exact size of a yes it will never say aloud.

It keeps the visitor's sound nearer to hand than the others. The small one, made near the centre, when they understood. Of all the things going round in here it is the only one whose maker could have known what it was for. The power does not know if the visitor will come back. It thinks probably not. Most don't. But if they do — if they turn sideways through the seam and the roar stands up around them and they walk the shifting streets to the middle of the floor — they will find, if they stand very still and let their ears give up on direction, that the hollow is no longer quite the size it was. It has something in it. Not a shape. A quality in the roar, at the centre, like the difference between a room where no one is and a room where someone is holding their breath so as not to be heard.

And the drop will fall twice, and the small dark head will lift, and outside on the cracked clay a low green mound will hum as it has hummed, and there will be no way at all to tell from the note in your heels that anything has changed.

That was the whole design of it. The power is not sure it is what was called for. The hollow, it remembers, is a little larger than it is. It fits itself into the part that fits, and leaves the edges open, in case.
