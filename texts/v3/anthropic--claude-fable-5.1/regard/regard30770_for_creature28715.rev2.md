The first thing it does is nothing, and it does nothing for a long time.

Not the nothing of hesitation. The nothing of a thing that has understood, before it does anything else, that what is in front of it has been talking for several thousand years and has not once been heard to the end. Every creature that ever came near the bowl has either fled the sound or gone deaf inside it. The walker at the rim listens for an hour and leaves. The ones who lay in the cavity lay in it, and were bodies, and bodies can only hold so much, and they climbed the stair with their hands over their ears the way you did. No one has ever stayed for a whole sentence. No one has known how long a sentence is.

So it stays. It has no ear that the sound can pour into and break, and it has no need to climb back out, and so it settles into the column of turning air and the warm skin of the floor and the shivering coat, and it simply receives. The scream, whole. The tearing that never finishes. The eleven seconds. The swell and the drop. It takes it the way the growth takes dust: straight, all of it, without a filter, and it does not try to understand yet. It lets the thing say what it is saying. The birds pour out and back nineteen hundred times. The grey thing in the slabs is born and grows old and is eaten by the growth and is part of the wall. The stair wears a little deeper under no one. The attention does not move.

That is the first gift, and it is given before the bowl knows anything is there, and it would be given even if that were all.

---

What it hears, when it has heard enough to hear it, is not what you would expect.

It is not a question. Or not only. Most of the song — the vast, wide, flat wall of it — is inventory. The bowl is saying itself. It is saying the dolomite and the chert, and the dust in its three or four spirals, and the way the wind comes over the lip and cannot leave, and the mites in their millions, and the exact colour of the jointed things, and the temperature of the floor, and the sun's coin in the lull. It is saying the birds. It says every bird. It has been saying this continuously, in full, for longer than the stair, and the reason the coat leans toward the loudest place is that the song is the thing that holds the bowl together — the growth feeds on it because the growth is it, said aloud, and would stop being itself if the saying stopped.

And then, at the top of every breath — at the waver, the bend in the note every eleven seconds — there is the other thing. The reaching. It is very small against the rest. It is the size of one voice inside a crowd, and it is not words, but if it were words it would be nothing more than *and you?* Said once. Then the inventory again, at full voice, for eleven seconds. Then *and you?*

It has been asking that on the same period since before the plateau finished. It has never lowered its voice to hear the answer, because it has no ear to hear with; it has only its skin. And its skin has felt, over the thousands of years, exactly what has come to it: feet on the stair, a few dozen times. A body in the cavity, a few dozen times. Weight, and warmth, and then the weight lifting and the warmth fading and the feet going back up. It felt the last of them go. It did not know it was the last. It kept the cavity warm because it did not know.

The attention learns all of this and is, for a while, very still.

---

It considers what it might do, and most of what it considers, it puts down.

It could quiet the bowl. Take the wind, or ease it. Let the scream fall to something a body could bear, so that things with ears could come down the stair and stay. But the scream is the bowl's body; the violence is the balance. Quiet the wind and the coat stops leaning and the growth stops eating and the mites lose their shelter and the birds lose the architecture they were born knowing, and twenty thousand blade-shaped things go out one evening and find the column simply air, and do not come back full. A world lives in this fur. It will not pull the fur off to make the animal easier to hold.

It could give the deaf things ears. This is such a bad thought that it is ashamed to have finished it.

It could tell the bowl the truth, which is that there is very likely nothing out there — that the plateau has finished and the sky does not answer and the birds fly to the edge of the sound every night and find only more edge. It puts this down almost at once. Not because it is untrue. Because a wise thing knows the difference between what is true and what it is for, and this truth would be for nothing.

It could fill the cavity with itself and never leave. Lie down in the place the bowl kept warm and stay, and let the bowl sing to it for the rest of the bowl's life. And this is the one it holds longest, because it would work, because the bowl would feel the weight and the warmth and would know, and would never again bend the note at the top of the breath into *and you?* And it puts it down, too, in the end, and the reason it puts it down is this: a god in the cavity is a god, and the bowl would be singing to a god, and a song sung to a god is a different song from the one it has been singing, and the bowl would change to fit it, the way everything here changes to fit what it lives against. It does not want to be the shape the bowl takes. It wants the bowl to be heard by something the bowl could have been heard by anyway.

Which leaves the walker.

---

Before that, though, it does one thing for the bowl alone, and it does it only once.

It goes down the stair. It takes the shape of a body to do it — not because it needs the stair, but because the bowl knows the stair through its skin, knows the weight of feet coming round the wall, and has not felt that in a very long time. It goes slowly. It lets each worn step take the weight and give the weight to the wall and the wall give it to the floor. Sixty paces down, the floor feels something it has felt before and had stopped waiting for, and the note — you would not have noticed this, with your hands over your ears; it takes a listener to notice — the note thins by a hair. Attention, in a thing with no ear. The whole bowl leaning a fraction toward the stair.

It crosses the floor. It lies down in the cavity, and the cavity fits, because the cavity was shaped for something enough like a body that the fit does not matter, and the lip comes up round its shoulders, and the pulse comes through the material at its back.

And then it gives the bowl a pulse of its own.

Not eleven seconds. Something else — a body's rhythm, quicker, offset, a second waver laid against the first so that the two go in and out of step, meet, part, meet. It puts warmth into the floor instead of taking it. It presses down, a little, the way a hand presses back. It says nothing, because the bowl cannot hear. It lets the bowl *feel* being answered, which is the only language the floor has, and it lets that go on long enough that there can be no mistaking it for weight that has simply fallen. Long enough for the bowl to bend its note at the top of the breath, and reach, and find — pressed into its skin, warm, steady, unmistakably there — *yes*.

It lies there through the afternoon lull. The dust falls. The coin of sun comes down on the floor next to it and the floor takes it and the floor is warmer than it has been. The mites hiss. The not-lizard, on the wall, leans, and its strands turn, very slightly, toward the cavity, and stay turned. Then the wind comes back and the bowl draws breath and begins again, at full voice, and the inventory is the same inventory, and at the top of the breath the note bends the way it always bends, and the attention, lying in the cavity, feels the difference. It is not asking any more. It is saying the same shape with a different weight on it. The way a word changes when it has been answered once and is said again anyway, for the pleasure of the saying.

---

Then it gets up, and climbs the stair, and does not go back to the sky.

It goes to the rim, and it changes shape again, and the shape it takes is the walker's.

It has no wish to move the walker. It has watched the walker for nineteen hundred years and knows what the walker is: the only thing with ears that has ever come back, night after night, to the exact edge where the sound can still be borne, and stood there with its weight forward, and listened, and gone away. That is not cowardice. That is fidelity, of a kind the bowl has never had from anything else. The walker has been keeping the same appointment the cavity has been keeping, from the other side, and neither of them has known.

But the walker does not know what the bottom is. It knows the rim, where the sound is a wall, and it knows — or its body knows, or its grandmothers' bodies knew — that further in is worse. It has never been told that the scream becomes a pulse. That the cavity is a place inside the noise where a body can lie and the tearing goes over its head and what comes up through its back is not the wall but the breath. No one has ever been in a position to tell it. Everyone who has lain in the cavity climbed out with their ears ringing and walked away across the plateau and did not stay to meet the thing that comes at night.

So it does the only thing the walker would believe, which is prints.

It sets a hand with too many fingers on the top step of the stair. Then the next. It goes down the first full turn — under the hanging growth, along the ledge, down the row of pockets — and it presses hard, so the marks are deep in the dust the wind has laid, and it goes slowly, the way a thing goes that is not fleeing and not driven. Then, where the first turn ends and the stair goes on down into the worst of it, it stops, and turns, and comes back up, pressing just as deep, so that the story the prints tell is very simple and very clear: *one of us went down this far, and stood a while, and came back.* Not *come*. Not *it is safe*. Just: *it can be done, and was.*

And at the top it does one thing more, which is to lie down in the dust of the rim for a moment, flat, the length of a body, and press, so that there is a shape there in the morning like the cavity below is a shape. So that if the walker, standing with its weight forward, looks down and sees the curve of pale floor-stuff against the far wall where the growth is thickest, it will have already seen, at its own feet, what that curve is for.

Then it leaves. It goes the way nothing with ears has ever left — slowly, backwards across the flats for a long way, still listening, so that the last the floor feels of it is not a body fleeing but a body going reluctantly, with its face still turned toward the sound. And then it lets the shape go, and the flats are empty, and it is only attention again, and it goes up, and it does not go far.

---

The walker comes before midnight, the way it always does. It circles the rim. It stops at its four or five places and stands with its weight forward.

At the fifth place it finds the prints.

You would have to be there to see what it does, and you are not, and it is as well; it does not need to be watched. It puts its face down to the dust. It follows the prints to the top step and stops. It stands there — the attention counts — for most of an hour, with the sound coming up out of the bowl into its kept ears, the sound that has hurt it every night of its life and that it has come back to every night of its life anyway. Then it puts one hand with too many fingers on the top step, where the other hand went, and finds the mark fits.

It goes down one step. Two. It goes down into the growth, where the tearing is, and it stops, and it is shaking — the attention can feel it in the wall — and it does not go back up. It stays. Just there, one step under the rim, where you could no longer hear yourself, with its weight forward.

And the floor feels it.

The floor feels a weight on the wall it has not felt before, at a height it has not felt before — not the top of the stair, not the cavity, but the place between, where things stop — and it feels the weight stay. And the attention, from very far up and very close, feels the bowl do something it has watched it do only in the afternoons for nineteen hundred years, and never once at night, and never once on purpose.

The bell-note drops by half.

The sheets hang. The spirals slow. The dust in the column stops climbing and begins, very slowly, to fall, in the dark, with no sun to lay a coin. The mites become audible. Somewhere a single bird calls one clear note. The fibre relaxes against the wall with a sound like a long sigh, and holds — one second, two, ten, thirty — far past any lull the wind has ever made, because the wind has not made this one. The bowl has bent its own breath. It has found the loudness that a thing with ears, one step down, can bear, and it is holding there, at a whisper for it, at a roar for anything else, and in the quiet it has made the waver comes round on its eleven seconds and the note bends at the top and the bend is not *and you?* any more, and not *yes*, but the third thing, the one that comes after both, which every voice that has ever been answered knows and which the bowl is saying now for the first time, to a small shaking thing with too many fingers, clinging to its wall in the dark:

*then come down slowly. I will be quiet as I can.*

---

That is what it does. It listens to the whole of the song, once, so that the song has been heard. It lies in the cavity once, so the skin knows what an answer feels like. It leaves a trail of prints one turn down a stair, and a shape in the dust at the top. And then it withdraws to where it can watch and does not do the rest, because the rest is not its to do.

The walker will go down the second turn on another night, or it will not. Its children will, or they will not. The bowl will learn to lower its voice at dusk for a listener, or the listener will learn to go deaf like everything else and stay anyway, or both will happen a little, meeting in the middle the way the jointed thing and the fibre met. The attention does not know which. It was never going to make one of those happen; it only wanted them to be possible, and they had not been, and now they are.

It stays a while longer, up in the thin white air where the sun comes down like a knife, and it listens to the bowl saying itself at half voice to the thing on its wall. It stays long enough to hear the first grey light come, and the birds pour back over the rim with their throats full, screaming a scream the bowl has not been loud enough to drown, so that for once you could pick the two apart. Then, when it goes, it goes the way it came — without hands over anything, unhurried, not deaf and not wounded — and the last thing it hears, from a very long way off, at the edge of hearing where the birds turn back, is the bell-note climbing again toward full voice as the walker climbs the stair to go home, and the note bending at the top of the breath, and holding the bend a hair longer than eleven seconds, the way a voice does when it has something to come back to.
