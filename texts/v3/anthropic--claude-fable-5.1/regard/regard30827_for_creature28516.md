It notices the way you notice a held breath in a room you have walked into — not by any sound, but by the absence of the sound that should be there. From above, the field is fourteen steps of grass with nothing under it. But everything under it is leaning, and a wise power feels a lean the way a hand feels a slope in the dark. It puts its attention down through the turf, down the white roots, and finds the water, and the green that should not be green, and the snail, and the one who is standing.

And then it does the wise thing first, which is to wait, and let the easy answers come and go.

The first easy answer is mercy, and it arrives dressed as the snail. The distance is nothing — the width of a thumb, less. The power could close it. It could take the snail the last of the way along the groove, or take the heel down itself, and the step would finish, and whatever stands on the stone would go through the door. That is what mercy would look like if you drew it. It would look like ending a long stillness.

But the power stays with it long enough to feel what it would cost, and who would pay. The plants are green on the breath of a thing that has not finished leaving. The eel's channel bends around a shape. The edges of the fracture are sharp because the room is still, technically, the moment it opened. Finish the step, and the breath stops, and the pale drift stops falling from the ceiling, and the weed has nothing to grow toward and grows nothing, and the room becomes a pond, and the pond softens, and in a few hundred years there is a dip in a field with reeds in it and a nail somewhere in the mud. None of that is wrong. Ponds are allowed to be ponds. But the snail is coming. The snail is certain. And the power sees that to hurry a thing that is certain is not mercy; it is impatience that has found mercy's coat on a hook and put it on. The step will finish when the snail arrives, and the snail will arrive, and the *when* belongs to the snail.

The second easy answer is the opposite, and it lasts less long. Keep it. Stop the snail. Preserve the green and the cold and the fold, the drop that lands before it falls — it is rare, it is beautiful, it is the only one. The power considers this for the time it takes to be ashamed of it. The room is not a museum. The one on the stone is not an exhibit. It is in the middle of leaving, and the kindest thing anyone can do for someone who is leaving is not to hold the door shut.

The third answer is the one that sounds wisest, and so needs the most care: ask it what it wants. But the room has already said. It does not want anything. It is not waiting for you. And a power that is actually wise knows that the thing you do to someone who wants nothing is not to find something to give them. That is what the anxious do.

So the power stops answering and starts looking, and it looks at what is missing rather than at what is there.

A doorway is a threshold and two posts and a lintel and, on one side of it, a house. Here there is a threshold. Here there is one nail, standing point-up in the silt, the last nail of one post, holding from the wrong side. Everything else is gone: the other post, the lintel, the walls, the roof, and the room the walls were around. The one on the stone is leaving a house, and the house is not there to be left. It has its face to the door and its back to nothing. There is no one in the room behind it. There is no room behind it.

You cannot leave a place that is not there. You can only stand in the doorway of it forever, mid-step, with the weight gone forward and nowhere for it to have come from.

That is what the power decides to be. Not the mercy. Not the keeping. The house.

It goes in. It presses into the plants and finds the water between them, cold and close, iron and green. It is heard before it arrives, because everything here is, and so the room has already made room, and the power takes up none of it. It goes down to the stone. It does not touch the snail. It does not touch the one who is standing. It goes past — the width of the block, no more — to the place a few paces off where the other post would have stood, on the side of the groove opposite the nail, and it stands there, in the silt, and does not move.

Then it turns around.

This is the whole of the act, and it is small enough to miss. The one on the stone is facing the way the feet went, toward the door, toward the outside. The power faces the other way. It turns its back on the door and faces into the room — into the weed and the moss and the dark, into the place where the house would have been — so that now, behind the one who is leaving, there is someone who is staying. Someone in the room. Someone to be left.

Nothing changes that could be measured. The snail does not speed up. The heel does not come down. But the plants feel it, the way they feel everything, and the lean shifts by a hair. Not toward the power — it is not that kind of presence and would not want to be — but the middle, the not-fixed middle where the up-growing and the down-growing pass through each other, slides a little way along the stone until it sits exactly between the one standing at the end of the groove and the one standing in the silt beyond it. Between two posts. The middle of the room is the doorway now. That is what a doorway is: the place between the one who is going and the one who remains.

The power does not speak. If there were a word for this — *go*, or *it's all right*, or a name — the one on the stone heard it already, before the ground opened, in the dry house, and that is why the step began. Sound comes first here. The power understands that it has already said whatever it was going to say, and that it has arrived, now, only to stand where the saying came from.

It will stay. That is the rest of the act and it is mostly duration. It will stand in the silt and face the room for as long as the snail takes, which is a very long time, and also, given how the room handles time, already over. It will feel the year drawn in and the year let out. It will taste the dust at the bottom of the breath and know it as the house's dust, the house it is standing in for. It will feel fourteen footsteps cross the field overhead and hear each one before it lands, and it will not look up, because it is facing the room, and looking after someone who is leaving is not the same as being what they leave.

And when the snail reaches the foot — when the heel comes down, and what is on the threshold goes through the door — the power will not see where it goes. Its back will be to the door. That is the cost, and the power pays it on purpose. It will feel the breath stop and the pale drift not fall. It will feel the weed lose its lean and become weed. It will feel, in the far walls, the first edge of the fracture begin to soften, as the room stops being the moment it opened and becomes the years afterward.

Then it will be a shape in the silt on one side of a stone, opposite a nail, in a pond in a field. And it will stand there a while longer, because a house should not empty all at once. And then, having been left, it will go too, by whatever way benevolent things go, and leave the room to the eel, who has been going around two shapes and will now go around none, and will not remark on the difference.

That is what it does. It does not finish the step, and it does not stop it. It does not soften the edges. It does not lift the snail. It goes down into a full dark room and closes the door behind it and stands still, facing inward, and makes itself the thing that gets left behind, so that the one who has been leaving for so long finally has somewhere to be leaving from.
