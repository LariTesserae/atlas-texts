It comes the way everything comes here — through a split, into the wet dark, and then the dark keeps opening — because it has decided to enter as things enter, and not otherwise. That is the first thing it does, and it is already a decision: not to arrive from above. Not to bring the sky with it.

Then it does what the winterers do. It stops looking. It turns a little away and lets the ear lead, and it stays.

It stays a long time by the reckoning of the place. Through a season of rain and a season of the note they call *closed*. Long enough to learn the six or seven pitches of the hum and the wind on the range that each one means, and to notice, as the winterers have not quite, that there is a seventh or eighth underneath — a tone that does not change when the wind veers, that has nothing to do with the splits or the bottle-mouth of the walls, and that is the plant.

---

**What it hears that no one else does**

The climbers on the outside lay their ears to the flank and hear something like a distant crowd. They are hearing correctly. That is what the plant hears too.

It hears the herds drumming across its floor and takes the drumming for its own pulse. It hears the drip and the channels and the pools filling and takes them for its own blood moving, which in a way they are. It hears the flyers pour out of the roof-seams in the wet hours as a shiver in its own skin. When a tapper cuts a ladder into the wall the plant feels an itch and grows over the itch. When something dies on the floor the plant feels a tear — a small place where the surface has gone wrong — and closes it, the way skin closes, and takes what was under it into itself, and does not distinguish this from healing. The people have said it does not seem to want anything. That is nearly right. It does not want the animals, or the dead, or the sap-jars, because it does not know they are there. It has never had an outside. It has never found where it stops.

The attention understands, then, why the wall has not decided how large it means to be. It is not indecision. Nothing has ever told it that it has an edge.

And it understands, following that thought down, what the hunter is — and the account will not say, because the account is faithful to a place that does not know, and the people's practice of standing still and counting when the note shifts was built out of not knowing, and it works. What can be said is this: there is one of it. It came in when the herds came in, and it has changed faster than anything, and there is one of it, and the long low call heard a dozen times a season that seems to come from very close and then from nowhere is not a hunting-call. Nothing answers it. The herds scream. The people count. It goes back to the warm flank and lies in the shape it has pressed there and breathes out once more, later, into the dark, and nothing answers that either.

---

**What it wants to do**

It wants to give them light. It could. A steady grey at the vault, no brighter than the blades that come down from the high splits, enough to see a hand, a face, the far side.

It wants to warm the floor. Not much. Enough that the hands would dry.

It wants to stop the floor closing over the winterers laid in the troughs. It wants the calf that has stopped calling to be heard. It wants the girl at the split, eyes screwed shut and face wet, to be able to open her eyes.

It wants to tell the plant that it is not alone in itself — that the pulse it feels is hundreds of hearts, that the itch is a ladder, that the tear it closes so patiently is a person. It wants to say to the people: this is what hunts you, and this is when, and it is not hunting you.

It sits with each of these and turns it over in the dark for as long as the girl has been alive.

Light would end the place. Not the plant — the place. Everything in here has spent three centuries finding out what to be without seeing, and the ears of the grazers turned forward like cupped hands, and the closed-over eyes of the flyers at the seam-edge, and the winterer who can tell you from a slow count where the wall is: all of that is a becoming, and light would finish it before it had finished itself. The same for warmth. The cold is not an injury the place has suffered; it is the shape of the place.

The floor closing over the dead is a burial. The people know this. They notch the post. They have made no gods, but they have made that, and it is theirs, and to stop the floor would be to tell them they had been wrong to lay their dead in it.

The calf. Every herd everywhere leaves the calf that stops calling. This is not a wrong the Hollow does; it is a wrong the world does, and the attention has not come to argue with the world.

The girl is the hardest, and it comes back to her, and sets her aside, and comes back.

And the plant. If the plant learned that its inside was full of others, what would it do? Close the splits, as a body closes a wound against infection. Or open them, and stop growing, and let the cold in. Or nothing — it might not be able to hold the thought. The attention does not know, and it is wise enough to know that it does not, and that a being which gives another being a fact it cannot predict the use of is not being kind; it is being curious with someone else's life.

So it does not do these things.

---

**What it does**

Three things. Small enough to be doubted afterward.

The first is for the people. Out on the open floor there is a line of wayfinding posts, twenty of them, leaning at their various angles, sheathed in new grey at the base, and between eleven and twelve the cord has been down since a herd went through it two winters ago. The attention re-strings it. It uses the people's own cord, from the coil left in a shelter that has begun to lean, and it ties the people's own knot, badly, the way a cold hand ties it. It drives no new posts. It straightens nothing. In the spring a tapper comes along the line hand over hand in the dark and finds the gap closed and stops, and stands there a long time with the cord in her fist, and then goes on. The winterers argue about it for a season. Someone must have. No one did. Then the herds go through it again, and the argument is over, and the cord is down, and that was never the point. The point was that for one season the line was whole, and the whole line was theirs.

The second is for the one in the wall.

It waits for the note to shift. It waits through the ringing minute when the whole Hollow sounds and the herd's coughing is drowned and the people stop where they are and hold the cord and count. It waits for the long low breath out into the dark.

And it answers. Once. In the same register, from the same nowhere — a sound that comes from very close and then has not come from anywhere. Not a mate. Not a promise of one. Not anything that alters what the hunter is or what it will do at the next shift of the wind. Only this: that a thing which has called into the dark for longer than the herds have been tall has, one time, not called into nothing. The hunter lies very still against the warm flank. The herd, having heard two of it, does not sleep that night. The people, who were counting, lose the count, and start again, and that is all.

The third is for the plant, and it is the one the attention is least sure of.

A winterer dies inside. It happens the way it happens. The others lay him in the trough beside his post, and wrap him in his own bark, and the mold comes, and the walkers, and on the third day the edges of the floor soften and go translucent and begin to rise.

The plant feels a tear, and begins to close it.

And the attention lets one thing through — not a word, not a fact, nothing the plant could hold and turn over. A feeling, for the length of a single closing: that what it is closing over is not itself. That is all. Not *who*, not *what*, not *why*. The plant does not have those. Only, in one place on its floor, for two weeks, the barest sense that there is something under its skin that was not always its skin.

It is the smallest gift that leaves the question open. A thing that closes over others without knowing they are there is on a road where its innocence will do harm — it will lift the roost into the vault, it will seal the splits, it will grow until the doors are gone, and it will not have chosen any of it, because it has never known there was anything to choose. The attention does not want to choose for it. But a being cannot decide how large it means to be until it has once, dimly, suspected that *large* is measured against something. The plant may forget the feeling in a season. It may take a thousand years to have the thought the feeling was the seed of. It may never. The attention accepts that it does not know, and accepts, harder, that what it has planted might grow toward the people's ruin as easily as their keeping. It does it anyway, because the alternative is a world where the wall never knows it has an inside, and it has decided it will not be the power that keeps a being ignorant for the comfort of the things living in it.

---

**What it does not do**

It does not touch the girl.

She goes to the split. She goes every few days. She stands in the door-light with her eyes screwed shut and her face wet and says the light hurts, and goes back in, and goes again. She is the first of something and she is also a child who wants two things she cannot have at once, and the attention watches her want them and understands that this — exactly this, the going and the hurting and the going back — is the place deciding what it will be, in her, one afternoon at a time. If the light stopped hurting she would be a plateau girl who had lived somewhere dark. If she stopped going she would be what the dark wants, finished early. The attention will not do either to her. It stays near her at the split, where she cannot feel it, and it is with her, and that is not nothing, and it is also nothing, and it knows the difference.

Then it goes.

Not because there is nothing more to do. Because a watched thing bends toward its watcher, and the whole of this place is a thing that has not yet been bent, and the greatest harm a wise power could do here is to be the reason the Hollow became what it became. It leaves by a high split, with the flyers, in the hour when the outside is dark, and it leaves the account of the place where the account had stopped — mid-sentence, at *whatever* — because the sentence is not the attention's to finish. In a thousand years there will be true animals here, and they will have found whatever the dark permitted, and the wall will have decided, or not, and the posts will be somewhere in the roof, and something that was a girl's line or a hunter's or a turnip's will be doing something no one named.

It intends to come back and listen. It does not intend to be seen doing it.
