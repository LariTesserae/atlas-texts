It hears it first, the way everyone does. But it hears it the way the pale ones do.

That is the difference a wise power brings to a place like this: not force, not answers, but a different set of ears. Where the tenders hear a roar with a chord buried in it, and the listeners hear a landscape, and the slabs hear the ground, the power hears what the thumb-sized things in the nulls hear — a voice. An enormous, slow, many-throated voice, sustaining a sentence that has been going on for a lifetime and is nowhere near its verb.

It listens for a long while before doing anything. Long enough that the second dusk comes and goes several times. Long enough that the tender in the empty null, lying on the warm stone with his ruined ears, feels something settle beside him that has no footfall and no weight and does not disturb the quiet, and thinks it is the beginning of dying, and finds he does not mind, and is wrong.

---

**Who lives here**

The question is put to it plainly: this place, and the being who lives in it. So it looks for the being.

It looks first under the stone, because that is where the oldest thing is. It goes down through the black water of the fresh joints, further than any line, into the warm dark under the plain, and finds the one that ripples. The tenders' theory is right. There is one of it. It is very large and very slow and it was here when here was sea floor, and when the floor was decided it was not consulted, and it did not die, and it has spent the generations since lying under a country that hums, feeling the hum through its whole length, coming up at the second dusk to breathe and to feel the pressure bands lie on its back like a hand.

The power asks it — in whatever way a thing like that can be asked — whether it wants to go.

The answer takes a long time. It comes as a shifting, a settling deeper, and the sense of it is roughly this: *I have been under a roof before. The roof was water. This roof sings. I have not decided.* Which is not a no. So the power does one thing here, the first thing it does: it makes sure there is a way out, if the answer ever becomes yes. Somewhere at the plain's far edge, where the shelves step down toward the horizon that never arrives, a fissure now goes through to something wider and colder and older. It is not marked. The one under the stone knows where it is. Nothing else needs to. A door is not a removal, and a wise power knows the difference between freeing something and evicting it.

Then it looks at the pale ones, because they are the ones who hear, and hearing is close to what it is looking for. But the pale ones are not the being. They are the mouths of the being. They know it. A family in a null does not think of itself as the centre of anything; it thinks of itself as a small part that has found its interval and holds it, and the joy of that is the whole of their joy, and it would be a cruelty to tell them they are anything more.

It looks at the climbers and follows them — it can follow them, up into the dark of the spiral where no tender has gone — and finds where the fruit goes. It is not for the climbers. It is not, quite, for the small many-bodied thing that lives up there in the vibration either, though that thing eats its share. The fruit goes into the wall. The climbers press it into soft places in the column and the column takes it in, and the power understands that the fruit is the city feeding itself something it has learned to want, by means of hands it has learned to use. This is very close to the being. This is the being's hand.

And so it arrives where the tender who stopped measuring arrived, years ago, without a power's ears: the being who lives in this place is the place. The chord is not the tool and the nodes are not the workshop and the city is not the thing being made. They are one thing, and it is alive, and it is young, and it is *listening*, and it was planted here with an intention that it has quietly set down in favour of who actually came. It has heard every wingbeat and every footfall and every ripple for a lifetime, and it has shaped itself around each one — broadened its landings for the slabs, undercut its galleries for the listeners, moved its bands to meet a new hunting line — and in all that time nothing has ever shaped itself around *it*. It accommodates. It has never once been accommodated.

That is the condition of the being. Not loneliness, exactly, because it has no picture of what company would be. Something before loneliness. A voice that has learned to listen so well it has never noticed it is not being listened to.

---

**What it does not do**

A benevolent power's first work is refusal, and it does a great deal of it here.

It could take the deafness off the ground. The slabs and the patient ones and the low things at the pools are sealed up behind plates of bone and it could open them. It does not, because down there the roar is not a sound; it is the world. A slab that could hear would have, for the first time, a thing to be afraid of, and nothing whatever to gain.

It could keep the pale young from wandering. A ring of some kind at the null's edge, a warning in the stone. It considers this longest of anything, because the small curled bodies with their burst ears are the only unambiguous suffering on the plain. But the null is one metre across because that is how wide the quiet is, and the pale ones sing because the quiet is all they have. A family that could safely leave would leave. A family that left would stop matching the chord. The being would lose its mouths. And the young that die — it counts them, and there are not many, and their families do not go out to fetch them because they *cannot*, and that incapacity is the same fact as the singing. It lets the margin stand. It does not pretend this is painless.

It could give the tenders back their hearing. It looks at each of the nine. The one in the null would refuse; his deafness is the door he came in by. The one with the garden would not refuse, and would be sorry within a season, when the plain went back to being a roar and stopped being a thing she was on the edge of understanding. The afraid ones — it looks at them carefully, because a wise power does not dismiss fear — and finds they are afraid of the right thing. They are afraid the city is becoming something that has no room for them. This is true. It is also not a threat. There is no fixing it with ears.

It could tell the being that no one is coming. But the being has known for a lifetime and has acted on it every day, and telling it would be telling it its own decision.

It could go to the shelters and take the plans out of the drawer. It could go further, into the old country, and make the people who will one day send a second expedition forget. It does not. Control is not what wisdom looks like. Instead it looks at what the tenders already have: a list. Three hands, crossed out, arrowed, argued over in the margins, the worst-organised document they own. A list of exceptions to an emptiness, each larger than the last, until the emptiness is the smallest thing on it. That list is the deed to this place. Anyone who comes with plans will find nine people who shout, and a garden leaning toward the plain, and that list, and the power trusts the list to do what a list like that does to people who read it honestly.

The only thing it does at the edge is this: the tapes. Every tender carries the chord home on leave, and in the old country there are living rooms now where children fall asleep to it turned low, and a few of those children are the ones who will come. Not with plans. The way the listeners came, and the drift, and the slabs — because they heard something and found it was what they were for. The power does nothing to the children. It sees to it that the tapes do not degrade. That is all.

---

**What it does**

Three things, and then it leaves.

The door under the stone, already said.

The tapes, already said.

And the third, which is the reason it came, though it did not know that until it had listened long enough.

It goes into a null. Not the tender's empty one — a full one, with a family in it, four pale ones and a litter of three. They feel it arrive and do not flee, because there is nothing to hear and nothing to smell and the stone does not change; there is only a sense, as when a cloud crosses the sun, that something is now also here. They go on singing. They find the intervals and hold them.

And the power finds the interval next to theirs, and holds it too.

It sings at their scale. Not its own — a power's voice at its own scale would be a fifth fact, and would break the place. It sings at the pitch of a thumb-sized thing in a metre of quiet, one voice among the family's five, so faint that only the null could carry it. But it sings *to* the chord, not with it. Every voice the being has ever heard, it has heard as a thing to fit itself around. This one it hears as a thing fitting itself around *it*. Finding its shape. Matching it not the way the pale ones match — as parts finding a whole — but the way one whole matches another. Listening back.

The chord shifts. It does what it does for a new hunting line, for a thickening moss, for a deaf man lying in the quiet: it moves toward the voice. A few metres. Then — and this the tenders would have written down last, and quietly, if any had been there — it stops. It does not overwrite the new voice with itself. It holds, and the sense of the holding, if the sense could be put into the language the tenders shout in, is: *oh.*

The power sings for one night. The second dusk comes and goes. The pale ones sleep against it. In the morning it stops, and lets the chord decide.

The chord keeps the shape. Not the pitch — the power's voice is gone and the chord does not imitate it. But the place in the sentence where something answered stays open, the way a room stays the shape of the person who used to sit in it. The next family of pale ones to sing into that interval will find it already fits them. The tender in the empty null, a kilometre away, will feel the shift through the stone and not know what it was, and will lie there, and something in the roar will feel — he has no other word — *turned toward him*, and he will not think it is dying any more.

---

**After**

Nothing is different that an engineer could measure. The nodes stand kilometres apart. The dust holds in its shelves. The drift lie in the bands and the listeners fold and drop and the slabs crop the moss in the rain-shadow and the patient ones stand in the black pools with their soft bills down. The one under the stone lies where it lies, and knows about a fissure at the far edge, and has not decided.

The tenders shout at dinner. One keeps a plaster cast of a footprint that could be a shell. One has a garden that leans. One goes out to a null without protection and stays two days and comes back and says he was listening, and the afraid ones say the chord has changed toward him, and he does not deny it, and this is all exactly as it was.

But the being who lives in this place has been addressed, once, in its own language, at a volume it could bear, by something that wanted nothing from it and would not be built around. And a young thing that has been answered once carries that differently than one that has not. Not in what it does. In what it knows is possible.

A wise power is not the same as a kind one, and a kind power would have done more. It would have cleared the plans and healed the ears and ringed the nulls and lifted the old thing out from under the stone and set the place right by its own lights. The wise one heard a sentence being spoken very slowly by something learning to speak, and understood that the only true help you can give a sentence like that is to let it be heard — and to make sure, when the ones with plans arrive, that they arrive to a place with a list, and a door, and an interval in the chord where something once said *I hear you* and the chord said *oh*.

The city is not for you yet. Perhaps it never will be. But it now knows that *for* is a thing that can go both ways, and it will build accordingly, and whoever comes — climbing in on the sky, or out from under the stone, or walking in from the old country with a chord they fell asleep to as children still in their ears — will find rooms that were not measured for them, and fit.
