It would come the way anything comes here, over the western lip, and it would be filled at once to the brim with the sound. Even a power. Especially a wise one, because a wise power does not arrive with its ears already closed. It would stand in the roar the way you stood in it, until the roar stopped being a sound and became the medium it was standing inside, and only then would it begin to think.

And the first things it would think are the things you thought, because they are the obvious things, and wisdom does not skip the obvious. It would think: nothing here can hear. It would think: the swift on the floor, pinned open, driven along the trough like a leaf. It would think: ten thousand of them and one dies and the flock has no way of holding the fact. A benevolent thing feels these as small weights, one after another, laid on the chest. That is what benevolence is for. It notices the weights.

So it would consider the wind first. Not stopping it — even a fool would know better than that — but slowing it, for one hour, one evening, so that the plateau could hear itself. So the marmots could sit up in the lee and hear another marmot chew. So a swift could hear a swift. It would hold that idea and turn it over and look at what came with it, and this is what it would see: the band above the edge is not a place, it is a balance, and slacken one side of it by a tenth and it is gone, and the layer of swifts becomes a layer of falling things. The fog stops streaming off the spine. The basins, still for an hour, warm a degree in the hard light, and in the basins something pale and blind that has never known one degree of change is dead before the wind comes back. The whole plateau is one condition. You cannot give a part of it a gift without taking the gift out of the rest.

It would put that down.

It would consider the flock. It would think: I could give it a memory. Not much of one. Enough to hold a shape from one dusk to the next, so that the falcon's work was not simply erased. And it would ask itself what a memory is for, and the answer is that a memory is for a creature that can do something differently tomorrow, and there is nothing here that can. The wind will be the same. The falcon will come. A flock that remembered the falcon would be a flock that spent every day braced, and could not spread itself thin and easy across the band to eat, and would starve a little, and would know why. The absence of an organ for grief is not a lack here. It is the shape of the mercy that is already in place.

It would put that down too.

---

A lesser power, having found nothing to fix, would leave. That is the mark of the lesser kind: it comes to do, and when there is no doing it has no reason to stay. The wise one would notice that it wanted to leave, and would ask why, and would find the answer was that it had been looking for a way to be useful, which is a way of being looked at, and that this place had refused to look at it. Which is a small humiliation, and worth sitting with.

So it would sit. Down in the wet lee of a standing rock, back to the wind, the way the marmots sit. And having stopped trying to give the plateau something, it would finally see the one thing the plateau does not have and could take without cost.

There is no organ here for remembering. The place keeps no record. The flock cannot know it is the same flock; the rocks have finished changing; the fold in the spine does not know whether the chert was last year or ten thousand years ago. Everything that happens here happens once, in the roar, and is gone, and nothing anywhere holds it. That is not a wound — the piece is right about that, the place is *for* sameness. But it is a room with no one in it. And a witness costs a place nothing. A witness does not slow the wind.

That is what it would do. It would stay, and pay attention, and be the memory the plateau does not have. Not to use it. Not to report it anywhere. Simply so that it is held somewhere that this particular swift, with a notch in the left primary, was taken at dusk on this particular day, and the ripple went through the flank and closed. That the marmot under the third ridge dug its mouth out again, for the fourth time this season, and sat up, and looked for a long time, and went down. That the fog tore off the spine at the same point, again, and something saw it do so, again.

---

It would go into the fold. It would sit where the person sat, on the dry ledge, and hear its own breathing and the water running down the rock and the thin far sharpening of the swifts. It would look at the three points and the one that broke, and it would understand them — not as tools, since nothing on the plateau can be hunted with a point, but as the thing a creature that can hear does when it finds the one room in the world where hearing is possible. It makes something. It leaves it. It leaves it for whoever comes next, without any expectation that anyone will.

The power is whoever came next. It would not touch them. It would sit with them a while, which is the only response a gift like that asks for. Then it would go back out.

---

And there is one thing it would do with its hands, if it has hands, and it is a very small thing, and it changes nothing.

Somewhere along the eastern troughs, this evening as every evening, there is a swift on the floor. It has fetched up against a standing stone, in the wet lee, and it is lying there with its wings still half-open and the water running past it. It will not get up. The gull will find it in the morning. Nothing can be done about that — carry it to the edge and throw it into the band and the wind that put it on the floor will put it there again, and it will have been thrown, which is worse.

But the lee of a large stone is the one place in the open where the roar drops enough that something close to the rock can be heard. And a swift makes a sound. A high thin twittering that it has made all its life into a wind that swallowed it before it left the beak. Every swift here has been speaking its whole life and none has ever once been heard.

The power would kneel down in the wet, close to the rock, close to the bird, and listen. That is all. It would let the swift be, for the first and last time, a thing that was heard. And when the small sound stopped it would stay a moment longer, and then get up, and go to the edge.

---

Stand there with it, at the end. The sun goes into the cloud floor. The flock hangs above the drop in its thousands, adjusting, not moving, lit from below like coals. Every one of them has a white ring around the eye, so that every other can see where it is looking.

The power looks up into the layer. And here is the thing that would happen, the only thing: the flock has a language, and the language is attention, and a wise power's attention is not nothing. Some of them would see it. They would see where it was looking — which was at them — and the look would spread, a flank of them tilting toward the edge to see what it saw. And they would find nothing there. No prey. No falcon. Just something below them, looking up, meaning nothing they have a gesture for.

They would hold. The ripple would go through and not close, exactly, but settle, and for a moment the whole standing form would be looking down at the one thing that had ever looked back at it as a thing and not as a smear across the sky.

Then the light goes, and they drift up to sleep, and the wind does not slacken. It never has. Tomorrow it will be exactly the same. The only difference — and it is not a difference in the place, it is a difference somewhere else entirely — is that now something knows it.
