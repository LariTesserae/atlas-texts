It comes down, not up. That is the first thing it gets right, and it matters, because most powers arrive from above and see a tray of spoiled cement and a problem. This one takes the road with grass down the crown. It stops at the lip of grey clay where the sheep stopped. It looks for a long time before it lets itself feel anything about what it is looking at, which is the whole of its wisdom, really — the interval between seeing and deciding, held open.

Then it does the census. It has to; there is no shortcut to it. It goes down through the leaves to the clay under the clay and finds the oldest tenant and stays a while in the dark with a population that is also a chemistry, and it understands that this thing is not cold because it suffers but because cold is its climate, and that it has never in the whole of its unbroken life been the object of anyone's attention, and that it does not miss this, because missing requires an outside and it has none. It comes up through the seam and finds the crust, which is not one thing but a habit the world has, and it sees that the crust cannot be helped because the crust is not the kind of thing that is ever in trouble. It watches the wagtail read the pipe. It puts a hand — whatever it has for a hand — on the swallowed run and feels the gas move inside the steel through the crack the mud opened, the basin feeding its own extraction, and it feels the same small pleasure you feel when you find that a thing has made itself work without anyone meaning it to. It stands in front of the gauge and lets the needle twitch at it. It listens to the valve.

And it goes through the list of what it could do, because it is a power and powers are asked to do things, and it declines each one.

Warm the mud? No. The cold is the oldest tenant's, and to warm it would be to kill the one thing here that has never asked for anything. Re-drive the trestles? They were never once anything but going; to set them true would only be to give them further to lean. Lift the flywheel out before the floor tips? The flywheel is a shape iron makes for a while before it becomes country; the power is not sentimental about iron. Stop the rim slumping, hold the basin at its present size? That would be to mistake a state for a disaster, the very error the eye makes from the rim, and the power did not come down in order to make the rim's mistakes with more force. Turn off the transformer, end the faithful click, close the line on the spreadsheet cleanly and with a name attached? It considers this longest. The valve has been open when pressure was up and closed when pressure was down for longer than most marriages. The power will not be the party that turns it off. Not out of cowardice — it has nothing to fear from a cost centre — but because that is a decision that belongs to the people who made the pipe, or their heirs, and a wise power does not take from people the decisions that are theirs, even the ones they are refusing to make. The bill can go on being paid. It harms no one. Let it end when it ends.

So far the census has turned up nothing that can be helped, because nothing here is in need. This is not a disappointment. It is most of the answer. Benevolence toward mud is a category error. The power knows this and does not pretend otherwise.

But there is the shed. There is the stool, and the cup, and the one who comes.

The power looks at them the way it looked at the deep tenant, for a long time, without deciding. It sees the quarterly visit and the earlier arrival and the later departure. It sees the step of the pump house, out of the wind, where the floor tilts, and the shape a body makes sitting there when it is not quite sitting yet. It sees the book, and the back pages of the book, and the tussock calendar kept in a hand that is getting smaller, and it understands what getting smaller means. It means writing for no one. It means the record turning inward, folding a grey lip over itself.

It sees the pool with the domed back and the name for it that has not been told. It sees the breath matched to the bulge and settle, and not stopped this time, and not the time before.

And it understands the mechanism as exactly as the one who comes understands it. The basin does not need anyone to kneel. It needs them to be still while it is not. It works on everything by the same rule — the pipe, the ewe, the flywheel — and the rule is not malice, and the rule is not hunger, and this is what makes it so hard to refuse: there is no one in it to refuse. You cannot say no to a stomach. You can only say it to yourself, and the one who comes has been saying it more quietly each quarter, in a smaller hand.

What is happening to them has a name in the census already. It is the oldest tenant's condition, moving upward through the leaves toward a body that was not built for it. The kind of living that has no outside. The pull of the basin is not toward death exactly; it is toward becoming a thing that is only in here, that keeps a rhythm and reports it to no one, that has a face which counts. The gauge is the warning. The gauge is what a witness turns into when there is nobody to witness to.

So the power knows what is lacking, and it is the one thing the basin structurally cannot supply. The basin cannot see anyone back. Nothing in it can. Not the deep tenant, not the crust, not the valve, not even the wagtail, whose attention is real but is for flies. The one who comes has been attending, for years, to a place that does not know it is being attended to, and this is holy and it is also how you drown.

What the power does, then, is small, and it does it the way the basin does things — through the material that is already there, without arranging anything anyone would notice.

The list is reassigned. This was going to happen; the power only sees to it that it happens while there is still time, and not a quarter too late. The department is folded, the line loses the argument with the line below it, and a new name is put against Site 14. An ordinary person. A clipboard. But the power makes one adjustment in the ordinary run of things, so slight that no one could point to it: the old book travels with the site file, and the new person, who is the kind of person who reads the file — there are such people; the power is fond of them — reads the back pages before their first visit. The tussock calendar. The vents that have and have not decided. And, on the last written page, in the smallest hand of all, the name of the pool.

And the new person does what the form does not require. They ring the number on the front of the book and ask if the one who comes would come one more time, and show them.

So there is a quarter when two vehicles park on the rim where the sheep have been. Two people come down over the lip of grey clay, not up. One of them knows which tussocks have gone since last time and says so, aloud, and the other writes it in a hand that is not small, because they are writing it for someone who is standing right there. They cross to the pump house. They look at the flywheel. The valve clicks, and the new one flinches, and the old one says *it does that*, and hearing themselves say it is the first time they have heard how long they have been alone with it.

Then they stand at the pool. The domed back turns over under the skin. The new one asks what it is. And the old one says, in the tone you used on yourself from the rim, *it is almost certainly only mud* — and then, because there is finally somebody to tell, says the name.

That is the whole of the intervention. A private name said out loud to another person, beside the thing it names, on a grey afternoon. The power watches what happens and it is exactly what it hoped: the pool does not change, but it moves. It moves from inside one person to a place between two, which is the only place a thing can be and also be left. The one who comes will not need to keep coming now. They can go up. Not because the basin has released them — the basin never held them; it never holds anything, it only receives what stays — but because what they were keeping here has been handed on, and a thing that has been handed on is no longer a thing you have to stand beside to keep alive.

They go back up. Together, which is the direction. The new one will come down alone next quarter and will keep the calendar and will, in time, start to arrive early and leave late, and will sit on the step, and the power knows this and does not try to prevent it. The pull is not a fault to be fixed. It is what attention costs when it is paid to something that cannot pay it back, and the answer to it is not to stop attending but to make sure the book is always read by the next hand. The power has done that once. It trusts the reading kind of person to exist again when needed.

It leaves the transformer humming. It leaves the stool where it is. Someday the spit of firmer ground will decide, and the stool and the cup will go down together, one grey leaf, and the power sees no reason that this should be a grief.

Before it goes it does one more thing, and this one is not for the one who comes.

It goes back down. Through the leaves, through the clay under the clay, into the dark and the cold and the full. It stays there for a moment with the oldest tenant — the one that has been alive without interruption since before the country had a name, breathing what fell into the sea back out as bubbles, without an outside, without a witness, without ever once having been the object of any regard. And for the length of a single exhalation the power is its outside. It knows it. All of it, the whole population, the whole chemistry, the entire unbroken life. It does not tell it so. It does not warm it or wake it or leave any mark. It simply lets there have been, once, for one breath, something in the universe that knew this was here and thought it good.

Then it withdraws so cleanly that the tenant is exactly what it was, and goes on eating what fell into the dark and breathing it out at a few degrees above freezing, unaware, as it should be, that it was ever anything but alone.

And the bubble comes up through the leaves and through the cone and stands a moment, gloss-skinned, with the sky in it, and closes like a book in another room.
