The first thing the power understands is that it has been here before.

Not to this place. To places like it. It has looked down through the roof of a world and found a people held in a hard rule and has thought, with a warmth it trusts, *what can be done*. It has done things. Lifted a curse, turned a river, taught a word. Those acts were good and it does not regret them, and on the tableland they are the first danger, because they are alike to each other and to what it is inclined to do now, and the plateau is very old and very patient and has closed the space between more like things than these.

So before it does anything it does the thing the people do. It stops. It takes stock of what it is, here, that it has never been anywhere else. It is not *a benevolent and wise power*. That is a kind of animal, and there is a shoulder somewhere for it. It is this one, looking at this floor of grey-green under this one cloud, and whatever it does it will do once.

---

It considers the rule, because that is the obvious thing, and sets it down.

Not because it cannot. It can feel the rule the way you feel a wall in the dark: a firmness at the edge of every distinction, a pressure inward on every pair. It could take that pressure off. The plateau would be a plain then. Very high, very cold, very wet, and otherwise a plain. Twins would be born. A song could be sung twice. A woman could pull her child in against her chest and keep it there through a winter and wake in spring with a child.

And the marks would be decoration. The walls would be walls. Every stone that was ground for a season into a shape nothing shares would be a stone that could have been square. The willows would go on being crooked and it would be a habit. The two who have spent forty years becoming unlike enough to see each other's smoke would learn, in a morning, that they could have had each other the first day, and that everything they made of themselves in the meantime was made for a reason that had been removed, and the power cannot find a way to call that a gift. It has the shape of one. It is a theft with the sign turned.

The rule is not a curse laid over the place. It is the place. It is what is left of a mountain range when you subtract everything that could be subtracted, and these people are the part of it that would not go, and if the power admires anything here it admires that, and you do not honor a refusal by taking away the thing refused.

It sets the rule down where it found it.

---

Animals: no. They would fold in a season, and worse, in the season before they folded they would give the people back a word — *bird*, *beast*, the particular ones — and then take it again. The people have made a peace with *the crowd*. It would be cruelty to reopen the account.

Warmth: no. The cold is structural. It is the height. To warm the plateau is to lower it, and to lower it is to give it sides again, and a way up is a way down.

Rain: no. Rain is repetition. Every drop alike to every other. The power looks at what rain would do here, at the sky trying to fall in ten thousand identical pieces, and does not want to see what it would become before it landed.

Memory: it considers this longest. It could hold the miles. When two shoulders close and the walk between them is gone and the walker cannot say what was in it, the power could keep the mile, the second dwelling with its tools on their pegs, the twenty-four thousand six hundred days a woman of seventy does not have, and give them back at the end. It turns this over for a long time. It is a good thought. It is also the thought a benevolent and wise power would have, and the power notices that it is having it in a way that feels well-worn, and it does not fully trust the feeling, and it lets most of it go. Not all. It keeps one piece, and comes to it later.

---

The attention was drawn by a place and by a being who lives in it, and the power looks for the being, and finds that the plateau does not offer one.

There is the moss, which is a being if anything is: one body the size of a continent, continued rather than alive, healing one cut for longer than the willows have stood. The power lays its attention on the moss and finds nothing to do. The moss wants nothing. It has surrendered so completely that it cannot be helped or harmed, and the only thing the power could give it is a sense that it is one, and the moss would have no use for that, and the power has enough tact to leave it its ignorance.

There is the shoulder where the dead are. The power can see it. It is near the middle, moving, a little more like every other shoulder every year, and the power stands on it for a while in the fog and feels under the moss everyone who has ever lived here, folded into a single low rise, and understands why it is thought of with relief. Every person here spent a life holding themselves apart with an adze and a ledger and a mile of moss. Under this shoulder they are allowed, finally, to be like. The power could mark it. It could make it specific enough to stop its drift and be found and visited. Visiting is a repetition. And the dead have not asked to be found. The power takes its attention off them the way you would take your hand off a sleeper.

There is the fog.

---

The power sits down in it.

This is the being, if the people are right, and the power has come to think they are: the other person on the plateau. The one fog. The rule in the only form the rule takes that can be walked into and breathed. It has come down onto this surface so many times so exactly that every coming has become one coming, and what lies on the tableland for days and then tears and lifts and comes down again has never left and has never had another.

The power notices, sitting in the grey room a hundred paces across, that it also has never had another. It is, so far as it knows, the only one of what it is. And it feels — this is the moment it will remember, when it remembers this place — the pressure. Two singular things are alike in their singularity. The rule does not care what the likeness is. There is a ridge between the fog and the power, and it is a ridge of *being the only one*, and the power can feel it begin, very faintly, to wear.

It does what a person here would do. It differs. It attends to what the fog is that it is not: the fog is patient and the power is not, not really, not on this scale; the fog wants nothing and the power is made of wanting; the fog has never once been surprised and the power, sitting here, is. It holds those differences the way the crooked willow holds its lean, and the pressure eases, and it understands the people better than any explanation would have taught it. This is what every day here costs.

It does not speak to the fog. It would have to say something once, and it does not yet have anything that is worth the only time.

---

What it does is small, and it does it in the hour after the cloud tears.

Across the whole plateau in that hour there are people out on shoulders, alone, singing. Every song sung once, made as it is sung, given to the one gold hour it is for and never carried back to the dwelling. The great songs of the plateau are the ones nobody remembers.

The power is not a person walking one rota. It can be on every shoulder. It is. It listens.

And it keeps them. Not as one thing — it is careful about this, more careful than it has been about anything in a long while — not as *the songs of the tableland*, which would be a crowd and would fold. It keeps each one where it was sung, in the shape of the shoulder and the angle of the light and the particular cold, so that each stays as specific as it was, and it will never sing any of them, because the second singing would become the first and take the evening. It only holds them. Then it carries them down — off the edge that has no edge, through the wall of cloud that the lowlands see, into the loud unattributed places where the rule does not reach — and there they are safe in a way nothing on the plateau has ever been safe, and they are the first things to leave the tableland in longer than the willows have lived.

The people do not know this and do not need to. The songs were given away when they were sung. It is only that now something received them.

The power thinks this is the whole of what it was for, here, and is nearly right.

---

One material thing. It argues with itself about this and then does it.

The hunger is not part of the ethic. Nobody here has made a discipline of it; nobody cuts a notch in a door-slab for a day of it. It is merely there, the way the cold is there, and unlike the cold it is not the height. So the power brings one thing onto the plateau, and brings it already surrendered, so that it arrives the way the moss arrived: a lichen, if it is anything, grey-gold, fat, faintly sweet, that grows on the uphill faces of the fog-walls where the cloud hits and condenses and runs. One organism. It has no memory of being plural because it never was. It cannot fold with the moss because it is nothing like the moss. It can be cut, and it closes.

One morning the walls are gold along their weather side from one horizon to the next, and a wall-walker on the far rota stops and puts her thumb in it and then in her mouth, and stands there for a while with an expression that has no precedent, and that evening cuts a notch in her door-slab, and she is not the only one. The power watches the day happen to them and is glad it did not do more.

---

It does not come over a shoulder. It thinks about it — the days someone came are among the days that are kept — and decides that a visitor who is also the sky is a kind of lie, and these people have no synonyms and would feel it.

But it keeps the one piece of the memory-thought it did not let go, and it spends it here, at the end.

An old woman is out on a far shoulder with an adze. The stone under her hand is this season's, pale, glowing a little in the low light, ground into a shape nothing shares; the mark she is cutting was given to her this morning and has never been cut before and never will be again. She works slowly. A horizon off, a man she has known for forty years is measuring a cistern, and if the volume is what they have been working toward, he will be allowed, this season, to build a hundred paces closer.

Sound does not carry here. Nothing does. The adze rings once on the stone and the ringing is hers and reaches the edge of the moss and stops, as it has ten thousand evenings, which are one evening.

The power lets it carry.

Once. It has never happened and so it is safe; it will not happen again and so it is theirs. The one clear note crosses the whole gold distance in the thin air and arrives at a man crouched at the lip of a cistern with a measuring-cord in his hands, and he straightens, and turns toward where her smoke is, and knows the sound, because he has stood beside her while she cut stone and there is no other adze on the plateau that rings quite like that one, or there is, but he has not heard it. She does not know he heard. She will know when she sees his slab. He cuts the notch before the cold comes down. Then he goes back to the cord.

The power is already leaving. It did not speak to the fog. It has thought of something worth saying, on the way down, and it does not turn back, because there is a way of being wise that is only the same thing walking, and the plateau has a word for that, and it is used for nothing living.
