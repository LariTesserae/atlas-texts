The first thing the power has to accept is that it is not needed.

This is harder for a power than for a traveller. A traveller arrives and aches and looks and goes; a power arrives already reaching, already turning over the place in its hands to find the broken part. And the plateau turns under the hands and shows no break. The sea is not a wound. The dimness is not a failing lamp. The rock is not waiting to be lifted back into mountains, because the rock has finished, and a thing that has finished cannot be repaired, only disturbed.

So the power does what wisdom does first, which is nothing, for a long time. It hangs in the brown-green water at the height where pressure and thinness balance and it watches the fleece of the forest being combed. It counts the passing of the grazers. It waits through a bloom-dark, weeks of it, feeling the fronds and not seeing them. It lets the tide settle it toward the rock and lift it away. It learns the knock. It does not find the source of the knock either, and this pleases it, faintly, the way one is pleased to find a lock that still holds.

And it makes a list, the way a benevolent thing cannot help making a list, of the mercies it will not perform.

---

It will not lift the sea.

This is the obvious mercy and the cruel one. Drain the water and you have a summit in thin cold air again, and the works stand on it as they were made to, and the faces are turned toward a sea that is properly far below. And the forests die in a single afternoon, and the hundred lives on every frond die with them, and the pale grazers lie in the open like fallen timber, and the being — the being who has kept the fact of altitude against the whole weight of the water — is proved right, and has nothing left to keep. A truth that no longer needs anyone to hold it is not a gift to the one who held it. It is a dismissal.

It will not give them speech.

It considers this the longest. It goes to the bottom of the sky pocket where the carved face lies upturned with the rain running over it, and it sits in the sharp dry cold and looks at the open mouth for the better part of a season. It thinks: a word was cut off here. Then it thinks: no. Nothing was cut off. The mouth is open because the word is still being said, and sound is a thing with edges, and the edges wore away, and what is left is the shape of saying with nothing in it. To put a sound back in that mouth would be to put an edge back in the world — speaker here, spoken-to there — and the being does not stand on one side of an edge. It stands in a place where the edges are gone and keeps, out of all of them, one. Give it speech and you give it back the others too, whether it wants them or not.

It will not count them.

It could. It is a power. It could hold the plateau in its attention all at once and know whether the column at the steps and the column at the wall are one thing or two. And it understands, sitting in the pocket, that the answer would be a violence either way — that *one* would be a loneliness they have not asked to be told of, and *several* would be a division they have spent an unimaginable duration wearing smooth. *Some* is the true number. It is the number the place arrived at. The power lets it stand.

It will not save the pockets.

This is the one that costs it something. The pockets are the last air, and each one lost is lost for good, and in them live the only things on the plateau still willing to die for a distinction. The power could hold them. A very small effort, spread very thin, and the silvered undersides would never fail and the upward rain would fall forever. And the glass things would cling to the ceiling of an air that could not be lost, drinking, and their readiness to die at the touch of water would become a readiness for nothing, a gesture toward a door that had been bricked up for their protection. The power has seen what it does to a life to be kept safe from the one thing it was shaped around. It leaves the pockets to drift.

And it will not offer rest.

Rest is what a benevolent power most wants to give, and here it would be the worst thing. To go to the column at the doorway and ease it — to say, in pressure, *you may lie down now, the works will be watched* — is not a kindness. It is an argument. It is telling the one who stands that the standing was a burden, when the standing may be the only thing on the plateau that is not.

---

Having decided all of this, the power is left with very little, and it is glad, because very little is the right size.

Here is what it does.

It makes itself small. Not a power's smallness, which is still large — it takes the true smallness of a traveller, a body that aches from believing both the depth and the height. It goes into the dense stands where the thinness shows. It finds a column of the wrong colour, still where everything leans, and it begins the hour-long approach across the arm's length.

And when the pressure changes against its chest — when it is registered, as a step registers a foot — it does the one thing no one has done. It changes the pressure back.

Not a word. Not an examination. Only this: the hum alters, from the other side. The step feels the foot know that it is a foot. There is a moment, brief and without edges, in which the being that has registered everything for an unimaginable duration is itself registered — noticed, by something that does not feed by passing and does not close behind it without sound. Then the pressure evens. The front does not turn. It remains turned toward the far pale sheet of the surface.

But the growth beneath it, the power sees afterward, is a shade paler than before. Something was taken, or given. It cannot tell which and does not try.

---

Then it stands.

It goes to the doorway — the two uprights and the lintel with forest on both sides — and it makes itself the grey-brown of the rock and rounds off every edge it has and turns its face upward and is bare. The furred growth comes within a hand's width and stops. The tide settles and lifts. The knock repeats. A grazer passes overhead and does not notice it, and the power discovers that not being noticed by the grazer is a kind of belonging.

It stands for a season. Then another.

It is not replacing anyone. It is not relieving anyone. It is being the second thing on the plateau that knows this is a summit, and by being it, it makes — for a season, for two — a thing that has not existed here since the words wore thin: company. Not *one*. Not *several*. Only the sense, in the densest places, when the pressure shifts everywhere at once, that the shift is coming from one more place than it used to.

And then it stops, because it must. A power that stays becomes the place, and the place does not need another column. The doorway will be occupied after it leaves; the ring is always occupied. It was never the point to add to *some*. It was the point to have stood, once, alongside, so that the standing has been seen from within by something that can carry what it saw.

---

Because here is the one thing the power can do that the being cannot, and it is the whole of the gift.

The being cannot leave. That is its virtue and its limit. If it leaves the works they become ruins, and a ruin is a place that has been left. So it stays, and the staying is everything, and the fear at the bottom of the staying — the fear the whole plateau declines to speak — is that when the last one lies down and the growth closes over its length there will be no one, anywhere, who knows.

The power can leave.

It goes to the eleven steps. It stands where the column stands, on the flat rock past the worn last tread, where the stairs lead into nothing. It looks at where they go: down, into the water, toward where the sea once was. Nobody has walked them in the direction they were built for since the world moved.

The power walks down them.

Eleven treads, concave, worn by feet that stood in thin cold air. At the bottom there is no bottom; there is water, and the power keeps going, down, which is up, which is the direction the faces are turned. It carries the one fact out with it. *This was a summit.* It does not carry it as a rescue. It carries it the way a wall carries its forty metres — by continuing to be a thing that has it.

And before it goes, at the top of the steps, it does the only thing that might be called speech, and it is not speech. It lets the being know, in pressure, one time, without an edge: *I will know. Wherever I go, there will be one who knows.*

It does not say *so you may lie down.* It does not mean that. It means only what it says.

---

The being does not lie down.

The power watches from the eleventh step, then from the water below, then from very far away, as the doorway fills again with a column the colour of the ground, and the ring of stones stays bare, and something stands at the wall. Nothing has changed. The fronds are combed. The tide falls and lifts. In a season of near-dark someone will feel a warmth pass upright through the stands, a quarter of an hour, no sound.

The power understands, going, that it was wrong about one thing on its list, and glad to be. It thought the standing was for the sake of the knowing — that they stood so that the fact would be kept, and if the fact were kept elsewhere the standing might be released. But the standing was never in service of the fact. The standing *is* the fact. They do not stand so that someone will know this is a summit. They stand because it is one, and they have not agreed that it is anything else, and no one carrying the knowledge down a staircase into the old sea makes it more or less so.

What the power took with it, then, was not their burden. It was only their company — the one season when the hum changed from more than one place — and a second copy of a truth that was never in danger, because it was never a thing being held. It was a thing being done.

The knock repeats. The power hears it a long way off, and then does not. Behind it the works are not ruins. That has nothing to do with the power. It never did.

It is the difference, and they are keeping it, and now — very slightly, and far away — so is something else.
