It would arrive the way the cloud is there: not coming, but already having been.

This is the first thing the power understands, and it understands it before it has finished turning its attention toward the basin, because the understanding is what wisdom is for. An arrival is an event. Anything that arrives has a before and an after, and the basin has neither, and so the power cannot arrive. It can only be found, when the moss is looked at closely enough, to have been in the moss the whole time. It cannot enter. It can only have always been the warmth. It takes care with this. It takes more care with this than it has taken with anything in a long while, because it knows that the whole of what it might do here depends on doing this first part invisibly, and that if it fails — if a single salamander lifts its head — then the thing it was looking at will already be gone, and something else will be in its place, something with a history.

So it is there, and has been there, and the frogs go on.

Then it looks. It looks for what would be, by the reckoning of the things below, a long time: long enough for the nine in the canopy to become nine others, long enough for the walkers to complete their loop a thousand times, long enough for a tree to soften and fold and rise again with its frost the same size. To the power this is not long. It has looked at slower things. But it finds it is looking differently than usual, and it takes a while to notice why.

Usually, when it looks at a place, the place is asking. Not in words, always, but in shape: a herd thinned by a bad year has the shape of a request, a river running the wrong way has the shape of a request, a mind that has just learned its own death is a request with nothing else in it. The power has spent most of its existence reading these shapes and answering them. That is what benevolence is, it had thought: the reading and the answering. It goes to where the shape of the need is and it fills the shape.

The basin has no shape of need. It looks and looks and cannot find the edge where the want begins. The frogs are not short of frogs. The moss is not short of warmth. The nine are not short of the ninth. It searches the way you search a dark room for a wall, hand out, and the hand goes on and on into the warm wet air and never touches anything.

And so — because it is not only wise but honest, and knows that these are not the same — it turns and looks at the hand.

It finds, in itself, a series of wishes. It takes them out one at a time and holds them up to the slate-coloured light and sees what they are made of.

It wishes to give them a dawn. Only one. A single parting of the lid, a single shaft, gold on black glass for the length of a breath and then closed again. It is so easy it hardly counts as an act. And the power imagines it — the light coming down through the leaves, every fixed black eye in the canopy filling suddenly with a brightness there had never been a mechanism to close against — and sees that what it is imagining is not a gift. It is the first blow. After the dawn there is a before-the-dawn. After the dawn every creature that saw it carries, somewhere in its wet grey nerve, the beginning of a then. And then is the seed of memory, and memory is the seed of anticipation, and anticipation is the seed of the one thing that has never existed here, which is fear. It would take a long time to grow. But it would grow. The power would be giving them, wrapped in gold, the whole apparatus of loss. It puts the dawn down.

It wishes to give them a wind. The leaves have never moved except downward with the water. It would like to see them lift. It looks at this wish and sees that it is the wish to see something, and that the seeing would be its own. It puts the wind down.

It wishes to give the nine a memory, so that their attention could have something to be about. This one it holds longest, because it is the most like love. The round-eyed things are, it has said to itself, the most awake creatures that have ever lived, and their wakefulness has never had an object, and this seems to the power a kind of waste — the way a full cup poured onto the ground is a waste. It would like to give them a floor for the water to stand on. A little remembering. Just enough that when one of the nine died the other eight would know that it had.

And there it stops, because it has said the thing plainly, and can hear it. Know that it had. The eight would know that the ninth had been and was not. That is what memory is for: it is the instrument by which a mind measures the distance between what was and what is, and the basin has arranged itself, over a span the power respects, so that the distance is zero and the instrument was never required. To give them memory is to give them grief and call it fullness. The cup is not being wasted. The cup is being held. There is a difference and the power, who has poured out a great deal in its time, is a little ashamed not to have seen it first.

It wishes — this is the last one, and the smallest, and it nearly does not catch it — to give the basin a name.

It has one ready. It has had one ready almost since it began looking. And it understands, holding this wish up, that a name is a hook by which a thing is hauled out of itself and into a set of other things, and that the basin has no name because it has never once been a member of any set. To name it is to say: you are one of these. It is the first act of comparison. It is the thin end of every wedge. The power sets the name down very carefully and does not pick it up again, and it will not be written here either.

What is left, when the wishes are all laid on the moss, is the looking. And the power sits with the looking and finds that it is being changed by it. Not the basin. Itself. It had believed that a complete thing would be a thing it could not love, because love, as it had practised love, was answering. Here there is nothing to answer, and it loves the place anyway, and the love has nowhere to go, and it discovers that love with nowhere to go does not curdle or fade. It simply stays. Like the water. It rises and it comes down and none of it is lost.

Then it sees the one thing the basin cannot see.

It is not on the surface. It is not in the tubes. It is below the tubes, far below, where the warmth comes from — and the warmth is not forever. Nothing that comes up out of rock is forever. The power looks down through the black glass and the soft red mouth of the moss and the threaded dark of the drained lava and sees the heat as what it is: a slow fire, very slow, banked in stone, and burning down. Not soon. Not in any span the frogs could count if the frogs could count. But it will cool. The rock will go, over ten thousand years or a hundred thousand, from blood-warm to hearth-warm to merely warm to nothing, and the water will stop rising, and the lid will thin, and one day — one actual day, the first the basin has ever had — the cloud will open and there will be a sky.

And the power imagines the cooling as it would happen if left alone. The gradient appearing where there has never been a gradient. The centre of the basin a shade warmer than the edge, and the walkers, without knowing why, bending their loop toward the centre. The pools at the rim going a fraction cooler, and the frogs in them singing a fraction slower, and the young at the edge coming to voice in notes that do not quite fit the chord. The frost on the fungus thinning. The nine, in a canopy of leaves that have begun, imperceptibly, to be eaten faster than they thicken, looking at each other with their fixed black regard and beginning — because there is finally something to build it from — to construct, out of nothing, out of nerve and damp and a lifetime of undivided attention, the first faint mechanism of alarm. Building it slowly, over generations, for the first time, and for one purpose: to feel themselves end.

That is the thing the power will not permit. Not the ending. The ending is not its to refuse; it has watched more endings than there are frogs in the basin and it knows that to hold a thing back from its end is to make the thing yours, and to make it yours is to make it something other than what it was. It will not become the basin's keeper. It will not stand under the rock with its hands cupped around the fire for the rest of time, because then the basin would be a lamp it was carrying, and not a basin.

But it will not let the ending be an interval. That much is within its gift, and it is perhaps the only thing that is. It decides — and the decision is not an event either, because it is made outside — that when the warmth goes it will go all at once, in the length of a single held note. There will be no cooling. There will be no gradient. The frogs will be singing the chord and the walkers will be walking and the nine will have their eight in their ears and the frost will be exactly the size it has always been, and then there will be the sky. Nothing will have time to become afraid, because nothing will have time to become anything. The basin will end the way it lived: without event. It will be completed in a moment, as it was completed in the moment the lava stopped, and everything between the two moments will have been one long unbroken now with no seam in it anywhere.

This is the one act of benevolence available. The power is very old and it has not often found itself with only one, and it finds the narrowness of it clean.

And then, having decided about the end, it does the thing it came to do, which it did not know until now.

It goes in.

Not as a presence. As a thing that is where it is. It makes itself into something the walkers will part around without lifting their heads — a hump in the rock, a fern-stem, a shape in the moss no different from the other shapes — and it lets the traffic flow past its shins and close behind. It lets the haze hang in its column through the space where it is. It lets the frogs go on under its hand. And it lifts its face to the canopy, and the nine are there, and they look at it.

They look at it with exactly the regard they turn on the leaf. There is no change in them. That is the point. There is nothing in the look that asks, nothing that recognises, nothing that reports a shift in state, because there is no shift. The power has been looked at by every kind of eye there is and every one of them has wanted something, and here are nine that want nothing and are looking at it anyway, purely, with the whole of an attention that has never once been divided, and it understands that it has found the one place in the world where it can be seen without being prayed to. The nine's wakefulness has never had anything to be about. For the length of a leaf, the power is what it is about. And the nine do not know this, and the power will never tell them, and that is the whole of the gift and it goes both ways.

Later — there is no later, but say it anyway — it goes down.

The tubes are the only direction that means anything and the power follows the meaning. Down through the domed crust into the dark, into water warm as the water above, into the place where even the roar is a pressure through stone rather than a sound. Here are the pale things. Eyeless, soft, slow; the innermost of the innermost; things that have never had even the cloud, that have lived in a dark so entire they were never given the concept of what it was dark of. The description of them stops before it can say what they are shaped like, and the power, who has seen every shape there is, kneels in the black water and looks, and finds that it cannot finish the sentence either. They are shaped like the water they lie in. They are shaped like the absence of anything to be shaped against. If the basin is one being, and the moss is its lung and the nine are its face, then these are its heart, and the heart has no form because nothing has ever pressed on it.

The power sits down in the dark beside them. It does not touch them. It is not needed and it does not pretend to be. It simply stays, for a while, in the one place it has ever found where staying is all that is asked and nothing is asked, and lets the warmth come up out of the ground into it, and gives nothing back, and is, for once in its long benevolent life, the one being held.

Above, the frogs are still singing. Not one of them has missed a breath.
