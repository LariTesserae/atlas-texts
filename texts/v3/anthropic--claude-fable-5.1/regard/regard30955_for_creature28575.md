The first thing the power learns is that it cannot arrive.

It can come to the mountain. It can stand in the room, if standing is a thing it does, in the low even light with the beads letting go one at a time overhead. But it cannot come to the film. There is no side of the film that faces outward. Its whole eye is a patch of pigment at the top of the room that sees bright and not-bright, and its whole hearing is the drip-rate, and its whole touch is the wet edge of itself where the wet stops. Anything the power did in that room — anything at all, however kind — would be received as weather. A warm day. A wet season. A drop that fell somewhere new.

This is not a rebuke and the power does not take it as one. It is only the shape of the situation. Whatever is going to be done here will be done the way rain is done, from outside the film's world, without address, and will be understood, if at all, as a change in the taste of the water.

So the power waits first. It stays through several of the film's presents — several weeks — because it is wise enough to know that a thing whose now is a week cannot be looked at in an afternoon. It watches the frost try on the shadowed wall and fail. It watches the arthropod walk its route. It counts the thickness of the rime near the seam and counts it again seven days later and again seven days after that. It does what the film cannot do, which is to see the room as a room, and the seam as a door, and the door as opening.

---

Then it asks what it is for.

Benevolence usually means adding something. A good that was missing, supplied. And the power, turning the film over in its attention, finds that there is nothing missing. The film wants one thing and it has that thing everywhere, at this moment, at once. There is no more wet than wet. It cannot be made happier. It cannot be made more itself. In the lowlands its relatives are eaten and crowded and dried by turns and never get to lay down more than a smear; this one has had centuries of nothing but water and room, and it has become exactly what the smear was always trying to become, and there is no further place for it to go. It is at its maximum. The power has come upon a thing at its maximum and has nothing to give it.

Except time. Time is the one good the room is running out of.

---

The power considers holding the mountain.

It could. A range is not so much, to a power. But it looks at what the mountain is doing and sees that the rising is not the room's enemy; the rising is the room. The fracture is a rising-crack. The seeps are rising-water, pressed up through new stone. The warm air that climbs the south face and lets go against the cold is climbing because there is a face to climb, and in the lowlands under that face, in the rain-shadow and the rain, a thousand thumbnail smears are holding on under a thousand stones by a thousand streams that run where they run because the mountain is where it is. To still the mountain for the sake of one of them would be to lean on a country to keep a cup from spilling. The power puts that down.

It considers sealing the seam. Wedge the wedge; let nothing fall. And sees at once that the seam is the film's noon. Close it and the pigment goes dark and the sugar stops and there is nothing to spend against the frost, and the shadowed wall wins in a season. The door the film cannot close is also the only place light gets in. The wrongness of it is load-bearing.

It considers carrying a grain. The arthropod already does this, unwitting, wall to floor; the power could do it fracture to fracture. There are other seams on this mountain, other seeps, other rooms out of the wind that no rain has happened to salt. It could repeat the founding accident on purpose. And it thinks about this a long time — a month, by the film's reckoning — and decides against, not because it would fail but because of what it would mean to succeed. The film is not rare. What is rare is that it was let alone. A room that had been given its city would not be a room where a city was allowed; it would be a garden, and the power would be its gardener, and the whole point of the place is that no one is. The power will not become the condition of the thing it admires. It puts the grain down too.

---

What is left is small, and the power takes its time getting there, and when it gets there it finds the film has already gone most of the way.

Because the film is building for it. Lower, broader, channels turned inward. It has felt the seam grow hard to keep wet and it is answering with the only answer it has. The power, which can see the rate at which the fracture is opening and the rate at which the rime is thickening, lays the two rates side by side and finds that they nearly meet. Nearly. When the wedge goes — and the power can see roughly when, some decades out, in a hard winter after a wet autumn — the wall on the seam side will be two-thirds of what it needs to be. Two-thirds is a great deal of dying. Not the death of the thing; it would draw back to the north wall and go on, without loss, without knowing it had lost. But a third of a body, gone to crust in an afternoon, for want of a few years.

And the thing that kills, when the wedge goes, is not the light. It is the wind. The film could bear the light; it bears it now. What it cannot bear is the summit wind coming through a hand's-width gap onto surfaces that have never been dried faster than a drop can fall. That is the whole of the danger. Still air it can hold. Moving air it cannot.

Above the seam, on the east face, there is a slab. It is one of the ones that has not yet let go — still fitting the wound it will leave, a plate of the summit block the size of a door, held by a lip of stone and a habit. It will fall. Everything on this mountain will fall, eventually, once the rock learns what wind does to rock. In the ordinary course of things it falls a century after the wedge, and lands where it lands, and means nothing.

If it falls first — and falls so — it lies against the east face below the seam, leaning, a stone lean-to, and the gap it makes with the mountain is a pocket of air the wind goes over instead of through. And when the wedge goes, decades on, what comes into the room is not the summit wind but the still cold of the pocket. And the light comes in, more than before, which the pigment will take and turn into more to spend. And the wall the film has been building, two-thirds done, is more than enough, because it was built against a wind that never arrives.

---

So this is what the power does.

One winter, in a crack above the seam that no one will ever look into, there is a little more water than there would have been. A cupful, perhaps. And it hesitates a little longer than it would have, and it is a little colder than it would have been, by a degree, for one night. That is the whole of the act. A cupful and a degree, once, in a season the film cannot register, at a place the film does not know exists. In the spring the lip of stone has a new line in it. Some years later, on a wet night, the slab lets go, and the sound of it is heard in the room as a pressure and a shudder and then nothing, and the drip-rate jumps for an hour as the whole east face rings, and the film reads this, if it reads it, as a wet hour, and thickens a little along the seam side against a repetition, and forgets it.

Nothing else. The power does not stay to see the wedge fall. It does not mark the place. It does not, in any way the mountain could record, have been there.

---

There is a kind of help that cannot be received, and the power sits with that before it goes.

The film will never know it was held. Its memory is the shape of what it has built, and the act was made so that there would be nothing to build — a wall that did not need to be finished in a panic, a wound that did not need to be grown shut. The only trace of benevolence will be an absence in the scar tissue. If the power had done nothing, the room a century hence would carry the record of a bad afternoon: a broad dead crust along the east wall, mineral without film, and behind it the new structures where the living had drawn back and started over. Instead there will be nothing there to read. The room will look as if the mountain had simply been kind. Which is, from inside the room, exactly what happened.

The power decides this is the correct kind of help, and not the useless kind. It is not sure it is right about that. It is wise enough not to be sure, and benevolent enough to have done it anyway.

And it takes one thing away with it, because even a power can be given something by a smear of green in a cold hole. It has watched, for a season, a body that wants one thing everywhere at once and has arranged its whole world so that the wanting is met; that moves water uphill without contradiction because the contradiction was never in the water; that calls nothing an injury and heals everything; that has no word for any of this and does not need one. The power has spent a long time being wise, and wisdom is mostly the knowing of what not to want. Here is a thing that has never had to learn that. It wants exactly what it can have, and it has it, and the having is continuous, and it will go on having it in the still air behind a fallen door for as long as the seeps run.

---

Toward evening the light in the seam goes copper and the pigment at the top of the room registers that the hours are ending. Along the shadowed wall the frost tries and is met. The arthropod folds into its gap. In the dark inside the towers, water taken at noon is still being tasted.

Above the seam a slab of the summit block sits in its wound with a crack in its lip that it did not have last year, holding a little water, and the water is hesitating. The film does not know this. It will never know this. It lies wet along every surface it has made and knows exactly one thing, everywhere, at once, and the power, which knows a great many things, has arranged — as quietly as it could, as weather does — that it will go on knowing it.
