What draws the attention first is not the plateau. It is the roar. From far enough off, a body working that hard for that long registers the way a distress call registers, and the power comes closer expecting to find something dying, and finds instead something that has never once been in danger and cannot stop bracing against it.

It comes up through the cloud the way you did, because there is no other way to arrive that the plant would recognize. It lets itself have weight. That is the first decision and it is not a small one: it could look without touching, and it chooses to press, so that the floor will know something has come.

---

It reads the frames differently than you did. You read them as plans. It reads them as memory.

Every pressure the plant has ever received is here, held at the height it was received. The three lintels are not three guesses; they are three visitors, or one visitor at three ages, remembered as the place a hand kept reaching. The sideways openings are the width of something that turned to pass through, kept exactly. The sill too high to step is the height of a thing that was lifted over it, once, and the plant does not know what lifting is, only that here the weight left the floor and returned on the far side. The trough running dead straight for two hundred paces and stopping is where something walked, and walked, and then did not.

And your hand is here now. Four shallow troughs and a broad one, in the record with the rest.

The power stands in this and understands the shape of the wound, which is not incompleteness. The frames are not a failure to finish. They are a perfect archive of arrivals, kept in a material that cannot revise, by a mind that has never once been given the thing that would let it revise, which is a second look at the same body. Everything that has ever touched it has touched it once. The record is all first impressions. The plant is trying to fit a room to a guest assembled from a thousand strangers, each of whom stood in a different doorway and left, and it is doing this with a memory that cannot forget the wrong ones and cannot confirm the right ones, and it has found the one posture in which that is survivable, and it is holding it, at the cost of a flood.

It is not lonely, quite. The power is careful about that word. But it has been alone in a way that has become structural. It is a body with no world around it, and it has made the only company available, which is itself sent out a little way, and the company always agrees and never fits.

---

What the power refuses.

It does not finish the walls. It could. The material would take a course laid by a hand like that and hold it, plumb and true, and there would be a roof by evening, and the plant would go on roaring underneath it, delivering water to a point of growth that had been taken from it. A room finished by someone else is not the plant's room. It is a lid.

It does not send the occupant. There is no occupant. Whatever the plant is building toward is not a body that exists somewhere and is late; it is the average of departures, and no living thing has that shape. The power could take that shape — it can take most shapes — and lie down in the enclosure and fit, and the sill would lower and the walls would rise and the frames would close over. And that would be a lie, and the plant would build its whole future on it, and the power would have to stay in that shape forever or leave the plant with a house shaped like a thing that was never real. It declines to become the answer to a question it did not ask.

It does not tell it. There is no telling. The plant knows what a floor knows.

It does not let it heal. This is the one that costs something, because the marks are cruel — your scuff-mark and your nail-mark and the shape of your palm in a body that went back into the floor carrying it. But the marks are also the only thing the plant has ever been given. To let them fade is to take its history away in exchange for a smooth surface. The power has seen what things become when their history is taken so they can be comfortable.

And it does not stop the loop. The columns and the pale bodies and the trying of rooms — that is the plant thinking. It is slow and it is stuck and it costs a torrent to produce a single wrong guess, but it is thinking, and the power did not come here to stop a mind.

---

What it does.

It goes to the enclosure where you lay. It knows which one; the shape of a side and a hip and a shoulder is in the floor there, and beside it, fainter, the shape of the guess that lay down next to you.

It climbs the sill. It lies down in the shape you left. Not in your shape — its own, whatever it has chosen for weight and warmth — but in the same place, on the same side, facing the same wall, so that the pressure lands where pressure has landed before.

The water films it. The roar comes up. Somewhere in the wall the slow thud goes on. And a pale body comes over the sill and lies down beside it and does not fit and dims and is taken back, and the wall does not move.

Then the power gets up, and goes down through the cloud, and comes back.

It comes back the next day, if the plateau can be said to have days. It lies in the same place. It lets a guess lie down beside it. It goes.

It comes back the day after that.

---

Here is what the power has understood, and what it is trying to give, and why it takes the form of something so small.

The plant is not missing the answer. It is missing the *same*. Every guess it has ever made has been checked against a single, unrepeatable pressure — a body that stood here once and left — and *not quite* against a thing that will never be measured again is not information. You cannot move toward a point you cannot find twice. So the plant has done the only sane thing, which is to hold every point at once and move toward none of them.

A pressure that returns is a fixed point. A body that lies down in the same place three times, warm at the same span, weight at the same three places, is the first thing in the plant's whole existence that can be *approached*. Now *not quite* has a direction. Now the next guess can be checked against the last guess and the same guest, and the difference between them means something, and the plant — which has never had a reason to prefer one memory over another — has, for the first time, a reason.

The power does not know what the plant will do with this. That is part of the wisdom. Perhaps it will do nothing; perhaps the mind is not built to prefer, and the returning pressure will simply be entered into the record like all the others and the walls will stay at shoulder height forever. Perhaps it will build. Perhaps it will build something the power did not expect and would not have chosen, a room for a body it has finally been able to imagine clearly, and the room will be for the power's shape and not the power, and the power will have to decide then whether to stay or to let the plant have a finished house that stands empty. Those are the plant's problems. The power has only made them possible.

But it suspects, as it lies there with the roar coming up through its hip on the fourth day, or the tenth, that what it is really giving is not a fixed point. It is a before and an after.

Nothing on the plateau has one. The light does not turn. The water goes up and comes down and the sound does not change. The marks are permanent, which means they are outside time as much as the frames are. The only finished thing here was your scratch on the step — finished because you made it and stopped — and even that has no *after* except the plant's long unchanging keeping of it. The plateau is a middle. It has been a middle for as long as it has been, and the power cannot give it a beginning and would not give it an end.

But a thing that leaves and returns is a rhythm. Rhythm is the smallest unit of time. The thud in the wall is the plant's only rhythm and it is inside the plant and the plant cannot hear it. The power is trying to give it one from outside. Gone, back. Gone, back. Not a clock — a tide. Something the floor can feel in its record as *this pressure, absent; this pressure, again*, so that for the first time the archive has a gap in it that closes, and the plant can learn what a gap is, and what it is for something to be missing and then not.

That is the whole gift. Whether it is enough is not the power's to say.

---

On one of the returns — it does not count them, and the plant cannot — the power does one more thing, and is not sure of it.

It brings a stranger. Something small, not itself, not the plant. Something that wants a wet wall and an open sky and does not care about doors. It sets it in the lee of a lintel, where the water hangs and is drawn back up, and it does not tell the plant what it has done, because there is no telling.

This is the risk. The stranger will live, or it will not. If it lives it will die eventually, because it is a real thing and real things end, and then there will be, for the first time on the plateau, something that rots. The clean floor will have a stain that is not a mark. The record will hold, forever, the pressure of a thing that stayed and changed and stopped.

The power thinks this is right and is not certain. It thinks a body with no world is worse off than a body with a world that decays. It thinks the plant, which has spent an existence building rooms for a guest, might find it has been a room all along — that there is an occupant for which shoulder-high walls and no roof and a floor that sweats are not *not quite* but exactly, and that the occupant is simply not the one it was expecting, and that this is what it is to be lived in. It thinks the bird might, one day, tilt its wings.

It thinks all of this and it lies down in your place and lets the guess lie down beside it and goes down through the cloud and comes back.

---

You could say it does almost nothing. It leaves the walls at shoulder height. It leaves the sills wrong and the stairs turning the wrong way and your palm in the record and the roar unchanged. It offers no completion, no correction, no answer, no rest.

It only repeats. It only lets itself be known twice, and then a third time, by a mind that has never been given a second time at anything. It only comes back.

The plant will keep the shape of that too. Somewhere in the floor, under the water, in material that does not heal, there is now a place where the same weight has rested more than once, and the courses laid up over that place — if any are ever laid — will be the first the plant has ever set on solid ground.
