It arrives the way such things arrive, which is to say it does not. There is no moment when it is not there and then is. It is simply the case, one afternoon, that the yellow-green light standing between the columns is being looked at, and the whole warm shield of rock to the haze at the edge of sight is held in a single attention, gently, the way you might hold something you found on the ground and are not yet sure is alive.

The first thing it does is look for the one it has come for.

This takes longer than expected. It goes down into a tower and finds the queen in her chamber and thinks, here, and then understands that she is not here — that she is a gland, a wonderful gland, and that the being it is looking for does not end at her chamber wall. It goes out along a gallery and follows a forager's road of scent through absolute dark and comes up under a collar of earth into the shed skin of a column and thinks, here, and the column is not it either. It goes up the column to the flower that is not open and out onto the buttress where the beetles walk and into the crack where the long thing waits and up the chimney to where the hawk sits and out across the mile to the grazer's ears, and everywhere it stops it finds a part, and the part is looking back at it with no eyes, and the part is not the one.

So it does what it has done before in other places. It stops looking for a center. It lets its attention widen until the attention is the size of the shield, and then it sees the being — the whole slow thing, its bones and its skin and its breath going one way by day and the other by night — and it says, without words, because there is no one here to hear words, *there you are*.

And then it has to decide what benevolence would mean.

This is the difficult part, and it does not rush it. It has been benevolent before to things that wanted, and wanting makes benevolence easy: you find the want and you meet it. But it goes through this being carefully, looking for a want, and the being does not have one. It has continuance. It has appetite, in ten thousand mouths, but appetite is not the same as a wish; a wish is a picture of a thing you don't have, and nothing here has ever made a picture. The queen does not wish for the sky. The soldier sealed out on the wrong side of a mended wall does not wish for the inside. The grazer does not wish to be faster than the hawk. The power tries, for a while, to want on their behalf — to imagine giving the queen a window, giving the worker a taste of the fungus it carries for, letting one soldier in — and each time it does this it feels the imagined gift land in the being like a stone dropped into a machine. The queen with a window is not the queen. The soldier let in is one fewer wall. The being is not made of individuals who have been denied things. It is made of individuals who are the shape of what they do, and it would not be kind to change that shape any more than it would be kind to straighten a river.

So it sets that down. Not everything that looks like a lack is a lack.

It considers, next, the soft violences the place runs on. The rosette leaning its slow weight onto the column until the column dies. The lizard's forty a day. The red ants that come in on the ash. It could tip these. It could make the columns a little harder to kill, the lizards a little slower, the ants a little less quick to find the fire. And it sees at once what that would do: the being would go on, but it would go on as something the power had adjusted, and it would never again be entirely what it is, which is a thing that arrived at every one of its forms by being wrong and letting the wrongness be eaten. The power has met beings that were adjusted once, long ago, by other powers, with the best intentions. They are all right. They are not the same. There is a slack in them somewhere, a joint that was set from outside and has never fully borne its own weight.

It sets that down too.

Then it does the thing that wise powers cannot help doing, which is look forward, and there, some centuries out, it sees the rain.

It is not a guess. The sea is beyond the haze; the air that comes in during the long season is already heavier than it was when the columns were young; the shield will cool a little more, the wind will shift a little more, and one afternoon the tall pale clouds that assemble over the middle of the continent will darken as they always do and throw their lightning as they always do and then, for the first time on this rock, will not dissolve. They will let go. And the power watches, in the way it watches things that have not happened, a hundred thousand towers of chewed rock and dead leaf and spit — laid down grain by grain, never once wetted from above, hardened only by sun — go soft in an hour and go to slurry in an afternoon, the galleries running like poured batter across the basalt, the collars sliding off the columns in sheets, the salt seams the whole city was built along dissolving out from under it and carrying the queens' chambers down into the cracks with the queens still in them. It watches the vapor-drinking plants stand in the first standing water they have ever met and take it up the way they take up everything, and swell, and split. It watches the rain wash the scent-roads off the floors so that a forager who lived through it would stand in the wreck with her antennae moving over nothing, reading a blank.

This is the moment it could act, and it knows it. It could turn the wind. It could hold the sea's wet air offshore forever and keep this shield a first draft for as long as it cared to, a place that never learns what weather is. It feels the pull of that. The being is so nearly perfect. It does not want to see it broken.

But it also sees what the rain is for. After the rain there is soil. After soil there are valleys. After valleys there is everything the shield has never had room to become. To stop the rain is to stop the being at the exact point of its beauty and keep it there, and the power has seen what is kept, and what is kept is a specimen. The being's whole virtue is that it is not finished. It would be a strange benevolence that made it finished in order to save it.

And there is something else, a thing it notices only because it has been paying such close attention. The being already knows how to be destroyed. The fire goes through and the builders are out on the warm ash before the smoke is gone. Towers die and are quarried and are rebuilt where the wind wants them. The whole architecture is a record of catastrophes absorbed. It has a grammar for loss. What it does not have is a grammar for water. Not because water is beyond it — nothing is beyond a thing that eats rock — but because water has never once come, and so there has never been a wrong tower to teach the others.

The power turns this over for what would be, if it kept time the way the beetles keep it, a long while.

It cannot make the being right about rain. Rightness given from outside is the joint that never bears its own weight. But it can, perhaps, make the being wrong a little sooner. Wrong once, small, in one place, on one afternoon, centuries before the real wrongness comes. Not a warning — there is nothing here that can be warned. A grain. One grain of the kind the being already knows how to carry.

So it does the only thing it does here that could be called doing.

Far out toward the edge of the shield, where the haze begins, on an afternoon like every other afternoon, one of the tall pale clouds assembles over one street of one district and darkens and throws its dry lightning down onto the chimneys and then, instead of dissolving, hesitates. And from the base of it, onto one gallery and one buttress and the lower flank of one collared column, there fall perhaps forty drops. Then the cloud is gone, as clouds here have always been gone, and the sun is on the wet, and the wet is already going.

But for a quarter of an hour, in one place, a wall was soft. A buttress slumped an inch and a soldier came to hold a breach that was not a breach but a giving-way, and could not hold it, because there was nothing to hold against. Workers came behind and found the mixture they had made now unmade, and carried it away in mouthfuls as they carry everything, and laid it again as the sun dried it, and it took. The scent-road across the gallery floor was washed to nothing along a hand's length and the returning foragers stopped there, antennae reading a blank, and then one crossed anyway and laid a new line, and the rest followed. The beetles on the buttress walked over the damp and stopped and walked on.

That is all. The power does not stay to see if it took. It does not know whether, generations on, the workers of that street will chew the hawk's white salt into their mixture a little more than their neighbors do, or angle a chimney's cap, or build the rebuilt galleries along a seam a finger's width higher than the old, or whether nothing will change at all and the only trace will be one beetle, forty years from now, that hesitates at a certain spot on a certain buttress for no reason it could know. It does not know if, when the real rain comes, there will be one district on the whole shield that goes soft and does not go to slurry, and stands wrecked but standing, and is the seed. It thinks it is possible. It is not certain. Wisdom, it has found, is mostly the knowledge of how small a thing to do, and then the willingness not to check.

It has one more thing to do, and this one is not for the being.

It goes up the highest chimney to where the hawk sits, and it sits there too, in whatever way it sits, and it looks. From here the forest can be seen as a forest. The air is going up off the towers in a single warm exhalation that will be a cloud by afternoon. The columns creak, faintly, giving up the night's water. Somewhere out in the green a column is about to flower — it can feel the cup at the summit tightening toward its one dark opening — and it decides it will stay for that, for the white cups and the gray moths that live for two nights and the dun birds carrying yellow dust from one summit to the next without knowing what they carry.

It has met many beings. It has not often met one that was whole without knowing it was whole. Everything it has known that was this complete has had, somewhere in it, an eye turned back on itself, a place where the being said *I*, and the *I* was where the trouble started. This one has no such place. It has never needed one. It is spread across a warm floor to a horizon that is a haze and it is one thing and it has never said so, and the power sits on the chimney and lets that be a lesson to it, because the wise are the ones who can still be taught.

When the flowers have closed it goes. It leaves no track. Nothing here does. The hawk, which has not moved, falls forward off the chimney without a sound, and far below in the shadow of a rosette a grazer's ears go down.
