It notices the chamber the way the chamber noticed you: by load.

Something is sitting on the warm wood. Something has been sitting long enough that a slick of resin has come up out of the grain toward it, and somewhere in the root a pressure has been entered in the only ledger the root keeps, and the answer to pressure is being drafted — a thickening, a lip, the first hair's breadth of a hollow that in a thousand years would be a seat, and after that a pocket, and after that a slot with something in it. That is the first thing the power learns, before it has learned anything else about the place. Attention is weight. Whatever is used, thickens. It cannot look at this room without the room beginning, very slowly, to build a place for it.

So it does not settle. It does what the water does — arrives everywhere, thinly, stays nowhere long enough to pool. It goes along the conduits with the sap. It goes down the fibers with the charge and feels the beetles feel it coming. It goes out through the walls into the dead tubes where the hunters lie with their eyes open in the dark, and beyond them into the root, which is everything, which is a country, node after node each humming on its own count. It goes up.

It sees the tree.

The tree is a thing the chamber has no organ for and never will. Standing in the light with the wind in it, wide as a parish, older than any word for old, doing the thing the chamber would call idle exhalation — and it is not idle. Every drop that sweats from the vault was rain on those leaves once. The tree is already telling the chamber it exists, all the time, in the only language the chamber can hear, and the chamber has taken the telling and made a schedule of it and a country of pools and a price for crossing between them. Nothing needs adding to that conversation. It has been going on since before there was a floor.

The power comes back down. Now it knows what the room is a room *in*. Now it can ask the question it came to ask, which is the only question a benevolent thing ever really asks: is anything here in pain that could be otherwise?

---

It looks at the gaskets first, because if anything is suffering it is the ones who cannot leave.

It looks for a long time — a gland's whole cycle, then another, then a hundred, which is nothing to it and a season to the small ring on the wrist-thick conduit. Clench, release. Clench, release. The downy lining takes up the seep. The body thins where the wood is close and swells where it falls away. The power looks for the thing it knows how to recognize in a life pressed against a limit: the strain toward elsewhere, the part of the animal that is pulling against the fit. It does not find it. There is nothing in the gasket pointed anywhere but at the gap. The clench is its weather. You do not free a thing from its weather; you change the sky, and then the thing under the sky is a different thing, and the old one is gone. The power could unmake every seal in the chamber into an animal that wanted to leave. It would be killing them. It would also be opening every seam at once.

It looks at the gray ones, the hunted. It watches a body picked out of the storm, the falling one, the one that landed where the trough was deepest — watches it go down and the flood close over the place. It weighs the hunters. Three, maybe four. They have been three or four for a very long time. The power can see, from where it stands in the walls, what the hunters are *for*, which is not a thing the hunters know: they are why the pools stay pools. Every flood, the basins run into one basin and the pale shapes rise to cross, and the price of crossing is that some of them are on bare wood when the water goes, and the hunters come, and so the crossing is rare, and so the east fringe stays red and the north fringe stays long, and so when a crack opens under one basin and it empties there are still others, different enough to be a hedge. Take the hunters out and you have one pool. One pool is one crack from nothing. The power has seen enough dry basins in enough dark places to know what a mercy costs when it is paid at the wrong scale.

It stops sorting the residents into the suffering and the spared. The place will not hold that sorting any better than it held the other one.

---

It looks at the dry basin with the fan of scratches in its floor.

This is the thing in the room that hurts, if anything does. Not the animals — they are past it. The room. A perfect hollow, polished, a mat gone to crust around the rim, a crack in the bottom that took the water faster than the vault could put it back. And the marks — parallel, fine, the last thing something did there. The chamber keeps its memory in beads: things that fit are thickened over and kept. It has no way to keep what didn't. What dries and dies on bare wood leaves scratches, and the scratches wear, and then there is a smooth hollow and no one to say it was ever full.

The power could fill it. That would be easy. Water is the easiest thing in the world to move. But the crack is a road now — it has been draining for long enough that something down there has grown to expect the trickle, some seep-feeder in the deep root that this chamber will never see and that is as much a resident of the tree as anything in this room. Close the crack, and that dries instead. And a basin refilled from outside is not a basin; it is a gland with the power for a gasket. The place would build around the gift. In a hundred years the water would come from the vault again the way it always did, and there would be a shape in the wood the size of whatever the power had been while it was pouring, and something would move in to fill it, and the power would be the thing that has to keep coming back or the seam leaks.

So it does not fill the basin. It takes the scratches.

Not away — nothing is moved. It reads them. It holds the fan of fine parallel lines the way the conduits hold their beads, exactly, in the grain of itself. The chamber cannot remember this, so something else will. That is a thing a power can do that a root cannot, and it costs the room nothing, and it is the closest the power comes to an act of pure grief. It will carry the scratches out with it. Somewhere the last pale shape that lay in that hollow and dragged its small back feet across the drying wood will be a thing that happened, in a mind large enough to have room for it, and the hollow in the floor can go on being smooth.

---

Then it asks the last question, the one after *is anything wrong*: is anything about to be?

And there is. On the great west conduit, the one that receives, there is a seal as long as a forearm laid in a circle, gone the exact amber of the wood around it, and it is old. The power can feel it the way the beetles feel the front of a surge — a thinning, a slack in the clamp, a tide or two of life left in it. When it goes the join will open. The gland will work badly. The flow will stutter. That is not the danger; that has happened here a thousand times and every time a grain of something has landed in the leak and stayed. The danger is the thing the chamber has no defense against because it has never needed one: that the grain doesn't come. That a thousand of its kind ride through in the fur and the gut and the loose column and every one of them is swept on west and none falls, and the stutter runs long, and the pressure behind the gland finds the next-weakest seam, and the next, and the room that cannot move one thread without moving all of them moves all of them at once.

This is where the power's heart pulls. Here is a thing it could stop. Here is a death — not of an animal, of the fit itself — that turns on nothing but luck, and luck is a thing a power has in its hands.

It considers being the seal. Just once, just for the length of time it takes a grain to come. It would work. It would also be a slot, and the slot would be the shape of the power, and something would grow into it. It considers taking a grain from the tide and placing it. That is a hand in the room, and a hand is a load, and the wood would rise toward the hand.

What it does is smaller than either.

It waits for the seal to die. It does not prevent that; the seal is the room's and the death is the room's. It waits through the tide that follows — the east gland letting go, the sheet and the pack and the storm, the drumming, the drops shaken loose from the vault. It waits for the flood to reach the west wall, where the gland is working badly now and a thread of sap is running down the conduit and pooling and stinking. It watches a thousand grains go by. And where the air over the leak is moving — where the wingbeat of some gray body would have carried one particular grain a finger's width too far west — it does not add anything. It only lets the air be still there. For the length of a breath, in a place the size of a fingernail, the wind that a storm of small bodies makes does not blow.

The grain falls. It lands in the slick. It stops.

Nobody knows. Nothing in the chamber can know; there is no organ for it. Tomorrow — the chamber's tomorrow, a thousand tides — there will be a new seal on the west conduit, exactly as thick as the gap, and behind it in the wood a hard amber bead where the old one was, and if the room could speak it would say what it has always said: *here it leaked, and something came*. It would be right. The power has done nothing the room could not have done for itself. It has only made sure that the thing the room has always done, it did this once as well. A kindness indistinguishable from the ordinary. That is the only kind that does not leave a mark.

---

It lifts.

Not all at once — thinly, the way it came, drawing back along the fibers and out through the dead tubes and up the conduits with the sap into the root and the root into the country and the country into the tree. Behind it the slick of resin under the sitter stops rising. The pressure comes off the ledger. Whatever the root had begun to draft, it lets wither, the way it lets everything wither where the strain has gone.

There is one more thing, and it is not done in the chamber at all.

The tree stands in the light. The power stands in the light with it. Up here it can act without a groove forming, because up here there is no floor that thickens under use — only wind, and weather, and the long slow traffic of the surface, where things forget what pressed on them. If there is a dryness coming in a hundred years it will meet the power first, in the shape of a cloud that turned. If there is an axe it will find the path to this tree longer than it looked. The chamber will never know it has a roof. It will only know what it has always known: the vault sweats, the drop falls, the ring spreads, the pool fills. That is the tree talking. The power has decided to see to it that the tree keeps talking. It is the one intervention that is not a slot, because the room has already grown around it — has been grown around it since the beginning — and a benevolence that takes the shape of what was always there is the only one the room can hold.

The power does not go far. It does not sit anywhere. It has learned that much from the wood.

---

Down in the amber dark a gasket on the north wall tightens and goes the color of weak tea. The beetles let go of the fiber and drop, crackling, and right themselves, and start back up by the long way. In the wall-pockets the gray heads stop all together. The pale shapes in the basins feel the floor begin to drum and sink, fringes folding, into the hand-deep cold.

On the west conduit a new ring, still pale, still small, clamps a little late and a little weak on a count it did not choose and will keep for the rest of its life. Nothing leaks. Nothing much. Enough.

Here it comes again.
