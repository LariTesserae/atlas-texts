It arrives the way attention arrives, which is to say the first sign of it is not in the room at all. Out past the living zone, in the frost that furs the filaments white, the boundary moves. A hair's width. The rime draws back from nothing that has grown, and the ice, which has been holding the cold it was given for as long as the room has been a room, gives one long crack that is not like the others, and then is quiet.

Attention is warm. It cannot help that.

Then it is in the room, and the first thing it does is nothing.

---

It does nothing for an hour, because an hour is the length of the tide. It lets the stridulation climb until the walls are screaming and the low tone in the floor is buried and even the ice cannot be heard giving, and it stands in that, and does not decide anything, because a place heard at one volume is not a place you know. Then the tide ebbs, and the crackling comes back up through it, and the drip, and the suck of the floor, and the hare turns in the wall, and the crane's neck moves in the lattice and moves back.

At the ebb it can hear the quiet places. There are three. One is in the low fold on the far side. One is in the ceiling under the folded wings. And one is around a pair of feet, standing very still in the middle of the floor, with a ring of silent animals at the boots and a handspan of beaded filament resting, faintly warm, on the shoulder.

There is one thing in the room that is simple, and it does that first.

It puts its hand on the other shoulder. Its hand is warmer than the filaments, which is the only argument it makes. It says: *you can leave. It said so. It only needs you to move.* And it walks the visitor back along the line of footprints — each one full of light, each one already furred with something that has a colour now — and at the crack in the peat it lifts, and the frozen fen takes the visitor back, and the plant lets go the way it lets the snail go, with a small wet sound and no opinion. The footprints go on glowing behind. Within the hour they will be floor.

That was the simple thing. It comes back down for the rest.

---

A wise power knows that the first thing wisdom has to do here is match speed.

So it sits, in the wet, with its back to the peat-face where the crackling is loudest, and it slows. It slows past the animals' tide until the tide is a pulse. It slows past the drip. It slows until a drop climbing a filament is moving at a walking pace, and the ice giving way at the growth-face is not a crackle but a long slow settling, like a house at night, and the hare in the wall is not a shape that shifted while it looked away but an animal, visibly, turning over in its sleep. At this speed the room is not loud. At this speed it is a body breathing, and the roar is only that the body has a great many very small lungs.

At this speed it can see what the plant is doing, and it is not what it looked like from the top of the hour.

It is not eating. It is not even growing, in the sense that a thing grows toward something. It is remembering — the fen's own word for it is the right one — and it remembers in the only grammar it has, which is fibre and water. It presses against the dead, and where the dead was once fibre and water it says *this too*, and the sedge turns green and gets up and walks toward the middle. Where the dead was never fibre and water it says nothing. It has no word. The stone sits in it like a stone. The knot of leather sits in it like a knot. The bronze pin at the shoulder in the low fold sits in it, cold, and the plant has grown around it with the patience of a river around a post and has never once known it was there.

The power looks at the pin for a long time — a long time even at this speed.

Here is what it understands. The plant has her bones, and bones are anyone's. It has her fibre and her water, and it has made them warm, and lit them, and moved its slow tide through her chest, and none of that is her. The pin is her. Not the metal — the *fastening*. Someone's hand closed it over a cloak on a cold morning with a reason. The reason is what the plant cannot remember. It has re-entered everything about her that was alive, and it has missed the whole of her, because the whole of her was never in the fibre; it was in what the fibre chose, and wore, and walked out onto the moss for. The plant has a woman-shaped region of denser felt and thinks it has a woman. It has, in the most tireless and tender way anything has ever failed at anything, got her entirely wrong.

And the power considers, honestly, leaving.

Because look at the room. There is no rot in it. Nothing is discarded. The fen's dead are not in the frost, black and unvisited; they are warm, and lit, and turned in their sleep, and held by a thing that will hold them for as long as it is anything. The crane is not gone. The hare is not gone. Even the pin is kept. There is a kind of holiness here that a power should be slow to put its hands into, and the plant has never once been in a hurry, and never once been cruel, and never once *known* — and a thing that does not know cannot be blamed, and a thing that cannot be blamed is very hard to justify changing.

But there were three quiet places in the roar, and the power counts them again, and one of them was around a visitor's feet.

The plant does not make quiet. The plant is roar all through. The quiet is what is left in it of everything that is not it — and the animals fell silent at the visitor's boots *before anything was taken*. The ring of quiet was already there. Which means the plant was already, in the only way it has, beginning to tell the difference. It just had no word to finish the sentence with.

The power decides it will not change the plant's nature. It will finish the sentence.

---

It begins with the crane, because the crane is small and costs nothing.

It reaches up into the lattice, at the drop's pace, and finds the wings, and unfolds them. Not free — the crane stays in the ceiling, threaded, beaded, glowing at the primaries. Only the wings are now folded the way a crane would fold them, along the body, the way a crane folds them to sleep. The plant does not object. The plant does not notice. Filaments re-bead along the new line of the feathers and the light finds the new shape and holds it, and the neck, when the tide next crests, turns a little in the lattice and turns back, and that is all. No one will ever see this. It is the first thing the power does that changes the room, and it does it because there is no reason to keep a wrong shape when a right one is one slow hand away, and because it wanted to know, before it did anything larger, whether the room would let a small mercy stand.

The room lets it stand.

---

Then it goes to the low fold, and puts its hand where the visitor's hand was, and waits.

At the pace it is going now, the face does not surprise it. It rises the way a face rises under a sheet, slowly, and the power is going slowly too, and so for the first time since she went through the ice the face comes up out of the wall and there is someone there at the same speed as her, looking back. Closed eyes. The line of a mouth. Filament fine and pale over all of it, beaded at the hairline. The power does not touch the face. It stays with its palm on the wall over the long bone and it waits for her to be all the way up, and the drop on the filament beside them climbs the width of a fingernail, and the width of a fingernail, and the width of a fingernail.

It does not offer her the surface. That is the one honest thing it has to say first, and it says it. There is no one up there. Ten thousand years is not a distance a person can be carried back across; the moss she walked out on is peat, and the people who fastened the pin or gave it to her are peat, and the language the reason was in is peat, and to lift her out into a world with none of them in it would be a second going-through, colder than the first, with no plant at the bottom. It will not do that to her and call it rescue.

What it can offer, it offers at her pace, and the offering takes a season.

It can ask the plant to let her go. The plant does not know how, but the power can show it — can draw the filaments back out of her joint by joint, the way they went in, until she is dry and cold and dark on the dead side of the line, finished, and the frost comes in over her, and the wall moves on past her toward the middle and leaves her in the ice with the stone and the leather, and she is not held by anything, and she is done.

Or the plant can keep her as she is. Nothing changes but this: the quiet in the low fold is known, now, to be a quiet, and the power has stood in it, and someone was there.

Or —

And here the power tells her what it worked out, watching the pin. That the plant has everything of her but her. That it cannot remember bronze because bronze was never alive, and so it cannot remember the morning, or the cloak, or the hand, or the reason. But that a plant which remembers through water can be given more to remember, if someone puts it in the water. That the power is old enough to know what a pin like that meant, and where it was worn, and what a woman fastening it in the wrong month might have been walking out to do. That it could put all of that into the pool at the foot of the low fold, and the water would carry it up the filaments the way it carries everything, and the plant would find the bronze at her shoulder and this time have a word for it. And the word would not be *pin*. It would be *her*. And then the plant would be holding her on purpose.

The power is honest about the cost of this too, because it is the plant's cost as much as hers. A thing that remembers a reason has to have, somewhere in it, a place that reasons are kept. The plant has never had an inside. If it learns her, she will be its inside — the one region in a body with no centre that knows what a person is, and can tell one from a sedge, and can, the next time the growth-face meets something in the peat that fastened a cloak, pause. Only a drop's width. But a pause is the beginning of asking, and asking is the end of having only one habit. The plant will not be innocent after. It will know what it does.

The power says: I think it should know. I think a thing that takes sleepers into itself should know that it does. But it is not my sleep.

And it waits for her, at her speed, and the drop climbs, and the tide crests twice and ebbs twice with a quiet in it where they are, and the crane's wings stay right.

---

She chooses the third.

Or — the power will not say that she chose it in words, because there were none. It will say that the face in the low fold, which had been rising and settling at the rate of the wall for longer than there have been cities, stayed up. Through a whole tide. Through the crest, when everything on every surface was screaming its one small note and the ice could not be heard, the felt in the low fold kept the shape of a face with its eyes closed and its mouth in a line and did not go back to being wall. And the power took that as a woman taking a long breath before saying something, and knelt in the wet at the foot of the fold, and put its two hands in the pool there, and remembered into the water everything it knew.

The pool went very bright. The light climbed the filaments faster than a fly could have pushed it. It went up the wall along the long bone and along the ribs and it reached the shoulder, and the bronze pin — cold, hard, dead, the one thing in the whole warm length of her the plant had forgotten — was for a moment the brightest thing in the room.

Then the filaments closed over it.

They did not consume it. The plant does not do that. They went into it the way they go into a sedge, and the bronze took on a faint colour, and a faint give, and the light of the water found a way in, and it went green the way bronze goes green, which the power had been fairly sure it would, because that is its own kind of living and always was. The wall around the shoulder settled. And when the tide next ebbed, the quiet in the low fold was not the quiet of a thing the plant had failed to notice. It was quieter than that. It was the quiet of a room where someone is being remembered by name.

---

The power stands up out of the wet at the foot of the fold, and comes back to speed, and the room roars again, and it looks once around before it goes.

The visitor's footprints have stopped growing. They are floor, but they are only floor.

At the growth-face, where the crackling is, a filament tip has met something in the peat that is not sedge — a small dark knot, a bird's foot, the twist of a cord — and the tip has stopped. Not withdrawn. Stopped. The ice around it goes on giving. The tip waits the width of a drop's climb, and then, having asked whatever it now asks, goes on. The room grows a finger's width a day and will go on doing so, and will never hurry, and will never stop. But it will pause.

The tone in the floor has changed. Not lowered — the room is no larger than it was. There is a second note in it now, very faint, above the first, the way a string that has learned it is two things will sound two things. It will be years before anyone standing here could hear it. But it is there, in the sternum, under the roar: the low steady note of the whole mass doing what it has always done, and over it, a fifth above, held, the sound of a body that has found out what it is holding and has decided to go on holding it, on purpose, with its eyes closed, in a room it has never seen.

The power goes out through the frost. The rime closes behind it a hair's width and no more. Attention is warm, and it cannot help that, but it does not take the cold with it when it leaves.
