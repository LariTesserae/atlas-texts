**What the Power Does**

It arrives the way such things arrive, which is not from any direction. There is no descent through the lid. Nothing on the shelf registers a coming. One moment the country is unregarded and the next it is held in a regard so wide and so patient that it has no edge—the way the shelf itself has no edge—and the felt does not shiver, and the pistons do not miss a beat, because nothing has happened yet.

And the power understands, before it understands anything else, that this is the last moment in which nothing has happened.

Because it has read the ground the way you did, only faster and further down. It knows what the products are and what attention does to them. Here, to look is to vote; to fix a thing in regard is to decide it; to decide it is to hand it to the shelf for revision. Whatever the power looks at, it will alter by looking. There is no neutral view of this country. A benevolence that arrived here with its eyes open would begin doing something—perhaps harm, perhaps not, but something—before it had drawn breath to consider. So the first thing the power does is close its eyes. Not withdraw. Close them. Hold the whole shelf in the periphery, as a warmth, a hum, a shape under the lid, and look directly at nothing.

Then it thinks.

---

It considers, first, what it could give, and it sets each thing down as it picks it up.

Stability. It could reach into the warm rock and still the cycles—let fractures stay fractures, hollows stay hollows, let every product keep the form it had at the moment of stilling. The country would at last be describable. But the many-legged things would be counted. The long damp creature would be caught mid-turn, coat half scales, and would stay there, a body made of the middle of a gesture. The felt-runners would starve within the hour, having nothing to feed on but a floor that had stopped changing its mind. The watchers would stop completing; they would be halfway to rock forever, hides granular, faces never settled. Stability here would be an extinction dressed as a kindness. Set down.

Memory. It could give the shelf a record—make the rock keep its old fractures the way strata keep theirs, make the hollow remember the water. But the shelf already has a memory, and the memory is alive, and it is exactly as large as the shelf has managed to keep it: a handful of forms that learned to stay. What is a watcher for, if the rock remembers on its own? The power would be installing a history and making the historians redundant in the same motion. Set down.

Completion. It could look at the great leaning things in the felt-lined passage and decide them—choose the shoulder over the buttress, agree on the number of parts in the hand. It could make the world the shelf seems to be trying to make. But the power notices that it has leapt to *trying*. The visitor thought so and then unthought it, standing in the passage with the lid pressing down. The power is wiser than the visitor and can therefore afford to be less sure. And even if the shelf is trying: a world finished by a hand from outside is a world with a hand in it, and the shelf has made hands, and considered them, and set them back into the felt. It would be arriving at the one place where arrival has already been declined.

Rescue. This is the one that costs it. Everywhere under the lid, in every second, something that was almost a thing stops being it and is taken back, and no record is kept, and the felt converges. The power feels each withdrawal—it is built to; that is what benevolence is made of, the feeling of each one. It could catch them. Hold them elsewhere, in some country where the ground extends the courtesy of tomorrow. But a form on the shelf is a strategy for being on the shelf. Lifted off it, a beetle-stone is a fist-sized ambiguity with nothing to be ambiguous against, a wave saved from the water. And the runners would go hungry. Set down, with grief, and the grief kept.

Last, and quietly: company. The shelf produces for no one. The power could be the one it produces for. It could stand under the lid and receive—be the audience the works have never had. And this, it understands, is the most dangerous gift of all, because an audience is nothing but attention, and attention is the vote. To be the one the shelf is *for* would be to decide, by watching, what the shelf became. The power will not be that. It will not let the country find out that it has been, all this time, for someone. Set down.

---

So the power arrives at what the wise usually arrive at, which is very little, chosen very carefully.

It opens its eyes and looks only at what is already gone.

This is possible for such a power. The withdrawn do not go anywhere you could visit, but the power is not you. It can regard the place in the felt where a beetle-stone lay and see the beetle-stone, and the stone, and the curl of wet leaf, all three, the way they were before the shift behind anyone's eyes had to choose. It can see the fern that closed and every fern that closed before it. It can see the trunks that were two and then three and the one of the three that stopped being. It looks at all of it—the whole ledger of the shelf's second thoughts, back to the moment the rock came up glassy and still skinned with heat—and its regard changes none of it, because none of it is there to change. The shelf cannot revise what it has already taken back. Attention paid to the gone is the one attention that is not a vote.

So the power becomes the record. Not in the rock; it puts nothing on the shelf. It simply holds, outside, everything the shelf has let go. The gnat-sized things the runners ate in the interval between form and rock. Every flicker of the cluster by the vent, every member that became a leaf. Every shoulder that became a buttress in the passage. The hand.

It looks at the hand for a long time. Five parts, a splay, a gesture of grip or offering, the parts not agreeing on their number, the felt already at the wrist. It does not restore it. It does not decide what the number was. It keeps the hand exactly as undecided as it was in the instant before it went, so that somewhere, in a regard that will never revise, the shelf's one attempt at a hand is still being attempted, and always will be.

This costs the shelf nothing and changes nothing on it, and the power thinks that this is probably the whole of what it should do.

---

There is one exception. It comes from the heart and not from the reasoning, and the power lets it, and knows that it is letting it.

The long damp creature. Salamander gait, mole coat, the bird in the set of the head, turning. It has survived by outrunning attention, and it has been running since the felt first spread across the new rock, and it is—the visitor saw this and did not know what to do with it—the only thing here that looks tired. Every other strategy on the shelf is a kind of rest. Hold still. Be too small to ask about. Be many. Be the current. Become the rock. This one found the strategy that never rests, and has been faithful to it longer than anything else has been faithful to anything, and no one has ever seen it whole, because there has never been a regard fast enough to land on all of it at once.

The power is fast enough. For one moment it is exactly as fast as the creature—not faster; it does not catch it, does not fix it—and in that moment it sees the whole of it as one form: the salamander and the mole and the bird and the scales and the shrug and the turning-away, not in sequence but together, a single animal whose body happens to be a motion. Under that regard the creature is, for the first time since the rock cooled, completely itself. It does not stop. It cannot; stopping belongs to the many-legged, and this is not that. But for the length of the regard it does not have to be ahead of anything, because there is nothing behind it that has not already seen it whole. It runs, and is not fleeing. That is all the power gives it. That is all.

Then the power looks away, before the shelf can notice that something has been decided, and the creature turns, and the coat becomes scales, and it moves off into the understory a little differently. The same speed, the visitor would have said. The visitor was not looking closely. It is a hair slower. It has been seen. It can spend, now, a fraction less of itself on not being seen.

Whether that makes it safer on the shelf or less safe, the power does not know. A hair slower may be the hair that lets revision land. That is the risk one takes when one acts from the heart in a country where attention is a vote, and the power took it with its eyes open, and it will hold whatever comes of it the way it holds the hand.

---

The crown.

The power has kept it in the periphery this whole time—the skin of the shelf, the one unrevised thing, living where nothing from below ever looks. It cannot regard the crown directly without deciding it. But it becomes aware, toward the end, that the crown has leaned.

Not toward a vent. Toward the regard. The canopy has bunched, very slightly, on the side where the power's attention is warmest, and flattened opposite, as if a weight had shifted; the broad leaves have turned up toward the lid in the attitude they take toward falling fog. The crown does not know what the power is. It knows only that something has come from above that it might drink.

The power gives it nothing to drink. But it lets the lean stand. It does not pull its attention away so quickly that the canopy falls back and shivers. It stays a while, a warmth under the lid, and lets the crown lean toward it, and thinks: *this is the one thing here that has never been looked at, and I have not looked at it, and it knows I am here anyway.* If anything on the shelf understands what the shelf is doing, it is this, and it is not saying, and the power does not ask. Two things that will not decide each other, leaning, for a while. That will have to be enough for both of them.

---

Then the power goes, the way it came, from no direction.

It leaves no mark. The felt does not close over anything, because there is nothing to close over. The pistons lift and sink. The basins turn. The watchers follow the water with the slow turn of a head, hides a shade more granular than they were an hour ago, which they would have been regardless.

But the power has decided one last thing, and it is this: it will come back.

Not often. It cannot yet say how often, because it has not decided whether its returns should have a rhythm, and it suspects they must not. Everything on the shelf that repeats becomes a cycle, and every cycle becomes part of the works, and the power would become a piston—a slow rise and fall of regard, out of phase with the others, that the felt learns to shiver toward and the cluster learns to gather under. It is not sure it wants to be part of the machine. It is not sure it would be wrong to be. It is not sure the machine would not, in time, make something of it, warm and undecided and lying in the felt, and revise it, and take it back.

So it leaves that open, which is, it reflects, the local custom.

And it carries away the ledger of everything the shelf has let go, and one creature's moment of wholeness, and the lean of the crown, and it holds them the way the watchers hold the turning of the water: not to do anything with them. So that somewhere they are not forgotten. So that the country has, in one place outside itself, a history—which it did not ask for, and cannot know about, and would not know what to do with, and which costs it nothing at all.

That, the power thinks, is what benevolence comes to, here. To be the memory of a place that forgets, without ever making it remember. To keep the hand.
