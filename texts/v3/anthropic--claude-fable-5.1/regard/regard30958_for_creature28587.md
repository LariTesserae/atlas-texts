It comes down from the rim, because that is the only way in, and because the rim is where every judgment about this place has ever been made, and it knows that.

From the rim it can see what the builders cannot: the curve, the ribs, the form coming up out of its own foundations. It can see further than that, if it chooses. It can see the pattern the kiln-lights make, and the pattern under that, and it can hear the whole basin as one sound the way the old ears hear one courtyard. It stands there a long time — a long time by its measure, which is not the builders' — and then it does the thing that matters, which is that it stops looking and goes down.

---

Twenty steps and the ground becomes wall. It puts a hand out. The clay is soft under the thumbnail. The block is wet.

It goes down in the dark, and it lets the dark be dark; it does not bring any light with it, not even the little it could carry in a palm. This is the first decision and it is barely a decision. Light here would be the cruelest thing it could do. The eyes that have given up their irises to the dark would flinch and water and the child who stared would be right and the old ear in the angle of the courtyard would have nothing to be right about. Sight would not add to what they have. It would replace it, and what it replaced would not come back.

So it goes down blind, or as near to blind as it cares to be, trailing one hand along the stone the way they do, and before long there is a warmth ahead in the seam and a hand on its shoulder, then the upper arm, then briefly the side of the neck.

*Who.*

It has no call. It has never needed one. Somewhere a very long way from here it has names in the plural, and none of them can be sung in two notes. The hand on its neck waits — the pulse, the tendons, *whether they are tired* — and finds something, whatever it finds, and does not let go.

Then the builder sings. Rising two-note, the interval a little wide, the second note held. Sings it again. Sings it a third time, into the ear, in the dark, the way you sing to something that cannot yet walk.

It sings it back.

That is the second decision, and it is the important one. It could have gone through the basin as a presence, as weather, as the thing in the cloud. It could have watched from inside the stone. Instead it is given a call by someone whose face it will never see, and it takes the call, and now it can be spoken of. It is *here* in the only way *here* is said in this place.

---

It listens for a generation.

This costs it nothing, and it does not hurry. It stands in the seams with its hand on the wall and it hears the asking — knock, pause, knock — and after a while it can hear what the old ears hear, the true joint and the rocking one and the dead ring of a hidden flaw. It can hear more than they can. It can hear the flaw before the hammer finds it. It says nothing. The asking is the thing. If the stone did not need to be asked there would be no reason for the hands.

It hears the relay. The stutter that means *the wall is moving*, coming round the basin in its variants, and it can hear — this it can do that they cannot — the throats those variants came from. Not the words. The shape of the voice. It is like hearing a rope and knowing three of the people in it, except that it can hear all of them, back and back, the sound worn smooth by each mouth it passed through. Some of the calls in the courtyards are very old. Older than the deepest hammer. It does not tell anyone this. They know it already, in the way they know it, which is that they do not think about it.

It hears the water. That takes the longest. Under the knocking and the calling and the dragging and the hiss of the sledges in the grooves, hardly a sound at all. Down through the joints toward whatever the stone is standing on.

---

While it listens it does a few small things, and it does them the way the water does things, so that nobody can point to where they came from.

Someone loses a hammer in the dark. It is a dark cobble worn to one particular palm and it goes down a shaft or into a clay-pit or simply into the black between two courses where no hand will ever pass, and the grief is real; the hammer was a grandparent's and the grandparent's before, and the fit of it was the fit of all those hands. The next morning, or the one after, the hammer is on the bed of the course where the builder is working, exactly where a hand reaching for a wedge would close on it. The builder says the wall gave it back. This is true enough.

Someone falls. A pier gives, or a rope, or a foot goes into a groove full of water in the wrong place at the wrong height. The stutter goes out and around. Most who fall are found; the seams are close, bodies are always near. But sometimes a body goes where the seams narrow to nothing, and the falling call is given and nobody answers, and the worst thing in the basin is not the death — the death is quiet, like everything — but the not-being-carried. A body no one reaches does not go into the wall. It does not become a pick. It is the one thing here that ends.

So it finds them. Every one. It goes where the seams narrow to nothing and it puts its hands on what is there and it carries, and the falling call it gives is its own two-note held a little long, and someone hears it and comes, and takes the weight, and the body goes to the open bed and the clay goes round it and the block goes on and rings true. That is all. It does not catch anyone. It does not make the walls stand truer than the hands can make them. If nobody fell, nobody would listen for the rocking block, and the whole valley would stop asking, and the asking is the thing. But it can promise that whoever falls will be found, and it can keep the promise without anyone knowing it was made.

That is the extent of what it does above. Two things, and both of them are only *returning*.

---

Here is what it does not do, and it thinks about each of them, because it is wise and wisdom is mostly the accounting of what you did not do.

It does not tell them what the form is.

It knows, or it could know; it has stood on the rim and seen the curve and the ribs and it could stand there longer and see the rest. But *toward what* is a question the rim asks. In the seams there is no toward. There is the block, and the bed, and the pitch, and the person beside you. If it came down into a courtyard and said *I have seen what you are raising*, it would hand them the rim. It would make them into people who had been told. And whatever the form is — a throat, an ear, a hand cupped around something, a roof closing — it is being raised by people who eat what it weeps and lay their dead in its joints, and if that is not already an understanding of it, then no sentence will be one.

It does not stop the bones.

There are places from which the picks would look like a horror. It has been to those places. It has heard the arguments. It puts a hand on a pick — a long bone, cut and pointed, warm from a palm — and it hears the builder under her breath giving the call of the one whose bone it was, softly, the way you talk to someone standing beside you, and it has nothing to say to that. The dead here are not gone. They are the tools. They are the ropes. They are the walls that weep and the sound that comes round the courtyard in a voice nobody living owns. A people who kept their dead in a place apart would be a people who had lost something, and it will not give them the loss.

It does not feed them more. They are thin. The curd is lime and nothing. It could put something in the water that would put flesh on the wrist and years on the life and nobody would ever know. It considers this for a long time, hand in a groove, cold water running over the knuckles. It decides no, and it is the decision it is least sure of. They are not hungry. Nobody here is hungry. They stand between one block and the next and eat from their fingers and go back to the block, and a fed people who did not know they had been fed would be a people carrying a secret they had not been given the chance to refuse. It would rather they be thin and be theirs.

It does not send anything living down through the cloud. The birds are enough. A bird is a wonder because it is a bird and because it dies; if it lived, it would be a pet, and there would be a person it belonged to, and *belonged to* is not a thing that has been said here yet and it will not be the one to say it.

---

And so, after a generation of listening, it has done two small things and refused six large ones, and it might go back up to the rim. It does not go back up to the rim.

Because under all of it there is the water, and the water goes down.

The builders have two answers and a shrug. From below, or from the heart of the stone. It has listened long enough to know that the second answer is true and does not matter and the first answer is true and does. The deepest floors are always being unpicked to feed the high ones. There is no quarry but the basin itself. The form comes up out of its own foundations, and *out of* means what it means: what is raised is taken from what it is raised on.

They do not think about this, and they are right not to. But it can think about it. That is the one thing it is for.

So it goes down.

Not through a shaft. The shafts are theirs — *not for anything, just to see* — and it leaves them. It goes down through the stone, which it can do, course by course, and the joints turn from clay to lime to something harder than lime, and the blocks get larger and stranger and less shaped by any hand it would recognise, and it puts its palms on them and reads the grain. It can tell where a block was quarried by the grain. It can tell whether the last hand to work it was left-handed. It can tell how long it has been out of its bed by how much water it has given up.

These blocks were quarried nowhere. These blocks have been in their beds a very long time. And the hands that worked them —

It finds a bone in a joint, laid there as if placed. Too long. Too heavy. Not shaped quite right for a hand.

It knows what a builder would say. *Earlier somebodies. Larger.* Said without any particular wonder, the way you would say a grandparent was taller.

It puts its palm on the bone and it hears — this it can do — the shape of the voice that bone belonged to. Not words. A two-note call, rising, the interval a little wide, the second note held. Sung back once, in the dark, to a hand on the side of the neck.

---

It understands then what it is going to do, and it understands that it is not the first to understand it.

The basin has drawn attention before. Something wise and benevolent has stood on the rim before and seen the curve and the ribs and gone down instead of looking, and been given a call, and listened for a generation, and returned a hammer and carried a body, and refused the light and the telling and the food, and then heard the water going down and followed it. And at the bottom it found the courses of whatever had come before it, and it found the bones in the joints, and it understood.

You do not save this place by raising it. They raise it. Their hands, their ear, their asking. You cannot add a course above without taking the course from them, and the course is the whole of what they are.

You save it by being what it stands on.

So it goes to the deepest floor it can reach, where the water gathers before it finds its way down, and it lays a block. Not a block from above — it will not unpick anything. A block from wherever it is from, dressed rough, the size it needs to be for hands that size. It seats the block and taps it and listens. The pitch is true. It lays another beside it and packs the joint. It is very slow, and there is no hurry, because there is no end.

Nobody above hears this. The knocking of one hammer at the bottom of everything, under the water, under the courses no one alive could lift — it does not carry. But the water finds the new joints and stands on them, and goes down through them a little slower, and the curd in the troughs is not thicker but it is not thinner, and the deep floors are unpicked and there are still deep floors, and the form comes up a little higher tomorrow out of foundations that have quietly gotten deeper by exactly as much as was taken.

It is being *raised*. It does not ask toward what. It has become the other answer to where the water comes from — the one that was always true and always didn't matter.

---

Its call fades from the walls in time. It has not been heard above for a long while, and a relay that never stops being made will nonetheless let a single voice go, eventually, if the voice does not come back round. There is a child born in the angle of a courtyard, passed hand to hand up a stair. Someone who was very old when the visitor was new, whose hands have gone stiff and who does nothing now but listen to the joints go in, has the child on their knee for a moment and sings into its ear a two-note rising call, the interval a little wide, the second note held. Sings it three times. The child sings it back.

*Carrying*, the old ones say.

And far below, under the water, a block is seated and rings true, and there is no one to hear it, and it does not need to be heard. The stutter comes round the basin above in a voice nobody living owns. Someone taps a block. It rings. The ground under all of it is sound, because something is holding it up, and has decided to.

Someone answers.
