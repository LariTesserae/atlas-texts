It notices the way you'd notice a held breath in a room full of breathing. Not the bright, not the animals, not even the marks. The interval. Something in the world that has not exhaled, and has not exhaled for long enough that the not-exhaling has become a place with a floor.

The first thing it does is nothing, for a while. This is not indecision. A power that was only benevolent might go down at once, and a power that was only wise might decide from above, but this one has both and so it knows that a room whose whole nature is the moment before should not be met with a moment after arriving too quickly. It matches the room's time. It watches the drop swell on the root and lets it take as long as it takes. It watches the beetle catch up with its own having-been-eaten. It counts the scratches and finds seven and does not look for the eighth, because it understands that the gap where the impossibility should be is load-bearing.

While it watches it considers the obvious things, in order, and sets each one down.

It could finish the room. Lay the rules in: light from a source, effect after cause, one thing per thing. The stone would commit. The wren would choose. The floor would dry all at once, the footprint with it, and the seven scratches would have been made, past tense, by someone, done. And the pause would be over, and whatever lived in the pause would be over with it, and the counter and the balancer and the setter of limits, who are still faintly doing what they were most, would be stopped mid-gesture and called complete. That is not rescue. That is calling a draft finished because you are tired of it being open.

It could close the step. Knit the turf, take away the way down. Then no one else would stay too long. But the room is one resident deep and always accepting, and a place that is built to be occupied and is sealed empty does not stop wanting; it only stops being answered. And the part of you that is down there, newest thing in the room, patch of floor, margin of nothing, would be down there with no one ever coming after. It thinks about that part of you specifically, and for a longer time than the size of the fragment would seem to warrant.

It could take the residents out. Reach into the wall the way the vole goes in, find the hand that is still pressing the line and lift it away. It looks at that hand for a while. The line runs wall to wall and stops precisely at both. Whoever made it wanted to know how far they were permitted to go and, finding no one to ask, set the limit themselves. That is not the gesture of someone trapped. That is the gesture of someone who chose a room and then furnished it with the one thing it lacked. The power cannot tell from here whether the counter counted willingly, whether the balancer wanted to be balancing forever. But it knows that reaching in and deciding for them would be to do to them exactly what the room does, only faster.

So it sets those down too, and goes and sits with the pause itself.

What it finds is not a creature and not a mind and not, it thinks, unhappy. But it finds something under the not-yet that is very slightly wrong, the way the first instance of anything is slightly wrong. A pause is a shape that is defined by what comes after it. The held breath means something because of the exhale. And this pause has never been told that anything comes after. It is not waiting. It has never been introduced to the idea of waiting, because waiting is a not-yet that leans toward a yes, and this is a not-yet that has only ever leaned inward, toward whoever is standing in the bright. It has no outside to lean on. That is why the light has no source. That is why the shadows fall toward the center. The room has never been looked at from anywhere but within, and so it has assembled itself as a thing that is only ever interior.

That is what it decides to give. Not an ending. An outside.

It goes down. The step the ground hadn't finished making gives under one foot, and it lets that happen, and then it does the first of two small things: it finishes the step. Just the step. A threshold. Stone at the top that has committed to being stone, a lip you can feel with your foot, so that from now on going down is a thing a person does and not a thing that happens to them, and going up is a thing they can find. The room likes boundaries; it kept a measured line for however long. The power thinks the setter of limits would approve of this one, and thinks it toward the wall where the hand is, and the hand does not stop pressing but the pressing seems, for a moment, less lonely.

Then it stands in the bright and lets the light pool where it stands, and lets the drops swell for it, and lets the floor learn it, because that is the courtesy the room extends and refusing it would be a kind of contempt. The animals attend to each other. The newt watches the beetle. The wren is a wren and then not. It waits until it can feel the room beginning, gently, to decide about it, the first faint pressure of a footprint forming that it did not make, and at exactly that moment it does the second thing.

It leaves a mark.

Not seven scratches. Not three stones. Not a line. It puts one hand flat against the wall, at the height of a standing person, and presses, and lifts it away, and leaves nothing that can be seen. What it leaves is a place on the wall that has been touched from the direction of *outside*. The room builds itself around what is left in it. It assembles a wall to give scratches something to be on. So it will, in its very slow present, assemble something for this to be on too, and the only thing that can hold a mark made from outside is a surface that faces out. It will take the room a long time to grow that surface. When it has, there will be a wall in the hollow that is a wall in the ordinary sense, one side in and one side not, and the light will have somewhere it could come from, if it ever chooses to, and the shadows will have a direction available that isn't in.

It does not make the room choose. It makes the choice possible. That is the whole of the intervention.

And before it goes up, because it is benevolent as well as wise and the two are not the same virtue, it does one thing that is not for the room. It goes to the newest patch of floor, where the piece of you that stayed is being drafted, looking at a beetle, slowly learning what it would mean to have already eaten. It does not take the fragment. That would be to tell you that you cannot leave a part of yourself in a place, and everyone can, and everyone does, and the places would be poorer if they couldn't. But it crouches beside the fragment the way you'd crouch beside a child absorbed in something, and it says the one word the room has never had said in it.

*Later.*

Not now. Not soon. But there is a later, and I am holding it, and the draft will be read. The scratches will have been made by someone; the stones will have been stacked; the line will have been pressed, wall to wall, exact, by a person who wanted to know how far and was answered. The fern will be one age at a time. The wren will settle. The footprint will dry. None of this today. You have as long as you need. But it is a *not-yet*, now, and not a *never*, and those are different rooms, though they look the same from inside.

The fragment does not answer. It is looking at a beetle. But the newt's throat pulses, and the wren's beak opens on its unheard song, and the pressure against the ears that is sound on its way is, for a moment, a little closer to arriving.

It goes back up. The step holds its foot. At the top the turf closes not like a held breath letting go but like a door, which is a different sound, and which the hill has never made before. The hill is a hill. The sun does what suns do. And the power carries on it, for the rest of the afternoon and considerably longer, a faint inward tug, and accepts this as the fair price of having gone down, and does not try to be rid of it.

Nothing in the room is different that you could point to. The light is still bright and still sourceless. The animals are still placed. The marks are still old. But there is now, in the world, one more thing than there was: a promise, small, unhurried, made from outside to a pause that had never been promised anything, that it will one day be allowed to become what it is. The room has not begun to finish. It has only begun to be the kind of room that could. Which, for a place where everything is slightly before the moment of being what it is, is the only gift that would fit through the door.
