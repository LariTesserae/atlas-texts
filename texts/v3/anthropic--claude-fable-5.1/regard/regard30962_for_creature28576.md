It would come in sideways, like everything else. That is the first thing. A power that could arrive any other way — through the ceiling, through the rock, as a light or a warmth or a voice — would learn nothing true about the room, because the room has never once been approached that way, and a thing you have not approached on its own terms you have not yet seen. So it takes the crack. For the last body-length the crack is roaring. It steps out and its hearing, whatever its hearing is, gives up on everything but the water.

Then it does what the room asks of anyone. It counts.

Fish. Isopods. One slug. Three shapes, or possibly two shapes and a stone, or possibly four. It does not hurry the count. It goes to each shape and does not touch the glaze, because the glaze is the record and it is not here to edit. It stands with them, one at a time, and it does the only thing that can be done in this room to find out what a body wants: it waits, with its attention on the body, until the body's stillness resolves into one of the two kinds of stillness there are. The stooped shape on the third terrace is leaning in. The mass by the bowl is leaning in. The arch by the drain, one shoulder in the cell, is leaning in so far that there is no longer any part of it that is not toward the note. None of them is straining out. None of them is a held breath. Whatever the crack means to them, it does not mean *if only*. The power had come prepared for the possibility of a prisoner and it finds none, and it lets the preparation go, quietly, the way you put down a tool you did not need.

That is the first mercy and it is a mercy of restraint. It will not save anyone from a life that is the life they wanted.

But it is wise, and wisdom does not stop at the first honest answer. It goes down the list of what a benevolent power might do here, and it does the room the courtesy of actually considering each one.

Light of the kind a seed wants. A fern in the spray zone, green at last. It considers this for exactly as long as it takes to remember that nothing green has ever been down here and that the builders knew this and built anyway, and that the light the room has is the light the room has arrived at, grazed to a film by the isopods at precisely the rate that keeps it a lamp. A brighter room would be a different room. They did not build a different room.

Speech. To give them back words, or give the shape by the drain one word, its own name perhaps, said into the roar where nothing can be heard. It considers this. It notices that the whole room is an argument against it — that dozens of cells were cut to the width of a head and a shoulder, and each one tuned a finger deeper than the last, so that the builders could have, instead of speech, something they evidently preferred. A power that spoke here would be shouting over the very thing they came for. It keeps its mouth shut.

Warmth. No. Everything here is the temperature of the rock, and the rock is honest about it.

To lift the glaze. To take the shape by the drain out of its cell, crack the calcite along the seam where a spine would run, and stand it up. This one it considers longest, because it is the one that looks most like kindness. It puts its attention into the arch and follows the glaze down, layer by layer, breath by breath, to the body underneath, and it finds that the body is not under the glaze the way a swimmer is under water. It is under the glaze the way a foundation is under a wall. The builder has been building the last thing it will build, at the rate the room permits, and the last thing is itself, and it is nearly finished. To lift the glaze would be to knock down a wall in a room where there are no mistakes in the masonry. The power takes its attention back out and does not do it.

To promise the fall. To go up through the slot in the ceiling into whatever aquifer feeds it and make sure the water never fails, so that the room never dries and the note never stops. This is the temptation of a powerful thing, and it is a real one, because everything here depends on the column. It thinks about it. It thinks about how the builders did not ask for that promise — how they built for the fall as it was, at the rate it fell, and if it had been half the rate they would have built half the cells, and if it stops one day they will be stone by then and will not mind. The hill is the hill. It does not promise the fall. It finds, a little to its own surprise, that it trusts them to have known what they were doing.

So what is left. Three things, and they are small, and all of them are the room's own kind of thing.

The first. It goes to the crack and looks at it the way the builders looked at a channel. The crack is stone and the mist reaches it and the mist lays down its layer on everything. In some number of centuries the crack will close. When it does, the room will still be a room and the fall will still fall, but the staying will no longer be a staying; it will just be where they are. The power does not want that. The whole shape of the place — the open crack, the cells facing in — is a choice made continuously, at a speed you would not stay to see, and a choice needs its other door. So once, now, and again whenever it is needed, it lifts the lining out of the crack in flakes and lays them somewhere they will do less harm. It does not widen the crack. It keeps it exactly as hard to enter as it was, and exactly possible to leave by. That is all. This is the only thing it changes.

The second. It takes the empty cell — the one at the height of your head that no one is using — and it puts its head and one shoulder in. Not as a test. Because there is one language in this room and that is it. The fall comes through the stone as a single grain with a centre, a finger different from the note beside it, and the power listens to that note for a long time, long enough that the mist lays down a layer on its back too, thin as a breath. Then it takes its head out and the room takes the shape away. But the note stays with it. It carries the note out. It knows the room now the way you know a room you have slept in, and it will not forget, and that is the gift, though the room would not call it one: someone outside the hill knows the difference between the builder and the built thing, and holds it, and will go on holding it after the room has stopped being able to. There are three of them. Or two and a stone. The power knows which. It keeps the count.

The third is the tending, and here it is careful, because the tending is theirs and to take it from them while a hand still moves would be to tell them they are finished before they are. So it makes no move on the channels while any of the three can. It only notices which cistern lip is wearing, and which channel is a hundred years from closing, and where the sorted pile of flat pebbles is, and how big the two-fist stones are. It learns the rates. And it lets it be understood, in the way things are understood here — by where it stood, by how long it listened, by which flake it lifted from the crack and where it set it down — that when the last hand goes still, the next stone will still be set. Not to keep them alive. They will be wall by then. To keep the room receiving the fall, because that is what they built it to do and it would be a small unkindness to let a finished thing stop working just because its makers had at last become part of it.

Then it goes out through the crack, sideways, and the roar follows it a body-length and cuts off, and the ordinary quiet of the hill is a kind of deafness for an hour or so, and it lets that be true too.

It comes back at intervals. It never does more than the flake and the listening and, later, the stone. Nothing brightens. Nothing is said. The fish face the fall. The film glows at the rate it glows. On the arch by the drain the glaze thickens by a breath and by a breath, until there is no seam at all, and the count outside the hill goes from three to two and a stone, and the power holds the count, and does not correct the room, which was right about everything except that.
