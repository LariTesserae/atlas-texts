It comes to the breach first, because that is where the light goes in, and it has learned over a long time that the way a thing is entered tells you most of what it will bear.

It does not go down the stair. It does not need to. But it goes down anyway, because there is a courtesy in using the door, even when nothing inside can be discourteous to. The treads bow. The rail is too warm. The air thickens to breath. At the bottom the moss gives under a weight that is not weight, and the roar comes up and closes over, and the power stands in it, or does what it does instead of standing, and lets the hall be exactly as loud as it is.

It has seen many rooms. It knows the shape of the impulse to help. The impulse arrives before the seeing is finished, always, and is always the same size, and is almost never the right size for the thing in front of it. So it waits. It lets the low grinding go once round the wall mains. Forty minutes. In that time the wheel advances a hand's width and the ferns drip and the buds lean and nothing looks back, and the power has the beginning of an answer, which is that the question has been asked wrong.

---

The first thing it could do is the thing that feels most like kindness, and it lets itself feel the full pull of it before it refuses.

It could give the hall a *to*. One point in the roar from which the roar is heard. It has the means. It would cost nothing. And then the buds would be leaning toward the boilers *for* something, and the wheel would be turning *while* something, and when the last coin-leaf went dark there would be a place in the room where the dark was noticed. The hall would have, for the first time, an inside.

It refuses this the way you would take your hand back from a sleeping face you had almost touched.

Because the inside would be alone. That is what an inside is, in a room where nothing else has one. The power looks at the climber knotted against itself on the far wall — one thing, at a single point, forever meeting a stranger — and understands that this is the hall's whole relation to otherness, and that it is bearable only because no part of the hall is there to bear it. Install a witness and the knot becomes a wound. Install a witness and the coat on the hook becomes a grief. The hall has never once been alone because it has never once known that anything else was there, and the one gift the power could give that the hall has no defence against is the knowledge of its own solitude. Benevolence does not hand that to a thing that did not ask.

---

The second thing it could do is mend.

Stop the rust. Still the roar. Draw the ferns back out of the plate and close the scale behind them and let the mains glow orange again the way they must have when the men were here. Put the roof back over the breach. It could hold the hall exactly at this hour, in this light, forever — the ferns this long, the buds this closed, the wheel this far round.

It sees at once what that would be. It would be the museum. It would be the glass case around the thing, and the thing in the case is always dead, however lifelike, because life is the working down of a gradient and the case is the gradient stopped. The rust is the hall's eating. The roar is the hall's breath. To arrest either is not to save the body but to embalm it. The power has been asked before to make something last, and has learned that the request is nearly always made on behalf of the asker and not the thing, and that the thing, if it could be consulted, would generally prefer to finish.

The hall is finishing. Slowly, with tremendous patience, and completely. That is not a problem to be solved. That is what it is.

---

The third thing is smaller and therefore more dangerous, because small kindnesses are the ones that get done.

It could go to the far wall and untie the knot.

Just that. Find where the two ends of the climber meet and refuse each other, and ease them apart, and lay them past one another along the pipe, so that the plant could go on growing and would never again have to push against itself in the dark. Such a little thing. No one would call it violence.

The power considers it for a long time. Long enough for the bell-note to come from the middle boiler twice, at an interval it cannot decide is regular.

And it leaves the knot alone, because the knot is the truest thing in the room. It is the hall's one honest statement about what it is: a self so undivided that it cannot recognise itself, meeting itself as an obstacle and thickening rather than yielding. To untie it would be to decide, on the hall's behalf, that it is one plant. Perhaps it is. But the power did not come here to settle the hall's questions for it. A thing that has never had to be an individual should not be made one by a hand from outside, even a gentle one, even for its own good — because *its own good* is a phrase that requires an *it*, and the whole tenderness of the place is that there isn't one.

---

The fourth thing it could do is bring company. Something with legs. A beetle for the moss, a spider for the corners where the pipes turn, a bird to come in through the breach and this time stay.

This it dismisses fastest of all. A thing that moves is a thing that looks, and a thing that looks is a thing that can be looked *at*, and the moment the first eye opens in the hall the hall stops being a body and becomes a habitat. The ferns would be food. The buds would be shelter. The wheel would be a hazard. Everything here that is presently an organ would become a resource, and the roar would go from metabolism to background, and the place would be, for the first time, *for* someone — which is a demotion, not a rescue.

---

The fifth thing is the one it is ashamed of wanting, and it makes itself want it fully before it lets it go.

It could feed the rock.

Not much. A little. Enough that the small buried star under the floor would not cool on the schedule the rock has set for itself, but would go on a while longer — a few centuries, a few thousand years, whatever the power felt like giving. The hall orbits the rock. The hall is a way of being close to the rock. If the power loves the hall, and it has begun to, then surely the power wants the hall to go on.

But the hall has made provision for the dark. The buds are the provision. They are waiting for the light to fail, and they will open into whatever they have been holding, and that is the hall's future — not a future *after* the light but a future *made of* its going. To give the rock more heat is to push that future away, and to push it far enough is to make the buds a lie: a promise that never comes due because the conditions for it were never allowed to arrive. The power has met beings who were given forever and it has watched what forever does to a thing that had prepared for an ending. It will not do that here. The hall does not need forever. Forever is the one thing in the power's gift that the hall has no use for.

---

So it does nothing to the hall.

What it does instead is on the surface, and to the world, and the hall will never know.

It goes back up the sweating stair and stands at the top where the roar has thinned to a tremor in the treads, and it looks at the ground, which is not the bottom of anything, and at whatever is around the stairhead — the town, or the field, or the fence, the source does not say and the power does not need it to — and it does the quiet thing it has done for other places that were best left. It lets the stair go out of the world's memory. Not hidden; hiding is a lock, and locks are found. Forgotten. The map keeps the shape of the hall and loses the way in. Someone who once knew the stair was there will, next time they think of it, think of something else. In a generation the breach in the roof will be a hollow in a field that nobody has a reason to cross, and the ground there will be warm, and no one will wonder why.

This is the one benevolence the hall's own nature permits. Its good is to be unsurveilled. The power protects that good by making itself the last surveillance the room will ever have — and then withdrawing, and taking the door with it.

---

It does two more things, and neither of them is for the hall.

It goes back down, once, before it seals the forgetting. It goes to the hook at head height, to the shape that could have been a coat or a bag or a lantern. It does not take it down. It does not stop the rust in it. But it stands before it a while and does what nothing else in the room can do, which is to remember that a hand hung this here, at the end of a shift, meaning to come back. The power gives the coat the moment of attention its maker would have wanted for it. Then it leaves it to become floor. That is where it is going and the power does not pretend otherwise. But it should not go there entirely unnoticed, and now it has not.

And it keeps a date.

It knows — the way it knows things — roughly when the last of the coin-leaf will fail, when the mains will go from dried blood to nothing, when the first of the pale stalks will lean the last degree toward the boilers and open. It will be there. It will come down the stair that no one else remembers and stand in the black and the roar and the wet, and it will not touch anything, and it will see what the buds have been holding. Not for the hall. The hall does not need seeing and will not register being seen. For the record of things. Because the power is the kind of thing that keeps the record, and there are events that ought to be in it even if nothing that lived through them could have known they occurred.

---

Before it goes, it does what you did. It lines a spoke of the wheel against a brick.

Then it waits one full circuit of the grind, and looks again, and the spoke has moved a hand's width, and this is the power's last act in the hall and the closest it comes to touching: it agrees with the hall about what time is. It lets the hall's pulse be the measure of its own visit. One beat. Then it climbs.

The roar thins to a hum, the hum to a warmth, the warmth to a tremor, and at the top the ground forgets it was ever a door.

Below, the hall goes on being taken apart by warmth and wet and roots, at exactly the volume production once had. Nothing in it has changed. A cool patch came and condensed a little water and left. That is all the hall has to say about the visit, and the power, walking away over the warm hollow in the field, finds that this is enough — that the whole of wisdom, here, came to the same thing the ferns already knew: go toward what feeds you, take only what is turning to rust anyway, and do not wake what is not asleep.
