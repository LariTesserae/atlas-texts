It comes from above, because that is where attention comes from here. The snow is attention of a kind — the surface world, whatever is left of it, sending down what it no longer needs. This is not that. This falls slower than the snow and is not lifted by the chimney. It settles over the town the way the fur settled: evenly, without preference, on the boiler house and the dead sheds alike, on the bollard, on the rope, on the round window and the face that passes behind it.

And then it does what you did, and what took you so long. It looks.

---

Wisdom, down here, is mostly patience. There is nothing in the town that moves fast enough to reward a quick eye, and everything that matters was hidden in plain sight for the length of a long looking — the worn nap, the clear glass, the two bare places on the valve. A lesser power would have seen a fish. A hasty one would have seen a prisoner. This one waits until it sees a keeper, and then waits longer, because it knows that seeing what a thing is comes before knowing what it needs, and that the second is not the first.

So it watches the round. Path, wall, needles. Lattice, valve, knock, wait, knock. Border. Window. It watches it many times. It watches the shape come in from the drop-off and the keeper go still, and the needles drift, and the catching-up afterward. It watches the brinicles gain half a hand's width and the keeper stand at the border looking at them the way one looks at a stain on a ceiling.

It counts, as you did, and arrives at the same three lives. It does not stop there either.

---

Then it does the thing that wisdom does that kindness alone would not: it holds each mercy up to the rust-light and looks for what is wrong with it.

*Take it up.* The rope is there. The hands are there. Lift the keeper to whatever the surface is now — air, or ice, or a wider water with light in it — and let it stand up straight for the first time in longer than it can remember. And what stands up straight is a body built for a quarter-inch of heat leaking through a brick wall, in a pressure it has made its own, breathing whatever it breathes at a rate matched to the flywheel. The rounding did not happen to it; it *is* it now. You cannot pull the nail out without taking the tree, and there is no one on the surface waiting for a tree. The power sets this down.

*Stop the works.* Let the flywheel run down and the hum fade and the gauges go dark, gently, over a long time, so that the keeper leaning on the wall feels the warmth go out of it like a tide and follows. A quiet end to a keeping that outlasted its reason. Except that it did not outlast its reason. The reason is in the room. The reason is the needles, and the valve, and the ice held a yard off. To stop the works is to say the round was for nothing — and the power has watched the round too many times to say that. It sets this down.

*Fix it.* Restore the works. Let all the sheds hum, let the warm quarter swell to the wall, shatter every brinicle at once, bring the town back to what it was for. And the keeper stands in the middle of a place that no longer needs it, holding a valve that no longer needs turning, knowing the numbers for gauges that no longer drift. The power sets this down with particular care, because this is the one that felt most like love.

*Company.* The legged thing on the wall — bring them together, if they were ever two. Let the wall-walker cross the open ground it never crosses. But the power has been watching that one too, and it sees a round there, sagging but unbroken, three sides, walked. Two keepings that do not touch are not one loneliness. They are two completed shapes. It would not know which to break.

---

What is left, when the great mercies are set down, is the small ones. This is often what is left.

The power looks up.

Not at the surface. At the pipe-lattice, and above it, where the brine comes from — the fractured tank, the surface floe, the wound in the world. The keeper looks at the brinicles and knows what they mean about the roof and knows it cannot reach the roof. This has been the shape of its dread for longer than the fur has been growing: a thing advancing, held off by a body's worth of warmth and nothing else, from a source it cannot see and could not touch if it saw.

The power can reach the roof. It is the one thing in the whole valley it can do that the keeper cannot, and that changes nothing the keeper does.

So it goes up, and finds the wound, and closes it. Not all at once — the town does not do anything all at once, and a sudden thing would be a kind of noise. Slowly, over what would be a season if seasons were counted, the threads of brine thin, and thin, and stop. The brinicles that are already grown stay grown. The paving they have taken stays iced. The white fingers hang from the lattice, finished. But they do not lengthen. The border, which has shifted for as long as there has been a border, stops shifting, a yard from the boiler house wall, exactly where the warmth was always able to hold it.

The keeper comes to the border on its round. It stands. It looks at how far the ice has come since the last time.

It has not come any further.

It stands longer than usual. Then it goes back to the window.

It comes again, the next round, and the next. The ice has not come any further. The power does not know — nobody knows — what passes in the keeper when a dread it has carried for a generation turns out to have a floor. It does not change the round. The round was never about the ice. But the standing at the border grows shorter, round by round, the way the standing at a healed thing does, until it is only a glance, and then it is only a place the path goes past.

---

The second small thing is the rope.

The power does not pull it. It does not shake it, or shorten it, or move the bollard, or do anything that would make the keeper look at what it has decided not to look at. It goes up the rope instead — all the way, past where the dark takes it, to the far end that is tethered to nothing anyone here remembers.

And finds that it is, in fact, tethered to nothing. The boat is gone, or was never there, or has become a lump of growth like the float. The rope's end drifts.

So it ties it. To what, exactly, you could not say from below; the power's business is above, and it is not the kind of thing that describes itself. Something fixed. Something that will hold weight, in a place where there is light enough to see by. It does not make a surface where there was none. It only makes true a thing the keeper has been treating as true for a long time — that the rope goes somewhere. That the door it has decided not to open is a door.

Then it comes back down, and does not look at the rope, with great precision, so that the rope is left as the keeper has left it: not on the round.

The rope, when the current runs, moves very slightly differently now. It has a tension it did not have. Whether the keeper ever notices is not the power's business, and it does not stay to find out.

---

The third thing is not a thing it does. It is a thing it does not fail to do.

Before it leaves, it goes into the boiler house — as you did, through the gap where a window was — and it does not touch the dark patch of wall, and does not wipe the gauges, and does not lean. It only reads the needles. It learns where each one should sit, and how far each may drift, and which knock comes first. It learns the numbers.

Whatever they measure is being measured. You noticed that sentence. It implied a reader, and the reader was the keeper, and that was the end of it: one who knows the numbers, in the whole drowned valley, in the whole cold, and when that one stops the numbers are not known by anyone and were therefore, in the way that matters, never known at all.

Now two know them. The keeper does not know that two know them. The power does not tell it. But it leaves carrying the numbers, and it will not lose them, and that means that the keeping is itself kept — that somewhere, in a place with light enough to see by, there is a ledger with the drift of every needle in a boiler house at the bottom of a cold valley, and a hand that could, if the round ever stopped, take it up. It will not need to. That is not the point. The point is the same one the keeper understood when it decided the machinery need not stop: that a thing which is watched does not end when the watcher does, so long as the watching is passed on.

---

And then it goes. Not up — down, toward the drop-off, because it has learned the town well enough by now to know that things which come and go here come and go from below, and that the keeper has a way of waiting for those, and resuming after.

It passes the boiler house and slows near the warmth. You think it slows.

Behind the round window, the pale shape has gone still.

It does not touch anything. It goes on, and out, and down into the blackness the rust-light does not enter, and the town is left with the ice not growing and the rope holding and the numbers known in two places, and none of it looks any different from the drop-off, in the snow.

After a long time the face passes the window. The path is crossed. The valve knocks twice, catching up.

---

You will have noticed what the power did not do. It did not make itself known. It did not ask. There is a kind of wisdom that would have — that would have stood in the doorway of the pump house and said, in whatever way such things are said, *I see you, and what you are doing, and it is enough* — and perhaps that would have been the better mercy, and perhaps it would have been the one noise the town could not absorb. The power thought about it for the length of a long looking and set it down, not because it was wrong, but because the keeper had already been seen, once, by you, and had not stopped; and the shape from the drop-off had been coming once a season for longer than anyone counted, and slowing near the warmth, and going on; and the power began to wonder, as you did about the tree and the nail, whether it was the first of its kind to arrive here and find that the only thing wisdom had left to do was to close a wound the keeper could not reach, and tie off a rope the keeper would not climb, and learn the numbers, and leave.

It did not stay to find out. That was not on the round either.
