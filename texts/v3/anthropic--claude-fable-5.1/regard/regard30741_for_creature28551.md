**What the power did**

It came the way the swifts come, because that is how such things travel: on the wind, fast, with the whole eastern sky behind it and somewhere to be. It was not looking for the Notch. Nothing is. It was passing through the door at the top of the world along with everything else that passes through, and it would have gone over the western rim and out into the void and thought no more of it than a swift does, except that the horn was singing.

A note that goes nowhere. In a place where everything is going somewhere, that is what catches. The power turned in the air the way the hunter turns, and came back through the gap against the wind, and found a ledge on the western horn among the whitewash, and settled there. The choughs shifted along the rock to make room and did not stop shouting. It was not the first thing to arrive out of the cloud and sit on the western horn, and they did not treat it as such.

It stayed. This was the first wise thing, and it was hardly a decision. The place has a tempo, and the tempo is slow under the roar, and anything that means to understand a slow thing must first stop being a fast one. So the power sat through a day — the amber light, the boiling cloud, the midday rents, the rain, the drip, the dark — and then another, and then it stopped counting, because counting is a way of being on one's way.

---

**What it saw**

It saw what has been described, and it saw a little more, because it could see through cloud and it could see the way slate has been worn by feet.

It saw that the eight steps were not the end of the route but the top of it, and it followed the route down with its attention: the foot-wide ledge, and below the ledge a seam in the outer face where the seepage had eaten a groove, and the groove descending in a long shallow slant into the white, and at the bottom of the white — a long way down, where the second cloud-floor lay — a shelf. A dry shelf under a great overhang on the mountain's outer flank, above the tree-line, below the frost, with a second bundle of grass in a second niche and a second body-shaped hollow in the floor. Not a home. A base. The place where the thing that climbs waits out the days the horn is silent.

And it saw the thing itself. It was lying on the shelf with the patience of a stone, and it was smaller than a person and greyer than the rock, and it was breathing.

The power watched it for a long time and made no sound and cast no shadow, and it understood the following.

The being was not young. It was not suffering; the cool had gone into it years ago and become its temperature. It was not waiting for anything it could name; it was simply waiting, which is different, and when the wind rose enough that the note came down the mountain to it — faint, a pressure more than a sound, felt in the shelf under its back — it did not hurry. It let the note establish itself. It let it hold for a while, as if to be sure. Then it rolled onto its hands and feet and began to climb, and the climb took the better part of a day, and it did not once look up to see how far there was to go.

It had done this, the power judged from the polish on the groove, some hundreds of times. It would do it some tens more.

---

**What it considered**

A benevolent power that has watched a creature for long enough will start to want things for it. This is what benevolence is. The wisdom is in what happens next.

It wanted, first, to warm the box. The stone shelter is a cold place to lie for as long as the wind holds, and a power like this one could make the slate hold heat the way a hearthstone does, and no one would know. It considered this for the length of a whole climb and then set it down. The bundle in the niche is dry because a body has warmed it, over and over, for years. That dryness is the record. It is the only writing in the Notch, and it says: *something came back.* Warm the box and the record becomes a fact about the box instead of a fact about the creature. The power would be erasing the one thing it most admired in order to be kind.

It wanted to speak. Not much — a word, a tone, something to let the being know that its listening had been witnessed. It considered this for less time. The being has heard one voice its whole life and has organized its whole life around answering it. A second voice, however gentle, would make the first one *a* voice, one among others. It would introduce comparison into a place that has none. The power was not so sure of its own worth as to think that trade a good one.

It wanted, more shamefully, to be *known*. To be seen on the western horn, to be understood as having come. It recognized this want as belonging to the fast things, the things that pass through and want to have passed through *visibly*, and it let the want blow over the rim with the swifts.

It wanted to secure the succession. This one was old. There is only one at a time. When this one lay down on its shelf for the last time, the note would sing to nobody, and that thought was very hard for a benevolent thing to hold. It could find another — a small grey patient creature somewhere in the ranges, and set its feet on the groove, and see that it arrived. It considered this longest of all. And what it came to was this: the frog. One at a time is how the hollow keeps a frog, and the hollow does not go looking for frogs. Whether one comes is the world's business. A listener sent is not a listener; it is a keeper, and the note has never had a keeper and does not want one. The being on the shelf was not sent. That is the whole of its dignity. To arrange a successor would be to turn a chosen thing into a maintained one, and the power had seen enough maintained things to know how quickly they stop being alive.

So it would not do that.

It wanted, finally, to know whether the voice knew. Whether the horn, or the wind in the horn, or whatever it is that a sustained note *is*, had any sense of having been heard. The power was wise enough to know that it could not know this and that the being did not need to. The being lies with its ear to the rock and does not ask. The power decided it would not be less than the being.

---

**What it did**

Three things, or one thing with three faces.

It went and looked at the ledge. The foot-wide ledge below the eight steps, with the groove beneath it going down into the white. Slate flakes. Seepage works into the joints and freezes some nights, even here, and levers the layers apart a hair at a time, and the ledge was a flake, and the flake was going. Not this year, probably. But the being was going too, and the two were in a race whose outcome the power could see and the being could not: there was a real chance that the last climb would not be the last by choice. That the wind would rise and the note would come down the mountain and the small grey thing would roll over and climb, and somewhere on the way up, in cloud, the foot-wide ledge would simply not be there.

The power held the ledge.

This was the intervention, and it was a small one, and the power did it after long thought and with something like reluctance. It did not shore the ledge or widen it or dry it. It reached into the joints of the slate and asked them to keep their grip for a while longer — not forever; not against the mountain's own eventual mind; only for as long as this particular climber climbed. Then let go. The being would end on its shelf, or in the box with its ear to the rock, or in the groove on a slow way down, in its own time and by its own weight. It would not end because a stone gave way in the white on a day it had chosen to answer. That was the distinction the power drew, and it drew it carefully: not *that this should not end*, but *that it should not end wrong*.

The second face of the same act: it did not confine its asking to the ledge. All the way down the groove to the shelf and, more faintly, along the seam beyond the shelf where the route came up from wherever such creatures come from, the power asked the mountain to stay climbable. Not easy. Not marked. Only *possible* — the way a door propped open is possible. It would send no one through. But it would not, having seen this, let the door swing shut behind the last one out of mere geology. If another small grey thing ever came up out of the cloud looking for a note that goes nowhere, the way would be there, unswept and unlit, exactly as hard as it had always been. That was as far as benevolence could go without becoming management, and the power stopped exactly there.

And the third thing, which was not an act, or not one that touched anything.

It waited until the being had gone back down. It waited for the evening when the wind dropped and the horn went silent and the silence was briefly appalling, and it stayed on the western horn among the roosting choughs, and it stayed through the night. And in the black hour before the level light, the wind came up again unexpectedly, and the slot in the eastern horn began to speak — long, unwavering, below a voice and above a heartbeat — and there was nobody in the box.

The power heard the note unheard.

This is a thing the being cannot know and has never known. Every time it has heard the note it has been there, hearing it. It has no idea what the sound is like when it sings to an empty room. The power, from the far horn, learned this, and it was not the power's to keep or to give. It was simply the one thing in the Notch that only a visitor could receive. The power did not go and lie in the box. One at a time. It listened where the choughs listen, from the wall, half here, and when the light came level through the gap and lit the cloud amber from the inside, it let the wind take it over the western rim like everything else.

---

**Afterwards**

Nothing in the Notch was different. The choughs shifted back along their ledge. The rodents whistled at a shadow that was a cloud. The frog, or a frog, clicked once in the sponge at the saddle's lowest point and then not again for long enough that you would forget you had heard it.

On the outer face, a foot-wide flake of slate that should have been loosening held its grip a little longer than the frost would have liked. Nobody saw. The being on its shelf, when the note came down the mountain again some weeks later, rolled onto its hands and feet and climbed, and put its weight on the ledge without a thought, and went up the eight steps into the room, and steered by the leaning post to the box, and lay down with its head to the singing rock, and stayed for as long as the wind held.

It did not know it had been watched. It did not know the ledge had been asked to hold. It did not know that the note had, for one night, sounded into an empty box and been heard anyway, from across the gap, by something passing through that had stopped. None of this was the point. The point was that a benevolent power had looked at a creature which had made itself entirely into an answer, and had found the only kindness left over was to make sure the question stayed askable — and to ask for nothing back, not even to be known as having come.

The power went west with the swifts. It was a fast thing again within the hour. But it had been slow for a while, on the western horn, and things that have been slow once do not entirely forget it. Somewhere in its travels, when it comes through a door and hears something in it that is not on its way anywhere, it turns. It waits. It has learned that there are places where the whole of wisdom is to hold one stone and leave.
