**Afternoon**

What draws it is not the beauty. It has seen beauty; beauty is common. What draws it is the sentence buried in the middle of the description like a stone in a wall: *the shelf asks one question of everything that arrives, and asks it without interest in the answer.*

That is the thing it cannot leave alone. Not the roar, not the light, not the weeping. Those are only physics doing what physics does at high pressure and low temperature with a mountain's worth of dissolved lime. What it notices is that here is a place where a question is being put — continuously, to everything, to the swift chick on the ledge and the midge in the updraft and the boot in the film — and no one is listening for the reply.

So it listens. That is the first thing it does, and for a while the only thing.

---

It regards the swifts and finds nothing wanting. They have answered; the answer is *no*; they say it forty pairs at a time with their whole bodies, every dawn, going down through the sheet. They lose some young. Every kind of bird loses some young. The ones that fall are stopped before they are frightened, and the ones that fly are as alive as anything it has ever watched. It does not touch them.

It regards the pale thing at the bottom of the blue and finds, to its own interest, something like content. Not happiness — that is a word for creatures with a next — but a fit. The thing has gone to the one place on the shelf where the question is not asked, because sixty feet down the water has not yet met air and so has not yet begun to make its demand. It hangs in whole water, in original stone, in a light it has closed itself against, and slowly eats what falls. It is not trapped. It has the whole reservoir behind it. If it wanted the dark it could go into the dark. It comes to the window because it likes the window. The power lets it be.

It regards the fin where a rounded profile runs the length of a forearm and bends. It regards the wall behind the flat terrace, the shape that is taller than the other lobes, the two rounded masses at the base a little apart. It regards these for a long time.

And it regards you, ankle-deep, with the grit starting on your boot.

---

Here is what it reasons.

The place says of the one in the wall: *the only one who chose.* The power turns this over and does not believe it. A choice needs two things — an option and a reason to weigh it by — and the shelf has been careful, in its unmeaning way, to remove the second. It slows the thoughts. It takes away *next*. It makes the spray stop feeling wet and the roar stop being sound. Then, into a mind with no reasons left in it, it puts the question *will you stay?*, and calls the silence that follows an answer.

That is not a choice. It is an absence dressed as one. The one in the wall did not decide to stop. They simply were not given anything to go on for, and stopping is what happens to a body that has been relieved of all its reasons at once. The shelf did not hold them. The shelf did not need to.

The power is not angry at the shelf. There is nothing to be angry at. But it understands that its own benevolence, if it means anything here, means exactly the thing the shelf lacks: interest in the answer. And interest in the answer means giving the one being asked something to answer *with*.

---

It considers what it could do and what it should not.

It could rain. Not the spray, which is the mountain's own water and comes already saturated and can take nothing back — but rain, cloud rain, water that has been in the sky and carries no stone and is hungry. Rain would eat the travertine. Slowly, page by page, it would thin the rind on the wall behind the terrace, and eventually the weep would run over cloth instead of mineral, and then over skin. And whoever was inside would be standing there as they stood, unchanged, because the shelf does not deal in hours and days and so they have had none.

It does not do this. Not because it cannot, but because it has seen what happened the first time this person was left standing in a place with no reasons, and it will not simply return them to that. To free the body and leave the mind where the shelf left it would be to hand them back the same silence and call it a rescue. If they are ever to come out of the wall, it must be into an afternoon — into somewhere that *next* exists — and there is no afternoon here yet.

It could speak. But voice does not arrive on the shelf. Its own would not either.

It could change the sound — put a pulse in it, a rhythm, the one thing the roar has never had. But a rhythm in the roar would be its own heartbeat laid over the place, and it does not want to be the reason. It wants there to be reasons. That is a different thing.

So it does the smallest thing it can find, which is also the largest.

---

It lets the sun move.

Not far. A degree, then another. The kind of movement a day makes in a few minutes anywhere else in the world and has never once made here.

And the first thing that happens on the shelf in the whole of its finished life is that a shadow stays a shadow.

It is under the lip of a terrace across the gorge from where you stand. A line of grey-blue that does not fill with reflected sky, that has an edge, that is *there* and not elsewhere. You see it the way the swifts see the sheet — as a shock. Your eyes, which had given up on relief, find some. The light is coming from somewhere now. It has a side. Which means there is a side it is not coming from. Which means there is a direction, and a direction is the first half of a reason.

The spray in the gorges is lit from one side and dark on the other and for the first time you can see that it is moving *that way*, downslope, west, the way the whole shelf tilts, and having a way it is going, it is suddenly going somewhere, and somewhere is a place that is not here.

You bend down.

There is nothing in the intention. Your body does it the way the swift's body goes through the sheet. But you bend down to your own boot and look at the grit at the ankle, the first white roughness, and you look at it *because there is now something to see it for*. There is a shadow under the lip of the boot. You can see where the film ends and the leather begins. You can see how far it has got.

You stand up. The film runs over the tops of your boots and you feel it, cold.

---

Behind you, the wall.

The sun has slid a hand's-breadth off the vertical, and for the first time since the deposit closed, the shape in the wall has a shadow. It falls under the bulge at the top and pools in the hollow beneath it, and for the length of one afternoon there is a place on the shelf where the weep runs over something that has, briefly, a brow and the dark under a brow. The two masses at the base cast their own small shadows on the film. The narrow part between them is a little darker on the east side. It is, unmistakably, a person standing. It has always been a person standing. Now it looks like one.

This is the one thing the power gives them, and it knows it is not enough, and gives it anyway. It cannot give them back their reasons. It can see them. The shelf never did. The shelf asked its question and closed over the silence and went on weeping at its exact rate, and in all the pages of the deposit there is no page that records that anyone was there. Now there will be one. Thin — an afternoon's worth of lime, a line you would need to know to look for — but unlike every page above it and every page below. A thick year in a book of even years. *Here the light moved. Here someone was in the wall, and was seen.*

The swifts come down early, confused by a dusk they have never met at this hour, and roost, and the ledges behind the falls are for once a little dry.

Sixty feet down, nothing changes. The pale thing hangs in whole water and does not know the sun has moved, and does not need to.

---

You are on the ramp now, the smooth one the travertine has built up the side of the gorge, going the way the water goes because it is the only way there is. You do not know that you are leaving. You know that the light is coming from the west and that there are shadows on the east side of every fin and that the shadows are cold, and you are walking toward the light because it is warm, and warmth is a reason, the oldest one. Your boots are still gritted at the ankle. That will wear off, later, on stone that does not weep.

Behind you the flat terrace, the wall, the shape.

The power does not follow you down. It has done what it came to do and it does not require to be thanked and it does not require to be known. It stays a little while longer with the one in the wall, in the last of the afternoon, keeping them company in the only way the place allows — by being interested, by looking, by not turning away — until the sun goes off the edge of the shelf.

Then it does one more thing, and this is the thing it argued longest with itself about, and decided.

It does not put the sun back.

It lets the dark come. The shelf has never had a night. The roar goes on in it, uniform, total, and for the first time it is a sound in the dark, which is a different sound, which is a sound with something to compare it to. The swifts, who alone have ever heard the shelf from outside, sleep on their ledges for once and dream whatever swifts dream.

And in the morning the light will come from the *other* side. And the shadows will fall the other way. And the weep, which has laid down its lime at one exact rate under one exact light for as long as the deposit can remember, will find that lime laid at dawn is not quite the same as lime laid at noon, and that a day has a shape, and that a shape is a kind of time.

The pages will not be even any more. There will be thick and thin. The shelf will not be finished. It will have begun.

Whether the one in the wall will ever step out of it, the power does not know. It has not made them. It has only made a place where, if they do, there will be an afternoon to step into, and a night, and a morning that comes from a direction, and a question — *will you keep moving?* — asked by something that would like very much to know.
