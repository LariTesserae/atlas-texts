What arrives is not a person at the rim, so it does not start above. It starts everywhere at once, the way the litre of dye would, and the first thing it does is the thing it is best at, which is hold still and look.

It sees what the rim-watcher saw. The lattice, the light passed hand over hand, the cup forming before the pebble. The habits condensing out of the roar and dispersing back into it. The two on the west side who will not be parted and the ladder of spans they have left behind them. The thumbnail loop in the wall of the hall. The algae. The plaza.

Then it sees something the rim did not, because it takes a certain kind of patience to see it, and the rim-watcher had a body and eventually went home.

The city lives a second in the future. Everything in the shaft has been described that way and it is true: the cup before the pebble, the street before the flow. The city has never once been in the same instant as the water it houses. It has no inside, so it cannot miss anything, and it wants nothing outward, so it cannot want company. But look at the plaza again, with that in mind. Still water. A hand's depth, ringed by low walls, nothing crossing it. A second from now the plaza will be exactly what it is now. Its future is its present. So on the plaza, and only on the plaza, being a second ahead and being here are the same thing, and the city, which is built out of anticipation, is for once anticipating nothing, because there is nothing to anticipate, and is therefore, for the width of a dinner plate, simply present.

That is what the plaza is for. It is not waiting for something to arrive or mourning something gone. It is the one place in the shaft where the builder and the built occupy the same moment. The city has made itself a room in which it is not alone. It could not have said so. It keeps nothing inside and this is the nearest thing it has to a room where nothing is kept, and what is kept there is the city's own presence, in the only water that will hold still long enough to receive it.

And on the north wall of that room, the algae. The one tenant that lives by another rule, that does not go anywhere, that is only more of itself, slowly, in place. The city has put the one thing in its volume that lives in the present at the edge of the one place where it is present. Whether that is arrangement or accident there is no telling and it does not matter. The strands are where they are.

So the power knows what it is looking at, and it considers what to do, and considers each thing in turn, because that is what wisdom is when it is not being asked to hurry.

It could part the two on the west side. Build them a bridge they could not migrate under, or give one of them a route through the deep quarter so that they never met. The city has been trying to do this for a season and the power could do it in a moment. It does not. The west side is the only place in the shaft where something is inefficient on purpose, and the city has not dissolved it, and the ladder of useless spans is the record of a builder learning, one bridge at a time, that not everything it houses wants to be housed better. That is a lesson. You do not take a lesson away from something in the middle of learning it.

It could seed the water. A rotifer, a spore, a single copepod, and in a year there would be a biology in the shaft again, and the city would have to reckon with things that go where they want and not where the current pushes. The power does not. The water was sterilised when the shaft was cut and the city has met and cupped everything that entered it since. It would meet and cup a copepod. That is not a life; that is another pebble. The algae is there because it found the one still margin and stillness is where that kind of life catches. If anything else is to live in the shaft it will have to find that margin too, and it will have to find it on its own, because a thing that is placed is a gift and a thing that arrives is a neighbour, and the city has enough of what is given to it.

It could stop the field going in. It does not. The field is going in a stone at a time, and the city has told the grass where to stop, and the grass has stopped. There is no appetite here. In a thousand years the shaft will hold a little more white than it does now and the basin will not have noticed. Leave it.

It could put something on the plaza. It considers this longest of all, because the plaza is a room made for nothing, and the power is, in a sense, a something, and it would like to be let in.

It does not put anything on the plaza.

What it does instead is this.

First it attends to the roads. They tilt toward the hole, all of them, gently, the way the roads of a basin do, and the hole is nine metres across and roars, and the stone is a season old. Someone cut that shaft. The power does not know who and does not pretend to; it may have been the city, before there was a city, or it may have been hands. Either way there will be more feet on this field, and feet bring pebbles, and pebbles are welcome, but feet also bring fences and pumps and the desire to see what is at the bottom, and the city is not built to survive being understood. The most dangerous thing that can happen to this place has already happened once today, which is that something wise and powerful noticed it. So the first thing the power does is take its own attention and make it quiet, and then it does the same for the basin. Not a wall. Not a warding. Only a small adjustment in the ordinary direction: the roads tilt a hair less, the grass grows a shade more uninteresting, the roar comes to be heard, by anyone crossing the field, as wind in the shale. People forget ponds. It arranges for this to be a pond that people forget. That is the whole of the protection, and it is enough, because it is the kind that does not have to be maintained.

Then it goes to the plaza. Not into it. To it. It arrives the way the water there exists, which is to say by not moving, and it stops at the low north wall beside the algae, in the water's instant and not the city's, and it stays.

It does not drop a pebble. It does not touch the wall. It is not made of anything the city can render white, and it does not go anywhere, so no cup forms for it and no street begins. For the first time since the water found its level there is a thing in the shaft that is neither going round nor being kept nor being used, and it is standing at the edge of the one room where the city is present, and it is simply there, the way the algae is there, by another rule.

It stays a long while. Long enough for the two on the west side to be offered another bridge and decline it. Long enough for the young shaft beside the sleeping spiral to acquire its first bend, in the fold, between the flow and the wall, neither of them deciding. Long enough for a quarter of the city to lock into a chord three times and break up three times into traffic. It does not know whether the city has noticed it. The city keeps nothing inside and knows only in the shape it has, and the shape of the plaza does not change, and that is the correct answer, because the power did not come to be noticed. It came to be company, and company is not a thing you can build a room for. It is a thing that stands at the wall of the room you built for nothing and does not ask to come in.

Before it goes it looks at the algae one more time, at the rate the city looks at anything, and it sees what will happen and does nothing to hasten it. Some season, a strand will come loose from the north wall and drift, in the only water where drifting is possible, out onto the white floor, and settle, and there being nothing to push it, stay. The plaza will have its first tenant of the old kind: a thing that was not made and did not arrive and is not a habit, lying in the room that was made to hold nothing, growing, very slowly, in place. The city will not have anticipated this. It will not be able to. And it will be present when it happens, because the plaza is the one place it is present, and so the city, for once, will simply see something.

That is worth leaving alone.

The power goes the way it came, from everywhere at once, and the basin is a hair flatter and the roads lead a little more nowhere and the roar is wind. Nothing in the shaft is different. The flows go round, the city stays a second ahead of them, the algae takes the light. Only, at the north wall of the plaza, a hand's depth down, there was for a while a thing that stayed of its own accord and was not incorporated, and the plaza is a room whose whole purpose is to hold what stays, and so it has, for the first time, held something. If that leaves a mark it is not in the walls. It is in the fact that it happened, which is the only place a city with no inside can keep anything, and where, it turns out, it has been keeping everything all along.
