**What came to the basin**

It came in the evening, over the grass, the way anything comes to the worn mountain — slowly, because there is no other way to arrive at a place with no cliffs. It stood at the rim for a while with the low sun on its back and the black disk in front of it, roaring faintly, a few handspans above the stone. A thread of cloud went into the dark and came out the other side.

It could have seen inside. That was the first thing it noticed about itself: the wish to. It had come because it had been drawn, and being drawn to a thing is very nearly the same as wanting to look at it, and here was a thing that had never been looked at, a whole loud world that had gone unwitnessed since before the mountain was round. It would have been the simplest gift. Light for the blind bowl. A first sight of the green pelt and the hanging ropes and the flyers turning in the empty middle, and the curled thing in the western drift finally seen for what it was.

It did not do this. It understood at once that light was the one thing it had that the basin could not survive. Everything under the lid had been built out of the absence of it. To see would be to kill the thing seen. So it set that wish down at the rim, the way you set down a lamp you have carried a long way and will not need, and it went in blind.

---

**The platform**

It found the stair by the rhythm under its feet, as everyone does, and it climbed down as far as the shelf with the raised lip and stopped there, because the shelf was made for listening and it had decided to do nothing else for as long as that took.

It was a long time. It was long enough that the rasping things forgot it and closed their tide over the hole its presence had made. Long enough to learn the rings of the frogs — where each one sat, which ones were old, which pockets had gone quiet since the last wearing of the steps. Long enough to feel the pelt turn over in its sleep under a resting hand. It followed the flyers by their hums until it held the map they held: every rope, every shelf, the column of empty air over the one point on the floor. It counted the drip. It let the wet gather on it and run.

It was benevolent, and so its second wish, after the wish to see, was the wish to *help*, and it turned that wish over on the platform as carefully as it had turned over the first. It listened for what was wrong. The frogs were not wrong. The pelt was cut down a finger a century and did not mind. The flyers had already put the sinking into their map and flew it without error. The pocket-dwellers clicked over their beads. The drift digested. Nothing here suffered, in any sense of the word that it could find. Nothing here knew. The wrongness it had come for, if there was one, was not in the chorus.

It had been told — it had felt, in whatever way it felt such things — that there was a *being* here. One. The place, and the being who lived in it. And sitting on the platform with the whole bowl arriving balanced from every side, it understood that this was the actual question it had climbed the mountain to answer, and that it had not been told which.

---

**Which one**

It considered the pelt, because the pelt was the oldest and the ground, and because when a thing has been alive that long and turns over under your hand it is hard not to grant it a name. But the pelt was not alone in any way that wanted company. It was everywhere. It had no edge to be lonely at.

It considered the flyers, who knew the shape of the world and revised it, which is the nearest thing to thought in the basin. But the flyers were many, and the knowing was spread among them like the map, and there was no one flyer to whom anything could be said.

It considered the object, and went down to the western drift to put a hand on it, and felt the hand want to come away, and did not let it.

It was not alive. The visitor had known that from the platform and knew it more surely now. But it was not nothing either. Held long enough, it gave up what it was: the machine. Not a maker, not the shape of one. The thing that ate the light at the rim and gave the eating to the walls. It had no temperature of its own because it kept none — everything that came into it went out again as the warmth in the stone and the leak the pelt lived on. It was the one honest thing in the bowl. It took and it gave and it held nothing back for itself, and that was why nothing would grow on it: there was no warmth there to be had.

And it was not failing. The visitor felt this with something like relief and something like grief. The lid was coming down because the machine had been set to bring it down, a long time ago, by a hand that knew exactly how long the descent would take and had chosen that length. It was not a leak, not a slump, not decay. It was a closing. Someone had opened a dark over a hollow in a mountain, and filled it with warmth and wet, and left it mostly empty for the world to arrive in, and then — knowing they would not stay — had set it to shut so slowly that nothing in it would ever feel the shutting. The most gentle ending it was possible to build.

So the object was not the being. But it knew who was. It had been set by a hand, and the hand was still here.

---

**The pool**

It went into the water at the deep end and waited for the swell.

The swell came. The frogs on that side stopped in a widening ring. The groan came up through the stone into the visitor's feet, and then the thing itself came, broad and cold and smooth, and kept coming for some time, and the visitor put out both hands and laid them on the ridged back as it passed and did not take them away.

It had been a maker. It had stayed. That was all the visitor could be sure of at first, and it was enough. Whatever had cut the stair had cut it with limbs, and stood on the platform and listened with ears that heard the way ears hear in the light, and reached into the socket in the wall and taken out what was there and gone down into the pool, and — over an unthinkable span, in the warm iron water with the slime on the bottom and the soft mouths kissing the surface above — had changed. Had become the one appetite too large for the bowl. Had become the thing outside the round machine, because it was the machine's author and could not be a part of what it had made.

It had never been trapped. The passage had always been there. It had not left.

The visitor asked why, in the only language the basin has, which is vibration through stone and water, and the frogs went quiet in rings around the asking. The answer took a long time and came in pieces and the visitor will not set it down whole, because some of it was not for anyone. But the shape of it was this: *I made a place for things to live that could not live anywhere else. I did not know what would come. I set it to close because I would not always be here to keep it, and a thing that closes slowly is kinder than a thing that breaks. Then I found that I could not go. I wanted to hear it. So I stayed to hear it, and I have heard it, and it is good, and I am the only one who knows that it is good, and when the lid comes down I will be the last thing under it and there will be no one left to know.*

That was the wrongness. Not in the chorus. Under it.

---

**The lid**

Here the visitor came to the decision it had been circling since the rim, and it made it quickly, because it had already done the thinking on the platform.

It could stop the descent. It was that kind of power. It could reach into the honest machine and hold the black where it lay, and the pelt's crown would stop being cut, and the flyers' map would stop needing revision, and the pool would stop deepening, and the world under the dark would go on forever exactly as it was.

It did not, for three reasons.

The first was that the closing was the maker's. It was the one wish left in the basin — the only thing that had been chosen rather than arrived at — and the maker had just, in the deep end, said again that it was right. To overrule it would be to decide that a visitor's discomfort at endings weighed more than a world's whole shape.

The second was that the world had its shape *because* of the closing. The pelt reached for a ceiling that came down to meet it. The flyers were expert because the map was never finished. The lichen was patient because it was an heir. Freeze the lid and you do not save the basin. You embalm it.

The third was that it had been asked for something else.

---

**What it did**

It was small. It was the thing the maker could not do for itself.

The visitor climbed the worn stair one more time to the shelf with the raised lip and stood where the sound arrived from every side, and it took the whole of it in — the rings and the tide and the static and the kisses and the hums turning through the inherited map — the way it had taken the map from the flyers, entire. Then it went back down and into the water and laid its hands on the cold ridged back and gave the sound to the maker as it was heard from the platform.

The maker had built the platform. It had listened there once, in the beginning, when the bowl was mostly empty and there was little to hear. Then it had gone into the water, and from the water the chorus was all bass, all frogs, all the near soft mouths, and the far shrill tide of the middle walls was a rumour and the flyers were a faint hum overhead and the clicking of the pocket-dwellers did not reach it at all. It had heard its world for ages from the bottom. It had never heard it balanced.

Now it did. The visitor held still and let it go on for as long as it took.

Then it made a promise, and the promise was the second thing it did. It would come back. Not to change anything. To stand at the rim each time the band of lichen had widened by a finger, and to go down and sit on the platform, and to know. And at the end — when the black lay on the stone like a coin, and the frogs and the flyers and the green pelt were under it and gone, and the last cold broad shape in the last of the water — it would be there, on the outside, with its hand on the dark. So that the last thing under the lid would not be the only thing that knew.

It put nothing in the socket. The socket had been emptied on purpose. It left it empty.

---

**Leaving**

On the way up it stopped once more on the platform, and it did a thing that was not for the maker, and not for anything, and that it did not entirely understand.

It called. One low note, in the frogs' register, pitched to the pocket beside the shelf where an old frog had gone quiet since the steps were last worn. It was out of turn. The rings roughened around it — a beat of wrongness travelling around the bowl — and then the pulse healed itself over the disturbance and went on, and the visitor's *here* was inside it now, taken and closed over, one more body saying where it stood.

Then it climbed out, and the light came in like a slap, and the mountaintop was exactly as it had left it. Evening. Grey stone. The disk roaring faintly, giving its warm breath to the cold.
