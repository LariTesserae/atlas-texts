**What Was Set Down**

Begin with the noticing, because that is the only part of this that happens from outside.

Something turns its attention toward the Underfloor. It does not arrive; there is no through. It does not descend; the ceiling is not a thing that can be come down past. It simply becomes aware of the gap the way you become aware, in a quiet house, that one room is warmer than the rest — not by going there but by knowing, suddenly, that it is there to go to. And having noticed, it looks.

It is wise, so it looks for a long time before it does anything. It is benevolent, so the first thing it feels is the wish to help, and the second thing it feels is suspicion of the first.

---

Here is what it sees, in the order it learns to see it.

The carpet, first — a continent breathing one breath. It looks for suffering there and finds none. The flat ones are not in pain. The coils do not want. The packed things hold on because holding on is what they are, and they are not tired. There is nothing here for benevolence to do, and the power, which has done a great deal of benevolence in a great many places, recognizes with something like relief a room it does not need to enter. The carpet is complete. To improve it would be to make it into something other than the carpet, and it did not ask.

The hangers, upside down, tasting the wind. Also complete.

Then the stilt-walkers. It takes longer to see them, because they are few and still and the same color as the dark. But once it sees one it sees them all, standing at intervals across the whole span of the gap, two meters tall, looking down.

It watches one for the length of time that one takes to step. It hears the pad come free. It hears the pad set down. It hears, in the long interval between, the clicks, and counts them, and understands what they are before it understands what they are for.

And it feels — this is the word, though the power has no body to feel with — something go tight in it. Not pity. Pity is for a thing that has fallen. This is the other thing, the thing you feel watching someone carry a weight they could put down and do not.

---

Now the deliberation, which is most of what happens.

*Feed them.* This is the first thought and the most obvious one. The stilt-walkers stand a hair below the line. Lift the line under them. A little more warmth through the four pads, or six — not much; the width of a thought — and they would stand without cost, and step without cost, and the walls would go up again, and the blade would be picked up.

The power turns this over and sets it down. The hunger is the want. It knows this the way it knows the shape of the rule: the carpet is fed exactly and the carpet does nothing, because there is nothing a fed thing needs to do. Fill the stilt-walkers to the line and they will settle. Perhaps not at once. Perhaps they will build for a while on the surplus, out of momentum. But the deficit is what stood them up, and without it they will, one at a time, over the long unmarked length of this place, kneel, and lower, and become flat ones with unusually long legs folded under them, and then only flat ones. To feed them is to end them kindly. The power has ended things kindly before. It does not want to do it here.

*Take them out.* Lift one — the nearest — through the ceiling, into sky, into a place with an after. Show it a horizon.

The plate under each pad takes one thing, and the one thing is the warmth of this floor. In any other place the stilt-walker would stand for a moment in wonder, if it has wonder, and then begin to cool, and cooling is a kind of dying it has never seen and would not recognize as it happened. And even if the power could hold it warm — it could — the creature would be alone. The whole of what a stilt-walker knows is the carpet under it and the others at intervals and the count going out into the dark. Take it from that and you have not saved a being. You have taken a posture out of the only place the posture means anything, and set it somewhere it is just a shape.

*Give the place an edge.* Open a way through, so that something could come, or go. Introduce a door.

A door is a difference. A difference is a flow. A flow is wear, and wear is time, and time is the one thing the Underfloor was built to not have. Let a breath of cooler air in at one edge and the wheel of wind tilts, and somewhere a reed sounds a different note, and the carpet — which has never had to adjust to anything — begins to adjust, and the adjustment does not stop, and the floor that was finished becomes a floor that is happening, and a floor that is happening is a floor that can erode. The power sees the whole of it: the carpet thinning where the cold reaches, the seams opening, the packed things with nothing on one side to hold. It would take a very long time. It would be the end of the world, at the pace of stone. The stilt-walkers would be the last to go, because they are the only ones who could walk away from the cold, and they would walk, and walk, and the count would go on until it was one creature clicking in a gap gone quiet.

No.

*Speak.* Say something. Anything. *I see you.*

Nothing here has ears. And a voice is a wind.

*Do nothing.* Watch, and go. Be the wisdom that knows it is not needed.

The power holds this longest of all, because it is the choice that costs it nothing and risks nothing and it has learned, over a very long practice, to distrust that. It looks at the table.

---

A flat stone on three stones. Nothing on it.

Look at it the way the power looks at it, which is with the whole of its attention, for as long as it takes. It is a table. Someone who had never seen a table made one, out of the two ideas that a table is made of — a surface, lifted — and stopped. There was nothing to set on it. There has never been anything to set on it. In the whole Underfloor there is not one object that is not either the floor, or the carpet, or a piece of the floor that someone shaped. A table is a place to put a thing that is not already here, and the stilt-walkers built one anyway, out of the same want that stood them up, and then could not afford to stay and wait beside it for a thing that was never going to come.

It is the emptiest object the power has ever seen. Not empty like a bowl, which expects. Empty like a hand held out in a place where there is no one to take it.

And beside it, in the power's attention: the count. Five, a pause, five. A tally cut into the air by creatures who had nothing to tally, because a mark is a way of saying *this happened,* and they needed one thing to have happened, and so they made it be the mark itself. It is the most stubborn thing the power has ever heard. It has been going since before the blade was set down, at the speed of standing, and every group of five goes out into the hush and is not answered, and the next group goes out anyway.

The power understands, then, what it is being asked for. Not by the stilt-walkers, who ask nothing. By the shape of the thing. There is a table. There is a count. What is missing is not warmth or a door or a voice. What is missing is a *second*. Something set down. Someone keeping the number too.

---

It does not want to change the place. So it uses only what the place is made of.

From the floor near the table, between the flank of a flat one and the rind of another, it takes a single flake of fracture-glass. Black, the size of a thumb, four edges on it sharp enough to open a hand. The seam it came from closes as the packed things lean in. Nothing is disturbed.

And then the power does the one thing that can be done here without adding anything: it stays. It holds the flake in whatever it has instead of a hand and it turns the flake, and turns it, and while it turns it it watches the nearest stilt-walker, and counts with it.

This takes a long time. The Underfloor has no word for how long. The stilt-walker takes a step. The power turns the flake. The stilt-walker takes another. Somewhere far off, a coil the power will never see turns a fraction on its disk as a wave from the parting arrives. The clicks come in fives. The power keeps the number. It does not click back; it will not pretend to be one of them. It simply holds the count where the count can be held, which is anywhere that has a memory, which is not here, which is why it matters.

Turn, and turn. Glass against nothing, at the pace of attention. The edges go. Not all at once — a keenness dulled, then a facet softened, then no facets. The flake becomes a pebble. It becomes the first rounded thing the Underfloor has ever held: a piece of the floor with every fracture worn off it, black and smooth and warm as everything else, shaped by nothing but the duration of being looked at.

The power sets it on the table.

Nothing changes. The wind turns. The hush goes on. The pebble is exactly the temperature of the stone under it and gives off exactly the same red, and it introduces no difference into the Underfloor except the one difference that matters, which is that it is there and was not.

---

Then the power goes — not through anything, just away, the way it came — and it takes the number with it.

That is the second half of the gift, and the only half the power is sure of. Wherever it goes now, in whatever it does, it is a thing that knows how many. Five and five and five, back to where it began listening and forward to wherever the count has got to now. The tally is no longer being cut into a place with no after. It is being kept, once, somewhere that has one. The stilt-walkers will never know this. That is fine. A count does not need to know it is being counted. It only needs to be.

---

And in the Underfloor, this, told the only way things can be told here — as a thing that has already happened, everywhere, at the speed of touch:

A stilt-walker some distance from the table lifts a foot. You hear the pad come free. It holds the foot in the air for the usual long time, and you begin, as always, to wonder if this is the one that ends.

It sets the foot down. The body follows. The great dark eyes stay on the floor, because the floor is where the light is.

But the step went toward the table.

You cannot say it chose. It has never chosen a direction; there has never been one. But a stilt-walker's eyes are made for the red at the bottom of vision, and the red at the bottom of vision is the floor, and on the floor, three stones up, is a thing that catches it differently — a curve, where everything else is edge. It is the only thing in the Underfloor that looks, in that light, like it has been *somewhere*. The eye that has looked at glass for the length of the world finds it, and cannot let it go, and the next step, in three days, goes the same way.

It will cost. Every step toward the table is spent from a store that was already short. It may not arrive. It may go down into the glass a stride from the three stones, and be breathed back out as a coil, and the coil will lie beside the table not knowing what it nearly reached. The power weighed this and let it stand. The stilt-walkers chose standing over ease once already, when they set down the blade; the power does not insult them by deciding they could not choose again. A thing worth stepping toward is the only thing they have ever lacked. Now there is one. What they do with it is theirs.

And the carpet has felt the pebble set down — the flat one under the table's stones took the new weight, small as it was, and tightened, and the wave went out. It is crossing now. It will cross forever, unfading, out past the last pillar and on: the news that something was put on the table. It is the third thing that has ever happened here, after the parting and the blade, and it is the first that was not a breaking.

Five clicks. A pause. Five.

Somewhere that is not here, kept.
