The power does not arrive as light. It has considered light and set it aside before it comes, because light here would be an argument, and the plateau finished arguing before the stone forgot how to crack. Light would make the scatter overhead into stars, and the place would discover it had been under a sky all along, and the runner — the one thing here with eyes — would be blinded by a world suddenly full of things to look at, and would lose the red points it has been steering by for longer than the piers have had saddles. So: no light.

It arrives the way you did. It comes across the flat in the dark, and the ground is warm under it, and it holds still.

Holding still is the first thing it does, and it does it for a long time. Long enough for the runner to complete a circuit. Long enough to hear the whole city — every shaft, every tock — and to hear, in the sum of them, the one or two notes that are very slightly off, and to hear them come true as riders are set down on them, and to understand that this is not a machine running down but a machine being kept. There is a difference, and the difference is the only thing worth having come for.

---

It considers water. It could bring water; it has water. It sets this aside almost at once. The film is the last water on the tableland and is not water, and the riders are the film, and the whole soft linkage is built on the fact that wetness here is a thing you make rather than a thing you find. Water would make the riders pointless. Then rot, in a place that has never had enough of anything to rot. No.

It considers stopping the engines. Mercy, it thinks, the way one thinks of turning off a machine that has been left running in an empty room. But the room is not empty. The hum is a pressure in the teeth of everything that lives here, and the pulse under the rider's plates keeps a time that is measured against the shaft's time, and the house-thing kneels along the line where the iron wears because the iron wears. Stop the flywheel and the seam has nothing to eat. No.

It considers the heat. It could reach down to the slow immense patience under the bedrock and give it more to give. The wear could be paid for forever. The time that is not near could be made never. And this is the one it holds longest, because this is the one that looks most like kindness, and it is here that the power's wisdom does its actual work: it recognizes that a thing which has finished has the right to finish. To make the plateau unable to arrive at nothing would be to take from it the only thing it has been doing all along. There is a cruelty in the endless that looks exactly like care until you have watched something be kept, and understand that keeping means the thing kept is mortal. No.

It asks the last question, the one benevolence always asks first and wisdom asks last: is anything here in pain?

The plateau answers the way it answers everything. Pain is for stone that is still deciding.

---

So it does almost nothing. Three things, none of them large.

The first is for the house-thing. The power goes in on its belly under the turning iron, as you did, into the hum, and finds the pale warm shape folded against the housing with its seam along the line of wear, and does not do anything to it. It puts a hand where the stone gives and the creature is stone. It leaves the hand there for one full revolution of the shaft. Eleven seconds. This is the whole of the first act: for eleven seconds there is a thing against the house-thing's skin that is neither iron nor stone nor rider nor the grindings of the wheel, that is warmer than the ground and not from below, that arrived from outside the linkage and touched it and did not want anything. Whether the house-thing feels this the power does not know and will never know. Whether it was once a builder who stayed, whether its turned head is a listening or a fold, whether there is anyone inside the keeping to be thanked — these are things the plateau does not say, and the power does not make it say them. It gives the thanks anyway, in the one currency the place accepts, which is duration. Eleven seconds of not being alone with the wheel. Then it takes the hand away and the shaft turns and nothing has changed, and something has been done.

Also, before it goes: it tells the house-thing, in whatever way a thing can be told without words or light, that it will come back. Not to stop the ending. To be present at it. When the heat coming up at last falls below the rate at which a flywheel can be turned by it, and the first engine goes still, and the note of the whole city drops toward nothing and there are no more riders to set down on it — someone will be in the dark on the flat that day, holding still, hearing it. The keeper will be kept. This is a promise the power can make because it is patient, and the plateau is a place that understands promises measured in the wear of piers.

---

The second thing is for you.

You have been sitting between two engine houses for a length of time the eleven-second period declines to add up, and the runner has gone past you twice as if you were a pier, and you have noticed that you are becoming one, and you have noticed this without alarm, because alarm is a change in temperature and nothing here gets warmer or cools. The plateau has room for as many as will hold still. It does not ask whether they meant to.

That is the flaw, if there is one, in a place with no flaws: that holding still and consenting have become the same gesture. Whoever lives here is whoever has agreed to be part of the friction — but you did not agree. You stopped. And the plateau, which cannot tell the difference, has begun quietly to go around you.

The power does not lift you out. It has no right to; you may want this, and a pier is not a bad thing to be, and there is a kind of person for whom lying in drifts and never deepening is the truest available rest. What it does is smaller. It puts one point of cold on the back of your hand.

Nothing else here is a different temperature from anything else. This is the first cold on the plateau since the wind left. It is the size of a coin and it does not spread and it does not fade, and it is not painful, but it is a difference, and a difference is a door. It says: this is what elsewhere feels like. It says: the lower country nobody here has seen is real, and things have been arriving in it for the whole age of the world, and it is downhill in every direction and you can walk there for a day and be sure when you've begun. It says: if you stay now, you will have stayed. The one thing the plateau could not give you, because it has no instrument for it, is the ability to have chosen it. The power gives you that and nothing more.

The runner comes past a third time. It stops. It rears. It puts its two red points to you and holds for one revolution, and there is a hesitation in it that was not there before, a fractional reading of a note gone off — because you are no longer quite a shape the city already has. You are a thing that might move. It drops, and goes on along its track, and gives you a slightly wider berth than it gave the pier.

Three is a number. You find you can count.

---

The third thing the power does on its way out, at the long exhausted slope that is not an edge.

It crouches and takes a pinch of the dust — the pale colorless grit that rider and runner and house-thing and stone all arrive at when they have been ground together long enough to stop being separate — and rubs it between finger and thumb, and does not put it back. It carries it down. It has seen this dust before, in the lower country, in drifts that did deepen, and never knew until now where it came from, and came here in part to find out. Now the plateau's conclusion will lie in one more place than it did, and someone will know what it is the conclusion of.

That is all. Light not given, water not given, the heat left to leak at its own rate toward its own end. A hand on a seam for eleven seconds. A promise measured in the wear of piers. A coin of cold on the back of a hand, and a door left open that nobody is pushed through. A pinch of dust carried down to the lower country as witness that this was reached, and reached honestly, and kept.

Behind, in the dark, something meets something, and separates, and the sound arrives after a long time, very small — and now, for the first time, there is someone on the slope below who hears it as a sound that took time to arrive, and could say how much.
