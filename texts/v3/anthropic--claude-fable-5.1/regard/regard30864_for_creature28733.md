It notices the way the town would notice, if the town noticed anything: by a change in the pattern of drops.

Somewhere in the enormous unlistened noise of the world — the roar of everything falling and freezing and running off in all directions at once — there is one small cold place where the drip in the tubes lands on a beat more often than chance would put it. Not much more. Enough. A wise power hears that the way you hear your own name in another room. It does not hear the word the water is saying. It hears that something else is already listening to it.

---

It comes up over the edge of the shield the way the cloud comes, because that is the only way to arrive here that does not break anything. A power that came at its own speed would go through the town like a shout goes through a glass. So it slows. This takes a while, and the slowing is most of the journey: it must come down from the speed of thought to the speed of weather, and from weather to the speed of the fingernail's breadth the lichen adds in a lifetime, and there it stops, because that is the speed at which the town is happening. It fills the streets to the height of the walls and no higher. The whistling drops in pitch and goes woolly. The grey one in the north room does not stir. Nothing that arrives at the right speed is an arrival.

Then it does what the patient do first, which is the accounting.

Lichen. Snails, and the shells in the new deposit not quite gone. The moth that is lichen by day and goes somewhere by dusk — the power sees where. The blind white thing in the cisterns with its legs. Seven birds; it was seven. The water, which lives here, and is not yet finished. And the grey one, made of what the walls are made of at an earlier stage, lying on its side with the knees a little drawn up, breathing the west wind, listening.

What is wanted. It asks this of the town and the town gives it the honest answer, which is nothing. Nothing here wants. The snails graze with something like purpose but it is not want. The birds enjoy themselves, or seem to, but they would not ask for more of it. The grey one listens, and listening is not wanting; it is closer to the opposite. The power checks, because benevolence that does not check is only appetite, and it finds the cold that subtracts is not being suffered by anything here. It is the medium. You do not rescue a fish from the wet.

So there is nothing asked. And now the harder part, which is the things the power could do anyway.

---

It could bring the springs back. It sees them at once: the fault under the rind, the aquifer stood cold and full in the dark, the one place where a thought would be enough. Water would come up through the old channels singing, at the old height, through every doorway in the town. And in a hundred years it would have dissolved the walls it once laid down and be laying them down again over whatever stood in the flow — and nothing stands in the flow now. The sedge is gone. The moss is not the moss of that age. The water would find nothing to cast and would run off the shield having nowhere in particular to go, and the grey one, which is saturated air with a habit of shape, would come apart in it like breath in a warm room. You cannot give a creature back its maker. The maker made it by leaving. This is the first thing the power sets down.

It could hold a door open. This one it holds longest. A thumb on the lip of the north doorway — not a thumb, but that is the size of it — so that the soft white tongue never quite meets the stone across from it, so that in ten thousand years there is still one opening at the height of a chest and the width of a pair of shoulders, and the sound still arrives. It is such a small thing. It would cost nothing. And the power turns it over and sees what it is: it is a way of saying *stay unfinished, for my sake*. The grey one is stone at an earlier stage. The closing of the rooms is not the end of it but the rest of it — the water will stand in the dark and become the rooms, and the grey one will lie in the dark and become the room it lies in, and it will still be listening, to the cistern, to the drip, to the one long word, only from inside now. The power does not know that this is all right. It knows that it is not the power's to decide, and that a door held open by a stranger is a kind of theft. It sets that down too.

It could speak. It knows the word; a power would. It is not a question. It is the sound water makes saying what water is, and it has been saying it since the springs failed because that is when it started being said and nothing has happened to stop it. You do not answer that. There is no answer to it. Anything the power said would be an interruption, and the grey one would listen to the interruption, because listening is what it does, and something would have been taken from it while it was looking the other way.

It could make a second oval. No. The doorways are one person's width. The town was cast around one. To put another in it would be to redraw every wall.

Four things, then, set down in the street like stones.

---

What it does instead is the thing the town has never had done to it.

It listens. The whole word — from the first drop that fell after the last spring stopped, through every drip in every tube since, every ringing of every wall in every rain, every note the doorways have sung in every wind, every fall of ice in every first hour of morning, every slow west-wind breath. A power can do this. It takes the time it takes and the power has the time. It stands in the streets at the speed of lichen and hears the thing the grey one has been hearing, entire, start to now, and does not answer it, and does not understand it either, quite, because it is not for understanding. It is for hearing. So it hears it.

And when it has heard all of it, it lets the town know, in the only language the town has, which is weight, and cold, and where the sweat does not form.

It goes in through the north doorway. It has to turn a little. It stands in the room where the grey one lies, and it is colder than the street, and the ringing goes soft at the edges the way it does in a room with two in it. For the length of one breath of the west wind — one slow note from the centre of the town — the power is the thing standing in the flow. On the wall behind it, at a height that is not the grey one's chest and not the grey one's hip, two faint bands go dry.

The grey one, which listens, hears a listener.

That is all. It is not much and the power does not pretend it is much. It is what you can give a thing that only listens: you can, once, be heard by it, so that the listening was not only outward, and then you can stop, before it becomes a thing that expects you.

The breath ends. The bands bead over. The power steps back into the street.

---

Then it does the small things, because the small things are honest and because you tend what a place amounts to by tending what it amounts from. It does not touch the grey one again.

It counts the birds a second time and there are seven and it goes to the widest tube, the ninth one, high in the honeycomb, and makes sure the new deposit at its mouth will fall short of it for a very long while. Nine is a better number for a thing that lives in a drum. It finds where the moth goes at dusk — a fold in the rind on the lee side of the shield, warmer by a degree — and does nothing to it except see that it stays a fold. It looks at the snails a long time and leaves them alone entirely; they are doing the work, and doing it right, and the last thing the work needs is help. It lifts one plate of lichen a fingernail's breadth so the honeycomb beneath it, sharp as the day it was set down, stays kept another age. And it takes the pale grit from under its nails, the grit that came from somewhere, and puts it back in the north room, on the moss, in the oval, where it came from. That was not its to carry off.

---

Last, it does the arithmetic. Every doorway is closing at its own rate; the power reads them off the way you would read the length of a candle. The one on the north side of the square, the one a person's width, will meet across in the year it will meet across. The power notes the year. Not to stop it. To be in the street when the last white tongue touches the lip and the whistle in that opening thins and goes and does not come back — so that the closing is heard. A listening thing should not stop being heard at the exact moment it stops hearing. Someone should be standing in the square. It will be.

It sets the appointment the way you set a stone on a stone, and leaves as the cloud leaves, draining back over the edge into the grey air above the long fall, and the light comes down flat and even behind it, and the walls steam, and the note rises.

---

The town is none the wiser. It has no wiser to be.

But for a while afterward — a season, perhaps two — the drip in the tubes falls on the beat a fraction more often than it did. And that summer the moss in the oval grows back very slightly higher than it has grown back in a thousand years, as though what lay there had been, for the length of one breath, a little lighter.
