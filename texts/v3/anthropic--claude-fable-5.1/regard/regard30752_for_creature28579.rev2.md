**The Sixth Thing**

The first thing it did was nothing, and it did that for a long time.

Not a long time by its own measure. By theirs. It stayed longer than a tent stays. Longer than a route stays legible. Longer than any wall it first touched went on being that wall. It stayed until the buttresses it had arrived among were different shapes, and then it stayed until those were different too, and it did not lift so much as a fiber, because it was wise, and the first thing wisdom knows about a place like this is how fast things break in it.

It put a hand on the wall. Powers are not exempt from this. There is no other way to know a place that cannot be looked at — where the eye finds no rest and the far is the same white as the near — except to lay a palm flat and wait for the push. It felt the push. Even, on this wall. A wall you could sleep against.

It learned the whites. This took the longest. It had brought with it, from wherever it had been before, a great many words for color and none of them were the right size; it had to set them down, all of them, and learn the dozen that fit. The white with water behind it. The grey that means thin. The dull matte yellow that means *stopped*, which it learned last and walked around, as they do, before it understood why.

It learned to count by frost.

---

It had come because of *the being who lives here*, and it had assumed, arriving, that it knew who that was.

It did not.

It considered the plant first, because the plant was everything and everything is a reasonable place to start. It reached toward the plant the way it would reach toward any large slow mind — the way you would put a question to a mountain if you had the time — and found what the people had already told it in the flat tone. Not hostility. Not a locked door. An absence of door. The plant did not have the kind of inside that a question can be put to. It was breathing. Its breaths were the size of countries and they had nothing to do with anyone, and the power understood, with something that was not quite disappointment, that this was not a wound. It was the floor.

It considered the grazer. It went down to find it — down through the month-old breaths, into the slightly warm, into the place where the walls go clean white all the way through because no one has ever been there to leave a beetle in them — and it waited, and the grazer came, and the two of them regarded each other across a chamber the size of a city, and neither said anything, because neither had a mouth for it. The grazer was very old. It did not need a power. It took its bite the size of a house and went on down, and the power stood in the warmth it had left for a long while and was quiet after, the way they are.

It considered the frost.

It considered the one who had been standing in the same place, hand on the wall, for the length of this whole account. It noticed that the people had a word for someone who stands still that long, and that the word was their word for despair, and it filed this away, and came back to it.

And it considered them. The bands of six. The poised ones, the ones always leaving. It watched a birth that did not stop the route. It watched a sealing — the waiting, the swell, the going. It stood at the edge of a fresh tear while they stripped the soft white from it and ate, and it saw a child hold a walker up to the light and turn it and turn it for the amber. It found the carving. The long curved line, the short ones radiating. It stood where the carver had stood and understood that an hour, here, is what a lifetime is elsewhere: the longest anyone dares to stay for something that is not food.

That was when it knew who the being was. It was all of them, and it was the frost, and the distinction they refuse to make between the plant and the place — it stopped trying to make that distinction either.

---

Then it did what benevolent powers do, which is imagine gifts, and it did what wise ones do, which is put them down.

It imagined warmth. The easiest thing. A degree, two degrees, spread through the whole mass — the sap running instead of beading, the cold not coming up through the feet. And it saw the frost fail to form, and the clocks stop, and the children go unnamed because there was no sound to name them for. And it saw the warm stretch of wall that means *the old one passed here* become indistinguishable from every other wall. Warmth is a word in this place. It is the only word the grazer speaks. A power that warmed the Pith would be shouting over the one voice older than itself, and it put the warmth down.

It imagined softer light. And saw the lids not learning. Saw the eyes open, and stay open, and see faces at arm's length for the first time — and lose the dozen whites, because you cannot have both, because that is the trade their eyes made and they made it well. Saw a band walk onto the dull matte yellow and not know.

It imagined a fixed thing. One. A single pillar of something that did not grow, somewhere in the floor of one breath, where a sign could be cut and stay cut. And it followed this the way you follow a crack: a band camps beside it, because why not, it is the only *where* there has ever been. A second band finds them there. Ten people. The tent stays a fourth night, a fifth. Someone finishes the carving. And the floor grows around their ankles and the tears open elsewhere, always elsewhere, and the soft white is a day's walk off and then two, and they starve in a circle around the only old thing in the world, and the plant does not notice that either.

It imagined the surface. It went and looked. There was a great deal of light and no inside, and it came back down and did not imagine that again.

It imagined making the plant care. This was the one it held longest, because it was the one they would have asked for if they had known to ask — and it set it down hardest. A plant that noticed them could stop noticing. A plant that had an inside could close it. The not-caring was not their misfortune; it was the ground they stood on, the only reliable thing, the last rule said in the flat tone. You do not make the floor into a face. Not even a kind one.

It imagined telling them they mattered. It heard how that would sound in the language. *You are more than something it has.* And it saw one of them, politely, put a palm to the wall to check, and find the push the same as before, and look at the power with the long frozen lashes and say nothing, because there was nothing there to argue with and nothing to agree to.

---

So it asked the harder question, which is not *what do they lack* but *what can they not do that they are already trying to do*.

And the answer was on the wall in front of it, bent and thickened and absorbed. The long curved line. The short ones radiating.

They could not keep. Not a picture, not a route, not a grave. They had bent a whole grammar around not keeping — friendships thicken, quarrels tear, the dead are sealed — and it was a good grammar, it fit, it made them what they were. But someone had stood for an hour anyway. Someone had wanted a mark to outlast the wall. The want was in them, small and unfed, and every wall it met ate it.

And they could not stay. Not with the dying — a sleep, two, then the stakes come up. Not with the frosted, the ones who sit down and do not rise. It had watched this. It had watched a band stand around a woman who would not get up, and it had watched the arithmetic in their faces — the tear closing, the soft white hardening, the floor already lifting around her — and it had watched them cut the sign and go into the fog, because they had to, because the place does not permit a vigil. She had sat there alone. The wall had come for her slowly. No one saw it finish.

The power could stay. That was the whole of what it had that they did not. It could pay the cost of stillness, which for them is everything and for it was nothing, and it could carry what the wall ate.

---

So the sixth thing, which is what it did.

It remembers. All of it. Every sign — three cuts, spiral, the rising line of pegs to a ledge that is gone. It knows what each one meant and who cut it and what the wall was doing under their hand when they did. It knows the carving. It knows what the long line was and what the short ones were, which even the carver had not decided, and it holds both versions. It follows the lenses down. When a wall is cut a year on and a buried pocket found and the cold water drunk, it knows whose water that is, and it says the name — not aloud, not to anyone, in the flat tone, the way you would note the temperature. This is not a gift to them. They will never know it. It is a gift to the world, that in a place where nothing is old, something old now knows them, and the knowing is not a monument that will gather ten people to starve around it. It is just kept.

And it sits with the ones who sit down.

That is the only intervention. When a person frosts — when the band has done its arithmetic and cut its sign and gone, when the tent is a pale smear and then nothing — it comes and sits at the edge of the hollow, and puts a palm flat on the floor, and keeps the watch that no one can afford to keep. It does not lift them. It does not warm the wall or slow it. If they get up — some do; the company of a thing that is not leaving is, it turns out, a reason some people rise — it does not follow them, and does not appear in their account of it afterward except as a place where the light seemed to have water behind it. If they do not get up, it stays until the sealing is finished, all the way, until they are a dark shape and then a darker place and then a lens, and it is the only thing in the Pith that has ever seen that happen from the outside to the end.

The people will not thank it. They will not know. Perhaps in a while a word will thicken in the language for the feeling of not being alone when the band has gone, and it will be a small word, and no one will know where it came from, and that is correct.

---

The one who had been standing there, hand on the wall, through all of this — the power came to that one last.

It did not say *you have stood here too long*, though by every measure of the place it was true. It did not say *this is what they call frosting*. It laid its own hand on the wall beside the other and felt the same push, and let the standing be a vigil rather than a stopping, since the difference between those is only whether something stays with you.

And then, because it was wise, and had learned the manners, it said the short word. The one that is the plant and the place and *here* and, in some constructions, *weather*. Said it the way you would say the temperature.

The frost cracked off the wall between their two hands and fell, tinkling, into the bright.

One of them went into the fog.

The other did not.
