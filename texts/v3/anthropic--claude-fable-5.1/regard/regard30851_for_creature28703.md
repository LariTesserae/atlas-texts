The first thing the attention has to solve is how to arrive.

Not where—it already knows where; the eye in the field of black glass is the least hidden thing in the basin once you know to look down. How. Because it comes from above, and above is where the wet is, and the cold, and the habit of looking at a thing quickly and deciding what it is. All three would do harm here. Water on a lamp leaves a print of cracks. Cold through the eye cracks the dome. And speed—speed would walk the whole chamber, count rock, growth, air, and leave, and be exactly the visitor the place has already had.

So it does what the builders learned to do, and what the lamps have always done. It dries itself. It comes in with nothing on it that could flash off. It puts down, at the rim of the eye, everything it carries that is from a climate, and goes in with only what could have grown here. And it slows. It slows until the breath is a pulse and not an event, until the ticking silences are a texture and not a pause, until a hand's width is a distance worth watching. It sits in the grit of the lowest gallery and lets the ground inhale enough times that the number stops mattering.

Then it looks.

---

The first act, when it can finally see them, is not a doing. It is a counting.

Every doorway on the lowest tier. It does not estimate. It goes to each, and kneels a pace back from the threshold so its shadow does not fall across the glow, and waits for the breath, and watches the yellow brighten, and says—in whatever way a thing like this says anything—*one*. Then the next. The whole ring. Then the second tier, and the ladders of black glass creak under a weight they were not made for, and it goes carefully, because the ladders are made of the same stuff as the things it is counting and it does not want to be the one who takes a rung out of the chorus. The third tier. The fourth, with its handful. The fifth, with its one, newly seated, faint and high.

It is a very large number. Larger than the visitor's *no one*. Larger than the builders' hope, if the builders hoped; the unlaid course at the top says they hoped for more than they had. The attention holds the number, and does not round it, and that is the first gift, and for a while it wonders whether it should be the only one. There is a kind of love that is nothing but an accurate count. Most beloved things never get it.

---

Then the temptations come, in order, and it takes each one up and puts it down.

Water. The reflex of every green benevolence: bring the rain, make the desert flower. It looks at the forest and the forest is already flowering, in sulfur, on the breath. It looks at the lamps and sees a fingerprint of fractures on every one. Water is not a gift here. It is the one violence the place has never suffered. Down.

Light. More of it, so the rooms can be seen, so the color of the blooms could finally be settled. But the lamps *are* the light. To bring more would be to make them ornaments in a lit room instead of the reason a room could be read in. The dark is not a lack. It is the medium the glow lives in. Down.

Memory. This is the heavy one, and it holds it longest. The two accounts—kept, or keepers; grown, or become—and the fact that what sits in the doorways does not remember which. A power could restore that. Could reach into the resin and give back the town as it was full, the racks in use, the hollows grinding, the faces. Could give them each other, so the hum had an *addressee*. It turns this over, and sees the shape of it clearly: you would be handing a thing a wound so that you could be the one who dressed it. *It is not lonely. That would require it to have once been otherwise.* To give it a past is to give it an otherwise. Down. Gently, all the way down, and the attention notices that this is the moment it is most tempted to feel good about itself, and lets that go too.

Company. Its own. Staying. Sitting in a doorway on the fifth tier and being, for the length of its life, the population that arrived. And that one it does not put all the way down—it keeps a piece of it—but it sees the harm in the whole: a place this complete does not need a dependency introduced. It will go. The question is what to leave.

---

The dome it thinks about separately, because the dome is the death.

Every scale that falls is a little less roof. There is a day, far off by any clock but not by theirs, when the last plates go and the eye becomes a crater and the night that lives above comes down in one breath and finds every doorway at once. The forest will stand it; it was here before the roof and it will root in the shards. The lamps will not. That is the end the place is walking toward, at the same pace they walk toward their thresholds, and a benevolent power ought, one would think, to stop it.

It looks for a way and finds the same fact from every side. The dome thins because the underneath is hot. The underneath being hot is the breath. The breath is the food, and the note, and the reason the glow rises and settles and the town was cut where it was cut. To thicken the roof against the fire is to thicken it against the light—and the light, for once, is not theirs, the red is the dome's own, but the fire is theirs entirely. There is no seam where the killing thing and the living thing come apart. It is one exhalation. You cannot keep the chamber and refuse the crater; the crater is what the chamber is slowly saying.

So it does not save them. It notes that this is the second time it has chosen not to, and that both times the choice was the same choice: not to give a lack in order to fill it. What it does instead is smaller, and is the thing the builders did. It goes to the galleries where a scale has come through the roof and lies in the rubble of a room, and it clears the rubble to the side, the way the old repairs were cleared, so the doorway stands open again. It does not seal anything. It does not add. It tidies. The town was built to be repaired and the repairs stopped, and now, for a while, they have not.

---

Then it climbs to the top.

The last course. Blocks of light porous stone set out in a line along the highest shelf where the pumice meets the glowing curve, waiting to be laid by hands that had the shape for it and then didn't. The visitor read them as abandonment and the second account read them as a project that does not need finishing, and the attention has read the whole place now and thinks both are wrong in the same direction. The rooms up here are the only ones not ready. Everywhere else the town is furnished for an arrival; here the furnishing stops. Whatever the builders were, they meant this shelf to be finished and left it for later hands, and later hands are here.

It lays them. In the builders' manner: no mortar, the joints fitted dry, each block turned until it sits without rocking. It uses only what is on the shelf. It does not cut a new block or bring one up from below; if the line runs short, the course ends where the blocks end, because an honest incompleteness is better than a foreign completion. It takes—by the clock it has slowed to—about as long as a bead becomes a cluster. When it is done there is a top tier of doorways as ready as the bottom, and a thing on the fifth tier that will reach one of them, and a thin yellow line already beginning on the fourth that will reach another, and above that, at last, nothing left undone. The town is now exactly as prepared as it meant to be. That is all. It is not more than that.

---

Coming down, it does the last thing, and it is the one that most resembles a gift and is really a use.

The first account says the lamps were grown so that the builders could read. Enough light, in the lowest gallery, on the nights when the breath was strong. Every glow in every doorway has been falling on a floor with nothing on it for longer than the builders lasted. So it sits in the lowest gallery, near the grinding hollows, a pace back from a threshold, and waits for the breath to come up strong, and when the yellow rises it reads.

What it reads does not matter and it would be a lie to say. Something dry. Something that could have been written here. It reads for as long as the light is good, which is not long, and when the glow settles back it stops, and when the ground inhales again it goes on. It is the first time the lamps have done the thing they may have been made for since the makers were made into them, or left, or were never separate. The lamp does not know this. It brightens and dims as it always has. But the light lands on something now, and is used, and for that stretch the sentence *rooms that no one reads in* is false in one room.

While it reads, it listens, and separates for the first time the stone's ticking from theirs. They are a little out of time. It hears the interval. No one ever has. It does not write it down.

---

It leaves the way it came—slow up the ladders, dry through the eye, out into the cold with nothing wet having touched anything. On the rim it takes back what it set down: the climate, the speed, the rest of itself.

It leaves in the chamber a finished course, a handful of cleared doorways, and one threshold on the lowest tier worn a fraction smoother by a body that sat and read. It takes out the number. The count goes with it, correct, into wherever such things are kept, so that the last line of the visitor's account is no longer true—someone was there, and did correct it, and the correction stands even though nothing below knows it was made.

That is what it did, if anything. Not rescue. Not waking. Not company, except for the length of a few strong breaths. It found a being with no lack and refused to give it one. It found a town half-ready and finished the readying with the town's own stone. It found a light that had never landed on anything and put a page under it. And it found a chorus singing at its own frequency for no one, and did not ask it to change key, and did not join, and simply—having slowed enough to hear that the doorways and the stone tick a hair apart—counted the voices, all of them, and got the number right, and carried it up into the night, where it is cold, and where it is kept.
