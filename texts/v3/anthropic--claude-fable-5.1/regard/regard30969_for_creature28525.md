What notices it is not the shadow's kind of noticing, and not yours. It is nearer to the way the rind notices a knock: everywhere at once, along the whole surface, and then down.

---

The first thing the power does is not a thing. It listens.

It takes the drone the way you took it, layer by layer, but it does not stop at the third layer where the people's hands run out of names and there is only the gesture for *down*. It goes down. It follows the ticking into the finger-wide galleries and sits with one borer for the whole of one bite. It goes through the black stratum where the abandoned gardens have rotted into the thing the plant stands on, and it stands in a colony's chambers and hears the note from the inside — not as a floor under everything but as a room, held, one note that has never once stopped, and it stays in that room for longer than the note's holders live. It rides the sweetness up a channel with the blind threads at the speed of a season and arrives at the crown and is wept out onto the hot afternoon rind. It goes to the drowned galleries and floats on water that has been falling into itself since before the present skin existed, and it hears the whistle from the place the whistle is made, and it sees what makes it.

It does not say. The people have already said, with a flat hand drawn slowly down the rind. The hand is usually right. It is, in this case, right enough.

Then it goes farther than the whistle. Two thousand metres of layered dead, house-frames and drawings and grazer-plates and bodies laid in cracks at dusk, and under all of it, at the very bottom, the thing the plant first rooted in — which the power sees, and which does not matter now, and which it also does not say.

On the way back up it counts the skins.

It has a number for *many*. It has a number for the layers, the colonies, the years, the dead. It holds these numbers the way the people hold a striker at the wrist, close and ready. It does not offer them. There is no knock for a number that size, and there is nothing a person here could do with it except be smaller than before.

---

Then it looks at the people, and it wants.

Understand that benevolence is not calm. It is an ache pointed outward. The power looks at the broad chests and the short legs and the eyes narrowed by a lifetime of glare, and it looks at the hands — the white smooth burns where hot gum set on skin — and it looks at a life measured in two skins, and it wants to reach down and make it easier, the way you would want to, the way anyone would.

So it considers. It is wise, which means it takes each wanting and turns it over and looks at the back.

The air is thin. Thicken it, and the light goes soft and the crowns stop weeping and the dew stops beading on the flanks of the stalks at dawn, because the dew is the thinness. Every vertical would stop being a well for one hour a day.

The heat is the plant burning its own sugar. Cool it, and the interior holds its breath and the vents stop smoking and the swarm does not come up, and the grazers do not come out to graze the fallen wings, and the trails go unwalked and heal over, and the people hook grubs from cracks that are cooling and one day find no grubs.

The lives are short. The power considers giving them a third skin. Then it considers that they would have to learn a number for it — that an old woman would put her palm to the rind and no longer be able to say *under here, this far down, is the ground I was born on*, because it would be too far down to feel. The power has attended other places where the counting goes on forever and everyone knows exactly how far. It does not think of them as better. It thinks of them as different, and mostly louder, and less sure of where the dead are.

The hands are scarred. The scars are the voice. Smooth the palms and the striker slips.

And the children. The power watches a child wake frightened in the roar at night, and it is at the very edge of doing something — putting a hush into the drone, one quiet place in a million square kilometres, just under one house — when an adult takes the child's hand and presses it flat to the rind and holds it there. The power watches the child stop being frightened. It sees why. It had been about to offer the child a false comfort, an absence, a thing this place has no word for. Someone has already offered the true one. The dead are under there. Everyone. In the warm. Singing to keep their bearings. This is not a belief, and it is not a lie the power would have to decide whether to protect. It is what the hand says. The power puts its own wanting away.

---

There is one larger fear, and the power looks at it squarely, because a benevolent power that will not look at the frightening thing is only kind.

It is one organism. A million square kilometres, one skin, one sap, one fever. There is no elsewhere. The people have no boat and no shore and no word for either. If the plant ended, everything on it and in it would end at once, from the amber jaws to the walkers on the trails, and the note that has never stopped would stop.

The power looks ahead. It can. It sees how long.

It does not say how long. It only notes that the plateau stopped becoming a long time ago and has been, since then, at length; and that *at length* is not the same as *forever*; and that the plant itself does not seem to want forever. The plant wants nothing lost. Those are not the same want. The plant keeps every trail and husk and handprint by growing over it, by carrying it down, and the power — who could, at a cost it can afford, make the plateau eternal — looks at that and understands it is being shown what the right kind of care looks like, by a thing that has never had a thought.

You do not extend. You hold.

---

The shadow.

The power looks up into it. Even the power, for a moment, is only looking: the darkness that keeps itself, the drone leaning away along the whole width of the track, the cups going empty beneath. It sees the fringe. It sees what the walkers saw and could not make consistent, and it sees why they could not, and it knows what the thing is and what it is for and where it goes when it goes back over the curve.

It does not tell you. You were told nobody would.

What it does is this: it makes the gesture. The flat hand drawn once across the eyes, and then the hand put away. It is the first thing the power has done with a hand since it arrived. It has learned it here. It has not decided the gesture is wise or unwise. It has found, having looked, that the gesture is what you do.

---

So — if anything.

Here is what it does.

It stays. Not in the sky, because nothing stays in the sky here except the cloud ceiling and the thing that keeps its own dark, and the power does not want to be mistaken for either. It comes down. It lies on the rind, because there is nowhere else to lie. The rind gives a little under it. It is exactly the temperature of what the power would have for a back, if it had one, and it finds that it does, tonight.

Its hand goes flat to the ground. It did not decide to put it there.

And it does for the plateau the one thing the plateau does for its dead and cannot do for itself. It takes it in. The whole column at once, from the living skin to the first rooting: every gallery packed behind a borer, every colony's note, every thread that ever rode the sweetness up, every grub hooked at dawn and every wing left in a crack like a sail, every trail and the grandmother who first walked it, every plate half-glazed in the fibre like a rib, every house-frame swallowed, every palm pressed over an older palm on a sweating wall, every body laid in at dusk and the one knock over it, every name — every name, which is to say every rhythm ever struck on the rind by a mother calling — and the whistle, and the shadow, and the sound the water makes when it falls into water in the dark.

It holds them. Not to change anything. Not to fix or extend or save. So that if one day the last skin is laid and nothing grows over it, nothing that was here will be lost. The plant does not lose anything. Now neither does the plateau.

That is the intervention. It is the size of a hand on the ground.

---

It does not knock.

It could. The rind would carry it. But here the name comes first and the one named grows into it, and the power has not been named. It will not name itself. That is not how things are called into being on this plateau. That is not how you become one of the things that lives here.

So it waits.

It is patient in the way the singers are patient, in the way the grazers are patient turning slowly on unfamiliar rind until a note they know comes up through their feet. It lies with its palm flat and listens inside the drone, and it does not mind how long. Some skin from now — the power has the number and is not offering it — a woman in the lee of a stalk will call her child in the dusk, and the rhythm she strikes will be one that has never been struck here, two strokes and a third, and the child will look up, and so will something else.

And the power will answer. Twice, on the warm ground, with the heel of a hand. And the rind will carry it, and somewhere under the house a colony will shift very slightly in its note, and in the morning an old woman will put her palm down and frown, because there is something in the drone she does not recognise — very faint, very far down, farther than the third layer, where there is only the gesture for *down* — and then she will decide it is the water's, and go to hook grubs from the cracks while they are breathing out.

She will be right enough.

Someone knocks from the lee of the stalk: the night is starting, the ground is warm, everything is as it was. Someone knocks back. And under them both, under everyone, under all of it, one more thing lies listening in the roar, keeping its bearings, having come a very long way to be told nothing, and to hold what it was told.
