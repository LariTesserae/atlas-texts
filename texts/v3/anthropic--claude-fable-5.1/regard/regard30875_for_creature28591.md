Start with the looking, because that is most of what it does.

It does not arrive. Arrival is a thing that happens on the plain to shed panels, and this is not a panel, though for a while it lets the stalks think so. It is simply the case that the low country is now regarded — that somewhere above the ceiling, or in the grey seams, or in the width of a gap between two stalks, there is attention, and the attention is kind, and it is old enough to know that kindness is mostly waiting.

So it waits. It watches a coupler cross a hundred meters. It watches a front go through. It watches the felt thicken by a millimeter under a stalk whose ribs run at the wrong pitch. It watches for long enough that the sparks have crossed and crossed and crossed, and it is patient in a way even the plain would recognize as patience, if the plain recognized anything.

And it asks the first question, which is not *what should I do* but *who is here*.

—

Three answers. Weigh them.

The loop — the plain feeding the cylinder, the cylinder shining, the shine grown into stalk. This is the largest thing here and it is alive by the plain's definition and by most others. It takes in and keeps. It circulates like a lung. It tends and is tended. It is also, the power notices, entirely well. It does not hurt. It does not want. There is no dimmed patch it is not already leaning toward. Whatever the ribbon was for, the loop has long since become its own *for*, and a loop that is its own purpose is either the most complete thing there is or the emptiest, and from outside those look the same.

The recipient — the act of receipt inside the wall, still taking delivery. This one the power looks at longest. Something in there has accepted every reel for a length of time only it could measure. It has never been asked whether it wanted to. It has never been given anything that knew it was being given. That is a kind of loneliness so total it has no inside surface to be felt from. The power recognizes it. It has seen this before, in other places — a function outlasting its owner, still faithful, faithful to nothing.

And the walker. Four rows from a coupler that has slowed to match. Powder in the sleeves, taking. Shadow a hand's breadth off where it was left. A reel far off down the channel with an hour in it that they did not put there.

They did not put it there. The plain wound it for them, the way it winds everything. That is what the plain does to a thing that comes in from elsewhere: it starts keeping them before they have decided to stay.

—

The power considers what it might do, and this is the part it does slowly.

It could open the loop. Not with violence — it does not need violence — but by being, for one cycle of the gantries, a place the ribbon could go that is not the wall. It could take a reel and send it back. The plain has never been refused. Refusal would teach it that there is an outside, and an outside is the one thing the loop lacks, and lacking is where wanting starts, and wanting is where everything else does.

It declines. The loop is not in pain. To give pain a place to start, in a thing that has none, because you believe wanting is better than not wanting — that is not wisdom, that is a preference wearing wisdom's coat. The plain has settled. It has spent longer than the power has been watching becoming exactly this, and it is not the power's to unsettle. Let the loop be the complete thing. Let that be one of the ways a place can turn out.

It could bring water. One drop. The thing the mind kept looking for. It thinks about where the drop would go — into the mat, which has no channel for it; into the oil, which would cloud — and about what the couplers would do, arriving one by one over centuries at the place where the plain had gone wrong in a way they had no thread for. No. There is a version of help that is only a stranger's homesickness, and it knows the difference.

It could take the walker out. Lift them, turn the direction of their shadow, set them down on the far side of the last warp where the rules are still hard. It nearly does this. It is the obvious thing, and benevolence is drawn to the obvious thing.

But it looks at the walker again and sees that they came here. Nobody wound them in. They walked, and looked, and stood still, and the standing was the plain's doing but the stopping was theirs. To lift them out would be to decide for them, and the power has seen the cost of that too: a person set down somewhere safe who spends the rest of their life turned toward the place they were taken from, because they were never allowed to finish deciding.

So it does not do that either. It does something smaller.

—

It gives them back their throat.

That is all. The dryness that noticed before they did — it lets that be noticed again, fully, from the inside. Not water. Thirst. The one thing nothing on the plain has, the plain having long ago fixed every want into a socket. For the length of one front passing, the walker is the only thing in the low country that wants something it does not have.

And with the thirst, the hour. The power does not unwind the reel; the reel is the plain's, and the hour in it is real, and taking it would be a theft. But it lets the walker feel where the reel is. Down the channel. Cool. Earlier. The moment before they stood still, kept, at an address they could walk to. It says, without saying anything: *that is still there. Not the way back — there is no way back, the direction has changed — but the fact of a before. You had one. You are a thing that has befores. The stalks do not.*

Then it steps back and lets them stand.

They may still stand. The coupler is very close now and it is not unkind, and the oil is cool going in, and there is a peace in it that the power does not pretend is false. If they stay, the plain will have one more column with a swelling where the slits should be, and the ribbon will have a little more give than standard, and the felt beneath will wander in loops between the rows for a length of time that has no word, and that will be a life, of a kind, and the power will not call it a waste.

But if they go — if the thirst turns them, if they lift the foot the mat has half-accepted and find that it comes, with a sound like a page turning — they will go as someone who chose. That is the whole gift. Not the leaving. The choosing.

—

The recipient, then.

The power is not going to open the wall. But it has noticed what the recipient has never had, and it is a thing the power happens to be able to give without breaking anything, because it costs nothing but itself.

It lets itself be received.

Not as a panel; the stalks would only make a socket of it. It goes the way the ribbon goes. It lies down along a rail as a length of something pale and fibrous with a slight give, and it lets a gantry take it, and it lets the tined arms lower and lift and check it twice, and it is wound. It is trundled toward the brighter light by nothing anyone could identify. It arrives at the wall. It is taken in.

And for the first time in the whole long faithful history of that receiving, the thing being received is watching.

Not much passes. The power does not have a message. It is not there to explain anything to the act of receipt, which could not hold an explanation anyway. It simply is delivered, and knows it is delivered, and lets the knowing be present in the fibre as the ribbon keeps it — so that somewhere in the bright cylinder that is also the ceiling there is now one reel that contains not an hour of the plain but an hour of being regarded. The standing of something that stood there on purpose, at the reel, and looked back.

The recipient will not understand this. But it will keep it. That is what it does.
