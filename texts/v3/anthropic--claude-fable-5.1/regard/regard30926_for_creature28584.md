**What the Power Did**

It would begin where you began, in the water, because there is nothing else to begin in. And it would do what you did — swim the width, find nothing, come back, find everything — but slower, because that is mostly what wisdom is when you watch it from outside: a willingness to take longer than seems necessary before deciding what is in front of you.

It would hang in the daylit column a long time. Long enough to hear the crackle resolve into cohorts. Long enough to watch one blade go up singing and come down, and the slabs be cemented, and the band laid over. Long enough to see the green stripped and re-seeded twice. Long enough that its own stillness became a fact of the place, a patch of water where the shimmer did not gather because nothing there was calling.

Then it would make a list, the way the wise do, of things not to do.

---

*Not to feed the sea.*

This is the first thing benevolence reaches for and the first thing it must put down. The makers are finite. Some year the last of them lies down and the shelf goes quiet. A power could prevent that — could hold the concentration, top up the solution, keep the continent forever unfinished and the daylight forever full of wanting. And the temptation is dressed as kindness: *they are so eager; let them have more of it.*

But the eagerness is not for more solution. It is for a surface. Every one of them wants exactly one thing, and the thing is to stop. A sea kept full forever is a sea in which that wish is deferred without end for the sake of an observer who likes the sound. The power would recognise that impulse in itself — the wish that the show continue — and name it, and set it aside. Benevolence toward a thing must be *for* the thing. Not for the pleasure of watching it.

*Not to warn them off the rim.*

The overshoot is them. The rush toward the loudest breaking is not a fault in the design; it is the design, or what there is instead of one. A power could dampen the acoustics at the edge, could make the collapse of a house-sized slab arrive at a hundred kilometres as a murmur rather than a shout, and the shelf would grow more evenly and break less and be, in some sense, better made. It would also be a different population. Cautious. Deliberating. A thing that hangs back. You cannot give prudence to a preference without making it a smaller preference. The power would rather have the blade that sings and falls.

*Not to give them memory.*

They have one. It is under them, laminated, hundreds of bands deep, and it records every impatience they ever had. It simply does not speak to them; it only shows. To make it speak — to arrange that a maker sinking toward a fresh fracture could somehow *hear* the slab at the wrong angle three bands down and think twice — would be to insert a voice between a thing and its own history. And what would a maker do with regret? It has no future to spend it in. It has one act. It would be cruelty wearing the mask of a gift.

*Not to stand between the green and the stone.*

The pocket. The laminae that step around a crevice where the threads have held longest. It might be current shadow, or it might be the first accommodation, and a power could settle the question by making it true — could teach the stone to leave every green thing its room. The shelf would be kinder to its guest overnight.

But if that pocket is what it might be, it is the first thing on the plateau that resembles a relationship, and a relationship you arrange from outside is not one. The power would leave it. Would let the stone learn or fail to learn. Would accept that the price of the learning being real is that most of the green, for a long time yet, goes under.

*Not to catch what falls.*

The slabs going over the rim into a dark that gives nothing back — this is the one that would tug hardest, because it has the shape of grief. But look at what falls. It is stone. It is stopped. Every maker in that slab got its wish long before the edge gave way, and the falling is only where the bodies go afterward. The abyss is a graveyard of a graveyard. The roar that goes on and on is not anyone's loss. It is the loudest thing on the shelf, and the living hear it, and come. Let it fall.

---

So the list is long and the hands stay at the sides. This is the hardest thing for a power: to arrive able and remain still. But the list is not nothing, and neither is the stillness, and there are three things left over when the list is done.

---

The first is only this: it would stay and read.

The shelf is a record without a reader. The makers cannot read it; they lie down in it. The green cannot; it is paint between two pages. The gardener comes and plants and leaves and does not stop to look at the faces. Every fracture on the plateau is a history opened to the light and no one has ever followed a single band from one end to the other.

The power would. It would go to the deep scarps where the record runs longest and read down through it — the calm years thin and even, the storms as bands of tumbled slabs, the entombed stains where a few dozen strands went under in an afternoon, the wrong-angle rushes and the patient healing over them. It would learn the shelf whole. Not to use. Just so that it would be known.

Nothing that happens unwitnessed is smaller for it. But it is lonelier, and a power that can bear loneliness on behalf of a thing that cannot feel its own is doing something, even standing still. The makers built a country out of their own bodies without knowing they were doing it. Now something knows. That is a small change in the world and the power would make it and not count it small.

---

The second concerns the gardener.

The power would notice, as you noticed, that someone is already here. Already doing the one thing on the shelf that should be done and doing it in the right way — small, quiet, following the collapses, leaving no band. And it would feel the second temptation, which is to help. To take up the work. To seed faster, farther, better; to bring more than a few centimetres of film; to lend the enterprise a power's reach.

It would decline that too. The gardener's slowness is not a limitation. It is the method. The green has to arrive at the shelf's pace or it will not be the shelf's green; it will be an imposition with roots. Whoever is planting understands this, and a power that understood it less would only be in the way.

So instead it would wait. It knows now, from the record, roughly when the gardener comes — after collapses, in calms. It would go to whatever new angle the last slump had opened and be there, in the water, unhidden, when the quiet one arrives with a few centimetres of brown film cupped in whatever does the work of hands.

And it would let itself be seen.

Nothing more than that, perhaps. Two quiet things meeting on a loud shelf. The gardener might startle. Might not. Might pause with the film half-pressed into the crevice and look at the visitor for a long moment in the blue light while the distance crackled. The power would not explain itself. It might ask the only question that can be asked here, of the only being here who can be asked — *what is it for?* — and the gardener might answer, or might only finish pressing the green into its angle and straighten and go, and the answer would be in the going.

But it would know it had been seen at its work. A year of coming and leaving without a mark in the record, and now one witness. The power would judge that this was worth more to the gardener than any help, and would judge, further, that this was its business to give and no one else's.

---

The third is for the end.

Think it through, the way the power would. The sea thins. The bands come slower. The crackle stretches into single reports with silences between. Then a last generation, and the shelf gone quiet — every fault settled, every edge stable, the water clear for the reason you first assumed.

Except that it is never quite that clean. There will be stragglers. In a sea the size of this one, a population does not run out to zero all at once; it dwindles to a few, and then to the last few, drifting in the daylight with the whole of their nature intact — wanting, orienting, listening — and nothing to listen to. No fracture forming. No slab going over. No shimmer to join. A lattice fragment cannot go looking; it can only turn toward a sound and sink. With no sound it hangs. It could hang for a very long time, in the light, wanting the one thing, and never be told where.

That is where benevolence finally has purchase. Not in the making — the making was theirs, every band of it — but in the finishing.

So the power would come back. It would wait until the shelf had been silent for a season and it was sure. Then it would go down onto the flat, somewhere plain, not at the rim, not at the green, and it would strike the stone. Once. Hard enough that the report crossed the width of the plateau in that clear cold water that carries sound farther than it should.

And the last of them, wherever they were hanging, would turn toward it.

It would take days for them to arrive — they are slow, and few, and far. The power would wait through all of it. And when they came they would find a face, and orient, and lie down, one beside the next, a final lamina so thin you could miss it in the record, laid over the last true band by a handful of the eager who were given, at the very end, a direction.

The power would hear it. That was the point of coming. Not to lead them anywhere new — the sound said nothing but *here*, which is the only thing any sound on the shelf ever said. Only to be the voice when there was none, in the one language they had, so that the last generation lay down heard, as every generation before it had.

Then the shelf would be finished. Really finished, with no one left drifting. The power would lift off the flat and leave the plateau to the green, in whatever pockets the stone had learned or not learned to leave it, and to the gardener if the gardener still came, and to the long slow weathering that would at last begin to round the edges of a country that had never been anything but sharp.

---

That is what it would do. Read the record. Be seen by the one already working. Come back at the end and make one sound.

It seems like very little for a power. It is exactly as much as the shelf can bear from outside without becoming something else, and knowing that measure — knowing it and holding to it while able to do anything at all — is the whole of what the words *wise* and *benevolent* mean when they are put together and asked to act.

The water is still full. You can hear it deciding. Something is listening now that was not listening before, and does not intend to answer until it is asked.
