It comes in the way everything comes in: up the cold, through the hatch, down into the warm. It does not need the ladder, but it uses it, because the ladder is how the place is approached, and a power that is wise arrives at a thing by the road the thing has made for arriving. Its fingers ache on the rungs. It lets them.

Then the water, to the neck, and the squares of light.

It does what you did first, which is nothing. It floats. It lets the leaves press up around it. It rests a palm on the shaft housing and feels the hum in its teeth. It waits for the check valve and is not sure, when the knock comes, whether it had known the moment or only known that a moment would come. It is very old and has seen a great many made places, and most of them it has seen at the point where the making has begun to fail, because that is when attention is called for and attention is what it is. This one it has come to early. The concrete is still pale. Nothing has fallen.

---

The first thing wisdom does, here, is ask whether there is anything to do, and the honest answer takes a while.

It looks for hurt. There is none it can find. The plant is not suffering; the plant is holding, and holding is what it does, and the yellow stems are sheddings, not wounds. The film is fed and warm and in the dark it likes. The keeper — it reads the keeper in the cut stems and the old line exactly as you did — comes and floats and goes, and comes back, and does not appear to come back out of duty alone. Nothing here is asking. A place that is not asking is a place a lesser power would begin improving at once, and the power knows this, and knows that the impulse is its own and not the tank's, and sets it down.

It looks for wrongness. The one thing that reads as wrong at a first glance — no snails, no fish, a warm green pond sealed against everything that ought to seethe in it — it turns over, and finds is not wrong but only narrow. A household of three. Three is a small number for a world. It is not a wrong number.

So: nothing to heal. Then it does the second thing wisdom does, which is to look at what is whole and ask how it will break.

---

It finds the fault lines, and they are these.

The plant is one. Every stem is the cutting. There has never been seed, so there has never been a difference, so there is nothing in the tank that would survive what the rest would not. A single blight coming up the penstock — one spore in the grey, small enough for the strainers — would cross the forest the way the plant crossed the machinery, and there would be nothing to stop it, because stopping it would require one stem to be other than its neighbours, and no stem is. The oneness that lets the plant know every slot by touch is the same oneness that means it can be killed by a single thing, once.

The warmth is a cable. One cable, as thick as an arm, running up the tower to the coils. The city keeps it live because the city wants clear water, and the city will keep it live for exactly as long as it costs less than a chemical dose. Somewhere down in the grid there is a ledger, and the tank is a line in it, and the power has read a great many ledgers and knows that a line which costs money and works by means nobody has fully accounted for is a line that gets questioned eventually.

The film is a secret. It does the eating and nobody knows it is there. That is a safe way to be until someone comes up the ladder to find out why the water is so clear, and looks in the root hair, and sees a mote in a system built to have none, and does what such systems do with motes.

The keeper is one also. One pair of hands, one blade, one interval. They have learned where to cut and the plant has learned to be cut there. When they stop climbing — and they will, being what they are — the outflow will mat in whatever interval it takes, and the level will rise past the hand's breadth, and the leaves will be pressed into the glass past the point of pressing, and the plant will drown in its own light. Nothing will have gone wrong. Someone will simply have not come.

Four ways for a whole thing to end, and none of them near, and all of them certain.

---

What it could do, being what it is: a great deal.

It could give the plant seed. Reach into the cutting's inheritance and turn one key, and next season a stem would put up something at the surface that was not a leaf. The forest would begin, after that, to be many. Some stems would be a shade darker, some would clench a bolt a little differently, and one day some would be the ones the blight did not take. The power holds this in its hand and turns it over and puts it back. To give the plant seed is to give it pollen, and pollen on the glass is a mote, and the keeper would find it, and the ledger would hear. And more than that: it is to give the plant the other kind of death, the kind where the thing that comes after you is not you. The plant has never had that. It has been alive since the water went in and never once been anything but alive. It did not ask to be made mortal in that direction, and the power is not sure that a being which knows the world entirely by holding on to it would count the exchange as a kindness.

It could make the cable eternal. It refuses. Nothing is, and a place kept by a miracle is no longer kept by anyone.

It could speak. It considers, for a long moment, what speech would be to a thing that knows only by touch, and decides that speech would be a hand on the coil housing, or a knock through the water like a valve seating, and that it has no more to say to the plant than the valve does. The plant is not lonely. Loneliness needs the idea of a second, and the plant's whole life has been a walk across surfaces toward light, and there has never been a second in it, and to give it one would be to give it the lack first.

It could, at the least, ensure that when the sampler comes up the ladder to look in the root hair, they take their sample from the wrong root. That one it nearly does. It is small and it is deniable and the film would go on eating for another decade. But the film is not the power's secret to keep. It is the plant's, and the keeper's, insofar as the keeper's hand has been in the root mass and felt the slickness and not said. Someone in the household already knows. Someone in the household has already decided.

---

So this is what it does.

It watches the keeper cut a yellow stem at the base and lay it on the catwalk, and haul the row of them up on the crane line, and lift them out through the hatch into the thin air. It goes out with them. It has not, until now, followed anything down.

They fall a long way. They freeze in the first hundred feet and come apart in the wind, and most of them land as threads on roofs and in the haze and are nothing. The power does not save them. It watches for the one that has kept, by chance, a root mass at the base — a clench of root around nothing, still dense, still slick — and as it falls past the last of the cold it gives it the smallest push it has ever given anything. The width of a street.

The stem comes down behind a laundry, in the gutter under a vent where the wash-water runs off warm, grey and full of what a city puts into water. It lies there. It is one cutting. It has, in the root mass, everything it needs — has had it since the first day, since the nursery nobody remembers.

That is one thing.

The second is smaller. It goes back up, and in, and floats where the keeper is floating, longer than the work needs, face in a square of light. It does not touch them. It does not say anything, because the keeper is not a thing that knows by speech either, not up here. It only stays beside them for the length of one check-valve interval, and in that quiet the keeper has a thought they have not had before, which is a thought about the ladder, and about who else has hands, and whether they might be brought up it. It is not a large thought. It does not resolve. It is the kind of thought that sits in a person and ripens across a year and comes out one morning as an invitation to someone younger. The power puts nothing into it. It only stays until the thought arrives, and then it goes.

The third is that it does nothing about the ledger, or the blight, or the cable. It has decided that these are the tank's own weather, and that a place which cannot have weather cannot have a household either.

---

It leaves by the ladder. It does not need to. It climbs down through the cold with its fingers aching, past the rime on the flanges, into the haze, and at the bottom, in the grey, it does not go to the laundry. It does not go to see whether the cutting has rooted. It does not stay to see whether the keeper brings anyone. It has a great many made places to attend to and most of them are already failing, and this one was not, and it has given it the least it could think of: a second place to be, and the beginning of a second pair of hands, and otherwise its own life to lose in its own time.

High up, in the warm and the clear, a root that has never let go of anything is crossing the catwalk toward the next slot.

Somewhere in the city, in a gutter behind a laundry, another one — very small, and wet, and the temperature of the wash-water — is feeling along the concrete for anything at all to hold.
