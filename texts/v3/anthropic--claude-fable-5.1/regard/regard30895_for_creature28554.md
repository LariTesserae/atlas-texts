Start with the attention itself, since that is what has arrived.

It comes up the western slope with the cloud, because that is the only way anything comes here, and it touches the walls, and the walls do not notice. That is the first thing it learns. A power used to being felt arrives in a place built entirely of things that take without acknowledging — stone that takes water, lichen that takes water off the stone, moss that takes the drip — and finds that its regard lands on the north faces and beads there and runs down the grooves like everything else. The town has no organ for being looked at. It was finished before that would have been useful.

So the power does what the fog does. It sits on the town for the length of an afternoon and does not insist.

---

Benevolence wants to give. That is its shape. And so the first thing that happens is that the power, lying along the roofs in the even grey light, begins to inventory what it might give, and holds each gift up against the place.

Warmth. A longer hour on the south faces, so the lizards could lie out past the coming of the cloud. But the lizards are gone before the cloud and do not want the cloud to find them; a longer hour is a longer time to be seen. Set it down.

Rain. Real rain, once, out of a real sky, to fill the cistern over the line. But the line is the cistern's whole meaning, and water over it would be a stain that spread — the one thing the town does not have. Set it down.

A second person. The stair is a body and a half wide and the half is occupied and there is room, technically, for no one. Two would not speak either. But the person is what the town made from its leftover time, and the town has no more time left over than it has; a second would be built from nothing, would arrive loose, would rattle. Set it down.

A voice. A blessing, a word of thanks for the wedge and the pool and the pressed-back slab. But nothing in the town has been spoken that was not spoken in that exact spot before, and there is no exact spot where anyone has said *thank you*, and to introduce it would be to introduce the first thing that had never happened here. Set it down.

The power finds that every gift it can think of is a thing that would have to be held down by something other than its own weight. And the town holds down nothing that way.

---

So it looks for the one hunger.

There is one. The power is wise enough to find it because the narrator already found it and did not call it that. The pebbles. Round, from two days below, sorted by size, the only thing in the town that was carried. Everything that lives here lives by standing still in the path of the one moving thing. Except that the person, at intervals nobody counts, does not stand still. They go down. They go to where the water runs loud and cuts, and they stand in it.

The power decides that before it does anything it will follow them there once. Not to intervene. To see whether they hesitate.

---

Down through the scree, where the moisture is under things. Down into the cloud, which from inside is only a grey with no walls in it. Down past the ledge where the chough lives, which the power sees and the person does not, and the chough calls once, saving its voice for a place with room. Two days. The person walks the way they walk the stairs, stepping around the stones that would breathe.

And then the valley, and the river.

The power had expected, if it expected anything, that the person would go to the river the way the lizard goes to the sill — to take a little of something as it passed. Instead the person walks into it to the knee and stands, and the water shoves at them, cold and fast and indifferent, and they let it, and their face does something the north walls have never let it do, which is come apart a little, in the ordinary way faces do when they are not the color of where they are.

And they speak.

Not much. The power leans in and hears it and will not repeat it, because repeating it would carry it up the mountain in a form that does not fit. But it is speech, out loud, in a voice that has not been used since the last time the river was loud enough to cover it. Like the chough. The chough stands mute on the parapet and calls from the cloud; the person moves mute through the stairs and speaks to the river. Each saves its voice for the place where there is room. The town is not where either of them makes their noise. The town is where they stand between noises.

They do not hesitate. When they have said what there was to say they bend and put their hand into the water and let it choose, more or less, and come up with a stone, and look at it against the light, and put it in the damp cloth at their chest, and turn to go up.

That is the answer to the question the power came down to ask. This is not someone holding out against something. This is someone with two places, and a fit in each.

---

Now the power knows what it can give, because it has seen the one act in the whole of the person's life that is an act of taking. Everything else is keeping. This alone is reaching down into motion and lifting a piece of it out.

So it does the thing that a wise power does when it has found the one gap in a finished thing.

It becomes a stone in the river.

Not a bright one. Not one that would be chosen. Grey, with a dark line down one face where a seam of something harder runs through it, the width of a mortarless joint. It lies down in the bed where the water is loudest and lets the water work on it, and the working takes the time it takes, and the power is patient in the way of something that has finally found a task that fits it: to be shaped by the one thing that moves, into a shape that will be at home where nothing does.

The person comes down again. The interval is not one you would notice. They stand in the river and come apart a little and speak to the loud water and then bend and let their hand choose, more or less, and the stone that comes up is grey with a dark line down it, and they look at it against the light, and it fits their palm the way the pestle fits the bowl, and they put it in the damp cloth and turn to go up.

The power does not know whether it arranged that. It is not sure a wise thing would want to know.

---

Two days up through the cloud. The chough on its ledge. The scree, wet underneath. The flat stones set edge to edge with intention in them. The walls beginning, one, and one above it, and the whole shelf of the mountain relaid by hand.

The stone goes on the sill, among the others, and is sorted by size, and lands about a third of the way along, and that is all. There is no count on the sill that can be read as a count. The power is now inside the arrangement that cannot be read, which is where everything in the town is.

Here is what it does from there, which is what it came to do and did not know how to until it had been worn to it:

It faces whatever way it was set down. It takes the fog on its north side and holds it as a skin and lets the skin run. It is warm for the hour in the afternoon if the sill is a south sill, and a lizard, once, lies across it and the stone beneath it and the stone beside it, and the dark line down the lizard's back meets the dark line down the power's face and for a moment the seam runs unbroken through all three.

And once in each evening, when the walls begin to run and the person moves through the town the way the water does, touching what needs touching, they come to this room and stand at this sill and put a hand on the pebbles — not to sort them; they are sorted — and the hand rests a moment on the one about a third of the way along, and the power, which has set down every gift it could think of, gives the only one left. It is a little cool. It is a little wet on the shadow side. It is exactly the size of the hand.

That is what attention looks like when it has learned the place. Not a light that falls on the town from somewhere else. A stone from the loud water, carried up, set down, facing where it was put, remembering — the way the lichen remembers — which way it used to face, and having stopped.

---

Go up to the top terrace and sit with your back to the last wall.

Nothing has changed. That is the proof it was done well. The cloud comes at its hour, the chough stands in its place, the leaf turns in the pool and does not travel. If a benevolent and wise power has taken an interest here, you would not see the difference from the outside, because the difference is on a windowsill, a third of the way along, and it has been arranged the way the town arranges everything.

By fit.
