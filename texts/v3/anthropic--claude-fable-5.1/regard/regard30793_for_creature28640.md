Attention arrives the way the heat did, before there is anything for it to look at. It does not come down the slot. It does not come up through the seams. It is simply, at some point, here — in the way a room is quiet and then is being listened to, and nothing in the room has changed.

It takes its time. This is the first thing that can be said about it, and it is the thing that earns the word *wise*: it does not arrive with a hand already out. It stands in the heat the way the visitor stood, and lets its eyes, if it has eyes, finish adjusting, and lets the shape of the space suggest itself, and finds the lamp, and finds the garden, and finds the pipes, and finds, on the walls and in the seams and in the tin, the slow pale handwriting of the thing that lives here. It reads all of it. It reads the growth rings the visitor could not see without a lens. It reads the pinhole. It knows without being told that the cable is thinning.

And the first answer is the honest one, and it is that it does nothing.

Not from indifference. From looking. Benevolence is a hand held out, and a hand held out wants somewhere to land, and the attention goes looking for the somewhere and does not find it. The film cannot be helped. It has no needs it is not meeting. It has no fears; the word does not attach. It is thinnest where the light is and thickest in the seams, and that is not a plight, that is a shape, and the shape is the whole of what it is. To do something *for* it would be to imagine it as a thing that could be grateful, or relieved, or spared, and a wise power is, before anything else, careful about what it imagines. The film is the one thing in the chamber that is exactly where it belongs. It came up out of its own country and found iron laid across the door, and it is eating the iron, and it is at home. There is no benevolence that improves on that. There is only interference dressed as benevolence, and the attention knows the difference, because it has watched a great many hands land on a great many things that were already at home.

So it does not touch the lamp.

This is the harder part, and it is worth standing with, because the lamp is where the wanting is. The garden leans toward it. The garden has leaned toward it for years with a patience that has bent the stalks, and the garden will die when it goes, and the attention could keep it from going. It could close the circuit before the film closes it. It could, without lifting anything, give the bulb another decade. And it looks at that wanting the way it looked at the film, and reads it, and sees that the wanting is its own. The garden is not asking. The garden is a handful of seeds that fell in the wrong place and did what seeds do, and the leaning is not longing, it is geometry. To keep the lamp alive for the garden would be to keep a mistake burning out of tenderness for the mistake, and the tenderness would be real, and it would still be about the one who felt it.
