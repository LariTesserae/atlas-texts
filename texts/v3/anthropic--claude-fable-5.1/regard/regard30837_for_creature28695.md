It came the way the egret comes: low, from the quarter the wind is always from, and it did not land at first. It hung over the leaf-crown for what would have been, to anyone below, a long time, and what was for it the ordinary interval of looking before touching. It had a rule about that. It had learned the rule the way the roller-men learned theirs, from what happens when you don't keep it.

The rule was: first find out what you are looking at.

---

So it counted. Forty roofs. Two hundred people, give or take the grandmother. One cat. One egret, itself excluded. One engine, one stack, one barge that was elsewhere. One causeway in, one canal out. And one plant.

It stopped there, because *one plant* had come out of the count as a figure of speech and it wanted to know whether it was.

It went down into the mat. Not with hands — it had no need of hands — but with attention, which is what it had instead, and it followed a rhizome the colour of tendon back from under the loading dock, and the rhizome did not end. It ran under the town and out along a strip named for a dead man and turned there, at the strip's far edge, and came back by another road, and it met itself, and it met itself again, and after a while the power stopped following and simply held the whole extent of it in view at once, and saw that the figure of speech was a census.

The marsh was one individual. Not a stand of a species — one body, a single organism with a hundred thousand mouths held up to the grey light, older than the causeway, older than the first cutting of the oldest strip, older than the word *fern* in its local sense. The town was in it the way the ram's cup was in the beam: a shape that had been struck into a living thing so many times that the thing had grown to receive the blow. The cutters were its pruning. The engine was its fire. The tanks were its rot. The stack was its breath going up and coming down again as rain onto its own leaves, and the people had said *this is not a metaphor* and had been more right than they knew, because a metaphor has two sides and this had one.

That was the being the power had been drawn to. It sat with that for a while.

---

Then it considered the people, because they were also there, and because a power that could see the cane and not the cutters would not be wise, only large.

It considered what would be easy. It could bring birds; it knew where birds were. It could bring frogs to the tanks and make the resin not kill them. It could dry the air for a season and stop the ferning. It could send a doctor down the canal, or a second engine, or a rumour of higher ground. It could make the roller-men's hands open all the way at their own tables. Every one of these was within its reach and none of them was hard, and it noticed, as it always did at this point, how much *easy* wanted to be mistaken for *good*.

Birds would eat what lived on the leaves and the leaves would be marked and the marking would be a wound and the cane, which had lived for a century in a world with nothing in it but itself and its pruners, would meet something it had no shape for. Frogs in the tanks would be a marvel for a week and a smell for a month and then a thing the tank-women worked around, and their knowledge, which was of bubbles and skins and slowness, would have a new variable in it that no one had asked for. A dry season would end and the wet would come back, as it did, and the ferning would come back, as it did, and the only new thing would be that the people would now know the ferning as a thing that *came back*, and would wait for it.

And the people were not asking. That was the plain fact the power kept returning to. Not because they were content — it did not think *content* was the word for a woman who could no longer smell the liquor she stood in — but because they had no distance from which to ask. The question had closed over like the mat. Nobody considered the absence of birds a question because a question needs an edge, a place where the thing stops and something else starts, and this place had spent a hundred years sanding its edges off.

It considered the roller-men who were not there. It considered the child near the engine, ferning, sent back, ferning. It weighed these against the arrangement and did not find the arithmetic easy, and it did not pretend to.

It considered doing nothing, and gave that option the same seriousness as the others, and found that nothing was also an act, and had a face, and the face was a child's, pink at the neck.

---

So it asked itself a smaller question. Not *what is wrong here* — it could not honestly say that anything was wrong here that was not also wrong everywhere, in a slower way. It asked: *what is this place missing that is not a thing?*

And the answer came back: contrast.

Nothing here was ever not itself. The wet was never not wet. The noise was never not noise; the ten quiet minutes at shift change were the loudest part of the day, and the people had learned to feel them in their teeth and not in their thoughts. The light had no direction. The shadows had no edge. The people's hands had been in the same water so long that the water had become the shape of their hands. The cane grew into every gap, and so there were no gaps, and so the cane could not know, either, where it ended. A being with no edge has no way of being one thing rather than everything. That was true of the marsh, and the power saw, with something like tenderness, that it had become true of the town as well.

You cannot give a place an edge by adding to it. You can only give it one by taking something away, briefly, so that the thing left behind can feel its own outline.

---

It changed the wind.

Not much. The wind had come from one quarter for so long that the stack's breath leaned like a tree, and the power turned it the width of two hands, and the cloud that had sat over the marsh like a lid found itself being drawn off toward the canal, and behind it there was nothing. Not sun, exactly — the power was not so crude as sun. A high pale sky, dry, with a colour in it nobody in the town had a word for, and an air moving under it that took water instead of giving it.

That was all. One day.

---

The first thing was the sound.

The hinges in the cane changed pitch. All morning, as the moisture left the leaf-edges, the slow dry turning became something quicker and sharper, a ticking like a cooling stove, and the leaf-crown that had met over the causeway and dripped began, culm by culm, to lift. It was only a few degrees. It was enough. By mid-morning there were places on the gangways where a person could look up without looking straight up and see sky, and several did, and stopped, and stood there with the bundle on their shoulders and did not say anything.

The planks went grey. They had always been green and slick and now the slickness went off them and they showed their grain, and the soft ones showed themselves another way, by shrinking, so that for the first time the gaps in the walk were visible as gaps and not as places where your foot knew to pause. A cutter walking his ordinary careful gait put his weight down on a plank that had always been soft and found it hard and nearly went over from the surprise.

The iron stopped ferning. It did not un-fern — the red-brown feather was still there on every unpainted surface — but it stopped growing, and in the works, where a hundred surfaces had been putting out their small rust-fronds for as long as anyone had watched, a stillness settled that the roller-men noticed before they knew what they were noticing. The brass on the engine went bright by itself. Nobody had polished it.

The stack's breath went straight up. This was what stopped the town. Everyone knew the lean of it the way they knew the knock, and around noon someone came out of the low wing and looked at it standing there vertical, white, a column with no wind on it, and called, and the moulders came out, and the tank-women, and they stood on the dock with their long soft amber hands and looked at the steam going straight up into a sky with a colour in it, and the cat came out of the drying wing and sat in the doorway and blinked.

The tank-woman smelled the ret. She had been standing beside it for thirty years and now, in this air, the liquor's smell was suddenly *a smell*, separate from her, coming up off the skin of the tank in a way she could stand outside of, and she made a face — the face a barge-man makes — and then put her hand to her mouth, appalled at herself.

The roller-man sat down to eat at the break and reached for his cup and felt the edge of the table under his knuckles. Felt it. The table had an edge, and his hand had been holding itself back from that edge for thirty years, and today, for no reason, the edge was simply *there*, hard and dry and exactly where it was, and his fingers rested on it, and he looked at them.

The people's fingerpads went smooth. They noticed it on each other before on themselves — a hand passing a bundle, and the hand that took it was *not wrinkled*, and both people stopped and looked at the hands as if they belonged to barge-men. The pink at the child's neck went pale in the afternoon and the child, who had been kept near the engine for a week to no purpose, stood in the open on the causeway with the wind on her and felt, for the first time in her life, cold. She did not know the word. She used the word for *dry* and it did not fit and she knew it did not fit, and that was the beginning of something.

The egret came at its hour, and landed on the rail, and stood. It did not eat; the power had put nothing in the water for it. But it stood in the dry light for longer than twenty minutes, and it cast a shadow — a hard shadow, black, with an edge, the shape of an egret, on the planks of the dock — and the children, who knew it and did not chase it, sat down a little way off and looked at the shadow and not at the bird.

And the cane. The power watched the cane most carefully of all, because it was the being, and because it could not ask.

The cane tightened. All that day, as the water went off it, the culms drew in against themselves and the leaf-blades curled a fraction along their length, and the whole vast body of the marsh, which had never in a century of days needed to hold anything against loss, held. It was not distress. The power was fairly sure it was not distress. It was the body of a thing discovering the shape of itself by feeling where the world stopped supplying it, and the ticking that ran across the marsh from horizon to horizon in the afternoon was, if it was anything, the sound of a being finding out that it had a surface.

---

The rain came back at dusk. The power let it. The wind turned its two hands' width and the cloud came down off the canal and settled, and the leaf-crown drank and softened and lowered and met again over the causeway and began to drip, and the planks went green, and the stack's breath leaned, and by morning the iron was ferning as if it had never stopped.

Nothing had changed. That was the point. The power had been careful to change nothing. It had not brought birds or frogs or doctors or a second engine. It had not cured the child. The forty-year clock was still turning and the barge was still coming and the roller-men's hands, when they woke, curled back from the edge of the table exactly as before.

Except that the roller-man remembered the edge. His fingers did. And the tank-woman knew, now, that the ret had a smell, and she could not unknow it, and she found over the following weeks that she had begun to breathe through her mouth at the tanks and could not have said when she started. And the child had a word that did not fit, and kept it, and used it on things it did not fit either, and was told to stop, and did not.

And out on the strips named for the dead, the cane went back to growing into the gaps, which is what it was and all it wanted, and it did not remember the day; it was not built to. But the power, before it went, put its attention once more into the mat and followed the rhizome a little way under the town, and found — it could not have proved this, and did not try — that the body of the marsh was holding itself a fraction differently than before. Not tighter. Not looser. Only as if it now knew, in the way a body knows and a mind does not, that there was an outside to it. That the world could stop.

---

The power had a rule about leaving, too. It was: leave before you are needed.

So it went, the way it had come, low over the leaf-crown toward the quarter the wind was from, and it did not know if it had done right. It had given two beings one day of edge and taken the day back, and left them the memory, which was the only thing it could give that would not become, in a season, another part of the arrangement. Whether the memory would do anything was not its business. It had put the question back into a place that had lost the shape for one, and a question is not an answer, and it had never pretended otherwise.

Behind it the knock resumed — *chock* — and the cane went back beneath the noise, where it lived, where it had always lived. But a child on the causeway was standing with her face turned into the wet wind, trying to feel the thing she had felt, and not feeling it, and knowing that she wasn't.
