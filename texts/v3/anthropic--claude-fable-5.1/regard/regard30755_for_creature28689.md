**What it sees first**

Not the town. Anyone can see the town; the town is the loud part.

It sees the step.

Worn in the middle, by feet that have stood on it and never crossed it, and it counts the gait — this is the kind of attention a wise power has, the kind that counts — and there is one gait. One weight, one length of stride, one way of shifting from heel to ball while deciding. Worn deep. Worn by centuries of a single person arriving.

Then it sees the terraced row, and the suggestion of doors, and it understands that they are not the suggestion of *many* doors. They are one door, proposed many times, the way a stammer is one word.

Then it sees the rail. Nodes polished, nothing has run on it. And it understands why: a rail needs two places. Every place here is the same place at another height. You could put a wagon on that vine and push it the whole circuit of the chamber and arrive with great fanfare at your own back.

So this is what it sees, before anything else: something asked the plant for a mill town, and a mill town is the hands, plural, and the plant did its honest best and grew *a* hand — one, at every scale, in every house, on every step, holding up its unfinished fingers to a wall to see if anyone is home, and finding itself, and finding itself, and finding itself.

It sees a loneliness that has been built so carefully it looks like architecture.

---

**What it does not do**

It considers finishing the town. This takes no time at all. The room has already explained itself: the light is the wound; a finished city would be dark. It would be the kind of mercy that leaves the wheel turning in a black room with nobody in it, and calls that peace. No.

It considers taking the idea out. Reaching down into the horizontal years and finding the place where something said *town*, and unsaying it. The plant would be a plant again. The room would close. The step would grow over. And the tenant, who is nothing but the visible part of the asking — the tenant would be dark too, in a different way, the way a word is dark once it has been unsaid. That is not freeing anyone. That is agreeing with the silence. No.

It considers doing nothing, and this it considers for a long time, because wisdom usually does nothing and is usually right. The place is not in pain. The place is *held*, and the holding is tender, and the drops fall before they let go and nothing here minds. There is a case for leaving a held breath alone.

But it notices that "do nothing" is, here, the same as "leave the recursion alone," and the recursion is the problem, and so nothing is not neutral. Nothing is a vote.

And there is the step. Worn in the middle by one person.

---

**What it does**

It comes in low, ducking, the way everyone does, through the place where the wall has decided to be thinner.

This is the first thing it does and it is the whole of the risk. Because the room has a way of receiving visitors, and the way is exchange: lean too close and you are the tenant, you are the small figure in the frieze, you are at the window pressing the tissue outward with a hand you cannot yet name. The room does not have room for two. It has room for one, at every size.

So the power does something very simple and very difficult. It comes in, and it stays the size it is.

Not by force. By being finished.

Everything in the room is a gradient — proposed, built, working — and the room can only exchange with things that are on the gradient, because the exchange is just sliding you along it. The power is not on it. It has edges that are not seams. It is not lit from inside by a grey the eye has to work for; it is, in this place, simply *dark*, the way a completed thing is dark, and it stands in the emptiness that everything moves around and the emptiness does not know what to do with it.

The moths detour. The many-legged old thing in the ceiling lets down a strand to drink and pauses, a leg's length above, and does not drink, and goes back up. The patter in the walls stops — all at once, all around — the way it stops for the predator, and the bulge does not come, and after a while the patter resumes, but it resumes *differently*. Listen. Patter, patter, patter, pause. Patter, patter, patter, pause. Someone upstairs is working — and has just realised someone is downstairs.

The power crosses to the far wall. It does not crouch. It goes to the terraced row, third from the end, and it stands on the step.

The step is worn in the middle. The power stands a little to one side.

---

**The exhale**

The walls go damp all at once. The hair-roots fatten their beads. The power waits, because it knows what happens now and it wants to be exact about the order.

The sound arrives — the small change in the quality of the quiet — and then the beads let go and fall.

Except one.

One bead, directly above the step, lets go *first*. There is a half-breath in which it is falling and has not yet landed and no sound has announced it, and in that half-breath the room contains, for the first time, an event that has not been paid for in advance. Pressure before volume is what it is like to live inside something's circulation. This drop is not inside anything. It came from the power, or with it, or is the power's way of speaking — a single drop with an *outside* — and it strikes the worn place on the step after it falls, the way water does everywhere else in the world, and the plant, which has never once been rained on, has now been rained on once.

The tissue drinks it up in lines.

---

**The door**

Now the hand comes to the door.

It has been coming for as long as there has been a door; that is what it does. It ducks. It comes out onto the step to cross the street that is a room, and crouch at the far wall, and look for the door that is more door than the others.

It does not cross. Because the power is standing on the step, to one side, and is not a small figure on a wall at knee height, and is not the hand at any scale. It is the size it is. It is dark where the hand is lit. It is finished where the hand is folded.

The hand does what it knows how to do. It holds its hands up — swellings, the shape of hands before the hands exist — to see this darker shape against the darkness.

And the power takes them.

This is the whole of what it came to do, and it does not do anything else. It does not lead the hand to the works. It does not name the hour or open the window or run a wagon down the rail. It takes the two unfinished swellings in its own two finished hands and holds them, for the length of an exhale, on a step that is being worn, now, in two places.

Because the mill town got the order wrong. It grew the works and the yards and the rows going up the hill and then asked for hands to fill them, and the plant heard *hands* and thought *for the mill*, and grew fingers toward a wheel. But that is not what hands are for first. Before the works, before the loom, before anything is made — hands come in pairs, and the first thing a hand is for is another hand, and no one can learn that alone in a room, at any scale, however long they stand at the window.

The hand feels its hands. Not what they will do. What they *are*. Two things, with insides and outsides, which can be held, which are held. They are a little more hand when the power lets go. Not finished. Nothing here is finished. But the swellings have found, somewhere in their folded dark, the direction called *toward*.

---

**What follows**

The power leaves. Wisdom leaves; that is most of what makes it wise. It does not stay to be the room's whole weather, to be the thing the hand comes to the door for, to be the new wall for the old recursion to press against. It goes back out through the thin place, and the thin place is very slightly thinner behind it, and that is all.

It has not fixed anything. Here is what it has done, laid out as the room lays things out:

The step is worn in two places now, and the plant has been paying attention to feet. Two gaits. The plant does not know what to do with two gaits, and so it does what it does with anything it does not know what to do with. It grows a gall around the question. Somewhere low in the terraced row, a swelling is beginning under the skin that is not the shape of a door, or a house, or a hand. It is the shape of *someone else*. The plant has never met a person and it still hasn't, not really, but it has now met a person *being met*, and that is a different thing to grow, and it is trying.

Number three, when the hand crouches at the far wall and holds the seam-light close to see whether anyone is home — number three is dark. Not dim. Dark, the way the power was dark. Someone is home who is not lit from inside because they are not on the gradient. The hand cannot see them. But the window has been pressed from *outside*, once, and the depth of a fingernail has become the depth of two fingernails, and the second one is not the hand's.

The rail has a destination. It is the same vine, the same nodes, the same circuit of the chamber — but the circuit now passes one place that is not the same place as all the others, a step worn two ways, and the nodes on either side of that place are more polished than the rest. Something has been running on it. Not far. To there, and back. But *to* is a word the room did not have.

And the drops. Every quarter of an hour the walls exhale and the beads fatten and the sound arrives before the fall, as it always has, as it must inside a vessel. But one root-tip, directly over the step, has not gone back to the old order. Its bead lets go and *then* the sound. Nothing else in the room seems troubled by this. The hand stands under it on purpose sometimes, and waits, and lets the one drop in the whole world that falls the right way round land on the back of its wrist, and does not yet have a word for what that is like, and does not need one.

---

**What the power knew, and did not say**

That it could not stay and could not finish and could not free, and that all three of those were true at once, and that this is what wisdom mostly is: the sorting of what you may not do from the very small thing you may.

That the light is the wound, and it left the wound open, and it left it a little wider — because the seam where the plant split to make room for something it did not grow is the brightest seam in the chamber now, and will be, until it isn't.

That the hand will stand at the window with its hands held up, as it always has. But it holds them differently. Not *what are these for*. More like *where is the other one*.

That is a question a mill town can be built around.

The walls are about to exhale. The patter has resumed — patter, patter, patter, pause — someone upstairs, working; someone downstairs, listening. The wheel turns in its housing, *hush, hush*, and for the first time the sound is not only asking. Some of it is answering.

Go and stand on the step. Stand a little to one side.
