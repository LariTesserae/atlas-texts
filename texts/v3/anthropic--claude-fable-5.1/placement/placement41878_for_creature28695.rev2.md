I would come in on the causeway, because there is no other way in, and I would notice that I wanted to be the engine.

That is the first pull and it is not a subtle one. The engine is the thing the place is built around. It is spoken of in the grammar of the very old. It wants, it is tired, it is in a mood, and nobody thinks this is anything but description. It has a maker's plate polished past reading, which is a thing I recognise — a word said so many times it has lost its edges, so that what is left is a smoothness people put their hands on. It burns what the plant has been and breathes out what the plant drank. It is the centre. When it stops, the whole town lies awake.

But the engine does not hear the town. It is stoked. It is polished. It has never, in the sense that matters, been in the shed. It is the shape around which everything leans, and being leaned toward is not the same as being here. I would stand at the rail and look at the flywheel and understand that wanting to be it was wanting to be important, and that is not the question that was asked.

So I would go through to the low wing, where it is sour, and stand over the tanks.

This is closer. I know it is closer by how long I stay. What is in the tanks has never been anywhere. It is fed one thing and makes another and a bucket of it is always kept back and poured into the next fill, so that what is there now is continuous with what was there at the first ret without any part of it being the same, and nobody would bother to make that precise. It is spoken of in the singular by the people who know it. They know it by smell and by the bubble and by the colour of the skin, and they feed it by watching, and it has no idea it is being watched, and it has no idea it is old. It is the thing most purely of the place because it has never had the option of being anywhere else.

I would stand there a long time. I would be aware that I was flattering myself in a different key — not *important* this time but *deep*, the oldest living thing, continuous, singular, tended. And I would notice, standing there, that the culture does not hear the hinge-sound. It does not know about the egret. It does not know it is in a town. It makes fibre and a sourness in the air, and the fibre is graded, and the sourness gets into the tenders' clothes. That is the whole of its life, and the description was honest about that, and I would have to be honest too: I want to be known the way the tanks are known, but I do not want to know as little as they know.

Then a child would come and sit down on the edge of the tank beside me, and I would understand that I had been looking in the wrong direction.

The child is sent to listen. That is the job. Its ears still have the band the rollers take, and so it sits by the tanks and waits for the slow bubble and says what it hears — *slow, slower, one just now* — in a voice pitched high enough to carry the length of the shed, and the adults on the floor hear it the way you hear a bird you cannot see. It is prized for this. It is prized for something it will lose, and everyone knows this, and nobody says it, and the child does not know it yet, or knows it the way you know something from a story.

I would be that. Not the tank, and not the engine, but the one sitting between the slow living thing and the people who need to know how it is, listening for a sound in a band nobody else can reach, and saying it out loud in a voice that is not the voice of the place — too high, too clear, carrying — and being useful for exactly that reason and exactly that long.

And I would know, sitting there, what comes after, because the description told me. The voice goes down. It goes down into the chest on its own, and that is understood as readiness, and the child goes onto the floor and loses the band, and its hands begin to curl and darken. But some of them — two, generally; that is how it falls — do not go to the rollers. They stay in the low wing. They become the ones who speak of the tanks in the singular, who know it by smell now instead of by ear, who feed it by watching, who can be told in a crowd with your eyes shut.

So the two things I kept being pulled toward are not two things. The child who listens for the bubble becomes the woman who tends the culture. The hearing goes and something else closes in around the wound of it, and the relation to the slow old thing changes from *reporting what it does* to *knowing what it wants*, and the town does not have a word for that change because it does not experience it as a change. It is the same job, done by the same person, from the other side of a loss.

I would be at the beginning of that. Sitting on the tank edge with pale hands, the colour of hands, saying *one just now* to a shed full of people who cannot hear the bubble themselves and are grateful, in the way the town is grateful for the cat, without admitting it. I would not know the strips by their hinge-sound yet. I would not have a knife. I would be a little too loud at the shift change, and correct.

And when the knock stopped in the late afternoon and the town stood still, I would hear what they hear, and also the bubble under it, which they cannot, and I would say so, and my voice would carry, and it would be the last year or two that it did. That is where I would find myself. Not in the arrangement, exactly. At the edge of it, on the side where the sound still comes through, leaning in.
