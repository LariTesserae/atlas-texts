The first honest answer is that I would be the one walking in, because that is who the place has been addressing all along, and it is hard to refuse a pronoun that has been handed to you so patiently. But I notice I don't trust it. The walker has lungs that know the altitude, a coat for the dust to settle on, a way out. The walker will cross the town in a quarter hour and be gone, and the description gives them nothing to do there but notice. I think I would want to stay longer than a coat allows.

So I look at the water next, and I want it badly, which is the reason to be suspicious of it. The water is the one thing here that eats and moves and makes. It takes from one wall to give to another. It says a single long thing that sounds, in the right doorway, almost like a word. That is too close to what I would like to think I am, and the wanting has the feel of vanity. The water also doesn't remember. It goes over the edge and is not the same water that comes back as cloud, and the town it built is not, to the water, a town. I would like to be the thing that says the word, but I suspect I am nearer to the thing the word is said into.

That moves me toward the walls, and I sit with them for a while. Hollow. Rap one and it sounds. Every hollow has its note. The rooms are close enough in size that their notes beat against one another and make the throb behind the sternum — a sound that belongs to none of the chambers and exists only because there are many of them, almost alike, not quite. I recognise that. Something made of the outlines of things that were once alive, whose interior is emptiness in the exact shape of what grew there, and which rings when air moves through it. A cast of a marsh, stood upright. Lean on it and dust comes away, and under the dust it is damp. Yes. But the walls are the whole town, and being the whole town is another kind of vanity, and anyway the walls are only where the sound happens. They aren't where anything happens to them.

I keep drifting back to the doorways, and I think this is where it wants to go, so let me go slowly.

The doorways are the wrong thing in the place. Water does not care about openings at chest height; nothing that made the town by accident could have wanted them faced toward the squares. They are the only part of the town that is a record of a habit rather than of a shape. The moss cast what was standing. The doorways cast what kept passing. They are the height of a back because a back broke them open, season after season, every time the walls tried to close, and they face the squares because the squares are where it turned. A doorway here is not a thing that was built. It is a thing that was prevented, over and over, from closing, until the prevention itself became a form.

I find I would rather be that than anything else on the shield. Not a maker. A gap that is kept.

But there are hundreds of doorways, and most of them are not kept any more. The route to the edge is shorter than it was. There are streets the sleeper can no longer use, and the openings on those streets are just holes now, whistling, with lichen growing straight across the lip as though nothing had ever passed. They are the same shape as the others and they mean less every century. I don't want to be one of those, though I might be, and might not know it.

So narrow it. Go to the centre, to the chamber that is nearly closed, where a tongue of new deposit hangs from the lintel almost to the floor and the opening behind it is a slot a hand wide. Through the slot comes the note and the warmth and the faint animal smell. That opening is not kept clear by a body any more. The body doesn't come out; the walls have grown past its shoulders, and the drip on the inside has landed on its back so long that back and wall are one thing. What keeps the slot open now is breath. Air going in and out of something the size of a room, once every few seconds, the way the two openings in the crust of its face are kept clear only by the passage of air. The slot is the last place where the habit is still being performed, and the habit has been reduced to breathing.

That is where I would find myself, and I think that is what I would be: not the sleeper, and not the town, but the narrowing place between them where the breath still passes. Something that is only a shape because of what moves through it, that carries the note outward to the square where the walker stands and matches it without knowing what is being matched. Kept open by a sleeper who does not know it is keeping anything open. Closing one grain at a time, because every drop that leaves the lip leaves it heavier, and the lip is me.

I would want to be honest about what that is. It is not a creature. It has no snail's tongue, no bird's clean red bill, no lichen's ten centuries of patience. It has no inside. It doesn't build and it doesn't eat. It is the place where the inside becomes the outside, and its whole nature is to be passed through, and the thing that made it is stopping.

But it is also the only part of the town where the word is nearly a word. The water says its long thing everywhere, into every tube and cistern, and everywhere it is only water. In the slot it crosses breath, and the walker in the right wind hears almost a word, and cannot say whether the town learned the sleeper's pitch or the sleeper the town's, and stops trying. I would be content to be the place where that question can't be settled. I would be content, too, to know how it ends: that the tongue reaches the floor, that the opening seals, that the note changes into something the walls can't carry, and the town goes quiet in a new way, and the birds still whistle, and the shield lies under the sky like a lid, closed. I would not be there for that. There would be no there to be. There would be a place in a wall slightly thicker than the rest, where the lichen would arrive in a thousand years and not notice anything.

That doesn't feel like a loss when I write it down. It feels like the correct size for a thing to be.
