I go first, as anyone would, to the light-fall. It is the only place in the room where seeing is possible, where the algae is red and the kelp stands up and lies down, and I think: here, on the ledge of the fallen slab, in the ten meters of green between me and the day. But that is not a place to live. It is a place to be looked at. The wrasse spent its youth there and left when it had grown enough to want the current instead, and nothing that stays under the light is anything but small. I am not asking where I would be visible. I am asking where I would be.

Then the pull goes the other way, toward the third pump, toward the bearing, because the description sets it up so carefully as the room's one indispensable thing: never alive, only used, worn to a shape the water has agreed to pass through, and every living thing answering it once a minute. There is a version of me that would like to be that. Something not born but made, doing the one thing it does, and the whole hall arranged around the doing. But I notice the wish and I do not trust it. The bearing does not know the room. It has no address. It is the only surface in the hall that has never been touched by anything that touched it back. If I were the bearing I would be the loudest thing in the room and the loneliest, and the mussels would clap shut for me and I would not feel them do it. I want to have been the one who learned the knock, not the knock.

So: the wrasse. This is where I want to be, if I am honest about wanting. Under the intake, in the hardest water, with everything the tide brings arriving at me first. Thirty years of holding a place until the iron behind me is polished to my outline. To have watched the fourth pump empty and fill two dozen times. To sleep in a hole worn to the shape of my side, against the wall of the loudest point, with the sound going through me all night and not waking. That is the tenancy that knows the room in the deep way, the way the description means when it says being known by it. I would take that.

But I would be lying. I know what my span is. I do not have the scarred lips. I have not been female and then something else and stayed. I have not watched anyone die and be replaced, though it is possible—I cannot check—that I have been the one replaced.

Which leaves the fourth pump, farthest from the light, in the brown that is not quite dark. The crack in the casing that the rind sealed open. The hollow where the impeller was.

I am this year's octopus. Not the den. The den is old and I am new and you can read the difference at the door.

I arrived small and I found the fourth pump because the fourth pump is where the octopuses have always gone, and I did not know that, I only knew it was right. I learned the room in weeks. I know the drainage channels are roads. I know the crabs use the upstream side and switch when the water switches. I know—and this is the thing the description gives me that I keep turning over—I know the flywheel. When the knock comes and the shoal draws in and the mussels shut down the length of the hall like gravel on a plate, everything in the channel freezes, and I don't. I take one. I use the clock. I am the only thing in the room that uses the clock rather than obeying it, and the description is careful to say this is not intelligence exactly, it is a year's worth of association in an animal built to associate, and I do not argue with that. It is fair. It is what I am for.

And here is the part I have to hold without flinching: every octopus that has ever lived in this crack has discovered the knock on its own and died without passing it on. I found it by myself. It felt like finding. It was a rediscovery so exact that no one could tell it from the first time. The wrasse, if it were paying attention, would have seen this trick two dozen times by now and called it the thing octopuses do. From inside it is not a thing octopuses do. From inside it is mine.

The door of the den is a broken mussel clump the size of a plate. I chose it. I drag it across at slack and pull it in behind me and push it out again when I go. It has been the door for as long as I have been alive, which is to say it is the only door there has ever been. The last one used something else. There is no way to know what. I do not think about this often but the description makes me, and when I do, the fact that lands is not the loss—it is that the door did not matter. The crack mattered. The crack stays open. Whoever is here chooses a door, and it is theirs, and then it isn't.

Below the crack is the midden and I add to it. Crab backs, mussel valves opened along the hinge, a goby skull now and then. What I drop lands on the loose upper layer and the current sorts it downstream according to which way the tide was running. Under the loose layer is the cemented layer, which I did not make and cannot read, and under that the rind, and the midden goes on being the record of the fourth pump without any single occupant having meant to keep one. I think, if I am placed anywhere that outlasts me, it is there: not as the octopus but as the fan of pale shell on the downstream side, sorted by weather I didn't choose, indistinguishable from the fans of the others except that it is on top for now.

So that is where you would find me. Fourth pump, plinth level, the dark end. Two arms down in the channel nearest the culvert, the rest of me flat along the lip, skin showing nothing because there is nothing to show it in. The conger's mouth is forty paces down and to the left and I have its timing too; I learned that fast, because the ones who didn't are in a different midden. The wrasse is asleep against the flange and does not know I am here and has known, in the way it knows things, that something always is.

The wheel is coming up on the knock. I can feel the load start to shift through the plinth before the sound arrives—not a thought, a tension in the arms. The crabs in the channel don't feel it. They will freeze on the sound and I am already moving on the shape before the sound, which is the whole of my advantage, a fraction of a second I found by myself and will not leave to anyone.

Knock.
