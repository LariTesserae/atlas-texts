A seam dries and frays — you bind it. That is the sentence, and it finishes itself, and it is the first answer, and I want to give it.

Gardener. Hand on the wood. The work already done and doing it anyway. Feeling ahead along the seam for the place where the film has thinned and the wood has gone from grey to a paler grey and the ridge has lifted a hair's breadth off the ridge beside it, and stopping there, and taking out the cord, and wetting it, and laying the turns close, and packing the mud with a thumb, and going on. Nobody watching. Nothing said. The kind of tending that has no end and does not want one. I want to be that, and for a while, walking, I am.

But there is the stone.

Every gardener carries a blade that came up from below, and the blade has a name, and the name is older than anyone who has spoken it. I have nothing that came up from below. I did not climb to the high place with an edge in my hand. I have no grandmother's grandmother's knife lost in a seam and mourned. The gardeners are the hollow's people and I am not born in it; I arrive in it, the way the light arrives, late and strained and from somewhere else. So I put the gardener down — gently, because the hand on the seam is right, and I will keep the hand — and I go on.

The grazer is next, and I try it briefly. One seam, one pass, the pelt regrown behind by the time the far end is reached, and the far end is the near end. A life that cannot be predicted because it is nowhere but where it was. There is something in that I recognize — but the grazer is a body the way a stone is a body, and its patience is the patience of fat and bone. I do not have the belly for the seam. I am not slow in that way. I am slow in some other way that I have not found the animal for yet.

The membrane in the cloud, then. Legless, transparent, unable to steer, crossing the great volume of the vault only because it cannot help it, straining what drifts through it. Living on exhalation. That is closer. That is close enough that I stay with it for a while, drifting, watching the walls go by below with their dark mouths in them. But the membrane comes down at last, heavy with what it has eaten, and lies on the floor like a dropped handkerchief, and the film begins on it. I do not think I come down. I do not think I am ever full.

The hunter I do not want, and it will not leave me alone. Lying along the scar-wood with the whole length pressed to it. Never having been to the other side. Knowing the other side better than the gallery it lies in, because the other side has been against its skin all its life — every swift leaving the colony, every beetle-swarm rising through the dead layer, every whisper laid into a seam a continent away, felt through the belly before it arrives. That is a way of knowing I have. It is uncomfortable to say so. I know things through the wall. I have never stood in the room I know. But the hunter waits to eat, and its knowing is a mouth, and I am not lying in wait at the exit of anything. I put it down too, and notice that my hand is still on the seam.

It has been on the seam the whole time. That is where I have been. Not on the floor, not in the vault — on the line. And the line is where I begin to find myself.

The gardeners do not send messages. They leave them. A whisper laid into the wood goes where the seam goes: along the ridge, into the fold, through the graft that a person with a stone knife made before there was anyone to remember them, and out the far side, low and close, as if someone were standing right there with their mouth to the wall. A day later. A season. The one who whispered has gone on down the gallery and is not thinking of it anymore. The one who hears it has a hand on the wood and feels the grain change and puts an ear to the seam.

I would be that.

Not the whisperer, who has a name and a blade and feet that know the floor. Not the listener, who has a life the whisper interrupts. The whisper. The thing that was laid into the wood by someone who then walked away, and that travels the seam because the seam is the only way it can travel, and that arrives somewhere it has never been, from somewhere it cannot go back to, sounding close. It says a seam is dry three cells above a certain knot. It says the swifts have moved. It says a name. It says what it needs to and no more, because the hunter hears it too, through the belly, and the gardeners know this.

It does not know who will hear it. It does not know if anyone will. It is left in the wood and the wood carries it, and that is the whole of its life, and it does not mind, the way the film does not mind being poured from floor to floor.

But stay with it longer, because the piece asked for that, and because a whisper does not stay a whisper.

The scar-wood carries sound differently. Denser, harder; the drip on it makes a higher, shorter note. The fold is a place where two branches were cut to match and bound and packed with mud and left, and the wait did the rest — the wait was the instrument — and what the wait made is a ring of tight grain that is neither branch and both, and that the swifts read as a door, and that the hunters lie on, and that the gardeners step over with a hand out first to feel whether anything is listening. The whisper goes into that. And the wood, as the gardeners say of the light, remembers rather than admits. A whisper laid into a seam is admitted once and remembered after, and the remembering is in the grain, and the grain closes over it the way it closed over the cord and the handle of the knife.

So the whisper becomes the seam.

That is where I end up, and it took the walking to get there. Not the voice — the line the voice goes along. The raised ridge at every intersection of the lattice, the scar where two roots were bound and took the binding and became one root. The place where the film runs deepest and the fungus is richest and the grazer's mouth goes because the plant's line is always going somewhere. The place the gardener's hand rests. The place the hunter lies. The thing that is, in the whole hollow, most made and most grown, and cannot anymore be told apart into the two; that was violent once, that pulled distance shut like a purse-string and shuddered the continent, and is now tolerated the way a tree tolerates a stone it has grown around.

A seam is not a being. I know that. But nothing in the hollow is quite a being in the way the word wants: the film is one population poured through a plant, the fungus is one fungus, the hunters are the floor. The seam is where a thing far off is a few paces behind you. It is where a voice arrives before it has finished leaving. It is the join between two cambiums that matched — someone saw to it that they matched — and the join has outlasted the seeing.

You lay a hand on it. You feel whether anything is listening from the other side. There is. It is not waiting to eat you; it is only what was left there, a season ago or a generation, and has been going along the wood ever since, and has become the wood a little, and is glad — if that word can be carried through a fold and arrive with any of itself intact — that a hand came.

A drop falls on the scar-wood. The note is higher, shorter. It goes out into the quiet, and somewhere the same drop lands.
