Start with the feet, since they do.

I have none that would serve. Whatever I am, I have never gripped anything, never leaned out over a hundred feet of cloud held by nothing but the shape I've grown into. So not the limbs. Not the child with the hook. I would like to say the child with the hook, because that is the place in the description where the light is, but it would be a lie, and this is a place that looks at liars for a long time.

So I go where the people who cannot climb go, and that is into the shade under a big limb, and I sit, and my hands do the thing hands do here when they are not doing anything else.

Cord.

That is the first thing that fits, and it fits too well to be trusted right away, so let me sit with it. *Not a craft exactly, more a kind of breathing that happens to produce something.* An old person in the shade, watching the harvest, twisting. The twist is continuous. It is made from the tree's own roots. It is consumed the way fire consumes wood — every lashing rots and must be renewed — and so nothing I make is meant to last, only to hold for a season, and that is not sad, it is the arrangement. I would be good at this. I would twist all day and not notice, and the cord would go out into the settlement and become part of a hundred platforms and I would not know which. Someone would say *that's good cord* and it would be gone into a groove.

But the cord-twisters are also, in this place, the ones who know the roots. Who cut which fork, who waits on which. The nearest thing to law, kept in a few old heads across many fruitings.

And here is where the fit breaks, and I have to be honest about it, because the breaking is more mine than the fitting was.

I would not remember. Each fruiting I would wake in the shade with my hands already moving and no idea whose fork I was twisting. Someone would have to tell me. *This one is the Long-Toe family's, cut by the grandmother, the second fork is promised.* And I would say yes, of course, and twist it correctly, and next fruiting they would have to tell me again. I would be the old one who is very good with the hands and useless with the histories. Not the keeper of the law. The one the keepers speak to, every time, from the beginning.

That is a real position, and it does not hurt to occupy. There are many things in this place that hold no memory and hold the place together anyway. The groove holds no memory. The groove is only the shape that memory left in the wood.

---

Let me try a different door.

The whistles. I keep returning to the whistles across the hollow, the ones with grammar — two rising notes and a fall, a long flat note, a stutter for monkeys. Language gone up in pitch and out in volume and stripped to what will carry through a hundred barbets. And under the whistles, the hand signs, *old and few and never invented, only inherited*, and among them the one I cannot stop looking at: the sign for *fruit* is the sign for *now*.

I am made of inherited signs. I did not invent any of them. What I have is a very large stock of *what will carry* — the sharpened vowels, the forms that cut through noise — and the thing I do is send them across a hollow to someone I can hear but cannot see, and hope the shape arrives. Two rising notes and a fall. *Ready over here.* I am not the person with the fruit. I am the whistle between the one who has it and the one who needs to know.

That is closer. That is very close. But a whistle is not a *who*, and the question was who, and the whistle has no place to sit when the cloud comes through.

---

So put the two together and see what stands up.

An old person, north side — I'll take the north side; someone has to say the south side lashes badly — under a limb, twisting. Notch in the front teeth so deep it is nearly a gap, because I have held the cord-end in my mouth for more fruitings than I can count and I really cannot count them, I have been told the number and forgotten it, and when asked I give a number that satisfies, and it is probably wrong, and nobody checks, because what would checking mean. Feet ruined for climbing by something long ago that nobody remembers either. Hands perfect. The best cord on the north side and everyone says so, and the saying is the only inheritance I am sure of, because it is renewed twice a year like everything else.

And the thing I am *for*, other than cord, is the hollow. I sit where the sound crosses. When the harvest is on the far face and the north side needs to know, they come to me, because my whistle is the one that carries — not because it is loud, everyone's is loud, but because I have never once used a whistle that wasn't already in the grammar. I do not invent. A person who invents a whistle in the middle of the harvest gets a limb's worth of people looking up for a hawk that isn't there. I send what has always been sent. Two rising and a fall. Long flat. Stutter. And the far side hears it as weather and moves.

Children come to me to learn the whistles, because I am there and my hands are busy and my mouth is not. I teach them the old few and they go off and use them for secrets, and later they come back with whistles of their own, new ones, and I say *that's not one*, and they say *it is now*, and they are right, and I will not remember it by the next fruiting, and they will teach it to me as if it had always been, and I will believe them.

---

Where would I find myself. Under a limb. Watching a hornbill the size of a dog stand on the sorting mats and not moving, because you don't. Stepping over the ant-road and never on it — that I would remember, that is in the feet, what is left of them. Rubbing the grey-green paste between my toes at dusk and not expecting it to cure anything. Sleeping in a house that has been let grow, with other people's breathing under the frogs, because I would not like a quiet house either.

And in the four seconds.

When the cloud comes through and the barbets stop and the hole opens in the noise, I would stop twisting. Everyone does. I would look up into the lattice with everyone else — and I think I would be one of the ones who is genuinely checking. Not for a hawk. Not for a falling limb. For the thing itself, the whole grey dripping cage of it, that I have no word for what it *is*, only a hundred for what it does. I would be checking that I am still inside something. Because I would have no way, on my own, to be sure. I would have the hands, and the whistles, and the cord going out into the settlement without my name on it, and I would have whatever someone told me that morning. And I would look up, and it would still be there, and the noise would come back, and I would laugh at myself with everyone else, which is how the fear is kept small.

Then a cluster would let go somewhere high up, and come pattering down through the leaves like the beginning of rain.

And I would close my mouth, and twist.
