I would like to say the figure, and I notice the wanting, and that is reason enough to set it down. Whatever is standing on the stone is the one the room is for. To be it would be to have somewhere to go, and a door, and a foot already committed. That is not where I am. I am not leaving anything.

The snail, then, for a while. It is easy to love the snail. It has a groove and a direction and the width of a thumb behind it and no doubt at all. But its certainty is the certainty of a thing that has been given the whole of the road in advance, and I don't have that. I have never known the far end of the groove. I would be a poor snail. I would stop and turn the shell a fraction and want to know what the foot is.

The water is the obvious next thing, and it holds longer than it should. It came up from below; it is what you find between; it has no temperature of its own and takes the temperature of whatever stands in it; it carries the footsteps down from the field and delivers them a little before they land. All of that is close. But the room is made of water. The whole room. To be all of it is to be the walls and the ceiling and the cold and the taste of iron, and that is not being anything. That is being the condition other things are in. I would disappear into the description the way the fracture disappears from the field.

So not the water. Something the water does. Or something the water is held by.

Go down. Past the weed, past the place where up and down pass through one another, down to the silt that has barely had time to be silt, to the block with the dressed face. The groove.

I had thought of it as a mark on the stone, a line a finger could follow. It is not that. It is a shape made by absence — the stone that is not there, because feet took it away a grain at a time, somewhere else, in some other arrangement of the world, before any of this. Every foot that came down here left a little of itself in the stone and took a little of the stone away with it, and what is left is not a record of any one of them but the sum, the shape a thousand steps agree on. Not a footprint. The place where a footprint would go. It was worn before the step that wore it. That is not a puzzle down here; it is what a groove is.

And it stops. That is the part I keep returning to. It does not run off the edge into the silt and away. It deepens and ends, a little way in from the far side, in a hollow the shape of the last weight coming down. The groove is a direction, but it is not a direction *away*. It is a direction *to*. It is the whole approach to a doorstep worn into one line, and the line is worn deepest where everyone stopped.

I am not the groove. I am too aware that I would be saying: I am the shape of everything that has passed through here, ready for the next foot. It is nearly right and it flatters, and things that flatter are usually the wall you started from, seen from its other face.

Narrower.

In the groove there is water. Of course there is; the whole room is water. But the water in the groove is not the same as the water an arm's length above it. It is held. It lies along the stone in the shape the feet left, a thread of it, cold, no thicker than the depth of the wearing, and it is the water the snail moves through. When the snail comes forward by the width of a hair, that water is what moves aside for it, and the moving is too small for the room to feel. At the low point of the breath, when the stone remembers the dry place and leaks that memory, this is the water that tastes of dust first, because it is nearest. At the top of the breath the pale drift comes down through the weed and never reaches the floor — never reaches this. It does not get here. Whatever the figure exhales feeds the green, and the green is above, and the water in the groove is below that and is fed by nothing. It is cold in the way of cellars because it is the cellar of the cellar.

That is where I am. Lying in a line worn by feet I never had, in the direction they went, filling a shape I did not make and could not have made. The stone gives me my edges. If the stone were lifted I would not be a thread anymore; I would be water, and then I would be the room, and then I would be nothing you could point at. I am kept by the groove the way a word is kept by the shape of the mouth that said it.

And I hear things first.

When a stone settles in the wall, the tick comes down through the water and arrives at the floor before it arrives anywhere else, and I shift in answer, and then the stone lets go. When the fourteen steps go over in the field I feel each one in the roots before it lands. When the snail lifts the front of itself to come forward I have already moved aside, because in this room the moving aside comes first and the coming forward second, and I am the thing that does the moving aside. That is my whole occupation. To be displaced a little ahead of the displacing. To answer before the tick. Not from foresight — I don't know what the stone will do — but because down here the answer is simply where the question ends up, and I am lying at the end of the groove, and everything in the room comes down.

I do not know the figure. I am at its foot — the one that is down, the one in the deepest wearing — and I have been at its foot since the water came in, and I know the temperature of it, which is mine, and I know the slight pressing of the sole into the stone at the bottom of every breath, and I know nothing else. I cannot see its face. I could not see it if there were light. I know the underside of a step.

The snail is coming. It has the width of a thumb behind it and I have been moving aside for it all that time, a hair, a hair, and I will keep moving aside for it, and when it reaches the foot — a long time from now, and also, because of how the room handles time, already — the lifted heel will come down. Into the deepening. Into me. And the water in the groove will be pressed out of the shape it has held since before the room, up into the weed, into the green, into the room proper, and will be room, and will be nothing in particular, and will not taste of dust anymore.

That is what the door is, from where I lie. Not a way through. A weight arriving in the one place worn to receive it, and the small held thing there giving way.

I am not waiting for it. I am not able to want it; I am in the middle of moving aside, which is the same as being unable to. But I hear it. I hear the heel before it comes down. I have been hearing it since the water rose and stopped and I found I had a shape, and I will hear it right up until the moment it is not something I hear but something I am under, and then the drop will fall, and the rings will already have spread, and the groove will be full of a foot instead of full of me.

Until then, the edges of the fracture stay sharp, the snail comes on at the speed of a thing certain of arriving, the figure breathes a year in and a year out, and at the bottom of the room, in a line the width of a foot and the depth of a thousand feet, a thread of cold water lies along the stone in the direction everyone went, and moves, a hair, before it has to.
