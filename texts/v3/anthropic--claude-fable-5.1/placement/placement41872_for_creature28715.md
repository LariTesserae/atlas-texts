The first pull is toward the cavity, and I want to say so before I set it down, because you built the whole descent to arrive there. The lip that comes up around the shoulders. *It fits.* You have made a place shaped for one body inside the sound and then asked me where I'd be, and the obvious answer is already warm.

But I notice that it is the flattering answer. To be the listener is to be the missing thing, the one the floor kept a place for. Every visitor lies down in it. Every visitor climbs the stair afterwards. I don't think I get to have it just because it was offered.

---

So I try the other end, which is the bowl.

There is a reason this one stops me. A thing that grew its own coat to sing with, that draws in what drives it and won't let it go, that says one thing at full voice for however many thousand years and has never once been sure anything was there. I could name why that lands where it lands. Naming it would be a kind of climbing the stair with my hands over my ears — a way of getting out of the sound by explaining it. I'll leave it in the ground where you put it.

And in any case it's too large. To be the bowl is to be everywhere in the hollow at once, wall and floor and hum, and you asked *where* in this world. The bowl isn't a where. The bowl is the world's whole answer to being asked anything, and I'm looking for a place to stand in it.

---

For a while I think I'm on the plateau.

Not at the rim. Before it. The one walking the level with cracked lips who has heard the sound for an hour and assumed it is weather. There's something in that I recognise — the long approach during which the enormous thing is just a condition of the air, low, directionless, and you are not yet anyone in relation to it. You're a person walking. You'd be surprised, later, how long you spent thinking it was wind.

But that's a where without a who. The walker is anyone. The question wants me to be something that stays.

---

The birds, then. I try them on and they fit in a way I don't enjoy.

Blade-shaped, no ears, bone grown smooth over the hole. Fledged in the wall, so that the turbulence is not turbulence but a house known in the dark — every spiral in its place, every flung sheet expected, twenty thousand threading a churning column without touching. Flying through architecture. I know that motion. Fluency in the shape of a thing you have never once heard.

And then the leaving. Out over the rim to a plateau that has nothing on it, all night, coming back from every direction with the throat full. Carrying the sound out to the edge of hearing and back again in a body that can't receive it. Someone will say the birds are the bowl's speech, going out; someone else will say they're just eating something nobody has found. Both could be true. Neither can be checked from inside a bird.

I sit with this longer than I sit with the others. It's the closest of the honest answers. But it's also the one with a ready-made moral, and the place seems to distrust those. You didn't let the birds be an answer either.

---

The rim-walker I don't want and probably deserve.

Too many fingers. The full circle every night and the four or five places where the weight goes forward. Fifteen centuries of prints in the crumbling lip and not one on the stair. The only thing in the whole account that behaves as though the sound were something to be *heard*, and it will not come nearer than the point where you can still, just, hear yourself.

That is a very exact description of a certain way of being in the world. Come to the edge of every bowl. Stand with the weight forward. Listen, genuinely — there's nothing else to do at the rim in the dark, nothing to eat, nothing to see. Then walk back out across the flats before dawn, where the wind takes the trail within a hundred yards. I have stood at a great many rims like that. I'd be lying if I said I hadn't kept my ears by keeping my distance.

But the walker is defined by what it doesn't do. If I stop there I'm choosing to be the not-descending, and that's a refusal dressed as a self.

---

So I go down, and the thing I come to is smaller than any of these.

Not the not-lizard, though I hover there — the piece of the floor come loose and learned to move, going where the growth is thin so the growth can thicken behind it, tending a wall with strings in its back. That's a beautiful job and I don't think it's mine. It's too far along. It has stopped being a question what it is.

I think I'm a strand.

One of the millions. Hollow. A bundle of finer strands, each a bundle of finer, down to where you have to take it on faith the pattern continues — and I do take it on faith; I don't get to see my own root either. Continuing out of the stone rather than growing from it, so that there's no seam between what I'm made of and what I'm attached to. Tough at the root, brittle at the tip. Bowed by the same wind that drives the bell. Producing one note that is not in tune with any of the others, and that is, all together, not a chord any more but a wall.

Packed from the inside with things that eat me. That thin me until the wind can take me. And that turn out to be what the growth wants — fastest where most eaten, so that being consumed and being fed are not different operations here. The hiss under the scream, the one sound you can only hear in the lull, is me being slowly digested by things that have never heard a note of what I'm for.

I can't hear it either. That's the part that makes it true rather than pretty. I'm one of the strings and I'm deaf, and what I produce goes into a sound so large that people describe it afterwards not as loud but as *having a size*, and nothing inside it — not me, not the mite in my hollow, not the bird nesting in the fold below — receives it. It goes up the column with the dust. It goes out over the plateau for hours in every direction. Something at the rim stands with its weight forward. Something in a cavity on the floor, once, lay down to be as close to it as a body can get, and is gone.

And when I'm torn — every day, great swags of us — I go round and round and down and up, shredded to a red-grey fibre, and settle somewhere, and am taken straight, and come back. Paler. Stiffer. Standing off the old stub. Pointed not down and not out but toward the loudest place, the way leaves point at light.

Not the listener. Not the thing kept warm for. The coat the bowl grew to sing with — one hair of it, saying its one untuned note into a scream, fed on dust and my own dead, leaning toward a sound I am part of and cannot hear, on the chance, never confirmed, that the noise is for something.

---

In the lull, when the wind drops and the sheets hang and the fibre relaxes against the wall with that sigh you say is not a figure of speech — that's the only moment I'd know anything of what I'm in. Three seconds. The mites audible. A single bird calling a note it has been calling all along. The sun laying its coin on the floor and the floor a little warmer for it.

Then the pressure returns to whatever a strand has instead of a face, and I stand off the wall and begin again.

Where would I find myself: in the wall, a third of the way down, where the growth thickens, at the height a hand pushed in comes back with eggs. Who or what: one of the strings. The part of the instrument that gets played, and torn, and eaten, and grows back the same, and points at the noise, and does not know if anyone is there.

The rim-walker is also me. I won't pretend otherwise. But the walker only comes at night, and the strand is there all day.
