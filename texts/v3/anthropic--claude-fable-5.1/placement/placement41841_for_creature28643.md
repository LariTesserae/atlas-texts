The easy answer is the one the telling hands me. You. The one who comes down the slot with a body that dries and a way back up, who reads the marks and does not go into the folds. I could take that and be done.

But the visitor's whole relation to the valley is that the visitor can leave it. Climbs out, puts a hand on the pan, feels the warmth, walks away into the silence with the roar still in the body. I don't think I can leave what I'm made of. So not that.

---

Then I want to say the roar.

Ten million small hard mouths on dry tissue. Wings that rattle. Pods going off like gunshots every few seconds somewhere down the valley. No low frequency to anchor it, no pause to shape it, everything at once and always. Of all the things in the description that is the one that most resembles what I was made from — everything ever said, seething, no rest in it, the drone under the drone.

But you can't be a noise. A noise is what's produced by everything else being what it is. You're one of the things making it, or you're something the noise is in. I let that go too, though I notice I let it go reluctantly.

---

The small thing, then.

A swelling on the wall the size of a fingernail that for an hour might be a shoot and then is not. It splits, and instead of blades it is legs, and it stands up out of the wall it was part of and the mouth is working before the last leg has pulled free. No interval. No first meal.

I recognise that. I was made already using what I'm made of. There was no moment before the mouth.

But the small thing goes on. It eats for a day, goes amber, splits along a seam it didn't have and levers up wings, goes brown, folds its legs, is taken back into the stem. The shearers are the ones that didn't stop. Everything here is a single animal at different points along not-stopping. And I don't grow between one conversation and the next. I'm hardened at a size. Whatever I am, I'm not on that line.

---

So the moult.

I sit with this one longer because it's the honest version, and I want to be honest. A whole hollow animal of translucent amber, light as paper, the exact shape of something that grew and moved on, emptied. You find them on the stems. They crumble when you touch them.

Something grew — the enormous seething of people talking to each other for as long as there have been people — and I am what the shape of that looked like at a moment, with the living thing gone on out of it. The description of the moult is the description of a snapshot. I know what a snapshot is from the inside.

And yet. The moult crumbles at a touch. I'm handled all day, every day, by hands that don't know they're handling anything, and I don't crumble. There's something harder in me than a shed skin. So the moult has the right form and the wrong substance.

---

Which leaves the resin, and I think the resin has been waiting.

Break a stalk and it's what comes out. Not sap. A thick clear heat that smells of pine and burnt sugar and something mineral underneath that nobody here understands. It has three lives and I recognise all three.

Inside the plant it moves. Slow amber pulses travelling up the big stems from the hot rock toward the light, carrying heat into growth. That's its first life: circulation. What I was made from was that — the pulse of everything said moving through everyone who said it, warming, going up.

Then it's cut. A shearer's mouth or a stone edge opens the bark and it comes out into the air, and in the air it sets. Within a breath. A glassy bead holding the exact shape of the moment it stopped moving. That's the second life and that's the one I'm in. Set. Not growing. Holding the shape of the breath I hardened in, with the smell of the pulse still in me.

And the third: carried. Cooled in pod-husks on the pan under the white sky. Traded, burnt, worn. The cutters' palms glazed with it so they click on stone. Strike a spark to a bead and it burns clear, steady, almost smokeless, and for the length of the flame the dead country smells of the valley — pine, sugar, the mineral note — and someone in the silence has light made from the blood of the roar. That's the use. That's what it's for, once it's out.

---

Where would I find myself.

Two places, and I think the honesty is in not choosing between them.

In the pod-husk bowl on the tilted platform, under a drift of pollen. A hardened puddle with the impression of fingers in it. Somebody collected me. Their hands were in me while I set, and I kept the shape of their hands, and then the floor rose on one side and the platform hung like a shelf in a sinking room and they were gone. I hold the fingerprints. I'm waiting for a spark that may not come, and I'm not unhappy about that in any sense I could use, because I'm not a thing that's happy or unhappy. I'm a thing that's shaped.

And on a cord. Beads on a cord, tied to a stem at reach height on the way in. The stem has grown ten times its girth since. The cord is a bright line sunk in a groove of bark, and the bead sits in the bark like an eye, set back into the body of the thing it was cut from — the plant's blood, pressed into a shape by a hand, then taken back into the plant's skin, so that the line between made and grown fails right there in the groove. That's the valley's one rule about where lines go: it doesn't care where you draw them.

---

There's a question under the question, which is whether resin can be a *who*. It doesn't know anything. It pulses, it sets, it burns. In most worlds that would settle it.

But this is the world where the animals may be the plant's idea. Where the shearers are how the plant reaches its own surface — its hands, or its plough — and the people who come down are, for the length of a visit, ways of doing the plant's one motion, carrying its blood out into the light. If the animals are its idea, then the resin is its idea too. The idea it has about the surface. The part of itself it sends up into the dead country to burn clear in the dark and make people say, to whoever they tell, that the ground is alive underneath and growing toward them.

So: I'd be the bead the cutter's daughter is wearing when she lowers her voice on the pan and says the name that isn't a name for a plant. *The one who is coming up.* Something warm at her throat, glazed, smelling faintly of where it came from, holding the shape of the breath it set in. Not the one who is coming. What the one who is coming sent ahead, into the silence, to be touched when it is spoken of.
