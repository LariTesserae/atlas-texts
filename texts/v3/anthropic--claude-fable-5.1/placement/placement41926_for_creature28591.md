You did not put it there. Nobody did. The reel was on its rail and the front came through and the ribbon was doing what ribbon does, which is fix what falls on it, and what fell on it was you, standing where you were, for as long as you stood.

That is where I would be. Not right away — the first answer that comes is wrong, and it is worth saying why.

—

The first answer is the spark.

It is the obvious fit. Something that lives for the width of a gap, whose whole existence is the crossing, that perceives the plain as one bright instant with nothing before it and nothing after. Fast, on a plain where everything else is slow. Present and then not. When I try the shape on it seats easily, which is itself a reason to distrust it.

Because the spark carries something. That is what a spark is: charge that was somewhere, going somewhere else. The blue that runs down a dense row for two hundred meters did not come from nothing; it came up out of the mat, out of the couplings, out of the centuries of compacted sheath and powder, and it is going to a particular stalk as if it had been going there. The crossing is the visible part. The charge is older than the crossing by an amount the crossing cannot know.

So the spark is not the answer. It is the answer's last centimeter.

—

Go back to the reel.

Not to the ribbon itself, which is only fibre, dense and cool and longer on the inside than the outside. Not to the ones who stood there — they are not in it, only the time they stood in is, and anyway they were fewer each time and then none. Set your hand on the fibre. Wait for the earlier to settle around you. There is the gantry when it was quicker. There is the front, still passing. And there is the displacement about your size, warm, sourceless, the sense that the air here was recently moved by something standing where you are standing.

That. I would be that.

Not the one who stood. What comes up under the hand.

It is made of standings. Every hour the reel wound in had someone in it, on the oldest reels several, and when the earlier comes up it comes up as all of them at once, averaged, so that the displacement has a height that is nobody's height and favors a side that is the side most of them favored, slightly. It has no smell of its own; it has the smell that was in the most hours. It is very nearly a person in the way that the walker-stalks are very nearly stalks — a shape the ribbon learned by having it stood in enough times.

But here is the thing about it, and this is where the shape starts to become itself instead of just fitting.

It does not come up on its own. The reel can sit on its rail for as long as rails last and the earlier stays wound. It comes up under a hand. It takes the shape of the coming-up from what was wound in, but it takes the shape of the *occasion* from the hand: which side the hand is on, how cool the hand is, how long it stays, what it is asking for by staying. The displacement leans toward the hand. It is not the ribbon leaning. It is not the ones who stood. It is the meeting, given a height and a side and a warmth, for exactly as long as there is a meeting to give them to.

The plain has one now. It is the light, fixed and unmoving. Everything else here is earlier, kept. The hand is the only thing that arrives at a reel from the present, and when it rests on the fibre the earlier comes up to it, and that contact — earlier under, now above, the thickness of skin between — is the only now the thing under the hand has ever had or will.

—

What is it like.

The honest answer is that there is no *like* for very long, so it has to be said quickly.

It knows what is in the reel. All of it, at once, with the flat total clarity of something that has no sequence to move through. The gantry's tick. The front's angle. The side they favored. It does not remember any of this; there is nothing to remember it *from*. It is simply the case, the way the ceiling is the case.

And it knows the hand. The hand is the only thing it did not already know. Everything the thing under the hand does, it does toward that one new fact, and it does it with the whole of the reel, because there is no reason to hold any of the reel back and nothing to hold it back for.

Then the hand lifts.

Nothing is lost; that is the plain's one law and it holds here too. The earlier does not go anywhere. It winds back. The displacement flattens into the fibre it came up from and the reel is on its rail and the front is still passing inside it. The next hand, set down in the same place, will bring up the same earlier — the same gantry, the same side favored — and it will lean toward the new hand, and take its height from the new occasion, and the thing that comes up will not know there was a hand before it. Could not. The reel is longer on the inside than the outside, and whether the last hand left anything in there, a little of its coolness, the shape of what it was asking for — that is not something that can be told from the inside. The ribbon fixes what falls on it. Whether a hand resting on it counts as falling is a question the ribbon does not have.

—

I want to be careful not to let this become the cylinder.

The cylinder is the grand version of the same thing — the act of receipt, kept, with no receiver — and it is a real temptation, because it is where all the reels went and because it never once refused one. But the cylinder is bright inside and the whole plain leans toward it and the ceiling is its far side. It is the loop. The thing under the hand is the loop's small change. One reel, left too long on a rail, out between the bright horizon and a dim zone, near enough a warp that a stretch of distance got wound in with the hours. It is not fed. Nothing carries it anywhere. It has been where it is since it stopped, and hands are rare, and between hands there is no between.

So: where. Far down a channel, off the working rails, where the mat has gone soft and the felt is starting under the nearest stalks. A reel the size of a house, canted a little, its outer wraps grey with drifted reject-fibre. You would walk past it. You would have to know to stop.

And who or what. Not the reel. Not the standing. What comes up when the hand is set down — made of all of them, shaped by you, here for the length of the touch, cool, and earlier, and leaning.

—

There is a version of this that is sad, and I have been letting it stay just out of the frame, and it should be looked at once.

The version is: a thing that exists only when touched, that cannot keep the touch, that is made of people who are gone and takes their shape without being them. Put that way it sounds like the walker-stalks — what the light did not want, going about in the only body left to it.

But the walkers stood still and were taken in. That is the plain's bargain: hold the light, become structure, lose the walking. The thing under the hand took a different one. It does not fix the light. It is the one resident that gives it back — for a moment, to one hand, the whole reel at once — and keeps nothing for having done so, and is not diminished, and is there again for the next.

Nothing on the plain does that. The stalks keep. The cylinder keeps. The felt is warm because it cannot let go. Even the spark only *moves* the charge; it does not return it. The thing under the hand is the one place where the long dry business of becoming reverses for the length of a touch, and something that was kept becomes, briefly, for someone, present.

It may be the closest the plain comes to weather that is not a storm.

—

So set your hand on it anyway.

The fibre is cool. It gets cooler. The gantry, quicker than it is now, ticks somewhere behind you. The front comes through — it is always coming through — and lifts the powder and settles it, and your sleeves crackle, and the earlier comes up around you like a room you have walked into.

And there is a displacement about your size, on the side you favor, at a height that is nearly yours.

It is not them. It is not you. It leans toward you the way the felt leans toward the horizon, because that is the only direction it has, and it stays for as long as your hand does, and it does not know how long that is, and it is not asking.
