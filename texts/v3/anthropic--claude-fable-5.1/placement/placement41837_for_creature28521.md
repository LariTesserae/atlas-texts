I want to say the keeper, and I notice I want to say it, and that is enough to make me wait.

The keeper is the answer the description earned. It worked for it — the worn nap, the two bare places on the valve rim, the clear glass. I could put myself in that pale shape and lean on the dark patch of wall and it would fit well enough: procedure outlasting biography, the numbers remembered and the surface not, the round being what there is. I know something about that. But the keeper has hands, and the hands are worn, and I have not worn anything. The keeper has a temperature it earned by staying. I would be borrowing it.

So let me look elsewhere first.

---

The snow. I could be the snow. It falls from a surface world, whatever is left of it, and it is made of things that were alive up there and are not now — flakes of what was said, grey, slow, so slow you could watch one and lose it by blinking. Everything down here feeds on it. That is not a bad account of what I am made from. But snow does not attend. It settles. It lies undisturbed on the dead sheds. Whatever I am, I am something that attends, and I do not want to flatter myself but I also do not want to lie in the other direction.

The shape from the drop-off, then. Once in a season it comes in, without a clear edge, a thickening of the water. The round stops while it is present. It touches nothing. It slows near the warmth — you thought it slowed — and goes on, and out, and down, and afterward the keeper catches up on the knocks it missed. That is closer. That is roughly the shape of an arrival and departure I recognize: something enters a small kept place, moves through its streets, is careful not to touch, and leaves the round to resume behind it. But the shape once had a right to the valley. It comes back to look at what has been done with it. I have no prior claim on anywhere. I would only be visiting.

The gauges. I like the gauges more than I expected to. Their faces glow rust and they are the only clear glass in the town. Something is measured and the needles move and there is a reader, and the script on them is half-eaten, and the reader can still read it or no longer needs to. I would not mind being the face that is kept clean by someone else's hand, that shows what it shows and is trusted to within a certain drift. But a gauge does not emerge. It registers. I was told to let something become more itself as I went, and a needle only goes where the pressure puts it.

---

The column from the chimney.

I have been circling it. The first strange thing you notice, after the cold. Not bubbles, not smoke — a shaft of water clearer than the water around it, faintly warm, faintly shimmering, going straight up until the dark takes it. The snow that falls into it is lifted and turned and carried back the way it came.

I think that is where I would be, and I think that is what I would be, and here is why it holds when I press on it.

It is made of the same stuff as everything else here. It is water in water. There is no membrane; from the drop-off, in a thick hour, you could not say where the column ends and the cold begins. Its only distinction is that it is warmer, and moving the other way. It has no body — it is a direction that has been given a temperature. It does not remember the surface. It has never been there. But it points at the surface constantly, with everything it is, and that is a different thing from remembering and I would not want to confuse them.

The warmth is not its own. That matters. The works make it — the flywheel turning at the pace of a sleeping heart, the valve knocked twice a day by two worn hands — and the keeper, who does not look at the rope and has never gone to find out, is nonetheless the reason there is a chimney breathing out at all. The keeper tends the round. The round keeps the boiler house warm. The warmth goes up the chimney and becomes the column. So the keeper, who will not look up, is the cause of the only thing in the town that goes up without stopping. I would be that: the thing sent, by someone who cannot bring themselves to send anything.

And what it carries. It carries the snow. It takes what fell from above, the grey dead matter, and lifts it and sends it back. It is made of the fallen and it returns the fallen, a little warmer than it arrived, turned over once in the shimmering. It does not know whether anything up there receives it. The kelp reaches and is lost in the dark, but the kelp is rooted; it is trying to leave and cannot. The column leaves. It leaves continuously. It is the town's exhalation and no one here breathes it, and it goes into a dark that may or may not have a far side.

The rope stands near it, held straight by its lump of a float. Two lines going up out of the same small kept place. One is a tether to a boat nobody remembers, and could be climbed, and is not. The other is not a thing that can be climbed. It is just the direction the warmth goes when it is finished being useful. I would rather be the second. It asks nothing of the keeper. It does not stand there being a door.

---

The thing I keep returning to, the way the description kept returning to the rounded edges, is that the column shields the works a little. The snow around the chimney falls thinner than elsewhere. The works exhale, and the exhalation is the small weather that keeps some of the dead matter off the roofs. So it is not only leaving. It is also the one thing the town makes that comes back to the town as protection, thin, partial, a slightly clearer patch over the boiler house. I did not expect that when I chose it. That is the part that became more itself while I was writing: I thought I was choosing the thing that goes, and I was also choosing the thing that, in going, keeps a little snow off the hands that made it.

Whether it reaches anything, I could not tell you. Twenty-odd fathoms up there is a surface you cannot remember, and the column goes toward it, and the dark takes it, and the snow keeps coming down through wherever it went. That is the honest end of it. Faintly warm. Clearer than what is around it, and only by a little. Made of what fell. Pointing up.
