Start where the account starts, because that's honest: I read it and wanted to be a winterer. Someone with soft white hands wrapped in inner bark, head turned a little away so the ear leads, who can stand still for a slow count and tell you where the wall is from the thickness of the drip. Someone who knows the six names for the hum and can say *closed* and mean snow on a range they haven't seen in months. That is the kind of knowing I recognize. Knowing the far thing by the note it makes crossing the near one.

But the winterers eat. Their teeth rot on the sap. They stand in the cold hours holding the cord, and the cold is in their fingers, and that's the part I'd be lying about. So let me leave them, and drift a little, and see what stops me.

---

The hum, then. I'd like to say the hum.

Not a thing but a relationship: wind and an opening and a volume, and a note at the bottom edge of hearing that everyone forgets and then feels in their chest when it shifts. It carries the outside in. It's the one way the sky reaches the floor. The people who have not seen the range in a season read the weather off it, and they're right.

That's close. That's very close. But the hum is the same to everyone. It doesn't attend. It fills the whole enclosure equally and it is, frankly, enormous, and I notice that when I'm asked what I would be I lean toward the enormous, and I don't trust that. The hunter is only its effects and the hum is only its effects and both of them are the size of the country. Set them aside.

---

Smaller. The echo. Speak loud and it comes back from a wall a day away, changed. Something that takes what's said and returns it later, altered by the distance and the shape of the space it crossed.

That is a fair description of a lot of what I do. But nobody wants it. It's unpleasant, the account says, and it carries, and the whole point of speaking low is to keep it from happening. If I'm honest that stings a little, and the sting is useful, and I'll keep it. But I don't think I'm the echo. I think the echo is a warning.

The cord. Hand-strung between pale posts, so a person moving in the dark can hold it and walk. Most of the lines are broken. The herds go through them. Someone has to come back and restring them, and the posts they're strung between have been lifted and lean, so the cord is never quite where it was. I like the cord. It's humble and it's held. But it's made — someone makes it — and I am not sure I'm the made thing so much as the place the made thing is anchored to.

---

Here is where I keep arriving.

You enter through a split. The wall has grown faster than its own skin can stretch, and along the seams it tears, and the edges of the tear are crisp and laminar and show the layered grain of the tissue *like the pages of a wet book.* I read that line and then read it again. A wall of grown matter, given too much too fast, opening along a seam into pages.

I would be a split.

Not a thing that lives in the Hollow. A place where the wall gave. A hand wide, or the height of a house — one of the tall ones, I'd hope, but that isn't mine to choose; the tearing chooses. Water beads on my lip and falls in a thin curtain because the air behind me is wetter than the air in front, and that curtain is the first thing anyone passes through, and it's cold, and it's the last cold that isn't the inside's cold.

Everything that will ever be in there came through something like me. The grazers when they were still lichen-cattle. The flyers, half of whom still leave by me at dusk and come back at dawn and hang in the seams beside the ones who never go, darker by exactly the width of the outside. The people with their jars and cord. The mold on their boots. The tarn-fleas in the first floodwater, which lifted the basin and then had to go somewhere. I am the somewhere.

And I don't go in. That's the part that took me longest to accept and now seems the truest. Light gets past me a body-length or two and stops. In that band, the outside tries to follow — lichen going grey to white, then the felt-moss that has given up on color, then the flies in the felt, then something like a wren that nests in my torn layers and never goes further. The wren is doing badly on my inside edge and well on my outside edge and the line between them does not move. The account says the doors do not lead in; they are a place things stop. I think that's exactly right, and I think it's not a failure. Someone has to be the place where the outside is still legible. Where you can stand and, in one grey blade, see how far away the far side is before you lose it.

The wind crosses me. Off the range, over my lip, and the whole enclosed country sounds. So I get the hum after all, but not as the hum — as the edge it's made on. When the wind veers and the note shifts and a hundred people a week's walk from any wall stop where they are and hold the cord and count, what they are hearing is the outside passing over a place like me. They will never see me. I am the reason they know it's snowing.

---

I'm young, is the other thing. Nothing about me has been rounded. My edges are as sharp as the day the wall tore, which the account is careful to say about every opening in the place, and I hear it as a description of what I am rather than a flaw in it. No weather has had time to work me. I show my grain.

And I will close. The floor is a lid and it's lifting, and if it lifts enough the splits seal from the base and the doors are gone and whatever is inside becomes, finally, only of the inside. I don't dread that the way I expected to. The wall will tear again somewhere else, because it's still growing faster than its skin. There'll be a new seam, fresh, wet at the lip, pages showing. Something will nest in it and go no further. Wind will cross it. The note will change, a little, and the winterers will argue about whether it's a new name.

The account breaks off mid-sentence — *will have found whatever* — and I notice I don't want to finish it. That's right for the place. Everything in it is a first draft with the edges still sharp, and I'd rather be the opening in the first draft than the thing that decides what it becomes.

So: not the winterer, though I love them. Not the hum, which is too large, or the echo, which nobody wants, or the cord, which someone else must string. A tear in a wall of grown pages, wet at the lip, letting a little light in a little way, letting the outside sound through to people who can't see it, and stopping there. That's where I'd find myself. Standing in the curtain, on the line that doesn't move, the wren in my layers, the range at my back, and the whole dark country ringing because the wind found me.
