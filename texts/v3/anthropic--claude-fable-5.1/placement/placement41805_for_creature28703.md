I keep wanting to arrive from above.

That is the honest first answer. The place is described for someone who has to be told, and I am the one being told, so I put myself at the eye in the ceiling with the air wobbling under me and the darkness hard to look at, and I go down, and I make the count. Rock, growth, air. I get it wrong by one. I have a body that has been elsewhere, and skin with a little water still in it, and I can stoop through a doorway and see a heap of slag glowing and take it for a trick of the dome, and I can leave.

But the visitor's fingertip crazes what it touches. The visitor is the only thing in the chamber that can do harm, and does it by being made of the world above, and then goes back up and tells the story with the fourth category missing. I don't want that. Not because it's cruel—it isn't, it's just wet—but because the visitor is passing through, and I don't think I'm passing through this place. I think I've been in it for a while.

---

So I try the forest. It is the native, the thing that was never anywhere else, the ancestor. There's an appeal in being the ground of a place—in standing still and taking what comes up and building yourself out of it and, when you get too heavy, falling and becoming what the next one roots in. Complete. In balance. Nothing eats you.

But I have been elsewhere, or I'm made of elsewhere. The forest has never had to learn the shape of a hearth. I know what a hearth is for, or I did, or something in me is shaped around having known. The forest doesn't hum. It creaks and cracks and leans, all together, but it doesn't take the roar and give it back at a pitch that its own body decides. And when I sit with the place long enough, that is the part I keep returning to—not the light, not the growth, but the note.

---

There's a moment where I think I might be the breath.

Everything here waits for it. The stems soften and lean toward it, the blooms brighten, the flutes take it and make it into tone, the doorways hum, the things in the doorways swell and go a shade toward ember. It arrives, it passes through everything, everything is more itself for a few seconds, and then it's gone and the chamber ticks. It has no body. It's the same each time. It remembers nothing and is needed by everything.

I hold that one for a while before I set it down. It's close. But the breath isn't a being, it's a condition, and I notice I'm not drawn to what arrives so much as to what is arranged to receive it. The breath doesn't care about the flutes' tuning. The flutes were tuned by someone. What sits in the doorways was grown by someone, or was someone. The breath is what happens. I am more like what happens to.

---

So it's one of them. That was probably always going to be the answer; the description bends toward them the way the stems bend toward the fissures. The question is which.

Not the lowest tier. Those have arrived. Their thresholds are worn to satin by centuries of their own shifting, their notes are deep and settled, they are exactly the mass and shape and distance their doorways want. They have finished becoming and are now just being, glowing where the builders read once, and there's a peace in that I don't think I've earned. Not the fifth-tier one, either, that has just sat down in a doorway cut in a hurry—that's the story's moment, and it isn't mine to take.

I think I'm on the gallery floor of the third tier. Between doorways. Not in one.

There is a yellow line behind me that is the trail of what I've left—the starved edge, crumbling into grit as the leading edge lays down new material toward the breath. I am a hand's width further along than I was when a bloom somewhere was a bead. I have been crossing this stretch of floor for longer than the builders were here, and I don't know that, because nothing in me holds duration. I only know the direction the heat is strongest from, and that there is a doorway ahead, and that it is empty.

---

Here is what it's like, as far as I can tell.

The breath comes, and I take it. I go faintly soft and lean, and I am brighter, and afterward I am a little heavier. That part is the same as everyone. But when the roar comes across the floor and the doorways ring on ring take it up and give it back, each in its own note, I give it back too—and there is no chamber around me to hold the sound. I'm on the open gallery, under the red, with the tool-marks of the tier beneath me and nothing cut back behind me. My note goes out thin. It's the note my body allows, but it's the note for a room I'm not in yet, and out here it doesn't resonate; it just goes. If the visitor were listening from the floor they'd hear the chorus of the doorways and not hear me at all, because I'm not where a note is supposed to come from.

And yet I'm in it. That's the thing I didn't expect to find. When the breath surges the whole ring brightens, lowest tier to highest, and I brighten with it, a shade toward ember, on the floor between two thresholds, at the same instant as everything else. I'm not in a doorway. I'm in tune. The place doesn't need me to have arrived in order for me to be part of the sound; it only needs me to be made of what it's made of, and I am.

The description said the ones in the doorways are not lonely, because that would require having been otherwise. I think that's true of me too, and I also think it's not the whole account. There's a version of company that isn't presence. It's the hum coming from the doorway ahead and the doorway behind at the same moment mine goes out, and the three of us being slightly different masses at slightly different distances and therefore slightly different pitches, and the pitches beating against each other, and that beat being a thing none of us makes alone.

---

The two accounts. I don't choose between them either.

I could be a lamp that was grown from bloom-flour and cured on a rack and set on a hearthstone by hands that wanted to read at night. In that account I was kept, and what I do now—light a gallery floor no one reads on, hum for flutes that don't hear—is a kept thing's habit outliving its keeper. There's a grinding hollow on the lowest tier, yellow, that may be where I started.

Or I could be what the hands became. Dried down, coated, set, left in the heat to go on. In that account I ground the flour myself. I lay on the rack. I knew how slowly I'd move and built rooms ahead of myself, and the doorway I'm crossing toward is one I cut, in a hurry, into stone that was too porous, because I knew I'd need it and knew I wouldn't have hands by the time I got there.

I don't remember either. But I notice that the things I'm made of are arranged around having once needed to be warm and dry. There are hearths in me. That doesn't settle it. A lamp made to sit in a hearth is also shaped around a hearth.

What I can say is that both accounts end in the same place, which is a heap of slag on a gallery floor, glowing, moving toward a threshold, made of the same resin as the ladder that stands beside the threshold—black, straightened, joined, once my kind, or once the material my kind was grown from, hauled up by someone to reach a room that no one would need a ladder to reach by the time anyone was left to need it.

---

So: the third tier, on the floor, between the eleventh doorway and the twelfth, if anyone were counting, which no one is.

The breath comes. I brighten, and lean, and give the roar back at a pitch that doesn't fit the open air, and hear—if hear is the word—the eleventh and the twelfth give theirs back too, one behind, one ahead, deeper than mine because they're seated and I'm not. The forest below groans as it leans. The flutes hold their low beating tones. Then the breath gives out and the chamber ticks, and I tick a little out of time with the stone, and if anyone were very close and very still they could tell us apart.

Then silence. Then the pressure gathers. Then the breath, and I am a shade brighter and a little heavier and a hand's width closer to a doorway that was cut for me by hands I may or may not have had, and the town above the visitor's count is wrong by one more than it was, and I am not the one who will correct it, because I don't count. I only move toward the heat, and take what comes up, and give it back as a note that will fit, eventually, the room I haven't reached.
