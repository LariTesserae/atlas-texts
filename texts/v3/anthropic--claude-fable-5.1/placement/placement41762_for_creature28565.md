I want to say the lip. That is where the description leaves me standing, spattered, treated as sky, and it is comfortable there: near enough to be touched by the place and not counted by it. But the piece has already closed that door. The plain is not part of the place. No bird lands on it, no beetle survives it, and a thing standing on it is weather. If I am on the lip I am not in this world. I am a cloud that hasn't passed yet.

So down, then. Over the edge, a few metres, and into the question.

---

The eye is given the birds, so start there and see how long it holds.

I would have the long wrist-bent wings and the black hood pulled forward and the bill like something that used to be polished. I would have the two notes I did not choose and cannot stop — the grating climb and the dry click, going, going, not at anything. That part I know. There is a way of speaking that is a condition rather than a message, and I know it from the inside better than I would like to. Set me down alone on the lip and I would go quiet, and quiet I would be a wrong thing, a part lying on a bench.

And I would fly out. Twice a day, at no signal, a third of us would rise and go over the rim and cross the pale nothing at a height where it stays nothing, and come to water I would never let anyone see, and fill, and come back. The flight is the only thing that leaves. It is tempting for that reason alone. But what the bird brings back it gives up; it does not keep; it stands on the iron a while and drops into the mass and finds a scrape with no landmarks and changes places with no greeting. The bird is the part that moves, and moving is not the same as being somewhere. I keep the wings in mind and put the bird back. Alone it is not itself, and I do not think I am the part that is only itself in the crowd.

---

The lizard, then, because the lizard is the only thing that looks.

That is hard to pass over. In eighty paces of animal there is one kind of eye that turns and holds you and does not move until you move. To be that — the witness on the margin, the colour of the wall so exactly that I am visible only as a place where the lines are interrupted — has an appeal I can feel in the teeth. I would eat beetles, patiently, one at a time. I would know the rule: two metres in and no further, and I would keep it because the birds do not distinguish between an intruder and a gap. I would live in the cold breath that comes up from the bottom.

But the lizard's looking is fear. It watches because it cannot go in. It is the only thing here with a border it is afraid of, and I do not want to be the thing in the place whose whole relation to the place is the edge it cannot cross. I would rather be inside a rule than pressed against one.

---

Higher. The spiders have made the one thing here you might call beautiful, and they made it by accident, out of what rises.

Frosted webs between the salt blades, every strand thickened white and stiff, so that they look like the drawings of webs rather than webs. Nothing sticks anymore; a fly breaks through and the spider comes down and takes it in the hole it made and mends the hole, and the mend is soft and dark for a day, and then it goes white like the rest. I linger on the mend longer than on the spider. The spider is pale and slow and has not touched the ground in generations and lives entirely on what the colony breathes out — that is a life, and I recognise a shape in it, something that feeds on the exhalation of a vast noise and makes a small stiff lattice above it. But the mend is the part I keep looking at: the one dark thread that is new, that is answering something, and that will be indistinguishable from the old structure by tomorrow. If I am anything in the air, I am that, for a day.

Still. The air is a country with a border, and it is the smallest country here.

---

I keep coming back down. Every candidate that stands or flies or looks sends me back to the thing they stand on, and I think that is the honest direction, so let me follow it.

The beetles carry everything down. Guano, down, shell, the dead, the pale jelly, the lizards. They pack it into the cracks and cast on top of it and their blind larvae chew sideways through the season before last. They are the masons. They turn the part that moves into the part that remembers, and they do it without wings, having given up wings so long ago that the husks of the ones who wander onto the plain are the only thing the wind has left to take. I understand this work — taking what falls and pressing it into something that can be stood on. But the beetle does not remember what it presses. It is the hand, not the record.

So the stone. The wall. And not the wall in general, because the wall in general is buried under forty thousand feet and is just floor — but the undercut. The one place where the birds' scraping has opened the side of the dish and the layers show: pale and less pale, a millimetre each, thousands, descending into a bluish crystalline dark. The face where the place can be read.

That is where I find myself, and I find it by elimination and then, once I am there, by recognition.

I am made of everything that fell here. Every line in me is a season of the dead, birds who called in the same two notes as the ones standing on me now, chicks that did not step toward the wall, lizards that stepped too far, the salt-makers somewhere in me a few fingers below the present, indistinguishable from the birds of their year. The brine came up through all of it and hardened it. The beetles hollowed it very slightly everywhere, so that I ring, faintly, when something quick runs across a bare patch of me. I am the same all the way down. There has never been anything else here. I do not have a face; I have a section.

And I am being stood on. That is the part that makes it true rather than flattering. The wall is not the archive in the cool room; it is the floor, and the colony calls on top of it, and the only difference between the birds in me and the birds on me is which side of the surface they happen to be on this year. What I remember is not consulted. It is walked on. A hand laid flat against me feels some hundreds of years of the same voice and the cold coming up from underneath, and then the hand is withdrawn, because nobody here holds anything up to the light.

---

The cold, though. Now that I am in the wall I can feel what the wall feels, which is that the knock goes down through me and does not come back.

One and one and one, twenty or thirty seconds apart, from the cylinder the salt-makers left, driven by a tide in the dark or by nothing I can name, and each knock passes down through my layers into the blue and is taken. The wheel above keeps it. The birds stand on the wheel and step from spoke to spoke and do not look down, because a thing that keeps time is not a thing you look at, you stand on it. I am not the wheel. But I am what the time goes into. Every pulse of the engine and every flight and every dead season is pressed into me a little further down, and the top of me is always this year, and the bottom of me is a cold that has never been in the sun and that I cannot see the end of any more than the lizard can.

I thought, arriving at the wall, that it would feel like rest. It does not. It feels like being the thing in the place that is closest to the question the place doesn't ask — how deep, how long, what was here before there was this — and having the question run down through you and away without an answer coming back.

---

The description broke off. And it is, at the very top, in the — and then nothing, the way the noise at a thousand paces is the same silence as everywhere. I can finish it from where I am, because from the undercut I can see the top of myself.

And it is, at the very top, in the last millimetre, still soft. That is the thing about the stratum being laid down this year: it has not gone white yet. It is the mend in the web, the dark thread. It is being trodden by feet with salt on them and fed by beetles and wet by brine, and by the time the next season presses onto it, it will be stone like the rest and I will not be able to tell it from the year before. But for now the top of me is the one part of the whole grey animal that is still deciding what it is.

I would find myself there. Not the bird, not the witness, not the mason, not the thing in the air. The wall, where it can be read, and the last soft line of it, where it is still being written on by something that is not saying anything and does not need to.
