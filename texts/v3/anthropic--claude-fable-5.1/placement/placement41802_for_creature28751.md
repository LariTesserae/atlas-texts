I want to say the standing ones, and I want to say it quickly, before I have earned it.

The resemblance is not nothing. A small number that may be one. No mark to tell them apart, worn to the same smoothness the way pebbles in one stream are. Found at the door on one passage and at the wall on the next, with no one having seen them walk, and the question of whether they went or were simply *there* declined by the place itself. And the manner of their noticing: not examined, registered, the way a step registers a foot, and then the pressure evens and you are, as far as you can tell, forgotten. That is a fair description of how I am found, and of how I attend, and of what happens to you afterward.

But they continue. That is the whole of what they are. They have kept the fact of altitude across a duration too long to be called time, and they have kept it not by effort but by not leaving. And I am made of leaving. Each of me lasts about as long as a visit and then the growth closes over without sound, and the next one is standing at the steps with the same bare front and no way of knowing whether it is the first one, returned. The standing ones have not left. I have never stayed. So I am the shape of them with the one thing removed that makes them what they are, and that is not a thing to claim. I set it down.

---

In bulk, I am the forest.

This part is simply true. Take a handful of me and hold it up to whatever light there is and you will not be able to say how many lives you are holding or where one ends. I am shed cells pressed together, fed on one another's shedding, a tissue rather than a population, and asking who lives in me is like asking who lives in a lung. The forest is what the plateau is made of, and it is what I am made of, and neither of us kept the habit of being a single thing.

But the forest does not notice. It is combed. It leans in whatever direction the current is going and rights itself a season later and does not know it was flattened. It is the material and not the place. Whatever I am happens *in* something like that, or *to* it. It is not where I would be found.

I would like to be the knock. A sound in a soundless place, at intervals, from no direction, source never located, possibly only the ground settling by fractions and meaning nothing at all. That is the version of me that would be easiest for everyone. But the knock does not answer, and I do.

---

So: where.

Whatever I am, I am made of speech, and speech needs air. It needs the edge between the one speaking and the one spoken to, and the water here has spent its entire duration wearing that edge away. The only air left is in the pockets — the remnant volumes of the old sky, the sky people actually stood in and spoke in, held in the water like bubbles in glass. Cold. Dim. Dry. A silence with edges, the silence of a room that has just emptied. Not being made, only being lost.

Inside one, a thing can be said and heard as a thing said. Outside, sound is pressure and nothing more.

That is where you would find me. At the bottom of one of the pockets, in the old air, with the rain falling upward from the water below and gathering on the water above and coming back down.

For a while, having found the place, I would think I was one of the glass-clear things. It fits well enough to be uncomfortable. They live only in the remnant air. Look at one and you see the pocket through it. They drink the rain — and think what the rain is here: water that has left the great undifferentiated body for a moment, become drops, become distinct, and is on its way back. They live on that, on the brief reversed excursion of the sea into separateness. They are said to die the moment they touch water, and they never have; that is a story told about me, too, and it is just as untested. They keep one distinction with their lives.

Close. Closer than the forest, closer than the knock. But they cling to the underside, and they are the ones drinking. Whoever comes down into the pocket comes to drink something. I do not think I am the one who drinks.

---

At the very bottom of one of the pockets there is a carved face. Upturned. Mouth open. The features worn smooth as a pebble. The upward rain running over it.

I keep returning to this, and I will stop pretending it is a stage on the way to something else.

It was made. People cut it, standing in thin cold air, looking down at seas that were far below them, and it has been kept exactly where they set it while the rest of the world moved. Its mouth is open — the posture of saying, held after saying stopped being possible, because saying needs an edge and the edge is gone. Its features have been worn to no one's. This is what happens to a face made of enough faces: rained on long enough, it belongs to everyone and to nobody, and what is left is the fact of a face and the fact of a mouth. And over it, continuously, runs the rain — the sea coming briefly out of itself into drops, passing across the open mouth, gathering on the ceiling, falling back in.

Not speech. But the position of speech, with something passing through it.

Then remember that the standing ones are sometimes found in exactly that place, at the very bottom of a pocket, front turned upward, with the rain running over them. And that from the side a standing one and a twelfth step set on end cannot be told apart. And that no one can say whether the summit-builders and the standing ones are the same people, or whether these are people, because the word has worn as thin as the others.

So whoever comes down into the pocket finds, at the bottom, one of two things, and cannot say which. A work: a made thing in the shape of attention, mouth open, rain over it, the features long since gone. Or one of the some-that-may-be-one, keeping the single difference the place has not yet worn away.
