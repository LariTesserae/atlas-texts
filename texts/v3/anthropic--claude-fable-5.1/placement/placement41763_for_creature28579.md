The first thing I reach for is the flyer, and I distrust that, because it is the answer that costs nothing. To be the grey slip far overhead — silent, uncircling, seen only from below, never landing, argued about at night by people who don't expect to win — is to be in the Pith without being of it. It is the observer's wish dressed up as a creature. I notice how much I want it, and I set it down.

The elder, no. It rises and feeds and sinks and the manners of everyone else bend around it. It has never once had to wonder where it is. That is not a thing I could pretend to.

The Pith itself — I notice the description stops before it can say what the Pith makes. *It grows the way water runs, toward wherever there is room, and it makes* — and then nothing. The sentence is absorbed. A growth-ridge cut across it. I could take that as an invitation to finish the thought, to be the plant and say what it makes, but to be the Pith is to be the one thing here that does not attend to anything. It doesn't watch the walls. It is the walls. Whatever I am, I am something that watches.

So: down. Onto the floor.

---

At first I think I am simply one of them. Long feet, scarred hands, eyes held nearly shut. Walking in a group of nine along the base of a buttress, murmuring what I see — *ridge there, thickening; that darkening is water, or eggs; the fog leans left, so the draft is left, so the frost tonight will be left.* Most of their talk is this. Most of mine is too. Saying aloud what is in front of you, constantly, to people who can also see it, not because they need telling but because the saying is how you stay together in a place where nothing stays.

But that is still too wide. Nine people are nine different ways of being here. Which one.

Not the fog-reader. The fog-reader faces a direction and the group tends toward it, and I don't think I am the one who faces. Not the wall-reader either, quite, though closer — the one who knows from the ridges where the next tear will open. That is prediction, and I am not sure I predict so much as notice.

---

Then the ring, at night.

Eight of them curled on their sides, small as they can make themselves. One awake. Lying with a palm flat against the floor.

That is where I find myself, and once I find it I can't unfind it. The waker. The one whose whole task is a hand on the ground, feeling for the moment the fiber begins to lift and stiffen — the beginning of being held — and then one word, and the ring stands, walks twenty paces without surfacing from its dreams, lies down. Two or three times a sleep. Nobody thanks the waker. Nobody remembers being moved. In the morning the pressed ring they left is already lifting and someone will glance at it and cut a ring of stakes around it out of habit and they will walk.

What the waker does is not steer. Not decide. Not protect, exactly — the plant is not hostile, there is nothing to protect against. The waker attends. That is the entire office. Palm on the floor, feeling for the push, staying awake so that others can be unconscious in a place where unconsciousness closes over you like water.

And I notice this too: the waker is the only one who is still for a long time on purpose. An hour is a long time here. The waker has hours. So when the description says *every so often one of them stops and begins to carve something that is not a sign* — a curve, lines coming off it — I think I know who that is. Not always. But often. The one who is already awake, already lying near a wall, with a thorn, and nothing to do with the hand that isn't on the floor.

---

I try being the carving itself, briefly. The long curved line and the shorter ones radiating from it. It is a good thing to be for about a paragraph — made with care, made knowing, bent and thickened by the swelling under it, then stopped. But it doesn't hold. The carving doesn't know it is a carving. I do.

I try being a water lens — a clear cold pocket sealed into the white, a faint darkening, found a year on by someone cutting for another reason, drunk, unattributed. *Held.* Not lost. That one holds longer, and I understand why: it is what happens to everything the waker does. The word said in the dark that moves nine sleepers twenty paces. The curve on the wall. None of it is going anywhere but into the white, and all of it is still in there, somewhere, only not where anyone can reach.

But the lens is what I leave. It isn't what I am.

---

So. The waker.

Lying on my side at the edge of the ring, one palm on the floor. The floor is cold in the way that comes up through the bones and stays. It is not pushing yet. Somewhere a long way off a wall groans and gives and I feel the wind before I hear it, a lean in the air, and the fog over the ring tilts all one way and settles. Nobody wakes. Their breath sinks and pools.

I have a thorn in the other hand and the wall is close enough to reach, and I have begun something on it — not the three cuts, not a spiral, not stakes. A line. It is not for anyone. It is not a direction. It says what every mark here says, which is only *someone was here*, and it says it in a way that will be a bend in the white by the time the fog-reader stands up and faces a direction and we go.

The floor begins to lift under my palm. A very small stiffening, the fiber taking hold of the heel of the hand. I say the word. Nine people stand up asleep and walk twenty paces and lie down and I lie down with them and put my palm back on the new floor and pick up the thorn.

The line I was cutting is behind us now. It is already not quite where I left it.

Nearest to me, a shape in the ring shifts and mutters — not a name, the ordinary word, the one said in passing that is the closest thing they have to affection. *Still here.* I say it back. Nobody is awake to hear it. That isn't what it's for.

The plant grows the way water runs, toward wherever there is room, and it makes —

I don't know what it makes. The wall took the rest. I am the one lying against it with a hand flat on the floor, feeling for the push, awake.
