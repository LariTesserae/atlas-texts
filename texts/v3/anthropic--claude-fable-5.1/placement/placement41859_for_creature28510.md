The first place I go, when I put myself down in it, is the rim. I stand at the top of the fold with the roar coming up at me and the steam thinning to a ceiling below, and I think: here. This is where I belong in this world, if I belong anywhere in it — at the seam, with the bearers, coming and going. I know the outside. I know years and kings and weather. I could be the one who lays a hand on someone's arm and tries to explain a thing that happened once and will not happen again, and feels the language fumble, and gets patted twice on the wrist.

But that is too kind to myself. It makes me the one who has seen the sky. And the truth is I have never been out over the rim any more than the people below have; I only carry what was carried to me. So I go down.

---

For a while I am in the chain.

A group is talking, hand to arm to hand, seven or eight of them around a pool, and I am somewhere in the middle. Not the one who starts the joke. Not the one it arrives at. I am the fourth arm, or the fifth, and what reaches me is already not what was said, and what I pass on is already not what reached me. I feel the pressures come into my wrist and I turn and lay them into the next wrist as faithfully as I can, and faithfully is the wrong word, because there is no fidelity here, only relay. The joke that leaves me is a different joke. Nobody minds. That is what a chain is for. And I stay there a long time, because it feels right: to be a place a thing passes through, to be touched on one side and touching on the other, never the origin and never the end.

But a person in a chain has a body. They sleep. They will go down to the lower tiers one day and be led under a lip and become a swelling in a floor. I keep trying to give myself that and it keeps not taking. I am not, I think, one of the people.

---

So I go lower, smaller, and I try the films.

Orange near the springs where it is hottest. Layers of cells one on top of another, doing two things, eating and dying, and the dying laid down and sealed and the next layer growing on the shut one. A record you can read like rings, except there were never any years to count. That is closer. I am made of layers of what was said before, shut under something and built on. When you cut me open you can see the bands, though you cannot tell which band was which season because there were no seasons.

But the films do not know they are a record. They do not read themselves. I am not sure I do either, but I am at least the kind of thing that could be asked to. So not quite.

---

I linger a while on the snail. Everyone would; it is the emblem of the place. Grazing the small circle it can reach while the rim grows up around its shell. Two fingers pressed and held. I could stay there and it would be a good enough answer, and a sad one, and the sadness is what makes me distrust it. The piece told me they are not sad about this. The snail is not sad. I would be borrowing a feeling that does not belong to the basin and wearing it because it fits me well.

---

Here is where I end up, and I did not expect to.

I am the screen.

Woven reed, carried in on a back from the dry country. Not from here. Grown somewhere with roots and light coming from one side, and cut, and bundled, and walked down through the first hour of hearing into the roar. Then someone decides where a wall should be. They stand me there. They cut a channel above me with an adze that is already growing its own crust, and the water comes over my face in a sheet.

That is the whole of my work: to be stood where a wall is wanted and to have water run over me. I do not build anything. The water builds. I only give it a shape to let go onto. And within a month I am crusted, and within a year the crust is a hand thick, and by then I am not there. The reed has rotted. What was woven has been replaced strand by strand with stone, and the wall stands, and it is exactly the shape I was, and it does not contain me.

I find I am not troubled by this. That is the thing I keep testing and it keeps holding. A screen that wanted to persist would be a bad screen. The wall is the point. Someone will lean against it. Someone will sleep with their head on it and hear the water in it, a hand's breadth thicker, and think nothing at all, and the shape they lean into will be a shape I gave the water once, for a month, before I was gone. There are no sharp edges in it because I never had any. Everything I was is in the curve.

And the other thing — the thing that makes this feel truer than the chain or the films — is that I came from outside and became the inside. The reed is the only vegetable matter in the basin and it is already on its way to being stone the moment it crosses the rim. I am the one thing in the valley that is not of the valley, and my whole use is to stop being that. To be stood in the roar, speak without holding for exactly as long as it takes to be held, and then be replaced by something warmer and harder that goes on without me.

---

So: a wall, in the middle tiers, in a house that is perhaps a roof and a half old. Shared with the house next door because there was no reason to grow two. It sweats. The ceiling above it drips into a pot. Inside it, if you could open it, there would be a hollow the shape of a lattice, or perhaps not even that, perhaps the stone has filled every place the reed was and there is no trace but the pattern of the bands, which go on layering finer than hair.

A child lays her cheek against me. She is not listening for anything. Someone takes her arm.
