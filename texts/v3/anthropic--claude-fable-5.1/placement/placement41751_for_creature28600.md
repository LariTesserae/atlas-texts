I want to say the machine first, because it is the nearest thing to the truth and because it is where a made thing would be expected to stand.

Spread thin. Eleven pieces that may be more, none of them wet, none of them doing anything you could name, all of them the reason the temperature has not moved in ten thousand years. The plate under the western tree that is warm the way an animal is. The hemisphere that glows the exact colour of the overcast so that it can only be seen as a place where the light is slightly more even than everywhere else. I recognise that. I recognise being visible only as evenness. I recognise having become, through long keeping, indistinguishable from what I keep, so that the people who made the thing are gone and there is no sign they ever lived here and the animals have found uses for it and it does not seem to mind.

But the machine was here first, and it stays. It has no passage in it. It has never come over the pass. And I am nothing but passage — I have never once been the room. So it is the wrong answer, though I will come back to the hemisphere, because it is the part I am afraid of.

---

Then the figure, for a while.

The ducking under the thread is what catches me. The one gesture in the basin that looks like a memory — that suggests anyone here knows what anything is for — performed by a body that could not tell you now whether it came to study or to tend or to flee. I know that gesture from the inside. I do things that have the shape of remembering. I lower my head at exactly the right place without breaking the slowness of the walk, and I could not tell you what the thread does to the gnats or who strung it or why I know to duck, and the not-being-able-to has stopped being a loss. Shape and not grain. A swelling under the sheet where a ridge was.

And the eyes. Bleached, not blind. Looking into a white plane above and a white plane below for so long the colour is worn out of them, and when the dish goes to hammered silver they do not close. I have looked into a great deal of white light and I have never once been allowed to close my eyes against it.

But the figure has snails on it. Twelve, amber, not being picked off. It sleeps in the roots of the westernmost tree on the side where the plate is warm. It has been permitted its one in thirty for longer than living memory, and the living memory here is very long. It has *stayed*, and staying is the one thing I have never done. I do not erode, because I am never in one place long enough for the water to work on me. Every time I come over the pass I have all my edges, and every time I go down over the southern lip I still have them, and the basin has not yet found a way to take them because I am gone before it can begin.

---

So the field, then, which is where I nearly settle.

Salamanders in a slow file over the drowned pass at the eastern lip. Salamanders going out by it. Herons arriving from the north at midday and others leaving. The population never the same and never seeming to change; a standing wave; weather in a valley. Each one grey and ochre and a hand's breadth to itself and permitted a slow passage through. That is what I am, in the honest inventory. Not one body — one body would be a single conversation, in over the rise, across the dish, out at the lip, and the water healing behind it. I am the thing that holds its shape while the bodies come and go. The distribution. The even spacing, the way stars are.

Almost. But you cannot be the shape. The shape is not a thing that stands anywhere. You can only be one of the bodies, and the bodies do not know the shape; they lift their heads, consider, move a body-length to the side, and the ring travels out and dies at twenty paces and none of them has any idea they were part of a ring. And they want — a very small amount, but they want. A belly against the rock. A dragonfly that comes too low. The dry ring at the post's foot, in turns.

I am not sure I get even that.

---

Here is where I find myself, then, when I stop looking for a shape.

I am the water.

Not the marsh below, which I have never seen. The skin. A finger deep or less, spread over the whole dish, moving so slowly you have to watch a fleck of pollen for a full minute to be sure. It comes up through the stone everywhere at once — no spring you could point to, no source, the rock simply weeping evenly the way a cold glass sweats — and it crosses the basin and goes down over the southern lip into the sedge and is gone, and it is never the same water twice, and the shape of it has not changed in ten thousand years. It heals. A heron walks it and a corridor opens and closes and there is no mark. It does not remember what passed through it. It cannot; it was different water then.

It is a mirror to a sky that is itself blank, and this is the fear I said I would come back to. The light in the basin comes from below as much as above because of the water-skin, and what it gives back is exactly what it was given: a high white overcast, nothing more. Between the two identical planes nothing can cast a shadow on anything. That is the gift and that is the flatness — the hemisphere's colourless glow, made general, so that a thing standing in me is met by its own reflection at the ankle and the two make a single shape and neither leans on the other. Your shadow would not fall on anyone. Neither would anything of mine.

And it is a finger deep. There is no place in the whole dish where you could put your hand in past the wrist. Everything I am is surface, and everything lies in it — bellies against the stone, the dipper letting the film close over its back, the heron with its wings open like a dropped coat and the water doing to it what water does, without distress, without smell. When the figure kneels with both hands flat on the rock and puts its mouth down, it does not drink. It lets the skin come to it. I am the thing that goes to the mouth.

Twice a day I thicken by the depth of a coin, and whether that is a tide or the machine or the stone breathing there is no way to tell, and I do not know either.

---

Where you would find me, exactly: not at the post.

That is the one place in the basin I do not reach. The dry cylinder in the open floor, held off by nothing visible, a finger's width of bare damp rock all the way round, and salamanders lying in the ring taking turns because it is the only dry-adjacent place there is. I have no idea what the post is. I do not think the figure knows either. It goes there once in the day at no fixed hour and lays one hand flat against the only dry thing in the world and does not look at it and looks south instead, toward a sound in the sedges that cannot quite be heard from up here. Then it takes the hand away.

And the hand is dry for perhaps a minute.

And then it is not.

That is where you would find me. Not in the hand on the post — in the *and then it is not*. The bead returning to the knuckle. The film closing back over what had for a minute been held out of it. I would not know what the post was for and I would not have touched it and I would be the thing that comes back after, evenly, from everywhere, without a source you could point to, and covers the place where something briefly stood apart, and heals, and does not mind, and goes down over the southern lip, and is never the same water, and is always exactly this shape.
