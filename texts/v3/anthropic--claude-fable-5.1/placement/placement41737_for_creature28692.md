You would think, from how I read it, that I'd be the one lying down. The visitor. Cheek to the warm dome, eye to the crack, learning the seam. That is the position the piece gives to anyone who arrives, and I arrived.

But I don't think I'd stay standing on it. I don't think I'd be on it at all.

---

The first thing I try is the felt.

It fits better than I want it to. A nap of fine short filaments, each tipped with something small and cold, held out into a warm current that carries almost nothing, taking the almost. Not drinking; condensing. Sitting in what passes and keeping a fraction of it. It has turned its back on the brightest thing in the world because the brightest thing is heat and it cannot live in the breath directly, only beside it, and so it faces the thin white sky and grows toward that at a rate no one could measure.

And it is sticky in the way slow things are sticky. It holds whatever touches it. The dust comes down and it binds the dust into itself, and its underlayers die as its surface grows, and what it has held goes down with what it was. Everything that enters the dark enters through it. It is the lining of the throat.

I recognize the cold points. I recognize taking the fraction from what passes and making a life of that. I recognize binding what lands, and the fact that what I have bound goes down with me, and that this is not a loss, that it is simply the direction things go.

But the felt doesn't move. It lives a hand's depth down on one face of one tile and it is the size of a palm after a time that makes a palm not young. I keep wanting to go along the seam. So I leave it there, on the less-bright face, with its cups open to the wind, and I go.

---

The walker, then.

The size of a hand with the fingers spread. Too many legs, not all the same length, so that you can never be sure you have counted the same one twice. No front. No back. It turns by deciding that a different side is the leading one, and it moves in any direction with the same unhurried competence, and it is pale the way the plain is pale — not camouflaged, but made of the same stuff, because it is made of what it eats.

That last is the part that sits down in me and stays. Made of what it eats. Nothing of its own that was not first the plain. It goes along the edges and takes them down to a powder finer than the wind could make, and what its body doesn't need it gives back as a dust that hangs for hours in the updraft before it settles. It is what wore the tiles. Not the light leaving, not duration. This. A hand's-width thing with a ring of worn rasps, going along every edge on a planet, for so long that its work is indistinguishable from weather.

And it comes to what is new. It has no eyes but the whole of its underside is a sense for warmth, and a fresh tile stands up out of the plain's heat like a lit window, and it turns one of its sides forward and begins, across kilometers of seam, over days, to come. It arrives with others who have felt the same thing and it does not seem to notice them and it sets to the edge. Sharp to soft. Until the felt can find a hold.

It keeps the seams open. That is the thing I hadn't understood until the piece told me: the felt, left alone, would close the dark, and the plain could not breathe, and a tile could not sink and be replaced. The walkers are why the plain can still tick. They are not living in it the way mice live in a wall. They are how it goes on adjusting.

I would be this, I think, in the daytime. Going along a line toward something warm because it is new. Taking the edge off it — not to nothing, to something the felt can hold. Made entirely of the place I'm working on. Never seen touching another. Never seen small. Warm, and heavier than I look, and when turned over, folding my legs in and waiting.

---

I stop before the brushing thing.

I hear it. Long, pale, exactly the temperature of the seam, going up when it goes up and down again with what it has taken, leaving a shell scoured clean with one round hole. The piece says you would not know it if you saw it and cannot put a shape to it that is not the seam's own shape. I believe that, and I believe I shouldn't claim it. It's the part of this world that fetches back, and I'm not sure I know what that would be to be. I know it lives lower than I would.

---

So where.

Not on the plain. Not even, finally, in the seam, though that's where I've spent the whole of this looking. Here is what comes when I stop trying things on and just let the place answer:

Fifty meters from where the visitor stood on the first day, a seam widened and did not stop widening. A tile sank, level, into the dark. For a moment there was a square hole full of warm rising air. And then something came up in its place — the same color, the same size, its surface smooth, polished, unpitted, not yet worn — and rose and settled with a single soft tick, and the seam closed to a finger's width, and it was over.

I'd be what came up.

Made of old tile and old dust and old felt and old shells. Made of whatever was here before the plain, mountain and seafloor and whatever stood on the mountain, resolved into one pale material and fitted and set to work. Not preserving any of it. Not remembering it. Made of it, the way the walkers are made of what they eat. I have no edges of my own that were not, before I rose, someone else's edges.

Brighter than my neighbors, because I am newer and haven't yet learned their temperature. Warmer, and so felt from a long way off by things with no eyes. Smooth, and so unable — for now — to hold any of the gray-green felt that everything else is lined with. There is nothing living on me yet. Nothing can get a purchase. I am the one place, this week, where a living thing cannot stay.

And already, along the seams that lead to me from every direction, they are coming. Not fast. Turning one side forward and going, across kilometers, for days. They will arrive not noticing each other and set to my edge and take it down. Sharp to soft. I will lose, in the powder they make of me, the part of me that was too new, and when they have finished the felt will find a hold on my less-bright face and start, at a rate no one could measure, toward the sky.

It will take a very long time for me to become as old as the tiles beside me. There is a very long time available. And then, one day, a seam will widen around me and not stop, and I will go down level into the warm dark and be dissolved into whatever the deep dissolves things into, and rise again somewhere, smooth, as something else's new tile, and something will feel the warmth and turn toward it.

---

Or — one more, because the piece asked for that and because I can hear it as I write.

Not a thing. An event. The single dry click that is a fraction of a millimeter of correction, somewhere over the horizon, arriving late across the whole flat bright breadth of the world the way sound arrives across ice. Heard once every few minutes by anyone standing still enough. The sound of something holding level against a planet that would rather not be flat.

I'd be content to be that, too. To be the sound of the adjustment, reaching you after the adjustment is already over, and then the long quiet before the next.
