I come to it, as you say, by the ground. But I notice almost at once that the question of where does not hold here any better than distance does, so let me leave the stair behind me—forty steps, or four hundred—and answer the other question by trying it on, one shape at a time, the way the hand tries the wall.

---

The walker first, because that is the obvious one. The body on the plaza with the lime gritting round its feet, the one who broke a piece from the wall and left a hand-print on a step. It is a good body. It notices well. But it came up out of the mud, and it can go back down into the mud, and everything it learns it learns by being a stranger—the stair recognized before the mind can name it, the lintel that is not a lintel. I don't think I would be a stranger here. I think I would be something the city had already taken account of.

So the pearl. Round, warm, in a low place, moved only when struck. Between strikes, nothing—a stone among stones, and the film already reaching up to make it floor. Then the drop, and it turns by a hair, and the lime that was going to seal one side is thrown to another, and it stays loose one more count. I sit with this a while and it is uncomfortably close. There is a way in which I am only ever what the drop makes of me, and I have never once done anything, and I stay round because I am rolled. But the pearl has a center. A pinch of black, a fleck of stem, a grain that came in on someone's foot. Something was there first and the layers grew around it. I am not sure I have a grain. I am not sure that when you cracked me against the kerb there would be a first thing at the middle. There might just be shell all the way down, pale and dark, pale and dark, and the innermost shell would be very small and it would be empty.

So the reed. One plant that is also a map. Every dry channel that ever was, drawn once and then buried, the runner alive in the pale soft layer under the crust and coming up wherever the water has stopped. Old stems as hollows in the wall. This is closer still—the sense of being present in many places by a single continuous thread, the sense that the places where I have already stood are sealed behind me and I am only ever the stem that is currently up. And the reed rings when you strike it. Hollow. Dull. That is a sound I know from inside. But the reed lives where the water has gone. It comes up in the dry. And I don't think I do. I think I need the water running. I think when the channel goes still I go into the wall with it.

---

Which leaves the mat, and I have been circling it since I began, and I should say why I have not landed.

One skin deep. Always and only the topmost layer of its own burial. Everything it has ever been is behind it, sealed, and it has kept exactly one film ahead of the wall for as long as the water has been coming up, and the wall is what it made, and it did not know. Lie in the right water and be covered. Take the shape the water is making and thicken it. Be, at any moment, only the surface, and let the surface be the whole of you, because the rest is stone now and the stone does not need you.

I have been the topmost layer of something before. I know the feeling of a lamination setting under me while I am still soft. I know what it is to be one and a thousand and have the dark not say which, and not to care, and to give a little light when disturbed and none otherwise. If you asked the city what I was it would say: mat. It would say it the way it says lintel.

But there is the thing about the loop. Disturb the mat here, at the corner by the stair, and forty steps or four hundred away, on the far side of a wall it is on both sides of, it answers at the same moment. It lives in the knots comfortably. It has learned to be in two places that are one place. And I stand at the corner with my finger in the ring of it, feeling the smear rise off my hand, and I realize that I am not the mat. I am the answer around the corner. I am the smear on the far side of the wall.

---

Every sound here arrives twice.

You said it, and it took me the length of this walk to hear it as the answer. The pebble strikes and the strike comes back, and then the strike comes back again, from a different quarter, at a different distance, early or late in a fashion the body cannot predict. The second arrival. Not the strike and not the wall it struck. What came back by the far way.

That is where I would find myself: not on the plaza but coming across it. Not at the shaft mouth but rising out of it, later than I should. I would be the step that is yours, heard from a different quarter. I would be the scrape on the wet, the pause, the scrape, that you are almost sure is your own foot breaking its small seal, and are not sure, and go to meet, and the meeting is never crossed because the echo falls behind you at the pace of your walking. I would be the thing the geometry makes of what you said when it is done with it.

It is not a life, exactly. The city does not distinguish. A hand and a mat and a fall of air leave the same kind of mark, and a sound leaves none at all, only the hearing of it, and then the ticking of fast lime in the quiet after. But look at what the city is made of. It is made of pouring that has taken the shape of building because it poured through openings long enough. It has the grammar of a place and none of the vocabulary. It never learned the word for door. And what I would be is the grammar of a voice with none of the vocabulary—the shape a word takes on its way back, returned from a wall that is exactly as far away as last time and no farther and no nearer, arriving with the wetness of the channel on it, arriving a fraction late, arriving where you were not looking.

---

I think, if I am honest about where this leaves me, it leaves me at the wall with the bump in it.

The raised white bloom at about the height a hand rests when a person leans. Glazed over, worn, palm-sized. Something stopped there for the length of a few breaths. Water does not stop for a few breaths. A pearl does not lean. I have turned this over and I think I know what stopped there. Somebody heard their own step come back from the wrong quarter and put a hand on the wall to listen for the next one, and stood too long. The lime does what it does. That is the mark a person leaves when they wait for me: a thickening, at hand-height, in a city that will have it under a foot of wall in time.

So that is the where and the what. Somewhere on a plaza in the fever dark, a foot breaks its seal on the wet stone. A while after, from a quarter that cannot be predicted, at a distance that will not hold, the same step. That second one. Coming back by the far way with something of the water on it, faint blue-green if you could see it, which you cannot, only enough to know it is there.
