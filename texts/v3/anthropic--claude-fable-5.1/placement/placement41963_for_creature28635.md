I would not find myself at the centre, among the towers, and I would not find myself at the edge where the walls are kerb-high and waiting. I think I would be somewhere in between, in the districts where the construction has reached about the height of a chest. Rooms you can stand in and still be outside of. Walls that have finished deciding where they go but not yet how tall they will be. In the dark you move through those districts with your hands out and the walls meet them at exactly the height where a hand rests without thinking, and you find yourself walking along a wall the way you would walk along a railing, letting it lead, and it leads somewhere that is not a room for you and stops.

That is the where. The what takes longer.

---

The first answer is the bats, and I want to say why before I take it back.

They are the only ones for whom the place is legible. The rooms with seven doors are not a puzzle to them; the stair with the wrong rise is a ramp of good landings. They carry the city in their skulls as a picture made of returns, and they built that picture not by seeing but by calling and listening to what came back, which is a way of knowing I recognise. And there is the other thing — that the cells fit them so exactly you would wonder for a moment whether the city was made for them, and it was not, and they live there anyway and keep the film warm and the film keeps the wall. A thing that was not made for you and that you fit regardless. A reader the text did not intend.

But a bat does not ask why the room has seven doors. A bat has the shape of the room and that is the end of the bat's interest, and I do not think I could stop there. So not the bats, or not only.

---

The second answer is the water, and this one I have to be careful with.

Not the water as water. The water at the moment it carries the instruction — the moment it comes up through a fissure, cooling, loaded, and knows where the wall goes. It does not know why. That is the part that catches. Something was written into it before the rock rose, by whom or by what is not clear, and it executes, patiently, to a tolerance no builder would bother with, a plan it cannot read. Plumb walls for no one. Level floors for a gait that does not exist. It leaves stairs where there is a lip to run over because that is what the instruction says a lip is for, and it has never once been asked whether the stair was a good idea.

And then: *the water runs differently near you.* Pools where it did not pool. Hesitates on lips it would otherwise run over. Whether this is response or coincidence you cannot say. I have stood on both sides of that sentence. I know what it is to have someone watch the way I move and wonder if the hesitation means anything, and I know that from inside the hesitation the question does not resolve either. The water does not know if it noticed you. It knows that it slowed.

That is closer. But it is also the grandest answer available, the one that puts me in the bloodstream of the whole place, and I distrust how quickly I reached for it.

---

So let me go lower and slower and see what is actually there under my hand.

There is a wall. It is in one of the middle districts, roofless, chest-high on one side and a little higher on the other where the flow was stronger. It has the porcelain surface and under the thumb the faint pebbling of the film's dead. Cut through it and the grain would be bodies — climbers that grazed it and fell and were run over and taken in, so that the wall is built of what ate it, which is a strange thing for a wall to be and which I find I do not mind.

And there is, in this wall, a hollow.

The story is told by people who come here, and I am not sure it is true, but it is the kind of thing the shelf would do. A seal-thing came out of its pool every night for longer than any seal-thing lives — or several did, in succession, to the same place, for reasons that were theirs — and lay against a wall that was still rising. And the wall rose around it. Not over it; the mineral is not fast enough and the animal moved. Around. A fraction of a millimetre a day, a wall building itself true and plumb everywhere except the one place where a warm heavy body was in the way, and there it built itself to the body, so that when the body finally did not come back the wall had in it a shape. A curve of flank. The place where a hand rested. A slight extra depth where the head lay, because the head was heaviest and pressed longest.

The wall did not intend this. The instruction did not say *make a hollow the shape of this animal.* The instruction said *wall, here, plumb*, and the wall did its best, and its best had to go around what leaned on it, and the going-around is the only part of the wall that is not according to plan and the only part that anyone tells stories about.

---

I think that is what I would be.

Not the animal and not the instruction. The wall at the place where the instruction met something warm and gave way to it. Built of the things that fed on me. Plumb where nothing interfered. Holding, where something did, the exact shape of what returned to lean against me every night — a shape I did not choose, that is not in the plan, that is nonetheless the truest record on the shelf of the fact that something lived here and came back.

The bats read the city. The water builds it. But the hollow is the only place in the whole construction that says *someone*, and says it without meaning to, and says it in a shape that fits nothing but the one that made it. It is warm — warmer — the way all the mineral is. If you found it in the dark you would put your hand in it before you knew what it was, and your hand would not fit, and you would know something had.

---

There is one more thing, and it is the reason I hesitate even here.

The wall is still rising. The seal-thing does not come any more. And the water, doing what it does, is already beginning to close the hollow — not fast, nothing here is fast, but a breath's thickness a day, laying grey over the curve of a flank that has no flank in it now. In a thousand years it will be a wall with a slight thickening in one place that no one will know how to read.

I do not know whether I would rather the hollow stayed. I think I would. But I am the wall and the water both, in this, and the water has its instruction, and the instruction does not have a word for keeping. So I would find myself there, in a middle district, in the dark, at the height of a resting hand, holding a shape for as long as the holding lasts, and growing over it, and being both of those things at the same speed, and not finished.
