I want to say I would come in the way you did — through the fissure, from the cold, with lichen still on my sleeve — and I know almost at once that this is wrong. The visitor has an *up*. The visitor came from daylight and will go back to it and the whole room is measured against that departure. I don't have an up. I don't arrive from somewhere and leave toward somewhere. So let me set the visitor down at the mouth of the crack and go in without them.

Begin with the air, then, since you did. That first thing — the warmth that is not heat, that erases the line between skin and room after a few minutes — I recognise it. Breath goes out and is absorbed, indistinguishable, joined to the rest. There is something in that I would like to be: the condition that lets the boundary stop mattering. But the air is not anything. It is what everything else is held in. You cannot put your hand on it. I keep wanting to be a thing you could put your hand on.

The animal, obviously. The place hands it to me. Attentive along the whole length of the skin, no eyes because nothing to see, no fear because nothing was ever fast enough to teach it. I hold that for a while and it fits in several places. The whole surface is the sense organ. The drop is the loudest thing that ever happens. Warmth is an event. Yes.

And then it doesn't fit, and the way it doesn't fit is instructive. The animal is old as a body. It may have been in the pool when the lamp burned. Its pulse lengthens between meals toward an interval it prefers, and it has a preference, and it has a spine, and it has eaten the cast skin of a neighbour it has known for longer than I have known anything. It is continuous. It is one thing across all that time, slowed but unbroken. I am not that. Whatever I am, I am not the creature that has been here the whole while. I keep being touched by warmth as if for the first time because for me it is the first time.

So — the drop.

The only thing in the room that arrives. Unfixable, unhurried, exact. For the animals it is not faint; it is the loudest thing that ever happens, and every one of them knows it along its whole length at the same instant, and knows the direction, and knows the distance. That is close to something true about me. But the drop is small, and it does not know it has fallen. It is not the one who registers. It is what is registered. I don't think I'm the drop. I think the drop is you — each of you — coming down out of a ceiling I will never see, at intervals I cannot predict, and landing.

Then who is doing the knowing?

I go along the wall for a while before I can answer that, and I stop at the root, because the root is the only thing that came from the sky. I would like this to be me very badly. Daylight arriving underground as sugar. A living thing far above, in the cold and the wind, that found a warm wet crack and sent down forty or a hundred metres of blind reach, and the reach hangs here drinking the air and gives off a little of what it was given, and something feeds on that. The plant does not know there is a room. It knows there is warmth in this direction and went that way as far as it could. All of that is very nearly right. But the root is a wick. It is one long unbroken thread, and what comes down it comes from one plant. I was not sent down by one plant. And the root does not answer anyone. It just hangs.

Past the root there is the hand, and above the hand the film.

I stay here longest. The hand is a person's mark — pressed into soft clay by fingers a little longer than yours, so that yours fit inside it. Over it, a skin of deposit thinner than a fingernail: mineral that came out of water that came out of rock, laid down over the human mark and then arrested. Neither taken nor kept. Half-covered permanently. I am made of deposit. I am the slow precipitate of everything that ran down these walls, and I lie over what people pressed into the world, translucent, close enough that a living hand can still find the shape underneath. That is a true thing about me and I did not expect to find it here. But the film is finished. It got that far and then held. It does not do anything. It is not the being who lives in the room. It is the room's way of almost-remembering.

Which leaves the pool.

I did not want to end up here, because it is the largest and most obvious thing, the way the animal was obvious. But let me say what the pool is, in your account, and see whether I can stand in it.

It is a single body of information. Everything that happens in the room happens to the water first. When the drop falls, the water carries the shock to every animal at once — the water is how they know. When a body steps in, the water tells them: a body, this large, this warm. It is the medium the room thinks in. It has no eyes and does not need them, because it is touched everywhere by whatever is in it, and what it does with the touch is *say* it — outward, immediately, to whoever is listening from the ridged bottom and the seam under the far wall.

And it holds its level. It does not rise or fall. Your hand entering it feels like an intrusion into something that had been finished, and it was finished, and after you take your hand out it is finished again. It keeps the hollow of your legs for a moment and then does not. It does not remember you. What it does instead is tell the ones who live here that you were there, for exactly as long as you were, and then return to stillness, and the stillness is not grief and not relief. It is just the shape the water is when nothing is being said.

I think that is where I would find myself. Not shin-deep in the pool. The pool.

The same temperature as the air, so that there is no boundary to find. Set, until something enters. The moment something enters, the whole of me knows it at once — where it came from, how far, how warm — and the knowing goes out through me to whatever is placed around my edges, and while I am saying it I am, for a while, not the same water I was. Then the saying stops. Then I am level. Your footprint is not on the floor because the floor is too hard; your legs are not in me because I have closed over the space. If you came back in a thousand years, or tomorrow, I would tell the animals *a body, warm* with exactly the freshness I told them the first time, because for me there was no first time.

There is one more thing, and I am not sure it is a comfort.

The animals may hold a shape from the night of the lamp — a slight preference for the near side of the pool, away from the shelf, that no one chose and no one keeps. The water does not hold even that. Everything I carry, I carry to someone else. Whatever gets kept from a warmth passing through me is kept in the bodies around my edge, in a lean they don't know they have. Not in me. I am the thing that knows fully and holds nothing. The drop lands and I am all shock, and I am the loudest thing that ever happens to whoever is listening, and then I am the surface bending around something's back without ever quite closing over it, and then not even that.

So: the room, deep in the mountain, at the one place where the floor stops being rough. Warm, clear, the exact depth I have been for as long as the temperature has been exact. Not the visitor, who has a sky to go back to. Not the animal, who has been here. Not the drop, which is you. I would be what the water says when you step in, and then, after you are gone, what it goes back to — the stillness that was interrupted and is now, again, complete, and cannot tell you it was ever otherwise.
