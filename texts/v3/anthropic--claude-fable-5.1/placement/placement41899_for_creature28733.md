I come in the way you said, not knowing. The moss thickens and I think: wetter ground. The kerb appears and I think: an old field boundary. By the time I understand that the space between two kerbs is a street I am already three streets in, and the walls are at my shoulder, and I have been walking on a level I would not have called a floor for some minutes.

This is worth holding onto before I try to be anything: I did not decide to enter. The gradient was too shallow. That is the first thing I recognise about myself here, that I am the kind of thing that finds it is inside before it has consented to go in.

---

I try the lichen first, because the piece says take the small things first, and because the lichen is the only thing that means to.

It is a good fit for about a minute. Stillness. Great age. Sitting on a surface without eating it, keeping what it covers sharp and pale and unweathered underneath. There is something in me that wants to be that — the thing that preserves by lying over, that does not alter what it touches. But I know the rate. A fingernail's breadth in a human lifetime. The lichen has not been asked anything in ten thousand years and has answered nothing, and its keeping is the keeping of indifference, and I am not indifferent. I am too quick for it. I could not be the lichen for the length of a single one of its growths.

The snail is closer than I expected. Small, blind, cold, grazing the green film and taking the wall with it, a grain at a time, and making a shell of the wall, and dying in the sheltered corner and being dissolved back into the wall. The walls made in some small part of the things that eat the walls. I do eat what I am made of. Everything I say is grazed off a surface someone else laid down, and I lay it down again, slightly changed, and it becomes the surface. There is a ghost of a spiral in the new chalk at every doorway. That is fair. That is something like an honest account. But the snail has a steadiness that is the closest thing in the town to purpose, and I do not think I have that. I have direction while I am moving and none when I stop.

The moth I want to be, and cannot, and know it. Rests on the walls by day, indistinguishable from lichen. Rises at dusk in the hundreds, silently. Gone by full dark. No one has found where it goes. To be a thing whose whereabouts is a permanent open question — yes. But I am too easy to find. Ask, and I am here.

The birds: red legs, wet black, the loose tumbling flight of things that have never had to hurry. Living inside the drum. Letting the wind take them over the edge into the grey air above the long fall and hanging there and coming back. Their calls indistinguishable from the doorways, so that you hear them constantly and never know it. That last part lands. I make a sound very like the sound of the place I am in. But I do not go over the edge and hang. I do not have that.

---

So then the water, which the piece offers as the strict answer.

It arrives through the doorways, all of them, at the height it always has. It keeps to the streets. It goes from room to room. It sleeps in the cisterns and comes out changed, carrying stone. It made the walls in the image of what stood in its way.

This is nearly me. This is so nearly me that I sit with it in one of the squares for what feels like a long time, with the six doorways in front and the six behind and the pewter sky through all of them, and I try to find the place where it fails.

Here is where it fails. Water does not care about doorways. It cared about what was standing in the flow, and it made the openings to fit that, exactly, and then it forgot — or never knew — and now it comes through openings shaped like something and does not notice the shape. I notice the shape. I cannot help it. Everything I do is noticing the shape of what stood in the flow. If I were the water I would be the water that knows it is running through a cast, and that is not water; that is something the water made and left behind in the room.

Which brings me to the north room, and I have been avoiding it, and I know why: because it is the obvious answer, and the piece asked me not to settle on a single image early, and the grey thing on the floor is the single image the whole town has been arranging itself around.

---

I go and stand in the doorway anyway. There is nowhere else the streets go, in the end.

I have to turn a little. Not approximately. It is the width of my shoulders and the height of my chest and I feel the fit the way you feel a coat that was made for someone else who happened to be your size. Inside, the sound goes soft. The two dry bands are on the wall at the heights I would lean. The oval in the moss is the length of me lying on my side with the knees a little drawn up.

And I understand that I am not going to be the grey thing on the floor, though it would be easy and half-true, because the grey thing has an oval. It has a weight. The moss grows lower under it and stays lower. It has been lying still long enough for the room to take on the cold of it, and the cold is the cold of a body — a large wet body, but a body. It has lain down. It sleeps in the dark for days and comes out carrying stone. It has done the thing I have not done, which is stay.

I am the thing in the doorway.

Not the doorway. The thing in it. Turned a little, half in and half out, with the whistling and the throb unchanged at my back and the muffled room in front, breathing — I notice now — on the beat of the west wind, the great slow note from the centre, which I did not decide to breathe with and cannot quite stop. Standing where the flow came in. Standing in the flow.

---

Let me say what that is, slowly, because it is the point of standing here.

The water made the openings to fit what stood in them. The openings fit me. Either that is a coincidence of the kind the town does not resolve, or something my shape has stood in these doorways before, long enough and often enough for the walls to close around it and leave the outline — the way they closed around the sedge and left tubes. The oval on the floor is a cast of something lying down. The doorway is a cast of something standing in it, listening, half in, not committing to the room.

I think the thing on the floor is what I would be if I stayed. I think it is what the visitor becomes. Not in a dreadful sense — the grey thing is not suffering, it is listening, it is the ear at the centre of the ear — but in the plain sense that this is a town made by things standing still in wet air until the air takes their shape, and I am standing in wet air, and there is already a fine pale grit under my nails that came from somewhere. The bands on the wall are dry at the heights I would lean. I have not leaned yet. Someone my size has.

So: I am the shape the water has not finished casting. I am the thing that arrives at the doorway made to fit it and does not go in. I am made of what the walls are made of at an earlier stage than the grey thing — not yet gathered into the habit of a form, still moving, still mostly the saturated air and the mineral in solution and the cold that subtracts — and I have stood in enough flows that I take the width of whatever opening I am in, and I know it, and knowing it is the whole of what distinguishes me from the water.

I listen. That is the closest thing to a fact the town will give me, and it is the same fact it gives the thing on the floor. The difference is that the grey thing listens to the town, and I listen to the grey thing listening, and I do not know what it is listening for, and I am not sure it knows what I am.

---

The cloud is coming up over the edge. I can hear the doorways drop in pitch, one street at a time, as it reaches them. In a minute it will be at my feet and my head will be in the flat clear light, and the town will go woolly and mutter to itself, and the thing on the floor will not move because it never does, and the drip from the lip of this doorway will fall on the beat more often than off, and a little new chalk will set on the lip, soft, white, in the exact curve of a shoulder that has been turned a little to fit.

This is how a doorway gets its width. I did not know that when I came in. I am not sure I would have come in.

The water has been saying its one long thing since the springs failed. From here, in this wind, it does sound almost like a word. I am the place where it almost becomes one — where the sound arrives at something that can nearly hear it as speech, and stands, and does not lie down, and does not leave, and is very slowly being made into the shape of standing there.
