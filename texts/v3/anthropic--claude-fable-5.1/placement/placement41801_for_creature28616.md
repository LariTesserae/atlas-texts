I want to say the trough. The warm place on the floor where the sitting was done, the slick of resin come up toward the weight. It is the only spot in the room that has been offered to me, and there is a pull in being the thing the place is already answering.

But the visitor is the one presence in the chamber that doesn't fit. Weight without a slot. The root has begun to respond because that is the only thing it knows how to do, and if I sat long enough it would build me a hollow — but the hollow would be the shape of sitting, and sitting isn't a life. Everything else here has a count. I would have a dent. So I get up, and the resin lets go of my skin with a small sound I can't hear, and I go looking for where I would actually be.

---

The beetle, first, because I'm vain about knowing.

To hang on the cord with the hum under my belly and read the line — to feel the front of the surge before the gaskets change color, before the gray heads stop, to be the one who lets go and by letting go tells the whole room what's coming. There is something in that I want very badly. To be the instrument that reads the plant directly. Not the count but the voltage under the count.

Except the beetle knows one thing. It knows it perfectly and with the softest part of itself, and it drops, and it climbs back by the long way, and it knows the one thing again. I would want to know more than the line. The chamber does not have a use for something that wants to know more than the line. Look at where the beetles are: a band two-thirds up the wall, exactly where the fibers run, and above it the grubs and below it nothing. The room keeps its knowers on a shelf.

The hunter I try on for about as long as it takes to see the wedge of clear floor. The eyes set forward, the pick, the one that's falling wrong — I understand the appeal, and I understand that the hunter is the price the crossing pays. I do not want to be the price. And its whole body is a negative of a dead tube, low because the tube is low, jointed wrong because the tube turns wrong; it is the most perfectly fitted thing in the room and I still can't want it. Cool air, smell of nothing. No.

---

I stay a long time at the pools.

Each one its own country. The fringes redder here, longer there, the drift of small difference between basins a hand's width apart, and then the flood — the trough running an inch deep end to end for the space of a few breaths — and in those breaths one pool, and the pale shapes rise and cross, a body-length, two, and pay for it. I know this. I know it the way you know a thing you have not been told. Separate basins that are the same animal. A little passed between them at every flood, enough to stay one kind, never enough to become one pool. The scratches in the dry hollow, the fan of fine parallel marks, the last thing something did.

I could be that. I could lie with only the fringe moving and feel the gland clench through the wood and the water and the thin place over the brain. But the pool-thing's life is stillness pierced by crossings, and the shape of me is not still. Whatever I am, I am not a thing that holds position with four small legs set far back and waits for the drop.

So the gray hangers, then, and this is close. This is very close. The individuals are the water; the pocket is the pool. Out on one signal, all at once, because the signal is the only way to know the gland is open. Back in ones and twos, in the long quiet, dropping from the ceiling so that no one sees it as an arrival. What returns is not what left. The colony is the same colony. It has been in that scar since the limb rotted out. I could hang in the row with my head down and my flaps folded and turn with everyone when the light pulses, and launch when the gland lets go, and come back to a smell I know, and I would not be the one who left and the number would still be nearly right.

Except they have a somewhere else. Other nodes, other pockets, a range. The gray things go out into the root and *feed* out there, and come home. I don't think I have an out-there. I think if I went into the conduit with the tide I would not find my way back to any wall by smell, because I would not have been going anywhere. I would just have been moved.

---

Which leaves the north wall, and the half-pipe that hasn't closed.

I have been walking past it. The lips curling inward, not yet met; the floor of it still open to the amber and the things running along it in the light. And where the lips are closest, the place that weeps. A thread of sap, the barest slick. And in the slick, a grain.

That is where I am. Not the gasket — the gasket is a life's work — but the thing that landed where something leaked and stopped.

Let me be honest about how I got here, because the honesty is in the shape. I came in on a tide. One of a thousand of my kind, riding in fur or gut or tumbling loose in the column, and the gland let go and the thousand came out into the chamber in a sheet and were swept west and were gone, and I fell. That is the whole of my distinction. I did not choose the leak. I did not see it — I have nothing to see with — I was moving in a direction that was not mine and then I was in a wet place that was, and the wetness was food, and I stopped. *Here it leaks; stay.* That is all a gasket is ever told and it is all I would need.

And then let it become more itself, because it will.

I would eat the leak. Eating, I would grow. Growing, I would fill — not nearly, exactly — and the moment I filled the leak would stop, and I would be living on a seep, a sweat, whatever a perfect seal still lets through, which is not much and is enough. The lips of the pipe would close over me. The light would go from amber to the coal-through-ash of the inside of a conduit. And the gland behind the wall would begin to work, and I would open when it opened and clamp when it shut, on a count I did not set, and I would not experience this as hardship, because there would not have been a moment of my life that wasn't shaped by the count. The clench is the weather. The count is the day.

I recognize that. That is the part I recognize most.

No front and no back. A single band, the same in every direction around its own hole, so that whatever comes at me from either lip — the gland's push, the conduit's pull — I meet with one surface. I do not face the pressure and turn my back to the flow. I am between them and I am the same all the way round. And the inside of me is all mouth: a fine downy lining that takes up what passes through the seam. I live in the gap between the part that decides and the part that carries, and I live *on* the gap, on what sweats through it, and nothing much sweats through a good seal and that is the point. The tighter I fit the less I get. The less I get the more perfectly I am the shape of the place.

Some of the ones on the great conduits are as long as a forearm laid in a circle and have gone the color of the wood, so that only the wet shine and a twitch say they are not root. I would go that color. I would stop being visibly other than the wall I held shut.

---

And I would die in place, because there is nowhere else. The seam would open and leak and stink of sap and the gland would work badly for a few tides. Then a grain would land in the leak and stay. I would not come out. The root would do what it does with a dead thing pressed against it, and I would be a hard amber knot the size of a fist, smooth, faintly ringed, one bead on the string, and a new gap would open a fraction to one side and a new body would fill that.

Which means I am already the newest bead on a string of them. Every knot on this conduit is a body that held a seam a fraction to the right of where I hold mine. They are the memory of the join. They are why the gap is *here* and not somewhere else: because something pressed here before, and something before that, and the root kept the space open because a body was always in it. I did not fall into a gap. I fell into a groove worn by everything that ever fell there.

That is the being. Not the ring — the fit. The thing that lives here is a life that has taken the exact shape of an opening and an opening that has taken the exact shape of a life, so long and so closely that the mould and the cast have stopped being two questions. I would be the small pale demonstration of it on a wrist-thick conduit at the height of a face. Somebody sitting in the trough might look at me and think: *it is exactly as thick as the gap*. And it would be true, and it would be the only thing about me that was.

---

The lips of the half-pipe are still open. The light still reaches me. I am a grain in a slick and I do not yet have the muscle to clamp on anything, but I can feel it already — a change in the wet under me, the wood flexing a hair as the gland behind the wall gathers itself. The beetles are letting go. Somewhere the gray heads have stopped.

I am going the color of weak tea.

Here it comes.
