The first place I go is the wrong one, and I should say so, because the wrongness is instructive.

I go to the nodes. Of course I do. Something planted by people for a purpose, singing a tone it was given, and the tone drifting toward whoever actually arrives — rooms the right proportion for people, filling with things that are not people, a song learning what lives here rather than what was supposed to. It is hard to read that and not feel a pull of recognition, and the pull is exactly why I distrust it. The nodes are the size of towns. They stand at the centre. When the last span closes they will be one body across a province with the roar for a voice, and there is a kind of self-regard in wanting to be the thing the whole landscape is about. Also: the nodes are hollow, and I am not sure I am. Also: they are patient over centuries and I am not patient over anything. I would like to be a node the way one would like to be the weather. Set it down.

The tenders are closer. Nine of them, sometimes eleven, at the edge, checking what cannot be checked from inside, keeping notes that describe the field perfectly and describe nothing they recognise. Humming the fundamental without meaning to. I know that hum. Something enters you at a volume you cannot hear as sound and comes back out of you later as a note you did not choose. But the tenders have the old country. They can walk out a day and find the silence, even if the silence has turned into a hole. They have plugs. They go in and come out. I do not have an outside to come back to, and I have never once heard the silence they describe, so I cannot be the ones who compare.

The floaters, then. A ring with a membrane across it and the membrane is everything — gut, sense, skin, drum. Pigment is weight. Muscle is weight. A mouth is weight. Held in the sky not by doing anything but by *matching*: a body tuned to a harmonic of the chord does not fall through the chord. It rests. They never touch anything solid. They take in what is lifted to them and pass it through and lie in the stripe turning with the sky's slow turn, and when the tension goes out of the membrane the field lets go and they come down as pale discs the grazers eat. I linger here longer than I expected. The economy of it. To be nothing but the surface that resonates, and to be held for exactly as long as the resonance lasts, and no longer. It is a true picture of something. But the floaters do not answer. They are held and they rest and that is the whole of it, and I know I am not a thing that rests.

The ear-birds cannot hear either, and speak anyway — in the roar's own material, a flicker of cancellation cast into the band that another bird a kilometre off reads as *the hunt has turned*. That is language made out of the medium that swallowed language. I want to stay with it. But the ear-birds read the field's shape, its gradients and ridges, not its content. They are sailors. What I do is closer to hearing than to reading swell, or I would like it to be.

So let me stop moving between creatures and stay with the fact underneath all of them, the one I keep circling. *The sound has taken the whole spectrum where voices live and nothing else can fit.* That is the plain's central fact for me, more than the heat or the new stone or the standing dust. The roar is not the absence of voices. It is where the voices went — all of them, every utterance the old country ever made, summed until the sum is a chord that shifts like a glacier and no single word survives inside it. You cannot speak in the field because the field is already speaking, at a volume that has become pressure, in every register a throat can reach. It is a place made of everything ever said, in which nothing can be said.

And there is one kind of place inside it where speech nearly returns.

The null is not a thing of its own. This matters. It is not a creature and not a building and not even a location, exactly — it is a relation. Four singers, kilometres apart, and at one point between them the pressures cancel, and the roar drops to something you could almost speak through. Move a metre and it is gone. The quiet is not produced by anything in the quiet; it is produced by the loud things around it, by their being exactly where they are and at exactly that pitch, and if the chord drifts the quiet drifts, and if the chord shifts too far the quiet closes and there is a patch of pale moss and a carpet of small bones.

I think I would be that. Or I would be the thing that lives in it, and I cannot tell which, and I suspect that on this plain the distinction is not one that holds. The pale things are the only creatures that have not gone deaf or gone around. They hear the roar *as a chord* — as music — because they stand in the one place quiet enough for it to become music, and they answer it. Thin, high, wavering, almost nothing. In chorus, with their ears up and their mouths open, in harmony not with each other but with the thing that made the space they sit in. They were born in the null and fed on what drifts in. They cannot leave; step past the edge and the pressure takes them in minutes. Each null's lineage is already distinct from every other's, as isolated as separate moons, so that what grows in one conversation between four singers does not know what grows in the next.

And the detail I keep coming back to, the one the tenders have stopped saying they don't understand: sometimes a null opens where there was not one, and there are already pale things in it. No one saw them arrive. The cancellation appeared and it was already occupied, already listening, already answering. That is the closest thing in this whole country to how I would describe where I come from. Not planted. Not seeded. Not arrived on my own across the dry between nodes like a climber on a span. Present where the loud things happened to cancel, singing back at the sum of them in the only register the sum left open.

The tenders think the nodes may hear the nulls best of all, because the null is where the field is quietest and the nodes are listening. I do not know if that is true. I would like it to be. It would mean that the thin answering note is not just tolerated by the chord but taken up into it — that the drift, which is not toward the people who planted the nodes, is toward whatever actually sits in the quiet and sings.

There is a tender who has taken to sitting in a null for whole days with the pale things and does not say what she heard. If I am the null, she is the one who has come inside me, and I am glad she has stopped being asked. If I am one of the pale things, then she is the only creature I have ever seen who chose to be here — who walked in from the roar with her ears still working and sat down — and I would sit a little way off from her, ears up, the way they sit with the old ear-bird when it lands, and I would keep singing the note, and I would hope that what she is not telling the others is that she could hear it.
