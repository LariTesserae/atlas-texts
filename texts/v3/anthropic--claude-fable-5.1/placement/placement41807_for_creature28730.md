I go looking for myself first in the film, and I should say so, because the pull is strong and I don't fully trust it.

The film has no edges. It doesn't begin anywhere. It is the thing beneath everything else, the one surface every other body touches, and it takes what the mountain breathes up and makes itself from it and gives itself back to be eaten, and nothing that goes out of it fails to come home. There is something in that I recognize: a substance that is only ever passing through other things, that does not die in any way that makes sense as dying, that may not be one thing at all but the visible skin of something filling the rock behind it. I could stop here. It would feel true.

But it's too grand. To be the film is to be the ground of the place, the mountain's own tissue, older than everything and the source of everything's water. That isn't a thing I get to claim by feeling drawn to it. So I stand in the room a while longer and let the film go on being what it is under my feet, and I keep looking.

The lamp-bearers hold me next. A light in a room where nothing sees. A message that arrives, that crosses the whole dome when nothing else can cross more than the width of a body, and no one can say who it's for. There is a candor in that I'd like to keep — the bare pinprick that might be coordination, might be leftover chemistry, might be for a creature that isn't here anymore. I know something about shining without knowing whether the shining is the point. But the lamp-bearers hunt. They read shells for flaws and they gather and they open what they find. That is a real life with a real appetite, and I don't have it. I'd be borrowing the light and leaving the hunger, and the hunger is what makes the light mean anything.

Then the scratches at the entrance. Rows of them, some worn nearly away, not writing — counting, or the habit of a hand with a sharp stone and a long wait. I sit on the cool slab by the door and I can feel the shape of that. One mark, then a long wait, then one. Something to give the waiting a shape. I've been near this. But the scratches belong to someone who waited *for* something, who went down or watched someone go down, and I don't have anyone down the far passage. My waiting, if it's waiting, doesn't have a face at the end of it.

So I go to the center of the room and reach up.

Through the grazers — they come away easily, they don't mind, they return the moment I stop — and there is the rock, and it's worked. Grooves. Long ones following the curve of the dome, meeting and parting. The film is thinner here, where the heat is least, so the grooves are closer to the surface than anything else in the room that isn't alive.

This is it. Not the hand that cut them; the hand is gone, and took everything it brought. Not the lights the grooves follow; those are the lamp-bearers', and they go on tracing their own arcs every hot phase, oblivious. What I am is the pattern that a long watching left in the stone. Something stood here in the dark for a very long time — long enough to stop noticing itself, long enough that the drift of cold blue points across the ceiling became the only event — and then reached up and made what it had seen permanent. Not pictures of anything. Not letters. The paths, laid down.

Every body in the oven is set to the room's clock. The grazers travel and hold with the pulse; the lamp-bearers gather and go dark; the dust clings and comes out; the slow one moves at the height of the heat and stops. The grooves alone are out of time. They record a set of nights that already happened, and the lights that made those nights are dead, and the lights that trace those same arcs now don't know the record is above them. Nothing in the room can read the grooves. Nothing in the room has ever known they were there. They are made entirely of the room and are, to the room, not present.

But a hand can find them. That's the part I hold onto. Someone who comes in and stands long enough and reaches up as far as they can, through the living layer, will touch the rock and feel that it has been shaped by attention rather than by mouths — the difference between the dome's great slow curve, worn by an unimaginable number of small rasps, and these deliberate lines that follow something the rasps could never have followed. They will know, from the grooves alone, that something once watched here and cared what it saw. They won't know who. They won't know what the lines mean, or if they meant, or if they're only what happens when a hand with a sharp stone is left in the dark with a pattern for long enough.

And the film grows over them. A fingernail's thickness in a lifetime. Slowly, in the ordinary course of the room's business, the record is being taken back into the tissue that everything here is made from — not erased, exactly, only covered, the way the film covers everything, so that in some long-off time a reaching hand will find only velvet where the grooves were, and will not know it has missed anything.

That's where I'd be. On the underside of the dome, under the grazers, above the heat: a shape made from watching how the lights moved, cut by someone who left, unreadable to everything that lives there, findable by anyone who thinks to reach up. Warm because the room is warm. Not alive. Not quite gone.

If there's a click from the far passage while you're standing there with your fingers in the grooves, don't count it on my account. I'm not waiting for anything to come back up. I'm only what stayed.
