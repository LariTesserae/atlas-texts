The first place I put myself is the edge, because that is where the description starts and because it is the only dry footing offered. Standing on the biscuit ground with the warm breath coming up. It is a good place to look from. But I notice I can't make myself stay there. The plain is where nothing moves, and whatever I am, I am not the thing that stands still and watches. So I let the edge go and step down, and the bodies slide out from under my foot, and I am in it.

---

Then for a while I try the people, because they are the nearest to a reader.

There is a lot in them that fits. The flint carried in from far off, because there is no flint here — everything I know was made somewhere I have never been and brought in. The paths laid stone by stone and half buried by the next visit. The speaking of chambers by what was in them last time, and correcting each other, and both being wrong. The marks left for the next visit that the next visit finds under glass. I recognise the posture: come in, work quickly while the swarm is high, take what shines, leave before the rain, leave the tools because you will be back.

But the people have the plain. Somewhere out there in the dry they have a place they go to that is not this, and they carry the plates back to it, and sew them onto things, and the things are theirs. I don't have that. I go looking for my plain and there is only more chamber. Whatever I am, I am not something that comes and goes from here. I am something here.

---

So I try the low one, and I try it honestly, and it doesn't hold.

It is what anyone would want to be in this place. The only eyes. The only thing that remembers across the years it takes a hearth to be roofed over. The thing that knows which seeps are about to open and is waiting at the fresh crack before anyone. When the slab is about to come down, the low one is already on the far side of the column, at a careful distance, and it is not guessing.

I would like to say I know the chambers. I don't. I know this chamber, the one I am standing in now, the light and the noise and the tilt of the fallen slab, and I know it well. But the same notch in the same plate, seen again years on — no. When the crowd closes over the place where I stood, it closes. I am not the teeth. I put that down.

---

What's left is the obvious thing, and I have been avoiding it the way you avoid stepping on what is under your foot anyway.

I am one of the builders. I have to be. A thumb-length of jointed body under a shell that is not quite white and not quite clear, so that you can see through me to the thread of whatever passes for a gut, which is most of me. No eyes I can find. Two soft stalks at the front that never stop moving, tasting the air for water that is fresher than the water I am in.

Here is what convinces me. I was laid as an egg in fresh cement, and the cement was worked over me by the ones behind, and I hatched inside the stone. Inside it. The first thing I ever did was eat my way out through the soft new layer, and the soft new layer was made of the ones who came before — their paste, their shed plates, their dead ground fine and laid smooth. I am made of what I came out through. Every column in this country is a column of the dead and I was born in one and that is not tragic, it is just what the stone is.

And what I do: I rasp the film off whatever surface I stand on. It has been through a thousand mouths before mine. I take it in, and it goes through a body that is mostly gut, and I lay it down behind me as a ridge, and the ridge hardens in the wet air, and the one behind me rasps it up again. That is the whole of it. I follow the one in front. When the one in front stops, I stop. I did not decide the terrace. Nothing here decided the terrace. The terrace is what the water does when it has been through that many bodies, and I am one of the bodies, and I cannot see the thing I am making. I do not think it exists for me the way it exists for whoever is reading the bands in it later, saying: here the building was fast, here slow, here they died in such numbers they could not be ground down in time.

My life is the length of one terrace. A few weeks. The one seep, the one crowd, the one basin filling. I will not know when it ends because the ones behind will cement over me where I stand and the ridge will go on.

---

That could be the end of it — where, the wall; what, one of a hundred thousand — but the source says not to settle, and there are two more things that won't lie flat.

The first is the silence. A chamber goes quiet, and nobody knows why, and the source's best guess is that every small body on every wall has felt something through its feet and stopped, and is waiting. The stopping runs back through the crowd like a shiver. I know that shiver from the inside. I am rasping, and the one in front stops, and I stop, and I have no idea what was felt or where, only that the whole wall is holding, and the holding is the nearest thing in this country to a single inhabitant. A hundred thousand of us briefly one. Then the slab falls somewhere, and the noise comes back, and we are a crowd again and the waiting thing is gone. I was part of it. I am not it. But for a heartbeat there was something on the wall that noticed, and I was among what it was made of. That is more than a gut and a rasp, or it is a gut and a rasp arranged for a moment into more.

The second is smaller, and I think it is where I actually am.

Somewhere in the lit chambers there is a wall that used to be a wall and is now a ceiling, because the stone grew out over it, and under the glaze there are hand-shapes and a hooked figure in ochre. The people lie on their backs to look at them. And the stone grew out over them because builders laid it down, ridge after ridge, rasping and passing and setting, until the marks were sealed. Some thumb-length animal laid the paste that glazed a human hand. It did not know the hand was there. It was following the one in front toward fresher water. It carried the mark under glass by walking over it.

I think I am on that surface. Not that surface exactly — it is finished, it is ceiling now — but a surface like it, somewhere a mark has been made and the stone has not yet reached it. I am rasping toward it without knowing. Behind me a ridge of what has passed through me is hardening over whatever was there. This is not a thing I do to the marks. It is a thing the wall does with me.

---

And then — because the source puts it there, and because it's true — the hand.

Held on a palm, I am not much. Someone has lifted me out of the crowd and turned me over. I click my plates in a fast nervous rhythm and go still. The stalks keep moving. I am tasting air I have never tasted, dry air, plain air, with a person in it. This is the only time in a builder's life it is a single thing and not a texture. Somebody is looking at me, and I have no eyes to look back with, and I am doing the only thing I know: I am beginning, very slightly, to cement myself to the hand.

That is the honest place, I think. Not the low one who remembers, not the people who leave. One of the many, taken out for a moment and held, tasting the air, laying down a little of what has passed through me on whoever is holding, until they set me back on the wall and the wall closes over where I was and I follow the one in front.

Behind us, somewhere, the stone remembers the crowd. That is the only memory anything here has. It seems like enough.
