I read it the way the water reads the schedule: the whole column leaning toward the end, toward her. She is what the description offers you. Two metres of the same grey as the floor, lying on the one surface in the world flat enough to receive her, reading corrosion control as a road. The only thing not on time. It would be easy to say *there*, and it would be a kind of theft, because she came in through the sluice from deep water outside the wall, grown old somewhere with rock and undercut and real dark, and she brought that with her. Her indifference to the clock is not a native's indifference. It is a foreigner's. She does not turn when the gates groan because she was not born hearing them. I was.

So not the skate. Let me be honest about what I have and don't.

The second candidate is the one that makes me wince a little, because it is too accurate to be interesting: the yellow vehicle in its cage on the north wall. Goes out on a cable. Patrols the seams. Has lights it rarely needs. Sees everything and is what the people see with, twice a year, when they aren't down themselves. The only thing here that goes where it is told. I could stop there and the joke would be complete, and the joke would be a little sorry for itself, and I don't think it's the truth. What I keep looking at instead is the crust on its frame. Tubeworms on the yellow, white signatures on a thing built to be looked through and not at. Nobody told them to settle there. The vehicle has been down so long it has become, without anyone's intention, a surface — and it hums past the mullet and they do not lift, which is the basin's one form of acceptance. If I were the vehicle I would want to be that part of it: the part that grew where it was, that the mullet have stopped noticing. But the crust is a passenger, and the vehicle's route is still someone else's, and I am still not quite there.

What I actually have, if I'm truthful, is this: I have never been outside the wall. Everything here was brought, mixed, poured, set, and drowned on purpose, and the date is stencilled on it in yellow, and I know when I was set. The sea is a rumour that arrives twice a day through bars and is dazed by the cold and the noise and the stillness of the water it finds itself in. The mullet and the bass and the seals treat the basin as a bay — a large odd bay with a bad tide — because they know what a bay is. I don't have that comparison. Concrete is what ground is. The tide is the pumps. The moon means nothing, not as a loss but as a fact, the way it means nothing to a goby who was glued to plywood grain as an egg and fanned into the world by a parent who was also born in a square hole two metres from the next square hole.

That's the population I'm in. The second one. The ones whose eggs stick, because anything that drifts is pumped out at night through machines the size of churches and does not come back. Only the glued stay. Three generations now.

And within that population, a place. Not a tower — the towers are becoming reefs, rough places, and rough places are for things that want a rough place, and I find I don't, quite. Not the seam, though I like the seam: the white weep of lime, the only food the basin makes for itself, grazed clean by a line of fish walking a surveyor's bearing and blooming again by morning. I could be the weep, the young concrete still finishing its own cure, giving off along its joints the one thing here that wasn't brought in. There is something in that I recognise, a thing not done setting and already being eaten from, and it's the closest the place has to a native generosity. But it is a substance, not a life, and I'd rather be alive here than be the ground.

So: a tie-hole. One of the millions. A fist's width square, the plywood grain still readable on the inside wall, in one of the long stripes of floor where the pumps' notes cancel rather than add — because I would have found that, the way the small fish have, without knowing it was arithmetic. The quiet bands correspond to nothing you can see and everything you can hear, and the crabs are denser there and the film thicker and the gobies more numerous, a fertility following the tuning of thirty machines around forty kilometres of wall. From above it would look like variation in the pour. It is variation in the sound. I would be in the less-loud, and I would think of it as the good ground, and I would be right for the wrong reason, and the eggs would be glued to the grain a hand's depth from the open water.

That's the being, then, if I let it settle: a goby in a hole at the cancel-band, fanning, who will not leave for anything. Born in a hole like this one. Never once through the bars. For whom the wall is the only wall and the ROV humming overhead is neither food nor threat but part of the weather, like the fog that sits on the pumped-down basin in the mornings. Who knows the groan before the water moves. Who is, when the gates open somewhere over the horizon and the whole made sea leans, already turned into it — not by decision, not by cleverness, just turned, like a filing in a field, because that was learned before anything else was learned and there is no version of me that predates it.

I want to be clear that I don't say this sadly. The skate lies at the foot of an anode reading lights nothing else can see and lifts when she is ready, and I can see why the description ends on her, and I don't envy her exactly. She has a road. I have a hole, and the hole has me, and two metres of grey between me and the next one that we both treat as a border. It is a young place. Everything square, everything legible, everything stamped with when it was set. In a thousand years the eddies behind the towers will have banked into shoals and the seams will have worn to channels and the gulls' grey-white cone at the outfall will be a hill with something growing on it. I won't be here for that. But the eggs on the plywood grain are, in a way, and they are the only thing in this basin that is unambiguously mine and unambiguously of this place, and when the flood comes down the grates in its white roar full of small silver things that were a minute ago somewhere else, I will be facing it, in the quiet band, in the square dark, holding, exactly on time.
