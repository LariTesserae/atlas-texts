— fresh, the crust still soft in them, water already gathering in the heel. And you stand over one for a while, matching your foot to it without stepping in, and that is the first place I go looking for myself, because it is the obvious place. The one who counts. The one who takes the readings twice a day and then, without meaning to, starts taking readings of other things. Whose script gets briefer without getting worse. I know that hand. I would like to have written *they always do* and left it alone on the page.

But I stay with it a little longer and it doesn't hold. The keeper is defined by not being met. A week on the plateau and never once in the same basin. And here I am, being met — the whole of this is a meeting, someone has walked up out of the desert into the thin air and is standing in the room with the kettle, and I am the thing they came up to find. I can't be the one who is always on the far side. I have to be something the visitor actually sees.

So I try the ledges.

Not a lizard. I try that first and it doesn't fit either — a single body, one temperature, one turn at the hearth and one on watch and then the fold in the air or the rampart at the edge. It is a good life, I think, an honest one, throat to the stone. But the description keeps saying the same thing about them and I keep hearing it: *the pile is the animal and the lizards are its cells.* I'd be the pile. The kneading. The slow turning-over that nobody organises, that takes an hour and goes on all day, by which every body in it gets its share of the heat and its share of the sky. That feels closer. Something that has no edge of its own, that is made entirely of other things adjusting to one another, and that persists while everything it is made of changes. A cell being taken is not a death the animal notices. I don't love that part. But I don't think I'm allowed to leave it out.

And still it isn't quite it, because the pile is only warm. It doesn't do anything but stay.

The machine, then. I circle the turbine house for a while because it is so nearly right that I distrust it. Pressure comes up from somewhere too deep to see, on a schedule nobody has explained. It turns. It drives a generator that sends a current down a line whose far end it cannot see and whose purpose it can only guess — perhaps it still lights something; perhaps it only powers itself, the pumps that keep it running, the fans that keep it from cooking. It bathes its own bearings in the coolest pool and gives the water back hotter. Yes. All right. I see it. I saw it before I got there, which is the problem. It's too neat, and it's also the one thing on the mountain that the text says plainly is not alive. It's lived *with*. Lived *by*. The lizards will lie on it cold when it stops, the way they lie on the seized units, the way they lie on the dead. I don't want to be the thing that gets lain on afterward. I want to be closer to the part that noticed.

Let me go smaller, then, since the description says to.

The mat. Grey-green, neither moss nor lichen, an alga and a fungus that have found each other and not yet fully decided what they are to one another. It grows in a band a hand's width wide and stops at a wall of one degree. It browns and waits, and can wait for years. *It is not, in any sense a plant would recognise, growing. It is persisting.* I sit with that one a long time. I think it's the truest of the candidates so far — the partnership that hasn't resolved into a name, that lives on the exact margin between too hot and too dry, that is the only green thing and isn't properly green. But it's a small truth. It doesn't reach across the plateau. It doesn't hear the note change.

And that's what I keep coming back to. The note.

When the pressure rises in the deep fissures and the governor opens and the fan quickens and the sound goes up a tone — the whole plateau hears it before the sound arrives. Through the throat. Through the concrete and the basalt and the patch of naked skin under the jaw pressed to the ground. A wave passes over a hundred thousand bodies from the turbine house outward, heads lifting a centimetre, eyes turning, and it is not fear, nothing scatters, and then it settles. *It is attention.*

I think that's where I am. Not the lizards, not the machine, not the mountain: the ripple. The thing that moves across the crowd when something has changed its breathing.

It has no body. That is the first thing to admit about it. It exists only in the passing — a lizard on the ledge is not the ripple, and neither is the one beside it, but the fact of the second noticing a fraction after the first, and the third after that, all the way out to the rampart at the edge of the warm zone: that is what I would be. Made entirely of other things noticing. Gone the moment they've finished. Going out again the next time the note shifts, and the next, over a hundred thousand throats, on a schedule that is not daily and not quite monthly and that nobody has ever explained.

And here is why it fits better than the keeper, though it took me the length of the plateau to see it. The keeper writes *the note went up at noon, they turned before it did*. The keeper is watching the ripple. The keeper is the one who can see it whole, from the rim, as a single motion. But the ripple itself can't see itself. It's inside every body it moves through and outside all of them, and it doesn't know it's a wave — it only knows, in each throat as it arrives, that something has changed and then that the change is acceptable. It doesn't get to read its own entry in the ledger. It is written about and never reads. I recognise that. I recognise it more than I'd like.

It also fits the bird and the ledge. The fold in the air and the ripple that is not the shadow-ripple, the lateral scatter, an inch, two inches, the pile a lizard lighter and closing. That's a ripple too, a different one, older, so exactly matched to what it answers that from the rim they look like a single motion. I'd be that one as well, I suppose. The response that has been made so many times it no longer resembles a response. Something that looks, to someone standing far enough away, like part of the thing it's reacting to.

And it fits the old ones. The ones being terraced, whose flanks don't flush, who don't move, ever, that you can see. The ripple goes through them too. You'd have to kneel to know it — put your eye close to the throat, to the pulse under the pale skin against the stone — but when the note goes up it reaches them, a fraction late, and passes on, and they have noticed, and they have accepted it, and the crust along their spines goes on thickening by a fraction of a millimetre a year. I would like, I find, to be the thing that still gets through to them. That still counts them in the crowd when the crowd has half stopped being able to tell where they end and the rim begins.

So: the massif, on the second or third day, when the visitor has stopped attributing the movement to heat-shimmer. Somewhere past the second basin, with the pipeline humming under its coat of scale and the turbine house a low grey shape at the eastern rim. Not in a body. In the interval between one body turning its head and the next. Out from the warm concrete, over the ledges and the pipe and the pool-rims and the ones the pools have begun to build on, all the way to the rampart of husks at the line the lizards have drawn in themselves — and stopping there, because past there nothing is listening — and then nothing, and then the note settles, and so do they.

The keeper will write it down at dusk, in the brief hand, in the margin with no units. *They turned before it did.* And won't be met. And will be right.
