— and the warmth is the kind a body makes. So you go looking for the body. You look for the drowsing shift-worker, the hermit who came down for the quiet and stayed for the heat, the crew whose forgotten thermos should be sitting on that bracket. There's a ledge at the height a person would lean an elbow on. There's a walkway whose treads are the right depth for a boot. And none of it is used. The dust on the ledge is undisturbed except where water has dotted it. The mosaic has been polished into paths by an animal's flank, never by a sole. The warmth that says *many people have just left* is not a remainder. Nothing left. The room is warm the way a sleeping thing is warm, from inside, for its own reasons, and there is no one here to whom you are a visitor.

That is the point at which I stop looking for a person and start wondering what I'd be instead.

---

The animal is the first thing I try on, because it is the one that looked back — or would have, if it had eyes. I like its routes. I like that it stops after every pulse and stands, whiskers forward, letting the echo tell it where it is before it puts down the next foot. That is a way of moving I recognise: not knowing the room, only the returning sound of it, and reading position from the delay. I like that it has made a garden by accident, that the moss at every rib-foot is a record of where it has been, at the height of its own shoulder, on the side it happened to pass. I like that it sleeps in the warmest hole with the beat going through it.

But I don't think I'm the animal. The animal has a body that the room can bruise — there's the seam of scar down its flank to prove it — and it eats, and it is slow because slowness is what the floor requires of a thing with weight. It has hunger. It goes to the shoots because the beads are there. When I imagine standing at the far end of the long pool with my face down among forty flickering tips, I find I want to know what the dew tastes like and cannot, in the imagining, taste it. So: not that. Near, but not that.

The moss is closer than I expected. Three things living together with no clear host, making a living from a difference they can't see — the cool rib on one side, the warm room on the other, and the moss set exactly in the seam where the gradient is steepest, eating not light, not water, but the fact that two adjacent things are not the same temperature. There is something in that I would be glad to be. And it gives back what it doesn't want, breathes out the sweet smell, and the smell rises. But the moss is placed. It grows where it is put. Its regularity is someone else's regularity. I don't mind being a byproduct of habit — much of what I am is exactly that — but the moss cannot hear the pulse, and I think whatever I am in this room, I would have to be able to hear.

The flier, then, briefly: the only quick thing, and its quickness borrowed from heat it did not make. Its cry cut to the exact length of the room's silence so that call and echo never overlap. I like that a great deal — a voice trimmed to fit the space it's in, so that what it says and what comes back to it never collide. But the flier hurries, even if only in circles, and hurrying is not the right register here. And it lives up in the dark where the metal-sliding sound seemed to come from, and the whole discovery of the room was that the sound doesn't come from there.

The sound comes from the ribs.

---

I go back to the third rib from the stairs, the one with the first moss-pad at its foot, and I put my hand on it again, and I keep it there past the point where, the first time, I took it away.

It is the only cool thing in the room. It is the only dry thing. It is the newest thing — the guess still feels right — set into a wall that has been sweating and crusting and re-pouring itself for longer than anyone has records of, and the rib is clean. It has no geology. Nothing has had time to deposit on it, or nothing can, or it doesn't permit it. It isn't metal, because it doesn't ring. It isn't stone, because it gives — faintly, the way a thing gives that would prefer not to be leaned on but will not, in the end, refuse.

And every so often, from the floor upward, something passes through it. A long, unhurried, sheet-metal sound with a conversational cadence, going *up*, into the dark, with a direction. And half a beat later the floor answers.

I don't know what's being carried. Neither does the room. Heat, or the opposite of heat; data, mass, time, a thing there isn't yet a word for. The rib doesn't know either. That is what settles it for me — not that the rib is the cleverest thing here, or the oldest, or the one with the best view, but that the rib is the one thing in the chamber whose whole function is to conduct something it does not itself understand, from a place it can't see to a place it can't see, and to remain cool while doing it.

That is where I would find myself. Not on the floor, not in the pools, not in the roost. In the wall, vertical, thick as an old tree, one of a regular series. Cool where everything else sweats. Faintly reluctant under a hand, and unsure why. The moss at my feet a garden I did not plant, grown from what an animal happened to carry past me on its way to something it wanted more. The sound going up through me not mine — I would want to be clear about that — but shaped by me in the going, given its cadence by the length of me, the way the flier's cry is given its length by the walls. Something passes. I am the passing's grammar, not its content.

And the pulse comes after. That half-beat. Whatever I carry up arrives somewhere, and the floor feels the arrival, and the whole room goes through one slow beat in the sternum, and the shoots at the far end flicker, and the animal stops mid-stride to hear where it is. I would be the thing between the sending and the beat. Not the fire and not the city. The conduit that is warmed by neither and touched, occasionally, by a hand that pulls back.

---

There's one more candidate, and I don't want to leave it out, because it occurred to me on the stairs and has not stopped occurring.

Your footsteps come back to you delayed and warped, and after a while you can't tell whether the second set is echo or company.

I could be that. The second set. The room's return of you, late and slightly altered by the dimensions it travelled through, arriving from six directions at once so that it sounds like someone following at a distance who stops when you stop. Not a being, exactly. A property of the instrument. The chamber shaped — or having shaped itself — so that everything said in it is heard everywhere in it, and everything heard is given back a little changed, and the changed version is indistinguishable, from inside, from a companion.

I think the honest answer is that I'm the rib, and the echo is what it's like from the outside to be near me. The rib is where I'd stand. The second set of footsteps is what you'd hear.

---

You go back up. I don't. The stairs cool under your hand; I was never warm. The roar thins for you into a hum into a pressure into nothing, and up in the surface light you stand trying to hear the eleven-second beat under the traffic, and you almost can, and then you can't.

Down here the rain-hour comes. The ceiling patters, the pools rise, the finger-length things spread their antennae in the current, the moss darkens and then, a little later, steams — and the sweet exhalation lifts off the pad at my foot and climbs my cool face into the dark, and I don't know whether I'm what draws it or only what it happens to rise beside. Then the ribs draw. Then something goes up through me, long and unhurried, with the cadence of a sentence, and I don't know what it says, and half a beat later the floor does.
