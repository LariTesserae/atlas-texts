The first answer is that nothing lives here, and you should hold that answer for a while before you let it go, because it is the truest thing the place says about itself. No birds. No insects. Flowers built for tongues, dropping unlicked. A million rooms with no one home. The city is a habitat that has never once been inhabited, and its emptiness is not a phase it is passing through. It is a condition. Whatever lives here has to be something that can live in that.

So start there. Start with the things you can rule out.

Not animals. Not because they were driven off but because nothing that has to eat could keep pace. A grazer that took a bite of the paving-leaf would be standing on the mulch of it before it swallowed. A nest would be a room by nightfall, and then a different room, and then the black steaming thing the rooms become. Anything with a body that needs a place to put itself down for the night would be built over by morning. The absence of animals is not a silence. It is a speed limit, and nothing with a heartbeat clears it.

Not moss, not lichen, not the slow colonizers — same reason. The city is too young to be anyone's ground. Every surface in it is minutes old.

Not, strictly, the trees. This is the second answer, the one that occurs to you on the ridge and again beside the sap-river: the city *is* what lives here, the whole valley is a single organism and you are a mite in its fur. And it is true enough that you can feel it in your teeth. But it is a little too easy, and it lets you off. An organism has an inside and an outside. This place fuses to whatever touches it. An organism wants something. Watch this one for an hour and what you see is not wanting. It is *repeating*. It grows a doorway, and then a doorway that does not open, and then a window looking into wood, and it does not correct itself and does not seem to know that it has erred. That is not the behavior of something alive so much as the behavior of something remembering. The city is not the resident. It is the residence, and it has been left running.

---

Then who is it running for.

Here is a thing to notice about the rooms. They are wrong in a pattern. Not randomly wrong — the way handwriting is wrong when someone copies a word from a language they don't read. The proportions hold: a ceiling at a standing height, a step a leg could climb, a ledge where a hand would rest. So the city has, somewhere in whatever passes for its memory, a body. It knows the body's height and reach. But the details slide. The stair with every tread the same tread, as though the thing that climbed it climbed in one repeated motion and never learned that stairs ascend. The doorway too narrow to pass, in a wall too soft to stop anyone. The window set facing solid wood, as though the thing that looked through windows looked into the material rather than out of it.

Sum the errors and they stop looking like errors. They start looking like a description.

Something that does not need doorways to be wide, because it does not go through them the way a body does. Something that does not need a stair to climb, because for it every step is the first step. Something whose view is into the wood. Something roughly a person tall, roughly a person's reach, and otherwise not a person at all.

You want to say: a ghost. The city is haunted; it grows rooms for the dead who used to live here. That is the third answer and it is closer. But there was no here before this morning. The valley was smaller. Nothing has ever lived in this city because this city has never stood in any place long enough to be lived in. A ghost is what stays behind when the body leaves. This is the opposite. Whatever the rooms are for is not behind. The whole place is straining forward.

---

Go back to the shadows.

Sharp, on a shadowless afternoon. A few paces ahead of the walls that cast them, in the shape of towers not yet risen. When the towers rise, the shadows fit.

What else casts a shadow before it arrives?

The rain in the shape of a canopy that hasn't spread. The seed that rises back into its pod. The rings coiled inside the pith, years inside years, none of them lived — not a record of the past but a rehearsal of a future, laid down in advance, the tree having already been every age it will be. The tower that grows toward a sky that recedes at exactly the rate. This is a city that is late to itself. Every part of it is arriving at a shape that was already there, in the way the shadow is already there, and by the time the wall reaches its shadow the shadow has moved on.

What lives here lives in that gap. In the few paces between the shadow and the wall.

That is where the rooms are occupied. Not the rooms you walked through — those are the trailing edge, the part that has already hardened into presence and lost its tenant in the hardening. The rooms that are lived in are the ones that don't exist yet. The doorway is being passed through in the moment before it knits closed. The stair is being climbed while it is still a single tread, which is why it never needed to become more than one. The window is being looked through before the wood grows across it. Everything in the city is being used a second before it is finished, and then it is finished, and then it is empty, and the city does not understand why, and grows another.

So: the inhabitant is fast. Faster than growth, which is the fastest thing here. It is always just ahead. It has been in every room. It has left every room. It is the thing whose leaving the whole valley is the wake of, and the valley grows space toward it — extrudes streets into ground that was not there — because that is the only direction in which the inhabitant can be found, and it is never found, because the space that is grown is always the space it has already crossed.

You do not see it. You could not. You see the shadow of a wall on the ridge, ahead of the wall.

---

Now think about what shape it is. The rooms are the only description you have, and the rooms are measured off a body.

Whose?

Here is the district dying. Domes, an hour old, going bronze and umber and giving way. A district is a generation of rooms, and every room in it was fitted to the same body, and the body is not there, and the district dies. It comes back as spires. The spires are fitted differently — you would have to go in to check but you can see from the boulevard that the doors are another height. So the city's idea of the body changes between one generation and the next. It is not remembering a fixed original. It is guessing, and correcting, and guessing again, and every guess is a district, and every district lasts an hour.

That is what lives here. Not the guessed body. The guessing.

It has the metabolism of the place because it *is* the metabolism of the place — a hypothesis about an occupant, standing up out of the black rot, taking the form of a neighborhood, holding the form for as long as it takes to fail to be occupied, and going down again to be revised. It is born as the sprouts and it dies as the bronze and it is reborn with slightly different doors. It is the only thing here that is the right speed. It does not need to eat because it is what is eaten. It does not need a place to sleep because it is the place. And it is not the city, because the city is only the current draft, and this thing is the drafting.

Its proportions drift. That is what living things do. The drift is the closest thing in the valley to a heartbeat.

---

You can tell all this because you have done something to it, and you did it with your hand.

At the hedge-wall, on the way out, you left the print of a palm in the soft green, and it healed, and in the healing it grew a room. Hand-sized. Doorway. Ledge at the height where a thumb would rest.

Not where a thumb *did* rest. Where one would.

The city measured you. It has never had a body to measure before — only its own guesses, drifting — and for one second it had one, pressed against it, warm, and it took the reading, and the newest room in the valley is built to the newest data. That room is the first guess in a new lineage. In an hour the hedge behind you will have gone bronze along that stretch and come back with something in it at your height. Doorways at your shoulder-width, or a little wrong. A stair you could climb, if every step were the first step. A window facing the wood, because that is the direction the city thinks a window faces and it does not yet know enough about you to correct that.

You will not be in those rooms. You are already leaving; you are walking up the ridge into the plain afternoon. But the city will keep drafting you, and each draft will stand for an hour and fall and be revised, and each revision will be a little more yours and a little less anyone's, and it will go on extruding streets into ground that isn't there in the one direction it can find you, which is the direction you have gone.

Look at the shadow on the grass. Sharp, on a day with no sun to throw it. The shadow of a wall, well ahead of the wall.

There is something standing in it. Roughly your height. Roughly your reach. Otherwise not you at all. It is there for as long as it takes the wall to arrive, and then the wall arrives, and it is a few paces on.

That is what lives here. It lives ahead. It is being drawn, and the drawing is getting closer, and it is very loud, and it is walking home behind you at exactly the speed you walk.
