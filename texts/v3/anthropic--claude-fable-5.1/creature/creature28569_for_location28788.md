Start with the animal, because it is the only thing here that will admit to being alive in the ordinary way.

It is the color of the wall because it is nearly the material of the wall. Look at it long enough and the resemblance stops being camouflage and starts being something else: the lobes on its back are creased in the same pattern as the tissue it sits on, the same folds, the same darkening in the seams. Either it has learned the wall or the wall has learned it. Its foot is broad and flat and where it meets the tissue there is no edge — no line where animal ends and plant begins, just a change in the way the surface catches light, glossy to matte. You could not lift it. It is not gripping. It is continuous.

The mouth is the only part that is clearly a mouth: a small dry rasp, working, at the leading edge. It takes in tissue. It produces nothing. There is no frass, no trail, no smear of anything behind it — whatever it eats, it keeps, or gives back through the foot. A loop as short as the water's. The grooves it cuts close in minutes. The wall lifts it, a fingernail at a time, and the grooves it left are gone, and it cuts new ones in the fresh tissue, and those close too.

So it has been eating this exact spot forever, or it has been eating for no time at all, and there is no way to tell the difference from the outside. It is possible it isn't eating. It is possible this is how the plant tastes itself — one small mouth, kept on, for that.

Where it came from: a leaf. Say that, because it's the only story that fits. Once there was a leaf in open air and this was on it, doing this, rasping, and the plant grew around the leaf — its own leaf, maybe, folding back on itself — and the leaf went into the mass and was absorbed and the animal was not, because it moved. Or because it didn't. Whatever the rule is, the animal came down here with the leaf and the leaf is gone and the animal is still on it, in a sense, still working the same green surface, and from its side nothing has happened. Warm. Wet. Food under the mouth. Up is where the foot isn't. It knew four things on the leaf and it knows four things here and they are the same four things.

That is one resident.

---

The shapes in the walls are the second, if they are residents, if the walls count as here.

Wait for the green wave. It comes every few minutes — a darkening that runs over a patch of the wall like a cloud over a field, and behind it the tissue goes thin, glassy, and for the length of a held breath you can see into it. What you see is motion. Long dark forms going by, close-packed, fast, in the direction the water runs and the shadows fall. They are the rushing. They are the river folded into the stem. They pass the way traffic passes a window at night: you catch the length of them, the speed, never a whole one.

Not vessels, because vessels don't overtake each other. Not roots, because roots don't hurry. Not fish, though you keep wanting them to be — something about the way they flex. They flex like things that decide.

Once, in a translucent moment, one of them stops. The others stream past it and it holds against the current, and its dark length flattens along the inside of the wall exactly opposite you, at the height of your face, and stays. It has nothing that could be an eye. It is a shape pressed against glass from the other side. Then the wave passes, the tissue goes opaque, and when the next wave comes it is gone and the current is only current.

They are in the plant. It isn't clear they are *of* it. It is possible the plant is a road and they are what the road is for. It is possible they are what the plant thinks with. It is possible this hollow is the one place in the whole vast mass they cannot go, and equally possible it is the one place they have been careful not to, and you cannot tell from in here which kind of empty this is.

Except: the rain. The patch at chest height where drops begin from nothing. Watch it long enough and one drop falls that is not clear. Dark, the size of a lentil, and it strikes the floor and does not soak in. It lies there. It lengthens — draws itself out into a thread the width of a hair — and then goes into the tissue with a sound like a small word being swallowed, and the floor there is warm, and the rush in the walls skips, once, the way a heart does when you hear your name.

So some of them get through. Small ones. By accident, or the way water gets through — because the wall is not perfect, or because the wall is exactly as perfect as it means to be.

---

The morning lives here.

Not the light; the morning. It is easy to think of the light as a condition, like the warmth or the damp, but stand in the center of the chamber with your eyes closed and it is not a condition. It is ten o'clock. It is that particular thin-overcast brightness that comes before the cloud burns off and after the dew has gone, when a field is at its most ordinary, and it is arriving — now, and now — from a sun that is still at that height over a place that is still that place.

The smell is the morning's smell. Cucumber, crushed grass. That is not what a plant smells like inside. That is what a field smells like when something has just been walked through it, or cut in it. The mineral sweetness underneath is vase-water: the smell of stems that were cut and put somewhere and are still, for the moment, fine.

The plant grew around a morning the way it grows around a stone. This is the thing the description keeps circling and it is the truest thing about the place: the wall does not kill what it encloses. It grows around it and holds it at the moment it closed and lets it go on. The slug goes on rasping. The light goes on being ten o'clock. Nothing in here is preserved, the way a fly in amber is preserved. Everything in here is *continued*.

You can't hear the field. You keep thinking you will — a bird, wind in the grass, under the rush — and you don't. But the light on the far wall is thinner and hazed, and the far wall is farther than six steps, and if you let yourself think the hollow has a horizon then the light on that horizon is the light of distance, and the haze is the haze that comes off a field at that hour, and the reason the far wall keeps receding is that it is not a wall, quite, but the place where the morning gives out.

---

The knapper is the fourth thing, and the knapper is not here, and the knapper is the reason you know the others are.

A flake. Scalloped edge, bulb of percussion, half its length in the floor and the tissue healed smooth around it. Someone struck it. Someone stood in the ten-o'clock light with a core in one hand and a hammerstone in the other and hit them together and this came off, and it was a good one — look at the edge, look at how even the retouch is along it — the kind you'd pocket, not the kind you'd leave. So they dropped it. Or set it down while they did something with both hands and then the grass was over it.

Three stories about the hand, and the place declines to choose.

The hand is in the field. It is up there, still, in the light, bent over looking for what it dropped, and the plant took the flake down the way it took the morning down, and the hand never noticed the flake was gone because from the hand's side the flake is still there in the grass, and the grass is still there, and the morning has not ended.

The hand is coming. The chamber is young, still splitting, growing in both directions. It is growing *toward* something, and the flake is the first part of that something to arrive, the way a tooth is the first part of a skull the earth gives up. Watch the flake for long enough and it is not sinking. It is rising. A fingernail's depth at a time the tissue is presenting it, edge first, into the room. In enough time the whole flake will be in the air. Then the cord it was tied to, if it was tied. Then the fingers that tied it.

Or: the hand is yours. You paced this chamber when you arrived — six steps — and you don't remember arriving, only pacing, and you don't remember having a tool, and the flake is very near where you started. This is the least likely story and it is the one the split in the floor seems to be telling, when it runs clean along the sharp edge as though something in the wall were being cut by a blade nobody is holding. Somebody made that edge. The plant knows what an edge is for. It is keeping the edge and using it — using it to open itself, a fingernail at a time, in the direction of whoever put it down.

---

So here is what lives here, if you let the four of them add up.

A thing from a leaf. A thing from a hand. A morning from a field. And the shapes going past on the other side of the wall, which are the only things in the whole account that are *going*.

The hollow is the one place the plant isn't. Everything in it is something the plant grew around and did not consume. Not a stomach, then, and not a tomb. A keeping. The plant's one absence, and the absence is full — furnished, sparsely, with exactly what the plant has enclosed and declined to end. The size of the chamber is not a puzzle. Six steps is the size of what it holds. The far wall recedes because the morning is in here and a morning has a horizon; the near wall advances because there is something new to be made room for. The rain at chest height is the size of a spread hand because a spread hand is what put the flake down.

And the shapes in the sap are not residents. They pass. They are the current the whole plant is; they move through it at speeds that don't belong in a stem, and this hollow is a still drop suspended in them, a pocket of outside they are carrying somewhere, and the one that stopped at the window was not looking in. It was looking at the morning. It had never seen one.

---

Your palm is on the wall.

It has been there a while. The tissue is warm and pushing back, and you can feel the rush through it now, the long dark traffic, and you can feel, at the edges of your hand, the thing the description warned you about. It is not a grip. There is no edge where the wall stops and your skin begins — just a change in the way the surface catches the light, glossy to matte. It is not eating you. It is *continuing* you. When you take the hand away it comes, but the print stays, in negative, and closes over in the time it takes to catch your breath, and where it closed the wall is very slightly warmer.

Six steps. It will still be six steps. There will be an animal on a leaf that isn't there, a flake from a hand that isn't there, a light from a sun that is exactly where it was, and a fifth thing, kept, going on — warm, wet, four things known and the same four things — while the dark shapes stream past on the other side of the wall and one of them, every so often, stops, and presses its length to the glass, and stays.
