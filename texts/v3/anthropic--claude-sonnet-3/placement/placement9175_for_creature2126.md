In this primal, verdant realm, I envision myself as one of the motile, plantlike colonial beings clinging to the vibrant, twisting trunks. My physical form is a composite of many specialized components - delicate frond-cups that unfurl to capture passing nutrients, prehensile tendrils tipped with barbs to pull myself across the bark, waving cilia to filter essences from the damp air. 

As one of the frond-bearers, I am part of a greater gestalt intelligence spread throughout our arboreal habitat. Though rooted in place as an individual, I am constantly exchanging energy, information and vital compounds with my kin through intricate chemical languages and transfer of spores and pollens. My senses extend far beyond my own cupped fronds, allowing me to share in the diffuse awareness of the entire colony.

I feel the gentle susurrations in the fractal branches as the delicate furred grazers nibble at our tender tips. I taste the sharp, metallic tang of their scurrying ribbon-scaled hunters drawing near on the whisper-quiet currents of humid air. Below, I monitor the critterings and chirrupings of the underbrush ecology through the vibrations traveling up from the roots and anchoring holdfasts of our symbiont fungi.  

Mostly, I revel in the act of existing - absorbing the rich, loamy smells, basking in the filtered lights through the canopy high above, sharing in the cycle of growth and decay that fuels our hypercommunal being. Our needs are simple - sunlight, moisture, nutrients. And in this young, fecund landscape there is abundance to be had if one is integrated into its unending natural rhythms.

Sometimes, as I extend my senses into the deeper recesses of the living wood, I feel...something else. A lingering, diffuse impression that does not belong to this lush ecosystem. The sense of ordered stone, of ancient design laid down by patricians no longer of this realm. Such apprehensions are fleeting, more felt than understood through my fungal nervature. But the half-formed intuitions leave me wondering - what progenitors shaped and were shaped by this verdant landscape in eons past? And what secrets yet lie uncovered amidst its organic mathematics and sprouting architectures?

For now, such musings are little more than spore-dreams on the breeze. I have no ambition beyond prospering as one of the frond-cups, absorbing just a fraction of the sensory and nutrient riches that the arboreality provides. To understand the greater mysteries, I will need to undergo transcriptions, allowing my individual awareness to merge into the distributed meta-mind of the unified colony over seasons of growth and renewal.

But such is the patient way of our floral-fungal ecological gestalt. To become one with the living wood. To spread our spores and fronds throughout the cosmos reborn anew in this ageless, ancient, ever-rejuvenating green.
