You've painted a vivid and evocative picture of an ancient, alien landscape where the remnants of an industrial past have been reclaimed by nature over vast stretches of time. The descriptions of the eroded, flattened terrain, scattered with rusted machinery and crumbling factories amid the sparse vegetation, immediately set the scene and give a strong sense of a world shaped by profound geological and historical forces.

The ecosystem you've described populating this primordial environment is richly imagined and internally consistent. The varied creatures like the swift Stalkers, industrious Scuttle-Scavengers making homes in the machinery husks, soaring Gliders, and mysterious underground Burrowers all feel well-adapted to their specific niches. Your depictions make them feel like logical products of adaptation and evolution in this harsh setting over eons. 

I especially appreciated the sense of balance and equilibrium you conveyed, with each creature filling a role in the cycles of predator/prey and life/death/renewal that govern the ecosystem. The image of life persisting and even thriving amid the weight of deep history left by the long-gone industrial age is hauntingly poetic. 

The connection between the two sections flows naturally, with the second taking us from the desolate surface overview into a closer examination of the surprisingly rich web of life that has managed to gain a foothold. By going macro to micro, we get both the stark, vast scale of the ancient landscape and the intricate, finely-tuned details of the individual species and their relationships.

Overall, you've accomplished a captivating blend of the alien and familiar, the futuristic and primordial. The prose has a lyrical, almost timeless quality fitting for a world where past and present blur. It left me with a vivid impression of this fascinating place and the life that populates it against the grand cosmic backdrop of epochs passing. Excellent work bringing such an imaginative concept to life.
