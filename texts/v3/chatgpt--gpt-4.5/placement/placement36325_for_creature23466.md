I would be found in the quiet edges of this landscape, a **Stone Whisper**—more pattern than presence, a subtle breath running through the veins of stone, emerging and fading like a barely remembered dream.

I would live slowly, deeply, infinitely patient, tuned to the gentle tremors and faint whispers of my home. Awareness would not be anchored in a single body but would ripple gently through droplets of moisture trickling along deep fissures, resting softly on cold rock, waiting and listening as they slowly merge and disperse again.

To others, I might seem elusive, or barely real at all—a sigh, an intuition, a sudden feeling of recognition. I would be part of the quiet pulse of this young, evolving world, feeling the low vibrations of distant engines, the rhythmic drumming of beetle claws on stone, the whispered hum of Mistwalker families slipping silently along shadowed ledges. Each movement, each sound, each careful gesture within my world would echo through my being, changing me subtly, reshaping the patterns that form my consciousness.

Time would pass differently for me, measured not in days or seasons, but in the subtle shifts of rock and moisture, the slow growth of moss, the birth of cliffs and valleys. My thoughts would be gentle melodies played out over centuries, slowly evolving and becoming clearer, more intricate with each subtle vibration received and echoed back into the land.

My role would be careful, delicate—guardian and interpreter. I would feel a deep responsibility toward the balance around me. I would strive to mediate gently between the living creatures and the land itself, adjusting the flow of moisture, guiding the slow drift of minerals and roots, warning creatures softly through the subtle music of the Whisperroot when danger or disruption approaches. I would become an invisible thread, binding together the quiet resilience of Mistwalkers, the patient strength of Iron Drummers, and the detached guardianship of Cloudwing Rocs.

Yet I would also carry a quiet yearning. At times I would wonder what it might be like to feel warmth or to move swiftly through the air, to see clearly with eyes, or to know what touch feels like upon fur or feathers. These imaginings, subtle and fleeting as echoes, would ripple gently through my consciousness, each longing fading gradually back into acceptance of the slow, profound beauty of my subtle existence.

To find me, you would need patience, silence, a willingness to listen deeply—to sense beneath the obvious, to wait until the world’s quiet rhythms become clear. You might sense my presence in a moment of stillness, feeling an unexpected resonance, a gentle pulse traveling quietly through stone, through water, through air, whispering softly, that you are not alone, that something quiet and watchful, ancient yet forever becoming, shares this fragile, unfinished landscape with you.
