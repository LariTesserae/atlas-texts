Beneath a low dome of softly glowing stone, hidden from sun or star, lies the dense, dim, subterranean city known as Dripsound. Moisture seeps persistently from the ceiling, forming innumerable rivulets that trace paths through stone furrowed by ages of patient water. The surfaces—slick, ancient, eroded into organic shapes—hold steady with quiet dignity, reflecting dimly the wan, phosphorescent glow emanating from fungal crusts and pale luminescent minerals embedded deeply into the walls.

Here, in a world without greenery, biology whispers in forms barely recognized: fungal patterns lace intricately through stone, glistening slime molds slowly shifting across damp surfaces, and sparse colonies of tiny creatures flicker like shadowy ghosts across ceilings and cracks—rare and wary of any movement. Plant-life never existed here; this is a world that never met the sun, whose life flourishes quietly, invisibly, modestly.

Dripsound is small, a secret pocket of layered infrastructure, intimate and labyrinthine. Homes are hollowed gently into curving walls, their openings curtained with sheets of hand-woven fibers made from fibrous fungi or harvested slime-webs. Pathways squeeze together in near-embrace, weaving intricately through the honeycomb-like settlement. Buildings are hewn painstakingly from dense stone, simple metal tools leaving grooves that generations of hands have polished smooth. The technology is primal—no engines hum, no electricity crackles; only careful craft, simple metallurgy, and delicate, continuous maintenance with hands roughened by honest labor.

And always, always, there is sound—a ceaseless symphony, loud and rhythmic, echoing through corridors and chambers. Drip, drip, drip—water cascading steadily, countless droplets striking stone floors, hollow metallic vessels catching droplets for drinking or ritual, pooling in carved basins and bowls. The collective resonance forms a rich tapestry, comforting in its constancy, echoing from wall to wall. It is a music the people of Dripsound know intimately, a music that grounds their lives, a music that has not shifted or quieted for centuries.

The air is cool and humid, scented faintly by minerals and fungi, an aroma of stone and water, fungal decay, and subtle metallic notes from tools left near constant moisture. Mist gathers and dissipates slowly, lazily. It condenses upon skin, clothing, and walls, perpetually renewing the city's quiet, glistening sheen.

At the city's edge, the corridors narrow further until they disappear entirely, swallowed by darkness and mystery. The residents, curious yet respectful, rarely venture beyond the known and settled space, for here is equilibrium. Stability reigns in Dripsound; nothing shifts, nothing ages beyond gentle erosion, nothing disrupts the eternal dripping rhythm that is this world's heartbeat. Change is unnecessary, unheard of, almost impossible.

Yet, unusual presences occasionally flutter through the shadows, indistinct shapes glimpsed in peripheral vision, barely tangible, whispers of strange forms adapted to endless dampness, beings neither animal nor fungus—perhaps echoes or dreams formed into fleeting flesh by a world steeped in quiet weirdness.

Dripsound persists beneath the surface, beneath notice, beneath disruption—a small, dense, water-blessed city humming gently, perpetually, under its unchanging canopy of stone.
