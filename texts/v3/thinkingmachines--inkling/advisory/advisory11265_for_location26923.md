**Preparation Briefing: The Mouth (Flat Delta, Post-Industrial Tidal Shelf)**

Before you step onto the black silt, understand the contract you are entering. This is not a ruin you tour; it is an equilibrium you enter. The mouth of the old delta—low, never rising more than a hand’s breadth above the tidal push—has settled into a wet, hot, silent permanence. The grid of streets and rail beds is still there, but the density is void. You will walk through a town-scale skeleton spread across miles, and you will be the emptiest thing present. The heat does not fluctuate. The water does not recede. The life—millions of armored bodies in silent sheets, eels rolling in black canals, clouds of winged things that have forgotten how to buzz—does not sleep and does not notice you. Prepare not for adventure, but for immersion into a place that operates on its own mineral and biological logic.

---

### I. Climate and Thermal Readiness: The Relentless Wet Heat

The air here is not merely hot; it is a substance. It is thick with condensation that never becomes rain, clinging to every surface, running in slow mineral tears down rusted steel. You will not experience cool morning relief or evening drop. The temperature holds steady, and your body will misjudge its own exhaustion because the humidity prevents sweat from evaporating.

**What to wear:**
- **Base layer:** Lightweight, synthetic, long-sleeved and long-legged. Not cotton, which will stay soaked with condensation and canal mist. You want fabric that wicks but does not cling, because the black slime—the thin dark film coating every surface—will transfer to you at every touch.
- **Mid-layer protection:** A loose, breathable shell jacket with sealed cuffs and a high collar. Not for rain, but for the constant weeping of structures and the spray of condensation falling from cooling towers. The collar protects your neck from the dense clouds of tiny, silent fliers.
- **Headgear:** A wide-brimmed hat with an integrated, fine-mesh neck and face veil. The air is dense with moths, flies, and smaller winged things that do not buzz. They will settle on you. The mesh should be breathable but tight enough to stop them from crawling into ears, nostrils, and eyes. Wear goggles or wraparound eye protection; the haze is thick, and the slime can flick from cracked concrete as you step.

**Heat management:**
- Carry at least **three liters of clean water per hour of exposure**. The wet heat disguises dehydration. Add electrolyte salts. Do not ration; drink before you feel thirst.
- Use a cooling towel soaked in your own water, wrapped around your neck—local water is black with sediment, choked with rust, and slicked with something darker. Do not apply it to broken skin.
- Take enforced rest in the hollow warehouses, but check the floor first. They sag, they weep, and the slime pools inside as it does outside. Rest against vertical steel, not leaning into walls that may be softer than they appear.

---

### II. Footwear and Ground Negotiation

The ground is never solid in the way you expect. It is black silt and crushed aggregate, stained orange with rust, layered with shallow pools that reflect the gray sky and hide deeper depressions. Every foundation is weeping. The grid is cracked, and beneath the cracks, rusted rebar waits.

**Boots:**
- **High, sealed, ankle-to-mid-calf wading boots**, not standard hiking boots. You need height because the pools are everywhere and the black water in the canals is barely contained by broken pavement edges.
- **Thick, puncture-resistant soles**—rusted steel, crushed glass from collapsed works, and the hard carapaces of the silent creatures will test every step.
- **Aggressive tread for slime**—the dark films make concrete and rusted metal treacherously slick. The boot material should resist chemical degradation from the slime and the mineral-heavy water.
- **Gaiters over the top**—to keep the silent sheets of crabs and beetles from crawling up into your pant legs. They are not hostile, but their biomass is overwhelming. You do not want them inside your clothing.

**Walking aids:**
- Carry a **sturdy walking staff or pole** to probe pool depth, test concrete stability, and gently part the sheets of armored creatures without crushing them. Crushing them releases an oily, mineral scent that lingers and attracts more.

---

### III. Water: The Black Constant

Water is not an event; it is the condition. The ground table breathes at the surface. Canals hold black water that moves barely, choked with sediment and an oil-slick darkness, rolling with unseen eels that break the surface only in silent ripples.

**Rules:**
- **Do not drink local water.** Filter, boil, and chemically treat if you must use it in an extreme emergency, but assume it is contaminated with rust particulates, industrial residue, and the biological chemistry of the slime.
- **Do not swim or wade in the canals.** The eels are large, silent, and you will not see them coming. The water is black; you will not see what touches your legs. If you fall in, exit immediately—do not thrash, which stirs the sediment and obscures vision further.
- **Bring a collapsible water container** for carrying your clean supply, and seal it in a waterproof bag inside your pack. The ambient humidity will try to enter everything.

---

### IV. Fauna Protocol: The Silent Overwhelming

This is the strangest balance. The empty lots are carpeted in motion—millions of small, hard-bodied creatures feeding and migrating in silent sheets. The air is hazy with wings. The canals hold rolling life. And yet the sound remains mostly quiet.

You are not prey, but you are an intrusion into a biomass that does not understand you.

**Protection:**
- **Ankle-to-knee armor or thick canvas gaiters:** The crabs, beetles, and other armored things move in silent sheets across the wet concrete. They will crawl over your boots. Most will not bite, but their shells are sharp, and in such numbers, abrasion and panic are risks.
- **Face and respiratory protection:** The flying clouds are dense. Wear your mesh veil and consider a light respirator or at least a dampened cloth mask for long traverses—not for toxins specifically, but for the sheer volume of wings, dust, and evaporative minerals in the air.
- **Gloves:** Thick, chemical-resistant, with grip. You will touch rusted steel, slime-covered railings, and wet concrete constantly.

**Behavior:**
- **Move slowly and steadily.** The creatures respond to vibration more than sight in this dimness. Sudden stomping or running will not scare them away; it will cause them to swarm upward in a silent, overwhelming wave.
- **Do not kill them unnecessarily.** The ecosystem is in equilibrium. Removing large numbers disrupts the feeding patterns and can cause localized swarms. Use your staff to guide them aside gently.
- **At night (if you stay):** The creatures do not sleep. The silent sheets continue. The flying things continue. You will need a fully enclosed shelter—tent with fine mesh, no open vents—because they will cover every exposed surface of your camp.

---

### V. Navigation in Flat Haze

The light is tired. Not darkness, but perpetual dimness—heavy cloud sitting low, thickened by humidity and haze rising from warm water meeting hot metal. The sun, when visible, is a pale disc. Shadows are soft, edges blurred by moisture, so structures dissolve into flatness rather than stand against it.

**Orientation tools:**
- **Do not rely on sight alone.** The grid extends past vision in every direction. The haze takes the rest. You can stand at one corner of a block and see three others separated by open space that holds no purpose. Without landmarks, you will walk in circles across the cracked foundations.
- **Use GPS with pre-loaded grid maps.** Mark the rail beds and the cooling towers as waypoints. The towers are your vertical landmarks; they sag and weep, but they stand, and their occasional distant creak can orient you by sound.
- **Carry a strong, directional flashlight or headlamp**, even in daytime. The dimness is not uniform; inside warehouses and under collapsed shells, it approaches true black. Use a focused beam to check floors before entering. The light will also reveal the slime trails and the silent movement of the ground sheets.
- **Mark your path.** Use reflective tape or bioluminescent markers (if available in your multiverse gear) at intervals. The flatness is disorienting, and the haze will erase your memory of which warehouse you passed.

**Sound navigation:**
- The only sounds are dripping water, distant creaks, and evaporation hisses. Listen for the creak of cooling towers contracting in the heat—they are directional anchors. Do not expect to hear danger; danger here is silent.

---

### VI. Structural Safety: The Settled Decay

The erosion is established, not accelerating. The rust patterns are ancient, layered like geology. The buildings do not collapse dramatically; they sag, stain, and hold.

**Entry rules:**
- **Do not enter any structure with more than one wall collapsed.** The equilibrium is delicate. Your weight, combined with wet rot and rusted support, can shift what has held for years.
- **Test every floor with your staff.** The concrete is cracked, and beneath it, black silt and pooled water hide hollows.
- **Avoid leaning on walls.** They weep. They are coated in slime. They may be softer than they appear.
- **Watch for falling water and rust flakes.** The constant condensation means nothing is dry. Wear eye protection even when looking up.

---

### VII. Health, Hygiene, and Medical Preparation

The environment is chemically active. The slime is neither plant nor fungus—just the chemistry of heat, water, and rust made visible.

**Medical kit:**
- **Antiseptic and wound care:** Any cut exposed to the black water or slime risks rapid infection in this heat. Clean immediately with alcohol or iodine.
- **Anti-fungal and anti-bacterial creams:** For skin exposed to constant moisture and slime contact. Apply to feet, ankles, neck, and wrists after washing.
- **Eye drops:** The air is dense with particles. Flush eyes regularly with clean water.
- **Heat exhaustion treatment:** Oral rehydration salts, cooling packs (chemical, activated by squeezing, kept in a waterproof pouch so they do not activate in the humidity).

**Sanitation:**
- Carry hand sanitizer and use it before eating. The slime is everywhere.
- Wash with clean water only—never with canal water, even to rinse.
- Dispose of waste by carrying it out. There is no soil here to absorb it, and the fauna will investigate it immediately.

---

### VIII. Psychological Preparation

This is perhaps the most important preparation. You will move through a hollow urban landscape, surrounded by the quiet pressure of a world that does not need you. The absence of people is total. The absence of vegetation is total. The color palette is black, rust-orange, gray, and the dull sheen of slime.

- **Expect isolation that is not lonely but alien.** The life around you is overwhelming in number but indifferent in intent. You are not being watched; you are being ignored by a biomass that has reached terms with its environment.
- **The silence is active.** Without the usual background of wind, birds, or distant traffic, your own heartbeat and breathing will seem loud. Do not mistake the quiet for safety; it is simply the sound of a place that has dampened its own noise.
- **Time dilates in flatness.** Without rising sun, without trees, without the usual markers of progress, hours will feel uniform. Set strict time checks on your watch. Do not wander “just a bit further” to see the next warehouse. The haze takes the rest, and the rest is always the same.
- **Bring a small, personal anchor.** A book, a sound recording from home, a specific scent in a sealed container—something to remind you that other rhythms exist. The monotony of wet heat and silent life can induce a kind of trance.

### IX. Equipment Checklist (Essential)

**Clothing & Wear:**
- Long-sleeved synthetic shirt and pants (2 sets, rotate)
- Sealed-cuff shell jacket
- Wide-brim hat with integrated fine mesh veil
- Wraparound goggles
- Chemical-resistant gloves (2 pairs)
- High wading boots with puncture-resistant soles
- Thick canvas gaiters
- Cooling towel (synthetic, quick-dry)

**Navigation & Light:**
- GPS with pre-loaded grid/waypoints
- Compass (magnetic, not affected by the industrial rust fields?)
- Strong directional flashlight/headlamp with extra batteries (sealed against humidity)
- Reflective path markers
- Walking staff (collapsible, metal-tipped for testing ground)

**Water & Sustenance:**
- 4+ liters clean water capacity
- Electrolyte supplements
- Sealed, non-perishable food (the heat will spoil anything fresh; the slime will cover anything exposed)
- Waterproof food bags

**Safety & Health:**
- First aid kit with heavy emphasis on antiseptics, bandages, anti-fungal cream
- Respirator or high-quality dust/slime mask
- Hand sanitizer
- Eye drops
- Chemical cooling packs
- Rope or paracord (for canal edges, for lowering into depressions, for emergency marking)

**Shelter (if overnight):**
- Fully enclosed tent with ultra-fine mesh (no-see-um grade)
- Waterproof ground sheet
- Elevated sleeping platform or cot—do not sleep directly on the concrete; the slime pools, and the silent sheets will cover you.

---

### X. Exit Protocol

Plan your entry and exit with military precision. The flatness extends in every direction until the haze takes the rest. You can see three blocks away, but not further. The grid is there, but it is hollow.

- **Set a hard return time.** Do not extend.
- **Leave markers every 200 meters.** In the haze, you will not recognize your own footprints.
- **Identify your extraction point visually before entering.** Use the cooling towers. Note which one is closest, its sag angle, its rust pattern—anything to distinguish it from the others.
- **Do not wait for rescue.** The density is near-void. No one will come. Carry a signaling device—flare, mirror, loud whistle (though sound does not travel well in the heavy air)—but assume self-extraction is your only option.

---

### Final Intuition

When you step into the mouth, you will feel immediately that you are the anomaly. The place is not hostile. It is full—full of water, full of rust, full of silent armored life, full of its own wet heat. It has reached a state of being that requires nothing from you and offers nothing back. Dress to become invisible to its chemistry. Move to avoid disturbing its sheets. Watch the black water, probe the cracked concrete, and remember that the emptiness you see is not empty. It is simply occupied by everything except you. Move through it with respect, with sealed boots and a full water pack, with your path marked and your return clock ticking, and you will cross the flat delta not as a conqueror, but as a temporary, careful visitor in a world that will not remember you left—because the slime, the rust, and the silent, overwhelming life will have continued in their established rhythm, wet and hot and perfectly, indifferently alive.
