### I. SENSORY AMPUTATION: PREPARING FOR 0.0

Do not pack lights. Not torches, not headlamps, not bioluminescent paint. The Roof-Plateau is not “dark” in the way a cave is dark; it is **0.0 made environment**—a parameter so complete that photons do not exist as reference. If you switch on a bright beam, you will see nothing for distance and you will blind your own adaptation to the local glimmers: the sickle-silver moss, the cold white sparks of sagging electrical lines, the phosphor-white edges of fungal spires. These are not decorations. They are your stars.

**Prepare your other senses to become primary.**

- **Echolocation training is mandatory.** Before arrival, spend weeks in anechoic chambers learning to navigate by tongue-click and return-delay. On the plateau, scale (2.8) is too vast for visual triangulation; you feel distance through pressure in your sternum and the way your voice dies or rebounds off porous basalt walls.
- **Tactile navigation gear:** Wear gloves with micro-vibration sensors calibrated to detect the low-frequency thrum (2.1 sound saturation) of distant engines and the splash-patterns of fauna moving through black water. Your boots should have pressure-mapping soles—elevation is 1.7, but local relief is swallowed by biomass and mist.
- **Accept disorientation.** There is no sky, no sun, no faint galactic glow to orient circadian rhythm. Your internal clock will fail within hours. Carry a mechanical metronome set to your home-world hours; let sound, not light, divide your day.

### II. THERMAL & HYDROSTATIC PREPARATION: LIVING AT 1.7 / 2.0

The warmth here is **1.7**—not scorching, but relentless, humid, and rising from below. Geothermal pressure and the autonomous machinery maintain a bath-like atmosphere that never cools. The air is thick, condensing, carrying spores.

- **Suiting:** Do not wear insulating layers. You need **anti-microbial, breathable membranes**—something that sheds fungal spore and allows sweat to evaporate into air already saturated with moisture. Think membrane-diving suits designed for hot springs, not for cold vacuum.
- **Hydration:** Water presence is **2.0**—it is never more than a shallow pool away. But do not drink it raw. The rock is compressed rust and weathered basalt (erosion 2.3); the black water is mineral-heavy, warm, and likely carrying dissolved metals from the industrial runoff. Carry filtration capable of handling heavy rust-particulates and geothermic bacteria.
- **Footwear:** The ground is not ground. It is porous, sponge-like formation weeping constantly, merged shallow pool merging into shallow pool. Wear **buoyant wading boots with suction-release soles**—you will be walking through stagnant black water layered over root-layered rust, and suction is real.

### III. NAVIGATING THE DENSITY: 2.8 VASTNESS, 2.1 CROWDING

The plateau is continent-sized (vastness 2.8). It generates its own weather—warm heavy rain that never breaks the humidity, mist that rolls in thick layers, and a “gravitational mood” you will feel as a persistent weight behind your eyes.

- **Movement is resistance.** Density 2.1 means vegetation, root, rust, and water crowd every available cubic centimeter. You will push through hanging drapes of wet biomass—fungal spires like dark cathedrals, vines feeding on rusted girders, moss that pulses with faint chemical light. Move slowly. Every step displaces something that will grow back by the time you return.
- **Do not attempt to “clear a path.”** The erosion here (2.3) is settled; the ecosystem is in long-state equilibrium. Cutting vines releases spore-clouds and disrupts the symbiotic fungi that grow on local fauna shells—those same shells that match the moss-glazer colors.
- **Edge awareness:** You are 1.7 elevation above a “lower dark.” The plateau’s edges do not glow. They are absences of pressure—sudden drops where sound falls away into nothing. If you travel the perimeter, use tether-lines anchored in porous rock (test every anchor; the basalt is hollow with age).

### IV. THE INDUSTRIAL WILDERNESS: TECH 2.5, BUILT 1.1

Do not come expecting ruins to loot or cities to map. The technology here was built at only **1.1 density**—scattered like bones in a single body, vast gaps of pure marsh between them.

- **The engines are alive, not dead.** Massive mechanisms sit half-submerged in peat-black marsh, turning slowly with deep vibration. They pump warm water, circulate the thick air, and generate the electricity that feeds the faint bioluminescence. They run autonomously, scaled to persistence rather than production. **Do not shut them down.** They are not separate from nature here; they are the heart that keeps the warmth (1.7) circulating. Interference will trigger unknown feedback loops in the weather system.
- **Electrical hazard:** Sagging lines between fungal towers throw occasional cold white sparks—your only bright lights, and they are dangerous. They illuminate nothing meaningful, but they can burn through membrane suits or startle fauna into defensive vibration-bursts. Give them wide berth.
- **Hollow mountains:** The factory shells are hollow, dripping, echo-chambers of settling erosion. They offer shelter from rain, but the condensation is constant and the floors are unstable—porous rock over deeper pools. Use them for temporary rest, not for camp.

### V. FAUNA: ABUNDANCE 2.0, STRANGENESS 2.0

The creatures here navigate by echolocation and vibration. Their eyes—where eyes exist—have been replaced by vibrating plates. They are flat, heat-sensitive, often coated in symbiotic fungi that mirror the plateau’s own pale glimmers.

- **Sound etiquette:** The acoustic environment is already at **2.1** saturation—waterfalls, engine thrum, rain, creature-calls. Your presence adds noise. Move with minimal vocalization; if you must signal your team, use **low-frequency pulses** that match the local fauna’s register. Sudden high-pitched sounds may be interpreted as predatory or mating calls.
- **Do not carry exposed warm metal.** The fauna feeds on vegetation, warm mineral runoff, and especially the mineral deposits on rusted machinery. If your gear has exposed steel or copper, it will smell (or vibrate) like food. Seal all metal in cool, matte composites.
- **Encounters:** They splash through shallow waters in herds. If you feel the water pattern change—rhythmic displacement approaching—**do not run.** Running creates chaotic pressure waves. Stand still, lower your thermal signature if possible, and let the herd navigate around you by its own echolocation map.

### VI. WEATHER & CLIMATE SURVIVAL

The plateau’s size (2.8) allows it to birth its own storms—warm, heavy rain that cools nothing, mist that holds spores, and air so thick it feels like swallowing warm silk.

- **Rain gear is redundant; dryness is impossible.** Instead, carry **spore-resistant skin sealant** and anti-fungal rinses for after-exposure. The rain never stops being warm; hypothermia is not your risk—**respiratory infection from constant spore inhalation** is.
- **Mist navigation:** Visibility is not reduced; it is absent. Mist is just another layer of the 2.0 water presence. Navigate by the changing pitch of distant waterfalls—where the plateau’s weeping rock releases into the lower dark—and by the varying density of engine-thrum.

### VII. PSYCHOLOGICAL PROTOCOLS FOR THE BLACK

This is the hardest preparation.

- **The absence of sky destroys scale perception.** Without stars, you cannot tell if you have walked ten meters or ten kilometers. The dynamic activity (2.1) is constant—everything moves, everything sounds—so there is no “quiet” to rest your nerves. You will feel the 2.8 vastness as anxiety in your chest.
- **Bring anchors of self:** A tactile object from home, a recorded voice (played through bone-conduction, not air-speakers), a rhythmic task. The darkness is not hostile, but it is **absolute**. Many travelers experience a form of sensory grief—the brain searching for light and finding only the faint blue pulse of moss.
- **Team linkage:** Do not travel alone. In 0.0 conditions, isolation is not metaphorical. Link yourselves with vibration-cord—physical tethers that transmit heartbeat and footstep through the cord, so you know your companions are present without speaking.

### VIII. EMERGENCY PROTOCOLS

1. **Fall into Lower Dark:** The edge is invisible. If you feel sudden absence of ground-pressure, deploy buoyancy anchors immediately. The drop from elevation 1.7 is not survivable by standard means.
2. **Entanglement with Machinery:** If caught in slow-turning gears or sagging cables, do not struggle against the rotation. The engines move slowly but with immense torque. Cut power only if you carry insulated tools—otherwise, wait for the rotation to pass and slip free.
3. **Fauna Overload:** If surrounded by vibrating plates and deep resonant calls, lie flat in shallow water and cover your heat-signature with moss-layer. Become part of the landscape density.
4. **Lost Orientation:** Stop. Emit three long, low-frequency tones. Listen for the return from the nearest engine-thrum. In 2.1 acoustic saturation, every point has an acoustic shadow; learn to read them.
