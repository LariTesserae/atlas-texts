**Location: The Unfinished Pocket (Subsurface Pre-Room)**

You are not entering a cave. You are entering a hesitation. This is a pocket-scale chamber—no larger than the hollow left by a single reclining body—buried not beneath soil but beneath the *idea* of soil, embedded in geology that has agreed to be solid only moments ago. The walls are not rock but *intention toward rock*: cool, damp, sweating mineral dew that arrives without cause and vanishes without farewell. The light is sourceless, cool, and brutally bright; it does not illuminate objects but reveals what they are *becoming*, erasing shadow entirely except for those that curl inward, falling toward their own centers. Water exists only as intermittent memory—condensation beads on half-formed calcite ribs, then forgets itself into the air. Sparse, uncertain vegetation clings in gray-green threads. The fauna—translucent, leg-variable, ceiling-crawling—move not to migrate but to acquaint themselves with space, secreting scattered biological architectures (needles, half-arches, abandoned pillars) that are offerings rather than tools. They exist simultaneously as egg, adult, and ghost. Time is local and loose; moments loop. The temperature holds at mouth-cool. Sound is dense with silence, broken only by the click of joint, the crack of new wall deciding its form, and moisture choosing whether to fall or retreat. This is not a destination. It is an unfinished thought that permits witnesses.

## Preparation Guide for the Ordinary Multiverse Traveler

### I. Philosophical Reorientation (Do This First)

Before you pack a single item, you must reframe your expectation of *place*.

- **Abandon the concept of “entering.”** You are not visiting a room; you are being tolerated by a thought that has not finished becoming architecture. Approach with a question rather than a purpose. If you arrive with an agenda—to map, to catalog, to conquer—the pocket will not reject you; worse, it will slowly include you in its accretion. Enter with an open ending.
- **Unlearn gravity.** The fauna treat orientation as etiquette, not law. Prepare your vestibular system. Spend three days prior sleeping in rotated positions—side, inverted, suspended—to remind your inner ear that “down” is a local language.
- **Practice seeing conditions, not surfaces.** The light here reveals becoming, not being. Train your eyes: study objects in transition (ice melting, dough rising, wounds closing) so you do not panic when you see a creature that is simultaneously larva, mature form, and dissolved echo.

### II. Physical Kit: What to Wear and Carry

Given the intimate scale (body-sized), bulky gear is not merely impractical—it is an insult to the space’s unfinished body.

**Clothing:**
- **A single-layer, non-reflective suit** in matte gray-green (to match the uncertain vegetation). Reflective fabrics will confuse the sourceless light and may attract or disorient the translucent fauna, who navigate by condition-reading rather than sight.
- **No pockets with hard closures.** Use soft, open pouches. The mineral dew is intermittent and forgetful; you do not want to trap moisture that wishes to evaporate. Let your clothing breathe like the walls do.
- **A close hood, but no goggles.** The brightness is near-painful and shadow-erasing, but it is not harmful—merely honest. Dark lenses will hide the conditions the light insists on showing you. If you must, wear a translucent, polarized veil that dims intensity without blocking revelation.
- **Footwear: optional, but if used, soft-soled and porous.** The floor and ceiling are interchangeable. You may crawl, cling, or float in orientation. Hard boots will damage the raw calcite ribs, which are not structures to be walked upon but ribs in a first-trimester body.

**Personal Gear (Minimal, Analog):**
- **A thread of unbleached silk or hair.** Not for tying, but for leaving. In a space where shadows fall inward and time loops, you need a linear artifact that is not technological. Tie it gently to one of the scattered calcite needles if you feel disorientation rising. Do not pull it taut; let it hang like the moisture, present and then absent.
- **A mouth-cool flask.** The temperature holds at body-cool/mouth-cool. Bring liquid that matches this—lukewarm tea, not hot coffee, not ice water. You are aligning with the pocket’s thermal language.
- **A blank, water-soluble journal.** You will want to record, but memory here is unreliable and loops carry impressions. Write lightly. If your notes vanish with the condensation, that is correct. The pocket teaches that forgetting is also a form of holding.
- **No electronics.** Technology is not forbidden; it is simply not *thought of* here. Devices may function, but they will report false surfaces. Your compass will spin not because of magnetism but because “north” is still deciding what direction to grow. Leave them behind, or carry them as dead weight only.

### III. Sensory and Psychological Armor

The reality breaks here are subtle but total. You need mental protocols more than physical ones.

**For the Light:**
The illumination shows what things are becoming. You may see yourself as you were at twelve, or as you will be decayed, simultaneously present in your form. Do not panic. The light is not prophetic; it is inclusive. Practice standing in front of a mirror for long minutes, observing not your face but your face’s transitions—blinking, breathing, aging in micro-movements—until multiplicity feels like companionship rather than horror.

**For the Fauna:**
- **Do not classify.** They are not insects, not spirits, not machines. They crawl across ceiling and floor interchangeably, building half-structures that stop when the builder “grew tired of existing.” Their architecture is biological poetry. If one approaches you, remain still. They may secrete a needle of calcite near you, or on you—a mark that you are part of the unfinished thought. This is not aggression. Accept it as you accept weather.
- **Do not touch them in their ghost-state.** They ripple through egg, adult, and ghost forms. Physical contact during transition may collapse their local multiplicity, harming not them but the delicate time-loop you share. Observe from the distance of a breath.

**For the Moisture:**
Water here is intermittent memory. A bead may gather on a rib, hold, then vanish—only to return later carrying “the faint impression of having been missed.” If you feel a drop fall on your skin that was not there before, do not wipe it away immediately. Hold it. It may be carrying information—perhaps your own absence, perhaps the pocket’s first memory of being witnessed.

**For Time:**
Moments loop back. You may speak a word, then hear it again a minute later, identical, carrying the impression of having been missed. Do not try to correct the loop. Speak softly, and if repetition comes, answer it differently. You are in dialogue with a loose local time.

### IV. Navigation and Orientation

- **No maps exist.** The space is intimate and embedded within a larger body of earth that “has not yet learned what shape to take.” You cannot navigate by feature; navigate by temperature. The cool breath of the wall will guide you back to the center, where the light is brightest and the shadows fall most deeply inward.
- **Gravity as suggestion.** If you find yourself clinging to what you thought was the ceiling, do not correct yourself violently. Rotate slowly. The fauna do not fall because they do not insist on falling. Learn this.
- **Mark your presence, not your path.** Use the silk thread, a drop of your own moisture, or a soft exhalation against the wall. Do not scratch. The geology is accreting slowly, visibly; your scratch might become permanent wall before you leave.

### V. Interaction Etiquette

This is the most critical section.

1. **Do not complete anything.** If you see a half-arch of saliva-mineral, do not finish it. Do not straighten a pillar that stops. The scattered built marks are not failures; they are thoughts interrupted by their own wisdom. Your human impulse to “fix” or “complete” is a form of violence here.
2. **Speak, if you must, in unfinished sentences.** The pocket understands syntax that trails off. Questions are welcome; conclusions are not.
3. **If the geology accretes near you—if you feel the wall growing closer, cool and damp—do not resist.** The space is dynamic. You may find yourself closer to the rib-frame, closer to being part of the chamber’s first trimester. Breathe. This is not entombment; it is inclusion. If you must leave, move slowly toward the area where the light grows slightly less bright—the edge of the unfinished thought.

### VI. Temporal and Exit Protocols

There is no door. Exit is a decision, not a location.

- **To leave, stop asking questions.** The pocket holds you not by force but by unfinished curiosity. When your purpose resolves—when you have become part of its thought without trying to become its master—the larger body of earth will seem to shift, and you will find yourself at a boundary that was not there before.
- **Check your pockets upon exit.** The moisture that vanished may have returned, carrying impressions. The silk thread may be gone, or it may have multiplied. The journal pages may be blank or full of writing you do not remember. Accept all of these as souvenirs, not errors.
- **Post-visit care:** You will carry the mouth-cool temperature with you for days. You may see shadows falling inward in ordinary rooms. You may hear the click of a creature’s joint in your sleep. This is not trauma; it is translation. You have been inside an unfinished room, and part of you remains there, politely, in its first trimester.

### VII. Final Intuitive Warning

The greatest danger is not the fauna, not the loose time, not the light that reveals your own becoming. The greatest danger is **comfort**. This pocket is intimate—body-sized, cool, damp, alive with quiet motion. It feels like being held before being born. You may wish to stay, to lie down and forget to leave, to become one of the scattered structures that stops halfway through existing.

If you feel this desire—if the unfinished thought seems more complete than any finished room you’ve known—remember: you are an ordinary human multiverse traveler. You have other unfinished rooms to witness, other pre-formations to tolerate. Tie your thread. Speak your trailing sentence. And when the moisture returns, carrying the impression of having missed you—let it fall, and step back into the larger earth that is still, always, learning what shape to take.
