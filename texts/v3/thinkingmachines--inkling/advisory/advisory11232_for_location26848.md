**Location:** The Sealed Bowl — a pocket-scale hollow hidden beneath a crack in the world’s skin, a low, enclosed plain where geothermal heat has replaced sunlight and water has never existed. It is a sparse, primitive city woven through lush, crimson-black vegetation that drinks steam and grows outward in ribbed arches, all sitting in loud, resonant equilibrium with the earth’s thermal breath.

---

### I. Pre-Departure: Understanding What You Are Walking Into

Before you pack, understand the physics of the place. This is not a desert you can “tough out.” The air itself is near-boiling in patches; your sweat will evaporate before it beads. There is no hydrological cycle—no rain, no dew, no hidden aquifer. The vegetation thrives on mineral seep and radiant heat, not moisture, which means *you* are the only source of water in a system that has forgotten it. The settlement is not abandoned in the usual sense; it is emptied but maintained by ancient logic—buildings arranged around invisible cold spots that the flora and architecture avoid together. The “weirdness” is not a monster but a pattern: plants frame doorways and curve away from emptiness, as if the city and the growth share a single nervous system.

You are not visiting a ruin. You are entering a working machine of heat, stone, and slow-growing life. Your goal is to pass through without becoming part of the erosion.

---

### II. Physical Conditioning (Start 6–8 Weeks Prior)

Your body must adapt to desiccation and thermal stress before arrival.

- **Heat Acclimatization:** Spend increasing time in dry saunas (not steam rooms—there is no humidity here) at 90–100°C (194–212°F). Train your cardiovascular system to maintain blood pressure as peripheral vessels dilate. Practice breathing slowly through the nose in hot, dry air; your nasal passages must learn to humidify air with minimal moisture loss.
- **Hydration Discipline:** Train your kidneys to handle extreme water retention and rapid loss. Practice calculating exact fluid needs. In the bowl, you will carry every milliliter you drink; there is no “finding water later.”
- **Respiratory Training:** The dust is fine, hot, and mineral-sharp. Practice wearing high-resistance respirators during cardio. Strengthen your diaphragm. Learn to control panic-breathing—hyperventilation here will dry out your mucous membranes in seconds and invite dust deep into lungs.
- **Skin Conditioning:** You will be covered head-to-toe. Still, practice moisturizing barriers and occlusive dressing. Your lips, nasal membranes, and eyes will face abrasion from wind-carried mineral dust that polishes stone over centuries.

### III. The Suit: Your Portable Environment

Do not dress for climate—dress to become a sealed, mobile microclimate.

**Outer Layer:**
- A **mineral-insulated, reflective exoshell** (silica-aerogel weave with polished ceramic plating). The heat is ambient and radiant, not solar, so you need to reject infrared from all directions—floor, walls, air. The shell should be loose enough to allow air circulation within but sealed at every joint.
- **Color/Finish:** Matte dark crimson or black-green. Not for camouflage—though it helps—but because bright colors attract psychological disorientation in the dim reddish-brown glow. You want to mirror the local palette to reduce sensory shock.

**Thermal Regulation:**
- A **closed-loop liquid cooling vest** with phase-change packs (salt-hydrate or paraffin-based, recharged by placing against cool stone in the rare cold spots—never expose to direct vent heat). You need active cooling; passive insulation is not enough near boiling vents.
- **Insulated boots** with thick, heat-reflective soles (multi-layer ceramic and compressed fiber). The ground is cracked clay, black salt, and worn mineral slabs—abrasive and hot. The boots should extend to mid-calf and seal with gaiters, because the dust will try to enter every gap.

**Respiratory:**
- A **full-face respirator with dual-stage filtration:** first stage for fine mineral dust (the abrasive polishing agent), second stage for geothermal gases (sulfur compounds are likely present though not named—assume them).
- **Moisture-recycling mouthpiece/nasal cannula:** Your exhaled breath must be captured, condensed, and returned to you. In absolute zero-humidity, losing breath-moisture is death over days. The system should also inject a minimal, measured mist of your carried water into incoming air.
- **Eye protection:** Sealed goggles with anti-fog coating and low-light enhancement. The glow is dim, reddish-brown; standard vision will fail in the distant corridors. Thermal imaging overlay is useful—vents and thermal currents become visible as heat-shimmers.

**Head:**
- A **hood with integrated sound-dampening and thermal shielding.** The sound here is loud—bass rumbles in your chest, echoes bounce off the low mineral ceiling. You need selective audio filtration: block the constant low-frequency drone to preserve sanity, but allow directional high-frequency sounds (snapping fronds, wind shifts) for navigation.

### IV. Entry: The Crack and the Descent

The entrance is not a door but a fissure in thicker earth. You will likely rappel or climb down.

- **Rope:** Use heat-resistant synthetic (aramid fiber), not standard nylon which weakens in sustained heat. Anchor to stable rock outside—test it. The crack is narrow; your pack must be streamlined.
- **Lighting:** Do not bring bright white light. The locals (if any remain, or the old logic of the place) respond to thermal patterns, not photons. Use **dim red-filtered illumination** or thermal-view only. Bright light will blind you in the dimness and may disturb the vegetation’s edge-glow chemistry.
- **Initial Orientation:** As you descend, notice the temperature gradient. Near the crack, it may be bearable; deeper in, it spikes. Mark your exit point with a **heat-resistant beacon** (a ceramic marker that glows faintly in infrared, not visible light). The bowl is enclosed; disorientation is immediate.

### V. Survival Supplies: The Mathematics of Zero Water

You must carry everything. Assume a stay of 3 days minimum (travel time plus exploration).

**Water (Your Single Greatest Weight):**
- Minimum **6 liters per day**—more if physically active in heat. For 3 days: 18 liters. Pack in **insulated, collapsible bladders** (stainless steel or ceramic-lined polymer) to prevent heating. Keep them close to your body inside the cooling vest so they don’t boil externally.
- **Electrolyte tablets:** You will sweat mineral salts despite desiccation; replace them precisely.
- **Recycling:** Any urine must be captured and processed through a portable membrane filter. In this environment, waste is resource. Do not relieve yourself onto the ground—the mineral balance is ancient and sensitive, and the dust will carry your scent (though there are no animals to smell it, the principle of non-contamination stands).

**Food:**
- **Caloric density is key.** You will burn energy maintaining body temperature.
- Choose foods that do not require water to digest: nut pastes, compressed fat bars, dehydrated meat that can be eaten dry (your saliva is too precious to waste rehydrating food).
- **Do not eat the local vegetation.** The crimson-black growth is bioluminescent, mineral-fed, and likely toxic or chemically reactive in human biology. It grows in patterns that avoid cold spots—this suggests a sensory or chemical awareness. Ingesting it is not “foraging”; it is poisoning yourself with an unknown heat-chemistry organism.

**Shelter (If Staying Overnight):**
- Do not build a fire. There is no fuel that isn’t already burning, and adding combustion to this system is dangerous.
- Use a **low-profile thermal tent** with reflective outer skin, placed in one of the sparse settlement structures if structurally sound, or against a wall in a thermal current “dead zone.”
- Sleep in shifts if traveling with others—listen for the vent cycles. The rumble changes pitch before pressure releases.

### VI. Navigation and Movement in the Hollow

The settlement is organized not by grid but by **heat-flow logic.** The lanes follow invisible thermal rivers.

- **Follow the Warmth, Not the Map:** The streets curve to catch or avoid currents. If you feel a sudden cool pocket—an absence of the usual chest-vibration—stop. These are the cold spots. The buildings avoid them, and the vegetation bends away from them. They are not “safe zones”; they are anomalies in the equilibrium. Do not camp there. Do not linger.
- **Sound Navigation:** Since echoes fill the space, learn to read them. A sudden deadening of echo means you are near dense vegetation (the ribbed arches absorb sound). A sharpening echo means open plaza or corridor. The low rumble is constant—use it as a baseline; when it shifts in pitch, a vent is opening or closing nearby.
- **Distance:** The bowl is vast relative to its contents. You may walk long minutes between structures. Do not panic at the emptiness. The density is hollow by design.
- **Footing:** The ground is cracked clay, black salt, and rounded mineral slabs worn smooth by centuries of abrasive dust-wind. They are slippery with mineral film. Move slowly. A fall in near-boiling air risks severe burn and desiccation of exposed skin.

### VII. Interaction with the Fabric: Settlement, Plants, and Equilibrium

This is not a museum. It is an active, settled system of erosion and growth.

**The Urban Remnants:**
- The buildings are compressed earth, hand-shaped brick, circular dwellings, storage pits lined with smooth stone. They are primitive—no metal, no machinery. This means they are durable but fragile to modern weight or tools. Do not lean on walls; the compressed earth is brittle after centuries of thermal expansion.
- The open plazas are paved with flat mineral slabs, rounded by abrasion. They are beautiful and dangerous—surfaces may be thinner than they appear, worn by the same dust that now moves in slow spirals around you.
- If you enter a dwelling, note the orientation: doors likely face thermal currents, not cardinal directions. The logic is atmospheric. Leave everything as you found it—do not move stones, do not take artifacts. The settlement is sparse; removing one object disrupts a pattern older than your world.

**The Vegetation:**
- The crimson and black-green mats are lush, low, spreading outward in tangled archways. They are not trees; they are root-fused to the floor, drinking steam.
- **Do not cut them.** They grow slowly and hold the soil (or what passes for soil). Their edge-glow is dull bioluminescence—observe it, but do not touch. The ribbed leaves are leather-thick and may be sharp or chemically reactive.
- Notice how they frame doorways and curve over fallen walls. They are not random; they have grown in response to the architecture. This is the “weirdness”—a shared logic between plant and stone. Treat it with the respect you would give a living cathedral.

**The Cold Spots:**
- When you encounter one—darker, quieter, with plants bending away—observe from a distance. These spaces are avoided by both flora and architecture. They may be thermal sinks, or something else entirely. Document them if you must, but do not enter them. Your suit’s cooling is calibrated for heat; sudden cold can cause condensation shock in your gear, and the psychological effect of stepping from roaring warmth into silent dark is disorienting.

### VIII. Health and Emergency Protocols

**Immediate Risks:**
- **Hyperthermia / Heat Stroke:** Even with cooling vests, failure is possible. Know the signs: confusion, cessation of sweating (though in this air, sweat is already invisible), rapid heartbeat. If this occurs, you must reach a vent-adjacent zone? No—actually, avoid extreme vent heat. Seek one of the sparse settlement structures with thick walls that moderate temperature, or use your emergency thermal blanket (reflective, not insulating) to create shade.
- **Respiratory Damage:** Hot dust abrasion can cause pulmonary edema over time. If you cough blood or feel sharp chest pain, you must exit immediately. There is no medical facility here.
- **Desiccation:** Monitor your weight and urine color (if recycling fails). Your mouth will feel coated in dust constantly—this is normal. If you stop producing saliva entirely, you are in crisis.
- **Psychological Dissociation:** The emptiness, the constant bass sound, the lack of animals or movement other than slow plant-growth, the architectural botany—these create an uncanny effect. You may feel watched, though nothing watches. You may feel the city is “breathing.” This is sensory deprivation plus thermal stress. Have a mental anchor: a mantra, a counting system, a scheduled communication check with your outside support (if any).

**Emergency Exit:**
- Your beacon marks the crack. If conditions deteriorate, move directly to it. Do not explore “just one more corridor.” The scale is deceptive; you can become lost in the hollow spaces between groves.
- If your cooling vest fails, use the cold spots—not to stay, but to cool your core quickly in 30-second intervals, then move. Prolonged exposure to the anomaly is unknown risk.

### X. The Mindset: Becoming a Visitor, Not a Disruptor

When you step onto the worn mineral slabs, between the crimson groves, listening to the deep rumble of vents and the dry crack of growing stalks, you will understand that this place does not need you. It has achieved equilibrium—erosion settled, plants holding form, buildings arranged around invisible rules.

Your presence is temporary. Move slowly. Breathe through your system, not the air. Drink your carried water with precision. Observe the architecture of growth and the growth of architecture. Note the cold spots and avoid them. Listen to the echo.

Then leave the same way you came—through the crack—carrying your waste and your memories, leaving the dust, the heat, and the slow, loud clockwork of the bowl exactly as it was: enclosed, empty, lush, and perfectly, unsettlingly itself.
