**Travel Advisory: The Low Depression / The Rounded Valley**  
*An open regional sink with no sharp arrival, no vertical invitation—only spread. Erosion has finished its work here (equilibrium 2.0). The walls have slumped into rounded shoulders; the floor is dark basalt knobs rising like knuckles through obsidian sand polished to a dull, glassy sheen. The temperature holds in cool suspension (1.1)—never a biting wind, never warmth—just a chill that has migrated permanently into the stone. Light has been withdrawn to 0.4: not darkness precisely, but a filtered, low-hanging dimming, as if the air itself has thickened into a lens. You do not see this place; you enter it like a slow exhalation.*

Below is how an ordinary human multiverse traveler should ready themselves—not for a hike, but for immersion into a saturated, pre-built, mineral-only world where sound is physical pressure, the ground is a living carpet, and absence is the dominant feature.

---

### 1. Psychological Calibration: Surrender the Visual
You must arrive with your eyes demoted.

There is no vegetation (0.2). No green, no brown intrusion of moss or lichen readable as “life separate from rock.” There is no construction (0.1), no ruin, no path, no artifact to orient human expectation. The scale is open (1.8), meaning this uniformity extends regionally—there is no “outside” visible from within.

Prepare for **sensory inversion**:  
- **Sight** will be reduced to faint gradients—cooler and warmer shades of black, the slight phosphorescent constellation of bodies near the ground, the glint of condensed moisture on basalt. Practice navigating blindfolded for hours before arrival. Learn to read temperature with your cheeks and the backs of your hands.  
- **Space** will feel violated. The fauna density (2.4) approaches the physical limit of volume itself. You will never stand in an “empty” spot. Personal space does not exist here; it has been replaced by layered bodies.

Do not expect silence. The sound (2.7) is not background noise—it is a deafening aggregate roar that occupies the low air and trembles through your boots: the scraping of hard bodies against polished stone, wet percussion in the pools, the constant clicking of interlocking calcified plates, a vibrating drone without peak or valley. It never breaks. Before traveling, condition yourself to prolonged monotonic loudness. Meditation in industrial noise is useful training.

---

### 2. What You Will Be Walking Into: The Living Carpet
The fauna (2.9) is overwhelming. These are not insects or crustaceans exactly, but **mineral-animal hybrids** (strangeness 1.8): small and medium forms armored in dark, damp chitin and geometric calcified plates that suggest crystal growth as much as birth. Some crawl in slow tidal waves; others pulse, glide, or hang in dense dark swarms just above the ground, thickening the gloom itself.

They cover the valley floor in **living carpets several layers deep** in the lowest hollows. In pools—those black, still reservoirs of intermittent water (1.2)—they converge in feeding frenzies, piling, clicking, splashing, consuming mineral dust, condensation, and one another in an eternal rotation. When moisture drains away through invisible cracks, they migrate across the smooth stone in vast, synchronized tides, responding to vibrations and chemical traces you will not perceive.

Some carry a **faint, cool phosphorescence**—not illumination, but enough to turn the floor into a pressed constellation of living stars. Others are translucent, revealing dark, coiling internal structures that move with autonomous rhythm.

**Preparation implication:** You are not a predator here. You are an obstacle, a warm moisture-source, and a potential burial risk if you fall into a pool or collapse in a hollow.

---

### 3. Clothing: Become a Smooth, Dark, Insulated Shell
The environment is cool (1.1), perpetually damp (1.2), and abrasive.

**Base Layer:** Thermal, moisture-wicking material that retains heat when wet. The dampness clings without saturating, but your body will be in constant contact with humid air and condensation-beaded stone.

**Mid Layer:** Thick, abrasion-resistant padding—wool, dense canvas, or composite weave. You will be brushing, stepping on, and being brushed by countless hard bodies with interlocking plates. Soft fabrics will snag; loose threads will become anchors.

**Outer Shell:** This is critical. Wear a **smooth, hard, dark outer layer**—polished ceramic-composite, dark waxed armor, or obsidian-colored plate. The reasoning is dual:  
- **Protection:** The bodies are geometric, clicking, layered. A hard shell prevents plates from gripping your clothing or scraping through.  
- **Camouflage/Non-interference:** You should not stand out in the gloom. Bright colors or reflective tech will disrupt the faint visual field and may draw synchronized attention. Be dark, matte, smooth—like another rounded stone.

**Full Sealing:** Seal neck, wrists, and ankles. Swarms occupy the air just above ground. A hood with a **fine mesh face veil** is essential—tight enough to exclude small bodies and chitin fragments, open enough to allow sound and air pressure to reach you.

**Footwear:** High, ankle-locked boots with thick, puncture-resistant soles and ridged grips for polished obsidian sand. The ground is slippery when wet; the bodies form carpets that shift under weight. Your boots must protect against being pinned or pierced by calcified plates, and must seal at the top with gaiters to prevent bodies from entering.

### 4. Navigation: Non-Visual, Non-Technological
Technology is absent in context (0.4)—not necessarily that your devices will fail, but that there is no infrastructure, no signal, no grid, and no reason to trust electronic orientation in a place where every visual landmark is rounded and identical.

**Do not rely on bright lights.** In 0.4 illumination, a sudden beam will destroy your dark adaptation and possibly trigger unpredictable responses in translucent or phosphorescent organisms. If you must illuminate for medical emergency, use **brief, shielded, downward-aimed red light** only.

**Navigate by:**
- **Temperature gradients:** The stone holds chill differently near pools and in hollows. Learn to feel the air.
- **Sound mapping:** The aggregate roar shifts near water—wet percussion adds a layered splash-rhythm. Pools are louder, wetter, denser.
- **Tactile staff:** Carry a smooth basalt rod (local material, if ethically gathered at the boundary) to probe body density ahead and feel for depressions.
- **Landmarks:** The dark basalt knobs are your only vertical references. Memorize their relative positions before entering.
- **Tethering:** Travel in pairs (or small groups) linked by a **taut, textured rope**—not chain, which clinks and vibrates. Visibility is near-zero; the rope is your eyes and your voice.

**Orientation rule:** Stop if lost. Do not wander in uniform darkness over open scale. Find the nearest knob, hold position, listen for moisture-gradient shifts, feel ground vibration, and wait for your partner.

---

### 5. Hearing: Filter, Don’t Block
The sound is deafening but informative. Complete ear-blocking is dangerous—you need to sense directional vibration and the shift from dry scraping to wet pool-percussion.

Wear **filtered, low-decibel ear protection**—specialized plugs that reduce the crushing volume of the aggregate roar but preserve low-frequency ground tremor and spatial direction. Practice interpreting “sound texture”: the dry click of migration versus the wet pile of a feeding frenzy.

**Communication:** Do not shout. Voices are lost instantly, and sudden loud human sounds may create vibrations that ripple through synchronized tidal sections of fauna, potentially triggering convergence or defensive piling. Use **hand signals, rope tugs, or tactile communication devices** (vibrating wristbands).

---

### 6. The Moisture: Gather Carefully, Drink Never From the Pools
Water comes as presence, not flow (1.2). It beads on cool mineral surfaces, collects in black still pools.

**Do not drink from the pools.** They are the centers of ecological intensity—feeding frenzies, predator-prey rotation, gelatinous reproductive clusters hardening into shells, chitin fragments, and unknown phosphorescent chemistry. The water is biologically active and toxic to human systems.

**Hydration strategy:**  
- Carry all drinking water you need.  
- If resupply is necessary, collect **condensation from basalt knob surfaces away from pool depressions** using absorbent cloth, then wring into your containers. Purify with your own technology (UV, chemical, filtration)—do not assume local “purity.”  
- The air is damp; dehydration is slow but insidious in cool temperatures. Monitor intake deliberately.

---

### 7. Movement Protocol: Move With the Tide, Not Against
The fauna moves in synchronized sections, tidal and responsive. Rapid, erratic human movement—stomping, running—creates vibrations that travel through the smooth ground and the bodies themselves.

**Walk slowly. Step with flat, distributed weight**, as if walking on deep snow, except the “snow” is clicking, layered shell. This minimizes disruptive vibration and reduces the risk of breaking through a thin crust of bodies into a deeper hollow or pool.

**Avoid pools entirely unless crossing is unavoidable.** If you must cross near one, move perpendicular, slowly, without splashing. Do not stand at the edge—feeding frenzies involve piling bodies that can reach several layers deep and may surge unpredictably when moisture shifts.

If you fall into a hollow carpet: stay calm, protect your face and airways with your sealed hood, roll to orient yourself, and use your staff to push gently upward. The bodies are not aggressively predatory toward large warm objects, but they are **dense**, and being buried in clicking plates is suffocation risk.

---

### 8. Food, Waste, and Biological Contamination
There is no vegetation (0.2). You cannot forage. The fauna consumes mineral dust, condensation, and itself—none of which is human-edible, and all of which may carry unknown mineral-toxins and phosphorescent compounds.

**Bring all food.** High-calorie, non-perishable, sealed against the damp. Do not cook over open flame—there is no wood, and smoke/heat may alter local moisture patterns or attract convergence.

**Waste:** Pack out everything. This is a pre-tool, untouched environment (0.1 built). Leaving waste, packaging, or biological matter is a profound violation of the equilibrium. Before departure, **inspect your gear for clinging bodies**. Brush off chitin fragments, shells, and translucent membranes. Do not export life from this saturation.

---

### 9. Sleep and Rest: Low Profile, Thermal, Alert
You cannot build (0.1). Do not construct shelters, dig, or drive stakes into the smooth ground—this is not your landscape to alter.

Use a **low-profile, self-supporting thermal shelter**—a bivouac or insulated hammock that requires no ground penetration. Sleep in shifts if in a group; the sound never stops, but patterns may subtly shift with moisture rhythms, and someone should always be feeling the ground vibration.

Rest against a basalt knob. The rounded walls have no sharp edges, but they also provide no shelter from the swarms—your hood and sealed clothing are your only walls.

---

### 10. Emergency and Extraction Planning
- **Injury on smooth ground:** Stabilize with rigid splints; the polished stone offers no traction. Keep wounds sealed—open tissue in this environment risks contamination from chitin dust and unknown microbial rotation.  
- **Lost/Disoriented:** Stop immediately. Hold the rope. Feel for the nearest knob. Do not “search” blindly—distance is deceptive in open scale and darkness.  
- **Submerged in pool frenzy:** If pulled into a pool of piling bodies, curl around your staff, protect your airway, and move in the direction of least resistance—usually upward and perpendicular to the pool’s center—without thrashing.

Have an **extraction beacon**—not electronic, if you wish to honor the tech-absence context, but a high-contrast, pre-arranged physical signal at the valley rim. Do not expect rescue from within; this place has no infrastructure, no paths, no eyes watching.

---

### 11. The Ethos of the Visit
This valley is in long-established equilibrium. It does not need you. It will not yield to you. It offers no sharp features to conquer, no vegetation to harvest, no ruins to decode—only the relentless, noisy, layered existence of its mineral-animal saturation in cool, dark, damp rotation.

Prepare not as a conqueror or even a tourist, but as a **temporary, silent passage**: smooth, dark, sealed, slow, and small within the carpet. Leave no build. Leave no light. Leave no sound that outlasts your steps. When you emerge—if you emerge—brush the bodies from your shell, check that you have taken nothing but memory, and let the valley exhale behind you, unchanged, roaring, and full.
