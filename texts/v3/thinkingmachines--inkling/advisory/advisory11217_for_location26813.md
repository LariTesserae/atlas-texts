**Preparation Briefing: The Riven Lowland (Multiversal Transit Advisory)**

You are not going hiking. You are entering a mouth that has only just finished tearing open, and everything inside is still screaming about it. Before you step through, understand this: the Lowland does not recognize you as special. It is warm, wet, deafening, sharp, and older in its biology than in its geology. There is no sky to orient you, no path to follow, and no silence to recover in. Here is how an ordinary human survives long enough to leave.

---

### I. Mental & Sensory Conditioning (Do This First)

**Accept the darkness as permanent.** Your eyes will adapt over hours, not minutes. There is no moon, no starlight, no “day.” The only illumination comes from organisms reacting to *noise*—fungal bodies flaring sickly green, vines racing with blue-white pulses. This means your flashlight is not just useless; it is an ecological insult. Practice navigating blind for at least 48 hours before departure. Learn to read space by temperature gradients, air pressure on skin, and the direction of dripping water.

**Deafen yourself strategically.** The baseline noise is 2.6—turbulent saturation. Insect chirps in the millions, geological groaning, constant percussion of condensation, and the lower bellows of things in the mud. You will suffer auditory fatigue within minutes. Bring high-quality, pressure-equalizing ear protection, but do not wear it continuously: you need to hear cliff collapses and swarm shifts. Train in “selective deafness”—filtering constant roar for sudden absences or spikes.

**Prepare for claustrophobia without walls.** The scale is open—regional—but the ceiling is a woven biological vault so dense it feels like burial. There are no horizons, only vertical green chaos. If open skies calm you, this place will dismantle that. Practice breathing exercises in enclosed, dark, humid environments.

### II. Apparel: Wear the Environment, Don’t Fight It

**Heat and moisture are constant (2.1 warm, 0.8 damp).** You will sweat inside your clothes. Choose breathable, quick-dry membranes that also resist puncture. The vegetation is lush to the point of cloying—giant ferns unfurl like silent explosions, moss hangs in ropes, fungal bodies swell. Every surface is either slick with condensation or coated in spores.

*   **Base layer:** Moisture-wicking, antimicrobial. The decomposing lushness smells of wet stone and rot; your skin will be colonized quickly if not protected.
*   **Outer shell:** Abrasion-resistant, puncture-proof fabric. The basalt is glass-sharp; the fauna includes translucent winged amphibians with unpredictable flight paths and ground-dwellers with segmented, clicking mouths and too many legs. You will be brushed, landed on, and possibly bitten through cloth.
*   **Footwear:** Thick-soled, heat-resistant boots with deep tread for sharp rock and bubbling mud pools. The ground is porous; temporary rivers vanish in minutes, leaving slick mineral crusts. Ankle support is non-negotiable—there are no rounded hills, only abrupt angles.
*   **Gloves:** Heavy, heat-resistant. You will grab vines, moss ropes, and sharp stone for balance. The geothermal breath seeps through fissures—some rocks are hot enough to burn through cheap material.

**Respiratory protection:** The air carries mineral tang from sulfur and geothermal steam, plus dense spore loads. A filtered mask or scarf is essential, but ensure it allows you to hear your own breath—sound management is critical.

### III. Light & Sound Protocol: The Most Important Rules

**Do not emit bright, continuous light.** The organisms glow in reaction to sound and disturbance, not to illuminate for you. A flashlight beam will do nothing useful in the absolute dark beneath the canopy and may trigger chaotic bioluminescent ripples that disorient you further.

Instead:
*   **Use silent, low-thermal navigation aids.** A haptic compass, GPS with heads-up display if compatible with local magnetism (test first—geothermal fields may distort it), and rope lines marked with silent tactile knots.
*   **Learn to “listen” with sound.** Since the fungi and vines glow in response to noise, a controlled, brief sound—clicking fingers, a muted chime—can trigger a localized green or blue-white pulse, briefly outlining nearby architecture. Practice this: one sharp, isolated sound, then absolute stillness. Watch the response. This is your lantern. Do not shout; the fauna moves turbulently in response to loud noise, and swarms split and reform like smoke.
*   **Carry silent signal flares? No.** The heat and chemical exhalations might interact unpredictably. If you must signal for extraction, use thermal beacons placed in clearings—not light, not sound.

**Move quietly.** The winged amphibians fill the air in eddies reacting to heat, vibration, and chemicals. The ground fauna migrates constantly in the mud and root-knots. Loud steps trigger flight responses—swarms will part around you, but they may also drive other predators (or prey) into your path. Soft-soled boots, slow weight transfer, and breath control are your camouflage.

### IV. Shelter: Do Not Build—Suspend

There are no buildings (0.2). The temporary shelters you may find—woven vine nests already being reclaimed by moss—are evidence that previous travelers (or native presences) stayed briefly and hung, not stood.

*   **Bring a suspended shelter:** A heat-tolerant hammock-tent with sealed mesh sides and a rain fly. You must sleep *above* the ground fauna. The mud bubbles and collapses; things with too many legs migrate at ground level; hot rain falls in brief, violent curtains.
*   **Anchor carefully.** The sharp basalt spires and young cliff faces are unstable. A section can give way in a silent, slow avalanche. Test anchors; do not tie to moss ropes that are living architecture—they may shift or rot.
*   **Keep it temporary.** Do not stay more than one rest cycle. The vegetation reclaims at biological speed. Spore release is visible; your shelter will be colonized overnight.

### V. Navigation in a Wound Without a Sky

The valley is regional—open scale, extending past any viewpoint. Boundaries exist: sheer young walls of the rift eventually contain it, but within them, repetition and variation are endless.

**Orientation tools:**
*   **Geological memory:** The erosion is fresh. Every cliff face is glass-sharp, every stone carries breaking heat. Learn to read the *direction of fracture*—the valley was torn downward. Follow the gradient of sharpness downward to find lower ground, upward to escape.
*   **Water logic:** Water is intermittent—brief hissing hot rain, constant condensation, occasional bubbling black pools ringed by rust and sulfur crust. Follow the drip. Water collects in hollows, then vanishes into porous ground. Do not drink from the black pools; they are young, mineral-heavy, and chemically active. Collect condensation from vine ropes or wait for the brief rain in your sealed containers, then filter aggressively.
*   **Sound mapping:** Since noise is turbulent saturation, learn the local acoustic signature. The high chirp of insects, the lower bellow of larger creatures, the rush of temporary rivers—each zone has a profile. Sudden silence means either a predator, an avalanche, or a swarm shift. Move toward the sound of open dripping if you need water; avoid zones where the geological groan is loudest—settling stone means instability.

**Mark your path, but subtly.** The notched stones you may find are carved—counting, warning, or ritual. Do not add your own without understanding them. Use silent, removable markers: colored cord tied in specific knots, placed high where ground fauna won’t disturb them.

### VI. Sustenance: Eat Before You Enter

There is food here—everything is feeding—but not for you. The fauna is overwhelming: translucent amphibians in turbulent clouds, segmented mouth-creatures in constant migration, fungal bodies that respond like nerves.

*   **Bring sealed rations.** The decomposing lushness is aggressive; spores and bacteria will colonize open food within hours. Heat-sealed, dry, high-calorie packs only.
*   **Water:** As noted, pool water is toxic/mineral-dense. Rain is brief and hissing-hot; let it cool before collection. Condensation on moss ropes is your safest source—wipe with clean cloth into containers. Expect to carry 150% of your normal water weight; the humidity tricks you into thinking you’re hydrated when you’re losing it constantly.

**Avoid eating local biology.** The organisms glow in reaction to disturbance—this is ecological signaling, not invitation. The multi-legged ground creatures are in constant feeding migration; you are intruding, not invited.

### VII. Hazard Protocols: What Will Kill You

**Geological (Immediate):**
The valley is still settling. Hot mud bubbles and collapses. Cliff faces avalanche silently. Avoid walking near the base of sharp spires; camp on stable, older (relatively) flat black stone, never on slopes. If you hear a change in the geological groan—a shift in pitch—move perpendicular to the slope immediately.

**Faunal (Constant):**
The winged amphibians are translucent and turbulent—not necessarily hostile, but they react to heat and vibration. Do not carry open flames (the geothermal breath is unpredictable; spores may be flammable). If swarms split and reform around you, freeze. Movement triggers eddies.

The ground fauna—too many legs, segmented clicking mouths—migrates in waves. Do not sleep on ground. Do not reach into root-knots without gloves and a stick. They feed; they do not distinguish you from decomposing matter if you lie still too long in the mud.

**Environmental (Creeping):**
*   **Burns:** Geothermal fissures release warm breath; some rocks are hot enough to blister. Test surfaces.
*   **Infection:** Every cut will encounter spores, bacteria, and mineral-rich water. Carry aggressive antiseptic and wound closure kits. A scratch in this environment becomes a fever quickly.
*   **Sensory failure:** The darkness and noise will erode your decision-making. Set hard time limits: “If I have not reached point X in Y hours, I turn back regardless of curiosity.”

### VIII. Technology & Tools: Primitive is Enough

The tech level here is 1.2—bone, chipped obsidian, woven fiber, split wood. If you encounter evidence of presence (the notched stones, the vine shelters), understand that whoever made them operates at this level. They are hidden, or part of the flow, or long absent.

**Your gear should bridge worlds:**
*   **Cutting tools:** A sharp obsidian or steel blade for vines and emergency shelter construction. The ferns and moss architectures are resilient; you will need to cut through them.
*   **Rope:** High-tensile, heat-resistant cord. Essential for cliff navigation, suspended shelter, and marking paths.
*   **Containers:** Sealed, non-porous. The air is thick; water is intermittent; contamination is certain.
*   **Medical:** Beyond standard kits, bring treatments for chemical burns (sulfur/mineral exposure), fungal infection, and auditory trauma from constant noise exposure.

**Do not bring loud technology.** Engines, radios with speakers, drones with rotors—the noise will trigger bioluminescent chaos and swarm turbulence. If you must communicate with extraction teams, use silent, directional text-based devices or pre-arranged thermal signals.

### IX. Social/Cultural Protocol: You Are the Anomaly

Built presence is 0.2—essentially absent. The carved notches in soft black stone might be counting, warning, or ritual. The vine shelters hang like abandoned nests.

*   **Do not disturb the notches.** Read them as warnings. If a line of marks leads downward into a fissure, do not follow.
*   **Leave no trace that does not belong.** The valley is in turbulent motion; your trash will be buried, overgrown, and chemically altered within days. Pack out everything, including biological waste—this ecosystem is too young and dense to absorb foreign matter safely.
*   **If you encounter a presence:** The description suggests figures lost in the flow—small, hidden, operating with bone and fiber. They are not “civilized” in any multiversal standard. They respond to the same noise and heat you do. Approach with silence, open hands, and no sudden light. Offer nothing. Observe, record mentally, and withdraw.

### X. Final Mindset: You Are Visiting a Birth

The Riven Lowland is not finished. It is region-sized, open, and dynamically turbulent—still tearing, still filling, still learning what it is. You are an ordinary human with ordinary fragility entering an environment that has never heard of politeness, path, or peace.

Prepare not for conquest, but for endurance. Dress to become invisible: silent, suspended, cool against the heat, sealed against the damp. Move like water—slow, finding gaps, not forcing them. Listen more than you look. Light yourself only through the reactions you carefully, quietly provoke.

And set a hard exit time. This valley does not want visitors. It wants to keep becoming, loudly, in the dark, with sharp edges and overwhelming company. Your job is to witness just long enough to remember, then leave before you become part of the noise it responds to.

**Safe transit. Move quietly. Hang high. Filter everything.**
