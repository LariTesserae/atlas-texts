### I. Physiological Preparation: The Body at 2.8

**Altitude is not a backdrop; it is the primary occupant.** At 2.8 elevation, the air is not merely thin—it is structurally different. Your blood will protest within hours.

- **Pre-acclimatization protocol:** Spend at least ten standard days at progressive altitudes before arrival. Use supplemental oxygen carriers, not as luxury but as baseline equipment. Your heart rate will be elevated constantly; train for cardiovascular endurance in low-oxygen environments if possible.
- **Pressure and joints:** The cool dry air (0.7 temperature, 0.2 water) will pull moisture from your mucous membranes, eyes, and skin with aggressive speed. Wear sealed, moisture-retaining layers—not just warm clothing, but active hydration suits for the skin. Lubricate nostrils and eyes with medical-grade sealant; nosebleeds and corneal abrasion from crystalline wind are common.
- **Respiratory protection:** The mineral wind does not blow sand; it scrapes dry, translucent flakes of salt and glassy residue. A sealed breathing apparatus with particulate filtration is mandatory. Do not trust cloth masks; the particles are sharp, dry, and chemically neutral but mechanically destructive to lung tissue over time.
- **Cold management:** 0.7 is not arctic, but it is relentless—dry cold that penetrates insulation through lack of humidity. Use layered thermal wear with vacuum-sealed inner liners. Hypothermia here is silent; without sweat or shiver reflex in such dry air, you may not notice your core temperature dropping until cognition fails.

### II. Water: The Absence That Defines Everything

With 0.2 water in the environment, there are no rivers, no condensation, no biological reservoirs.

- **Bring all water.** Calculate not for your stay, but for twice your stay—extraction is unpredictable. Use closed-loop hydration systems; every drop of urine and breath-moisture must be reclaimed if your stay exceeds forty-eight hours.
- **Electrolyte management:** Without environmental humidity, your electrolyte balance will destabilize faster than in any desert. Supplement aggressively with mineral salts—ironic, given the ground is made of them, but your body cannot absorb them transdermally.
- **No foraging:** Vegetation is 0.5—functionally nonexistent. There are no edible analogues. Do not attempt to consume local crystal or salt formations; they are compressed, weathered (2.1 erosion), and chemically inert, but physically they will lacerate your digestive tract.

### III. Navigation: When Space Lies

This is the most dangerous preparation gap. At 2.5 scale, the distances are already vast, but the 2.9 weirdness makes standard navigation lethal.

- **Do not trust your eyes.** From certain angles, a tower lies horizontally across a valley though it stands vertical upon approach. Shadows fall upward or not at all. Distances contract or expand based on your focus.

- **Timekeeping:** Near larger forms, time moves viscously. Bring at least three independent mechanical timepieces—mechanical, not digital—worn on different parts of your body. If they disagree, you are in a temporal thickening zone. Leave immediately.

### IV. Psychological Preparation: The Silence and the Bend

- **No touching without protection:** The surfaces are weathered (2.1 erosion) into sharp, brittle geometries. Wear abrasion-resistant, sealed suits—no exposed skin. The crystals may be cool and dry, but they can slice through standard fabric like glass.
