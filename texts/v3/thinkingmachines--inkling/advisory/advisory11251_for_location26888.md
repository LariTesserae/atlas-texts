Before you pack for this vault, understand what you are actually entering: not a cave, not a ruin, but a **sealed moment of absolute equilibrium**. It is small enough to cross in minutes, yet so densely layered with life, machinery, and sharp fresh rock that it feels infinite. There is no weather, no decay, no narrative—only a hot, dry, loud stasis where thousands of hard-shelled bodies cover every surface like living grout, and a single gear turns imperceptibly in the east wall, forever. You will not be exploring; you will be inserting yourself, briefly and anomalously, into a system that has never needed change. Prepare accordingly.

---

### **1. The Suit: Sealed, Cooled, and Cut-Proof**

The air is oven-dry and thick with suspended metallic dust and the constant exhalation of machinery. Your skin and lungs will begin desiccating almost immediately.

- **Wear a fully sealed environmental suit** with integrated humidified respiration—not just a filter mask, but a closed-loop rebreather that recycles your exhaled moisture back into your airway. Ordinary lungs are not built for air that remembers no water.
- **Active cooling is mandatory.** The chamber is sustained at a steady industrial warmth; in a sealed suit you will overheat within minutes. A micro-climate cooling vest or liquid-circulation layer is essential.
- **Abrasion and puncture resistance everywhere.** The floor, walls, and ceiling are ribbed with fresh mineral spurs and fractured black salt and dark ceramic. There are no rounded edges. Your suit should be rigid-panelled at knees, elbows, shoulders, and especially the crown of your head—the ceiling is only about twelve meters up, and sharp spurs hang downward.
- **Gloves:** Thick, cut-resistant, with tactile feedback. You may need to brace yourself against walls that slice, and you will be touching—or being touched by—layered shells.

### **2. Footwear: For Walking on Living Grout**

Every surface is saturated with fauna: click-shells, scaled dust-feeders, vibration-drinkers, arranged in near-contacting strata. They do not move away. They are already exactly where they belong.

- **Rigid, high-ankled boots with ceramic or metal plate soles.** You will be stepping onto—or crushing through—layers of mineral-coated chitin analogs that fill the gaps like sharp mortar. Standard boots will be pierced by ceramic fractures or shell edges.
- **Wide, stable tread.** The floor is embedded with tracks that lead nowhere and pipes that end in darkness. There is no soil to grip, only hard, uneven geology and living sediment.
- Move slowly. Though the creatures are in stasis, a sudden fall could send you sliding through sharp mineral layers or into an exhaling pipe.

### **3. Hearing: Surviving the Drone**

The sound is not chaotic; it is a perfect, unvarying, low-frequency architecture that fills your ribs. Silence is physically impossible here.

- **Infrasound-dampening ear protection.** Ordinary foam plugs will not stop the saturation you feel in your bones. You need active low-frequency cancellation or specialized dampeners.
- **However:** Do not deafen yourself completely. Beneath the drone are sharper textures—clicking, the flutter of hard parts, the hum of current through embedded conduits. These sounds provide your only spatial cues in a space where shadows never move. Use bone-conduction headsets or filtered communication gear that suppresses the drone’s damage but preserves directional audio.
- **Psychological prep:** Your brain expects auditory privacy. Here, even your own heartbeat may seem to sync with the machine-song. Practice mental anchoring before entry. The absence of dynamic sound—no rise, no fall, no urgency—can induce dissociation.

### **4. Eyes and Light: Navigating Sullen Darkness**

Illumination is scarce and sickly: faint orange glows from buried conduits, sparse green phosphorescence clinging to the crystal lichens and wire-vines.

- **Bring your own broad-spectrum headlamp—amber or clean white—adjustable and wide-beamed.** The green glow is too sparse and too biological to trust; it may mark organisms that prefer heat concentration, not safe paths.
- **Eye protection with dust seals.** The metallic suspension is constant. Goggles also protect against hanging mineral spurs when you look upward.
- **Mark your path with a distinct color.** Everything in the vault glows orange or green. Carry chemical light sticks or reflective tape in blue or violet to mark your entry point and any turns, since the fragments—gear, rusted control frame, dead tracks—are your only landmarks, and they do not move or change.

### **5. Hydration and Supplies: You Are the Only Variable**

There is no chemistry for liquid water here. The atmosphere holds none, and none will appear.

- **Carry far more water than the small distance suggests.** The dry heat and low-frequency vibration cause subtle, constant dehydration. Use a hydration bladder with a sealed bite-valve; do not open your suit to drink.
- **Bring no food that requires moisture or produces waste.** This is not a picnic site. Pack dense, dry caloric reserves, and assume you will carry all waste out—there is no ecosystem to absorb it, only perfect equilibrium that should not be burdened with your biology.
- **First aid:** Cuts will happen. Pack antiseptic, closure strips, and pressure bandages. Wounds in hot, dry, metallic air heal poorly and are vulnerable to the sharp ceramic fragments that cover every handhold.

### **6. Navigation: A Bounded Moment with No Horizon**

The space is intimate—walkable in minutes—but the density makes it feel compressed and endless.

- **Orient by the fragments.** The great gear is half-emerged from the east wall; it turns imperceptibly and serves as your cardinal point. The ghost control frame, rusted into permanence with dials locked at single values, lies elsewhere—memorize its position relative to your entry. The tracks embedded in the floor lead nowhere; do not follow them expecting an exit.
- **Your entry point is your exit.** You descended “just beneath the skin.” There are no doors, no slopes away, no horizons promising escape. Mark the descent point with your distinct-colored beacon immediately upon arrival.
- **Move deliberately.** The boundaries are definite—walls that do not open, a floor that does not slope, a ceiling that holds. You cannot get lost in distance, but you can become disoriented by the uniformity of sharp edges and layered life.

### **7. Interaction Protocol: Do Not Break the Stasis**

This is not a ruin decaying; it is geology functioning without purpose. The technology runs without change, sustaining the warmth that allows the dense ecology to exist in frozen balance.

- **Do not touch the gear.** It is turning imperceptibly in place, unworn, eternal in its youth. Your hand on it might be the first intentional change this vault has experienced.
- **Do not collect samples.** The fauna are distributed exactly in equilibrium—thousands per cubic meter, covering one another like scales. Removing a layer, however small, introduces absence into a system that has never known it.
- **Avoid altering heat or sound locally.** The organisms drink vibration and live by heat gradients that never shift. Your body heat and equipment noise are anomalies; keep movement minimal and avoid clustering near the sparse wire-vines or metallic moss, which grow only where heat concentrates.
- **Respect the pipes.** Some run into darkness and end in nothing, exhaling warm dry gas. Do not block them, and do not stand in the flow—whatever they release is part of the equilibrium.

### **8. Psychological Armor: Abundance Without Flourishing**

You will be overwhelmed not by danger but by **presence**. Every cubic meter is saturated. You will stand ankle-deep in shells that do not acknowledge you, surrounded by a drone that denies silence, in a space where time does not erode the sharp mineral edges. Nothing decays. Nothing grows beyond its fixed measure. There is no story here—no before, no after, only this.

- **Prepare for existential crowding.** You will be physically small against the density. The shells do not move away because they are already perfect.
- **Accept that you cannot change anything.** The vault is a moment frozen in industrial warmth. Your presence is temporary; its stasis is absolute. Do not seek purpose in the machinery, growth in the crystal lichen, or interaction in the fauna.
- **Travel with a partner, but stay within sight.** Verbal communication is useless against the drone. Use pre-arranged hand signals. If you experience dissociation or sensory overload, your partner must be able to guide you physically back to the marked exit.

### **9. Emergency Exit Strategy**

- **Keep your beacon visible.** If your light fails, the sullen orange and sick green glow will not guide you; they only reveal the layered life pressing in.
- **If disoriented, stop.** The space is small enough that random walking will eventually bring you to a wall. Use your suit’s tactile sensors or a short physical tether to trace back to your marked entry rope.
- **Ascend immediately if your suit’s cooling or respiration fails.** The warmth and dust are relentless but not instantly lethal—unless you are trapped in a sealed environment without moisture recycling or heat exchange.

---

**In summary:** Dress for a hot, sharp, underwater-less factory made of glass and shell, where the air is metal-flavored dust and the only music is eternal. Protect your ears without deafening yourself, cool your body in a sealed shell, bring your own water and your own light, mark your path in a color that does not exist here, and walk slowly through the living grout without expecting anything to move for you. You are visiting a cathedral of perfect, crowded, unending now. Do not try to wake it. Just survive it, observe it, and leave it exactly as you found it—frozen, loud, and complete.
