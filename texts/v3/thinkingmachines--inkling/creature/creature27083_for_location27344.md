They are not born here; they are assembled, like everything else—layered into being by the same patient hands that knotted the salt-fiber seams and pressed the mineral felt flat. You find them first in the undergrowth, hundreds upon hundreds, each no larger than a human palm, though “palm” is a measure from elsewhere and applies here only loosely, since distance itself is negotiable. Their bodies are soft, yielding, lacking the brittle architecture of bone or the wet urgency of blood. Instead they are sheathed: each one wrapped in a shell of woven dry fiber, the same material that binds the walls, so that they appear less like animals and more like small, mobile knots—living stitches that have learned to wander.

Some crawl. They move through the dense rosettes on the ceramic wool floor with the sluggish grace of things that have never needed to escape, never needed to chase. Their undersides are slightly adhesive, leaving no stain, no trace of moisture—only a faint, temporary flattening of the rubbery leaves beneath them, as if the vegetation yields to their weight with recognition. Others do not crawl at all. They hang suspended in the vine-curtains, rotated slowly by air currents so faint they barely qualify as breath. In suspension they look like dry seeds caught in honey that has forgotten its wetness, turning millimeter by millimeter over months, their shells gently rasping against the fiber-lattices with a sound like paper remembering fire.

But it is the faces that undo the simple idea of creature. They are present—arranged in patterns that feel intentional rather than evolved—yet they do not express in the way faces usually do. The mouth is not a mouth but a hinge, a functional opening that parts along a seam of darker fiber, widening only when the being draws near to a leaf, not to bite or tear, but to stand in proximity and feed. There is no tearing. The vegetation dims; the being rotates slightly; then it moves on, leaving the leaf intact but subtly less green, as if light itself had been gently borrowed. The eyes, when they can be found, are set in configurations that suggest architecture rather than biology: pairs arranged like small doors, or single apertures placed where a latch might sit, and they do not blink with wet urgency but open and close with the slow, dry precision of mechanisms settling into alignment. They see, or they register, or they align—whatever they do, they do it with an awareness that is not intelligence, not instinct, but something closer to instruction. They move as if following directions written in a language that has not yet been spoken aloud, and sometimes, when the space shifts—when a wall lengthens imperceptibly or a shadow pools upward in dry spirals—they adjust their paths not with surprise but with the calm correction of something that knew the map was never fixed.

They are silent, almost entirely. No call, no song, no warning. The only sounds they contribute are incidental: the click of a joint in their woven shells, the faint rustle as a suspended one turns and its fiber body grazes a vine, the barely-audible settling of a hinge-mouth as it closes after feeding. Even these sounds are swallowed by the dense green and the padded surfaces, so that their presence becomes a visual abundance rather than an auditory one—layered one behind another, patient, dark-green against dark-green, until the small space feels crowded with living, yet remains utterly quiet.

They vary in small ways. Some are more crawler than hanger, their shells flattened on one side like pressed flowers. Others are nearly spherical, rotating in place near the floor, feeding only on the lowest, oldest leaves that have begun to dull to brown-grey. A few cling to the walls themselves, adhering with the same dry adhesion as the vines, their shells pressed flat against the woven salt-fiber, motionless for spans long enough to seem like part of the construction—until one hinge opens, slowly, and you realize it has been waiting not for prey but for proximity, for the right density of warmth and light to justify a single, minimal movement.

There is no hierarchy visible, no leader among the hundreds, but there is coordination in the slowness. They do not cluster randomly; they distribute themselves through the vegetation in patterns that echo the seams of the shell above—radial, repeating, as if they understand the geometry of the place better than any map could show. They avoid the brightest patches of filtered light, preferring the deeper green shadow where the vines knot thickest. They do not sleep, exactly—there is no collapse, no unconsciousness—rather they enter long phases of stillness, suspended or curled, their hinge-mouths closed, their door-like eyes shut, present but withdrawn, conserving the purpose they were given or discovered.

And they are recent, like the walls. Their shells show no wear from weather—no erosion, no staining—only the fresh, bright cuts of fiber where primitive tools shaped them, or where they shaped themselves in imitation. They do not decay; nothing here decays quickly. The sharp edges of their construction remain sharp. The young formation holds them with stubborn pride. They are part of the made world, not intruders into it, perhaps its intended inhabitants, perhaps its purpose made mobile: a sealed, low, warm hollow filled with rootless green, tended by silent woven beings who feed without tearing, who navigate unstable space with calm correction, who wait.

In the thick, slow time of this place, they are always in the process of becoming more themselves. A vine extends downward; a being turns beneath it. A shadow falls wrong, then evaporates upward; the beings near it shift their positions by millimeters, aligning to a reality that has already changed. They do not rush. They do not fear. They exist in abundance, layered, silent, functional-faced and soft-bodied, moving through the lush dry green with the patience of things that have never known thirst, never known urgency, and perhaps never needed to be named—only made, placed, and left to wait in the warm, waterless dark, growing slowly, entirely strange, entirely here.
