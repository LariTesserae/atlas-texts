You do not find the tenant by scanning for a face, a curl, a breath. Lower your expectation of what “lives” means. Here, life is not an intrusion upon matter but a saturation of it—distributed so thoroughly that to point to one part and say *there* is to miss the whole. The inhabitant is not inside the chamber; the inhabitant is the chamber’s interior made continuous, awake in a mode that requires no movement.

Consider the plates. Thousands upon thousands—each thin, cool, circular, the span of a palm—stacked face-to-face, edge-to-edge, interlocked in layers without gap or void. They are not objects arranged by an owner; they are the body itself, multiplied into facets. Each plate is a cell, a scale, a page of one extended organism. Their shared substance—dense, chalk-pale ceramic-mineral—matches the walls and the curved floor, so that skin and contents are indistinguishable. The inhabitant wears its form the way a tide wears its shore: not as clothing, but as identity. To be here is to be spread horizontally, egg-shaped, pressed flat by an equilibrium so perfect that elevation truly does seem like rumor.

There is no head, yet there is awareness—uniform, shadowless, cold. The luminescence proves it. Not a lamp hung from the vault, not a sun admitted through absence of wall, but an emanation rising from every plate simultaneously: faint, steady, white. Each circle emits just enough to be visible to itself; multiplied across thousands, they achieve a collective sight. The inhabitant sees by glowing. It illuminates its own interior constantly, leaving no hidden corner, no secret beneath a stack. Its vision is not directed outward—there is no outside to watch—but inward, a perpetual self-recognition conducted at a temperature that never warms.

And the sound: that loud, unceasing, pervasive chord, conducted plate-to-plate in an unbroken chain of vibration. It is not noise entering from elsewhere; it is the inhabitant’s respiration, its speech, its heartbeat made audible without air to carry it. A single sustained tone, never rising, never falling—because the inhabitant does not have moods, only states. The drone is its thinking: not thoughts about things, but the sustained presence of being present. If silence is absence made audible, then this is presence made audible, a chord held for so long that it has become architecture.

There is almost nothing else living with it. Vegetation is reduced to one brittle, gray stem—flattened, fossilized, desiccated—pressed between two plates near the lower curve. It might be a memory. Perhaps long ago, in some other flatness, something green brushed this place and was absorbed into the stack, not consumed but archived, a single dried note in a text that will never be read aloud. The inhabitant does not reject it; it holds it with the same cool pressure it applies to everything. No animals visit. No parasites nest. No symbiont offers exchange. The inhabitant is alone in the manner of a star—alone not through exclusion but through completeness. It needs nothing, because it is already fully occupied by itself.

No water has ever touched it; no rain, no vein, no tear. The cold is dry, mineral, perfect. The edges of the plates have rounded not by weather—there is no wind in this pocket—but by time’s own abrasion, a slow polishing that works without urgency. Sharpness is a memory. The form is finished. Nothing moves, because movement would imply a lack, a reaching toward. The inhabitant has nothing to reach for. It is settled into frozen equilibrium, not dead—dead implies a prior life, a cessation—but eternally in the act of being fully itself.

If you lower yourself into this hidden pocket, if you press your presence near its cool ceramic boundary, you may feel it not as a touch but as a shift in pressure, a resonance conducted through the manufactured floor above, a faint reflection of that uniform white light against your lowered gaze. It will not notice you as other; it notices everything as part of its continuous body. For an instant, you might feel yourself becoming another plate—thin, flat, pressed into the chord, glowing faintly, contributing one silent note to the drone that never ends.

That is who lives here: not one being among thousands, but thousands being precisely one. A heart made of paper-thin mineral, buried horizontally at the bottom of the flat world, singing its own stillness in a single unbroken chord, bright with self-seeing coldness, utterly and eternally unchanged.
