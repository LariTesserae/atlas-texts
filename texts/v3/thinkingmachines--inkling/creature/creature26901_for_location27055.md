What lives here does not distinguish itself from the place; it has become the place’s metabolism, its permanent weather.

The machinery is the first and most faithful resident. It occupies the broad, scattered factory blocks and the narrow control towers not as tools left behind, but as organisms that never learned to sleep. Generators exhale a steady thermal breath through vent stacks; conduits throb with pressurized blood; valves hiss in the same rhythm they have always kept. There is no shift change, no maintenance that alters the rust’s precise location, no hand that advances or retreats the faded paint. The engines are not breaking down, nor are they being repaired—they exist in a state of continuous, identical operation, loud and unwavering, a chord without resolution. They are part of the system, and the system is them.

Beneath the brittle concrete lips and within the warm channels under the floors, rats maintain their number with absolute fidelity. They do not breed into excess; they do not diminish. The same populations move through the same dark routes, sustained by the constant heat and the weeping moisture, never migrating away because there is no elsewhere worth reaching. They are not intruders in an abandoned settlement; they are indigenous to the equilibrium.

Above ground, in the dim amber-brown twilight between structures, the air is never empty. Thick swarms of black-winged beetles and heat-loving moths hang in unmoving clouds, their wings generating a continuous, papery rustle that does not swell or fade. They do not land in new patterns; they do not disperse. Their abundance is locked, a living haze that hovers in the same densities, feeding on whatever the stagnant warmth produces, returning generation after generation to the identical postures of flight.

At the edges of the shallow, mirror-still pools—locked forever at their perfect level—small, pale amphibians rest. They do not jump, do not hunt with variation, do not cluster differently from hour to hour. They sit at the water’s margin in identical postures, adapted fully to the heat and the machinery’s drone, as much a fixture as the rust on the rails.

Even the nearly absent vegetation belongs to this census. Black reeds cling only to the wettest margins, and dark moss survives in cracks where water seeps through pavement. They do not spread; they do not wither. They exist in frozen abundance, neither thriving nor dying, marking the boundary between liquid earth and built surface without ever crossing it.

There are no people visible in the scattered density—no footprints that accumulate, no voices against the industrial chord—yet the control towers stand, observing nothing and being observed by nothing. The settlement feels half-inhabited not because anyone has left, but because the inhabitants have dissolved into function: the rats in the warmth below, the insects in the air between, the amphibians at the water, the moss in the cracks, and the machines themselves, running at identical speed forever.

Everything here lives in the same condition of stasis. Movement is cyclical rather than progressive. The water drips at one rhythm, the wings rustle at one pitch, the generators hum at one depth. To live in this small, sharp-edged bowl is not to grow or decline, but to persist—exact, unchanging, and entirely at home in the never-ending now.
