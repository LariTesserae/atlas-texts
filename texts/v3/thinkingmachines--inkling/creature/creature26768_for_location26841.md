What lives here does not announce itself; it is revealed only as an error in the turbulence, a wrongness in the sediment cloud that does not disperse on schedule.

They are rare—so rare that the valley’s long silences contain no expectation of them. When one is present, it is not “seen” so much as inferred: a pale, elongated disturbance dragging slowly along the interlocking stone plates of the floor, moving not with fins or propulsion but with a kind of deliberate heaviness, as if the creature and the saturated water share the same mass and must negotiate every shift together. Their bodies are not streamlined for swimming; they are flattened, broad, almost geological in outline, pressed close to the basalt mosaic by the weight of the column above. Skin—if the word applies—is translucent in the way of deep things, not luminous but emptied, allowing the faint 0.4 darkness to pass through them rather than reflect. They appear bone-colored, mineral-stained near the joints, and they carry no bioluminescence of their own; when they disturb the sediment, the only light is that accidental mineral flare, catching for a moment on a ridge of shoulder or flank before dying.

Their metabolism belongs to the temperature: glacial, viscous, counting time in the slow roll of thermal currents. They do not chase. They do not swarm. Along the flat, ancient floor—worn smooth over eons into that town-sized expanse of interlocked rock—they crawl or slide in paths that follow the subtle thermal seams, where the sparse chemosynthetic stalks cluster and the thin films of cold-adapted algae cling to raised edges. Near these seeps, they pause for long intervals, feeding not with visible mouths but with broad, ventral surfaces, absorbing or scraping at the bacterial films and the stalk-bases with a patience that outlasts the storm-cycles around them. Then they move on. They do not gather at the seeps; they visit alone, separated by distances that make each encounter with another of their kind a statistical impossibility across a human lifespan.

Their form suggests manual capacity without the language of hands as we understand them. Broad fore-limbs—flattened, callused, edged with dense cartilage—end in digits thick enough to grip stone in a world where everything else is suspended mud. These are the makers of the scattered artifacts. Not builders—there are no walls, no shelters, no accumulation of material—just intermittent, solitary craft. A flaked basalt edge, shaped not for beauty but for the specific grip of those thick fingers. A heavy object worn smooth everywhere except one deliberate depression, fitted to a palm. Lines scratched into rock with harder rock, not messages but traces of passage: I was here, I passed, I left this weight behind because I could not carry it through the turbulence. The marks are low, near the floor, half-buried and then revealed again by the shifting sediment, as temporary as the beings themselves intend.

They do not speak. At 0.4 on the scale of sound, communication—if it exists—happens below the threshold of hearing: vibrations transmitted through the packed water, felt in the body rather than heard, or perhaps not at all. They may be solitary by nature, or the valley may be too large and the food too sparse to support proximity. What is clear is that they do not defend territory, do not court visibly, do not bury their dead—there are no dead, only wear, only the slow abrasion of suspended rock against rock and the bodies that eventually become part of the sediment cloud.

In the turbulence, they endure. Their broad, flat shapes allow them to hold the stone floor when thermal currents roll through in crushing spirals, anchoring by weight rather than grip, letting the hyperactive storm pass over and through them. They are part of the wear, part of the settled 2.8 erosion, part of the absence of emptiness. They live at the speed of the valley: not dead, not dormant, but aligned with a cold that refuses warmth and a darkness that does not need to be black to be absolute. They pass through the open, flat abyss, leaving only what hands of cartilage and patience have shaped, then vanish back into the thick, suspended mass—pale disturbances that resolve, in the end, into nothing but the silence that was always there.
