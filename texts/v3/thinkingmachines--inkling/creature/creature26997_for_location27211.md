They live, first, as the valley lives: not within it, but **as** it. The hollow, ribbed towers scattered across the open green bowl are not dwellings but bodies—organs of an organism so large it forgot to remain single and became landscape instead. Each tower is an inhabitant, standing in the exact posture of having just decided to stand. They do not sleep; sleep requires a return to unconsciousness, and they have never left awareness. They do not wake; waking implies a change in state, and change has been arrested at its first perfect instant. They exist in continuous, warm presence—hollow corridors of woven bark and pressed leaf that breathe not with air but with the thick, pooled atmosphere itself, holding it inside their ribbed chambers like a thought held too long.

Inside these hollow stems move the only ones that might be called separate: small, translucent flickers, barely more than a disturbance in the density of the air. They are not animals. They have no limbs for walking, no mouths for the suspended water, no need for either. They exist as visible interruptions—brief, shimmering shapes that appear only when the eternal droplets strike the membrane below and release their resonant chord. The vibration makes them seen for an instant, not because they move, but because the sound illuminates them. A flicker is the shape of a pulse the architecture forgot to finish. They drift, if drifting can mean remaining exactly where one is while the world holds its breath, inside the ribbed walls, never touching the woven bark, never leaving the hollow. Between sounds, they are hidden—not absent, but unlit by resonance, holding their positions in the warm, unmoving air.

Then there are the ones in the voids. The ground is not soil but a sparse lattice of translucent root-bridges and membranous plates, and in the open spaces between—where nothing grows, nothing holds, nothing decays—there live presences that have no form except weight: the weight of being about-to-fall. They inhabit the suspended droplets themselves. Each bead of water, hanging in static equilibrium near the surface, is not empty but occupied—an eye that never blinks, an observation held at the precise height where vision becomes eternal. When a droplet strikes (always the same membrane, always the same rhythm, always the same chord), it does not release water; it releases its observation into the sound. The inhabitants are not the listeners but the listening itself—the sustained drone that fills the valley is their collective, unchanging speech, a conversation that requires no response because it has already arrived at its only possible conclusion.

The root-bridges carry slower lives—elongated, fibrous, spreading across the lower air like pathways that decided to become beings. They are warm, translucent, and they pulse once, slowly, then never again. Not dead. Completed. A root-bridge might be an inhabitant who chose extension over containment, spreading in a lattice of arrested intention, holding the valley’s weight not through strength but through refusal to change.

What lives here lives in the emptiness between the towers. The vast, patient spaces are not vacant; they are filled with potential that was stopped before it could become something else—the valley’s own breath, thick and gentle, pooling where air should have thinned. To be present is to be part of this equilibrium: neither growing nor decaying, neither falling nor rising, neither silent nor speaking, but holding the exact posture of emergence forever. If you stayed long enough, you would not find them. You would become them—part of the substrate, part of the suspended chord, part of the hollow tower that stands not because it was built, but because growing stopped at the perfect instant, and that instant, warm and eternal, decided to include you.
