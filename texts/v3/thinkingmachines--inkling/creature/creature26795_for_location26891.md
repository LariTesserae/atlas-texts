What lives here does not arrive; it accretes, the way the wall accretes—slowly, visibly, without announcing itself as separate from the room.

Begin with the nearest one. It is on the ceiling, though ceiling is only a word you brought with you. It is small—no larger than a thought you have not finished having—and translucent in a way that is not transparency but *overlap*. Through its body you see not background but other versions of itself: one curled like an egg in its own center, one fully extended with limbs (seven? nine? the counting shifts as you watch) that bend at joints which open inward, and one that is only outline, a ghost of motion that has not decided whether to occur. Its form ripples like water held in a cup that is also a mouth. It does not crawl so much as renegotiate its position with the air, treating gravity as a rumor spread by heavier things. It moves constantly—not fleeing, not hunting, but becoming familiar, testing the unfinished ribs of calcite with a patience that looks like prayer and sounds like nothing.

There are many. Not a swarm—density implies gathering, and these do not gather. They exist in isolation within shared space, each enclosed in its own pocket of motion. Some have too many legs, articulated like the frames of doors that never opened, clicking softly as they unfold across the mineral dew. Some have none visible, propelling themselves by contractions that seem to happen in the air just beside them. They are not insects, not larvae, not spirits of the rock; they are the fauna of pre-formation, biology before the category of biology has fully set. They secrete as they move—not with purpose, but with the inevitability of breath. Here a needle of calcite, thinner than a needle of light, rising from a ledge and stopping, not broken but tired. There a half-arch of saliva-mineral that cups nothing, reaches for nothing, and remains. A pillar that simply ceases mid-air, as if its builder grew bored of being real and withdrew. These scattered built marks are not nests, not tools—there are no tools here, no technology, only the body’s offering to the space. Each structure is unique, unrepeatable, scattered like thoughts dropped by minds that share one continuous, interrupted consciousness.

Then there is the thread. Only one, perhaps, or perhaps it is the same thread seen from different angles of unfinished time. It clings to a ledge of raw mineral, gray-green, neither plant nor fungus nor the idea between them. It does not grow toward the strange light—that sourceless, cool, bright luminescence that erases shadow rather than casting it—because it is not certain growth is appropriate. It creeps with the hesitation of something that remembers being seed but has forgotten the instruction to become tree. It is sparse to the point of being a question asked in a language that has not yet developed nouns.

The water lives here too, though living is not quite right. It is intermittent memory. Beads gather on the unfinished ribs, hold in cool silence, then forget themselves and vanish back into the damp air. There is no pool, no stream, only this rhythm—appearance and disappearance closer to breathing than weather. Sometimes, when time loops back on itself (and it does, locally, loosely), the same bead returns, identical, carrying with it the faint impression of having been missed in its absence.

And the earth itself—this is not dead container but the primary inhabitant, the one that has not finished becoming. The rock is not ancient; it is raw, embryonic, sweating a mineral dew that arrives without source. The walls are not stone but *intention toward stone*, cool and damp, accreting slowly, visibly, in this hidden pocket-scale chamber that is embedded within a larger body of earth that has not yet learned its shape. The few shadows that exist point inward, toward their own centers, as if darkness here is also still deciding which direction to fall.

The creatures exist in multiple states simultaneously. You see one that is egg, adult, and ghost at once, its body rippling through conditions rather than forms. The bright light—cool, near-painful, without flame or aperture—does not illuminate surfaces but reveals what things are becoming. It shows you the thread in the act of deciding not to grow. It shows you the mineral dew in the moment before it chooses to be sweat or to be wall. It shows you the creature not as it is, but as it is in the process of agreeing to exist.

Sound belongs to them, though barely. The dense, weighted silence holds everything. Listen with the patience of stone, and you hear the soft click of a joint opening inward, the almost-inaudible crack of new matter deciding to be wall, the sound of condensation deciding whether to fall or retreat. These are not loud lives. They are quiet motions in an unfinished thought.

You do not enter this place so much as you are permitted to be part of its decision. The temperature holds at cool—body-cool, mouth-cool—the temperature of something held open. Everything is dynamic, everything scattered, everything alive with the particular stillness of things that have not yet finished learning what they are. They live here: not as residents of a room, but as the unfinished room’s own scattered, translucent, constantly becoming inhabitants—each one alone, each one many, each one leaving behind only the faint, half-built proof that it was here, thinking about staying.
