What lives there are not creatures in the usual sense, but suspended questions made of membrane and breath.

They hang—hundreds, perhaps thousands—within the pillar of light, though “hang” implies weight, and these have almost none. Each is the size of an open palm, a flattened, translucent bell or veil of tissue so thin it reads as a tear in the air itself. Their bodies are gel and gas held together by some patience of surface tension, bodies that are mostly hollow, mostly absence given outline. In the golden-white column they appear to glow from within, not with their own light but with the borrowed radiance passing straight through them, illuminating internal structures like frost patterns on glass: hair-thin filaments, delicate as spider silk, that might be veins or wings or something older, something that never needed a name. They do not fly. They drift, or rather they are drifted—moved by the faintest thermal breath of the chamber, by the rising warmth from the shallow pools, by currents so subtle that to watch one for an hour is to see it shift only the distance of a finger’s width, turning slowly, like a thought turning over.

They feed without urgency. Near the pools, where the warm water gathers in shallow mirrors, they lower themselves—if lowering is the right word for something with no weight—until their undersides, delicate as wet paper, almost touch the surface. There they drink the moisture, not with mouths but through the whole of their bodies, absorbing the condensation, the mineral dust that glitters in the light, the very thinness of the air itself. They are sustained by scarcity. Their abundance is a kind of emptiness multiplied: so many, and yet so insubstantial that the chamber remains empty, the space between them vast and untouched.

Sometimes they gather near the broken quartz at the edges, not in crowds but in loose, dissolving patterns. They rest upon the sharp flakes, or arrange them with a slowness that makes intention impossible to confirm—moving one stone a fraction, then pausing for what might be days, then another. The patterns never complete themselves. They begin like nests, like maps, like rituals half-remembered, then dissolve as the creatures drift away, leaving the quartz scattered, primitive, unclaimed by any true design.

They make sound, but barely. The soft tick of one membrane brushing another in the still air. The faint, distant plink—not of them, but of water falling into the pools, a rhythm they seem to listen to, or perhaps cause, in their slow passage. They do not call to one another. There is no song, no swarm intelligence, only the shared patience of beings that exist more like weather than like animals—present everywhere, acting nowhere, changing the chamber not by what they do but by the quiet fact of being there, translucent and waiting.

Higher up, near the vault where the walls curve with impossible smoothness, hang the other lives: thin, translucent threads—roots, or fungal breath—pale as bone, reaching downward like drawn-out sighs. Near the slit, where the pillar strikes the floor, a few dry grasses have rooted in the white sand, fragile, persistent, almost an afterthought. And in the darker margins, where the light does not quite reach, the creatures are paler still, nearly invisible, ghost-versions of the ones in the column, as if the hollow produces them in gradations, lighter near the sun, darker near the earth.

Together they form an ecology of near-nothing: bodies of glass and gel, roots of silk, water that falls like hesitation, stones broken by time alone. They are not intelligent, not conscious in any way the world above would recognize. But they are strange—strange in the way the walls feel grown rather than eroded, strange in the way time stretches to accommodate their slowness, strange in the way they seem made of the same substance as the light itself. They are the hollow’s breath made visible, its slow metabolism, its patient, hollow heart—living not in the place, but as the place, suspended between falling and being held, in the bright, empty, warm dark below.
