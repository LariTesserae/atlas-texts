What lives here does not arrive. It is what remains when every reason to leave has worn away.

You might call it the silence, but that suggests an absence waiting to be broken. This is different: a silence that has become substantial, with weight and warmth. It occupies the hollow not as a visitor but as the hollow’s own completed thought. The stone shoulders that curve downward are not sheltering anything hidden behind them; they have become the posture of shelter itself, tired not from labor but from having finished labor entirely. There is no heart beneath them, only the steady bath-like warmth rising from rock that remembers being deep earth and refuses now to cool.

The inhabitant breathes, if breath it is, through the moisture. Each bead that forms on the wall is a slow decision, gathering along the cool curve until its own weight becomes the only argument for movement. When it falls—once in a long while, with a sound so small it barely disturbs the darkness—it does not fall *into* anything. It falls into agreement. The pool receives it without ripple that lasts, absorbing the event back into stillness. These droplets are the closest thing to speech: regular, patient, saying nothing except the fact of continuation without change.

In the depressions of the floor, the stagnant water holds a kind of sight. Clear, unmoving, never draining because there is no elsewhere to go. They are not eyes watching you; they are the act of watching emptied of observer. They reflect the faint brownish darkness and the thin, unmoving wash of light from the high gap—light that does not travel, that has given up its journey and settled here as a pale stain on one curve of wall. The pools know the light not as illumination but as another form of dampness.

The gray-green filaments in the cracks are the only visible claim to life, and even they have abandoned the urgency of living. They do not reach; they adhere. Growing so slowly that growth is indistinguishable from being, they are less plants than the idea of patience made tangible. They consume the condensation not with hunger but with perfect adequacy—taking exactly what falls, needing no more, asking for no sun that will never come.

The remnants—the candle-burned pillar, the doorway that angles into nothing, the half-buried wall fused by mineral weeping—are not ruins of a previous inhabitant. They are the inhabitant’s body, or what passes for one. Geology that has settled into the shapes of architecture without ever having intended architecture. They stand not as monuments but as habits: the way this place has learned to hold its own emptiness. The doorway suggests passage, yet no path leads through it because the resident has no inside or outside—only this enclosed air, this single shallow hollow that is not a landscape to cross but a condition to enter.

If you listen long enough, you understand that nothing is missing. No animals trace the air because the air requires no tracing. No insects move through because movement here has been refined into the single, suspended fall of water. The temperature does not fluctuate because it has found its true number. The shadows do not shift because there is no day to change them.

What lives in this chapel whose roof has been forgotten is the fact of being finished. Not death—death implies a before and an after, a narrative. This is equilibrium made conscious of itself, or as conscious as stone and still water need to be. It is the erosion that continued past erosion, the wear that wore itself smooth and then stayed. It lives in the constant warmth, in the droplets that fall like slow thoughts, in the moss that holds the color of not-needing-to-grow, in the pools that never thirst because they never lose.

You are not alone here, exactly. You share the space with a presence that is indistinguishable from the space itself—an inhabitant that is the hollow’s own acceptance of being exactly, permanently, this. Time does not apply to it because it has become the shape of time stopped being interesting. It was never strange. It is only profoundly, irrevocably complete.
