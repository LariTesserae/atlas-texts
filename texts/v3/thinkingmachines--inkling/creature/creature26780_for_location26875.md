That which lives here does not live *upon* the Velum so much as live *within* its agreement—an agreement older than the rounding of the ridges.

Begin with the ground itself, for it is not ground. The Root-Veil is not merely vegetation but a single, distributed being with no center, no brain in any one place, yet thinking slowly in chemical gradients. It breathes in those long thermal expansions, and what breathes with it are the substrate-dwellers: tiny, translucent organisms—more process than creature—that live inside the fiber-matrix, neither plant nor animal but the negotiators between them. They move through the grey-green weave like thoughts through tissue, carrying mineral messages, repairing fractures in the mat by secreting new lignin-glue, tasting the crystalline vapors that condense on every surface. They are countless and nameless, and they are the true citizens of the open spaces, abundant beyond counting yet invisible unless you lie still and feel the carpet pulse against your back.

Then there are the Stalks. Each Chorus-Stalk is not dead architecture but an elder—ancient, hollow, resonant—whose growth stopped not because of failure but because it reached the end of its necessary form. They are organisms of immense patience. Inside their ceramic-thick walls, in chambers high above the root-carpet, live the Filter-Hangers: beings of long, filamentous bodies, almost weightless, suspended in the circulating vapors. They do not eat in any hungry sense; they absorb. Their hollow bones—echo-chambers evolved or grown—tune themselves to the Stalk’s internal drone, and they feed on the nutrient-mists that spiral upward through the hollows. They hang in clusters like strange fruit, slow-moving, communicating not with voice but with adjustments to their own resonant cavities—changing their internal geometry to alter the sound they return, speaking in harmonics. They are old companions of the Stalks, perhaps symbionts, perhaps descendants of whatever first colonized these towers when they were young and growing. They never descend to the Veil. They live entirely in the vertical world, in the cool, dim air of the hollow, and they migrate between Stalks not by walking—there is no walking for them—but by releasing themselves into thermals of cool vapor, gliding on compressed fields, drifting for days between the cathedral-spaces.

Below, across the carpet, move the Disc-Gliders. These are the ones you might see—if seeing is the right word—crossing the open kilometers. Flat, broad, almost two-dimensional in their resting state, they propel themselves over the Root-Veil by generating fields of compressed air beneath their bodies, sliding like leaves on a silent wind. Their metabolisms are cold, glacial; they feed on mineral exudates that the Veil sweats in its brief flowering events, on spores, on the sound-energy that vibrates through the substrate. They are solitary, mostly, though they gather in the resonance-shadows of the Stalks during the spore-storms. Their bodies are hollow, segmented, producing low-frequency calls that travel through the root-fiber for kilometers—calls that are not language exactly but mapping, announcing presence, announcing the location of bloom-spikes, announcing danger. They are abundant in the ecosystem’s ledger but sparse in any single view; you might travel half a day and see only one, gliding past like a dark, silent plate, leaving the carpet unbroken.

But the settlements—those geometric clusters suspended in harmonic fields, grown from black root-carbon—are inhabited by something more deliberate.

They are not human. They never knew water, never evolved toward it, and their bodies reflect the world’s chemistry. They are cool-skinned, perhaps translucent, with internal structures that circulate viscous saps rather than blood—saps that freeze at temperatures that would only chill a human, but here flow easily in the perpetual cool. Their skeletons, like the fauna’s, are hollow lattices, not for flight but for resonance; they hear with their bones, feeling vibrations through their frames. They are built for listening.

They live in the nodes—archipelagos of black geometric forms that hang or rest upon hardened root-crust. Their buildings are alive, growing slowly, repairing cracks by exuding new fiber, adjusting their tension-cables as the Veil shifts millimeters per century. The inhabitants do not build with tools so much as negotiate with the architecture—singing or vibrating specific frequencies that encourage the root-carbon to grow in desired shapes, guiding walls upward, opening chambers, sealing others. They wear garments not of cloth but of woven crystalline films that regulate temperature and filter the vapors they breathe—vapors that are not air but chemical mists, cool and sweet-tasting, carrying nutrients and messages alike.

Their society is sparse, matching the world. A node might hold only a few hundred, arranged not in dense streets but in distributed clusters—each dwelling separated by open harmonic space, connected not by roads but by resonance-corridors. They travel rarely. Distance is measured not in kilometers but in tonal separation; to reach another node is to enter a different harmonic register. When they do move, they travel in small craft—shells grown from hardened sap, suspended in vibration-fields—that glide silently across the Veil, never touching the root-carpet, carrying messages or travelers through the sound-curridors that link the archipelago.

They are ancient in culture though not necessarily in individual lifespan. They remember the Stalks not as landmarks but as elders, addressing them with specific resonant greetings when they pass. They understand the Root-Veil not as ground but as a vast, slow mind with which they are in constant, subtle negotiation—adjusting their own outputs so they do not interfere with the substrate’s biological rhythms, living within the cycle rather than against it.

In the hollows of the Stalks, near these settlements, there are others—perhaps priests, perhaps engineers, perhaps merely those who have chosen proximity to the elders—who spend their lives listening. They sit in chambers where the Stalk’s drone is strongest, letting the vibrations pass through their hollow bones, and they learn. From this listening they derive their technology: not engines of combustion, but molecular batteries woven into their own bodies and buildings; not electricity through wires, but storage in crystalline lattices; not vision through light—though the bioluminescence of the Veil offers some illumination—but through sensing pressure changes, chemical gradients, resonant echoes.

The world hums, loudly, actively, and they are part of that hum. They do not dominate it; they harmonize with it. The fauna migrates, the Stalks drone, the Veil pulses, the spore-storms roll like weather made of thought, and the inhabitants of the nodes adjust—always adjusting—living in perfect, strange equilibrium with everything that shares this high, flat, ancient place.

To live here is not to conquer but to listen, not to build upon but to grow within. The population is scattered like seeds in a wind that never stops—abundant in total, sparse in presence, immense in the space between them, all of them—root-negotiators, filter-hangers, disc-gliders, and the hollow-boned listeners of the nodes—existing together in the cool, dry, resonant continuation of what the Velum has become.
