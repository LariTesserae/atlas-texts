Here live the ones who have not yet learned to call themselves citizens, though they build with the logic of streets.

In the shallow pools where condensation gathers—those precise, silent droplets—there are amphibians no larger than a knuckle of root, with skin like wet parchment held up to the submerged gold-green light. You see them not by movement but by pulse: a slow, dark rhythm visible through translucent flesh, as if each one contains a small heart that also belongs to the chamber itself. They do not swim. They rest in the moisture, leaving footprints in the curing mud that harden around them like signatures being archived. They drink the falling water, and in drinking they alter the mineral breath of the air, leaving behind traces that the crawling builders will later use as mortar.

The air between the sparse groves is never still; it is traffic. Winged things—delicate, their wings like fragments of the translucent dome made portable—move not in swarms but in lines of quiet industry. Each one bears a load: spores caught in the fine hairs of their legs, fibers torn from hanging vines and rewoven in flight, droplets held in mouthparts that never cease their gentle work. They are messengers and assistants both, delivering the materials of tomorrow’s walls to the places where growth is waiting.

Along the floor, through the empty spaces that let the body move freely, crawl the weavers: segmented, many-legged, blind in any conventional sense, yet guided precisely by the damp chemistry of fresh-formed surfaces. They press root-fiber into the calcified mud, shaping the low archways and the clusters of walls not through intention but through appetite—feeding, excreting, pressing. Their bodies leave trails of secretion that cure within hours, smooth and yielding, still warm.

But the true inhabitants—the ones who dissolve the boundary between resident and residence—are embedded within the structures themselves. They are larger only in comparison, elongated and soft, lacking eyes, voices, or any feature that suggests a face turned toward the world. They dwell inside the walls they have grown, their bodies extending like warm tubes through the secretions that become archways and floors. To call them architects is inaccurate; they are the architecture in its living state. When one contracts—rarely, with a movement too slow for casual sight—the mud around it ripples, the root-weave tightens, and the settlement adjusts its posture, breathing out a new curve. They do not leave. Leaving is a concept that requires an outside, and for them, the wall is skin, the floor is bedding, the pocket is the full extent of being.

The vegetation holds its own citizenship. The vines that hang in loops from the low ceiling reach downward with patient intent, touching the pools, wrapping the archways, feeding on the same mineral air that tastes of birth. Ferns unfurl in scattered groves, each frond a green presence holding droplets in its architecture, contributing shade and moisture without crowding. The moss carpets the ground in deep green patches, soft enough to receive footprints, alive enough to grow over them within a day.

Together they form no hierarchy—only dynamics, constant and gentle. The amphibians pulse in their pools; the winged carriers move spores like unspoken messages; the crawlers weave; the embedded ones grow the town outward from their own mass; the plants hold the humidity and filter the light into its subdued gold-green. There are no predators here, no spectacle of violence, because nothing is wasted. One creature’s excretion becomes the wall that shelters another. One’s breath hardens the mud that becomes another’s floor.

They do not know they are in a pocket. The curved dome is sky enough; the absence of horizon is not a loss but the shape of home. They are born into this dampness, work within it, and dissolve back into it—not into death, but into fiber, into secretion, into the next body that will extend the archway a little further.

The settlement is not inhabited by them.

It is them—moving, secreting, carrying, pulsing—pretending to be a town, and succeeding so completely that there is no distinction left between the builder and the built, between the creature and the chamber that holds its breath as it becomes.
