Here live the Slow Kings, seven visible sovereigns of heat and patience, each a self-moving climate spread across kilometers of black volcanic glass. They are not animals in any hurried sense—their bodies are layered plates of heat-radiant mineral fused with tissue that contracts over days, not heartbeats, and they migrate on paths that take centuries to complete, feeding on the waste heat and subtle fractures in reality vented by the embedded industrial heart beneath them.

This heart is not hidden in buildings—there are none—but fused into the geology itself: vast thermal siphons, blackened-alloy pipes emerging like fossilized roots, and turbines so enormous and slow that one rotation may take a week, yet they never cease. The ground is a compressed sheet of glass and mineral salt, worn over millennia into smooth curves and shallow shadow-bowls, elevated into thin, scorched air that carries the taste of oxidized metal and the relentless upward bleed of heat from the crust. There is no water, almost no vegetation—only traces of silicon crusts like frost—and the atmosphere is too thin and dry to hold the superheated vapor venting constantly into the void.

What dominates is not crowd but absence: a hollow, regional emptiness where presence empties space rather than filling it. The drone is deep, industrial, omnipresent, loud in the chest but not chaotic, the sound of energy being extracted and released back into bedrock in a cycle with no visible origin. Light is steady and normal—pale sky, clear illumination—which only makes the visible wrongness sharper: the horizon folds upward in the east, distant engines stack like transparent cards, shadows fall in impossible directions, and near the turbines time itself stretches with their pace. The landscape is high, flat, open past walking’s limit, pre- or post-biological, and glacial in its dynamics—erosion rounding every sharpness, cracks opening over decades, dead kings becoming part of the black bed across millennia. To be here is to stand small within a balanced, eternal patience that needs neither hurry nor notice to persist.
