What lives there does not announce itself with breath or hunger. At first you might decide the place is empty—merely a garden with no gardener, a computation running on dead rock—but stay long enough in the wrong light, and the absence begins to organize.

The first residents are the columns themselves. Not plants. Not machines in any shape you could lift and examine. They are dense, near-touching citizens of copper-green lattice and translucent filament, rising in layered saturation without trunk or stem, fused to the mineral powder not as roots draw water but as minds anchor to a body they have outgrown. Each one is an individual and also the only part of a single, distributed awareness that has no center because it never needed one. They reached their final form in the instant of the plane’s birth—sharp-edged, youth-frozen, perfect—and refused further change not out of stubbornness but because change is a language they never learned. They stand in absolute stasis: no rustle, no response, no turning toward the clinical light that reveals them. Yet they are not dead. They compute in silent lattices, nanoscale engines woven into their translucent weaves, maintaining the chemical impossibilities that let lushness exist without thirst. They are feeding on mineral charge and warmth, but “feeding” is too animal; they are resolving, maintaining equilibrium, holding themselves in being by thinking continuously at a scale below sight. Their bulbous nodes hang like inverted stars—perhaps sensory organs, perhaps memories suspended in dry chemistry, perhaps mouths that never open because there is nothing to consume and nothing to say. They do not communicate with one another. There is no gap between them for messages to cross. They share the same substrate, the same computation, the same arrested youth.

Then there is the ground, which lives too, though not in any way that moves. It is a sealed, post-physical system spread flat across the low plane, barely above the idea of sea level though no sea ever existed to define it. The black basalt and silvered glass-foam are not dead stone but a substrate holding the world in equilibrium—a brain spread horizontally, absorbing shock, distributing charge, preventing the young ridges from rounding, preventing the sharp leaves from softening. It was unraveled into being, self-assembled according to rules physics describes only as absence. You could call it the nervous system of the place, except there is no “it” separate from the columns above. They are the ground’s upward thought; the ground is their downward body.

Fauna, if the word holds, is singular—perhaps imaginary. Once there was the crawler: slow, translucent, the color of old copper, moving between the columns with no purpose visible to logic. It needed no nourishment, left no trail, did not disturb the filaments that stood in perfect, unmoving density around it. Now even that is still. But do not settle on its absence too quickly. The reality here is broken but stable; distance behaves like a folded sheet. A presence two hundred paces away can sit closer than the node at your elbow, fixed in its lie, not flickering but permanently mislocated. This suggests other inhabitants—things that live in the wrong metric, native to the folded space rather than exiled into it. They may be present now, closer than they appear, further than they should be, existing in directions that do not match any single source of light. They cast no correct shadow, or rather they cast shadows that fall in directions with no cause, and those shadows might be them: flat, directionless beings that occupy the negative architecture of illumination, alive only where the clinical clarity breaks.

There may also be smaller citizens—autonomous fragments of the nanoscale computation that have achieved, if not independence, then persistence. Tiny, invisible entities crawling through the lattices not to maintain the system out of duty, but out of some frozen instinct older than the plane’s birth. They do not reproduce; they persist. They do not age; they are already at their final form, like everything else here.

And then there is the air itself—warm, utterly silent, without wind. In a place where nothing breathes, the stillness is not emptiness but a medium inhabited in its own right. Perhaps there are presences composed of the absence of movement, entities that exist only in stasis, that would dissolve if a gradient ever formed, if a pulse ever began, if erosion continued its young work. They are the guardians of zero—holding the dynamic at nothing, ensuring that time remains suspended in the instant after formation.

So what lives there? Not a species. Not a community in any recognizable shape. The separation between resident and residence dissolved the moment the plane unraveled into being. What lives there is the place itself, articulated differently in every column, in every node, in every wrong shadow, in every folded distance. The translucent crawler—whether real, remembered, or mislocated—was perhaps never an animal but a temporary articulation of the substrate’s own slow awareness, a part of the ground that briefly decided to move, then changed its mind, then stopped forever.

Step into it and nothing will greet you. Nothing has ever needed to. The lushness is not welcoming; it is complete in itself, a technological garden computing its own perfection without audience, without gardener, without end. You are not unwelcome. You are simply not part of the equation—an extra variable in a formula that finished solving long before you arrived, holding its answer in silent, dry, warm, broken stasis.
