What lives here does not yet agree upon its own outline.

In the thick, suspended cold, they drift—not as individuals so much as intentions given temporary membrane. Thousands, yes, but number is misleading; they are fragments of a single metabolic argument spread across the chamber, each one a translucent argument against emptiness. Their bodies are glassy scars, barely thicker than the water they displace, and through them runs a pale, slow circuitry of fluid that is neither blood nor sap but some older negotiation between mineral and intent. They do not cast shadows; they filter light. A builder might be mistaken for a column until its body flexes, exuding a thread of calcified chitin that hardens in the biting cold like a thought turning solid. They work without tool, without plan, only secretion—mucus that remembers silk, minerals that remember bone—raising spires and weaving avenues that dead-end not by mistake but by patience, waiting for the next generation of bodies to continue the corridor from the other side.

There are others who do not build. They thread—scavengers, perhaps, or priests of decomposition—slipping through the gaps between living architecture with bodies narrowed to ribbons, feeding on the fallen husks, on the secretions that never hardened, on the dead whose shells are already being folded into the walls. They move with the same silence, but their motion is different: reactive, searching, leaving chemical signatures that are questions or warnings or invitations pressed into the growing silk. And there are the slow ones—the ones whose bodies are heavier, armored in partial plates, who move not to build or feed but to observe, to wait, to displace water in patterns that others read. Predator and prey are not categories here but temporary roles in a choreography that takes hours to complete, a slow collision of bodies that ends not in violence but in incorporation—one consumed, its minerals redistributed, its cavity becoming a window, its membrane a translucent dome over a new district.

They do not call out. The water takes everything. Instead, they speak in pressure and chemistry: a shift in the current, a change in the mineral breath of the walls, a bioluminescent pulse—blue-green, sickly, intermittent—that passes through a body like a memory of fire. The older structures glow faintly, coated in symbiotic films that are neither plant nor animal but agreement made visible, and in that weak light one can see how the boundary dissolves. A corridor breathes. A pillar drifts half an inch and resumes. The ceiling—if it can be called that—is a thickening mat of tissue still deciding whether to become sky or skin, and sometimes it lowers slightly, not by collapse but by growth, enclosing the space more intimately than before.

The dead do not leave. They become the architecture they served. A builder dies, and its hollow form is not cleared away but sealed, filled with silk, transformed into a chamber or a bridge or a lens through which the dim light fractures. Those who live now inhabit the bodies of those who lived before, walking through streets that are also ribcages, sleeping—if they sleep—inside domes that are also craniums. The vegetation is sparse: dark filaments clinging to the highest spires, tenants in a city built for other metabolisms, absorbing what little thermal difference the decomposition provides.

Time is visible here. Watch long enough, and a spire completes its crown. A dead-end becomes a boulevard as a scavenger’s death is incorporated and hardened. The water circulates not by tide but by the collective urgency of bodies—cold, yes, but moving, carrying nutrients downward and waste upward toward the living ceiling, which receives it and grows thicker, more decisive.

To live here is to be both citizen and material, never fully separate from the wall that holds you, never fully individual because the chemical language of the place runs through your translucent body as easily as through the silk corridors. They are emerging still—not yet what they will be, not yet fully named, building themselves into being one secretion at a time, in the silent, viscous dark, inside the unfinished flank of a world that is only now deciding to become real.
