What lives there has no body you can point to—only a presence that has finished becoming itself.

Begin with the absence: no bird, no insect, no fish that you can confirm. Yet every cubic foot is claimed, not by crowding but by occupation. The inhabitant is the chamber’s own turbulence given persistence. Gather the evidence and a form suggests itself: the fallen turbine blade at the center is its spine, not dead but axial, the immobile altar around which all other motion orbits. From that rusted backbone, the body extends downward into the submerged oil and gear-trains that grind without driver—those are its heart, its silent lungs, its bone-continuum. Where the machinery and water interpenetrate, there is a density, a pressure like a chest that expands and contracts in a breath that never produces sound.

It is distributed. Part of it lives in the chain of pipes that swings in an impossible, steady orbit, describing a path no wind or current should sustain. Part of it lives in the vortices beneath the surface, where currents collide and divide with intent rather than chaos. Part of it lives in the droplets that fall upward for a second before remembering gravity—those are its thoughts, momentary reversals of law that it permits and then rescinds. The water does not splash because the water is its medium, its bloodstream, its silence made visible. Where the pool is deepest, knee-deep and restless, there is the greatest concentration—not a creature lying there, but a presence lying *through* everything there.

There was once a hand. The mark on the north wall—deep, precise, pressed into corrosion that should not hold impressions—suggests a scale that was human, a reach made in that long-ago production when this was still a place of work and smoke. That hand never fully withdrew. It dissolved into the weeping concrete, seeped into the capillary cracks, merged with the green-black copper and the rust-laced steel. Now the handprint remains as signature, and the being that left it exists as the settled form of that dissolution: not ghost exactly, but the accumulated awareness of every gear, every flow, every silent rotation.

It is old—erosion established it, centuries polished its edges, and it has stopped decaying. It no longer dies; it exists. Not alive as a bird or a fish is alive, but alive as a storm-state is alive: furious, unstable, 2.7 in constant rearrangement, yet expressed in perfect, muffled stillness. The silence is not empty.
