What lives here is not a single creature but a refusal of emptiness made flesh—if flesh is the word for something that behaves like tissue and thinks like water.

At first they seem only an extension of the dampness: beads of condensation that have learned to hold their shape too long, strung together into bodies that range from grains of rice to ribbons of wet silk stretched across the bent mesh tube. Translucent, yes—segmented, always—though the segments shift as you watch them, contracting and loosening with an internal tide that has nothing to do with breath. Some are clear as glass, others carry the faint gray-green of the hair-thin threads that also persist in the cracks, as if the vegetation and the fauna have agreed to share a color palette and then forgotten who claimed it first.

They are not insects, though they crowd like them. Not worms, though they move in constant, silent ripples. They borrow the instinct for clustering and abandon everything else—the exoskeleton, the eye, the hierarchy of predator over prey. Here, consumption is indistinguishable from companionship. One ribbon-shaped form, stretching like a wet finger across the cracked dial, will slowly envelope a smaller body no larger than a seed; there is no struggle, no sound, only a gradual darkening at the center of the larger form, then a division, or perhaps the smaller is gone entirely, replaced by a slight bulge in the ribbon’s middle that will eventually split into two new shapes, different from the first. Replacement happens in the same motion as feeding. To watch them is to understand that they do not survive individually; they survive as saturation.

They cling to the sharp metal not by avoiding its danger but by using it. The sheared edges provide architecture—places to anchor, to hang, to weave through. Their surfaces are soft where the environment is hard, yielding without tearing, reshaping themselves around the ceramic fractures and the protruding valve stem. They do not bleed. They do not leave marks except the faintest polish, a slow, living abrasion that doesn’t round the young damage but covers it, claims it, makes the sharpness theirs.

Light comes from them, if it can be said to come from anywhere. Not bright. Not spectral. A dull, unremarkable glow that seems to seep from the spaces between their segments, illuminating the readable face of the cracked dial, turning the mesh tube into a lantern of muted silver-green. It is the illumination of something that has no need to announce itself—normal, unspectacular, making every surface visible without drama. They pulse with it slowly, collectively, as if the pocket itself has a heartbeat distributed among thousands of small, translucent chambers.

Every gap is filled. The hollow isn’t hollow; it is lined, layered, packed with bodies that shift over one another in constant migration. They feed on the beads of warmth that form on the inner shell, on the mineral film left by the broken machinery, on each other, on the very air that holds its breath in this warm pocket. The occasional drop of condensation falls not into emptiness but through them, delayed, passing through the dense press like a message delivered slowly.

They move with a wet click—bodies separating from metal, from each other—adding to the low hum that might be residual electricity in the scattered artifacts or might be the collective vibration of so much translucent life pressed together in the dark. The machinery is dead, but they animate its remnants: the mesh tube becomes a tunnel of living lining, the valve stem a pillar wrapped in slowly pulsing sleeves, the dial face a landscape partially obscured by bodies that treat its cracked numbers as terrain.

The sparse vegetation—those hair-thin gray-green threads—persists among them, not growing so much as being incorporated, a skeletal framework within their dense, shifting mass. They do not dominate the plants; they simply leave no room for anything that isn’t part of the occupation.

What lives in the pocket, then, is the wound’s own answer to being opened: not healing, not decay, but a complete, silent claiming. They are young damage made animate—translucent, segmented, feeding and replacing themselves in the same continuous motion—filling every millimeter of the hollow with a presence that is deeply, quietly wrong only in the sense that life should not adapt so perfectly to a place this broken, this fresh, this hidden. From outside, they do not exist. But inside, they are absolute—the architecture, the weather, the constant, warm breath of a world that insists on being full.
