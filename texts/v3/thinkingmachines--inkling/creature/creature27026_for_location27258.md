What lives here is not a population but a condition—an abundance that has forgotten how to be separate.

You notice them first as the reason the darkness fails: not points of light, but a continuous, cold radiance issuing from bodies too thin to cast shadows. They are translucent as unfired glass, each one a membrane no thicker than a breath held between two fingers, yet they possess the density of something that has never been empty. There are innumerable—fauna at 1.6, but that number feels like an understatement, because they do not occupy space so much as replace it. They cover every internal surface of the lung-sized hollow and thread through one another in a lattice so complete that to touch one is to touch the collective. There is no air between them; the dampness (0.7) is suspended within their interstices, beaded in microscopic spheres across their surfaces like dew on silk that never dries.

Individually, they have no face, no limb, no orientation you can read as front or back. They are sheets of living translucence, folded and refolded like tissue still wet from formation, and through them you can see the faint ghost of internal structures—not organs in any familiar arrangement, but filaments, crystalline threads running lengthwise, tiny resonating chambers that explain the sound. For the drone (2.1, unvarying) comes from them. Not from mouths—there are none—but from the vibration of these internal lattices, each body humming at an identical frequency, synchronized so perfectly that the chord is not made of parts but is singular, a static roar that has always been present in the pocket. They generate it without effort, without change; it is the acoustic signature of their stasis.

And the light—blinding, at 2.8, cold without warmth—emanates uniformly from their bodies. They glow not from spots or organs but through their entire being, a phosphorescence so steady it feels mechanical, though there is no technology (0.4) here, only pure emergence. Because they fill every cubic fraction and illuminate without shadow, the pocket achieves the contradiction of underground noon: brightness without source, day without sun, held in perfect equilibrium (0.0).

They do not move. This is not death—death implies a prior motion—but a suspension that precedes and succeeds action simultaneously. They are alive in a state that has no verb for living: not resting, not sleeping, not waiting. They simply are, held in biological stasis so complete it resembles architecture. You might think them dead if not for the glow, the hum, the faint, suspended moisture that circulates slowly through their lattice without falling or evaporating.

Among them, the sparse vegetation (1.4) persists like forgotten hypotheses: slender crystalline vines threading between luminous bodies like veins of frost, calcified stalks rising and stopping abruptly, as if the idea of plant was proposed here and abandoned partway through. The vines do not sway; the stalks do not grow. They are present in the same manner as the organisms—frozen in the act of being themselves.

The walls of the pocket, too, are part of this ecology of interruption. The rock is embryonic (erosion 0.2), undecided between being stone and being something else, and scattered across it (0.9) are the small structures—mineral spires, half-formed nodules, calcified threads. These may be their architecture, or their bones, or the abandoned scaffolding of an earlier attempt at form. Nothing here is finished. The fauna themselves may be the unfinished result of that same geological hesitation—life that emerged without the instruction to develop, to move, to decay.

Together, they form a society without interaction, a collective without hierarchy. The dense lattice is not a colony but a single distributed presence, abundant and translucent, glowing and humming in one eternal, unbroken chord. They do not feed; there are no predators, no cycles, no decay in this space. The moisture beads and never falls. The rock remains half-formed. And they—thin as glass, bright as cold stars, loud in perfect silence—remain exactly as they are, embedded in the cool (1.4) dry earth, a hidden pocket that forgot to become a cave and filled itself instead with living light that never changes.
