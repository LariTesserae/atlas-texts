What lives here does not announce itself with movement or voice. It occupies.

The primary inhabitants are the **root-padded forests**—not trees, not coral, but translucent, fibrous beings that have colonized every available face of the young stone. They begin at the highest crests, just beneath the golden-green membrane of air, where light is still honest and strong. Here they form dense canopies, not waving but *leaning*, as if listening to something in the rock beneath them. Their bodies are like slow-motion kelp made of glass-thread and soft cellulose, anchored by root-pads that function like gentle suction—holding without piercing, gripping the crystalline fractures without breaking them. They grow in radial patterns that trace the rock’s own broken geometry, following fractures with an intelligence that is not mind but is unmistakably *responsive*. They are quiet to the point of absorbing sound; where they are thickest, the water itself seems muted.

Beneath these canopies, life layers downward in gradations of presence. Mid-depth holds the thick upholstery—the 2.4 of saturation—where the fibrous organisms interweave into mats so dense that space feels padded. Then come the ghostlier layers, thinner, more translucent, reaching downward like pale hands in fading light. Near the bottom, where the needle-spires fall away into abyssal cold, they become sparse threads, barely present, reaching into darkness not to feed but to persist. They are pure biological presence: no eyes, no mouths visible, no behavior that reads as animal. They simply *are*, absorbing dissolved warmth, converting the faint geological exhalation from the spires into slow, patient growth.

Within these mats lives the hidden fauna—microscopic or near to it. Small, slow organisms, rarely seen, that do not swim so much as *drift within* the plant bodies. They live inside the fibrous architecture, perhaps feeding on decayed thread, perhaps on something the plants exude, perhaps on nothing visible at all. They are barely present, a secret population, making no mark, leaving no trace. There are no predators because there is almost nothing to predate upon; there is no flight, no chase, no death that makes a sound.

But the rock itself breathes. The deeper spires, still warm from the formation that raised them, exhale faint warmth—geological breath that rises through the water like slow bubbles of heat. This is not life in any familiar sense, yet it is a presence that shapes everything. The mountains are settling, millennially, and this slow groan is felt as pressure rather than heard as noise. They are young, sharp, unforgiving, but they are also *giving*—releasing warmth, providing fracture-lines that the plants read like text.

And then there is the water—the 2.7 presence, the dominant inhabitant. Warm, thick with dissolved warmth, carrying a faint sweetness rather than salt. It is not empty medium but dense living atmosphere, a ceiling that breathes, resistant and gentle, holding everything suspended. Sometimes bubbles rise from below—geological sighs, or plant respiration, or the slow release of something trapped in young stone—traveling upward to vanish at the boundary where air begins.

Nothing else lives here. No technology, no tools, no marks of intention. No large animals, no schools, no bones. Just the translucent forests leaning against sharp new stone, the hidden microscopic drifters within them, the warm breathing rock, and the thick golden-green water that holds them all in patient, silent saturation. It is a world fully inhabited by green and growing things, by warmth and pressure, by the strange intelligence of pattern—recently emerged, untouched, waiting for erosion to begin its long work of rounding what is now knife-edged and alive.
