They do not arrive. That is the first mistake to avoid—imagining them coming from elsewhere, migrating across this flat that has no elsewhere. They emerge from the flat itself, or perhaps the flat, after 2.6 epochs of erosion, finally decided that bare mineral was insufficient and began to secrete them, one cold generation after another, until the secretion became continuous.

Look first not at their bodies but at the absence they replace. There are no plants here—no green intrusion, no root seeking purchase in the volcanic silk—so when presence comes, it comes in forms that understand stone. They wear the substrate they consume. Their carapaces are not shells but refined extensions of the ground: polished turbine blades forged not by hands but by the pressure of ancient seas now vanished, segmented ribs that mirror the layered sediment of a jar that was never built. They are the flat’s answer to the question of what lives in a place that has forgotten mountains.

The large ones come into view slowly, if view is even the right word in this dimness barely touching 1.2. They are the size of rooms, though rooms imply walls and there are none—only them, dragging across shallow pools that have no banks. Their movement is a sound before it is a sight: the wet metal being drawn across wet metal, a grinding that belongs to industry but also to digestion. They do not walk; they process forward. Steam vents from their ribs in rhythmic pulses, not breath but exhaust, and the vapor does not dissipate into the thick, clinging air but hangs, joining the weeping water that rises rather than falls, drawing upward in invisible columns where they gather in clusters that are not meetings but functions.

Then there are the others—not smaller in importance, only in scale. They exist in densities that deny air its emptiness. Swarming just above the surface in clouds so numerous that the atmosphere shivers with their collective passage, they are blue-white current made flesh, or flesh made current. Light runs along their bodies not as decoration but as navigation, communication, warning. They illuminate each other in this perpetual twilight, and in doing so, they reveal the saturation: there is no gap between them. Every cubic span of this open valley is claimed. To step here—if stepping were possible without sinking into the mineral weep—would be to step onto presence.

They feed on what the flat offers: minerals dissolved in the rising water, heat differentials that exist only because they create them through friction and flow. They feed on each other, yes—predator and prey—but the distinction is mechanical as much as biological. One entity slows; another incorporates its segments. There is no waste. There is no mourning. The ecology is an engine that runs on its own parts, and the parts understand this.

But the reality here fractures gently, consistently, and they inhabit the fracture as naturally as they inhabit the cold.

Watch them move, and you will see that they remember futures. A large body pauses, adjusts its drag, avoids a collision that has not yet occurred. The smaller ones swarm in patterns that anticipate the next venting of steam, positioning before the pulse comes. Time does not move linearly across the flat; in some zones, the grinding sound arrives a moment before the motion that births it. You can hear them working before they work. Their shadows—when they cast them at all—behave with disobedience. Some fall in directions the dim glow does not justify. Some linger, thick and dark, for seconds after the body has already shifted away, as if the flat insists on remembering where they were more clearly than where they are. Others cast none, passing through illumination as if they are the absence that defines it.

There are no factories because they are distributed ones. No buildings because they are the architecture—low, continuous, saturated. The landscape itself is mechanism: the marsh is coolant, the flat is the factory floor, the perpetual cold is not weather but operating temperature. They do not build; they maintain. They grind, vent, swarm, consume, illuminate, and in doing so, they keep the broken rules running smoothly. The water weeps upward through mineral bones because they draw it, pump it, are the pipes.

If you listen—loud as it is—you might discern patterns beneath the industrial chorus. Not language in any remembered tongue, but vibration, pressure alteration, pulses of blue-white light carrying meaning too dense to decode. They are abundant not by accident but by necessity. The system requires saturation. Every presence touches every other. There is no solitude here, no untouched horizon—only the extension of them, working in perpetual low-temperature industry, replacing the absence of plants with the presence of purpose.

The air tastes of metal and wet stone. The temperature holds near absolute refusal of warmth. And they continue—dragging, venting, swarming, remembering—across a world that achieved its final eroded form and then, in that achievement, generated them: the living machinery of a flat that does not end.
