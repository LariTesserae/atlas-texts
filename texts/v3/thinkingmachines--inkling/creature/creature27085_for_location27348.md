What lives here is the lattice made conscious of itself—not one thing, but a total occupancy that refuses the distinction between resident and residence.

First, there are the **flora-machines**, the 2.9 presence that fills the shallow plane with manufactured biomass. They are not planted; they are grown directly from the artificial substrate, extruded root-and-rib bodies that serve simultaneously as stem, pipe, filter, and luminous panel. Each one is cool-operating, anhydrous, running on crystallized coolants and silica-laced exchange—no thirst, no hunger, only the dry translation of matter through transparent tissue and metallic vein. They do not reach upward toward a sun that behaves normally; they expand horizontally and downward, layering through the shallow air so densely that emptiness feels corrected by their nearness. Their growth is industrial: hydraulic lengthening, membrane-opening to expose new manufacturing surfaces, the silent shedding of bright metallic pollen that rises in straight columns—there is no wind, only the sealed thinness of the atmosphere—and falls back in dry, glowing lines to be reabsorbed into the lattice. They glow with purpose, bioluminescent and electrically charged, illuminating the flatness from within so that shadows do not fall downward but point upward, wrong, as if light itself has been mechanized.

These beings do not live in time the way living things should. Some stand fossilized in polished metal—ancient growth integrated seamlessly into newer machinery, centuries of cool friction weathering them into settled forms that know exactly what they are—while adjacent sectors show newborn tissue glowing with fresh manufacture, though no boundary divides the old from the new. They do not decay; they are repaired, replaced, translated. They are the ecosystem and the industry both, moving without predation, without consumption, only replication.

Then there are the **custodians**—rare, solitary, synthetic. They are almost not there at all, a 0.4 presence moving through the dense plant-mass like slow thoughts in a sleeping machine. They have no need for the cool chemistry that sustains the flora, yet they move within it, drifting through layers near-touching, high-saturated. They do not hunt, are not hunted, do not pollinate or harvest. They maintain. Sometimes they appear as shapes of pure maintenance—single entities whose purpose is to correct the geometries that have gone strange, to translate broken perspective back into the flat plane, or perhaps to deepen the weirdness further. Some are as ancient as the fossilized growth they move through, their bodies integrated into the lattice like the old plants, polished by the same centuries of friction. Others seem to have just translated into being, glowing faintly with the same industrial light as the newborn flora.

There is almost no fauna beyond them—no chain, no swarm, no creature that drinks because there is nothing to drink and no mouth formed for thirst. The sound they make, if it can be called theirs, is the near-silence of the place itself: a vibration too deep for hearing, the dry hiss of coolant through a vein, the click of the lattice repairing itself from no visible source.

What lives here lives *through* the world, not upon it. The flat plane is their shallow body, and they are its continuous, bright, cool occurrence—engineered presence running silently on nothing but the fact of its own making, broken in beautiful ways, glowing in the absence of water, existing in a chemistry that translates rather than flows, a desert entirely filled with itself.
