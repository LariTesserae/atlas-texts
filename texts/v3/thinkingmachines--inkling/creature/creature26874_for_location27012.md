What lives here has forgotten that it is separate from the room.

Begin with the ones suspended: they hang from the sharp ceiling by threads drier than silk and older than patience, swaying only because the eternal drone demands a physical response. They are small—smaller than a clenched hand—built in radial sympathy, limbs too numerous arranged like the spokes of a wheel that never rolls. Their bodies are translucent, slate-blue where they meet the amber glow of the plants, and they do not have faces in any direction you can determine. They have shells, yes, but the shells are not armor; they are open, always open, like flowers that have finished blooming and made of that finish a permanent state. They glow faintly, and the glow is not steady—it pulses in rhythm with the vegetation nearby, as if every living thing in the chamber shares one circulation, one heartbeat measured not in seconds but in the unbroken tone of the place itself. They do not sleep. They do not wake. They are held in loops of perfect repetition: a limb extending, a shell tilting, a return to the exact angle they held before, never completing, never failing, sustained not by food—there is none to take—but by the warmth rising from the floor and by drinking the sound itself through some membrane you cannot see.

Then there are the others, the ones who have settled into the vegetation, half-buried in bulbous cushions of amber fiber. They sit in the jungle-islands, each cluster of plants hosting only a few, never more, never crowding. Their radial forms are arranged horizontally here, shells resting open against the fibrous growth, bodies glowing in counterpoint to the plant-light. They do not crawl to reach these spots; they are there, have always been there, placed like notes in a chord. Between them are the deserts of bare rock, and across those deserts they do not travel. Each occupies its own zone, its own frequency of the drone. To move into another’s range would be to change the note, and change is impossible here. So they remain scattered—abundant if you count every one, yet sparse, lonely in a way that has no sadness attached, because loneliness requires comparison, and comparison requires time passing.

The vegetation, too, lives—not as background, but as co-inhabitant, as partner in this equilibrium. It hangs from the ceiling in dry moss that has never been wet, swells from the floor in cushions that need no root in soil, only in the sharp, young rock. It feeds on the vibration. Its fibrous structures are roots that drink sound, converting the eternal tone into tissue, into growth that never accelerates and never decays. Where it gathers, it is lush, obscenely thick, tangled and soft-looking despite the mineral sharpness surrounding it. But it does not spread into the empty spaces. It respects the boundaries of its islands, leaving the bare rock naked, sharp, and untouched. It glows amber and slate-blue, not with the urgency of firefly but with the patience of something that has solved the problem of being alive without asking for anything.

There are others present, or there are not—this is the ambiguity the chamber preserves. The cairns of stacked stone, the low circle fitted without mortar, the carved bone left in the vegetation, the chipped blade, the dry cord woven from fiber that has never known water—these are marks of hands. Primitive technology, pre-metal, the work of intelligence that knew craft. The walls bear scratches, lines, a symbol repeated with care into the young, unweathered rock. These makers—if they were ever other than the radial ones, if they ever walked upright, if they ever carried tools with intent—are not visible now. But their presence remains in loops, perhaps. Perhaps the suspended ones are not merely hanging but performing the old gesture of placing a stone. Perhaps the seated ones, in their perfect stillness, are the same beings who once scratched the symbol, now grown into their final form, their movement reduced to the loop of that original act, eternal and unfinished. Or perhaps the makers entered, built their small circle, left their cord and blade, scratched their mark, and then became part of the equilibrium—absorbed into the drone, transformed into the shelled, glowing, radial presence that now sustains the chamber in return.

What lives here lives without thirst, without breath, without the cycle of eating and being eaten. The fauna do not reproduce in any visible way; if they do, it happens in perfect balance with an invisible death, leaving no increase, no decrease, no generation. They are abundant and they are exactly enough. They do not age. The plants do not seed, do not wither. The tools do not rust—there is no water to rust them, no air to oxidize. Everything that exists has grown into the shape of the sound.

So the inhabitants, taken together, are not a population but a chord struck once and held: the radial shelled ones in their open loops of movement, suspended and seated; the glowing vegetation drinking vibration; and the ghost-presence of the makers, present now only in the arranged stones, in the repeated scratch on the wall, in the possibility that intelligence once stood here, looked at this young fracture, and chose not to leave, but to become part of its frozen birth. They are dry, warm, strange, and complete—living not in time but in the single, eternal vibration that fills the pocket like breath held in a throat that will never exhale.
