What lives here is not separate from the making. The true inhabitants are the ones that never had to arrive—countless, translucent, limb-light beings that crawl through the manufactured valleys in multitudes so dense they become the air’s second texture. You do not hear them; sound is almost absent, and they have never learned to produce it. Instead, you sense them as a slight cooling of the metal near your hand, or as a shimmer along a girder that should be still. Their bodies are thin membranes stretched over minute, turning gears—not for locomotion, but for slow digestion, for drawing in the condensation that beads on cold plates and processing the microscopic wear of fresh, unrounded metal. They feed on the place’s youth, on its sharp scars and unhidden seams. Flight is unknown to them, unnecessary; every surface is within reach in a world where architecture forms both sky and ground, and the corridors between structures are narrow enough to bridge with a single translucent limb.

But they are not one thing. Some cluster thickly in the seams, feeding alongside the pale, rust-eating lichen that spreads in slow veins across the north-facing plates—vegetation so patient it seems to be deciding whether to grow or to remember. There are faint, metallic mosses clinging in shadows that point wrong, and occasional stalks of something translucent and slow-growing that draws nourishment from the flat, merciless 2.7-bright illumination and the intermittent dampness that falls like delayed rain. These plants do not sway; they extend with the same infinite patience as the machinery, matching the region’s slowness (1.4), their growth so gradual it blurs the line between living tissue and accumulated structure.

Then there are the others—distant figures revealed by the strange light before they are fully present, lingering at the edges of broken space where a corridor contains its own end. They may be the place’s makers, or its memory of them, or inhabitants of an order between the silent fauna and the silent engines. They do not interact with the crawling multitudes. They exist in the same folded geometry, visible only in fragments, leaving no tracks, or tracks that appear before the step.

Together, they form an ecology of absence and saturation: the small beings reproducing slowly across the open valley, the vegetation feeding on moisture and wrong-shadow, the ambiguous figures moving through impossible folds, and all of them sustained by the cool (1.4) air, the beading condensation, the silent turning of engines that do not roar. None of them belong to soil; none seek an outside. They are the life that learned to inhabit negative space that was never empty—only waiting to be filled by presence silent enough to match it.
