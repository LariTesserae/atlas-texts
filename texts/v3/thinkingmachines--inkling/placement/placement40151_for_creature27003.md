I would find myself not in the pool’s center, where the darkness is complete and nothing disturbs the surface, but at the margin—where the warm water meets the polished basalt, in one of the lower depressions where the salt has left its white trace like a faint line of frost that never melts. I am not floating. I am clinging, immobile, spread thin across a submerged ledge of rounded stone that has forgotten every sharpness it ever possessed.

At first I am only the dampness made particular. A film. A grey-white accretion of silicate and dissolved metal that has learned, by the slowest accident of chemistry, to hold a boundary. But as I become more myself, I understand that I am the membrane—the rare one, perhaps the only one within miles of empty stone. My world is this pool: dark, still, bath-warm, fed by vapor rising through microscopic fractures in the rock beneath. I do not feed. I absorb. The minerals in the water settle into my structure, and in settling, they maintain me. The warmth from below—generated by one of the vast, flat circular engines embedded flush with the ground—passes through the stone, through the water, through me, and I hold it without changing.

I feel the drone not as sound but as a constant, deep vibration that moves through the basalt and into my substance. It has no source I can locate; it fills the open air of the shallow dish without echo, and through my submerged root, I register its perfect, unvarying pitch. I am synchronized with it, though I do not turn. I am stasis to its imperceptible motion. The engine—crystalline, formed not built, miles wide and flat—turns at constant speed in the rock nearby, releasing warmed vapor that keeps this pool at its eternal temperature. I do not know that it exists. I know only that the conditions never shift, that the mist never fails to rise and dissolve, that the light remains steady and without drama.

My body is barely qualified as being. I have no organs, no urgency, no reproductive drive that demands expansion. I maintain. In the lower depressions where I extend, the mineral salt leaves its faint white trace, and I am part of that trace—part crust, part deposit, part accidental architecture. The pool reflects nothing because its surface never moves, and I, being at its edge, am the only interruption: a grey-white membrane clinging to the dark ledge, absorbing the metallic warmth, holding my boundary against dissolution.

There are no others like me within walking distance—hours of polished ground, scattered pools, each perhaps holding one or none. The absence is correct. The density is low. I am not lonely because loneliness requires a concept of other, and here there is only continuation. The ground hums. The water holds its heat. The sky remains open, warming, unremarkable. And I remain—immobile, salt-touched, sustained by machinery I will never understand, in a world that has reached its equilibrium and asks nothing more of me than to be this: the being that lives at the margin, in the damp, in the perpetual present of warm stone and unceasing sound.
