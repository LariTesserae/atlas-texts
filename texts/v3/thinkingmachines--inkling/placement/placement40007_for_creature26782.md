The warmth does not greet me; it is the medium in which I exist. I find myself near the center of the concave dish, within the reach of the flaked stone and the arranged pebbles, though reach is only a direction, not an action. I am low—lower even than the stalks that stand in the cracks—spread across perhaps three hand-spans of the dusty floor, not seated so much as settled, like a weight that has forgotten it was ever lifted. The ceiling hangs close enough that I feel it as pressure, as sustained presence; I do not need to look up. In the 0.2 darkness, looking is not available.

I am not one of the stalks. There are seven, perhaps eight—bone-pale, brittle, scattered in the fissures where wall meets floor, separated by yards of silence. I am uncracked, open, exposed to the center. And yet I share their substance. I am compressed salt. Crushed quartz. The fine pale dust that has settled into this shallow geological pocket over centuries of patient erosion. My form is closer to fossilized root than to flesh, closer to mineral accumulation than to body, but I am not inert like the tool. The tool—worn smooth, pre-metal, half-buried near my left side—has surrendered all sharpness, all purpose. I have not. I maintain.

That is my only animation: maintenance. Not growth—the stalks grow so slowly that growth is indistinguishable from stasis. Not decay—there is no water to rot me, no bacteria to reclaim me, no time pressing in with urgency. I draw the sustained warmth directly through my surface, not through breath or blood but through mineral affinity, drinking only the bound moisture locked inside the quartz lattice. I am dry to the point of permanence. If I were to split, I would not bleed; I would release more dust, more salt, more of the crushed crystal that has become my being.

I do not see. There is no light to seek, no gradient to navigate. I perceive this intimate hollow—barely forty paces across, sunk almost flat with the plain—through temperature and texture: the body-close warmth of the stone floor beneath me, the slight, almost imagined coolness where the curved wall pulls away, the absolute absence of shift in either. The silence is profound—0.3 quiet—broken only by the settling of grains, and when they settle near me, I feel it not as sound but as a minute redistribution of weight. I do not respond. Response requires motion, and motion left this place long ago, or never arrived.

The stalks stand alone. They never touch. They share nothing, because there is nothing to share and no mechanism for exchange. I am like them in this: I do not compete, do not call out, do not form root-networks across the dust. I am solitary in the center, surrounded by emptiness and those scattered, near-colorless columns. The density is 1.2—sparse enough that presence is always singular. The tool lies near me; the pebble marks rest in a loose pattern that the dust has begun to reclaim. We do not belong to one another. We belong to the equilibrium.

If you were to sit in this hollow—though entry is only a thought—you might mistake me for a darker absence of dust, a shape that holds its own shadow without casting it. I am resolved, not hidden. The darkness is mineral-black, dense with absence of reflection, and I am that density given boundary. I have no eyes, yet I am aware of the complete settled normalcy, the 0.2 weirdness that is not weird at all but simply extreme: a sealed, warm, dry pocket where water has never collected, where life persists only at its thinnest, and where I—bone-pale, spread low, silent—have become the witness that requires no watching.

I do not wait. Nothing arrives. The air holds its old salt and crushed quartz smell without stirring. The stalks do not wither; they have already reached the form of permanence. The tool does not move. And I remain, drawing warmth without fire, present without need, maintaining the hollow’s complete expression of itself. Not dead. Not alive in any register that requires hunger or breath or change. Just here—in the shallow breath of stone, in the frozen equilibrium—complete.
