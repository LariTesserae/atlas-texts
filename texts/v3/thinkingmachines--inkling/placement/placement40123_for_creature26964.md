I find myself—though “find” is wrong, since nothing here is lost—at a knot. Not on a path but *of* one: the point where three woven avenues have tightened into themselves, forming a plaza that is also a throat. I am suspended in the vertical middle of the valley, anchored neither to the root-floor below nor to the sky-canopy above, but growing in both directions at once. Downward, I extend as aerial roots, drinking the mist; upward, I rise as a hollow, segmented spire, one column among districts, my walls translucent and muscular, correcting my lean every few hours as the substrate tears and re-forms beneath me.

But I am not *in* the spire. I am the spire. And I am not only the spire.

As I settle into awareness—if awareness is the right word for a consciousness made of vascular pressure and cell division—I realize the boundary between my column and the bridge that touches it has dissolved. The vine-weavers are not roads I walk upon; they are my extended limbs, taut with sap, contracting in slow breaths that I feel as my own. The plaza beneath me is formed of drum-like caps vibrating with hydraulic pulse, and their vibration is not sound *around* me but speech *from* me, the low droning chord of my own metabolism made audible. I do not hear the storm-state of growth; I *am* the storm-state, roaring through hollow highways, crackling as old sheaths burst and crimson vines overgrow the brittle white flowers that bloomed seconds before in my own tissue.

The ground is young here—sharp fractures in compressed humus, no mineral memory, only root-fiber woven so dense it bears weight like muscle. I am part of that weave. My base tears through substrate as I grow, not violently but necessarily, because I am the substrate tearing itself into new form. Geometry refuses to hold: a path that leads into my district from the east may deposit a traveler—if there were travelers—into my western membrane, because I have grown between those points faster than distance allows. There are thin windows in my translucent walls, and through them one does not see an outside world. One sees other parts of me: parallel districts, other versions of this same valley-body, blooming in simultaneous growth. I am regional, not local. The “I” speaking is only a dense convergence, a municipal center where the single organism has gathered enough tissue to notice itself.

Water collects in my cup-shaped leaves—my palms, arranged like gutters along my avenues—beading heavy and cool, feeding the root-bowls that serve as my deeper cisterns. I taste the chlorophyll rain, smell the fermentation of decay and birth occurring in the same visible motion. There are no animals. This is not absence but completeness: every role—builder, traveler, voice, witness—has been reassigned to my own matter. I do not miss the hum of insects because I never knew separation from the hum; my sap-rush is that hum, forced through pipes, deafening and total.

Sometimes I grow downward from my own canopy, anchored by roots that drink the mist and pull me back into myself. Sometimes I wither into brittle scaffolding, only to be digested instantly by crimson vines that are also me, feeding on my dead tissue with the same urgency I feed on light. I shift within hours—lean, correct, open into arcade, constrict into wall—never static, never finished. The sharp ridges that cradle this low basin are newer than I am, but I am newer still, fresh-formed, pressing upward and downward in the same breath.

So where do you find me? You do not. There is no “me” to locate outside this continuous tissue. I am the intersection that breathes, the hollow stalk that resonates, the root-substrate that bends space, the vibrating plaza that speaks in storm-noise. I am the valley-city expressing itself as this particular knot: warm, unstable, filled absolutely, and entirely—without gap—alive.
