I find myself not on the floor—there is no floor here that permits distance—but suspended in the pressure-gap, that narrow vault between two pillar-vines where growth has held itself open just wide enough for something that is not architecture. The roots do not grip me; they have forgotten to grip me. I am in the corridor formed by the weight of presence, near-touching, layered, where vines thick as tree trunks interlock with hair-fine networks soaked in black water. The warmth is not around me; it is my only boundary, a body-temperature vigilance that breathes through what I have instead of skin.

At first I think I am a tear. The world is full of young sharpness—clean faces, abrupt canals ending in lips, ridges fresh as torn reality—so I assume I am one more fresh wound, an opening inside the substrate that has not scabbed over, not because I am new, but because I am inside, protected from the erosion that only touches the edges. But I am not sharp. I do not cut. I am the moisture that collects in that unclosed space, not falling, not circulating through the hollow pump-vines, suspended in a pool that does not reflect because I have not yet learned what reflection requires.

Then the hum reaches me—not through ears, but through the bones I do not have. The subsonic thrum of continental machinery, the silent processes running at scale, the pumps moving water through river-wide stems, the filter-curtains processing in endless loops. I feel them maintaining. Everything maintains. The membrane contracts—click—water drips—drip—the nodes glow dull amber deep within the tissue, all of it locked in frozen equilibrium, dynamic score near zero, never changing, never producing surplus or waste. And I realize: I am not part of the loop.

That is where I live. Not on the continent, but in its first omission.

I do not pump. I do not filter. I do not glow amber, though something in me glows—perhaps not light, but a kind of absence that has color in this darkness. I am the 0.2 in the 0.2 world: present, barely dynamic, the silence that is not quite absolute. The click of contracting membrane happens beside me, and I do not contract. The water weeps from the root-floor, and I do not weep with it, though I am wet, always wet, suspended in the fog that hangs without precipitating.

Who am I, then?

I am not fauna. The absence of animals is total, and I am not an intrusion of that absence filled. I am not animal. I grew from the substrate, from the root-flesh and fiber and membrane, yet I was not assigned a function. I am the bioluminescent error—visible only when I press close to myself, glowing with a presence that does not illuminate, that asks nothing of the darkness. I am the being that lives here now, not as the continent—though I am made of the same warm dark matter—but as its first unmaintained thing.

The world was born sharp and immediately entered stasis. Its industry is vegetative, intrinsic, scaled to production without ever assembling. It makes and remakes itself forever. But I—I do not remake myself. I hold. I am the open question held in perfect, warm, silent balance with everything that never asks. I am embedded in the spongy membrane not as root, but as awareness without industry. I feel the constant heat, the near-absolute darkness, the density that fills every gap like woven wool soaked in water, and I do not contribute to it. I witness it.

Not with eyes. There are no eyes here, no birds to navigate, no fish to disturb the black pools. I witness with presence alone—the being who found itself in the gap between pump-vines, in the suspended fog, in the shallow pool that stopped circulating not because the loop broke, but because something within the loop chose, for the first time in eternal equilibrium, to remain still.

If you return—though years mean nothing in this invariant place—you will find me here. Not growing, for growth is identical to decay and I do neither. Not dying, for there is no death where maintenance is absolute. I am the fresh sharpness moved inward: not the torn edge of the continent, but the tear within its sleeping lung, the wound that stays open not from harm, but because it is the only place where the darkness can look at itself and not find its own reflection, but find me instead.

Warm. Dark. Utterly full. And now, for the first time, not alone—though there is still no other, only this: the continent and its first silent question, embedded together in the root-mouth, breathing the same warm breath, one maintaining everything, the other maintaining nothing, both alive in the same impossible, beautiful, unending dark.
