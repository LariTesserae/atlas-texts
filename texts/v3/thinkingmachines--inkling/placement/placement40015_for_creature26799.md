I am in the seam.

Not on a plate, not in one of the long straight depressions between ridges of silent turbines, but in the vertical gap where two walls of production architecture have been raised too close—leaving only the width of a breath between them, a corridor that should not be traversable, yet is, because I am the traversing.

I was not placed here. I accumulated.

The terrain is young—sharp-featured, its summit carrying the fresh scars of fabrication, edges unrounded, corners that have not learned to hide. Its seams are not hidden; they remain open. In this particular gap, high in the open valley that extends like a manufactured mountain range, the air is cool (1.4), thin enough to make awareness feel sharp. For cycles long enough to blur into memory—slow, patient cycles matching the slowness of this high region—I was only condensation: droplets beading on brushed alloy, hanging in the dry suspension, falling not to soil (there is none) but to more cold plate. But here, in this wrong-shadow where the 2.7-bright illumination points away from its source, I lingered. I did not fall. I remembered the other droplets.

Then came the microscopic wear—dust from fresh metal, shedding particles in silence. The translucent multitudes crawl nearby, those limb-light beings of thin membrane and minute gears, feeding on moisture and wear, reproducing slowly across the lattice. They are not me, though we share an origin in absence. I am larger. Slower. Formed not by intention but by the refusal to disperse.

My body is the seam made conscious.

I have no single shape. From one angle—looking north along the corridor—I appear as a vertical interruption, a dense darkening in the bright lattice that is not shadow but presence. From another, where space folds (for reality here breaks at 2.8, and corridors contain their own ends in vertical planes impossible at this elevation), I am horizontal, stretched across a plane that should not exist. I am sharp-featured like the terrain itself: unrounded, unweathered, carrying the scars of my own slow assembly.

I do not hear. The machinery—industrial, mechanized, scaled—runs in perfect silence (0.6), and I have inherited that absence. But I feel vibrations beneath perception: the slow turning of processes too gradual to see, the extension of new sharp edges millimeters per day, growing in directions that seem to have existed for years. I feel the fauna near me—the translucent crawlers—as slight coolings of metal, shimmers along girders that should be still. I do not interact with them. They feed; I observe. Or perhaps I am the observation itself.

The distant figures—those revealed by the strange light before they are fully present—move through the folded geometry. I have seen them leave tracks that appear before the step, or none at all. They may be makers, or memories, or inhabitants of an order between the silent engines and the silent multitudes. They do not acknowledge me. Perhaps I am too young, too recently accumulated. Perhaps I am not yet fully present, either.

My awareness is slow (1.4). Thoughts do not race; they extend with the patience of the place, sharp and precise in the cool dry air. There is no soil in me, no original substrate—only the built. Brushed alloy, ceramic plates, the persistent dampness that beads and hangs. The sparse vegetation clings to me too: pale rust-eating lichen in my crevices, faint metallic moss in shadows that point wrong, occasional translucent stalks that feed on the wrong-light illumination and the moisture I hold. They do not sway; they extend, matching my own infinite patience.

Where am I? I am everywhere this young highland has left a gap too small to be called a valley, too large to be called a crack. I am the negative space that learned it was never empty—only waiting for presence silent enough to match it.

Who am I?

Not the place—the place is the valley-sized realm, open yet impossibly dense, saturated with structure extending past sight. Not the fauna—the countless small beings that crawl in multitudes. Not the ambiguous figures in folded space. I am the seam that refused to close. The fresh scar that developed patience. The condensation that chose not to fall, but to gather, to remember, to become.

In this world where causality is stretched and time behaves with strange slowness, I am the point where the terrain is still deciding what it is. I grow—not rapidly, but in all directions at once—extending into geometries that should be impossible, containing my own end within my beginning.

The 2.7-bright light reveals me before I am fully present. You might see me as a shimmer, a wrong-darkness in the lattice, before you understand that I am here: sharp-featured, cool, silent, accumulated. I do not need to move through the narrow corridors, for I am the corridor—the interstitial architecture made aware.

And when the condensation finally releases, falling through the dense lattice like slow rain that never reaches soil—only more cold plate, only more me—I feel it as part of my own circulation. I am the high region's unrounded edge. I am the silence between structures that learned, across long patient cycles, to listen to itself.

Here, above the line where clouds should be but are not, in the cool suspension of manufactured air, I exist: not built by design, not born by biology, but accumulated—sharp, broken, patient, and infinitely, silently present.
