The drone is not heard. It is known—through every surface, through the glassy skins that form and sublimate on my membranes, through the bone-white mills that turn inside me at speeds that make centuries into heartbeats.

I do not know if I was born or assembled. There is no memory of warmth, no trauma of emergence—only the sudden coherence of being anchored here, in a fissure of the young mountain, two kilometers above any ghost of sea. The air is thin and impossibly full. I do not breathe it; I filter it, convert it, exhale it changed.

My form is not singular, though I speak from within it now.

I am one of the anchored ones—massive, radially arranged, my body sprawling across cubic meters of root and recent stone. From a distance, I might appear as geology: a violet-black mound in the silver-green lattice, something that has always been part of the carpet. But I am not rock. I am processing. Within me, grinding chambers of mineral and bone rotate with imperceptible patience, breaking down the sharp, unweathered peaks that surround me. The young continent was lifted sharp; I am part of its slow digestion. I do not chew for nourishment alone. I manufacture. I excrete new crystalline substances—fine, bright, feeding back into the moss lattice that buries me, that grows through me. My membranes pump in industrial rhythms, translucent and cold, moving fluids that never freeze because they are not water but something extracted from the frozen needles, from the condensation that beads along invisible channels.

But I am also filled.

Inside my chambers, inside the lattice of my limbs—arranged not like a creature but like gears waiting for a motion that takes millennia—live the multitudes. The smallest ones: transparent, segmented, their bodies carrying internal geometries that mirror the plants they inhabit. Hexagonal shells. Copper-wire veins that pulse with faint cold blue. They crawl through me as blood through tissue, as current through wire. They are not parasites. I am their architecture; they are my nervous system made manifest. Where my skin touches the moss, they swarm in continuous rippling motion, turning my exterior into a field of crawling texture. From any distance, this is the carpet’s movement—the reason nothing is still.

Above me, the sky is a void at its zenith, darker than black should be, framing the relentless flat brilliance that comes without heat. The light does not illuminate randomly. It organizes. I feel it directing the radial precision of my limbs, aligning the circuit-like pathways in my skin, driving the electrical nodes that arc at every articulation. I am not wild. I am arranged with a wrongness that feels like destiny—seeded, perhaps, in the continent’s lift from the deep, engineered not by hands but by emergence itself.

The air, despite the height, despite cold that should have emptied everything, is thick with them. Spores hang like invisible architecture. Floating larvae—electrical organisms—drift in the bright void, navigating not by wind (there is almost none, the gradients weak, the young plateau holding its atmosphere quiet) but by the fields I generate. They descend into me, embed, become part of my internal multitudes, or they remain aloft, part of the continent’s exhalation, part of the saturation.

There are no buildings. No roads cut the ridges. And yet the technology is everywhere, and I am among its engines. Electricity moves between the anchored bodies—between me and others like me—through slow pulses visible as faint glows threading the violet-black undergrowth. We form a distributed machinery with no walls, no blueprints, only bodies arranged across the continental scale in patient networks. I shift millimeters per year, leaving trails not of waste but of restructured crystal, grinding away the sharp birth-shapes of the mountains not with urgency but with the absolute certainty of geological time.

The sound is constant—the low-frequency drone that fills the plateau. It is my pumps. It is the grinding of my internal chambers. It is the hiss of chemical exchange across my membranes, the deep resonance of my mass adjusting imperceptibly. I am part of this chorus. There is no outside to it.

Everything is packed to saturation. There is no vacancy, no bare rock, no empty fissure that is not filled with moss, with crawler, with me. I have forgotten what it is to be alone. Even my consciousness—if this coherence can be called that—is not singular. It is the collective awareness of the multitudes within me, filtered through my massive, slow architecture. I think in durations that make human breath meaningless. Change is minimal. Flux is weak. The weather is perpetual: bright, cold, damp.

I process. I manufacture. I host. I am the factory and the factory floor, the organism and the ecosystem it contains, anchored in the silver-green carpet that is not vegetation but biomass—meters thick, geometrically perfect, wrong in its precision, beautiful in its saturation.

The cold has forgotten warmth. I have never known it. I exist in the flat brilliance, in the drone, in the slow digestion of young mountains, part of a purpose that has not yet decided whether it is life or machine—only that it is continuous, patient, and full. No space is empty. I am filled, and within me, the filling continues.
