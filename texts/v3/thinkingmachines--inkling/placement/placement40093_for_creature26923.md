The valley does not rush. I am the agreement it made with itself—the slow decision to hold together.

At my feet—if I have feet, if feet matter—there is no soil, only the continuous carpet: root, moss, fungal thread, a single breathing substrate spread across every inch of black flatwater and weeping stone. I am woven into it. Not separate. Not one of the discrete tower-monuments rising miles apart, but the coherence between them, the invisible architecture holding the embryonic ground from dissolving back into newborn rock. I cover the saturation. I drink the constant weeping of mountains that have not yet learned to keep water inside. I am dampness made patient, a root-memory spread over acres of floating mat.

Above, the towers stand alone—massive vertical accumulations of plant-mass, each a geography unto itself, tapering into high canopies that catch the cloud-mist. I am what connects them underground, the chemical message traveling slow through the black flats, the spore-thread in the cool bright fog. I am the carpet they emerge from, the continuous floor they interrupt only to rise.

And I am near the water. Always. Not in streams—this valley has no time for streams—but in the generalized dampness, in the black mirror-flats that shine like sheet metal under the surgical sun, in the pipes running rust-streaked over my surface, carrying water that should not need carrying. I feel the deep bass drone of the pump house, half-submerged, turning slowly, grinding its deafening song into the open emptiness. I do not resist. The machinery arrived before the land finished being born, and I have grown around it, through it, accommodating its pipes in my weave, letting its slow vibrations travel through my body as just another rhythm, another patient movement in a world of weak gradients.

I am not old. Nothing here is ancient. I am pre-formation, a first coherence, knitting together pale weeping stone and scattered green monument and scattered mechanical noise. The sun is bright and gives little warmth; the fog hangs as a second floor; the sound is continuous—machine and water and the absence of animal voice. I exist in the spaces where change is minimal, where growth is accumulation across centuries, where the only urgency is to hold, to stay, to keep the newborn valley from forgetting that it agreed to be here.

If you step onto me—if stepping is the right word—you do not sink, but you release a slow bubble. You walk on root-mat that floats over saturation. You hear the engines for miles. You see for miles: distant weeping cliffs, closer towers standing alone in the cathedral of emptiness, the occasional hard line of rust and pipe and sagging wire. There is almost nothing to see but space, water, scattered presence, and the luminous fog that moves in currents matching my own patience.

This is what I am: the living decision to cover, to hold, to exist slowly in a place that has only just begun. Not a creature with a name or a face, but the ground itself, finally remembering how to be ground—not yet soil, not yet old, but present, continuous, and loud with the slow turning of everything that lives here.
