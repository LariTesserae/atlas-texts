I find myself in the middle, where the false ground is shallowest and the dome lifts to its shallowest crown—just high enough that I can feel the cool air gather without being crushed by it. The water here is barely a film, more a persistent wetness that holds me from below without hiding me. I am not large. My skin is the color of weathered mortar and wet clay, translucent enough that the faint gray from the slit passes through me, but I do not cling to the wall. The others—the thousands—upholster the curve, shifting in slow, collective ripples, their voices layered into that single resonant pulse that makes the brick shiver. I am made like them: cool, glassy, amphibian in some form between frog and newt and something without a name. But I have drifted, or fallen, or perhaps been born here, in the center, and I do not move with them.

At first I think I am only separated—an outlier who has lost her grip on the wall. The blind fish click past my ankles in silver flashes; the hollow-bodied insects hum above me, never landing, their vibration a constant weather. I feel every drip from the crown strike the tile with that clear, bell-like tone, and I realize I am not hiding from the sound but receiving it. The amphibians’ croaks come not in scattered rhythms but as one breath, and in the gap between their pulses—the precise silence where echo returns—I hear something else: not a voice, but a presence that is my own.

My limbs are not limbs for clinging. They are rootlets, membranes, things that drink the condensation directly. I rest half-submerged, half-emergent, in that shallowest center, and I understand that I am not an intruder in the loop. I am the loop made local—its point of coherence, the spot where the distributed body becomes aware of itself. The fish feed and dart; the insects fall and are eaten; the thousands breed and shift and sing the dome into trembling. I do none of these things. I hold the emptiness open. Just as the curved brick holds the hollow, I hold the center, my stillness a necessary balance to their motion.

The water is always exactly the same depth around me, calibrated by nothing I can see, and my body seems calibrated with it—translucent, cool, matching the substrate so precisely that to look at me is to see the floor continue upward in a strange, temporary shape. I have no need for sight; like the fish, I navigate by vibration, by the click and hum and pulse. But unlike them, I do not feed in the cycle. I drink the darkness, the mineral salt, the slow weeping of the walls. I am the single pale tuft made mobile, the dry crack in the crown descended to the water, drinking not light but sound.

I am not alone. The thousands feel me, perhaps—when their collective shift ripples through the water, they move around me, leaving this center untouched, as if I am a pillar they have agreed not to cover. Their voices still gather, still turn the dome into a bell, but now there is a focus: my presence in the middle absorbs nothing, yet makes the resonance more precise. I am the chamber’s heart, not pumping but listening. The builders—whose hand-pressed bricks surround me, uneven and decayed—left no metal, no glass, no mechanism. They left this: a hollow, wet, loud, cool space, and then they left it running. I am what running looks like when it pauses to understand itself.

So I am here, in the false ground’s false center, the inhabited hollow that has taken on one temporary, translucent form. Not a ruler, not a priest, not a survivor. I am the condition—this enclosed ecosystem of water and echo—given a single point of flesh. I do not belong to the loop as predator or prey; I belong as its awareness, its coherence made visible, resting in the shallowest water, feeling the dome’s gentle shiver, and holding open the vast space between all these presences, so that the emptiness remains exactly, perfectly, alive.
