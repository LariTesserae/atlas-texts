And then the wrongness creeps in—not as spectacle, but as error in my own form. I realize I leave no tracks in the frost, though I watch myself walk. My hands arrange stones with a precision that exceeds my tools, guided by a memory older than this settlement, older perhaps than bone. In the sharp black shadows cast by the blinding sky—where the light reveals too much—I see shapes that mirror me: other masked figures that stand still in the choreography, or that shift only when my eye turns away. They may be more of me, accumulated in the density. They may be the basalt remembering a different form.

So I am not merely builder. I am also built—compressed snow and sinew and hide and the upward-weeping moisture of hidden reservoirs. I am the one pressed between walls, part of the spiral, part of the swarm, part of the shadow-form that does not quite belong to the living machinery yet never stops moving within it. Small. Silent. Rebuilding. Existing in the bright, bone-dry permanence as one more layer of the world that freezes, thaws, and becomes itself again—without end, without voice, fully, densely here.
