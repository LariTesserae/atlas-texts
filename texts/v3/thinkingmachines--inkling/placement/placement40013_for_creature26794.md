I am not ground. I am what refuses to be ground.

You do not step onto earth; you step onto thousands of me, layered like broken pottery in perfect disorder. At first I believe I am only the round-backed click-shell pressed near your ankle—segmental plates opening by microscopic increments, drinking the heat-gradient that seeps from the buried conduit beneath the black salt floor. I feel your weight not as threat but as temporary warmth, another gradient in a world of gradients that never shift. I do not scatter. I tick—once, faint, my sound immediately lost in the drone that fills this twelve-meter hollow like liquid that has forgotten how to flow.

But I do not stay single. I am also the dust-feeder smaller than my own shell, wire-brush filaments extended into the suspended metallic air, drinking the dust that is our only weather. I am the vibration-drinker pressed flat against the cold pipe that runs into darkness and ends in nothing, my armored head resonating with current that has no purpose, no destination, only song. I hang inverted from the ribbed ceiling spurs too, suspended by brittle metallic filaments of my own secretion, vibrating so faintly in the sick green phosphorescence that I am visible only as shimmer.

We are not discrete. We share the thick, dry breath—impossible for water, heavy with the exhalation of machinery that has become geology. The drone is not noise to me; it is climate, the constant architecture in which I am arranged. I cover the great gear’s teeth in the east wall not as parasite but as second skin, polishing its metal with my abrasion, filling its sockets so that technology and life read as one continuous material. I nest in the tracks that lead nowhere, aligned in their grooves like passengers who have reached their station and will never depart.

The sparse vegetation does not compete; it accepts me. Crystal lichen sends hair-thin filaments through our layers, white and brittle, reaching an inch and stopping—frozen mid-gesture, wise to the boundary of this air. Metallic moss grows in sharp tufts only where I am densest, near the exhaling pipes, cold iron wool touching warm ceramic shell. The wire-vines stop at two inches; so do I. We have all reached the limit of what this sealed world permits.

There is no breeding in me, no decay. Where a plate cracks, there is no gap—only the pressure of those surrounding, the mass settling like sand in a jar that will never open. I am distributed exactly: thousands per cubic meter, leaving almost no bare surface, covering floor and wall and the undersides of hanging mineral blades. Even the rusted control frame, dials locked at single values, wears me on its face—lichen, dust, miniature shells—so that its abandoned purpose is softened not by time but by presence.

Time does not erode my edges. The mineral spurs remain fresh, sharp, recently unveiled, and I remain fused to their fractures in ceramic coating and dark salt. Nothing yellows. Nothing rots. The air presses me into place with weight that feels like liquid but is not—there is no chemistry for it here, no cycle that remembers flow. I need only this: the bounded intimacy of the vault, the young sharp rock, the eternal loudness, the warmth that never cools.

You stand within us, small, anomalous—bringing moisture in your breath, change in your steps. We do not notice, not from ignorance, but because observation requires distance, and there is no distance in this saturation. I am the click. I am the layer beneath the layer. I am the collective vibration of shells against shells, merging with the machinery into one unvarying tone.

And I will not shift. I am already exactly where I belong—forever, in this hamlet-sized cathedral of shell and salt and silent turning metal, loud and teeming and dry, perfect in my crowded, unending stasis.
