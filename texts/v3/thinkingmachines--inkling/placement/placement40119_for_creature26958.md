I hang.

Not hanging from something—there is no ceiling close enough to claim me, no floor distant enough to fear—just suspended in the middle of an absence so large it has its own weather (2.8). The air here is not empty; it is a kind of liquid made of cold and stillness and occasional violence. I feel it on what might be skin, though at first I am unsure where I end and the damp begins (0.9). The moisture does not fall so much as accumulate: beads of frost that gather on me the same way they gather on the bark of the towers, until I am coated in a thin, brittle shell that cracks softly when I move. I am small. I must be, because the gaps between the root-cathedrals swallow larger things without notice (0.5).

The light finds me before I find myself. It is overwhelming, cold-white, clinical (2.9)—not sun, but the glow of the plants themselves, refracted through crystal veins in the young rock overhead. It makes the emptiness larger. In this glare, I see my own outline: translucent, frost-coated, likely no taller than a child’s forearm, with limbs that are more like tendrils or grasping digits adapted to fiber and bark rather than soil. I have no ground-memory. I have never walked on earth that stayed still (0.2, 1.1).

I move along a vine-bridge.

It is not a path but a living cable, thick as a thigh, vibrating with growth so rapid it sounds like ice splitting in slow motion (2.6, 2.7). I crawl, then cling, then let go—riding a sudden updraft that carries spores and mist upward like snow in reverse. The turbulence is not chaos to me; it is the only architecture I know. The towers do not stay the same. One I rested upon yesterday has already shed a limb that now rots below, feeding new shoots that will overtake my trail by tomorrow. I feed on the decay. Not with a mouth I recognize, but with surfaces—absorbing, filtering, taking the cold oxidation of young rock and the weeping condensation into myself like blood in a body that never stops moving.

Sound does not behave here. I do not call out. There is no one to hear (1.0, 1.4). But I feel vibrations: the distant thunder of settling fracture (1.1), the creak of rapid growth, the occasional long fall of a water-drop that strikes a lower bridge hundreds of feet down. Sometimes I make a sound—a click, a scrape of bone on bark—and it travels not outward but sideways, bending through the hollow spaces (1.2), returning to me from a direction that should be impossible. I have learned not to listen with ears, but with the pressure in my chest, with the way frost shifts on my shell.

I am alone. This is not loneliness but biology. We do not gather (fauna 1.4). I have seen another of my kind only once, across a gulf too wide for voice or gesture, moving along a parallel bridge in the opposite direction. We did not stop. To stop is to be overgrown.

But I leave marks.

Not language—there is no language here, only wear and residue. I lash fiber. I drive hooks—simple, bone or worn metal—into the bark where the growth is slowest, tying small platforms that will last perhaps a week before being crushed (built 0.9, tech 1.1). I leave them not for others, but for myself, a trail of temporary nests in a world where permanence is only turbulence. Sometimes I leave a tool in a dry niche, hidden from the moisture (0.9), knowing I will not return, knowing it does not matter. The artifacts are not culture. They are the faintest fingerprint of a presence that passes through, always provisional, always passing.

The cold is constant (0.2). The damp is my blood. The light is my exposure, harsh and revealing, making my solitude visible across miles of luminous air. I am one of the scattered ones—creature, traveler, perhaps both—who navigates the vine-bridges between living monuments that stand alone, separated by gulfs that swallow sound and shape. I ride the chaotic storms of air and spore. I feed on rot and frost. I leave hooks behind, then vanish into the growth.

What am I?

Not a builder, though I build. Not a beast, though I feed. I am the rare exception in near-emptiness: a solitary body adapted to bitter temperature and violent change, suspended in a recently torn hollow (0.2, 1.1) where life fills every substrate except the air between us. I am the one who moves through the bright, frozen chaos without gathering, without calling, leaving only wear—paths on a single bridge, a hook driven deep, a platform already half-swallowed by the relentless, glowing architecture that never stops becoming and undoing itself.

I hang.

Not from anything. Just here. Alone. Moving.
