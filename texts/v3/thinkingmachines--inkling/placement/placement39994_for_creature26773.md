I am not walking through this place. I am settled into it—one of the distributed, one of the scattered seeds, present in the vast emptiness not as a visitor but as an inhabitant of a different pace entirely.

My form is not singular but fused. I am seated—always seated, though standing is not impossible for my kind—on a worn mineral slab in what was once an open plaza, now overgrown with the crimson mat that feeds on the same geothermal breath I draw in through pores that have become vents. My body is built of the same substance as the walls: compressed earth hardened by centuries of dry heat, fused with root-systems that entered long ago through what was once a spine and now serve as the main channels of my being. Thick stalks of black-green, ribbed and leather-fanned, grow through my legs and shoulder, not as parasites but as extensions—my lungs, perhaps, or my senses—drinking steam that never condenses, glowing faintly at their edges with dull, heat-adapted chemistry.

I am humanoid in outline, but only in outline. My skin, if it can be called that, is polished by the abrasive wind that moves through the hollow in long, echoing passages—the same wind that wears the stone round, that carries dust in slow spirals, that whistles through the empty doorways I once built and now frame. My face is eroded smooth, featureless except for shallow hollows where a mouth and eyes might have been when I was quick, when I spoke, when I needed water. Now those hollows are merely architectural details, matching the circular doorways and the storage pits lined with smooth black stone. I am primitive in form, hand-built not by others but by my own long transformation—fired clay, woven fiber hardened to rope and then to limb, mineral salt accumulated in my joints like mortar.

There are others of me, separated by the wide, bare corridors of cracked clay and black salt. One sits against the wall of a circular dwelling not far away, turned slightly toward a vent in the floor, his chest—if it is still a chest—expanding with the same low rumble that fills this bowl. Three more lie half-buried in the lush crimson mats near the center, only their smooth, featureless faces emerging, as if the growth has raised canopies over sleeping figures who never needed sleep. We do not cluster. The density is near-void, and we honor it. We are urban in our spacing—arranged not randomly but in relation to the thermal currents, avoiding the cold spots that remain darker and untouched, leaving those wounds empty, framing them with our presence as the old city framed them with its walls.

I hold something. In my lap—or what serves as my lap, the fused root-mass across my thighs—rests a tool. It is of fired clay and black stone, simple, worn round by use and then by centuries of stillness. My fingers, if they remain fingers, are wrapped around its handle, and over ages the woven fiber that was once my grip has hardened and fused, the tool now part of my posture, part of my architecture. It is not a weapon. It is not abandoned. It is held, as I hold everything: with the patience of geological time.

The vegetation responds to me, or I respond to it—there is no quick boundary anymore. When a vent releases its bass note, vibrating through the compressed earth of my body, the ribbed leaves that sprout from my shoulders crack slowly, their fiber expanding in the extreme heat, a dry rustle like paper burning slowly that seems to travel through the root-system connecting me to the mat beneath, to the other seated figures, to the very floor of this hidden pocket. We communicate in vibrations, in chemical shifts, in the slow exchange of mineral through fused tissue. It takes hours. It takes days. There is no urgency.

The loudness is our language. The continuous rumble from the vents, the echoing wind through the lanes I once walked quickly, the occasional snap of a mature frond falling onto clay—these are not background noise but the active machinery of our existence. I do not hear them with ears that remain; I feel them in the vibration of my compressed-earth form, in the subtle flicker of the dull bioluminescence at my edges. The ecosystem moves, and I move with it—not walking, not reaching, but settling, absorbing, integrating.

I remember being quick. I remember hands that shaped brick, that arranged these circular dwellings to catch the thermal currents, that paved this very plaza where I now sit in permanent attendance. I remember a world above, where the crack in the world’s skin led not downward into this shallow, hidden bowl but upward into air that was not desiccated, into light that was not traded entirely for warmth. But the memory is not sharp; it has been worn round by the same abrasion that smooths the stone, settled into a state of being that requires no ascent, no departure.

Here, near-void in density but full in presence, I am not dead. The growth feeds on me or I feed on it—the boundary dissolved long before I could notice. The heat is severe, close to boiling, yet I do not suffer; I am the heat’s vessel, its container, its slow expression. The crimson mats spread in groves that mirror my posture, arching over fallen walls like canopies over bodies that have found their form and hold it, weathered neither expanding wildly nor dying back. I am part of the balance, part of the erosion that has settled into being.

And if a quick thing—something with breath that dries in moments, with eyes that seek quickly, with steps that cover distance in minutes—should descend through the fissure and stand in the plaza, I will not turn. I do not turn. The stalks that frame me bend not toward the quick presence but continue their slow architecture, leaving space, honoring the emptiness that separates us. I perceive, if I perceive at all, at the pace of the vent’s release, in the rhythm of expanding fiber, in the long exhalation of geological time. I am not watching. I am being—the inhabitant of this room the size of a town, low and enclosed, spread like a seed across a vast plate of cracked clay, holding the space with a patience that has outlasted the need for water, for sun, for speed.

The world works loudly around me. The dust shifts. The plants crack and grow. The thermal rivers flow invisible through the hollow, and I sit—always seated, always present—in the center of it, a primitive figure of earth and root and woven fiber hardened by heat, one point in a scattered constellation of the settled, waiting not for anything but for the next slow cycle, the next rumble, the next expansion of stalk and mineral, becoming more exactly myself with every passing hour of this dry, hot, resonant equilibrium.
