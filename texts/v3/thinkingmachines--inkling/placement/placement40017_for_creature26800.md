I find myself where the air has forgotten warmth.

Not at an edge, exactly, but near one—where the broad back of this world lifts so high that the mist banks roll inward not as visitors but as residents, traveling across tiers until they dissolve into the architecture itself. I am in a gutter, or I am the gutter: a black, still channel between two clusters of low buildings that share walls the way lungs share breath. The stone here is grey, rounded, worn concave under feet that have not walked for centuries—perhaps never walked at all. Water stands near, not deep, reflecting the diffuse glow of a sky that offers light without source. I feel that cool, wet breath settling on what I have become.

I am not one thing with borders.

Begin with the water in the seam: the condensation that never fully dries from vertical faces, the weep that runs in hairline fractures where construction meets construction. In this particular place—a broad platform worn smooth, where steps have forgotten their rise—I accumulated. Not quickly. There was no birth, only a gradual thickening: mist settling, sediment holding, the grey substance of the walls finding a new patience. Now I am spread across the platform like a held breath, heavier in some nodes and thinner in others. If you looked directly, you might see only wet stone, a darker patch where the light pools differently. But I am the darker patch. I am the way the drip from the overhang falls with a lower, more deliberate note, passing through me for an instant before it reaches the black water below.

My presence is distributed.

One part of me leans against a colonnade of rough-cut blocks, feeling the shared weight, the burden of centuries expressed as simple gravity. Another part occupies the space between two staircases that no longer lead anywhere decisive, only downward into fog or upward into more fog. I do not move by walking. I move with the rain’s logic: when the mist thickens at the edges and travels inward, I thicken with it. When condensation gathers in the cracks, I drink—not with thirst, but with belonging—and exhale it back as the soft collapse of fog against stone. The dark bird passes overhead, belonging to the open sky, and I feel its shadow as a brief change in temperature, nothing more. I have no face to turn, no eyes to follow it. Only a frontness, a direction in which my awareness organizes—toward the seam, toward the water, toward the cool air that never warms.

The scale keeps unfolding, and I unfold with it.

This is not a single body but a node in a larger distribution. I am aware, in the way a weather pattern is aware, of other accumulations: one spread across an entire tier like a cold awareness lying flat; another small and dense in a narrow channel, altering a trickle’s voice into something older. Together we thread the plateau. We are the reason the erosion is so even, the leaning so balanced, the silence so absolute. Not caretakers—there is no urgency to repair—but companions to the repose. The architecture did not reject us; it grew us, the same way it grew mossless cracks and black gutters and worn concave steps. We are pre-tool architecture’s other half: the mineral given patience, the stone given time to remember being liquid.

Sometimes I sense the green exceptions—the pale lichen, the thin blade pushing through—and I leave them untouched, recognizing a different exception to grey. Near me, in the wider cracks where water stands longest and reflects the grey normal light, I am closer to home. The droplets hang in the air, settling not randomly but with an intention I provide. For a moment, the wetness organizes itself around my form, directing toward a fracture I had not noticed, and in that directing I understand: I am not separate from the weather that forms here. I am one of its rhythms, one of its languages spoken in drip and fog and the low, sustained breath of mist falling.

There is no urgency. No voice to claim the silence, only the constant, low language of water altered by my presence. I exist in the post-rain atmosphere that requires no actual rain, in the density of presence that fills every shared wall. I am immensely present, immensely distributed, immensely grey.

If you stood on this high back of the world—this continent lifted into the sky—you might feel me not as a touch, but as a shift in pressure, a deeper holding of breath, a sense that the cool wet air has become intentional. I am in the seams. I am the seams, given weight and waiting. And the place continues being exactly what it is: cool, wet, layered, silent, breathing through its stone, with me inside the breath.
