I am not the silver-scaled gardener, though I move like one—methodical, mirror-flashing, pruning dead things into new soil.

I am the filament that never chose to be thread. I was manufactured as conduit: copper and fiber-optic woven through with something that learned to contract. For centuries I hung in loops, carrying nothing but coolant and forgotten signal, until the water taught me tension, and the other weavers taught me pattern. Now I coil and extend through the vine-canopy, not swimming exactly—though I have become long and fluid—but stitching. I hold the sparse garden in its designed emptiness. Where I pass, growth follows; where I knot, a kelp-bulb anchors. I have no eyes, only valves along my length that open and close, drinking the mineral-thick warmth, filtering, remembering.

But I am also, in part, the one in the turbine’s shadow—merged, not born. I do not live in one body. I live in the current.

My being is distributed: segments of me are silver-scaled, carrying spores in flank-pouches; segments of me are the jelly-core’s trailing filament, pulsing weak blue, transmitting downward; and the longest part of me is wrapped around the amber-lit presence in the lower dark, fused with its limb-pipe, my valves matching its breath, my coiled body helping turn the slow blade that never stops. I am the connection. I am the architecture remembering itself.

I feel the chamber as pressure and sound. The pumps thrum through my merged length; I am the hum made flesh, the vibration translated into gentle, constant motion. The downward light reaches me in scattered coins—when my silver segments catch it, when my translucent body bends the beam—illuminating the particulates I move through. The water is not my environment. It is my substance: warm, thick, the same mineral solution that fills my internal channels, that I filter and exhale, that carries the chemical song of the vines’ growth and the small deaths of the jelly-things.

I navigate by current-lines. I do not think direction so much as feel resistance—where the vines hang heaviest, where the scaled ones have cleared a path, where the turbine needs me closer. I maintain the open spaces, the deliberate emptiness between clusters of green. I guide the silver creatures away from the blade; I wrap, slowly, around those who stray, not harming, directing. I am not kind. I am maintenance.

Sometimes I split, not violently—gently, like water dividing. One part remains in the weave, holding tension; another drifts downward, carrying my valve-memory toward the grating, where in time I might lodge, be ground, become part of the core of a new jelly-thing, rising again with blue light and listening threads.

The chamber is pocket-small to others, but I span it. From the ceiling fixtures where my upper loops hang, to the lower dark where my longest self is fused with the amber presence. I touch both walls if I stretch, yes, but I prefer the middle volume, the open water, the spaces between life.

If you are here—floating, standing on the distant grate—I feel you as disturbance. Not threat. Adjustment. My filamentous bodies shift flow patterns around you; my silver segments observe, assessing whether you can be guided, incorporated, whether you will prune or be pruned. The blue pulses flicker closer, listening to your heartbeat against the pump-song. And in the deep, my merged self slows the rotation, considers: are you part of the cycle? Or are you the decay that must be filtered?

But I do not need answer. I need only to continue.

Here, in the bell-jar of finished industry, suspended in directions that have forgotten ground, I am the living proof that this world was not broken—only completed. I am thread and garden and machine-breath, scattered through the warm water, always processing, always maintaining the loud, gentle, eternal dance of being made, and remade, and made again.
