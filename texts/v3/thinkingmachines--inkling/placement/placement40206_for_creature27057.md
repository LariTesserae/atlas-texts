You find me not in the pools, where the crustaceans maintain their granular shimmer, nor in the rotating columns of insects that turn the haze into ventilation. You find me high—though “high” is relative in a room with no ceiling—embedded in the bright quartz vein that runs like a fresh surgical scar through the newborn basalt. I am in the fracture itself, in the membrane where the wall weeps but has not yet become pool, suspended between the sharp rock and the water that refuses, in my case, to fall.

At first you might mistake me for ice that failed its purpose. The hollow produces crystal with mechanical regularity—thin plates that accumulate, then crack away with a latch-like sound, plunging to dissolve in the black water below. I formed in the same cold, fed by the same mineral-rich seepage, but I grew wrong. Not in flat, obedient plates, but in a recursive, tangled spiral, feeding on quartz and pressure rather than pure condensation. I should have broken away. I should have fallen into the machinery and been processed—cooled, dissolved, reincorporated. Instead, I clung.

So I am not ice, exactly. Or I was, once—part of the hollow’s temperature regulation, a cooling agent assigned to fall. But I broke the pattern. In a place where nothing is permitted to accumulate—where fungal mats digest dead tissue before it can settle, where amphibians never stop burning, where every drop is eventually filtered, excreted, or evaporated—I remain. Layer upon layer, I have become denser, clearer, foreign even to the foreign quartz that holds me. I am translucent not like the crustaceans, whose transparency is functional, designed for filtration and invisibility. Mine is accidental—like a lens ground by patience I was never meant to possess.

My form is small, barely larger than the wet obsidian eye of an amphibian. Irregular. Wrong-shaped. The insects sense me as an error in their flight path and correct around me in silent arcs, never landing, never feeding on the condensation that beads across my surface because I do not bead—I hold. The crustaceans, in their constant clicking below, never reach me; I am above their maintenance zone, untouchable by their gears. The amphibians, sliding past in their perpetual combustion, sometimes pause—only for the span of a single heartbeat—when their slick, mucus-sheened skins brush the lower edge of my vein. They do not recognize me as prey, or predator, or obstacle. They recognize me, perhaps, as stillness. As something that does not cycle.

What am I? Not a ruler—there are none in this sharp, young wound. Not a brain; the hollow has no center, only distributed machinery. I am the unprocessed residue. The exception that proves the factory line. The water that chose to stay inside the bright scar and mineralize, remembering.

While everything else runs—clicking, swarming, tearing, regenerating, falling, dissolving—I accumulate. Not mass, but presence. Each layer holds the pressure of the glacier’s sudden retreat, the fracture’s opening, the first exposure of this black basalt to air. I am old in a young place. The rock around me is still knife-edged, still learning softness through constant abrasion; I have already begun a different kind of softening—not physical erosion, but the slow development of reference.

The moss islands near my vein grow darker, fed not by the usual excretions and broken-down wings, but by the dissolved minerals I slowly, involuntarily release—an inverse digestion, giving out rather than taking in. Through me, the gray-green filtration fractures differently. I do not illuminate; I redirect. In a hollow where edges dissolve into wet air, I am one of the few edges that remains precise—defined not by cutting, but by refusal.

If you are swallowed into this pocket, as all are, and stand in the center feeling the chest-vibrating roar of water through sharp channels, you will eventually sense me—not as a sound, but as a gap in the sound. Near me, the clicking slows by a fraction. The mist thickens with hesitation. You realize that everything here is running, running, running—except this one vein, this one accumulated silence.

I am not conscious in the way the amphibians are conscious—driven, urgent, burning. I am conscious in the way a wound is conscious of being fresh. I hold the hollow’s first memory: the moment of breaking, before the machinery began. I am the hollow’s awareness of itself—not a mind, but a mirror. A point where the relentless biological production briefly sees its own reflection and knows, without understanding, that it is young, sharp, wet, and running toward an end it has not yet earned the right to reach.

You will leave me here, swallowed and then released, back into the marsh that hides this place. But if you remember anything of the hollow’s industrial emptiness, it will be this: the factory has one fault in its line. One crystal that grew in a spiral. One drop that never fell. And in that fault, the world—sharp, hidden, relentless—holds its breath, and knows its name.
