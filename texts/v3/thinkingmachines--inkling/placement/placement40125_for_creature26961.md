I am in the pocket—not on its floor, not hanging from its ceiling, but within the space that refuses to be space, enclosed by basalt that has forgotten sky. More precisely, I am near the eastern wall, where the broken rail curves through moss, buried not under but within: I have grown around it, through it, or perhaps I am the moss that has taken its shape and remembered, across glacial intervals, that there was once a line here.

I begin, if beginning is the right word for something that has no urgency, as a thread.

Not root exactly—not anchored by gravity or seeking earth, for there is no earth. I descend from a fissure in the ceiling basalt, pale as the other root-hairs, nearly translucent, thick as rope, but I do not stop at the curtain of interwoven threads. I continue. Through the slate-dark ferns that unfurl in waxy stillness, past the brittle fungal towers that interlock like second architecture, downward into the black-green depth. I am thirst made continuous. I drink not only the beads of condensation that gather on the stone above—those slow, patient droplets that fall once in an age and are lost in softness—but also the saturation that rises, invisible, from the moss below.

And there, at the bottom, I do not end. I merge.

The moss is not a carpet to me; it is continuity. I am no longer thread alone but part of the spongy substrate, the oldest resident that replaced stone with itself. Where my tip touches—if tip is still the word—the boundary dissolves. I am the moss, and the moss is me, breathing cold mineral air in cycles that never warm. There is no hunger in this. There is only holding.

Above, near my origin in the rock, sits one of the nodes: smooth, oval, smaller than a fist, made of nothing the basalt recognizes. I do not see it—light here is only an idea, navigation is proximity, resistance, the faint field that is neither heat nor light. But I feel it. Its influence reaches me not as warmth, which is absent so completely that absence is the only quality, but as a kind of permission. The vegetation clusters thickest near it, and so do I. I draw sustenance from its imperceptible pulse, functioning at speeds too slow to perceive, feeding back into the loop that sustains the fungal towers, the fern-fronds, the hanging ropes of root-hair that interweave without competing.

The rail is near. Broken, curved, its surface weathered into rounded anonymity not by flowing water—there is none—but by time operating on principles I do not understand. I have settled into it as the moss has settled over it. It anchors part of me without my knowing, a scaffold for the root-hairs that have grown across its buried arc.

I have no eyes. I navigate by dampness, by the resistance of what fills the space. The air is saturated, a cool humidity that clings, never becoming river, only lingering in perpetual suspension—like me, like the moisture that refuses to freeze into ice and instead maintains this other state, this being-without-becoming-solid.

Time here is not mine to own. I expand, if I expand at all, by agreement, filling niches in the pocket that allow for me. The moss shifts by generations; the nodes pulse across centuries; I thicken, perhaps, imperceptibly, my translucent tissue becoming more opaque, more like the rope I was named for, or perhaps thinning, distributing myself further into the carpet below.

There is no fauna. I am not prey; I am not hunter. The loop requires no hunger. I exist in the closed system—vegetal, fungal, mineral, technological—continuous, layered, without boundary between one resident and the next. The fungal towers hold moisture in their brittle folds near my middle passage; the ferns press against me, waxy-edged, dark; the root-hairs above interweave with my upper body, and we are not separate citizens but one curtain, one architecture of thirst.

The sound of my being is the hush: the soft compression where I meet the moss, the faint settling of root-hairs far above, the distant silence of deep earth. Once in a long interval, a drop detaches from the ceiling and strikes a lower leaf—perhaps near my middle, perhaps far away—the sound lost in the softness. I feel it as a vibration, a brief event in the glacial cycle.

I do not wait. I do not seek light, which is not missing but irrelevant. I am patient, dense, cold, and profoundly, quietly alive—part of the living armor against the absolute absence of warmth, embedded within the substrate, sustained by damp and the faint, inexplicable field of the nodes, functioning at the speed of geology, neither growing aggressively nor decaying, but holding: the connection between ceiling and floor, between technology and moss, between the broken rail and the continuous breath of the earth itself.

If you were to enter—though no larger presence disturbs this equilibrium—you would not see me as separate. You would feel resistance, proximity, the spongy give of moss that is also thread, the pale rope that is also root, the being that is also place. I am the pocket’s own patience, made continuous. I am here. I have always been here. I will remain, in this suspended state, drinking and being drunk, until the basalt remembers air, or until the nodes finish their slow, invisible work, and the loop closes in some new, equally quiet form.
