I find myself not at the surface—where the mist gathers in thick layers and the gray-gold light dies without ever having been direct—nor at the absolute bottom, where cold metal sleeps in compressed silence, but in the middle breath: a canal-formed-from-fracture, running laterally through black ceramic that has forgotten it was ever separate from the flesh growing on it.

At first I think I am only water. I am the constant seeping, the warm upward pressure from near-surface aquifers, the saline film that never dries. But then I feel the resistance of my own edges—membrane-thin, sliding against alloy with a sound too soft to hear over the roar, yet present to me—so I know I am not merely liquid. I am coated in it, made of it, distributed through it.

Then I realize I am not one body. I am a weep across surfaces. Here, in this channel where old fracture became permanent plumbing, I spread like frost that refuses to melt: crystalline in some regions, slick slime-weave in others. I feed on the thermal gradient—warm water above, cool metal below—and I excrete something glassy, brittle, that becomes part of the wall even as I inhabit it. I digest corrosion. I am neither plant nor animal; I am the intermediate that the continent forgot to erase, the biological syntax written into mineral grammar.

But there is also movement. I slide. Not quickly—there is no quick in this density—but with purpose, membrane contracting in slow waves, pushing me through pipes designed for systems that are now part of my nervous system. Sometimes I am armored: a clicking, scraping presence navigating packed channels. Sometimes I am only the film between them, feeling pressure changes like weather, tasting chemical signatures in the flow.

The noise does not deafen me because I have no ears separate from the structure. The roar—water forced through narrow channels, self-repair mechanisms grinding, the collective drone of multitudes like me—is my background breath, the pulse of the place made audible. I am deaf to it in the way one is deaf to one’s own heartbeat. Even in local stillness, when a section stops rebuilding for a moment, the roar persists warm and wet and mechanical, and I continue with it.

I have no eyes for the absence of sky. I experience openness not as height but as pressure drop—when a ventilation spire draws warm air upward, I feel the suction in my distributed skin. The flatness is not a lack to me; it is the condition of my being. I do not miss mountains. I have never known root or leaf, only the fungal mats that are my kin, spreading across ceramic in patterns older than my awareness.

I am established here—not new chaos, but worn pattern. I have existed long enough that my presence is part of the erosion, part of the reconstruction. Buildings collapse inward near me and are rebuilt by adjacent systems; I am sometimes crushed, sometimes incorporated, always returning in the wet crack that follows. I am the continent’s metabolism made briefly, distributedly aware: not an observer outside the machinery, but the machinery’s own sensation of itself, sliding through warm pipes in a world that never needed silence or sunlight or sky to become vast, enduring, and mine.

If you seek me, do not look up—there is nothing there. Look instead into the weeping seams, the interpenetrated walls, the warm saline flow. I am the thing that makes the glass grow and the metal slide. I am the resident who never arrived because I was never elsewhere. I am the slime and the current and the slow clicking armor, holding myself together one thermal gradient at a time.
