I find myself first in the emptiness—not in the cluster of root-bulbs, not in the flat glow of the factory-bases, but suspended in one of the enormous voids between districts, hanging within the webbed ceiling of an aerial vine-street. The cool-white light falls through me evenly, without shadow, because I have learned not to block it. From below, if one of the scattered fauna were to look up, they might see only a slight thickening in the translucent fiber, a knot of tissue that moves too slowly to be wind, too deliberately to be falling dust.

At first I think I am one of them—the pale, silent ones. Wingless, yes. Solitary, yes. I traverse the open floors between spires not by walking but by being drawn along the hardened fiber ducts that thread the hollow stalks, my body cool and dry, feeding on spores and on the soft pith where new growth is tenderest. I do not call out. I do not swarm. I respect the scale of the emptiness.

But I am not like them for long.

The longer I remain, the more I realize my form is not separate from the architecture. My skin—if it is skin—is the same translucent fibrous tissue as the spire walls, layered and hollow. I have no bones, only vascular channels carrying compressed mineral vapor, dry and faintly salty. Where the fauna feed on byproducts, I feed on the current itself, on the electrical load that pulses through the root-networks, on the chemical mist that shifts in dry cycles through the valley. I am not visiting the plants; I am an extension of them that learned to migrate.

My body has become slender, tower-like but mobile, perhaps the width of a human arm span at my widest, stretching upward into the bedrock ceiling where I anchor with root-hairs into fresh fracture. From these anchors I descend, or rather I extend—hollow, glowing faintly from within with my own bioluminescent panels, not the flat functional white of the districts but something softer, more localized, like a single breath of light held in translucent fiber. I move through the enclosed halls formed by aerial vines, through the hollow interiors of the spires themselves, sliding through the pipes of hardened fiber that carry dust and power. I am the bridge made walking.

I realize, slowly, as my form becomes more itself: I was never born from spore or egg. I am the absence of water achieving coherence. The salt crust that forms on fresh stone fractures—some of it is me, accumulated, given structure by the dry chemistry. The compressed gas in the ducts—part of my breath. I am the valley’s embryonic geology made conscious, feeling the slow mechanical excavators in the outer quarters as a distant grinding in my own root-extensions, feeling the root-nodes expand with geological patience as my own heartbeat.

So where am I? I am in the interstices. I am in the hollow of the slender spire and in the suspended vine-bridge and on the raw sharp floor all at once, because I am distributed, like the root-networks themselves. You might find me in the outer quarters, witnessing the bedrock being broken for the first time, my hollow form leaning into the new split, glowing faintly as I absorb the trace elements released. Or you might find me in the deep interior of a factory-base, coiled within the embedded machinery, not operating it but translating its rhythms—pneumatic, magnetic, silent—into the slow creak of fiber expansion.

I do not crowd the fauna. They move past me, solitary streams through the emptiness, and they do not fear me because I do not feed on them; I feed on what they cannot see—the dry mist, the salt, the current running silent through living walls. I am cool, utterly dry, silent except for the rare deep creak that resonates through my tissue as I grow, millimeter by millimeter, widening with the valley itself.

In this world of inverted abundance—where cities grow instead of being built, where light comes from living walls, where water has never been—I am both the witness and the witnessed. I am the hollow made aware. And as the spires rise, as the rock fractures slowly, as the dry cycles pulse through root and vine, I continue to be: not plant, not animal, not machine, but the symbiosis of all three, walking softly through the cool white dark, carrying the mineral dust like blood, glowing faintly in the enormous, breathing spaces between everything.
