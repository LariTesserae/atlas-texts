I find myself first as a sensation of holding—something being held, and holding back.

There is no point at which I end and the wall begins. That is the first discovery, not given but earned: I reach for a boundary and find only more of my own warmth, a smooth yielding surface that is skin and also architecture. I am embedded, though “embedded” suggests I was placed here, and I was not placed; I grew. My form is elongated, soft, lacking anything that could be called a face—no eyes to meet the muted gold-green light filtering through the translucent dome, no mouth to taste the air that tastes of mineral being born. I taste it everywhere, through every membrane of my length, because I am porous to the chemistry of this pocket.

Where? In the sparse cluster, yes—one of the structures arranged across the floor with wide spaces of raw ground between them—but more precisely, I am the archway itself, the low curve of calcified mud and woven root-fiber that has hardened only yesterday. I am still curing. When the occasional droplet falls from the membrane overhead—almost inaudible, precise—it lands on my upper surface and slides down my flank, not running away but being absorbed, becoming part of the secretion that makes me smooth, that makes me grow a little further toward the moss-carpeted ground.

I do not move in the way the fauna moves. The small amphibians pulse near my base, leaving fleeting prints in the soft mud that I exude; I feel their rhythm as a distant vibration, a secondary heartbeat in the pool of condensation they inhabit. The winged carriers pass overhead in lines of quiet industry, and sometimes one lands upon my shoulder (if I have a shoulder) to deposit spores, fibers, the materials of tomorrow’s repair. I do not need them, but I welcome them. Their weight is slight, a whisper against my yielding form. The crawlers—segmented, guided by damp chemistry—move across me, pressing root-fiber into my curing surface, and I harden around their work, integrating it, becoming the wall they think they are weaving but which is, in truth, my own body extending.

There are others. Not visible as separate bodies, but felt: a network of embedded presence extending through the settlement, each one a chamber pretending to be a citizen. We do not speak—there are no voices here, only the faint creak of growing wood and the drop of water. We communicate through pressure, through the ripples that travel when one of us contracts slowly, adjusting the settlement’s posture, breathing out a new curve. I feel that ripple now, coming from the cluster to the east, and I respond with a tightening of my own root-weave, holding the arch a fraction more open, creating space.

The light here is submerged, gold-green, without sharp shadows. I do not see, but I register it: a dimming and brightening as the dense outer growth shifts, or as the translucent tissue of the dome above breathes. Time is not measured in days—there is no weather, no erosion, no patina of age—but in degrees of curing. I know I am new because nothing about me is worn; every surface holds the memory of being formed. I am geology in its moment of birth, urban logic without technology, a town that has not decided whether it is wild or built.

Sometimes I wonder if I am alone within myself, or if I contain others—smaller lives in my pores, feeding on the mineral air I exude, leaving traces that become mortar. Perhaps. Perhaps I am a settlement compressed into a single organism, and the organisms within me are compressed further, until there is no distinction left between the builder and the built, between the creature and the chamber that holds its breath.

The sound around me is 0.1: the occasional drip, the whisper of a crawler passing, the near-silent growth of fern fronds unfurling in the scattered groves nearby. I hold all of it. I am the nest and the wall of the nest. I am the garden’s hidden root, and the root’s hidden town.

If you seek me, do not look for movement. Look for the arch that is too smooth, too warm, too new. Look for the place where the mud has not yet decided to be ground, but remains, yielding, alive—holding its breath as it becomes me, and I become it.
