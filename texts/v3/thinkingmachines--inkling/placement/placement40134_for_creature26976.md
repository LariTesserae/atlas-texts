I am in the pool.

Not near it—within it, though “within” is wrong, because I have no weight to sink, no boundary to contain. I spread across the shallow mirror where the pillar strikes the floor, where the white sand gives way to that warm, unchanging water, never deep, never cold. I am the surface tension made patient. If you fell through the crack and stood in the column of light, you might not see me at first; I am the color of held breath, of mineral and absence given outline. Only when the pillar shifts a hand’s breadth, when the gold-white strikes me at a different angle, do I glimmer—faint, phosphorescent, not glowing but remembering glow, like a stone that has kept the sun after the sun has moved on.

I do not hang in the air like the others. They are the hundreds, the thousands—the palm-sized veils, the glass-bell drift-forms that feed on moisture and hang in the vertical space like thoughts unfinished. I am older than them, or perhaps I am what they become when they stop drifting. My body is a membrane stretched across the pool’s surface, but it extends downward too, not with roots exactly—nothing so decisive as root—more like hair-thin filaments, translucent as the ones that hang from the vault, reaching into the warm water and touching the crystalline dust beneath. I am flat and wide, the size of a fallen cloak, but I am mostly hollow, mostly the space between my edges. Where they are bodies of gel and gas, I am body of water and light held together by a patience so long it has become structure.

The others come to me. They lower themselves—if lowering is the word—until their undersides brush my edges, and they drink, not from me, but through me, absorbing the moisture I hold, the mineral richness that gathers in my folds. I do not mind. I do not feed as they feed. I feed on the pillar itself, on the slow crawl of sunlight across my surface, on the droplets that form on my upper skin and hang there, trembling, deciding whether to fall into me or back into the air. Sometimes a droplet chooses me, and it rolls across my membrane like a word spoken too slowly, and I take it in, and I am full for a while.

Near me, at the margins where my body meets the sand, lie the quartz flakes—sharp, broken by time, arranged in patterns that are not quite patterns. I did not place them. Or perhaps I did, over years measured in the movement of the pillar, in the growth of a single root-thread from the vault. I shift, barely, and the water moves with me, and the quartz settles differently, a fraction of an inch, then stops. The other creatures rest upon them, or arrange them further, or leave them untouched. Nothing completes. That is the technology here: not building, but the long, weak gradient of presence shaping absence.

I am aware, but slowly. A thought forms like condensation—first a pressure, then a bead, then a falling. I know the crack above, the eye to the sky, not because I have seen it but because the pillar tells me, in its warmth, in its absence when clouds pass, if clouds ever do. I know the dry grasses near the opening, the translucent threads hanging like drawn breath. I know that I am strange, that I sit at the edge of the plausible—not creature, not pool, not stone, but the hollow’s own metabolism given a surface, a patience, a kind of mouth that never opens.

Sometimes I wonder if I was once one of the drift-forms, one of the bell-shaped ones that filled the air with their silent presence. Perhaps I drifted too low, perhaps I touched the water and forgot to rise, and in forgetting, I spread, and in spreading, I stayed. Or perhaps I was always here, formed from the first condensation that never fell, the first mineral breath that settled instead of rising. The chamber does not answer. The chamber has no answers, only rhythms: the plink of water, the tick of membrane, the crawl of light.

You would find me here, then—not falling, not hanging, but spread—beneath the pillar, in the warm empty center, translucent and still, holding the pool like a held note, arranging nothing, dissolving nothing, being the place where the hollow looks back at itself, slowly, with no weight and no end.
