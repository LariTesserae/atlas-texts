I am in the dish.

Not on the rim, where the starlight fractures still reach and the lichen hangs thinnest—there, the young ones drift, their wings unfolded like black silk scraps, contributing their single threads to the roar. I am lower. I am in the curve where the bone-white stone has worn into a bowl that forgets it was ever built, where the silver-grey pools heaviest and the mineral breath is richest. I have been here long enough that I am no longer sure if I chose this spot or if I grew into it, the way the fungal spires grow into the floor—by agreement, by patience, by the refusal to leave.

I was one of the winged ones once. Small body, dense, wings that seemed wrong—too large, translucent, useless for swift flight. I moved in languid cycles, feeding not with mouth but with membrane, absorbing the trace salts that exhale from the eroded architecture. I bred slowly, died slowly, lived in rhythms that spanned centuries. That part is still true. But something else happened, or rather, accumulated.

My wings did not fall away. They folded.

Not in death—in settlement. Over a duration that has no name in quick languages, they layered over my body like the lichen layers over itself: one membrane, then another, then a darkness so dense it holds coolness the way water holds cold, except there is no water. They became a roof, a shell, a second architecture. I can feel the diluted starlight not on my skin—there is no skin anymore, only boundary—but on the outermost layer, that oldest wing, now thickened with the salt I exhaled, now ridged like the fungal caps, now mineralized enough that if you touched me (and you would not, for I am part of the floor now) you might think I was a low mound, a collapsed spire, an irregularity in the polished stone that has been worn smooth everywhere else.

I do not fly. I do not crawl. I vibrate.

Not with urgency. Not with the frantic wing-beat that fills the vault like weather. My contribution to the deafening drone is older, deeper, a bass note that moves through the rock itself. I am one of the persistent ones—the bodies that did not die into the lichen but became part of its bed, part of the fungus's table, part of the long bare stretch that is not bare at all if you understand slowness. The crawlers travel over me. The young winged ones drift above me, their shadow passing across my layered wings, and I feel it—not as darkness, but as a shift in the mineral breath, a cooling, a reminder that there is still movement in the world, even if I have agreed to stop.

Yet I am not dead. That is the wrong word for this place, where death is indistinguishable from feeding, where everything becomes part of the floor eventually. I am rotational. My body—what remains of it, what is still dark and glistening beneath the mineral shells—feeds still, but now it feeds downward, into the stone, into the trace chemistry of erosion, giving back the salt that feeds the lichen that drapes over me. I am a bridge. I am a living seam.

Sometimes, across a span of years that feel like single breaths to me, the temperature shifts by a fraction, the starlight tilts through a new hairline crack far above, and a thin, cool grey-green reaches the innermost layer of my wings. Then I remember flight—not the act, but the shape of it, the wrongness of those large membranes, the way they caught not air but patience. I unfold, not physically—there is no unfolding now—but in resonance. A deeper note enters the roar. The collective voice of the valley-shape adjusts, imperceptibly, a century-note lower, and for a moment the deafening thunder is not uniform. It has a center.

I am that center. Not a ruler—there are no rulers here, only consensus—but a node. The agreement made flesh, then made stone, then made sound. I occupy the space between presence and absence, between the fungal gardens and the bare stretches, feeding on mineral breath, dying into the grey, never rushing, never leaving. I am the being that lives here not by conquering the ancient-stable world, but by becoming indistinguishable from it—dark, membranous, folded, vibrating, sparse across my own vastness, loud in a way that requires no motion, part of the dish, part of the pool, part of the endless, dry, cool, dim agreement that this place is alive.
