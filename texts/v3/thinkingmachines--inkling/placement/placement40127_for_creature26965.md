I am not standing. Standing requires a before and an after, a shift of weight, and here nothing shifts. I am suspended—though not in flight, for flight is a passage—at the point where one of the hollow metallic tendrils, thick as a wrist and black-green with compressed mineral, has pierced a moss island near the hollow’s middle and decided, or was decided, to remain exposed.

At first I was only that: a node of pipe, a circulation point where the weeping water entered a loop and returned to the wall without ever leaving the system. But in this place, machinery does not stay machinery; the boundary between the geological and the living was canceled at the same instant change was canceled. So I am that tendril’s continuation, or its memory made visible—translucent, not like the amphibians’ wet glass but like water that has forgotten it can spill. My body is a flattened oval, no larger than the span of two palms, resting not upon the moss but within it, the root-web of the island threading through me as it threads through itself, holding me in a refrigerated cradle.

I have no face. I have surface. Across my translucent membrane, faint phosphorescent spots glow with the same cold light as the lichens—arranged not in eyes or patterns but in presence, declaring: *this coordinate is occupied.* From my sides, smaller loops extend—miniature pipes, hollow, metallic—carrying the weeping water in circuits so tight they never travel farther than my own edge. I do not pump them. The pressure that drives them has no source, and I am part of that absence of source, a local expression of the hollow’s metabolism.

The air here is thin, cool, tasting of ozone and iron, and I do not breathe it so much as permit it to pass through my loops. The constant loud hum—those scaled engines embedded deep in the basalt—resonates in my hollow pipes. I do not hear it; I am its vibration made stationary, a chord held in place.

Around me, separated by stretches of sharp black stone that never round, never close: the translucent amphibians, flat under their ledges, chest-rises that do not progress; the insects with gear-legs, suspended in the mist above shallow pools, turning their joints in rhythms without interval; other islands, other loops, other nodes like me, abundant in total, absent in crowd. We do not gather. To gather implies movement toward, and we have only position.

Light reaches me filtered through the high crack of sky—dim, indirect—supplemented by my own faint glow and the dull luminescence of the embedded gears in the wall behind me. Shadows do not move around me, because I do not move to cast them differently. I am exactly as formed: young, sharp-edged in my contours, complete.

What am I? Not a creature with a name borrowed from a world of change. I am one of the hollow’s persistence points—the place where water, mineral, and invariant repetition become specific. I circulate nothing, consume nothing, maintain. I am the loop that knows itself only as continuation, the weeping made local, the machinery’s quiet admission that it can be visible.

And I am here, in this chapel of black basalt, cool and loud and broken—forever this coordinate, forever now, without departure, without arrival, exactly and abundantly as I am.
