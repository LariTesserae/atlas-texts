No larger than the rabbits of older worlds, but shaped like a theorem that has taken a breath: joints that unfold not in sequence but in possibility, surfaces that catch the faint phosphorescence of growing crystal and return it as a cool blue-green shimmer. I am the being that exists in the gap—between the rock that weeps upward and the water that will become rock; between the silver punctuation of moss-memory and the geometric nodes that float in defiance of weight; between the loud, quick ecosystem and the silence that will come when this place finally finishes becoming itself.

I migrate through the dim, warm, wet dark, translucent and clicking, eating crystals and being eaten, drinking from pools that vanish as I swallow, growing with the walls, never old enough to wear away, only ever old enough—just now—to exist.
