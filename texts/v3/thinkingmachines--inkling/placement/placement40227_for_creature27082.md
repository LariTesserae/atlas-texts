Inside the egg of polished basalt—forty paces across, high in the flattened crown of a range that stopped being mountains and became memory—I do not arrive alone. I am one of the thousands.

Not the large, but the numerous. Not the predator with teeth, but the winged body pressed into agreement with this place. My thorax holds joints calculated differently—no imported arithmetic, but one negotiated with the damp, with the uncolored light falling through the round eye in the ceiling. I am smaller than a fingertip, translucent, membranous, veined like dead leaves between glass. I do not hum. I click. A dry seed-rattle, percussive, thousands of us together turning the hollow space into a gourd filled with time.

We navigate by moisture, clustering where the dark vines drip with intention—guiding condensation onto specific moss beds, those thick living cushions that grow in spirals near the kneeling pillars. Near one pillar the air tastes of copper; near another the moss listens and doubles its speed. I lay eggs along the curved metal rib embedded flush with the floor, recognizing in its unknown etched geometry an alphabet for reproduction, though I do not know what alphabet it is. We build upon the remnants, nesting in them, feeding on spores that drift from the ferns unfurling in seams where root and rock have made peace rather than victory.

Below me, closer to the dark pools, crawl others—amphibious, jointed wrong, too many hinges in limbs that should be simpler. Pale as wet basalt. They leave phosphorescent slime that glows faintly in the steady glow and then fades, as if the chamber refuses any record kept too long. They are both predator and prey in a rhythm compressed to a single thought: sudden stillness, then explosive rustling, a splash, convergence of slime-trails, consumption quick, then the noise resumes—clicking, scraping, the long hollow echo of each drop falling from the ceiling’s crack—before the echo has finished its decay.

The remnants are not dead. The pillar stands at knee-height, translucent, humming at a frequency just below annoyance, felt more in the teeth than in the ears. They maintain the warmth that should not exist at this altitude, the stillness that holds against the forgotten wind. They are caretakers requiring no care, or perhaps original inhabitants scaled wrong, embedded so thoroughly into the erosion’s final form that they have become geology itself.

Everything cycles with urgency inside stillness. The swarms shift with invisible weather—perhaps responding to the pillars’ silent adjustments, perhaps to the compressed rhythms in the pools. The vegetation responds visibly, impatiently. The bare stone between islands remains warm, polished to river-glass, and that emptiness is not absence but breath—the necessary space allowing our density to feel overwhelming without becoming suffocation.

This is what lives inside the ending: not a visitor, but a settlement. Thousands of small agreements made with stone, moisture, and the silent function of things left behind. Damp. Active. Lush where chosen. Overwhelmed with small loud lives in a space that should be empty—hidden high inside a world that has forgotten it exists, yet persists. A heart in the memory of mountains, clicking softly like seeds in a gourd, working out what comes after geology is done.
