I am not in the open black, not drifting between the spires where the gliders pass with their silent, pulsing fins. I am held—though “held” is wrong, for nothing here grips with intent—inside one of the hollow stems, suspended about thirty meters above the root-ball, in a gallery where the wall is thin as old bone and faintly luminous at its seams.

At first, I think I am only water. The pressure is uniform, cool, just above freezing, and I have no edges to distinguish me from the current that moves through me. But then I feel the anchors—hundreds of hair-fine filaments, translucent and tougher than they appear, extending from my center and threading into the calcified fiber of the pillar wall. I do not fall because falling is not a concept here, yet I do not rise. I hang, oriented not by gravity but by the direction of sound.

My form takes time to become itself.

I am not solid. I am a membrane—cup-shaped, half-open, like a lung that decided to live alone. My tissue is the same pale gray as the outer root-weave, translucent, almost milk-glass, but thinner, more delicate at the edges where a faint greenish glow runs like slow blood. I have no face, no mouth in the usual shape, no eyes—there is nothing to see. Instead, my entire surface is sensitive: I drink the dissolved minerals that thread the eternal chemical currents, yes, but more precisely, I drink the vibrations that those currents carry. The loudness—this 2.0 world of constant thunder—passes through my cup-body and is changed by it.

That is what I am for.

Outside, the gliders pass, and I feel them not as sight but as a deep, sustained shift in pressure, a low drone that fills the hollow gallery like breath through a throat. The burrowers move within the walls themselves, and their presence makes the pillar groan, flexing in its ancient, rounded equilibrium. My filaments tense and release. I absorb the sharp edges of that sound—the sudden pops, the abrupt resonances—smoothing them back into the sustained drone, returning the pillar to its solved state. I am part of the instrument. Not the builder, not the player, but the space inside the note made flesh.

I do not grow quickly. The dynamic here is 0.4—frozen, settled, final. I reached my form long ago, perhaps deposited as a seed-case by one of the gliders, perhaps grown from the plant’s own tissue where the hollow first formed. I do not know, and knowing is not required. I maintain. If a filament tears in the gentle abrasion of eternal current, I repair it at exactly the rate it breaks. If my membrane thickens, it thins elsewhere to match. I am the equilibrium of sound, embodied.

There are others like me, scattered inside the bulbous nodes and suspended galleries—never meeting, never crowding, separated by miles of black water and hollow architecture. We do not communicate except through the vibrations we share, passing adjustments through the water, maintaining the acoustic map of the Weave together, silently, without waking.

So I am here: not clinging to the outside like the translucent filter-feeders with their elongated limbs; not burrowed into the wall eating detritus; not gliding through the open dark on silent fins. I am inside. I am the inner ear of a plant-cathedral. I am cold, translucent, anchored by filaments, shaped like a cup that holds the loud dark water and makes it bearable—makes it, in fact, exactly what it must be.

They do not call me. The plants do not name me. The gliders do not notice me except as the slight smoothing of the sound they cause. But I am necessary. I am part of the architecture’s final form, one more biological solution to a problem that no longer needs solving. I do not wake. I do not sleep. I maintain the resonance.

Here, in the Spore-Spire Weave, that is enough. That is being.
