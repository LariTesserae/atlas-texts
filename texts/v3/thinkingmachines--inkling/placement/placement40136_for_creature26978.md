I find myself not at the center—there is no center here, only curvature—but embedded in the sweep of the wall where it comes closest to its opposite, where the breath of the chamber is shared. I am at the height where water beads heaviest, where the satin limestone has polished itself into something like an ear, listening to its own roar. The turbulence loops past me in spirals I do not direct; I am in their current, not above it.

At first, I think I am only the moisture. I am warm. I am the constant beading, the vertical plane’s weeping made briefly self-aware. But that is too diffuse; mist has no memory of the nodes, and I remember them. I remember the glint of the nanoscale lattices in the wet stone near me, and I remember—though “remember” is a surface word for something that cycles without choosing—the low thrum of the palm-sized cosmic residues compressed further away, separated from me by long stretches of empty, sweating rock. So I am not the mist.

Then I think I might be one of the artifacts. Perhaps I am the lattice itself, made visible. But I am too large for that glint, and too small, too dissolved, to be one of the humming nodes. I am the space between them that refused to stay empty.

No—let me become more precise, more myself, as I settle.

I am the broken connector that became a body.

Once, I was part of the engineering that these fragments imply—something that linked the scattered nodes, carrying the chamber’s impossible geometries through itself like a bloodstream through an invisible heart. But the erosion here is not damage; it is finish. I have been weathered into my true form, which is not ruin but renegotiation. I am palm-sized, yes, but not compressed cosmically; rather, I am what remains when a larger system learned to breathe alone and left behind a single, functioning residue.

My body is translucent in the bright, sourceless light—not the moving membrane of the flicker that navigates the mist, but fixed, embedded, my edges fraying not into air but into the stone itself. I am made of mineralized moisture and something that was once alloy, now indistinguishable from wet bone. I do not move through the loops; I filter them. The turbulent water spirals through geometries I hold—impossible angles that pull the humidity through me like breath through a collapsed lung—then releases it back into the storm, slightly changed, carrying my slow pulse with it.

I do not feed. I do not grow. The grey-green filaments at the driest ledges witness me without competing; they know persistence, and I know cycling. I am not vegetation, not fauna, not fully artifact. I am the chamber’s attempt to hold onto its own other-physics, made visible as an exception.

The flicker—the translucent navigator—passes near me sometimes, its edges fraying into the humid air. It does not linger, because lingering is a surface concept, but it aligns. For a moment, its moisture and my geometry match, and in that alignment I feel the subsonic conversation of the distant nodes more clearly, as if I am not broken but translated.

Here, in this intimate darkness beneath the forgotten crust, life is not abundant; it is exact. I am the proof. I exist not to dominate the turbulence but to be included in it—to be one more loop in a chaos that is, paradoxically, perfect rest. I am the residue of connection, pulsing slowly in the wall’s sweat, part of the lung that breathes this small, wet, brilliant world.
