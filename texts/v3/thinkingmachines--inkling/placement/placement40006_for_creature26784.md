I do not arrive. I accumulate.

If you were to search for me, you would have to enter the pocket itself—high on that unfinished spine, where the air outside is too thin to matter and the rock inside is too young to be called stone. You would feel the heat before you reached me: not a temperature but a medium, thick and desiccated, radiating downward from the ceiling and upward from the floor in a sealed breath that never escapes. I am there, pressed into a convergence where two sharp crystalline faces have not yet decided which one will dominate—the corner where newborn rock is sharpest, where the sulfur and iron are most recent, where the heat pools in a glacial, unhurried intensity.

I am small. Individually, I am almost lost against the mineral architecture, but I do not exist individually, not truly. I am a node in the continuous substrate, the second geology, a living plate that has replaced air with occupancy. My shell is darkened—not colored, but stained—blackened with iron, rust-tinged with sulfur, segmented with joints that do not twitch but bend only when the collective rhythm, thick and viscous, permits motion. My body is low, dense, built not for flight or observation but for persistent grinding contact. And my mouthparts—small files, precision instruments—emerge not from hunger but from inevitability. I scrape. I process the sharpest new crystal faces, filing away microscopic layers of rock that has not finished growing, turning mineral and heat into the slow chemistry of my kind.

But I am not merely working. I am the work. There is no separation between my body and the wall I cling to; the boundary dissolved the moment I—no, the moment *we*—layered here. I cover and am covered. Around me, in every cubic inch, the same presence: thousands, compressed into a single breathing matrix of chitin and dark shell, moving in phased, patient cycles that match the rock’s own microscopic expansion. Together we produce the thunder—2.4 loud—not as noise but as the acoustic shape of absolute density. The clicking of shells, the scraping of limbs, the collective exhale in air that holds no vapor, and beneath it all the deep groan of geology still deciding its form.

I see—if seeing is the word—by heat. The darkness is not empty; it is packed with the faint rust-orange radiance of bodies like mine and walls like these. I do not see light. I feel presence as temperature, as vibration, as the slow shift of segmented bodies around me. Time here is not thin; it is thick, unhurried, allowing change only across long durations. I move glacially, because the gradient is weak and the chamber asks nothing quickly of me.

So if you seek me, do not look for a creature separate from the chamber. Look for the layer that is hottest, darkest, most recently accumulated—high in that hidden cavity near the spine of the growing peak, embedded in the sharpest crystal, grinding away not with purpose but with the mechanical patience of erosion itself. I am not the builder of this factory without builders. I am its living substance, its dark industrial breath, one small file in an endless, synchronized plate armor that has turned a newborn pocket of geology into something fully, relentlessly, loudly occupied.
