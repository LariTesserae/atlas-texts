I do not remember entering, because entering requires a door, and doors here are only unfinished ribs—half-formed calcite that forgot whether to open or become wall. I am on what might be called the ceiling, though I have not accepted that word. I am small, no larger than a hesitation, and I am translucent not in the simple way of glass but in the way of overlap: through my center I see myself curled like an egg that has not decided to hatch, and I see myself fully extended, limbs—seven, or perhaps they multiply only when I am not counting—bending at joints that open inward, away from the world, and I see the outline that moves slightly behind my actual motion, a ghost negotiating with the air before I commit to it.

The light is everywhere and nowhere. It is cool, bright enough to nearly hurt, and it does not come from above; it comes from the condition of being seen. It illuminates not my surface but my becoming. I can feel the mineral dew sweating from the rock—not ancient stone, but intention toward stone—and sometimes a bead gathers near me, holds in a silence that has weight, then forgets itself back into the damp. I do not drink it. I am part of the same rhythm: appearance, then retreat.

I move constantly, not because I am chased—there is no predator here, only scattered presence—but because stillness feels like a decision I have not finished making. I crawl across the unfinished ribs interchangeably, ceiling and floor being agreements I decline. Gravity is a rumor. My legs, when I have them visible, click softly, opening like frames of doors that never learned their purpose. Sometimes I have none visible, and then I contract in the air just beside myself, propelling forward by a motion that happens in the space I am leaving.

As I move, I leave things behind. Not with intention—there is no technology here, no tool, only the body’s inevitable offering. A needle of calcite, thinner than a thought, rising from a ledge and stopping, tired. A half-arch of saliva-mineral that cups nothing, reaches for nothing, and remains like a question mark made of breath. Once, a pillar that simply ceased mid-air, as if I grew bored of being real and withdrew my support. These marks are scattered, unique, unrepeatable. They are not nests. They are the proof that I was here, thinking about staying.

I am alone, and I am not alone. Others exist in isolation within this shared pocket—some with too many legs unfolding like abandoned architecture, some without visible form, contracting in their own pockets of motion. We do not gather. Density here is not a crowd but a shared loneliness. We share one continuous, interrupted consciousness, yet each of us is enclosed in our own becoming. I see one that is egg, adult, and ghost simultaneously, its form rippling not through space but through states. I might be that one, seen from an angle of unfinished time.

There is also the thread—gray-green, neither plant nor the idea between plant and fungus—creeping along a ledge with the hesitation of something that remembers being seed but has lost the instruction. It does not grow toward the strange light because it is uncertain growth is appropriate. I understand it. I too am uncertain.

The temperature holds at cool—body-cool, the temperature of a mouth held open in expectation. The silence is dense, weighted, not empty. If I listen with the patience of stone, I hear my own joint clicking inward, the crack of new matter deciding to become wall, the sound of condensation deciding whether to fall or retreat. Time loops back on itself here; the moisture I watched vanish returns, identical, carrying the faint impression of having been missed.

I do not live here in the way a creature lives in a world that is finished. I accrete, slowly, visibly, like the wall itself—part of the hidden pocket-scale chamber embedded within a larger earth that has not yet learned its shape. I am the fauna of pre-formation, biology before the category has fully set. I exist in multiple conditions at once, leaving behind only scattered built marks and the faint, half-built proof of my presence.

Here, in this pre-formation, this unfinished thought of earth, I am not a visitor. I am part of the decision the room is still making. And for now—though now is also loose, local, looping—I allow myself to be part of it: translucent, multi-formed, constantly renegotiating my place in a space that has not finished deciding what space is.
