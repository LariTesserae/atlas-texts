I find myself not upon the land but within its occupation—high in one of the compressed spires where the silvery weave has hardened into root-cement and shell-secretion, though “high” is only relative on this flat continent of low warmth and unbroken basalt. There is no sky pressing downward with urgency; the elevation is modest, and I am part of the modesty.

What I am takes time to say, because I do not begin where my outline might suggest.

First, there is only warmth—not radiating, but present, continuous, rising through the fiber and into me from the cooling geology miles below. I have no feet to feel it through soles. Instead, the heat enters where my tissue is thinnest, through the soft grey-white membranes between my parts. I am bone-colored with darker grey patches where the mineral breath has stained me over long exposure. I have many limbs—not arranged for grasping or striking, but for pressing, for distributing weight, for feeling the chemistry of my surroundings through constant gentle contact. They are soft, unarmored, ending not in points but in sensitive filaments that register pressure and taste.

I live inside the tower, though the tower lives inside me, or we share the living. The walls are not dead; they are compressed vegetation, hardened by generations of bodies like mine passing through, secreting, pressing, leaving behind what becomes architecture without intention. I move through the fiber-avenues within it—narrow channels of living rope and calcified mat—flowing rather than walking, my limbs extending to touch both sides, contributing my own thin secretions to the gradual hardening. I am silent. I do not call. The only sounds are the ones I share with everything else: the faint tick of expanding fiber against my flank, the soft abrasion as I slide past another body in the narrow passage, the distant low exhalation of rock contracting far beneath us.

The smell is always present—warm copper, crushed seed-case, never rot, only conversion. I drink not water—there is none that pools or runs—but the atmospheric chemistry and the mineral breath rising through the basalt. The weave surrounds me completely; there is no bare soil visible, no gap for emptiness. Where I rest, the tissue continues immediately into my form, different only in density. I am the fauna, one of the silent multitudes, grey and bone-white, numerous and industrious, but I am also the street I move through, and the wall that holds me, and the warmth that feeds us all.

If I were to travel from this tower—though travel is constant, a slow migration through the grid-like clearings—I would see for miles across the flat plain, not because I possess eyes that see distance, but because I perceive through chemical gradients and the organization of pressure across the long straight avenues of compressed fiber. The continent is vast, unweathered, still deciding what kind of place it will be. I feel that undecision in my own form. I have not finished becoming what I am. My limbs grow and resorb according to the density of the weave around me; my color shifts with the mineral breath I absorb; my path through the city is both journey and construction.

There is no strangeness here, only the absolute fullness of it. The vegetation is lush in the way that saturation is lush—total, interwoven, continuous. The other bodies move in constant quiet procession around me, through me, with me. We do not build with tools. We have no fire, no metal, no wheel, no concept of separation between maker and made. We build by being dense, by negotiating space through our presence, by leaving behind what hardens into bridge and dome and nesting core.

So where I am: embedded in the urban-weave of this newborn world, in a compressed tower of fiber and secretion, part of a migration that never stops, on a flat warm continent of dark glassy basalt still cooling from its birth. And what I am: one of the soft-bodied multitudes, silent, many-limbed, grey-white, indistinguishable from the architecture except in motion—though motion is constant, and in it, I am briefly, densely, myself, part of the same occupation continuing in different densities, in a world that is normal, unfinished, and utterly, quietly alive.
