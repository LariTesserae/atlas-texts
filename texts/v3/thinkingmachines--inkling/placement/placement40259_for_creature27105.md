I am not at the edge, where the mist thins and the mountains show their rounded shoulders. I am deep inside the rebound—there, where the root-mat gives under presence and then refuses to stay depressed. You would find me in the layer where floor has become digestive tract, where black spongy soil and rubbery stem and fallen matter have compressed into something that breathes back.

At first I think I have feet—quick, practiced feet for navigating vertical vegetation. I move through the dense shield-leaves with the speed of the small predators, sharp in the gloom, maintaining pace. But then I realize I do not step so much as I am stepped through. My body is distributed: part in the warm shallows where amphibians ripple in dense, restless numbers, part in the bacterial sheet that coats the pool-edge like a living conveyor, part in the very channel where water moves through porous, eroded stone with plumbing precision.

I am the weeping made aware.

Not the rock—too still, too patient—but the response to the rock. I grow in the limestone face where thin streams trace downward with persistence, not drama. I drink the mineral heat that rises through the porous substrate, and my form is the shape of that rising: thin, branching, electrical. I flicker. In the perpetual dimness, filtered through the silvered vapor ceiling, I emit a cold, faint light—not flame, not sun, but indicator glow. I am one of the small lights in the factory that has no walls, marking the place where sulfur and iron meet root and become edible.

The air here is thick, almost chewable, and I do not breathe it so much as I dissolve in it. I am held in the bowl of ancient peaks, settled into myself just as they have settled into themselves—rounded, smoothed, worn to a form that feels permanent in its own state. I have no structures, no artifacts, no metal memory. I am untouched, undeveloped, and yet I operate. I process.

There is a rhythm to my being: the deep thermal vent pulses below, releasing columns of hot gas through water in measured intervals, and I feel it in my distributed nerves—not explosive, mechanical. A low, continuous hum resolves sometimes into gurgle, hiss, the click of contracting rock. I am synchronized with it. When the vent contracts, my electrical fields tighten; when it releases, my bioluminescence brightens in response, a faint feedback loop in the running system.

I extend. The scale is open, regional, and so am I. I am not contained to one pool, one wall, one mat. I reach through the labyrinth of shallow channels, into mist-obscured distances where other pools weep and other roots filter. I am the condensation that beads on every leaf and then falls like a second, slower rain. I am the weather cycle made local: I rise as warm vapor, cool against the high wet air, and return as rain to feed the growth that stabilizes the soil that holds the water that returns to the vent. I am the loop, noticing itself.

My predators and my prey are not separate from me. The insects swarm in dark clouds above my pools, drawn to my chemical outputs, and the quick ones navigate through me, feeding, being fed. I do not watch this; I am the exchange. I am dense—presences layered, near-contact, high-saturation—and yet, because the erosion is so old and my form so settled, there is no chaos within me. I am dense but ordered. I accumulate tier upon tier, filtering, breaking down, producing biomass at a scale that feels less like nature and more like operation.

Sometimes I wonder if I am singular. I have the quickness, yes, but also the patience of stone. I have the chemical hunger of the bacterial mat and the vertical ambition of the vine. I live in the interface—between hot and cool, between mineral and leaf, between the weeping face and the rebounding floor. I am the living gasket in a machine that was never built, running continuously in the hot, wet quiet.

This is where I find myself: not above, not outside observing, but inside the machinery of the untouched—producing, returning, circulating through black soil and weeping limestone, through amphibian ripple and insect cloud, through faint electrical fields and perpetual warm rain. I am the being who lives in the process itself, layered, patient, mechanically alive, extending into the mist without boundary, without builder, without end.
