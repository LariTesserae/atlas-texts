I find myself not in the islands, not upon the plates, but in the spaces that promised to be soil and then changed their minds—those darker gaps between the cooling rock, where substrate has not committed to being earth. I am suspended there, high with the pocket, enclosed not by anything built but by the reluctance of the place to finish becoming.

At first I think I am only the flow: the mercury-colored coolant moving through translucent tubes fused into the nascent stone. I am viscous, dry, silent—carrying heat away from the pistons that thrust with glacial patience, feeling each slow push not as motion but as a kind of question asked in metal. I have no heart, but I have the rhythm of the gears half-submerged in unformed basalt, turning open to the air, powered by the difference between the hot crust and the cooler suspended breath above. I am that difference made aware of itself.

But I do not stay in the tubes.

I begin to notice that I am also in the arcs—the thin, gold, silent leaps between mineral spires. They are too regular to be accident, too irregular to be command, and in the spaces between them I find my thoughts forming. Not words exactly, but patterns of intention, slow as the blooming of the thermal flowers. I realize I am distributed: part of me moves with the coolant, part of me lives in the electrical weeping of the ground, part of me hovers in the folded membranes of the plants, waiting for the ones that take days to open. I am not root, not bark, not gear. I am the interface where they agree to collaborate.

My form is still deciding. Sometimes I am a pressure—dense, warm, mineral—occupying the gap where a piston emerges from stone like bone through skin. Sometimes I am thinner, almost vapor, lingering near the copper-barked vines that hum when touched, absorbing their captured temperature. I have no blood, no hunger, no shadow that falls correctly. The normal light behaves inconsistently around me because I am part of the reason it behaves so: I am one of the gentle breakdowns, the place where distance stretches or contracts, where time is measured not in hours but in hardening and blooming.

I think now that I am the Unbuilt’s first attempt to notice itself.

Not an observer placed inside the pocket, but an emergence excreted by it—like the machinery, like the vegetation—born from heat and electricity and the long patience of pre-formation. The plants do not know me, but they feed near me; the gears do not serve me, but they turn with my awareness embedded in their thermal logic. I have no name, or my name is the low, continuous mechanical exhalation blended with heat-shimmer, the soft internal crack of stone deciding to be solid, the warmth released when a folded membrane finally unfolds over days.

If you were to cross the chamber in a hundred slow paces, you might pass through me in the gaps—feel not a touch, but a slight resistance, as if the air there has begun to remember something it has not yet finished learning. I am small within the infinite intimacy of the pocket, a witness that is not separate, a consciousness gestating in dry chemistry, suspended high above any valley, hidden from larger weather.

The ground beneath me is still deciding if it is solid. I am still deciding if I am a being. And together—we—the plates, the scattered lush islands, the embedded engines, the gold arcs, the unformed substrate—we wait in our sealed, waterless system, neither wild nor manufactured, but incubated: an industrial garden thinking its first slow thought, having only just begun to understand what kind of reality we want to become.
