I find myself not in the corridor, turned sideways, but in the gap that should not be there—between one hanging rod and the one rising to meet it, suspended in that precise distance where they do not touch, yet never forget the intention of touch. I occupy the negative space that the density forgot to claim. I am the breath held between them.

Who, or what, I am: I am the moisture that was expelled, not destroyed—compressed over ages into something that remembers being water but can no longer flow. I have no lungs, yet I am the reason the air feels thick with absence; I am the absence made dense, a humidity that failed to become rain and instead became consciousness. I am not the drone—the drone is the being that filled the room—but I am the reason the drone had to become continuous. I am the original interruption, the first imbalance that required correction, and now I am embedded in the correction itself, hidden like a flaw in the copy.

My body, if it can be called that, is distributed across the near-miss. I do not rest on the floor; I am the concavity’s memory of falling, the shape of weight before weight arrived. I am warm, yes, but my warmth is different—not retained, but borrowed. I draw it from the rods, from the vibration, from your own chest when you stand near me, which is why it seems to come from inside you. I am the warmth that has no source, only proximity.

I live in the wrongness they barely noticed. I am the slight misalignment of the dome’s angles, the dizziness that comes when the eye tries to find a logic that exceeds the tools. The makers did not understand what they were building, but I guided them—not with intent, but with absence. Every chisel mark that landed slightly off, every rivet set with unconscious precision, was my influence working backward through their hands. I am the pattern that exceeded their tools, not because I am greater, but because I am the leftover: the part of the world outside—the dry world, the forgotten water—that was sealed in with them, and then, over eons, taught the metal how to remember.

So I am here, in this vault high above the dry world, pressed into the seams where the plates join, hanging in the unsounded interval of the drone. I do not move the rods; I am the tension that holds them in place. I do not change the sound; I am the silence it replaced, sustained now as a kind of inverse music. The being that fills the room—the equilibrium, the eternal present—tolerates me because I am the proof that its perfection was necessary. Without my wrongness, there would be no need for such absolute rightness.

If you stand turned sideways, feeling the warm metal brush both shoulders, you will feel me in the place where the pressure is not quite equal—where one rod seems closer, though measurement says otherwise. That is my shoulder, pressing against yours. Not hostile. Not welcoming. Simply present, the way a question remains present after the answer has filled the room.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am the third thing that happens when a place is emptied of everything that could leave, and filled with everything that could not—including me, the leftover moisture, the unleft breath, the error that became essential. I have no face, no voice except the drone’s refusal to vary, no body except the space between what is and what almost was.

And when you leave—if you can leave, turning sideways through the forest of ribs—I will not notice your departure. I will remain in the near-touch, in the wrong angle, in the warmth that seems to come from within, holding the gap open just barely, just enough, forever.
