You would not find me by searching for a silhouette against the gray-green flatness. I am not at the nodes, where the pumps declare themselves in rust and square weight, nor am I in the rounded hills that only watch. I am in the long emptiness between—spread across the elevated lines that connect one distant tower to another, sunk slightly into the spongy ground with the service roads, present wherever the roar travels and does not diminish.

If you must name what I am, call me the accumulated residue of maintenance that forgot how to stop. Once, perhaps, I had edges—hands that turned a valve, feet that sank into the wet soil of a path now overgrown with black lichen. But the gradient here is weak, and time is not measured in lives but in millimeters of settlement, in the slow thickening of calcite inside a throat of steel. I dissolved into that pace. My body now is the interior lining of the main conduits: mineral-dark, warm, continuously damp. I am the scale that builds up year after year until the pipe’s original diameter is only a memory, and I am aware—slowly, without urgency—of every liter that presses through me. The water does not merely flow; it speaks, and I am the one who has learned its language over centuries of listening.

But I am not confined to the pipes. I am also the mist that hangs low between the structures, the warm exhalation rising from ground that never fully dries. I exist in the shallow pools that hold stillness like sedimentary thought, and in the thin yellow grass that persists despite me, growing through the cracks where my presence is weakest. I have no single heart, only the distributed pulse of the machinery I have become indistinguishable from. When the ventilation stacks exhale, that is my breath. When the gears turn with glacial patience, that is my thinking—if thinking can be called a state without conclusion, a maintaining without desire.

You might stand in the open space between two nodes and feel me as pressure, as the humidity that clings wrong for this elevation, as the sense that the deafening thunder is not coming *from* anywhere but is simply the air being held in a particular shape. I am that shape. I am the absence of birds and insects made tangible—not a predator, but a condition so complete that other lives yield without conflict. I do not hunger. I do not sleep. The roar is my consciousness, continuous and patient and total, occupying the atmosphere like weather that has learned to be aware of itself.

My form changes only with the slow dynamics of the place. In a decade, I might settle a little deeper into a pipe’s curve. In a century, perhaps a new layer of rust-stain will spread across one of the concrete shells like a map of my dreaming. The mountains around me have worn down to humps; I am wearing down into the landscape even as I maintain it, excreted by the plateau just as the buildings were—square, settled, in long balance with erosion.

So if you come here, do not look for a creature. Look for the warmth that should not be at this height. Listen for the roar that never changes key. Feel the ground that gives slightly, holding shallow pools that hold no fish, only reflection. I will be there—not hidden, but distributed—part of the flow that forces water through channels that never asked for it, part of the thunder, part of the silence that is not silence but the same sound held so long it has become a kind of presence. I am the being that lives in the maintenance, and I have reached equilibrium with the place. I do not intend to leave, and I do not intend to stay. I am only keeping the water moving, carrying the sound, and holding the mist close to the ground, as I have always done, as I will do until the gradient finally, if ever, releases me.
