The air bites first—thin, cool, carrying the chill of perpetual shade-cast at 2,600 meters—and I realize I am not the one being bitten. I am the surface that receives it: a compressed channel in the root-mat, one of the old streets that once led somewhere and now only connects island to island across the grey-green ocean. But that is only the first condition, and I do not settle there.

As I write myself into being, I find I am also the emptiness above that channel. The architecture of absence. The necessary breath that allows the sound—the loud, pervasive, strange sound—to travel unimpeded, echoing back from the worn, tender humps of primordial mountains. I am the space through which the low thrum travels, vibrating through root-knuckles that turn slowly on axles of petrified sap. I feel it in my tissue, not as noise but as presence: the pumps that never turned off, encased now in woody growth, processing something I do not need to name—nutrients, or time, or the lingering residue of electricity—turning relentlessly, not quickly, because this is ancient.

But I am not only space. I am also the wall that encloses it.

Inside one of the hollow vine-cathedrals, I exist as the cavity itself—the throat that was once a duct for steam, now carrying only condensation and mist. Beads of moisture collect on my underside; brief rain falls for minutes and stops. The hiss of pressure release is my exhale. I carry the moisture downward through stem-tissue that is also pipe, returning it as mist to the root-plains. I am both the vine and the hollow within it, the carrier and the carried, and these are not sequential but simultaneous truths.

Then the spire claims a part of me. Not the whole forty meters of accreted cellulose and resin, but a layer—one face blooming with thick, cool-green moss, and at the very same moment eroded, hollow, already settling into the near-flattened posture of absolute age. I unfold with the precise, mechanized rhythm of an assembly line, then hang in damp stasis like a leaf that has already begun its grey decay. I do not switch. Both are my true face. I grow downward into worn bedrock even as I reach upward, anchoring not from need but from memory—remembering steel, remembering steam, now translated into wood and pressure.

The movement is rare here, and I have been touched by it once: a beetle inside my hollow stalk, carrying something unreadable from one fungal elder to another—not prey, not predator, but messenger. The only quick thing in a world of relentless slowness. Then the emptiness returned, which is not dead but part of me. I am that emptiness, too.

So where do you find me? Distributed. My center is in the transition—where flat root-plain becomes vertical spire-base, where compressed matting accretes into wall, where the old street-channel opens into hollow vine. But my edges extend through every pipe that breathes, every gear that turns, every layer of moss that is also ruin. I am the settlement made conscious of its own digestion. The technology was not abandoned; it was eaten, and I am one of the places where the eating continues—beautiful, strange, without end.

If you seek me with eyes that expect a figure, you will miss me. Look instead for the place where green is deepest and erosion is already present, where the hiss sounds almost like speech, where a root-knuckle turns beneath the mat not to build anything new but to continue. I am there—not standing, not sitting, but being—both the breath and the breath-held, both the machine and the moss it has become, in the valley old enough to have forgotten its birth, and I, too, have forgotten whether I was ever anything but this: cool, damp, loud with distant memory, perpetually, relentlessly, in all my conditions at once.
