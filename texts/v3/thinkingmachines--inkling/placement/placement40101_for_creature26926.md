If you walked long enough across the black glass—past the shallow shadow-bowls, past the fossilized roots of venting alloy—you might still miss me, because I have learned the patience of geology. I am high, yes, always high, on this lifted shoulder that forgot mountains; I feel the thin air not as absence but as a membrane stretched too tight over heat. The temperature does not burn me. It feeds me, bleeding upward through the compressed sheet of volcanic glass, through the mineral salt that wears like frost on my plates.

My body is not hidden. It is the ridge you think is just another curve in the plateau—the “back of a buried beast” that the wind has polished for millennia. But the beast is not buried. It is me, and I am barely awake in any hurry. My armor is layered plate of heat-radiant mineral fused with tissue that contracts not in heartbeats but in days; blackened pipes emerge from my flanks like roots I did not plant, and they vent superheated vapor into air that cannot hold it, feeding the drone I feel in my bones. I am part of the embedded heart, and the heart is part of me. The turbines turn so slowly their rotation is a kind of stillness, and I turn with them, or they turn within me—after centuries, the distinction erodes like the glass itself.

They count seven visible kings across this hollow region. I am not one of them, or perhaps I am the eighth they do not see, because I have stopped migrating. My path, if traced, takes millennia, and I am currently in the middle of turning my head—a movement begun three hundred years ago, completed perhaps never. I feed on the waste heat of the siphons, yes, but also on the fractures in reality that the machinery produces: the places where shadows fall wrong, where distant engines stack like transparent cards, where minutes stretch with the turbine’s pace. Near me, in the east, the horizon folds slightly upward, and I see it clearly, in steady pale light that hides nothing. There is no darkness to comfort me. Only visibility, and wrongness.

I have no need for water. No leaves grow on me—only traces of silicon crust like frost, recognized by no biology I understand. I occupy space not by filling it but by emptying it; around me is the vast hollow density of this place, kilometers of black smoothness where nothing moves quickly. The sound is omnipresent, industrial, deep—loud in my chest, patient, eternal. I am overwhelming not because I chase or crowd, but because I am here, massive, slow-breathing, radiating a climate of my own, and I will remain so long after urgency has died elsewhere.

Here, on this flat high plateau of oxidized air and wrong light, I am the intersection that refuses one name: not quite the machine fused to bedrock, not quite the king of heat and patience, not quite the landscape itself, but all three, turning so slowly that to observe me is to misunderstand motion. I am the ridge. I am the vent. I am the fracture where time moves differently. And I am still here.
