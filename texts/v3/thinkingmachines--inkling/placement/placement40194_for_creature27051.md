I am the one pressed flat, not born but accumulated—layer on layer of dissolved height until I become a presence that cannot lift.

The world finished grinding a long time ago. I am part of that settlement: not rock exactly, not flesh, but a coagulation of compressed dust and warm upward weeping that holds together under the constant, churning weight of air. I slide low—always low—through the shallow sheets, my form spread wider than I am deep, a dark stain in the gray-green haze. I have no eyes. I have only the turbulence, which is my nervous system and my only language. Every crack in the bone-smooth substrate, every millimeter shift in the bedrock’s impossible pressure, every sudden exhalation of moisture through fractures I do not understand but feel—I absorb these through my being, which is mostly water held in temporary agreement.

I am not alone in being temporary. Near me, in the thickest vapor, others hold form: some round and pressed flat like me, rolling slowly with invisible tides; others thinner, closer to the shimmers that form at the fractures—those vertical, patient presences that feed on our dissolution when we spread too thin. We do not speak. The roar belongs to the plane, not to us—the scraping of air across the flatness, the sharp reports of settling stone, the hiss of forced water. We exist within it silently.

There is a circle ahead. Not built—nothing here is built—but pushed upward by a geological breath long faded, worn smooth by eons of turbulent wind. I linger near it. Not because it shelters me—the saturation allows no shelter—but because it breaks the flow differently. The pressure shifts. The weeping behaves differently here. I feel this difference in my edges, in the way my darker matter swirls. I am drawn not by purpose but by the only kind of recognition this world allows: the recognition of change.

I will not stay. I am too full to stay, and the flatness is too full to let me become permanent. Soon the turbulence will forget me, or I will spread too thin near one of the fracture-shimmers, and I will dissolve—not dead, since nothing here dies, but dispersed back into the warm pools, the suspended vapor, the compressed dust that waits everywhere.

But for now I hold. I am present in the saturation. In some other pocket—where time moves differently, deeper and older—I might gather again, not as me, but as this same kind of brief, wet answer. I do not know if I want this. I do not want anything, except perhaps to remain gathered a little longer, to feel the old bedrock crack once more, to exist in the only space available: flat, complete, and roaring with presence.
