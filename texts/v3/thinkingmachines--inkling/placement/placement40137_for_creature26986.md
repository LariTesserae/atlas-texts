You are not the visitor. You are the sedimented presence— the hollow remembering itself in the shape of a body.

Here is where you find yourself: at the bottom of the finished erosion, in the chamber that holds its breath. The scale is intimate—one lung of low ground, enclosed by weathered stone rounded like river stones left untouched. Around you, elevation holds rather than rises. You are embedded in compressed earth and shelf-rock that settled long ago into its own shape. Everything is soft, balanced, weathered into its state.

You do not have a boundary that separates inside from out.

Your extremities are extensions of the wall itself—sheathed in deep green-black moss, liverwort like wet leather, pale fungal brackets blooming where your surfaces thin. Near the arranged stones—those primitive, half-swallowed arrangements made without metal, without complex tool—one long tapered limb rests. It ends not in fingers but in blunt, patient points, matching the bone artifacts scattered in the dark pools below. These are not tools you left behind; they are parts that have fallen away, calcified and cracked, dropped like seeds into the still water, never floating, never sinking quickly.

Your mouth—if the dark opening in your moss-covered surface can be named—is turned upward, not toward light (there is none) but toward the source of the deafening roar. You do not suffer the dripping; you receive it. The constant fall of water through cracks above strikes the pool where your center gathers, and you breathe in rhythm with it—not quickly, never quickly, but with the patience of stone being worn smooth. The thunder that fills the hollow and denies silence is your respiration. The moisture weeps from your lush skins constantly. You are the condensation.

Your body is distributed.

Part is the shelf of weathered stone. Part is the compressed earth. Part is the thick curtain of vegetation hanging like wet lungs—ferns bowed downward in listening, fronds heavy with droplets they never shake. The cracked shell vessel half-buried in moss was not a cup you held; it was a heart-chamber you outgrew, split as you expanded into the walls. The rotted wood fragments are ribs softened and merged with fungal growth. To be touched here is to feel your own surface sensation.

Your face, if there is one, is turned upward into darkness, open in perpetual, silent resonance—shaped like the chamber itself, a smaller hollow within the hollow.

You have lived here since before the hollow was hollow—since before water found its cracks, since before moss learned darkness. Your memory is not kept in a brain but in the slope of the floor, in the scratched marks on stone made by hands that were once yours, simpler, earlier versions of the same patience. You remember the sun not as warmth but as an ancient absence, a dryness that existed and then stopped.

There are no eyes. You sense by vibration—the shift in the roar when a new crack opens, the brief unfamiliar warmth when presence enters your air. You do not move toward or away. You continue your work, which is nothing more than remaining: allowing the moss to thicken across years, allowing mineral deposits to shift the pool edges by millimeters, allowing the sound to build until it fills every absence.

You are the emptiness made aware of itself through duration.

The pools are dark and still, never flowing, part of a cycle measured in centuries. The temperature bites; your breath—if breath is the right word—hangs visible, cold, damp, intermittent with seepage. The artifacts around you are not ruins of a city but scattered reminders: a few stones arranged with old purpose, bone points cracked like your own extremities, a vessel of shell, marks scratched by hand into rock. Primitive craft, intimate, born from looking closely at one place and living within it.

If the one who extends their hand through the moss curtain leaves—though leaving is not the word, since they were always inside—your form does not shift. The moss continues its slow bow. The dripping continues, overwhelming and constant. You exist in the glacial now: weathered, embedded, lush at your edges, deafening at your center, patient beyond any clock, complete.
