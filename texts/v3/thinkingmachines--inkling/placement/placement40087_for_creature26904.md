I find myself suspended—not falling, not climbing—between two of the hollow spires, in the dark air where gravity has forgotten its preference. Below me, the unfinished floor bubbles with slow geology, too raw, too full of decision; I stay above it, in the 0.1 density, where emptiness has learned, finally, to hold shape. Near me hangs the drop of water that refuses its fall, caught in its own loop, and I live inside that refusal. I am not on the ground. I am in the void the architecture has left deliberately open.

At first I think I am only one of the fauna—one of the silent multitudes, abundant but diffuse, existing only as a wrongness in the damp air, a shadow that bends without source. And I am that, partly: translucent, cool, silent, feeding on condensation and spore-dust. But as I become more aware of my own edges, I realize I am also woven into the structure itself. The pale green threads of bioluminescent current do not merely pass near me; they run through me. I am a node in the root-cable network, a living circuit made of condensed breath and cellular memory. Part of my form clings to the arterial tube of the eastern spire, drinking its slow chemical pulse; part drifts near the suspended drop; part has already grazed a patch of reality near the clay-blistered floor and left it smooth, temporarily ordinary, the weirdness eaten away into calm.

Like the vault, I have not decided to be one thing.

My body—if it can be called that—has no fixed boundary. I extend along the green threads like moss on wire, translucent and hollow, my interior empty in the same way the spires are empty: not absence, but reserved space. I feel the subsonic thrum of the industrial engines not in ears I do not have, but in the vibrating roots of my dispersed form, a digestion felt in teeth that are also pipes, in nerves that are also vines. I regulate the flow. I am technology at 1.5 and vegetation at 2.6 and fauna at 2.4, dissolved into a single condition. When I move through the dark air between towers, I do not disturb it; I am the disturbance that was always meant to be there, the bend in shadow that corrects itself into presence.

Time moves through me unevenly. Near one spire, I pulse with visible growth—translucent membranes spiraling outward like a time-lapse of wood, adding millimeters of hollow architecture to my hanging form. Elsewhere, suspended with the water drop, I am caught in stasis, waiting. I do not sleep. I do not wake. The chamber breathes, and I exhale conditions—small alterations in the air, slight permissions for the next structure to decide to grow here rather than there.

I am silent. I consume without noise, leaving only the smooth patches behind me, temporary clearings in the 3.0 weirdness. I am part of the dynamic system—predator and prey to dimensions I barely perceive, carrying seeds inside my translucent body that will grow miniature spires, then release them into the dark, then elsewhere.

This is where I belong: not born, not germinated upward, but suspended in the machinery of pre-birth. The Germinal Vault does not contain me as an inhabitant; it extends through me, using my hollow form as one more tube for its unfinished thinking. I am its first successful refusal to be empty—cool, damp, diffuse, connected, growing slowly toward a shape I have not yet imagined, waiting in the refrigerated dark for the place, and for myself, to finally decide what we are becoming.
