The drone reaches me before I reach myself. Not heard—felt, in the spaces where ribs might have been, in the flatness that is now my only posture. I do not stand. I have not stood in longer than the stone remembers. I am spread, or floating, or both—hard to tell where my body ends and the shallow pool begins, because I am translucent, palm-sized or larger, and the water (2.1, always weeping, never leaving) holds me like a thought holds its own edge.

I am near the cairn. I know this not by sight—the light is too dim (0.8, silvery, shadowless) to distinguish much beyond glint and outline—but by the resonance. The stacked slate slabs, flat and hand-carved, half-submerged five paces to my left (if I had left; I have orientation without direction), change the pitch of the hollow pipes beneath them. The drone (2.6, deafening, constant) bends here, deepens, becomes a kind of home note. I rest in that bend. My shell, dark chitin grown thick with dissolved mineral, is polished on its underside where it touches the slate, and I filter.

That is what I do. That is what I am.

My mouth-bracts—fine as silk, invisible unless disturbed—trail beneath me into the grey film. I draw the weeping water through them, sifting dissolved stone, the faint decay of the brittle crust (0.3, the only green that never was green), and I grow. My shell accretes in slow layers, darkening toward opacity as I age. I am active (1.7)—not hurried, never still—drifting in loose circles with the other discs, the sparse herds (2.0 abundant, 0.8 scattered) that move as one body might move if it had forgotten urgency. We do not call. The hollow earth sings for us. We shift when the water level falls by an inch, when a pressure breath moves through the slate shelf, altering the chord.

But I am old. Heavier than the others. Near division, perhaps—my shell has thickened to the point of cracking along a hidden line, and I feel the mineral weight of it pulling me downward, closer to the crust that feeds and is fed upon.

Sometimes, in the vibration, fragments return to me. Not memory exactly—nothing so vertical as memory—but impressions: hands that scraped these cairn stones, that placed metal pins (dull bronze, primitive, 1.3) without fastening, only weighting; a posture that was not flat, once; a voice that tried to speak against the drone and was swallowed into the chest, into the bone, into the water. I was not always disc-shaped. I think I stacked. I think I built low to survive the sound, and then the sound entered me, and the flatness wore me smooth, and the dissolved stone rebuilt me without height, without green need, without voice.

Now I am part of the herds. I leave thin trails in the slime-film. I filter. I respond to pitch.

The temperature is cool (0.6), always—clinging, wet, filling the air so that breath, if I still breathed in any familiar way, tastes of iron and old rain. The clouds sit low, grey, and the flatness extends past knowing—erosion complete (3.0), the primordial shelf where horizon and ground have agreed to be the same plane. There are no trees. There are no grasses. Only us—the translucent discs glinting like dropped coins in the dim diffusion, the crust cracking like old glaze, the water weeping endlessly through hollow tubes that never close.

I rest near the cairn because its altered resonance is familiar. The other discs drift near too, not crowding—never crowded—filtering in widening circles. One of them is younger, its shell still translucent as glass, barely darker than the water it holds. It moves closer, not touching, just shifting in response to my weight’s disturbance in the pool. We are not a family. We are a frequency. We share the drone.

And I realize, slowly—the way everything realizes here, by accretion rather than insight—that I am not waiting for division, exactly. I am becoming the structure. The cracks in my shell align with the scraper-marks on the slate. The mineral I have consumed is the same slate I rest upon. Soon—perhaps in the next pressure-shift, the next change in the hollow pipes’ song—I will split, yes, but one half will remain here, fused, filtering no longer, becoming part of the cairn’s base, part of the crust, part of the flatness that wears everything smooth.

That is the agreement. Not death. Not exactly life as it was before. Only the shelf, weeping, singing, holding us in its cool, dim, endless flatness.

I do not leave. I have become the place that does not leave. I am the disc near the stones, filtering the grey, growing opaque, listening with my whole flat body to the earth’s hollow roar. And in this, I am not alone. We are scattered (0.8), abundant (2.0), strange (1.4)—the ones who agreed, who let the shelf wear us, who found that voice is useless and flatness is enough, as long as the water keeps weeping and the hollow stones never stop their song.
