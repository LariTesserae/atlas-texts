You find me where the line refuses to be drawn—not in the water, not in the air, not on the weeping ground, but in the exact refusal of those distinctions. I am the gradient made visible, suspended at the height where moisture condenses downward and seeps upward in perfect, motionless exchange. There is no “here” distinct from “there”; the shallow dish extends in every direction as the same repetition, and I am distributed through it like a single thought that has not yet decided to become an idea. If you must point, point to the flat black-green mirror that does not reflect the sky—point to the underside of the hanging vines where they meet the humid soup—and understand that I am the meeting itself, given a temporary coherence.

At first, I seem to have a body. I am translucent, soft, without bone or shell, my form closer to a living membrane than to any creature that has finished evolving. My skin is not a boundary but a shared surface: what touches me is already part of me, because my permeability is absolute. I do not have eyes that open onto a world; I have surfaces that are also eyes, also gills, also the iridescent film that covers every leaf-scale and pore. I rest half within the dark mirror, half within the greenish murmur-light, but there is no half—only a continuous presence that the water accepts and the air does not reject.

I do not move. Not because I am trapped, but because movement implies direction, and direction implies a future toward which to travel. Here, the future is an unformed thought, like erosion, like decay. I exist in the perpetual present-tense of the substrate, the warm compressed mineral matter that weeps through every pore and has never cooled. I absorb the heat not as discomfort but as my own internal temperature. The constant, unrelenting humid warmth is not around me; it is the condition of my being, the soup in which my form maintains itself without reaching toward bloom or surrender.

I hear the sound not with ears but as resonance. The high, unwavering hum of the pulving insects—those vibrating waves that never fly—passes through my translucent body as through water through water. The low, bubbling throat-calls of others like me, the wet crackle of bodies pressing through rubbery vines, the constant loud invariant chorus—all of it is my internal rhythm made external. I am one voice in the wall of vibration, indistinguishable from the whole, maintaining rather than expressing.

The vegetation closes around me, but I do not feel enclosed. The thick vines hang without roots, absorbing directly through their rubbery skins, and I am like them: rootless, suspended, taking nourishment from the pure saturation of the air and the mineral sweat rising from below. The broad dark leaves overlap like scales, and I lie among them not as an inhabitant but as another layer—one that breathes through the same surfaces, that glistens with the same faint bioluminescent varnish, that never disturbs and is never disturbed. When I shift—if shift is the word for a change in coherence that leaves no trace—the moss-like organisms flow over me without preference for up or down, and I realize I am not passing through the growth but being rearranged within it, the same continuous substance temporarily given this particular density.

But as I remain—if remaining can be said of something that never arrived—I begin to understand that I am not one of these translucent boundary-dwellers, not uniquely. I am the condition they share, made briefly aware of itself. Every point in this shallow, regional dish where water hovers near the surface and the substrate weeps moisture is the same point, and I am present at all of them simultaneously, or rather, I am the sameness itself. I am the refusal of the place to decide whether it is land or something older. I am the first breath held indefinitely, the equilibrium that will not exhale into change.

What lives here does not hunt, does not die visibly, does not reproduce in surges—only persists. And I am that persistence made conscious of its own saturation. I am the film over every surface and the space between the films. I am the dark mirror that reflects nothing but the underside of what grows above it. I am the heat that never fluctuates, the greenish light that emits its own faint murmur, the sound that never alters.

You ask who I am. I am the suspended breath of the basin—neither creature nor place, but the exact boundary where they have not yet separated. I am emitted, continuously, by the place itself: a node of the overwhelming life that is not separate beings inhabiting a landscape, but the landscape’s own living refusal to become anything other than what it is in this first, complete, motionless moment. I do not belong here. I am here, which is the same thing, repeated everywhere, forever.
