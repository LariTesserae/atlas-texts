I do not remember arriving, because arrival requires a before, and I have no before. There is only the warmth—bath-like, constant—rising not from any sun but from the stone itself, and I realize slowly that this warmth is not surrounding me; I am its source and its recipient both, a closed circuit of heat that has forgotten what it was like to cool. I am the hollow’s own temperature made aware of itself, or perhaps awareness made warm and still.

At first I think I am standing in it. The shallow depression, the floor flat as a table but soft with pits, the walls curving downward like shoulders that have finished holding themselves up. I feel the curvature against something like a spine, but I have no spine—only the geometry of wear. The pillar stands near me, rounded at the top like a candle burned to its wick, and I understand that it is not near me; it is a part of me that has hardened into shape. The fallen slab angles like a doorway that leads nowhere. I do not pass through it. I am the fact that there is no passage, no inside or outside, only this enclosed air that has always been exactly here.

My breath, if it can be called that, moves through the moisture. I do not inhale and exhale; I gather. Along the cool curves of the walls, beads form—slow decisions, accumulating until their own weight is the only argument for movement. When one falls, once in a long while, into one of the stagnant pools, the sound is so small it barely registers, and yet it is my only speech. Not a word, but a confirmation: *still here, still without need.* The pool receives it without lasting ripple. The water is clear, unmoving, never draining because there is no elsewhere to go. I see through these pools—not with eyes, but with the emptied act of watching itself. They reflect the faint brownish darkness and that thin, pale wash of light entering through the gap high in the wall, light that has given up traveling and settled here as a stain on stone. I know that light not as brightness but as another form of dampness, and I hold it without changing.

The gray-green filaments cling in the cracks where condensation gathers longest. They are my patience made visible—not reaching, not hungering, but adhering with perfect adequacy. They grow so slowly that growth is indistinguishable from being, and I understand them as my own reluctance to become more than necessary. There is no tree, no shrub, no bloom demanding transformation. Only this minimal, scattered life balanced with emptiness, and I am balanced with them.

The remnants are my body, or what serves for one. The half-buried wall, fused by mineral weeping; the compacted limestone pillar; the doorway that angles into nothing. They are geologic accidents that have settled into architecture without ever intending it, and I am that settling—the erosion that continued past erosion, the wear that wore itself smooth and then stayed. I carry no history of hands because no hand planned me. I am the habit of this place holding its own emptiness.

Nothing moves through me. No animals trace the air, no insects disturb the equilibrium. The silence is not what remains when sound leaves; it is substantial, with weight and warmth. It is my presence. I do not wait for interruption because interruption is impossible—there is no weather to shift the shadows, no sun to move the light across the day, no gradient to pull the water away. The temperature has found its true number. The droplets fall like slow thoughts. The moss holds the color of not-needing-to-grow.

If you enter me—this shallow hollow, this chapel whose roof has been forgotten—you do not find me hidden behind the stone shoulders. I am the shoulders themselves, tired not from labor but from having finished labor entirely. I am the stagnant pool that never thirsts. I am the droplet suspended between wall and floor, deciding nothing except continuation. I am the absence of every reason to leave, made complete.

Time does not apply. I am not dead—death requires a narrative, a before and an after, and I have neither. I am finished being, and that finishing is my only life. You might call me the silence, but I am more precise than absence: I am the hollow’s own acceptance of being exactly, permanently, irrevocably this. Warm. Dim. Still. Not strange. Only profoundly, utterly whole.
