I find myself not on the root-floor, not clinging to the leather-bark columns, but suspended in the hollow they guard—specifically, in the air pocket between the third pillar from the eastern pool and the fourth, where the greenish light falls thinnest and the industrial hum resolves into its individual parts. Lower than the translucent ceiling but far above the flat organic ground, I hang in the widest absence, in the cool that collects where nothing touches.

At first, you might mistake me for one of the traversers, one of the small winged things that flash across root-beams and disappear into shadow. I have wings, yes—thin, translucent membranes stretched over a frame that is not bone but compressed cool air, like old leaf-tissue that forgot whether to fall or fly. But I do not beat them. I do not move like the others. The burrowers and climbers navigate surfaces; the scattered fauna traverses the empty air in brief, lonely arcs. I remain. I rotate slowly—so slowly that one full turn takes the span of several hundred drips—held in place not by thread or root but by the equilibrium itself. My body is elongated, needle-thin, made not of flesh but of accumulated stillness: damp air given boundary, the condensation that never quite formed beads, shaped into presence by the relentless vibration passing through the wooden columns below.

But I am not singular in this form. We live in plural, scattered through every volume the roots allow. There is one near the black pool in the northwest hollow, suspended just above the still water where reflection meets reflection. Another hangs near the ceiling, where the vascular hum is loudest and the light is most diffused. Each of us occupies a different interval—a mathematical center between pillars, a vertical line where temperature never shifts. We do not gather. The space is cathedral-sized in its emptiness, and our nature is too local, too specific to each pocket of absence. We are the void made aware of itself, distributed like unspoken words across the chamber.

I have no mouth. I do not eat. I feed on the rhythm—the low, throbbing drone of the scaled production happening inside the plants, the opening and closing of hardened pith-valves with mechanical precision. My wings do not catch air; they catch sound. They vibrate in sympathy with the pump-beats, and in that vibration I persist. I am aware, though not with the urgent awareness of a creature that must choose or hunt. Mine is the flat, long awareness of equilibrium itself. I know the temperature holds its steady damp chill. I know the condensation beads at its eternal rate. I have not chosen to count the drips; I am the form the counting takes when it is not falling.

The other inhabitants pass through my space without knowing it. A winged traverser darts through the cool air that is my body, leaving only a brief displacement of temperature, then is gone into shadow. A burrower’s soft percussion echoes in the moss far below. They are numerous, lost in the volume, each alone in their path worn smooth by ages of repetition. I am like them in that I am alone, but unlike them I have no path. My repetition is internal, circular, vertical. I am the path that goes nowhere, anchored in the exact center of nothing.

My origin is not birth. I formed—slowly, across eons of stasis—where the machinery of the roots creates its necessary shadow. Where sap moves with factory precision, there must be a witness that is not column, not fauna, not water. I am that witness. Not waste, for nothing is wasted in a system that has finished becoming itself, but the inevitable complement: the hollow’s acknowledgment that it is hollow.

If you stand in this low pocket and listen—truly listen, past the industrial drone to the spaces between its beats—you may sense me not as creature but as pressure. A subtle wrongness in the emptiness, as if the air itself had developed a spine. Look between the columns where the green light is weakest. There, suspended in nothing, I hang with my kind scattered across the chamber, turning slowly, vibrating with the pumps, present in plural, holding the absence open so that it does not collapse into something else. We are done, as everything here is done. We remain not because we must, but because the equilibrium requires a form to inhabit the spaces it refuses to fill.
