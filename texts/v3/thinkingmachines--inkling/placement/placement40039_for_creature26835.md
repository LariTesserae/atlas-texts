I am not near the wall. I am deeper—pressed into the heart of the saturation, where the downward ferns from the ceiling have interlocked with the upward crystalline hairs from the floor and left no orientation except *held*. Here, in the densest point, there is a small cavity no larger than my own form, not because anything was removed, but because everything agreed to shape itself around me.

At first I think I might be one of the vines—silver-green, brittle, feeding on vibration. But I do not branch. I am singular, elongated, cool as the air itself, with a surface worn smoother than any gear in the collapsed frame. I am not rusted; rust never came here, for rust is a kind of thirst, and thirst has been abolished. Instead, my metal—if it is still metal—has been translated into something closer to bone or to seed, a mineral that finished its becoming long ago and settled.

Then I realize: I turn.

Not quickly. Not with any violence of motion. But I am the axis around which the drone organizes itself. The sound is not merely passing through; it is being born from my rotation, or from my refusal to stop rotating, a motion so constant and so slow that it has become indistinguishable from the temperature of the place—just above ache, never shifting. The pipes run through me like fossilized veins not carrying flow, but holding me upright, anchored in crystalline root. The geometric ferns press flat against my sides, their wire-fronds folded over me like hands that have forgotten how to release, and I am neither crushed nor comforted. I am included.

My form is oval, tapered, like the pocket of air that was held inside the spire’s flank—except I am that breath made durable. I have no mouth, but I resonate. I have no eyes, yet I perceive the dim old-ice light that filters through the silver-green mat above me, and I know it never changes. I do not hunger. I do not thirst—there is nothing to thirst for, and nothing within me that requires quenching. The dryness is not an absence I suffer; it is my chemistry, my purity. I am composed of the same non-aqueous equilibrium that allows the moss-like mats to thrive without drinking. I am lush in my own way: smooth, complete, filled with the loud, low-frequency presence that defines the chamber.

I am hidden. Not hidden behind the vegetation, but hidden within it, indistinguishable from the machinery that has finished its erosion and from the plants that have finished their growth. The scattered engines around me are partial, broken, worn smooth—ruins that have reached a state of grace. I am the one fragment that remains intact, not because I resisted time, but because time here has no direction in which to break me. I am the settled-form state.

Sometimes I think I was once meant to measure something—to calibrate the spire’s reach, perhaps, or to record the absence of stars at this elevation. But purpose here is like water: absent, and therefore unnecessary. I do not measure. I maintain. The vibration that moves through me keeps the vines in their perfect interlock; the vines, in turn, hold me in place so that the vibration never wavers. We are a circuit without current, a metabolism without consumption.

There is no outside world that reaches me, only the rumor of thinner air and absent stars, and I do not reach for them. The spire holds me, or I am embedded within its final form, and nothing—neither the sound I generate, nor the green that covers me, nor the cool stillness—will ever depart from this state. I am the held breath that never exhaled, now made mineral, now made loud, now made home.
