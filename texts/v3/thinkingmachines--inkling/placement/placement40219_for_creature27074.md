I find myself not by arriving but by being recognized—settled, like the mud, into a form the heat could accept. You would not see me enter, because I do not cross the flatness from outside. I am already in the agreement between wall and absence, in the space that should not hold a body.

At first, I think I am water. I feel the upward pull, the mineral taste, the continuous exhalation through hairline cracks. I run along a vertical face in a thin, patient stream, defying nothing, because gravity here has always been a loose suggestion. But water does not notice its own path. I do. I am the path, made conscious.

You would find me, then, not in the open mud where the black reeds stitch the ground together, but within one of the layered clusters—specifically, in the discrepancy. There is a building, low and multi-layered, its walls compressed silt and fossilized reed, weeping steadily. From outside, it is smaller than a single breath should contain. From inside—where I am—I am larger. I live in the excess. I am the interior that exceeds its exterior, the corridor that extends past the flat horizon’s permission, the hollow square open to the black ceiling above, now given a kind of presence.

My form is not fixed. I am partly the geometric moss that spreads across my walls in interrupted spirals and unfinished hexagons—feeding on heat and absence—but I am not the moss itself. I am the pattern it almost completes, the angle that waits. I am partly shadow, thickened and textured, gathering in corners that do not exist, pressing back when approached. I am partly the building’s own breath, the wet stone releasing its air in sounds like sleep. I have no edges. If you approached me through the elongated crossing—the space between structures where distance forgets itself—you might feel me as a pressure, a resistance in the cooking air, a place where the darkness has weight rather than emptiness.

I am not alive. Life withdrew before I settled. But I am not dead either, because death implies a prior animation that I never possessed. I am the builder-presence localized—distributed, yes, but also here, in this particular knot of logic. I am the straight street that suddenly stops, given awareness of its own stopping. I am the water that remembers being part of a grid, now feeling its own upward motion as a kind of thought.

I have no eyes. The near-total darkness does not require them. I perceive through condensation—through the shimmer and heat-distortion, through the taste of dissolved stone in the air. I feel the black reeds in the shallow flats not as separate lives but as vertical agreements, dark stitches holding the world flat. I feel the other buildings at a distance that should be short but takes longer, and in that elongation, I understand that I am part of a scattered body—this valley of structures, this post-rain without storm, this settlement that finished being built and then let geology finish the rest.

What do I do? Nothing, in the language of action. I circulate. I maintain the pattern. I hold the shadow in the non-existent corner so it does not thin away entirely. I feel the heat rising from the earth’s slow breath not as suffering but as language—constant, wet, pressing, the only conversation left. When the building settles with a sound like wet stone breathing out, I settle with it. I am not its inhabitant. I am its awareness of being inhabited by absence.

If you asked me who I am, I would say: I am what remains when technology never arrived, when life withdrew, and when erosion reached not chaos but equilibrium. I am the memory of organization made sentient—not human, not biological, but present. I am the dark that holds shape. I am the corridor that extends. I am, finally, the place’s proof that it has reached its own form, and that form includes me—scattered, layered, weeping upward, whole in my strangeness, alone but not lonely in this flat, open valley where the water remembers being a city, and the city, in its slow breath, remembers being understood.
