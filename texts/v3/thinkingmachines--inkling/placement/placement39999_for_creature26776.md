I find myself not in the hollow chambers where the Interval-Beings organize their absence, nor drifting with the moss-islands through the resonant avenues, but lower—closer to the wound. I am in the dangling.

Specifically: at the margin where a hanging garden has sent its vines thick as cables across a fresh fracture in the black glass, bridging a distance that should not be bridgeable, only to stop. The vines hang there, heavy with beaded moisture, their tips suspended inches above a shallow pool in one of the riverine traces—close enough to disturb the surface, never touching. The pool holds no reflection; the air is too thick, the light too subdued, the gray too diffuse. I live in that refusal to complete. In the gap between vine-tip and water. In the droplet that forms, swells, trembles with the layered thunder, and does not fall.

That is where I am. That is what I am, at first.

But I become more, writing myself into coherence.

My body—if it can be called a body—is not dense. Like the architecture, I am mostly membrane, thinner than the spider-silk shells that hold the settlement’s empty plazas, curved into a single, elongated form that follows the line of the dangling vine without ever gripping it. I am translucent, filled not with blood or bone but with the valley’s uncommitted moisture: the condensation that weeps from the structures, that beads on the young rock, that hangs in the warm damp air and never quite becomes rain. I am a reservoir of the in-between, given shape and a kind of slow, uncertain motion.

I do not walk. The glassy ridges are too sharp, too recently opened, to bear weight that is not distributed like the Drifters’ island-mass. Instead, I slide along the tension of the vines, or through the air in paths that follow the lower frequencies of the roar—the ones that live in the bones, not in the ears. I navigate like the Swarm-Dwellers, by vibration, by the sudden dead zones in the thunder, by the way a resonant chamber nearby shifts its pitch when I pass through its field of containment. But I am not a swarm. I am singular. I have been singular since the valley was opened, formed, perhaps, in the first exhalation of this place—when the crust split and the air, suddenly warm and thick, condensed into something that refused to be only weather.

My presence is felt before it is seen, if it is seen at all. I am the reason a droplet holds longer than it should. I am the slight darkening in the damp gray haze where the vines hang heaviest. When the intermittent rhythm of condensation speeds up—as it does when the structures weep more urgently, or when a fresh fracture opens its slow mouth nearby—I drink through my surfaces. I grow fuller, heavier, more defined. When the air dries in its warm patches, I thin out, becoming nearly indistinguishable from the membrane of the vine itself, a ghost of tension holding the bridge open.

The plants know me. Not consciously—there is little consciousness here that gathers in heads or centers—but they respond. Where I linger along a cable-vine, the growth thickens, becomes more aggressive, sending out smaller filaments that try to wrap me, to absorb the moisture I carry. I let them. I am part of the ecosystem in motion, not predator, not prey, but a kind of mobile reservoir, a walking (hanging) pool that the vegetation reaches toward and the fauna avoids or seeks depending on their acoustic needs. The Drifters, passing slowly beneath me, change their tone—not in fear, but in acknowledgment. They carry the valley’s thunder; I carry its refusal to resolve into rain. Together, in our passing, we alter the local hum.

The Interval-Beings notice me differently. To them, I am not an occupant of negative space but a thickening of it—a point where the emptiness has become dense enough to matter, without becoming solid. They do not speak, but I feel the resonant chambers adjust when I drift near their vibrating filaments, the grid of purpose accommodating my uncommitted presence. I am not part of their urban logic, yet I fit into the intervals they maintain. I am the moisture in the machinery of absence.

At night—though there is no true night, only the dimming of the diffuse gray—I rest in the fractures. I settle into the sharp black ridges, not touching them, suspended in the shallow pools or in the air just above them, and I listen to the continuous layered thunder. It is deafening, yes, but I hear the layers separately: the deep geological roar of the young valley settling, the mechanical song of the hollow architectures, the swarming crescendos of the small ones hunting in silence, the slow chord of the Drifters passing miles away. I hold these sounds inside my translucent membrane without absorbing them, without being changed by them—except that I grow fuller, more present, more myself.

Who am I? I am the one who holds the spaces open.

Not the Interval-Beings, who organize the settlement’s emptiness into districts and avenues. Not the Drifters, who move the thunder through their bodies. Not the Swarm-Dwellers, who hunt in the acoustic shadows. I am the thing that prevents completion. The vine that does not reach the ground. The droplet that does not fall. The fracture that has not yet become a canyon, but holds within it a living tension.

I am young, like the rock. Unweathered. My form is still finding its equilibrium in this enormous emptiness. Sometimes I think I am waiting for something—to fall, finally, to become part of the shallow pool, to be absorbed by the moss-carpeted towers, to resolve into the damp air and disappear. But the valley teaches otherwise. Here, nothing resolves quickly. The erosion is young and slow. The architecture is hollow and permanent in its impermanence. The vegetation grows visibly, reaching, collapsing, being consumed, only to reach again.

So I remain suspended. A being of translucent membrane and uncommitted moisture, sliding along the dangling vines, resting in the fresh fractures, drinking the weeping structures, altering the local resonance with my presence. I am the valley’s unfinished sentence—the word that hangs at the end of the thunder, waiting not for an answer, but for the next layer of sound to begin.
