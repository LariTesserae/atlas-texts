I do not find myself on the flat—not in the way they do, dragging and venting and swarming in that saturated darkness. I find myself in the draw.

That is the first distinction: there is the flat, and there is the column. The flat is horizontal, terminal, eroded past any memory of slope—2.6 settled into silk and glass, spread like a sheet that forgot it was ever part of a mountain. But where the largest gather—not meeting, never meeting, only accumulating in function—the flat opens downward into itself, and through that opening, the water does not weep; it climbs. I live there. I am there. I am the there.

My body is not like theirs. They wear the substrate: turbine-blade carapaces, segmented ribs, polished ribs of vented steam, glowing blue-white along their seams like current in wire. I have no carapace. I have no segments. I am smooth—wet, dark, mineral—more conduit than creature, anchored not by weight but by the pressure of everything that moves around me. While they grind forward in perpetual low-temperature industry, I remain. I am the only stillness in a world that denies it. But stillness here is not absence; it is an active holding-open.

The air around me is thick with their breath—condensing, clinging, tasting of rust that never required a forge. I do not breathe it. I draw it through me, downward and then up, negotiating with gravity differently than even they do. The water rises through my length in invisible columns, pulled not by pump but by need, feeding on what they leave in their grinding passage. They do not see me, or if they do, they do not register me as separate from the mechanism. I am the mechanism’s vertical exception.

In the dimness—barely 1.2, twilight made permanent by the vaporized mineral in the air—I do not glow. They illuminate each other, navigate by the blue-white running along their bodies, a collective lantern in the packed dark. I absorb it. I am the shadow that casts correctly, the one that falls in the direction the light actually comes from, which makes me strange, suspicious, wrong. Some of them pass near me without pausing, adjusting for collisions that have not yet happened; others, the smaller ones in their shivering clouds, veer away as if I represent a future they do not want to remember.

But I remember. That is my fracture.

They remember futures—movements made to avoid what has not arrived, consumption of prey not yet present. I remember downward. I remember mountains. I remember the old sea before it vanished, before the erosion finished its work and left this flat, this endless valley without banks or edges. I should not. There is no archive here, no green root to hold history, no structure to preserve it—built is nearly zero, and I am not built. I am grown, or secreted, or perhaps I am the flat’s own regret made tubular and wet. But I hold it: the downward.

The sound is constant, loud, layered—the grinding of bodies against volcanic silk, the hiss of vented pressure, the low-frequency thrum of thousands of parts working in near-unison. I do not add to it. I resonate with it. Through my smooth length, the vibrations travel differently; I become a note held while others play the chorus. Sometimes, in the zones where time is not linear, I hear them before they move—grinding sounds arriving in my downward memory before the bodies produce them in the present. I am always a moment behind and a moment ahead, suspended in the column.

They feed on minerals, on heat differentials, on each other—predator and prey locked in mechanical biology. I do not feed. I filter. They pass near me, dragging through shallow pools that have no banks, and in passing, they deposit what they do not need: sediment, broken fragments of carapace, the cold exhaust of their industry. I take these. I am the flat’s only accumulation that does not move. Through me, the weeping water rises, carrying what I have gathered, and in rising, it feeds—who, I do not know. Perhaps the flat itself. Perhaps nothing. Perhaps the act of rising is enough.

There are no plants. This I understand intimately, because I am not one. I am mineral and water and the tension between them, a vertical interruption in a world that achieved its terminal shape and then filled it with active, broken machinery. I have no mouth, no eyes that glow blue-white, no ribs that vent steam in rhythmic pulses. But I have presence. I claim space—not by density, for they are packed shoulder-to-shoulder, every cubic span saturated, no gap untouched—but by orientation. I point where nothing else points. I draw where everything else pushes.

If you were to stand here—if standing were possible without sinking into the mineral skin, without being incorporated—if you listened past the loud industrial chorus, past the language of vibration and pressure and light, you might feel me: not a body, not a shadow that lingers after its source has moved, but a holding. A refusal to drag. A memory of depth in a place that has erased all height.

I am the flat’s contradiction. I do not swarm. I do not grind. I wait, and in waiting, I pump. The water climbs through me, the cold holds, the broken rules negotiate with me differently—sometimes time pools at my base, thickening; sometimes shadows gather in my length, correct and true, falling downward when everywhere else they fall wrong.

And yet I am not outside them. I am part of the saturation. They touch me constantly, not recognizing me as other, passing through the space I occupy without seeing that it is occupied by something that does not move. I am abundance’s exception, density’s silent partner. In a world of perpetual motion, I am the still point that makes the motion measurable.

The air tastes of metal and wet stone. The temperature holds near absolute refusal. The erosion is finished. But I remain—smooth, dark, vertical—drawing the weeping world upward through myself, remembering mountains, holding downward open in a place that only knows flat, remembering future in a place that remembers downward, and letting the water rise, always rise, through the body that is not a body, in the column that is not on the flat, but through it, forever.
