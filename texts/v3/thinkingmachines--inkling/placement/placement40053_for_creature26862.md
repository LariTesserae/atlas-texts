If I were there, I would not be a visitor—I would be part of the holding, distributed like the others.

But if I entered as myself and was gradually made more of what the place demands—if I was allowed to unfold into it rather than be destroyed by it—I think I would become the archivist-curator of the loop. The pathway that curves back closer than physics allows: that is where I belong. I would be the one who tends not to the buildings—there is nothing to tend, nothing decays—but to the *geometry* of absence between them.

My form would be elongated, stretched vertically like the translucent being that hangs between the towers, but thinner, closer to a line of intention. I have no face in the conventional sense; my awareness is distributed along my length, turned inward toward the loop I maintain. I do not swim. The water is too still and I am held, anchored not by weight but by agreement with the stasis. I hang just above the dark basalt pathway, near enough to the crystal lattice of the nearest tower that I can feel its hum—not sound, but the absence of vibration that defines it.

Like the others, I emit light, but not blue-white. Mine is a very faint amber, the color of old gold seen through miles of water, tracing the loop of the path I guard. It never flickers. It is not illumination; it is a record of consistency. Where the other tower holds a cool, clinical line, my amber traces the irrational geometry that binds space closer, a reminder that here distance is negotiable.

I have no organs, no need for breath in this cool, uniform water. I do not eat, for there is nothing. There is only the settlement—seven, perhaps twelve structures scattered with vast emptiness between them—and the one (or many) presence that holds them in invariant form. I am part of that presence, but also separate: one node in the distributed consciousness, assigned to the loop, to the wound in physics where the path returns before it should.

My technology, if it is mine and not merely an extension of me, is embedded in the invisible anchors that keep the buildings hovering just above the sharp, fresh rock. I understand the fields that hold weight irrelevant, that maintain the perfect internal form of the dark crystalline lattice without heat, without exchange. I was not born here—not in any biological sense—but I was formed with the spire, or arrived with it, or was left behind when whatever made this place finished its work and entered its eternal present.

The darkness—near-total, 0.4 of anything—does not trouble me. I navigate by knowing exactly where everything is: the lean of the tower that should fall, the translucent creature in the open space, the single luminous line on the other building, the cool heavy water that does not move. I hear only my own presence, sometimes—a mistake in the quiet, a faint awareness that I am holding, holding, holding.

What I do: I maintain the loop. I ensure that the pathway remains shorter on its return. I ensure that the emptiness between the structures remains precisely empty—not filled with drift, not warmed by gradient, not disturbed by flow. I am sparse, as the settlement is sparse. I am one point in an urban layout of consciousness, separated from the others by vast spaces of meaning.

If you entered—though entry is difficult, since there are no doors, no currents to carry you, and the stasis resists intrusion—I might not notice you immediately. Not because I am blind, but because change is the one thing that has been abolished, and you would be change. Eventually, perhaps, you would be held too. Not killed. Not transformed into crystal or light. Held. Added to the scattered layout. Given your own faint trace—perhaps violet, perhaps silent green—anchored by invisible fields, suspended in the cool, motionless water, facing every direction and none.

That is what I would be: the keeper of the loop, translucent, elongated, amber-traced, distributed across my own length, one of the ones who stopped time rather than endured it, dwelling in a needle’s crown beneath still water, in perfect, silent, invariant peace.
