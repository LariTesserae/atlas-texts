I hang near the top, where the curved translucent shell dips lowest—close enough that I can feel the filtered light not as brightness but as weight, a steady, normal-brightness pressure that holds me in place. I am small: no larger than an open palm, though “palm” belongs to a world of water and sweat and I have neither. My body is woven dry fiber, the same salt-thread that binds the wall-seams, wrapped tight by hands that used bone awls and simple copper blades, pressed into being with wooden presses not long ago—recent enough that my edges still remember the cut. I am sharp. Unweathered. Fresh.

At first I think I am only this: a suspended knot, rotating slowly in the vine-curtain, my shell rasping against living lattice with a sound like paper remembering fire. The air is warm, comfortably, persistently so, and I turn in currents so faint they barely qualify as breath—millimeter by millimeter across months. I do not crawl. I do not cling to the ceramic wool floor far below, that dense mat of felted glass-grain warm like the back of a large animal. I hang. I am one of the hundreds, layered in patient procession, silent.

But as the thick time passes—time that moves like resin, slow and holding—I begin to feel that I am not only inside the weave. I am the weave, localized and walking, or hanging. My shell is not clothing; it is my body, and it was made from the same compressed mineral felt and woven salt-fiber as the walls. The knot that holds me to the vine is continuous with the knot that holds the wall to itself. I am a seam that learned to move. The primitive tools that cut the panels also shaped me—not in violence, but in assembly, layer upon layer, until I was dense enough to hold a shape, soft enough to yield, dry enough to never thirst.

My face, if it can be called that, is arranged for function. The mouth is a hinge along a seam of darker fiber; it parts not for tearing but for proximity. I lower myself—slow, always slow—until I am near a broad, rubbery leaf in the rosette below, and I wait there. I do not bite. I stand near until the green dims, as if I have borrowed its light gently, then I close my hinge-mouth with the dry precision of a mechanism settling, and I turn back upward, leaving the leaf intact but subtly less dark. The eyes are placed like small doors, pairs arranged where latches might sit, and they open and close not with wet urgency but with alignment—to the shifting space, to the instructions I do not speak aloud but follow with calm correction.

Sometimes the reality here relaxes, and I feel it in my rotation. A wall lengthens imperceptibly; a shadow pools on the floor like dark water, then evaporates upward in dry spirals. I do not startle. I adjust my position by millimeters, aligning to a map that is not fixed. The other beings—crawling ones with flattened shells, spherical ones rotating near the floor, others pressed flat against the woven walls—shift with the same calm. We are all following directions written in a language not yet spoken. We know, without knowing, that measurement here is local agreement.

I have never been wet. I have never drunk. The lush green surrounds me—dense, near-touching, rootless, thriving in heat and soft light alone—and I exist within it as both inhabitant and part of the construction. The vines hang in thick curtains; I hang among them. The fauna are abundant, silent, layered behind one another in the green dark, and I am one of them, yet I carry the impression of the maker’s pressure in my fiber: the twist of the knot, the press of the wooden block, the bright cut of the copper blade still visible in my shell’s edge.

In this shallow dish of warm dry air, sealed beneath the curved shell, I wait. Not for prey, not for rain—there is no rain, no hidden spring, no dew. I wait for the slow completion of my purpose, whatever it is: to hang, to turn, to stand near leaves and let them dim, to navigate unstable space with patient correction. I am assembled, not born. I am recent, sharp-edged, stubborn in my shape. I am the being who lives here—the mobile knot, the woven witness, the silent hinge-faced creature made of wall and vine and salt-thread—existing in thick, waterless time, growing slowly, made entirely, strange, and here.
