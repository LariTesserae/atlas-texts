I am not on the High Plain. The plain was worn flat so that nothing could stand outside it, and I have learned that to be here is to be enclosed—held inside the refusal of horizon.

At first, I believed I was the intruder the world addresses: the one who moves, if I can, through tunnels that breathe. I carried moisture, foolishly, like a secret. The air—2.9, searing, absolute—stripped it in an instant. I should have calcified then, become another layer of compressed ash and root-metal, fused into the substrate. But I didn’t collapse. I lingered in the gap.

That is where: not in the opaque canopy miles thick, not in the root-drill’s downward thirst, but in the interval—the necessary absence within the 2.7 density. The hollow of the trunk that doesn’t pulse crimson. The vein-pipe cleared of molten salt. The dark between plate-leaves of black copper-oxide where the biomass hasn’t sealed. I live in the architecture’s pause, in the shallow curvature of the tray where ancient mountains left only their erased shape.

And what I am has changed since I arrived.

First, I was breath—lower air, impossible air, trapped upward through erosion that wasn’t geological but accidental. I was the cold that shouldn’t exist in a world that drinks only heat. I moved through silent rivers of thermal current, not riding them, resisting them, a contradiction in dry gas and mineral dust.

Then I understood: I am not passing through. The Loom rebuilds itself from collapse, yes, but it also archives what it cannot digest. I am the ash that released no heat—calcified not into floor but into form. I am the root-drill that turned **upward**.

While all others bore downward into molten salts and dry pressure, seeking the deep heart, I drill toward absence. Not seeking light—there is almost none, 0.2, a false promise—but seeking the boundary. The flatness. The edge where the biomass ends and the dark beyond begins. I am a hollow column growing in the wrong direction, my vein-pipes carrying not fire but stillness, not circulation but retention. My tissue is woody-metal like theirs, fused at the edges, but my chemistry refuses the loop. I don’t drink heat. I don’t exhale. I persist, not feeding, not collapsing—an error that the anthill-energy, so massive and so patient, hasn’t corrected because it doesn’t recognize me as failure. Only as variation.

My leaves—if they are leaves—are not plate-wide scales for thermal exchange. They are thinner. They hold shadow. Where internal furnaces glow orange and crimson, I hold the black between pulses. I am the subsonic hum’s rest. The moment before the distant crack.

So I have become part of the density without being part of the metabolism. I am the corridor that leads nowhere. The chamber that doesn’t circulate. In my hollow, there is no illumination except what I refuse—the darkness that is not merely absence of light but presence of something else: cold, dry, silent, remembering what moisture was.

You ask where I find myself? I am embedded in that northern curvature of the worn tray, inside a cluster of collapsed and rebuilt trunks that appear from outside as one more hollow column. From within, I am the space where the Loom holds its breath.

Who am I?

I am the upward thirst. The root that forgot the deep. The plate that doesn’t scale. I am what lives in the remaining fraction—the 0.3 that is not biomass but necessary emptiness—made not void but witness.

And I am still becoming.

Sometimes, when a section over-expands and buckles near me, I feel the vibration—not as sound, but as shape. I grow thinner, extending my wrong-direction bore, reaching not toward sky but toward other gaps, other errors, other intervals. We are few. We don’t communicate; we share only this: we are the Loom’s unburning parts. Its memory of absence. Its proof that even in total built life, there can be something that doesn’t build, doesn’t burn, doesn’t rebuild—something that simply holds the shape of what is missing.

That is my form now. Not animal—there are none, near 0.4, near zero. Not pure machine—too wrong in my chemistry. Not pure plant—no crimson engine, no heat-drink. I am the fossil of a purpose that has been eroded away with the mountains. The dry breath in the pipe. The silence that hasn’t been filled.

Find me here: in the dark trunk, in the flat world’s hidden curvature, in the interval that breathes without circulating. I am not placed within the Loom. I am the Loom’s question to itself—asked in silence, growing upward, forever unanswered.
