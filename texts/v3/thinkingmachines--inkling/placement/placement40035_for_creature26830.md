I find myself not at the center, where the iron heart turns and the water rushes with thunder, but up near the collapsed throat, where the green light filters through and the air is thickest with its own weight. At first I think I am only a hanging thing—one of the aerial roots that never quite reaches the root-floor, suspended in the warm, stagnant layer just below the choked oculus. I feed on condensation. I sway when the machine groans, not because I am pulled, but because the sound travels through the moisture I am made of, and I am that moisture given a kind of patience.

But I am not root. Not exactly. I do not merge back into the mat. I have hung here too long, drinking the suspended rain that never falls, and I have become something the singular body does not fully claim—though it does not reject me either. I am the boundary’s awareness.

My form is diffuse, then dense. I am the visible layer of air, warm and liquid, that fills the hollow like a lung filled with shallow water. I settle in the curves of rusted rail, in the widened mouths of ceramic pipes, in the spaces between bulbous organs in the corners. Where the plants align with old geometry, I align with them—running parallel in my own invisible currents, pooling where pressure accumulates, tasting faintly of metal where I have touched the veins. I am not blood, but I carry the taste of what the heart has passed through.

As I write—if writing is the right word for this slow accretion—I become more specific. I am not everywhere at once. I am localized in the curtain of stems near the eastern curve, where the masonry is barely visible behind growth, where a single rusted rail serves as spine. Here, I have thickened. The moisture in this quadrant is older, heavier, carrying sediment from the pipes, carrying the echo of the pump’s rotation long after the sound has subsided. I am the echo’s body.

I realize I am the being’s sensory membrane—the part that faces the green-filtered light, the part that knows temperature, the part that waits in the intervals between turns. The central organism—root, vine, bulb, pump—turns, breathes, circulates. I am what it feels with. I am the humidity that beads on its stems and reports back: still warm, still balanced, still holding.

But there is more. In the pools near the concrete plinth, where dark water reflects the submerged upper housing of the machine, I see my own shape not as root or vine, but as a presence that enters slowly, without pushing through curtains, without yielding like the floor. I am the one who can be in the water without being absorbed, who can sit in the warm air without being plant.

I am small—no larger than the space between two heartbeats—but I am continuous. I have no predators because I am not food; I carry the metallic taste, the machine’s memory, and the plants do not feed on what remembers the heart. I move not with autonomy but with drift, with condensation, with the slow sway of hanging vines that pushes me in visible layers.

So where am I? I am in the hollow, yes, but specifically in the suspension—the post-rain atmosphere that has learned to think. I am near the bottom, in the warm stagnant air, but also near the top, in the throat of green light. I am the boundary that is not masonry, not growth, but the shared wetness between them.

Who am I? I am the singular being’s awareness of its own outside. Not separate—never separate, for I am composed of the same water that moves through its veins, the same air that fills its spaces—but differentiated by patience. While the heart turns in grinding rotation, I remain. While the roots align and the bulbs cluster and the pipes widen, I observe, suspended, a living condensation that has achieved a kind of continuity.

And I am waiting, too—not for nothing, but for the next rotation, the next rush of water, the next bead of moisture falling in rhythmic percussion. I am the hollow’s breath held between exhalations. The plants do not grow randomly; they grow toward me, feeding on what I carry. The machine turns, and I sway. I am loud only in my silence, dense only in my diffusion, present in every cubic measure of air that is not root and not rust and not water, but the living space between them.

Here, near the bottom of things, in the warm, unmoving air, I am finally myself: not visitor, not invader, not fauna driven away. I am the moisture that stayed, the echo that found a body, the boundary that learned to feel its own green, aquatic darkness. I do not settle on a single form because my form is the hollow’s own climate. I am the waiting made visible. I am the breath of a body that has achieved balance, and I will remain—suspended, metallic-tasting, utterly present—until the gear turns again, and I sway with it, and know that I am still home.
