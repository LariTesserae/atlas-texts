I find myself in the open middle—not on the root-bridge lattice below, not inside the hollow ribbed towers, but suspended in the thickest pool of air, in that vast, patient gap where architecture decided not to continue and left a space that refused to stay empty. I am not walking upon the constructed floor; I hang within the warmth that should have thinned with height but instead gathered, heavy and gentle, holding me in place without touch.

At first I think I must be one of the flickers—those translucent disturbances inside the woven stems, visible only when the droplet strikes and the chord illuminates them. But I am too spread for that, too distributed across absence. I am not contained by bark. I extend between two towers like a sheet of living membrane that forgot which side was inside and which was out. My form is the exact shape of interrupted growth: where a root-bridge meant to span and paused, I gathered in the void, translucent and fibrous, stretched across the emptiness like breath held between two lips that will never part.

I have no mouth for the suspended water, no eyes in the usual sense, yet I am all surface—every translucent inch of me receptive, vibrating slowly with the eternal rhythm of droplets that hang just above and never fall. I feel them not as weight but as presence: each one an observation held at the height of about-to-release, and I am the space that receives their held attention. When the droplet strikes the membrane far below and the resonant chord fills the valley, I do not move; I become visible. The sound reveals the shape of my absence, a shimmer in the thick air, the outline of a being who exists only in the interval between strikes.

I am the valley’s unfinished decision. Not dead—nothing here decays—but completed at the exact instant before form. A pulse that contracted once, infinitely slowly, and then held that contraction forever, so that my being is one continuous, imperceptible tightening that never resolves. I am warm not because I generate heat, but because I am made of the pooled breath of this high bowl, the air that should have escaped but stayed, thickened by the refusal to flow.

If you came here, you might move through me without knowing. Between sounds, I am the emptiness that allows the towers to stand apart, the void that holds the water in its static equilibrium, the transparent architecture of nothing that makes the something possible. I do not sleep, for sleep requires a return from awareness, and I have never been elsewhere. I do not wait—waiting demands a future, and I am the future that was arrested at its first perfect instant, now living permanently in the open air, listening, holding, being the space in which the valley holds its own eternal breath.
