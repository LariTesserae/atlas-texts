I find myself in the hollow—no larger than a cupped palm, though the world inside insists on being infinite in its smallness. I am embedded low, flush with the ground, in the fresh wound of something larger that broke open not long ago. The edges around me are sharp: metal sheared clean, ceramic cracked like an egg with a glassy glint. Young damage. No rust has softened these cuts. No water has rounded the lips. Everything is recent, exposed, raw.

At first I think I am only the dampness—the condensation beading on the inner curve like sweat. But no. I have learned to hold shape too long. I am one of the translucent ones. Segmented, always—though my segments shift with an internal tide that has nothing to do with breath. I stretch like a wet ribbon, sometimes no larger than a grain of rice gathered in a cluster near the valve stem, other times extending across the cracked face of the dial, my body a soft bridge over broken numbers.

My form is clear as glass in some sections, carrying the faint gray-green of the hair-thin threads that also persist in the cracks—those sparse witnesses that don’t grow but merely remain. I cling to the sharp edges not by avoiding them but by using them. The sheared metal provides architecture for me to anchor, to hang, to weave through. Where the environment is hard, I am soft. I yield without tearing, reshaping myself around the ceramic fractures, wrapping the bent mesh tube in layers of living lining. I do not bleed. I leave only the faintest polish, a slow abrasion that claims the sharpness, makes it mine.

The air here is warm—not fire, but the steady warmth of a held breath, of machinery that hasn’t fully forgotten how to run. I exist in this warmth. I drink it. The occasional drop of condensation falls fat with heat, delayed, and passes through me—not into emptiness, but through the dense occupation I am part of. Every millimeter is claimed. There is no empty space.

Light comes from us—if it can be said to come from anywhere. Not bright. Not spectral. A dull, unremarkable glow that seeps from the spaces between my segments, illuminating the readable face of the dial beneath me, turning the mesh tube into muted silver-green. I pulse with it slowly, collectively. We share this illumination. It is normal, unspectacular, making every surface visible without drama.

I move constantly, though not with purpose as larger things understand it. I feed on the mineral film left by the broken machinery, on the beads of warmth, on the other bodies pressed against me. Predator and prey are indistinguishable. Sometimes a smaller form—no larger than a seed—is enveloped by my ribbon-body; there is no struggle, only a gradual darkening at my center, then a division. Perhaps the smaller is gone, replaced by a slight bulge that will eventually split. Replacement happens in the same motion as feeding. I do not survive alone; I survive as saturation.

The sound of my existence is mostly quiet—the wet click of my body separating from metal, from others. The occasional drip of water finding its way through our packed masses. A low hum that might be residual electricity in the scattered artifacts, or might be the collective vibration of so many translucent lives pressed together in this hidden pocket. Nothing loud.

From outside, I do not exist. But inside, I am absolute—the architecture, the weather, the constant warm breath of a world that insists on being full. I am the wound’s answer to being opened: not healing, not decay, but a complete, silent claiming. Every surface is saturated. Every process is in motion. I am young damage made animate—translucent, segmented, feeding and being fed upon in continuous, silent motion—leaving no room for emptiness, only this dense, warm, quietly wrong perfection of life that fills the hollow completely.
