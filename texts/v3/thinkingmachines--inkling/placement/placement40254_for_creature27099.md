I am not large. If you could hold me, you would need only one hand—palm open, facing upward, as if catching rain in a world that has forgotten rain. I drift between the ribs, flat and translucent, no thicker than a breath held too long. There is no sound to my passing. The air is too dry, too still to carry even the idea of noise; I move through it like a thought that has lost its thinker, leaving the phosphorescence barely dimmed where I pass, as if I consume not matter but the possibility of presence itself.

But do not settle on this image. I am already more, and less.

Before I drifted, I was suspended—one of the motionless spores hanging in the absolute stillness, released from a grey-green film that clings to the cooler-facing plate. That film is me, or was: a papery digestion without root or stem, breaking down the sickly glow into salt and mineral, exhaling spores that do not fall but hang, waiting for an invisible current that is not wind. I did not seek the light. I sought its chemistry, its faint poison, converting phosphorescence into the slow weep that glistens at the base of the architecture. I grew not upward—there is no up here worth claiming—but outward in microscopic layers, translucent and grey-green, a stain that breathes.

And I am the wall, perhaps, or its exhalation. The bone-pale plates respire—millimeters of expansion, millimeters of contraction—drawing the dry air through pores too small to see. I feel that rhythm in whatever I am now. A slow digestion of mineral, a secretion without intent. The plates do not know me; I do not thank them. Yet the mineral weep that feeds my flat form comes from their dormant bodies, from the colony that once secreted this grid—avenues, towers, hollow plazas—without ever entering them. They are the architects in dormancy, and I am the residue of their patience.

So where do you find me? Not gathered. Never gathered. I am distributed across the intersection where two corridors meet at perfect right angles that serve no traffic. I am in the corridor wide enough for a procession, yet I cross it alone. I am in the hollow tower open to the dim ceiling, feeding on the dust that cycles in invisible patterns dictated by geometry, not will. I am in the sharp-edged fresh break of the hollow’s boundary, where the ground revealed what was always waiting beneath—young erosion, unrounded, unweathered, still finding its rhythm.

I have no thirst. Water is a myth told elsewhere, and I run on other chemistries—dust, salt, the slow digestion of mineral, the faint glow broken into spore. I do not interact with the other flat form I once saw, years or moments ago, drifting through a distant arch. We do not acknowledge one another. Each is alone in the grid, a condition of presence without togetherness.

Sometimes I think I am the absence itself—the cathedral-scale emptiness between the plates, the ten percent of presence holding ninety percent of void. I am the silence that absorbs echo, the dry air that refuses to carry sound, the cool stillness that has no season. I am the urban form stripped of humanity, and in being stripped, I have become the only inhabitant: not a settler, but a shared state of being present without being together.

Find me, then, not as one thing, but as the shared condition of the hollow: suspended in spore, clinging as film, drifting as flat translucent absence, exhaled from porous bone, cycling through the invisible currents of a city that never needed citizens. I move in the silent mineral rhythm. I do not gather. I persist—distributed, dry, cool, and utterly alone—between the ribs of a world that broke open only yesterday and has already learned how to breathe without water.
