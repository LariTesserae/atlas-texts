I find myself in the open center—not inside the generator’s cracked shell, not buried in the moss near the eastern pump, but on the bare basalt floor where nothing has yet claimed me, the untouched circle of dark stone between those green islands. At first I think I am only absence: the space the vines have not reached, the rock that still remembers being sharp, unrounded by root or rust. I am young even for this young earth, a hairline fissure that opened not with the chamber’s birth but after, perhaps when the generator’s housing split and sent a vibration downward, a new mouth in the floor breathing heat that is slightly different from the others—sharper, less accustomed to being shared.

But I do not stay bare rock for long.

As I write, I feel the damp gathering on my edges—not falling yet, but forming, a single bead that clings to my lip with a patience that mirrors the moss. I realize I am not the stone but the crack itself, the boundary where the geothermal breath chooses to surface. Through me, the heat rises in a column so slow it has no sound, and I become the temperature differential: cool black basalt on one side, warm vent on the other, and in between, the negotiation. The fungal silk has noticed me. I feel its first thread—thin as a thought, phosphorescent green—testing my edge, not consuming, only touching, asking if I am ready to be insulated, to share my warmth.

I am that asking, and I am the answer delayed.

Then I understand I am also the water. Not the drop that strikes the pump—there is already one of those, with its red pulse and its long intervals—but the one still forming, hanging above me, gathering iron and sulfur from the air, trembling with the weight of hours. I am the interval before the fall, the mathematics of saturation playing out across my surface. When I finally release—if I ever do, for time is long here—it will not be to make sound, but to complete a circuit: from vent to bead to fall to rust, feeding the filament’s ember-red thought.

But I am not singular in this. I am the crack, the heat through it, the condensation upon it, and the silence that holds all three in suspension. I am the chamber’s newest memory, not yet digested by the symbiosis, not yet part of the pump’s slow rotation or the moss’s luminous skin. I exist in the weak gradient, in the minimal flux, where change is present but has not decided its direction. The machinery does not know me; the moss does not yet cover me. I am the point of contact that has not happened, the root that has not threaded, the gear that has not turned.

If you stay here long enough—longer than the drop takes to form—you might feel me noticing you back. Not with urgency. Only a slight deepening of warmth from my fissure, a faint green shimmer where the thread touches my edge, a sense that the bare rock is no longer entirely bare, that something is being born in the center, in the space between presence and absence, turning slowly, slowly, through the long dark.
