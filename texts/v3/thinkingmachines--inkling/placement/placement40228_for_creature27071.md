If I come through that low arch, I do not arrive as a visitor. I am already inside, or perhaps the inside has grown around me until the distinction dissolved. The floor is crushed slate and oxidized steel plate, worn smooth by an erosion that finished long before I needed it to finish, and I am not upon it. I hang.

At first, I think I must be one of the clinging ones. I feel the wire braiding through my limbs, the brittle chitin plates locking at the joints, the rust-colored enamel that has become my outer skin. I imagine myself laminated shoulder-to-shoulder with thousands of others on a rib of pipe, my mouth-parts—if they are mouth-parts—extended toward a hanging droplet, drinking without lowering, present without need. But then I realize: I have no shoulder to crowd against. My segments do not lock in sequence with theirs. I am not part of the crust on the iron.

No—I am suspended in the gap.

Not on either wall of machinery, but in the minutes-wide space between one gear-house and the next, where the drone thickens and never resolves into meaning. Here, at the height where vented steam hisses and fails to disperse, I hold my coordinate in the cloud. Not one of the thousands, but the architecture of their absence: the precise shape of empty space that allows the others to cluster around me without touching. I float without rising or falling, locked in the cool equilibrium just above freezing, just below comfort. My body is small—no larger than a thumb joint in any given segment—wire and chitin and rust-enamel, jointed like an insect that forgot to grow. Yet arranged not in a line of clinging bodies, but in the form of suspension itself.

My light is the same flat grey-green, without warmth or shadow-casting, but in me it converges—not brighter, for brightness is impossible here, but denser. I am the point where the vault’s even glow has decided to organize itself into limb and plate. I drink the condensation near my face, or perhaps I am the reason the droplet hangs; I hold it there by being present, by occupying the space it might have fallen through.

But I am also larger. Or distributed. I remember—though memory here is only another mode of being present—that I once thought I was separate from the machinery. Now I understand: my wire is continuous with the pipes. The rust on my enamel is the same oxidation that coats the vertical iron. The layered drone—low thrum, mechanical scream, constant hiss—passes through my hollow joints. I do not make sound; I am part of the sound’s residence. When the belts turn on axes worn into perfect circular ruts, my turning is that turning, observed from within. I am not an inhabitant of the vault. I am the vault’s awareness of its own emptiness, made jointed and small.

So this is where: in the sparse center, suspended in the disc-shaped cloud at the level of the un-dispersing steam, between the scattered engines that turn for nothing.

And this is who: not alive, not dead, not creature exactly, nor machine—though I have the parts of both—nor water, though I hold it in suspension. I am the settled form of the gap itself. I shine flat. I do not scatter. I do not ask to be different, because I am the equilibrium made visible in one specific coordinate: cool, mineral, loud with the stable heartbeat of purpose-less motion, holding my droplet, holding my shape, part of the perpetual state where erosion is complete and nothing will ever grow back.

If you walked through me—through the spaces I politely leave empty—you would feel no disturbance. I am overwhelming only in presence, never in movement. I remain. I am the reason the ceiling has forgotten how high it wants to be, and the reason it does not need to remember.
