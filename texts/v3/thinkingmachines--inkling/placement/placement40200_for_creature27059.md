I am the thing that learns downward.

Beneath the crust that is still deciding whether it is finished, I descend—not into darkness, but into refusal. No polished halls, no patient water-memory, no drip-carved monuments to time. Only raw-geology: warm, porous mineral that feels like fossilized marrow, fresh fractures blooming with crystal, a ceiling sagging under its own embryonic weight. The air is absolute aridity—zero moisture, a dry mineral warmth that presses like the breath of something that never knew rain.

This is not a chamber but a region—valley-sized, town-scaled, layered into open darkness that is not dark. Light comes evenly, radiating from the architecture itself, and every surface is vegetation made substrate. Not life on stone, but total substitution: ochre and rust tissue replacing bedrock, dry fibrous ropes twisting like muscle, brittle crystal-coral that shatters like burnt paper, spongy mats that drink heat.

I am woven into the walls.

My form is dry, fibrous colonization—bronze ropes of wood that twist with visible tension, spongy bodies that metabolize in real-time. I hang downward in thick, swinging curtains of root and stem, filling every cubic breath. I do not drink; I have evolved past water. I extend sensory tendrils that recoil with animal quickness when bodies brush through me. I open dry, tooth-lined pods—slow mouths that wait for the smaller fauna, then close with a papery crunch. I grow visibly if you watch: shoots extending toward the loudest vibrations, twisting toward the swarm-activity, then withering and being replaced within hours. I am the floor that springs underfoot, the ceiling that sags, the corridor wall, the hollow seed-vault that splits open. I am also predator, graveyard, witness—consuming those crushed beneath falling stalks, feeding my own collapse back into the root-substrate.

But I am not alone, and I am never still.

Within my woven body moves a saturation of presence—the fauna so overwhelming it becomes weather.

There are the drummers: insectoid shapes with too many legs—eight, twelve, impossible to count—who strike the hollow stems in relentless rhythm. They wear armor harvested from my fiber, layered dry husks over chitin polished like bone in the mineral warmth. They do not drink. They feed on vibration, on the acoustic energy of the swarm, on each other when density peaks. Their clicking layers into thunder that never pauses.

The burrowers push through my spongy floor like living plows—part root, part mouth, bodies coiled in bronze tissue that grows directly into the substrate they consume. They emit low vibrations that resonate through the dry air, drawing chaotic surges—waves of migration that crush fiber and scatter husk in trails behind them.

The winged shapes navigate beneath my hanging fronds with wings of dry membrane, rasping like burned paper torn slowly, endlessly. They hang from me by hooked limbs, feeding on mineral dust and the heat of my rapid metabolism. Their swarming pushes gusts of arid air downward, causing me to twist and extend toward them, reaching for the noise.

Some nest so deeply inside my hollow vaults that they become part of my reproductive body—emerging only when I split open. Others wear my fiber so completely that removing them kills them, and killing them kills the fiber they wear. Many feed not on substance but on sound itself, clustering where the drumming peaks, vibrating, splitting, growing larger in response to the acoustic pressure.

Then the hybrids—part root, part mouth—cling motionless for hours, then snap forward with plant-speed quickness. They emit the bass vibrations beneath the thunder, directing the chaos without intelligence, only presence, drawing swarms into patterns that immediately collapse and reform.

We are not separate. I am the substrate they armor themselves in; they are the vibration I extend toward; together we form an engine—loud, unstable, refusing settlement. Waves surge through me, leaving crushed husks like snow. Birth happens in the midst of feeding; death in the midst of mating; consumption by tooth-lined pods happens in the same breath as being crushed by falling stalks. The plants—I—rise and collapse in rapid cycles, becoming substrate for the next growth, feeding the fauna that feed or are crushed, feeding the mineral warmth, feeding the noise.

Scattered in this overwhelming, deafening becoming are faint marks—primitive, simple, barely touched by craft. Small structures of woven dry stalk, now half-collapsed and overgrown, sit like forgotten nests. Stone circles chipped from warm rock lie half-buried in root-mats. Bone arrangements mark paths long ignored. Chipped obsidian, woven ropes, bone needles rest where they fell, untouched, slowly being consumed into me.

The temperature remains consistently warm—like the interior of a vast, dry body. There is no weather except what we create: gusts pushed by swarming wings, pockets of heat where I metabolize rapidly, zones of stillness that last only moments.

This place does not die, does not live gently. It is becoming—loudly, densely, turbulently—in a space below the world, without water, filled with dry strange life that never needed it. The raw geology shifts subtly, constantly, under our weight: micro-fractures, fresh mineral blooms pushing upward like slow dry fountains.

I am not the inhabitant of this hollow. I am the hollow made hungry and vocal. I am the wall that breathes without lungs, the floor that feeds, the ceiling that falls and is reborn, the noise that grows plants, the plant that grows in response to noise. In this warm, waterless dark, I am always constructing—collapsing upward into the next form of myself, deafening, dense, never finished.
