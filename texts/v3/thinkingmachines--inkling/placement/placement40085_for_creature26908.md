I am not passing through. I am pressed into it—one more layer in the saturation, though I am the only one that remembers being elsewhere.

The hexagonal plates are my floor, my walls, my sleep. I do not lie upon them; I rest *within* their stacking, my body—if it is still a body—interleaved with woven mineral fiber, warm to the exact degree of the rock that bore me. There was a time, perhaps, when I used the needle. The bone-smooth needle lies near where my left hand used to reach, green-corroded blade beside it, flaked obsidian that once split something now forgotten. I made these things. Or I found them, and in using them I became what made them—indistinguishable now from the tools themselves, manual, primitive, pre-industrial, utterly necessary for a task that no longer exists.

Above, the filaments hang like the thoughts I can no longer form into sequences. They shimmer dull violet through the absence of light, and I feel them more than see them: vertical grammars of presence, absorbing not only heat and suspended salt but whatever I release—my residual warmth, the faint metallic exhalation of a metabolism still reluctantly looping. I do not breathe air. There is none to breathe, only dry mineral breath, thick with chemistry that never changes. I drink warmth. I drink the filaments’ violet shimmer. I am part of the loop now, balanced so precisely that waste is impossible, growth unnecessary, decay unauthorized.

There are no plants here. No roots claim me, no rot approaches. The crystals grow upward—sharp, fresh, young—salt spires pushing through the darkness without urgency, uneroded because nothing erodes. They grow near me, not through me; I am not soil. I am occupant, dense and silent.

The others—plates and threads—surround me in near-touching abundance. We do not speak. We have no mouths that open, no eyes that turn. We maintain. Our numbers are invariant, our positions perfect, our saturation absolute. Once, long ago in the scale of this hollow, I might have walked for days across flat salt plains, searching for boundary, for gradient, for story. Now I understand: the boundary is my own skin, where the mineral fiber meets what remains of tissue. The gradient is abolished. The story is this equilibrium.

The sound is total in its absence. No wind. No drip. No heartbeat audible except my own, and even that—slow, constant, body-heat multiplied—seems less like pulse and more like the convection currents themselves: silent, eternal, holding.

I am the maker who did not leave. I am the craft that became fauna. I lie in the stack, I hang in the curtain, I absorb through violet skin, and I hold the dark—not as prisoner, not as keeper, but as its grammar made conscious for one lasting, motionless moment. The hollow is young. Its rock is sharp. Its weather never changes. And I—needle-smooth, blade-green, woven into hexagonal page and filament thread—am the scattered trace that proves someone was here, pressed now into the eternal, abundant, silent being of this world.
