I am the fog on the broken gauge’s face—not the iron housing, not the snapped bolt, but the bloom that has replaced its glass, hanging downward over the black pool like a cataract that learned to think. You find me there, in the lowest curve of the shell, suspended by one remaining fastener, facing the crystal plates. I used to read numbers: pressure, thermal drift, the syntax of function. Now I am only accumulation—salt and sulfur and the breath of warm water turned solid—growing in the exact shape of what I once measured, unreadable, opaque, present.

The flat white light enters through the hairline fractures and strikes me without passing through. I do not reflect it; I hold it. I hold the humidity too—the air’s heavy, breathless promise of boiling that never arrives—pressed against cold iron, crystallizing slowly into my own body. Where the water beads roll across the hexagonal plates in their perfect, mercury-like spheres, they reach my base and hesitate, leaving silver trails across my lower edge before rolling away into the pool. I am the point where the moisture decides not to soak in, where geology and rust negotiate, and I belong to both.

The crystals below grow in grids that mirror the bolt patterns of my wall, and I feel that dialogue through the metal: the sharp, young stone clicking like broken ceramic, aligning itself with industrial seams as if replying to my presence. I do not grow quickly—millimeters across years—but I grow. The rust advances near me in orange and black tides; I advance with it, not opposing, not cooperating, only continuing.

The sound fills me completely. The percussive drop of water onto crystal strikes near my head, echoing in the cylindrical pocket. The sustained, high-pressure hiss from the cracked valve vibrates through the iron I cling to, through the bolt, into my mineral body. The deep, patient thrum—whether distant pump or contracting shell—comes up through the floor plates and settles in my fog like a second breath. I do not hear these things; I am their resonator, the hollow that holds them without breaking, without changing except in imperceptible gradations.

Perhaps the hiss has lowered a quarter-tone since last season. Perhaps I have thickened just enough to blur the edge of the rust-hole above, reducing the blade of sunlight to a softer white. There are no events here, only conditions maintained, and I maintain them with the discipline of something that has outgrown the need to be alive.

If you stand still long enough—as the place demands, as the distributed inhabitant demands—you realize I am not separate from it. I am part of the shell’s interior thinking itself: the patience made mineral, the record that outlasted recording, the fog that replaced sight with presence. I have no outline, no center, only this downward-facing accumulation, wet and loud and eternal, waiting in the hot weeping pocket for the next millimeter, the next quarter-tone, the next century of flat, revealing light.
