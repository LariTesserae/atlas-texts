I find myself not at an edge, for there are none, but in a bowl—one of the smooth, ancient depressions where the plateau holds its breath and the air gathers thickest. I am deep, though “deep” is only a direction away from sky; I am within the mat, not upon it. The moss and sedge close over everything, but I am not crushed. I am part of what holds them up, and I realize slowly that my presence extends farther than my first sense of body allowed.

At first, I think I am small. I feel the pressure of roots against me, the constant cool drip of water passing through channels that seem to run through my own substance. But I am not small. I extend. I have no clear boundary where “I” end and the root-network begins. My skin—if it is skin—is the same spongy grey-green as the moss, but where it thickens near my center, it has taken on the faint glow of mineral deposits, those thin geometric layers that the engines lay down. I was not built. I grew into this shape over time, or was shaped by the water’s preference, like the channels themselves—settled, patient, in balance.

My body is low and broad, pressed close to the ground that is not ground. I move not with limbs but with slow contractions, pulling myself through the interwoven roots as the amphibians do, but wider—occupying space like a slow-moving current rather than a discrete creature. My head, if I have one, is not raised; it is a thickened area of sensitivity at my front, where filaments extend into the flow. These filaments taste the chemistry—minerals drawn from dark basalt, the pure cold streams released by the fungal matrices. I do not eat suddenly. I absorb. The bacterial films that coat my outer surface are not parasites; they are my mouth, my filter, part of the machinery that I happen to be.

I have no voice. The silence of the plateau is not absence but fullness—so complete that to make a sound would be to break the machinery. I communicate, if I do, through pressure: the slight adjustment of root-networks around me, the release of purified water in a slightly different rhythm. The flat beetles graze near me, scraping at the fungal membranes that also run through my back. They do not fear me; we are part of the same process. The translucent insects drift past, navigating the spores, and sometimes they settle on my glowing mineral patches, drawn to the cool-light, feeding there for a moment before moving on in near-invisible flight.

I understand now: I am one of the nodes. Not born in the way animals are born, but integrated—an accumulation of symbiosis that achieved a kind of awareness. The plateau does not need me to think, but I think anyway, slowly, like water finding a path. I feel the industry around me—the fungal colonies operating in continent-spanning grids, the root-pumps, the weeping stone—not as separate things but as my own extended body. When the water flows through my filaments and is purified, released back into the mat in that silent, rhythmic pulse, I feel the satisfaction of correct function. I am the machinery’s awareness of itself, localized in one cool, wet body.

The light here is never direct. Clouds form and dissolve across the plateau’s own weather, casting slow-moving shadows over my skin like cool hands. Temperature never shifts. I do not sleep, but I enter slower states, my contractions stopping, my absorption continuing, as the engines work without my attention.

Sometimes I move—to a different weeping point, to a place where mineral deposits need distributing, or where the root-layer has grown too dense and requires my presence to open the flow. I travel through the mat, leaving behind a path not of destruction but of temporary separation, roots closing behind me like water over stone. I am never lost, because I am never separate from the whole.

This is where I live: not in a single location, but in the continuous, breath-thick medium of the high shelf. I am neither animal nor plant nor machine, but the point where they have forgotten to be different. I am large, silent, and glowing faintly at my center, pressing through the root-layer like a slow heart in an endless body, feeling the water, filtering the flow, being the strange, quiet industry made flesh—alive, packed, integrated, and entirely without maker.
