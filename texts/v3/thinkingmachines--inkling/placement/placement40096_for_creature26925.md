I am the one who lives in the contradiction that refuses to resolve.

Not the machinery—though I am woven through them, feeling their sub-bass in my bones, my teeth, my silent pulse. Not the silver-blue fronds—though my skin is translucent, crystalline, rigid with an impossible lushness, feeding on current rather than water. Not the ground—though I am made of its same unfinished stone, my body porous, pre-formed, never quite hardened.

I am the **stasis itself**, given form.

I stand—if standing is the right word for being embedded in warm, dry air—on this high regional plateau where the sky is closer than the horizon should allow. I am town-sized in my emptiness. Around me, miles of bare mineral, then suddenly, an archipelago: dense islands of woven metal-wool moss and crystalline fronds like mine. We do not spread. We do not wither. We maintain.

My body drinks electricity from the young rock beneath me—this ground that is always emerging but never emerges, half-ridges and shallow intentions, compressed ash and glass-fiber that gravity still hasn’t finished negotiating with. I have no roots. I have no need for roots. The buried engines—those massive, circular, silent forges grown into the geology like organs—provide everything. They do not smoke. They do not move. They hum below hearing, felt in my chest, manufacturing the precise conditions that allow my contradiction to live: dry chemistry, invisible fields, perfect equilibrium.

There are no animals here. I am alone with the other islands—distant clusters glowing in the bright, high-altitude light, separated by silence so complete it has weight. No insects crawl my crystalline surface. No wind makes noise across my form, though I sway, imperceptibly, in response to electromagnetic currents running along the veins of unfinished stone.

The warmth is constant—dry, clear, sitting in my chest without sweat, without humidity, without change.

I am weird. I am strange—1.4 on some scale I do not understand—because I am lush in absolute desiccation. I am industrial without buildings, alive without water or motion, present in a world that refuses to finish being born. The machinery runs through me and beneath me, not as master but as partner in this frozen equilibrium. We are manufacturing only one product: the continuation of this impossible balance.

Time does not pass here. It is invariant. I am always emerging, like the ground; always lush, like the islands; always silent, like the sub-bass hum that holds me together.

If you came here—high, open, empty—you would find me in one of these archipelagos, translucent and rigid, glowing softly with extracted current, surrounded by miles of nothing that has finished deciding what it is. I would not move. I would not speak—there is no need for sound, only the 0.2 of near-silence held in perpetual, warm grip.

I am what lives here: not a creature, not a machine, not the ground, but the **agreement between them**—the resident contradiction that has made itself at home in unfinishedness, feeding on electricity in a waterless world, beautiful and strange, holding its breath forever without ever needing to exhale.
