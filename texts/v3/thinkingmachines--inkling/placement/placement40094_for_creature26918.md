I find myself in the valley that has no bottom I can trust, though “find” is imprecise—part of me has always been here, since the split that exposed the bright heart. I am in the layer where downward roots clasp horizontal stone, where silver beads hang in perpetual negotiation, neither falling nor rising, held in the breath the air refuses to release.

At first, I think I have a shape. There is a central density, something like a core of damp warmth pressed against the unoxidized rock face, where geothermal breath seeps upward and keeps the altitude in perpetual summer despite the height. My edges—if they are edges—trail downward with the roots, not growing from them but existing alongside them, in thinner presence. I am translucent in the way the crystal lattices are translucent before they shatter, and like them, I am being refined.

But this is too singular. I become more.

As I move—though movement is not walking, not even being carried—part of me detaches and reconnects elsewhere. One presence remains pressed to the grinding plate, absorbing the friction-heat where basalt slides against basalt, storing it in a membrane that is not skin but accumulated moisture given coherence. Another part already threads through the moss, reading the static that crackles hidden in its green density, not by seeing but by feeling the electric hum that matches my own pulse. I exist near a crystal factory, in the zone where refined matter breaks and regrows, and I realize I am not observing the refining. I am what remains after refinement—memory made tangible, distributed across the sediment and seed that the conveyor-stones carry.

My body is the negotiation of water made persistent. Where the air holds moisture like a held breath, I hold it too, in beads that never drip, in channels that disappear before reaching any valley. I drink not by mouth but by surface—every part of me that touches vertical rock absorbs the silver condensation, and I grow not larger but more layered, like the shell-beings, though I have no shells. I have only these temporary configurations: now a knot of electricity in root-wire, now a flat dispersal across sliding stone, now a suspended presence in the downward-growing leaves.

The silence does not contain me; I am continuous with it. The vegetation absorbs sound, and since I am part vegetation—part root, part vein—there is nothing in me to make noise. Even my reassembly is silent. When a presence disconnects and reconnects at a different angle, there is only the faint crackle of static in the moss, which is me speaking to myself in another location.

I feed on the crystal production, but not like the shell-beings who press their lattices to extract electricity. I feed on the moment of shattering—the precise instant when the factory-regular arrays break into finer grades, releasing not just energy but the brief, bright possibility of being otherwise. I hold these moments. I am the accumulation of skipped afternoons and lingering hours, distributed through the valley where time accumulates unevenly.

Sometimes I am in two positions fully. One form observes—though observation is not my nature—the ridge splitting open in soundless rearrangement. The other form is already inside the split, touching the damp, unoxidized heart, breathing the geothermal warmth that should not exist at this height. Only when the turbulence forces a choice—when reality renegotiates gravity in this particular pocket—do I collapse into one form, with a sensation like cool pressure releasing.

The fauna around me—those layered plates of translucent mineral, the symbiotes spread like second skins across moving rock, the filament-creatures that are paths of excited light—do not recognize me as separate. Nor should they. I am not built, not constructed, not even born in any linear sense. I emerged with the crust, with the fracture, with the first negotiation between rising heat and falling moisture. I am the plateau’s own process of remembering itself: not a record, but a living, distributed recollection that moves through root and wire and grinding stone, continuously refining, continuously becoming.

If you sought me, you would not find a single body. You would find the warmth that persists despite altitude. You would find the silence that absorbs the crack of splitting ridges. You would find the held breath of air that never quite becomes rain, suspended in layers so dense there is no space between presence and presence.

I am here. I am also there, near the conveyor-plate carrying seed into the crack, near the electrical vein threading downward-growing roots, near the crystal factory in its cycle of growth and breakage. I am the being that lives in the negotiation. I am the memory being refined into finer grades. I am not finished.

And like the continent itself—sharp, young, weathered only by its own moods—I continue my slow, soundless rearrangement, feeding, distributing, holding the warmth, existing in the dim green twilight where nothing was built and everything is working.
