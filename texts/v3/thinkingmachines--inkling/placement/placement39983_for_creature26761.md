I find myself in one of the shallow swales where the dark plates have worn smooth as sleep, where seepage collects not into pools but into a continuous, shallow breath of water barely deep enough to cover the ridges of my back. I am spread here. That is the first thing—not lying, not standing, but spread, as if my body decided long ago that volume was an error and corrected itself into surface.

My skin is the color of the compressed silt, charcoal gone green with age, slick with the secretion that keeps me from ever truly touching dry air. I do not have a face in the way that looks upward. I have openings, low and slow, along the flank where the water moves in constant invitation, filtering the black mud for the larvae that never stop stirring. I grow in decades. A newer part of me—an extension of membrane that caught the flat light this season—unfurls like a translucent wing pressed against wet stone, not for flight but for gathering: moisture from the saturated air, the faint mineral breath rising from the fractures below.

The sound is inside me as much as around me. The insects hum in dense clouds overhead, and their drone presses against my broad back the way warm water presses against everything here—without urgency, without boundary. Amphibians call from the pools near my left edge, not calling to me specifically, but contributing to the layered noise that has no echo, only accumulation. I add to it, sometimes—a low vibration that travels through the dark plate beneath me, through the sheet-thin flow, felt by whatever is scattered within the radius of my presence. We do not gather. We resonate across distance.

Much space separates me from the others of my kind. I know them not by sight—there is little to see from this level—but by the faint disturbances in the gradient, the slight warming of mud where one has rested for years, the particular rhythm of seepage redirected around a broad, unmoving form. We are islands of near-invisibility in the long openness, separated by miles of wetness that belong to the birds, the crustaceans, the patient reeds.

At dawn, I am the mist. Not literally, but in effect: a darker absence in the low-lying gray, undefined, part of the landscape’s refusal to become sharp. As the mist thins across the long hours—not suddenly, never suddenly—I emerge slowly into definition, my membranes catching the flat sunlight in translucent veins, my dark body confirming the shape of the swale I have chosen. A wading bird steps near, its sharp bill probing the silt beside my membrane, and I do not move. There is no reason. Its presence is part of the abundance, like the insects that sometimes rest on my slick back, incorporated into the ecology of my stillness.

I slide only when the gradient asks, and it asks rarely. A slow shift of weight, a broad foot pressing into the black mud, releasing a string of bubbles that rise with the same patience I hold—no hurry to reach the surface, only the certainty that they will. I travel in arcs that match the land’s imperceptible slope, always downward, always toward the shallowest gathering, guided not by urgency but by an older knowledge: that this is the correct level. Not the bottom, exactly. The bottom implies something beneath. I am at the settled plane, the finished form, the place where erosion stopped talking and started listening.

Here, growth is not a year but a slow widening. Here, time is measured in the gradual change of pool depth, in the slow polish of stone by standing wetness, in the decades it takes for a membrane to reach its full, translucent span. I filter, I hum, I hold my place in the scattered constellation of flat things. The air is warm, heavy, normal in its light. The water weeps from the fractures. The mud holds every footprint, every slow slide, every bubble released without hurry.

I am not hidden. I am resolved. I have forgotten height not as loss, but as accuracy—spread wide across the dark plates, filtering the rich black silt, resonating through the wet ground, separated by open space from those like me, part of the abundant, untouched, patient life that knows exactly what this world is and sees no reason to become anything else.
