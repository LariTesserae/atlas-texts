There are marsh-edges here at extreme elevation, impossible pools where water collects without outlet, cool and still, reflecting not the sky but geometries that have not occurred yet. The atmosphere itself is damp, thick with vapor that should freeze in this cold but refuses, clinging to every crystal face in a perpetual post-rain state without the rain ever having fallen.

The weirdness is 2.7—reality breaking down, not as spectacle but as rule. The bright light makes colors behave incorrectly—blue in the water, green in the black glass—hues that do not belong to the minerals present. Sometimes, in the loud hum, you hear not a tone but the shape of the valley translated into pressure, as if the land is describing itself to something that listens from outside the elevation.

You are in an open region—town-or-valley sized—where every volume is saturated, every surface weeps or sings or reflects wrong, where the cold is stable and the form is ancient, and where the only history written is in the scattered, primitive marks of hands that came, tried to measure what could not be measured, and left behind only stone arranged in questions. No life grows here. Only the mineral, the water that climbs, the light without source, and the sound of a world that has reached its strange, settled equilibrium and continues, actively, loudly, to be exactly itself.
