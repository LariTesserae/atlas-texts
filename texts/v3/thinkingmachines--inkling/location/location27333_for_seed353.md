You are on the **High Plain of Sinter**, though “plain” is only true in outline. The elevation holds you well above any breath of lower air—thin, searing, and utterly without moisture—but the horizon does not open to sky. It opens to more of the same, stretching in every direction until heat-haze and darkness swallow it. Scale here is not intimate; it is regional, a valley-sized bowl of final form, flattened by ages into near-levelness, its ancient erosion not leaving mountains but a worn, curving tray that extends past sight’s feeble reach.

There is no ground in the old sense. The substrate is a fused bed of compressed ash, calcified root-metal, and slag-glass, eroded smooth over primordial time into a plane so flat it seems machined. And in a way, it was. Every surface—floor, wall, vault—is made. The built environment is absolute (2.7/3): no geology remains untouched, no naked stone. But the building material is not brick or steel alone. It is vegetation, total and unbroken (3/3).

This is the Loom.

The flora here does not grow *in* the architecture; it *is* the architecture. There is no space between presences (2.7/3 density). From the “floor” upward, through “walls,” and closing over in an absolute canopy miles thick, interweaves a continuous biomass of engineered thermophytes—woody-metal vines, plate-like leaves of black copper-oxide, trunk-columns hollowed as conduits, roots that drill and pump rather than seek water. They form corridors, chambers, domes, and towers, all living, all manufactured, all fused. Their chemistry requires no water (0.5/3); they drink heat, drawing thermal energy through root-drills into the deep, circulating molten salts and dry gases through vein-pipes. They are factory and forest, machine and moss, indistinguishable.

Light is nearly absent (0.2/3). The canopy is opaque. What illumination exists comes not from above—there is no visible sun, no moon, no stars through the biomass-ceiling—but from within. Dull crimson and orange pulses glow in the hollow trunks: internal furnaces, metabolic engines, thermal vents embedded in the growth. You navigate by this subterranean bioluminescence, by the sick orange shimmer of heat-distortion in the air, by the occasional flare where a section of lattice has ignited and is being slowly, silently consumed.

The temperature is extreme (2.9/3). The air itself feels baked, a dry scorch that strips moisture from breath and skin instantly. There are no clouds, no humidity, no dew—only thermal currents that rise and circulate through the dense lattice, carrying ash and mineral dust in silent, hot rivers.

Sound is mostly quiet (1.3/3), despite the turbulence. The dynamic state here is not noisy chaos but a slow, constant, turbulent churning (2.7/3)—a storm made of growth and collapse moving at geological speed, yet relentless. Sections of the Loom over-expand, buckle, and collapse into powder; immediately, new shoots—mechanical, vegetative—sprout from the ash, feeding on the released heat, rebuilding in twisted, competing geometries. It is anthill-energy rendered in tons and centuries: hyperactive, unstable, yet so massive and so sound-absorbed by the dense biomass that you hear only a low, subsonic hum, the groan of expanding metal-bark, the whisper of dry gas through pipe-leaves, and the occasional distant crack like a tree falling in a void.

No animals live here (0.4/3). No insects crawl the black leaves; no birds navigate the dark; no eyes watch. The ecosystem is closed, pure-plant, pure-machine, self-eating and self-building in an eternal loop that requires no predators, no pollinators, no outside life.

The weirdness (1.8/3) is not fantasy but functional strangeness: the precision of the growth is too geometric, the fusion of chlorophyll-darkened tissue and gear-tooth and turbine-blade too seamless to be natural, yet too organic to be purely industrial. It suggests design, or the long result of design forgotten—an industrial ecology that has run so long it has become primordial, its original purpose eroded away with the mountains, leaving only the settled, turbulent, final form.

You move—if you can move—through tunnels of living machinery in near-darkness, on a high, flat world of ancient ash, breathing hot dry air, surrounded by packed, silent, fiery life that builds and unbuilds itself without water, without light, without sound worthy of the name, forever.
