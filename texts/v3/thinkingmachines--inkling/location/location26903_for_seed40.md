Above the cloud line—though there are no clouds here, only a cool, dry suspension—the air is thin enough to make thought feel sharp. The elevation is not geological; it was raised. You stand on a high region recently assembled, its summit still carrying the fresh scars of fabrication: edges unrounded, corners unweathered, seams that have not yet learned to hide. This is young terrain, sharp-featured, a highland that emerged from purpose rather than pressure.

The scale is open, a valley-sized realm extending in all directions, yet it is impossibly dense. Not enclosed—there are no roofs, no rooms—but saturated. Every cubic meter of the open air is shared with structure: girders, ceramic plates, conduits of brushed alloy, lattice works stacked and interwoven so closely that they form a second, manufactured sky and a manufactured ground simultaneously. You move through it not by walking on earth but by navigating the narrow, consistent corridors left between near-touching presences. From a distance, the region reads as a bright, industrial mountain range; up close, it is a tissue of machinery with almost no negative space, layered and high-saturation, extending past sight.

Everything is made. There is no rock, no soil, no original substrate—only the built. The floor is a grid of cold plates. The “hills” are accumulations of vertical engines and silent turbines arranged in ridges. The “valleys” are long, straight depressions between dense walls of production architecture, open to the bright sky that is not sky but illumination.

That light—intense, flat, merciless—comes from everywhere and nowhere. It is 2.7 bright: a luminosity that does not cast shadows correctly. Some beams glow; others absorb without darkening; shadows, where they occur, point toward the source or linger where objects have not yet arrived. The temperature remains cool (1.4), and the air holds an intermittent dampness—not rivers, not pools, but condensation that beads on metal surfaces, hangs in droplets for long durations, and releases slowly into lower levels, falling through the dense lattice like slow rain that never reaches soil, only more cold plate.

Vegetation is sparse (1.2): pale, rust-eating lichen in the seams; a faint, metallic moss that clings to the north-facing plates; occasional stalks of something translucent and slow-growing that feed on the moisture and the strange light. They do not sway; they extend with the patience of the place.

The fauna, however, is abundant (2.0). Countless small beings inhabit the interstitial architecture—translucent, limb-light creatures that crawl along the girders in silent multitudes, feeding on condensation and the microscopic wear of fresh metal. They do not fly; flight is unnecessary in a world where every surface is within reach. They are industrial in origin or adaptation, bodies of thin membrane and tiny gears, reproducing slowly across the open valley. They make no sound.

Indeed, sound itself is mostly absent (0.6). The machinery—industrial, mechanized, scaled—runs without noise. Engines that should roar operate in perfect silence, turning processes too slow or too strange to perceive. The fauna is mute. The wind, if it moves through the high corridors, produces no whistle, no rush. What remains is a quiet so dense it feels like pressure: the sound of reality running on idle.

That reality is breaking (2.8). The young, sharp structures do not obey continuous space. A corridor that extends north may suddenly contain its own end, folded into a vertical plane that should be impossible at this elevation. The bright light reveals details—wires, distant figures, machinery—before they are fully present, and withholds others that should be immediate. Time in this high built region behaves with the same slowness (1.4) and strangeness: change is gradual, but causality is stretched. The structures grow, millimeters per day, extending new sharp edges that seem to have existed for years in other directions. The fauna leaves tracks that appear before the step, or none at all. The condensation beads form, hang, fall—not in minutes, but across long, patient cycles that blur the boundary between observation and memory.

This is an open, regional world—town-or-valley in scale—yet every part of it is occupied. The industrial purpose is total but dormant, or perhaps running at a frequency beneath perception, transforming the damp cool air into more structure, more light, more quiet presence. You can travel for miles through the dense lattice and never encounter soil, never hear an echo, never step outside the made environment. Only occasionally, in a pocket where moisture gathers thickly and the bright illumination falters, do you sense the place is not stable, that its young sharpness is still deciding what it is, growing into its own broken logic with infinite, silent patience.
