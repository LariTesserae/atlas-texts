You find it by accident—an embedded pocket only revealed because one wall of a larger dead structure has sheared away, leaving a mouth no taller than a crouching person. The space inside is not a cave but a *room that forgot to be large*, a single contained chamber perhaps twenty paces long and as many wide, its ceiling so low you could touch it with an upraised hand. Scale here is intimate, pocket-sized, hidden within something vaster that has long since collapsed into anonymity. You must lower yourself and crawl forward into the cool, still absence of water.

The temperature never rises above a patient chill—around 0.9 on that invisible scale—dry as powdered bone. There is no moisture in the air, no dampness on the stone. The atmosphere feels sealed, preserved, as if the outside world never learned to reach in.

Every sharp edge that ever existed here has been rounded off by uncounted centuries of abrasion. The ground is not dirt but a compressed, layered sediment—metal filings ground to dust, ceramic splintered and polished smooth, rust-flakes settled into a dense, dark laminate that crunches softly under pressure. And yet—life is not absent. It is overwhelming.

They feed slowly, constantly, on oxidation, on the mineral residue of dead industry, on each other when the older ones weaken. That is the technology—scattered, faint, but present. At the far end of the pocket, half-buried in the creature-layer, sits a single engine-block, its cylinders rounded smooth by the same ancient wear that shaped the floor. Beside it, a generator or compressor, its housing cracked like an egg, still cycles. It never stops. From its core comes a steady, cool white light—normal, unremarkable, but bright enough to fill the chamber evenly, casting no deep shadows, illuminating the swarming bodies in flat clarity. This is a world without sky, without rain, without green. Only the cool, the flat, the ancient, the loud, the crowded. A hidden chamber where industry decayed into ecosystem, where overwhelming fauna thrives on rust in perpetual darkness-made-light, and where time has worn everything—machine, rock, and life—into its final, smooth, inseparable form.
