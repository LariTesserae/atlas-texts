You find yourself not in a place but in a pocket—an intimate chamber embedded within the greater warmth of low-lying lands, so small that its edges seem to dissolve into the enclosing body of the world. There is no horizon here, only curvature: a shallow dome, perhaps a hollow formed by the collapse and immediate regeneration of living stone, or a chamber grown within the root-mass of something vaster. Scale is intimate—this is a single room pretending to be a settlement, a town compressed until it breathes like an organism.

The temperature is constant, a warm humidity that clings to skin and never chills. Water is intermittent, not a river but a condition: beads of condensation sliding down translucent membranes overhead, occasional droplets falling with almost inaudible precision, pockets of moisture held in the crevices of fresh-formed surfaces. There is no dryness, only degrees of damp. The air tastes of something mineral being born.

This is urban, yes—organized construction, settlement logic—but built without technology, without tools, without the hard geometry of human intent. The structures are arranged in sparse clusters across the floor, separated by wide spaces of raw ground. They are made of woven root-fiber, calcified mud still curing, secretions that have hardened only yesterday into walls and low archways. Nothing is eroded. There is no weathering, no patina of age, no crack filled with the dust of years. Every surface is new: smooth, slightly yielding, still holding the memory of being formed. The place is under construction not by laborers but by process—growing, being excreted, woven by the abundant fauna that moves through it.

The vegetation is lush but not crowded. Thick vines hang in loops from the low ceiling, ferns unfurl in scattered groves, moss carpets patches of the floor in deep green—yet between these presences there is emptiness, deliberate or inevitable, a low density that lets the eye travel and the body move freely. The settlement is not filled; it is planted, arranged with space.

Light is dim—filtered through the translucent tissue of the dome above, or perhaps through dense outer growth, arriving in muted gold-greens that make color feel submerged. Shadows are soft, lacking sharp edges. You can see, but not far; the pocket rewards close attention.

Fauna is abundant and active. Small amphibians pulse in the shallow pools of condensation, leaving fleeting prints in the soft curing mud. Insects—winged, crawling—move in constant, quiet traffic, carrying spores or fibers, building, feeding. There is no predator spectacle, only the steady dynamics of an ecosystem in motion: consumption, construction, reproduction, migration through the sparse streets of this embryonic town. Everything is alive, and everything is working.

The sound is nearly silent—0.1 on any scale—just the occasional drip, the faint creak of growing wood or hardening shell, the whisper of bodies passing over surface. No voices, no machinery, no wind because there is no open sky. Only the quiet sounds of a place being born.

And it is being born. The erosion is zero because nothing here has been worn away; the geology is raw, the buildings fresh, the settlement emerging. This is pre-formation made visible—a hidden micro-environment, normal in its strangeness, warm and damp and dim, where urban order and wild growth have not yet decided they are different things. It is a town that is also a nest, a chamber that is also a garden, active and sparse and hidden, holding its breath as it becomes.
