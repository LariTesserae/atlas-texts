The chamber is not dug; it is grown open—an intimate wound in the world below, pocket-scale and hidden within the greater mass of root and rhizome. You do not enter it so much as become embedded in it, swallowed by plant-architecture that has suddenly, recently, torn itself hollow.

Everything here is vegetation—total, absolute, plant-as-substrate. There is no stone, no soil in the conventional sense, only the living walls: fibrous root-bark thick as hide, vascular membranes stretched taut like drum-skins, and slender pillar-roots that rise not from ground but from the very air, anchoring to a ceiling of woven mycelium and translucent sap-bladder. The vegetation fills all space, yet it fills it as boundary, not as presence. Between these living walls, the volume is near-void—density so low the air itself feels like an absence. Vast distances separate one fibrous surface from the next. The chamber is perhaps the size of a small room, but within it, emptiness dominates; you stand in the middle and perceive every wall at once, suspended in a hollow cathedral of warm, damp air.

The formation is young—erosion here is fresh, sharp-featured, not weathered by centuries. The edges of the root-membranes are ragged, recently ruptured, bleeding slow rivulets of golden sap that never quite clot. The wound that made this pocket is still healing, or perhaps refusing to heal; young tendrils reach across the void in thin, searching threads, bridging the emptiness too quickly, as if the space were an injury the organism urgently needs to close.

Below surface—elevation zero—yet light is normal, bright, wrong. It does not filter downward; it pools upward from the plants themselves, bleeding through translucent tissue in a steady, warm amber glow. Shadows behave poorly here. In some corners they fall upward, clinging to the ceiling; in others they are absent entirely, leaving objects illuminated but not anchored. The reality-break is not violent—it is pervasive, gentle, and absolute. The chamber is larger inside than its exterior boundary permits. Distance is unreliable; a wall that looks an arm’s reach away may take ten steps to reach, or none, depending on where you stand. Time moves unevenly—near the bleeding ceiling, growth is visible, a slow-motion bloom of fiber; near the floor, everything holds its breath.

Water is intermittent, damp, present as atmosphere rather than flow. The air is humid with metabolic breath. Condensation beads constantly on the cool vascular surfaces—tiny droplets of pure, warm moisture that swell, hang, and fall in random rhythms, not quite obeying gravity, sometimes drifting sideways through the hollow or hovering mid-air before deciding. Small depressions in the root-floor collect shallow pools, black mirrors reflecting the impossible light, but there are no rivers, only these pocketed moments of moisture in larger dryness.

Temperature is warm—womb-warm, compost-warm, the heat of rapid biological process. The walls radiate a low, constant warmth like skin.

Sound fills the emptiness despite the void. It is loud—not from many sources, but from amplification. The hollow chamber acts as a resonator. You hear the rapid, sharp cracking of new fiber splitting; the long, wet sigh of membranes stretching; the rushing of sap through thick veins near the walls, like water in distant pipes, but closer, louder, intimate. Dripping condensation echoes with disproportionate weight. And beneath these biological sounds, there is another noise—a continuous, low roar, not quite wind, not quite voice, perhaps the sound of the reality-fracture itself, breathing through the fresh wound in the world.

Fauna is rare—almost none. The ecosystem here is pure plant, self-contained, self-eating, self-growing. Yet there is one presence other than the living walls: a primitive artifact, simple technology embedded in a lower pillar-root. It is a hand-carved implement—bone or hardened root-wood, pre-metal, manually fashioned with rough precision—perhaps a scraper or a cutting wedge, its edges worn not by use but by overgrowth. The plant has begun to reclaim it, weaving fibers through its handle, drawing it back into the substrate. No maker remains. The tool is the only evidence that something with hands once knew this hidden pocket, long before the recent rupture opened it.

The place is active, dynamic, in constant motion. The roots thicken visibly. The ceiling-fracture—a ragged tear that should reveal earth but instead reveals a strange, bright void—breathes, widening and contracting with a slow, organic pulse. Condensation cycles form and dissipate like weather inside a skull. The searching tendrils cross and recross the hollow, altering paths through the air. Nothing settles.

You are inside something that is not dead, not exactly alive in the way animals understand, but urgently, loudly, warmly present—an empty chamber made entirely of living edges, recently born, breaking quietly, and holding within its near-void the impossible light of a world that has not decided what rules apply here.
