The place is not a ruin. It is a conception.

You are suspended—everywhere is suspension—within a warm, pressurized dark that has never known air. This is the high country of a drowned world, but “high” here means nothing to the sky; it means altitude measured against the abyss. A cluster of young volcanic seamounts rises through the water column, their basalt flanks still cooling, still contracting, still *becoming*. The geology is embryonic: raw, uneroded, pre-formation. There are no smooth stones worn by ages, only jagged, porous rock that looks like half-formed bone, crusted with fresh mineral bloom. The elevation is dramatic—peaks thrust upward for thousands of feet, yet every summit remains deep beneath any surface, a high plateau submerged in a warm, silent ocean that extends in all directions with regional, open immensity.

The water itself is thick with warmth—around 2.5 on that scale—heated not by sun but by the magma breathing slowly beneath the plateau. It moves barely at all. Currents are patient, almost absent; the dynamic is one of gradual cycles, of change across long durations. Sound, too, is subdued: mostly quiet, the muffled stillness of deep liquid, broken only by the occasional low groan of contracting stone or the soft release of geothermal bubbles that rise like slow thoughts through the dark.

And yet, upon these raw, emerging heights, there is a settlement.

It is urban—dense, layered, settlement-scale, spreading across the valleys and ridges of the seamount cluster like a crust of intentional form. The construction is organized, infrastructural, but the technology is primitive: simple metal, hand-tools, manual craft. The builders have carved terraces into the cooling volcanic rock, stacked pumice blocks into stairways and plazas, and woven massive kelp-cables to bridge the chasms between peaks. There are plazas here, and roads—if “roads” can describe paths of packed ash and coral-sand that wind through the structures. The density is high; buildings and pathways layer over one another, near-touching, creating a vertical city that climbs the slopes of the underwater mountains rather than reaching toward a nonexistent sky.

The vegetation is lush—1.8, thriving in the warm, mineral-rich flow. This is not sparse deep-sea life. Giant stalks of soft coral grow like cathedral spires, their branches interlacing with the hand-built terraces. Kelp forests rise in dense, vertical thickets, their fronds waving with such slowness in the weak current that they seem to meditate. Sponge-towers and anemone groves claim every available surface, growing through windows carved in basalt, draping over stairways, integrating so fully with the architecture that it is hard to tell where the settlement ends and the forest begins. The ecology is plant-dominated, dense with presence, a living saturation.

Light is dim—1.1—barely present. No sun penetrates here. Illumination comes from cultivated bioluminescence: phosphorescent fungi tended in stone lanterns, ropes of glow-worms strung between terraces, and the faint orange pulse of the geothermal vents far below, coloring the lower depths in a distant, warm murk. The settlement glows softly in pockets, a scattered constellation of blue-green and amber in overwhelming black.

Fauna is rare—1.2. This is not a place of abundant animal life. A few slow-moving filter-feeders drift through the kelp like drifting clouds of gray tissue. Small scavengers pick at the edges of the structures. But mostly, the inhabitants—if they are inhabitants, for the place feels inhabited without showing its builders—move slowly, or not at all. The life here is vegetative, geological, architectural.

The weirdness is substantial—1.8. The rock itself behaves like embryonic tissue; walls seem to grow, to seal their own cracks with fresh mineral secretion. The boundary between built and grown is dissolved. The city is under construction in a way that feels biological: not being built so much as being gestated. There is a sense that this plateau, this settlement, this lush submerged highland, is in a state of pre-formation—not decaying, not finished, but slowly, patiently, coming into being.

You stand—if standing is possible in such suspension—within an open region of this developing world. The scale extends past immediate sight: other peaks visible in the murk, other terraces climbing them, a valley of dark architecture and darker vegetation stretching into the regional distance. Everything is layered, dense, close, yet open-ended, extending. It is urban and wild simultaneously. Primitive and complex. High and sunken. Emerging, always emerging, in the slow, warm, quiet dark.
