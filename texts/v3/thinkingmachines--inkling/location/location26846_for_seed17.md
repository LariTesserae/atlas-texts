The ocean here is not an ocean so much as a condition—an all-enveloping, near-freezing liquid that fills every direction with the same flat, black-blue clarity. Temperature holds at a knife-edge near absolute stillness; there are no warm currents, no thermal breath, only a uniform cold that has never varied and never will. This is water at 2.7, submerged to the root of things: not merely deep, but total, a planetary bath in which everything that exists is either anchored, floating, or drowned in place.

Yet the density is 0.1—emptiness disguised as fullness. For all its volume, the fluid carries almost nothing within it. The spaces between presences are continental, abyssal, hollow in the way only true scale can make hollow. You could travel for days through this cold and find only the pressure of your own movement.

The scale is 2.9, vast enough to generate its own physics, though it has chosen not to. There is no changing weather here—dynamic sits at 0.4, frozen in equilibrium—only one eternal state: a static sky of dark, unmoving water above, and below, the sharp, young bones of the world.

Elevation reads 1.6, and it manifests as spires. Not mountains worn smooth by age—erosion is 1.0, fresh, recently born—but crystalline and basalt towers thrust upward from the plain, their edges unrounded, their fractures still open like mouths that have never spoken. They rise high through the liquid, some breaking near the upper dark, others lost in the downward black. They are scattered across a distance too wide for eyes, separated by hundreds of miles of silent, creature-empty water.

Vegetation is 0.2—there is none. No kelp, no slime, no fungal mat. Only mineral surfaces: black glass, white crystal, rusted iron where the built world touches them. The world is pre-plant, post-plant, or simply never-plant—a mineral architecture hosting other lives.

And there are lives—fauna at 1.5, abundant, though abundance here means number spread across infinity. They drift in thin, endless schools: translucent, cold-adapted forms without eyes that need light, bodies like folded glass and wire, filtering the chemical traces that seep from the scattered structures. They do not swarm; they maintain precise, unchanging distances from one another, moving in loops that have never deviated. They are strange, but only at 0.9—strange like a familiar dream misremembered, not like nightmare.

The built presence is 0.6—scattered, faint, occasional. Small industrial installations cling to the spires like barnacles that learned to think. At tech level 2.0, they are mechanized, scaled, electrified: pump stations, smelters, engines that draw nothing visible and produce nothing visible, running on cycles that do not complete because they never began. They emit a flat, normal light—2.0—harsh and white, cutting through the dark liquid without warmth or flicker, visible for impossible distances across the empty water.

But the defining feature is the sound: 2.9, deafening. Not loud in the way thunder is loud—variable, shocking—but constant, absolute, a roar that fills every cubic inch of this cold void. The machinery produces it, yes, but the water carries it without loss. It is a drone that never shifts in pitch, never fades, never echoes because there is nothing to interrupt it. In this near-empty density, the sound travels as if through vacuum and solid at once, pressing against everything with identical force. It is the only sound, and it has always been here, and it will never change.

The atmosphere—if liquid can hold atmosphere—is one of suspended stasis. The spires do not grow; they were born sharp and remain sharp. The creatures do not breed or die in visible ways; they persist. The machines do not break; they run. Even the erosion is frozen at its youngest stage, as if time were permitted to form the world but forbidden to wear it.

Weirdness at 0.9 reveals itself in the margins: the way the creatures align to invisible axes, the way light from the installations reflects upward in straight lines that do not bend, the way the cold feels not biting but absent—temperature withdrawn rather than present. There is a sense that this place was constructed and then abandoned not to decay but to continue exactly as built, in perpetual, deafening, unchanging equilibrium.

You stand—if one can stand here—on a young, sharp peak in an infinite cold bath, surrounded by scattered lights that never dim, filled with abundant but infinitely dispersed life that never touches you, drowned in a roar that has no source and no end, in a world large enough to be its own universe, empty enough to hear every detail of its eternal, frozen song.
