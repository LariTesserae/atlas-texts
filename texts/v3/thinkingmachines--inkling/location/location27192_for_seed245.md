The ground is not ground. It is a sheet laid down yesterday—yesterday being a unit that has no currency here—and it has not decided yet whether it will be floor, skin, or bone. You stand on a continent that is still being made, low and flat as a table, stretching until the dimness eats it. There are no hills because hills require time to negotiate. This place has not had that conversation. The elevation barely exists; the world sits at 1.3, a shallow dish of synthetic mineral spread across a planetary scale, so vast that weather belongs to it alone, yet so sparse in its presences that you can walk for hours without encountering anything but the plane itself.

Everything is made. Nothing grew. The substrate is a dark, porous ceramic—ash compressed into stone by heat and pressure—still curing. It breathes warm, chemical breath from every pore. The temperature is 2.6, not the clean heat of sun but the fever-heat of matter undergoing its first transformation, exothermic, impatient. Where the sheet is thinnest, near the surface, water weeps up in slow, oily beads—1.7 on the wet scale—not rain, not river, but the plane’s own sweat, a marsh-edge of chemistry. It pools in the shallow depressions left by tools: black, still, reflecting the dimness back up without light.

There is almost nothing alive to see. Vegetation is 0.5—absent—not dead, but never invited. No trees, no moss, no fungal whisper. The fauna is 0.4, a null value. You will not see an insect, not even the suggestion of one. The only life-forms, if they can be called that, are structural: the landscape itself is the organism, turbulent and embryonic.

And the turbulence is everywhere, though it makes almost no sound. The dynamic is 2.6—chaotic change, Solaris-like, creation and destruction in the same breath—but the sound scale is 1.4, mostly quiet. So the world shifts without announcement. Sections of the plane rise a finger’s width and settle back, not like earth but like a chest breathing in a sleep from which it will not fully wake. New material extrudes at the horizons, dark paste hardening into plate, while elsewhere older sections flake away in silent clouds of black dust, not eroded by wind (wind is minimal, the air thick and motionless with steam) but undone by their own unfinished chemistry. It is erosion in reverse: not wearing down, but not-yet-become.

The builders—if they were builders—left primitive marks. Technology here is 1.2: hand-tools, simple metal, manual craft. At this scale, that fact is madness. You find, scattered sparsely across the vastness (density 1.4), enormous levers of crude bronze, half-buried in the weeping surface. Ropes woven from some synthetic fiber, thick as a thigh, lie in loops that seem to measure distances now meaningless because distance itself breaks. There are trenches dug by something with patience and no machine—straight lines that go on for miles, shallow, filling with the black weeping water, as if someone tried to plumb a continent with a spoon.

But no bodies. No camps. Only the tools, and they are wrong. Some are scaled for beings that could not exist in this gravity, which shifts subtly from quadrant to quadrant. Others are miniature, toy-sized, lying in the pools like offerings. The weirdness is 2.8; reality here is not broken in one dramatic fracture but in a thousand small disagreements. The horizon behaves like a liquid mirror. You look left and see the trench you just walked away from, closer than your own shadow. The dim light—1.4, a dull glow from the curing plane itself, no sun, no stars visible through the thick, condensing atmosphere—makes perspective unreliable. Shadows do not fall in consistent directions. They pool like the water.

The air is wet with condensation, 1.7, never quite raining, always on the verge. It clings to the synthetic stone in beads, runs in slow rivulets toward the trenches, disappears into seams that open and close with the plane’s silent turbulence. The smell is mineral and hot: burned clay, hot iron, something like ozone but older, pre-electric.

You walk, and the world is quiet. No wind-howl, no crack of cooling stone, no creature-call. Only, sometimes, a sub-bass tremor felt through the soles—too low to hear, the sound of the sheet adjusting its chemistry, of the primitive tools being slowly swallowed or rejected by the surface that never asked for them.

In the far distance—though “far” is a lie—the plane seems to turn upward, not into sky but into more plane, a vertical wall of unfinished world. Clouds of black curing-dust hang there, motionless, and in them, occasionally, shapes that are not shapes: the negative spaces left by tools that have already dissolved back into the material they shaped.

This is a world without substrate, without origin myth, without life except the life of its own becoming. It is vast, low, hot, wet with its own labor, built entirely and primitively, quiet in its chaos, breaking the rules of space and time in small, relentless ways. You do not visit this place; you witness it in the act of deciding whether to exist at all.
