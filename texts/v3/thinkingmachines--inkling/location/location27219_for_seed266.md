What emerges, then, is a place of warm, dry, extreme height and vast emptiness, organized not for haste but for endurance. A continent of rounded old stone, scattered with silent, slow lives and older, slower machines, all operating in a shared quiet, all part of a chemistry without water, a biology without green, a city without crowding. You stand on the plateau, and the wind that has shaped everything moves past you, thin and warm, carrying nothing, promising nothing, and the Drifter on the horizon does not look up. It does not need to. It has time.
