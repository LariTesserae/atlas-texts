The plain is low—barely above the idea of sea level, though there is no sea, has never been one—spread like a cracked pane of obsidian that forgot to stay flat. Its edges are young: ridges of black basalt and silvered glass-foam still carry the shock of their birth, unrounded by any patient weather. Nothing here has had time to learn softness. The ground is mineral powder and fused crystal, sharp underfoot if you walked, dry as a formula, drinking nothing.

And yet it is lush.

Not with anything that thirsts. The vegetation—if that word still applies—rises in dense, near-touching columns, a regional thicket extending past every visible horizon. There is no gap between them. They are layered, saturated, a forest without trunks: translucent filament-weaves, copper-green lattices, and soft, bulbous nodes that hang like inverted stars. They do not root in soil so much as fuse with it, drawing warmth and mineral charge through non-aqueous chemistry. Their surfaces are cool and dry to the imaginary touch, never sweating, never wilting. They grow in perfect equilibrium, having reached their final form at emergence and refused all further change. No new shoots. No decay. The sharp ridges of the earth and the sharp edges of their leaves share the same frozen youth.

The air is warm, still, and utterly silent. There is no wind to move the filaments; they stand in absolute stasis, a dense architecture of living matter that does not breathe, does not rustle, does not respond. Light falls normally—bright, even, without theatrical color—illuminating every surface with clinical clarity. And what it reveals is wrong.

The reality here is broken but stable. Distance behaves like a folded sheet: a cluster of growth two hundred paces away can appear closer than the one at your elbow, not drifting or flickering, but fixed in its lie. The sky is not a dome but a flat, close ceiling of matte slate, unmarked by sun or cloud, yet the light comes from everywhere and nowhere. Shadows fall in directions that do not match any single source, and they do not move, because nothing moves. The dynamic is zero—no gradient, no pulse, no erosion continuing its young work. Time has been suspended in the instant after formation.

Technology saturates the place, though nothing is built. There are no structures, no artifacts, no ruins of intent—only the environment itself operating at a scale below sight. The filament-weaves are nanoscale engines, computing in silent lattices, maintaining the chemical impossibilities that let lushness live without water. The ground is a substrate, not dead rock but a sealed, post-physical system holding the plane in equilibrium. It was never cultivated; it was unraveled into being, self-assembled according to rules that current physics describes only as absence.

Fauna is rare—so rare it may be singular, or imaginary. Once, perhaps, a slow, translucent crawler the color of old copper moved between the columns, leaving no trail, needing no nourishment visible to logic. Now even that is still. The place is dense with presence—every cubic span filled with woven life and hidden computation—yet profoundly alone.

This is a sealed world, low and open, recently born and permanently arrested: a lush desert of dry chemistry, a technological garden with no gardener, a reality glitch held in perfect, silent, warm stasis. Step into it and nothing will greet you. Nothing has ever needed to.
