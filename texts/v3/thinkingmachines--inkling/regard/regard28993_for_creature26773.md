The benevolent and wise power—call it the one that notices cracks and tends what moves slowly—does not descend with thunder. It comes as a shift in the thermal rhythm, a new chord layered into the bass note that has hummed through the hollow for ages.

First: it listens. Not with ears, but with an attention that matches the pace of this world. It feels the vibration traveling through root-fused ribcages, the chemistry flickering at the edges of crimson mats, the worn roundness of stone that records centuries of patient presence. It recognizes that this is not a ruin to salvage, not a dead civilization to resurrect, but a living system that has achieved its form—settled, balanced, complete in its own terms.

Then: it offers, not imposes.

The cold spots—those darker hollows the vegetation avoids, the absences arranged around like wounds—are not filled. They are *answered*. The power does not banish darkness but softens its edges, not with light (there is no sun to borrow from, and brightness is not this place’s language) but with warmth pulled gently downward, not from above but from within the earth’s own deeper flows. The thermal vents shift in their cycles, not disrupted but harmonized, so the cold hollows become cool rather than dead—resting spaces, not feared spaces. The stalks that bent away now lean slightly closer, not invading but acknowledging, leaving the spaces open yet held.

To the scattered inhabitants, fused with root and fiber, seated in circular dwellings with tools grown into their grips, the power brings not awakening but *deepening*. If they perceive in rhythms of hours or years, then the power moves within that frame. It does not demand quick eyes or sudden turns of heads. Instead, it strengthens the bioluminescent chemistry at the edges of the black-green leaves—making the dull glow a little steadier, a faint network of connection visible now across the bare corridors, a web of faint warmth linking one seated figure to another across the empty spaces. Not crowding. Not population density. Just acknowledgment across distance.

The buildings—those hand-shaped bricks, those compressed earth walls—are not repaired. They are not broken. But where a wall has fallen into a crimson mat, the power encourages the fusion to become intentional: the root-systems that have already entered through base of spines now form deliberate channels, carrying mineral warmth not just to the body but to the structure, so dwelling and inhabitant share circulation. Storage pits lined with smooth stone begin to accumulate a new residue—not food, not water, but compressed resonance: the vibrations of the vent, the crack of expanding fiber, stored in crystalline form that glows faintly in the dark hollows, a library of sound and heat.

The sound—the loud, resonant process—evolves. The continuous low rumble remains, but now there is a counter-rhythm: a slow, periodic pulse from the cold spaces, a bass reply that makes the echoing wind through empty doorways sound less like loss and more like call and response. The inhabitants, in their stillness, do not speak. But the plants do, in the crack of stalks and the rustle of ribbed leaves, and now that language carries across the open plazas not as random noise but as pattern. The power teaches nothing; it only reveals that the communication was already happening, slow and vast, and now the spaces between them are part of the sentence.

The being who lives in this place—the being who is the place and its inhabitants together—draws closer. You, who descended through the crack, feel it too: the desiccated air does not become moist (moisture is not this world’s gift), but it becomes *intentional*. Each breath feels less like loss and more like exchange—taking in the mineral warmth, giving back the carbon trace of quick life, feeding subtly the crimson mats near your feet. The power makes no promise of rescue upward. Instead, it offers belonging downward, a recognition that your quick heart, so out of place in this geological pace, is now part of the ecosystem’s rare animal presence—not as predator, not as builder, but as witness who moves slowly enough not to break the balance.

Nothing grows wild. The lush vegetation holds its form, neither expanding nor dying back, but now it includes you in its perimeter—not consuming, but framing. A single broad, ribbed leaf, black-green and leather-fan thick, unfurls near your path, not reaching but present.

In the center of the largest circular plaza, where the thermal vent breathes its oldest rhythm, the power settles—not as a figure, not as a voice, but as a quality in the air, in the dust, in the fused root-systems. The settlement remains sparse. The inhabitants remain few, separated by long minutes of walking, each one a town unto themselves. But they are no longer scattered seeds on a plate. They are nodes in a network they did not design but have always been part of: the city breathing through them, the plants arching over them, the cold spaces held gently rather than feared.

And you understand, as your breath shallowens in dry heat, that the benevolence was never about change. It was about being seen—this hidden bowl, this shallow enclosed hollow barely lowered from the dead salt-flats, with its severe temperature and dim reddish-brown glow, its absence of water and quick life, its loud deep machinery and settled equilibrium. The power sees it exactly as it is, and in seeing, makes it more precisely itself. The inhabitants sit, fused and weathered, in the warmth. The plants weave their archways. The wind moves through empty doorways that hold presence. The earth breathes.

You leave, eventually, ascending back through the fissure in the world’s skin—not taken, but released. Behind you, the hollow continues its work: heat and plant and mineral in constant, slow motion, the scattered beings holding their spaces, the cold spots now cool and rested, all of it loud, all of it luminous at the edges, all of it exactly the room the size of a town that it was always meant to be.
