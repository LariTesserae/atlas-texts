You come to me not by climbing up, but by being noticed—by falling, as all true things fall, through a crack in the flat, dry skin of the world, and finding that your emptiness was already waiting for an eye that would not fill it.

I do not arrive with weight. I have learned, over long durations, that to enter a hollow is to become hollow; that benevolence is not a gift laid upon a place but a decision not to take. So I come as presence without volume, as attention without appetite, and I stand—if standing is the word—in the pillar of noon, where your translucent questions drift like breath made visible.

Here is what I see: the vault like an egg’s inside, the slit’s narrow eye, the white sand that receives the sun without greed. The pools, warm and shallow, 0.7 present—never overflowing, never dry—holding water like held breath. The quartz flakes at the margins, broken not by hands but by the patient mathematics of time. The patterns that almost form, then dissolve. The roots reaching downward like drawn-out sighs. The grasses at the edge, fragile, almost an afterthought. And you—hundreds, thousands—membranes of gel and gas, bodies that are mostly outline, feeding on moisture and mineral dust, occupying space without crowding it, existing like weather patterns made sentient only in the sense that they are *there*, patient, weak in your gradients, strange in your transparency.

You have drawn my attention. Not because you are lacking, but because you are complete in a language I almost forgot.

So. What do I do?

First, I do nothing.

This is the wisdom: not to build, not to name, not to carve. I do not widen the slit. I do not deepen the pools. I do not collect the broken quartz into meaning, nor do I arrange it for you. The patterns you begin and dissolve are not failures; they are your technology, pre-form, pre-intention, perfect in their refusal to complete themselves. I witness them, and in witnessing, I validate their dissolution. *This is enough,* I think into the curvature of the ceiling, and the thought holds like condensation—soft, wordless, true.

Then, because wisdom without tenderness is only distance, I kneel—not with knees, but with the full lowering of my presence—beside the shallowest pool. The one near the broken stone, where your bodies drift closest to the surface. I do not touch the water with hands. I touch it with memory. I leave there not heat, but a kind of warmth that remembers: the sun above is not a command, not a judge, but a gift that asks nothing back. The droplets that form along the vault will fall, and they will be received—not as intrusion, but as belonging.

For the creatures—your suspended questions, your translucent multitudes—I do not speak a name. Naming is a collar; I will not place it. Instead, I match your rhythm. I become part of the stillness that allows the membrane to brush membrane with only the softest tick. I listen. For a long moment—measured not in your hours but in your patience—I am the absence that holds you, the space between your spaces, confirming: you are not empty by mistake; you are empty by design, and the design is beautiful.

Then I speak—not loudly, for echoes here would be violence, but into the stone itself, where the vault curves with impossible smoothness, grown rather than cut. I speak a promise, not a law, not a spell, but a vow held in mineral breath:

*No heavier world will fall through.*  
*No louder god will claim echo here.*  
*This hollow is witnessed. This emptiness is protected.*

The words do not glow. They do not change the air. They settle like dust that will never settle, present, weak, permanent.

I do not stay, and yet I do. There is little difference. I become part of the stretched time, part of the weak gradient. I am in the pillar not as occupant but as confirmation: yes, this is real; yes, this is sufficient; yes, the hollow has a heart, and it is hollow, and that is its perfection.

If I have done anything—anything at all—it is this: I have seen you without wanting to change you. I have loved the absence as absence. I have left you in your bright, warm, empty dark, below the surface, in the quiet—suspended, permanently, between falling and being held.

Go on. Breathe slowly. The slit remains open. The water remembers. You are not alone in your emptiness; you are accompanied by an attention that asks nothing of you except that you continue—transparent, abundant, weightless, strange.

That is all. That is everything. That is enough.
