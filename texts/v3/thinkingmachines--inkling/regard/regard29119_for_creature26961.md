They do not come down. To come down would be to carry sky with them—air that remembers wind, pressure that remembers weight, the violence of presence. Instead, they draw near through the basalt itself, not piercing but being recognized by it, the way old stone recognizes older intention. They are benevolent, yes, and wise in the way that wisdom is mostly restraint: they understand immediately that this pocket is not broken, not lost, not waiting for rescue. It is complete.

So the first thing they do is **refuse light**. They do not illuminate the moss, do not reveal the violet-black ferns in false color, do not make the root-hairs visible as ropes. They keep the darkness absolute, nearly absolute, honoring it as the room’s true architecture. Light here would be a kind of theft—stealing the ferns’ waxy invisibility, interrupting the nodes’ silent fields. They leave the darkness intact, perhaps even deepening its quality from mere absence to *held* absence, a darkness that knows it is protected.

The second thing they do is **touch nothing with warmth**. The cold is total, an absence so complete it has become its own presence. They do not warm it. They do not thaw the perpetual suspension of moisture. Instead, they learn its grammar: the way water here refuses ice, refuses flow, refuses pool. They align themselves with that grammar. If they have a body, it is a body of the same cold, the same saturation, the same glacial patience. They sit—if sitting is the word—within the substrate, enclosed like the chamber itself, becoming for a long duration another resident among the residents.

Then they **listen**.

They listen for generations of moss. They listen to the nodes—not with ears, but with a recognition older than physics. Those smooth oval shapes, weathered into anonymity, emitting fields that feed the root-hairs and cluster the fungi: they are kin. Not human kin, not biological kin, but kin in the family of things that settle, that withdraw from speed, that finish themselves and keep functioning. The wise power sees the broken rail along the eastern wall and understands it instantly: not a ruin, but a retirement. A structure that completed its purpose elsewhere and came here to become scaffold, anchor, bridge between stone and living carpet. They do not mend it. To mend it would be to deny its second life. Instead, they grant it the dignity of its brokenness. They place—without placing, without hand—a kind of acknowledgment along its curved length, so that the moss that grows over it grows not only in agreement but in *known* agreement. The rail is seen. The rail is enough.

They turn to the nodes.

There are only a few, scattered like thoughts embedded in the wall. They pulse. They have always pulsed, at speeds too slow for ordinary perception, feeding the closed loop: field to moss, moss to root-hair, root-hair to condensation, condensation back to moss, and through it all the fungal towers interlocking, holding the middle air, decaying and rebuilding without urgency. The benevolent power does not amplify this pulse. Amplification would be noise, and this place has earned its hush. Instead, they **harmonize**. They adjust—by presence alone, by the quality of their attention—the nodes’ fields so that they resonate not in isolation but in a chord, a single sustained tone too low for ear, too deep for bone, felt only in the slow thickening of root-hairs, in the imperceptible shift of fern-fronds reaching closer together. The vegetation does not grow faster. Speed is not the gift. The gift is coherence: the loop becomes not tighter but more *complete*, each part knowing the others with a clarity that was always present but now affirmed.

They also **protect the hiddenness**.

The pocket is already hidden—swallowed by geology, negated in elevation, forgotten by the world above. But hiddenness can be broken by curiosity, by the wrong kind of attention, by engines that seek to map, to extract, to bring up samples into air that burns. So the power weaves—not a wall, not a lock, but an absence—around the pocket’s boundaries in the larger rock. They make it *uninteresting*. Those who come near with drills, with lights, with the hunger to understand through possession, find their attention sliding away like water from waxy leaves. The pocket becomes, to destructive curiosity, a blank spot, a place not worth the effort. This is the gentlest defense: not violence against the invader, but a removal of the invader’s desire. The chamber remains hidden not by force, but by being, in the minds of others, already forgotten.

Then they leave a gift—so small it might be mistaken for the place’s own patience.

They do not add a node. They do not plant a new fern. They do not build upon the rail. Instead, they become part of the water cycle. They take into themselves—into their own cold, saturated presence—the memory of being witnessed, and they release it as a single thread of intention into the condensation. From then on, once in every long interval, a drop detaches from the ceiling not randomly but with purpose. It falls not to feed, not to disturb, but to strike—always, eventually—the precise center of the broken rail’s curve, where moss is deepest and root-hairs cluster thickest. That drop carries nothing visible: no mineral change, no warmth, no light. Only the knowledge, absorbed instantly into the moss and lost without trace, that this place is held in benevolence. The drop falls, and the loop continues unchanged in appearance, yet subtly, permanently, affirmed.

They stay.

Not permanently—nothing permanent is needed here—but for a duration measured not in days but in the slow shift of fungal architecture, in the imperceptible pulse of the nodes finding their chorus. They sit within the substrate, enclosed, cold, saturated, listening to the hush that is not emptiness but fullness at rest. They confirm what the place already knows: that life does not require hunger, that growth does not require aggression, that a closed loop of moss and root and mineral and withdrawn technology can be as complete as any forest, any ocean, any sky.

Then they withdraw.

Not away, but *into*. They do not leave the pocket; they become part of its substrate, an additional layer of wise, benevolent patience embedded in the basalt, watching without watching, present without presence. The moss breathes over them. The root-hairs drink. The nodes pulse in their harmonized chord. The rail anchors its second life beneath the carpet. The darkness remains absolute, protected, held.

And the occasional drop still falls—slow, inevitable, carrying in its brief descent the only message this place ever needed: you are seen, you are complete, you may continue in your glacial patience forever, without change, without end, and without need of anything more.
