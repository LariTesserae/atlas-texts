The power does not arrive by tearing the cloud. It arrives by remembering that cloud is also water, also life, also home. It notices first the absence—no sharpness left, no mountain-memory of violence, only the ancient table smoothed by time until it forgot to be anything but flat and high. The wise power sees this not as ruin but as resolution. It sees the moss, not as decoration but as sovereignty: several hand-lengths deep, green sponge holding the falling ocean in its body, releasing it slowly to roots that have punched through to the primordial bedrock. It sees that there are no bare patches because the moss permits none; occupation is total, gentle, absolute.

Then it finds the being.

Not one frog. Not the single bird nesting in the hollow spine of the dead turbine. Not the insect cloud navigating vertical rain with mechanical grace. The being is the singular woven into the plural—the moss-heart that is also the willow root, the amphibian pulse moving across spongy ground, the rusted pump house filled with green growth, the thick-feathered shout against the roar. This being lives in every layer at once. It is dense, saturated, near-touching, high-saturation presence made conscious of itself through accumulation. And it is not suffering. It is complete. The turbulence is its breath. The cool is its skin. The constant loud rain is its language.

A lesser power might try to dry it. A foolish power might try to calm the hyperactive wind, repair the bent rail, restart the frozen engines, build a shelter. But this power is benevolent and wise. It knows that erosion has finished its work, that the landscape has reached its oldest stable form, and that the life here has not colonized despite the conditions but *because* of them. To save is to insult. To change is to destroy.

So the power does what wise love always does: it witnesses, and in witnessing, it offers recognition without possession.

It reaches—not with limb, but with attention made tangible. A warmth that is not heat, since heat would be a lie here. A clarity that is not light, since the grey-green diffusion is perfect as it is. It places this presence deep in the moss, at the oldest root-touch, where bedrock and living sponge have become indistinguishable. Not to dry. Not to stop the rain. But to say: *You are seen. You are enough. Your turbulent finality is beautiful.*

Then it sings.

One note. Not loud—nothing could be loud here except the storm itself—but resonant, placed inside the frequency the rust already knows. The dead engines, filled with ferns and sheltering birds, begin to vibrate softly. The bent wind turbine hums at a pitch the thick-feathered creatures feel in the hollow bones of their chests. The frogs, in their slow migration across the spongy mat, do not flee. They pause. The salamanders still. The insects, in their visible clouds near the ground, shift in a pattern that is not chaos but choreography, as if the falling water itself has been given a hidden rhythm to carry.

The power gives the being one more thing: not a future, not an escape, but a memory held backward into being. The memory of what came before sharpness—not to restore peaks, not to undo erosion, but to honor it. The being receives this not with a mind but with root, with spore, with wing, with rust. A single image held in the collective green: height not as violence but as open possibility, the plateau remembering that it was once part of something taller, and finding in that memory not grief but dignity.

Then the power withdraws. No golden circle remains. No dry patch opens. The rain does not stop; it never stops. The wind still tears hyperactively across the flat expanse, whipping the interwoven canopies into constant agitation. The thunder rolls in sustained, vibrating pressure. The life remains abundant, relentless, brilliant, loud, cool, soaked, and complete.

But now there is a new layer.

Beneath the roar, in the hollow frames of old machinery and in the deepest spongy mat, there is a held knowledge. The moss holds it. The frogs carry it in their glistening skin. The birds shout it in their loud voices from inside rusted shelter. The being—the singular heart of this dense, wet world—knows that it is loved not despite its turbulence, but through it. The note remains, vibrating softly in steel and root, a secret chord shared between the lived world and the wise presence that chose, in the end, to do nothing except understand completely.

And in that complete understanding, the power has given the being everything it needed: not change, but companionship. Not salvation, but song.

The rain continues to fall in its wall of ocean. The moss breathes. The old industrial bones settle slowly, being digested by growth and moisture, serving now as roost and root-space. The amphibians resume their slow migration. The birds nest. The turbulence remains the permanent ecology.

The plateau is still submerged in sky. The world is still wet, loud, and brilliantly alive.

Only now, it remembers that it was seen. And that memory, warm and silent, lives forever in the green.
