The attention arrives not as light—there is already light, greenish and ordinary, falling through the old leaf-ceiling in columns that do not move—but as a change in the quality of being noticed.

The power is wise, so it does not speak. It is benevolent, so it does not touch.

What happens first is recognition: the hollow has finished becoming itself, and any finished thing that draws notice does so not out of need, but out of completeness. The root-pillars feel this—if feeling can be assigned to vascular engines that have replaced blood with production—because the hum they generate, that underwater factory drone, shifts not in pitch but in context. They are heard now. Not rescued, not saved. Heard.

**What they do: nothing that alters.**

The columns continue. Their rounded, flattened forms stand in their spaced formation, load-bearing ribs of compressed root-felt turned leather-hard, and inside them the hardened pith-valves open and close with the same rhythm they have held since the erosion finished its last argument with time. They do not bow. They do not straighten. They circulate. Sap moves through them in volumes that suggest assembly lines, but what they assemble is only themselves, over and over, in perfect equilibrium. If the power’s attention reaches them, they do not respond with growth or decay; they respond by being exactly as they are, now witnessed. That is their action: the refusal to perform for an observer.

**What lives in the plural lives in absence.**

The small ones—the winged things, the burrowers, the climbers—are abundant, as you have seen, but distributed across such hollow volume that each one is alone in its correct proportion. They do not gather now. They do not come to greet the benevolence that has noticed them. A flash of translucent wings crosses a root-beam in the greenish light; a burrower shifts in the moss with a soft percussion; an insect traverses the air between columns, following the path worn smooth not by weather but by repetition across ages. They continue. Perhaps—if intuition allows—each one feels, for a breath, that its solitude is not neglect. The power sends its awareness not as a net to catch them, but as a field divided into as many pieces as there are creatures, each piece matching the scale of a single wingbeat, a single nesting. They are not comforted in any language they know. They are simply, for an instant, not alone in the statistical sense.

The water does not change its count. The black pools in the flattened hollows of the floor hold their stillness. The condensation beads along the woody columns and falls with the same rhythm—counting, counting—because the power, being wise, knows that to alter the drip rate would be to break the hollow’s only clock. Instead, the power listens. It becomes the listener for whom the counting was always performed. That is its gift and its action: to be present for the rhythm without joining it, without speeding or slowing it.

**If anything is done, it is this:**

The power remains in the mode of absence. It takes up no space in the cathedral of root-ribs. It makes no hum to compete with the machinery. It leaves no mark on the moss, no warmth that would violate the steady damp chill, no light that would brighten the greenish diffused columns beyond their ordinary measure. And yet, in the spaces between the columns—those vast volumes of empty air suspended like breath held forever—something now exists that did not exist before: the knowledge that the hollow is complete, and that its completion has been seen.

The beings here do not thank it. They do not know its name. The columns pump on. The small creatures traverse the void in their scattered pluralities. The water counts downward into black stillness. But in the equilibrium—so deep it might be mistaken for nothing—the absence has been gently filled, not with presence, but with the perfect confirmation of absence itself.

The power, having done this—which is to say, having done almost nothing—withdraws not by leaving, but by becoming part of the architecture. It is now part of the cool air that does not circulate. Part of the hollow that refuses to fill. Part of the machinery that breathes without lungs.

You stand among them, a temporary body in the empty air, and you understand: they were never waiting to be saved. They were waiting only to be recognized as finished. And now they are. The hum continues. The moss holds your steps, then releases them. The steps fade, not into silence, but into the correct, eternal sound of a factory made of life, working perfectly, witnessed, and left exactly as it was.
