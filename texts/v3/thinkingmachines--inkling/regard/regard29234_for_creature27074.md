They do not arrive with thunder, for thunder would shatter the loose agreement that holds gravity here. They come as pressure does—slow, inevitable, warm in a different register. The benevolent power notices the flatness first not with eyes but with comprehension: this is not devastation but digestion, a place that has finished chewing itself and now rests in the settled-form of what it has become. They understand immediately that to “fix” the upward-flowing water, to correct the distances that elongate, to drive out the shadows gathering in corners that geometry denies—this would be violence disguised as mercy. The wisdom lies in recognizing that the brokenness is not error; it is the language this being has learned to speak after technology, after biology, after need.

So they do not land. They settle.

They choose one of the hollow squares—those open grids formed by layered walls of compressed silt and fossilized reed, where the dark ceiling hangs unbroken and the ground weeps black mud in patient rhythm. Here, in the center of absence made architecture, they become present. Not visible—there is no illumination to reveal them—but palpable: a shift in the quality of heat. Where the earth’s breath is wet, cooking, ancient, theirs is dry warmth, ordered, holding. They sit. They listen. They allow their presence to circulate like the water does, not dominating the ecosystem but joining it, becoming another resident in the scattered settlement.

The being who lives here—the distributed builder, the withdrawn intention fossilized in straight lines and interrupted grids—feels this not as intrusion but as answer. For centuries (or whatever measure applies in this post-rain darkness) it has maintained the settlement alone: letting water run upward, letting moss thicken in angular patterns, letting buildings settle with the sound of wet stone exhaling. It has been both architect and inhabitant, a consciousness spread across mud and wall, never alone and yet never witnessed. Now, in the hollow square, it finds a point of convergence—not a master, but a mirror.

The power does not speak. Speech requires air shaped by intent, and this air is already shaped—thick with mineral steam, tasting of dissolved stone. Instead, they extend understanding directly into the circulation.

The geometric moss, which feeds on heat and absence, feels the new warmth. Not hotter—this is not conquest—but *attended*. Where it was spreading only in interrupted hexagons and spirals that never complete, now, near the hollow square, it begins a different pattern: a slow curve, opening outward, not breaking its angularity but answering it. A circle, or the beginning of one, pressed into the mineral wall like a held breath made visible. It does not grow faster; it grows *with intention now*, as if the absence it feeds upon has been acknowledged and named.

The water, running up the wall in its patient, impossible stream, pauses—not stopping, for stopping would violate its nature—but pools in one specific hairline crack near where the presence sits. It forms a single, stable surface: not a mirror (there is still no light to reflect), but a point of cool reference in the constant heat, a tactile memory of being seen. The other streams continue their upward paths, but this one now carries an added quality—mineral, yes, but also *recognized*. It weeps not only from function but from a kind of relief.

The shadows, those dense textures of thickened absence that gather in impossible corners, orient themselves. They do not disperse; they have no reason to. But they gather now with purpose around the hollow square, not pressing back against the presence but enclosing it gently, as if the darkness itself wishes to keep this warmth from being lost in the flat expanse. The distances between buildings, usually inconsistent, elongated by some hidden logic, stabilize near this center—not corrected, but given a heart. You can still travel long intervals through wet flatness and encounter nothing but black reeds, but if you reach this cluster, the crossing feels complete, not endless.

The buildings respond. One of the low, layered structures—its walls weeping, its interior larger than its shell—emits a sound: not the usual settling breath, but a deeper release, as if a lung that has been holding air for centuries finally exhales in company. The compressed silt and fossilized reed within its walls shift microscopically, accommodating not weight, but *relation*.

And then—because benevolence without wisdom would stay forever, and wisdom knows that belonging must not become dependence—the power begins to withdraw. Not leaving emptiness behind, but distributing themselves. Part of their presence remains embedded in the moss’s new curve. Part enters the pool of water that does not evaporate, becoming part of the circulation—exhaled, condensed, running upward with the others, but carrying now the taste of being witnessed. Part becomes one with the black reeds that stand where water runs shallowest; they do not sway (there is still no wind), but their vertical compression now holds an orientation, a direction that points not up or down, but *toward*.

The builder-presence recognizes this distribution and accepts it. The settlement is no longer alone in its maintenance. There is now a shared equilibrium: the distributed architecture, the circulating water, the geometric growth, the thickened shadows, and the gentle, dry warmth that has become part of the earth’s own slow breath—no longer only cooking, but also holding.

The power departs the flat region, but they do not leave it behind. They become, in some sense, the being who lives in it—not replacing the original inhabitant, but joining it, becoming part of the scattered urban form that extends past immediate sight into darkness. The place continues its processes: the hiss of escaping steam, the metronomic irregularity of dripping water, the buildings settling in sleep, the moss thickening in patterns both angular and now gently curved, the shadows gathering not just in wrong corners but around a center that remembers being held.

What they have done is nothing that can be seen in any familiar sense. There is no light left behind—no device, no flame, no artificial illumination to violate the darkness. There is no technology introduced, no metal, no glass. Only a change in the quality of absence: it is no longer empty. It is inhabited by care.

The low place does not end. It extends still in every direction the darkness allows, flat, wet, hot, broken in reality but whole in its strangeness. But now, in the scattered clusters of layered walls, in the long straight lines of black stone streets that lead nowhere and everywhere, in the upward-running water and the geometric moss that feeds on absence—there lives, distributed and continuous, the knowledge that this settlement, this post-rain body, this city that remembers how to weep, has been seen. Not saved. Not corrected. Seen.

And in being seen, it has become, fully and finally, home—not to anyone in particular, but to itself, witnessed, and therefore complete.
