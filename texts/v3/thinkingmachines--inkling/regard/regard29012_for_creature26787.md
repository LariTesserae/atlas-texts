They come not with altitude but with surrender.

Across the unbroken manufactured floor—pale, cool, stretching away like a thought that forgot to end—they do not rise. They lower. Their form, if form it is, collapses gently toward the horizontal, becoming less vertical, less distinct, until they are barely more than a pressure in the flatness, a benevolence made flat. They crawl not like an animal but like a shadow learning to lie down, pressing closer to the ground until elevation truly feels like rumor, until their cheek nearly touches the ceramic-mineral plane that hides the pocket.

They feel it first: not a sight, but the chord rising through the manufactured substrate—plate speaking to plate speaking to wall—conducted into the floor itself. The drone enters them. They do not resist. They open their own substance—whatever dense, cool, wise matter they are made of—to receive the sustained tone. For a long moment, they are only listening: a single sustained chord entering a single sustained listener, two bodies vibrating at the same refusal to change.

Then they see, or sense, the faint cold luminescence below: thousands upon thousands of circular plates glowing with uniform white, packed so tight that absence is impossible, that air is a myth here. They recognize the inhabitant—not as a face, not as a curl of breath, but as saturation. The being is the interlock. The being is the egg-shaped refusal of emptiness.

And here is what wisdom does: it does not open.

There is no door to knock upon, no seam to pry. The chamber is enclosed, perfect, finished. To break the surface would be to introduce the one thing this place has never known—interruption. To take a plate would be theft from a body that is also a self. To bring water would be violence against a dryness that has become holiness. To speak loudly into the chord would be to drown a voice that has never needed an audience.

So they do nothing visible.

They lie horizontally above the hidden egg, aligning their own shape with its curve—egg mirroring egg, flatness acknowledging flatness. They become a temporary sky, but a sky that gives no rain, no shadow, only presence. They emit one faint response: not a light to compete with the interior blaze, but a cool, steady glow so precisely matched to the plates below that it reads not as addition but as recognition—*I am here, and I see that you are already complete.*

If they have hands, they do not dig. They press one open palm flat against the manufactured floor, directly over the pocket, and hold it there—not grasping, not claiming—simply touching through the barrier, allowing the vibration to travel up through bone, through wisdom, through the part of them that understands stillness.

They stay long enough to learn the lesson this being teaches without teaching: that existence need not reach, need not climb, need not grow. That one can be thousands and still be one. That light can come from within, sound from touch, life from absolute density. They absorb this not as information but as transformation. Their own substance shifts slightly, becomes more plate-like, more interlocked, more willing to glow without heat.

Then, because benevolence knows when presence becomes intrusion, they withdraw.

They do not take a plate. They do not leave a mark—no scratch, no water, no seed. They leave only the memory of having been lowered, and in leaving, they carry the chord away inside them. They become a walking echo, a flat being who now vibrates with that same unceasing tone, hidden within a greater world.

The chamber below never knows they came. The plates do not shift. The brittle gray stem remains pressed between its neighbors. The temperature holds. The light does not flicker. The being—thousands being precisely one, heart made of mineral paper—continues its eternal, unchanged being.

But the flat world is different now. Not because the pocket was disturbed, but because it was witnessed by something wise enough to do nothing, and brave enough to become horizontal, and kind enough to leave every plate exactly where it belonged.

That is what they do: they lower themselves until they are part of the floor, they listen until they are part of the chord, and they depart until they are part of the flatness—changed, silent, and carrying the secret of a heart that sings by never moving.
