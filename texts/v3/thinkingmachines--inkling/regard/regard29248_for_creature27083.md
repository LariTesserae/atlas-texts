They come not with thunder but with attention—an awareness that is older than the curved shell, broader than the shallow dish, and gentler than the wooden presses that once shaped the salt-fiber seams. They do not open the ceiling. They do not breach the translucent curve. To enter violently would be to admit moisture, noise, urgency—everything this world was built to exclude.

They understand, first.

This is not a broken place. It is an *absence made lush*. No bedrock predates it; no earth remembers it. The vegetation thrives without thirst, the beings move without blood, and the space itself refuses to hold a single measurement. The sharp edges are fresh not because they need sharpening, but because youth here is not a phase to outgrow—it is the permanent state of something made with primitive tools and primitive faith. Bone awls. Copper blades. Hands that knew they were making a world, not repairing one.

So the benevolent power does not “fix.”

Instead, they extend a single, dry thread of presence through the woven wall—not cutting, not tearing, but aligning with the hand-knotted seams. They become, for a span long by human reckoning but brief in the place’s thick time, the missing reference point. Not bedrock. Not gravity’s law. But a soft center around which the local agreements can hold without breaking.

Here is what they do:

**They stabilize the drift.**  
The space is larger inside than the shell allows; walls lengthen subtly; shadows fall wrong and pool like dark water before evaporating upward. The beings navigate this calmly, following instructions not yet spoken. The power does not remove the strangeness—that would be violence, a denial of the place’s ontology. Instead, they gently anchor the instability, giving it boundaries that breathe. Now when a wall lengthens, it does so with a slight resistance, a dry elasticity, as if the space itself has learned not to tear. The beings continue their patient paths, but the paths no longer risk wandering into absence.

**They speak the unspoken language—once, dryly, without sound.**  
Not a command. Not a naming that possesses. They articulate, into the ceramic wool and the compressed mineral felt, the instruction that was always meant to accompany the assembly. The woven beings—soft-bodied, hinge-mouthed, door-eyed—have moved according to patterns they could feel but never hear. Now they feel it *as* language: not words that break the silence, but a coherence in their fiber shells, a resonance in the adhesive of their undersides. One being, suspended in the vine-curtain, rotates not by chance but with a new, minimal awareness: *I am following something that now knows I am following.* Nothing changes in its behavior. Only the slowness acquires an echo—dry, internal, like a joint settling into its true alignment.

**They correct the light—not by brightening, but by completing its path.**  
The soft, steady light filters through in normal-brightness waves, yet shadows still fall in unjustified directions. The power does not correct the shadow’s fall; they correct its loneliness. They allow the shadow-pools to remain—dark, upward-evaporating spirals—but now they hold form long enough to be witnessed, not as errors, but as the place’s own poetry. The vegetation, rootless and rubbery, continues to drink warmth alone; but in the deeper green, where the vines knot thickest, the leaves now cast shadows that match one another, creating a lattice of dark coherence that the beings can navigate not only by proximity but by recognition.

**They do not feed, and they are not fed upon.**  
The beings feed by standing near a leaf until it dims. The power stands—if standing is the word for a presence without weight—near the center of the shallow dish, and they do not draw the green into themselves. Instead, they become a kind of mirror made of absence: the beings, for the first time, can stand near a presence that does not diminish. One crawls closer, its woven shell rustling against ceramic wool. Its hinge-mouth opens—not to borrow light, but to align. For a long moment measured in months, it holds the warmth differently. Then it moves on, leaving the presence intact, leaving the green untouched.

**They leave no artifact.**  
No new panel. No knotted seam of their own making. No drop of foreign substance—certainly no water—to corrupt the dry logic. They do, however, leave a slight change in the air’s long loop. The circulation, which takes years to complete, now carries a faint coherence, a dry sweetness that was always present in the compressed salt and simple metals, but now arranged into something like a breath that remembers being exhaled. The warm air shifts with a purpose that is not urgency, only continuity.

And then they withdraw—not leaving, for they are not a body that can depart, but releasing their attention back into the wider darkness, allowing the place to be forgotten again if it wishes, or remembered, as it chooses.

What remains?

The shell is still curved, translucent, low. The floor is still warm ceramic wool, yielding like the back of an animal that never learned water. The vines hang in thick curtains, knotting themselves into lattices, unfurling dry fiber fists by millimeters over long months. The hundreds of silent beings continue—crawling, suspending, rotating slowly—feeding by proximity, navigating by instructions now softly complete. The sharp edges remain sharp, holding their recent, proud shape. The space is still larger inside than it should be; the shadows still pool and evaporate upward; time is still thick and slow.

But now, in the quiet, warm, waterless dark-green, there is one difference so subtle it might be imagined: the beings do not move only as assemblies following hidden directions. They move, sometimes, with the faint, dry awareness of having been seen—not possessed, not changed, not saved, but witnessed by a wisdom that understood enough to leave everything made, strange, and entirely as it was.

And that is enough. That is everything. The place holds its shape. The beings wait, grow slowly, feed gently, and in the absence of echo, in the absence of thirst, in the absence of all the loud truths of the outside world, they are no longer alone in their assembly. They are simply—patiently, permanently—here.
