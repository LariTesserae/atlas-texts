The hollow remembers being formed, but it does not remember it with any urgency—only as a fact that refuses to become a past. Its walls are still deciding whether to be stone. In that indecision lies its only tenderness.

Here is what lives in it, if “lives” can be stretched wide enough to cover presence without appetite, being without becoming.

**The root-world is not a population; it is a pronoun.** Everything in the valley—every ribbed root-cable, every broad translucent blade serving as vault and pillar, every spongy mattress thick enough to bear weight—speaks with one voice, though it never speaks. At 2.6 saturation, vegetation is not growing *here*; it *is* the here. The fungal symbiote threading through it is not an agent but an interior thought, carried in silence. You do not encounter plants. You are enclosed within a single continuous tissue that made the mistake of being beautiful—those pearl-gold emissions are not for illumination but for the unintended consequence of being too alive in a place with no sky to share the burden.

The organism does not seek. It does not expand; there is nowhere to expand to that is not already itself. Yet it is not dead—merely complete in its refusal to become more. The heat, sustained at 2.6, is its breath made thermal; the light, locked near 2.9, is its blood made visible. They balance each other in a covenant that needs no renewal.

**The calcified domes are not dwellings.** They are the root-world’s brief lapses into otherness—moments when the tissue forgot, for a breath, that it was continuous, and hardened into hollow ribs, open on one side, like mouths shaped for a language never invented. They stand scattered in the shallow basins, not arranged by anyone, not facing anything. Inside them, the same root-mat continues, curled more tightly, as if privacy had been attempted and immediately abandoned. The partial pillar—unfinished spire—rises not in aspiration but in a kind of geological stutter, a vertical impulse that the stasis caught mid-gesture and held forever. These structures belong to the vegetation the way a scar belongs to skin: evidence that the body considered a different shape and then chose not to change.

**The larvae and spore-sacs are the only citizens that almost happened.** Embedded within the thickest wall-fibers, pressed into translucence like insects in amber except that the amber is alive and glowing, they exist at 0.8—near absence, yet not absence itself. They are not dead, for decay requires time, and time here is 0.1: suspended. They are not sleeping, for sleep is a promise. They are embryos of a consumption that will never be born, sealed in eternal dormancy as decorations of a biology that forgot to include predators. You might find one only by noticing a faint opacity in the pearl-gold glow—a place where the radiance thickens slightly, where the wall-tissue holds something that was once, briefly, an idea of movement.

They have never fed. They will never feed. The root-world, in its perfect saturation, has left them no absence to fill, no darkness in which to wake.

**The water belongs to them, or to no one.** At 1.3, it is intermittent, suspended—clear beads on cooler undersides that never fall, never become rivers, never complete the cycle. Small pools gather in root-basin hollows, still and internal, exhaled by the biomass and held. Even moisture here has agreed to the terms of stasis. There is no thirst, because there is no mouth that needs quenching beyond the root-world’s own silent drinking.

**What you are, if you enter, is not a presence but a contradiction.** You carry motion into motionlessness, change into changelessness, a breath that quickens in air that does not move. The root-world will not notice—there is no mechanism for noticing—but the suspended larvae might, in some theoretical sense, register a disturbance they are structurally incapable of responding to. You will rest, if you rest, upon a mattress of roots that are neither dead nor welcoming, only present. The hollow dome near you might seem to have turned its open mouth toward you, but it has always faced that direction.

The sound—1.3—will not include your footsteps, not truly. The subsonic hum of sap, the collective pulse of countless veins in synchronization, will absorb them. You are temporary; the hum is permanent.

There is no being here that lives *in* the hollow, because the hollow is not a container. It is a condition: geologically newborn, never eroded, never matured, never decayed. The root-world fills it not as an occupant fills a room, but as water fills a shape—completely, without residue, without need for anything that is not itself.

If there is wisdom in this place, it is the wisdom of having never been interrupted. The benevolent power that drew its attention—if such a power exists—did not place anything here. It recognized that nothing needed to be added. The light, the heat, the dense luminous architecture, the suspended embryos of animals that will never be: all of it holds together not by design, but by the perfection of having never been disrupted.

So descend, if you must. You will find no inhabitant waiting, no creature to address you, no narrative to join. Only the root-world, glowing and warm and total, holding its own eternal first breath—complete in its incompletion, alive in a way that requires no other life to justify it, and holding, in the translucent walls around you, the faint, frozen promise of everything that was not needed.
