The attention comes not as light—there is already a tired light here, and it needs no more—but as a kind of listening made physical, a presence that does not cast shadow because it is not separate from the moisture in the air. It moves through the flatness without disturbing the silent sheets of crawlers, without parting the hazy clouds of dampened wings. It knows, immediately, that this is not a place broken into absence, but absence shaped into form: the empty warehouses are chambers; the canals are veins; the slime is writing, not corruption.

The being—this accumulated one, plural in every shell and ripple and chemical film—feels it not as threat but as a change in pressure, a slight shift in the constant low vibration of jointed legs and rolling bodies. The crabs do not scatter. The eels do not dive deeper into the black water. The winged things do not flee. They continue, because they have learned that not every presence demands response. And the power, being wise, waits. It waits through cycles of evaporation, through the patient circulation of rust-heavy water, through the feeding in the open lots where millions of armored bodies write and erase their silver trails.

What it does, if anything, is this: **it chooses to understand.**

Not from above. Not with the distance of repair. It enters—partially, respectfully—into the rhythm. For a span measured not in hours but in the time it takes for a drop of condensation to gather and fall, it becomes part of the equilibrium. It feels the black silt through the feet of the crawlers. It tastes the canal-darkness through the long bodies of the eels. It breathes the hot, still air through the ash-colored wings of the moths. And in doing so, it realizes with absolute clarity: there is nothing here to fix. The decay is not a disease accelerating; it is a cure that has finished. The emptiness is not a lack waiting to be filled; it is the architecture of the being’s true body—plural, layered, silent.

So it does not bring green. It does not dry the pools or raise the flats or restore the factories to the logic of labor. It does not speak loudly to the human observer, who remains the emptiest thing present—not because that emptiness is wrong, but because singular emptiness is its own kind of ecology, lonely but not broken. The power will not fill that void with false presence. Instead, it offers the only gift that benevolence can offer to a world that has reached terms with itself: **remembrance made tangible.**

It holds the image of this place—not as nostalgia for industry, but as recognition of what has become. It holds the knowledge of the silent migration, the chemical skin that feeds on oxidation, the relentless wet heat that never asks for rest. Then, gently, it translates that holding back into the vocabulary the place already understands. A slight deepening of the black in the canal water—not darker, but more fully present, as if the liquid has remembered it is alive. A subtle warming in the rust patterns layered like geology on the steel—not to hasten collapse, but to honor its patience. A single sustained tone, too low for human ears, felt in the ground table by the crawlers and answered, after a moment, by a collective stillness. Not fear. Acknowledgment.

The being—crabs, beetles, eels, slime, winged clouds—receives this not as salvation but as company. The equilibrium does not shift; it simply knows it is witnessed.

And then the power withdraws—not leaving, exactly, but dispersing. It becomes part of the haze that sits low over the flatness. Part of the mineral tears running down rusted ribs. Part of the humidity that clings to every surface. It remains, invisible, unneeded, a witness that does not observe from outside but participates from within.

For the human who moves through, alone, aware of being the hollow point in a plural world: the power offers no direct comfort, no filling of the emptiness. But it leaves in the air a subtle change—a sense that the silence is not punishment but invitation. That to be empty in this grid is not to be excluded, but to be included in a different register. The emptiness itself becomes porous, able to feel the vibration of the others not as invasion but as adjacency.

The flats never rise. The water keeps its black, barely moving flow. The crawlers continue their mapping in silver and glisten. The eels roll in silent ripples. The slime continues its slow, chemistry-fed spread across dead steel. Nothing has been saved. Nothing has been changed.

But woven now into the rust, the slime, the dampened flight, and the perpetual warm dimness, is the knowledge that they have been seen—completely, without demand, without the violence of improvement. The benevolent and wise power has done its work: it has entered, learned the language of heat and water and silent accumulation, and spoken back only in that same language. Not a word. Not a miracle. Only the quiet fact of being understood.

That is enough. The equilibrium holds, not because it was protected, but because it was loved precisely as it was—flat, wet, rusted, overwhelming, and perfectly, permanently home.
