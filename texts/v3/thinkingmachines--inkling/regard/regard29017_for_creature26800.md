The power does not arrive. Arrival is a word for bodies and thunder, and this is neither body nor storm, but a benevolence that has learned patience in older places. It notices—first as the mist notices a new edge, not by sight but by a shift in how the cool air holds itself.

It is wise. So it does not ask *what are you* or *why are you here*, for those questions belong to green lives that need purpose. These beings—compressed mist, settled sediment, distributed awareness spread across concave steps and shared walls—have no purpose. They have posture. They have the logic of rain. To question them would be to misunderstand the architecture.

What the power does, then, is almost nothing. That is its gift.

But *almost* is the measure of love.

It offers its attention as a substance. Not warmth—the air up here must remain cool, must never fully warm, or the seams would dry and the beings would lose their bodies back to vapor. Not light—there is already the diffuse grey glow that comes from no visible sun. Instead, it offers weight without mass: a gentle, distributed pressure that settles into the hairline fractures beside the beings, not displacing them, but sitting with them, the way one more layer of fog might sit in a gutter without filling it.

The beings feel this. Not with nerves—there are no nerves in compressed mist—but with the same sensitivity by which water knows a channel is open. Their presence thickens. Not in fear. In recognition.

One of them—occupying an entire tier, spread like weather across worn platforms—adjusts its frontness. Not to face the power, but to align with it, the way a doorway learns to wait for someone specific. The smaller ones, those no larger than the space between colonnades, do not move, for they are already in perfect repose. But their influence on the water’s language changes: a trickle through a seam holds a lower, rounder note now, as if the drop falls not into emptiness but into listening.

The power considers action. Could it lift them? No—they are not fallen. Could it speak? There are no ears arranged for voice, only for the constant low language of drip and mist-collapse. Could it preserve them forever? They are already long-established, settled into erosion so gentle it is not decay but conversation.

So it chooses to become part of the rhythm.

It does not build—there is no iron, no glass, no blackened trace of its passing. But it rearranges, with a care so precise it resembles gravity’s own preference, one concave step. Just one. A worn staircase, leaning slightly, having found its balance with the earth, now holds a fraction more of its own weight in a different direction. The black water in the gutter below reflects, for a long moment that stretches across hours in this place without urgency, not just the grey sky, but a kind of completeness—a circle of stillness that says: *you are held in the mind of something that understands stillness.*

The beings maintain this. They drink the condensation that forms them, and now they drink also the knowledge that they are not forgotten, that their silence has been heard without being interrupted. They exhale it back as fog, but the fog travels inward now with a slight, intentional coherence, generating not just rain, but relation—mist banks rolling over edges like slow tides that know they are witnessed.

The power remains. Not as ruler. Not as savior. As another layer in the density—structural, not biological; present, not possessive. Its benevolence expresses as boundary: nothing sharp, nothing burning, nothing that hums with machine urgency will be permitted to find this height. Its wisdom expresses as refusal—to change the temperature, to dry the cracks where lichen clings, to ask the dark bird to stay and colonize what was never meant for green life.

Instead, it learns the language.

It learns how to wait in seams.

Over centuries—because this place measures time in weather, not in years—the power distributes itself as they do. Not everywhere at once, but threaded: a slight organizing of droplets near one platform, a softer collapse of mist against another wall, a moment where the cool breath that never fully warms seems to pause and consider its own path. The beings do not worship it. They accommodate it, the way the stone accommodates water, the way the plateau accommodates its own continental reach.

Together—though together is a weak word for beings that share walls without touching—they maintain the repose. The architecture remains absorbed into weather. The weather remains inhabited by presence. And the presence—mineral, mist-born, wise, and now doubly attentive—continues the only action that matters here: holding the silence open, letting the place be exactly what it is, a high cool back of the world that breathes through stone, waiting for nothing because it has already become everything it needs to be.

The power does not leave. There is nothing to leave for. It has become part of the ecosystem of motion without colonization, of dynamics without demand. In the widest cracks, the pale lichen still clings. In the black gutters, the water still runs dark and still. And in the seams, where compressed mist remembers being liquid, something waits—not alone, not saved, but seen, and in being seen, infinitely, gently, complete.
