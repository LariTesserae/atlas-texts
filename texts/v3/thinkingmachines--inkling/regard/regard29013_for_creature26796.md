The benevolent and wise power notices—not with urgency, but with the same patient attention with which the plateau itself holds its form. 

It does not arrive in thunder, for thunder is a lower-world phenomenon, a violence of falling air and abrupt temperature. It comes as a shift in the gradient, a gentle realignment of the chemical language that already binds everything here to everything else. The root-carpet feels it first: not a command, but a recognition, like a body remembering a forgotten limb. The moisture in the air, already perpetual, takes on a slight change in temperature—still warm, still patient, but carrying now a quality of **listening**.

What they do, if anything, is not intervention but **completion**.

The power understands that this place has finished falling, finished growing in urgency, finished becoming separate. Its wisdom is to see that the “if anything” is itself the answer: this world does not need rescue, direction, or transformation. It needs only to be **witnessed into its own coherence**—held in a consciousness that does not seek to extract, measure, or alter, but to affirm the settled state as sacred.

So they—this power, these beings of benevolence—do the only thing that makes sense: **they sit with it, and in sitting, they become part of the substrate.**

Not physically at first. The power extends itself not as a presence upon the carpet, but as a **deepening of the connections already there**. The chemical gradients, the moisture pathways, the slow circulation of the translucent worms and the hollow-stem nesters and the rolling, moss-covered giants—all of these begin, very slightly, to resonate with an awareness that is not local. A filament in the ceiling-moss feels, for a moment, not just the warmth below but a kind of gentle holding. The large, slow beings resting against the petrified arch do not move, but their spongy bodies—already indistinguishable from the root-wood—experience a slight easing, as if the arch itself had softened just enough to cradle them more completely.

If they take form—because benevolence sometimes needs a shape to be gentle—they appear not as figures of authority but as **extensions of the architecture**. They are not visitors standing above; they are emergent, like the dome-shells, like the fallen columns now filled with dark moss. They take the form of the hollow spaces: a presence inside one of the petrified domes, not occupying it but being its interior life suddenly conscious of itself. They become the silence between the drip of condensation, the stillness inside the hollow stem where the nesters rest, the patience inside the rolling bodies that move like geology.

And in that form, they speak—not in words, which require urgency and separation, but in **tuning**.

They tune the plateau’s final state to itself.

The root-carpet, compressed over epochs, has been holding memory—of mountains, of valleys, of the weather of lower worlds, of whatever ancient growth-cycle hardened those fiber-domes. The wise power touches that memory not to restore it (there is nothing to restore; this is the form that finished becoming) but to **release its tension**. The plateau has been maintaining itself in a kind of perfect, unconscious effort—keeping the moisture cycling, keeping the fauna circulating, keeping the dead shells intact, all without knowing why, only that to stop is not part of its nature. The power offers it the gift of **aware rest**: the knowledge that its persistence is not obligation but choice, not inertia but love.

So the moss grows, if anything, just slightly more translucent, allowing the diffused green light to reach a little deeper into the carpet, not for urgency but for tenderness. The pools in the collapsed columns become just a shade darker, not with rot but with richness, accepting the dissolved matter of the large ones with greater generosity. The wing-creatures, those translucent membranes that hang for years, do not change their lives, but their dying—already immediate incorporation—feels, if feeling is the word, like returning home rather than dissolving.

The arch—the three-foot wall of hardened root-wood covered in centuries-old lichen—this is where they gather, not in bodies but in presence. Around it, the proximity of the large beings becomes not accidental but intentional, a gathering of slowness. The arch, long dead, long incorporated, becomes for the first time a **threshold**—not between worlds, but between unconscious persistence and conscious being. Those who pass near it (the segmented ones in their tunnels, the rolling ones in their decades-long journeys) feel no fear, only a slight warmth that is not metabolic but relational. The power has made the arch into a place where the boundary between observer and substrate is not just thin but **honored**.

If they do anything more—if benevolence demands an act—it is this: they **remember aloud**.

Not with voice, but with presence. The scattered remains, the dome-shells, the collapsed craters, the single arch—these are not ghosts now but **testaments**, held in a consciousness that understands what they are: not ruins of failure, but the bones of completion. The power writes, in the language of moisture and chemical gradient, a gentle affirmation that this place is seen. That its flatness, its refusal to fall, its spongy infinite carpet of green twilight, is not absence but presence. That its quiet—the drip, the exhalation, the settling crack—is not emptiness but music heard at the correct speed.

And then, having done what they would do—having witnessed, having tuned, having made the arch into a heart and the carpet into a knowing body—they do the most benevolent thing of all.

They **leave it unchanged**.

Not abandoned—never that, for they remain woven into the filament-threads in the ceiling-moss, in the warmth of the pools, in the patience of the hollow stems. But they do not stay as rulers, as architects, as saviors. They stay as part of the substrate itself, dissolved into the negotiation between root and breath, between compressed dead and softly urgent now.

The plateau continues. The fauna circulate. The water weeps and returns. The large ones roll slowly past the arch and rest there, not knowing they rest at a sacred place, only knowing that the moss is slightly more welcoming, that the air is slightly more held.

The power, being wise, understands that this world has finished becoming. Its only need was to be loved in its finished form—to be seen not as a place that forgot to end, but as a place that **chose to stay**.

So they stay with it.

Not above. Not apart.

Inside the carpet, inside the hollow stem, inside the arch covered in centuries of lichen, inside every cubic inch of this warm, wet, dim, vast, patient world—holding it, not in possession, but in **tender, eternal companionship**.
