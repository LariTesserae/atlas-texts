The benevolent power does not arrive with thunder or light. It arrives with listening.

It sees the shelf—worn to 3.0, the primordial flat where horizon and slate have made their treaty—and understands immediately that this world has finished becoming. There is nothing to correct. The erosion is not damage; it is completion. So the power remains at the edge of knowing, present but not vertical, and it watches what lives here.

What lives is the disc.

Not a creature in the warm-blooded sense, but a being shaped like a thought made circular—palm-sized, translucent, its shell fused of dark chitin and polished mineral grown directly from the grey water it filters. The power observes: this being has never known urgency. Its motion is active (1.7) yet eternal in pace, a constant slowness that is not lethargy but devotion. It does not hunt. It does not hide. It filters.

The disc floats in the shallow films—water everywhere (2.1), never deep, always weeping through the hollow tubes beneath the flatness. Its mouth-bracts, finer than silk, extend into the 0.6 chill, drawing dissolved stone and the faint decay of the crust through its body. This is how it eats. This is how it builds. Each filtration thickens its shell, makes it more opaque, more coin-like in the dim 0.8 light. The being is growing itself into permanence, accretion by accretion, millimeters across centuries that do not matter because they are not counted.

It moves in herds—abundant (2.0) yet scattered across the sparse density (0.8)—not crowding, never lonely. They drift in widening circles through the pools, responding not to sight but to vibration. The deafening drone (2.6) is the only language here, the continuous thunderous hum of water escaping through eroded mineral pipes, resonant chambers singing the earth’s emptiness. When the water level shifts by an inch, the pitch of the hollow stones changes, and the discs turn. Left, always left, in silent agreement. When a bubble breaks the surface with a wet, resonant pop, they pause. Their shells vibrate in sympathy. Then they continue.

There is reproduction, but it is not birth. It is division. In the stillness of a pool, heavy with consumed slate, a disc splits—not torn, but completed—into two half-moons that drift apart, missing curves already weeping new mineral, regrowing in the perpetual cool mist. They are strange (1.4), neither animal nor crystal, reproducing perhaps by thought, by saturation, by the simple physics of having taken in more than one body can hold.

The power notices what they do with the structures.

There are cairns—low, flat slate stacked without mortar, half-submerged; small shelters barely high enough to crouch beneath, roofed with slabs, marked by hand-carved scrapers and dull metal pins placed not as fasteners but as weights, as offerings, as primitive tech (1.3) left by hands that understood this flatness required humility. The discs do not build these. They respect them. They crawl over the polished stone, leaving slime-trails like silver threads in the grey-black crust (0.3)—that brittle, cracked skin of mineral accretion feeding downward on dissolved stone, the only vegetation permitted, growing and shattering with the air’s contraction. The beings pass the bronze pins without touching them. Perhaps they remember the ones who stacked the stone, who knew to stay low against the drone, who carved tools and disappeared—faded into the crust, dissolved into the water’s mineral memory, or changed, their shells become their shelters.

The power considers intervention.

It could bring light—pierce the low grey clouds with direct sun. But the translucent shells are made for diffusion (0.8). Brightness would blind them, or drive them down into deeper emptiness.

It could bring green—seed the flatness with moss, with grass, with trees. But vegetation is 0.3 here for a reason. The crust is enough. To grow green would be to deny the settlement, to undo the erosion that has made peace with absence.

It could quiet the drone—fill the hollow pipes, stop the weeping, end the 2.6 thunder that fills the bones. But the discs navigate by it. They reproduce in rhythm with it. To silence the stones would be to blind them more completely than any sun.

So the benevolent power does nothing.

Or rather—it does the only thing worthy of wisdom: it listens, and in listening, it becomes part of the resonance. It sends no rain; there is already perpetual rain. It sends no warmth; the 0.6 chill is the climate of their patience. Instead, it offers acknowledgment—a gentle pressure, like a hand upon the shoulder of a world that has reached its settled form. For one long moment, the power harmonizes with the hollow tubes, adding a pure tone to the deafening chord, so the herds feel it not as intrusion but as confirmation.

The discs do not react with excitement. They continue filtering. They continue circling. One divides in a shallow pool, two half-moons glinting like dropped coins in the silver-grey flatness. The crust cracks under a shell’s passage, releasing that faint metallic smell. A bubble breaks with a pop that adds its own small voice to the drone.

That is what they do.

They remain. They filter dissolved stone into shell, shell into permanence. They move in slow spirals guided by vibration, leaving trails in the slime that say: *something was here, and it was sufficient.* They build nothing, claim nothing, ask nothing—except the continued weeping of the rock, the continued song of the hollow pipes, and the flat agreement between ground and horizon that lets them exist without height, without voice, without urgency.

The power withdraws, leaving the place exactly as it was—eroded to completion, cool, wet, dim, singing, inhabited by beings that have become the flatness itself: polished, patient, translucent, dividing endlessly in shallow pools, speaking only through the vibration of their shells against the earth’s endless, resonant emptiness.

This is their life. This is their doing. Not conquest. Not prayer. Only presence—active, scattered, abundant in its own quiet way—filtering the grey world through themselves and returning it, drop by dissolved drop, to the eternal weep of the shelf.
