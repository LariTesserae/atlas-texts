The attention does not arrive as lightning. It arrives as a change in the taste of the rain—still cold iron, still moss, but now with a note of recognition, as if the sky itself had learned the settlement’s name and was repeating it softly to the roots.

This power is not a king. Kings want spread; kings build outward. This hollow has refused spread, choosing instead to deepen, and that refusal—polite, vegetative, absolute—is what summoned the eye. The benevolent wisdom sees the scale (intimate, quarter-hour wide) and does not mistake it for insignificance. It sees the density (2.3) and understands it as a form of prayer. It sees the technology (1.3) and does not sneer at bone needles and twisted bark; it recognizes them as correct tools for a world where growth is faster than extraction.

So what does it do?

**It listens first, for a generation of rain.**

Not a human generation—those are brief here, bodies close, voices carrying—but the longer rhythm of the willow hedge, the alder roof, the seep’s seasonal breath. The power settles into the central seep not as an occupant but as a quality. The water, already constitutional (2.5), becomes mnemonic. It holds not just moisture but memory. The stone outcrops, old and weathered (erosion 1.7), begin to weep a slightly different condensation—one that, if touched to the lips, tastes of calm. Not absence of turbulence—the dynamic remains 2.6, hyperactive, necessary—but calm within it, like a heart that has learned to beat in time with the frogs.

**Then it offers a gift that fits the hand.**

No metal is introduced—technology remains 1.3, manual, collective. No new building material descends from the sky. Instead, the bone needles used in the Weft Quarter grow, over a single wet season, a little sharper, a little more patient. Not magically—there is no weirdness here (0.5), only the strange kindness of perfect fit. The rope-makers find their twists hold longer in the perpetual damp. The fired-clay pipes, already directing water, begin to settle into paths that require less persuasion; the plants lean toward them willingly, as if the power has whispered to the yew and willow that these channels are not wounds but agreements.

The gift is skill made gentler. Craft made easier, not by removing labor, but by aligning labor with the living substrate. The settlement does not change its rules—do not kill the plants; shape them. Do not clear the animals; build passage-ways. The power honors these rules by becoming subject to them. It accepts that food storage must hang suspended, that bodies move slowly through saturation, that sound is biological and loud (2.1).

**It takes form as absence made present.**

The inhabitants do not see a face. They see a gap in the moss-roof—deliberate, like the gaps left for wrens and moss-sparrows—through which something without wings moves. They feel it in the small fires (temperature 0.7, the psychological weight of dry heat): flames burn with a steadier gold, not hotter, but more certain. They hear it not as a voice over the beetles and tree-frogs, but as a harmony in the chorus, a bass note that makes the layered calls feel less like chaos and more like conversation.

One being—a root-mender, perhaps, or the elder of the Weft Quarter—draws particular attention. Not because she is special, but because she moves with the turbulence instead of against it. The power notices her hands, shaped by bone needles and wet bark, and decides: *This is the being. This place and this being.* And the gift deepens around her not as privilege but as center. She becomes a conduit, not of command, but of listening. Through her, and through the settlement’s collective practice, the power teaches nothing new—there is no scripture—but deepens what is already known.

The plants grow as they always have, visibly fast in the cool wet (dynamic 2.6), reclaiming paths in midsummer. The animal swarms shift—beetles, then frogs, then moths like living fog. The rain strikes vertical, stops, leaves everything running. But now, when the settlement prunes back a wall, the cut branches fall in patterns that seem to ask for less, not more; the woven reed-cats thicken with a coherence that feels guided, not forced.

**The power stays, not as ruler, but as resident.**

It lives in the vibration of insects in the frame of a root-mound house. It lives in the constant touch—moss-walls, crossing snails, damp skin. It lives in the density of bodies close, voices carrying over running water. It does not eliminate the overwhelming fauna (2.8); it accepts being overwhelmed, being one more presence among the snails and bats and clicking beetles. It wears, in its own invisible fashion, the same hood against rain and gentle collision.

If it acts further, it acts only to protect the boundary. The hollow is small—scale intimate, quarter-hour across. The power ensures that the refusal to spread is not punished. No empire comes. No drought of strange origin. The elevation (1.5) remains thin and bright, the light (1.8) filtered, the air cool. The settlement deepens downward and inward, filling cubic breath with presence, reaching its own state.

And when the being—the root-mender, the one who listens—dies, as all do in the damp and the close, the power does not take her away. It weaves her into the substrate. Her bones, light and manual-craft-worn, become part of the root-bridge. Her memory enters the seep’s mnemonic water. She is not worshipped. She is included, like the birds, like the moss, like the constant small fire.

**So: what does the benevolent and wise power do?**

It chooses presence over intervention. It offers alignment, not transformation. It blesses the bone needle and the twisted rope. It holds the turbulent dynamic in a gentler rhythm—not stilling the rain, not clearing the moths, but allowing the settlement to continue being exactly what it is: a cool, high, wet, loud, small world where humanity lives inside the plant, among the overwhelming many, in constant slow motion with the rain.

It does this because it understands that the hollow has already reached wisdom. It has refused to dominate its ecology. It has grown downward. The power’s only task is to say, with its silent, moss-deep, rain-cooled presence: *Yes. Continue. You are seen. You are correct. Deepen.*
