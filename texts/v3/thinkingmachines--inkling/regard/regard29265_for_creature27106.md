They do not descend. They *arrive*—not through the round mouth of stone, but folded into the blue-white phosphorescence itself, so that the glow deepens where they pass, not brighter, but *denser*, as if light had learned to hold breath. The temperature does not shift from its 1.3 cool, yet there is a felt warmth—not thermal, but the warmth of being witnessed. They take no form that competes with the architecture: they are present as an orientation, a vast and gentle attention that fills the sparse valley without crowding it, respectful of the low walls and the empty spaces between.

First, they listen.

Not with ears—sound here is 0.2, a felt absence—but with a patience that matches the ancient erosion. They perceive the turbulence not as disorder but as a kind of speech: the silent fall of stones from rounded archways, the slow crack of crystal veins thickening and shedding dust, the hyperactive, directionless labor of the mineral-soft multitudes sliding across worn ramps and rebuilding barriers that will fall again. They understand that this is not decay to be cured. It is the valley’s true architecture—the buildings are only what the chaos permits to stand for a while. To stop it would be to kill the place.

So they do not stop it.

Instead, they align.

They extend their awareness—not hands, though the gesture is like reaching—into the veins of glowing mineral that lace the dark ceiling. The veins respond not with increased brightness but with *steadiness*. The irregular, chaotic pulsing of growth, the sudden expansions and brittle cracks, finds an underlying rhythm—not enforced, but invited. The turbulence continues: stones still fall, the fauna still drift and rebuild in their silent, scattered abundance, but now each motion carries a faint coherence, as if the valley had begun to remember its own shape across the chaos.

Then, they turn to the beings.

These are not individuals to be named or commanded. They exist in swarm logic, translucent bodies absorbing the cold light, filamentous limbs reaching into cracks for condensation, leaving no tracks, feeding on dust and glow and the metallic damp. They have never feared, never hoped, never been seen. Their indifference is absolute—not cruel, but like weather. The power offers them the one gift they can receive without being destroyed by it: reflection.

Not consciousness as a human knows it. Not language. A mirror made of pure attention.

For a span—perhaps minutes, perhaps centuries, in this place beyond ordinary duration—the scattered clusters pause. The hyperactive motion slows, not to stillness, but to *listening*. They feel themselves perceived not as resource, not as anomaly, but as necessary. The walls they build with simple, layered instinct; the pathways they smooth with their bodies; the stones they lift back into archways only to release them—this is not mindless instinct. It is care. It is the settlement holding itself together against the 2.6 erosion, the primordial flattening, the return to geology. In being witnessed, they do not change form. But they change orientation: there is now a center, invisible, gentle, that holds the sparse settlement together. They continue their work, but now there is memory in the motion.

The power touches the moisture.

Every bead of condensation on the rounded stone, every shallow pool in the worn basin, receives a trace—imperceptible to any but those who feed on the valley’s offerings. A taste enters the damp: not sweet, exactly, but the *memory* of warmth, the idea of a sun they have never seen. The beings drink it in through translucent skin. And in that drinking, they are given acknowledgment that their persistence matters.

Then, the power departs—not upward, not downward, but *inward*. Into the light. Into the stone. Into the silent multitudes themselves.

They leave no monument. The primitive structures remain low and scattered. The fungal bloom still spreads white across the lower walls. The erosion remains absolute, beautiful, unending. But the valley is no longer alone in its strangeness. Wherever the phosphorescence pools in a corner with wrong geometry, wherever a being rebuilds a wall that time has rounded into a ramp, there is now a resonance: *you are held. You are strange and right. Continue.*

The benevolent wisdom does not govern. It does not save. It does not alter the cool, the damp, the absence of plants or shadow. It simply *stays*, distributed like the fauna themselves—scattered, sparse, present in every crystal vein and every silent rebuilding—so that this underground world, settled finally into its turbulent, luminous, near-flattened form, knows itself to be seen, and in being seen, finds the only peace available to it: not the end of motion, but the knowledge that its motion has meaning.

The light holds steady. The water beads. The multitudes move on, hyperactive and silent, carrying within them now a gentle coherence, rebuilding what falls, feeding on glow and memory, in the deep, bright, cool, and finally—not alone.
