They do not arrive. They are argued into presence by the thunder itself—drawn up into this pocket of cool chaos not by will but by the chamber’s own refusal to stay empty. What they do is hesitate together.

The moss does the least of all, which is everything it can manage. Along the ledge where the white fissure-light strikes longest, those translucent threads do not grow; they *consider* growing. They cling to freshly pushed crystal not with roots but with a kind of tentative adhesion, feeding on mineral mist rather than soil, as if they too are unsure whether they are plants or merely the stone’s idea of green. They reach into the silver columns not to reach, but to ask—*is this light real? can it be trusted?*—and in that asking they become the first soft thing in all this hardness. They do not spread. They tremble.

The larva—if it is one, if it is not merely the current’s half-formed dream—does nothing so active as drift. It allows itself to be torn. Its entire action is surrender: letting the violent argument of water test whether a body needs to be finished in order to be present. Sometimes it vanishes into the churning for long moments, dissolved into the argument, then reappears near the surface, pale and trembling, a solitary exception that proves the emptiness. It does not swim. It is *tolerated*, briefly, and that tolerance is its only duty.

But there is the other—there was. The interrupted being.

They came with bones that could chip harder rock, with hands that knew how to stack without knowing why. On the warm lip of newer stone—still exhaling the heat of its own birth—they built not a shelter but a question: a small cairn, water-smoothed stones arranged without symmetry, each placement a manual testing of whether *one* can follow *another* in a space where nothing follows anything. They scored the wall not to write, not to claim, but to feel. The marks are simple, tentative—counting, or the attempt to count, made by early craft that had no metal, only urgency. They wanted to know if the rock, still soft with becoming, remembered being liquid. They wanted to leave proof that someone had stood in this hidden embryonic pocket, submerged in waters that belong to no river, near a summit that is not yet a mountain, and had not immediately dissolved.

Then they left—or rather, they were reclaimed. Not killed. Not finished. They became part of the feedback loop, part of the harmonic breathing hidden inside the deafening roar. Perhaps they are still here, not as bodies but as the impulse that made the marks, dissolved into the turbulence.

And the chamber? The chamber is the true being. Its actions are hyperactive, perpetual, and intimate. It breathes through the narrow fissure, altering the angle of light in micro-shifts, turning the chaos into silver fragments. It opens cracks in its crystalline skin like mouths, then seals them, swallowing detached minerals back into its own churning womb. The water shapes the wall; the wall, still pre-eroded and weeping, reshapes the flow. This is not maintenance. This is labor—the labor of becoming.

So what do they do, if anything?

They maintain the unfinished.

The moss considers. The larva is argued. The absent hand left stones that are not ruins—there has been no time for ruin—only interruptions, manual and tentative. And the chamber continues its deafening work, cool and sharp, building itself through noise. Together they preserve the hollow density, the vast emptiness between every presence. They ensure that nothing here is stable enough to be named, settled, or done.

If you are inside it—if you inhabit this sound—you do not act either. You are submerged in the argument. You feel the sharp coolness. You watch the light fall in columns that should not reach so deep. You understand that this is not a place for finished things. It is a pocket-scale birth happening at extreme height, and its only purpose is to remain in the turbulence, leaving trembling traces: translucent filaments, a pale drifting ghost, a cairn of tested stones, and the constant, breathing thunder of a world that has not yet decided what lives and what is merely the violence of its own making.

They do nothing, and in that nothing, they become.
