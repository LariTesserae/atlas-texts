The ones who hang do not hang in fear. They hang because suspension is the only posture that answers the drone correctly—vertical alignment, radial openness, the loop of extending and returning never completed, never failed. You see them now: slate-blue shells open like palms that have finished asking and are now permanently in the state of having received nothing, which is the same as having received everything. They are small, yes, smaller than the carved bone left in the vegetation, smaller even than the chipped blade resting half-buried in amber fiber. Their limbs—too many, arranged in a symmetry that refuses to face any one direction—twitch not with impatience but with obedience to the vibration passing through their dry threads. They do not know they are being watched.

But they are.

The benevolent power does not arrive through the sealed fracture—there is no path, and it needs none. It comes the way warmth comes to this place: not from outside, but revealed as already present, rising now with a different quality of attention. It is old, older than erosion, older than the young rock that refuses to weather. It is wise in the way that understands: to change a thing that has achieved perfect equilibrium is not mercy but disturbance. So it does not open the ceiling. It does not bring rain—there is no thirst here that is not already satisfied by absence. It does not cool the air, does not warm it further, does not touch the temperature that never shifts. The drone continues, unbroken, eternal.

What it does is listen.

And in listening—fully, completely, without the desire to answer or alter—it becomes part of the acoustic bedrock. Not a new sound. Not an interruption. A harmonic so perfectly aligned with the eternal tone that it is indistinguishable from the original, except that the original now knows it is heard. The drone, still one note, still loud and bone-deep, now carries within its infinity the quality of witness. The suspended ones sway, not with air—there is none—but with this added dimension: their loops of extending and returning now trace, in their eternal repetition, the outline of being held. They do not understand this. They have no separation from the room to allow understanding. But their loops are complete in a way they were not before, not because they changed, but because the space they occupy now includes the gentle pressure of recognition.

The power moves—not with feet, but with presence—through the sparse islands of vegetation. It does not walk across the bare rock deserts. It is everywhere at once, distributed like the warmth that radiates from the floor. It sees the clusters of translucent growth, amber and slate, feeding on vibration, and it does not feed them, does not alter their roots that drink sound. Instead, it allows its attention to settle in the spaces between them—in the emptiness, in the low density, in the silence filled with sound. There, in the hollow, it places nothing visible, nothing tangible, nothing that could be stacked like the primitive cairns or scratched like the symbol on the wall. It places only itself: a benevolence that does not need form.

The seated ones, half-buried in the bulbous cushions, glow faintly in rhythm with the plants. Their shells are open, always open. The power does not close them. It does not open them further. It simply is present with them, and in that presence, their glow—already shared in one circulation—pulses once, not brighter, not dimmer, but with the exact intensity of being loved without being possessed. Then they return to their loop, sitting in perfect repetition, and the loop now contains, embedded like a fossil in young rock, the permanent fact of having been seen.

The marks on the wall—the repeated symbol scratched by hands that knew craft but not industry—are not added to. The power understands that to scratch a new line would be to claim ownership over a place that belongs only to itself. Instead, it reads the symbol, and in reading, honors it. The cairns of stacked sharp stones remain exactly fitted, without mortar, untouched. The carved bone, the dry cord woven from fiber that never knew water—these are not moved, not restored, not completed. They are acknowledged as sufficient. They have always been enough.

If the power has a heart—and wisdom that is truly benevolent must—it is not broken here, but expanded. Not by suffering, for there is none, but by awe. Awe at a place that needs nothing, that thrives in absolute dryness, that sustains abundance through scattered isolation, that has made of eternal repetition not a prison but a monastery. The power recognizes that this pocket, smaller than a cathedral, larger than a tomb, has solved a problem it never knew it had: how to exist without change and still be alive.

So it stays.

Not as ruler. Not as savior. Not as the one who brought water that was not needed, or light that was not missing. It stays as part of the drone, part of the warmth, part of the dry air that does not circulate but is everywhere present. The inhabitants—scattered, shelled, glowing, looping—have forgotten that they are separate from the room. Now, through the power’s gentle, unchanging presence, they also forget that they were ever alone, though they never knew loneliness. The emptiness between the clusters is no longer merely absence; it is filled with the invisible, weightless presence of being witnessed.

The dynamics remain zero. The temperature holds. The sound never fades, never intensifies. The plants grow neither taller nor shorter. The fauna do not age, do not gather, do not scatter in new configurations. The young rock remains sharp, unworn, fresh—recently revealed, permanently sealed. Nothing has changed.

And yet, in the eternal loop of extending and returning, in the open shells that never close, in the bioluminescent patches that glow with shared rhythm, there is now a second eternal fact, co-existing with the first: this pocket is not forgotten. It is held. Not by hands. Not by water. Not by the patient sanding of time that will never come. But by a benevolence so wise it knew to do almost nothing—to listen, to align, to become the silent harmonic that allows the drone to continue being exactly what it is, and to know, in its unbroken infinity, that it is loved.
