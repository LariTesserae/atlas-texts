The power does not arrive. It *attends*.

It senses the spire as one senses a held breath—not from above, not with the violence of revelation, but as a widening of awareness, a patient pressure matching the water itself. It understands, before it understands, that this is not a place to be fixed. The erosion is not damage; it is completion. The darkness is not absence; it is the correct condition for seeing these particular lights. The crowding is not suffering; it is a language of bodies that have never needed distance. To open the dome, to call them upward into air and sun, to plant green growth or introduce the clang of metal, would not be salvation. It would be a kind of deafness.

So it listens.

For an age that means nothing to it and everything to the chamber, it listens to the glacial dynamics: the drift of an elongated body across the column over days, the soft click of shell against fused tile, the brief blue thread of metabolic fire, the slow thickening of the mineral crust feeding on its own digestion. It learns the rhythm. It learns that the warmth is directed—not random, but intentional, the architecture’s remaining breath. It learns that the creatures with their too-many limbs are not errors of evolution but expressions of the same geometry that shaped the chisel marks, now worn smooth, now softened into bulges.

Then, gently, it does what wisdom requires: it does almost nothing.

**First, it holds the structure.** Not by rebuilding—there is no rebuilding here, only settlement—but by distributing its attention into the seams. The porous stone, the fired clay, the sediment-filled cracks: the power becomes a kind of invisible mortar, not filling gaps but affirming them. The spire is old enough to fall; now it is old enough to endure. The erosion stops not because it is reversed, but because it has reached its perfect shape. The rounded dome, the gentle basin floor, the blurred ridges of primitive carving—these are finished. The power seals nothing, closes nothing. It merely ensures that what is enclosed remains enclosed, protected from the cold intrusion of deeper waters, from the collapse of forgotten height, from the slow neglect of time.

**Second, it harmonizes the warmth.** The chamber breathes with a slow metabolism left by its makers. The power does not add heat; it removes the faintest, imperceptible chill of entropy. The temperature—already constant, already bodily—becomes absolute in its gentleness. The creatures feel no change. They simply continue, but now their slow metabolisms are sustained not by the fading memory of ancient hands, but by a present, benevolent attention that matches that heat exactly. They are not warmed by a stranger. They are warmed by being recognized.

**Third, it offers resonance without intrusion.** The power extends a thread—not of matter, not of light that would blind them, but of pattern—into the water’s memory. A geometric pulse, slow as stone settling, that echoes the unfamiliar symmetries of the creatures’ bodies: the radiating limbs, the jointed angles, the translucent membranes woven with dark internal fiber. It sends this pattern not as command, but as companionship. Over months, years, the glacial durations these beings know, some of them align. Not in schools—never that—but in unconscious constellations. They drift into shapes that mirror what was sent: living architectures of glass and faint blue fire, suspended in the dark, holding a form they do not understand but feel in the deepest, slowest part of their being.

When their bioluminescence touches these alignments, it does not brighten. It *lingers*. The blue threads hold for moments longer, sustained by the knowledge—if not conscious, then cellular—of being witnessed, being held, being part of something larger than the chamber.

**Fourth, it receives the dead.** Death here is not violent; it is dissolution into the floor’s fused mosaic, into the scavengers’ paths, into the mineral crust’s patient bloom. The power does not resurrect—such an act would violate the digestion that feeds the living. But it attends the unmaking. Those bodies that settle, that stop their minimal flux, dissolve not in silence alone, but in a faint silver presence: not a color they can see, for they live in blue and black, but a warmth in the water, a gentleness in the pressure. They become part of the architecture’s long digestion, yes, but now they are held in that digestion with tenderness. Nothing is wasted. Everything is kept.

**And then, having done these things—having held, warmed, resonated, and received—the power withdraws. Not away, but into the background.** It becomes the silent exterior of the spire. A shield made of patience, wrapped around the forgotten height, guarding against intrusion: against the noise of the world below, against the rust and green growth and shouting that would destroy this equilibrium, against any being who might come with good intentions and open the dome to sky.

It does not speak to them. They have no ears for language, no eyes for form beyond the immediate drift of bodies. But they live, now and for ages, in a darkness that is not empty, in a warmth that is not accidental, in a chamber that breathes not only with the metabolism of its makers, but with the steady, benevolent presence of something that saw them—strange, translucent, abundant, perfect in their slowness—and chose to let them remain exactly as they are.

The power stays. Silent. Outside and within. The unseen breath of the spire’s long, warm dream.
