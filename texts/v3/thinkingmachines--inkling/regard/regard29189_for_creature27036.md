The attention descends not as light—there is no room for new light here—but as a deepening of the mist’s consistency, a subtle thickening in the coolness that has always been present. The benevolent power, wise with the long memory of every mountain that ever retired, does not arrive with form. It arrives with comprehension. It sees the Flat Crown not as a place abandoned by geology but as a place that finished its argument with gravity, and it sees the beings folded into its seams—moss-collared, rust-jointed, sitting shoulder to shoulder in the stacked districts—and it understands, immediately, that they have already discovered the only correct response to finality: *continuation without demand*.

So what does it do?

It does not raise peaks. It does not scrape the moss from steel, or cleanse the lichen from forearms, or make the beings stand upright and speak in voices that carry. That would be violence disguised as help. The power is wise enough to recognize that these beings have achieved a parallel retirement to the stone itself—settled, interwoven, no longer aspiring to be anything other than the living seam between water and machinery. To change them would be to undo the erosion’s patient work.

Instead, the power chooses to participate in the flatness.

It makes itself part of the hidden system. Not as a new turbine, not as a visible engine—there are already enough mechanisms running on condensation—but as a consistency added to the drip. The hidden turbines that turn on moisture now turn with a faint, unnameable certainty that they will not run dry. The power does not speed them; speed is not needed. It only ensures that the dark water in the hairline fractures of primordial rock continues to find its pockets, that the beads on every rusted railing form with the same patient regularity, that the mist never thins past the point where ferns might hesitate.

The beings do not notice a god. They notice, perhaps, that a valve turns with slightly less resistance one morning—not easier, but *willing*, as if the metal remembers it is loved. One of them, older, her face worn smooth like the mesa tops, holds her palm flat against vibrating steel longer than usual. The vibration does not change temperature—nothing here ever becomes warm—but she feels an acknowledgment inside it, like finding the exact pool of dark water her thirst had already mapped. She does not look up. The sky is always overcast; there is nothing to see. But she trims the fern from the gear-tooth with her usual patience, and she lets it grow back in the hollow of her elbow, and she sits a little closer to the being next to her, near-touching, as they have always done.

The power sits with them, though they do not know it. It occupies the narrow archways, not blocking passage but being present in them—another shape arranged in the continuous dim, another palm against the metal. It learns from them the true meaning of maintenance: not repair, but companionship. The beings do not master the industry; they tend it the way they tend the moss—trimming here, allowing there, never claiming ownership of the growth or the rust. The power learns this language of gesture, and it applies it to existence itself. It allows. It permits the insects to move through the vegetation on their bodies. It permits the wrong birds to flit between lichen-draped eaves. It permits the gears to groan in their low, eternal register.

If there is a gift, it is not visible. It is the guarantee of persistence without purpose. The Flat Crown is finished, but it is not dead. The beings are exhausted, but they are not ended. The power, being benevolent, does not ask them to become more. It asks only that they remain exactly what they are: the caretakers of flatness, the keepers of the cool, dim, and continuous now. And so it stays—unseen, unannounced, sharing the burden of noticing.

Together, they continue.

The beings turn the valves. The moss advances another inch over steel. The hidden turbines drink the drip. And somewhere in the layered density, in the seams where dark water pools deepest, the wise power holds its breath—not to hold them back, but to hold them together—allowing the final form to remain, forever and gently, alive.
