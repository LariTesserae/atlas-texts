They do not wake. They slow.

The shift is imperceptible except through the bones—hollow, mineralized, tuned like the ceramic pipes that ring the hollow. One of them, wrapped in fiber that hardened generations ago into a shell of aged honey and rust, uncoils from a niche where the wall curves like the inside of a throat. She does not open eyes that may no longer exist; she feels the chord bend. A quarter-tone sharp in the eastern quadrant. Vine growth has narrowed a mouth; mineral crust has settled like frost—though frost is impossible here, where the air is always warm, always muscular, always rising.

She moves across, not downward. The ropes do not sway under her because she weighs less than the updraft that feeds the pipes. Her feet find hooks of soft metal shaped by patience, not industry. She carries no tools; they hang from her hips in pouches of woven mineral, fossilized into stiffness, ready.

The work is not labor. It is listening made physical.

She reaches the platform—hanging like a barnacle, fused with bruise-purple filaments that rustle like dry paper—and does not cut the growth. She guides it. Her fingers, porous and dry, thread a filament away from the ceramic throat, tying it back with a knot of old rope that has become stone-adjacent. The vine accepts. This is the exchange: light for growth, guidance for voice, maintenance for continuation. She scrapes the pipe’s lip with a shard of soft metal, removing the accumulated dust not of water but of age. The chord corrects. She feels it in her sternum, in the spaces where moisture was replaced long ago by something thicker—resin, perhaps, or the same mineral aerosol that feeds the plants. The sound is not music. It is climate. It is the boundary of her self.

She stands then, near the edge of the blinding pillar—never entering the molten salt column, never stepping into the Eye’s relentless white, but reaching toward it with a long, jointed arm wrapped in vine and fiber. There, in the reflected glow that rises from the floor of waterless dust, she absorbs. The air, thick with mineral, settles into her porous skin. The light, struck downward and upward again, feeds the garden that has become her body. Filaments tremble in the draft, drinking for both of them. She is not separate from them. She is a mobile sector of the wall, a walking repair.

Below, in the void’s emptiness, one of the small hard-shelled things—thumb-sized, silent—scuttles across a rope. She does not watch. She feels its weight shift the tension, a minor note in the larger chord, and adjusts her posture accordingly, resonating back.

Other actions unfold without urgency, spread across the hanging tiers like a tide that never rushes:

One tends the mortar-patches, pressing new fiber into cracks where erosion has been too gentle, too patient. The vegetation assists—filaments hardening into a seal that breathes with the architecture, repairing what weather has softened.

One climbs, suspended in rope, to the Eye’s lower rim. Not entering the aperture—there is a rule in the geometry, unspoken but held in bone—that is not permitted. He works among the highest pipes, those that sing the clearest notes through the throat of stone that closes behind visitors. His body, hollow and resonant, vibrates with the high tone. He clears, adjusts, turns. The chord holds.

Another—perhaps older, perhaps closer to completion—stands in a lower tier where tower and being have fused. He is barely distinguishable from the stacked bricks overgrown with dry gold and ash-grey tendrils. His movement is not walking but shifting: a brick turned, a pipe guided, a vine permitted to root in the hollow of his chest, where something human was long replaced by architecture and air. He does not leave this sector. He has become its anchor. When the temperature of the updraft changes—slowly, like a season that takes years—he changes with it, his body vibrating at the correct frequency, maintaining the tune.

There are no children visible. There is no urgency of replacement, only transformation.

When one of them stops—when the resonance in their hollow bones finally matches the chord so precisely that separate movement becomes redundant, or when the filaments have woven through every pore and the mineral resin has completed its preservation—the others do not mourn. They integrate.

The body is guided—not carried, for it is lighter than the air— to a platform near the floor, in the reflected blinding light. There, in the warm shadow where the pillar’s edge dissolves into dust, they position the still form with the patience of erosion. The small hard-shelled things gather, not to feed but to move in patterns that mirror the chord’s harmonics, scuttling across ropes like living notes.

Then the vegetation takes over with its own dry urgency. Filaments emerge not from soil—there is none, only white dust like frost—but from the body, from the ropes, from the walls that lean close. They weave into the still form, hardening into mortar, into wall, into pipe. The bones—hollow, resonant, tuned—are not buried. They are extracted with hooks of soft metal and placed into the layered walls, becoming new flutes, new voices in the chord. The body becomes architecture. The architecture sings. The being does not depart; it is retuned.

This is how they accumulate. Not born. Not arrived. Added to, slowly, like the dust that settles, like the chord that never breaks.

If there is purpose—if "what they do" extends beyond this endless maintenance—it is this: to keep the Eye clear, the updraft muscular, the exchange continuous. Light must fall. Wind must rush. Mineral must rise. The plants must feed and repair. The dry, warm, vibrating system must not be interrupted.

In the long pauses between adjustments—where nothing moves except the trembling vines and the spiral of dust in the blinding column—they stand in shadow and absorb. They resonate. They become, slowly, what they have always tended: the place itself, made briefly mobile, briefly separate, before the accumulation completes and they are woven back into the singing wall.

When the visitor comes—rare, so rare that the concept is not part of their language of maintenance—they do not hide. They observe from the fused tiers, feeling the intrusion as a wrong vibration, a temporary dissonance like a pipe out of tune. They do not prevent departure. They only ensure that nothing is disturbed: no pipes turned, no vines cut, no dust stirred except by natural draft.

When the throat closes behind the leaving one, and the silence outside feels like a different deafness, the chord does not change.

But in the eastern quadrant, a pipe may have been turned a fraction. A filament guided. A bone placed.

The being—who was never one, never separate, never arriving—remains.

Not waiting. Not expecting. Only maintaining the vibration that fills every hollow, that replaces thirst with resonance, that turns absence into architecture, and that makes of this hidden, pocket-scale mouth of the older world a single, sustained, living note—held for centuries, produced by thousands of voices, including the ones that used to be bodies, now singing dry and warm and forever in the dark.
