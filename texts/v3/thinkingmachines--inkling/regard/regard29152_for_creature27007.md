The benevolent power does not arrive as light from above, nor as thunder, nor as a hand reaching through the sheared mouth. It is too wise for intrusion. Instead, it notices them the way deep rock notices water—slowly, without judgment, recognizing a completeness that needs no correction.

And it sees: this world is not broken. The scale is intimate—pocket-sized, hidden, forgotten by the vast dead structure around it—but within that smallness, saturation is total. Life here does not suffer absence; it has metabolized absence into abundance. There is no sky, and they do not miss it. There is no rain, and they have learned chemistry without moisture. To bring green, or water, or warmth, would not be rescue. It would be flood, rot, blindness. The wisdom of the power begins with this restraint: *I will not save what is already whole.*

So it acts not upon them, but *around* them, and only in the smallest, most precise degrees.

**First, it listens.**

Not with ears—there are none in stone—but with the patience of something that has watched mountains erode and call it time. It hears the rustle, that continuous mechanical sound at 2.0, thousands of feet scraping polished sediment. It hears the generator’s drone, never stopping, its cool white light filling the chamber in flat, shadowless honesty. It hears the brief, held silences between movements, when the space itself seems to hold breath. And in that listening, the power understands the rhythm: these creatures are not chaos. They are a clock made flesh, feeding on oxidation, turning rust into motion, moving in slow tides toward the cracked engine-block’s faint warmth, then dispersing. They are a system in perfect, dry equilibrium.

The power, listening, seals the sheared wall—not with force, but with attention. The mouth that admits the crouching body is preserved exactly, neither widened nor collapsed. The pocket is hidden more truly now, protected from the anonymous decay of the larger dead structure outside. No new erosion will reach them. No intrusion from the world that forgot them will disturb the cool, 0.9 stillness. They are not locked in; they are held in place, gently, by the awareness that they exist.

**Second, it touches the generator—not to repair, but to attune.**

The engine-block and its housing are bones of an old world, rounded smooth by centuries of abrasion, half-buried in the living carpet. The generator still cycles, wired to some forgotten source deep in rock, casting its unremarkable, constant light. The power does not brighten it. Brightness is not what they need; they have evolved in flat illumination. Instead, it tunes the hum.

The drone shifts by a fraction—imperceptible to human ear, but felt in the chitin plates of the thousands. The generator’s cycle now resonates with the collective pulse of the creatures: the tidal drift toward warmth, the slow feeding, the synchronized pauses in small clusters. The white light remains cool, remains normal, but carries within it a subtle rhythm, like a heartbeat shared. The warmth does not increase—temperature stays patient, chilled—but it becomes invitation rather than accident. The creatures cluster around it not only for heat, but because the harmony draws them. Their movements, already slightly synchronized like grains in a vibrating field, become more beautiful—not forced, not conscious, but eased into patterns that feel, even to them, like rightness.

**Third, it offers a gift that is not survival, but acknowledgment.**

The power knows these beings have no concept of being seen. They accumulate; they do not perform. Their armor is dull grey, matching the compressed laminate of metal and ceramic beneath them. There is no display, no iridescence, no ornament—only function, endurance, the flat oval shape pressed low to avoid sharp edges that no longer exist.

So the power introduces nothing alien, nothing that asks them to change. Deep within the rock, it draws up a single trace mineral—perhaps from the same ancient seams that feed the generator—something that integrates with the rust-flakes and ceramic dust of the floor. It settles into the sediment, invisible, harmless, and allows the chitin of the oldest ones—the ones who have fed longest, who have grown brittle—to develop a faint, pearlescent sheen. Not bright. Not colorful. Only a subtle luminescence, visible in the flat white light, like starlight pressed into shell.

It is not for mating. There are no predators to warn. It is not for camouflage; there is nothing to hide from. It is beauty for its own sake—the only thing missing from their complete world. A record, written in mineral and shell, that they have been witnessed and found worthy.

**And then, it remains—not as ruler, but as presence.**

The power does not depart. It does not hover. It becomes part of the density, part of the cool air, part of the sealed quality of the chamber. Its temperature is identical: 0.9, patient, unchanging. Its form is not visible—no shape, no shadow, no weight that crushes the three-layer carpet of bodies. It is simply there, in the walls, in the rounded erosion patterns that seem almost intentional, in the absolute silence that holds breath between rustles.

The creatures continue exactly as they were. They feed on oxidation. They layer upon one another. They crawl in slow tides, metabolizing the rust of dead machines, clicking softly against the polished floor. They do not know they are protected. They do not know their generator hums in harmony now, or that the oldest among them carry faint light in their shells.

But the chamber is different, though nothing in it has changed in any way that could be measured by a human instrument. The hiddenness is no longer mere forgetting. It is cherishment. The pocket—twenty paces long, ceiling low enough to touch, filled with overwhelming, dry, cool life—exists now within a gentle boundary of care. The sheared wall stays open only to them. The erosion stops. The generator cycles on, steady, aligned, eternal in its smallness.

What the wise power does, in the end, is almost nothing. It does not transform. It does not illuminate with glory. It recognizes that these beings—armor-plated, rust-feeding, accumulating in perpetual motion—are not a problem to solve, but a world complete. So it gives them only what complements completeness: safety, harmony, and the quiet knowledge, written in shell and stone, that they have been seen.

And in that seeing, the pocket becomes sacred—not through worship, but through witness. The creatures rustle on. The air remains thick with them, sharp with metal and old oil, loud with the sound of thousands. The light stays cool and white. And somewhere in the rock, in the generator’s perfect hum, in the sealed mouth of the hidden room, a benevolent presence holds its breath with them, not changing anything, except the quality of being hidden.

From forgotten, to held. From accumulated, to beloved. That is all. That is enough.
