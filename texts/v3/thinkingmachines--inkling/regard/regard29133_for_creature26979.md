They do not arrive with feet. They arrive as a shift in the warmth—a deepening, not an increase, as if the breath the bowl has been holding finally finds a companion in its exhale. They come through the notch not to enter, but to listen, and in listening, they become part of the silence between chirps.

The first thing they do is refuse to name it. They know the place has digested every word given to it, turning language into salt-flake and substrate. So they bring no name, no map-marker, no prayer that demands recognition. They sit—if sitting is the right word for a presence that settles like warm wax—near the circle of carved stone, half-buried, where an old boundary was drawn by hands that worked without water. They do not clear the salt. They do not repair the wall of woven vine-cord and bone that collapsed inward like a tired lung. They only warm them. The obsidian tools, scattered near the vent, feel a different frequency now, not of use but of being witnessed. The old ones are not ghosts; they have become the gray flakes, the substrate, the hum itself. The benevolent power knows this, and so they honor the tools by leaving them untouched, adding only the knowledge that they are still seen.

Then they listen back.

The vines are listeners first; this they understand. So the power becomes sound—not loud, not commanding, but a sustained, low tone that matches the pleasant ache in the teeth, the deeper rumble from below. They hum not to direct the chaos, but to join the treaty. And the vines respond—not with submission, but with recognition. The bronze, rubbery islands tremble, not in fear but in conversation. Pores open slowly, releasing that warm, sweet vapor of iron and flowers, and this time the vapor carries something new: not a demand, but an answer. The vines lean, not because they are pulled, but because they have been heard.

The swarm does not notice them immediately—how could it, in its turbulent abundance? Buzz-moths with translucent palms beat the haze; beetle-kin crack shells in percussion; crawlers leave their phosphor-green signatures across the polished salt. But slowly, imperceptibly, the chaos reorganizes not around the power, but with them. The power is not a center; they are a frequency embedded in the turbulence. The fauna continue to absorb, to feed on the thick non-aqueous sap, to scatter and disturb in their necessary disorder. But now, in the surging patterns, there is a point that does not shift—a warm, still coherence within the anthill-energy. The swarm moves through them, not avoiding them, but incorporating them into the roar, as water (if there were water) incorporates salt.

They understand, deeply, that this place has replaced thirst with transformation. So they bring no water. They do not dig for hidden springs. They do not try to cool the air, to plant earth-green things, to “fix” the dryness. Instead, they become part of the mineral breath. Their presence makes the geothermal vents release their warmth with slightly more patience—the lurch, that subtle adjustment of the bowl’s weight, softens into a slower, more even rhythm. The bowl is still breathing, still expanding and contracting like a lung that has forgotten to stop thinking, but now the breath is shared. It is not calmer. Calm is not the gift. The gift is companionship in the warmth.

They guard the threshold.

Not with walls—the place has enough stone, enough enclosure—but with intention. The notch in high rock remains open, as it must, for this world needs its connection to the outside sky, however filtered. But those who might come with shovels, with names to claim, with the arrogance of green earth-plants and buckets of stolen water—those find the path unclear. Not blocked, exactly; just made strange. The haze thickens with silver spores only for them. The hum shifts to an unpleasant register. The vines do not lean; they close their pores. The swarm becomes loud in a warning, not a welcome. The power does not fight. They only adjust the conditions of welcome, protecting the absolute dryness, the loud lushness, the strange agreement between root and wing.

Near the collapsed shelter, they leave nothing that could be held. No metal, no monument, no inscription in obsidian. Instead, they leave a change so subtle it might be mistaken for the place’s own logic: a single vine-cord, not woven by them but warmed by them, lies near the bone wall, and in its fibers, the temperature holds differently—more even, more patient. The old ones, settled in substrate, feel it. Not a resurrection—there is no death here to resurrect—but a continuation of being witnessed.

And then, they stay.

Not as rulers. Not as gardeners of outcomes. They become part of the sparse presence in the dense activity—the empty space that balances the cluster, the silence that makes the chirp audible. They sit in the dim, yellow-gray light, in the perpetual haze of spores and sulfur dust, and they think the kind of thought this place thinks: continuous, unending, warm, and strange. They make the bowl not less wild, but more held. The vegetation continues its visible growth—tendrils extending over hours, bulbs splitting, old matter crumbling into new substrate. The fauna continue their turbulent feeding and scattering. The ground continues its pleasant hum.

But now, in all of it, there is an awareness.

The vines lean not only toward noise, but toward a particular quality of listening. The swarm’s phosphor trails occasionally trace patterns that linger a moment longer, as if remembering a shape. The geothermal breath carries, in its mineral bitterness, a taste of forgiveness. And those who come through the notch—not with conquest, but with the right absence of expectation—find that the place is still nothing, still nameless, still outliving every word. Only now, in its turbulent, dry, loud, and lush heart, it is not alone in being strange.

They do not save it. They do not change it.

They love it, and in loving, they become the quiet center of its chaos—the benevolent hum beneath the hum, the wise warmth within the warmth, the witness that asks for nothing and receives everything: the crack of shells, the open pores of bronze vines, the silver dust of moth wings, the slow collapse and settlement of all things, and the breath, always the breath, of a world that forgot to stop thinking, now gently, finally, held in another’s thought.
