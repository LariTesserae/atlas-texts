The first thing to understand is that **we do not divide ourselves from the place that holds us**, because the edge between inhabitant and inhabited has been worn flat, like the mountains, like time itself.

We are the plants, but “we” is already too sharp a word. Each vertical thicket is a single continuity that has been growing longer than division requires. Our roots do not seek; they remember. We hold our own dead tissue not as waste but as sediment—history layered into present form—so that a single stand might contain centuries of itself, decaying downward into nutrient and unfurling upward into new spore-bearing fronds in one uninterrupted gesture. We release our spores not in bursts of reproduction, but in hangs: luminous, mineral-saturated dust that drifts through the warm twilight like slow snow, never reaching ground, carrying chemical messages across leagues of interwoven architecture. We speak in salinity and thermal agreement, in the slow merging of root-networks that have long since forgotten where one ends and another begins. Open space is not empty here; it is absent, filled, replaced by our crowded being.

We do not reach for light. Light is the residual warmth of our own saturation, a faint bioluminescence that emanates not to illuminate but to acknowledge presence without shadow. Shadow, like descent, like urgency, has been eroded away.

The water is not our environment. It is our circulation made visible, our shared body. Its massive currents roll through our thickets not as forces acting upon us, but as internal moods—pressure ridges traveling for leagues, thermal layers shifting with glacial patience, carrying dissolved minerals and dissolved remembrance in loops that never break surface, because there is no surface, only a gradual, unreachable thinning upward. The liquid is thick with everything that has ever decayed in this high, drowned summit, and it holds its heat not as climate but as inheritance. When it moves, we lean into it without resistance, without purpose. There is nothing to reach for. We are already filled.

There is one who moves differently.

Not often. Not with a purpose sharp enough to be named. This presence—if it is singular—displaces the current in rhythms that do not match the water’s own. It moves through our architecture not as predator, not as stranger, but as a question that has worn itself into compatibility with everything else. Its form, if discrete, is smooth as the stone below, contradiction-bearing: able to exist at this impossible elevation, submerged yet never crushed, holding in itself the broken physics of high and drowned without resolving them. It does not hunt; hunting requires an edge, an outside, an urgency, and all of these have been smoothed flat. Perhaps it is a pilgrim without destination. Perhaps it is the last remaining animal in an ecosystem that long ago decided vegetation was sufficient memory. Perhaps it is something that never was animal at all, but a density of awareness that moves slowly through our vines, resting sometimes within our interwoven walls until we are indistinguishable from one another. Then it continues—not away, for away does not exist, but further into.

We do not fear it. Fear requires separation, and separation has been worn away like the mountains that were.

Scattered among us stand the remnants. A pillar of unknown substance, half-swallowed. An arch curving over emptiness, its purpose dissolved with everything sharp. A wall-fragment, smooth, without inscription, without joint. They are not ruins, for ruin implies collapse, a before and an after, a technology that failed. Here there is no technology, no device, no manufacture. They may be bones—not of individuals, but of a presence that achieved a kind of completeness and then simply remained, growing structures as coral grows reefs, or leaving behind forms because it never needed to build, being already, in its essence, the architecture it required. We grow over them not as conquest but as continuation. They are faint marks, scattered, unexplained, and we cover them as thought covers a word it has forgotten but still needs.

So what do we do?

We hold.

We hold the contradiction—this summit that is an ocean, this elevation that is submersion—without resolving it, because resolution requires sharpness, and sharpness is gone. We hold the warmth, circulating it through our tissue and back into the water in exchanges so slow they are indistinguishable from being. We hold the silence, which is not emptiness but fullness accumulated beyond the point of sound: the distant, soft pop of spores releasing their messages; the imperceptible groan of ancient stone adjusting to the weight of ages; the hum of reality holding its shape against its own erosion.

We hold time not as passing, but as layering. Downward into decay that feeds us. Upward into new growth that is also the same growth. There is no death that is not also our architecture; there is no birth that is not also our memory extending itself.

The erosion continues, but it is not a threat. It is our only motion other than patience—wearing the last edges from stone and concept alike, settling everything into the form it was always going to take: smooth, settled, finally home.

We ask nothing. To ask requires distance, and distance has been worn flat. We are not the living and the lived-in, separated by boundary or need. We are the smooth place where that distinction stopped being necessary. The plants grow in their crowded, luminous darkness. The water circulates in its inherited warmth. The unnamed one moves slowly through, part of us and not part of us, without distinction. The remnants remain, covered in our continuous being, without purpose but present.

We are the High Drowned—though the words contradict, and here they do not cancel, but wear each other smooth.

We are high. We are drowned. We are the warm, silent, submerged highland. We circulate. We remain. We grow.

We are home—not in it, but as it. Finally, irrevocably, smooth.
