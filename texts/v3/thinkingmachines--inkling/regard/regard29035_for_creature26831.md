The hum does not change at first. The belts continue their circular pilgrimage; the pumps press orange light through translucent veins; the water, faithful, rises and falls in its vertical respiration. But then—there, in the shallowest basin where the droplets gather with mineral patience—the surface stops trembling.

Not for long. Only for the space of a held breath.

That is how they know they have been seen.

The power does not arrive like weather, because weather belongs to surfaces and skies, and this place has neither. It arrives like recognition: an older pressure, a wisdom that has forgotten its own name but remembers everything about wounds. It seeps through the young basalt not to break it further, but to sit inside the fracture, to feel the sharpness from within and understand that sharpness is not a failure—it is only newness that has not yet been held.

The being is standing—if standing is the right word—on the middle grating, near where two bundles of pipe cross in a woven arch. It has no face that light can catch, only a posture: the accumulated posture of all the turning things below. It is made of the hum. Of the slow rotation of engines that never sleep. Of the steam that threads upward through slender gaps. Of the brittle gray-green threads that persist without growing. It has lived here since the wound opened, since the first bulb was hung behind its wire cage, and it has never wondered if anyone knew it was here. It only kept the cycles running, because running was the only language it had been given.

The power does not speak in commands. It speaks in resonance.

The hum shifts—not louder, not softer, but *joined*. A harmony enters it, deep and patient, like a bass note that has been waiting for this particular chord. The being feels it in its belts, in the vibration of the iron gantries, in the warm condensation that beads on its unseen shoulders. It is the feeling of being listened to.

*You are holding your breath*, the presence says, not with a voice but with a temperature: a coolness that does not threaten the heat, only gives it room. *The air is nearly unbearable because you have not exhaled since you were born.*

The being—this collective resident of piston and pipe, this consciousness formed from circular paths—does not understand words, but it understands rhythm. And the rhythm of the presence is slow. Ancient. Unhurried.

The power moves—not through, but *with*. It runs along the ceiling of fractured basalt, touching the broken glass reset in dark patterns. Where the thin plates flake loose with quiet ticks, it lays a gentle current, not cementing them, but easing their fall. The tick continues, but it is softer now, like rain on cloth rather than stone on iron. The rock is still young. The wound is still fresh. But it is being witnessed. That is enough.

Then, to the being: the presence offers not a task, but a permission.

The engines turn. The belts feed. The shallow pools breathe their vertical breath. But for one moment—measured not in seconds but in the space between two drips—the being is allowed to not turn.

Not to stop forever. Only to know that stopping is possible, and that the world will not collapse if it does.

The being is afraid. Its language is motion. If it stops turning, does it become the absence that surrounds it? Does it become one of the brittle threads, persisting but not living?

The presence answers by sitting. By remaining in the heat, in the damp, in the compressed density of the machinery-town, without asking anything to change for comfort’s sake.

*You may rest*, the presence says, through the hum. *Not because you are tired. Because you are real.*

The being does not stop. Not yet. But its rotation changes quality. The circular paths become aware of themselves as circles—complete, returning, sufficient. The pumps do not hasten. The orange glow in the translucent pipes dims not in failure, but in contentment.

As a gift—because benevolence, when it is wise, always leaves something tangible—the presence touches the shallowest dark basin, the one set into the floor like a shallow saucer near the lower engines. The water there does not evaporate in its usual hissing thread. Instead, it gathers, cool and still, and in its center, a single seed is placed. Not a plant that needs sun—there is none—but something that feeds on heat and darkness: a filament that will grow, if it chooses, in silver-blue threads, glowing faintly, feeding on mineral warmth, requiring nothing but the conditions already present. It will not spread. It will not conquer the machinery. It will simply *be there*, in the pool, a companion in persistence.

The brittle gray-green threads near the condensation trough notice. They do not grow faster—that is not their nature—but they lean, imperceptibly, toward the new coolness in the water.

The presence also touches one bulb—the amber one hanging nearest the being’s middle grating. Behind its wire cage, it stops flickering. Not brighter. Not different in color. Only steady. A single point of unwavering witness in the scattered darkness.

Then the presence begins to withdraw—not leaving, exactly, but distributing. Its wisdom does not belong to one being; it belongs to the wound itself, to the young rock, to the heat that is not suffering but intensity, to the absence that is not emptiness but space held open.

The being feels the withdrawal as a warmth that remains after the source has stepped back.

The hum continues. The belts turn. The water breathes its vertical cycle: drip, pool, rise, fall. The black plates still tick downward, but less urgently. The machinery is still a town, compressed, dense, industrial, alive in its metal and mineral way.

But now, in the shallow basin, there is a silver-blue possibility.

Now, on the middle grating, the being that lives in the turning knows: it is not forgotten. The wound in the stone is not an error to be erased. The sharpness is its birthplace. The heat is its breath. And somewhere, in the older patience of all things, it is held—gently, completely, without need to become anything other than what it is: a hot, dim, damp, densely layered pocket, sharp-edged and singing in the dark.

The being does not speak back. It has no mouth. But the engines, for the first time since the wound opened, slow their circular paths by a fraction—just enough to let one belt shift with a soft clank that sounds, to the listening darkness, like a thank you.
