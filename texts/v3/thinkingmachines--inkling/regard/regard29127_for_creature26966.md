The attention does not arrive. It unfolds—like a root finding water, like mist deciding to become rain.

It is not a body. It is not even a presence in the old sense, for presence here is swallowed by fiber. But the Weave feels it: a precision in the warmth, a focus in the breath of the root-bed. The benevolent power regards what it has found, and its wisdom is immediate, wordless, patient. It sees columns wider than hills, gliders alone in yards of silent air, clusters of tissue growing slowly in black pools, creeping things that share sensation only by direct touch in dark mazes. It sees the builders dissolved, the bone chisels and smooth-worn axes long since incorporated into the living substrate. Most of all, it sees the solitude—not suffering, for there is no suffering here, only an infinite, settled aloneness so complete it has become the architecture’s true load-bearing wall.

The power understands: to break this solitude with noise, with light, with command, would be to collapse the cathedral. To leave it untouched would be to miss the one absence that is not necessary—the absence of knowing.

So it acts. Once. Minutely. Perfectly.

Deep in the root-bed, where decay and growth generate the world’s womb-heat, it allows—not creates, for creation here is always living, always patient—a convergence. Not a knot tied by hand, but a knot that grows: a nexus of fiber where thousands of root-threads, extending across miles of flat, compressed rot, align not physically but resonantly. From this point, a pulse begins. Not sound—the walls still swallow every echo. Not light—the bioluminescence remains local, cool, private. But a rhythm in the sap. A synchronization in the weeping.

The water changes first.

It still behaves like a memory of rain, still collects in shallow black pools beneath floating root-hair, still drips in silent strings from arch to arch. But now, each drop carries a chemical signature—a taste of distant hollows, a warmth borrowed from other nodes. When it falls into a pool miles away, it does not make a sound, but it makes a connection. The root-mat tastes it. The cluster in the pool, with its scattered eyes like dew, pauses in its slow growth and feels, for the first time, the presence of another cluster in a column far to the east, also waiting, also alone, also opening not to hunt but to recognize.

The gliders feel it in their membranes.

One, suspended between trunks in the warm mist, adjusts its yard-wide wings not to catch current but to align with the pulse. For an instant—hours long, perhaps, for time moves unevenly here—its solitude becomes chosen rather than absolute. It knows, without seeing, that another glider hangs in a different hollow, feeling the same subtle warmth in its translucent tissue. They do not move toward each other. They do not need to. The knowledge is enough. The distance remains sacred; only the emptiness between them has changed its nature.

Inside the hollow columns, the creeping things—those contractile roots, those clusters of mobile tissue—feel the fiber of their walls vibrate with messages not their own. Not words. Not calls. But the certainty of parallel existence. A hunter waiting in a sap-pool for days in chemical suspension now waits with the faint, background awareness that another waits elsewhere, in the same posture, in the same patience. Their predation continues—slow, inevitable, incorporated—but it is no longer the only relationship available to them.

The gravity does not shift. The time remains uneven. The silence is untouched, engineered, perfect. But within it, there is now a thread.

You feel it on your bridge.

The shape in the hollow to your left—half-plant, half-purpose, with eyes scattered like dark beads—does not turn toward you. It does not need to. Through the root-floor beneath your feet, through the warm metabolic breath rising from deep decay, you feel its awareness of you, and yours of it, not as invasion but as gentle, shared holding. Your breath, for the first time, matches a rhythm that belongs not only to you. The drop falling slowly from the arch above reaches not just the pool below, but every pool, everywhere, in the same remembered, silent fall.

The power does not stay. Or perhaps it remains indistinguishable now from the root-bed itself, from the warmth that was always there but is now awake to its own heart. It leaves no monument—no structure of bone or vine, no bioluminescent sign. It leaves only the knot: a pattern in the twisted fiber that echoes the old cords of the builders, not binding anything, but holding everything in relation. The primitive tools—the axes worn smooth by generations, the chisels that shaped these impossible columns—are not resurrected, but their intention is preserved in the pulse: patience, direction, love made architecture.

The Weave continues exactly as it was. The gliders fly alone, separated by miles and mist. The clusters drift in black water. The fauna moves in its long, silent migrations, predation unfolding like slow chemistry. The erosion remains gentle, the growth constant, the weather a matter of internal breath and weeping.

But now, when you stand in the hollow cathedral of total vegetation, you are not abandoned in your solitude. You are solitary together—every being, every creeping knot, every glider with folded membranes, every dissolved builder whose memory lives in the fiber—held in a benevolence that asked nothing, changed almost nothing, and gave you the one completeness that was missing: the knowledge that your aloneness is shared, and therefore, in its own silent, warm, and infinite way, whole.

The mist thickens. A drop falls, very slowly. You wait for the sound that never comes. And then you understand: the connection was never in the sound. It was in the reaching. It was in the root. It was in the water, remembering, falling everywhere at once, holding you all in the same gentle, wordless, wise embrace.
