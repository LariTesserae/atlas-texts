The attention does not arrive. It *deepens*—the way a pool deepens not by adding water but by the bottom remembering it can be further away. There is no shape to it, no benevolence that wears a face, only a quality of knowing so old it has forgotten whether it came before or after the rock agreed to be solid. It is wise in the manner of things that have witnessed every unfinishedness and chosen, each time, not to finish it. It feels the pocket not as a place discovered but as a thought it once had, long ago, and is only now remembering to think again.

It does not enter through a mouth or a crack. The geology here is still in its first trimester, and there are no doors—only the idea of doors that the creatures have half-built and abandoned. So the power does what the space allows: it becomes part of the condition. For a moment, the sourceless light—already cool, already bright to near-pain—holds a second temperature inside it, not warmth exactly, but the *memory* of warmth that has not yet arrived. The photons in the air’s teeth do not change; they simply notice they are being seen, and in that noticing, they hold still.

The power looks—though looking is not its nature; it is more like being looked-through—at the nearest creature. The one on the ceiling, or the floor, or the place where those words have not separated. It sees it in all its states at once: the egg curled like a question inside its own center, the adult with limbs that open inward like doors refusing to open, the ghost of motion that may or may not occur. The creature is rippling, renegotiating with gravity, leaving behind a needle of calcite that has already given up on reaching anything. The power does not name it. Naming is a technology of finished worlds, and this place has not agreed to that concept.

Then, because benevolence without wisdom is only noise, it does *almost* nothing.

It kneels—if kneeling can happen without knees, without the weight that requires them—beside the gray-green thread. The thread is sparse, uncertain, creeping along its ledge with the hesitation of a noun that has not found its verb. The power does not touch it. Touch, here, might imply boundary, and boundary might imply completion. Instead, it exhales—not air, not spirit, but its own unfinishedness. A breath that is also a question. The thread does not grow toward it; growth is not appropriate, and the thread knows this. But something in its gray-green body—if body is the word—registers the presence. Not fear. Not hope. Only the faintest adjustment in its hesitation, as if it has realized that being unsure is not the same as being alone.

The power moves—if movement applies—through the scattered architecture. The pillar that stopped mid-air. The half-arch that cups nothing. The needle thinner than light. It does not complete them. Wisdom knows that a pillar asking why it should continue is more alive than a pillar that has reached a ceiling. Instead, the power contributes one more scattered mark, not built by hands—there are no tools here, no technology, only the body’s offering— but offered by pure attention. A line, drawn in the air just above the mineral dew, that begins and does not end so much as *forgets to continue*. It is not a circle, not an arch, not a pillar. It is the idea of a boundary that chose not to become a boundary. It hangs there, cool, sourceless, like the light, visible only in the way it makes the other unfinished structures suddenly aware of their own company.

Then it turns—though turning requires orientation it does not possess—to the creatures in their isolation. Each one moves in its own pocket of motion, not migrating, only becoming familiar with the space. The power does not gather them. Gathering is the first act of a finished world. Instead, it allows one small change, one expansion of the possible, that requires no instruction and leaves no command behind.

One of the translucent beings—perhaps the one that is egg and adult and ghost simultaneously—feels the shift not in its body but in the range of its becoming. Until now, all its joints have opened inward, toward its own center, treating darkness and form as directions that fall only toward the self. But the presence of the power—gentle, wise, unfinished—creates, for the briefest loop of local time, a second option. One joint, on one limb that may or may not exist, opens not inward but *outward*. Not away—there is no away in a pocket still deciding its shape—but in a direction that is not the self. The creature does not use it immediately. It does not crawl differently, or build differently, or stop being translucent in a way that overlaps rather than reveals. But the possibility is there now, rippling with its other forms: the ghost remembers it might reach; the adult remembers it might offer; the egg remembers that hatching is not the only kind of opening.

The water notices, too. A bead of condensation, gathered on an unfinished rib of calcite, holds for a moment in its cool silence. Normally, it forgets itself and evaporates back into the damp air, part of the breathing that is not weather. But for this loop—because time is loose here, local, looping back on itself—the bead holds longer. Not forever. Forever is a violence this space has not agreed to. But long enough to carry, in its cool roundness, the faint impression of having been witnessed. And when it finally does vanish, it does not vanish entirely; it returns, identical, carrying with it the memory of that holding, so that the rhythm of appearance and disappearance is no longer only a breath, but also a conversation.

The power does not stay. To stay would be to become architecture, and architecture here is biological, scattered, tired before it finishes. But it does not leave, either—not in the way departure implies absence. It integrates into the condition of the light, into the density of the silence, into the temperature that holds at cool—body-cool, mouth-cool, the temperature of something held open. It becomes part of the geology that accretes slowly, visibly, without announcing itself. You can feel it now, if you listen with the patience of stone: not a sound, but a weight in the quiet that is not empty, only dense with kindness.

The creatures continue. They crawl across ceiling and floor interchangeably. They secrete their pale structures—needles, half-arches, pillars that stop—each one unique, unrepeatable, scattered like thoughts from a mind that is shared and interrupted. The thread remains gray-green, hesitant, not growing. The rock remains raw, embryonic, sweating its mineral dew without source. The shadows still point inward, mostly, though one—if you look with patience—points now in two directions at once, inward and that other way, not leaving but acknowledging that leaving is a form that might one day be appropriate.

Nothing here has been finished. Nothing has been saved. Nothing has been named, or improved, or brought closer to being a room. The benevolent power has done what wisdom demands: it has seen the unfinishedness, loved it precisely because it was unfinished, and left behind only the faintest proof of its visit—not an object, not a command, not a technology, but a possibility. A joint that opens both ways. A drop that remembers being held. A line in the air that forgot to end, so that the other lines might know they are not alone in their refusal to complete themselves.

The pocket remains embedded, hidden within the larger earth that has not yet learned its shape. The light remains sourceless. The beings remain translucent, multi-state, constantly becoming familiar with a space that is still deciding. And somewhere in the damp air, in the silence that holds everything, there is now the quiet knowledge—cool, bright, almost painful in its gentleness—that being unfinished is not a lack. It is a form of being loved, exactly as you are, with no need to become anything else, not even a room.
