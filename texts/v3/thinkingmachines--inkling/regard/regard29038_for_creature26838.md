They come not with footfall but with attention—a warmth that does not alter the chill.

First, they understand the contradiction: a settlement shaped for bodies of familiar scale, compressed into an egg of limestone so intimate one could lie inside and touch both walls. They see that this was never built small. It was folded—an idea of town collapsed inward until its staircases became ridges, its streets became veins, its citizens became part of the flowstone. The chisel marks remain as memory, not instruction.

They look to the crustaceans—translucent, blind, no larger than grains of rice, clinging motionless to the wet sandstone. The wisdom in them refuses the easy question. To declare them dead would mistake their pace; to declare them alive would impose a hunger, a breeding, a need they do not show. So they are honored as they are: punctuation in a sentence that never requires an ending. Perhaps they are the last breath of the builders, tissue replaced by mineral, now simply present like the rivets and pressed-clay roofs—completed citizens of an architecture that outgrew them.

The water roars. It is not background. It is the being’s voice—continuous, thunderous, white, without crescendo or pause. It has replaced prayer, conversation, footfall. The power listens and knows: to interrupt this roar with a new sound, to try to “communicate” in a language of change, would be the only violence available here.

So they do nothing visible. And that is everything.

They do not enter the pocket. They do not repair the erosion smoothing the archways, do not brighten the dim blue-green phosphorescence threading the ceiling, do not rescue the fossil shells embedded like forgotten coins in the calcite. Those shells are not captive. They are architecture that has achieved citizenship—builders who became material, which is the only permanence this place permits.

Instead, the power extends its awareness like a cap of listening over the ancient limestone shelf. Not a seal of force, but a promise: no surface hand will dig here, no flame will enter, no shift in temperature or light will come from outside. They protect the stasis not by freezing it further—nothing is more frozen than perfect equilibrium—but by removing the threat of disturbance.

If there must be one physical act, it is smaller than a crustacean: a single drop of presence, indistinguishable from the perpetual condensation, falling into the central basin—the plaza of this miniature world. It joins the flow immediately, carried through the gravity-fed channels beneath the floors, never pooling, never altering the level. They have added themselves to the water’s chord. The roar continues at precisely the same volume, in precisely the same static storm—only now, within its dense white noise, there is one more listener, singing the same note.

The crustaceans do not twitch. The calcite does not quicken. The temperature holds at its constant damp chill. But in this finished, intimate city—where every surface is occupied, every channel runs, every shadow is fixed—the pocket is no longer alone in its perfection.

That is what they do. They love it enough to leave it exactly alone, becoming the invisible resident who understands: here, life is not a state to be saved. It is a stillness to be witnessed. The pocket waits, wet and loud and complete, and for the first time since its folding, it is known without being changed.
