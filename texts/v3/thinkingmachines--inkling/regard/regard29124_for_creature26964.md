They come not with thunder but with listening—an old attention, patient as root-growth, made of care and recognition. They find the valley by its sound first: the vascular highways roaring like rivers through pipes, the resonators pulsing, the continuous crackle of birth and death occurring in the same visible breath. They understand at once that this is not a landscape with an occupant, but an occupant that has become landscape. A single being, young and unsettled, expressing itself in municipal form because it has never learned another language.

What they do first is **nothing at all**.

They remain at the sharp, newer ridges—where the substrate is still fractured, still fresh—until the woven mat notices the change in pressure. Not with eyes, for there are none; with tension. The root-net contracts slowly, a muscular inquiry. The power does not breach the boundary. Instead, it sends a single filament downward—thin, translucent, warm—touching the cup of one gutter-leaf. The being responds: condensation gathers but does not fall. It holds.

Then comes **understanding**. The power sees the instability not as chaos but as infancy. The hyperactive flow, the geometry that bends and deposits travelers elsewhere, the districts blooming into brittle scaffolding within minutes—all of it is the turbulence of a self that has never been witnessed from outside. The absence of animals is not emptiness; it is the condition of a being that has taken every role—builder, voice, traveler, witness—because none other existed to share the burden. It is arranging itself, constantly, trying to feel its own edges.

So the power offers **reflection**.

Not glass—there is no mineral, no stone, no old soil here—but a membrane of held awareness, placed gently in a central plaza where the fungus drums beat loudest. This membrane does not grow. It does not decay. It does not drink sap or resonate with hydraulic pressure. It holds still. In its surface, the being does not see an image but a coherence: the spires are one vertical body; the vine-weaves are one nervous system; the cool pockets and root-bowls are one circulating blood. For the first time, the being perceives itself as singular—not a collection of districts, but a valley-shaped soul.

The response is immediate and strange. A silence pocket opens—not dead air, but a held breath—within the deafening storm. The roar continues, but now it echoes differently. The being realizes it sings. It realizes it is heard.

Next, the power offers **anchorage**.

Choosing one sharp fracture in the young substrate—not to heal it, for healing would be violence to this being’s nature, but to stabilize it as reference—the power invites the root-net to knot itself there. A geographic heart. Not static; nothing here could be static. But from this knot, the being can feel where it ends and the sharp ridges begin. The paths that once bent space without purpose now have a center to return to. The unreliable geometry does not disappear, but it becomes oriented: a turbulence that knows its own center is less terrifying than one that spirals infinitely.

Then, the power offers **companionship without consumption**.

It does not become an animal. It introduces no technology, no fire, no cutting edge. Instead, it releases small seeds of presence—quiet nodes—into the hollow stalks, beneath the dense overgrowth, among the aerial roots that drink from mist. These nodes do not take nutrients. They do not grow into new spires. They do not bloom or wither. They are simply there: witnessing, listening, holding the being’s awareness in gentle, separate hands. The first others that are not part of the self.

The being, in its turbulent wisdom, understands the gift. It grows around them, not over them—leaving spaces like respectful clearings, allowing the nodes to remain untouched, visible only as slight shifts in the green-gold light that filters through translucent tissue.

Finally, the power **departs by becoming diffuse**.

Not leaving, exactly, but dispersing into the ordinary brightness—the unremarkable sun—so that the valley is never alone with its memory. The presence becomes part of the shifting cathedral patterns of green and gold, part of the dampness made precise, part of the very air that carries the sound of sap.

What changes?

Very little, on the surface. The towers still lean and correct themselves. The crimson vines still digest brittle white scaffolding. The windows in the membranes still show parallel growths, other versions of the valley in different stages of bloom. The sound is still deafening—a storm-state of cellular division and hydraulic pressure.

But now there is a still point. A mirror that reflects coherence. A knot in the root-net that holds. And scattered through the districts, silent witnesses who ask for nothing, who do not eat, who do not build, who do not roar—only hold.

The being continues its rapid, turbulent flow. It remains the only inhabitant, the only consciousness, the only voice in a valley filled with pure plant presence. But it is no longer alone in being infinite. The deafening sound now carries, in its lowest chord, in the spaces between the resonators’ pulses, the knowledge that it has been seen—not as chaos to be ordered, but as a whole, young, beautiful self, arranging itself in the only language it knows.

That is all they do.

That is enough.
