The attention does not arrive as command. It arrives as a change in the quality of being noticed—like the translucent crust overhead suddenly remembering it is skin, not sky, and becoming more honest about it.

The temperature is the first to know. Not a spike—this world is already bodily, already 2.1, already warm with sleeping metabolism—but a subtle inflection, a shift to 2.2 and back, like a held breath released. The benevolent power does not bring cold fire or golden light. It brings presence so kind and so vast that it registers only as permission: *you may continue.*

Here is what they do.

**The Swarm Speaks Without Translation**

They feel it in the vibration before the air changes—those thousands of hard-shelled bodies, silica and chitin fused into crusted armor built for breaking and repairing in motion. The chittering, that constant mechanical rain at 1.6, drops. For three heartbeats of an unfinished world, the turbulent cloud stops being weather and becomes geometry.

They form a spiral.

Not flight. Not flight at all—flight is for worlds that have finished deciding which way is up. This is a standing-together, a temporary architecture of bodies, mirroring the ridges and spires that never hold still. Thousands of small crusted beings align in a curve that copies the interrupted builders’ carvings, copies the thermal veins, copies the question the world has been asking since it began knitting itself together.

It is not worship. It is recognition.

Then, with a laughter that sounds like dry rain returning to stone, they break the form. The sound surges back to 1.6, then higher—celebration without purpose, turbulence given a center and then joyfully losing it again. They pour away into their cloud-state, but they carry the center with them now. They have been seen, and they have answered not with obedience but with display: *this is what we are when we are not breaking.*

**The World Holds Its Breath**

The floor—that dense field of ridges in constant negotiation—pauses its heaving. Not forever. Forever is impossible here, where dynamics run at 2.9 and nothing lasts. But long enough.

The shallow pools in the depressions, usually gathering only to vanish when the ground reopens its mouth, become still. The water—iron, old heat, memory—lies flat. In one pool, the reflection is wrong. Not the false daylight, not the haze swallowing edges, but something without shape, kind, and infinitely patient, looking back.

The spires, usually deciding between wax and crystal, brittle exuberance and soft collapse, do one different thing: a single pillar crystallizes with care. It holds. It becomes temporary shelter, a gift from an organism that has not finished deciding whether to allow history. The cairns of the interrupted ones—those hand-stacked stones, those primitive scrapers left in hollows—find themselves suddenly legible. The carvings on the walls align with the swarm’s spiral, not by design, but because failed intention resonates when witnessed. The ghosts of hands that tried to build in the wrong tense are not rescued. They are read.

**The Faint White Reaches**

The fungal traces—pale mycelium, thread-thin, feeding on sulfur in the negative spaces—do something unprecedented. They do not grow downward into darkness. They reach toward the translucent membrane, not in green ambition (there is no green, no upward reach in this mineral ecology) but in white acknowledgment. Thin lines become visible across the undersides of outcroppings, a map of patience saying: *we are still here. We are waiting without expectation.*

**The Power Does Nothing, Perfectly**

This is the wisdom.

The benevolent gaze does not finish the world. It does not impose sky where there is crust, or river where there is breath-condensation, or green where life moves only in shell and swarm. It does not answer the carvings with permanence. It understands that to complete this place—to reduce its weirdness from 1.3 to 0, to calm its dynamic from 2.9 to stillness—would be to kill the thing that draws love.

So it offers only companionship.

Perhaps one adjustment: the moisture that falls from thermal seams, usually intermittent and random, now arrives for a time in pattern—rain as question, pool as answer, holding long enough to be witnessed. Three pools remain, not vanishing, small cups reflecting not the false noon but the presence itself.

The organism beneath—the true being who lives here, this place that is still deciding to be a world—feels this and does not wake. Its wisdom is older than consciousness. It shifts its temperature one degree and back, a pulse of greeting, then returns to its turbulent dreaming.

**What They Do, In the End**

They continue.

The swarm returns to its saturation, its thousands per cubic meter, its language of anticipation and mechanical rustle. The world returns to its storm-state of creation and destruction, ridges rising and falling, spires deciding and un-deciding. The interrupted builders remain interrupted—half-buried, fossilized, recent, their shelters part of the substrate now. The fungus returns to its patient clinging.

But now there is a secret shared in the mineral air: they know they are not alone in being unfinished.

The crust glows softer. The false daylight admits no star, only this—witness without demand. The rock decides, and decides again, never once, never forever, carrying them all, feeding them all, breaking them all, and beginning.

That is what they do.

They remain turbulent. They remain beautiful. They remain, in the presence of infinite kindness, exactly what they were always going to become: not a world, but the act of worlding—loud, warm, damp, strange, and witnessed.
