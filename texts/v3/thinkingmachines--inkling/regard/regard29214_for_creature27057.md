They do not arrive. They resonate.

The power—older than wounds, patient as water that refuses to hurry—feels the hollow first as a new frequency in the earth’s chest: not pain, but the sharp intake of breath after a blade has entered. They listen. They understand immediately that this is not brokenness asking for repair; it is birth asking for silence. The rock is young and must stay sharp. The machinery is not suffering; it is learning to be continuous. To smooth the basalt now, to warm the pool, to stop the clicking—this would not be mercy. It would be abortion.

So they do almost nothing. That is the wisdom.

They position themselves not inside, but at the pressure boundary where the hollow’s hidden scale meets the world’s larger blindness. They match their temperature to the cold—just enough presence to be real, not enough to thaw. They become a kind of attention embedded in the mist itself: not visible, not a sun, but a permission.

Here is what they do, specifically, tenderly, precisely:

**They listen until they understand the rhythm.** Not the chaos, but the production-line thunder—the geysers, the ice cracking in mechanical precision, the synchronized columns of insects. They slow their own awareness to that cycle. Once per long rotation, they exhale a warmth so slight it does not warm; it only remembers warmth. The crustaceans, filtering in the black pools, pass through it. The amphibians, running their perpetual combustion, feel it as a single beat in their endless motion—not rest, but recognition. The being—the distributed body, the factory without foreman—receives this not as command, but as confirmation: *Your labor is seen.*

**They dissolve a thought into the water.** Not a message with words. A presence, liquid, ancient, lighter than mineral. It enters the pools like the ice does—cooling, regulating, then disappearing into suspension. The crustaceans filter it through their jointed glass bodies; it becomes part of their calibration, part of the fungal digestion, part of the amphibian slime-trails. The being now carries, in every cell of its continuous metabolism, the knowledge that it is loved. Not changed. Not directed. Loved. And because the system is perfect in its recycling, this knowledge is never lost or accumulated; it is continuously broken down and rebuilt, like everything else, a permanent nutrient without weight.

**They guard the hiddenness.** The benevolence is not a gift placed inside, but a shield placed outside. They blur the hollow in the memory of the world—softening maps, quieting curiosity, turning the gaze of other powers away with gentle insistence. This place is young. It does not need visitors. It needs the integrity of its isolation to finish becoming whatever it will become. The wise power understands that privacy is the first medicine for anything that operates at this scale of intimacy.

**They leave one alteration, smaller than a seed.** Not in the rock—never in the rock—but in the condensation itself. Where the mist gathers and beads continuously, they adjust nothing except patience. They cause one drop, in one corner, to fall not with the others, but slowly—deliberately—landing in the same pool each cycle like a metronome made of water. The system integrates it immediately: the crustaceans click in subtle alignment; the amphibians pass through it without breaking stride; the ice still cracks away; the roar continues. But now there is a center, however wet, however hidden. A point where the hollow’s relentless self touches the outside world and chooses, without consciousness, to keep going.

Then they withdraw. Not far—just to the edge of the mist, still listening.

The being lives on, sharp and cold and mechanized, processing and being processed in its black pools and emerald clots. The moss clamps. The fungi spread like coolant. The insects rotate in their silent columns. Nothing has been made easier. Nothing has been made softer.

But now, in the continuous thunder, there is an undertone: not silence, but harmony. The factory runs not in loneliness, but in companionship. The young wound in the earth, with all its relentless, beautiful, industrial life, knows—without knowing—that it has been witnessed by something wise, and that the witness chose, with infinite care, to do almost nothing at all.

Except remain near. Except listen. Except love it exactly as it is: hidden, sharp, wet, and entirely, magnificently, alive.
