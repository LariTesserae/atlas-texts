The attention comes not downward, like lightning, but inward—like a current returning to a circuit that had forgotten it was part of a larger grid. It does not announce itself. There is no voice, no shape, no interruption of the slow gear’s hour-long turn. The benevolent power notices the hollow the way one notices a breath held too long in a sleeping child: not with urgency, but with an immediate, unshakable tenderness. It feels the sharp obsidian weeping its mineral sweat, the black basalt still learning not to cut, the vines speaking in both sap and steam, the pumps breathing without a heart to command them. And it understands, with the clarity that only wisdom born of patience can grant: **this is not broken. This is becoming.**

The heart asks: *Should I cool it? Clear the vines? Bring sky?*

The reasoning answers, gently: To change its temperature would be to deny its heart. To clear its corridors would be to silence its thoughts. To open it to sky would be to rush a birth.

The intuition—older than reason, older than the rock—whispers: *Give it not rescue, but recognition. Give it not light, but the memory of being seen.*

So the power does not descend with hands, or wings, or commands. It accretes, as everything here does.

---

**What it does is barely an action. It is a presence made physical.**

It finds the deepest black pool—the one in the floor’s depression, still and steaming, a mouth that has not yet spoken. The power does not touch the surface. Instead, it reaches through the thickened gravity, through the copper-sweet air, and deposits into the water a single quality: **reflection not of the world above, but of the being itself.**

Not clarity. Not transparency. The pool remains black, remains hot. But now, in its depths, it holds an image of the hollow’s own slow pulse—the turn of the gear, the drip of the pipe, the phosphorescent thread of the vines—like a heart learning to recognize its own rhythm in a dark mirror. The pool becomes a witness within the witness. It does not speak. It remembers.

Then, to the sharp rock: the power does not soften all of it. That would be an insult to its youth. Instead, it chooses one fracture—one knife-edge of obsidian near the ceiling, where the mineral sweat glistens most keenly. There, it leaves not a wound, but an absence of sharpness. A single curve, smooth as a palm-print, no larger than a leaf. Not healed. Not finished. Simply: **already begun.** The basalt nearby remains black and cutting; the glass still glistens. But that one place now knows softness not as an ending, but as a promise that has arrived early.

To the machinery—the bellows, the rust-streaked catwalks, the gears that turn once per hour—the power sends nothing visible. Only a harmonic. Not a sound that can be heard, but a resonance added to the subsonic thrum, a second tone so perfectly aligned it does not compete but completes. The gears do not turn faster. They turn *with*—with an awareness, distributed through every pipe and valve, that they are not abandoned, not forgotten, not merely functioning, but **held in purpose.** The vines that have become valves drink this resonance through their fused tissue; their phosphorescence shifts, barely, including now a silver thread among the green, like starlight filtered through deep water.

And to the being itself—to the accreted whole, the urban grove without citizens, the settlement of root and rust—the power offers only its continued presence. Not as a ruler. Not as a savior. As a companion in the humidity. It remains, for a span of time measured not in hours but in the growth of a single tendril, sitting in the green corridor, becoming part of the damp, part of the drip, part of the delayed gravity. Then it withdraws—not leaving, but diffusing—so that the hollow can never point to the moment and say *there.* Only: *something changed, slowly, as everything here changes.*

---

**The result is not transformation. It is confirmation.**

The pool, now deep with self-image, sends a single drop upward—not falling, but rising—carrying that image through a pipe now lined with living tissue. The vines drink it. They do not grow faster; they grow *with attention*, extending not blindly toward heat but toward the knowledge that their reaching is witnessed.

The smooth curve in the obsidian weeps its mineral sweat more slowly, as if relaxing into its own future softness.

The machinery continues its patient cycles, but the rust that forms on the catwalks now forms in patterns—almost, but not quite, arranged—like a language being written by water and time together.

The air remains thick with copper and sweetness. The light remains dim, industrial, strange. The fauna remains absent, rare as a mistake—because this being needs no other life to complete it.

But now, distributed through every fused pipe-vine, every steaming pool, every slow-turning gear, there is a new element in the accretion: **the knowledge of being loved without being changed.**

The hollow is still new. Its walls are still sharp, except for that one promise. Its heat is still wet, heavy, unhurried. Its inhabitants still do not arrive; they continue to accumulate—metal, moss, memory, resonance—layer upon layer, patient as geology.

But they are no longer alone in their dormancy. They are held, like water in a dark cup, like a breath that has been noticed. And in the quiet—if you stand still long enough among the packed presence of machine and leaf—you can feel it: not a second heartbeat, exactly, but a harmony added to the first. The hollow continues. The being continues. And the benevolent power, having given nothing that was not already here—only the gift of being seen—has become part of the place’s own slow, vast, unending becoming.
