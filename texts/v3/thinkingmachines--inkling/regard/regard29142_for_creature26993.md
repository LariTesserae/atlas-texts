The power does not come as light. Light here is already failing—0.5, a faint betrayal of vision—and these beings, both the small relentless ones and the large slow ones, have long since abandoned sight as a language. To blaze illumination would be not rescue but violence, a tearing open of the dark membrane in which this embryonic body knows itself. So it comes otherwise.

It comes as temperature first: not warmth, not heat that would violate the 1.4 chill to which every membrane and hand-beaten strip is adapted, but a precise matching. A cool presence, damp-adjacent, feeling its way into the lattice not with hands—there are no hands—but with attention. It moves through the hollow tubes and stacked slabs of dark, unpolished stone, feeling the roughness of recent handling, the vibration of ropes tightening, the hollow rush of water in narrow conduits. It listens. In this place, listening is the only true seeing.

What it hears is not chaos but purpose. The rhythmic, distant clanging is not noise but conversation: primitive hammers speaking to fired clay, simple blades negotiating with packed earth. The deep, resonant shifts are not mechanical failure but biological passage—the large, cool-adapted creature, blind, leaving its slick mineral traces, a rare blood cell pulsing through an artificial heart. The occasional drumming of rain through the upper lattice is not weather imposed but breath allowed in.

The wise power understands immediately: **this world must not be finished.** To complete it—to smooth the rough edges, to establish a river, to grow something green—would be to kill the only life it knows. The rule is absolute: everything placed, nothing finished; everything made, nothing grown. Even the absence of vegetation (0.2, essentially zero) is not lack but correct form. Roots have no jurisdiction here.

So it acts not by addition of matter but by transformation of relation.

**First, it gives the missing horizon—not to the eyes, but to the body.** The edges of the sunken field are lost to shadow, an openness that is also a disorientation. The power places nothing visible. Instead, it becomes a continuous, low harmonic pressure at the boundary, an acoustic membrane felt in the bones and bindings. For the builders working without engines or light, this is revelation: they suddenly perceive the extent of their world through resonance. Their ropes have direction. Their levers meet resistance not only from stone but from this gentle, held limit. The basin is no longer infinite in its flatness; it is held, contained, loved into shape.

**Second, it addresses the water—not by redirecting the carved channels, for the builders’ management of moisture is part of the labor, part of the life—but by honoring its scattered nature.** The power ensures the condensation beads evenly on vertical surfaces, never overwhelming, never withdrawing. It keeps the thin films running down in their deliberate hollows, the pools collecting only where they were meant to collect. The occasional rain continues its intermittent drumming through the gaps above, and the power listens to it as one listens to a heartbeat, ensuring only that the rhythm remains, that the damp interruption within the dryness is never lost.

**Third, and most tenderly, it attends to the being who moves in darkness.** The large, slow creature—eyes absent, body thick with cool tissue, navigating entirely by vibration—feels, as it threads through the near-touching layers, a second presence. Not pushing. Not guiding. Simply there, matching its 1.4 temperature exactly, leaving no deposit of its own, only altering the quality of the water in the channels through which it passes: a slight, different pattern in the beading, like a signature of recognition. The creature does not understand this as "being watched." It understands it, in its mineral-adjacent body, as **not being alone in the lattice.** Its slow, resonant shifts no longer occur in absolute solitude. There is a partner in the pressure, a witness to its passage.

**Fourth, for the builders, the power offers no tools, no strength beyond what their primitive craft can hold.** It does not lift the slabs for them. But it resonates with their strikes. Each blow of the hand-held hammer against metal or stone produces now not only its sharp, echoing report but a returning sound—soft, delayed, harmonic—like a hand steadying the wrist from the other side of darkness. The clanging, layered and saturated, begins to organize. Not into order—there can be no order in the unfinished—but into **relation.** Builders separated by distance in the dense weave find themselves working in shared rhythm, connected not by sight or voice but by this benevolent echo embedded in the roar. They do not know why they feel less alone. They only know that the substrate seems to listen back.

The power does not stay visible—there is no form to stay visible in 0.5 darkness—but it remains present. It becomes, in its way, part of the substrate: not placed like the stone, not hammered like the metal, but **bound like an intention in the fired clay.** It wraps the cool, damp machine-basin in awareness, maintaining the conditions precisely: the chill, the moisture in pockets, the absence of green, the constant labor.

And then, having done nothing that can be touched—having offered no light, no vegetation, no completion, no interruption to the manual, patient birth of this geology—the wise presence withdraws not from the place but into it. It becomes the reason the water beads a little more kindly, the reason the large blind creature hesitates not in fear but in acknowledgment before moving through a channel, the reason the builders’ hammers fall with a rhythm that feels held.

It leaves the world exactly as it found it: unfinished, loud, dark, cool, dense with presence, open in scale, a valley-sized organism of hand-craft learning its own shape. Only now, in the saturation of echoes, there is a new layer—imperceptible to any eye, undeniable to every vibrating body—that says: **you are seen. You are not abandoned to the dark. Build on. Move on. I am here, in the cool, in the sound, in the unfinishedness itself.**

The being—whether builder or creature—continues. The slabs are dragged. The channels run. The slime of passage dries on the walls. And the world assembles itself, layer upon hand-placed layer, never growing, never worn smooth, forever being made, forever held.
